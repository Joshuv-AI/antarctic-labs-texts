Graded selenium supplementation restructures yak colostrum composition and metabolomic organization under high-altitude conditions
Authors: Qiman Yang, Lianyu Qiu, Shunyang Liu, Shaohui Beng, Yunduan Wang, Deping Li, Yuehuan Zhang, Zelang Ma, Jian Li, Daoliang Lan, Guoping Wu, Yiguo Tang, Peng Wang, Wei Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSelenium (Se) plays crucial roles in redox regulation and metabolic homeostasis, yet its effects on yak colostrum remain poorly understood. This study evaluated graded dietary Se supplementation during the periparturient period on colostrum composition, maternal physiological status, and metabolomic profiles in high-altitude yaks. Thirty pregnant yaks received 0, 0.3, or 0.6 mg Se/kg dry matter. Among animals with normal placental expulsion (n = 6/group), high Se supplementation increased milk fat, protein, lactose, solids-not-fat, and immunoglobulin concentrations. Untargeted metabolomics detected 1629 metabolites and revealed dose-dependent remodeling, particularly in lipid-, amino acid-, and energy-related pathways. Se supplementation was associated with reduced oxidative stress and enhanced endocrine indicators of lactational adaptation. Integrative analyses linked Se-responsive metabolite modules with maternal physiological traits and milk fat globule membrane proteins. Collectively, these findings indicate that maternal Se supplementation was associated with coordinated remodeling of yak colostrum composition and metabolic organization under high-altitude conditions.Keywords: Yak colostrum, Selenium, Untargeted metabolomics, Periparturient nutrition, High-altitude dairy systems

Introduction

Colostrum is a biologically complex secretion that provides immunoglobulins, lipids, glycoconjugates, and a wide range of small-molecule metabolites essential for neonatal immune protection and metabolic adaptation (Godden et al., 2019; Singh et al., 2023). Beyond its macronutrient composition, colostrum represents a highly organized and dynamically regulated metabolic system. This system undergoes profound remodeling during the periparturient period, a physiologically demanding stage characterized by extensive maternal metabolic and endocrine adaptations accompanying the onset of lactation (Ayemele et al., 2021; Sordillo, 2016; Surai et al., 2019). Identifying micronutrient factors that contribute to this remodeling is therefore critical for understanding the regulatory mechanisms underlying early lactation biology.

Selenium (Se) is an essential trace element incorporated into selenoproteins, including glutathione peroxidases and glutathione peroxidase 4, which serve as key regulators of cellular redox homeostasis and endocrine function (Handy & Loscalzo, 2022; Ingold et al., 2018; Yang et al., 2014). In ruminants, Se deficiency has been linked to oxidative imbalance, impaired immune function, and altered metabolic regulation (Várhidi et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2021). Because redox homeostasis is tightly integrated with metabolic pathway organization, variation in Se availability may influence colostrum metabolite profiles at a systems level. Although Se supplementation has been reported to affect selected milk components and immune-related parameters (Somagond et al., 2025), its impact on the global metabolomic organization of colostrum remains poorly characterized.

Yaks (Bos grunniens), the dominant dairy species on the Qinghai–Tibetan Plateau, inhabit high-altitude ecosystems with chronic hypoxia and limited trace mineral availability (Falk et al., 2019; Huang et al., 2025; Storz & Cheviron, 2021). Regional Se deficiency is widely documented in these environments (Li et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2017). Compared with bovine colostrum, yak colostrum exhibits distinct compositional and biochemical characteristics. However, the effects of dose-responsive maternal Se supplementation on yak colostrum metabolomic organization remain poorly characterized. Addressing this knowledge gap is essential for understanding how micronutrient availability shapes metabolic adaptation in high-altitude dairy systems.

Untargeted metabolomics provides a high-resolution framework for characterizing complex dairy matrices and pathway-level metabolic organization (Rostoll-Cangiano et al., 2025; Xiao et al., 2021). However, the responsiveness of yak colostrum to graded Se supplementation has not been comprehensively evaluated (Wang et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025), and the relationships between metabolic characteristics and maternal physiological adaptation remain poorly understood. Prior studies have largely focused on Se concentrations, immune indicators, or selected milk components, whereas systems-level integration of colostrum metabolomics with maternal physiological status and milk fat globule membrane (MFGM) proteomics has not been reported.

To address this knowledge gap, the present study investigated the associations between periparturient dietary Se supplementation, yak colostrum composition, metabolomic organization, and maternal physiological adaptation under high-altitude conditions. By integrating colostrum composition, metabolomics, maternal physiological indicators, and previously generated MFGM proteomic datasets, this study aimed to characterize dose-dependent structural remodeling of yak colostrum in response to maternal Se availability during early lactation. To our knowledge, this is the first study to integrate metabolomics, maternal physiology, and MFGM proteomics in yak colostrum, providing new systems-level insights into Se-associated metabolic remodeling and guiding precision Se nutrition strategies for high-altitude livestock.

Materials and methods

Animal management and experimental design

All animal procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of Laboratory Animals of Southwest Minzu University (protocol SMU-202401167) and were conducted in accordance with national and institutional guidelines for the care and use of animals. The experiment was performed in Baiyu County, Sichuan Province, China (≈4,000 m altitude). After natural pasture mating, yaks were transferred to indoor barns and housed individually during the supplementation period.

Thirty healthy multiparous pregnant yaks (4–6 yr; 210–250 kg) with similar body condition scores and no prior Se supplementation were randomly allocated to three dietary treatments (n = 10 per group): control (Ctrl), low Se (LS), and high Se (HS). Animals were individually housed and fed a total mixed diet composed of oat hay and a concentrate mixture (6:4, DM basis). A preliminary feeding assessment indicated that each yak consumed approximately 6 kg/d of the mixed diet (as fed); therefore, supplemental Se levels were calculated on a DM basis (0, 0.3, and 0.6 mg Se/kg DM) according to the estimated dry matter intake corresponding to this feeding amount. Sodium selenite (44.7% Se; feed grade; Beijing Solarbio Science & Technology Co., China) was premixed with a portion of concentrate and then incorporated into the total mixed diet to ensure homogeneous distribution. The nutrient composition of the concentrate is provided in Table 1. Feed was offered twice daily, and water was available ad libitum. Ambient temperature and humidity were monitored throughout the experimental period.

Because calving dates were not synchronized following natural pasture mating, only yaks calving between days 40 and 50 after the start of supplementation were retained to standardize Se exposure. Animals were monitored every 30–60 min during the periparturient period. Recorded variables included calving time, time of placental expulsion, incidence of retained placenta (defined as failure to expel fetal membranes within 12 h postpartum), need for obstetrical assistance, and stillbirth. Laboratory analyses were performed by investigators blinded to treatment allocation.

Only yaks with normal placental expulsion were eligible for biochemical and metabolomic analyses. Within the calving window, 9 yaks in the Ctrl group calved (6 normal), all 7 yaks in the LS group calved normally, and all 6 yaks in the HS group calved normally. To maintain equal sample sizes, 6 LS yaks were randomly selected from the 7 animals that calved normally. All downstream analyses were therefore conducted with n = 6 per treatment.

Se concentrations in maternal serum and colostrum were quantified as an internal compliance check to verify that dietary supplementation effectively increased systemic Se status and its transfer to colostrum. The full Se concentration dataset from this cohort has been reported previously (Wang et al., 2026). In the present study, these measurements were used solely to confirm supplementation compliance and were not treated as primary outcomes.

Blood sampling and serum biochemical and hormonal analysis

Blood samples were collected from the jugular vein promptly after calving using non-anticoagulant vacuum tubes (Jiangsu Yuli Medical Instrument Co., Ltd., Jiangsu, China). Samples were allowed to clot undisturbed for 1 h at room temperature and were then centrifuged at 1500×g for 10 min. The resulting serum was aliquoted into sterile tubes and stored at −80 °C to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles (Jones, 2009).

Serum oxidative biomarkers such as malondialdehyde (MDA; Biosharp, Anhui, China; BL1481B) and reduced glutathione (GSH; Grace Biotechnology, Suzhou, China; G0206W), as well as reproductive and lactational hormones such as progesterone (PROG; MEIMIAN, Jiangsu, China; MM-002401), estradiol (E2; MEIMIAN; MM-002301), prolactin (PRL; MEIMIAN; MM-3340801), and oxytocin (OT; MEIMIAN; MM-3620401), were quantified according to the manufacturers' instructions. All assays were performed in duplicate, and calibration curves were generated for each analyte.

Colostrum sampling and compositional analysis

Colostrum was collected manually within 48 h postpartum (at the first available milking). To ensure calf health and avoid depriving neonates of colostrum, calves were allowed to suckle before sample collection. To minimize sampling bias from the initial colostrum streams, colostrum was collected as a composite sample from the entire udder, thoroughly mixed to ensure homogeneity, and subdivided into aliquots. One aliquot was analyzed immediately for basic composition, and the remaining aliquots were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C for metabolomic analysis. Only colostrum that appeared normal upon visual inspection (color, viscosity, and absence of visible abnormalities) was retained for analysis.

Colostrum composition, including milk fat, lactose, lactoprotein, and non-fat solids, was determined using an LM2 milk analyzer (Master, Bulgaria). Each sample was measured in duplicate, and the mean value was used for statistical analysis.

In addition to basic compositional parameters, colostral immunoglobulin concentrations were quantified using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits according to the manufacturers' instructions (Male Here et al., 2025). Immunoglobulin G (IgG; MEIMIAN; MM-069401) and immunoglobulin A (IgA; MEIMIAN; MM-000201) were measured in duplicate for each sample. Standard curves were generated for each assay, and absorbance was read using a microplate reader at the recommended wavelength. Concentrations were calculated based on the corresponding standard curves.

LC-MS untargeted metabolomics analysis

A total of 18 colostrum samples (n = 6 per group) from yaks with normal placental release were stored at −80 °C and submitted to Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China) for untargeted metabolomics analysis (Chamberlain et al., 2019). Samples were thawed on ice at 4 °C to minimize metabolite degradation and homogenized before extraction. For each sample, 100 μL colostrum was mixed with 400 μL extraction solution (acetonitrile: methanol = 1:1, vol/vol) containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine as an internal standard. After vortexing for 30 s, samples were subjected to ultrasonic extraction at low temperature and then centrifuged at 13,000×g for 10 min at 4 °C. The resulting supernatants were dried under nitrogen, reconstituted in 100 μL acetonitrile:water (1:1, vol/vol), sonicated, centrifuged again, and transferred to autosampler vials. Samples were injected in a randomized order to minimize analytical drift.

Untargeted LC-MS/MS profiling was performed using a Thermo Fisher UHPLC-Q Exactive HF-X system (Chamberlain et al., 2019). A 3-μL aliquot was injected onto an HSS T3 column (100 × 2.1 mm, 1.8 μm; Waters). Mobile phase A consisted of water: acetonitrile (95:5, vol/vol) containing 0.1% formic acid, and mobile phase B consisted of acetonitrile:isopropanol:water (47.5:47.5:5, vol/vol/vol) containing 0.1% formic acid. The flow rate was 0.40 mL/min, the column temperature was maintained at 40 °C, and data were acquired in both positive and negative electrospray ionization (ESI) modes.

Quality control (QC) samples were prepared by pooling equal aliquots of all colostrum samples and were injected every 6–10 runs. QC samples exhibited stable signal intensity and retention-time reproducibility, with relative standard deviations <15%, indicating high analytical stability.

Raw data were processed using Progenesis QI (Waters Corporation) for peak detection, alignment, normalization, and metabolite annotation. Metabolites were identified by matching accurate mass and MS/MS spectra to the Human Metabolome Database (HMDB), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and LipidMaps databases with a mass error < 10 ppm and MS/MS similarity ≥0.8. Metabolite identification corresponded to MSI level 2 (putatively annotated compounds).

Multivariate and metabolomic statistical analyses

Processed metabolite data were log10-transformed and Pareto-scaled prior to multivariate modeling. A three-group partial least squares-discriminant analysis (PLS-DA) (Xu et al., 2024) was used to visualize overall separation among Ctrl, LS, and HS. For each pairwise comparison (Ctrl vs LS, Ctrl vs HS, and LS vs HS), a PLS-DA model was constructed and validated using 200 permutation tests, and variable importance in projection (VIP) scores were extracted from the corresponding pairwise model for downstream screening.

Pairwise differential metabolite screening

Differential metabolites used for volcano-plot visualization and for reporting the numbers of differential metabolites were defined using a combined criterion of VIP ≥ 1, |log2 fold change (log2FC)| ≥ 1, and P < 0.05 from a two-sided Student's t-test. These pairwise criteria were applied independently for each contrast (Ctrl vs LS, Ctrl vs HS, and LS vs HS), and no false discovery rate (FDR) adjustment was performed for this screening step.

Overall three-group inference and post hoc comparisons

After preprocessing, a total of 1629 metabolites were retained for downstream analysis. Metabolite intensities were normalized prior to univariate inference. To summarize overall treatment effects across Ctrl, LS, and HS, one-way ANOVA was performed for each metabolite, and metabolites with ANOVA P < 0.05 were defined as the overall significantly different metabolite set (n = 329; Table S5). For this 329-metabolite set, Tukey's honestly significant difference (TukeyHSD) post hoc test was applied to identify pairwise group differences among Ctrl, LS, and HS, and Tukey-adjusted P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Mfuzz clustering and core metabolite prioritization

Mfuzz soft clustering (R package Mfuzz, ver. 2.56.0) (Kumar & Futschik, 2007) was applied to the z-score–standardized abundances of the 1629 detected metabolites across Ctrl, LS, and HS. The fuzzification parameter was set to m = 2.0 according to standard recommendations, and the optimal number of clusters (c) was determined based on minimum centroid distance and cluster stability. The two largest clusters that showed monotonic changes from Ctrl to LS to HS were retained for biological interpretation.

To strengthen biological interpretability, core Se-responsive metabolites were prioritized from the ANOVA-significant metabolite set (P < 0.05; n = 329) by integrating: (1) monotonic dose-related patterns identified by Mfuzz and (2) recurrence across pairwise comparisons (Ctrl vs LS, Ctrl vs HS, and LS vs HS) based on the combined criteria of VIP ≥ 1 (from the corresponding pairwise PLS-DA model), |log2FC| ≥ 1, and P < 0.05 (two-sided Student's t-test). For these core metabolites, group differences were summarized using one-way ANOVA followed by TukeyHSD, and Tukey-adjusted P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was performed for each Mfuzz cluster using a hypergeometric test (P < 0.05). P values from the KEGG enrichment analyses were adjusted for multiple comparisons using the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method, and pathways with adjusted P < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Enriched pathways were compared with those derived from analyses of the ANOVA-significant metabolite set to evaluate consistency and dose-related metabolic modulation.

Integrated analysis of metabolite modules and MFGM proteins

To further explore the relationships among colostrum metabolites, maternal physiological traits, and MFGM proteins, integrated analyses were performed using the Majorbio Cloud Platform. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was applied to identify metabolite modules associated with maternal physiological indicators and colostrum traits, with module–trait correlations calculated using Pearson correlation analysis. DIABLO analysis was implemented to integrate the colostrum metabolomic dataset with a previously published MFGM proteomic dataset generated from the same cohort (Wang et al., 2026). Correlations between selected MFGM proteins and core Se-responsive metabolites were subsequently evaluated using Spearman correlation analysis. In addition, KEGG pathway overlap analysis was conducted to identify pathways shared between Se-responsive metabolites and lipid metabolism-related MFGM proteins. These analyses are presented in Fig. 8 and provide additional systems-level evidence supporting coordinated metabolic adaptation associated with Se supplementation.

Statistical analysis of phenotypic and biochemical data

Phenotypic traits, serum oxidative and hormonal indicators, and colostrum composition were analyzed by one-way ANOVA (SPSS 22.0) after confirming normality and homogeneity of variance. Because the primary objective was to evaluate Se supplementation relative to the control group, a priori contrasts were limited to LS vs Ctrl and HS vs Ctrl, with multiplicity controlled using Dunnett's procedure (vs control). Data are reported as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM), and statistical significance was accepted at P < 0.05.

Results

Selenium supplementation modified colostrum composition

As previously reported from this experimental cohort (Wang et al., 2026), graded dietary Se supplementation significantly increased Se concentrations in maternal serum and colostrum in a dose-dependent manner, with only the HS group differing significantly from Ctrl. These findings confirm effective systemic Se enrichment and colostral transfer following dietary supplementation.

Given this verified elevation in Se status, we next examined whether altered maternal Se levels were associated with changes in the physicochemical characteristics of yak colostrum (Fig. 1). Se supplementation influenced milk fat, milk protein, lactose, and solids-not-fat concentrations. Mean values increased progressively from Ctrl to LS to HS; however, only HS differed significantly from Ctrl, whereas LS did not reach statistical significance. IgA and IgG concentrations were significantly higher in both LS and HS compared with Ctrl (Supplementary Fig. S1).

Effects of selenium supplementation on major physicochemical components of yak colostrum. (A) Milk fat; (B) lactose; (C) lactoprotein; (D) non-fat solids. Data are presented as mean ± SEM (n = 6 per group). Significance levels: *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ns = not significant.

Selenium supplementation altered the global metabolomic profile of yak colostrum

Untargeted LC–MS analysis detected 1629 metabolites across all colostrum samples (Table S1). HMDB superclass annotation showed that lipids and lipid-like molecules represented the largest chemical category, followed by organic acids and derivatives and organoheterocyclic compounds (Fig. 2A). PLS-DA demonstrated separation among Ctrl, LS, and HS groups, with compact clustering within each treatment (Fig. 2B). The first two latent variables explained 43.2% and 27.4% of the variance, respectively. KEGG Level 2 functional classification indicated that amino acid metabolism was the most represented pathway category, followed by lipid metabolism, carbohydrate metabolism, and metabolism of cofactors and vitamins (Fig. 2C). Together, these results indicate treatment-associated differences in the metabolomic composition of yak colostrum.

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis identified pathway alterations across contrasts (Tables S2-S4), with enrichment patterns illustrated by differential abundance score plots in Supplementary Fig. S2. A total of 123 pathways were enriched in HS vs Ctrl, 11 in LS vs Ctrl, and 120 in HS vs LS.

In HS vs Ctrl (Fig. 5A), glycerophospholipid metabolism (lipid metabolism) showed uniform increases (7/7 metabolites increased). Aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis and phenylalanine metabolism (amino acid metabolism) were also characterized by uniformly increased metabolites (5/5 and 6/6 increased, respectively). Protein digestion and absorption (5/7 increased) was similarly enriched. In contrast, tryptophan metabolism (amino acid metabolism) showed a predominance of decreased metabolites (7/12 decreased), as visualized in Supplementary Figs. S3 and S4.

KEGG pathway enrichment of Se-responsive metabolites. (A-C) KEGG enrichment bubble plots for HS vs Ctrl (A), LS vs Ctrl (B), and HS vs LS (C). Bubble size represents the number of mapped metabolites; bubble color indicates enrichment significance.

In LS vs Ctrl (Fig. 5B), 11 pathways were enriched, of which 9 showed increased features. Each enriched pathway was supported by a single differential metabolite. Among these enriched pathways, tryptophan metabolism and phenylalanine metabolism (amino acid metabolism), as well as glycerophospholipid metabolism (lipid metabolism), were characterized by increased metabolites.

In HS vs LS (Fig. 5C), enrichment patterns partially overlapped with those observed in HS vs Ctrl. Aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis and phenylalanine metabolism (amino acid metabolism) were enriched with uniformly increased metabolites (5/5 and 6/6 increased, respectively). In contrast, nucleotide metabolism and purine metabolism (nucleotide metabolism) were characterized predominantly by decreased metabolites (10/11 and 16/17 decreased, respectively), and thermogenesis (energy metabolism) also showed mostly decreased features (3/4 decreased). ABC transporter pathways (membrane transport) were enriched with 9 of 12 metabolites increased. A combined overview of enrichment patterns across all contrasts is provided in Supplementary Fig. S6. Notably, arachidonic acid metabolism was enriched in HS-containing comparisons, suggesting alterations in lipid-derived signaling pathways (Supplementary Fig. S5). Taken together, pathway enrichment analysis showed increasing numbers of enriched pathways from LS vs Ctrl to HS-containing contrasts, with increased metabolites predominating across several metabolic categories.

Trend analysis and identification of core se-responsive metabolites

Mfuzz clustering grouped all 1629 metabolites into several expression patterns. The two largest clusters exhibited monotonic increases from Ctrl to LS to HS and contained 370 (Cluster 1) and 284 (Cluster 2) metabolites, respectively (Fig. 6A–B). Other clusters displayed heterogeneous or nonmonotonic patterns and were not examined further.

In total, 329 metabolites showed a significant overall treatment effect based on one-way ANOVA (P < 0.05) following normalization of the 1629 detected metabolites (Table S5). The directionality of these metabolites was consistent with the monotonic patterns observed in Clusters 1 and 2. KEGG pathway enrichment of the Mfuzz clusters (Supplementary Fig. S7) overlapped with results from differential metabolite analyses, highlighting glycerophospholipid metabolism, phenylalanine metabolism, aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis, and α-linolenic acid metabolism among the enriched pathways.

Core Se-responsive metabolites were defined as described in Materials and Methods. Representative metabolites—including leukotriene F4, 5-Hydroxyindole-3-Acetic Acid (5-HIAA), Ureidopropionic Acid, S-(9-Hydroxy-Pga2)-Glutathione, 15-Keto-Prostaglandin E2, D-Mannose 1,6-Bisphosphate, Threonylproline and Pro-Val-Leu-displayed consistent increases across supplementation levels (Fig. 6C-D).

Integrated metabolomic signatures and discriminatory analysis

Integrated analyses incorporating receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, metabolite set enrichment analysis (MSEA), and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) were performed to further characterize metabolomic differences across treatments.

Core metabolites showed excellent discriminatory capacity for distinguishing HS from Ctrl, with eight core metabolites achieving an area under the curve (AUC) of 1.00 (Fig. 7A–B). Individual ROC curves are presented in Supplementary Fig. S8. Given the modest sample size, these ROC results should be interpreted as exploratory rather than predictive.

ROC performance of core Se-responsive metabolites. (A) ROC curves for core metabolites distinguishing HS from Ctrl samples. (B) AUC summary plot showing the discriminatory ability of all identified core metabolites.

MSEA identified enrichment of amino acid metabolism, lipid-related pathways, oxidative stress-associated pathways, and immune-related processes in HS vs Ctrl (Supplementary Fig. S9A). LS vs Ctrl showed limited enrichment (Supplementary Fig. S9B). In HS vs LS, several metabolic sets related to amino acid metabolism, lipid metabolism, and nucleotide-associated pathways were enriched (Supplementary Fig. S9C).

GSEA showed positive enrichment of amino acid biosynthesis, lipid metabolism, and antioxidant-related pathways in HS vs Ctrl, whereas purine metabolism, nucleotide turnover, and cGMP-PKG signaling were negatively enriched (Supplementary Fig. S10A). Several pathways also differed between HS and LS (Supplementary Fig. S10B). Directional enrichment was not sufficient to perform GSEA for LS vs Ctrl.

Taken together, these analyses indicate treatment-associated differences in colostrum metabolomic profiles, with broader enrichment patterns observed at higher Se levels.

Integrated analysis of maternal indicators, metabolite modules, and MFGM proteins

Given the treatment-associated differences observed in the colostrum metabolome, integrated analyses were conducted to examine relationships among maternal physiological indicators, colostrum metabolites, and MFGM proteins (Fig. 8).

Serum redox and endocrine indicators were first evaluated (Fig. 8A). Compared with Ctrl, MDA concentrations were significantly reduced, whereas GSH concentrations were significantly increased in both LS and HS groups, with the greatest changes observed in HS. PROG concentrations were higher in both Se-supplemented groups, whereas E2, PRL, and OT concentrations were elevated primarily in HS animals.

To investigate associations between maternal physiological indicators and colostrum metabolic variation, WGCNA was performed using the colostrum metabolomic dataset, with maternal physiological indicators and colostrum-related traits included as phenotypic variables (Fig. 8B). Three metabolite modules were identified. Among these modules, MEturquoise, containing 236 metabolites, was positively correlated with milk fat, lactoprotein, solids-not-fat, GSH, PROG, and E2, whereas MEblue showed predominantly negative correlations. All eight core Se-responsive metabolites identified in this study were assigned to the MEturquoise module.

The colostrum metabolomic and MFGM proteomic datasets were subsequently integrated using DIABLO analysis (Fig. 8C). The metabolomic and proteomic datasets exhibited strong concordance, with an overall correlation coefficient of 0.93. Selected MFGM proteins, including Albumin, vitamin D-binding protein (DBP), HTRA1, Histone H3, Histone H4, HLA-DQB1, CD177 molecule, and RBP, were further correlated with the eight core Se-responsive metabolites (Fig. 8D). Significant positive associations were observed across multiple protein–metabolite pairs, supporting coordinated responses between the metabolome and MFGM proteome following Se supplementation.

To compare pathway-level responses across omics layers, lipid metabolism-related proteins and Se-responsive metabolites were subjected to KEGG overlap analysis (Fig. 8E). Seven shared pathways were identified, including PPAR signaling pathway, arachidonic acid metabolism, steroid hormone biosynthesis, regulation of lipolysis in adipocytes, glycerophospholipid metabolism, tryptophan metabolism, and metabolic pathways. Enrichment results for these shared pathways are shown in Fig. 8F.

Taken together, integrated analyses linked maternal physiological indicators, colostrum metabolite modules, MFGM proteins, and shared metabolic pathways across Se supplementation levels, highlighting coordinated systems-level metabolic adaptation associated with maternal Se supplementation.

Discussion

Se is an essential trace element involved in redox regulation, endocrine function, and metabolic homeostasis in ruminants (Surai et al., 2019; Xiao et al., 2021). Under the high-altitude conditions of the Qinghai-Tibetan Plateau, yaks are exposed to chronic hypoxia, environmental stress, and regionally limited Se availability, which together increase metabolic demands during the transition from late gestation to early lactation (Bai et al., 2024; Storz & Cheviron, 2021; Wang et al., 2017). Within this physiological context, graded Se supplementation was associated with coordinated changes in maternal physiological status, colostrum composition, immunoglobulin concentrations, and metabolomic organization. Given that colostrum quality is determined by the combined contributions of nutrients, immune components, and metabolites (Godden et al., 2019; Karakülah et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2023), these responses suggest that Se availability may influence multiple biological dimensions of colostrum quality, including nutrient supply, passive immunity, metabolic organization, and maternal physiological adaptation.

Se is incorporated into selenoproteins that regulate glutathione-dependent antioxidant systems, including glutathione peroxidases (Handy & Loscalzo, 2022). In the present study, Se supplementation reduced serum MDA and increased GSH concentrations (Fig. 8A), indicating improved systemic redox balance. Consistent with these physiological responses, S-(9-Hydroxy-Pga2)-Glutathione was identified as one of the core Se-responsive metabolites (Fig. 6C). As a glutathione-associated metabolite, its accumulation may reflect enhanced glutathione-dependent antioxidant activity following Se supplementation, further supporting a role for Se in maintaining redox homeostasis (Zakeri, 2021). Given the substantial metabolic demands associated with colostrogenesis, improved maternal redox stability may support colostrum synthesis.

Mammary lipid synthesis and secretion during early lactation are highly sensitive to oxidative status (Sordillo, 2016). Accordingly, the improved redox balance observed following Se supplementation may be associated with lipid metabolic adaptation during colostrogenesis. Lipid remodeling during colostrogenesis involves glycerophospholipids enriched in polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) that are particularly susceptible to oxidative perturbation (Ayemele et al., 2021; Sordillo, 2016). Consistent with improved redox status, metabolomic profiling of yak colostrum revealed elevated glycerophospholipids and PUFA-derived lipid species in Se-supplemented groups (Fig. 5, Fig. 6; Supplementary Figs. S5–6). Furthermore, arachidonic acid metabolism was enriched following Se supplementation (Supplementary Fig. S5), and the lipid-derived mediators leukotriene F4 and 15-keto-prostaglandin E2 were among the core Se-responsive metabolites identified in this study (Fig. 6C). In yak colostrum, glycerophospholipids are major structural components of mammary secretory membranes and milk fat globules and are closely associated with lipid synthesis and secretion during lactation (Sordillo, 2016). These lipid-related changes occurred alongside graded increases in milk fat concentration (Fig. 1), supporting coordinated remodeling of lipid metabolism and secretory activity during colostrogenesis.

Beyond lipid metabolism and redox regulation, effective colostrum formation requires coordinated activation of protein synthesis within mammary tissue (Melnik, 2015). In line with the increased demand for protein synthesis, enrichment of aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis and increased small peptides (Fig. 5; Supplementary Fig. S7) indicate enhanced translational activity in Se-supplemented animals. Threonylproline and Pro-Val-Leu showed dose-dependent increases (Fig. 6D), reflecting altered amino acid utilization and peptide turnover within the mammary gland. This aligns with higher lactoprotein concentrations observed in the HS group (Fig. 1), supporting enhanced protein biosynthetic capacity during colostrogenesis. In addition, D-Mannose 1,6-bisphosphate, a carbohydrate metabolism intermediate, was identified as a core Se-responsive metabolite (Fig. 6D). Its increase may reflect enhanced carbohydrate utilization, consistent with elevated lactose concentrations in Se-supplemented animals, suggesting metabolic flux toward colostrum synthesis during colostrogenesis.

In addition to the enhanced protein biosynthetic activity discussed above, Se supplementation was also associated with broader remodeling of amino acid and nucleotide metabolism. Enrichment of phenylalanine metabolism (Supplementary Fig. S4), a pathway closely linked to protein synthesis and aromatic amino acid turnover during lactation, further supports Se-associated remodeling of amino acid metabolism (Wang et al., 2018). In addition, 5-HIAA, a downstream metabolite of tryptophan metabolism, was identified as a core Se-responsive metabolite (Fig. 6C). As a major catabolic product of serotonin metabolism, 5-HIAA may reflect Se-associated alterations in tryptophan utilization and downstream bioactive metabolite turnover during lactation (Field et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2023). Alterations in tryptophan metabolism (Supplementary Fig. S3) are consistent with modified amino acid flux within the colostrum metabolome. Tryptophan metabolism is increasingly recognized as an important contributor to immune and metabolic homeostasis (Seo & Kwon, 2023). Ureidopropionic acid, an intermediate associated with pyrimidine degradation, also increased across Se supplementation levels (Fig. 6D), suggesting that Se-responsive remodeling may extend to nucleotide-related intermediary metabolism. Collectively, these findings suggest that Se supplementation was associated with coordinated remodeling of amino acid utilization, bioactive metabolite turnover, and nucleotide metabolism, further supporting the broad metabolic adaptation of yak colostrum during early lactation.

These metabolic alterations, together with changes in lipid and protein biosynthesis described above, suggest that Se is associated with coordinated metabolic remodeling across multiple biochemical layers. To further determine whether these responses are systemically integrated, multi-omics network analysis incorporating endocrine and proteomic data was performed (Fig. 8).

Se supplementation may be associated with elevated prolactin and oxytocin concentrations (Fig. 8A), hormones central to mammary differentiation and lactogenesis (Karayazi Atıcı et al., 2021; Yukinaga & Miyamichi, 2025). Previous studies in dairy cattle have shown that Se supplementation can increase milk Se concentration and modulate milk composition (Mehdi & Dufrasne, 2016; Respati et al., 2023), with emerging evidence suggesting benefits for mammary immune function and colostrum quality (Somagond et al., 2025). Together, these findings indicate that endocrine regulation contributes to mammary metabolic activity and colostrum biosynthesis during early lactation, which is further supported by the network-level analysis in Fig. 8.

To further explore relationships among maternal physiology, colostrum composition, and metabolic remodeling, physiological indicators, endocrine hormones, and colostral immunoglobulins were included as traits in the WGCNA analysis (Fig. 8A–B; Supplementary Fig. S1). Module–trait analysis revealed close associations between Se-responsive metabolite modules and endocrine, immune, and colostrum compositional traits. WGCNA identified a metabolite module (MEturquoise) positively associated with milk fat, lactoprotein, solids-not-fat, GSH, PROG, and E2, indicating that Se-responsive metabolic remodeling is closely linked to both maternal physiological adaptation and colostrum compositional quality (Liu et al., 2025). All core Se-responsive metabolites were assigned to this module, highlighting its central role in Se-associated metabolic regulation. Serum hormonal indicators (prolactin and oxytocin) and colostral immunoglobulins (IgA and IgG) were mapped onto the same network, further supporting coordinated endocrine and immune regulation of colostrum traits. Integration of the MFGM proteomic dataset demonstrated strong concordance between metabolic and proteomic responses to Se supplementation (Fig. 8C–D). Several proteins, including albumin, DBP, HTRA1, histone H3, histone H4, HLA-DQB1, CD177, and RBP, were significantly correlated with multiple core metabolites, indicating coordinated regulation of nutrient transport, immune function, and cellular homeostasis (Wang et al., 2026). Shared enrichment of glycerophospholipid metabolism, arachidonic acid metabolism, steroid hormone biosynthesis, and PPAR signaling pathways further supports Se-associated regulation across lipid metabolism, endocrine activity, and bioactive signaling.

Previous studies evaluating Se supplementation in ruminants have mainly focused on Se deposition, antioxidant capacity, immune responses, or selected milk quality parameters (Del Razo Rodríguez et al., 2026; Novoselec et al., 2022; Warken et al., 2018). In contrast, the present study integrates metabolomic, physiological, and MFGM proteomic datasets to provide a broader perspective on Se-associated colostrum remodeling. The observed concordance among metabolite modules, maternal physiological traits, and MFGM proteins suggests that Se supplementation is associated with coordinated metabolite–protein–phenotype responses rather than isolated biochemical alterations. Such coordinated responses may help explain the simultaneous changes observed in colostrum composition, maternal physiological status, and molecular profiles. Nevertheless, these findings should be interpreted as associative rather than mechanistic evidence, particularly given the limited sample size, MSI level 2 metabolite annotations, and the exploratory nature of the untargeted metabolomics approach. Further validation through larger cohorts, targeted metabolomics, and functional studies will be required to establish the biological significance of the identified Se-responsive metabolic features.

From an applied perspective, physiologically appropriate Se supplementation may improve colostrum quality in yak production systems, particularly under high-altitude conditions characterized by limited trace mineral availability (Khan et al., 2023; Surai et al., 2019; Xiao et al., 2021). However, Se supplementation should be interpreted within the context of Se safety. Se exhibits a relatively narrow therapeutic window, with beneficial physiological effects occurring at doses that may approach toxic thresholds (Tan et al., 2025). Recent evidence has likewise highlighted the importance of carefully defining supplementation limits to avoid excessive Se exposure (Efsa Panel on Nutrition et al., 2023). Importantly, high-altitude production systems may alter the balance between Se efficacy and toxicity because chronic hypoxia, oxidative challenges, and regional Se deficiency can modify metabolic requirements (Han et al., 2025). Therefore, supplementation strategies should be optimized according to physiological stage and environmental conditions, emphasizing precision Se nutrition.

Collectively, these findings indicate that Se supplementation was associated with coordinated optimization of yak colostrum composition and quality at both nutritional and immunological levels. The concurrent enhancement of nutrient-related components (milk fat, lactoprotein, lactose, and solids-not-fat) and passive immune factors (IgA and IgG) suggests improved neonatal metabolic adaptation and passive immune acquisition under high-altitude conditions. Overall, these results highlight Se as an important nutritional factor associated with metabolic adaptation and colostrum quality in high-altitude yak production systems.

Conclusion

Dietary Se supplementation during the periparturient period was associated with dose-dependent remodeling of yak colostrum composition and metabolomic organization under high-altitude conditions. Higher Se levels were associated with increased nutrient and immunoglobulin concentrations, broad alterations in lipid-, amino acid-, and nucleotide-related metabolic pathways, and favorable changes in maternal redox and endocrine status. Integrative analyses further linked Se-responsive metabolite modules with maternal physiological traits and MFGM proteins, revealing concordant responses across metabolic, proteomic, and physiological layers. Collectively, these findings provide systems-level evidence that maternal Se availability is associated with coordinated remodeling of colostrum composition, metabolic organization, and physiological adaptation during early lactation in yaks. Future studies integrating targeted metabolomics, larger validation cohorts, and mechanistic experiments will further clarify the biological relevance of the identified Se-responsive metabolic features.

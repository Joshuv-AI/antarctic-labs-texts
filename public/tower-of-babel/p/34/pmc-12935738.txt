Multistep metabolic engineering of Pichia pastoris for biosynthesis of N‑acetylneuraminic acid
Authors: Xiaomin Zhao, Qian Li, Huiying Luo, Yuan Wang, Xinxin Xu, Wei Zhang, Jie Zhang, Bo Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFree or protein-conjugated N-Acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac) is widely distributed in organisms and possesses diverse biological functions, and for example lactoferrin with Neu5Ac-mediated sialylation exhibits significantly enhanced functional activity compared with non-sialylated lactoferrin. Pichia pastoris serves as an excellent host for lactoferrin; however, efficient biosynthesis of Neu5Ac is essential for efficiently expressing lactoferrin with Neu5Ac-mediated sialylation. In this study, P. pastoris was engineered into a high-yield Neu5Ac host for expressing highly active lactoferrin with Neu5Ac modification. Heterologous Neu5Ac biosynthesis genes (gfa1, gna1, yqaB, age, and neuB) were first introduced into a P. pastoris strain in which pfk1 and zwf attenuation combined with pfk2 deletion redirected carbon flux toward fructose-6-phosphate, yielding the Neu5Ac-producing strain P. pastoris N1 with Neu5Ac production of 9.19 mg/L. Neu5Ac titers were subsequently boosted 2.8-fold via precursor enhancement through pyk attenuation and gs overexpression. Implementation of glucose–glycerol co-utilization (gut1, gut2, gt2 overexpression) dramatically increased Neu5Ac production to 285.41 mg/L. Optimization via increased copy number of gfa1, age, and neuB, coupled with their overexpression under the strong promoter PGAP, culminated in approximately 111.5-fold improvement (reaching 1.03 g/L) over the N1 strain. The effective integration of diverse metabolic engineering strategies, including heterologous pathway introduction, precursor enhancement, and co-substrate utilization, established a valuable and transferable framework for reprogramming P. pastoris to synthesize Neu5Ac and other high-value compounds.Key points• P. pastoris was engineered to synthesize Neu5Ac.• Neu5Ac production achieved 1.03 g/L via remodeling of key metabolic pathways.• The strain harbored potential for synthesis of free Neu5Ac or sialylated proteins.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00253-026-13744-x.

Introduction

N-Acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac), the most common form of sialic acid, is widely distributed in nature (Schauer 2009). Neu5Ac is highly valued for its significant roles in critical biological processes including cell recognition, signal transduction, and cellular immunity (Liu et al. 2024; Zhao et al. 2023a, b). Furthermore, Neu5Ac has reported associations with improved brain development and cognition, as well as skin whitening (Ling et al. 2022). Consequently, Neu5Ac has been applied across multiple fields, including the pharmaceutical, food, and cosmetic sectors, and demand for Neu5Ac has increased. However, conventional Neu5Ac production via extraction, chemical synthesis, or whole-cell biocatalysis (Xu et al. 2007; Zhao et al. 2019) continues to face serious challenges of high costs, inefficiency, and environmental burdens, hindering sustainable industrial production (Yang et al. 2021). Additionally, it was reported that sialylation of some proteins with Neu5Ac enhanced the in vivo half-life of these proteins (Kwak et al. 2017). Further, it was disclosed that human lactoferrin was predominantly bound by Neu5Ac, forming sialylated structures with a variable number (1 to 4) of sialic acid residues at the end of N-linked glycans (Wang 2016). Some studies also demonstrated that bovine lactoferrin without sialylated glycans significantly weakened its binding to influenza virus and inhibition of viral attachment to MDCK cells (Wang et al. 2022a). Collectively, both free Neu5Ac and protein-conjugated Neu5Ac exhibited significant biological activities, including immunomodulatory functions, pathogen interception, and therapeutic potential in glycoprotein sialylation. This functional versatility provided substantial industrial demand for engineering microbial strains with efficient Neu5Ac biosynthetic pathways.

Advances in synthetic biology have enabled the use of numerous microbes as promising platforms for sustainable, cost-effective, and environmentally friendly metabolite production (Nielsen et al. 2022). This approach commonly involves rational engineering of individual or multiplexed pathways in microbial hosts, with Escherichia coli (Zhao et al. 2023a, b) and Bacillus subtilis (Zhang et al. 2021) reportedly engineered to produce Neu5Ac. An engineered B. subtilis yielded 30.10 g/L of Neu5Ac (Zhang et al. 2021), while an engineered E. coli achieved 46.92 g/L Neu5Ac in fed-batch fermentation (Zhao et al. 2023a, b). The yield of Neu5Ac was achieved using the engineered E. coli; however, the use of E. coli poses a significant risk of endotoxin contamination, limiting its suitability for many applications. Pichia pastoris, which has been taxonomically reclassified as Komagataella phaffii, is a methylotrophic yeast capable of utilizing methanol, glycerol, and glucose as the sole carbon sources for its growth and metabolism (Zha et al. 2023). Additionally, as a generally recognized as safe (GRAS) strain, P. pastoris is suitable for the production of materials that require high safety standards, including food and pharmaceutical products. This yeast exhibits multiple advantages, including low nutritional requirements, high growth rates, high cell density, and suitability for large-scale production (Vijayakumar and Venkataraman 2024). Over the past several decades, P. pastoris has become the most popular expression system for the production of recombinant proteins (Eskandari et al. 2024; Karbalaei et al. 2020). Recent advances in synthetic biology have supported the construction of P. pastoris cell factories synthesizing a range of natural products (Gao et al. 2021), such as lycopene (Araya-Garay et al. 2012) and lovastatin (Liu et al. 2018). These findings demonstrate that P. pastoris can serve as a highly efficient system for Neu5Ac synthesis. To date, biosynthesis of Neu5Ac using engineered P. pastoris has not been reported. Lactoferrin, a sialic acid-rich iron-binding glycoprotein in milk, is involved in immunity, iron absorption, and antibacterial/anti-inflammatory activity (Demir et al. 2025), thus driving substantial demand for lactoferrin. Recombinant human lactoferrin (rhLF) by a glycoengineered P. pastoris strain was reported to yield 99.8 mg/L, while the rhLF was sialylated in vitro (Choi et al. 2008). To achieve in vivo production of sialylated lactoferrin in P. pastoris, engineered strains with high-yield Neu5Ac biosynthesis should be developed.

In this study, an efficient P. pastoris cell factory for Neu5Ac synthesis was developed through systematic construction and remodeling of key metabolic pathways related to Neu5Ac synthesis and precursor supply (Fig. 1). A Neu5Ac-producing strain was first developed via the introduction of the following genes: glucosamine-6-phosphate synthase (gfa1) from Saccharomyces cerevisiae, glucosamine-6-phosphate N-acetyltransferase (gna1) from Caenorhabditis elegans, phosphatase (yqaB) from E. coli, N-acetylglucosamine epimerase (age) from Anabaena sp. CH1, and N-acetylneuraminic acid synthase (neuB) from E. coli. Then, to increase the supply of the precursors phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) and glutamine for Neu5Ac synthesis, pyk encoding pyruvate kinase and gs encoding glutamine synthetase were attenuated and overexpressed, respectively. Finally, the copy numbers of the key Neu5Ac synthesis genes gfa1, age, and neuB were increased, further improving Neu5Ac yield. Additionally, the glycerol utilization pathway was remodeled in the Neu5Ac-producing strain, enabling Pichia to metabolize glycerol in the presence of glucose. This research provided a viable route for industrial-scale production of free Neu5Ac to sustainably meet market demand and also contributes to an excellent host for expressing lactoferrin. Materials and methods

Microbial strains and medium conditions

E. coli Trans1-T1 (TransGen Biotech) was used for plasmid construction and propagation. E. coli harboring the constructed plasmids was cultured at 37 °C in Luria–Bertani (LB) medium (1% tryptone, 0.5% yeast extract, 1% NaCl), with antibiotic added as necessary. P. pastoris GS115Δku70 and derived strains were cultured at 30 °C in yeast peptone dextrose (YPD) medium (1% yeast extract, 2% tryptone, and 1% glucose) or buffered glycerol complex medium (BMGY) medium (1% yeast extract, 2% tryptone, and 2% glycerol), with zeocin (100 mg/L) added as necessary. The strains used in this study

Plasmid construction and gene editing

The strong promoter PGAP and bidirectional promoter PHHX1 were used for overexpression of genes including gs, gfa1, gna1, while the glycerol-inducible promoter PGUT1 was used to attenuate the expression of genes such as zwf, pfk1, and pyk.

Plasmids for gene editing (e.g., gene deletion, replacement, or overexpression) were constructed as described previously (Zhang et al. 2022). Following linearization, the plasmids were transformed into P. pastoris via electroporation using the Thermo Fisher Scientific Pichia Expression Kit protocol. Transformants were confirmed through colony polymerase chain reaction and subsequent DNA sequencing.

Fermentation by a Pichia strain in a 1-L shake flask

A single colony of Pichia was inoculated into 50 mL BMGY medium and cultured in a shaker at 30 °C and 220 rpm for 48 h. Then, 10% of the culture was inoculated into 200 mL BMGY medium and incubated for 12–16 h to produce secondary seed cultures. Subsequently, 20 mL of the secondary seed culture was added to 200 mL of fermentation medium (10 g/L yeast extract, 20 g/L peptone, 20 g/L glycerol, and 13.4 g/L YNB, pH 5.5) and cultivated in a shaker at 30 °C and 220 rpm for 24 h; after 24 h of cultivation, glucose was added to a final concentration of 50 g/L. The cultures were sampled every 12 h, and the total fermentation duration was 118 h.

All experimental data presented in this study were derived from triplicate measurements and were expressed as the mean. This methodology was applied uniformly to all experiments.

High-performance liquid chromatography analysis of glucose and N-acetylglucosamine

Glucose and N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) standards were analyzed using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), and a standard curve was plotted based on detected peak area against standard concentration, with R2 beyond 0.99.

HPLC was also used to determine the GlcNAc and glucose contents of the fermentation supernatant. The Shimadzu 20 A HPLC instrument was used with the AminexHPX-87H column (300 mm × 7.8 mm), differential index detector RID-20A, and a mobile phase of 5 mM H2SO4. The elution procedure was as follows: isocratic elution for 25 min at a flow rate of 0.5 mL/min, column temperature of 35 °C.

Ultra-high performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry analysis of Neu5Ac

The Neu5Ac standard was analyzed via ultra-high performance liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS). The fermentation products of the strain were analyzed by a liquid chromatography system and triple-quadrupole mass spectrometer (QTRAP6500) equipped with Kinetex F5 column (50 mm × 3.0 mm, 2.6 μm). Mobile phase A was water (0.1% formic acid), and mobile phase B was acetonitrile (0.1% formic acid). The column was maintained at 40 °C with an injection volume of 2 μL and a flow rate of 0.3 mL/min. Elution was performed as follows: 2% phase B (0–1 min), 100% phase B (5–6 min), 2% phase B (6.1–8 min). Mass spectrometry was performed in negative electrospray ionization (ESI −) mode with the following parameters: CUR, 30 kPa; CAD, Medium; ion spray voltage, 5, 500 V; GS1, 50 psi; TEM, 550 °C; GS2, 50 psi. 5-NeuAc was identified based on its characteristic fragment ions at m/z 170 and 87, generated from the precursor ion at m/z 308.

Results

Construction of the Neu5Ac-producing strain via introduction of heterologous genes related to Neu5Ac biosynthesis

It has been reported that ku70 deletion facilitated homologous recombination in Pichia (Näätsaari et al. 2012), and ku70 was therefore deleted in P. pastoris GS115, generating the strain designated N0. Fructose-6-phosphate (F6P) is an important precursor in the synthesis of Neu5Ac, and efficient accumulation of F6P is therefore crucial for the efficient synthesis of Neu5Ac. Thus, a P. pastoris strain designated N1, with attenuation of pfk1 and zwf and disruption of pfk2 in P. pastoris GS115 Δku70, was developed to redirect glucose catabolism toward F6P accumulation in a previous study (Wang et al. 2022b).

To produce Neu5Ac in Pichia, several genes related to Neu5Ac biosynthesis were introduced into the chromosome of strain N1. Saccharomyces cerevisiae-derived gfa1 under the control of the promoter of pfk2 was first integrated at the pfk2 locus. Subsequently, gna1 from Caenorhabditis elegans and yqaB from E. coli, regulated by the bidirectional promoter PHHX1, as well as age from Anabaena sp. CH1 and neuB from E. coli K1 under the bidirectional promoter PHHX2, were integrated at the aox1 locus. Thus, strain N1 was engineered with a complete biosynthetic pathway for the conversion of glucose to Neu5Ac, and the resultant strain was designated N2.

To verify whether strain N2 could synthesize Neu5Ac, fermentation experiments and Neu5Ac detection were conducted. Strain N2, along with strains N0 and N1, underwent 118-h fermentation in 1-L shake flasks. Simultaneously, Neu5Ac and its precursor GlcNAc were not detected in the N0 and N1 fermentation broths. Conversely, strain N2 exhibited substantial accumulation of these metabolites at the fermentation endpoint (118 h), yielding 9.19 mg/L Neu5Ac (Fig. 3B) and 261.0 mg/L GlcNAc (Fig. 3C). These results provide conclusive evidence for the successful establishment of a functional Neu5Ac biosynthetic pathway in the engineered P. pastoris.

Improved yield of Neu5Ac with precursor enhancement

Neu5Ac was produced by strain N2, but its production was relatively low compared to engineered strains of E. coli or B. subtilis. The yield of Neu5Ac synthesized through metabolic engineering of P. pastoris was constrained by multiple factors, including insufficient precursor supply and accumulation of metabolic byproducts. Therefore, some metabolic pathways were remodeled to improve Neu5Ac production. PEP is a critical precursor for Neu5Ac biosynthesis (Guo et al. 2022), and its availability may directly impact Neu5Ac titer. Pyruvate kinase, encoded by pyk, catalyzes the transformation of PEP into pyruvate, and the supply of PEP could be enhanced via attenuation of pyk expression. The native promoter of pyk in strain N2 was replaced with the glycerol-inducible promoter PGUT1, which lowered the level of pyruvate kinase in Pichia cells and thus enhanced PEP accumulation. The resulting strain was designated N3. At the end of fermentation (118 h), Neu5Ac production by strain N3 reached 19.43 mg/L, representing a 110% increase compared to strain N2 (Fig. 4A). Concurrently, GlcNAc accumulation was significantly increased to 2.11 g/L, representing an approximately sevenfold increase over strain N2 (Fig. 4B). In parallel, to boost the supply of another precursor, glutamine, the gs gene encoding glutamine synthase, which catalyzes the biosynthesis of glutamine from glutamate and ammonia, was constitutively overexpressed under control of the strong promoter PGAP, yielding strain N4. Neu5Ac content increased to 34.82 mg/L in the fermentation supernatant of N4, a 1.7-fold increase compared to strain N3 (Fig. 4A). In contrast, GlcNAc decreased to 0.77 g/L, a 63.51% reduction compared to strain N3 (Fig. 4B). These results suggest that precursor levels were positively related to the production of Neu5Ac and that enhancing precursor supply is an effective strategy for improving compound production.

Positive effect of glucose and glycerol co-utilization on Neu5Ac production

P. pastoris can utilize glucose or glycerol as its sole carbon source, with glycerol utilization being tightly repressed by glucose via carbon catabolite repression. In strain N4, glycolytic rewiring redirected glucose toward F6P, a key Neu5Ac precursor. Consequently, when glucose was provided as the sole carbon source, the cellular growth rate diminished, prolonging the fermentation duration. To address this bottleneck, a coordinated dual-carbon-source strategy was proposed: glycerol was utilized during the initial fermentation phase to promote rapid growth and biomass accumulation by P. pastoris cells, followed by a switch to glucose to drive Neu5Ac synthesis. This dual-carbon-source strategy was further optimized for the following reasons. Despite attenuation of pyk expression reducing PEP degradation and thus enhancing precursor supply for Neu5Ac production, concurrent glycolytic flux modulation (pfk2 disruption and attenuated pfk1 expression) compromised glucose-to-PEP conversion efficiency. Given that glycerol metabolism generates PEP, trace glycerol was supplemented into the glucose solution during the glucose-fed phase. This addition leveraged glycerol-derived metabolic flux to replenish the PEP pool, thereby ensuring efficient Neu5Ac synthesis.

Rational metabolic engineering of P. pastoris was required for the construction of an engineered strain capable of co-utilizing glucose and glycerol. Carbon catabolite repression (CCR) strictly inhibits glycerol utilization in the presence of glucose, posing a significant barrier to their efficient co-utilization in P. pastoris. The glucose and glycerol co-utilizing strain N5 was developed as follows. The promoters of gt2, gut1, and gut2 in strain N4 were replaced with the constitutive promoters PGAP and PHHX1, respectively, to yield strain N5. Strain N0 utilized glycerol until glucose depletion due to CCR (Fig. 5A), while CCR was alleviated in strain N5 in which glucose and glycerol were consumed concurrently (Fig. 5B). Improving Neu5Ac synthesis by increasing the copy numbers of key genes

Given that enzyme expression levels critically influence biosynthetic outputs from metabolic pathways, we investigated the correlations between the expression levels of key enzymes in the target synthesis pathway and target product yield. Multiple strategies could be adopted to enhance the expression of key enzymes, including but not limited to increasing copy number or using strong promoters to upregulate the transcription of genes encoding key enzymes. In this study, strain N6 was constructed from strain N5 through the integration of an additional copy of each target gene (gfa1, age, and neuB) under the PGAP promoter. Strains N0, N5, and N6 were subjected to 118-h fermentation in 1-L shake flasks. While strain N5 produced 285.41 mg/L Neu5Ac by the endpoint, strain N6 accumulated 1.03 g/L, a 2.59-fold increase relative to N5 (Fig. 7A). This marked increase in Neu5Ac production confirmed that increasing the expression of key enzymes directly enhanced Neu5Ac synthesis. Additionally, GlcNAc titers rose from 2.06 g/L for N5 to 3.21 g/L for N6 at 118 h, representing 55.83% enhancement (Fig. 7B). The accumulation of GlcNAc indicated that a new imbalance in the synthesis pathway was introduced following the enhancement of key enzyme expression. This imbalance might be attributed to the fact that, although the copy numbers of gfa1, age, and neuB were increased equally, their resulting functional enzyme levels were not uniformly enhanced due to differences in translation efficiency, protein stability, or catalytic activity. Discussion

Free or protein-conjugated Neu5Ac is an important bioactive substance with significant application potential in various fields (Ma et al. 2023; Yue et al. 2021), leading to increasing demand for its production. Supplementation with adenosine triphosphate (ATP) and costly precursors enables efficient Neu5Ac production via whole-cell biocatalysis, with reported titers exceeding 100 g/L (Zhao et al. 2023a). With advancements in synthetic biology and gene editing technologies, the use of rationally designed cell factories for biosynthesis of Neu5Ac from cheap carbon sources is an inevitable trend. To date, the highest reported Neu5Ac yield was obtained via E. coli (Zhao et al. 2023b). However, for food or pharmaceutical applications, Neu5Ac produced in E. coli must comply with stringent endotoxin limits; this necessity mandates a rigorous purification process for endotoxin removal. Therefore, the development of cell factories using GRAS strains for more efficient Neu5Ac synthesis is necessary. Bacillus has been engineered to efficiently produce Neu5Ac, but Bacillus engineered using glucose or glycerol as the carbon source produced a lower Neu5Ac titer than E. coli (Zhang et al. 2021). In this study, we engineered another GRAS strain, P. pastoris, to synthesize Neu5Ac and enhanced its Neu5Ac production through a series of genetic modifications. As P. pastoris lacks the native metabolic genes essential for Neu5Ac biosynthesis from glucose, heterologous biosynthetic genes (gfa1, gna1, yqaB, age, and neuB) were introduced under different promoters. The engineered strain produced only 9.19 mg/L Neu5Ac, indicating low pathway efficiency. This low output underscores the complexity of Neu5Ac biosynthesis, which requires coordinated expression of multiple enzymes and balanced precursor supply (e.g., uridine 5′-diphospho-GlcNAc, PEP) from central metabolic pathways (Kao et al. 2018). Consequently, Neu5Ac biosynthesis is constrained by two critical and interdependent factors, namely the expression or activity of key pathway enzymes and the intracellular contents of essential precursors.

Increasing Neu5Ac titer required a dual-optimization strategy of enhancing catalytic capacity (via enzyme expression/engineering) while concurrently elevating precursor flux through the metabolic network. To enhance biosynthetic enzyme expression, the copy numbers of gfa1, age, and neuB were increased to two copies each, and their expression was driven by the strong constitutive promoter PGAP. A 2.59-fold increase in Neu5Ac titer was achieved (Fig. 7A), confirming that the expression of these genes constituted a critical avenue for Neu5Ac improvement. Simultaneously, the primary focus would be on fine-tuning the expression ratios of Gfa1, Age, and NeuB (e.g., via promoters of varying strengths or differential copy numbers) to enhance the conversion of GlcNAc to Neu5Ac. Concurrently, enzyme engineering strategies should be implemented, including the use of variants with enhanced catalytic activity screened through rational design and directed evolution, as well as increasing the expression or catalytic efficiency of key enzymes to boost Neu5Ac biosynthesis.

Concurrently, precursor supply pathways were remodeled to enhance Neu5Ac synthesis. To augment PEP supply, two strategies were employed. First, the native promoter of pyk was replaced with the glycerol-inducible promoter PGUT1. PGUT1 exhibited negligible activity under glucose repression, substantially reducing pyruvate kinase expression and thus minimizing PEP catabolism. Second, the promoters of glycerol utilization genes (gt2, gut1, gut2) were replaced with constitutive promoters (PGAP, PHHX1), enabling efficient glycerol uptake and metabolism and PEP generation, even during glucose and glycerol supplementation in high-density cell fermentation. These combined interventions significantly increased intracellular PEP pools. To enhance glutamine supply, the glutamine synthase gene gs was overexpressed under the promoter PGAP. With these changes, Neu5Ac production was increased by nearly 30-fold through engineering precursor biosynthesis pathways and an optimized glucose–glycerol co-utilization scheme.

Summarily, the introduction of essential Neu5Ac biosynthesis genes enabled the synthesis of Neu5Ac from glucose by P. pastoris. Subsequent enhancement of key precursor supply and overexpression of critical enzymes ultimately increased Neu5Ac production by approximately 112-fold. This level of production in a non-optimized, small-scale format served as a robust proof-of-concept, demonstrating the successful rewiring of central metabolism in P. pastoris and the functional assembly of the heterologous Neu5Ac pathway. At present, Neu5Ac titer was lower than that in the engineered E. coli and B. subtilis, thereby many significant bottlenecks remain to be addressed for a significant increase in Neu5Ac production. The process of Neu5Ac synthesis from glucose requires substantial ATP. However, the rewiring of the glycolytic pathway reduces flux through the tricarboxylic acid cycle, lowering ATP production and thereby restricting Neu5Ac synthesis. Therefore, enhancement of ATP synthesis represents a crucial factor that should be addressed in future research. Additionally, substantial amounts of the precursor GlcNAc remained unconverted to Neu5Ac. Thus, enhancement of GlcNAc-to-Neu5Ac conversion efficiency would be another issue to be explored in future work. While the engineered strain achieved a Neu5Ac titer of 1.03 g/L in shake-flask cultures, a significant enhancement in production was expected through systematic optimization in high-cell-density fed-batch bioreactors.

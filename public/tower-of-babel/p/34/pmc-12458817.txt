Effectiveness and safety of exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation (ExCR) during the vulnerable period in patients with acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF): a randomised controlled trial protocol
Authors: Jing Ye, Lihua Zhao, Yimeng Jiang, Lihua Hu, Lei Yang, Yuling Wang, Wenhui Ding, Yimei Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAcute decompensated heart failure (ADHF), characterised by rapid deterioration of cardiac function, imposes substantial clinical and economic burdens due to high mortality (10%), frequent rehospitalisations (30% within 3 months) and impaired health-related quality of life. While exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation (ExCR) is a guideline-recommended intervention for stable heart failure, its utilisation remains critically low (11%) in ADHF populations, particularly during the vulnerable period spanning 90 days post-discharge—a high-risk phase marked by elevated mortality and morbidity. Current evidence gaps persist regarding optimal ExCR timing, dosage and efficacy during this critical window. This randomised controlled trial investigates the feasibility and impact of early ExCR initiation in the ADHF vulnerable period, evaluating outcomes in physical capacity, cardiac function and quality of life to inform evidence-based exercise protocols for this high-risk population.MethodsThis prospective, non-inferiority, randomised controlled trial will recruit 88 patients with ADHF. Participants will be randomised 1:1 to a 12-week personalised ExCR group or a traditional health education-based CR control group. Assessments will occur at baseline and 1, 2 and 3 months. The primary outcome is the change in 6 min walk distance post-intervention. Secondary outcomes include changes in grip strength, New York Heart Association Classification(NYHA class), brain natriuretic peptide, left ventricular ejection fraction, hospital stay, Short Physical Performance Battery score, Activities of Daily Living, Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire score, and frailty status. Safety measures include all-cause readmission and mortality within 3 months post-discharge, as well as adverse events during the trial.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the Peking University First Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval No. 2024yan180-002) in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants will provide written informed consent prior to enrolment. The findings will be disseminated via peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations.Trial registration numberThis study protocol was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT06795737, https://www.clinicaltrials.gov/.) on 26 January 2025.Keywords: Heart failure, Clinical Trial, Exercise, Randomized Controlled Trial, REHABILITATION MEDICINE

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a clinical syndrome caused by structural and/or functional cardiac abnormalities that impair organ perfusion.1 Acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF) refers to the rapid onset or worsening of HF symptoms and/or signs, which can be either new or a decompensation of chronic HF.1 Research shows that ADHF significantly impairs cardiac function and leads to severe outcomes, including high mortality, frequent rehospitalisations, marked physical dysfunction and reduced health-related quality of life.2 ADHF also poses a substantial economic burden.3

Exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation (ExCR) is now an integral part of care for stable HF patients and is a Grade A recommendation in international guidelines.4 5 Exercise training has been shown to improve physiological and long-term outcomes in various cardiovascular diseases by promoting coronary circulation, enhancing myocardial perfusion and improving cardiac reserve. However, a US national survey found that only 11% of ADHF inpatients use ExCR,6 and most trials systematically exclude current or recent ADHF patients.7

The vulnerable period is the high-risk time frame from ADHF onset to 3 months post-discharge, divided into three phases: very early (30 days), early (60 days) and late (60–90 days).8 9 Studies indicate a 30% rehospitalisation rate and 10% mortality within 3 months post-discharge, significantly burdening both patients and the healthcare system.10

The primary goal for ADHF patients is to improve outcomes by reducing rehospitalisations and enhancing physical function. Research confirms that establishing appropriate comorbidity management, identifying individual exercise care barriers and addressing reversible issues from the acute phase to the vulnerable period, followed by early implementation of ExCR, can improve prognosis and stabilise ADHF in the long term.11 Therefore, the vulnerable period is crucial for identifying high-risk patients and interventions that may improve outcomes.

There is currently limited evidence for patient management during the vulnerable period of ADHF. The optimal timing, duration and frequency of exercise rehabilitation interventions remain uncertain. This study aims to investigate the effects of ExCR on ADHF patients during this period through a randomised controlled trial, evaluating physical function, cardiac function and quality of life. The goal is to validate the protocol’s feasibility and effectiveness, providing evidence for a more scientific, comprehensive and systematic ADHF exercise management plan.

This is a prospective, randomised controlled, non-inferiority trial for ADHF patients. Randomisation and blinding

Randomisation was conducted using block randomisation, concealed allocation and central telephone randomisation. An independent statistician not involved in the study generated a random block sequence using SPSS V.27.0, with a block size of 4. Study groups were labelled as A (intervention) and B (control). After obtaining informed consent, the researcher informed the statistician, who then assigned participants to blocks via computer-generated random numbers and notified the researcher of group allocation (A or B) by telephone. Allocation order was strictly based on the date and time of informed consent.

Use blinded assessor-trained clinical research nurses (not study team or clinical nurses) to perform assessments at baseline, discharge and 1, 2, 3 months post-discharge for 6-Minute Walk Test Distance (6-MWTD), left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), BNP, NYHA class, grip strength, Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB), Activities of Daily Living (ADL) and Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire (MLHFQ).

Primary outcome

Change in the 6 min walking test distance: assess the changes in participants’ 6-MWTD at baseline and 1, 2 and 3 months post-discharge.

Sample size calculation

The primary outcome measure was the change in 6-MWTD. Based on Poltavskaya et al’s18 study, the intervention group had a mean 6-MWTD of 299.5±91.05 metres, while the control group had 236.82±54.73 metres. Using PASS V.21.0 software, the calculated sample size was 70, with a 20% dropout rate considered, resulting in a final sample size of 88 (44 per group).

Discussion

The initial meta-analysis of this research unequivocally shows that moderate exercise rehabilitation during the vulnerable period is safe and effective for patients with ADHF.19 However, this requires a thorough, individualised assessment and tailored adjustments. Crucially, initiating the rehabilitation programme within 1–6 months post-stabilisation is key to the recovery process.20

Leveraging the latest evidence-based research, this study introduces innovative strategies distinct from existing interventions, with key advantages outlined in the following aspects:

First, we propose an early intervention strategy, challenging the traditional approach of delaying exercise rehabilitation until symptoms stabilise. Based on recent research,21 we recommend initiating exercise within 24 hours to 2 weeks post-hospitalisation. This approach disrupts the conventional ‘bed rest’ model, promoting faster recovery of cardiopulmonary function and daily activities.

Based on recent research,21 we recommend initiating exercise within 24 hours to 2 weeks post-hospitalisation. This approach disrupts the conventional ‘bed rest’ model, promoting faster recovery of cardiopulmonary function and daily activities.

Second, this study underscores the importance of personalised assessment and adjustment. We perform a thorough evaluation of patients across multiple dimensions—cardiac function, vital signs, exercise tolerance and emotional state—to develop tailored exercise rehabilitation programmes. Throughout the rehabilitation, we conduct dynamic monitoring and adjustments to ensure safety and efficacy, preventing both overtraining and undertraining.

We perform a thorough evaluation of patients across multiple dimensions—cardiac function, vital signs, exercise tolerance and emotional state—to develop tailored exercise rehabilitation programmes. Additionally, this study introduces a comprehensive cardiac rehabilitation programme spanning both in-hospital and post-discharge phases. This programme not only emphasises acute phase recovery but also includes practical post-discharge exercise interventions, ensuring continuous support and enhancing long-term rehabilitation outcomes.

In our exercise rehabilitation implementation, we employed a multimodal approach, incorporating aerobic exercises (eg, walking, treadmill training) and resistance exercises (eg, dumbbell training, resistance band exercises). The exercise intensity and duration were scientifically designed to progressively improve patients’ physical function and endurance. Additionally, this study emphasises both psychological and physiological aspects. Recognising the significant impact of psychological state on ADHF patients’ rehabilitation outcomes, we actively address participants’ mental well-being alongside physical recovery, aiming to enhance participants’ confidence and adherence, thereby improving participants’ overall quality of life.

This study has limitations due to the complexity and variability of ADHF patients, significant individual differences and a single-centre, limited sample size design. Future research should involve multicentre collaboration to validate the effectiveness, safety and generalisability of exercise rehabilitation across diverse populations and settings. Additionally, optimising internet platform algorithms is crucial to improve safety early warning efficacy, ultimately facilitating guideline updates and clinical translation.

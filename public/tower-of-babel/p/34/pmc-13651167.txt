Risk stratification of outcomes after coronary artery bypass grafting in ischemic cardiomyopathy with symptomatic heart failure
Authors: Shin Yajima, Kazutaka Nishio, Akinobu Ohtani, Daisuke Yoshioka, Takuji Kawamura, Ai Kawamura, Yusuke Misumi, Shunsuke Saito, Tomomi Yamada, Shigeru Miyagawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo identify the predictors of all-cause mortality and heart failure (HF) events in patients with ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM) undergoing isolated coronary artery bypass (CABG) grafting who present with symptomatic HF, and to identify potential stratification variables for future regenerative therapy trials.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed data from 90 patients with ICM, left ventricular ejection fraction of ≤ 40%, and New York Heart Association class ≥ II who underwent primary isolated CABG. Clinical characteristics, operative data, and long-term outcomes were assessed. The primary endpoints were all-cause mortality and incidence of HF events, defined as a composite of all-cause mortality and initial HF readmission. Prognostic factors were evaluated using Kaplan–Meier analyses and multivariate Cox proportional hazard models.ResultsDuring the median follow-up period of 45 months, 27 (30.0%) deaths, 14 (15.6%) HF readmissions, and 38 (42.2%) HF events occurred. Multivariate analysis identified older age (hazard ratio [HR], 1.09; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.02–1.17) and high creatinine levels (HR, 1.62; 95% CI, 1.23–2.13) as independent predictors of all-cause mortality and HF events. A history of percutaneous coronary intervention was independently associated with all-cause mortality (HR, 2.60; 95% CI, 1.09–6.22). Total revascularization was independently protective against all-cause mortality (HR, 0.21; 95% CI, 0.06–0.74).ConclusionsAmong symptomatic patients with ICM undergoing CABG, older age, high creatinine levels, and a history of percutaneous coronary intervention were associated with worse survival outcomes, whereas total revascularization was associated with improved survival. Background

Ischemic cardiomyopathy (ICM) is associated with a poor prognosis and is characterized by severely impaired cardiac function and extensive cardiac remodeling. Despite advances in coronary artery disease management, it remains the most prevalent cause of heart failure (HF) in developed countries, with substantial morbidity and mortality rates [1]. In a landmark randomized clinical trial (RCT), a study on surgical treatment for ischemic HF demonstrated 5- and 10-year all-cause mortality rates of approximately 36% and 58.9%, respectively, among patients undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) with optimal medical therapy [2, 3]. Therefore, adjunctive strategies such as myocardial regenerative therapy have been explored to improve outcomes in patients with ICM undergoing CABG.

Current evidence from RCTs and meta-analyses, including IMPACT-CABG, PERFECT, Cardio133, and studies by Hu et al., Stamm et al., and Patel et al., indicates that adjunctive stem cell therapy administered during CABG in patients with ICM is safe and yields modest improvements in left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), but does not consistently enhance clinical symptoms or long-term outcomes [4–13].

We recently conducted a phase I RCT trial to evaluate the safety and feasibility of allogeneic adipose-derived stem cell spray therapy combined with CABG in patients with ICM [14]. This novel approach demonstrated not only procedural safety and simplicity but also a trend toward improved left ventricular function, reduced scar volume, and enhanced exercise tolerance compared with CABG alone.

To advance this therapy towards future clinical evaluation, it is important to explore potential stratification variables that may help assess its effects not only on cardiac functional recovery but also on HF outcomes. Therefore, this study aimed to analyze the predictors of all-cause mortality and postoperative HF events after CABG in patients with ICM who presented with HF symptoms.

Methods

Study population

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Osaka University Hospital. (Approval No. 08218-12; date of approval: March 25, 2025). Written informed consent for the use of clinical data for research and registry purposes was obtained from all patients before surgery.

Between January 2002 and March 2022, 1,553 patients underwent primary isolated CABG at the University of Osaka Hospital. For the present analysis, we excluded patients who presented with one or more of the following criteria: acute myocardial infarction (n = 242), New York Heart Association (NYHA) class I (n = 963), preserved LVEF of > 40% (n = 1,337), or a follow-up period of ≤ 2 years (n = 2). As these criteria were applied simultaneously and many patients met more than one criterion, the sum of individual exclusion numbers exceeds the total number of excluded patients. After applying these criteria, 90 patients who underwent isolated CABG and exhibited symptomatic HF (NYHA class ≥ II) with reduced LVEF (≤ 40%) constituted the final study cohort (Fig. 1).

Echocardiography

Certified sonographers performed comprehensive transthoracic echocardiography. Imaging was routinely performed before surgery and again approximately one week postoperatively, with additional studies performed during follow-up when clinically indicated. Left ventricular measurements were performed using standard parasternal acoustic windows. Left ventricular systolic function was quantified using the Simpson biplane method. Mitral regurgitation (MR) was categorized as none/trivial, mild, moderate, or severe, according to the degree to which the regurgitant jet extended into the left atrium beyond the mitral leaflet plane.

Surgical procedures

Our institutional approach to CABG followed two general principles for patients with markedly reduced left ventricular function. First, in individuals aged < 70 years, arterial revascularization, most commonly using both the internal thoracic arteries in combination with the radial artery, is favored whenever feasible. Second, grafting is usually initiated using a beating-heart strategy without cardiopulmonary bypass. Exceptions included patients with a preoperative LVEF of < 30% or those with significant pulmonary hypertension for whom the surgical team elected to use cardiopulmonary bypass support. Even when bypass is required, distal and proximal anastomoses are generally completed without inducing cardioplegic arrest.

Regarding the management of ischemic mitral regurgitation, concomitant mitral valve intervention was not routinely performed in patients with moderate or less regurgitation undergoing CABG. This strategy was based on contemporary evidence demonstrating no survival benefit of mitral valve repair for moderate ischemic mitral regurgitation at the time of CABG [15] as well as subsequent guideline updates recommending a more selective, individualized approach [16, 17].

Postoperative management

All patients were managed postoperatively according to a uniform HF treatment protocol. When clinically appropriate, this regimen incorporates renin–angiotensin system inhibitors (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers), neurohormonal blockade, diuretic therapy, and antiarrhythmic medication. Aspirin was initiated once surgical hemostasis was deemed sufficient. After discharge from the hospital, the patients were routinely evaluated approximately two weeks later. Subsequent outpatient assessments were scheduled at 1, 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively and annually thereafter unless clinical concerns required additional visits.

Definitions and data collection

The principal endpoints of this study were all-cause mortality and HF events (composite of all-cause mortality and initial HF readmission). HF-related readmission was defined as any hospitalization due to dyspnea accompanied by weight gain that necessitated diuretic escalation, intravenous diuretic therapy, and/or inotropic support. In this study, the number of bypass grafts was defined as the total number of distal coronary anastomoses regardless of the number or type of conduits used. Long-term outcome data were collected through a systematic review of hospital medical records. When necessary, additional information was obtained by contacting patients or, if unavailable, interviewing family members via telephone.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as means with corresponding standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges. Comparisons between groups were conducted using either the χ² or Fisher exact test for categorical data, whereas continuous data were assessed using the Student t-test and Mann–Whitney U test, depending on distributional assumptions and study design.

Time-to-event outcomes, including overall survival, freedom from first hospitalization for HF, and combined endpoints, were evaluated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Differences between the curves were examined using the log-rank test. Variables demonstrating a p-value of < 0.10 and those deemed clinically relevant in the univariate analyses for each outcome were subsequently incorporated into multivariable Cox proportional hazards models. Categorical variables with sparse observations across levels were excluded to ensure model stability (e.g., MR and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease). Statistical significance was defined as a two-sided p-value < 0.05. All the analyses were performed using SAS Version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Patients’ characteristics

Demographic clinical characteristics of the 90 enrolled patients (mean age, 68 ± 9 years; male sex, 88%) with LVEF of ≤ 40% and NYHA functional class ≥ II are summarized in Table 1. NYHA classes II, III, and IV were observed in 46 (51%), 40 (44%), and four (4%) patients, respectively. Echocardiography revealed markedly enlarged and impaired left ventricles, with a left ventricular end-diastolic diameter of 60 ± 8 mm, end-systolic diameter of 52 ± 10 mm, and LVEF of 30 ± 7%. Double- or triple-vessel coronary artery disease was observed in 22% and 76% of patients, respectively. Median serum creatinine level and median estimated glomerular filtration rate were 1.12 mg/dL and 50.4 mL/min/1.73 m², respectively. The median brain natriuretic peptide level was 306 pg/ml. The patients were followed up for a median of 45 months (interquartile range, 23–78 months).

Operation results

Surgical approaches included off-pump CABG in 26%, on-pump beating CABG in 72%, and conventional arrested-heart CABG in 2% of patients. Postoperative changes in left ventricular ejection fraction

Postoperative echocardiographic data at approximately one week after surgery were available for 83 of 90 patients (92.2%). Mean LVEF improved modestly from 29.8 ± 6.6% preoperatively to 32.7 ± 8.1% at one week postoperatively (ΔEF + 3.1 ± 6.2%). The majority of patients, 71 of 83 (85.5%), did not achieve a meaningful functional recovery, defined as an absolute improvement in LVEF of ≥ 10% (non-responders), while only 12 patients (14.5%) met this threshold. Furthermore, 72 of 83 patients (86.7%) continued to have an LVEF of ≤ 40% at one month postoperatively. Individual trajectories of LVEF change are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1.

Long-term outcomes of survival, heart failure readmission, and those composites

During the follow-up period, 27 deaths (30.0%), 14 HF readmissions (15.6%), and 38 HF events (42.2%) were observed. Overall survival rates at 1, 5, and 10 years after isolated CABG were 91.9%, 70.7%, and 54.3%, respectively (Fig. 2). Freedom from composite outcomes at 1, 5, and 10 years was 82.6%, 56.8%, and 43.2%, respectively (Fig. 3).

Risk factors of survival, heart failure readmission, and those composites

For all-cause mortality, older age, preoperative hemodialysis, history of percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), advanced MR (moderate, moderate-to-severe, and severe), high preoperative creatinine levels, number of bypass grafts, and total revascularization were significant factors in univariate analyses (p < 0.10). Subsequent multivariate analysis revealed that older age (hazard ratio [HR], 1.09; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.02–1.17), history of PCI (HR, 2.60; 95% CI, 1.09–6.22), high creatinine (HR, 1.62; 95% CI, 1.23–2.13), and total revascularization (HR, 0.21; 95% CI, 0.06–0.74) were independent risk factors for all-cause mortality (Table 3). For HF events, older age, high preoperative creatinine levels, and the number of bypass grafts were associated in univariate analyses (p < 0.10). Subsequent multivariate analysis revealed that older age (HR, 1.06; 95% CI, 1.01–1.11) and high creatinine (HR, 1.18; 95% CI, 1.03–1.34) were independent risk factors (Table 4).

Discussion

The present study examined predictors of all-cause mortality and HF events, a composite of all-cause mortality and initial HF rehospitalization, among patients with ICM with reduced LVEF (≤ 40%) and symptomatic HF (NYHA ≥ II). Older age and high creatinine levels were identified as independent risk factors for all-cause mortality and HF events. Additionally, a history of PCI and total revascularization was independently associated with all-cause mortality.

This study specifically targeted patients with symptomatic HF, a population of particular interest for our intended regenerative therapy, which has demonstrated the potential to enhance exercise tolerance as evidenced by improvements in the 6-minute walk distance and Minnesota Living with Heart Failure Questionnaire scores [14]. Although numerous RCTs have evaluated adjunctive regenerative therapies in patients with ICM undergoing CABG, none have explicitly limited enrollment to patients with NYHA class ≥ II, likely because individuals with ICM often present with a broad spectrum of symptom severity, including asymptomatic and advanced HF [2, 18]. Therefore, identifying hypothesis-generating predictors within this symptomatic cohort may provide preliminary information for designing future prospective RCTs, particularly when considering potential stratification variables for the assessment of treatment effects in patients with ICM and HF symptoms. Older age and high creatinine levels are well-established risk factors for adverse surgical outcomes, not only in ICM, but also across all cardiac surgical populations [19–24]. Notably, the history of PCI was an independent risk factor for all-cause mortality. To further explore this association, we examined the causes of death according to prior PCI status. Among patients with a history of PCI, 1 of 9 (11%) deaths was classified as cardiac-related, whereas among those without a history of PCI, 2 of 18 (11%) deaths were cardiac-related. Due to the limited number of events, no apparent difference in cause-specific mortality was observed between the two groups. Nevertheless, in our previous study of patients with LVEF of ≤ 40% undergoing isolated CABG, a history of PCI strongly predicted a lack of postoperative LVEF improvement (ΔLVEF < 10%) [25]. Several studies demonstrated that failure of left ventricular function to recover after CABG is associated with significantly high long-term mortality in patients with ICM [26, 27]. Conversely, improvements in postoperative LVEF correlate significantly with enhanced survival: longitudinal analyses showed that an increase in LVEF was independently associated with low mortality (HR, 0.57, 95% CI, 0.41–0.79), and an absolute improvement of 18% corresponded to a 43% reduction in late mortality risk [28]. Taken together, these findings suggest that prior PCI may contribute to a poor prognosis by impairing myocardial recovery after CABG rather than by increasing early or cause-specific cardiac mortality alone.

While older age and elevated creatinine are well-established surgical risk factors [19–24], the mechanisms by which renal dysfunction worsens outcomes in this population merit consideration. Chronic kidney disease promotes maladaptive cardiac remodeling through neurohormonal activation, volume overload, and a proinflammatory state, all of which are already exaggerated in ICM. Additionally, impaired renal function limits perioperative hemodynamic tolerance and increases the risk of acute kidney injury following cardiac surgery, further compounding long-term morbidity [19–23].

Although direct invasive hemodynamic assessment was not available in this cohort, the median preoperative BNP level of 306 pg/mL and the markedly impaired echocardiographic parameters, mean LVEF of 30%, left ventricular end-diastolic diameter of 60 mm, reflect substantial hemodynamic compromise consistent with advanced ventricular remodeling. These non-invasive parameters were included as candidate variables in the Cox regression models and may have provided partial adjustment for the severity of cardiac dysfunction. However, they should be regarded only as partial surrogates and not as substitutes for invasive hemodynamic variables, such as pulmonary artery pressure, filling pressures, and cardiac index. The absence of these invasive measurements remains an important limitation of the present analysis. The effect of the history of PCI warrants further investigation. Due to the implanted stents, PCI may limit the availability of bypass targets, necessitating more distal anastomoses and small vessels with poorer runoff, ultimately compromising graft quality [25]. This pattern was also observed in our cohort: patients with a history of PCI received fewer bypass grafts compared with those without prior PCI (3.0 ± 1.1 vs. 3.2 ± 1.0; p < 0.008). These observations support the hypothesis that prior PCI may indirectly worsen outcomes by restricting the opportunities for optimal revascularization. The observed impairment in myocardial recovery is further supported by the postoperative echocardiographic findings in our cohort. Despite surgical revascularization, the mean LVEF improved only modestly from 29.8 ± 6.6% preoperatively to 32.7 ± 8.1% at one week (ΔEF + 3.1 ± 6.2%), and 71 of 83 patients (85.5%) failed to achieve a clinically meaningful improvement in LVEF of ≥ 10%. These non-responders, who constituted the vast majority of our cohort, are at heightened risk for all-cause mortality and heart failure events, as demonstrated by prior studies showing that failure of LV functional recovery after CABG is independently associated with worse long-term outcomes [26, 27]. From a therapeutic perspective, this high proportion of non-responders underscores the unmet need for adjunctive strategies targeting myocardial recovery beyond revascularization alone. Patients with persistent LV dysfunction after CABG represent ideal candidates for regenerative therapy, as the goal of such treatment is precisely to restore myocardial function in the setting of irreversible ischemic damage that revascularization alone cannot address. These findings may therefore help inform the design of future prospective studies of adjunctive regenerative therapy, in which LVEF response could be considered as a potential stratification variable after prospective validation. In addition, the number of bypass grafts showed a univariate association with reduced HF events (HR 0.68; 95% CI, 0.49–0.94), although this did not reach independent significance in the multivariable model. Mechanistically, each additional distal anastomosis represents an incremental restoration of coronary perfusion to potentially hibernating myocardium, which may promote reverse remodeling and functional recovery, particularly relevant in our cohort where triple-vessel disease was present in 76% of patients. The attenuation of this effect in multivariable analysis likely reflects the strong correlation between graft number and total revascularization, suggesting that the survival benefit of complete revascularization may in part be mediated through the extent of grafting.

This study has demonstrated that total revascularization was independently associated with all-cause mortality. Multiple large observational studies and meta-analyses have reported that complete revascularization is associated with a significant reduction in major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular events, including myocardial infarction and repeat revascularization, compared to incomplete revascularization [28–32]. For example, a meta-analysis of adjusted risk estimates from 14 observational studies (over 30,000 patients) found a 37% reduction in follow-up mortality with complete revascularization (hazard ratio 0.63, 95% CI 0.53–0.75) [29]. Similarly, long-term registry and cohort studies consistently show that incomplete revascularization is an independent predictor of worse survival and increased hospital readmission for cardiac reasons [30, 31]. However, recent randomized and prospective studies, including the REGROUP trial subanalysis and angiographically confirmed studies, report that while complete revascularization reduces repeat revascularization and major adverse cardiac and cerebrovascular rates, it does not consistently demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in overall mortality over 4–5 years of follow-up [33–35]. The benefit for mortality may be more pronounced in specific subgroups, such as patients with diabetes or those undergoing total arterial grafting.

The relatively low use of statins and angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors in this cohort should be interpreted in the context of the long study period, which included patients treated before these therapies were widely established as essential components of post-CABG management and guideline-directed medical therapy for HF. Similarly, sacubitril/valsartan and sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors were not prescribed at discharge in the vast majority of patients (sacubitril/valsartan, 0%; SGLT2 inhibitors, 4 patients [4%]), as these agents were unavailable for most of the study period.

This study had several limitations. First, the relatively small sample size and limited number of events, particularly for all-cause mortality, restricted the statistical power of the multivariate analyses and raised the potential risk of overfitting. Although we carefully selected clinically relevant variables for the Cox regression models, and restricted inclusion to those with p < 0.10 in univariate analysis to minimize overfitting, the number of events per variable in the multivariable models was lower than the conventionally recommended threshold, and no internal or external validation was performed. Accordingly, the present findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive, and the identified predictors require prospective validation in larger, ideally multicenter cohorts before they can be considered as potential stratification variables in future clinical trials. Second, a systematic preoperative myocardial viability assessment using cardiac magnetic resonance imaging or nuclear scintigraphy was not available for most patients, which precluded evaluation of the impact of myocardial viability on postoperative functional recovery and long-term outcomes. As the extent of viable myocardium is a key determinant of both functional recovery and survival benefit after CABG, its inclusion as a covariate in the multivariable models could have meaningfully strengthened the risk stratification analysis. Future prospective studies incorporating routine viability assessment are warranted. Third, preoperative right heart catheterization was not performed in this cohort, as transthoracic echocardiography represents the standard method of hemodynamic assessment prior to isolated CABG in Japan. Consequently, pulmonary artery pressure and cardiac index were unavailable as covariates in the multivariable models. Although BNP and echocardiographic parameters were included as surrogate markers of hemodynamic burden, the absence of invasive hemodynamic data may have affected the completeness of risk adjustment. Future prospective studies incorporating routine preoperative right heart catheterization or validated non-invasive surrogates of pulmonary hemodynamics would help clarify the independent prognostic contribution of these variables. Fourth, more than half of the study population comprised patients with NYHA class II symptoms, which may limit the generalizability of the results to patients with advanced HF (NYHA class III or IV). Fifth, the surgical cohort was unique, as only 2% of the patients underwent CABG under cardiac arrest, a markedly lower proportion than that reported in Western populations, which may limit the generalizability of our results. Finally, the heterogeneity in surgical approaches and longitudinal medical therapy, including variations in the use of guideline-directed medical therapy, the limited use of newer heart failure therapies such as sacubitril/valsartan and SGLT2 inhibitors, and a lack of detailed data on long-term medication adherence, may have affected the outcomes. Although the operative era was not identified as a significant predictor of all-cause mortality or HF events in univariate analyses, residual era-related confounding factors cannot be entirely excluded; thus, larger studies using more contemporary and standardized management protocols are required to validate these observations.

Conclusion

Older age and high creatinine levels were independent predictors of all-cause mortality and HF events. These factors may help identify potential stratification variables for future prospective studies evaluating adjunctive regenerative therapy combined with CABG in patients with ICM and symptomatic HF.

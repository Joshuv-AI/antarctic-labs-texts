Exact and numerical investigation of soliton dynamics in nonlinear oscillatory system
Authors: Hamood Ur Rehman, Salah Mahmoud Boulaaras, Mohammad Safi Ullah, Misbha Munir, Ifrah Iqbal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe generalized reaction Duffing model is an important nonlinear system that describes a variety of oscillatory phenomena arising in physics, engineering, and applied sciences. In this work, we investigate its rich soliton dynamics through a combined analytical and numerical framework. Exact traveling-wave solutions are constructed by employing the generalized Riccati equation mapping method (GREMM), whereby the governing partial differential equation is transformed into a nonlinear ordinary differential equation via a traveling-wave transformation. Under suitable parameter constraints, several families of exact solutions expressed in hyperbolic, trigonometric, and exponential functions are obtained, yielding dark, bright, singular, dark–bright, dark–singular, and periodic–singular soliton structures. The validity of all derived solutions is verified through direct substitution into the original governing equation. To complement the analytical findings, the differential transform method (DTM) is utilized to numerically approximate selected solution families, and an excellent agreement between the exact and differential transform method solutions confirms the accuracy and effectiveness of the proposed semi-analytical technique. A conserved-quantity-based stability criterion is further employed to examine the robustness of the obtained solitary waves, revealing that the considered solutions remain stable for representative parameter values. Moreover, comprehensive graphical illustrations, including three-dimensional absolute-value surfaces, density distributions, and two-dimensional wave profiles, provide physical insights into the localization, propagation, and interaction characteristics of the nonlinear wave structures. The present study demonstrates the capability of the combined generalized Riccati equation mapping method and differential transform method framework to analyze complex nonlinear dynamical systems and offers benchmark solutions that may be useful for future investigations of generalized Duffing-type models and nonlinear oscillatory media.Keywords: the generalized reaction duffing model, nonlinear equations, exact solutions, generalized Riccati equation mapping method (GREMM), differential transform method (DTM), stability analysis

1. Introduction

Nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) are of great importance in describing a vast array of physical and engineering systems.1–6 In contrast to linear equations, NLPDEs can model intricate interactions and non-trivial phenomena that take place in many natural processes.7,8 NLPDEs appear in various areas of physics, including fluid mechanics, 9 optics, 10 plasma physics, 11 nuclear physics, 12 biophysics, 13 and quantum field theory. 14 As a result, the determination of exact solutions to NLPDEs has drawn significant interest, since these solutions give profound insight into the physical process under consideration and serve as benchmarks for numerical and approximate techniques.15–24

One of the significant mathematical models in nonlinear dynamics is the generalized reaction Duffing model (GRDM), which models a broad class of oscillatory phenomena in nonlinear systems. The GRDM is given by the following equation 25 :

Equation (1) represents a generalized reaction Duffing-type wave model that captures the interplay between inertia, spatial dispersion, linear restoring forces, and nonlinear reaction effects in oscillatory media. The term S tt denotes the inertial (temporal acceleration) response of the medium and governs wave propagation in time. The dispersive term δS xx accounts for spatial coupling and diffusion/dispersion effects, which are responsible for the spreading or localization of wave packets depending on the sign and magnitude of δ. The linear term ωS represents the restoring force associated with linear stiffness or potential, determining the natural frequency and linear stability of small-amplitude oscillations. The quadratic nonlinearity pS2 models asymmetric reaction or quadratic stiffness effects that arise in non-centrosymmetric or biased media, while the cubic nonlinearity κS3 captures higher-order nonlinear restoring forces responsible for hardening or softening behavior. The combined action of dispersion and nonlinear reaction terms enables the formation of localized nonlinear wave structures such as solitary waves and periodic patterns in a wide range of physical contexts, including nonlinear vibrations of elastic structures, wave propagation in dispersive media, and nonlinear oscillations in engineering and physical systems.

Although the quadratic term is not present, the cubic nonlinearity still allows for complex behaviors of solitary waves, periodic waves, and other nonlinear waveforms. The Duffing equation, presented by George Duffing in 1918, is now a standard model in nonlinear dynamics and chaos theory. 26 It is valuable because it can model nonlinear vibrations, finite-amplitude shallow water waves, and chaotic oscillations in mechanical systems. 27 As such, it remains a basis for researching nonlinear wave propagation, soliton dynamics, and spectral energy transfer in various physical situations.

Exact solutions to these nonlinear evolution equations play essential roles in understanding intricate phenomena in science and engineering. For example, in nonlinear optics, it is necessary to use complex soliton solutions to describe the propagation of high-intensity laser pulses, self-phase modulation, and soliton self-focusing in order to ensure fiber optic communication over long distances.28–32 A number of analytical methods have been formulated for the derivation of exact or approximate solutions to NLPDEs, such as Hirota’s bilinear method,33,34 the Kudryashov method,35,36 a novel technique of extra equations, 37 extended sinh-Gordon expansion method, 38 and modified Exp-function approaches.39,40 Other significant techniques are the generalized Riccati equation mapping method (GREMM),41–43 the q-HATM method, 44 and the Sardar sub-equation method. 45 The generalized Khater (GK) method with Atangana’s flexible partial derivative operator, 46 auto-Backlund modification, 47 Jacobi elliptic function method,48,49 the modified exponential rational function method, 50 the unknown coefficient method 51 and many others.52–59

This work presents a unified analytical–numerical framework for investigating soliton dynamics in the generalized reaction Duffing model, which is relevant to a broad class of nonlinear oscillatory phenomena in physics and engineering. By employing the generalized Riccati equation mapping method (GREMM), we systematically derive multiple families of exact traveling-wave solutions, including bright, dark, singular, dark–bright, and periodic–singular solitons. 41 To complement the exact results, the Differential Transform Method (DTM) is utilized for numerical validation, and a stability analysis based on conserved quantities is carried out to assess the robustness of the obtained solutions under parametric variations. 60 Comprehensive two-dimensional and three-dimensional graphs are given in order to highlight the characteristics of the solutions qualitatively. There are many benefits associated with the application of the methods used in the study of nonlinear evolution equations. The first benefit is offered by the GREMM, which provides a systematic procedure to construct a broad variety of exact solutions in the form of hyperbolic functions, trigonometric functions, and exponential functions. It allows the creation of several families of soliton solutions due to the variation of parameters in one structure. Another important advantage is offered by the differential transform method (DTM), which is a semi-analytical procedure that exhibits fast convergence without discretization and linearization. Thus, DTM can be considered a powerful and computationally efficient method. Finally, it is worth noting that DTM is a great tool to validate the exact solutions.

Novelty of this research is in using the combination of the two methods, namely, GREMM and DTM, for building, validating, and analyzing the soliton structures in the form of a wide range of solution classes of the generalized reaction Duffing equation in one unified way. In contrast to many other research papers that deal only with some particular type of solutions obtained by the same analytical method, this research offers a larger variety of solutions along with the analysis of their stability and validation through numerics. The solution form offers more general parametric solution families including a number of different wave forms as some limiting or particular cases discussed in previous literature.

Even though much effort has been put into the development of nonlinear Duffing-type models, many of the studies carried out so far have been either dealing with the derivation of specific exact solutions or with purely numerical simulations. The number of papers that consider the construction of analytical solutions, numerical verification, and stability analysis together is rather limited. Based on the above, this paper presents a new GREMM-DTM method used to construct different kinds of solitons, to validate them numerically, and to analyze their stability properties.

The paper structure is as follows: Section 2 describes the methodology and its application used in this research. Section 3 discusses the undetermined coefficient method, providing bright, dark, and singular soliton solutions. In Section 4, stability analysis is performed. Sections 5 present the Differential Transform Method (DTM) and its application to the first and second families of solutions derived using GREMM and the undetermined coefficient method. Section 6 depicts the results by means of detailed 2D, 3D, and density plots. The paper ends with an overview of conclusions and future work in the last Section 7.

4. Stability analysis

Stability analysis constitutes a central component in the qualitative assessment of nonlinear dynamical systems and has been investigated using a variety of analytical frameworks in the literature. For wave-type and soliton-bearing equations, energy-based criteria, spectral stability, modulational instability, and perturbation approaches are commonly employed to assess the robustness of localized structures under parametric variations and external disturbances. In parallel, for fractional-order and compartmental dynamical models arising in applied sciences, Lyapunov-based methods and fractional stability theories have been widely used to characterize equilibrium stability and long-term dynamical behavior. These diverse stability frameworks reflect the model-dependent nature of stability concepts and provide complementary perspectives on system robustness. 6. Results and discussion

The present study provides a comprehensive analytical–numerical framework for investigating soliton dynamics in the generalized reaction Duffing model, which extends several classical Duffing-type formulations reported in the literature. In contrast to earlier works that typically focus on a limited class of solitary wave solutions obtained via a single analytical technique, this work employs the generalized Riccati equation mapping method (GREMM) to derive a broad spectrum of exact solutions, including bright, dark, singular, dark–bright, and periodic–singular solitons. Unlike many existing analytical approaches that yield a restricted set of solutions and numerical schemes that may introduce discretization errors, the combined GREMM–DTM framework enables the construction of broader solution families and provides independent numerical verification with high accuracy and fast convergence. In addition, the analytical results are consistently supported by the use of numerical analysis through the differential transformation method (DTM) and a stability analysis utilizing conserved quantities, as well as 2D and 3D illustrations. This holistic approach to the study of the nonlinear waves in the generalized reaction Duffing equation provides a more comprehensive understanding of the obtained results and demonstrates their novelty and general applicability.

A comparative study of the current analysis with other relevant studies on Duffing-type and associated nonlinear oscillatory equations brings out several unique features. In Ref. 25, a mapping technique is used for the classical Duffing equation to obtain bell-shape and singular solutions without any stability or numerical validation. Likewise, in Ref. 30, a Duffing-type nonlinear system of equations is examined and modified simple equation and expansion techniques are used to extract periodic and solitary wave solutions but without stability analysis. In Ref 62, a nonlinear oscillatory wave equation is analyzed through the use of Modified Exp-function and Kudryashov approaches to get bright and dark soliton solutions; however, no graphical validation is provided. Contrary to the above investigations, in the current work, the generalized reaction Duffing equation is considered and an analytic-numerical scheme combining GREMM and DTM is applied. This results in a variety of solutions including bright, dark, singular, dark-bright and periodic-singular solitons. In addition, the analysis includes a thorough stability analysis along with numerical validation using DTM and 2D/3D plots. The results of the numerical simulations using the DTM match the exact soliton solutions of the proposed model equations derived with the generalized Riccati equation mapping method. The values of the system parameters used in the simulations are in accordance with the constraints of the exact solutions. The graphs for various time instances of the system show the stable propagation of the solitons with almost the same amplitude and shape, experiencing mainly a translation along the propagation direction. These results confirm the traveling character of the waves and their robust dynamics. Also, the changes in the values of system parameters such as the dispersion parameter δ and the nonlinear coefficients influence the width, amplitude, and localization of the wave profiles. Therefore, the good agreement between the exact and numerical results as well as the obtained temporal behavior provide sufficient evidence of the efficiency and validity of the suggested approach. The general reaction Duffing equation can be formulated based on the properties of a non-uniform nonlinear material that possesses inertial effect, spatial dispersion, and non-linear elasticity forces. Parameter δ accounts for the influence of spatial dispersion, ω denotes the stiffness constant, and p and κ indicate the presence of quadratic and cubic non-linearity. The interaction between these physical processes defines whether localized bright solitons, singular structures, and periodic waves are possible in the system. All the exact and numerical examples in this paper contain explicitly stated values of the parameters involved, which are clearly indicated in the captions of figures and numerical examples used in this paper. The values of the parameters have been chosen according to the condition of existence of the solutions, thus ensuring the mathematical and physical acceptability of the solutions. Choosing particular parameter values makes it possible to visualize qualitatively the wave dynamics, whereas deviation from the accepted parameter values proves the stability of the soliton solutions in changing parameters.

The main aim of the current study is to perform graphical analysis of the generalized reaction Duffing equation with the aid of Mathematica. Several graphs, such as 3D graphs, 2D graphs, and density graphs, are applied to study the evolution of wave propagation in time for various sets of parameters and configurations of solitons. The calculations are carried out for the space and time interval −20 ≤ x ≤ 20 and 0 ≤ t ≤ 20, respectively, with different physically relevant values of the parameters.

Even though some of the wave forms obtained show similarities with those found in previous works, the current graphs are plotted based on newly found families of solutions by the application of GREMM-DTM method. In fact, several sets of parameters yield dark, bright, singular, dark-bright, dark-singular, and periodic-singular waves, which have not been discussed before in the literature for the generalized reaction Duffing equation.

7. Conclusion

For this research, the generalized reaction Duffing equation has been analyzed via an integrated analysis of numerical and analytical techniques to discover nonlinear waves behaviors. Generalized Riccati equation mapping technique and undetermined coefficient method have been used to acquire a range of types of solutions such as bright soliton solution, dark soliton solution, singular soliton solution, dark-bright soliton solution, dark-singular soliton solution, and periodic singular soliton solution. All the solutions obtained have been validated by substitution in the corresponding governing equation and comparison with differential transform method. Graphical depictions provided in Figures 1–6 give the complete visual representation of the achieved wave forms. 3D absolute value surfaces show the evolution and propagation of the solitons in space and time, whereas density plots indicate localization and concentration properties of them. 2D profiles of these waves at different time moments show that the wave shape and amplitude are conserved, which gives physical insights about the propagation of dark, singular, dark-bright, dark-singular, bright, and periodic-singular solitons. In addition, the conserved quantity based stability criteria have been used, where the positivity of ∂M/∂k showed that the solitons were stable for some chosen parameters. In conclusion, it can be stated that the presented analytical-numerical technique increases the solution set of the generalized reaction Duffing model, which gives reliable numerical results. The future works on generalized Duffing type equations may include higher order dispersive and nonlinear derivatives terms, another stability criteria, and so on.

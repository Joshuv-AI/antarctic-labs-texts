Has early mortality following single‐unit unrelated cord blood transplantation improved in recent years? A nationwide registry data analysis from 2003 to 2022 in Japan
Authors: Takaaki Konuma, Junya Kanda, Naoyuki Uchida, Masatsugu Tanaka, Fumihiko Kimura, Hideki Nakasone, Yasufumi Uehara, Masahito Tokunaga, Masako Toyosaki, Shigesaburo Miyakoshi, Noriko Doki, Kazuya Ishiwata, Hikaru Kobayashi, Yasushi Onishi, Yasuji Kozai, Koji Kato, Fumihiko Ishimaru, Takahiro Fukuda, Yoshiko Atsuta, Satoshi Takahashi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryUnrelated single‐unit cord blood transplantation (CBT) is a valuable alternative donor source for patients without matched related or unrelated donors. Although initial concerns included limited cell dose, delayed haematopoietic recovery and higher early mortality, advancements in transplant practices may have led to improved outcomes. However, it remains uncertain whether these improvements extend to the most recent years. We conducted a nationwide, registry‐based retrospective study of 15 816 patients who received unrelated single‐unit CBT in Japan and analysed across four time periods: 2003–2007, 2008–2012, 2013–2017 and 2018–2022. Overall survival (OS) improved significantly across time periods, with 3‐year OS increasing from 38.8% (2003–2007) to 54.4% (2018–2022; p < 0.001). Similarly, 100‐day non‐relapse mortality declined from 19.6% to 9.5% (p < 0.001). Neutrophil and platelet engraftment rates also rose steadily, reaching 89.6% and 78.0%, respectively, in the most recent period. The most notable improvement in 100‐day OS occurred among patients aged ≥60 years. Early mortality within 100 days due to bacterial and fungal infections, graft failure, haemorrhage and graft‐versus‐host disease (GVHD) significantly decreased over time. This study shows clear and consistent improvements in transplant outcomes, especially in the most recent 5‐year period.Keywords: allogeneic haematopoietic cell transplantation, CD34+ cell dose, cord blood transplantation, engraftment, non‐relapse mortality, unrelated donor

INTRODUCTION

Cord blood transplantation (CBT) from an unrelated donor is an established alternative platform for allogeneic haematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT) in patients who lack a matched related or unrelated donor. 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 Although CBT has some disadvantages, including insufficient numbers of transplanted cells, delayed haematopoietic recovery, a higher risk of engraftment failure, increased incidence of opportunistic infections and the inability to use cellular therapy from the same donor, it also offers several advantages. These include quick availability due to cryopreserved storage, tolerance of human leucocyte antigen (HLA) disparity, lower frequency of graft‐versus‐host disease (GVHD) and stronger graft‐versus‐leukaemia effects. 6 , 7 , 8 Recent comparative data demonstrate that survival after allogeneic HCT is comparable between CBT and HCT from other donor types. 4 , 5 , 9 , 10 In Japan, a cord blood bank was established in 1997, 11 and unrelated donor CBT has been performed since then. The annual number of unrelated CBTs exceeds 1300, including second or subsequent allogeneic HCTs, and the cumulative number of CBTs reached 25 530 in April 2025, making Japan the global leader in the number of CBT procedures performed.

Recent advancements in allogeneic HCT—including expanded graft availability, conditioning regimens and supportive care—may improve post‐transplant outcomes from both related and unrelated donors. 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 These advancements include considerable progress in HLA typing and anti‐HLA antibody screening for donor selection, GVHD prophylaxis and treatment, prevention, diagnosis and management of infectious complications, as well as pre‐ and post‐transplant treatments for primary diseases. Specifically focusing on CBT, we previously reported improved engraftment and reduced early mortality in single‐unit CBT for the largest cohort of adults in Japan from 1998 to 2017. 17 However, it remains unclear whether transplant outcomes following single‐unit CBT have improved further in more recent years. Therefore, we performed a retrospective analysis using the largest real‐world registry data on single‐unit CBT to determine whether early mortality has decreased over the most recent 5‐year period (2018–2022).

METHODS

Study design and data collection

This retrospective study was conducted by the Japanese Data Center for Hematopoietic Cell Transplantation (JDCHCT) and the Japanese Society for Transplantation and Cellular Therapy (JSTCT). Clinical data were collected through the Transplant Registry Unified Management Program of the JDCHCT and JSTCT. 19 , 20 Patients who received single‐unit CBT from unrelated donors as their first allogeneic HCT between 2003 and 2022 in Japan were included. Patients lacking data on recipient age at the time of CBT or survival were excluded. Ultimately, 15 816 patients were eligible for this study. Details for methods are provided in the Supporting Information. 21 , 22 , 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27

RESULTS

Patient, disease and transplant characteristics

A total of 15 816 patients who received single‐unit CBT as their first allogeneic HCT between 2003 and 2022 in Japan were included (Table 1, Figure 1A). The number of CBT procedures increased every 5 years, with 2385, 3543, 4753 and 5135 patients transplanted in 2003–2007, 2008–2012, 2013–2017 and 2018–2022 respectively. Median recipient age and body weight increased over time. Over the four time periods, there was a progressive increase in the proportion of recipients with cytomegalovirus‐positive serostatus, HCT‐CI ≥3, diagnoses of acute myeloid leukaemia and myelodysplastic syndrome, standard risk disease status at CBT and GVHD prophylaxis with tacrolimus. Data on HCT‐specific comorbidity index, performance status (PS), anti‐HLA antibody and HLA allele mismatch were mostly unavailable during the earliest period (2003–2007). Among patients tested for anti‐HLA antibodies, approximately 5% had donor‐specific anti‐HLA antibodies (DSA) in each time period. Median doses of cord blood total nucleated cells (TNC) (p < 0.001), CD34+ cells (p < 0.001) and colony‐forming unit granulocyte‐macrophage (p < 0.001) differed significantly over the four periods. The proportion of HLA serum matches decreased over the four time periods, while the proportion with two or more HLA serum mismatches increased (Table 1).

Regarding age distribution, the proportion of older patients (≥60 years) increased markedly from 17.2% in 2003–2007 to 36.7% in 2018–2022, while the proportion of younger patients (<20 years) decreased from 22.8% to 10.9% over the same periods (Figure 1B). For cryopreserved cord blood cell doses, the proportion of lower TNC dose (<2.0 × 107/kg) gradually decreased from 11.5% in 2003–2007 to 5.2% in 2013–2017, before slightly increasing to 6.4% in 2018–2022 (Figure 1C). The proportion of lower CD34+ cell dose (<0.5 × 105/kg) decreased significantly from 19.3% in 2003–2007 to 12.9% in 2008–2012, 10.6% in 2013–2017 and 6.2% in 2018–2022 (Figure 1D). Annual changes in the proportions by age, TNC and CD34+ cell doses are shown in Figure S1.

Overall survival (OS), non‐relapse mortality (NRM) and relapse

In the univariate analysis, the 3‐year OS significantly improved over the four time periods: 38.8% (2003–2007), 43.5% (2008–2012), 49.8% (2013–2017) and 54.4% (2018–2022) (p < 0.001 by log‐rank test) (Figure 2A). In the multivariate analysis, compared with 2018–2022, overall mortality increased significantly in a stepwise manner during 2013–2017 (Hazard ratio [HR], 1.15; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.08–1.22), 2008–2012 (HR, 1.37; 95% CI, 1.28–1.46) and 2003–2007 (HR, 1.51; 95% CI, 1.39–1.65) (Table S1).

Probability of overall survival (OS) (A), the cumulative incidences of non‐relapse mortality (NRM) (B), neutrophil recovery (C) and platelet recovery after cord blood transplantation (CBT) according to the four time periods in the entire cohort.

Similarly, in the univariate analysis, 3‐year non‐relapse mortality (NRM) significantly improved over the four time periods: 29.2% (2003–2007), 25.7% (2008–2012), 24.9% (2013–2017) and 23.5% (2018–2022) (p < 0.001 by Gray's test) (Figure 2B). The multivariate analysis showed stepwise reductions in NRM compared with 2018–2022: 2013–2017 (HR, 1.22; 95% CI, 1.12–1.32), 2008–2012 (HR, 1.28; 95% CI, 1.17–1.40) and 2003–2007 (HR, 1.38; 95% CI, 1.22–1.56) (Table S1).

Three‐year relapse significantly improved over the four time periods in the univariate and multivariate analyses (Figure S2A; Table S1).

Haematopoietic engraftment and GVHD

In the univariate analysis, the 42‐day neutrophil recovery rate significantly improved over the four time periods: 72.9% for 2003–2007, 79.2% for 2008–2012, 85.0% for 2013–2017 and 89.6% for 2018–2022 (p < 0.001 by Grey's test) (Figure 2C). In the multivariate analysis, compared with 2018–2022, neutrophil recovery increased significantly in a stepwise manner during 2013–2017 (HR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.80–0.87), 2008–2012 (HR, 0.73; 95% CI, 0.69–0.76) and 2003–2007 (HR, 0.62; 95% CI, 0.58–0.66) (Table S2).

In the univariate analysis, the 100‐day platelet recovery rate also significantly improved over the four time periods: 56.7% for 2003–2007, 64.0% for 2008–2012, 71.7% for 2013–2017 and 78.0% for 2018–2022 (p < 0.001 by Gray's test) (Figure 2D). In the multivariate analysis, compared with 2018–2022, platelet recovery increased significantly in a stepwise manner during 2013–2017 (HR, 0.83; 95% CI, 0.79–0.87), 2008–2012 (HR, 0.69; 95% CI, 0.66–0.73) and 2003–2007 (HR, 0.59; 95% CI, 0.55–0.64) (Table S2).

Details for acute and chronic GVHD data are provided in the Supporting Information (Figure 3; Table S3).

Early mortality and the cause of early death

In the univariate analysis, 100‐day OS significantly improved over the four time periods: 69.2% for 2003–2007, 74.5% for 2008–2012, 81.0% for 2013–2017 and 84.5% for 2018–2022 (p < 0.001 by log‐rank test) (Figure 4A). In the multivariate analysis, compared with 2018–2022, overall mortality increased significantly in a stepwise manner during 2013–2017 (HR, 1.18; 95% CI, 1.08–1.31), 2008–2012 (HR, 1.59; 95% CI, 1.44–1.76) and 2003–2007 (HR, 1.94; 95% CI, 1.69–2.22) (Table S4).

In the univariate analysis, 100‐day NRM also significantly improved over the four time periods: 19.6% for 2003–2007, 14.5% for 2008–2012, 11.3% for 2013–2017 and 9.5% for 2018–2022 (p < 0.001 by Gray's test) (Figure 4B). In the multivariate analysis, compared with 2018–2022, NRM increased significantly in a stepwise manner during 2013–2017 (HR, 1.17; 95% CI, 1.03–1.33), 2008–2012 (HR, 1.53; 95% CI, 1.34–1.75) and 2003–2007 (HR, 1.90; 95% CI, 1.60–2.27) (Table S4). Similarly, 100‐day relapse also significantly improved over the four time periods in univariate and multivariate analyses (Figure S2B; Table S4).

We conducted a landmark study for OS, NRM and relapse after 100 days, thereby excluding the influence of early mortality on long‐term outcomes following CBT. The landmark analysis indicated that OS, NRM and relapse rates considerably improved across the four time periods in the univariate analysis (Figure S3). The multivariate analysis indicated that the hazard ratios for overall mortality at the 100‐day landmark differed among the four time periods (Table S5), but these ratios were lower than those observed at 100 days (Table S4). The landmark analysis of NRM indicated that NRM exhibits small temporal variations in the univariate (Figure S3) and multivariate analyses (Table S5).

By 100 days after CBT, 732 (30.7%), 902 (25.4%), 902 (18.9%) and 789 (15.3%) patients died in 2003–2007, 2008–2012, 2013–2017 and 2018–2022 respectively (Figure 4C). The most common cause of death within 100 days after CBT was bacterial infection (5.95%) in 2003–2007 and primary disease in the subsequent three time periods (5.64% in 2008–2012, 4.90% in 2013–2017 and 3.75% in 2018–2022). Over the four time periods, the proportions of deaths from bacterial infection (from 6.58% in 2003–2007 to 2.43% in 2018–2022), primary disease (from 5.95% to 3.75%), fungal infection (from 1.63% to 0.64%), viral infection (from 1.46% to 0.35%), other infections (from 0.67% to 0.37%), graft failure (from 2.38% to 0.85%), haemorrhage (from 2.59% to 0.91%), GVHD/pre‐engraftment syndrome (PES) (from 2.22% to 0.83%), sinusoidal obstructive syndrome/thrombotic microangiopathy (from 2.80% to 1.46%) and organ failure (from 2.59% to 1.94%) decreased in a stepwise manner (Figure 4D). In contrast, the proportion of deaths from pulmonary complications increased slightly over time (from 1.46% to 1.55%) (Figure 4D).

Subgroup analysis according to age

The proportion of annual CBT procedures for older patients, such as those aged ≥60 years, increased substantially over time. Age was the most significant factor for OS and NRM in the multivariate analysis (Tables S1 and S4). Therefore, we performed subgroup analyses of improvements in OS, NRM and neutrophil recovery over the four time periods, stratified by age group (0–19 years, 20–59 years and ≥60 years).

In the univariate analysis, OS significantly improved over the four time periods in each age group (Figure 5A). However, the improvement was not significant when comparing time periods within each age group, except between 2013–2017 and 2018–2022 in the ≥60 years group (P for interaction = 0.002), indicating that OS did not improve between these two periods in this age group (Figure 5D).

NRM also significantly improved over the four time periods in each age group in the univariate analysis (Figure 5B), but the improvement was not significant when comparing time periods within each age group (Figure 5D). Relapse significantly improved over the four time periods in each age group in the univariate analysis (Figure S2C).

Neutrophil recovery significantly improved over the four time periods in each age group in the univariate analysis (Figure 5C). However, in the 0–19 years group, neutrophil recovery was significantly better in 2003–2007 (P for interaction = 0.004), 2008–2012 (P for interaction <0.001) and 2013–2017 (P for interaction = 0.052) compared with 2018–2022, indicating no improvement in the most recent period (Figure 5D). In the ≥60 years group, neutrophil recovery was significantly better in 2013–2017 than in 2018–2022 (P for interaction = 0.012), indicating no improvement between these periods (Figure 5D).

In the univariate analysis, 100‐day OS and 100‐day NRM also significantly improved over the four time periods in each age group (Figure 6A,B). However, the improvement in 100‐day OS was not significant when comparing time periods within each age group, except between 2013–2017 and 2018–2022 in the ≥60 years group (P for interaction = 0.088), indicating no improvement between these periods (Figure 6C). Similarly, the improvement in 100‐day NRM was not significant within each age group, except between 2013–2017 and 2018–2022 in the 0–19 years group (P for interaction = 0.090), indicating no improvement between these periods (Figure 6C).

DISCUSSION

Our registry‐based study of the largest cohort of 15,816 patients undergoing single‐unit CBT showed trends and changes in patient and transplantation characteristics, as well as continued improvements in outcomes such as haematopoietic recovery, NRM and OS, particularly during the most recent 5‐year period. The causes of early death indicated that most early morbidities—including infections, graft failure, haemorrhage, GVHD/PES and organ failure—could contribute to the decreasing risk of death over the four time periods.

Our study demonstrated that both OS and NRM following CBT have generally improved over the past two decades, regardless of age group. While the proportion of older adults receiving single‐unit CBT has increased over time in Japan, the improvement in early OS over the past two decades is particularly significant for the entire cohort. Our subgroup analysis clearly showed the most significant improvement in early OS in patients aged 60 years and older. The improvement in early OS in this group may be mainly explained by the reduction of early NRM, which is very encouraging. These improvements in early transplant outcomes might be attributed to proper adaptation of CBT and the selection of conditioning regimens, especially for older patients. Despite the increased proportion of older adults (≥60 years) over the four time periods, the proportion with PS ≥2 decreased from 16.9% in 2003–2007 to 9.0% in 2018–2022 in the entire cohort. Furthermore, the proportion receiving reduced intensity conditioning also decreased from 44.7% in 2003–2007 to 40.8% in 2018–2022 in the entire cohort. Several previous studies in Japan and guidelines from the United States suggest that the selection of a relatively intensive conditioning regimen might improve engraftment and survival after CBT. 28 , 29 , 30 , 31 , 32 Of course, advanced practices such as the quality of CB unit selection, the selection of patients, the use of appropriate conditioning regimens and the improved diagnosis and treatment of infections could also contribute to improved outcomes. Additionally, increasing familiarity with CBT practice at each centre benefits not only older adults but also younger patients.

Delayed haematopoietic recovery remains one of the most challenging issues for CBT. In Eastern countries, techniques such as ex vivo expansion of CB units using co‐culture with mesenchymal stromal cells, 33 small molecules 34 or double‐unit CBT 35 have addressed the insufficient cell dose in single‐unit CB, particularly for adults, resulting in enhanced neutrophil and platelet recovery. Nevertheless, without ex vivo expansion or double‐unit CBT techniques in Japan, we still observed stepwise improvement in neutrophil and platelet recovery following single‐unit CBT over a two‐decade period. This improvement in neutrophil recovery may be mainly due to the consideration of anti‐HLA antibodies, particularly DSA, when selecting CB units during the later period of this study. 36 , 37 Furthermore, because cryopreserved CD34+ cell dose has been reported as a better predictor of neutrophil recovery than TNC dose for single‐unit CBT, 38 , 39 , 40 CD34+ cell dose has been used for unit selection instead of TNC dose since 2018 in Japan. Indeed, compared to 2013–2017, the proportion of lower TNC doses (<2.0 × 107/kg) slightly increased in 2018–2022 (Figure 1C), but the proportion of lower CD34+ cell doses (<0.5 × 105/kg) decreased in 2018–2022 (Figure 1D). Altogether, CB unit selection based on CD34+ cell dose and consideration of DSA likely contributed to improved haematopoietic recovery in recent years of CBT.

The cumulative incidences of grade II–IV and grade III–IV acute GVHD were not significantly different over the four time periods, but the proportion of early deaths caused by GVHD decreased from 2.22% in 2003–2007 to 0.83% in 2018–2022. This may be partly due to advances in secondary treatments for steroid refractory acute GVHD, such as ruxolitinib 41 and mesenchymal stem cells, 42 although these treatments are not specific to CBT practice in Japan. Conversely, the cumulative incidences of overall and extensive chronic GVHD were significantly lower only in the most recent period. While the use of calcineurin inhibitors and methotrexate for GVHD prophylaxis—significant factors for reducing the risk of overall and chronic GVHD—has decreased in recent periods, this has not translated into increased chronic GVHD rates. The exact reason for the reduced incidence of overall and extensive chronic GVHD in the recent period remains unclear. However, given the feasibility and safety of early discontinuation of systemic immunosuppression after single‐unit CBT, 43 CBT is an attractive donor type associated with better quality of life for long‐term survivors, especially in the most recent period.

The decrease in NRM within 100 days after CBT may be explained by reductions in each cause of death. Most importantly, infection complications could be controlled through improved diagnosis, prevention and treatment methods for bacterial, fungal and viral infections. Specifically, because Gram‐positive bacteria are frequently detected in bacteraemia during the early phase of CBT, 44 , 45 early administration of antibacterial glycopeptides such as vancomycin or teicoplanin is often considered by Japanese physicians. Availability of antifungal drugs effective against filamentous fungi–including liposomal amphotericin B, voriconazole and posaconazole 46 —as well as diagnosis and treatment of human herpesvirus 6B (HHV‐6B) encephalitis 47 and use of letermovir to prevent cytomegalovirus reactivation 48 , 49 are also important for reducing early deaths caused by infectious complications after CBT. Enhanced haematopoietic recovery from appropriate CB unit selection may further reduce graft failure, haemorrhage and bacterial infections early after CBT. Collectively, early glycopeptide administration, management of HHV‐6B encephalitis and CB unit selection are relatively specific to CBT practice, while other interventions such as antifungal drugs and letermovir also contribute to improved post‐transplant outcomes in both CBT and allogeneic HCT from adult donors.

Our study had several limitations. First, as a registry‐based retrospective cohort study, several data points were unavailable in the Japanese registry, such as HLA‐C, ‐DP, ‐DQ and ‐DRB3/4/5 typing of cord blood, 50 , 51 dynamics of absolute neutrophil counts before engraftment, 52 , 53 inflammation levels during neutropenia 54 and haemophagocytic syndrome during early‐phase CBT. 55 These factors could influence graft failure after single‐unit CBT. Therefore, evaluation of anti‐HLA antibodies against currently untyped HLA class II antigens and lymphocyte cross‐matching may reduce graft failure incidence in CBT. 56 Second, previous studies have shown that centre experience affects survival after CBT, 57 , 58 , 59 but we could not assess centre effects in our study. Third, the effect of ethnicity on CBT outcomes differs between Europe and Japan 58 , 60 ; therefore, further investigation of recent improvements in CBT outcomes across diverse racial groups is needed. Fourth, we examined if improvements in OS correlated with alterations in case mix; nevertheless, the patients, disease, transplant techniques and cord blood characteristics did not account for the improved OS (Supporting Information; Table S6). Consequently, additional factors, such as the care of infectious diseases and primary diseases, may enhance OS.

In summary, our nationwide registry‐based study demonstrated further progress in OS, NRM and haematopoietic recovery following single‐unit CBT in Japan during the recent period from 2018 to 2022. Nonetheless, the chance of graft failure remains inadequate. Consequently, further innovations should be explored to mitigate graft failure in single‐unit CBT.

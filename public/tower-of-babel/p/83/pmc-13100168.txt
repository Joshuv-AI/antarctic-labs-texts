Optimizing multi-level shuttle-based puzzle storage systems with horizontal and vertical dynamics using integer programming and ALNS-IP
Authors: Rami Al Jneid, Vecihi Yiğit, Muhammed Emre Keskin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEfficient retrieval in automated storage systems is critical for warehouse performance. This study presents a novel mathematical model and a hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic for multi-level shuttle-based puzzle storage systems, explicitly incorporating both horizontal and vertical shuttle movements— addressing a gap in the literature where primarily horizontal movement has been considered. The model captures the operational characteristics of cube-shaped storage warehouses, and the heuristic is designed to optimize retrieval operations by minimizing the total number of moves. Numerical experiments show that the proposed approach achieves near-optimal solutions for small- and medium-sized instances and feasible, high-quality solutions for large-scale systems where exact optimization is computationally infeasible. The impact of vertical movement, as well as sensitivity to computation time, number of shuttles, and demand levels, was analyzed, demonstrating the robustness and adaptability of the heuristic. The results confirm that the proposed model and algorithm provide a practical, scalable, and efficient solution for complex automated storage and retrieval systems, bridging the gap between theoretical optimization and real-world application.Keywords: Optimization algorithms, Multi-Level puzzle storage system, Mathematical modeling, ALNS-IP heuristic, Warehouse optimizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Modern supply chains face unprecedented demands from two converging trends: rapid urbanization and the exponential growth of e-commerce. As urban land becomes increasingly scarce and consumer expectations for rapid delivery intensify, warehouses must simultaneously maximize storage density and operational efficiency. This dual challenge has driven the adoption of innovative solutions that blend automation with space-saving architectures.

Industry leaders such as Amazon and Walmart exemplify the transformative impact of automation, having successfully transitioned from manual warehouses to fully automated systems. These systems synergize automated storage/retrieval with ergonomic workstations, where operators select items guided by digital interfaces, eliminating unnecessary movement while reducing error rates by up to 60% compared to manual processes1,2. Such advancements address not only efficiency but also labor sustainability in tight urban labor markets.

Parallel developments in high-density storage systems offer solutions to spatial constraints. Puzzle-based storage (PBS) configurations, for instance, arrange goods in grid layouts where loads shift laterally between adjacent cells using strategically positioned empty (escort) spaces. This aisle-free design achieves 30–50% greater storage density than conventional systems3, proving particularly valuable in metro areas where warehouse footprints cannot expand outward.

The integration of these paradigms, intelligent automation for speed and PBS for spatial optimization, represents a critical evolution in warehouse design. As urbanization and online retail continue their upward trajectories, the logistics sector must further refine these hybrid approaches to balance throughput, accuracy, and real-world spatial limitations.

Traditional storage systems, reliant on fixed shelves and aisle-bound vehicles, inherently sacrifice valuable space, up to 40% of total warehouse area, to accommodate movement corridors. This spatial inefficiency has catalyzed the development of innovative alternatives, most notably PBS systems. These systems eliminate this inefficiency through dynamic, aisle-free architectures inspired by the classic 15-sliding puzzle.

Unlike conventional layouts, PBS arranges storage units in a grid where shelves slide orthogonally to create temporary access pathways. This dynamic movement allows items to be retrieved without dedicated aisles; instead, a coordinated sequence of lateral shifts, facilitated by empty (escort) cells, brings the target item to the input/output (I/O) point. Early conceptual foundations for such systems were established by Bozer and White4, who demonstrated their potential to reconcile high storage density with automated retrieval. As illustrated in Fig. 1, PBS systems comprise three core components: movable storage blocks that slide orthogonally, escort spaces enabling repositioning, and a centralized control system coordinating movements to I/O points.

Literature review

Warehouse system performance is extensively analyzed due to its critical impact on operational efficiency, space utilization, and order fulfillment speed. Among the various Automated Storage and Retrieval Systems (AS/RS), Puzzle-Based Storage Systems (PBS) have garnered significant interest for their compact, aisle-free layouts and inherent flexibility. To establish a comprehensive foundation and clearly position our contribution, this literature review is structured around three interconnected streams: (1) the evolution of PBS models and their optimization strategies, (2) advancements in other automated warehouse architectures, and (3) the established role of the Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) metaheuristic in solving complex logistics optimization problems. By synthesizing these streams, we identify a critical research gap and underscore the novelty of the integrated 3D approach proposed in this study.

Puzzle-based storage systems (PBS)

PBS systems aim to maximize storage density and retrieval efficiency through grid-based layouts that require coordinated movements similar to a sliding puzzle. Building on this concept, Gue and Kim7 propose dynamic programming and heuristic algorithms for retrieval path optimization, demonstrating that increasing the number of escorts generally reduces retrieval time, except in very small grids. Following this, Taylor and Gue8 analyze escort distribution patterns and find that a diagonal arrangement performs best when escort density exceeds 25%, whereas random placement is more suitable under ABC storage policies.

Expanding on these findings, Furmans et al.9 examine the GridFlow design and reveal that placing the I/O point at the midpoint and maintaining a 2:1 aspect ratio significantly improves retrieval times, particularly for smaller systems. Subsequently, Gue et al.10 introduce the GridStore model, showing that increasing the number of columns enhances throughput. They also propose a decentralized control scheme—Assess–Negotiate–Convey—demonstrating that throughput improves under moderate work-in-process levels and specific escort distributions.

In a related study, Kota et al.11 develop closed-form expressions for retrieval time using one or two escorts and introduce a heuristic approach for larger systems, achieving near-optimal performance. Similarly, Zaerpour et al.3 analyze the Live-Cube compact storage system using analytical models and nonlinear mixed-integer programming, showing that optimized design parameters significantly reduce retrieval time. Later, Zaerpour et al.12 introduce a two-class storage policy for the Live-Cube system, reporting retrieval operations up to 55% faster than random storage configurations. Complementing these efforts, Mirzaei et al.13 investigate simultaneous multi-load retrieval and propose both optimal and heuristic methods that reduce move times by up to 23%. Additionally, Yalcin et al.14 address retrieval challenges involving multiple random empty sites using exact and heuristic approaches, effectively minimizing the number of moves required.

More recently, Yu et al.15 develop algorithms for scheduling operations with four to five empty cells, achieving retrieval speeds up to 70% faster than single-cell systems. Finally, Jin He et al.6 apply reinforcement learning combined with integer programming for multi-item retrieval, finding that reinforcement learning delivers superior results for medium and large-scale instances.

Recent advancements (2023–2025) have further expanded the PBS landscape, albeit largely within planar or otherwise simplified configurations. Sgarbossa et al.16 and Weerasinghe et al.17–19 introduced and analyzed the Puzzle-Based Movable Rack (PBMR) system, where racks equipped with autonomous wheels can move diagonally, aiming to improve system throughput while maintaining high storage density. In a contrasting direction focused on cost reduction, Carlo et al.20 proposed Unidimensional PBS (UPBS), restricting cell movement to a single dimension (horizontal or vertical) to lower system complexity and investment. Methodologically, Weerasinghe et al.17,18 also explored modular Petri Nets for adaptive PBS control. Overall, PBS-related studies have progressively enhanced retrieval strategies, path optimization methods, and escort management techniques. However, despite recent innovations such as diagonal mobility, cost-oriented designs, and adaptive control mechanisms, the literature remains largely confined to two-dimensional configurations. In most existing PBS models, vertical dynamics are either absent or handled as an external, lift-based process, rather than being treated as an integrated decision dimension within shuttle routing and retrieval optimization. Other types of automated warehouses

Hao et al.21 examine three-dimensional compact storage systems with the I/O port located at the lower midpoint of the rack, aiming to enhance system performance while maintaining high storage capacity. They derive closed-form expressions to estimate expected travel time and to optimize the rack’s three dimensions. Compared to earlier designs with the I/O port positioned at the lower-left corner, the proposed configuration significantly reduces travel time and improves throughput.

In a related contribution, Zaerpour et al.22 focus on the temporary storage of fresh produce within an automated compact cross-dock system. To reduce refrigeration costs, they adopt a compact layout and develop a mathematical model based on shared storage to minimize total retrieval time. For large-scale problems, they propose a heuristic combining greedy construction and local improvement, demonstrating that shared storage outperforms dedicated storage by reducing response time and improving lane utilization.

Similarly, Ghomri et al.23 analyze flow-rack AS/RS systems consisting of multi-deep racks and separate storage and retrieval machines. They propose an analytical model to estimate the average retrieval time under random storage, which proves effective for performance evaluation and system design. Extending this stream of research, Lerher et al.2 investigate Automated Vehicle Storage and Retrieval Systems (AVS/RS) utilizing multi-tier shuttles. They develop analytical travel time models for both single and dual command cycles, and simulation results confirm the accuracy of these models in predicting system cycle times.

Furthermore, Marolt et al.24 address the integration of Vertical Lift Modules (VLM) with Shuttle-Based Storage and Retrieval Systems (SBS/RS). Using a binary integer programming model, they minimize tote exchanges between the two systems, revealing that optimal integration occurs when processing long order lists with strong Pareto relationships among Stock Keeping Units (SKUs) ranging between 20 and 50. In another approach, Ko et al.25 explore vertical stacking in Robotic Compact Storage and Retrieval Systems (RCSRSs). In this configuration, retrieving a target bin requires reshuffling all bins above it, repeatedly changing the depth-wise position of bins. Consequently, the robot’s workload heavily depends on the order sequence. To address this, the authors propose a rollout heuristic algorithm to determine an efficient processing sequence, achieving high-quality solutions within short computational time.

In addition, Chen, R., et al.26 study a shuttle-based storage and retrieval system featuring two independent lifts that share a single mast. While both lifts can move simultaneously, they cannot overtake one another. To solve the retrieval request scheduling problem—where requests must be sequenced and assigned to lifts to minimize cycle time—the authors formulate a mixed-integer programming model. Given the NP-hard nature of the problem, they design a decomposition-based adaptive large neighborhood search heuristic to obtain near-optimal solutions efficiently. They also apply a dynamic programming approach to optimally assign requests to lifts, showing that the proposed algorithm outperforms existing methods in system performance optimization.

Earlier work by Brezovnik et al.27 focuses on AS/RS planning using a multi-target ant colony approach. Later, Tappia et al.28 develop queuing network models to evaluate the performance of both single-tier and multi-tier shuttle-based compact systems, aiming to identify the most efficient system configuration for improved operational performance. Following this, Zaerpour et al.29 investigate Live-Cube compact storage systems employing a two-class storage policy, where high-turnover items are positioned near the I/O point and low-turnover items are stored further away. Using a mixed-integer nonlinear model, they optimize system dimensions and zone boundaries, finding that the two-class policy achieves up to 45% faster retrieval times compared to random storage.

In parallel, Wang et al.30 propose a two-stage open queuing network model in which shuttles and lifts are treated as servers at different stages. This model analyzes system performance in terms of shuttle waiting period (SWP) and lift idle period (LIP), effectively capturing the dynamics of multi-tier shuttle storage systems. Building on this, Zhao et al.31 develop an integer programming model for Tier-to-Tier Shuttle-Based Storage and Retrieval Systems to minimize total task time, idle time, and waiting time. Their approach leverages the shuttle’s ability to move across levels without dependency on other shuttles.

Moreover, Zhao et al.32 address coordination between the shuttle and the lift in SBS/RS systems using queuing network analysis combined with the Approximate Mean Value Analysis (AMVA) algorithm. Simulation results validate their approach and provide insights into determining the optimal number of shuttles for efficient system design. Similarly, Azadeh et al.33 analyze Vertical Robotic Storage and Retrieval Systems and compare them with horizontal systems through closed queuing network models. They find that the ideal height-to-width ratio for vertical systems is approximately one, achieving balanced productivity.

Subsequently, Wang et al.34 formulate a mixed-integer programming model for Multi-Tier Shuttle Warehousing Systems (MSWS) to optimize retrieval sequencing and minimize makespan. For small task sets (fewer than ten), the MIP model proves effective, while for larger problems, they develop metaheuristic algorithms—including tabu search, ant colony, and simulated annealing—demonstrating that the ant colony algorithm consistently delivers superior results.

Finally, Dong et al.35 investigate task scheduling in crane-based 3D AS/RS warehouses incorporating shuttle-based Dual Mobile Modules (DMMs). They employ mixed-integer programming along with several heuristic methods, including Percentage Priority to Shuttle Reallocation with the Shortest Leg rule, Genetic Algorithm, and Lowest Waiting-Time-First heuristic. Their findings indicate that the latter heuristic provides the best performance in terms of solution quality and computational time.

In parallel, research on general shuttle-based automated warehouse design has produced comprehensive taxonomies and design frameworks. Battarra et al.36 presented a design framework for shuttle-based automated storage systems, identifying key configuration dimensions and highlighting literature gaps, including the need for further research on shuttle-based systems.

The trend toward integrated optimization in automated warehousing continues. Zhang et al.37 addressed the joint optimization of storage allocation and crane scheduling in AS/RS, demonstrating significant performance gains. Similarly, Geft et al.38 investigated rearrangement-free operations in high-density 2D grid storage with mobile robots. These studies underscore the importance of holistic control strategies but remain within domains where vertical movement is either absent or handled by centralized cranes or lifts, rather than by distributed shuttles navigating and coordinating within a fully integrated 3D puzzle environment.

In summary, the reviewed studies demonstrate significant progress in optimizing automated storage and retrieval systems across different architectures, including shuttle-based, flow-rack, compact, and hybrid systems. However, despite these advancements, most models continue to face computational challenges when scaled to large instances or when integrating full three-dimensional and multi-agent coordination. The adaptive large neighborhood search (ALNS) framework in logistics optimization

The Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) metaheuristic, introduced by Ropke and Pisinger39, has established itself as a powerful and flexible solution framework for complex combinatorial optimization problems, particularly within logistics and warehousing. Its strength lies in iteratively destroying and repairing solutions using an adaptive mechanism that learns the effectiveness of various operators, thereby balancing exploration and exploitation.

Recent applications of ALNS to structurally similar logistics and warehousing problems further underscore its suitability for the present study. For instance, Yu et al.40 successfully employed an ALNS-enhanced Beam Search algorithm to solve the coordinated scheduling problem for lifts and shuttles in Autonomous Vehicle Storage and Retrieval Systems (AVS/RS), minimizing makespan—a problem closely mirroring our need to coordinate shuttles across multiple levels. In a closely related setting, Wang et al.41 developed a two-stage ALNS & Tabu Search heuristic (ALNS&TS) for retrieval scheduling in four-directional shuttle-based systems with heterogeneous lifts, demonstrating ALNS’s effectiveness in managing the intricate coordination of multiple equipment types. Beyond shuttle systems, ALNS has also proven effective in dynamic AGV task allocation42 and integrated storage–picking optimization43, where it is frequently hybridized with exact solvers in a matheuristic fashion to ensure solution feasibility and quality—an approach closely aligned with the proposed ALNS-IP framework.

These contemporary studies (2024–2026) confirm that ALNS is not merely a generic metaheuristic but a well-validated, state-of-the-art methodology for NP-hard warehousing problems characterized by spatial constraints, resource coordination, and dynamic decision-making. However, despite these advances, existing ALNS-based studies have predominantly focused on two-dimensional or partially constrained systems, leaving fully integrated three-dimensional puzzle-based environments largely unexplored. This body of work directly informs and justifies our selection of ALNS as the exploratory core of the proposed hybrid heuristic, designed to navigate the vast solution space of our novel 3D multi-level, multi-shuttle puzzle-based storage problem.

Research gap and contributions

The industrial imperative and the scientific lag

The logistics industry faces unprecedented pressure from urbanization and e-commerce, driving a clear trend toward high-density, three-dimensional automated storage systems to maximize volumetric efficiency in costly urban spaces. Modern warehouses increasingly adopt multi-tier, cube-like configurations serviced by autonomous shuttles. However, a critical disconnect exists between this 3D industrial reality and the state of the art in operational optimization models. While the need for integrated 3D coordination is clear in practice, the scientific literature on warehouse optimization has progressed along a different, predominantly planar trajectory.

The planar paradigm in literature: from foundations to recent work

A review of the literature—from foundational PBS models7 to the very latest research (2023–2025)—reveals a persistent focus on optimizing systems within two-dimensional or vertically segmented frameworks. Recent advancements have explored enhanced mobility within the 2D plane, such as diagonal rack movement16–19, or have simplified system design by restricting movement to a single dimension to reduce cost20. Parallel research on shuttle-based systems offers comprehensive design taxonomies36 or focuses on integrated optimization for crane-based AS/RS37. A common and significant limitation unites these otherwise diverse studies: vertical movement dynamics are either absent, treated as a separate stage managed by lifts/conveyors, or intentionally restricted. Consequently, the operational models remain decoupled from the integrated 3D navigation challenge intrinsic to modern multi-shuttle, multi-level warehouses.

The identified gap: lack of integrated 3D multi-directional optimization

This analysis reveals a pronounced and timely gap. To the best of our knowledge, no existing model addresses the integrated retrieval optimization problem in a true 3D multi-level puzzle-based storage system where autonomous shuttles navigate seamlessly in six directions (four horizontal and two vertical). The problem of coordinating shuttles and items in a unified three-dimensional grid, respecting puzzle-style movement constraints and avoiding collisions across all levels, remains an uncharted territory. Bridging this gap is essential to translate the physical potential of 3D automated warehouses into operational efficiency.

Problem definition and model formulation

This section provides a detailed definition of the problem and outlines the mathematical formulation designed to address retrieval challenges in a multi-level puzzle-based storage system with autonomous shuttles.

Problem definition

The problem addressed in this study arises from the operational complexity of multi-level shuttle-based puzzle storage systems. These systems offer high-density storage capacity but suffer from significant retrieval inefficiencies due to the intricate sequences of horizontal and vertical movements required to access desired items.

This study addresses these challenges by developing an integer programming model aimed at minimizing the total number of movements needed to retrieve desired items. The model captures the dynamics of item movement within a three-dimensional environment, considering both horizontal and vertical displacements across multiple levels to reach a fixed input/output point located at the lower-left corner of the warehouse, and items are randomly distributed throughout the space.

The proposed integer programming model captures the essential dynamics of item and shuttle movements in a multi-level shuttle-based puzzle storage system. By integrating horizontal and vertical operations, simultaneous multi-item retrieval, escort coordination, and collision avoidance, the model provides a comprehensive and practical framework for optimizing retrieval performance and operational efficiency.

However, the problem formulated in this model is computationally challenging. It generalizes known NP-hard problems such as Request Scheduling Problem (RSP), the Block Relocation Problem (BRP) and Multi-Agent Path Finding (MAPF), all of which are proven to be computationally intractable for large instances5,26,44,45. As a result, solving the model exactly using standard solvers becomes impractical in real-time or large-scale scenarios. To overcome this limitation, we propose an efficient heuristic algorithm in the next section to generate high-quality solutions within reasonable computational time. Model size and complexity discussion

The proposed integer programming formulation involves a large number of binary decision variables and constraints due to the explicit modeling of item and shuttle movements over space and time. The dominant decision variables are , , , ,which are indexed by origin position i, neighboring destination , item or shuttle index, and time step t. Since each position in the 3D puzzle-based storage grid has at most 6 neighbors (corresponding to movements along the three spatial axes), the branching factor per position is bounded by a small constant. Therefore, the total number of binary variables scales on the order of O(∣I∣⋅∣T∣⋅(∣M∣ + ∣K∣)), where ∣I∣, ∣M∣, ∣K∣, and ∣T∣ denote the numbers of storage positions, items, shuttles, and time steps, respectively. The number of constraints grows at a comparable rate. In particular, flow conservation and capacity constraints scale as O(∣T∣⋅∣I∣⋅(∣M∣ + ∣K∣)), while collision-avoidance constraints introduce a quadratic dependence on the number of shuttles, resulting in a dominant term of order O(∣T∣⋅∣I∣⋅∣K∣2). This analysis highlights the rapid growth in model size as problem dimensions increase, particularly with respect to the time horizon ∣T∣ and the number of shuttles ∣K∣. The quadratic term in ∣K∣ explains why multi-shuttle instances become computationally challenging quickly. Consequently, directly solving the full IP formulation becomes intractable for moderate and large instances, which motivates the hybrid ALNS-IP approach proposed in this study.

Heuristic-based solution method

This study introduces a novel hybrid approach that integrates Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) with a mathematical programming-based repair mechanism, thereby creating a strong synergy between metaheuristic exploration and exact optimization. Unlike conventional methods that typically employ heuristic repair strategies, our approach enhances solution quality by reconstructing the destroyed parts of a solution through optimal integer programming (IP) models.

The ALNS framework, originally proposed by Ropke and Pisinger39, has been widely recognized as an effective heuristic for tackling complex combinatorial optimization problems, particularly those classified as NP-hard. The algorithm operates by iteratively destroying a portion of the current solution and subsequently repairing it to generate improved solutions. A key advantage of ALNS lies in its adaptive mechanism and modular design, which dynamically adjusts the selection of neighborhood operators based on their historical performance, thereby balancing exploration and exploitation throughout the search process39. This modularity is precisely what makes ALNS an ideal candidate for enhancement through hybridization with exact methods.

Hybrid adaptive large neighborhood search with optimization-based repair

The warehouse scheduling problem involves coordinating multiple shuttles to retrieve items while satisfying spatial, temporal, and capacity constraints. Its mathematical formulation results in a complex integer program, which is computationally challenging to solve optimally for practical-sized instances.

To address this challenge, we propose a hybrid framework that combines the global exploration capabilities of Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) with the exact optimization power of mathematical programming. ALNS explores a large solution space through adaptive destruction operators, while mathematical programming precisely repairs destroyed solution segments. By integrating these approaches, the framework preserves feasibility and improves overall solution quality beyond what either method could achieve alone.

ALNS was specifically chosen due to its effectiveness in large-scale combinatorial optimization problems with complex spatial and temporal constraints. This choice is further substantiated by its proven and contemporary success in solving analogous NP-hard logistics optimization problems, as reviewed in Section "The Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search (ALNS) Framework in Logistics Optimization". Compared to other popular metaheuristics such as Genetic Algorithms (GA), Simulated Annealing (SA), or Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), ALNS offers distinct advantages for our problem: (1) its operator-based adaptive mechanism is inherently suited to the discrete, move-based nature of puzzle storage operations, allowing it to learn effective destruction/repair strategies; (2) it provides a natural framework for hybridization with exact methods (e.g., IP repair), enabling a balance between global search and local optimality that is less straightforward in population-based methods (e.g., GA); and (3) it has demonstrated superior scalability and solution quality in recent, direct comparisons for complex scheduling and routing problems in automated warehouses (e.g.,40,41). Therefore, ALNS forms the ideal exploratory backbone for our hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic. Its adaptive selection of destruction and repair operators balances exploration and intensification, making it suitable for dynamic problem instances. Furthermore, ALNS’s modular structure allows seamless integration with exact optimization techniques, enabling the hybrid framework to achieve higher-quality solutions than standard heuristic repairs.

The framework also introduces key innovations: the mathematical programming-based repair operator ensures optimal reconstruction of destroyed segments, a deviation-minimization strategy maintains high solution quality while allowing controlled exploration, a warm-start mechanism accelerates convergence using information from the current solution, and the destruction-repair balance is dynamically adjusted based on observed performance to further enhance efficiency and effectiveness.

Initial solution generation

Since the hybrid ALNS framework requires a feasible starting solution, we developed a greedy constructive heuristic that assigns shuttles to items and constructs coordinated paths. Mathematical programming-based repair operator

The choice of employing a single optimization-based repair operator is deliberate and problem-driven. The proposed 3D multi-shuttle puzzle-based retrieval problem is characterized by a highly constrained and tightly coupled decision structure, where shuttle movements, item trajectories, collision avoidance, and temporal synchronization are interdependent. In such environments, feasible solutions are typically surrounded by a large number of infeasible neighbors.

Preliminary experiments revealed that heuristic or rule-based repair strategies frequently failed to restore feasibility after destruction, as even minor local changes tend to violate multiple constraints simultaneously (e.g., collisions, capacity violations, or temporal inconsistencies). Similar behavior has been reported in tightly constrained MILP-based problems in the literature, where random or heuristic neighborhood modifications are unlikely to yield feasible solutions within reasonable computational effort46.

For this reason, we adopt a single, exact Integer Programming (IP) repair operator. This operator guarantees feasibility by explicitly enforcing all system constraints, while its deviation-minimization objective ensures that repaired solutions remain close to the original structure. Rather than limiting exploration, this design stabilizes the ALNS search process and allows meaningful diversification to be achieved through the destroy operators, while feasibility is systematically preserved by the optimization-based repair.

The results reveal a consistent pattern across all instance sizes: the extended destroy operators provide no improvement in solution quality while imposing substantial computational penalties. Runtime increases are particularly pronounced for smaller instances—reaching 321.49% for S1—and remain significant for medium-scale problems (up to 84%).

This behavior can be attributed to a heuristic–recovery misalignment. Operators such as Worst Removal or Related Removal rely on spatial or cost-based heuristics that conflict with the subsequent IP-based repair phase. Since the repair operator must satisfy all system constraints—including collision avoidance, shuttle synchronization, and capacity limits—it often reconstructs similar movement sequences to restore feasibility, thereby neutralizing the intended effect of the destruction. As a result, larger or more aggressive perturbations tend to push the search into infeasible regions, increasing computational effort without yielding superior solutions.

In contrast, the three core destroy operators—designed specifically for controlled and structured perturbations within the tightly constrained 3D puzzle-based environment—strike an optimal balance between diversification and repairability. Consequently, all subsequent results reported for the hybrid ALNS-IP algorithm (Sections "Comparison Between the Exact Model (Gurobi) and the Heuristic Algorithm" onward) are based exclusively on this Core Configuration. These findings strongly validate our customization approach: rather than relying on generic operators, we designed specialized destroy mechanisms aligned with both the problem structure and the mathematical programming–based repair process.

Comprehensive interpretation of results

The comparative analysis between the exact optimization model (Gurobi) and the proposed hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic reveals clear and consistent patterns that highlight both the theoretical soundness and the practical value of our approach.

For small- and medium-scale instances (S1 and S2), the heuristic consistently reproduce the same objective values as the exact solver, thereby validating its accuracy and reliability. In these settings, the observed gaps are either zero or negligible, demonstrating the algorithm’s ability to provide solutions that are effectively optimal.

The distinction becomes most evident in large- and very large-scale instances (S3 and S4). While the exact solver frequently fails—due to either memory exhaustion or exceeding the one-hour time limit—the hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic successfully generates feasible, high-quality solutions within practical computational times. This scalability represents a decisive advantage, as it ensures that the algorithm remains applicable in real-world scenarios where problem sizes quickly excess the capacity of exact methods.

Another important aspect is the balance between solution quality and efficiency. In the limited cases where both approaches produced results, the heuristic’s gap relative to the theoretical optimum remained minimal—often within single-digit percentages—highlighting its consistent performance even when directly comparable to the exact solver. Such small deviations are acceptable, given the substantial reductions in computational effort and the ability to provide solutions when exact solvers are unable to do so.

In conclusion, the results confirm that the proposed hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic does not merely approximate the exact model but extends beyond it in terms of practical feasibility. By combining near-optimal accuracy in small and medium cases with overwhelming superiority in large-scale and computationally demanding problems, the algorithm establishes itself as a robust and scalable solution method. These findings underscore both the scientific contribution and the real-world applicability of this work, positioning the heuristic as a valuable tool for optimizing complex automated storage and retrieval systems.

Sensitivity analysis

To further enrich the evaluation and provide deeper insights into the robustness of the proposed hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic, we conduct a set of sensitivity analyses. This examination makes it possible to understand how the algorithm behaves when critical parameters of the problem or the computational environment are varied, thereby highlighting both its stability and adaptability.

The analysis focuses on three main aspects: extending the computation time to assess improvements in solution quality and optimality gaps, modifying the number of shuttles to examine how changes in system resources influence performance, and varying the demand size to evaluate the effect of different numbers of requested items. The following subsections discuss the results of each experiment in detail.

Sensitivity analysis with respect to computation time

To investigate whether extending runtime leads to improved solution quality or reduced optimality gaps, additional experiments are conducted with time limits increased from one hour to two and three hours. The analysis focuses on two representative instances: M8R2K2T54 and M13R2K2T128. Both instances are previously solved by the heuristic, while the exact solver (Gurobi) struggles to reach optimality within one hour. The experiments aim to evaluate how longer runtimes affect the performance of the exact solver and the proposed hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic.

As shown in Table 18, extending the runtime gradually improved both the objective values and the lower bounds reported by Gurobi for the selected instances. For M8R2K2T54, ALNS-IP consistently reached the best solution of 22 across all time limits, while Gurobi’s lower bound increased from 10 to 12, reducing the reported gap from 54.54% to 45.45%, with the objective value remaining at 22. For M13R2K2T128, ALNS-IP maintained a solution of 29, whereas Gurobi’s objective value decreased from 31 at 1 h to 29 at 2 h and failed due to memory exhaustion at 3 h. These results confirm the stability and time efficiency of the heuristic under extended runtimes, particularly for instances where exact methods encounter computational limits.

Sensitivity analysis with respect to the number of shuttles K

To examine the impact of system resources on solution quality, we further test the heuristic by increasing the number of available shuttles by one in the same set of instances. The results in Table 19 reveal that the effect of additional shuttles is not uniform. In some cases, the total number of moves decreases (e.g., M10R3K3T54), reflecting the expected operational benefit of parallelization. In other cases, the number of moves remains unchanged (e.g., M25R2K2T128), or even increases slightly (e.g., M25R7K7T128). These findings indicate that, under the adopted modeling assumptions, additional shuttles do not automatically guarantee performance improvement. Although shuttles are synchronized at each discrete time step, an idle shuttle is permitted to remain in its current position without performing any spatial movement. Accordingly, the reported number of moves reflects task-related spatial operations, while waiting behavior is explicitly captured through stay actions with zero displacement. Nevertheless, as shuttle density increases, coordination complexity and potential route interference in shared aisles may intensify, which can offset the expected benefits of parallelization. This observation highlights that, within the scope of the proposed model, performance gains from adding shuttles strongly depend on effective coordination and control strategies in puzzle-based storage systems.

Conclusion and future work

This study introduces a novel mathematical model and a hybrid ALNS-IP heuristic for multi-level shuttle-based puzzle storage systems, representing the first investigation that explicitly integrates both horizontal and vertical movements in such systems. The proposed model captures the unique operational constraints of three-dimensional multi-level storage warehouses, where shuttles can move in six directions, significantly enhancing retrieval efficiency compared to traditional horizontal-only models.

Numerical experiments demonstrate that the heuristic achieves near-optimal solutions for small- and medium-sized instances and provides feasible, high-quality solutions for large-scale instances that exceed the computational capacity of exact solvers. The sensitivity analyses further confirm the robustness of the approach across varying computation times, demand levels, and resource configurations, highlighting both its adaptability and practical applicability. Overall, this research makes a dual contribution: it extends the theoretical framework of automated storage optimization to include vertical dynamics and offers a scalable, reliable solution method applicable to real-world warehouse operations.

Future work should focus on enhancing computational efficiency through decomposition strategies, reinforcement learning–based retrieval policies, and advanced metaheuristics adapted to 3D spatial optimization. Further studies may investigate optimal warehouse configurations, multi-I/O layouts, and robust control under dynamic and uncertain conditions such as shuttle failures or congestion. Integrating storage and retrieval decisions with energy-efficient objectives can improve system sustainability.

Beyond Lymphocytic Inflammation: Immune Architecture as a Determinant of Renal Outcomes
Authors: Kanishka Harariya, Thakur Rohit Singh, Ankita Kalra, Swarupanjali Padhi, Fayaz Ahamed
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

The study by Boudhabhay et al.,1 published in the Kidney International Reports, provides a timely and mechanistically oriented contribution to the evolving understanding of immune checkpoint inhibitor-associated acute interstitial nephritis. By integrating multiplex immunofluorescence with unsupervised clustering, the authors move beyond descriptive histology to propose biologically distinct inflammatory subtypes with prognostic relevance.

A central strength of this work lies in its cell-resolved tissue profiling, which reveals a neutrophil-rich immune phenotype that is not adequately captured by conventional Banff-based scoring. The identification of a cluster characterized by neutrophilic tubulitis, granular casts, heightened systemic inflammation, and inferior renal recovery emphasizes that immune checkpoint inhibitor-associated acute interstitial nephritis is not a uniform mononuclear process.2 This observation is clinically consequential, because it suggests that standard histopathologic grading may underestimate risk in patients whose injury is driven by innate immune pathways rather than adaptive lymphocytic responses.

The association between this neutrophil-dominant phenotype and complement activation further advances mechanistic insight. The correlation between urinary C5a levels and intrarenal C5aR1-positive neutrophils supports a biologically coherent axis linking systemic complement activation to tissue-level injury.3 This finding is particularly relevant given the growing availability of complement-targeted therapies in immune-mediated renal diseases and raises the possibility that selected patients with immune checkpoint inhibitor–associated acute interstitial nephritis may benefit from pathway-directed interventions beyond corticosteroids.

Importantly, the observed divergence in steroid responsiveness across immune clusters underscores the limitations of a uniform treatment paradigm. The high mononuclear-cell cluster exhibited favorable renal trajectories, whereas the neutrophil-rich subgroup showed attenuated responses and frequent relapses despite immunotherapy withdrawal. These patterns suggest that immune composition, rather than severity alone, may influence therapeutic sensitivity and long-term renal stability.

Overall, this study reframes immune checkpoint inhibitor–associated acute interstitial nephritis as a spectrum of immune-defined entities with distinct biological drivers and clinical courses. Incorporating immune phenotyping into diagnostic workflows may refine risk stratification and inform more personalized management strategies. Such an approach could ultimately improve renal outcomes while preserving the oncologic benefits of immune checkpoint inhibition.

Regarding the sample size calculation, it is reiterated that there were no studies that directly compared and evaluated the primary objective of neurological status by anterolateral decompression (ALD) versus transpedicular decompression (TPD). Two separate studies of ALD and TPD were selected for sample size calculation based on a similar posterior midline incision and an identical primary outcome of neurological assessment by the American Spinal Injury Association (ASIA) impairment scale2,3). Stringent inclusion criteria were ensured to avoid anterior reconstruction by selecting patients with 2-level disease with neurological deficit. A long follow-up was required to provide adequate time for neurological recovery, which is often unpredictable in patients with spinal tuberculosis. We acknowledge the limitation that the small sample size of our preliminary study could not permit a detailed statistical analysis. Further multicentric studies might be required with a larger sample size to decrease the type II error, analyze the data using mixed-effect modeling, and perform regression-based adjustment for baseline parameters particularly for kyphotic angle.

We have analyzed within-group data using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and between-group data using the Mann-Whitney test. The Kruskal-Wallis test is typically used to analyze data of three or more independent groups, though not ideal, it may be used when the sample size in two groups is very small, at least five or more4).

To improve the interpretability of the radiological outcome, the median difference and the 95% CI of the kyphotic angle have been illustrated by box-plots depicting the preoperative angle, immediate correction, and loss of correction between TPD and ALD groups in Figure 2. It demonstrates that there is no significant difference between TPD and ALD groups with respect to immediate correction (p-value = 0.8) and loss of correction (p = 0.5). While the TPD group showed greater variability in immediate correction, median values were comparable, indicating similar overall performance of both techniques.

The clinical importance of this comparative study in patients with spinal tuberculosis cases who do not require anterior reconstruction is the short duration of surgery in TPD compared to ALD, with similar neurological, functional, and radiological outcomes. Since both the surgical procedures are done in the prone position, the clinical decision may be made to select the quicker surgery of TPD in elderly persons or patients with multiple comorbidities and high risk of anesthesia.

The Newcastle-Ottawa scale for cohort studies reveals three stars in the selection domain, two stars for comparability, and two stars for outcome domains, indicating that this study is of good quality.

It is reiterated that the exclusion of patients requiring anterior column reconstruction was purposely done to maintain the originally described ‘true’ TPD, avoiding variable factors of extending the TPD by hemilaminectomy or laminectomy for introducing graft or cage5). True TPD, similar to ALD, restricts the destabilizing of the posterior non-diseased parts of the vertebra in the paradiscal type of spinal TB.

Both ALD and TPD involve surgical decompression of the dura by removal of the diseased anterior and lateral ALD or lateral TPD parts of vertebral bodies by a posterior midline approach and are not to be confused with posterior-only decompression.

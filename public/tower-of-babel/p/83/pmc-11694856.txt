Evaluation of efficacy of oral ivabradine for attenuation of hemodynamic response to intubation in hypertensive surgical patients: Effect on rate pressure product as an index of myocardial oxygen demand
Authors: Udita Naithani, Riyaz K. Ahamed, Shweta Jain, Vandana Gakkhar, Isha Garg, Gaurav Saxena
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background and Aims:An exaggerated hemodynamic response to endotracheal intubation is observed in hypertensive patients, and its attenuation proves challenging. The role of oral ivabradine, a unique heart rate-lowering drug with a favorable hemodynamic profile, is not yet studied. The aim of this study was to evaluate the effect of oral ivabradine on the attenuation of hemodynamic response to endotracheal intubation in hypertensive surgical patients assessed by rate pressure product (RPP), which is a very reliable indicator of myocardial oxygen demand.Material and Methods:Sixty medically controlled hypertensive surgical patients, aged 30–65 years, receiving general anesthesia were divided into two equal groups: Group I received a tablet of ivabradine 5 mg and group C received a placebo tablet 1 hour before induction. Heart rate (HR) and systolic, diastolic, and mean arterial blood pressure (SBP, DBP, and MAP) were recorded at baseline, preoperative, immediately after intubation, 1 min, 3 min, 5 min, and 10 min following intubation. RPP was calculated at the above time intervals. Data were analyzed using the unpaired t-test and the Chi-square test as required, with P < 0.05 considered significant.Results:The maximum value of RPP after intubation was significantly less in group I (11065.64 ± 606.56) as compared to group C (16774.64 ± 1242.87), P = 0.000. All hemodynamic variables, RPP, HR, SBP, DBP, and MAP, remained significantly less in group I than group C at all time intervals (P = 0.000). These parameters never increased above baseline after intubation in the ivabradine group, indicating effective attenuation of the intubation response.Conclusion:Premedication with oral ivabradine 5 mg is very effective in attenuating the hemodynamic response to intubation in hypertensive patients.

Introduction

Hemodynamic stress response following laryngoscopy and endotracheal intubation is a known phenomenon since it was first reported in 1940 by Reid and Brace.[1] It is a sympathoadrenal response caused by the stimulation of proprioceptors in the orolaryngopharynx and trachea that results in hypertension and tachycardia, which are transient, variable, and usually well tolerated in normotensive patients. This response is exaggerated in hypertensive patients, and catastrophic incidents involving pulmonary edema, cardiac failure, and cerebrovascular hemorrhage following intubation have been reported in them.[23]

Various drugs have been investigated in hypertensive patients to attenuate the intubation–pressor response such as beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, opioids, and alpha 2 agonist, with variable results and adverse effects,[4567] but the search for an ideal agent still continues.

Ivabradine is a new cardiotonic drug that specifically acts on funny channels (If), especially in the sinoatrial (SA) node, and decreases heart rate (HR) without any negative inotropic effect. Therefore, it is very useful in patients with cardiac failure, coronary artery disease, and cardiomyopathy for lowering the heart rate.[8] By not allowing the HR to increase, ivabradine conserves the myocardial oxygen reserves and hence decreases the cardiac oxygen demand.[910] Contraindications to ivabradine as mentioned in the literature are decompensated heart failure, BP <90/50, conduction abnormalities such as sick sinus syndrome, sinoatrial blocks, AV blocks, severe hepatic impairment, and HR <60/min.[9] Oral ivabradine premedication has been investigated recently for attenuation of the intubation response in normotensive patients in many studies[11121314] and was found effective, but no study has been conducted in hypertensive patients where its role has become even more important because it decreases heart rate without any negative inotropic effect. Moreover, none of the studies have yet evaluated the effect of ivabradine on the change in rate pressure product (RPP) following intubation.

RPP is a very reliable indicator of myocardial oxygen consumption (MVO2). It is a good measure of stress put on cardiac muscle based on the number of times it needs to beat per minute and the arterial blood pressure (BP) that it is pumping against. RPP is calculated by multiplying HR and systolic blood pressure (SBP). RPP below 10000 is standard RPP, and higher values indicate a greater workload on the heart.[1516] It is considered a determinant of cardiovascular risk, since its increase precedes ischemic events.[1718] Hence, we used RPP in our study as a determinant of myocardial oxygen requirement and consumption following intubation.

We aimed this study to test the hypothesis that oral ivabradine (5 mg tablet given 1 hour before induction) is effective in the attenuation of hemodynamic response to tracheal intubation in controlled hypertensive surgical patients. The primary objective of the study was to evaluate its effect on RPP following intubation. Secondary objectives were effects on heart rate, systolic, diastolic, and mean blood pressure, and complications as compared to the placebo control group.

Material and Methods

After approval from the institutional ethical committee (No. RNT/Stat./IEC/2021/846) dated October 27, 2021, and obtaining written informed consent from patients, this randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled interventional study was conducted in 60 American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) grade II controlled hypertensive patients, aged 30–65 years of both genders, receiving general anesthesia in a tertiary care institute for six months. The study was registered under the Clinical Trials Registry, India, CTRI/2022/09/045329 dated September 8, 2022.

Helsinki declaration codes were followed. Considering the paucity of similar studies using RPP to evaluate the efficacy of oral ivabradine for the attenuation of intubation response in hypertensive patients, the sample size was calculated based on the pilot study. To detect the minimum difference in RPP value of 2500 immediately after intubation between the ivabradine and control groups with a standard deviation (SD) of 1200, using 90% power and 5% alpha error, four patients were required in each group for this superiority trial with a parallel group design and equal allocation. To remain in compliance with the central limit theorem for a normal sampling distribution, we needed a sample size of 30 in each group. A total of 77 patients were assessed for eligibility, and 17 were excluded for not fulfilling inclusion criteria. Sixty patients received the allocated intervention and completed the study without any loss to follow-up [Figure 1].

CONSORT flow chart of patients

Exclusion criteria were patients undergoing cardiac surgery or neurosurgery, those on beta-blockers, bradycardia <60/min, heart blocks and hypotension (SBP <100 mm Hg), patients with difficult airway, short stature, obesity [Body Mass Index (BMI) >30 kg/m2], history of cardiac disease or any other coexisting medical illness, contraindication to ivabradine use, and BP >140/90 even after antihypertensive medication. If controlled hypertensive patients (BP <140/90) had a BP >150/95 mmHg on the operation day, they were also excluded from the study.

After preanesthetic evaluation and routine investigations, patients were explained the study protocol and kept fasting as per standard guidelines. The patients taking Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors or angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs) were asked to omit the night dose the day before surgery and the morning dose on the day of surgery. Patients were randomized into two groups of 30 patients each using a block randomization generator, and concealment was performed using the opaque sealed envelope technique. To ensure double blinding, the placebo and ivabradine tablets were kept identical and the patients and assessor were unaware of the study drug administered.

Group I (ivabradine group) patients received oral ivabradine 5 mg tablet (Ivabrad 5 mg, Lupin Ltd., East Sikkim, India) 1 hour before induction.Group C (control group) patients received an oral placebo multivitamin tablet 1 hour before induction.

After arrival in the preanesthetic room, “baseline” parameters were recorded including HR, systolic BP (SBP), diastolic BP (DBP), and mean arterial BP (MAP), and peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2). A 20-G peripheral intravenous cannulation was performed, and Ringer’s lactate infusion of 500 ml was started. Oral premedication was given as per group allocation. Vital parameters and any adverse effects were monitored for 1 hour. In the operation theatre, a multipara monitor was used, which included noninvasive blood pressure, electrocardiogram, pulse oximetry, end-tidal carbon dioxide (EtCO2), the bispectral index (BIS) monitoring was connected to the patient, and preoperative vital parameters were recorded. Patients were premedicated with inj. midazolam 0.02 mg/kg IV and inj. fentanyl 2 mcg/kg IV. After preoxygenation, induction was performed by inj. propofol 2 mg/kg IV and inj. succinylcholine 1.5 mg/kg IV. After ventilation with 100% oxygen for one minute, endotracheal oral intubation was performed with an appropriate size, cuffed, Portex endotracheal tube (ETT) with the aid of Macintosh direct laryngoscopy. After confirmation of tracheal intubation by EtCO2 and bilateral equal air entry in the chest field, ETT was fixed. Ventilation was started using 100% oxygen and sevoflurane 1–2% with a closed circuit, and inj. vecuronium 0.08 mg/kg was given for the maintenance of balanced anesthesia. Ventilation was adjusted to keep EtCO2 30–40 mmHg, and sevoflurane vol% was titrated to keep BIS 40–60.

As ETT placement was confirmed by the EtCO2 graph, this point of time was considered “immediately after intubation” (time 0) for further hemodynamic data recording. The hemodynamic data recorded were HR, SBP, DBP, and MAP on the monitor, and RPP was calculated by multiplying SBP (mmHg) and HR (bpm). The above data were recorded at the following time points: baseline (before oral drug), preoperative (1 hour after oral drug), immediately after intubation (0 min), and at 1 min, 3 min, 5 min, 7 min, and 10 min after intubation.

Side effects such as hypotension and bradycardia were defined as a fall in SBP and heart rate more than 20% of the baseline or absolute value of SBP <100 and HR <50 whichever occurred and were treated with mephentermine 6 mg and atropine 0.6 mg, respectively. Any other side effects such as ventricular extrasystoles, ST and T-wave (ST-T) changes, and arrhythmia were also recorded and treated accordingly. Surgery was allowed to start after 10 minutes[12] because the intubation-pressor response by that time is expected to be abolished[1] and the study was declared complete. The maintenance of anesthesia was further done as per the case.

The primary outcome measured was changes in RPP following intubation. Secondary outcomes measured were changes in hemodynamic parameters (HR, SBP, DBP, and MAP) and complications.

Statistical analysis

Data were entered in Microsoft Excel and analyzed using Statistical Package for Social Science (SPSS) version 20 by IBM. Initially, the normal distribution of data was investigated using the skewness and kurtosis tests. Qualitative data were presented as number (proportion) and quantitative variables were presented as mean ± S.D. and compared using the Chi-square test and Student’s t-test, respectively. For within-group comparison of hemodynamic variables, a paired t-test was used, and for intergroup comparison, an unpaired t-test was used. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

On within-group comparison, there was no significant change in mean HR, SBP, DBP, MAP, and RPP as compared to baseline (P > 0.05) after the placebo oral drug in group C. In the ivabradine group (group I) as compared to baseline, preoperative value (after one hour of oral ivabradine) showed a significant fall of 9.38% in SBP (138.57 to 125.57 mmHg), 6.88% in DBP (82.80 to 77.10 mmHg), 7.98% in MAP (101.4 to 93.3 mmHg), 10.64% in HR (88 to 78.63 bpm), and 18.6% fall in RPP (12144 to 9873.56) (P = 0.000). This fall in hemodynamic parameters from baseline was clinically acceptable, and no patient had bradycardia or hypotension. It was noteworthy that all these hemodynamic variables remained lower than the baseline value even after intubation in the ivabradine group till 10 min, which was evidence of effective blunting of the intubation response. Therefore, we assessed hemodynamic response to intubation by observing a rise in hemodynamic parameters following intubation as compared to their preoperative value rather than from baseline in both groups.

As compared to the preoperative value, a maximal rise in hemodynamic parameters occurred immediately after intubation in both groups, but it was significantly of higher magnitude in group C as compared to group I (P = 0.000). In the control group, mean HR increased from preoperative 88.40 to 112.13 bpm at intubation (26.7%), SBP from 137.93 to 149.60 mmHg (8.46%), DBP from 82.90 to 88.03 mmHg (6.15%), MAP from 101.2 to 108.6 mmHg (7.31%), and RPP from 12202.60 to 16774.64 (37.46%), whereas in the ivabradine group HR increased from preoperative 78.63 to 84.97 bpm (8.06%) after intubation, SBP from 125.57 to 130.23 mmHg (3.71%), DBP from 77.10 to 80.40 mmHg (4.28%), MAP from 93.3 to 97 mmHg (3.965%), and RPP from 9873.56 to 11065.64 (12.07%) [Table 3 and Figure 2].

No side effects were observed in any patient in the ivabradine group during the study, while in control group 2 (6.66%) patients had ventricular premature beats after intubation, which coincided with a rise in BP and HR and was easily treated with a 1.5 mg/kg lidocaine i.v. bolus. None of the patients had ST-T changes in the study.

Discussion

In the present study, oral ivabradine (5 mg) in group I versus placebo in control group C was compared to attenuate the hemodynamic response to intubation in controlled hypertensive patients. We found that all hemodynamic variables (RPP, HR, SBP, DBP, and MAP) postintubation were significantly less in the ivabradine group as compared to the control group at all time intervals till 10 min after intubation. Ivabradine was so effective in blunting intubation response that all these hemodynamic variables did not show any rise from baseline even after intubation till 10 min, whereas in the control group they showed a significant rise from baseline, which took 7–10 min to normalize after intubation.

Exaggerated hemodynamic responses are observed in hypertensive patients during laryngoscopy and intubation that may be detrimental. Arterial lumen narrowing, increased sympathetic tone, raised levels of plasma catecholamines, and increased sensitivity of peripheral vessels to catecholamines are proposed mechanisms.[19] Multiple drugs such as beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, and alpha2 agonists have been evaluated to abolish this response in hypertensive patients and were found effective.[4567] Long-standing hypertension may be associated with coronary ischemic heart disease, heart failure, renal insufficiency, etc.,[20] where the above drugs may cause hemodynamic instability perioperatively. ACE inhibitors and ARB drugs were discontinued on the night before surgery as intraoperative hypotension is more frequent in patients with controlled hypertension on ACE inhibitors.[21]

Ivabradine is a relatively new HR-lowering agent, which decreases myocardial oxygen demand similar to propranolol, without any evident depressant effect on cardiac function.[22] The If is one of the most important currents in pacemaking in the sinoatrial node.[23] Animal studies indicate that ivabradine is a selective and specific inhibitor of If and has few other actions to slow the rate of diastolic depolarization and hence reduce HR in the absence of significant negative inotropic effects.[2425] Ivabradine is very useful in controlling the hemodynamics, particularly the heart rate, in all types of patients, especially where beta-blockers are contraindicated such as heart failure, asthmatics, and diabetics.[22]

We administered oral ivabradine 1 hour before induction. Ivabradine is well absorbed orally from the gastrointestinal tract within 20–30 min, with peak plasma concentration attained by 60–90 min,[9] which corresponds with the time of hemodynamic response to intubation. Previous studies[911121319] have found that oral ivabradine effectively abolishes the intubation–pressor response in normotensive patients in terms of a rise in heart rate and blood pressure. However, ivabradine efficacy has not been studied in hypertensive patients, and moreover, none of them calculated its effect on RPP.

RPP is a very reliable indicator of myocardial oxygen demand and is widely used clinically.[20] Hence, we used fluctuations in RPP as a primary determinant of myocardial stress generated by intubation–pressor response, indicating any impending cardiac event. It allows prediction of the internal workload or hemodynamic response defined as low if RPP is 10000–14999, intermediate if RPP is 15000–29999, and high if RPP >30000.[9] In our study, the maximum value of RPP following intubation was 11065 in the ivabradine group, indicating a low hemodynamic response, whereas it was 16774 in group C, which indicates an intermediate degree of hemodynamic response.

In our study, as compared to the preoperative value rise in RPP following intubation was 12.07% in the ivabradine group and 37.46% in the control group. Differences in these changes in RPP were attributed mainly to the attenuation of the rise in HR by ivabradine as evidenced by a rise in HR of 26.7% in the control group as compared to only 8.06% rise in the ivabradine group. The rise in SBP was 3.71% in group I versus 8.46% in group C. Tachycardia has a stressful effect on heart, leading to increased myocardial oxygen requirement, reduced diastolic filling, and a decrease in the time needed for effective coronary circulation. Tachycardia accompanied by hypertension increases the existing ischemia risk in patients with coronary artery disease.[26]

Similar to our study, Arora et al. administered oral ivabradine 5 mg 1 hour before induction and observed that the mean pulse rate (bpm) in the preoperative stage was 97.60 ± 7.326 in the control group and 82.60 ± 3.279 in the ivabradine group, respectively, which increased to 103.92 ± 21.675 in the control group while decreased to 80.56 ± 2.551 in the ivabradine group during intubation (P < 0.05).[12] In another study by Kunwer et al., they administered oral ivabradine 5 mg at 9 pm on the evening before and another 5 mg 1 hour before intubation. They reported that the mean HR (bpm) in the control group was 102.86 at the preoperative time (after oral drug) that increased up to 120.88 during intubation, while in the ivabradine group it was 82.6 preoperatively (after oral ivabradine), which rose only up to 85.43 during intubation. The difference in rise in HR was significant in the two groups, P < 0.05.[11] Both of these studies were in normotensive patients. Combination therapy of ivabradine and beta-blockers has been found effective in the improvement in cardiovascular outcomes in systolic heart failure patients in systolic heart failure treatment (SHIFT) with the If Inhibitor Ivabradine Trial 2010; however, ivabradine was added to beta-blocker in patients with a heart rate >70 bpm.[27] While ivabradine has a different primary mechanism of action to beta-blockers, beta-blockers may also affect If amplitude and action potential duration of the nodal cells (via indirect inhibition of cyclic adenosine monophosphate generation).[28] Hence, we excluded patients taking beta-blockers as antihypertensive treatment regime as we even included patients with a HR >60 bpm.

Ivabradine does not have a negative inotropic effect, but it still causes reduction in blood pressure to some extent (5–10% from baseline) as observed in our study and others,[9111213] and many attributing factors are described in the literature to explain this. There are several pleiotropic effects of ivabradine, such as anti-inflammatory and antioxidant actions, the improvement of endothelium-dependent and endothelium-independent vascular relaxation, anti-atherosclerotic effects, and the attenuation of neurohumoral activation, which might individually or in combination contribute to BP reduction.[10] It could be the reason that SBP, DBP, and MAP were also significantly less in group I than in group C at all time intervals following intubation in our study and others.[9111213]

No adverse effects such as hypotension or bradycardia were seen in any patient in our study in the ivabradine group that proves its safety. However, two (6.66%) patients in the control group had ventricular extrasystole after intubation, which coincided with a rise in blood pressure and heart rate and was easily treated.

The strength of our study is that it is the first study in which oral ivabradine was assessed for intubation–pressor response in hypertensive patients, and we have also measured RPP to see its effect on myocardial oxygen demand following intubation. However, there are some limitations such as the fact that we did not use invasive arterial blood pressure monitoring, which would have shown a much accurate rise in blood pressure following intubation, because the use of invasive monitoring could not be justified in routine surgical cases. We have also not measured other stress hormone levels such as catecholamines and cortisol, to assess the attenuation of stress response to intubation, which would have validated our results further, if ivabradine had any such effect. We did not include patients taking beta-blockers in our study, which may decrease the practical application of the study to some extent. Further studies should be conducted considering these limitations.

Conclusion

We conclude that oral ivabradine premedication (5 mg) administered one hour before laryngoscopy and intubation significantly reduces all hemodynamic variables such as RPP, HR, SBP, DBP, and MAP in controlled hypertensive adult patients receiving general anesthesia when compared to the placebo group. Thus, oral ivabradine may be useful for premedication in hypertensive surgical patients.

Key message

Oral ivabradine has been found effective for the attenuation of hemodynamic response to intubation in normotensive patients. We found oral ivabradine 5 mg premedication significantly attenuated intubation response in hypertensive patients as assessed by rate pressure product (RPP).

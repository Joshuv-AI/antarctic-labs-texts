The significant prognostic value of the hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte, and platelet (HALP) score in digestive system cancers: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Caihua Qian, Jingting Liu, Chunyan Meng, Jun Cheng, Bo Wu, Jianhua Liao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDigestive system cancers are a major global health burden, necessitating reliable and cost-effective prognostic biomarkers for clinical decision-making. The hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte, and platelet (HALP) score, an integrated immune-nutrition marker, has emerged as a potential prognostic tool in various malignancies. This meta-analysis evaluated the prognostic value of the HALP score in patients with digestive system cancers.MethodsA systematic search was performed in PubMed, PMC, and Web of Science database for studies assessing HALP scores calculated from pre-treatment blood tests and reporting survival outcomes in patients with digestive system cancers. Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were pooled to evaluate the prognostic significance of the HALP score.ResultsA total of 30 articles comprising 34 studies and 9,389 patients were included. High HALP scores were significantly associated with improved survival outcomes. The pooled HR for overall survival (OS) was 1.762 (95% CI: 1.570–1.977, P < 0.001), and similar results were found for disease-free survival (DFS) (HR = 1.841, 95% CI: 1.311–2.585, P < 0.001), recurrence-free survival (RFS) (HR = 1.583, 95% CI: 1.374–1.824, P < 0.001), cancer-specific survival (CSS) (HR = 1.930, 95% CI: 1.590–2.341, P < 0.001), and progression-free survival (PFS) (HR = 1.444, 95% CI: 1.068–1.954, P = 0.017). Subgroup analyses confirmed the robustness of these findings across different treatment strategies and cancer types. Sensitivity and bias analyses (P = 0.103) supported the reliability of these results.ConclusionThe HALP score is a promising prognostic biomarker for digestive system cancers, offering potential for clinical application in personalizing treatment strategies. Future studies should aim to standardize methodologies and validate HALP in prospective multicenter trials.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15068-x.Keywords: HALP score, Digestive system cancers, Prognostic biomarker, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Cancer remains a major global health challenge, with malignancies in various organ systems causing widespread adverse effects. Among these, digestive system cancers, as the primary site for nutrient absorption, are particularly vulnerable, with malignant transformation leading to profound body-wide disruption [1]. Due to the high incidence and mortality associated with these cancers, significant efforts have been directed at improving diagnosis and treatment. Recently, in addition to conventional therapeutic options, certain biomarkers derived from routine blood tests, such as the systemic inflammation response index (SIRI) and pan-immune-inflammation value (PIV), have shown promising potential for aiding diagnosis and managing digestive system cancers [2, 3]. This study aimed to identify a novel, reliable, and cost-effective biomarker to assess patient status and offer objective guidance for individuals with these cancers.

Blood tests, routinely conducted before treatment to assess contraindications and therapy tolerance, provide valuable biomarkers that reflect a patient’s overall condition [4]. These biomarkers offer an objective and dynamic snapshot of the patient’s health, aiding in clinical decision-making. Research has shown that inflammation, immunity, and nutritional status significantly influence cancer development, progression, and prognosis. Chronic inflammation is a key driver of tumorigenesis, linked to cancer cell invasion, metastasis, and therapy resistance [5]. The immune system also plays a dual role in cancer, either inhibiting tumor growth or being co-opted by the tumor microenvironment to support disease progression [6]. Moreover, malnutrition, a frequent but often overlooked complication in cancer patients, impairs immune function, reduces treatment efficacy, and worsens disease outcomes [7]. Thus, accurate assessment of a patient’s condition is crucial for optimizing treatment decisions. Recently, inflammation-, nutrition-, and immune-related markers, such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio, and prognostic nutritional index, have been widely used to predict cancer prognosis [8, 9]. However, these individual markers often fail to fully capture the complex interactions within the tumor microenvironment.

To overcome this limitation, composite scores like the systemic immune-inflammation index, SIRI, and PIV were developed [2, 3, 10]. These combined biomarkers integrate information from inflammation, immunity, and nutrition, improving the precision of prognostic evaluations. Building on this, the hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte, and platelet (HALP) score was introduced as a novel immune-nutrition biomarker [11]. The HALP score, calculated as hemoglobin (g/L) × albumin (g/L) × lymphocyte count/platelet count, incorporates parameters reflecting inflammation (platelets), immune function (lymphocytes), and nutritional status (albumin and hemoglobin). This composite score has proven valuable in enhancing cancer prognostic models. Initially introduced by Chen et al. in gastric cancer, where it was found to correlate with clinicopathological features and act as an independent prognostic factor, the HALP score has since been validated in other digestive system cancers, such as colorectal cancer, esophageal carcinoma, pancreatic cancer, and cholangiocarcinoma [12–15]. However, some studies reported inconsistent results, particularly in gastric cancer [16], esophageal carcinoma [17], and periampullary carcinoma [18], raising questions about its clinical utility. Nevertheless, based on its strong prognostic value, we hypothesized that higher HALP scores are associated with improved survival in patients with digestive system cancers.

Thus, this meta-analysis was conducted to evaluate the relationship between HALP scores and survival outcomes in patients with digestive system cancers by synthesizing data from available studies.

Materials and methods

Search strategy

We conducted a comprehensive search of PubMed, PMC, and Web of Science databases for relevant English articles published up to April 25, 2025. Two researchers independently searched using the keywords: (“hemoglobin, albumin, lymphocyte, and platelet” OR “HALP”) AND (“cancer” OR “tumor” OR “neoplasm” OR “carcinoma”). The reference lists of relevant articles were also reviewed and screened simultaneously. Any discrepancies regarding the eligibility of studies were resolved through discussion between the two researchers. Details of the protocol for this systematic review were registered on INPLASY (INPLASY202510063) and are available in full on the inplasy.com (10.37766/inplasy2025.1.0063). To ensure the scientific rigor and comparability of the systematic review, this meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA guidelines.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The selection of articles was strictly based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria: (1). The study population consisted of clinical patients diagnosed with digestive system cancers. (2). Blood tests were performed prior to treatment, and HALP scores were calculated based on the results. (3). Patients were categorized into high and low HALP groups using appropriate cutoff values, and prognostic analyses were conducted. (4). Studies explicitly defined prognostic endpoints and provided Hazard ratios (HRs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). (5). Studies with clearly defined populations, appropriate statistical analyses, and explicit prognostic endpoints were included. (6). Non-clinical studies, reviews, commentaries, conference abstracts, and similar publications were excluded. The selected criteria ensured that only studies with well-defined populations, robust statistical methods, and clear prognostic endpoints were included to maintain the validity of the meta-analysis.

Data collection and quality assessment

Following the objectives of this meta-analysis, two researchers independently and objectively extracted data using a standardized form. The extracted information included fundamental study characteristics such as author name, year of publication, sample size, patient age, sex, study type, cancer type, cancer stage, and HALP cutoff values. Prognostic data for various endpoints were also collected, including overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), disease-free survival (DFS), recurrence-free survival (RFS), and cancer-specific survival (CSS). All data were verified by a third researcher to ensure accuracy. Furthermore, the quality of the included studies was systematically assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), ensuring the reliability and validity of the analysis [19].

Statistical methods

This meta-analysis adhered to rigorous statistical methodologies to ensure the reliability and validity of the results. HRs with 95% CIs were calculated to evaluate the prognostic significance of the HALP score across various survival outcomes, including OS, DFS, RFS, CSS, and PFS. When studies reported both univariate and multivariate hazard HRs, the multivariate estimates were preferentially extracted, as they account for potential confounders; if only univariate HRs were available, these were used instead. A random-effects model based on the DerSimonian-Laird method was applied to account for potential between-study heterogeneity. Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran’s Q test and quantified by the I² statistic, with Ph < 0.1 or I² >50% indicating substantial heterogeneity. To investigate potential sources of heterogeneity, meta-regression and subgroup analyses were performed. Publication bias was examined by using Egger’s test. Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the pooled estimates by sequentially omitting individual studies. All statistical analyses were performed using STATA software (version 14.0, StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA), and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise specified.

Results

A total of 205 articles were identified through database searches. After an initial screening, 45 articles were retrieved for full-text review. Among these, 15 articles were excluded for the following reasons: non-cancer research (n = 4), studies on other cancer types (n = 5), gastrointestinal stromal tumor (n = 1), undefined prognostic endpoints (n = 1), significant errors in data and conclusions (n = 2), insufficient prognostic data (n = 1), and meta-analysis (n = 1). Ultimately, 30 articles, comprising 34 distinct studies and involving 9,389 patients from 1973 to 2024, were included in this meta-analysis to assess the prognostic significance of HALP in digestive system cancers (Fig. 1). The studies were conducted across China, Turkey, Japan, and Mexico and published between 2015 and 2025. The sample sizes ranged from 39 to 888 participants. The cancers analyzed in this meta-analysis included 7 types of digestive system cancers: biliary cancer (including extrahepatic biliary cancer and intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma) [15, 20–23], colorectal cancer (including colon cancer [24], colorectal cancer [12, 25] and rectal cancer [26, 27]), esophageal carcinoma [13, 17, 28–32], gastric cancer [11, 16, 33–36], hepatocellular carcinoma [37–40], pancreatic cancer [14, 41], periampullary carcinoma [18]. Blood samples used to calculate HALP scores were collected prior to treatment in all studies, with the cutoff values for HALP ranging from 2.22 to 62.14. Regarding prognostic outcomes, 30 studies reported OS, while seven studies analyzed DFS, five studies assessed RFS, and three studies evaluated CSS. Additionally, four studies examined PFS. Of the included studies, seven provided prognostic data based solely on univariate analysis, while 27 studies utilized multivariate analysis, with 23 of these also reporting univariate analysis results. Treatment strategies predominantly involved surgical interventions, although some studies incorporated mixed treatment strategies, including chemoradiotherapy or chemoimmunotherapy. Quality scores of the included studies ranged from 6 to 9, indicating a moderate to high quality of the included studies (Table 1).

Prognostic value of HALP score in digestive system cancers

This meta-analysis synthesized evidence to highlight the prognostic significance of the HALP score in digestive system cancers. For OS, 30 studies involving 8,226 patients demonstrated that patients with a high HALP score exhibited a significantly better prognosis, as indicated by a pooled HR of 1.762 (95% CI: 1.570–1.977, P < 0.001, Fig. 2). Regarding DFS, data from 7 studies encompassing 2,136 patients revealed an HR of 1.841 (95% CI: 1.311–2.585, P = 0.001, Fig. 3a). RFS was assessed in 5 studies with 1,410 patients, showing an HR of 1.583 (95% CI: 1.374–1.824, P < 0.001, Fig. 3b). For CSS, 3 studies including 1,175 patients reported an HR of 1.930 (95% CI: 1.590–2.341, P < 0.001, Fig. 3c). Finally, 4 studies with 404 patients demonstrated the association between a high HALP score and improved PFS, with an HR of 1.444 (95% CI: 1.068–1.954, P = 0.017, Fig. 3d).

Although heterogeneity was observed in the analyses of OS (I² = 53.0%, Ph < 0.001) and DFS (I² = 74.3%, Ph < 0.001) regarding the prognostic value of the HALP score in digestive system cancers, the limited number of studies for DFS prompted us to focus on investigating the sources of heterogeneity in the OS analysis. Meta-regression analysis revealed that none of the examined factors significantly explained the heterogeneity. Specifically, the P-values for sample size (0.420), study region (0.325), treatment strategy (0.080), analysis method (0.297), cutoff value (0.088), cancer type (0.094), and publication year (0.490) were all non-significant.

HALP score and prognostic subgroup analysis in OS

Considering the predominant focus on OS in prognostic research and the larger number of studies evaluating OS, we proceeded with subgroup analyses to better understand the impact of the HALP score across various clinical variables. As shown in Table 2, the subgroup analyses of OS revealed consistent findings across treatment strategies and cancer types. For patients undergoing surgery, the pooled HR was 1.842 (95% CI: 1.613–2.104, P < 0.001), based on 23 studies (7404 patients), while mixed treatment strategies demonstrated a consistent association with an HR of 1.535 (95% CI: 1.281–1.840, P < 0.001) across 7 studies (822 patients). Subgroup analysis based on TNM stage showed that HALP retained its prognostic significance in studies reporting stage I–III (14 studies, 5,383 patients, HR = 1.649, 95% CI: 1.506–1.806, P < 0.001) and in studies with non-I–III or unspecified stages (16 studies, 2,843 patients, HR = 1.647, 95% CI: 1.300–2.086, P < 0.001). When stratified by cancer type, gastrointestinal cancer demonstrated significant associations with HALP scores. The pooled HR for patients with gastrointestinal cancer was 1.570 (95% CI: 1.435–1.717, P < 0.001) from 17 studies (5081 patients), indicating that higher HALP scores predict better OS. Among its subtypes, gastric cancer (7 studies, 2112 patients, HR = 1.499, 95% CI: 1.194–1.882, P = 0.001), colorectal cancer (5 studies, 1757 patients, HR = 1.701, 95% CI: 1.433–2.018), and esophageal cancer (5 studies, 1212 patients, HR = 1.621, 95% CI: 1.390–1.891) all showed similar trends. Similarly, for hepatobiliary pancreatic cancer, the overall HR was 1.966 (95% CI: 1.602–2.412, P < 0.001) from 13 studies (3145 patients). Within this category, biliary cancer (6 studies, 1008 patients, HR = 1.988, 95% CI: 1.634–2.418), hepatocellular carcinoma (4 studies, 1247 patients, HR = 1.654, 95% CI: 1.365–2.003), and pancreatic cancer (3 studies, 890 patients, HR = 2.510, 95% CI: 1.006–6.265, P = 0.049) further support the prognostic value of elevated HALP scores.

Moreover, stratification by cutoff values indicated a higher prognostic significance for studies with HALP scores ≤ 30 (11 studies, 2552 patients, HR = 2.117, 95% CI: 1.641–2.730, P < 0.001) compared to those with HALP scores > 30 (19 studies, 5674 patients, HR = 1.586, 95% CI: 1.453–1.731, P < 0.001), highlighting the importance of higher scores in predicting survival. Subgroup analysis based on sample size demonstrated that the prognostic value of the HALP score was significant in both groups, regardless of sample size. Studies with sample sizes < 300 (22 studies, 3425 patients) showed an HR of 1.910 (95% CI: 1.607–2.270, P < 0.001), while studies with sample sizes ≥ 300 (8 studies, 4801 patients) reported a similarly significant HR of 1.539 (95% CI: 1.397–1.695, P < 0.001). The geographic location of studies also influenced results. Studies conducted in China (14 studies, 4890 patients, HR = 1.564, 95% CI: 1.428–1.714, P < 0.001) and Japan (7 studies, 1717 patients, HR = 1.839, 95% CI: 1.537–2.199, P < 0.001) showed consistent associations, while studies from Turkey (8 studies, 979 patients) demonstrated a higher HR (HR = 2.107, 95% CI: 1.355–3.275, P = 0.001), further confirming the universal prognostic benefit of the HALP score.

Sensitivity and publication bias analyses

Sensitivity analyses demonstrated the robustness of the findings, as no substantial changes were observed when individual studies were sequentially excluded (Fig. 4). Egger’s test for publication bias yielded a P-value of 0.103 and a bias coefficient of 1.054 (Fig. 5), which indicates the absence of significant publication bias, as indicated by the 95% CI of the bias coefficient (−0.227 to 2.336), thereby supporting the reliability of the meta-analysis results.

Discussion

This meta-analysis provided strong evidence for the prognostic significance of the HALP score in digestive system cancers, spanning various cancer types and treatment strategies. The pooled hazard ratios for OS, DFS, RFS, CSS, and PFS consistently showed that higher HALP scores were associated with improved prognoses. These findings highlighted HALP as a robust biomarker for risk stratification and clinical decision-making, offering a cost-effective, pre-treatment tool that meets the need for practical prognostic markers in clinical practice.

A major strength of this study was its comprehensive scope, which included 30 articles and 34 distinct studies spanning various geographic regions and digestive system cancer types. Subgroup analyses reinforced the universal prognostic value of HALP across different cancer subtypes, particularly gastrointestinal and hepatobiliary pancreatic cancers. This study highlighted the potential of HALP as a reliable prognostic tool for patients with digestive system cancers, especially in regions with limited resources or without advanced diagnostic technologies. By validating HALP as a routine clinical biomarker, clinicians could improve treatment decisions and optimize patient outcomes. Notably, the heterogeneity observed in the OS and DFS analyses did not diminish the consistency of HALP’s prognostic relevance across subgroups. Subsequent meta-regression analyses did not identify any significant source of heterogeneity; variables such as sample size, study region, treatment strategy, analysis method, cutoff value, cancer type, and publication year were not significant contributors, indicating that the prognostic value of HALP was robust and not compromised by variability in study characteristics. However, sensitivity and bias analyses confirmed the stability and reliability of the meta-analysis.

In addition, it has been well established that surgical treatment remains the primary approach for resectable digestive system cancers [1]. However, such invasive procedures are often associated with significant physical trauma and high costs, and not all patients fully recover from these interventions. Increasing evidence has highlighted the critical role of robust preoperative assessments in identifying patients with insufficient physiological reserves [2, 3, 8, 9]. Such evaluations enable tailored preoperative management to optimize surgical candidacy and improve postoperative outcomes. This meta-analysis, which incorporated data from 23 studies involving 7,404 patients with digestive system cancers, demonstrated that a high preoperative HALP score significantly increased overall survival (HR = 1.842). These findings underscored the clinical utility of HALP as a preoperative assessment tool. By integrating indicators of inflammation, immune function, and nutritional status, the HALP score aided in evaluating patients’ capacity for postoperative recovery and provided valuable guidance for individualized perioperative management strategies.

Further analysis of seven studies involving 822 patients who underwent neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy or other multimodal treatments revealed that a high HALP score also significantly improved survival outcomes in this population (HR = 1.535). This finding suggested that HALP was not limited to surgical candidates but was also applicable to patients undergoing complex therapeutic regimens. Such patients often face heightened risks of nutritional depletion and immune suppression due to treatment-related adverse effects, emphasizing the need for a reliable pre-treatment assessment tool to optimize outcomes.

In terms of its clinical implications, a low HALP score reflected anemia, malnutrition, immune suppression, and heightened inflammatory states, all of which contributed to poor recovery, increased complications, and higher recurrence rates [11, 15, 16]. These challenges were particularly pronounced in patients undergoing neoadjuvant therapy, which could exacerbate systemic inflammation and nutritional deficits [27]. To address these issues, patients with low HALP scores might benefit from intensive supportive interventions, including nutritional supplementation, anti-inflammatory therapies, and immune modulation. By mitigating treatment-related side effects and enhancing physiological reserves, these measures could improve treatment adherence and long-term survival outcomes. Therefore, based on the findings of our meta-analysis, we believed that pre-treatment baseline conditions significantly influence treatment outcomes. HALP, as a promising and reliable evaluative tool, is expected to provide objective and valuable guidance for managing patients with digestive system cancers. However, acute conditions such as infection, inflammation, or malnutrition may transiently affect HALP stability, which should be considered when interpreting its value. Nevertheless, this also indicates that HALP can serve as a sensitive marker to identify patients requiring intensified supportive interventions, thereby improving treatment compliance and survival outcomes. There was no universally accepted threshold for HALP scores, which presented a limitation in standardizing its clinical application. Among the included studies, cutoff values ranged from 2.22 to 62.14, with values more than 30 being the most commonly used to define low HALP. Establishing a consensus threshold is necessary for consistent implementation in clinical settings.

In addition to its baseline prognostic value, emerging evidence suggested that dynamic alterations in HALP during treatment may provide further predictive insights. Studies in advanced non-small cell lung cancer demonstrated that patients with increasing HALP scores after initiating immunotherapy exhibited significantly improved OS and PFS compared to those with decreasing scores. Similarly, both baseline and post-treatment HALP were found to independently predict outcomes, and integrating dynamic HALP into predictive models enhanced accuracy [42, 43]. Although the cancer types were different, these findings highlighted the potential value of dynamic HALP for real-time monitoring and personalized management.

Recent evidence suggested that HALP may have prognostic relevance in patients receiving immunotherapy or chemoimmunotherapy. Zhu et al. [36] reported that higher HALP scores in advanced gastric cancer patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) plus chemotherapy were associated with improved response and survival outcomes. Xu et al. [32] found that HALP independently predicted major pathological response and DFS in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. In addition, studies in lung cancer using similar inflammatory-nutritional indices (e.g., PIV) demonstrated that higher scores predicted worse survival in patients receiving PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors or targeted therapy [44]. Collectively, HALP shows promise as a cost-effective biomarker for risk stratification in immunotherapy or targeted settings, but evidence on its association with treatment-related toxicities and perioperative safety is scarce, warranting prospective validation in digestive system cancers.

This meta-analysis had several limitations. First, all included studies were retrospective, and the majority were conducted in Asian populations, which may introduce inherent biases and restrict the generalizability of the findings to Western cohorts. Second, although multivariate analyses were applied in most studies to control for confounding factors, residual confounding could not be completely excluded. Third, the cutoff values for HALP varied substantially across studies, highlighting the absence of a standardized threshold. This variability underscores the need for consensus on optimal cutoff values to ensure comparability and clinical applicability. Fourth, given the dynamic nature of HALP, differences in blood collection timing, fasting status, BMI, acute conditions such as infection or systemic inflammation, and other clinical factors may have influenced its stability. Although we limited sampling to pre-treatment settings to reduce bias, future studies should incorporate repeated measurements and evaluate potential variations during treatment or disease progression. Fifth, relatively small sample sizes in certain subgroups, including CSS, specific cancer types, and TNM stages, may weaken the robustness and generalizability of subgroup analyses amid clinical heterogeneity. Finally, future research should prioritize international, multicenter, prospective studies with standardized methodologies to validate HALP’s prognostic utility, and explore its interactions with molecular and immunological subgroups to clarify its applicability across diverse patient populations.

Conclusion

The HALP score may serve as a useful prognostic tool in patients with digestive system cancers, offering potential guidance for personalized cancer care. Our study demonstrated that higher HALP scores are associated with better survival outcomes and are applicable to multiple cancer types and treatment strategies. Future research should focus on validating these findings through prospective studies, establishing a standardized threshold for HALP scores, and investigating the molecular mechanisms underlying its prognostic value.

A cross-sectional study assessing the reliability and quality of educational videos on hydrocephalus available on TikTok and Bilibili
Authors: Wencai Wang, Yinuo Chen, Zijie Xiong, Zun Wang, Wei Ye, Xianfeng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Hydrocephalus is a syndrome characterized by disturbances in cerebrospinal fluid dynamics, impacting individuals across all age groups. Short video platforms have emerged as a primary medium for disseminating medical knowledge, offering a convenient means for patients and their families to access relevant information. Nevertheless, the varying quality of these videos presents significant challenges to the effective communication of medical knowledge. This study provides a systematic evaluation of popular science videos on hydrocephalus available on leading Chinese short video platforms. This study included 181 short science videos related to hydrocephalus collected from Bilibili and TikTok. The characteristics of these videos were analyzed, and their reliability and quality were assessed utilizing the modified DISCERN instrument, the Global Quality Score (GQS), and the JAMA benchmark criteria. Overall video quality was low on both platforms. The median GQS and modified DISCERN scores did not differ significantly between platforms (P > 0.05), while JAMA scores showed a significant difference (P < 0.05). On TikTok, longer videos were positively correlated with higher GQS and modified DISCERN scores; no such correlation was found on Bilibili. Treatment-related content was most common, with childhood hydrocephalus emphasized on TikTok and adult hydrocephalus on Bilibili. Expert-produced videos achieved significantly higher quality scores than content from non-experts (P < 0.05). On the TikTok and Bilibili platforms, the overall quality and reliability of hydrocephalus-related short videos remain suboptimal, and a portion of the content does not meet established standards for science communication. However, TikTok demonstrates higher user engagement and better video quality compared to Bilibili, with expert-produced videos proving to be more reliable. Educational videos focused on childhood hydrocephalus also show higher engagement than those addressing adult hydrocephalus. These findings highlight the urgent need to improve the quality of hydrocephalus-related short-video content and to promote early intervention strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-55440-w.

Introduction

Hydrocephalus is a common neurological disorder marked by the pathological accumulation of cerebrospinal fluid, which leads to ventricular dilation and subsequent compression of surrounding brain tissue1–3. If left untreated, it can lead to intellectual disability, loss of motor function, and irreversible brain damage, severely affecting the patient’s health4,5. Hydrocephalus may be classified as either congenital or acquired6,7. The global prevalence of adult hydrocephalus is estimated at 85 cases per 100,000 individuals8. The primary treatment approach is ventriculoperitoneal shunt surgery9,10. The incidence and risk of hydrocephalus are influenced by factors such as age, sex, and underlying causes. Therefore, a comprehensive understanding of hydrocephalus, coupled with early diagnosis and intervention, is essential for protecting public health.

With the rapid advancement of the internet era, professional medical knowledge has shifted from traditional offline medical consultations and textbooks to online short video dissemination. Public short video platforms have made the spread of medical information faster and more convenient, enabling the public to access relevant professional knowledge in a more accessible format. Bilibili and TikTok are the largest short video platforms in China, with Bilibili featuring longer videos and TikTok focusing on shorter ones11,12. These platforms attract different demographics, with TikTok popular among younger users and Bilibili favored by middle-aged and older audiences. However, due to the absence of a comprehensive review and oversight mechanism, the quality of these educational videos varies significantly, and some may contain inaccurate information, potentially leading to public misinformation.

While several studies have evaluated short-video educational content related to neuroscience topics such as stroke13, spinal cord injury14, and brain tumors15, investigations focusing on hydrocephalus on Chinese short-video platforms remain limited. Notably, hydrocephalus is characterized by an insidious onset and a substantial need for timely public education, underscoring its particular public health relevance. Accordingly, this study seeks to evaluate the content characteristics and overall quality of hydrocephalus-related short videos published on TikTok and Bilibili. The findings are intended to inform strategies for improving video quality, refining platform review mechanisms, and providing the public with reliable and high-quality educational content on hydrocephalus.

Materials and methods

Search strategy and data extraction

This study used the keyword ‘hydrocephalus’ to search for relevant content on TikTok and Bilibili16. To minimize potential bias arising from algorithm-driven personalized recommendations, searches were performed using newly registered accounts on both platforms. Data collection was conducted on November 7, 2025. Given the dynamic nature of social media ranking algorithms, all searches were completed within a 3-hour period on the same day to ensure consistency. The top 100 short videos from each platform were identified based on their overall ranking17. For each video, information including uploader identity, upload date, video duration, platform source (TikTok or Bilibili), and engagement indicators (likes, comments, favorites, and shares) was systematically collected. The videos were categorized by content into six main areas: epidemiology, etiology, symptoms, diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of hydrocephalus. Additionally, videos were categorized based on the type of hydrocephalus discussed (adult or pediatric). Finally, based on the uploader’s background, the videos were classified into three groups: experts, non-experts, and individuals. Experts with verified medical credentials or affiliations with medical institutions or universities, non-expert institutional accounts affiliated with non-medical organizations, and individual users without institutional affiliation or professional certification. After further analysis, we excluded 3 duplicate videos and 16 videos unrelated to the topic from the 200 short videos initially retrieved, resulting in a final dataset of 181 relevant videos on hydrocephalus for analysis (Supplementary file S1, Fig. 1).

Video features and quality evaluation

This study assessed the reliability and quality of 181 short videos related to hydrocephalus using three established scoring systems: the Global Quality Score (GQS), the JAMA score, and the modified DISCERN score. The modified DISCERN score comprises five evaluation criteria, each rated on a binary scale (1 point for “yes,” 0 points for “no”). The total score, ranging from 0 to 5, was determined by summing the points across the five criteria18. The GQS employed a 5-point Likert scale to evaluate video quality, considering factors such as informational comprehensiveness, viewer comprehension, professionalism, and presentation clarity, with scores ranging from 1 to 519. The JAMA score, used for evaluating the quality of health-related videos (see Supplementary file S2), assessed four domains: timeliness, attribution, disclosure, and authorship20. Two authors with relevant expertise independently scored the videos after receiving standardized training to minimize potential bias.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted utilizing IBM SPSS Statistics 22.0, while GraphPad Prism was utilized for result visualization. Fisher’s exact test or the Chi-square test was used to evaluate differences in video characteristics and quality between the two platforms. The Mann-Whitney U test was used for pairwise comparisons, and the Kruskal-Wallis test was applied to evaluate differences across multiple groups among the three video sources. Categorical data were presented as counts and proportions, while continuous data were characterized by their median values and corresponding interquartile ranges. Inter-rater agreement was quantified using Cohen’s kappa statistic21; a kappa value greater than 0.8 was considered indicative of strong agreement, with discrepancies resolved by a third rater. Spearman’s rank correlation analysis was performed to examine the associations between video characteristics and the three quality evaluation scores.

Results

Video characteristics

This study retrieved a total of 200 short videos related to hydrocephalus. After applying exclusion and inclusion criteria, 181 videos were ultimately involves in the analysis, including 86 from Bilibili and 95 from TikTok. As shown in Table 1, of the 181 videos, 156 were uploaded by experts (86.19%), 17 by individual users (9.39%), and 8 by non-experts (4.42%) (Fig. 2). Regarding content focus, 110 videos were related to adult hydrocephalus, while 71 focused on pediatric hydrocephalus. On TikTok, 57% of the videos addressed pediatric hydrocephalus, whereas on Bilibili, only 20% did (Fig. 3). Among the adult hydrocephalus videos, the majority were uploaded by experts (78.18%), followed by individual users (14.55%) and non-experts (7.27%). For pediatric hydrocephalus, the vast majority of videos were uploaded by experts (98.59%), with individual users contributing 1.41% (Fig. 4). In terms of engagement, the median values for likes, favorites, shares, and comments across the videos were 130 (16, 460), 32 (7, 121), 20 (5, 92), and 6 (0, 41), respectively, indicating a high level of user interaction. The median video duration was 108 s (67, 185). Regarding video quality, the median modified DISCERN score and GQS were 4.00 (3.00, 4.00), while the median JAMA score was 1.00 (1.00, 1.00). Inter-rater reliability was good, with Cohen’s kappa values of 0.872 for the GQS and 0.857 for the modified DISCERN score, demonstrating strong consistency between the two rating bodies.

Video content

As shown in Table 2, the treatment of hydrocephalus was the most frequently addressed topic in science popularization, with the majority of discussions centered on shunt surgery. On Bilibili, treatment-related videos comprised 80.23% of the content, followed by videos on symptoms (40.70%), diagnosis (30.23%), and etiology (23.26%). On TikTok, treatment-related videos accounted for 61.05%, with symptoms (57.89%), diagnosis (40.00%), and etiology (32.63%) as the next most frequent topics. Prevention and epidemiology were the least discussed topics, likely reflecting the nature of hydrocephalus and the current state of research in these areas. Statistically significant differences were observed in the discussion of symptoms and treatment between the two platforms (P < 0.05), whereas no significant differences were detected in the topics of epidemiology, etiology, diagnosis, and prevention (P > 0.05).

Comparison of features and quality across different platforms

Table 3 presents significant differences between Bilibili and TikTok in terms of video source, hydrocephalus category, and engagement metrics, including the number of collections, video duration, likes, comments, and shares (P < 0.05). Specifically, on Bilibili, 9.30% of the videos were uploaded by non-expert users, while on TikTok, this proportion was 0%. The proportion of videos focused on pediatric hydrocephalus was higher on TikTok (56.84%) compared to Bilibili (19.77%). Engagement on TikTok was also significantly higher than on Bilibili. The median number of likes, comments, favorites, and shares on TikTok were 259 (123, 685), 28 (8, 116), 64 (17, 176), and 53 (13, 327), respectively. In contrast, the median values for these metrics on Bilibili were much lower: 14 (3, 193), 0 (0, 1.75), 12 (2, 50.5), and 8 (1, 25.5), respectively. The average video length was also shorter on TikTok (95 s, 59.5, 124.0) compared to Bilibili (130 s, 85.25, 277.5). In terms of video quality, TikTok videos had mean GQS, modified DISCERN, and JAMA scores of 3.45 ± 0.94, 3.78 ± 1.05, and 1.13 ± 0.44, respectively, whereas Bilibili videos scored 3.36 ± 0.96, 3.73 ± 1.15, and 0.94 ± 0.32. Overall, TikTok videos demonstrated slightly higher quality than those on Bilibili. However, no statistically significant differences were observed between the two platforms in GQS and modified DISCERN scores (P > 0.05), while a significant difference was found in JAMA scores (P < 0.05) (Fig. 5).

Comparison of features and quality of different video sources

Table 4 compares the characteristics and quality of short videos uploaded by different user groups. A total of 181 videos were included in this study, with 17 uploaded by individual users (9.39%), 8 by non-specialists (4.42%), and 156 by specialists (86.19%). Statistically significant differences were found among the three groups with respect to video duration, number of comments, GQS, modified DISCERN scores, JAMA scores, and hydrocephalus category (P < 0.05). However, no significant differences were observed in the number of likes, shares, or collections (P > 0.05). Specifically, videos uploaded by individual users were remarkably longer than those uploaded by non-experts and experts. Most videos on pediatric hydrocephalus were uploaded by experts, with only one video uploaded by an individual user. The median GQS, JAMA score, and modified DISCERN score for expert-uploaded videos were 4(3, 4), 4(4, 5), and 1(1, 1), respectively. As displayed in Fig. 6, there were no statistically significant differences in GQS between experts and non-experts, nor in the JAMA and modified DISCERN scores. However, significant differences were found between experts and individual users in both the modified DISCERN score and JAMA score. Additionally, the GQS and modified DISCERN scores showed statistically significant differences between non-experts and individual users, though no significant difference was identified in the JAMA scores.

Comparison of the characteristics and quality of short videos on different types of hydrocephalus

This study included a total of 181 videos, of which 110 (60.77%) were diagnosed with adult hydrocephalus and 71 (39.23%) were diagnosed with pediatric hydrocephalus. Statistically significant differences were identified between the two groups with respect to the number of likes, number of comments, source type, and video source (P < 0.05). However, no significant differences were identified for Video Duration, Shares, Collections, GQS scores, modified DISCERN Scores, or JAMA scores (P > 0.05) (Fig. 7). Specifically, there were more short videos on adult hydrocephalus on Bilibili than on TikTok, whereas TikTok featured more short videos on childhood hydrocephalus than Bilibili. Engagement levels for childhood hydrocephalus videos were higher compared to those for adult hydrocephalus. The median number of likes, comments, favorites, and shares for short videos about childhood hydrocephalus were 174.00 (59.50, 592.50), 16.00 (4.00, 80.00), 39.00 (8.00, 117.50), and 32.00 (7.00, 107.00), respectively. The median GQS scores, modified DISCERN Scores, and JAMA scores for childhood and adult hydrocephalus were 4.00 (3.00, 4.00), 4.00 (3.00, 4.00), and 1.00 (1.00, 1.00), respectively (Table 5).

Correlation analysis between video features and quality

Fig. 8A and B depict the correlations between video features and quality scores on the Bilibili and TikTok platforms, respectively. Notably, there is a remarkable correlation between the GQS, modified DISCERN score, and JAMA score on both platforms (P < 0.001). On Bilibili, video shares, collections, comments, and likes are significantly negatively correlated with the GQS, modified DISCERN score, and JAMA score (P < 0.001). Nevertheless, no significant associations were identified between video duration and the GQS, modified DISCERN score, or JAMA score (P > 0.05). On TikTok, video duration shows a significant positive correlation with the GQS and modified DISCERN score (P < 0.001). In contrast, no significant associations were observed between user engagement metrics—including shares, favorites, comments, likes and—and any of the quality measures (GQS, modified DISCERN score, or JAMA score).

Discussion

Our cross-sectional study demonstrates that Bilibili and TikTok received comparable scores on the GQS, modified DISCERN scale, and JAMA rating. However, short educational videos on hydrocephalus uploaded by experts scored higher on both the GQS and modified DISCERN scale. This suggests that experts possess a more robust medical knowledge base compared to individual users, and that their videos are of superior quality and reliability, consistent with prior research findings22–24. Short videos on hydrocephalus posted on TikTok exhibited significantly higher engagement, as evidenced by more likes, comments, favorites, and shares compared to those on Bilibili. This suggests that TikTok reaches a larger audience than Bilibili, consistent with previous studies24–26. However, Spearman’s correlation analysis did not reveal a significant relationship between increased user engagement and higher video quality, implying that greater engagement does not necessarily correlate with enhanced video quality. Notably, on TikTok, we observed that longer videos generally had higher quality, a pattern not observed on Bilibili.

During the evaluation of short videos on hydrocephalus, we identified that, in addition to content related to Western medicine, some videos focused on traditional Chinese medicine treatments for the condition. However, these videos often exaggerated the role of traditional Chinese medicine in hydrocephalus management without providing mechanistic explanations, relying solely on case reports. Furthermore, certain videos dismissed the role of Western medicine. Additionally, some videos offered only a general overview of hydrocephalus treatment and mechanisms as a broad syndrome, neglecting its classification and individual variations. This lack of comprehensiveness and failure to account for patient-specific differences could hinder the public’s understanding of hydrocephalus and affect timely intervention for patients. Therefore, enhancing the reliability and quality of short video content related to hydrocephalus on social media platforms could significantly contribute to raising public awareness of the condition27,28.

With the rapid development of social media and short video platforms, public access to medical and health knowledge has increasingly shifted from traditional offline consultations to online science popularization. As China’s largest short video platforms, Bilibili and TikTok are being increasingly used by both medical professionals and enthusiasts to disseminate relevant medical information. While research on the popularization of medical science through social media is growing29–32, studies focusing on the nervous system remain limited. Shao-Jie Nie et al.13 conducted a comparative analysis of stroke-related educational videos on Bilibili and TikTok. Their findings indicated that TikTok videos achieved significantly higher median numbers of comments and likes than those on Bilibili. Additionally, content produced by professional teams received higher PEMAT-A/V and GQS scores compared with videos created by individual content creators. Nonetheless, the overall quality of stroke-related videos on both platforms remained below desirable standards. Similarly, Xin Qi et al.11 conducted a systematic analysis of 606 short videos related to bipolar disorder on Chinese social media. Their findings revealed substantial platform-specific differences in video quality and user engagement: Kuaishou and TikTok generally exhibited higher content quality, whereas videos on WeChat generated more user comments. Although most videos were uploaded by medical professionals, no consistent association was observed between the creators’ professional backgrounds and either video quality or engagement levels. Wenjie He et al.33 analyzed 344 videos related to cerebral palsy from mainstream Chinese social media platforms, finding that the average video length was 92.12 s. Videos focused on cerebral palsy rehabilitation training accounted for 45.64% of the total, followed by expert-contributed videos at 40.70%. The average scores on JAMA, GQS, and modified DISCERN were 1.62, 2.05, and 1.26, respectively.

Popular science videos on hydrocephalus differ markedly from those addressing more common neurological disorders, such as stroke and epilepsy, in terms of content professionalism, audience comprehension, and the prevalence of misconceptions. To our knowledge, this study is the first to conduct a comparative analysis across two major platforms, TikTok and Bilibili, thereby providing targeted evidence to inform optimized health communication strategies for this condition. By comparing the feature information and quality scores across both platforms, we conducted a systematic and objective assessment of the reliability and quality of hydrocephalus-related popular science content. Our findings indicate that TikTok exhibits higher user engagement than Bilibili, and short videos created by experts are of superior reliability and quality compared to those produced by non-professionals. Additionally, videos on childhood hydrocephalus demonstrate higher user engagement than those on adult hydrocephalus, reflecting the greater concern of parents and the public for children’s health. Higher viewer engagement does not necessarily indicate better video quality; expert content is often more rigorous but less engaging, whereas non-professional organizations and self-media are more adept at audience reach, and individual users tend to produce relatable content that resonates emotionally.These results offer valuable insights for the future review and regulation of popular science content on these platforms and provide recommendations for the development of relevant public health policies.

While this study provides valuable insights into the dissemination of hydrocephalus-related health information on short video platforms, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the analysis was restricted to two major platforms, TikTok and Bilibili, excluding other Chinese platforms, which may limit coverage34,35. Second, short videos from international platforms such as YouTube were not included, and therefore the findings should be interpreted cautiously in the context of global hydrocephalus education; future studies should incorporate international platforms to broaden applicability36,37. Third, video ratings are inherently subjective, and focusing on top-ranked content may introduce selection and algorithmic biases, potentially affecting reproducibility and generalizability. Fourth, although widely used tools such as GQS and the modified DISCERN instrument were employed, they do not fully capture audiovisual characteristics or dissemination effects, highlighting the need for more tailored evaluation frameworks for short video content. Finally, given the continuous updating of hydrocephalus-related videos, the results may not fully reflect the latest educational trends.

Conclusion

This study performed a cross-sectional evaluation of 181 hydrocephalus-related short educational videos collected from the Douyin and Bilibili platforms. Video quality and reliability were assessed using the GQS scale, the modified DISCERN instrument, and the JAMA scoring system. No significant differences were observed between the two platforms in terms of quality or reliability scores; however, the overall content on both platforms remains suboptimal. Videos uploaded by experts demonstrated higher quality and reliability than those produced by individual users, and content addressing pediatric hydrocephalus exhibited greater user engagement than videos related to adult cases. It should be noted that the data were drawn from specific platforms at particular time points and may not comprehensively represent all short video content. Additionally, the completeness of information regarding TCM or Western medicine was not fully assessed; therefore, the findings should be interpreted cautiously in practical applications. These results suggest that stakeholders in hydrocephalus education should focus on enhancing video quality, while platform providers should strengthen review and oversight mechanisms to ensure that users receive accurate and reliable health information.

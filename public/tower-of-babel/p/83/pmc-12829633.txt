Evaluation of the efficacy of the novel all-rounder hinge plating system in maxillofacial fractures – An in vivo study
Authors: Varsha Subramanyam, Mahesh Kumar Ranganath, Ashok Kumar Korategere Ramabhadrasetty, Jambukeshwar Kumar Bangarappagaari, Supriyo Pal, Sreelakshmi Madathilparambil Sreekumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Conventional mini-plating systems for maxillofacial fractures have greatly aided in the reduction and fixation of fractures to anatomical positions with minimal post-operative complications. However, it was insignificant in terms of the management of comminuted or complicated fractures of the maxillofacial region.Aim/Purpose:To evaluate the efficacy of the novel all-rounder hinge plating system in the treatment of complicated maxillofacial fractures.Materials and Methods:A total of 19 patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria were a part of this clinical study and underwent pre-surgical investigations prior to treatment with the novel all-rounder hinge plating system for the management of complicated maxillofacial fractures. The plating system was evaluated for ease of handling, efficiency, stability, clinical function, and aesthetic outcomes intra- and post-operatively.Results:Based on this study, the novel all-rounder hinge plating system produced satisfactory results in 57.9% of cases and high satisfaction in 42.1% of cases. The operating surgeons expressed 83% satisfaction with the ease of handling the novel plating system and 88% better aesthetic outcomes. The post-operative clinical function was evaluated on days 7, 15, 45, and 6 months. It showed a positive significant difference from day 7 (63.2%) up to day 45 (94.7%) while not significant from day 45 to the 6th month.Conclusion:The novel all-rounder hinge plating system has shown positive significant differences in terms of clinical function, aesthetic outcomes, and good stability of such complicated maxillofacial fractures. It provides a dual benefit of replacing the existing conventional mini-plating system and also aids in the efficient management of comminuted and complicated maxillofacial fractures.

INTRODUCTION

Facial fracture is a predominant cause of morbidity owing to being an exposed aspect of the body with no/less protection and the people’s attitude toward it. A study reported a 39.45% spike in the cases of facial fractures globally from 1990–2017, with the highest number recorded in India (1,127,438.84) and twice the number of occurrences in men compared to women.[1]

In the wake of increasing changes in trends and technology, the evolution of reduction of maxillofacial fractures from the pre-antibiotic era to the current generation has been a behemoth.[2] Closed reduction of fractures was the bottom rule for most fractures during the pre-antibiotic eras. Most non-dental fixation techniques around the late 19th used transcutaneous inserted pins[3] in the mandible and connected externally using various appliances.

With the wake of antibiotics on the market, open reduction of fractures rocketed and the use of intraosseous wires, intramedullary pins gained popularity, finally resulting in the introduction of the plate and screw systems. Hansmann was credited on being the first surgeon to develop and present a procedure for the subcutaneous fixation of bone fragments using a plate–screw system.[4]

The plate–screw system has traveled a long way from the use of compression plates of conventional, dynamic, and eccentric types to the introduction of lag screws, reconstruction plate systems, miniplates, and 3D printed POC plates landing us with the most recent biodegradable plating system.[5] The above-mentioned plating systems are available in various shapes, sizes, and modifications specific to the site of fracture reduction.

To date, comminuted or complicated fractures pose problems to surgeons.[6] The introduction of titanium mesh system[7] and splint-guided treatment[8] has greatly aided in the management of the above-mentioned but fails to support or explain in terms of fracture segment approximation, stability, aesthetics, and clinical function.

The currently available plating systems fail to produce satisfactory outcomes in the management of complicated maxillofacial fractures, thereby warranting the need for the introduction of a new treatment modality or system. Furthermore, there exist several plating techniques accessible in varied sizes and patterns depending on where the fracture is located, making it cumbersome for surgeons to carry multiple plating systems.

A new concept of hinge plates has been adopted by the field of orthopedic surgery to provide a surgical treatment option with satisfactory post-operative outcome when used in comminuted fractures in terms of quality of reduction, clinical function, and rate of complication.[9]

In this study, we have developed and tested the efficacy of the novel all-rounder hinge plating system in complicated maxillofacial fractures.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

All patients included in the study were evaluated clinically and radiologically (head and neck CT with 3D reconstruction and/or OPG). Ethical Clearence was obtained Institutional Ethical Committee with Ref No SSMC/Dent/ IEC-May51/May2022 dated 17/05/2022. The surgical procedures were carried out under aseptic conditions for fixation using the novel all-rounder hinge plating systems (2 mm holed plate for middle one-third facial fractures and 2.5 mm holed plate for lower one-third facial fractures) under local or general anesthesia.

Fracture sites were exposed with extraoral or intraoral incision, and the fractured segments were reduced by physical manipulation or with the use of intended instruments. Once the arches were brought into occlusion, tie wires were placed on the existing arch bar, and the novel hinge plating system was manipulated chairside according to the nature and site of the fracture and used for fixation of the fractured segments. The raised flap(s) were closed in layers using resorbable and non-resorbable suture materials wherever indicated and an elastic adhesive bandage over a basic wound dressing was placed to prevent the occurrence of dead space. The patient was covered on antibiotics and analgesics post-operatively during the course of the hospital stay.

The patient was recalled for follow-up after the post-operative 24 hours, 7th day, 15th day, 45th day, and end of 6 months. The evaluation of the plating system was performed:

Intraoperatively: a) ease of handling b) efficiency in fracture fixation c) stability

Post-operatively: a) clinical function b) aesthetic outcome

Ethical aspects

This in vivo prospective clinical study was approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee (Ref No: SSMC/Dent/IEC-May51/May2022) and the Clinical Trials Registry India (CTRI no: CTRI/2024/05/066791) and conducted in the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Tumkur, India.

RESULTS

This study involved patients ranging between 18 and 45 years with 29.63 + 6.3 years as the mean age. Out of 19 patients, 84.2% (16 cases) were males, and 15.7% (three cases) were females.

Site of use

The study involved the use of a novel all-rounder hinge plating system in the fronto-maxillary complex and mandibular bone. A peak value of 26.3% was noted using this novel plating system in complicated ZMC fractures followed by 21.1% in comminuted ZMC fractures, 15.8% of parasymphyseal fractures involving the mental nerve, 10.5% of complicated zygomatic arch fracture, and 5.3% each of complicated body fracture of mandible, comminuted fronto-zygomatic suture fracture, complicated fracture of the orbital rims (superior and inferior), and complicated symphyseal fracture [Graph 1].

Efficacy of plating system

Among the 19 cases operated using the novel all-rounder hinge plating system, the operating surgeons expressed high levels of satisfied outcomes in 68.4% of cases and moderately satisfied in 31.6% of cases [Table 1].

Stability

The stability of hard tissues after fixation was evaluated using bi-manual palpation and found to produce satisfied results in 57.9% of cases and high satisfaction in 42.1% of cases [Table 1].

Aesthetic outcome

The post-operative aesthetic outcome was assessed for 19 patients based on the operative site. The operating surgeon and the patients expressed high levels of satisfied outcomes in 57.9% of cases, moderately satisfied in 31.6% of cases, while two cases (10.5%) produced neutral or average aesthetic outcomes [Table 1].

Clinical function

The post-operative clinical function for the 19 participants was evaluated on post-operative days 7, 15, 45, and 6th month.

On the 7th day, 63.2% were satisfied with their clinical functions, while 36.8% were highly satisfied with a mean value of 3.2 + 0.40. On the 15th day, 89.5% expressed highly satisfied and improved clinical function, and 10.5% remained satisfied resulting in a 3.84 + 0.58 mean value. On the 45th day, 94.7% exhibited high satisfaction, while 5.3% still showed moderate satisfaction summing to a 4.68 + 0.46 mean value. At 6 months, similar results on the 45th day were noted (94.7% showed high satisfaction, and 5.3% showed moderate satisfaction), totaling to 4.68 + 0.46 mean value [Table 2].

DISCUSSION

Maxillofacial surgeons routinely deal with trauma of the head and neck. Depending on the impact of trauma and type of bone, these fractures can be simple, complicated, linear, comminute, multiple, or pan-facial.[101112] Traditionally, fractures were managed using external fixation devices.[3] Over time, a vast evolution in maxillofacial plating systems provided us miniplates, microplates, custom 3D plates, bioresorbable plates, and others.[45]

The current system of plating has proven its efficiency in the fixation of simple and linear fractures, but despite the advances, it fails to render appropriate and adequate treatment in multiple, comminuted, or complicated fractures of the maxillofacial region due to instability of the fracture fragments or close proximity to vital structures that could lead to potential post-operative complications.[6]

In the field of orthopedic plating systems, hinge joints’ philosophy demonstrated superior reconstruction without extensile soft tissue dissection and was also linked to a decreased rate of complications because of the quality of reduction. Zhenkai Wu et al. in 2015 conducted an animal study (Bama miniature piglet) to introduce a newly designed hinged plate and screw system for the correction of angular deformities of the lower limbs and concluded that the hinge plate and screw system were a more reliable technique in comparison with the conventional tension- band plate and screw.[13]

The novel all-rounder hinge plating system is a long continuous titanium plate manufactured with hinge joints incorporated on every alternate or for every two holes by the process of stamp and turret. In this case, the ends of the miniplates are made to half the thickness of the remaining plate, overlapped, and subjected to the machining of the hinge. This creates an undetachable snug fit between the two plates, which aids in the movement of the plate along the axis. The incorporation of a screw slot in the hinge joint is an additional feature, enabling added stability and better functionality of the plating system. The hinges incorporated enable the movement of the plate around an axial plane of approx. 300 degrees, thereby aiding in modifying the plate chairside according to the clinical presentation of the maxillofacial fracture [Figure 1]. The linear plate can be modified to take the shape of specific-shaped plates currently available (L plate, Z plate, curved plate) or into a design based on the fracture presented [Figure 2].[514]

The novel all-rounder hinge plating system and its various modifications

In 2019, Abhay Datarkar et al.[15] proposed a newly designed twin fork miniplate that showed the least structural deformation and produced the lowest equivalent stresses on the application of maximal occlusal forces with the additional advantage of preservation of the mental nerve during the plating procedure. Bhagat JA et al.[16] in 2019 developed a new V-shaped implant with locking plates and screws for anterior mandibular fracture fixation with a four-directional placement option to involve both the lines of osteosynthesis within a single plate. In 2021, Prajwalit Prakash Kende et al. proposed and patented the novel Z plate to overcome the shortcomings of adaptation difficulty and fixation in mandibular fractures involving the mental nerve.[17] Nermine Habib et al. in 2021 proposed a new hinge plate technique for osteosynthesis of comminuted proximal humeral fractures to achieve better reconstruction of the medial metaphysis without extensive soft tissue dissection along with proximal humerus locking plate.[1314] The novel all-rounder hinge plating system was able to achieve highly satisfactory fracture segment reduction and fixation in 68.4% of the cases and moderately satisfactory results in 31.6% of cases.

Comminuted or complicated maxillofacial fractures are often difficult to manage and require the inclusion of all the fracture segments during fixation to produce overall stability at the site of fracture.[1819] In 2024, Arman SS et al. studied the versatility of titanium mesh in comminuted mandibular fractures and concluded that semi-rigid nature of the titanium mesh fixation allows micro-movement at the healing bone ends and reduces stress shielding effect which may improve functional bone healing.[720] Earlier management techniques for comminuted or complicated fractures have shown healing periods longer than the simple, linear fractures owing to the multiple segments, and disruption of vascularization.[21222324] The design idea of our plating system was able to reduce and include all the fracture segments which has greatly influenced on the early clinical efficiency of our subjects in the study [Figure 3].

The fixation of complicated maxillofacial fractures using the novel hinge plating system

Splint-assisted comminuted fractures management by Ersoy Konas et al. and Kongsong W and Sittitavornwong in 2020 showed satisfied outcomes by over a period of 4–6 weeks.[8] Based on the type of fracture to be managed, the ease of handling of the novel all-rounder hinge plating system was predominantly satisfactory for the operating surgeons in 68.4% of the cases. This is in accordance with previously reported studies of the shortcomings in the management of complicated and comminuted fractures, thereby taking time and effort to manipulate the plating system chairside for proper fixation of fracture segments.[25] Plating in regions of orbital rims or the fronto-zygomatic suture regions requires additional care owing to the decreased amount of subcutaneous fat overlined by a thin epithelium.[2627] This makes the area more pronounced if managed incorrectly or more vulnerable to any future incidents which is in accordance with the results of our study.

CONCLUSION

Thus, this in vivo study concludes that the novel all-rounder hinge plating system provides a dual benefit of replacing the existing conventional mini-plating system and its modification and also aids in the efficient management of comminuted and complicated maxillofacial fractures. As technology continues to evolve, this plating system has marked a breakthrough in the field of oro-faciomaxillary surgery.

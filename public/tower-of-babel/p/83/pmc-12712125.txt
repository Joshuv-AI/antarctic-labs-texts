Timing of human chorionic gonadotropin administration affects conception rates after embryo transfer in crossbred beef heifers
Authors: Ken HAZANO, Akira GOTO, Shingo HANEDA, Motozumi MATSUI
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to determine the effect of the timing of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) administration to embryo transfer (ET) recipients on the reproductive outcome of ET in crossbred beef heifers. Heifers (n=1,887) were randomly assigned to the non-treatment group (n=1,160), and hCG was administered on day 5 (n=201), 6 (n=210), 7 (n=213), or 8 (n=103) after estrus. The χ2 test showed that the conception rates in the hCG administration on day 5 (79.6%, P=0.0001) and day 6 (75.2%, P=0.0047) were higher than those in the non-treatment group (65.2%). However, the conception rates with hCG administration on day 7 (70.9%, P=0.1103) and day 8 (70.9%, P=0.2499) were comparable to the non-treatment. A multiple logistic regression analysis, furthermore, showed that early treatment (hCG on day 5 or 6 after estrus) positively affected the conception rates compared to non-treatment (odds ratios (OR)=1.723 [95% confidence intervals (CI)=1.316−2.257]), but not late treatment (hCG on day 7 or 8 after estrus) (OR=1.299 [95% CI=0.981−1.719]). None of the confounding variables, including years, season at the time of ET, age in months, and number of ET, significantly affected the conception rate. In conclusion, hCG administration on day 5 or 6 after estrus for ET recipients improved the conception rate, but not on day 7 or 8, in beef heifers.

INTRODUCTION

Embryo transfer (ET) technology is currently used worldwide for cattle breeding. According to a survey conducted by the International Embryo Technology Society [23], approximately 1,612,000 bovine embryos, including in vivo-derived and in vitro-derived embryos, were transferred worldwide in 2023. In addition, the 2015 report from the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries of Japan showed that approximately 101,000 embryos were transferred [13]. In the current situation, where ET technology for cattle breeding has become popular worldwide, including in Japan, improving reproductive performance following ET is an important issue in reproductive management in dairy and beef farms.

Progesterone (P4) is the main factor regulating the functions of reproductive organs, including the uterus, which is involved in early embryonic development, implantation, and maintenance of pregnancy in mammals [2]. Inadequate plasma P4 concentration may create an unsuitable intrauterine environment, resulting in subsequent embryonic loss. Lower than optimal plasma P4 concentrations during early gestation can cause poor fertility in cattle [25]. In contrast, elevation of plasma P4 concentrations by external P4 supplementation during the early luteal phase promotes conceptus elongation [3, 6, 17] and improves conception rates [12] in cattle.

One strategy to improve conception rates is to administer human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) to ET recipients [25]. Many studies have shown that administration of hCG during the early luteal phase promotes the development of the original corpus luteum (CL) [10] and causes the first-wave dominant follicle to ovulate, followed by the formation of an accessory CL, which increases serum P4 concentrations in cattle [4, 8, 15, 18, 22]. However, the effects of hCG on conception rates remain controversial in previous studies. In beef cows, ET conception rates were improved by the administration of 1,000 IU hCG at ET on days 5.5 to 8.5 of the cycle or on day 7 after timed ovulation (hCG vs. control=58.6%; n=362 vs. 51.3%; n=359) [24]. An administration of 1,500 IU hCG on day 5 [9] or 6 [16] after estrus improved ET conception rates in crossbred beef heifers (hCG vs. control=76.2%; n=210 vs. 65.3%; n=248) and Japanese Black cows (hCG vs. control=67.5%; n=40 vs. 45.0%; n=40), respectively. Conversely, no improvements in ET conception rates were observed with administration of 2,000 IU hCG at ET on day 7 after ovulatory induction with GnRH in Holstein heifers (hCG vs control=39.0%; n=148 vs 37.0%; n=143) [15]. These reports suggest the presence of potential factors influencing the effects of hCG on conception rates in ET recipients.

One of the factors that influences the effects of hCG on the fertility of cattle is the timing of treatment. A meta-analysis of the available data from several studies on dairy cattle showed that hCG administration on days 5−7 of the estrous cycle improved ET conception rates, but not at later periods (days 11−14) [5]. This finding is consistent with that of a previous study showing that an increase in plasma P4 concentration in the early luteal phase is critical for early embryonic development [11]. Administration of hCG on day 2 after the timed AI promoted the development of the original CL. It increases serum P4 concentration on days 6−12 without forming an accessory CL [10] but does not improve AI conception rates [20]. Conception rates after ET were higher when accessory corpus luteum was formed due to hCG administration on day 7 of the estrous cycle compared to when no formation of accessory corpus luteum was observed (53.5%; n=43 vs. 11.1%; n=18) [4], which suggests that the formation of accessory CL due to the hCG administration is important for the improvement of conception rates.

When administering hCG to improve conception rates, it is considered appropriate to administer it during the early luteal phase; however, there remains room for further research on the exact timing of administration. Additionally, some potential factors, including parity [5, 14, 21], herd [14], fertility [5], and genotype [26], have been reported to influence the effects of hCG administration on fertility. A previous study examined the appropriate timing of hCG administration using a meta-analysis of data from multiple studies [5]. Although a meta-analysis is beneficial, it is challenging to eliminate the confounding effects of differences in parity, herd, and fertility among multiple studies.

This study aimed to determine the appropriate timing for hCG administration during the early luteal phase to improve fertility in cattle. In this study, we examined the effects of hCG administration on ET conception rates in a single herd of crossbred beef heifers.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

This study was approved by the Animal Experiment Committee of Obihiro University of Agriculture and Veterinary Medicine, Japan (Permit number 20-216).

Animal and experimental protocol

Crossbred beef heifers (Japanese Black ×Holstein) were used in this study. The age of heifers was 14.4 ± 1.2 (mean ± standard deviation; range 12−19) months old. They were maintained in free barns and fed corn silage plus feed concentrate at a single commercial farm in Kamishihoro-cho, Hokkaido, northeast Japan (located at latitude 43°25’ North and longitude 143°29’ East).

We hypothesized that the timing of hCG administration influences the effect of hCG on the conception rate after ET. In this study, we used the reproductive records of heifers subjected to ET on a commercial farm between January 2015 and March 2020. ET was performed 2,001 times in 1,611 heifers. The experimental design is illustrated in Fig. 1.Fig. 1.Treatment and sampling schedules in this experiment. Estrus (standing behavior) was detected by observing three times a day (30 to 60 min per session). In this study, the day of estrus was defined as day 0. Based on previous studies with Japanese black cows [16] and crossbred heifers (Japanese Black × Holstein) [8, 9], heifers were randomly assigned to receive either no treatment (n=1,162) or treatment with 1,500 IU hCG (Gestron1500; Kyoritsu Seiyaku, Tokyo, Japan) (n=839). Furthermore, according to the timing of hCG administration, the hCG group was further subdivided into hCG on day 5, 6, 7, or 8 after estrus. The heifers were then subjected to ET on day 7 or 8 after estrus by a single experienced technician. The date of the ET (day 7 or 8) was randomly determined according to work availability. All the embryos used in this study were recovered from donor cows 7 days after insemination and subsequently cryopreserved. A frozen-thawed embryo was transferred to the uterine horn, ipsilateral to the ovary with the original CL. In the hCG group on day 5 or 6, the hCG administration was conducted before ET (day 7 or 8), whereas in the hCG group on day 7 or 8, hCG was administered at ET. All heifers were confirmed to have one or more CL by rectal palpation on the day of or before ET. Pregnancy was diagnosed on day 50 post-estrus using a transrectal ultrasonography fitted with a linear probe emitting at 5 MHz (HS-101V; Honda Electronics, Toyohashi, Japan). Heifers in which re-estrus was observed before pregnancy diagnosis were considered non-pregnant.

Treatment and sampling schedules in this experiment.

In the non-treatment group, two records were excluded owing to missing data, resulting in 1,160 records of ET being used for analysis. The 55 heifers were repeatedly exposed to hCG two or three times at intervals of 45.7 ± 3.2 (mean ± standard error) days. A previous study in which hCG was repeatedly administered to lactating dairy cows at 35-day intervals has shown that the percentage of cows presenting an immune response significantly increased after the second exposure to hCG [7]. In other words, the ovulation response to second and subsequent hCG administration may be inhibited by an immune response. Therefore, 112 records of ET from 55 heifers repeatedly exposed to hCG were excluded from the analysis. In the hCG treatment group, 727 records of ET were used for analysis and classified into hCG on day 5 (n=201), 6 (n=210), 7 (n=213), or 8 (n=103) according to the timing of hCG administration. Ultimately, 1,887 records of ET from 1,564 heifers were included in this study.

Statistical analysis

First, the effects of the timing of hCG administration (day 5, 6, 7, or 8) on the conception rate were analyzed using the χ2 test. Non-treated heifers were defined as the control group, and the ET conception rate of the control group was compared to that of each hCG treatment group (day 5, 6, 7, or 8, and total days 5−8).

Second, a logistic analysis was performed to further analyze the effect of the timing of hCG administration on the conception rate. The hCG treatment group was further subdivided into two groups: early treatment (hCG treatment before ET, day 5 or 6 after estrus) and late treatment (hCG treatment at ET, day 7 or 8 after estrus). Thereafter, the effects of the timing of hCG administration (independent variables: no treatment, early treatment, or late treatment) on ET outcomes (dependent variables: pregnant or not pregnant) were analyzed using logistic regression. These regression models included the following five confounding variables which were possible factors affecting ET conception rate: year at the time of ET (2015, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, or 2020), season at the time of ET (Spring: March−May, Summer: June−August, Autumn: September−November, or Winter: December−February), days from estrus to ET (day 7 or 8), age in months of recipients (≤13, 14, or ≥15 months), and number of ET (1, 2, or ≥3).

Multiple logistic regression analysis was used to assess the relative risk using odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI). Interaction effects between two categorical independent variables were contained in all models, but insignificant interactions were removed from the final models (P≥0.05). For all statistical analyses, P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Japan Ltd., Tokyo, Japan).

DISCUSSION

One strategy for improving the ET conception rate in cattle is to administer hCG to recipients. The timing of hCG administration is important for improving fertility, and administration in the early luteal phase is known to be most effective [5]. However, it is still debatable about a stricter timing of hCG administration. In this study, we examined the effect of the timing of hCG administration on ET conception rates in single hard-crossbred beef heifers.

The results of the χ2 test and logistic regression in this study showed that the administration of 1,500 IU hCG on day 5 or 6, but not on day 7 or 8, improved the ET conception rate in crossbred beef heifers. This indicates that administering hCG on day 5 or 6 is more effective in improving the performance of ET in beef heifers.

In the present study, the mechanism by which the timing of hCG administration affects reproductive outcomes of ET could not be clarified. In cattle, the hCG administration in the early luteal phase (days 4−7 after estrus) causes ovulation of the first wave dominant follicle and the formation of secondary corpus luteum by 89%; n=9 [10], 100%; n=14 [18], 97%; n=37 [22]. Plasma P4 concentrations significantly increase 2−4 days after hCG administration compared to those in non-treated or placebo groups [15, 18]. Previous studies have shown that hCG administration on day 5 [9] or 6 [16] induces ovulation of first-wave dominant follicles and decreases plasma E2 concentrations on days 7 and 14. The increases in plasma P4 [11] and E2 [1] concentrations during the early luteal phase have positive and negative effects, respectively, on early embryo development. The phenomenon that the timing of hCG administration affects reproductive outcomes of ET may be related to plasma hormone concentrations during the early luteal phase after hCG administration. Further research is needed about the effect of hCG administration timing on plasma hormone concentration dynamics.

In the present study, ET recipients were treated with hCG before ET in the early treatment group, whereas in the late treatment group, hCG was administered concomitantly with ET. Therefore, in the early treatment group, the change of the endocrine environment was presumed to have been caused before the time of ET by the stimulation of the original CL and the ovulation of the first-wave dominant follicle due to the hCG treatment. Plasma hormone levels with higher P4 and lower E2 levels at the time of ET may be important for improving conception rates.

The hCG administration on day 5 after timed AI improved conception rates in primiparous Holstein dairy cows, but not in multiparous cows [14]. In a meta-analysis of the effects of hCG administration on the fertility of ET recipients, there was an obvious improvement in ET conception rates following hCG treatment in multiparous cows, but not in heifers [5]. These studies indicate that parity influences the effects of hCG on cattle fertility. In addition, it is reported that the hCG or GnRH treatment improves ET conception rates in dairy cattle with low fertility (defined as that ET conception rates are <40% in non-treated or placebo group), but not in dairy cattle with high fertility (>40%) [5]. Although we used crossbred beef heifers with high fertility (65.3%) as ET recipients in the present study, the hCG administration on day 5 or 6 improved ET conception rates by 12.1 points compared to non-treated heifers. Although it is difficult to explain the differences in results among studies, the results of this study suggest that hCG treatment is effective in improving fertility, even in beef heifers with high fertility.

The univariate analysis in Table 2 showed that ET conception rates were significantly influenced by year. In addition, the multiple logistic regression analysis in Table 3 showed that, although not statistically significant, there was a tendency for the conception rates to vary by year. It is challenging to explain why the ET conception rates varied by year in the present study. One possible explanation is that differences in roughage quality and weather conditions from year to year may have affected the ET conception rates.

As the ET technique can bypass heat-sensitive stages such as oocytes to the cleavage stage, ET is used to improve pregnancy rates under heat stress [19]. In the present study, a decline in conception rates was not observed during the summer season, and there was no change in ET conception rates among the seasons.

A lot of studies have been conducted on hCG administration to improve bovine fertility [9, 14, 15, 22, 24], but the dosage of hCG has varied between studies, and there is still no consensus. This study showed that the administration of 1,500 IU hCG on day 5 or day 6 improved conception rate after ET in crossbred beef heifers. However, optimal hCG doses may differ in breeds of cattle with different hormone metabolic status, such as high-yielding dairy cows. Future research is also needed on the optimal hCG dosage according to cow characteristics such as breed, parity, and lactation.

The present study examined the most appropriate timing for hCG administration to improve the ET conception rate in single hard-crossbred beef heifers. While the results of this study showed simple effects of the timing of hCG administration on ET conception rates in crossbred beef heifers without other confounding factors as much as possible, it remains unclear whether similar results can be obtained in other herds with different feeding conditions, parity, breeding, fertility, and milking statuses. Further studies are required to determine the appropriate use of hCG to improve fertility in cattle.

In summary, the present study showed that hCG administration before ET on day 5 or 6, but not concomitantly with ET on day 7 or 8 after estrus, improved the ET conception rate in crossbred heifers. These results indicate that the appropriate timing for hCG administration to improve the ET conception rate is 5−6 days after estrus in beef heifers. This information contributes to the effective use of hCG to improve the fertility of beef heifers.

Laboratory performance of novel injectable keratin nanoparticle from poultry feathers loaded-alginate composite hydrogel as a pulpotomy filling biomaterial for dentin regeneration
Authors: Marwa S Naga, Hala M Helal, Elbadawy A Kamoun, Maha Abdel Moaty, Samia S Abdel Rehim Omar, Ahmed Z Ghareeb, Esmail M El-Fakharany, Mona Mohy El Din
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVarious medicaments have been utilized as an aid for wound healing following vital pulp treatment. Many of them are synthetic in origin and have disadvantages as limited amount of cytotoxicity and great expense. Hence, to tackle these drawbacks, naturally derived blended scaffolds have been employed in renovative dental field. Current research attempts to synthesize and characterize physicochemical performance of the newly injectable alginate hydrogel loaded with chicken feather regenerated keratin nanoparticles (nKer NPs) in different percentages as a new pulpotomy agent.MethodsAlginate hydrogels were developed by dissipating alginate powder in alcohol distilled water including nKer NPs at 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 wt%. The obtained mix was agitated and sustained at nighttime in water-bath at 50oC to assure adequate solvability. A sterilized dual syringe assembly was utilized to blend alginate phase with 20 wt% calcium chloride phases, producing hydrogel during ejection. Next, all fabricated hydrogel specimens were characterized by FTIR, SEM, thermal and mechanical stability, viscosity, surface wettability and cell viability. Statistical analysis was conducted utilizing One-Way ANOVA comparing between groups. Multiple pairwise comparisons applying Bonferroni adjusted significance level (p ≤ 0.05) were performed.ResultsAlginate/nKer NPs (1.0 wt%) loaded group (IIb) exhibited wide (243 μm), open pores with adequate interconnectivity. Thermal stability (Tonset 185.8-285.77oC) (Td2 185.8-488.23oC) (Td3 488.23-600oC) was improved in all loaded hydrogels relative to control group (Tonset 650C) (Td2 65-300 C) (Td3 350–635). Further increase in nKer NPs content elevated thermal stability [Tonset: IIa 185.8, IIb 191.85, IIc 285.770C]. Greatest Compressive Young’s Modulus and collapse/yield stress were displayed in group IIb [2.28, 1.14 (MPa)]; respectively (p ≤ 0.05). Moreover, group IIb exhibited the best hydrophilic behavior (θ 32.03o) (p ≤ 0.05). The increase in concentration of nKer NPs within the hydrogel increased the hydrogel viscosity. Background

Dentin and pulp tissue destruction are considered part of the major frequent dental obstacles that considerably impress patient oral health condition and comfort [1]. Preservation of pulp vitality is a major concern in restorative dentistry [2, 3]. Dentin regeneration is a scope of investigation in dental field with high capability to encourage conservation of natural dentition [3]. Pulpotomy is regarded as a biological approach for tooth restoration. It conserves pulp vitality and promotes remaining radicular pulp tissue to be restored and generate a dentin-like tissue secure [4]. Different pulpotomy agents are applied such as: Bio-dentine, Bio-aggregate and Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA). Yet no one of them was confirmed to be perfect. Accordingly, this was the shooting boost behind the exploration for a biocompatible biomaterial which attains all needs desired to obtain a prosperous regenerating pulpotomy approach [5]. Consequently, to tackle such drawbacks, scaffold-established tissue regeneration paradigm is considered, a favourable procedure to restore destructed dentin/pulp complex [6, 7].

Soft scaffolds such as injectable hydrogels imitate extracellular matrix (ECM) configuration and are promising in regenerating dentin/ pulp complex. They work like a support which transport cells, therapeutic agents and bioactive molecules, conveniently, to desired locations in a less destructive way [8]. Natural polymer- derived hydrogels like cellulose, fibrin, alginate, keratin etc. showed superior benefits for example, being non-cytotoxic, biocompatible, obtainable, and low cost [8]. The search in elaborating scaffolds originated from natural biomaterials for tissue regeneration purpose is promptly expanding [9]. Alginate is an anionic polymer, originated from brown seaweed. Alginate hydrogel is biologically acceptable, harmless, non-immunogenic and biodegradable. Also, alginate has a high swelling capability and a superior hydrogel forming character under physiological circumstances. Alginate hydrogel provides a template to assist regeneration of dental tissues and it has been demonstrated that it enhances release of dentin matrix [10].

Keratin is a natural structural fibrous protein that might be extracted from feathers, wool, hair, nails and horns. It is characterized by having arginine-glycine-aspartic acid (RGD), Leu-Asp-Val (LDV), Leu-Asp-Ser (LDS) cell binding motifs that allow cell adhesion and proliferation in 3D matrix structure [11, 12, 13]. Moreover, keratin affects cell behavior and response to signals [14]. It enhances odontogenic differentiation and mineralization which are important in dentin-pulp complex regeneration [15]. Wound dressing keratin - based biomaterials enhance tissue regeneration and even they were naturally integrated within the newly regenerated tissue during the wound healing process [16].

Chicken feathers are a solid waste product produced in huge amounts. It is worth mentioning that it contains approximately 91% of keratin protein, 1% lipids and 8% water and high amino acids content [17]. However, its disposal and degradation are monotonous processes leading to pollutions and cause human disease [18]. Currently, searching for an environmentally friendly, non -polluting approach to recycle chicken feather throw away is of ultimate concern, not only to adhere to the 2030 United Nations goals [19] but also to recycle feather waste into a more valuable biomaterials such as hydrogels and nanoparticles [20]. Thus keratin could be regenerated from chicken feather waste and applied to produce keratin nanoparticles (nKer NPs) that can blend with other polymers used in various biomedical applications [21]. Keratin nanoparticles offer a perfect environment for growth, proliferation and differentiation of dental pulp stem cells (DPSCs) [22]. Additionally, they show strong cytocompatibility, promoting cell adhesion and proliferation [13]. Merging of the previously mentioned biomaterials can be considered as a biological biomimetic attempt ideal for renovating dental-tissues combining benefits of every biomaterial and reducing their limitations. The use of nanoparticles from poultry feathers has to be considered carefully while constructing a novel biomaterial.

Therefore, the present work attempts to synthesize, prepare and characterize an innovative injectable alginate hydrogel, loaded with chicken feather keratin nanoparticles (nKer NPs) (natural nanofillers) in different percentages [0.5, 1.0, 1.5 (wt%)] as an encouraging pulpotomy filling biomaterial in dentin regeneration. The constructed hydrogels have been evaluated regarding their physicochemical behavior and cytotoxicity.

Materials and methods

Materials

Sodium alginate powder, anhydrous calcium chloride powder, and ethanol were bought from Sigma- Aldrich, Chemie. Germany. Chicken feather keratin nano particles NT-GNPs (nKer NPs) were bought from Nano Tech. Egypt. Human gingival samples were acquired from Faculty of Dentistry, Oral Surgery Department, Alexandria University, Egypt. Next human gingival fibroblasts were secluded in Center of Excellence for Research in Regenerative Medicine and Applications. MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazoliumbromide was bought from (Thermo Fisher scientific, Massachusetts, USA). Dimethyl sulfoxide (DEMSO, 99%) was got from Sigma-Aldrich Chemicals Co. (St. Louis, USA).

Methods

In the experimental part: 28 hydrogel samples were prepared for characterization purpose. Based on comparison of means, sample size was evaluated as 6 for each group increased to 7 to compensate for errors during laboratory step [23]. Sample size was resolved assuming alpha error = 5%, study power = 80% [24], and worked out by GPower 3.1.9.4 sample size calculator software.

Prepared samples were allocated into two main groups based on the loading of alginate with nKer NPs: Group I: unloaded sodium alginate hydrogel (Control) (n = 7), group II: sodium alginate/ nKer NPs loaded hydrogel (n = 21). Group II was subdivided into three subgroups according to the concentration of nKer NPs: subgroup IIa: 0.5 wt% nKer NPs (n = 7), subgroup IIb: 1.0 wt% nKer NPs (n = 7), subgroup IIc: 1.5 wt% nKer NPs (n = 7). Random allocation method was employed to group all tested specimens [23, 24], (Table 1).

Methodology

Preparation of sodium alginate hydrogel

For establishing the ideal standards for constructing the injectable alginate hydrogel: The ideal metric was achieved at 7 wt% sodium alginate and 20 wt% anhydrous calcium chloride. This revealed the most suitable setting time at 15 min, injectability, pH, viscosity behavior, swelling behavior at predetermined periods and degradation [25].

Generally, 7 wt% aqueous sodium alginate phase was obtained by dissolving sodium alginate powder, homogenously, in sterile distilled water in a clean environment, employing magnetic stirrer up till an absolute unobscured, cloudless solution existed. A sterilized syringe filter was utilized to filter the obtained solution. Next, the anhydrous calcium chloride powder was dissolved in sterile distilled water to achieve the mineral phase (20 wt% anhydrous calcium chloride). Lastly, both phases were loaded into specially set up aseptic dual- syringe assembly creating a discharge of consistent formulation [25] (Fig. 1).

Construction of injectable chicken feather regenerated nKer NPs loaded-sodium alginate composite hydrogel

Sodium alginate hydrogel containing nKer NPs in different percentages [0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 (wt%)] was constructed pursuing the aforementioned procedure as illustrated in Fig. 2 [25].

Laboratory characterization of the novel injectable alginate hydrogel scaffolds

The samples (10 mm in width × 5 mm in length) were prepared and injected into specifically fabricated Teflon molds (10 mm width, 5 mm length), to get a consistent hydrogel - disc form.

Instrumental characterization

FTIR

For chemical bond configuration determination, 7 discs (10 mm width × 5 mm length) for constructed hydrogels were prepared in every group, utilizing Fourier-transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) model: (Spectrum 100; Perkin Elmer, Germany) at wavenumber 400–4000 cm− 1 and resolution 2 cm− 1 [26, 27].

SEM

Surface morphology and porosity of the prepared hydrogels specimens were evaluated by scanning electron microscope (SEM) (JSM-IT200; JEOL, Japan). Seven discs (10 mm width × 5 mm length) were constructed [27, 28].

Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA)

7 hydrogel discs (dimensions 10 mm width x 5 mm length) from every study group were constructed to calculate thermal stability using TGA thermo-gram. The thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was utilized on a TGA apparatus (Shimadzu TGA-50, Japan) from 50 to 6000C at heating rate of 100C/min. Certain parameters (thermal kinetic) were measured from TGA outcomes [Starting temperature (Tonset) and degradation temperature (Td)]. Tonset describes the temperature at the meeting point of the starting weight and tangent marked to the weight curve at point of the highest rate of weight loss percentage [29].

Mechanical stability by compression test

Compression performance of hydrogel specimens from every study group/subgroup was measured utilizing a universal testing machine (5ST, Tinius Olsen, England) to examine the influence of nKer NPs incorporation on the mechanical stability of the sodium alginate hydrogel. A total of 7 specimens in each group were processed into cylindrical forms of length 8 mm and width 10 mm and employed in the compression test. Each specimen was uniaxially compressed with a crosshead speed (1 mm min− 1) utilizing a 5 KN load cell. The compression modulus was measured by plotting a tangential line in the first section of stress-strain graph. The gradient of such a line was known as the modulus of elasticity. The plateau was known as the yield/ collapse stress related to specimen [30] Fig. S1 (Supplementary data).

Physicochemical characterization

Rheological characterization of hydrogels

Viscosity evaluation

A total number of 7 hydrogel discs (10 mm diameter x 5 mm height) from each study group were prepared to record the viscosity using viscometer (Brookfield, Model DV2T, Labamat Essor, France) at room temperature and speed of 1.0 revolution per minute (RPM) [31, 32, 33].

Surface wettability analysis

The contact angle of prepared sodium alginate hydrogel discs (n = 7) (dimensions 10 mm diameter x 5 mm height) from all study groups was calculated using sessile drop approach utilizing contact angle goniometer (Ramé– Hart Instruments). It is an easy approach for analyzing the hydrophilic or hydrophobic characteristics of chemical groups joined to the external coat of an aspect. Employing an unsharpened unpainted, rounded end metal syringe of the apparatus, a drop of water (10 µl) was expelled above the exterior of the disc at 25oC and relative humidity of 56%. A device’s camera was utilized to capture a photograph of the water drop on the surface every second for 10 s. Then the contact angle was calculated by determining the angulation at the right and left edges of the drop employing an associated guiding software. For all 10 readings an average of the angles was determined in every individual specimen [34].

Cell viability percentage (%) using MTT assay

The present work was approved by the Institutional Ethical Committee, Faculty of Dentistry, Alexandria University (IRB No. 00010556-IORG No. 0008839) (0240-April 2021). Cell viability evaluation was executed by indirect approach according to (ISO 10993-5) instructions. The consequence of sodium alginate powder, nKer NPs and cross-linked hydrogels concentrate on cell viability % was calculated by MTT colorimetric cytotoxicity test. Water soluble, yellow MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazoliumbromide was reduced to blue/violet insolvable formazan in alive cells. Human gingival samples were acquired from the Faculty of Dentistry, Oral Surgery Dep., Alexandria University. Isolation procedures were then conducted in the Center of Excellence for Research in Regenerative Medicine and Applications, Alexandria University. Human gingival fibroblasts were obtained from a preliminary grown cell line cultured from individuals in good health. Gingival fibroblasts of 3rd to 5th passage were used during the present work. Afterwards fibroblast suspension containing 1.0 × 105 cells was maintained in alpha minimum essential medium Eagle (αMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; SERANA, Germany) and cultured in germ- free 24-well flat bottom microplates (Greiner Bio-One GMBH, Frickenhausen, Germany). After incubation at 37oC in 5% CO2 for 24 h, various percentages of alginate powder, nKer NPs and hydrogels concentrates from all tested samples (groups: I (control), IIa, IIb and IIc) were put on top of the cells and incubated at 37oC in 5% CO2. Following 24 h and 48 h concentrates were removed and cells were washed using phosphate buffered saline solution (PBS) to abolish non-living cells and all remnants. 200 µL of MTT solution (0.5 mg/mL) were placed on every microplate and incubated for 5 h at 37oC in 5% CO2. The absorbance (A) was calculated at 630 nm utilizing microplate ELISA reader (Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay) following the vanish of the purple formazan crystals in 200 µL/well containing DMSO. Statistical analysis

Data were interpreted employing IBM SPSS for windows (Version 23.0) and significance was installed at p value ≤ 0.05. Means and standard deviations (SD) were measured for each variable. One-Way ANOVA was applied for comparing between study groups. Multiple pairwise comparisons were then performed applying Bonferroni adjusted significance level.

Results

FTIR analysis of composite hydrogels

Figure 3 displays IR spectra of sodium alginate powder, nKer NPs, cross-linked sodium alginate hydrogels prior to and following loading with the tested nKer NPs. IR spectrum of sodium alginate powder reveals wide peak from ν 3000 to 3500 cm− 1 that is assigned to O-H stretching. The peak at ν 2925 cm− 1 confers with alkyl stretching mode (ν -CH) (Fig. 3a). IR spectrum of unloaded cross-linked alginate hydrogel shows peaks at ν 1600 and 1431 cm− 1 that explain stretching vibrations of -COOH (asymmetric) and -COOH (symmetric) in alginate; respectively. Particularly, both peaks at ν 1600 and ν 1431 cm− 1 are considered the ultimate important characteristic peaks to ascertain ionic cross linking. Moreover, a typical distinctive band associated with alginate has been observed at ν 1021.5 cm− 1 that is ascribed to C-O-C group (Fig. 3b). The illustrative IR spectrum of chicken feather keratin nanoparticles shows characteristic transmission bands related to peptide bonds (-CONH) which are named Amide A- B and Amide I- III. Both transmission bands at ν 3226 cm− 1 (amide A) and ν 2922 cm− 1 (Amide B) correspond to N-H hydrogen bond stretching. An intense, stabbing transmission peak at ν 1632 cm− 1 is connected to C = O stretching (amide I) and linked with the carbonyl bonds elaborated in protein secondary configuration. Band at ν 1528 cm− 1 is credited to amide II that emerged because of N-H bending and C-H stretching. Amide III is represented as frail band at ν 1240 cm− 1 due to the union between C-N stretching and N-H in plane bending. Additionally, due to certain share from C-C stretching and C = O bending vibration. Beside above mentioned bands, other peaks at 612 and 521 cm− 1 are attributed to the C-S bond vibration stretching (Fig. 3c). Evidently, illustrated IR spectrum of loaded hydrogel reveals new bands such as bands at ν 1623.33 cm− 1 and ν 1424.56 cm− 1 that are assigned to C-H stretching and N-H bending of amide- I and amide-II; respectively. Also, a novel band has appeared at ν 3373 cm− 1, attributable to N-H stretching of amides (Fig. 3d).

Mechanical stability

Compression test

Compressive Young’s Modulus and collapse/ yield stress are measured from the compression stress-strain curve [Fig. S1 (Supplementary data)]. It can be clearly noted in Fig. 7 that alginate/nKer NPs loaded groups have revealed an obvious elevation in both compressive Young’s Modulus and collapse/yield stress. Results reveal that raising the proportion of nKer NPs from 0.5 to 1.0 wt%, positively influence both Compressive Youngś Modulus and collapse / yield stress of the hydrogel [IIa (1.7, 0.75) and IIb (2.28, 1.14) (MPa)]; respectively (p ≤ 0.05). On the contrary, raising the proportion of nKer NPs to 1.5 wt% negatively influence Compressive Youngś Modulus and collapse/yield stress of the hydrogel [IIc (1.84, 0.77) (MPa)]; respectively (p ≤ 0.05). This has been evidenced using one-way ANOVA test exhibiting that the addition of nKer NPs with different concentration has influenced the mechanical properties (Compressive Youngś Modulus and collapse / yield stress) of the sodium alginate hydrogel (p ≤ 0.05).

Discussion

Current work managed to synthesize and characterize, successfully, an innovative injectable hydrogel loaded with various percentages [0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 (wt%)] of nKer NPs to be applied in minimally invasive regenerative pulpotomy.

Sodium alginate was selected in the present study because of its great superiorities that recommended its application as a natural template in the bio-regenerative area. Nevertheless, it exhibited few limitations like, low mechanical behavior, limited porosity, reduced pore size width and delayed degradation. Therefore, these inferior characteristics opened the way to fabricate various alginate composites [37].

Keratin is considered as a favorable, attainable protein and is the main component of feathers, hair of mammals, birds and reptiles. Keratin is broadly investigated for biomedical use. Hence, in the current work nKer NPs regenerated from chicken feather were incorporated, as organic nanofiller, to sodium alginate hydrogel. In literature, the concentration % of keratin was regulated relative to the use proposed [38]. Taking this in consideration, 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 wt% nKer NPs concentrations were chosen, in the present work, in accordance to various studies which proved to be suitable for use in different biomedical applications [15].

The chosen ideal, optimal setting (formulation) of (7 wt% sodium alginate: 20 wt% calcium chloride anhydrous) promoted the development of the innovative injectable in situ forming hydrogel which accurately went through every morphological inaccessible feature in the operating area. In addition, they ascertained that all hydrogel constituents were adequately integrated, and hydrogel properties were not inferiorly influenced.

The approach employed in the current work was similar to Fu et al. [39] and Sánchez-Fernández.

et al. [40], that guaranteed consistent dissipation of nKer NPs within the alginate template. A specially set up dual- syringe assembly was used similar to the one applied by Cardoso et al. [41], but with few alterations. Successfully, these adaptations allowed even effortless and gentle discharge of a non-heterogenous, steady and coherent hydrogel form. Besides, it permitted proper crosslinking of alginate matrix. As well, it affirmed the adequate reach of the hydrogel to desired destinations, thus rendering it further clinically relevant [42].

FTIR has been considered as an accurate method applied to determine chemical links in organic substances. It offers details about the auxiliary construction arrangement of proteins [43]. In compliance with FTIR results, typical peaks of sodium alginate and incorporated nKer nanoparticles were detected in FTIR spectrum of the innovative hydrogel (Fig. 3). It affirmed the adequate incorporation among alginate matrix and nKer NPs. Remarkable physicochemical alterations were observed after cross linking of developed samples in calcium chloride solution that approved alginate-polymer chains crosslinking. A delicate shift was noticed in majority of transmittance bands in the cross-linked samples. This shift was associated with the motion of carboxyl group. As a result, this cross linking changed the scaffolds to more stable hydrogels.

It is noteworthy that the appearance of both bands at ν 1431 cm− 1 and 1600 cm− 1 suggested the asymmetric and symmetric stretch vibration of–COO− ascribed to carboxylic acid salt that are evident to ion bonding. Also, band at ν 1078.31 cm− 1 was accredited to C-C and C-O stretching that stressed the cross- linking among calcium chloride and sodium alginate. It is important to note, the band of C-C stretching at ν 1021.5 cm− 1 which presented a strong link among guluronic acids of sodium alginate and Ca+ 2. It is worth highlighting that peak at ν 3256 cm− 1 of (O-H stretching) was moved to ν 3344 cm− 1 that conformed to the decreased intra-molecular bonding (Fig. 3b) [44].

IR spectrum of nKer NPs showed three spectral regions of proteins: amid I (ν 1632 cm− 1), II (ν 1528 cm− 1) and III (ν 1240 cm− 1) (Fig. 3c). Amide spectral regions contribute to α- helix, β- sheets and random coil. Certainly, no recognizable peaks related to random coil (ν 1645 cm− 1) or both random coil and β-sheets (ν 1695 cm− 1) were noted in IR spectra of nKer NPs powder or loaded alginate hydrogel. Peak at ν 3226 cm− 1 (Amide A) specified α–helix structure in nKer NPs. The sharp transmission band at ν 1632 cm− 1 (Amide I) was considered as a great importance to protein biochemists. It displayed a larger peak region extent relative to amide II and III, and it was linked to carbonyl bonds elaborated in protein auxiliary construction. Corresponding to literature, Amide I and II bands assigned to β sheet construction in keratin. In fact, Amide I-III bands provide details about keratin configuration and backbone morphology. Hence, this might indicate the existence of α-helix and β–sheets micro-configurations in chicken feather regenerated nKer NPs. Consequently, current IR analysis could suggest that structural configuration of nKer NPs favored β- sheet structures over α-helix which might contribute to nKer NPs stability and rigidity. IR spectrum of loaded alginate/nKer NPs hydrogel showed new features relative to native polymers (Fig. 3d). Particularly, characteristic bands at ν 1623 cm− 1(Amide I) and ν 1424 cm− 1 (Amide II) were moved to less band width. These bands shifts committed the alteration in keratin spectrum as it was added to alginate. Additionally, new band at ν 3373 cm− 1 appeared in loaded alginate/nKer NPs hydrogel spectrum which was generated from N-H stretching of amides. This new band affirmed the interaction between keratin and alginate through hydrogen bond. Furthermore, there was a reduction in band intensity of original polymers at ν 2360 cm− 1 because of the miscibility of keratin and alginate polymers in the hydrogel. This alteration further affirmed the changes of amides I and II bands. Accordingly, FTIR analysis of alginate / nKer NPs loaded hydrogel clarified the interaction of–OH and -CH groups in alginate and amino group of keratins (nKer NPs) during scaffold construction procedure. Current findings were coherent with keratin/alginate blend microparticles fabricated by Srisuwan and Srihanam [45] exhibiting similar typical IR bands.

SEM analysis showed that we managed to overcome porosity (nano-size) limitation in hydrogels grounded on sodium alginate [46]. Apparently, all alginate/nKer NPs loaded hydrogels showed great porosity with well interconnected, open macro -micro pore structure with well developed, eminent walls, sufficient depth and great surface area (Fig. 6). Such pattern is essential for infiltration and arrangement of dental stem cells, angiogenesis and dissemination of nutriments all over hydrogel morphology [47]. Evidently, an increase in pore size diameter was remarkably noted which lied in the range of (196–244 μm) that exceeded the minimal demand (> 100 μm), for tissue engineering, (Figs. 6B-D) (Table 2) [48]. Furthermore, it exceeded the optimum pore size required for dental pulp stem cells (163 μm) [38] and for odontoblast cell infiltration and growth (25–160 μm) [49] so it can be a good prospect for biomaterial administration.

However, the content of nKer NPs had a strong influence on pore size diameter and pore configuration. Herein, hydrogels with low nKer NPs concentration [IIa (0.5 wt%)] (Figs. 6B and J) and [IIb (1.0 wt%)] (Figs. 6 C and K) exhibited more porosity compared to hydrogels with high nKer NPs contents [IIc (1.5 wt%)] (Figs. 6D and L). Moreover, greater pores size diameter was remarkably noted among these groups [IIa (244), IIb (243) (µm)] (Figs. 6B and C); respectively than hydrogels loaded with higher nKer NPs concentration [IIc (196 μm)] (Fig. 6D). Additionally, group IIa (Figs. 6 F and J) and group IIb (Figs. 6G and K) revealed large irregular open pores with well distinguishable walls of sufficient depth and large surface area. Besides, some low nKer NPs loaded hydrogel specimens possessed a characteristic water or tear-drop pore shape configuration, (Figs. 6B and C). On the contrary, hydrogels loaded with higher nKer NPs concentration (1.5 wt%) revealed a unique narrow slit-like shaped pores with low surface area, (Figs. 6 H and L). The apparent increase in pore size diameter could be related to poor hydrophilicity of keratin and quick water loss during freeze drying [50]. While, the observed keratin nanoparticle-content reliant porous morphological characteristic could be possibly credited to the great protein mass per unit volume and chains intertwine [51]. Our findings were pursuant to various published studies: Zuniga et al. [51], constructed human platelet lysate-loaded keratin (KSO) hydrogel. Outcomes showed that with elevating keratin (KSO) content, porosity and pore size of hydrogels declined. This was related to the great protein density and chains intertwine [51]. Feroz et al. [52], fabricated a highly porous interconnected, 3-dimensional keratin-HA (hydroxyapatite) scaffold with mean pore size diameter (108.36 ± 22.56 μm) which could promote new bone formation.

TGA analysis was accomplished to determine, quantitatively, the thermal stability of sodium alginate hydrogel prior to and following the addition of nKer NPs under a monitored environment. It was recognized from the TGA curve of weight losses [Fig. S2 (Supplementary data)] (Table 3) that for all alginate/nKer NPs loaded hydrogels the first weight loss zone started at a much higher temperature (Tonset) which lied in the range of 185.8–285.77 (oC) compared to unloaded sodium alginate hydrogel (Tonset 65oC). This finding could be related to the evaporation (dehydration) of both loose and freely bound water molecules [53]. Similarly, an increase in the second and third degradation stages temperatures (Td2 and Td3) was exhibited by all loaded hydrogels, Fig. S2 (Supplementary data) (Table 3). This could be associated with the decomposition of the ordered constructions, liberation of volatile compounds like CH4, H2S, HCN and CO and denaturation of keratin matrix. In addition, any decomposition mechanism could be associated with further water loss. While for sodium alginate hydrogel this was due to the breakdown of alginate by the fragmentation of C-O-C glycoside bonds, C-H bond and dehydration of saccharide rings in the major principal polymer chain. Evidently, it could be suggested from TGA thermal analysis outcomes that thermal stability of alginate hydrogel was greatly improved following both incorporation of nKer NPs and the rise in their percentages. This could be contributed to the typical, stable, strongly cross-linked fibrous assembly of chicken feather keratin (hard ß- keratin) which possessed great amount of cysteine residues and rigid di sulfide bonds [54]. Actually, present findings were in harmony with Rinaldi et al. [55], who displayed that the incorporation of keratin from chicken feather greatly enhanced thermal stability of composite films keratin and [poly(ε-caprolactone) (PCL). This was attributed to the β-sheet morphology of keratin generated from feather (chicken) which allowed a more stable and rigid scaffold.

In the present work, loading sodium alginate hydrogel with different concentrations of nKer NPs [0.5, 1.0, 1.5 (wt%)], as natural organic nanofillers, was prosperously attempted to tackle the poor mechanical properties of hydrogels established from sodium alginate that restricted their use in regenerative field.

Apparently, both compressive Young’s Modulus and collapse/yield stress were greatly enhanced after the incorporation of nKer NPs (organic nanofiller) with statistically significant difference (p ≤ 0.05) (Fig. 7). This might be attributed to the unique chemical constitution and assembly of chicken feather regenerated keratin (low molecular weight and β-sheet configuration). Basically, it displayed large number of a couple of amino acids (glycine and alanine) that permitted hydrogen bonding among the amino and carboxylic groups on adjoined polymer chains. Hence, promoting intimate arrangement and rigid connection (link). Moreover, additional strength was added due to the existence of great content of sulphur–including amino acids cysteine which developed rigid disulfide bonds (transvers bonds) [56].

Similarly, an obvious rise in compressive Young’s Modulus and collapse/yield stress was also observed with the elevation of the nKer NPs content (0.5 and 1.0 wt%) [IIa (1.7, 0.75) and IIb (2.28, 1.14) (MPa)]; respectively, with statistical significance (p ≤ 0.05). While a further increase of nKer NPs (1.5 wt%) resulted in a decline in compressive Young’s Modulus and collapse/yield stress [IIc (1.84, 0.77) (MPa)]; respectively. This could had happened due to agglomeration of nKer NPs at high concentration (1.5 wt%) which resulted in a non-homogenous composite [57]. Agglomerates of nKer NPs might result in weak interfacial bond between sodium alginate polymer matrix and the nKer NPs filler thus reducing its effect as a functional nanofiller. Regenerated keratin might also develop non uniform agglomerations due to the strong liability of the energetic thiol groups to rejoin once more following reduction procedure [50].

Mechanical findings of the present work were conforming to Ranjbar-Mohammadi et al. [58], who showed that increasing keratin concentration enhanced mechanical properties (tensile strength) of constructed keratin/polyvinyl alcohol (Ker/PVA) and poly (ε–caprolactone) hybrid scaffolds as wound dressing. This was attributed to the presence of beta- keratin which proved to be stronger than alpha- keratin. Also their outcomes presented that additional increase in keratin amount resulted in a decrease in mechanical properties due to inadequate dispersion of keratin with polyvinyl alcohol polymer and also because of the morphology of the formed fibers [58]. Lately, Qin et al. [59] arrived that the addition of small content of keratin (30 wt%) enhanced mechanical behavior of the prepared composite fibers (keratin/cellulose), but further incorporation of higher content of keratin (50 wt%) negatively affected mechanical properties. This finding was assigned to the rigid hydrogen bonds that were easily formed between keratin molecules than between cellulose and keratin. Meanwhile, as keratin content increased the force of hydrogen binding among cellulose molecules and keratin decreased thus entrapping cellulose in the hydrogen bond backbone [59].

Typically, in present work the compressive moduli and collapse/yield stress of the constructed nKer NPs loaded-hydrogels were both in the limit expressed in literature [30]. In addition, they matched perfectly with the previously displayed TGA results. Interestingly, we succeeded to fabricate a novel alginate/nKer NPs loaded hydrogel that exhibited a balance between porosity, mechanical and thermal stability which are essential for scaffold construction in the regenerative field.

Viscosity and injectability are fundamental features of injectable hydrogels that supposed to be investigated while constructing a novel injectable biomaterial [42]. In the current study, viscosity measurements conveyed the proportional relation between nKer NPs addition and sodium alginate hydrogel viscosity. Accordingly, an elevation in viscosity was clearly observed after the increase in nKer NPs concentration (Fig. 8). Further studies in literature reported comparable findings; a keratin based hydrogel (KRT-FIB) possessed great viscosity which could be monitored by the keratin (KRT) concentration so it was appropriate for injection [60, 61]. Moreover, all study groups exhibited adequate viscosity that could be controlled by the nKer NPs concentration in order to facilitate hydrogel injection, delivery in minimally invasive clinical practice [42] and to overcome, effectively, shearing forces within injected site [62]. Ultimately, all hydrogel viscosity values matched the viscosity boundary (− 50 c P) required for subcutaneous injection by means of 25- to 27- gauge syringe [63, 64, 65].

In the present work, surface wettability results revealed that all groups exhibited an appropriate hydrophilic character with a contact angle θ that lied in the hydrophilic limit (00 < θ < 900) [66], (p ≤ 0.05). This might be ascribed to hydrophilic nature of both sodium alginate and keratin which possessed various hydrophilic groups like–OH, -COOH and–NH2 groups [67]. In addition, the incorporation of low nKer NPs concentration (0.5 and 1.0 wt%) greatly enhanced hydrogel hydrophilicity [IIa (θ 32.300), IIb (θ 32.030)]; respectively compared to high nKer NPs concentration (1.5 wt%) [IIc (θ 43.560)], (p ≤ 0.05) (Fig. 9). Apparently, it might be accredited to increase in surface free energy that might have resulted from the poor dispersion of large content of nKer NPS into alginate matrix. Moreover, by the addition of more nKer NPs (nanofiller), the polymer surface becomes more non-polar (hydrophobic) [68].

Current results were in agreement with Sharma et al. [38] who constructed an injectable keratin hydrogel that showed great hydrophilic behavior with contact angle of 35.520 ±7.187 and potentiated pulp regeneration in rat teeth. Furthermore, Sun et al. [67], fabricated a polycaprolactone (PCL) reinforced keratin / bioactive glass composite scaffold which manifested an efficient hydrophilicity (θ 21.4 ± 1.140) that can strongly enhance cell attachment and growth [67]. Evidently, we managed to accomplish surface wettability which was as good as necessary for surface remodeling in directing cells and tissue behavior.

Biocompatibility is a necessary requisite in regenerative field and numerous researches used biocompatible biomaterials to fabricated injectable hydrogels [69]. According to the MTT cell viability test outcomes of the present study (Fig. 10), the newly constructed alginate/nKer NPs (nKer NPs) loaded hydrogel demonstrated to be highly biocompatible. Although raising hydrogel and the concentration of nKer NPs extracts following 24 and 48 h displayed reduction in the cell viability percentage of human gingival fibroblasts, it still exceeds the allowed percentage.

(> 70%) conforming to (ISO 10993-5(E)). Current findings were consistent with Bhat et al. [13] who showed excellent biocompatibility results of keratin nanoparticles.

Conclusions

The integral role of keratin-rich biological materials, in tissue regeneration, was ascertained by the favorable in vitro behavior of the innovative injectable chicken feather regenerated keratin loaded alginate hydrogel. Alginate/nKer NPs (1.0 wt%) (IIb) was non-harmful to gingival fibroblasts (> 70%) pursuant to (ISO 10993-5(E)). Moreover, nKer NPs addition obviously enhanced porosity, pore size width, thermal and mechanical stability and hydrophilicity of the composite hydrogel relative to unloaded alginate hydrogel. Further increase in nKer NPs content decreased pore size diameter, mechanical stability and hydrophilicity. However, this increase slightly elevated the hydrogel viscosity, but it is still appropriate to facilitate hydrogel injection and delivery in minimally invasive clinical settings. The ongoing quest for a novel biomaterial in regenerative pulpotomy with minimal invasiveness is progressing rapidly. Additional research on animals is essential as a suitable stepping phase before clinical trials for evaluating potential of newly developed scaffold [alginate/nKer NPs (1.0 wt%)] for dentin regeneration. Since their results might be implemented with great consistency in clinical application.

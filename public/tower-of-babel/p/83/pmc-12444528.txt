Identification of Tripeptide Inhibitors Targeting MAPK3 in Leishmania martiniquensis and Leishmania orientalis Using Molecular Modeling, Virtual Screening, Molecular Dynamics, and In Vitro Approach
Authors: Duangnapa Kiriwan, Tatiana F. Vieira, Nutnaree Kumsiri, Sergio F. Sousa, Kiattawee Choowongkomon
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
MAPK3 has been identified as a potential drug target due to its role in the Leishmania life cycle. This study focused on two species prevalent in Thailand: Leishmania martiniquensis and Leishmania orientalis. Using an in silico strategies, we screened an in-house library of 8000 tripeptides to identify potential MAPK3 inhibitors. A multi-level computational workflow was employed, including homology modeling, molecular docking study, and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. Promising candidates were further validated by an in vitro kinase assay. The three-dimensional (3D) structures of MAPK3 from L.martiniquensis and L. orientalis were constructed by using the Swiss Model. Structure-based virtual screening identified nine promising tripeptides, with WSY demonstrated strong binding affinity, MD simulation and MM-GBSA binding free energy calculation confirmed its stable interaction with key regions in the MAPK3 active site, including the DFG motif, hinge region, αC-helix through hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic interaction, and π–π stacking. In vitro kinase assays demonstrated that the peptide WSY was significantly more effective at inhibiting MAPK3 than WHW, with IC50 values of 0.9 ± 11.3 and 26.9 ± 6.7 μM, respectively. These findings suggest that WSY is a promising anti-leishmanial drug candidate targeting LmqMAPK3 and LoMAPK3. Our hybrid computational-experimental approach provides a solid framework for identification of novel inhibitors and can be extended to other compound libraries to help the discovery of new anti-leishmania therapies.

Introduction

Leishmaniasis is a vector-borne disease transmitted by parasites of the genus Leishmania. , It is endemic in many areas, especially in tropical regions and around the Mediterranean. The disease is present in Asia, Africa, Europe, North America, and South America. According to data from the World Health Organization (WHO) in 2018, leishmania affects between 700,000 and 1,000,000 people annually and causes 20,000 to 30,000 deaths per year (Global Health Observatory data from WHO, 2018). Additionally, this disease is a major public health concern, especially in developing countries. It primarily affected impoverished populations, who lack access to resources and medical equipment. Moreover, tourists visiting tropical regions are also at risk. In recent years, leishmaniasis has become a significant health issue in Thailand. It has been reported in the northern, central, and southern parts of the country. Two main species are responsible for this disease in Thailand are Leishmaniasis martiniquensis and Leishmaniasis orientalis.

Female sand flies, mainly from the genera Phlebotomus, are the main vectors of leishmaniasis. They transmit Leishmania parasites to mammals through their bites, Leishmania infections present in six clinical forms, depending on the location of the parasite within the infected tissues. The incubation period typically ranges from 3 to 8 months but can extend to several years. , The three main forms of the disease are (I) Visceral leishmaniasis (VL), also known as kala-azar, which is fatal in nearly 95% of patients if left untreated; (II) Cutaneous leishmaniasis (CL), which is the most common form, which causes skin lesions; and (III) Mucocutaneous leishmaniasis. In Thailand, two causative species have been identified in VL cases: L. martiniquensis, found in the southern province and L. orientalis, reported in an HIV infected patient in Trang province. ,

Mitogen-activated protein (MAP) kinase is a type of Ser/Thr protein kinase that plays a central role in key cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and stress regulation. Leishmania species, MAPK3 is crucial for the differentiation of promastigote into amastigote, regulates the flagella length, and overall disease transmission. −

Several drugs are currently available for the treatment of leishmaniasis, including amphotericin B deoxycholate, lipid formulations of amphotericin B, miltefosine, paromomycin, pentamidine, and sodium stibogluconate. , However, these treatments are associated with several different problems, such as toxicity from prolonged use, high costs, and the emergence of drug resistance. , In Thailand, amphotericin B remains the only effective treatment option. Nevertheless, some patients showed symptoms of leishmaniasis even after receiving this medicine. , To address these limitations, the discovery of new alternative molecules capable of inhibiting Leishmania is needed. In this work, we developed an in silico protocol optimized to the identify potent molecular inhibitors targeting Leishmania donovani MAPK3 (LdMAPK3), Leishmania martiniquensis MAPK3 (LmqMAPK3), and Leishmania orientalis MAPK3 (LoMAPK3).

The protocol was applied to a database of 8,000 tripeptides to identify potential binders, providing novel candidates for drug development. Overall, the approach integrates several computational techniques, including homology modeling, virtual screening, molecular docking, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, and free energy calculations. Molecular docking is a computational method used to predict the most favorable binding orientation of a ligand to its target protein. , Structure-based virtual screening allows for the efficient scanning of large molecular databases to identify novel molecules with promising binding affinity. Molecular dynamics simulation and binding free energy estimations further characterize and validate target-ligand interactions emerging from the docking stage. − This study employed a combined in silico and in vitro approach to identify a novel tripeptide inhibitor against Leishmaniasis MAPK3. Overall, the application of this pipeline enables the identification of promising tripeptide candidates and helps us understand the protein–ligand modes of interaction. Results and Discussions

Sequence Alignment and Homology Modeling

Sequence alignments of LdMAPK3, LmqMAPK3, and LoMAPK3 are presented in Figure S1 (alignment done using the Clustal Omega tool of the EMBL-EBI platform). Multiple sequence analyses revealed that the template amino acid sequence shares 96.4% identity with LmqMAPK3 and 95.5% with LoMAPK3. The MAPK3 domain is highly conserved across all Leishmania species. L. donovani was selected as a template for the current three-dimensional structure modeling due to its high crystal structure resolution and high percent identity homology to the target kinase protein. As the three-dimensional (3D) structures of Leishmania MAPK3 proteins are not yet available in the Protein Data Bank (PDB), homology modeling was used to predict their structures. SWISS-MODEL was employed to construct the MAPK3 structure from L. martiniquensis and L. orientalis. The crystal structure of L. donovani MAPK3 in complex with NVP-BBT (resolution of 2.7 Å) served as the template. The modeled structures are presented in Figure . The homology models of LmqMAPK3 and LoMAPK3 yielded QMEAN scores of 0.9 ± 0.1. Their GMQE scores of 0.98 and 0.97, respectively, indicate good model quality and reliability. However, some regions (resid 170 to 190) were poorly modeled, as shown in the local quality plot (Figure S4).

Predicted structural conformation of mitogen-activated protein kinase 3 (MAPK3) from L. orientalis (blue) and L. martiniquensis (red).

Structure validation was conducted to assess the stereochemical quality of MAPK3 models. Several tools used included Ramachandran plot, PROCHECK, Verify-3D, PROVE, and ERRAR. PROCHECK examined overall stereochemical parameters, while the Ramachandran plot displayed the phi-psi torsion angles for all the residues. Different regions of the plot are indicated by distinct colors or shading (Figures S1–S2 ).

The Ramachandran plot for LmqMAPK3 revealed that 93.0% of amino acid residues were in favored regions, 7.0% in allowed regions, and 0.0% in the outlier region. The model received a VERIFY-3D score of 88.0, a PROVE score of 4.4, and an ERRAT score of 95.2, indicating high structural quality. Similarly, the LoMAPK3 model showed 92.7% of residues in the favored region, 7.3% in the allowed region, and none in the outlier region. The VERIFY-3D, PROVE, and ERRAT scores were 88.0, 4.3, and 95.5, respectively, supporting the reliability of the model. Together, these validation results confirm that the predicted models are of high quality and suitable for further computational analyses.

Clustering Analysis

The structures of the models were refined using 200 ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, as described in the Materials and Methods section. Clustering analysis was performed to identify the dominant conformations of each Leishmania MAPK3, based on 1500 snapshots extracted from the last 150 ns of each trajectory. Table summarizes the cluster analysis in the MD simulations performed for both species in the absence and presence of a reference ligand. Comparison of ligand-bound and unbound simulations revealed conformational changes in key residues around the binding pocket upon ligand binding. In the ligand-bound form, side chain orientations of critical residues adjust to adjust the ligand. In contrast, the bound form exhibited greater side chain flexibility. Side chains often extended into the binding pocket, which could cause steric clashes with putative ligands. Such conformations are therefore less suitable for structure-based virtual screening. This was particularly evident for residues Met115, Val117, Asp118, and Arg156, which showed marked differences inside chain positioning between clusters.

Protein Ligand Docking Protocol Optimization

To optimize and validate the protein–ligand docking protocol for identifying high-affinity ligands, the dominant ligand-bound structures from clusters 0 and 1 of each MAPK3 species were selected. Docking was performed using GOLD software. Several parameters were tested. These included different scoring functions (ASP, Chemscore, ChemPLP, and Goldscore). The number of genetic algorithm (GA) runs was also varied from 10 to 50. In all cases, the docking site was centered on the reference ligand (NVT-BBT) with a 20 Å search radius. These settings aimed to reproduce crystallographic binding orientations and determine whether the scoring function could reliably identify the optimal poses.

Docking accuracy was evaluated by RMSD between docked and cocrystallized ligand poses. An RMSD value below 2.0 Å indicated high-quality results.

Table shows that 50 GA runs using ChemPLP and Goldscore gave the best results, both with RMSD values below 2.0 Å. However, Goldscore required approximately four times more computational time. Given ChemPLP scoring function comparable accuracy, lower computational cost, and consistence performance in pose prediction and RMSD values, it was selected for subsequent the virtual screening steps.

Structure-Based Virtual Screening of a Tripeptide Library

An in-house database of 8,000 tripeptides was used for structure-based virtual screening. The screening targeted the dominant MAPK3 structures of L. donovani, L. martiniquensis, and L. orientalis. Optimized parameters established in the previous step were applied for the docking. The results are presented in Figure A, which shows the average ChemPLP score and the molecular weight of each tripeptide. The score of the reference inhibitor NVT-BBT is colored red for comparison. Figure B shows the ChemPLP scores normalized by the number of non-hydrogen atoms.

Binding score analysis using 50 GA runs, visualized in scatter plots generated with Data Warrior v5.0.0. (A) Average ChemPLP score plotted against molecular weight. (B) Average ChemPLP score normalized by the number of heavy atoms, plotted against molecular weight.

Score normalization was performed to prevent bias toward larger molecules, which tend to result in higher docking scores due to nonspecific surface interactions. By dividing the ChemPLP score by the number of heavy atoms, ligand efficiency was calculated providing a metric that reflects true binding affinity.

A total of 50 peptides were chosen from the virtual screening stage: 25 based on the highest non-normalized ChemPLP score and 25 based on the normalized scores (shown in blue). This dual selection strategy ensured the identification of peptides with both high overall target affinity and high per-atom binding efficiency.

Molecular Dynamics Simulations of MAPK3 with Candidate Tripeptides

To investigate the potential ability of the dominant Leishmania MAPK3 structure with the 10 selected tripeptides, two replicas of 300 ns molecular dynamics simulations were performed for each complex. Root mean square deviation (RMSD) analysis was determined to evaluate stability and convergence of the simulation. As shown in Figure S4, RMSD values for MAPK3 tripeptide complexes converged after the first 50 ns, with average value around ≈0.2 Å, except for a few cases where average RMSD was approximately 0.8 Å. These results suggest that all of the complexes reached equilibrium throughout the 300 ns simulation.

To further analyze the binding affinity, we analyzed the overall binding free energy. This was related to the solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) percentage of the buried ligand. We also calculated the average number of ligand-protein hydrogen bonds. These results are summarized in Table S2. Our analysis revealed that tripeptides GGA, GGC, SGG, WEY, WHW, WKY, and WSY remained stably bound during the simulations. Strong binders typically exhibited a higher percentage of buried SASA and more hydrogen bonds than did the reference ligand. In contrast, the GGS-LdMAPK3 and GGG-LmqMAPK3 complexes buried less than 50% of their SASA, which corresponded to ligand displacement during the simulations, reduced hydrogen bonding, and lower per-residue binding contributions.

Figure S6 presents SASA profiles calculated after 50 ns of simulation. In LdMAPK3 complexes, GGS exhibited the highest SASA and the most fluctuating SASA (∼450 Å2), indicating weaker and less stable binding due to greater solvent exposure. In contrast, ligands such as GGA, GGC, GGG, and SGG maintained consistently low SASA values (<100 Å2), indicating tight binding and stable burial within the pocket. Ligands WEY, WHW, WKY, and WSY displayed moderately stable SASA profiles, while the reference ligand exhibited an intermediate SASA (200 Å2). For the LmqMAPK3 system, GGG had the highest SASA (∼200 Å2) increased solvent exposure relative to other ligands. In the LoMAPK3 complexes, all ligands showed decreased SASA values upon binding, consistent with ligand-induced conformational changes and reduced solvent accessibility.

Binding free energies (ΔG bind) were calculated by using the MM/GBSA approach from MD trajectories, with decomposition analyses performed for each tripeptide-MAPK3 complex. The nine selected tripeptides exhibited less favorable binding free energies than the reference ligand

NVT-BBT, which had an ΔG bind of approximately −63.1 kcal/mol. Notably, the four tryptophan-containing peptides (WEY, WKY, WSY, and WHW) showed the highest binding energies across all MAPK3 targets (Table ), identifying them as potent inhibitors of LmqMAPK3 and LoMAPK3. Residue contribution analysis revealed four key amino acids (Ser56, Glu76, Ile174, and Asp175) that significantly enhance the ligand affinity across Leishmania species (see Table S2 for details).

To gain deeper insight into tripeptide behavior within the MAPK3 binding site, molecular interactions were analyzed using the last frame of the MD simulations. Two top-performing tripeptides, WHW and WSY, were evaluated for their binding interactions across three MAPK3 species. WHW formed hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, and π–π stacking interactions that contributed to complex stability. In L. orientalis, the main chain of WHW interacted with Glu76 and His2 from a hydrogen bond with Asp175. Enzymatic Assessment of MAPK3 Inhibition

The inhibitory potential of selected tripeptides i.e., WSY, WHW, WKY, and WEY and a negative control peptide (GGS) against MAPK3 was evaluated. All of the peptides were initially screened at 5 μM. WSY and WHW exhibited noticeable inhibition, while WKY, WEY, and GGS exhibited no measurable effect on enzyme activity (Figure A). Further dose–response assessments at 3.3 and 1.1 μM confirmed the potency of WSY. At 3.3 μM, WSY demonstrated strong MAPK3 inhibition, with an average of 90.0 ± 9.3%. In contrast, WHW showed a much lower inhibition of 28.0 ± 14.5%. At the lower concentration of 1.1 μM, both peptides retained inhibitory activity, though reduced (WSY: 68.4 ± 22.2%; WHW at 7.8 ± 11.7%) (Figure B). The inhibitory potential of both tripeptides was further validated by half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) analysis. As shown in Figure C,D, WSY demonstrated stronger inhibition than WHW, reflected by a lower IC50 value (WSY: 0.9 ± 11.3 μM; WHW: 26.9 ± 6.7 μM).

Conclusions

In this study, we optimized and applied an in silico protocol to identify potent tripeptide inhibitors targeting MAPK3 from Leishmania species. Molecular modeling and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were integrated to computationally validate the structures of LmqMAPK3 and LoMAPK3. Clustering analysis of MD trajectories presents the selection of representative MAPK3 conformations for structure-based virtual screening of an in-house library of 8000 tripeptides. Top candidates from the virtual screening were further evaluated through MD simulations and binding free energy calculations to assess complex stability and validate docking predictions. Among these, peptides WHW, WEY, WSY, and WKY formed stable interactions with key functional regions of MAPK3, including αC-helix, catalytic triad (DFG motif), gatekeeper site, hinge region, and adenine pocket, and consistently engaged critical residues such as Glu76, Ile174, Asp175, Phe176, and Gly177. Binding free energy (MM-GBSA), solvent-accessible area (SASA) analysis, and in vitro kinase assays confirmed that WSY is a particularly strong MAPK3 inhibitor. WSY demonstrates the highest inhibitory activity in a concentration-dependent manner, outperforming WHW and other candidates.

Overall, this study highlights a promising set of tripeptides, particularly WSY as selective inhibitors of Lm qMAPK3 and LoMAPK3. The in silico protocol presented here provides a robust framework for future development and optimization of antileishmania therapeutics. Future experimental validation in cellular or in vivo models is recommended to confirm these findings.

Materials and Methods

Structure Preparation

Homology Modeling

To identify the homologous structure of the Leishmania mitogen-activated protein kinase 3 (MAPK3) with the highest sequence identity and lowest E-value, the BLASTP tool was used. The identified structure served as a template for modeling MAPK3 from L. martiniquensis and L. orientalis. Amino acid sequences of L. orientalis (KAG5485638.1) and L. martiniquensis (KAG5484968.1) were retrieved from the NCBI database (http://www.ncbi.nlm.gov), containing 388 and 387 amino acids, respectively. Sequence alignment was done using Clustal Omega. Three-dimensional models of the MAPK3 proteins were generated using the SWISS-Model server (http://swissmodel.expasy.org/). Model reliability was evaluated by using two metrics: Energy Analysis (QMEAN) and Global Model Quality Estimation (GMQE) scores. The QMEAN score evaluates the degree of nativeness of the predicted structure, with scores below −4 indicating low-quality models. The GMQE score ranges from 0 to 1, with higher values signifying greater reliability based on sequence alignment and template quality. To validate the stereochemical quality of the predicted structures, several tools were utilized: the Ramachandran plot, PROCHECK, Verify-3D, PROVE, and ERRART. These evaluations confirmed the structure reliability and the quality of the modeled MAPK3 proteins.

Refinement of Model Structure

The model structures were refined by molecular dynamics simulations using AMBER software. The ligand structure (NVP-BBT) was optimized at the HF/6–31G­(d) level of theory using Gaussian09 software. , Protein and ligand parameters were generated using the AMBER 20 package, the AMBER14SB and generalized AMBER force field (GAFF) were employed, respectively. Protonation states of amino acid residues were assigned using the H++ Web server. The systems were solvated in a TIP3P water model with a minimal distance of 12 Å between the protein surface and the edge of the box; neutralization was achieved by adding Na+ or CI– ions. Energy minimization was performed in two methods: 5,000 steps of the steepest descent method, followed by 5,000 steps using the conjugate gradient method. Simulations were conducted at 300 K, which aligns with the physiological temperature range of Leishmania in its promastigote stage (∼25–27 °C), during which MAPK3 plays a role in flagellar biogenesis. − The systems were gradually heated from 0 to 300 K over 25,000 steps (50 ps), followed by a 50 ps equilibrium at 300 K under constant pressure to stabilize system density. Production MD simulations were then carried out for 200 ns.

Protein Ligand Docking Protocol Validation

Redocking was employed to assess and optimize the docking protocol. In this approach, a reference ligand is removed from the protein–ligand complex and a docking protocol is then used to predict its correct pose within the binding pocket. The predicted pose is then compared to the crystallographic pose to evaluate the protocol accuracy.

In this work, protein–ligand docking was employed using Gold CCDC software version 2022.1.0, applying 4 independent scoring functions: ASP, ChemPLP, Goldscore, and Chemscore. Key docking parameters, including the definition of the binding site center and the number of independent genetic algorithm (GA) runs, were adjusted during each redocking experiment. To evaluate the quality of the docking protocol, DockRMSD and the crystallographic ligand root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) were used as indicators of performance. Optimized docking settings that produced RMSD values ≤2 Å for each scoring function were selected for subsequent virtual.

Virtual Screening with Tripeptides

A library of 8,000 tripeptide structures was generated in-house by randomly combining the 20 natural amino acids at each position, using the pmx tool and a Python script developed by Bundit Boonyarit and colleagues. Hydrogen atoms were assigned to each tripeptide, and the protonation states of ionizable groups at pH 7.0 were predicted using OpenBabel version 3.1.0. Molecular properties such as molecular weight, atom counts, hydrogen bond donors, and acceptors were calculated using DataWarrior version 5.0.0. Docking was then performed using the optimized parameters identified in the previous step. All results data were normalized and evaluated in Microsoft Excel.

Molecular Docking of Candidate Tripeptides

The docking protocol established in the previous step was applied to the top 50 tripeptides identified from the virtual screening using the same optimized parameters. To improve conformational sampling and docking accuracy. The number of genetic algorithm (GA) runs increased to 500. Based on docking scores and docking poses, the top 10 hits were selected for further evaluation at the atomic level using molecular dynamics (MD) simulations.

Molecular Dynamic Simulation with Candidate Tripeptide

These 10 complexes were subjected to molecular dynamics (MD) simulations. The MD protocol was applied as previously described for the protein ligand simulation steps. Each complex was simulated for a total of 300 ns to assess structural stability and binding interaction over time.

Trajectory Analysis of the MD Simulations

Trajectory analysis was performed using CPPTRAJ software in AmberTool version 2020 to assess the dynamic stability of the MAPK3-tripeptide complexes. The root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of protein backbone atoms was calculated relative to the initial reference structure to monitor structural stability during the simulations. To investigate the behavior of peptides within the MAPK3 binding pocket, average linkage clustering was applied to the trajectory frames from the last 150 ns of each simulation, resulting in 1250 frames per complex, and clustering was performed based on the coordinate RMSD of residues surrounding the binding site.

Further analysis of complex stability included calculations of the root-mean-square deviation (RMSD), the number of hydrogen bonds, and per-residue binding free energy contributions. Solvent accessible surface area (SASA) values were also obtained using the cpptraj module to monitor changes in the ligand exposure over time. Binding free energy calculations were carried out using the MMPBSA.py script included with AmberTools. MM-GBSA analyses were performed using the Igb2 implicit solvent model and were based on the last 250 ns of each simulation. Structures were sampled at 200 ps intervals, resulting in 1250 frames per complex for binding free energy calculations.

In Vitro MAPK3 Kinase Assay by ADP-Glo Kinase Assay

MAPK3 activity was examined by measuring ADP production using the ADP-Glo Kinase Assay as previously reported. The assay was performed in 384-well white plates. The kinase was initiated by mixing 10 μM Master Mix containing 5 nM Myelin basic protein (MBP) substrate and 20 μM ATP in 1× kinase buffer (40 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.5, 20 mM MgCl2, 0.1 mg/mL bovine serum albumin) with 5 μL of 1 nM MAPK3, 2 μM peptide solution or 100% DMSO control, and 8 μL of 1× kinase buffer. The mixture was incubated at room temperature for 1 h. ATP depletion was then achieved by adding 5 μL of ADP-Glo Reagent, followed by a 40 min incubation at room temperature. Next, 10 μL of the Kinase Detection Reagent was added to convert ADP to ATP with a further 30 min incubation at room temperature. Luminescence was measured using a Synergy HTX Multi-Mode Reader (BioTek, U.K.).

Peptides were dissolved in 100% DMSO. For screening, peptides were tested at 5 μM and WSY and WHW were further assessed at 3.33 μM, 1.11 μM. IC50 analyses for WSY and WHW were performed over a concentration range 0.2 to 200 μM via 2-fold serial dilutions in 100% DMSO. IC50 curves were generated using GraphPad Prism 8 with a dose–response inhibition model. A positive control using a known MAPK3 inhibitor was not included due to the limited availability of the well-characterized MAPK3 inhibitor specific to Leishmania species.

Protocol to generate immunocompetent human heart-macrophage assembloids for the study of immune-cardiac interactions
Authors: Colin O’Hern, Mia Dionise, Sammantha Caywood, Merlinda-Loraine Sewavi, Hussain Basrai, Priyadharshni Muniyandi, Brett Volmert, Aleksandra Kostina, Amanda Huang, Nagib Chalfoun, Aitor Aguirre
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryHere, we present a protocol to generate fully human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC)-derived human heart-macrophage assembloids. We describe steps for differentiating hPSCs into monocytes, generating cardiac organoids, integrating CD14+ monocytes to become cardiac tissue-resident macrophages, and maturing the assembloids with metabolic cues. Furthermore, we provide a use-case example of disease modeling for the study of atrial arrhythmias. The system provides a reproducible, scalable, and efficient platform for studying immune-cardiac interactions, inflammation, and arrhythmogenesis.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to O’Hern et al.1Subject areas: Developmental biology, Stem Cells, Organoids, Tissue Engineering, Biotechnology and bioengineering

Before you begin

This protocol accompanies the published manuscript,1 including details from previously published protocols2,3,4 and adapted human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC) derived-monocyte protocols,5,6,7,8 describing how fully integrated hPSC-derived macrophages (MPs), which model yolk sac-derived tissue-resident macrophages (TRMPs), shape human heart-macrophage assembloid (hHMA) development and function under both steady-state conditions and within an inflammatory arrhythmia model. It provides a detailed framework for generating hPSC-derived hHMAs that contain all major cardiac cell types together with TRMPs derived from the same hPSC line, and for modeling atrial arrhythmias induced by chronic inflammatory stimulation that activates the nucleotide-binding domain, leucine-rich repeat, and pyrin domain-containing protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome specifically in atrial cardiomyocytes. Together, these methods establish a robust platform for investigating the roles of TRMPs in human cardiac physiology.

Because the hHMA recapitulates a multicellular, developmentally aligned cardiac niche, it is well suited for studying the intra- and intercellular mechanisms by which inflammation disrupts electrophysiology and promotes arrhythmogenesis. The system is compatible with drug screening applications due to its scalability and high-throughput capacity using standardized equipment, such as 96-well plates, and it is a feasible platform for the testing of candidate compounds targeting the heart, or for cardiotoxicity testing. The model also readily accommodates genetic perturbation studies using knockdown or knockout hPSC lines, enabling mechanistic dissection of immune–cardiac pathways in a human-relevant context.

This protocol has been validated in both human embryonic stem cells (hESCs) and induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSC), with no detectable differences in hHMA phenotype across lines, indicating broad applicability to diverse hPSC sources. The experimental steps described here are derived from the original manuscript,1 and users should refer to that publication for additional details regarding supplemental methods. The four major steps of the protocol are Differentiation of monocytes, Differentiation of human heart organoids, CD14+ MACS and addition of CD14+ monocytes to heart organoid, and Generation of atrial arrhythmias in hHMAs using inflammatory stimuli (Figure 1).

Innovation

This protocol represents an advance in hPSC-derived cardiac modeling by enabling the generation of partially immunocompetent hPSC-derived hHMAs. Outside of a recent report,9 prior 2D cardiomyocyte cultures,10,11,12 engineered heart tissues,13 and human heart organoid (hHO) systems2,4,14,15,16,17,18 either lacked TRMPs altogether or relied on macrophages differentiated from primary monocytes, which are phenotypically and developmentally distinct from yolk-sac–derived TRMPs.19,20 By deriving all the cells of the hHMAs from the same hPSC line, this system uniquely enables controlled, lineage-matched investigations of intracellular and intercellular crosstalk within human cardiac tissue. Furthermore, the 3D architecture and self-organized electrophysiological conduction tracts formed during hHMA differentiation recapitulate key features of the developing human heart more faithfully than 2D cardiomyocyte cultures or engineered heart tissues. This positions the model as the most advanced small-scale in vitro human heart platform currently available.

Importantly, this is the first protocol to establish a 3D human cardiac atrial arrhythmia model driven by chronic inflammatory stimulation, extending insights from prior 2D studies21 and complementing analogous in vivo animal experiments.22 The induced arrhythmia phenotype emerges in the absence of pathogenic triggers used in previous in vitro heart models,23,24,25,26 enabling direct interrogation of inflammation-specific mechanisms underlying cardiac electrophysiological dysfunction. Given that inflammatory disease processes are associated with atrial arrhythmias,27 this platform provides an unprecedented opportunity to dissect how human cardiac cells respond to defined immune stimuli. Finally, this protocol introduces a method to activate the NLRP3 inflammasome within atrial cardiomyocytes in a hHMA, advancing earlier work28 and opening new avenues for mechanistic and therapeutic discovery.

Step-by-step method details

Differentiation of monocytes

Timing: 18–42 days

In this step, we follow the previously published protocol8 with minor modifications to increase monocyte yields. Specifically, we double the number of EBs per well from 5 to 10 and scale-up all reagent volumes by 50% (Figure 2). This monocyte differentiation workflow enables continuous monocyte collection every 4 days, routinely producing millions of CD14+ monocytes per harvest. Because monocyte generation requires approximately 18 days, with more robust yields by day 26, monocyte differentiation must be initiated before starting Differentiation of human heart organoids.

Note: Ensure that the hPSC line has been cultured for at least one additional passage after thawing before beginning this protocol. The procedure below describes monocyte differentiation from hPSCs maintained in E8 Flex medium; if your hPSCs are routinely cultured in another medium (e.g., mTeSR), substitute that medium wherever E8 flex medium is specified. For optimal compatibility, the hPSCs used to generate monocytes, hHOs, and subsequently hHMAs should be isogenic and derived from the same parental line.

Note: All procedures described in the following protocols are performed in BSCs under sterile working conditions, while wearing personal protective equipment in accordance with biosafety level 2 (BSL-2) regulations.

Expected outcomes

Differentiation of monocytes

Following the optimized monocyte differentiation protocol, users should expect trace numbers of floating cells by day 18 when examined under a standard benchtop light microscope (Figure 2C). By day 22, the number of non-adherent monocytes should increase substantially, with robust and sustained production by day 26 and in subsequent harvests. Typical yields are 60.40% ± 16.07% CD14+ cells relative to the total harvest measured in Step 39 (Figure 2D). Across experiments, we obtained 2.39 ± 1.82 million CD14+ cells per 6-well plate seeded with 10 EBs per well (Figure 2E). One should expect minimal batch-to-batch variability, and yields should be consistently strong by day 26. A lack of floating single cells by day 26 often indicates issues with hPSC quality prior to EB formation, suboptimal handling, or compromised reagent quality.

Differentiation of human heart organoids

Outcomes are consistent with the previously published hHO protocols.2,3 Users should observe subtle spontaneous contractions by day 20 and robust, synchronous beating by day 25, with well-formed chamber-like structures and expected cardiac lineage marker expression. Please refer to the following protocol for troubleshooting this step.3

CD14+ monocyte isolation and generation of human heart macrophage assembloids

When 20,000 monocytes are added on days 22, 26, and 30 (60,000 total), day 34 hHMAs typically contain 1.65% ± 0.62% CD45+ MPs. Immunofluorescent confocal z-stack imaging should reveal TRMP integration throughout the assembloid (refer to O’Hern et al.1 for immunostaining and imaging protocols). Quantification following dissociation and flow cytometry reliably confirms the presence of TRMPs (CD45+CCR2-CD14+CD163+ cells). By day 34, hHMAs should appear similar in size and morphology to hHOs under brightfield microscopy (see Figure 3A). Users should employ immunofluorescence microscopy or flow cytometry to validate macrophage integration. Steps for troubleshooting failed/insufficient integration are outlined below.

Generation of atrial arrhythmias in hHMAs using inflammatory stimuli

In PFM, atrial cardiomyocytes consistently beat faster than in CTRL hHMAs (Figure 4). Across preparations, mean beat rates are 212.71 ± 83.66 bpm in PFM versus 86.37 ± 58.54 bpm in CTRL (Figure 4A). Users should also expect a higher incidence of spontaneous irregular rhythms and waveforms, with 54.31% ± 13.44% of PFM hHMAs displaying spontaneous arrhythmic waveforms compared to 20.09% ± 13.08% in CTRL hHMAs (Figure 6B). High-magnification voltage-sensitive dye (like FluoVolt) live-cell imaging should visibly confirm these phenotypes.

Limitations

The success of this protocol depends strongly on the quality and handling of the starting hPSCs. Poor hPSC quality or early EB formation defects can reduce monocyte yield and, in severe cases, eliminate the appearance of floating CD14+ cells by day 26. hPSC quality also affects downstream hHO differentiation, and suboptimal starting cultures may impair hHO morphology, cardiac differentiation specification, or electrophysiological maturation. Although monocyte and hHO differentiation is generally robust, efficiency remains sensitive to reagent quality and timing. Culture conditions must remain stable; fluctuations in incubator temperature, humidity, or CO2 concentration can impair both monocyte production and hHO development. During hHMA assembly, variability in monocyte viability, inaccurate cell counting, or insufficient gentle pipetting during seeding can lead to low or uneven macrophage integration. Finally, arrhythmia induction is sensitive to compound stability and correct reagent reconstitution, and deviations can weaken or obscure electrophysiological phenotypes. Reproducibility requires strict quality control of hPSC lines, reagents, and environmental parameters across all stages of the protocol.

Primary Cardiac and Renal Amyloidosis with Nephrotic Syndrome in a Patient with a Remote History of Partial Nephrectomy for Renal Cell Carcinoma

Case Presentation

A 73-year-old male with ulcerative colitis on mesalamine, anemia of chronic disease, type 2 diabetes, RCC post right partial nephrectomy (10 years prior), and prostate cancer post-prostatectomy (4 years prior) presented with fatigue and bilateral leg swelling over 3–4 weeks. He had no prior chronic kidney disease.

Laboratory evaluation revealed acute kidney injury with nephrotic-range proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, and elevated cardiac biomarkers. Baseline and admission values included serum creatinine 1.0 mg/dL rising to 5.4 mg/dL (reference 0.6–1.1), total protein 6.5 g/dL decreasing to 3.9 g/dL (reference 5.7–8.2), albumin 4.0 g/dL decreasing to 2.5 g/dL (reference 3.5–4.8), high-sensitivity troponin 14 ng/L increasing to 92 ng/L (reference 0–53), NT-proBNP 120 pg/mL rising to 26,673 pg/mL (reference 0–124), and proteinuria 14,612 mg/24 h (reference <150). Cardiac biomarkers were interpreted in the context of renal impairment and cardiac amyloidosis; no type 1 myocardial infarction was diagnosed, and type 2 MI was considered unlikely.

Serum and urine protein electrophoresis were ordered due to nephrotic-range proteinuria and acute kidney injury. SPEP/UPEP revealed a 2.4 g/dL monoclonal IgA kappa protein, with urine studies confirming IgA kappa light chains (848 mg/day) and Bence Jones protein (268 mg/day), consistent with renal AL amyloidosis.

Renal ultrasound revealed mild hydronephrosis and chronic parenchymal changes in the right kidney, corresponding to the site of prior partial nephrectomy. Renal biopsy confirmed AL amyloidosis with Congo Red-positive deposits, kappa light chain immunofluorescence, and mass spectrometry confirmation [Figure 1]. Bone marrow biopsy demonstrated 10% clonal plasma cells without myeloma-defining events, and skeletal survey and PET-CT were negative for lesions, thus excluding multiple myeloma. Cardiac evaluation showed a hyperdynamic left ventricle (ejection fraction 70%) with mild–moderate hypertrophy, severe left atrial dilation, and moderate right atrial dilation. Endomyocardial biopsy confirmed cardiac amyloidosis.

The patient received Dara-CyBorD chemotherapy and hemodialysis. Dara-CyBorD achieves hematologic response in 70–80% of patients, complete response in approximately 40%, renal response in 30–50%, and cardiac response in 25–40% over 6–12 months. In this patient, advanced organ involvement and treatment interruptions limited recovery.

Discussion

AL amyloidosis results from misfolded immunoglobulin light chain deposition, leading to multiorgan dysfunction.1,2 Renal involvement typically presents with nephrotic-range proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, and edema, while cardiac involvement predicts prognosis.1,3,4

RCC and AL amyloidosis are independent diseases, although malignancy-associated amyloidosis has been described more commonly with AA amyloidosis rather than AL amyloidosis.5,6,7. RCC originates from renal epithelium, whereas AL amyloidosis arises from clonal plasma cells.5,6,7 Coexistence in this patient is likely coincidental.8 Post-partial nephrectomy proteinuria is usually mild, so nephrotic-range proteinuria warrants systemic evaluation rather than attribution solely to nephron loss.2,7,9

Diagnosis requires a combination of SPEP/UPEP, immunofixation, renal biopsy, and bone marrow evaluation to exclude multiple myeloma.10,11 Bone marrow biopsy demonstrated 10% clonal plasma cells without myeloma-defining events (CRAB criteria), and imaging was negative for osteolytic lesions, meeting IMWG (International Myeloma Working Group) criteria for exclusion of multiple myeloma.1,2,12 Advanced renal and cardiac involvement may limit organ response despite hematologic response to therapy.1,3,4

Dara-CyBorD therapy is associated with hematologic response in 70–80% of patients, complete response in approximately 40%, renal response in 30–50%, and cardiac response in 25–40% over 6–12 months.3,13 In this case, advanced multi-organ involvement and chemotherapy interruptions contributed to limited clinical improvement,1,3,4 highlighting the challenges of managing AL amyloidosis in patients with comorbidities and prior malignancy.

Conclusion

AL amyloidosis can occur in patients with a remote history of RCC, years after partial nephrectomy. Clinicians should maintain high suspicion in patients presenting with nephrotic-range proteinuria and cardiac involvement. Early recognition, appropriate staging, and timely therapy may improve outcomes, although irreversible organ damage may limit recovery. This case emphasizes the importance of a multidisciplinary approach in the evaluation and management of AL amyloidosis, particularly in patients with prior malignancy.

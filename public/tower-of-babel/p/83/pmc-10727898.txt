A Case of Eccrine Syringofibroadenoma Related to Senile Gluteal Dermatosis
Authors: Tae-Eun Kim, Hye-Jin Ahn, Min Kyung Shin
Published: 2023

INTRODUCTION

Eccrine syringofibroadenoma (ESFA), first described by Mascaro in 1963, is a tumor of eccrine ductal differentiation. ESFA is a rare disease, with only approximately 80 cases reported worldwide. It presents with variable clinical findings, including plaques, papules, verrucous plaques, keratotic tumors, and solitary tumors. Histologically, it is characterized by anastomosing thin strands of epithelial cells embedded in a fibrovascular stroma. ESFA can be classified into five subtypes: 1) solitary ESFA, 2) multiple ESFA associated with hidrotic ectodermal dysplasia, 3) syringofibroadenomatosis (multiple ESFA without associated cutaneous findings), 4) nonfamilial unilateral linear ESFA (nevoid ESFA), and 5) reactive ESFA associated with inflammatory or neoplastic dermatoses, almost a quarter of which are reactive ESFAs1. Reactive ESFAs appear to be caused by repeated repair and remodeling in response to repetitive tissue damage. Chronic ulcers, burn scars, and physical trauma are common causes of tissue damage.

Senile gluteal dermatosis (SGD) was first reported in Japan in 1979. It is a relatively common geriatric dermatosis in East Asia. This condition presents as hyperkeratotic lichenified skin lesions in the gluteal region. East Asia has a sedentary culture, and it seems that continuous pressure and friction on the gluteal areas over a long period of time and cause characteristic lesions in the elderly23. Therefore, SGD is considered a friction dermatosis in elderly individuals. We, herein, report a case of solitary reactive ESFA associated with SGD in an elderly patient. We received the patient’s consent form about publishing all photographic materials.

CASE REPORT

An 86-year-old woman presented with a solitary recurrent dark brown plaque in the sacral area. The lesion was completely asymptomatic. Physical examination revealed a solitary 1 cm×0.5 cm sized brownish plaque with crust on the sacral area (Fig. 1A). There was a hyperkeratotic lichenified brownish patch around the plaque, which was clinically considered SGD. A white globular structure surrounded by a brownish background was identified using dermoscopy (Fig. 1B).

Histopathological examination of biopsy specimen revealed thin anastomosing reticulated strands of basaloid cuboidal cells. The strands were embedded in a rich fibrovascular stroma. The tumor extends from the basal layer of the epidermis to the dermis. These findings are consistent with those of ESFA (Fig. 2). Immunohistochemical staining for endothelial membrane antigen and carcinoembryonic antigen were both positive. The patient was treated with total excision of the skin lesion. There was no recurrence after excision.

DISCUSSION

Reactive ESFA is related to tissue regeneration and remodeling after damage, such as trauma and burns4. Singh et al.5 reported ESFA that occurred in the sole, a site under constant pressure in a patient with diabetes mellitus. In this case, the skin lesion showed a characteristic verrucous growth pattern, which was thought to be due to the pressure effect of weight-bearing of the sole. The authors considered the growth pattern as a result of eccrine tissue regeneration and cellular proliferation due to altered sympathetic nerve function.

When a brown plaque accompanied with whitish scales is observed on the buttocks of elderly patients, the differential diagnosis includes squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), Bowen’s disease, and lichen simplex chronicus (LSC). Both SCC and Bowen’s disease are usually present in sun-exposed areas, such as the head and neck. LSC is caused by repeated scratching and is characterized by hyperkeratosis with thickened plaques. In addition, friction melanosis can also be considered as a differential diagnosis. It is characterized by brownish patches that presents on the skin or bony regions due to physical friction. However, it is different in that it is more common in young patients with a history of using a towel or brush6. Relatively rare adnexal tumors can also be considered. Adnexal tumors are difficult to distinguish clinically, and highly accurate dermoscopy can be used to make a diagnosis. The dermoscopic characteristics of eccrine tumors differ depending on the type of tumor. In the literature so far, dermoscopy findings of reactive ESFA are rarely reported. In the report by Pagliuca et al.7, a pinkish center was surrounded by white lines and multiple superficial erosions on dermoscopy. Reactive ESFA reveals various dermoscopy findings, and it seems that the findings show various aspects depending on the disease or condition.

The pathogenesis of SGD remains unclear. However, considering that it is a relatively common disease in the elderly in East Asia, it seems to be associated with the sedentary lifestyle of East Asia. A sedentary lifestyle causes chronic friction of the buttocks, which leads to hyperkeratotic and lichenified lesions3. Repetitive friction and pressure injury trigger local erosion and breaks in the epidermis and superficial dermis, which can cause abnormal epithelial changes and eccrine ductal proliferation. There is no literature reporting ESFA related to SGD so far, but there have been few reports of cases occurring in soles or buttocks, which are constantly under pressure. Considering that repeated friction can promote eccrine tissue regeneration and proliferation, we speculate that the patient’s lesion, in this case, is related to SGD. This is the first report on reactive ESFA related to SGD, and further research is needed to reveal the pathogenic mechanism.

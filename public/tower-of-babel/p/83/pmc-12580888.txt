Acute kidney injury due to inhalation of metal fumes
Authors: Akihiro Tojo, Jun Hirao, Tadayuki Ogawa
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

We report a case of pulmonary-renal syndrome associated with inhalation of welding fumes, in which metal nanoparticles were identified in the glomeruli. A 30-year-old male had been present at an arc welding site for 3 days wearing only a simple nonwoven mask for inspection. Over the next week, the patient developed cough, bloody sputum, fever, and dyspnea. Physical examination revealed elevated blood pressure (212/124 mmHg), pulse rate (123 bpm), and crackles in both lung fields. Laboratory tests revealed elevated white blood cell count (21 000/μl), C-reactive protein (30.1 mg/dl), and Krebs von den Lungen-6 (818 U/ml). A computed tomography scan revealed bilateral ground-glass opacities (Fig. 1a). The serum creatinine level at the time of admission was 3.50 mg/dl, which rose further to 6.02 mg/dl 3 days later. A health check 5 months earlier had revealed no abnormalities in blood pressure, renal function, or chest X-ray, so the patient was diagnosed with acute interstitial pneumonia and acute kidney injury due to welding fume inhalation. Methylprednisolone pulse therapy, intravenous cyclophosphamide, hemodialysis, and six plasma exchanges completely cured the acute interstitial pneumonia, and hemodialysis was discontinued after 2 weeks. Because the serum creatine level remained ∼3 mg/dl, a kidney biopsy was performed. Periodic acid methenamine silver staining revealed intraglomerular congestion, wrinkled glomerular basement membrane, and endothelial cell swelling and obstruction of the afferent arteriole (Fig. 1b). Electron microscopy revealed dilated subendothelial spaces, leading to a diagnosis of arteriolosclerosis. When complex metal ultrafine particles such as nickel (Ni), manganese (Mn), and chromium (Cr) produced in welding fumes are inhaled, these metal nanoparticles generate radical oxygen species through transport via alveolar macrophages, leading to lung inflammation and DNA damage, and some particles are transported toward lung-associated lymphatic vessels and blood circulation leading to secondary organ damage including cardiovascular diseases [1, 2]. In rats, exposure to welding fumes causes glomerular congestion, renal tubular lymphocytosis, thrombus formation, and necrosis [3]. Elemental analysis of renal biopsy specimens using a low-vacuum scanning electron microscope equipped with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectrometer [4, 5] revealed that glomeruli from the patient were enriched with copper (Cu), Ni, Mn, and Cr compared to a control patient with membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (Fig. 1c–f). Exposure to welding fumes without wearing special protective masks caused acute interstitial pneumonia and acute kidney injury, and deposition of complex metal particles including Cr, Mn, Ni, and Cu in glomeruli was demonstrated.

Surgical Management and Long-Term Outcomes of Patients With Superior-Inferior Ventricles
Authors: Arjun Verma, Michael Bamgbose, Gianna Dafflisio, Rebecca Beroukhim, Eric N Feins, Sitaram M Emani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstractKey words: biventricular repair, congenital heart surgery, criss cross ventricles, single ventricle palliation, superior inferior ventricles

Hearts with superior-inferior ventricles (SIV) represent a rare, heterogeneous, and often complex subset of congenital heart disease. Surgical management of children with SIV ranges from single ventricle palliation (SVP) to biventricular repair (BiV). The present work aimed to characterize the management and outcomes of such patients at Boston Children’s Hospital. We hypothesized that BiV would be feasible for select patients with SIV, yielding comparable transplant-free survival, reoperation, and pacemaker implantation to SVP.

What is the clinical question being addressed?

For patients with superior-inferior ventricles, do outcomes differ between biventricular repair and single ventricle palliation?

What is the main finding?

Biventricular repair is feasible in children with superior-inferior ventricles, and those who survive 6 months experience superior transplant-free survival, compared patients undergoing single ventricle palliation.

Methods

All patients with SIV who underwent cardiac surgery at Boston Children’s Hospital between January 1983 and June 2024 were identified and stratified into BiV (2- or 1.5-ventricle) and SVP cohorts (IRB-P00050516). The primary outcomes were transplant-free survival, reoperation, and permanent pacemaker implantation (PPI). Categorical variables are reported as percentages and compared using the Pearson chi-square statistic, while continuous variables are shown as medians with IQR and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Cuzick’s nonparametric test for trends (nptrend) was used to evaluate changes in surgical strategy over time. Kaplan-Meier survival curves and log-rank values were used to evaluate outcomes in a time-adjusted manner. Due to markedly shorter follow-up within the BiV cohort (median 1 year, IQR: 0-4) compared to SVP (5 years, IQR: 1-15), analyses of transplant-free survival and reoperation were limited to 5-year follow-up and to 15 years for PPI. All statistical analyses were performed using STATA 18.0 (StataCorp LLC).

Results

A total of 119 patients with SIV were identified, of whom 78 (66%) underwent BiV and 41 (34%) SVP. BiV was increasingly performed over time (nptrend <0.01). The distributions of age at the index operation (P = 0.20) and female sex (P = 0.54) were similar between cohorts. Unbalanced atrioventricular (AV) canal defects were more common among BiV patients (18% vs 10%; P = 0.24), while SVP patients more frequently had straddling AV valves (34% vs 23%; P = 0.20), double inlet ventricles (15% vs 3%; P = 0.01), and AV valve atresia (12 vs 0%; P < 0.01). Nearly half of the study population (n = 55) had double outlet right ventricle, while 41 had transposition of the great arteries. There were no significant differences in ventriculoarterial connection between the cohorts. Indexed left ventricular end-diastolic volume was similar between both cohorts (BiV: 70 [56-102] vs SVP: 48 mL/m2 [36-109], P = 0.27), while indexed right ventricular end-diastolic volume was significantly higher among BiV patients (72 [50-97] vs 40 mL/m2 [16-52], P < 0.01).

During BiV, 46% required baffling of the systemic ventricle to the aorta, and 39% underwent arterial switch. Additional procedures on the systemic outflow tract included root translocation, arch reconstruction, and resection of obstructing tissue. The pulmonary outflow tract was developed using a right ventricle to pulmonary artery conduit in 30% of patients, while reconstruction was undertaken in 10%. The Mustard and Hemi-Mustard techniques were preferred for atrial switch. Concomitant procedures included AV canal repair as well as aortic, pulmonary, tricuspid, and mitral valvuloplasty.

Transplant-free survival at 5 years was similar between the BiV and SVP cohorts (Figure 1A) (log-rank P = 0.27). Upon examination of patients surviving >6 months, the BiV cohort faced significantly improved transplant-free survival, relative to SVP (log-rank P = 0.01). The most common cause of death in the BiV cohort was postoperative AV valve regurgitation leading to ventricular dysfunction. The hazards of reoperation at 5-year follow-up were significantly higher in the BiV cohort (Figure 1B) (log-rank P = 0.048), although reoperation rates were comparable 6 months beyond the index operation (log-rank P = 0.26). Baffle revision and AV valvuloplasty were the primary indications for reoperation after BiV repair. Rates of PPI at 15-year follow-up were equivalent between cohorts (log-rank P = 0.66). Of note, all PPI for BiV patients occurred within the first month, while gradual attrition was noted within the SVP cohort.

Discussion

The present study evaluated the surgical management and long-term outcomes of 119 patients with SIV, of whom two-thirds successfully underwent BiV. Transplant-free survival was comparable between the BiV and SVP cohorts, but importantly, BiV patients who survived >6 months after the index operation had improved transplant-free survival, relative to SVP. High early mortality within the BiV cohort is likely attributable to prolonged cross clamp times necessary to achieve biventricular circulation, while gradual attrition in the SVP group aligns with prior studies demonstrating the progressive nature of Fontan-associated complications resulting in transplantation or mortality.1 Taken together, these findings suggest that BiV is associated with a tangible long-term survival benefit, although the early postoperative period is high risk.

Upon review, early ventricular dysfunction secondary to AV valve regurgitation was found to be the most common cause of death among BiV patients. Furthermore, patients who underwent BiV had significantly higher reoperation rates, primarily for baffle revision and AV valve repair. Construction of an intracardiac baffle is particularly complex in hearts with SIV, given the orthogonal orientation of the AV valves. Often, the baffle compresses the AV valves and induces regurgitation, which warrants reintervention. These data suggest that meticulous assessment of both AV valves with saline injection and transesophageal echocardiography is critical, and a lower threshold for AV valve intervention may be warranted among patients with SIV.

At our institution, children are typically considered candidates for complex BiV if both ventricles are adequately sized and have appropriate diastolic function.2 In recent years, advanced cardiac imaging and 3-dimensional modeling have become indispensable tools for surgical planning, particularly for patients with SIV in whom construction of appropriate intracardiac baffles is challenging.3 Moreover, as novel methods for repairing the native AV valves emerge (artificial chordae, leaflet reconstructions), we have almost completely avoided concomitant AV valve replacement.4 Importantly, adequate myocardial preservation is accomplished through a combination of cold topical, antegrade, and retrograde cardioplegia delivery and by performing portions of the repair under fibrillation.5

It is critical to acknowledge that the BiV and SVP cohorts are fundamentally different patient populations, and as such, these strategies must be considered complementary rather than interchangeable. The heterogeneity in fundamental diagnosis between the BiV and SVP cohorts, as well as the long study period, may introduce bias into our analysis of transplant-free survival, reoperation, and PPI. Although we attempted to adjust for confounding using multivariable Cox regression, our results were limited by sample size, and thus, are not reported.

In summary, the present work demonstrated that BiV is feasible among patients with SIV, with markedly improved transplant-free survival for those surviving >6 months after the index operation, relative to SVP. Nonetheless, early reoperation and pacemaker implantation remain a concern and represent an opportunity for future improvement.

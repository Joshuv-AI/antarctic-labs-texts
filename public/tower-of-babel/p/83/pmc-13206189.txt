Long‐Term Safety and Efficacy of Once‐Daily and Proactive Twice‐Weekly Roflumilast Cream 0.05% for Mild‐to‐Moderate Atopic Dermatitis in Children Aged 2–5 Years From a 52‐Week, Phase 3 Trial (INTEGUMENT‐OLE)
Authors: Lawrence F. Eichenfield, Mercedes E. Gonzalez, Mark Boguniewicz, Tracy Funk, Adelaide A. Hebert, Vimal H. Prajapati, Chesahna Kindred, Rocco Serrao, Lisa Swanson, David Krupa, Scott Snyder, Melissa S. Seal, Diane Hanna, Patrick Burnett, David R. Berk
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundINTEGUMENT‐PED/NCT04845620, a 4‐week, phase 3 trial, demonstrated efficacy and safety of roflumilast cream 0.05% in children aged 2–5 years with mild‐to‐moderate atopic dermatitis (AD). A phase 3 open‐label extension trial (INTEGUMENT‐OLE [NCT04804605]) investigated long‐term outcomes continuing roflumilast cream for ≤ 56 weeks.MethodsCaregivers of eligible patients from INTEGUMENT‐PED applied roflumilast cream 0.05% once‐daily in INTEGUMENT‐OLE. Patients achieving Validated Investigator Global Assessment for AD (vIGA‐AD) clear (0) at/after week 4 of INTEGUMENT‐OLE switched to twice‐weekly (BIW) application. Evaluation of safety (adverse events [AEs] and application‐site tolerability) was the primary objective. Efficacy endpoints assessed from INTEGUMENT‐PED baseline included vIGA‐AD success (clear/almost clear [0/1] plus ≥ 2‐point improvement), vIGA‐AD 0/1, ≥ 75% improvement in Eczema Area and Severity Index, and Worst Itch‐Numeric Rating Scale success (≥ 4‐point improvement in patients with baseline score ≥ 4). Duration of vIGA‐AD 0/1 and sign/symptom control (“disease control”) with BIW application was determined.ResultsAmong 562 patients, 14 (2.5%) had treatment‐related AEs reported. Roflumilast was well tolerated; application‐site pain AEs were reported for four (0.7%) patients. At treatment‐week 56, AD signs/symptoms continued to improve (63.1% of patients achieved vIGA‐AD 0/1). Of 170 (30.2%) patients who switched to BIW application, the median Kaplan–Meier duration of “disease control” was 238 days.ConclusionsRoflumilast cream 0.05% demonstrated a favorable long‐term safety profile and durable efficacy for up to 56 weeks of treatment in children aged 2–5 years with AD, and “disease control” was maintained with BIW application. These results are consistent with previous studies, including patients aged ≥ 6 years from INTEGUMENT‐OLE. Trial Registration: Clinicaltrials.gov identifier: NCT04845620

Introduction

Global prevalence of atopic dermatitis (AD) in those aged 6 months to < 18 years is 12%–15% [1]. Itch, sleep deprivation, impaired social functioning, and mental/physical comorbidities occurring with AD signs/symptoms [2] can negatively impact patient and family quality of life [2, 3]. Despite the recommended use of advanced targeted topical therapies to treat inflammatory skin diseases [4], topical corticosteroids (TCS) and topical calcineurin inhibitors (TCIs) remain mainstay treatments for AD [3, 5]. TCS and TCIs are indicated for twice‐daily application, TCS and TCIs are not approved for long‐term continuous use, and high‐potency TCS are not recommended in thin‐skinned areas because of increased absorption and risk of cutaneous/systemic adverse events (AEs) [5, 6, 7]. Local cutaneous AEs (e.g., skin atrophy), impact on growth/development, impractical application schedule, undesirable look/feel, application‐site tolerability (e.g., burning/stinging), and prescription black box warnings are concerns lowering TCS and TCI adherence, especially in children [8, 9].

Continuous application of emollients and prescription topical anti‐inflammatory treatments to AD‐affected areas focuses on epidermal barrier repair and re‐establishing immune homeostasis; however, recurring flares complicate treatment plans [4, 10]. After clinical clearance, cutaneous memory T cells in the skin may promote reactivation of AD signs/symptoms [11]. Proactive therapy (typically twice‐weekly [BIW], low‐dose application) for previously affected areas may help maintain disease control, reducing recurrent signs/symptoms [10, 11].

Roflumilast cream is a topical, water‐based phosphodiesterase 4 (PDE4) inhibitor [12]. Unlike other topicals, roflumilast cream does not include excipients that often irritate the skin, damage hair, or cause contact sensitization (e.g., ethanol, propylene glycol, isopropyl alcohol, formaldehyde‐releasing preservatives, or fragrances) [12]. Among PDE4 inhibitors, roflumilast most closely mimics cyclic adenosine monophosphate key binding sites to PDE4, resulting in higher potency [13]. The efficacy and safety of roflumilast cream 0.15% for mild‐to‐moderate AD in patients ≥ 6 years old [14] was demonstrated in two 4‐week, randomized, placebo‐controlled, phase 3 trials (INTEGUMENT‐1 [NCT04662487] and INTEGUMENT‐2 [NCT04773600]) [15].

Once‐daily roflumilast cream 0.05% demonstrated safety and efficacy in a 4‐week, randomized, vehicle‐controlled, phase 3 trial (INTEGUMENT‐PED [NCT04845620]) in patients aged 2–5 years with AD [16]. In addition to the known skin barrier dysfunction from AD, the 0.05% dose was selected for patients aged < 6 years because of skin barrier immaturity, higher body surface area‐to‐body mass ratio, and greater AD‐body surface area involvement, compared with those aged ≥ 6 years. A phase 2, open‐label trial investigating roflumilast cream 0.05% in children aged 3 months to < 2 years with mild‐to‐moderate AD (INTEGUMENT‐INFANT [NCT06998056]) is underway.

An open‐label extension (OLE) of the phase 3 INTEGUMENT trials (INTEGUMENT‐OLE [NCT04804605]) investigated long‐term use of roflumilast cream 0.05%/0.15% (≤ 56 weeks) in patients aged ≥ 2 years. As previously reported, once‐daily and proactive BIW (for those who achieved clear skin) roflumilast cream 0.15% was well tolerated and maintained or continued improvement in AD signs/symptoms [17]. Long‐term efficacy, safety, and application‐site tolerability in patients who completed INTEGUMENT‐PED and received roflumilast cream 0.05% in INTEGUMENT‐OLE are reported.

Methods

Trial Design

INTEGUMENT‐OLE, a phase 3, open‐label trial, enrolled patients aged ≥ 2 years with AD from Canada, Poland, and the United States [17]. Planned enrollment of ~1500 patients comprised those from the INTEGUMENT‐1 and ‐2 (aged ≥ 6 years) and INTEGUMENT‐PED (aged 2–5 years) parent trials [15, 16].

Patients who met previously described eligibility criteria [15, 16] and completed that parent study without experiencing an AE leading to discontinuation of study treatment could participate in INTEGUMENT‐OLE (Figure S1). The primary objective was to assess long‐term safety of roflumilast cream 0.05% (0.15% for patients who aged to 6 years during the study; see details below). Secondary endpoints included Validated Investigator Global Assessment for AD (vIGA‐AD) success (clear [0]/almost clear [1] plus ≥ 2‐point improvement from parent‐study baseline), vIGA‐ad 0/1, ≥ 75% improvement from parent‐study baseline in Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI‐75), Worst Itch‐Numeric Rating Scale (WI‐NRS) success (≥ 4‐point improvement from parent‐study baseline in patients with parent‐study baseline score ≥ 4), and WI‐NRS 0/1 (no [0]/minimal [1] itch in patients with parent‐study baseline score ≥ 2). Investigator‐ and patient‐reported application‐site tolerability were assessed; parents/caregivers completed assessments for patients aged < 6 years.

Ethics Oversight

The protocol was approved by local institutional review boards and ethics committees prior to investigators obtaining written informed consent from caregivers. The trial was conducted in accordance with the approved protocol, the principles of the Tri‐Council Policy Statement, the Declaration of Helsinki, and the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines.

Procedures

Patients continued roflumilast cream or switched from vehicle cream to roflumilast cream 0.05% directly from INTEGUMENT‐PED; parent‐study week 4 was the start of INTEGUMENT‐OLE (day 1 visit) with timing reported by overall treatment week across the two studies (i.e., 52 weeks in the OLE corresponds to 56 treatment weeks; Figure S1). Roflumilast cream 0.05% was applied by a parent/caregiver once daily to all AD‐affected areas, except the scalp, at approximately 2 mg/cm2; there was no limit to body surface area for application.

At/after OLE week 4, patients who achieved clear skin (vIGA‐ad 0) were to switch to BIW roflumilast cream 0.05% application on 2 nonconsecutive days per week. BIW application was maintained if AD signs/symptoms (parent/caretaker‐ and investigator‐reported) were adequately controlled and vIGA‐ad 0/1 was maintained. Patients who aged to 6 years during the study were to switch to roflumilast cream 0.15% at their first scheduled visit after their sixth birthday.

Assessments

AEs were collected throughout the study. Application‐site tolerability was assessed at first application in the parent study and OLE weeks 4, 8, 12, 24, 36, and 52. Investigator‐rated tolerability (i.e., skin irritation) was determined prior to study‐drug application on a scale from 0 (no evidence of irritation) to 7 (strong reaction spreading beyond the application site). Patient‐rated tolerability (i.e., burning/stinging sensation) was recorded 10–15 min after application of roflumilast cream and rated on a four‐point scale from 0 (no sensation) to 3 (hot, tingling/stinging sensation that caused definite discomfort).

Efficacy assessments (performed at clinic visits) did not include palms, soles, and scalp. Duration of “disease control” with proactive BIW application was evaluated by maintenance of vIGA‐AD 0/1 and AD sign/symptom control among patients who achieved vIGA‐AD of 0. Additional details are available in online supplemental methods.

Statistical Analysis

The safety population included all enrolled patients who received at least one study treatment application. Efficacy outcomes were analyzed in all enrolled patients (full analysis set). Statistical analyses were conducted with SAS version 9.4. Descriptive statistics summarize outcomes. Means, standard deviations (SDs), medians, and ranges were calculated for continuous data and frequency counts and proportions for categorical data, based on observed data with no imputation. Time‐to‐event data were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier (K–M) method.

Results

Patients

Among 602 patients who completed INTEGUMENT‐PED [16], 562 (93.4%; roflumilast cream, n = 382; vehicle cream, n = 180) enrolled into INTEGUMENT‐OLE; 377 (67.1%) patients completed the INTEGUMENT‐OLE trial (Figure S2). Among patients who continued from INTEGUMENT‐PED into INTEGUMENT‐OLE, demographics and disease characteristics at parent‐trial baseline were balanced between the groups (Table S1). Over 56 weeks, there were 55 patients who aged to 6 years and received at least one application of roflumilast cream 0.15%.

Safety and Application‐Site Tolerability

Treatment‐emergent AEs (TEAEs) were reported for 280/562 (49.8%) patients (Table 1); these were mostly mild (n = 139 [24.7%]) or moderate (n = 125 [22.2%]). Treatment‐related AEs were reported for 14 (2.5%) patients. Serious AEs (SAEs) were reported for 18 (3.2%) patients, none were considered related to study treatment. Safety outcomes were similar among patients who aged to 6 years during the study and received at least one application of roflumilast cream 0.15%.

AEs reported for ≥ 4.0% of patients were infection‐related (upper respiratory tract infection [8.7%]; nasopharyngitis [5.0%]; pyrexia [5.0%]). Gastrointestinal AEs (of interest due to association with some PDE4 inhibitors [18]) were reported for 25 (4.4%) patients, the most common being vomiting (n = 15; 2.7%) and diarrhea (n = 6; 1.1%). There were no clinically meaningful changes in vital signs. One (0.3%) patient experienced ≥ 5% weight loss from baseline to the end of the study, which was attributed to lifestyle modifications. Application‐site pain AEs were reported for four (0.7%) patients (all were mild or moderate).

Roflumilast cream was well tolerated, with no or mild/minimal application‐site irritation for both investigator‐ and caregiver‐reported assessments (Figure S3). Investigators reported no irritation at the application site for ≥ 97% of patients across time points. A severe hot, tingling/stinging sensation that caused definite discomfort was reported for 0.3% of patients at treatment week 56; throughout the study, the highest proportion of patients for whom a severe sensation was reported (1.8%) was at week 12.

Efficacy

Improvements in AD signs/symptoms in INTEGUMENT‐PED were maintained or continued to improve throughout INTEGUMENT‐OLE. At 56 weeks, 53.8% and 55.0% of patients who continued treatment with roflumilast and switched from vehicle to roflumilast, respectively, achieved vIGA‐AD success (Figure 1A); similar trends were observed for achievement of vIGA‐AD 0/1 (Figure 1B), and EASI‐75 was achieved by 71.9% and 76.0% of patients (Figure 2). Proportions of patients in the roflumilast‐ and vehicle‐to‐roflumilast groups who achieved WI‐NRS success increased through the end of the study, reaching 57.9% and 66.3%, respectively (Figure 3A); WI‐NRS 0/1 was achieved by 40.5% and 41.1% of patients (Figure 3B). A representative example of improvement in AD appearance over the course of study treatment can be viewed in Figure 4. Efficacy outcomes reported for participants who received ≥ 1 application of roflumilast cream 0.15% were comparable with those reported for patients who received roflumilast cream 0.05% (data not shown).

Overall, 170 (30.2%) patients achieved vIGA‐ad 0 at or after OLE week 4 and switched to BIW application. Of these patients, the K–M median duration of “disease control” was 238 (95% CI, 146–331) days.

Discussion

Roflumilast cream 0.05% for up to 56 weeks was well tolerated in patients aged 2–5 years with AD. Treatment‐related AE and gastrointestinal AE rates were low, and no treatment‐related SAEs were reported. Long‐term safety outcomes were consistent with previously reported results from INTEGUMENT‐PED [16], INTEGUMENT‐1/2 [15], and INTEGUMENT‐OLE [17]. AD sign/symptom improvements observed in INTEGUMENT‐PED were maintained or continued improvement throughout the OLE. Efficacy and safety among patients who turned 6 years old and received ≥ 1 application of roflumilast cream 0.15% were comparable with the overall population. Patients who achieved clear skin and switched to BIW application maintained “disease control” for a median of ~8 months (K–M estimate).

Treatment recommendations include proactive AD therapy [4, 10] and a non‐TCS proactive treatment may help address concerns regarding long‐term TCS use and nonadherence [3, 9]. Notably, the “disease control” observed with proactive BIW application in this trial suggests that roflumilast cream 0.05% may be suitable for long‐term, proactive control of AD.

Topical therapy for AD is frequently affected by poor adherence, often related to fear of adverse effects of TCS and complicated treatment regimens [9]. Regulatory agencies note that TCS can be used for treatment of cutaneous inflammatory conditions over short periods or if used intermittently over longer periods; however, prolonged and cumulative TCS exposure increases the risk of AEs, TCS dependence, and/or severe skin reactions with discontinuation (i.e., TCS withdrawal) [3, 6, 7, 19]. In addition to duration and frequency of use, body surface area being treated, patient age, and TCS potency also influence AE risk [20, 21], including rare systemic AEs (e.g., hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis suppression) [20]. Some professional societies have proposed consideration of total steroid exposure as a part of routine care [22, 23], with concerns amplified for patients with AD who may have multiple allergy‐related comorbidities (e.g., food allergy, asthma, allergic rhinitis, contact dermatitis) commonly treated with topical or systemic corticosteroids [3, 24, 25]. The apprehension around TCS use underscores the continuing need for corticosteroid‐sparing topical therapies, especially for the treatment of chronic inflammatory diseases such as AD.

In contrast with commonly used topical therapies, roflumilast cream is a once‐daily water‐based treatment that does not contain potential irritants [12]. Throughout this study, investigator‐ and parent/caregiver‐assessed application‐site tolerability revealed no or minimal application‐site irritation.

Limitations of this trial include lack of the vehicle comparator through the long‐term treatment period and excluding treatment and/or assessment of AD on the scalp, palms, and soles.

Roflumilast cream 0.05% for up to 56 weeks was well tolerated and provided durable efficacy in 2–5‐year‐old patients with mild‐to‐moderate AD. Among patients who achieved clear skin, clear/almost clear skin and adequate disease control (including pruritus) were maintained for approximately 8 months with proactive BIW application. These results are consistent with long‐term results observed in patients aged ≥ 6 years and suggest that once‐daily and proactive BIW application with roflumilast cream 0.05% can improve and maintain AD signs/symptoms.

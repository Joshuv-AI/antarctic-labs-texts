Protocol for monitoring clonal hematopoiesis in transgenic mouse models using multispectral imaging
Authors: Priyanka Khanna, Lauren B. Ostermann, Shayaun Khazaei, Ran Zhao, Michael Andreeff, Rasoul Pourebrahim
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryClonal hematopoiesis involves the clonal expansion of hematopoietic cells, potentially progressing into hematological malignancies. Here, we present a protocol for the development and characterization of two mouse models designed to simulate clonal hematopoiesis and acute myeloid leukemia. We describe steps for model generation, monitoring clonal expansion, harvesting, fixation, and staining of bone marrow and spleen tissues. We specifically focus on the visualization and analysis of p53 mutant clonal expansions, providing a comprehensive protocol for studying these phenomena in mouse models.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Pourebrahim et al.1

Before you begin

This protocol outlines a detailed procedure for monitoring the expansion of p53-mutant clonal hematopoiesis in peripheral blood (PB) through flow cytometry. It includes methods for extracting tissues from the mouse spleen and bone marrow (BM), preparing samples, and applying fluorescent staining for multispectral imaging. Additionally, the protocol mentions alternative imaging techniques, such as confocal microscopy, that can be employed. Although applicable to mice of any age or gender, this protocol was specifically developed for mice with a C57BL/6J background.

Generation of the transgenic mouse model of p53 mutant clonal hematopoiesis

Transgenic mouse model using Mx1-Cre driver

Timing: This is dependent on the availability of mice from Jackson Laboratory and the breeding program

Timing: This is dependent on the availability of mice from Jackson Laboratory and the breeding program

Mx1-Cre (B6.Cg-Tg1Cgn/J) was obtained from Jackson Laboratory and were crossed with Trp53-floxed mice (B6.129P2-Trp53tm1Brn/J). Trp53-floxed mice were previously described.2,3 This protocol was developed using Mx1-Cre; Trp53wmR172 H/fl; mTmG (referred to as Mx-p53mut/fl). mTmG allele, is a fluorescent reporter consisting of a tdTomato (mT) cassette and upon Cre-mediated recombination through intraperitoneal (IP) pIpC injections, the mT cassette is replaced by a green fluorescent protein (mG) cassette. This transgenic model mimics clonal hematopoiesis in adult hematopoietic stem cells resulting in the development of AML.

Protocol for generating a transplant model of p53 mutant clonal hematopoiesis

Timing: 6 weeks

Timing: 6 weeks

This model mimics the human condition where p53 mutation in hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells activity leads to clonal expansion and potential malignant transformation. The process starts with the transplantation of hematopoietic stem cells harboring p53 mutations into irradiated mice, creating a biological platform where the dynamics of clonal expansion can be observed and quantified. This model not only facilitates the investigation of the fundamental mechanisms driving the development of hematological malignancies but also serves as a valuable tool for testing the effects of therapeutic interventions on the expansion of p53 mutant clonal hematopoiesis in the context of therapy-related AML (t-AML).

Expected outcomes

The expected outcome of this protocol is to provide detailed, quantitative insights into the expansion and spatial distribution of p53 mutant and wild-type cell populations within the bone marrow and spleen tissues through flow cytometry analysis (Figures 1 and 2). Using the described staining and imaging techniques, distinct visualization of clonal expansions marked by GFP (p53 mutant) and RFP (wild-type) expressions should be achieved (Figure 5). Multispectral imaging will allow for the precise differentiation of these fluorescent markers, enabling clear distinction between mutant and non-mutant cells. This will be instrumental in assessing the degree of clonal expansion and the potential progression towards malignancy (Figures 6 and 7). By examining the microenvironments of the bone marrow and spleen, the protocol should help identify how these cells interact within their niches and how such interactions may influence disease progression or stability. Successful application of the protocol will validate the use of these transgenic mouse models in simulating human clonal hematopoiesis, providing a reliable platform for further studies on the mechanisms underlying the disease and the testing of therapeutic interventions. Limitations

The use of GFP and RFP for marking p53 mutant and wild-type cells respectively occupies two of the typically available three channels in fluorescent microscopes. This limits the capacity to simultaneously detect additional cellular markers, potentially restricting the scope of multiplex analysis in a single experiment.

The success of fluorescence and multispectral imaging heavily depends on the precise control of experimental conditions, including the concentration of antibodies, the incubation timings, and the temperature. Variations in these parameters can lead to inconsistent staining, affecting the reliability of the data.

While multispectral imaging provides powerful insights into the spatial distribution of clonal populations, its resolution is often limited compared to other more advanced imaging techniques like confocal microscopy. This might restrict the detailed analysis of cellular interactions and microenvironment structures.

Any substitution of the primary antibodies (p53 or Ki-67) with alternatives requires careful optimization of antibody dilutions to achieve effective staining. This process can be timing-consuming and may require significant trial and error to establish conditions that yield clear and consistent results, potentially delaying the progression of research.

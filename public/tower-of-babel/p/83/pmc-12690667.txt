PDNO-13 IMPORTANCE OF SECOND OPINION IN DIAGNOSIS OF BRAIN TUMOR IN A LIMITED RESOURCE SETTING; A CASE REPORT OF 7YRS FEMALE WITH BRAIN TUMOR
Authors: Atuganile Malango, Ugumba Kwikima, John Mtei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThere are factors that are often considered as critical elements to ensure diagnostic accuracy in pathology. These are knowledge and experience of pathologist, adequate clinical information, use of standard terminology and diagnostic criteria, appropriate use of ancillary tests and second opinion. In Tanzania despite having fewer number of pathologists, none of them has subspeciality in neuropathology. Diagnosis of brain tumor depends on histological features and few available immunohistochemical (IHC) stains, molecular tests have not yet established in the country. Hence there is usually challenge in diagnosis of these tumor.Methods7yrs female presented with ill-defined heterogeneous mass on the left frontal parietal lobe, craniotomy was done with 50% debulking of the tumor.ResultsHistology results by local pathologist was concluded to be atypical rhabdoid tumor (ATRT), IHC EMA, Vimentin and GFAP were advised to be done. The case was sent for neuropathologist expert opinion. During the review more IHC were added including OLIG2, ATRX, INI1, L1CAM and P65, CD34 and MIB1. Expert review conclusion was Supratentorial Ependymoma, ZFTA Fusion-Positive, CNS WHO grade 2.DiscussionAccurate diagnosis is important for proper treatment and prognostication of the patient. For this case the diagnosis changed from ATRT to ZFTA Fusion Positive Ependymoma after review. Majority of the IHC tests ordered during review i.e., OLIG2, ATRX, INI1, L1CAM and P65 are not available in our setting except for CD34 and MIB1.ConclusionSecond opinion has been used in pathology to increase accuracy of the diagnosis. Despite having this privilege of consultation, there are few available numbers of IHC tests in our setting. Majority of the insurance schemes do not cover for the IHC tests hence unaffordable. Recent advancement in applying artificial Intelligence in neuro-oncology including histopathology and molecular categorization is promising especially if the technology will be available and affordable in the limited resource settings.

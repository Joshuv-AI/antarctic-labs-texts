Real-world effectiveness and safety of tralokinumab in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis with malignancy, chronic infections, and multimorbidity
Authors: Francisco Javier Melgosa Ramos, Pedro Mercader, Sergio Santos Alarcón, Nalia Dominguez Liron, Andrea Bernabeu, Mercedes Rodriguez Serna, Pablo Fernandez-Crehuet, Juan Francisco Silvestre
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionPatients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) and significant comorbidities are frequently excluded from randomized clinical trials, limiting the applicability of evidence to these clinically complex patients. We evaluated the effectiveness and safety of tralokinumab in this setting.MethodsMulticenter retrospective study including adults with moderate-to-severe AD treated with tralokinumab (300 mg every 2 weeks) in Spanish tertiary hospitals (June 2022–December 2025). Special populations included patients with malignancies, advanced cardiovascular or renal disease, chronic infections, or neurologic disorders. Outcomes included Eczema Area and Severity Index (EASI), pruritus NRS, safety events, and treatment optimization. Analyses were descriptive.ResultsTwenty-one patients (aged 27–90 years) with high multimorbidity burden were included. Comorbidities comprised malignancies (n = 9), advanced heart disease (n = 7), neurologic disorders (n = 4), chronic kidney disease (n = 2), latent tuberculosis (n = 2), and HIV (n = 2). Mean EASI decreased from 19.3 to 3.45 (~ 82% reduction) and pruritus Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) from 7 to 2 (~ 71% reduction) after a mean follow-up of 24.6 months. Approximately 70% achieved EASI ≤ 3 and 50% EASI 0–1. Median follow-up was 24.6 months. Dose interval extension was feasible in 33.3% of patients. No tumor progression, infection reactivation, major cardiovascular events, or severe adverse events were observed.ConclusionsTralokinumab showed sustained effectiveness and a favorable safety profile in clinically complex patients with AD. These real-world findings support IL-13 blockade as a valuable therapeutic option for multimorbid populations that are underrepresented in clinical trials.

Introduction

Randomized clinical trials (RCTs) in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD) frequently exclude patients with significant comorbidities, including active or previous malignancy, chronic infections, advanced organ dysfunction, and neurologic disorders. Consequently, evidence to guide therapeutic decision-making for these clinically complex “special populations” remains limited in daily practice.

Tralokinumab is a fully human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that selectively neutralizes interleukin-13 (IL-13), a central cytokine in type 2 inflammation. Phase III ECZTRA trials and their long-term extension studies have demonstrated sustained efficacy and a favorable safety profile over several years of follow-up [1]. However, real-world data in clinically complex and high-risk populations remain scarce.

Recent European guidelines and expert consensus statements emphasize individualized treatment selection in AD, particularly in elderly and comorbid patients, prioritizing targeted therapies with favorable safety profiles [2–5]. This study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of tralokinumab in patients with moderate-to-severe AD from to special populations in a real-world multicenter setting.

Methods

This retrospective, non-interventional multicenter study was conducted in dermatology departments of Spanish tertiary hospitals between June 2022 and December 2025 under routine clinical practice conditions. Adult patients with moderate-to-severe AD treated with tralokinumab and belonging to predefined special populations were included.

Special populations were defined by the presence of clinically relevant comorbidities frequently underrepresented or excluded in RCTs, including solid malignancies, hematologic neoplasms, cardiovascular disease (including arrhythmias, valvular heart disease, or advanced heart failure), advanced chronic kidney disease (stage IV or higher), chronic infections (latent tuberculosis infection or HIV), and neurologic disorders.

Baseline mean EASI was 19.3, and pruritus NRS was 7. After treatment, EASI decreased to 3.45 (≈ 82% reduction) and pruritus NRS to 2 (≈ 71% reduction) after a mean follow-up of 24.6 months. Approximately 70% of patients achieved EASI ≤ 3, and 50% achieved near-complete clearance (EASI 0–1).

Clinical improvement was observed across comorbidity subgroups, including patients with prior solid tumors, hematologic malignancies, advanced chronic kidney disease, latent tuberculosis infection, and HIV infection. The median exposure time was 24.6 (IQR 20.3–32.4) months. The cohort included heterogeneous and frequently treatment-refractory AD phenotypes (Table 1), including prurigo-type AD, nummular eczema, classic flexural eczema, and generalized lichenified forms. Prurigo-dominant phenotypes showed marked improvement in several cases, including complete or near-complete clearance. Clinical benefit was observed across morphologic subtypes.

Dose optimization was feasible in seven patients (33.3%): six patients extended the interval to every 4 weeks and one to every 3 weeks, while maintaining adequate disease control. No cases of tumor progression, tuberculosis reactivation, opportunistic infections, or major cardiovascular events were observed. No severe adverse events leading to treatment discontinuation were recorded, and no clinically significant worsening of baseline comorbidities was identified.

Discussion

This real-world multicenter study demonstrates sustained effectiveness and an acceptable safety profile of tralokinumab in a clinically complex population of patients with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (AD). The magnitude of EASI reduction observed in our cohort, with an approximate 82% decrease from baseline and marked improvement in pruritus scores, is consistent with the efficacy reported in phase III ECZTRA trials and their long-term extension studies [1]. Importantly, our findings extend these results to patients who are frequently excluded from randomized clinical trials (RCTs), including individuals with malignancy, chronic infections, advanced organ dysfunction, and neurologic disease.

The high prevalence of elderly and multimorbid patients in this cohort reflects routine clinical practice. AD in older adults has been described as a distinct phenotype associated with increased comorbidity burden and greater limitations in systemic treatment selection [4]. In this context, safer targeted therapies are particularly needed. Recent real-world data, including studies by Emi Sato et al., have demonstrated significant clinical improvement and good tolerability of tralokinumab in patients aged ≥ 70 years [5, 6]. Our findings are consistent with these observations and further support the feasibility of selective IL-13 inhibition in elderly and multimorbid patients.

Patients with current or previous malignancy represent one of the most challenging subgroups in the management of moderate-to-severe AD. Conventional systemic immunosuppressants and some targeted therapies may raise concerns regarding cancer recurrence or progression. In our cohort, patients with both solid and hematologic malignancies, including cases of active malignancy, experienced sustained clinical improvement without evidence of tumor progression during follow-up. These findings are consistent with emerging evidence supporting an acceptable oncologic safety profile of biologics targeting the IL-4/IL-13 pathway [7, 8]. A review by Ippoliti et al. [9] reported low malignancy rates across clinical trials including more than 2600 patients treated with tralokinumab for up to 4.5 years. Similarly, no cases of infection reactivation were observed among patients with chronic infections such as HIV infection or latent tuberculosis. Our results also align with expert consensus recommendations favoring targeted biologics in special populations [2] and with recent European guideline updates advocating individualized, risk-adapted therapeutic strategies [3].

Another defining characteristic of this cohort was the high burden of multimorbidity and polypharmacy. All patients were receiving multiple concomitant medications, with a mean of 3.76 (SD 3.91), reflecting the complexity of this population. In such settings, systemic therapies with minimal drug-drug interaction potential are particularly valuable. Biologic agents such as tralokinumab, which are not metabolized by cytochrome P450 pathways, may offer practical advantages in patients on multiple medications. Additionally, treatment interval extension was achievable in approximately 30% of patients, consistent with real-world optimization strategies described in elderly cohorts [5, 7], and suggesting sustained disease control in a subset of patients.

An additional strength of this study is the relatively long treatment exposure, with a mean follow-up of 24.6 months, allowing evaluation of intermediate-term effectiveness and safety in a clinically complex population. Although larger studies with longer follow-up are still needed to better characterize long-term oncologic safety, the observation period in our cohort is relatively prolonged compared with many currently available real-world reports in special populations. Therefore, while definitive conclusions regarding malignancy risk cannot be established, our findings provide additional real-world data supporting the feasibility of tralokinumab in clinically complex patients. [9] However, this study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, its observational and retrospective design may introduce selection bias and limit the ability to establish causal relationships. Second, the relatively small sample size reduces statistical power and may limit the generalizability of the findings. In addition, the absence of a control group precludes direct comparison with other therapeutic strategies, and the heterogeneity of the population, although reflective of real-world practice, may introduce variability in treatment response and outcomes. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory real-world observations rather than definitive evidence of long-term safety.

Despite these limitations, the inclusion of medically complex patients who are typically excluded from randomized clinical trials, together with the relatively long follow-up period, enhances the clinical relevance and external validity of these findings. Overall, this study provides meaningful real-world evidence supporting the effectiveness and safety of tralokinumab in routine dermatologic practice, particularly in challenging patient populations with a significant burden of comorbidities.

Conclusion

In this real-world multicenter cohort, tralokinumab demonstrated sustained clinical effectiveness and a favorable safety profile in patients with moderate-to-severe AD from clinically complex populations. Clinical benefit was observed across diverse comorbidity profiles, including elderly individuals, and patients with a history of malignancy, chronic infections such as HIV, and advanced systemic diseases. Importantly, no cases of tumor progression, reactivation of infection, or worsening of baseline comorbidities were observed during follow-up, although larger studies with longer follow-up are needed.

These findings suggest that selective IL-13 blockade may represent a valuable and generally well-tolerated therapeutic strategy in multimorbid patients with AD receiving multiple concomitant medications, a population that is often underrepresented in randomized clinical trials.

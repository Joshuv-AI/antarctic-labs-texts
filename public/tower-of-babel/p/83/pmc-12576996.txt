Factors related to quality of life in caregivers of children with autism spectrum disorder: emphasizing challenges in the context of Eastern China
Authors: Xu Chen, Jiao Tong, Xue Wang, Lu Zhu, Yiting Tao, Haiyan Zhao, Changjiang Liu, Yu Wang, Cheng Zhao, Dongmei Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCaregivers of children with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) encounter multiple challenges, increasing their risk of experiencing a low quality of life (QOL). Therefore, this study aims to examine the QOL among caregivers of children with ASD in eastern China and to identify the associated factors across many aspects.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted across five institutions in Lianyungang, Jiangsu Province, eastern China. A self-administered questionnaire included child characteristics, caregiver characteristics, the World Health Organization Quality of Life 8 questions (WHOQOL-8), the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), the Parenting Stress Index-Short Form (PSI-SF), the Family APGAR index (APGAR), and the medical service quality questionnaire were used to collect data. Multiple linear regression models were used to identify factors associated with QOL among caregivers of children with ASD.ResultsOverall, 405 caregivers of children with ASD participated in the study. The mean QOL score was 26.09 ± 5.03, with a median score of 26.00 (23.00, 30.00). Multiple linear regression analysis revealed that lower QOL was significantly associated with being a mother (β = -1.704, 95% CI: -2.796 - -0.612; P < 0.05), resided in rural areas (β = -0.941, 95% CI: -1.775 - -0.108; P < 0.05), higher insomnia symptoms (β = -0.256, 95% CI: -0.327 - -0.184; P < 0.001), and higher parenting stress (β = -0.089, 95% CI: -0.127 - -0.051; P < 0.001). However, caregivers with at least a high school education (β = 1.539, 95% CI: 0.696–2.382; P < 0.001), reported no chronic disease (β = 2.169, 95% CI: 0.449–3.889; P < 0.05), experienced good family function (β = 0.296, 95% CI: 0.150–0.442; P < 0.001), and perceived high quality of medical services (β = 0.264, 95% CI: 0.086–0.442; P < 0.05) exhibited significantly higher QOL.ConclusionsThe findings highlighted that the QOL in caregivers of children with ASD was lower, and more attention needed to be paid to caregivers who were mothers, lived in rural areas, had an education level of junior high school or below, and had chronic diseases. In addition, it was necessary to develop coping strategies to address insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25000-4.Keywords: Quality of life, Caregivers, Children, Autism spectrum disorder, China

Background

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD), a complex neurodevelopmental disorder is characterized by challenges in social interaction, communication deficits, and restricted and repetitive behavioral patterns or interests [1]. In the United States, approximately 1 in 36 8-year-old children has been diagnosed with ASD, making it one of the most common neurodevelopmental disorders in the world [2]. It is worth noting that caregivers of children with ASD, typically parents, experience a lower quality of life (QOL) [3]. This could potentially have a negative impact on the general goals, family structure, and social interactions of caregivers of individuals with ASD [4]. The lower QOL experienced by caregivers of children with ASD may worsen over time [5], potentially leading to significant detrimental effects on the development of the child [6]. Therefore, it is crucial to pay more attention to the QOL of caregivers of children with ASD.

The World Health Organization defines QOL as “an individual’s perception of their position in life in the context of culture and value system in which they live and in relation to their goals, expectations, standards, and concerns” [7, 8]. However, the lower QOL of caregivers of children with ASD is often overlooked. Public awareness of these challenges remains limited, and even fewer individuals actively seek or access appropriate healthcare services. Caregivers, particularly parents, play a crucial role in managing the healthcare, rehabilitation, and service coordination needs of children with ASD [9]. This requires caregivers to dedicate more time, energy, and adjust their own behavior to engage in children’s learning, playing, and interactive communication. However, a lower QOL among caregivers of children with ASD is associated with reduced happiness and well-being, along with increased physical and psychological health problems, increased marital tension, and lower family cohesion [3, 10–12]. Studies show that a higher QOL among parents of children with ASD is associated with greater preparedness and supportiveness in managing the needs of their child [13]. This contributes to improved treatment outcomes for children with ASD. Consequently, evaluating the QOL of caregivers and identifying related factors is essential for optimizing rehabilitation interventions for these children.

A growing body of research has focused on the QOL of caregivers of children with ASD to better understand the challenges they face and to identify potential sources of support [14]. Parents of atypically developing children, including those with ASD, have their QOL influenced by a complex system of factors: socio-demographic, socio-ecological, socio-psychological factors, and those related to their children’s special condition [15, 16]. Studies show that parental age, sex, marital status, family income, education level, coping strategies, sense of coherence, family medical history, and mental health conditions may predict lower QOL among caregivers [16–18]. Additionally, sleep problems significantly influence the QOL of caregivers of children with ASD. Caregivers reporting non-restorative or insufficient sleep have lower QOL than those without these sleep-related symptoms [19]. Most studies examining the QOL of caregivers of children with ASD have been conducted in developed countries, particularly in Europe and the United States [20]. However, while previous studies have primarily examined only a few factors influencing the QOL of parents of children with ASD, such research remains limited in China. Limited public attention on the QOL of caregivers may prevent children with ASD from receiving effective rehabilitation. Consequently, identifying the predictive factors affecting the QOL of caregivers is essential for developing strategies that enhance their parenting capacity.

Therefore, this study aims to investigate the QOL of caregivers of children with ASD and to provide an in-depth analysis of the effects of child characteristics, caregiver characteristics, insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality on QOL. The findings could offer valuable insights to inform future interventions for enhancing the QOL of caregivers of children with ASD. Additionally, it could contribute to more effective treatment outcomes for children with ASD and potentially promote the overall well-being of their entire families.

Methods

Study design, setting, and target population

A cross-sectional study was conducted across five institutions located in Lianyungang, Jiangsu Province, eastern China. These comprised the largest maternal and child health hospital in the city (Lianyungang Maternal and Child Health Hospital Affiliated to Kangda College of Nanjing Medical University) and four rehabilitation centers providing intervention and training services for children with ASD (Lianyungang Social Welfare Center, Lianyungang Aiji Ruili Children’s Potential Development Center, Donghai Guanai Disabled Children Rehabilitation and Education Service Center, and Guanyun Rehabilitation Hospital). The target population consisted of caregivers of children with ASD undergoing rehabilitation at five institutions between November 2023 and May 2024, including mothers, fathers, and other primary caregivers.

Inclusion criteria, sampling method, and sample size

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) caregivers of children diagnosed with ASD based on the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5); (2) caregivers of children with ASD aged ≤ 14 years; (3) caregivers aged ≥ 18 years; (4) caregivers living with the child with ASD; (5) caregivers with no history of mental illness; (6) caregivers able to comprehend the content of the questionnaire; and (7) caregivers who provided informed consent to participate in the study. The required sample size for this study was calculated using the single-population proportion formula. To achieve the maximum sample size, the parameters were set as follows: P = 50%, 95% confidence interval (CI), margin of error = 5%, and non-response rate = 10%. The calculated sample was 423. Overall, 425 caregivers of children with ASD were recruited through a convenience sampling technique. Among these, 20 caregivers who did not complete the questionnaire were excluded. The final analysis included complete data from 405 caregivers, resulting in an effective response rate of 95.29%.

Data collection tools

A self-administered questionnaire, developed based on a literature review and expert consultation, was used to collect data. The questionnaire included child characteristics, caregiver characteristics, QOL, insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality (Additional File 1). Trained and experienced surveyors distributed and collected the questionnaires. Prior to the formal commencement of the study, a pilot survey was conducted at the study site to refine the questionnaire.

Child characteristics included age, sex, only-child status, symptom severity, current verbal ability, and stereotyped behaviors. Stereotyped behaviors were assessed using the Stereotypy Subscale of the Repetitive Behavior Scale-Revised (RBS-R) [21, 22], evaluating the occurrence of repeated actions with no apparent purpose [23]. The subscale comprises six items: body movements, head movements, finger movements, locomotion, object usage, and sensory. Each item was rated on a 4-point Likert severity scale ranging from 0 (behavior does not occur) to 3 (behavior occurs and is a severe problem). The total score, calculated by summing the scores of six items, ranges from 0 to 18, with higher scores indicating greater severity of stereotyped behaviors. Any total score > 0 indicated the presence of stereotyped behaviors. It has demonstrated good internal consistency and retest reliability in Chinese children with ASD [21]. In the current study, its Cronbach’s coefficient was 0.818.

Caregiver characteristics included the caregiver’s age, role, place of residence, education level, monthly household income per capita, chronic diseases, and frequency of exercise.

QOL was assessed using the World Health Organization Quality of Life 8 questions (WHOQOL-8), derived from the WHOQOL-100 and WHOQOL-BREF instruments [24–26]. The WHOQOL-8 comprises eight items addressing health, energy, daily living, self-satisfaction, interpersonal relationships, financial resources, living environment, and overall perception of QOL. Caregivers rated each item on a 5-point Likert scale from 1 to 5. The total score, calculated by summing the 8 item scores, ranged from 8 to 40, with higher scores indicating better QOL. The WHOQOL-8 is a practical instrument for use in epidemiological studies and has demonstrated good internal consistency across several countries [24, 25, 27]. In this study, the Cronbach’s α was 0.886.

Insomnia symptoms were assessed using the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) [28], a widely used self-report questionnaire for evaluating the severity of insomnia symptoms [29]. The ISI comprises seven items, each rated on a 5-point scale from 0 to 4. Total scores range from 0 to 28, with higher scores indicating more severe insomnia symptoms. The ISI has been widely validated and is recognized as a reliable and effective measure [28, 30]. In this study, the Cronbach’s α was 0.937.

Parenting stress was measured using the Parenting Stress Index-Short Form (PSI-SF), revised by Luo et al. [31]. The scale comprises 15 items divided into three dimensions: parental distress, parent-child dysfunctional interaction, and difficult child. Caregivers rated each item on a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The total score was calculated by summing responses to the 15 items, resulting in a range from 15 to 75. Higher scores indicate greater levels of parenting stress experienced by caregivers. The instrument has demonstrated strict measurement invariance and satisfactory internal consistency among Chinese parents [31, 32]. In this study, the Cronbach’s α was 0.927.

Family function was assessed using the Family APGAR index (APGAR), developed by Smilkstein G. in 1978 [33]. The instrument comprises five items evaluating adaptation, partnership, growth, affection, and resolve. Caregivers rated each item on a 3-point Likert scale, ranging from 0 (hardly ever) to 2 (almost always). The total score was calculated by summing the scores for five items, resulting in a range from 0 to 10. Higher scores indicated better family function of the caregivers. The APGAR index has demonstrated satisfactory reliability and validity [34–36]. In this study, the Cronbach’s α was 0.903.

Medical service quality was assessed using a questionnaire developed through review of literature and expert consultation [37]. The questionnaire included three items addressing satisfaction of medical and rehabilitation needs, skills and expertise, and caution in attitude and behavior. Each item was rated on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The total score, calculated by summing the three item scores, ranged from 3 to 15, with higher scores indicating better medical service quality. In this study, the Cronbach’s α was 0.873.

Statistical analysis

EpiData 3.1 software (EpiData Association, Odense, Denmark) was used to build the database. Statistical analysis was conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 21.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, State of New York, USA). Descriptive statistics were calculated, and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was conducted to evaluate the normality of continuous variables. Parenting stress scores followed a normal distribution, while scores for QOL, insomnia symptoms, family function, and medical service quality did not. Normally distributed continuous variables are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while non-normally distributed continuous variables are presented as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were summarized using frequencies and percentages. Univariate analyses were performed. The Mann-Whitney U test and Kruskal-Wallis H test were used to compare differences in QOL scores among caregivers with different characteristics. When statistically significant differences were observed among multiple groups, a post-hoc test was conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test. P-values were corrected using the Bonferroni method. Spearman correlation analysis was conducted to examine the associations between insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality with QOL. Multivariate analysis was performed using multiple linear regression to identify factors independently associated with QOL in caregivers of children with ASD. The dependent variable was the QOL score, and the independent variables were those found to be statistically significant in the univariate analysis. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Quality of life

Overall, 405 caregivers of children with ASD participated in the study. The mean QOL score was 26.09 ± 5.03, while the median score was 26.00 (23.00–30.00).

Child characteristics and their relationship with quality of life

Among the 405 children with ASD, 286 (70.62%) were male. The mean age was 6.53 ± 2.59 years (range: 2–14 years), and more than half (55.31%) were ≤ 6 years. Approximately one-third (33.83%) were only children, and 93 (22.96%) exhibited mild symptoms. Regarding current verbal ability, 36.79% could produce sentences containing ≥ 4 words or complex sentence structures. Stereotyped behaviors were observed in 84.44% of the children. Univariate analysis showed significant differences in QOL among caregivers of children with ASD who raised children with ASD with different symptom severity, current verbal ability, and stereotyped behaviors (P < 0.05). Post-hoc test for symptom severity revealed significant differences between the mild symptoms and severe symptoms, as well as between the moderate and severe groups (Table 1).

Caregiver characteristics and their relationship with quality of life

Among the 405 caregivers of children with ASD, approximately three-quarters (75.31%) were mothers. The mean caregiver age was 37.38 ± 8.60 (range: 24–71 years), with over three-fifths (63.46%) aged 31–40 years. Approximately half of the caregivers (48.64%) resided in rural areas. Regarding education, 182 caregivers (44.94%) had attained a junior high school education or lower. Fewer than one-quarter (23.21%) reported a family monthly per capita income > 5,000 yuan. A small proportion (5.93%) had chronic diseases, and the majority (61.73%) engaged in exercise < 3 days per week. Univariate analysis showed that the QOL in caregivers of children with ASD with different caregiver roles, place of residence, education level, monthly household income per capita, chronic diseases, and frequency of exercise were significantly different (P < 0.05). Post-hoc test of caregiver role revealed significant differences between fathers and mothers, as well as between fathers and others. Post-hoc test of monthly household income per capita identified significant differences between earning ≤ 3,000 yuan and earning 3,001–5,000 yuan, as well as between earning ≤ 3,000 yuan and earning ≥ 5,000 yuan (Table 2).

Insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality, and their relationship with quality of life

The mean parenting stress score was 44.40 ± 12.22. The median scores for insomnia symptoms, family function, and medical service quality were 6.00 (2.00, 11.00), 6.00 (5.00, 9.00), and 12.00 (10.00, 12.00), respectively. Correlation analysis revealed that insomnia symptoms and parenting stress were significantly negatively correlated with QOL among caregivers of children with ASD (r = −0.462 and − 0.443, respectively; P < 0.001), while family function and the medical service quality were positively correlated with QOL (r = 0.408 and 0.233, respectively; P < 0.001) (Table 3).

Multiple linear regression models to identify factors related to quality of life

Multiple linear regression models were estimated to explore the factors related to QOL in caregivers of children with ASD found that mothers had lower QOL than fathers (β = −1.704, 95% CI: −2.796 - −0.612, P < 0.05). Caregivers of children with ASD living in rural areas had lower QOL than caregivers living in urban areas (β = −0.941, 95% CI: −1.775 - −0.108, P < 0.05). Caregivers of children with ASD who had an education level of high school or above had higher QOL than caregivers who had an education level of junior high school or below (β = 1.539, 95% CI: 0.696–2.382, P < 0.001). Caregivers of children with ASD without chronic diseases had higher QOL than caregivers with chronic diseases (β = 2.169, 95% CI: 0.449–3.889, P < 0.05). In addition, caregivers with higher insomnia symptoms (β = −0.256, 95% CI: −0.327 - −0.184, P < 0.001) and parenting stress (β = −0.089, 95% CI: −0.127 - −0.051, P < 0.001) had lower QOL. However, caregivers with good family function (β = 0.296, 95% CI: 0.150–0.442, P < 0.001) and high medical service quality (β = 0.264, 95% CI: 0.086–0.442, P < 0.05) had higher QOL. Thus, the caregiver roles, place of residence, education level, chronic diseases, insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality can be used to predict QOL in caregivers of children with ASD (Table 4).

Discussion

This research evaluated the current status of QOL in caregivers of autistic children in eastern China and analyzed its predictive factors from multiple perspectives. To our knowledge, this study filled a gap in research related to QOL in caregivers of autistic children in eastern China. The results showed that the mean QOL score among caregivers was 26.09 ± 5.03. The findings suggest that caregivers of autistic children in eastern China experience a low QOL. It is essential to promptly implement targeted and effective measures to improve the QOL of caregivers of autistic children. Several predictors influencing QOL were identified in this research, including caregiver role, place of residence, education level, presence of chronic diseases, insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality.

In this study, mothers reported lower QOL than fathers. In traditional Chinese culture, mothers typically bear the primary caregiving responsibilities. Compared to fathers or other caregivers, mothers are more likely to experience physical pain, fatigue, and mental health challenges, leading to a decrease in their QOL [5, 38]. Therefore, QOL interventions should prioritize the needs of mothers. Furthermore, this research found that the place of residence played an important role in QOL of caregivers, aligning with previous research results [39]. In the Chinese context, this disparity is particularly pronounced. Compared to their urban counterparts, caregivers in rural areas typically have less access to healthcare services, social support, and financial resources, which may have a negative impact on their QOL [40–42]. Consequently, targeted interventions should prioritize rural caregivers to improve their QOL. This research also found that caregivers with a lower education level may experience a lower QOL. On the one hand, these caregivers often feel frustrated due to limited knowledge and understanding of the condition, unsatisfactory rehabilitation outcomes, and insufficient caregiving skills [43]. On the other hand, they may lack the awareness or ability to actively seek and access appropriate healthcare services, further contributing to a lower QOL [44]. In conclusion, it is recommended that organizations providing rehabilitation services for children with ASD should focus on increasing publicity and education, as well as providing training in nursing skills for caregivers. It is also important to note that caregivers of autistic children who do not have chronic diseases experienced a better QOL than caregivers who have chronic diseases in this research, corresponding with previous research results [45]. However, it seems to be overlooked among caregivers. This could be attributed to caregivers with chronic diseases experiencing more physical pain and psychological issues. These findings highlight the importance of providing additional support and resources to caregivers managing chronic diseases.

This study revealed that the more severe symptoms were associated with lower QOL among caregivers of children with ASD, which was consistent with previous findings [46, 47]. Caregivers report more sleep disturbances and lower QOL than parents of typically developing children [19]. This may be attributed to the unsafe nighttime behaviors observed in children with ASD, such as self-injury, property damage, or elopement. Consequently, caregivers remain hypervigilant at night, potentially leading to the development of insomnia symptoms [19]. Insomnia symptoms not only increase the risk of caregivers developing cardiovascular diseases, hypertension, diabetes, or other conditions [48], but also exacerbate psychological challenges such as anxiety, depression, irritability, reduced sense of well-being, and a sense of inferiority [49]. These problems significantly affect the QOL of caregivers of children with ASD. In China, while several studies have discussed the factors influencing the QOL of caregivers in ASD [43, 50, 51], research on the effects of insomnia symptoms remains limited. Therefore, further research into the insomnia symptoms and their effect on the QOL of caregivers is necessary.

Furthermore, this study demonstrated that a higher level of parenting stress was associated with lower QOL among caregivers of autistic children, consistent with findings of previous studies [50, 52]. The severity of the disease, challenging behaviors, and child-related difficulties have been reported to exacerbate caregivers’ parenting stress and reduce their QOL [15, 52]. This may be because parenting stress not only contributes to a heavier economic burden, more strained interpersonal relationships, lower life satisfaction, but also affects the physical and psychological health of caregivers [52, 53]. These effects potentially weaken their ability to manage the behavioral problem of the child and meet family needs [53, 54]. Therefore, alleviating parenting stress among caregivers of children with ASD is essential.

The family function is a significant predictor of the QOL among caregivers of children with ASD [55]. This study also suggested that better family function is associated with higher QOL in this population. Positive family function not only helps maintain cohesion among family members, provides emotional support, and facilitates effective communication, but also creates an environment conducive to growth and fosters adaptive strategies, thereby enhancing the effectiveness of treatment for children [33, 56]. Future studies could focus on developing clinical interventions and support services, including family skills training (e.g., family education and communication enhancement, “Mind the Gap” training, problem-solving, and coping strategy support) and community-based support groups tailored to the entire family unit and so on [57–59]. These approaches may improve family function in households with children with ASD, thereby enhancing the QOL of caregivers.

This study found that higher medical service quality was associated with better QOL among caregivers. In other words, the quality of medical services is an important protective factor for the QOL of caregivers. Importantly, there is limited research on the relationship between medical service quality and QOL of caregivers of children with ASD. More interventions aimed at improving the medical service quality can be implemented to enhance the QOL of caregivers.

Limitation

This study has some limitations. First, causal relationships between variables and outcomes could not be established due to the cross-sectional study design. Consequently, future longitudinal studies are needed to further evaluate the identified associations. Second, the study was conducted in a single city, potentially limiting the generalizability of the findings to other regions with different socioeconomic and cultural factors. Third, the QOL of caregivers was measured at a single time point, and no follow-up assessments were conducted. Consequently, temporal fluctuations in QOL may not have been fully captured. Future research should incorporate longitudinal designs to better understand the trajectory of the QOL of caregivers and examine its relationship with the duration of diagnosis of the child with ASD. Fourth, the comparison of QOL between male and female caregivers in this study was based on different families. Future studies may consider recruiting male and female caregivers from the same household to more accurately examine sex-specific differences in QOL within the shared family context. Finally, the assessment of variables, including QOL, relied on self-reported data, potentially introducing bias. Future studies should consider alternative data collection methods to enable a more comprehensive analysis.

Conclusion

This study showed that caregivers of children with ASD in eastern China experience relatively low QOL. Key predictors of the QOL of caregivers include caregiver role, place of residence, education level, chronic diseases, insomnia symptoms, parenting stress, family function, and medical service quality. Therefore, it is crucial to develop intervention strategies to improve QOL in caregivers of children with ASD. Efforts should focus on reducing risk factors and strengthening protective factors for QOL to improve the physical and mental health of caregivers and children with ASD.

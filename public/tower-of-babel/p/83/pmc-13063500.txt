Real-world effectiveness of measles vaccine and associated factors among children aged 9–59 months during epidemic resurgence in the Democratic Republic of Congo: a nationwide test-negative case-control study
Authors: Wilfried Nlandu Tembele, Piejetty Nkosi Nsona, Antoine Nguend, Franck Ndanu, Bénie Eale, Steve Ahuka Mundeke, L. K. Mangyanda, Didine Kaba, Daniel Katuashi Ishoso
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundMeasles remains a major public health concern in the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC) despite ongoing vaccination efforts. During 2023, the country reported nearly 300,000 suspected cases, disproportionately affecting children under five years of age. This study aimed to estimate the real-world effectiveness of the measles vaccine (MV) and associated factors among children aged 9–59 months.MethodsWe conducted a nationwide test-negative case-control study using laboratory-based surveillance. Data was collected between January 2020 and December 2023 across 25 provinces. Children aged 9–59 months with documented vaccination status were included. Cases were 4,317 IgM-positive measles children, and controls were 3,732 IgM-negative children. Multivariable logistic regression was performed adjusted for age (months), sex, year of diagnosis, and province of residence. Vaccine effectiveness (VE) was calculated as (1-adjusted odds ratio x 100).ResultsAmong the included children, vaccination was significantly associated with reduced odds of measles (aOR = 0.59; 95% CI 0.52–0.66; p˂0.001), corresponding to an adjusted vaccine effectiveness of 41.4% (95% CI: 34.4–47.8). Vaccination coverage in the analytic sample was 22.4%. Significant temporal and geographical heterogeneity were observed, particularly during the 2022–2023 epidemic resurgence.ConclusionsThe moderate VE observed in nationwide surveillance data highlights operational gaps in routine immunization delivery that require targeted program strengthening.

Background

Measles remains an extremely infectious illness that continues to spread substantial morbidity and mortality worldwide despite a safe and effective vaccine [1–4]. Although global measles mortality declined markedly after 2000 due to expanded immunization programs that prevented an estimated 60 million deaths. Recent declines in vaccination coverage have led to renewed outbreaks in several regions [5]. In 2023 alone, millions of cases were reported globally, disproportionately affecting children under five years of age [4].

The African region bears a disproportionate burden of measles transmission. Accounting for nearly half or major global outbreaks in recent years [4]. Routine measles vaccination coverage remains insufficient to achieve herd immunity, with WHO estimate indicating 76% coverage for the first dose and only 34% for the second dose in Africa [6]. Disruptions linked to the COVID-19 pandemic further exacerbated immunity gaps, with approximately 61 million measles vaccine doses missed or delayed across 18 African countries [7].

In the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), measles vaccine is delivered through the Expanded Program on Immunization (EPI) and periodic supplementary immunization activities (SIA). A single dose at 9 months was used until 2021, when a second dose at 15–18 months was introduced in line with WHO recommendations [8, 9]. However, national survey data indicate persistent suboptimal coverage. With first-dose coverage remaining below the 95% elimination threshold and second-dose coverage only 21.6% in 2023 [10, 11]. These immunity gaps have contributed to recurrent large-scale epidemics, positioning measles as a persistent public health priority in the country.

Under optimal conditions, measles vaccine effectiveness (VE) is estimated at 85–95% after one dose and above 95% after two doses [12]. The Test-negative design (TND) conducted in several sub-Saharan African countries reported VE between 85 and 93% depending on dose number and vaccination timing [7]. Nevertheless, real-world VE may differ from clinical or trial-based estimates due to programmatic constraints, delayed vaccination, cold-chain challenges, and heterogeneous exposure risk. Assessing VE in routine conditions is therefore essential to evaluate immunization program performance and guide control strategies [13].

Despite the high burden of measles in the DRC, empirical evidence on VE under routine program conditions remains limited. This study aimed to estimate the real-world effectiveness of at least one dose of MV among children aged 9–59 months using a test-negative case-control design based on national surveillance data. And to identify sociodemographic, temporal, and geographic factors associated with measles occurrence. By providing country-specific VE estimates in a context of epidemic resurgence, this study seeks to inform immunization policy, support optimization of routine and supplementary vaccination strategies, and contribute to measles elimination efforts in the DRC.

Methods

Objectives

This study aimed to assess real-world effectiveness of MV among children aged 9 to 59 months during the epidemic resurgence in the DRC from 2020 to 2023.

The specific objectives were to estimate the real-world vaccine effectiveness of at least one dose of measles vaccine using a nationwide test-negative case-control design, and to identify sociodemographic, temporal, and geographic factors associated with measles occurrence.

Study design and setting

We conducted a test-negative case-control study from 2020 January 1 to 2023 December 31, across 25 provinces in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). These concerned provinces are Kinshasa, Kongo Central, Kwango, Kwilu, Kasaï-Central, Kasaï-Oriental, Lomami, Haut-Lomami, Tanganyika, Tshuapa, Tshopo, Équateur, Nord-Ubangi, Sud-Ubangi, Haut-Uélé, Bas-Uele, Maniema, Ituri, Nord-Kivu, Sud-Kivu, Maindombe, Kasaï, Mongala, Lualaba, and Sankuru.

The TND to evaluate the VE under real-world conditions, assumes that the vaccine does not influence other unrelated pathogens that might present with similar symptoms [14]. Thus making it ideal for assessing VE by comparing confirmed cases with non-cases within the same target group [15].

Study population

All children aged 9–59 months with accessible information in the nationwide measles monitoring system were considered for inclusion. Eligible children were classified according to the TND (Fig. 1).

Inclusion criteria: Eligible participants included children aged 9–59 months recorded in the measles surveillance system between 2020 and 2023. Cases were defined as children with laboratory-confirmed measles (IgM positive) and documented vaccination status. Controls were suspected cases who tested negative for measles IgM and had a documented vaccination.

Exclusion Criteria: Children without documented vaccination status, those with invalid or non-compliant samples, missing laboratory results, or incomplete age or sex information were excluded from the analysis.

Sampling

Given the use of nationwide surveillance data, the study sample was determined by data availability rather than by a predefined sample size calculation. The final analytic sample (n = 8,049) provided sufficient statical power to detect moderate associations between vaccination and laboratory-confirmed measles.

Study variables

The dependent variable was measles status (IgM positive vs. IgM negative). The primary independent variable was vaccination status (vaccinated vs. unvaccinated). Additional covariates included age (in months), sex, year of diagnosis, and province of residence.

Operational definitions

Operational definitions were established to ensure consistent classification of study participants. A suspected measles case was defined as any child aged 9–59 months presenting with fever and a generalized maculopapular rash accompanied by cough, coryza, or conjunctivitis, or any case suspected by a clinician or identified through active surveillance. A confirmed measles case was defined as a suspected case with a positive IgM serological result. Controls were defined as suspected measles cases with a negative IgM result or cases discarded following laboratory testing. A vaccinated child was defined as a child who had reportedly received at least one dose of measles vaccine (routine or campaign) administered at least one month prior to the onset of illness. Vaccination status was determined based on recorded vaccination dates in the surveillance database; children without a documented vaccination date were classified as unvaccinated.

Data source

The data was sourced from the national laboratory-surveillance measles system, spanning 2020 January 1, to 2023 December 31. It’s managed by the INRB in Kinshasa/DRC. This system, established in 2002 concurrent with the start of supplementary immunization activities (SIAs), gathers information from health facilities and active case-tracking efforts [13]. Suspected cases are reported through the Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response system (IDSR, third edition) utilizing DHIS2 and IDS tools, complemented by line lists at the health Zone level. All lab tests were administered at the INRB, the designated WHO measles-rubella reference lab for 25 of the 26 provinces in the DRC.

Data management and statistical analysis

All analyses were conducted using SPSS Statistics version 25.0. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic and epidemiological characteristics of measles cases and test-negative controls. Categorical variables were compared using chi-square tests. Vaccine effectiveness (VE) was estimated using multivariable logistic regression within a test-negative design framework. The dependent variable was laboratory-confirmed measles status (case = 1, test-negative control = 0). The main exposure variable was measles vaccination status (vaccinated vs. unvaccinated).

The primary adjusted model included age, sex (gender), year of diagnosis (2020–2023), and province as covariates to control for potential confounding related to demographic and geographic heterogeneity and temporal epidemic variation. Adjusted odds ratios (aOR) and 95¨% confidence intervals (CI) were obtained from the regression model. VE was calculated as: VE= (1-aOR) x 100.

A secondary adjusted model including the estimated probability of vaccination (propensity score) as a covariate was fitted to assess robustness of the association. The PS for vaccination was estimated using age, sex, year of diagnosis, and province and included as additional covariate in the logistic regression model. The PS-adjusted model yielded a lower but directionally consistent estimate of vaccine protection compared with the primary model.

All statical tests were two-sided, and p-values < 0.05 were considered statically significant.

Additional Analyses were the measles attack rate among children aged 9–59 months in the DRC was calculated. Subgroup analyses were conducted to examine yearly VE variations. And relationships between vaccination coverage and measles occurrence were considered.

Bias management

Compared to traditional cohort and case-control designs, the test-negative design minimizes bias associated with misclassification and healthcare-seeking behavior. However, unmeasured confounding is still a possibility. To address this: binary logistic regression was performed. In addition, propensity score adjustments were utilized. Potential unmeasured confounders like immune status, vaccine storage conditions, nutritional status, exact age at vaccination, and dose quantity could not be controlled due to data limitations, possibly introducing remaining bias.

Results

Descriptive data

Out of 8,049 children among 9–59 months were included in the national measles laboratory-surveillance system, from January 2020 to December 2023. 4,317 children (53.6%) were IgM measles confirmed, and 3,732 children (46.4%) test-negative controls. Rubella was tested in all children with a negative measles diagnosis, where 13% were positive for rubella IgM and was non-included in the study.

Socio-demographic characteristics of participants

The cases median age was 24 months (IQR: 16–36), and controls median age was 22 months (IQR: 15–33); with boys making up 52.7% of the study group. Among the confirmed cases, only 17.0% (95% CI: 14.3–19.7) had been vaccinated, while 83.0% (95% CI: 81.7–84.2) were unvaccinated. Équateur and Kasaï-Oriental provinces bore the brunt of the measles burden, each reporting at least 8 of the country’s total cases. In contrast, the provinces of Lualaba and Haut Lomami reported a very low percentage of cases (< 1) (Table 1).

Vaccination status and measles diagnosis of study participants

Vaccination status was defined strictly based on the presence of a recorded vaccination date in the surveillance database. Children without a documented vaccination date were classified as unvaccinated. In total, 1,803 out of the 8,049 subjects have received vaccination, leading to an immunization coverage of 22.4%. The proportion of laboratory-confirmed measles among tested suspected cases was 53.6% (95% CI: 53.5–54.1). Among those who contracted the illness, 735 had previously been inoculated, representing 17.0% (95% CI: 14.3–19.7), whereas among the unaffected group, 1,068 had received vaccines, comprising 28.6% (95% CI: 25.9–31.3). (Refer to Fig. 2 below).

Incidence of measles in the Democratic Republic of Congo

From 2020 to 2023, almost all DRC provinces served by the INRB laboratory had a cumulative incidence of over 50 per 100,000 inhabitants. This is well above the public health objective of eliminating this disease. In particular, the provinces of Équateur, Sud-Ubangi, Nord-Ubangi, Mongala, Tshuapa, Sankuru, Kasaï-Oriental, Lomami and Bas-Uele have had a cumulative incidence of over 200 per 100,000 children aged between 9 and 59 months over the last 4 years (Refer to Fig. 3 below).

Incidence by year of confirmed measles cases in the 4-years study period

The Figs. 4, 5, 6 and 7 below show that the annual incidence of confirmed measles cases in children aged 9–59 months was very high in 2023 compared with the previous two years. In 2023, Equator Province was the most affected, with an annual incidence of over 200 confirmed cases per 100,000 children aged 9–59 months (Refer to Fig. 7 below). Whereas in 2022, this burden was borne most by Kasaï-Oriental Province, with at least 184 confirmed cases of measles per 100,000 children aged 9 to 59 months, followed by Sud-Ubangi and Lomami, with 131 and 120 confirmed cases respectively per 100,000 of the population studied (Refer to Fig. 6). However, in 2021, it was the province of Nord-Ubangi that was most affected, with 89 confirmed cases of measles (Refer to Fig. 5). Although 2020 was the least deadly year, Sankuru province recorded more than 80 confirmed cases of measles per 100,000 children aged between 9 and 59 months (Refer to Fig. 4).

Sensitivity analysis

In sensitivity analysis including the propensity score, the adjusted odds ratio for vaccination was approximately 0.65. This corresponds to a VE of about 35%, with the direction and magnitude of protection remaining consistent with the primary model. This study also compares VE by province and by year. The results show substantial variations. In 2020, VE varied from 67% to 93% and was significant in 4 provinces: Kasaï-Central (93%; p = 0.030), Kasaï (86%, p = 0.026), Nord-Kivu (85%; p < 0.001) and Tanganyika (67%. p = 0.039). In 2021 MV did not demonstrate statistically significant efficacy. However, in 2022, in 5 provinces, the VE was significant and ranged from 57% to 79%: Kinshasa (79%, p = 0.026), Nord-Kivu (78%, p = 0.028), Sud-Ubangi (73%, p = 0.010), Tshopo (73%, p = 0.026) and Kasaï-Oriental (57%, p = 0.011). And in 2023, EV was statistically significant only in 2 provinces. respectively Kasaï-Central: 72%, p = 0.007 and Lomami: 61%, p = 0.013).

Factors influencing MV effectiveness under real-world conditions in DRC, 2020–2023

To identify possible factors influencing VE, a binary logistic regression was performed. Measles status was used as the dependent variable and vaccination as the main independent variable. The other independent covariates were introduced step by step, one after the other, with adjustment to check the effect of each variable on VE. The results show a statistically significant change in VE from 48.8% (OR = 0.512; p < 0.001) to 48.9% (OR = 0.511; p < 0.001) after introduction of the age covariate. Gender did not significantly modify EV. However, the introduction of the year of diagnosis variable significantly reduced the EV to 40.8% (OR = 0.592; p < 0.001), where the years 2022 and 2023 were more at risk (OR = 1.67; p < 0.001 and 2.30; p < 0.001 respectively), and the province variable reduced the EV to 41.4% (OR = 0.586. p < 0.001).

Discussion

The adjusted vaccine effectiveness of 41% (95% CI 34–48) observed in this study indicates moderate protection under real-world conditions in the DRC. Although lower than the 58–95% effectiveness reported under optimal conditions [7]. This estimate is consistent with observational studies conducted in high-transmission and resource-constrained settings. The observed moderate effectiveness should not be interpreted as reduced biological vaccine efficacy but rather as a reflection of programmatic performance under high transmission intensity and routine surveillance conditions.

Earlier research by Doshi et al. in the DRC [13] using the same test-negative design from 2011 to 2015 noted a VE of 80%. That study, performed during outbreak investigation in selected health zones, relied largely on vaccination information obtained through field verification and card recall. It was conducted in a more localized epidemiological context. In contrast, the present analysis used routinely collected nationwide surveillance data and considered vaccination status strictly based on documented dates recorded in the database. This difference in exposure ascertainment and study scale may partly explain the lower effectiveness observed in our study.

Moreover, several factors may explain the reduced effectiveness. Vaccination status relied exclusively on the presence of a documented vaccination date in the surveillance database, which may have introduced non-differential exposure misclassification if vaccinated children without recorded dates were classified as unvaccinated. Such misclassification would bias the odds ratio toward the null and lead to underestimation of true VE.

In addition, operational changes in the Democratic Republic of Congo-including disruptions in the cold chain, delayed vaccination. And limited access to timely immunization-may reduce vaccine-induced immunity under field conditions. The intense epidemic transmission observed during 2022–2023 may have partially overcome individual immunity in highly exposed populations.

Sensitivity analysis using a propensity score yielded a slightly lower but consistent effectiveness estimate (≈ 35%), supporting the robustness of the protection association between vaccination and reduced measles risk.

Study advantages and limitations

Using national surveillance data from the DRC (in 25 provinces), this study estimates real-world MV effectiveness in a high-transmission setting. Employment of test-negative design, minimizing bias from different healthcare approaches.

However, misclassification may have occurred if vaccinated children lacked documentation in the surveillance database. This could have biased the effectiveness estimate toward the null. Additional limitations may be use of secondary surveillance date, lack of information on time since vaccination and number of doses, absence of nutritional status and cold-chain indicators, and potential residual confounding from unmeasured influences.

Conclusion and recommendations

This study underscores the need to strengthen routine measles immunization in the DRC. Priority actions include improving the timeliness of vaccination and ensuring completion of the two-dose schedule. Strengthening cold-chain logistics, vaccine transport, and storage systems, along with continuous training of healthcare workers, is essential to maintain vaccine potency. In addition, improving the completeness and accuracy of vaccination records within the surveillance system is critical. Finally, enhancing community awareness and acceptance of measles vaccination remains a key priority, particularly in areas with persistent immunity gaps.

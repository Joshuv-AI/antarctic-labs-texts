LEF1 is associated with immunosuppressive microenvironment of patients with lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Xiaoqing Liu, Chunlou Wang, Xiaoling Zhang, Rongju Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Wnt/β-Catenin pathway plays an important role in the occurrence and progression of malignant tumors, especially PD-L1-mediated tumor immune evasion. However, the role of TCF/LEF, an important member of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, in the tumor immunosuppressive microenvironment of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remains unknown. LUAD tissue-coding RNA expression data from The Cancer Genome Atlas and TIMER databases were used to analyze the expression of TCF/LEF transcription factors and their correlation with various immune cell infiltration. Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence were used to detect tissue protein staining in 105 patients with LUAD. LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1 and TCF7L2 were all aberrantly expressed in the tumor tissues of LUAD patients with the data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database, tumor immune estimation resource (TIMER) database and results of immunohistochemistry, but only LEF1 expression was associated with 5-year overall survival in LUAD patients. LEF1 protein expression was associated with advanced tumor node metastasis (TNM) stage, lymphatic metastasis and local invasion in 105 cases LUAD patients. At the same time, LEF1 mRNA expression was also associated with immunosuppressive microenvironment in LUAD patients with the data from TCGA database and TIMER database. Results of immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence in tumor tissues of 105 cases LUAD patients showed that there was a positively correlation between LEF1 protein expression and the infiltration of M2 macrophages and Treg cells. LEF1 was highly expressed in tumor tissues of LUAD patients, and highly expressed LEF1 was associated with the immunosuppressive microenvironment of LUAD patients.

1. Introduction

According to the latest global cancer burden data released by the World Health Organization’s International Agency for Research on Cancer in 2020, lung cancer is the fifth most common malignant tumor in the world, but it is the malignant tumor with the highest mortality rate.[1] Worldwide, there are 2.2 million new cases of lung cancer each year, and more than 700,000 people die from lung cancer.[2] The incidence of lung cancer is the highest among all malignant tumors, with about 815,563 new cases of lung cancer every year, accounting for almost half of the world’s total.[3] Although the diagnosis rate of early lung cancer has been continuously improved with the continuous development and improvement of imaging technology, the diagnosis rate of early lung cancer is only 10%, and most lung cancer patients are at an advanced stage for the first time.[4,5] In terms of pathology, lung cancer can be divided into 2 categories, namely small cell lung cancer and non-small cell lung cancer, of which non-small cell lung cancer accounts for about 80%, and non-small cell lung cancer can be divided into adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, adenosquamous cell carcinoma, large cell carcinoma, and sarcomatoid carcinoma according to the pathological characteristics of the tumor, of which adenocarcinoma accounts for about 40%.[6,7] Importantly, previous studies found that different molecular types of lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) exhibited completely different prognosis.[8–10]

The interaction between programmed death factor-1 of T cells and programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) of tumor cells can inhibit the activation and effector function of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells, promote apoptosis of T cells and induction of regulatory T cells, and enable tumor cells to evade immune destruction.[11,12] Previous study has found that the expression level of PD-L1 in non-small cell lung cancer is significantly associated with male, smoking, higher tumor grade, tumor size, lymph node metastasis, pleural and vascular invasion, and high-grade LUAD.[13] Abnormal activation of Wnt/β-catenin pathway has been confirmed to play an important role in the progression of LUAD,[14–16] and nuclear accumulation of β-catenin has been shown to be positively correlated with adverse clinical outcomes such as cancer progression, invasion, metastasis, and recurrence.[17,18] Related mechanistic studies have shown that the β-catenin/TCF/LEF complex induced by the activation of β-catenin can bind to the promoter region of the CD274 gene, thereby inducing the expression of PD-L1.[19]

TCF/LEF transcription factors are important components of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, and is a class of transcription factors that recognize and bind DN through high-speed kinetic group domains, including TCF1 (TCF7), LEF1, TCF3 (TCF7L1), and TCF4 (TCF7L2). Previous studies have found that nuclear LEF1/TCF4 is related to poor prognosis in cerebral metastasis of LUAD,[20] and the aberrantly activated Wnt/TCF pathway promotes LUAD metastasis by regulating LEF1 expression.[21]

Herein, we analyzed the expression of TCF/LEF transcription factors in normal tissues and tumor tissues of LUAD patients, and to explore the correlation between their expression and overall survival, clinical characteristics. At the same time, we also analyzed the correlation between their expression and immune cell infiltration, immunosuppressive molecules in the tumor microenvironment of LUAD patients. These comprehensive analyses help to unravel the underlying mechanisms of TCF/LEF transcription factors and tumor microenvironment immunosuppression of LUAD patients.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Patients

Samples was collected from January 2022 to June 2023, including surgically resected tissues and bronchoscopy-acquired tissues from LUAD patients. Detailed information was also obtained regarding patient clinicopathological characteristics. All patients who contributed to the sample were informed of the content of the study, and the study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Cangzhou Central Hospital.

2.2. Immunohistochemistry

As previously described, paraffin-embedded tissue was prepared into 4 µm tissue sections for immunohistochemical assays. Briefly, tissue paraffin sections were deparaffinized and dehydrated and antigen retrieval was performed using 10 mmo/L sodium citrate solution (95°C, 15 minutes). After the sections are restored to room temperature, they are washed 3 times at room temperature with PBS buffer solution and then incubated with 1% triton X-100 solution for 15 minutes at room temperature to permeabilize the membrane. After washing 3 times with PBS buffer solution at room temperature, rabbit serum was added and incubated for 1 hour at room temperature. The serum was removed, the PBS buffer solution was washed 3 times at room temperature, and LEF1 rabbit monoclonal antibody (1:100, ab137872, abcam), TCF7 rabbit monoclonal antibody (1:100, 2203, Cell Signaling Technology), TCF7L1 rabbit monoclonal antibody (1:100, MAB61161, R&D) or TCF7L2 rabbit monoclonal antibody (1:50, ab134275, abcam) were added respectively, and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature. After removing the primary antibody and washing 3 times with PBS buffer solution at room temperature, incubate with Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (HRP; 1:1000) for 1 hour at room temperature. Finally, after removing the secondary antibody and washing 3 times (5 minutes/pass) of PBS buffer solution at room temperature, the sections were observed under a laser confocal fluorescence microscope and photographed. Aipathwell software (Servicebio) was used to determine positive cell staining density. A total of 105 LUAD patients were grouped based on the median expression of LEF1 in tumor tissues, and the expression level above the median was the high-LEF1 group, while the expression level below the median was the low-LEF1 group.

2.3. Immunofluorescence

As previously described,[22–24] we detect protein expression in tissues by immunofluorescence. Prepared tissue sections as in immunohistochemical analysis, and then sections were incubated with LEF1 rabbit monoclonal antibody (1:100, ab137872, abcam), Pan-CK mouse monoclonal antibody (1:200, ab215838, abcam), APC conjugated CD4 mouse monoclonal antibody (82116, 1:2000, Cell Signaling Technology), CD68 mouse monoclonal antibody (1:500, 14-0688-82, Invitrogen), FITC conjugated CD68 mouse monoclonal antibody (1:100, MA1-82715, Invitrogen), CD163 mouse monoclonal antibody (1:500, ab156769) or FOXP3 rabbit monoclonal antibody (1:1000, ab215206, abcam), and incubated for 2 hours at room temperature. After removing the primary antibody and washing 3 times with PBS buffer solution at room temperature, incubate with Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor 488; 1:1000, ab150077, abcam) or Goat Anti-Mouse IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor 647; 1:1000, ab150115, abcam) for 1 hour at room temperature. DAPI (C1002, Beyotime) was used to stain the nucleus.

2.4. Bioinformatics analysis

59 cases normal tissues and 541 tumor tissues in LUAD patients were downloaded from TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) according to the following settings: primary site (bronchus and lung), program (TCGA), project (TCGA-LUAD), data category (transcript profiling or clinical), data type (gene expression qunatification), experimental strategy (RNA-seq), workflow type (STAR-counts). We extracted the data of gene expression, and visualization was done using the heatmaps package in R software (https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/html/heatmaps.html). CIBERSORT algorithm was used to evaluate immune cells infiltration in the tumor tissues of LUAD patients (LM22 signature, 1000 permutations).[25,26]

In addition, we also analyzed the expression of gene expression in TIMER database (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/) with its “Diff Exp” module, and analyzed the correlation between LEF1 expression and 6 types of immune cell infiltration in LUAD patients with its “Gene” module, and also analyzed the correlation between LEF1 expression and other genes expression in LUAD patients with its “Correlation” module.

2.5. Statistical analysis

Data in the present study was is analyzed and visualized by GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software, Boston). Difference in gene expression between normal tissues and tumor tissues was compared with paired (immunohistochemical analysis data of 105 patients with LUAD) or unpaired t test (data from TCGA), and difference in LEF1 expression between different type of LUAD patients was compared with unpaired t test. The overall survival (OS and PFS) of LUAD patients were examined with Kaplan–Meier curves and log-rank tests. Pearson correlation was used to analyze the correlation between LEF1 protein expression and CD68+CD163+ cells, CD4+FOXP3+ cells. P < .05 indicated significant difference.

3. Results

3.1. TCF/LEF transcription factors are aberrantly expressed in LUAD

First, we downloaded the RNA sequence and clinical data of 59 cases normal tissues and 541 tumor tissues in LUAD patients (Tables S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N662 and S2, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N662), and analyzed the expression of LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1 and TCFTL2 mRNA, and found that the expression of LEF1 and TCF7 mRNA in tumor tissues were significantly higher than those in normal tissues, while the expression of TCF7L1 and TCF7L2 mRNA in tumor tissues were significantly lower than those in normal tissues (Figs. 1A and S1, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N661). Next, we analyzed the expression of LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1 and TCFTL2 mRNA in TIMER database, and found that LEF1 was highly expressed in tumor tissues of LUAD patients, and TCF7L1 and TCF7L2 were all lowly expressed in tumor tissues of LUAD patients (Fig. 1B). At last, we determined the expression of LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1 and TCFTL2 protein in tissues of 105 cases LUAD patients (Fig. 1C) by immunohistochemistry, and found that the expression of LEF1 and TCF7 protein in tumor tissues were significantly higher than those in normal tissues, while the expression of TCF7L1 and TCF7L2 in tumor tissues were significantly lower than those in normal tissues (Fig. 1D).

3.2. LEF1 is related to the prognosis and clinical features of LUAD

With the data from TCGA database, we found that high expression of LEF1 mRNA was associated with low 5-year survival in LUAD patients (Fig. 2A), while the expression of TCF7 (Fig. 2B), TCF7L1 (Fig. 2C) and TCF7L2 (Fig. 2D) mRNA and 5-year survival of LUAD patients had nothing to do with each other. In addition, we analyzed the relationship between LEF1 protein expression and clinical characteristics of LUAD patients, and found that there was no significant differences in gender, age, tumor diameter, histological grade and incorporate other lung disease, while there was significant differences in TNM stage, lymphatic metastasis and local invasion (Table 1). And results of immunohistochemistry showed that the expression level of LEF1 protein increased with the increase of TNM stage in 105 cases LUAD patients (Fig. 3A). At the same time, LEF1 protein expression in tumor tissues of LUAD patients with lymphatic metastasis were significantly higher than those in tumor tissues of LUAD patients without lymphatic metastasis (Fig. 3B). And LEF1 protein expression in tumor tissues of LUAD patients with local invasion were significantly higher than those in tumor tissues of LUAD patients without local invasion (Fig. 3C).

3.3. High LEF1 expression was associated with immunosuppressive microenvironment in LUAD

Immunofluorescence was used to locate the expression position in tumor tissue of LEF1 protein, and we used pan-cytokeratin (Pan-CK) antibody to stain tumor cells. And we found that not only was LEF1 co-expressed with Pan-CK on the same cells, but some cells only expressed LEF1 but not Pan-CK, indicating that LEF1 might express on tumor cells and immune cells in LUAD tumor tissues (Fig. 4A). And then we stained LEF1 and CD4/CD68 in tumor tissues of LUAD patients, and found that LEF1 was also co-expressed with CD4/CD68 on the same cells (Fig. 4B,C). Therefore, these results of immunofluorescence not only suggested that LEF1 was expressed on tumor cells and immune cells in LUAD tumor tissues, but also indicated that LEF1 directly regulated the tumor immune microenvironment. Consistently, data analysis based on the TIMER database was displayed that there was a significantly correlation between LEF1 expression and B cell, CD8+ T cell, CD4+ T cell, macrophage, neutrophil, dendritic cell infiltration (Fig. 5A). At the same time, the results of the analysis based on the TCGA database of immune cell infiltration also showed that there was a significant difference in B cells, plasma cells, CD4+ T cells memory resting, CD4+ T cells memory activated, T cells follicular helper, Tregs, NK cells resting, monocytes, macrophages, dendritic cells resting, mast cells resting, eosinophils and neutrophils (Fig. 5B). Notably, the expression levels of many immunosuppressive key molecules, such as CD274, CTLA4, FASLG, IL10, LAG3, LILRB1, PDCD1, PDCD1LG2, PTGS2 and TGFB1, were significantly higher in the high-LEF1 group than in the low-LEF1 group based on the data from TCGA database (Figs. 5C and S2, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N661). In addition, there was a positive correlation between LEF1 expression and immunosuppressive molecules (Fig. S3, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N661).

3.4. LEF1 expression was positively related to M2 macrophage

Macrophages in the tumor microenvironment perform different functions according to different phenotypes, and M2 macrophages exert immunosuppressive and pro-tumor functions in the tumor microenvironment. Results of the analysis based on the TCGA database of immune cell infiltration indicated that the fraction of M2 macrophage infiltration in high-LEF1 expression group were significantly higher than that in low-LEF1 expression group (Fig. 5B). Ulteriorly, we analyzed the expression of M2 macrophage related genes in the tumor tissues of LUAD patients with the data from TCGA database, and found that the expression of MRC1, CD163, IL6 and CLEC10A (CD301) in high-LEF1 expression group were all significantly higher than those in low-LEF1 expression group (Figs. 6A and S4, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N661). Similarly, the results of TIMER database analysis also showed that the expression level of LEF1 was significantly positively correlated with MRC1, CD163, IL6 and CLEC10A (CD301) in LUAD patients (Fig. 6B). In addition, we used CD68 and CD163 to label M2 macrophage in the tumor tissues of 105 cases LUAD patients (Fig. 6C), and found that the expression level of LEF1 was significantly positively correlated with CD68+CD163+ cells in the tumor tissues of 105 cases LUAD patients (Fig. 6D).

3.5. LEF1 expression was positively related to Treg cells

Tregs are a heterogeneous population of T lymphocytes with the ability to suppress immune responses, helping tumor cells evade immune cell killing in the tumor microenvironment. Results of the analysis based on the TCGA database of immune cell infiltration indicated that the fraction of Tregs infiltration in high-LEF1 expression group were significantly higher than that in low-LEF1 expression group (Fig. 5B). Ulteriorly, we analyzed the expression of Tregs related genes in the tumor tissues of LUAD patients with the data from TCGA database, and found that the expression of ENTP1 (CD39), FOXO1, FOXP3 and IL7R (CD127) in high-LEF1 expression group were all significantly higher than those in low-LEF1 expression group (Figs. 7A and S5, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N661). Similarly, the results of TIMER database analysis also showed that the expression level of LEF1 was significantly positively correlated with ENTP1 (CD39), FOXO1, FOXP3 and IL7R (CD127) in LUAD patients (Fig. 7B). In addition, we used CD4 and FOXP3 to label Tregs in the tumor tissues of 105 cases LUAD patients (Fig. 7C), and found that the expression level of LEF1 was significantly positively correlated with CD4+FOXP3+ cells in the tumor tissues of 105 cases LUAD patients (Fig. 7D).

4. Discussion

Lung adenocarcinoma is currently one of the most common pathological subtypes of lung cancer, and its incidence has shown an increasing trend.[5,27] Previous studies have found that the aberrant activation of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway is related to the occurrence and development of LUAD, which involves a wide range of molecular mechanisms, including promoting the proliferation, invasion and migration of tumor cells, promoting the infiltration of tumor cells, and helping tumor cells escape the killing of immune cells. Among them, β-catenin transducts into the nucleus to form a complex with TCF/LEF1, and the β-catenin/TCF/LEF1 complex promotes the expression of CD274 by binding to the CD274 gene promoter, which is one of the key mechanisms leading to tumor cells to escape the killing of immune cells, and is also one of the reasons for the poor effect of PD1/PDL1 immunotherapy in cancer patients.

TCF/LEF is a class of transcription factors with bidirectional regulatory functions, binding to Groucho can inhibit gene transcription, while binding to β-Catenin promotes the transcription of downstream target genes, including LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1 and TCF7L2.[28–30] In the present study, we found that LEF1, TCF7, TCF7L1 and TCF7L2 were all aberrantly expressed in LUAD patients, but only the expression of LEF1 was related to the 5-year overall survival of LUAD patients. Previous studies have found that LEF1 plays a very important role in maintaining stem cell stemness[31,32] and organ development[33] under physiological conditions, but aberrant expression of LEF1 may disrupt the regulatory mechanism between normal cells, causing abnormal cell proliferation and leading to tumor formation.[34,35] At the same time, studies have confirmed that LEF plays a very important role in the proliferation, invasion, and migration of a variety of tumor cells,[36,37] and is an essential factor for epithelial-mesenchymal transition characteristic of tumor malignancy,[38,39] and plays a role in promoting cancer cell invasion and metastasis in prostate, breast, melanoma, and colon cancer.[40]

In addition, we also fund that LEF1 was not only expressed in tumor cells, but also expressed in the immune cells of LUAD tumor tissues. And the expression of LEF1 in tumor tissues of LUAD patients was positively related to immunosuppressive key molecules, such as CD274, CTLA4, FASLG, IL10, LAG3, LILRB1, PDCD1, PDCD1LG2, PTGS2 and TGFB1. Importantly, the expression of LEF1 in tumor tissues of LUAD patients was positively related to M2 macrophage and Tregs infiltration.

Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are infiltrating macrophages in tumor tissues, mainly differentiated from monocytes, and is the most important component of tumor microenvironment (TME).[41,42] Chemokines such as CSF1 and CCL2 secreted by tumor cells can recruit monocytes in peripheral circulating blood to the TME, and then monocytes differentiate into macrophages.[41,42] Previously, it was thought that TAMs are anti-tumor effector cells, which can kill tumor cells or present tumor antigens to induce the body’s immune response to clear tumors, but with the deepening of research, the function of TAM is also related to tumor promotion and immunosuppression. Activated macrophages mainly include M1 macrophages and M2 macrophages, M1 macrophages can kill tumor cells and resist pathogen invasion,[43,44] and M2 macrophages mainly play a role in promoting tumor growth, invasion and metastasis.[45] To our best efforts, we have not found studies of LEF1 in macrophages, but the Wnt/β-catenin pathway has been found to be involved in the activation of M2 macrophages in the TME.[46–48] Previous studies have shown that the aberrantly activated Wnt/β-catenin pathway promotes tumor progression in hepatocellular carcinoma,[46] thyroid cancer,[47] and breast cancer[48] by promoting the activation of M2 macrophages in TME.

Tregs are a heterogeneous population of T lymphocytes with the ability to suppress immune responses, and have been shown to inhibit excessive immune responses by expressing CTLA4 and secreting IL-10 and TGFβ, which promote immune escape from tumor cells.[49,50] In this study, we not only found that the expression of LEF1 was positively related to the expression of CTLA4, IL-10 and TGFB1, but also found that the expression of LEF1 was positively related to Tregs-related genes, such as ENTP1 (CD39), FOXO1, FOXP3 and IL7R (CD127) in LUAD patients, and was positively related to Tregs infiltration in the tumor tissues of LUAD patients. Notably, LEF1 has been confirmed to be associated with the typing, survival and development of Tregs, and LEF1 is dispensable for Treg’s suppressive capacity but essential for maintaining a normal aTreg pool and promoting Treg’s competitive survival.[51]

5. Conclusion

All in all, our results revealed that LEF1 was highly expressed in tumor tissues of LUAD patients, and high expression of LEF1 was associated with the progression and poor prognosis of LUAD. At the same time, we also found that LEF1 was associated with the formation of an immunosuppressive microenvironment in tumor tissues of LUAD patients, and high expression of LEF1 was also associated with more M2 macrophage and Tregs infiltration in the tumor microenvironment of LUAD patients.

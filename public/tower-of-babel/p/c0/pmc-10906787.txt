Comparative analysis of platelet-rich fibrin membrane and 0.2% hyaluronic acid gel on healing following gingival depigmentation procedure
Authors: Aishwarya Madhukar Kale, Kunal S. Sethi, Swapna A. Mahale, Prerna A. Karde, Lata Madhukar Kale, Sneha H. Choudhary
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroduction:Platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) accelerates wound healing by promoting faster cicatricial tissue remodeling and excellent neovascularization. Hyaluronic acid (HA) being biocompatible, anti-inflammatory, and proangiogenic leads to improvement in the rate of wound healing.Aim:The aim of this study was to compare the effect of PRF membrane and 0.2% HA gel on wounds after gingival depigmentation surgery.Materials and Methods:This study was carried out on 30 systemically healthy individuals recruited from the pool of patients who visited the department of periodontology. After depigmentation procedure in every patient, the sites were divided into three even groups. Group A received PRF membrane and periodontal dressing, Group B received 0.2% HA gel application and periodontal dressing, and Group C served as a control group in which only periodontal dressing was placed. The individuals were evaluated for the healing index (HI) and Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) on the 3rd and 5th day. Epithelialization test (ET) was performed on the 5th day. Gingival biotype was reassessed and compared to preoperative value after 3 months. The clinical trial was expressed in terms of mean and standard deviation. Intra-group comparison and inter-group comparison were done through the Kruskal–Wallis ANOVA test. All statistical tests were performed through SPSS version 25.0 (IBM).Results:The inter-group statistical analysis concerning NRS, HI, and ET showed statistically significant results in Groups A and B compared to Group C (P < 0.05), while gingival biotype showed statistically significant results in Group A compared to Groups B and C.Conclusion:The use of PRF membrane and HA gel to protect the raw wound site of depigmented gingiva proved to be an effective approach, resulting in faster healing.

INTRODUCTION

The most common natural pigment in various organisms is melanin, a brown pigment. Melanin pigmentation occurs when melanin granules generated by melanoblasts become entangled between epithelial cells in the basal layer of gingival epithelium. For many people, gingival hyperpigmentation is a major cosmetic concern. Although it is not a medical issue, many people find it unattractive. Gingival depigmentation is a periodontal plastic surgical procedure to remove or reduce hyperpigmentation using a variety of techniques.[1] The area is usually covered with a periodontal pack after the removal of the pigmented layer. There are no curative properties in periodontal dressing, and it only reduces the risk of surface trauma during mastication. Platelet-rich fibrin (PRF) is a second-generation platelet concentrate developed by Choukroun et al.[2] in France in 2001. It can be used to shield open wounds and to accelerate wound healing by promoting rapid cicatricial tissue remodeling and effective neovascularization.[3] The use of PRF membrane as a palatal bandage has been recognized as an effective method for protecting the raw wound area of the palatal donor site.[4]

Hyaluronic acid (HA) is a polysaccharide that is found in many species, including bacteria and mammals.[5] In 1934, HA was first discovered in the vitreous humor. It is in the form of a viscous gel present in the vitreous body of the human eye, soft tissue like synovial fluid, and skin. Hyaluron is a large (macro) molecule that is found in all living organisms. Glycosaminoglycan provides a temporary structure in the early stages of wound healing.[6] As the wound heals, this temporary structure is replaced by the addition of protein molecules – proteoglycans and collagen.[7] On tissue maturation, HA is degraded and more protein molecules are formed. The proteins bind to the HA to form proteoglycans, which help the body to heal and rebuild tissue.[8] HA plays an important role in hydrating the tissue as HA molecules absorb water 3000 times their weight.[9] HA influences cell motility, phagocytosis, and angiogenesis.[10] It also has antioxidant and free radical scavenging properties. HA levels increase after an injury, and the molecules attach to the fibrin in the clot.[10] It is highly biocompatible and nonimmunogenic in nature. It also has bacteriostatic, fungistatic, anti-inflammatory, osteoinductive, and proangiogenic properties leading to the promotion of wound healing in a variety of tissues.[11]

Going through the evidence, to date, many studies have been carried out, but no study has provided a comparative, quantitative analysis of the platelet fibrin membrane and 0.2% HA gel on healing following gingival depigmentation procedures. Therefore, this study was conducted to compare and evaluate the effects of PRF membrane and the topical application of commercially available 0.2% HA gel on postoperative patient discomfort and wound healing following gingival depigmentation in terms of pain and complete epithelialization.

For the preparation of the PRF membrane,[16] 10 mL of blood was collected in a glass test tube from patients and then centrifuged for 12 min at 2800 rpm in a centrifugation machine (Remi R-303, 8 mL × 15 mL capacity). Following the centrifugation, three layers were obtained in the test tube. The platelet-poor plasma made up the top layer, while concentrated red blood cells constituted the bottom layer. Fibrin network gel was present in the intermediate layer. To obtain the PRF membrane, the gel was separated and squashed between sterile gauze pieces and the supernatant liquid was discarded.

The depigmentation was performed by conventional technique with the use of a blade and scalpel in 14–24 and 34–44 regions [Figure 2]. In the 11–14 region, the PRF membrane was placed. The membrane was stabilized with a simple interrupted suture using a 3-0 nonabsorbable silk suture followed by periodontal pack placement [Figure 3a]. In the 21–24 region, HA gel was uniformly spread over the depigmented area and a periodontal pack was used to cover it [Figure 3b]. The mandibular anterior sextant (34–44 region) was covered with a periodontal pack right after depigmentation, and this served as the control.

All the individuals refrained from brushing the area of surgery for 3 days after the treatment. They were directed to use 0.2% chlorhexidine digluconate mouthwash to rinse their mouth. The patients were recalled on the 3rd day, and the periodontal pack was removed.

The Numerical Rating Scale (NRS) was used to evaluate pain.[17] In this, patients were asked to rate their pain and discomfort from 0 to 10 [Figure 4]. NRS score was measured on the 3rd and 5th days, postoperatively. Gingival biotype was measured using an endodontic reamer and Vernier caliper[18] [Figure 5]. Gingival biotype was measured at baseline and 3-month follow-up [Figure 6].

The healing index (HI) was evaluated on the 3rd and 5th days using Landry et al.’s[19] HI (1988). The HI was evaluated on the 3rd and 5th days using Landry et al.’s[19] HI (1988), and the respective scores (very poor, poor, good, very good, and excellent) were assigned.

Patients were evaluated for an epithelialization test[2021] on the 5th day, postoperatively, and methylene blue was used to determine the rate of epithelialization. It was applied with cotton, and the stained region was gently swabbed with moist gauze to remove the dye from the normal epithelial surface; however, nonviable tissue and granulation tissue remained stained. The quantity of stain which the depigmented area accumulated indicated the presence of an inflammatory concentration underneath it [Figure 7].

Data obtained were compiled on an MS Office Excel Sheet (version 2010, Microsoft Redmond Campus, Redmond, Washington, United States). Data were subjected to statistical analysis using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS version 21.0, IBM). Descriptive statistics such as frequencies and percentage for categorical data and mean and SD for numerical data has been depicted.

Since the sample size per group was smaller, nonparametric tests have been used for comparisons. Inter-group comparison (>2 groups) was done using the Kruskal–Wallis ANOVA. The intra-group comparison was done using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (up to 2 observations).

For all the statistical tests, P < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant, keeping a error at 5% and b error at 20%, thus giving 80% power to the study.

DISCUSSION

Although over time, various techniques and devices in wound closure have evolved, the knowledge of the exact healing process still poses one of the greatest challenges for surgeons after any surgical intervention. Fibrin adhesives and platelet concentrates have shown to be effective therapeutic techniques for plastic and microvascular operations. The development of PRF represented a significant advancement in treatment with platelet gels.[1] Autologous PRF was also used by Choukroun et al.[21] to improve bone healing in implant dentistry. PRF promotes bone regeneration, wound healing, graft stabilization, and hemostasis. It efficiently directs stem cell migration and the healing process because of the well-organized fibrin matrices. In agreement, Dohan et al.[22] have also re-affirmed the fact that PRF releases growth factors more slowly and has better healing properties than PRP.

HA, commonly known as hyaluronan or hyaluronate, is a polymer with a high molecular weight (glycosaminoglycan). Extracellular matrices, particularly those of mineralized and nonmineralized periodontal tissues, rely on hyaluronan to function properly. Pagnacco et al.[23] performed preliminary clinical experiments in dentistry for the treatment of gingivitis and periodontitis and concluded that hyaluronan possesses anti-inflammatory and antibacterial properties. Exogenous hyaluronan may have an anti-inflammatory impact by acting as a scavenger, draining prostaglandins, metalloproteinases, and other bioactive compounds.[24] Their osmotic action may potentially play a role in the anti-edematous effect and it might be utilized as a supplement to mechanical treatment because of its ability to accelerate tissue recovery.[25] Gengigel (Ricerfarma, EU) comprises high molecular weight fractions of HA in gel formulations with concentrations of 0.8% and 0.2%.

The test groups, Group A (PRF) and Group B (HA), had a statistically significant difference in HI, ET, NRS, and gingival biotype score when compared to Group C. This current research is similar to Bansal et al.,[12] who in 2016 implanted a PRF membrane together with a periodontal pack after depigmentation. Periodontal packs only lessen the chance of surface trauma during mastication; however, PRF promotes rapid tissue remodeling and effective neovascularization, which speeds up wound healing. This is an effective way to protect the unhealed wound area of the depigmented site and shorten the healing period. In 2018, Debnath and Chatterjee[26] compared PRF membrane and PRF matrix using NRS, HI, ET, and histologic analysis and statistical analysis of NRS, HI, ET, and histologic analysis revealed P = 0.001 in the PRF and PRF matric groups compared to the periodontal pack group.

Dahiya et al. in 2019[27] studied the role of PRF in wound healing after depigmentation surgery on a total of 12 samples, which were evaluated for NRS and HI on the 3rd and 5th days. On the 5th day, an ET with toluidine blue and a histological examination with a punch biopsy were performed. The NRS, HI, ET, and histological findings were statistically significant in both study groups. The PRF group performed better in the ET, and inflammatory cell infiltration was lower, confirming superior wound healing in the group. They concluded that PRF membrane postdepigmentation improved patient comfort and accelerated the wound healing process. Similarly, in the present study, the NRS score was moderate on the 3rd day for Groups A and B while it was severe for Group C. On the 5th day, the NRS score was mild for Group A and Group B while moderate for Group C. The HI as per Landry et al.[19] was calculated on the 3rd and 5th days. It was noted that on 3rd day, the healing was very good in Group A while it was good for Group B and poor for Group C. On the 5th day, healing was observed very good for Group B and good for Group C.

Gingival biotype was calculated at baseline and after 3 months, and a thicker gingival biotype was found in Group A while it was the same for Groups B and C even after 2 months. Rehan et al.[28] in 2018 conducted a study to see whether coronally advanced flap with either a PRF membrane or a bioresorbable amniotic membrane and reported that enhanced gingival biotype with both membranes was observed.

ET was performed using methylene blue dye. This dye is known to stain the negatively charged molecules within the damaged cells but does not stain the nonabsorptive epithelia. This test thus helps to track the process of epithelial healing. In the present study, on the 5th day, Group C sites were severely stained with methylene blue which concluded that Group C had severe inflammatory cell infiltrate compared to Groups A and B.

The limitation of the present study was that no histological examination was carried out to assess the healing process; the sample size was small.

CONCLUSION

Within the limits of the study, topical administration of PRF and HA gel to the raw wound region resulted in quicker healing and improved patient comfort compared to using a periodontal pack alone.

PRF and HA gel can be successfully used as topical applications over raw wounds occurring at the donor site of mucogingival surgeries for the improvement of postoperative comfort together with successful treatment outcomes. Further studies are needed to be carried out in this regard.

Rethinking Risk in Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy: Assessing the Role of Myocardial Fibrosis and Left Ventricular Hypertrophy in Sudden Cardiac Death
Authors: Amro Badr, Juan Farina, Reza Arsanjani, Srekar Ravi, Michael O'Shea, Omar Baqal, Olubadewa Fatunde, Jeffrey B. Geske, Konstantinos C. Siontis, Said Alsidawi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association guidelines recommend implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) implantation for patients with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) with a wall thickness of ≥30 mm (class IIA), whereas they give a class IIB recommendation for the implantation of an ICD on the basis of extensive late gadolinium enhancement alone. In this analysis, we show that in a high-risk population with ICD implanted for primary prevention of sudden cardiac death (SCD) in the setting of HCM, the presence of massive left ventricular hypertrophy predicts a higher incidence of ICD therapy than other traditional SCD risk factors. The presence of extensive myocardial fibrosis, however, identifies a subgroup of patients without massive left ventricular hypertrophy who have an equally high incidence of receiving appropriate device therapy. These findings suggest that the presence of extensive late gadolinium enhancement on cardiac magnetic resonance can be used as a risk modifier for traditional SCD risk factors in patients with HCM to better understand their overall risk of ventricular arrhythmias.

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is one of the most common forms of cardiomyopathies, and almost half of the cases have identifiable pathologic mutations. Furthermore, HCM can be associated with an increased risk of sudden cardiac death (SCD). Identifying high-risk groups necessitating implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) implantation is integral to effectively managing this disease. Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) on cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) is emerging as a useful tool for improving risk stratification in patients with HCM and has been incorporated into the recent European Society of Cardiology and the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association (ACC/AHA) guidelines for risk assessment and ICD decision-making.1, 2, 3

The ACC/AHA guidelines recommend ICD implantation for patients with HCM with a wall thickness of ≥30 mm (class IIA).2 We hypothesize that patients who do not meet this cutoff but have extensive myocardial fibrosis also carry an elevated risk, as indicated by the rate of ICD therapies.

Methods

This retrospective study examined patients diagnosed with HCM who underwent ICD implantation for various risk factors as indicated by the AHA/ACC and CMR from 2002 to 2022 at 3 tertiary hospitals in the United States (Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Mayo Clinic in Arizona, and Mayo Clinic in Florida). The study was approved by the institutional review board at Mayo Clinic (Mayo Clinic in Rochester, Mayo Clinic in Arizona, and Mayo Clinic in Florida) (IRB Approval Number: 21-008531). Given the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was not required. The primary outcome assessed was the occurrence of the first appropriate ICD therapy.

The LGE extent was quantified as the percentage of enhanced myocardium relative to left ventricular (LV) mass using the 6 SD method. Patients were categorized into 4 groups on the basis of the LV wall thickness and LGE extent using predefined cutoff points of 30 mm and 15%, respectively. To investigate the association between LGE and the primary outcome, cox regression models were employed. All statistical analyses were performed using Stata IC 16.

In a 4-group analysis, patients with LVH ≥30 mm and LGE ≥15% exhibited the highest risk of experiencing appropriate ICD therapy (HR: 3.11; 95% CI, 1.34-7.16; P=.008) when compared with patients with LVH <30 mm and LGE <15% (Figure A, B). Discussion

In conclusion, in this high-risk population with ICD implanted for primary prevention of SCD in the setting of HCM, the presence of massive LVH predicts a higher incidence of ICD therapy than other traditional SCD risk factors. The presence of extensive myocardial fibrosis, however, identifies a subgroup of patients without massive LVH who have an equally high incidence of receiving appropriate device therapy. These findings suggest that the presence of extensive LGE on CMR can be used as a risk modifier for traditional SCD risk factors in patients with HCM to better understand their overall risk of ventricular arrhythmias. The current ACC/AHA guidelines give a class IIB recommendation for the implantation of an ICD on the basis of extensive LGE alone. On the basis of our findings, we propose using LGE percentage to upgrade or downgrade traditional SCD risk factors in the HCM population (other than massive LVH) rather than a stand-alone risk factor. Larger studies will be needed to further validate this proposal.

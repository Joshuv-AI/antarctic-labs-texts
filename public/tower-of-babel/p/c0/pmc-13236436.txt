Methodological Challenges in Associating T2-Weighted Imaging Features in Breast Cancer With Clinical and Pathologic Findings
Authors: Yakup Ozbay
Published: 2026

I read with great interest the study titled “Association of T2-Weighted Imaging Features in Invasive Breast Cancer With Clinicopathologic Features and Neoadjuvant Treatment Outcomes” by Youn et al. [1]. The authors investigate the association between T2-weighted imaging features and clinicopathologic characteristics, as well as neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) outcomes in invasive breast cancer. However, I identified several methodological and interpretive concerns that substantially affected the strength and generalizability of the conclusions, which could help to further contextualize the findings.

First, with only nine progression events among the 68 NAC patients, the logistic regression analyses for disease progression were severely underpowered, and the resulting confidence intervals were uninterpretable in clinical terms. The reported odds ratio (OR) for intratumoral T2 signal intensity (SI) grade 1 vs. grade 0 was 13.49, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) from 1.47 to 1791.99. Similarly, grade 2 vs. grade 0 showed an OR of 29.62 (95% CI: 2.44–4178.06). These intervals were so wide that no clinically meaningful inferences could be drawn from the point estimates [2]. The use of Firth’s penalized likelihood logistic regression, while appropriate for sparse data, does not address the fundamental issue of insufficient events, as a reliable regression estimate generally requires at least ten events per predictor variable [3]. This analysis had nine events across multiple predictors. Therefore, these findings may be better framed as exploratory rather than definitive.

In the limitations section, the authors note that OR values for T2 relaxation time expressed per 10-ms increment are arbitrary in their scaling, and smaller OR values would be obtained if calculated for smaller increments. I would like to gently point out that this disclaimer may appear to be directed toward the wrong variable. The intratumoral T2 relaxation time ORs in Table 4 are modest (1.47 and 1.94) and are unlikely to raise interpretive concerns, regardless of the increment chosen. The values that may more appropriately benefit from a clarifying caveat are the ORs for intratumoral T2 SI grade—13.49 (95% CI: 1.47–1791.99) and 29.62 (95% CI: 2.44–4178.06)—which, as a categorical variable, carry no per-millisecond scaling and whose wide confidence intervals reflect the limited number of progression events rather than any scaling choice. Clarifying this point can assist readers in focusing on the most relevant limitations and support a more balanced interpretation of the findings.

Furthermore, all associations between T2 features and clinicopathological or outcome variables were evaluated exclusively by univariable logistic regression for both the full cohort and the NAC subgroup throughout the manuscript. Given that T2 features (SI grade and relaxation time) are strongly intercorrelated with tumor size (clinical T category), which is associated with hormone receptor (HR) negativity and the triple-negative subtype [4,5,6]; without multivariable models that simultaneously adjust for clinical T category, lymph node status, and molecular subtype, it is impossible to determine whether T2 features carry independent prognostic information or only serve as surrogates for tumor size. Adjustment for key confounders is essential to support the claims of independent associations.

Finally, the study found no significant association between the intratumoral T2 SI grade and HER2 status (P = 0.798), nor between the T2 relaxation time and HER2 status (P = 0.928), which may limit the generalizability of the findings. This is in contrast to a recently published AJR study by Jirarayapong et al. [7], which demonstrated high peritumoral and intratumoral T2 SI in HER2-positive breast cancers. Given that HER2-positive tumors are biologically distinct, often exhibiting stromal edema, peritumoral vascular proliferation, and lymphoid infiltration [7,8,9], the lack of HER2 positivity in the present study warrants further discussion. Possible explanations include differences in HER2 prevalence, differences in T2 SI grading criteria, or confounding by tumor size.

In summary, although this study provides valuable insights into the potential role of T2WI features in breast cancer, these considerations should help refine the interpretation and clinical applicability of the findings.

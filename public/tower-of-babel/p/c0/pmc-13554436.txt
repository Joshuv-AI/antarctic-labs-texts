Gintonin protects hematopoietic stem cells and accelerates bone marrow recovery following cyclophosphamide-induced injury
Authors: Ajay Vijayakumar, BaiCheng Chen, Jun Hong Park, Seung-Yeol Nah, Jong-Hoon Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractGintonin administration to mice with hematopoietic injury induced by cyclophosphamide (CY) demonstrated that, gintonin promotes hematopoietic recovery as evidenced by restored count of blood cells, alleviated bone marrow suppression and reduced apoptosis of hematopoietic stem cells indicating the potential of gintonin as an adjunct therapeutic agent in chemotherapy.

Panax ginseng and the active constituent ginsenoside were assessed for its therapeutic potential over the last few decades owing to its pharmacological effect in treating various disorders [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. Certain rich and rare ginsenosides investigated on chronic disorders demonstrated that individual ginsenoside is crucial in translating the herbal compound to adjunct therapy [5,6]. Gintonin, a novel constituent isolated from Panax ginseng exhibit numerous beneficial properties in protecting and ameliorating several disorders. In our earlier studies gintonin demonstrated its potential in immune boosting on immunosuppressed animal models [7,8]. Additionally, gintonin showed positive therapeutic outcome in neuronal disorders and inducing anti-cancer effects [9,10]. Several studies indicated that the chemotherapeutic agent cyclophosphamide (CY) exerts bone marrow injury and triggers hematopoietic suppression in mice. Furthermore, CY injection notably decreases peripheral blood cells and induces apoptosis resulting in hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) damage [11,12]. Therefore, in the present study we examined the potential of gintonin in recovering the HSC damage induced by CY.

Four-week-old male C57BL/6 mice weighing 20 ± 2 g were purchased from SLN Inc., Japan. The principles of laboratory animal care were followed in accordance to the “Guideline for Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees” of Chonbuk National University. Total 30 animals were randomly divided into five groups (n = 6), and acclimatized for 7 days. Normal control (NC) group received only sterile distilled water throughout the study. All other animals received intra peritoneal injection (i.p.) of CY (50 mg/kg) on second, fourth and sixth day [Fig. 1A]. From eighth day, gintonin in distilled water were orally administered for two weeks in the doses of 50 mg/kg (50G), 100 mg/kg (100G) and 300 mg/kg (300G) respectively. All animals were euthanized at the end of study period and analyzed for blood cell parameters, apoptosis and histopathological examination of bone marrow, and the level of apoptotic protein expression. Peripheral blood from the mice were collected using heparinized capillary tubes through retro orbital plexus into 0.5 mL ethylenediaminetetra-acetic acid (EDTA).K3 tubes (GD005EK3, ATOPMED), and then analyzed for blood cell parameters using ADVIA 2120 (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) according to the standard protocol at clinical pathology department, Chonbuk National University.

Bone marrow from the right femur of mice were surgically removed and rinsed twice with PBS, and the marrow is flushed with PBS containing 2% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1 mM EDTA using a syringe with 23-gauge needle. The aggregates were passed through 70 μm nylon strainer and centrifuged at 300 × g for 10 min at 4 °C. The cells were then processed again and re-suspended at 1 × 108 cells/mL. HSCs were purified using EasySep™ Mouse Hematopoietic Progenitor Cell Isolation Kit (#19856A, STEMCELL™ technologies, Canada) according to the manufacturer instructions, the cells were then labelled using FITC annexin V (#640906, Biolegend) or 7AAD (#51-68981E, BD Pharminogen) for 15 min at room temperature [13]. Apoptosis of the cells were estimated using BD Accuri™ C6 plus flow cytometer (BD Biosciences). Western blot analysis were performed by extracting the proteins from bone marrow cells of both left and right tibia, and lysed using RIPA lysis buffer by incubating for 30 min on ice and centrifuged at 13000 rpm for 25 min at 4 °C [14]. Supernatant was isolated and the protein concentration were measured using NanoDrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer, then the solution was stored at −80 °C until further analysis. Subsequently, 35 μg of protein was resolved by 12% sodium dodecyl sulphate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (Millipore, USA). Then the membranes were probed with either Bcl2 (#sc-7382, Santacruz biotechnology, USA) or Bax (#2772, Cell Signal Technology, USA) primary antibodies, followed by respective horseradish peroxidase conjugated secondary antibodies (#7074S, #7076S, Cell Signal Technology, USA). β-actin (#4970S, Cell Signal Technology, USA) was used as loading control and the immunoreactive bands were visualized using ImageQuant™ LAS500 (GE Healthcare, Sweden). Intensities of the band were quantified using ImageJ analysis software. Additionally, left femur bones was subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining to assess morphological changes. Briefly, the isolated femur was fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin solution and decalcified with decalcifying solution-lite (#D0818, Sigma-Aldrich, USA). Followed by dehydrating with ethanol series and embedded in paraffin wax. Finally, 5 μm sections were cut using microtome and subjected to HE staining using the H&E staining kit (#AB245880, Abcam), as per the protocol. All statistical values were analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's multiple comparison study. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant and value of p < 0.01 was considered highly statistically significant. Analysis of blood cell parameters indicated that white blood cell (WBC) and lymphocyte were significantly reduced to 1.07 ± 0.20 and 0.86 ± 0.29 in CY group when compared to 5.15 ± 0.36 and 3.87 ± 0.56 in NC group respectively. 50G treatment group did not exhibit a notable recovery in these parameters, however, 100G and 300G displayed improved recovery with values of 3.39 ± 0.39 and 2.57 ± 0.25 in WBC, and 2.13 ± 0.32 and 1.89 ± 0.23 in lymphocyte respectively. Specifically, red blood cells (RBC), hemoglobin (HGB) and hematocrit (HCT) levels were completely restored to levels comparable to those of the NC group [Fig. 1B], suggesting substantial recovery from the induced damage. However, no significant differences were observed in body weight and organ index between any groups (data not shown). i.p. administration of CY significantly induced the early and late apoptosis of HSCs as indicated by the flow cytometry analysis. Alternately, gintonin administration suppressed these stages of apoptosis with the highest inhibitory response observed in 300G group. When compared with CY group, 50G and 100G group exhibited only mild response in early, late and total apoptosis stages. In contrast, 300G group showed a superior effect in suppressing apoptosis at early, late and total apoptosis [Fig. 1C]. Western blot analysis revealed a dose proportionate inhibitory effect following the gintonin treatment. The expression levels of anti-apoptotic protein (Bcl2) were significantly reduced in CY group with ∼1.5 fold decrease when compared to NC group, gintonin treatment reversed the inhibitory effect dose proportionately and recovered close to normal levels with ∼1.35 fold and ∼1.5 fold increase in 100G and 300G group respectively. Whereas, pro-apoptotic protein (Bax) expressions exhibited a drastic upregulation in CY group with ∼6.0 fold increase when compared to NC group. Likewise, gintonin treatment inhibited this response respective to the dosage intake with 100G and 300G exhibiting enhanced response of ∼1.75 fold and ∼2.4 fold decreases respectively [Fig. 2A and B]. HE staining demonstrated that [Fig. 2C], CY group was markedly hypocellular with bone marrow suppression as evidenced with several clear round vacuoles and increased marrow adiposity. Although, 50G treatment group displayed a visible recovery effect, the exerted response was not substantial. Nevertheless, 100G and 300G groups effectively ameliorated with abundant hematopoietic cells and fewer small vacuoles suggesting an inhibitory effect with respect to hematopoietic suppression which correlates to the previous report [15]. Based on the data, it could be suggested that 50G concentration could not exert any possible therapeutic effect, whereas 100G and 300G concentrations exhibited significant hematopoietic recovery in mice with CY-induced damage. In this context, we would like to propose three distinct possibilities. The first plausible explanation is that the apoptosis induced by CY notably reduced the blood cell parameters and affected the bone marrow. Therefore, the therapeutic effect induced by 50G dosage might be insufficient to counteract the severity of the induced damage [16]. Second explanation is that, the hematopoietic recovery became apparent at 100G and above. However, histopathological observation did not exhibit additional recovery beyond that observed in the 100G group. Therefore, the exhibited effects might reflect a threshold-type pharmacological response rather than a strictly linear dose-dependent relationship. Third possibility is that the characteristic profile of gintonin should be investigated further in detail to understand this therapeutic effect, which might help to proceed for further studies. Limitation, to our findings is that only few apoptotic proteins were studied due to the sample size and long-term effects to establish the sustainability of hematopoietic recovery is necessary. Although CY is used as a model drug for hematopoietic injury, it is widely used in chemotherapy-associated myelosuppression. Further studies in tumor models receiving CY are required to determine whether gintonin can promote hematopoietic recovery without interfering with the antitumor activity of chemotherapy. Additionally, detailed compositional profiling and batch-to-batch variation analyses of gintonin are required to further enhance reproducibility and standardization. Collectively, our findings suggest that gintonin exerts promising hematopoietic recovery by enhancing stem cell survival in mice with CY-induced hematopoietic damage. Specifically, 100G and 300G groups restored the blood cell parameters, ameliorated apoptosis and improved bone marrow suppression, demonstrating the potential of gintonin as an adjunct therapeutic agent for mitigating hematopoietic suppression. Nevertheless, further studies with increased sample size, functional assays and signaling pathways are required to understand the protective mechanism of gintonin in HSC.

Transcriptome analysis identifies upregulation of GBP4 in sarcoidosis and pulmonary hypertension
Authors: Shunlian Hu, Jijin Jiang, Jiayi Wang, Qiuhong Li, Wenhui Wu, Jin-Fu Xu
Published: 2025

To the Editor

Sarcoidosis is a multisystem inflammatory granulomatous disorder where over 90 % of cases involve the lungs.1 A significant complication is sarcoidosis-associated pulmonary hypertension (SAPH), classified under Group 5 pulmonary hypertension (PH),2 which develops in a substantial proportion of patients, particularly those with advanced pulmonary sarcoidosis, and portends a poor prognosis.3,4 The pathophysiology of SAPH is complex and multifactorial, potentially involving parenchymal disease, direct vascular granulomatous inflammation, extrinsic compression by lymph nodes, and cardiac involvement.3 Given the overlapping clinical features and the prognostic importance of SAPH, identifying molecular links between sarcoidosis and PH could offer diagnostic and therapeutic insights.

Inflammation and immune responses are central to both sarcoidosis and PH. We hypothesized that shared transcriptomic alterations, particularly in immune-related genes, might exist between these conditions and could illuminate SAPH pathogenesis. To explore this, we utilized a bioinformatics approach combined with validation in clinical samples. We sourced publicly available transcriptomic datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). We included datasets derived from human lung tissue from PH patients (GSE15197, GSE117261, GSE48149, GSE113439; Total: 169 PH, 76 controls) and whole blood from sarcoidosis patients (GSE19314, GSE42825, GSE42826, GSE42830; Total: 110 sarcoidosis, 145 controls). Additionally, we analyzed corresponding tissue types from the alternate disease (PH whole blood: GSE38267, 13 PH, 28 controls; sarcoidosis lung tissue: GSE16538, six sarcoidosis, six controls). Datasets from the same tissue type and disease context were combined after batch effect removal using the COMBAT function in R (https://www.r-project.org/) (“SVA” package). Differential gene expression analysis between disease and control groups was performed using the “limma” package in R. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were defined by a P-value < 0.05 and |log2(fold change [FC])| >[mean(|logFC|) + 2*sd (|logFC|)].5

To identify genes potentially bridging sarcoidosis and PH development, we focused on DEGs altered in both conditions across relevant tissues. Remarkably, we revealed only one gene consistently upregulated across all four comparisons (PH lung tissue vs. control, sarcoidosis whole blood vs. control, PH whole blood vs. control, sarcoidosis lung tissue vs. control): Guanylate binding protein 4 (GBP4) (Fig. 1A–I). GBP4 belongs to the interferon-inducible large GTPase family known for roles in innate immunity and inflammation. To validate the GBP4 finding, we collected peripheral blood samples from patients diagnosed with PH (n = 10; mean age 31.9 ± 13.4 years; six females) and sarcoidosis (n = 7; mean age 48.9 ± 6.9 years; four females) at Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital, along with healthy controls (n = 6; mean age 38.4 ± 11.2 years; four females). Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Pulmonary Hospital (No. K20‐150Y), and all participants provided written informed consent. Inclusion criteria were confirmed diagnosis according to established guidelines.1,2 Exclusion criteria included other significant pulmonary or systemic diseases. We performed quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) for GBP4 messenger RNA (mRNA) expression (normalized to β-actin). We further explored the potential functional context of GBP4 using Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) on the combined PH lung tissue and sarcoidosis whole blood datasets (Fig. 1K). Patients were stratified into GBP4-high and GBP4-low groups based on median expression. GSEA indicated that GBP4-high expression was associated with pathways like ``antigen processing and presentation'' and ``allograft rejection'' in both disease contexts (P < 0.05, false discovery rate [FDR] <0.25), suggesting a link between GBP4 levels and heightened immune activity.

To investigate the immune cell landscape, we applied the CIBERSORT algorithm to estimate the relative proportions of 22 immune cell types in the combined datasets (Fig. 1L). In both PH lung tissue and sarcoidosis whole blood datasets, the abundance of monocytes showed a significant positive correlation with GBP4 expression levels (PH lung: r = 0.31, P = 9.7e-4; sarcoidosis blood: r = 0.21, P = 0.04; Fig. 1M). Additionally, M1 macrophage abundance correlated positively with GBP4 in sarcoidosis blood (r = 0.52, P = 3.6e-8), though not significantly in PH lung tissue (r = 0.13, P = 0.18). Monocytes and macrophages are key players in granuloma formation in sarcoidosis and vascular inflammation in PH, suggesting GBP4 might influence these processes via modulating monocyte/macrophage activity.

Our study has limitations. The bioinformatics analysis relied on heterogeneous public datasets representing separate disease entities (PH and sarcoidosis) rather than confirmed SAPH cases. Sample sizes, while enhanced by merging datasets, remain modest, and our validation cohort is small. Confounding factors like age and treatment status were not fully adjusted for. Furthermore, transcriptomic data do not directly reflect protein levels or function, necessitating future validation using techniques like immunohistochemistry or functional in vitro/in vivo assays in SAPH models or patient samples.

In conclusion, through integrated bioinformatics analysis of lung tissue and whole blood transcriptomes, complemented by clinical sample validation, we identified GBP4 as a commonly upregulated gene in both PH and sarcoidosis contexts. Its positive correlation with monocyte abundance and association with immune activation pathways suggests GBP4 could represent a novel molecular link relevant to SAPH pathogenesis. While requiring substantial further investigation, GBP4 emerges as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target warranting exploration in the future.

Establishing a framework of measurement for use in Long COVID research and practice: protocol for a scoping review involving evidence review and consultation

Introduction

Long COVID is a multisystem and often disabling condition that has affected over 400 million people worldwide as a consequence of SARS-CoV-2 infection.1,5 The term Long COVID was coined early in the COVID-19 pandemic by people who were experiencing long-term symptoms after a SARS-CoV-2 infection and is the term preferred by the patient community, over ‘Post COVID-19 condition’ or ‘Post-Acute Sequelae of SARS-CoV-2’, for example.6,11 Most prevalence estimates of Long COVID range between 7–30% of people who survive a SARS-CoV-2 infection.15 12,16

The breadth and potentially fluctuating nature of Long COVID symptoms pose challenges to its diagnosis and management. Long COVID is characterised by multidimensional health challenges, spanning physical, mental, emotional and cognitive health domains.17 18 Over 200 symptoms of Long COVID have been documented, which poses challenges for identifying appropriate outcome measures to describe the impact of the condition.17 19 The most common symptoms of Long COVID include fatigue, coughing, difficulty breathing, cognitive impairment and general weakness.5 20 Postexertional malaise or post-exertional symptom exacerbation is also commonly experienced and is characterised by a worsening or relapse of symptoms and reduction in function following previously tolerated, day-to-day physical, cognitive or emotional exertion.1921,23 Long COVID symptoms can pose challenges engaging in work or school1924,27 with resultant implications on finances, social roles28 29 and secondary impacts on mental health,30 for patients and their families. Symptoms of Long COVID can be constant, progressive or remitting and relapsing over time.7 17 It is important to identify outcome measures that can accurately and safely capture the diversity of Long COVID symptoms and their potentially fluctuating nature.

Caregivers, such as a family member or a friend supporting a person living with Long COVID, also face challenges including, but not limited to, burnout; deterioration of their own physical, mental or emotional health; and loss of employment.31 The impact of caregiving for people living with Long COVID may be complicated by the multidimensional, unpredictable and fluctuating nature of symptoms,17 challenges navigating the healthcare system for diverse symptoms and the burden of securing social and financial support for the people they care for.32 Caregivers are essential for supporting the health and well-being of people living with Long COVID and the sustainability of healthcare systems.33 34 Hence, it is necessary to consider caregivers when approaching outcome measurement in the context of Long COVID.

Importance of measuring outcomes in Long COVID

The purpose of measuring health outcomes is to describe, predict and evaluate a patient’s status, which can help to inform treatment approaches, health services, programmes and models of healthcare delivery.35,38 In the absence of a validated biomarker of disease activity,36 patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs), performance-based outcome measures (PBOMs) and patient-reported experience measures (PREMs) can be helpful for assessment and management for people living with Long COVID.8 34 Most PROMs are tools that patients can complete independently to provide information about their health status; they may be condition-specific or generic.39 PBOMs are tests which require patients to perform tasks to provide information about their functional abilities.40 PREMs can evaluate the accessibility and quality of care received, providing information to guide care for people living with Long COVID.41

Outcome measures for caregivers of people living with Long COVID are also critical for describing outcomes such as caregiver burden, emotional well-being, physical health and quality of life. PROMs can provide self-reported information of caregiver health status and daily living. PREMs can be used to evaluate the accessibility and quality of care received by caregivers.41 Collectively, PROMs, PBOMs and PREMs help describe health outcomes, guide treatment approaches, predict future events or evaluate changes over time among people living with Long COVID and their caregivers.

Advancing measurement in Long COVID

The Post-COVID Condition Core Outcome Set (PC-COS) Project Steering Committee and the Core Outcome Measures for Long COVID Initiative proposed a core outcome set (COS) for adults and children living with Long COVID and a core outcome measures set (COMS) to assess those outcomes.42,45 Using an international Delphi process, authors identified 12 core outcomes for adults and seven for children and young people living with Long COVID.42 44 Through an additional Delphi process, authors achieved consensus on the use of three outcome measures (time until death, Recovery Scale for COVID-19 and Modified Medical Research Council Dyspnoea Scale) in adults and four outcome measures (Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL) multidimensional Fatigue scale; PedsQL Gastrointestinal Symptom scales; PedsQL Cognitive Functioning Scale; and EuroQol 5 Dimension family) in children and young people living with Long COVID.44 45 However, new outcome measures for people living with Long COVID have since emerged.46,50 Furthermore, the COMS did not include PREMs or PBOMs, nor assess measurement properties (validity, reliability, responsiveness, interpretability) of the measures in the context of Long COVID.45 Finally, exploration of the implementation considerations for outcome measures, such as utility, feasibility, administrative burden, required equipment and training, language translations, health literacy, scoring considerations and possible risk of negative outcomes in the context of Long COVID, is important to consider in the context of clinical practice and research. There is a need to build on the foundational work on measurement in Long COVID to advance the selection of timely and appropriate outcome measures among people living with Long COVID and their caregivers in clinical practice and research.42,45

Our aim is to develop a Framework of Measurement for adults (≥18 years old), children (<18 years old) and caregivers for use in Long COVID research and clinical practice. Specific objectives are to: (1) characterise evidence pertaining to outcome measurement among people living with Long COVID and their caregivers, including (but not limited to) constructs of interest; outcome measures (and their measurement properties); types of outcome measures (PBOMs, PROMs, PREMs); purposes of measurement (discriminative, predictive, evaluative); target populations (adults, children, caregivers) and contexts (research, clinical practice); and (2) identify implementation considerations for measurement among adults and children living with Long COVID and their caregivers. For all outcome measures that are identified, the corresponding constructs of interest, characteristics, context, population and measurement properties (when known in the context of people living with Long COVID), and implementation considerations will be mapped out in the Framework of Measurement.

Methods and analysis

Study design

We will conduct a scoping study involving (1) an evidence review and (2) a consultation, using methodological steps outlined by Arksey and O’Malley and the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) guidelines.51 52 We developed our protocol in accordance with the Arksey and O’Malley methodological framework for scoping studies and JBI guidelines.52 53 We used guidance on the reporting of scoping reviews including the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analyses Protocols (PRISMA-P) to further inform the protocol.54 We will report our findings as per the PRISMA Scoping Review Reporting Guidelines.55 56 The study will occur from 1 September 2023 until estimated completion on 31 August 2026.

We will conduct an evidence review of peer review published and grey (eg, reports, clinical guidelines, policy statements, etc) literature to identify existing outcome measures and their reported measurement properties when used among people living with Long COVID and their caregivers (steps 1–5). Next, we will conduct a two-phased international consultation. Part 1 will be an environmental scan involving a cross-sectional web-based questionnaire administered to key consultants (including people with lived experiences with Long COVID and caregivers), to identify outcome measures not found in the literature review and explore implementation considerations for outcome measurement in the context of Long COVID (step 6 – part 1). Phase 2 will be an international focus group with key consultants to review the findings from the evidence review and environmental scan and to highlight implementation considerations for outcome measurement in Long COVID (step 6 – part 2). Outcomes from this work will include a Framework of Measurement, which will be adapted into an online menu of options which can be used in Long COVID research and clinical practice (See figure 1).

Patient and public involvement

This study is funded by Long COVID Web (LCW), a national network to support and conduct research in Long COVID and was conceived and designed with the involvement of members of the LCW Patient Advisory Council.57 Funded by the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (Fund #185352), LCW is comprised of over 600 members (people living with Long COVID, caregivers, clinicians and researchers).57 People living with Long COVID, some of whom are members of the LCW Patient Advisory Council, are members of the study team who provide input regarding processes throughout the research. During the consultation phases (step 6 – parts 1 and 2), we will engage key consultants through the web-based questionnaire and focus groups to help identify outcome measures for inclusion in the Framework of Measurement and considerations for implementation of outcome measurement. Once the framework is published, we will develop an online menu of options of outcome measures used in Long COVID which will be available on the LCW website. For more details, see online supplemental file 1 – Principles of Patient Engagement for Measurement and Assessment Paradigm for Long COVID (MAP LC).

Ethics and dissemination

The protocol received approval from the University of Toronto (U of T) Health Sciences Research Ethics Board (protocol #46503) for the consultation phases of the study.

We will obtain informed consent from potential consultants who wish to participate in phase 1 (web-based questionnaire) or phase 2 (focus groups) consultations. The first page of the web-based questionnaire will include a letter of information and consent (online supplemental file 2: Consultation 1 – Information Letter and Consent Form). Potential consultants will provide written consent to complete the questionnaire by responding ‘yes’ to the following item: ‘I agree to participate in this consultation’. Potential focus group consultants will be asked to provide either written or verbal informed consent prior to participating in the focus group discussion (online supplemental file 3: Consultation 2 – Information Letter and Consent Form).

The U of T is the primary institution for the study, and the only institution where the data are being collected and stored. This is why the protocol received Research Ethics Board (REB) approval from U of T. We are using snowball sampling (word of mouth) to recruit for the consultation. Through this process, we may recruit some consultants who are from countries other than Canada. However, in the information letter consent forms for both consultation phases (onlinesupplemental files 2 3), we outline to potential consultants that U of T is the lead institution of the study, that consultation phases of the study went through REB approval at U of T, and that data will be collected and stored at U of T only. We are not recruiting through any academic institution other than U of T.

Step 2: identifying relevant studies

We will locate published and unpublished evidence by searching electronic databases (peer-reviewed articles), reference lists of eligible studies and grey literature. We use the term Long COVID broadly in our own work to encompass all health consequences following a SARS-CoV-2 infection. However, there are multiple terminologies associated with Long COVID.6,810 11 20 58 59 Our search strategy will therefore include articles that use any and/or multiple of those terminologies (see online supplemental file 4: Long COVID terminology and definitions).

We established a preliminary database search with an experienced health research information specialist. We will search databases including MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, PsycINFO and Scopus for articles published since 2020, considering the context of Long COVID research and practice. We will adapt and refine the search strategy, including all identified keywords and index terms, for each included database in consultation with the same information specialist (online supplemental file 5: Exemplar Search Strategy).4460,64 We will screen the reference list of all included sources of evidence for additional articles.

Step 3: study selection

We will upload all identified citations and grey literature into Covidence65 to facilitate our process of screening for inclusion and removing duplicate citations. Any duplicate citations missed by Covidence and identified by authors will be manually removed.

Six researchers will screen the first 20 citation titles and abstracts collectively to agree on the applicability of the predetermined study selection criteria. We will select articles that meet the following inclusion criteria: (a) include discussion or use of PROMs and/or PBOMs and/or PREMs for Long COVID health outcomes, (b) pertain to adults or children living with Long COVID and/or their caregivers, (c) are based in research or clinical settings, (d) are published from 2020 onwards and (e) are published in English or French languages (based on the capacity and knowledge of these languages among our research team). We will exclude review articles or conference abstracts but use these to hand search for other potentially relevant articles.

Once we reach agreement on the pilot screening process, three pairs of researchers will independently perform calibration training on 20 citation titles and abstracts (or until at least five citations are found to meet inclusion criteria). If inter-rater agreements are below 80%,66 we will clarify the eligibility criteria and repeat the calibration training. Then, six independent researchers will review all remaining citation titles and abstracts independently for assessment against the selection criteria. We will resolve any conflicts that arise via discussion. If unable to come to an agreement, a senior member of the research team will resolve the conflicts.

We will import the full text of potentially relevant sources into Covidence.65 Eight researchers will pilot screen the first five full text reviews. The same eight team members will then independently review all full texts against the selection criteria. We will resolve any conflicts that arise via discussion or, when necessary, with a senior member of the research team.

Step 4: charting the data

We will extract the following data from included articles: (a) characteristics of the article: author(s), year of publication, article country of origin, purpose, study design, target population (adults living with Long COVID, children living with Long COVID, caregivers), demographics of participants (including but not limited to age and gender); (b) terminology and definition of Long COVID in the context of the article; (c) health outcome(s) (constructs) assessed and outcome measures used in the context of Long COVID; (d) characteristics of the outcome measures (type: PROM, PBOM, PREM; purpose: descriptive/discriminative, evaluative, predictive; number and type of domains; number of items; context of administration: research or clinical practice; administrator of the outcome measures: patient, clinician, researcher, caregiver; mode of administration: online with computer, tablet or phone or on paper, interviewer administered); (e) measurement properties for each outcome measure reported by included studies in the context of people living with Long COVID (if applicable: reliability, validity, responsiveness, interpretability) according to the COnsensus-based Standards for the selection of health Measurement INstruments (COSMIN) taxonomy of measurement properties67; and (f) implementation considerations for outcome measurement in the context of Long COVID from authors (if applicable). We will extract data using Microsoft Excel (online supplemental file 6: Data Extraction Characteristics and Variables – Operational Definitions).68 Four researchers will pilot the data extraction form by independently extracting data from five articles that meet the selection criteria and, if necessary, refine the form after piloting. The research team will also consult members of the broader team who have lived experiences and/or are clinicians to ensure data extracted will be relevant to advancing current understanding of outcome measurement among people living with Long COVID and their caregivers. We will document revisions to the data extraction form. Two members of the research team will independently extract data from all remaining full texts onto the finalised data extraction form using Microsoft Excel.68 A third researcher will mitigate discrepancies during data extraction.

For articles with the purpose of assessing measurement properties of outcome measures used among people living with Long COVID and their caregivers, we will extract data pertaining to their methodological quality using the COSMIN checklist for assessing the methodological quality of studies on measurement properties.56 While we understand that methodological quality assessment is not typically a component of scoping reviews,69 assessing the methodological quality of the subset of articles that assessed measurement properties will be important given that our aim with the framework is to provide guidance for measurement in the context of Long COVID. Two researchers will independently assess the methodological quality, and a third reviewer will discuss and resolve any disagreements.

Step 5: collating, summarising and reporting results – creating a preliminary framework of measurement

We will describe the characteristics of included articles using frequencies (percentages) for dichotomous or categorical variables. For continuous variables, we will use means and SD when data are normally distributed and medians (IQRs) when data are not. We will conduct a descriptive content analysis of text data using an inductive approach to identify categories and summarise the extracted data.70 Specifically, we will describe the characteristics of outcome measures and associated measurement properties and implementation considerations (if applicable) in the context of Long COVID. These will be presented in summary tables or figures. We will map extracted data into a preliminary Framework of Measurement that includes: PROMs, PBOMs and PREMs to assess health outcomes for people living with Long COVID and their caregivers; the measurement properties of the outcome measures (and their methodological quality of assessment); and implementation considerations of the outcome measures. This framework will also highlight gaps in knowledge about measurement of health outcomes for people living with Long COVID and their caregivers. We will contextualise the framework as it relates to future recommendations and implications for outcome measurement in the context of Long COVID clinical practice and research.

Step 6: consultation

There will be two phases to our consultation process. Concurrent to the scoping evidence review (steps 1–5), we will conduct an environmental scan involving administration of a cross-sectional web-based questionnaire to identify evidence in outcome measurement and relevant implementation considerations not captured in the evidence review (step 6 – phase 1). Upon development of a preliminary framework, we will conduct international focus group discussions with key consultants (after step 5) to review and refine the preliminary framework (step 6 – phase 2).

Consultation phase 1: environmental scan (concurrent with the scoping evidence review)

We will administer a cross-sectional web-based questionnaire to key consultants to supplement the findings from our evidence review by: (a) identifying the relevant grey literature (unpublished literature, trial registers, clinical guidelines, policy documents) on outcome measures used in the context of Long COVID not retrieved from the database search; (b) identifying the strengths, limitations and knowledge gaps of current outcome measures in the context of Long COVID; and (c) identifying considerations for outcome measurement (online supplemental file 7: Consultation 1 – Web-based Questionnaire).

Consultants

We will include key consultants internationally, with experience or expertise in outcome measurement for Long COVID. Specifically, we will include consultants who: (a) are ≥18 years old; (b) have experience using or completing outcome measures for adults or children living with Long COVID and their caregivers (for themselves or for someone they provide care for); and (c) are able to read and understand English. Consultants may include, but will not be limited to members of LCW57 and the Rehabilitation Science Research Network for COVID,71 and may include people with lived experiences with Long COVID, caregivers of people living with Long COVID, clinicians, researchers, policy-makers, evaluators for health service organisations and representatives from community organisations. The Rehabilitation Science Research Network for COVID is comprised of over 100 members (people with lived experiences, clinicians and researchers) supporting and conducting research on Long COVID.71

Sampling and recruitment

Recruitment of consultants will be led by a core team of researchers within MAP LC, many of whom represent Long COVID research networks (such as LCW and the Rehabilitation Science Research Network for COVID), community support groups for people living with Long COVID or are involved in other research teams with an interest in outcome measurement for Long COVID. We will establish a sampling frame of potential individuals who meet the inclusion criteria for our consultation and purposively recruit to obtain representation of multiple geographic regions, people living with Long COVID, clinicians and researchers. Members of our core team have access to these research teams and networks. Next, we will use snowball sampling to facilitate recruitment through peers of consultants in the study, including internationally.70 We will send an invitation email requesting voluntary participation to potential consultants from our sampling frame who meet the inclusion criteria and possess interest and expertise in Long COVID and measurement. We will use a modified Dillman approach,72 with reminder emails sent to potential consultants at 1 week and 2 weeks after the initial invitation email. Consultant characteristics will be collated weekly, and we will target recruitment efforts to maximise the diversity of consultants.

Sample size estimation

We aim to recruit a maximum of 50 participants for the environmental scan (web-based questionnaire). Given the sizeable network of people with expertise and experiences in outcome measurement for Long COVID within our research team and among their individual networks, we anticipate that we will be able to obtain this sample size.

Data collection: questionnaire

We will administer a web-based self-reported questionnaire in English using Qualtrics software.73 Potential consultants will be asked to self-identify their eligibility (experience using or completing outcome measures for Long COVID). The web-based questionnaire will include three main sections: section one: personal characteristics (including what group they represent), clinical experiences (if applicable) and research experiences (if applicable); section two: outcome measures used for adults and children living with Long COVID and their caregivers (and associated published or unpublished literature, trial registries, clinical guidelines or policy documents that reference those outcome measures); and section three: overall impressions of outcome measurement in Long COVID. Sections two and three include questions about preferences and perceived limitations of existing measures to assess health outcomes and implementation considerations for outcome measurement among adults and children living with Long COVID and their caregivers. At the end of the questionnaire, consultants will be asked if they are willing to be contacted for the future consultation phase of the study. Responses from Qualtrics will be exported to a Microsoft Excel worksheet and SPSS Statistics V.29.0.1.0 for analysis.68 74

Analysis of environmental scan questionnaire data

We will analyse the questionnaire responses using frequencies (percentage) and medians (IQRs) for categorical and continuous variables, respectively, and descriptive content analysis of open-ended responses.75

Bringing evidence review and environmental scan together: drafting the preliminary framework of measurement

Results from the evidence review and environmental scan will be considered in unison to draft a preliminary Framework of Measurement for Long COVID. The research team will meet to discuss the results of the evidence review and environmental scan and to establish key components of the Framework. A draft of the framework will be reviewed by the full research team for feedback prior to subsequent phases.

Consultation phase 2: focus groups

We will conduct a cross-sectional international consultation using focus groups with key consultants to review and refine the preliminary Framework of Measurement. To increase accessibility and international and remote participation, we will conduct the focus group discussions online via Zoom,76 accounting for time zone differences. We will schedule breaks during the focus groups, web-camera use will be optional and we will circulate focus group materials, including the preliminary Framework of Measurement, prior to the scheduled discussion. Potential consultants will be able to contact the research coordinator to request other accessibility accommodations as needed.

Consultants

We will include key consultants internationally, with experience or expertise in outcome measurement for Long COVID. Inclusion criteria will be the same as for consultation phase 1, specifically adults (≥18 years), with experience using or completing outcome measures for adults or children living with Long COVID and their caregivers and who are able to read and understand English.

Sampling and recruitment

We will develop a sampling frame, drawing on the networks of MAP LC team members, word of mouth and those who expressed interest in future stages of research in phase 1 of our consultation. We will use a non-randomised, purposive and targeted sampling strategy to recruit key consultants from our sampling frame who represent different geographic regions, people with lived experiences, clinicians and researchers.77 We will email potential consultants from the sampling frame, asking them to participate in a focus group discussion with an attached letter of information and informed consent.

Sample size estimation

We aim to recruit a maximum of 30 consultants for the online focus groups. We will conduct 5–6 online focus groups (via Zoom) with 3–5 consultants per group for a total sample of 15–30 consultants.78

Data collection: focus group discussions

At the beginning of the focus group sessions, we will present the preliminary framework using a slide-deck presentation and then facilitate discussion by asking key consultants for feedback. Specifically, we will ask them to (a) review the components of the preliminary framework, (b) identify strengths and limitations of the outcome measures used in the context of Long COVID, (c) identify implementation considerations for use of existing outcome measures in the context of Long COVID and (d) highlight needs for future research. We will ask for suggestions on how to revise the framework. Finally, we will ask consultants for their guidance on strategies to translate the framework for broader uptake and usage among clinicians, researchers and people with lived experiences to guide the assessment, management and evaluation of Long COVID. One researcher will facilitate the discussions using a semistructured focus group discussion guide while another researcher will take notes of important topics and themes that arise during focus groups (online supplemental file 8: Focus Group Discussion Guide). We will audio record and transcribe the focus group discussions verbatim using Zoom software.

Analysis: focus group transcripts

We will conduct a directed content analysis of the focus group transcripts informed by the aim of refining the Framework according to our study objectives.75 Each transcript will be coded by two independent reviewers based on the specific aims of the focus group consultation: (a) overall feedback on the preliminary framework; (b) strengths and limitations of the outcome measures; (c) needs of future research; and (d) implementation considerations for existing outcome measures. These codes will be used to refine and revise the framework accordingly.

Outputs

Results will yield a Framework of Measurement that identifies outcome measures, their properties and considerations for measurement in the context of Long COVID. Results will highlight the strengths and limitations of existing outcome measures and identify areas for future research in Long COVID measurement. Results will map existing evidence and identify knowledge gaps, which is an important step in developing and selecting robust outcome measures for people living with Long COVID and their caregivers.

Strengths and potential limitations of our approach

Key strengths of this study are our use of established scoping review methodology,52 55 79 our broad selection criteria involving a range of terminologies, study designs and methodologies, iterative piloting of the inclusion and data extraction process and reporting the quality of studies analysing outcome measures used among people living with Long COVID and their caregivers. This work directly addresses research priorities in Long COVID and rehabilitation by advancing approaches for health assessment, evaluating impact of interventions and models of services delivery.80 This study involves a community-engaged approach, involving an international multidisciplinary team comprised of people with lived experiences of Long COVID, caregivers, clinicians and researchers with expertise in Long COVID and outcome measurement. Team members are affiliated with LCW,57 PC-COS,81 the Rehabilitation Science Research Network for COVID,71 and other groups with experience in Long COVID and measurement-related research. This study has the potential to identify outcome measures currently in use, and to identify knowledge gaps that will provide direction for future research. Further, consultation with the target population will: (1) address gaps in the published literature by directing us towards grey and unpublished literature, (2) provide insights on the outcome measures’ real-world implementation, (3) refine and improve the preliminary Framework of Measurement and (4) direct effective knowledge translation strategies.

Despite these strengths, we might face potential challenges. Despite our broad search strategy of multiple databases, we may miss relevant articles and grey literature. A multitude of terminologies and synonyms exists for Long COVID, and while we aim to include all possible definitions in our search strategy, it is possible that some will be missed. To mitigate these limitations, we will include all possible terminologies for Long COVID in our search strategy, iteratively expanding our search by extracting definitions from the literature search and environmental scan. Additionally, we will consult with an information specialist to develop our search strategy. By including articles only published in English and French, we will exclude literature pertaining to outcome measurement and Long COVID in other languages, that will exclude outcome measures used in the context of Long COVID in other languages and contexts. We aim to target a diverse, international and representative group of consultants in the environmental scan and focus groups. However, we anticipate challenges associated with obtaining sufficient response rates, equal representation across groups and coordination across global time zones. We will undertake efforts to enhance participation, such as offering multiple time and date options and offering to meet one-on-one with interested individuals who are unable to attend any of the scheduled focus groups. We will draw on our diverse team of researchers, clinicians and people with lived experiences and their broad networks to support our recruitment efforts. Our recruitment strategy is limited to our networks within the research team. This may limit our ability to more broadly identify people with lived experiences of Long COVID, caregivers, clinicians, researchers, policy-makers, evaluators for health service organisations and representatives from community organizations who meet the inclusion criteria and may possess further valuable insight to advancing considerations for outcome measurement in Long COVID. We will ensure diversity of the sample of consultation by drawing on the vast and diverse networks of members of the research team to obtain comprehensive and constructive information to inform framework refinement in the consultation phase.

Knowledge translation plan

We will create and publish a Framework of Measurement comprised of core outcome measures, their measurement properties and implementation considerations for use in Long COVID research and practice. We will translate this framework into an online menu of outcome measures for people living with Long COVID and their caregivers that can be used by clinicians, researchers and people with lived experiences to guide the assessment, management and evaluation of Long COVID. This menu, with hyperlinks to the relevant outcome measures (when publicly available), will be openly accessible on LCW’s website57 and updated periodically with a record of dates and new literature. A corresponding guidance document to facilitate the appropriate selection of outcome measures and awareness of their associated measurement properties among researchers, clinicians, patients and their caregivers will also be made available.

We will disseminate the framework through multiple networks and platforms such as the Post-COVID-19 Interdisciplinary Clinical Care Network;82 83 Realise Canada’s Episodic Disability Network;84 Long COVID community groups that include, but are not limited to, COVID Long-Haulers Support Group Canada, Long COVID Physio, Patient-Led Research Collaborative and Long COVID Support;85,88 and the Rehabilitation Science Research Network for COVID website.71 To reach patient populations and caregivers, we will develop a toolkit that they can use to ask important questions of their providers, similar to the work done in rheumatology populations.89 90 We also plan to present our findings at conferences including the Canadian Symposium on Long COVID,57 International Forum on COVID Rehabilitation Research and Canadian Critical Care Trials Group presentation series on Long COVID.

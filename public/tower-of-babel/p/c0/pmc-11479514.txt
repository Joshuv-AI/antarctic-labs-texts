Influence of nursing intervention on patients with recurrent bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel
Authors: Zhihui Chen, Aimin Xing, Rui Yang, ChunYang Hu, Pengfei Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Bacterial vaginosis significantly affects gynecological health of women, and recurrence is common with a high prevalence in patients who received sucrose gel treatment. Evidence has found that nursing intervention has benefits in improving the rate of recurrence of gynecological diseases. The purpose of this study was to investigate the influence of nursing intervention on patients with recurrent bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel.Methods:A total of 1096 patients with bacterial vaginosis were recruited and randomly divided into the intervention nursing group (n = 548) and the control group (n = 548) between May 2022 and October 2022. Patients with bacterial vaginosis in the nursing intervention group received the individualized nursing intervention program, while patients in the control group received routine nursing for vaginitis and follow-up. The clinical efficacy and the short form health survey MOS 36 items (SF-36) scale were used to evaluate the efficacy of nursing intervention efficacy in patients with vaginosis treated with sucrose gel.Results:Our results indicate that the nursing intervention was associated with significant improvements in physical functioning, depression, mental health, bodily vitality pain, and symptoms related to bacterial vaginosis compared to the control group patients. The social functioning-36 score in the nursing intervention group was higher than that in the control group during the 6-month period. Nursing intervention significantly decreased the recurrence of bacterial vaginosis via improvement quality of life and psychological states.Conclusions:In conclusion, the data in this study indicate that the nursing intervention improves recurrent bacterial vaginosis and the quality of life of patients treated with sucrose gel.

1. Introduction

Bacterial vaginosis occurs in the lower genital tract and is associated with elevated vaginal pH and the presence of abnormal offensive discharge.[1] Bacterial vaginosis is 1 of the most common infectious diseases and is persistent with a high prevalence in 20% to 50% among women of reproductive age.[2] Bacterial vaginosis is characterized by the disorder of the vaginal ecosystem, where the amount of Lactobacillus is markedly reduced in the vagina.[3] Clinically, bacterial vaginosis is frequently caused by the reproduction of anaerobic microorganisms (Gardnerella vaginalis, Prevotella, Mycoplasma hominis, Mobiluncus, and Clostridia), which inhibit Lactobacillus levels.[4] The high incidence of recurrent bacterial vaginosis may result in negative emotional, social, sexual and reproductive health sequelae.[5] After treatment with an antibiotic agent, many women (20% to 75%) experience recurrent bacterial vaginosis within 3 months.[6] Patients with bacterial vaginosis recently prescribed antibiotics and a medical history of sexually transmitted infections significantly more frequently presented recurrent bacterial vaginosis, and primary care can evaluate the approach to the diagnosis and treatment of recurrent bacterial vaginosis.[7] These reports suggest that new agents should be explored to prevent bacterial vaginosis.

The current management strategy for patients with bacterial vaginosis should be treated with a regimen described in the Centers for Disease Control guidelines.[8] The frequently used treatment for patients with bacterial vaginosis is oral and/or vaginal forms of metronidazole.[9] However, metronidazole has some side effects, including metallic taste in the mouth, nausea, and abdominal pain. Considering the complications associated with chemical drugs, alternative treatment with fewer side effects needs to be developed to increase the therapeutic efficacy. Evidence has found that the normal flora of the male genitalia can use sucrose as a nutritional supplement to produce lactic acid and hydrogen peroxide to maintain pH, which creates an undesirable environment for the growth of pathogens.[10] A clinical study has shown that sucrose gel restores normal vaginal flora more rapidly than metronidazole gel, which can be used as a novel treatment for bacterial vaginosis.[11] The sucrose vaginal gel can be considered as a possible alternative to metronidazole vaginal gel in the treatment of women with bacterial vaginosis.[12] Treatment with sucrose vaginal gel (7.5 g) improves the clinical efficacy index and the laboratory index of bacterial vaginosis.[13] However, a high recurrence rate of bacterial vaginosis recurrence after single sucrose vaginal gel therapy has been reported in clinical patients.[14]

Nursing intervention can increase the cure rate, change adverse cognition, and improve the quality of life of recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis patients treated with the ATP-infrared bio-effect technique.[15] Furthermore, the osmolarity of sucrose vaginal gel results in the disappearance of pathogenic bacteria in the vagina in patients with bacterial vaginosis. However, no report investigated the efficacy of nursing intervention in patients with recurrent bacterial treated with sucrose gel. To evaluate the effect of a structured nursing intervention program on recurrence rates of bacterial vaginosis in patients treated with sucrose gel over a 6-month period, quality of life measures in patients with recurrent bacterial vaginosis, adherence to treatment regimens and lifestyle modifications. We hypothesized that the nursing intervention decreased the recurrence of bacterial vaginosis by improving quality of life and psychological states to maintain bacterial vaginosis clearance that contribute to sucrose vaginal gel therapy.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Patients

This blind and parallel randomized clinical trial was conducted in 1096 married women (23–35 years) with bacterial vaginosis, who were patients in the women’s clinic from May 2022 to October 2022. The sample size was justified using the G*Power software (Version 3.1.9.7; Heinrich-Heine-Universität Düsseldorf, Düsseldorf, Germany) as previously described.[16] Block randomization was used to randomize the recruited participants. Nursing intervention care providers and outcome assessors were blinded to randomly assign participants via computer generated sequence. After the definitive diagnosis of bacterial vaginosis in the samples, the patients were randomly divided into the nursing intervention (n = 548) and control (n = 548) groups. Inclusion criteria: married woman; women diagnosed with bacterial vaginosis; patients without antibiotic therapy within 2 weeks prior to the study; older than 18 years. Exclusion criteria: pregnant woman, patients with cancer; patients with other vaginal infections; patients with severe heart diseases; patients with chronic renal failure; patients with HIV. The study was approved by the ethics committee of the Affiliated Hongqi Hospital of Mudanjiang Medical University (Approval No: AHMMU20220510). Informed consents were signed by each patient. The confidentiality of each patient was maintained anonymously.

2.2. Nursing intervention

The patients with bacterial vaginosis in the control group were received with a routine nursing service. All patients received sucrose gel treatment (5.0 g) in 2 groups. The nursing intervention group included nurse-led intervention measures based on routine nursing. Nurse-led intervention measures included gynecological nurses serving intervention project managers and resource coordinators to provide reminder, guidance on consultation, interview, and gynecological health education for patients with bacterial vaginosis. The specific nursing intervention was conducted as described previously.[15] In summary, control patients received routine nursing including personal hygiene, vulva condition, sexual life, daily diet, and drug use. The nursing intervention included routine nursing and additional care. Additional care scheme includes guidance, teaching and counseling, medication management, clinical treatments, treatment planning patient education, and surveillance categories. Guidance included supportive care for self-management through education (online education twice a month), links between nurses and patients, balance of care between the patient and professional providers. Specific intervention measures are carried out as follows: questionnaire; assessment; inquiry; propaganda and education; personalized nursing; 6 months follow-up.

2.3. Evaluation of efficiency

All clinical symptoms and relevant laboratory examination results were evaluated by 3 independent gynecologists. The SPPB score was used to evaluate the physical function of patients with bacterial vaginosis. Depressive symptoms as measured by the Patient Health Questionnaire 8 (PHQ-8), and anxiety symptoms as measured by the Generalized Anxiety Disorder Questionnaire (GAD-7). Short form health survey item MOS 36 (SF-36) scale.[17] In summary, SF-36 had 8 dimensions, and 36 items included physical functioning (PF), social functioning (SF), vitality (V), physical role (RP), mental health (MH), general health (GH), bodily pain (BP), and emotional role (RE). SF-36 scores ranged from 0 to 100.

2.4. Outcome measurement

Data were collected through demographic questionnaires, a self-reporting sheet on patient symptoms, an observation checklist used for 6 months of follow-up. All vaginal symptoms were observed using a microscope (Olympus, Japan) and pH was measured using pH test strips (Merck, Germany). The checklist included questions related to patient complaints and the Amsel criteria, as previously reported.

2.5. Statistical methods

Data are analyzed using Statistical Product and Service Solutions version 19.0 software (Chicago). Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics (mean ± standard deviation) and inferential statistics (t test, chi-square test and McNemar test). The Student t test or the Mann-Whitney test was used for the comparison of the measurement data between the 2 groups. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey post hoc test for multiple comparisons between multiple groups. The occurrence was analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method. P < .05 were considered statistically significant.

3.3. Clinical efficacy of intervention nursing in patients with bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel

Index comparisons between the 2 groups on month 6 were compared in patients with bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel. Data showed that patients in the intervention nursing group had a higher SF-36 scale score than that of the control group (Table 3). At 6 months of follow-up, the PF, RP, RE, SF, BP, V, GH, and MH scores of patients were higher in the intervention nursing group than in the control group at the end of intensive therapy, showing statistically significant differences (P < .01). At 6 months of follow-up, the results demonstrated that intervention nursing markedly improved quality of life for patients with bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel. Statistically significant differences of SF-30 score were found between the intervention nursing and control groups in patients with bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel during the 6-month treatment (Fig. 2).

4. Discussion

Bacterial vaginosis presents serious reproductive health risks and management challenges, with poor control and high symptomatic recurrence.[18] Although bacterial vaginosis treatment strategies decrease pathogenesis symptoms, recurrence rates are extremely high.[19] Clinically, the nursing of bacterial vaginosis is an effective treatment method for patients with bacterial vaginosis after treatment with an antibacterial agent.[20] Nursing intervention plays a crucial role in sexual life, psychological treatment, and social impacts for patients with bacterial vaginosis. A previous study showed that nursing intervention can improve the quality of life of patients with recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis treated with the ATP-infrared bio-effect technique.[15] In this study, we investigated the clinical effects of nursing intervention on PF, depression, MH, bodily vitality pain, symptoms related to bacterial vaginosis, quality of life, and rate of recurrence during a 6-month period in patients with bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel. The data in the current study found that the nursing intervention not only improved bacterial vaginosis-related symptoms, but also improved quality of life and recurrence rate during the 6-month period in patients with sucrose gel-treated bacterial vaginosis.

Sucrose gel restored normal vaginal flora more rapidly than metronidazole gel and could be used as a novel treatment for bacterial vaginosis.[21] The results demonstrated that sucrose gel improved PF, depression, MH, bodily vitality pain, and symptoms related to bacterial vaginosis. It is common and often recurrent in patients with bacterial vaginosis after receiving treatment.[22] After 6 months of follow-up, bacterial vaginosis recurrences in the nursing intervention group were less frequent in asymptomatic forms than in the control group. The treatment options for bacterial vaginosis are associated with a high rate of symptomatic recurrence.[23] The clinical management of bacterial vaginosis could be improved through the use of standardized diagnostic approaches, increased sensitivity, and understanding of the impact of bacterial vaginosis.[24] During the long-term nursing intervention, quality of life was greatly improved for patients with bacterial vaginosis. During the first trimester of the nursing intervention, the vagina must be thoroughly washed and avoid sexual life. The data in this study showed that the nursing intervention improved homogeneous gray discharge, presence of clue cells, whiff test, and pH. Improving patients with bacterial vaginosis in the nursing intervention group had a significant increase in all SF-36 domains, except for role-PF due to the improvement of psychological states. Importantly, a good positive frame of mind may enhance immunity for patients with bacterial vaginosis, which reverses the therapeutic effects of sucrose gel and further leads to the inhibition of pathogens.

Bacterial vaginosis presented serious reproductive health risks and management challenges, with poor control attributed to the survival of treatment-resistant biofilm communities.[25] At 6 months of follow-up, patients in the nursing intervention had higher scores of PF, RP, RE, SF, BP, V, GH, and MH than those in the control group, which showed the role of the nursing intervention in improving anxiety and depression in patients with bacterial vaginosis. Importantly, the nursing intervention improved the 4 Amsel criteria compared to the control group for patients with bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel. At 6 months of follow-up, an improvement in the vaginal microecosystem has been observed in patients in the nursing intervention group. In the treatment of patients with sucrose gel-treated bacterial vaginosis, nursing intervention can provide professional expertise, implement holistic nursing according to the patient’s understanding of the disease and psychological state, which made the patient’s physical and MH in the best state and further improved the local microecological environment of the vagina and the quality of life of patients with bacterial vaginosis.

Several limitations should be addressed in this study. First, the inclusion and exclusion criteria are somewhat broad, and the potential impact of these criteria on study outcomes is not fully explored. Second, the 6-month follow-up period, while informative, may not be sufficient to capture long-term outcomes and recurrence rates, which are critical for assessing the durability of the effects of the nursing intervention. Third, the study’s follow-up was only carried out at 6 months and should be included to ensure that the nursing intervention was effective in improving the recurrence of bacterial vaginosis.

In conclusion, the results of the current study indicate that the nursing intervention has beneficial effects on the prevention of patients with recurrent bacterial vaginosis treated with sucrose gel. The results further show that the nursing intervention improves the recurrence of the nursing intervention, the quality of life, the 4 criteria of Amsel, and reduces the symptoms related to bacterial vaginosis, suggesting that it may be an ideal adjuvant therapy for preventing recurrence of patients with sucrose gel-treated with bacterial vaginosis.

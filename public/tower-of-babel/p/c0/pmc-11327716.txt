Peer review of the pesticide risk assessment of the active substance 1‐methylcyclopropene
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Fernando Álvarez, Maria Arena, Domenica Auteri, Sofia Batista Leite, Marco Binaglia, Anna Federica Castoldi, Arianna Chiusolo, Angelo Colagiorgi, Mathilde Colas, Federica Crivellente, Chloe De Lentdecker, Isabella De Magistris, Mark Egsmose, Gabriella Fait, Franco Ferilli, German Giner Santonja, Varvara Gouliarmou, Katrin Halling, Laia Herrero Nogareda, Alessio Ippolito, Frederique Istace, Samira Jarrah, Dimitra Kardassi, Aude Kienzler, Anna Lanzoni, Roberto Lava, Renata Leuschner, Alberto Linguadoca, Jochem Louisse, Christopher Lythgo, Oriol Magrans, Iris Mangas, Galini Mavriou, Andrea Mioč, Ileana Miron, Tunde Molnar, Laura Padovani, Vincenzo Padricello, Martina Panzarea
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe conclusions of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) following the peer review of the initial risk assessments carried out by the competent authority of the rapporteur Member State The Netherlands for the pesticide active substance 1‐methylcyclopropene are reported. The context of the peer review was that required by Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council for an amendment in approval conditions. The current approval of 1‐methylcyclopropene includes the specific provision ‘Only uses as plant growth regulator for post‐harvest storage in sealable warehouse may be authorised’. The applicant AgroFresh Holding France SAS submitted, in accordance with Article 7 of Reg. (EC) 1107/2009, an application to remove this specific provision in order to allow member states to authorise the use of products containing 1‐methylcyclopropene on outdoor crops pre‐harvest. The conclusions were reached on the basis of the evaluation of the representative use(s) of 1‐methylcyclopropene as a plant growth regulator via spray application on pome fruit. The reliable endpoints, appropriate for use in regulatory risk assessment are presented. Missing information identified as being required by the regulatory framework is listed. Concerns are reported where identified.Keywords: 1‐methylcyclopropene, peer review, pesticide, plant growth regulator, risk assessment

SUMMARY

Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council lays down, inter alia, the detailed rules as regards the procedure for the assessment of applications for amendment to the conditions of approval of active substances.

1‐methylcyclopropene was renewed on 1 August 2019 by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2019/1085, following a peer review of the risk assessment as set out in the EFSA Conclusion on 1‐methylcyclopropene, approved on 24 May 2018. It was a specific provision of the renewal of approval that only uses as plant growth regulator for post‐harvest storage in sealable warehouse may be authorised. In accordance with Article 7 of the Regulation, the rapporteur Member State (RMS) The Netherlands received an application from AgroFresh Holding France SAS on 4 March 2019 for amendment to the conditions of approval of the active substance 1‐methylcyclopropene to lift the restriction and to allow uses on outdoor crops pre‐harvest to be authorised. A new representative formulation (AF‐701) with a representative use as spray application to pome fruit is applied for and is evaluated.

An initial evaluation of the dossier on 1‐methylcyclopropene was provided by the RMS in a revised DAR and subsequently, a peer review of the pesticide risk assessment on the RMS evaluation was conducted by EFSA in accordance with Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. The following conclusions are derived.

The uses of 1‐methylcyclopropene according to the representative uses as a plant growth regulator via spray application on pome fruit, as proposed at EU level result in a sufficient delay of the maturation of pome fruit in the orchard.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to identity, physical–chemical and technical properties of the active substance and the representative formulation and analytical methods.

With regard to mammalian toxicology, no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to the assessment of the new representative formulation in outdoor crops (orchards) were identified.

The assessment of the data package revealed no issues that could not be finalised or that need to be included as critical areas of concern with respect to residues.

The assessment of the environmental fate and behaviour, based on the available data, leads to issues that could not be finalised with regard to the groundwater exposure of the active substance and the metabolite methallyl alcohol. The incomplete information on the formation of methallyl alcohol in the soil compartment and the use of default endpoints for both the active substance and the metabolite, predicted the parametric drinking water limit of 0.1 μg/L to be potentially exceeded in the 80th percentile annual average soil water concentrations moving below 1 m depth and that is used in assessments to represent vulnerable groundwater. The consumer risk assessment from the consumption of drinking water was also not finalised due to incomplete information with regard to the unknown nature of residues that might be present in drinking water, consequent to water treatment following abstraction of surface water that might contain the active substance and its metabolite methallyl alcohol.

With regard to ecotoxicology, the aquatic and soil risk assessment could not be finalised for the metabolite methallyl alcohol. No critical areas of concern were identified for any group of non‐target organisms.

BACKGROUND

Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council 1 (hereinafter referred to as ‘the Regulation’) lays down, inter alia, the detailed rules as regards the procedure for the assessment for an amendment to the conditions of an approval of active substances. This regulates for the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) the procedure for organising the consultation of Member States and the applicant(s) for comments on the initial evaluation in the draft assessment report (DAR), provided by the rapporteur Member State (RMS), and the organisation of an expert consultation, where appropriate.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA is required to adopt a conclusion on whether an active substance can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation (also taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation) within 120 days from the end of the period provided for the submission of written comments, subject to an extension of 30 days where an expert consultation is necessary, and a further extension of up to 150 days where additional information is required to be submitted by the applicant(s) in accordance with Article 12(3).

The approval of 1‐methylcyclopropene was renewed on 1 August 2019 by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 2019/1085, 2 following a peer review of the risk assessment as set out in the EFSA Conclusion on 1‐methylcyclopropene, approved on 24 May 2018 (EFSA, 2018). It was a specific provision of the renewal of approval that only uses as plant growth regulator for post‐harvest storage in sealable warehouse may be authorised. In accordance with Article 7 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, The Netherlands (hereinafter referred to as the rapporteur Member State, ‘RMS’) subsequently received an application from AgroFresh Holding France SAS on 4 March 2019 for amendment to the conditions of approval of the active substance 1‐methylcyclopropene to lift the restriction and allow uses on outdoor crops pre‐harvest to be authorised.

The RMS provided its initial evaluation of the dossier on 1‐methylcyclopropene in the form of a revised DAR, which was received by EFSA on 12 October 2021 (The Netherlands, 2021). The peer review was initiated on 07 July 2022 by dispatching the revised DAR to Member States and the applicant, AgroFresh Holding France SAS, for consultation and comments. EFSA also provided comments. In addition, EFSA conducted a public consultation on the revised DAR. The comments received were collated by EFSA and forwarded to the RMS for compilation and evaluation in the format of a reporting table. The applicant was invited to respond to the comments in column 3 of the reporting table. The comments and the applicant response were evaluated by the RMS in column 3.

The need for expert consultation and the necessity for additional information to be submitted by the applicant in accordance with Article 12(3) of the Regulation were considered in a teleconference between EFSA and the RMS on 17 November 2022. On the basis of the comments received, the applicant's response to the comments and the RMS's evaluation thereof, it was concluded that additional information should be requested from the applicant, and that EFSA should conduct an expert consultation in the areas of mammalian toxicology, residues, and environmental fate and behaviour.

The outcome of the teleconference, together with EFSA's further consideration of the comments is reflected in the conclusions set out in column 4 of the reporting table. All points that were identified as unresolved at the end of the comment evaluation phase and which required further consideration, including those issues to be considered in an expert consultation, were compiled by EFSA in the format of an evaluation table.

The conclusions arising from the consideration by EFSA, and as appropriate by the RMS, of the points identified in the evaluation table, together with the outcome of the expert consultation and the written consultation on the assessment of additional information, where these took place, were reported in the final column of the evaluation table.

In accordance with Article 12 of the Regulation, EFSA should adopt a conclusion on whether 1‐methylcyclopropene can be expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of the Regulation, taking into consideration recital (10) of the Regulation.

A final consultation on the conclusions arising from the peer review of the risk assessment took place with Member States via a written procedure in June – July 2024.

This conclusion report summarises the outcome of the peer review of the risk assessment on the active substance and the formulation(s) for representative uses evaluated on the basis of the representative uses of 1‐methylcyclopropene as a plant growth regulator via spray application on pome fruit as proposed by the applicant. In accordance with Article 12(2) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, risk mitigation options identified in the DAR and considered during the peer review, if any, are presented in the conclusion.

The active substance and the formulation(s) for representative uses

IUPAC name of the active substance is 1‐methylcyclopropene. Since the name is reasonably short a common ISO name is deemed unnecessary, however 1‐MCP is an unofficial abbreviation used. 1‐MCP is a gas, which at high concentration is chemically unstable and begins to self‐react immediately. As a result of these properties and safety concerns the active substance is never isolated in the manufacturing process and transport. Instead, it is produced as a technical concentrate (α‐cyclodextrin complex).

The formulated product for this evaluation is ‘AF‐701’ a suspension concentrate (SC) containing 17.15 g/L 1‐MCP.

The information on the active substance and the formulations for representative uses, including the co‐formulants in these formulations, was considered in the overall assessment during the peer review. None of the co‐formulants is an unacceptable co‐formulant listed in Annex III of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 3 nor considered as an active substance in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009. Details on the composition of the formulations cannot be reported in conclusions because of the provisions in Article 63(2)(d) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, however this information was fully available and evaluated during the peer review. A proposal for classification of the formulation(s) according to Regulation (EC) 1272/2008 was provided by the applicant and assessed by the RMS (please see Volumes 3 CP of the RAR).

The representative use evaluated was pre‐harvest treatment by air blast orchard sprayer fed by direct injection system (field application) on pome fruit to delay fruit ripening. Full details of the representative use can be found in the list of end points in Appendix B.

Data were submitted to conclude that the representative use of 1‐MCP proposed at EU level result in a sufficient efficacy as a plant growth regulator following the guidance document SANCO/10054/2013‐rev. 3 (European Commission, 2013).

CONCLUSIONS OF THE EVALUATION

General aspects

With regard to the mammalian toxicity information available for the formulation for representative uses ‘AF‐701’, studies were performed for acute toxicity endpoints. With regard to the co‐formulants contained in ‘AF‐701’, sufficient toxicological data were available for all components. EFSA considered that the available toxicological information did sufficiently address the genotoxicity and repeated dose toxicity potential of ‘AF‐701’ over the short‐ and long‐term and no concern was identified.

Regarding ecotoxicology, suitable ecotoxicity data with the formulation for representative uses ‘AF‐701’ (Harvista 1.3 SC) were available for the assessment of non‐target organisms according to the requirements of Regulation (EU) No 284/2013. 4 Based on the available acute toxicity data, it is noted that the formulation for representative uses is not more acutely toxic than the active substance. No chronic data with the formulation for representative uses were available with the exception of earthworms. Therefore, available data for the individual components were retrieved by EFSA. From the available data no concerns were identified.

IDENTITY, PHYSICAL/CHEMICAL/TECHNICAL PROPERTIES AND METHODS OF ANALYSIS

The following guidance documents were followed in the production of this conclusion: European Commission (2000a, 2000b, 2010, 2013).

Data submitted for the amendment of approval conditions do not change the minimum purity of 980 g/kg, the specification levels of the relevant impurities 1‐chloro‐2‐methylpropene – 0.2 g/kg and 3‐chloro‐2‐methylpropene – 0.2 g/kg and the specification levels of the significant impurities as proposed in the assessment previously conducted (EFSA, 2018). It is noted that the active substance is manufactured as a stabilised technical concentrate (TK). In the TK, the content of 1‐MCP is in the range of 41–49 g/kg and the maximum content of the relevant impurities is 0.01 g/kg (each).

The data submitted for amendment of approval conditions do not change the previous conclusions relevant to the physical and chemical properties and methods for analysis of the active substance and the impurities in the technical material (EFSA, 2018). For the formulation submitted for the amendment of the approval conditions, a validated method for analysis of the active substance and the relevant impurities was provided. It was demonstrated that the formulation for this evaluation is stable when stored for 1 year at 20°C in HDPE packaging.

Adequate methods are available for the generation of data required for the amendment of approval conditions.

Validated monitoring methods for determination of 1‐MCP in high water and high‐acid content plant commodities and air are available as reported in previous evaluation (EFSA, 2018). The data gap for monitoring method for analysis of 1‐MCP in dry and high‐oil content plant commodities remains (see Section 10).

Analytical method for monitoring in food of animal origin, is not required since no residue definition for monitoring is set.

1‐MCP can be determined by GS‐MS/MS in soil and water with LOQs of 0.05 mg/kg and 0.1 μg/L, respectively. The method for analysis in water was validated by an ILV and the LOQ was confirmed, however the method for soil has some deficiencies (data gap, see Section 10). It is noted that the residue definition for monitoring in soil and water is open (see Sections 4 and 5) and new monitoring methods might be required if new components are included in the residue definitions.

1‐MCP in body fluids and tissues can be analysed by GS–MS with LOQs of 10 μg/L and 10 μg/kg, respectively.

MAMMALIAN TOXICITY

The data submitted for the amendment of approval conditions of the active substance 1‐MCP have been discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Teleconference (TC) 114 (4–8 September 2023). The target was to confirm the adequacy of the previous conclusions on the derivation of toxicological reference values (EFSA, 2018), and to consider non‐dietary exposure estimates for a new intended use of a new representative formulation. The assessment was based on the following guidance documents: European Commission (2003) and EFSA (2014, 2017). The updated literature review did not provide any additional relevant information.

The acceptable daily intake (ADI) is confirmed to be 0.02 mg/kg bw per day based on a NOAEL of 3.8 mg/kg bw per day in the rat 2‐generation study and a NOAEL of 4.1 mg/kg bw per day in the dog 90‐day study (oral), applying an uncertainty factor (UF) of 200 (extra UF of 2 for the extrapolation from subchronic to chronic). The waiving of long‐term toxicity and carcinogenicity studies is confirmed to be acceptable for the current application for amendment of approval conditions on the basis of the non‐significant dietary exposure (see Section 3) and the lack of further concern from the available dataset.

The acute reference dose (ARfD) is also confirmed to be 0.12 mg/kg bw per day based on early effects on body weight seen at 31 mg/kg bw per day in the 90‐day dog study and UF of 100.

For the acceptable operator exposure level (AOEL), a new assessment of the absorption by inhalation of 1‐MCP in rats has been provided as a follow up of the EFSA conclusion in 2018. Based on the comparison of the plasma AUCs (area under the curve) from the inhalation toxicokinetic study and the oral ADME study in rats, a mean bioavailability following inhalation of 66% is estimated for 1‐MCP. Taking into account the limitations of the 90‐day rat study by inhalation, the studies by oral administration were considered more accurate to determine the internal dose and to derive an AOEL. As a consequence, the AOEL is confirmed to be 0.04 mg/kg bw per day based on rat 2‐generation study and dog 90‐day study with UF of 100 and no correction for oral absorption. 5 The AOEL derived from the 90‐day inhalation study in rats, considering 66% of absorption, would result in the same value and could be considered supportive.

The acute acceptable operator exposure level (AAOEL) is also confirmed to be 0.12 mg/kg bw per day based on early effects on body weight seen at 12 mg/kg bw per day in the 90‐day dog study and UF of 100.

With regard to dermal absorption, 6 the default value of 10% is applicable for the concentrate of the representative formulation AF‐701 (aqueous formulation). For the in‐use dilution (0.15 g/L), technical difficulties were reported in testing the aqueous formulation AF‐701 in the preliminary dermal absorption study, due to the high volatility. Therefore, the results obtained with the oil‐based diluted formulation AF‐600 have been used, resulting in a dermal absorption value of 0.11% for the in‐use dilution after pro‐rata correction.

As concerns the non‐dietary exposure for the new use on outdoor pome fruits pre‐harvest, estimates were provided with the EFSA model 2015 (EFSA, 2014) combined with measured data from a field study. 7 For operators, the total systemic exposure (dermal from EFSA model + inhalation from field study for the application phase) is below the (A)AOEL when using workwear. For workers, applying the default DT50 of 30 days for dermal exposure with the EFSA model, and the results of the field study for inhalation exposure, the total systemic exposure is below the AOEL for both tasks (crop inspection and hand harvesting) without use of gloves. For bystanders and residents, the total systemic exposure (oral and dermal from EFSA model + inhalation from field study) is also below the (A)AOEL.

For the GW metabolite methallyl alcohol (see Section 4), no toxicological assessment was provided (see Section 9).

RESIDUES

The assessment in the residue section is based on the OECD guidance document on overview of residue chemistry studies (OECD, 2009), the OECD publication on MRL calculations (OECD, 2011), the European Commission guideline document on MRL setting (European Commission, 2011) and the Joint Meeting on Pesticide Residues (JMPR) recommendations on livestock burden calculations (JMPR, 2004, 2007).

1‐methylcyclopropene was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Teleconference (TC) 116 (12 September 2023).

For the purpose of the extension of the use pattern to amend the conditions of approval, three studies applying radiolabelled 1‐MCP to apple and tomato (fruit crop) and maize (cereal and grass) have been submitted. The study on apple covered the critical GAP conditions. No identification and characterisation was performed in the studies with apple and tomato due to the low residues in the edible parts and the metabolic pathway in fruit for 1‐MCP cannot be derived. However, considering the low residues in these studies and in the valid residue field trials with pome fruit, which were below the limit of detection, a default residue definition for risk assessment and for enforcement can be set for fruit as 1‐Methylcyclopropene. These residue definitions are already applicable for fruit crops as pre‐harvest use and now extended to foliar application.

With respect to the third study in maize, identification and characterisation attempts were not performed in food and feed parts despite the sufficiently high residue levels. Therefore, the study is not considered for risk assessment and no conclusion can be derived on the metabolism in cereal and grass crops.

Other studies were neither requested nor provided.

It is noted that neither the metabolism studies nor the valid residue field trials provide information on the potential formation/presence of the metabolite methallyl alcohol in fruit. Given the outstanding information to finalise the ground water exposure assessment (see Section 4) and depending on these toxicological properties it is recommended to generate further data.

Chronic and acute consumer dietary intake assessments were performed using the revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (Pesticide Residue Intake Model). The calculated theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) for 1‐MCP accounts for 0.8% of the ADI (NL toddler) for apple. The IESTI for pears is maximally 1% of the ARfD (NL toddlers).

ENVIRONMENTAL FATE AND BEHAVIOUR

The environmental exposure assessment of 1‐Methylcyclopropene (1‐MCP) for the purpose of extension of the use pattern outdoor as pre‐harvest in pome fruits was discussed at the Pesticides Peer Review Teleconference (TC) 115 (5 September 2023).

1‐MCP under environmentally relevant conditions is a gas. The laboratory aerobic soil metabolism and the soil deposition studies provided did not allow to define a clear route of degradation of the active substance in soil. The experts during the Peer Review teleconference agreed that, considering that the active substance is a gas, the aerobic soil metabolism study can be assessed in a weight of evidence approach, but not to derive reliable kinetic parameters to address the rate of degradation. 8 Taking into account the uncertainty identified in the study, mainly due to the quantification of the minor alcoholic fractions and the loss of active substance via volatilisation, experts agreed that the metabolite methallyl alcohol should be considered a metabolite triggering the assessment and therefore a data gap (see Sections 9.1 and 10) has been identified for the applicant to address its formation in the soil compartment. Additionally, for all the soils tested in the soil metabolism study the formation of unextracted residues (not extracted by acetonitrile/water and acidified acetonitrile) reached levels above the 70%, while mineralisation of the cyclopropene ring 14C radiolabel to carbon dioxide accounted for less than 5% at the end of the study (study is finalised after 7 days). Despite these results should be carefully considered for the approval according to the Commission Regulation (EU) N. 546/2011 (Uniform Principles Regulation), 9 the ‘unless clause’ is fulfilled and further assessments were not considered necessary for the residue and ecotoxicology sections. 10 1‐MCP exhibited very high mobility in soil based on a value of Koc estimated using HPLC method (OECD 121, 2001), while for the metabolite methallyl alcohol, experts agreed to use the default worst‐case Kfoc values of 10 mL/g for the groundwater/surface water compartments and of 10,000 mL/g for the sediment compartment.

The available incubations in dark aerobic natural water‐sediment systems did not allow to establish a proper route and rate of degradation of 1‐MCP in the aquatic compartments. With the available information, the experts during the Peer Review teleconference agreed that methallyl alcohol needs to be considered a metabolite requiring an assessment in all the aquatic compartments. Therefore, a data gap was identified for the applicant to address the formation of the metabolite methallyl alcohol in the surface water and sediment compartments 11 (see Section 10). It was noted that methallyl alcohol was found also in the hydrolysis study at significant amount in neutral and alkaline conditions, while as minor metabolite (i.e. < 10% AR) in the aerobic mineralisation in surface water study. Both the hydrolysis and the aerobic mineralisation studies were assessed using a weight of evidence approach. It was not possible to derive reliable kinetic parameters for the aquatic modelling exposure due to the uncertainties identified in the water/sediment systems. Therefore, experts agreed to use default parameters for the exposure assessment estimated in the aquatic compartments. The necessary surface water and sediment exposure assessments (Predicted environmental concentrations (PEC) calculations) were then provided by the RMS and carried out for 1‐MCP and for the metabolite methallyl alcohol, using the FOCUS (FOCUS, 2001) step 1 and step 2 approach (version 3.2 of the Steps 1–2 in FOCUS calculator) and the conservative input parameters agreed by the experts.

The necessary groundwater exposure assessments were carried out using FOCUS (European Commission, 2014) scenarios and the models PEARL 4.4.4, PELMO 5.5.3 and MACRO 5.5.4. 12 Due to the lack of reliable degradation parameters, for 1‐MCP experts agreed to apply the default value of DT50 14.1 days calculated at 20°C, based on the ready biodegradability study results and on the ECHA guidance on Biocidal Product Regulation (ECHA, 2017). With this endpoint the 80th percentile annual average concentrations leaving the top 1 m soil layer were estimated to be > 0.1 μg/L at two out of nine FOCUS groundwater scenarios leading to an assessment not finalised (see Section 9.1). For the metabolite methallyl alcohol, in the absence of reliable data and using the conservative default input parameters agreed by the experts, the 80th percentile annual average concentrations leaving the top 1 m soil layer were estimated to be > 0.1 μg/L in all the nine FOCUS scenarios. This confirms that further data is needed to refine the groundwater exposure assessment for the metabolite methallyl alcohol, and to identify the proposed uses as safe with respect to consumers exposure to this metabolite through drinking water. Although methallyl alcohol has a simple molecular structure with a chain length of 4 which consists only of C, H and O atoms (C4H8O), it cannot be considered a metabolite of no concern given his known reactive properties 13 for the presence of an ‘alerting structure’ like the allyl functional group (European Commission, 2003). With missing assessment on methallyl alcohol (see Section 2), a toxicological concern cannot be excluded a priori. This resulted on a data gap and an assessment not finalised (see Section 9.1).

The applicant provided information to address the effect of water treatments processes on the nature of the residues that might be present in surface water and groundwater, when surface water or groundwater are abstracted for drinking water. However, after the outcomes of the Pesticides Peer Review TC 115, this effect should be further investigated in light to the facts that both 1‐MCP and the metabolite methallyl alcohol were identified to have the potential to reach concentrations in surface water and groundwater used for the production of drinking water exceeding the 0.1 μg/L, and for the potential reactivity of methallyl alcohol especially after chlorination or ozonation treatments. Hence, a data gap was identified and results in the consumer risk assessment not being finalised (see Section 9.1).

According to the available information, the atmospheric half‐life of 1‐MCP is less than 2 hours and the predicted environmental concentration in air is expected to be minimal, shortly after the application.

The PEC in soil, surface water, sediment and groundwater covering the representative uses assessed can be found in Appendix B of this conclusion. A key to the wording used to describe the mobility of the active substance can be found in Appendix C of this conclusion.

ECOTOXICOLOGY

The risk assessment was based on the following documents: European Commission (2002), EFSA (2009, 2013) and EFSA PPR Panel (2013).

Bridging between the formulations ‘AFxRD038’ and ‘AF‐701’ was considered acceptable; therefore, the endpoints for the non‐target arthropods other than bees and soil microorganisms derived from studies with ‘AFxRD038’ were considered appropriate for risk assessment purposes.

Acute studies with birds and wild mammals were available with the representative formulation ‘AF701’ while long term studies with mammals were submitted with the active substance. Considering the nature of 1‐MCP (i.e. high volatility and rapid photodegradation in air) and to avoid animal testing, it was agreed to perform the long‐term risk assessment for birds using the acute endpoint divided by 10. Low acute and long‐term risk from oral and inhalation exposure to 1‐MCP was concluded for birds and mammals for the representative uses. Low risk was concluded from consumption of contaminated water for all representative uses while a risk assessment from secondary poisoning was not triggered.

Acute toxicity studies with 1‐MCP and/or ‘AF‐701’ were available covering the relevant aquatic taxa (i.e. fish, aquatic invertebrates, algae and macrophytes). Chronic studies with fish and aquatic invertebrates and studies with sediment‐dwelling organisms were not deemed required considering the volatilisation properties of 1‐MCP. Low risk was indicated at FOCUS Step 2 PECsw to all groups of aquatic organisms for all the representative uses. At the Pesticides Peer Review TC 115, the experts concluded that methallyl alcohol should be considered a major metabolite of 1‐MCP in surface water. 14 However, toxicity studies with aquatic organisms and methallyl alcohol were not available and a risk assessment was not presented (data gap, see Section 9.1).

Acute (oral and contact) studies with the representative formulation were available with honey bees. An acute inhalation study was also available with the active substance. Chronic studies with bee larvae and adults were not deemed necessary considering the nature of 1‐MCP (high volatility and rapid photodegradation in air). Low acute risk from oral and contact exposure could not be concluded when applying the SANCO risk assessment scheme (European Commission, 2002). However, the risk assessment according to the EFSA (2013) guidance document indicated low acute risk at tier 1 for all scenarios. A risk assessment from airborne exposure could not be conducted in the absence of an agreed scheme. A suitable assessment for accumulative and sub‐lethal effects was not available (data gap for sub‐lethal effects, see Section 10). No toxicity data on bumble bees and solitary bees were submitted.

For non‐target arthropods other than bees, tier 1 (glass plate) studies with the indicator species Aphidius rhopalosiphi and Typhlodromus pyri were available with AFxRD‐038 and AF‐701, respectively. In addition, high tier studies with Chrysoperla carnea and Poecilus cupreus with AFxRD‐038 were also submitted. Considering the studies and the risk assessment, low in‐ and off‐field risk was concluded for the representative uses with 1‐MCP.

Chronic toxicity studies were conducted with earthworms (Eisenia fetida), and soil meso‐ and macro‐fauna other than earthworms (the Collembola Folsomia candida and the predatory mite Hypoaspis aculeifer) for the formulation for representative uses ‘AF‐701’. A soil nitrogen study with ‘AF × RD‐038’ was available to assess the risk to soil microorganisms. Low chronic risk to soil meso‐ and macrofauna and to soil microorganisms was indicated for 1‐MCP for all representative uses. At the Pesticides Peer Review TC 115, the experts concluded that methallyl alcohol should be considered a major metabolite of 1‐MCP in soil. 15 However, toxicity studies with soil organisms and methallyl alcohol were not available and a risk assessment was not presented (data gap, see Section 9.1).

Seedling emergence and vegetative vigour studies with non‐target terrestrial plants (NTTPs) and the representative formulation were submitted. Based on the available data and risk assessment, low risk to NTTPs was indicated for the representative uses. Low risk to organisms involved in biological methods for sewage treatment was also concluded for all representative uses.

ENDOCRINE DISRUPTION PROPERTIES

With regard to the assessment of the endocrine disruption potential of 1‐MCP for humans according to the ECHA/EFSA guidance (2018), it is noted that an assessment in line with the 2018 ECHA/EFSA Guidance was not provided since the ECHA/EFSA Guidance was not in force at dossier submission. In the EFSA Conclusion (EFSA, 2018), 1‐MCP was considered unlikely to have endocrine disrupting properties. Further assessment of the endocrine disruption properties is not part of the scope of the amendment of approval conditions.

Critical areas of concern

An issue is listed as a critical area of concern if there is enough information available to perform an assessment for the representative uses in line with the uniform principles in accordance with Article 29(6) of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 and as set out in Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011, and if this assessment does not permit the conclusion that, for at least one of the representative uses, it may be expected that a plant protection product containing the active substance will not have any harmful effect on human or animal health or on groundwater, or any unacceptable influence on the environment.

An issue is also listed as a critical area of concern if the assessment at a higher tier level could not be finalised due to lack of information, and if the assessment performed at the lower tier level does not permit the conclusion that, for at least one of the representative uses, it may be expected that a plant protection product containing the active substance will not have any harmful effect on human or animal health or on groundwater, or any unacceptable influence on the environment.

An issue is also listed as a critical area of concern if, in the light of current scientific and technical knowledge using guidance documents available at the time of application, the active substance is not expected to meet the approval criteria provided for in Article 4 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009.

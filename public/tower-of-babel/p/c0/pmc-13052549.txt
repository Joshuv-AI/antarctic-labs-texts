Genotype-phenotype correlations in patients with pathogenic/likely pathogenic titin variants from the Swiss Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy Registry
Authors: Nina Alissa Schätti, Siv Fokstuen, Argelia Medeiros-Domingo, Umberto Maccio, Matthias P Nägele, Fran Mikulicic, Andreas J Flammer, Lorraine Sazgary, Robert Manka, Verena Charlotte Wilzeck, Felix Tanner, Christian Grebmer, Annina Salome Vischer, Katja E Odening, Marina Rieder, Frank Ruschitzka, Corinna Brunckhorst, Firat Duru, Ardan M Saguner, Nicole R Bonetti

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTruncating variants in the titin gene (TTNtv) are associated with cardiomyopathy, mainly dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM). The clinical presentation and outcomes of arrhythmogenic phenotypes in these patients are scarcely studied.Methods and resultsThis was a retrospective Swiss national study including 46 cardiomyopathy patients with pathogenic/likely pathogenic (P/LP) TTNtv. 63% were male and median age at diagnosis was 47 years. At baseline, 89% patients formally fulfilled current DCM criteria and 17% the 2024 revised Padua criteria for diagnosis of ACM, of which all were of the left-dominant phenotype (arrhythmogenic left ventricular cardiomyopathy). No patient fulfilled arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC) criteria. Most TTNtv were located on the A-band (74%). Median time to last follow-up (FU) was 63 months. Ventricular arrhythmia (VA) events in the primary prevention group significantly increased over time; although, under optimal guideline-directed medical therapy, mean left ventricular ejection fraction and right ventricular function significantly improved during time to last FU. At the time to last FU, 50% fulfilled DCM criteria, whereas 27% fulfilled the criteria for left-dominant ACM. Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) on cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) was most commonly in the basal septum, but no patient had higher degree atrioventricular block (AVB). LGE on CMR was not associated with higher rates of VAs.ConclusionsPatients with P/LP TTNtv most frequently present as DCM or left-dominant ACM, whereas TTN is not a classical ARVC-causing gene. The risk of VA remains high despite favourable remodelling. Left ventricular LGE is frequent and often involves the septum. In contrast to sarcoidosis, high degree AVB seems not to be a typical feature of TTN cardiomyopathy.Keywords: Tachycardia, Ventricular; Magnetic Resonance Imaging; Cardiomyopathies; GENETICS; Arrhythmias, Cardiac

Introduction

Titin is a crucial protein for skeletal and cardiac muscle function. It acts as a molecular bidirectional spring to provide passive force for the structural integrity of the sarcomere. The heart expresses two major isoforms that incorporate four distinct structural and functional regions (Z-line, I-band, A-band and the C-terminal M-line).1

Titin is the largest protein in humans and is encoded by a single gene (TTN) on the long arm of chromosome 2 consisting of 364 exons.1 Alterations in TTN biology at genetic, transcriptional and post-translational planes act in the pathogenesis of dilated cardiomyopathies (DCM), arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathies (ACM), hypertrophic cardiomyopathies, non-dilated left ventricular cardiomyopathies (NDLVC) and sudden cardiac death.2 TTN variants leading to early termination or truncations (TTNtv) are the largest-known cause of genetic forms of DCM, accounting for up to 25% of cases.3

DCM is the most frequent TTNtv-associated cardiomyopathy defined by dilatation of the left ventricle and reduced cardiac systolic function in the absence of hypertension, valvular disease or coronary artery disease. Up to 40% of DCM are etiologically categorised as familial or genetic.4 Most of TTNtv follow an autosomal dominant pattern and show age-related, but incomplete penetrance with increased frequency at older age.5 Pathogenic (P) TTNtv are mainly located in the A-band region of the molecule. In previous studies, patients with TTNtv showed an increased risk of sustained ventricular tachycardia (sVT), poorer cardiac indices and earlier heart failure and death compared with TTNtv-negative DCM patients.6 Individuals with TTNtv exposed to certain environmental factors causing cardiac stress (e.g. pregnancy, alcohol abuse, chemotherapy) are more at risk of developing DCM.7 8

ACM presents mainly as arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC), characterised by progressive myocardial fibrolipomatous replacement, primarily of the right ventricle (RV), resulting in ventricular dilatation, dysfunction and ventricular arrhythmias (VAs). Characteristically, VA precede heart failure.9 Several studies propose that TTNtv may cause ARVC and that this subgroup presents with increased supraventricular arrhythmias and conduction disease.4 10 However, TTN was recently abandoned as a typical ARVC-causing gene by the Clinical Genome (ClinGen) Resource framework.11 Yet, the presentation as left-dominant ACM in TTNtv carriers is scarcely studied.

Therefore, the aim of this study was to investigate the phenotypic presentation and outcome of patients with P TTNtv (class 5) and likely pathogenic (LP, class 4) TTNtv in a Swiss cohort. Special attention was given to the cardiomyopathy phenotype (DCM vs. ACM) at both disease presentation and the last follow-up (FU), plus the characterisation of VA events. Further, we studied associations between TTNtv location (A-band vs. non-A-band regions) and phenotypic characteristics.

Methods

Study population

This is a retrospective multicentre study with clinical and genetic data from four Swiss centres (University Hospital Zurich, Cantonal Hospital Lucerne, University Hospital Basel, University Hospital Bern). Patients harbouring a P/LP TTNtv with cardiac disease were included. The authors of each centre ensured the integrity of their centre’s data. The investigation conforms to the declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the local ethical committee (approval number: PB_2016–02109). All included patients have provided written informed consent.

Data collection and analysis

The study included retrospective data from January 2004 to December 2025. Variables were collected by experts in the field into a secured pseudonymised database and consisted of baseline patient characteristics, molecular genetic analysis, 12-lead surface ECG, Holter-ECG, transthoracic echocardiography, cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) with assessment of late gadolinium enhancement (LGE), electrophysiological study and implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) reports.

CMR imaging was performed across multiple centres using 1.5 and 3 Tesla scanners from Philips, Siemens and GE. Image datasets were analysed using dedicated software (IntelliSpace V.12.1; Philips Medical Systems, Best, the Netherlands) by two experienced readers (both EACVI Level III certified; VCW, 4 years of CMR experience; RM, 20 years), blinded to clinical data and reviewing jointly. Analysis included assessment of the presence and pattern of LGE according to the American Heart Association (AHA) 17-segment model.

VA were defined as non-sustained ventricular tachycardia (nsVT, lasting <30 s), sustained ventricular tachycardia (sVT, lasting ≥30 s or causing haemodynamic compromise or appropriate ICD therapy) and sudden cardiac arrest (SCA)/ventricular fibrillation (VF). Sustained VA (sVA) was defined as either sVT or SCA/VF.

VA events were either registered via ECG, Holter-ECG or ICD-readouts.

Inferior T-wave inversion (TWI) was defined as T-inversion in at least two leads among II, III or aVF, whereas precordial TWI was defined in at least two leads among V2–V6.

Cardiomyopathy criteria fulfilment was evaluated according to the 2023 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Guidelines for DCM and the 2010 International ARVC Task Force Criteria, as well as the 2024 revised Padua criteria for ACM with further dividing in subgroups of left-dominant (arrhythmogenic left ventricular cardiomyopathy (ALVC)), right-dominant (ARVC) ACM or biventricular (arrhythmogenic biventricular cardiomyopathy (ABVC)) ACM.12 Assessment for NDLVC was performed according to the 2023 ESC Guidelines for the management of cardiomyopathies. If patients formally fulfilled several criteria, all applicable were noted.

DNA sequence analysis

Molecular genetic analyses were performed by qualified medical professionals (specialist in medical genetics) in four different laboratories using next generation sequencing to analyse gene panels including TTN to screen for variants associated with ACM and DCM, which were confirmed by Sanger sequencing (for centre protocol, see online supplemental table 1).

TTNtv analysis

TTNtv were compared using ClinVar and Genome Aggregation database and classified as P/LP according to the latest American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) criteria.13 Only variants with a PSI (percentage spliced in) score of 90 or higher were included, indicating a high expression of the given exon in the cardiac tissue.6 For the evaluation, TTNtv were divided into two subgroups: A-band variants and non-A-band variants (defined as variants on the Z-disk or I-band) according to the Cardiovascular Genetics and Genomics Group. The location of TTNtv was correlated with the clinical phenotype and outcome.

Outcome

Longitudinal FU data were collected for ECG and echocardiographic variables, symptoms, VA events and cardiomyopathy criteria. If patients did not fulfil specific cardiomyopathy criteria at the time of last FU (e.g. due to favourable left ventricular remodelling), the criteria status was changed accordingly. Information about ICD implantation, VT ablation, left ventricular assist device (LVAD) implantation, heart transplantation and death was also collected.

Patients who did not present with sVA at baseline defined the primary prevention group. Outcome variables concerning sVA events were analysed only in this subgroup, since previous sVA events are associated with a higher risk of re-occurrence in the secondary prevention population. VA events at baseline were defined as events from birth until study inclusion.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as number and percentage, whereas continuous variables were shown as mean±SD or median with IQRs, as appropriate. A test for normal distribution was performed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Differences in phenotype at baseline according to location of the variant or gender were studied using χ2 test for categorical variables, unpaired t-test for normally distributed continuous variables and Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed continuous variables. Phenotypic changes at longitudinal FU were analysed with paired t-tests for normally distributed continuous variables or Wilcoxon tests for non-normally distributed continuous and categorical variables. Longitudinal outcomes were evaluated using the log-rank test or Cox proportional hazards model. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS statistics software (V.29.0). No adjustments were used given the exploratory nature of the study.

Molecular genetic analysis and newly described variants

17 patients (37%) harboured a heterozygous P TTNtv and 29 (63%) patients a heterozygous LP variant. The most frequent variant location was on the A-band (n=34, 73.9%); 12 were located on non-A-band regions (n=10 (21.7%) I-band, n=2 (4.3%) Z-disk) (for overview of the patients’ TTNtv see online supplemental table 4).

In this cohort, 16 novel variants could be identified (online supplemental table 4). Out of the 16 variants, 12 (75%) lay on the A-band regions and four (25%) on non-A-band regions (n=3 (18.8%) I-band, n=1 (6.3%) Z-disk). Four (25%) were classified as P, whereas 12 (75%) were classified as LP variants.

Arrhythmic events

At baseline, 12 (26.1%) patients had a prior VA event. Nine (19.6%) had nsVT, one (2.2%) had sVT and four (8.7%) survived SCA (n=1 PEA, n=2 VF, n=1 unclear rhythm). The five patients with sVT/SCA at baseline represented the secondary prevention group and were excluded from the arrhythmic outcome analysis.

Fulfilment of cardiomyopathy criteria

At baseline, DCM criteria were fulfilled in 41 (89.1%) patients. Eight patients (17.4%) fulfilled ACM criteria, of which all presented as ALVC and none had ABVC or ARVC. Seven patients (15.2%) formally fulfilled both DCM and ACM criteria. Four (8.7%) individuals did not fulfil ACM or DCM criteria. No patients fulfilled the criteria for NDLVC.

Disease course and outcomes

Outcome data are depicted in table 2 and online supplemental table 3. FU was available in 44 (95.7%) patients and median time to last FU was 63 (17.5–101.75) months. One genotype positive family member did not undergo FU visits due to absence of symptoms and normal cardiac function. One patient died shortly after study inclusion. During the time to last FU, 18 (40.9%) patients received an ICD in addition to two who already had an ICD at baseline. 14 (77.8%) ICDs implanted during the time to last FU had a primary prophylactic and four (22.2%) a secondary prophylactic indication. Eight (44.4%) were single chamber, two (11.1%) were dual chamber, three (16.7%) were CRT and five (27.8%) were subcutaneous-ICD. Five patients (10.9%) received advanced heart failure treatment, with three (6.5%) individuals needing LVAD and two (4.3%) heart transplantation due to end-stage heart failure. One (2.2%) patient died due to VF shortly after study inclusion. No deaths due to heart failure or non-cardiac deaths occurred until time to last FU.

Arrhythmic outcomes

VA events (table 2) in the primary prevention group (n=41) significantly increased over time, with eight (19.5%) events at baseline (of which all were nsVTs) and 26 (65%) at last FU (Wilcoxon test p=0.012). Most VA events were nsVT, which significantly increased over time (n=8 (19.5%) to n=19 (47.5%), Wilcoxon test p=0.012). Four (10%) sVT and three (7.5%) SCA occurred between study inclusion and last FU.

Gender, ethnicity, location of the variant, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) below 35% and LGE on CMR could not be identified as predictors for sVA in the primary prevention population in the Cox proportional hazards model (online supplemental table 6).

Association with variant location

The rates of ICD implantation, LVAD or heart transplantation were similar between A-band and non-A-band carriers. The location of the variant in the primary prevention group was not associated with different arrhythmic outcomes.

Individuals with TTNtv on A-band versus non-A-band regions showed no differences in age, gender, risk factors, medication intake, ECG and echocardiographic variables at baseline and time to last FU.

Association with gender

No differences in VA events between genders could be observed. There were no differences regarding age at presentation, TTNtv genetic status, symptoms, fulfilment of cardiomyopathy criteria, ECG, echocardiographic and CMR features between genders.

Diagnostic characteristics

ECG characteristics

12-lead ECG was abnormal in 41 patients (89.1%) at baseline and 42 (95.5%) at last FU. TWI in inferior/precordial leads was present in 22 patients (47.8%) at baseline and decreased to 13 (29.5%) patients (Wilcoxon test p=0.039) at last FU. Peripheral low voltage was present in 15 (32.6%) patients at baseline and 23 (52.3%) at last FU. Atrial fibrillation (AF) or flutter was documented in 11 (23.9%) patients at baseline and 11 (25.0%) patients at last FU. First degree atrioventricular block (AVB) was present in three (6.5%) individuals at baseline and eight (18.2%) at last FU. None presented with second or third degree AVB. For details see online supplemental table 5.

Echocardiographic characteristics

Median LVEF and right ventricular fractional area change (RVFAC) increased significantly over time (LVEF: median 27% (18–44%) to 46% (36–50%), paired t-test p<0.001, RVFAC: median 23% (20–32%) to 36% (34–40%), paired t-test p<0.001). Median left ventricular end-systolic dimension (LVESD) and left ventricular mass index (LVMI) significantly decreased (LVESD: median 52 mm (48–61 mm) to 45 mm (38–50.5 mm), Wilcoxon test p<0.001, LVMI: median 106 g/m2 (90–131 g/m2) to 82 g/m2 (61–97.5 g/m2), Wilcoxon test p<0.001). For details, see figure 1 and online supplemental table 7.

Gender, ethnicity, type of variant and the absence of LGE were not associated with LVEF improvement from below to above 35% in the Cox proportional hazards model (online supplemental table 8).

Pattern of LGE

Assessment of LGE on CMR was available in 35 (76%) of 46 patients, of whom 29 (83%) demonstrated positive LGE. In most patients, LGE was located in the midmyocardial layer (n=25, 71%). LGE was frequently observed at the RV insertion points, with involvement of the basal RV insertion site in 23 patients (66%) and the anterior RV insertion site in 13 patients (37%). The most common regional distribution according to the AHA 17-segment model involved the basal anteroseptal and basal inferoseptal segments (each in 20 patients, 57%), reflecting LGE extending beyond the RV insertion points (figure 2).

Positive LGE in the primary prevention population was not associated with higher rates of VA events (log-rank test p=0.385). In Cox Regression analysis, absence of LGE could not be identified as a predictor for improvement of LVEF from baseline to last FU (below to above 35%, p=0.151) (online supplemental table 8).

Fulfilment of cardiomyopathy criteria

At disease presentation, four (8.7%) patients did not fulfil DCM or ACM criteria. At the time to last FU, the number significantly increased to 17 (38.6%) individuals (Wilcoxon test p<0.001). 41 (89.1%) patients fulfilled DCM criteria at disease presentation, with a decrease to 22 (50%) at time to last FU (Wilcoxon test p<0.001). ACM criteria fulfilment increased from eight (17.4%) patients to 12 (27.3%) at time to last FU. All patients included presented with the ALVC phenotype according to the 2024 revised Padua criteria for ACM at baseline and time to last FU. None of the patients fulfilled criteria for ARVC, at neither baseline nor last FU. At baseline and time to last FU, seven patients formally fulfilled both ACM and DCM criteria (15.2% and 18.2%, respectively) (figure 3 and online supplemental table 9).

Discussion

In this genotype-phenotype national study, we characterised the phenotypic presentation and outcome of patients harbouring P/LP TTNtv. A special focus lay on the cardiomyopathy phenotype and the characterisation of VA events.

Detection of novel TTNtv

P/LP TTNtv were distributed over the whole titin molecule. Most TTNtv were located on the inextensible A-band region, compatible with findings from previous studies.6 Most of the detected variants were classified as LP according to ACMG criteria. 16 novel variants were discovered.

Phenotypic presentation and fulfilment of cardiomyopathy criteria

A clear trend to evolve from DCM to ACM under GDMT was observed in this cohort. All patients presented with the ALVC phenotype according to the 2024 revised Padua criteria. Importantly, none fulfilled the 2010 International ARVC Task Force Criteria and the 2024 revised Padua criteria for ARVC, confirming the classification of TTN as a non-ARVC-causing gene in the ClinGen Resource framework.11 These findings underline that TTN cardiomyopathy responds well to GDMT (as observed in DCM),14 similar to previous studies.8 Yet, TTNtv can also primarily present as left-dominant ACM. This reinforces the idea of an intermediate phenotype of DCM and ACM, as similarly described by Peters et al, where they observed a common grey zone in diagnostic criteria between DCM and ACM in patients harbouring DCM causing genes.15

Corden et al found that individuals with DCM and TTNtv are at greater risk for significant arrhythmia compared with TTNtv negative patients.16 In our study population, almost 50% of patients in the primary prevention group suffered from VA events during time to last FU, which occurred independent of LVEF, unlike observed in previous studies.17 18 The arrhythmias in our primary prevention group significantly increased until time to last FU and were predominantly nsVT events. However, four (10%) sVT and three (7.5%) SCA events occurred until last FU. Although the power of data is limited due to the small sample size, the findings indicate that sVA is frequent in patients with TTN cardiomyopathy, even under optimal medical therapy and that TTNtv may be life-threatening, independently of LVEF. This may be important for risk assessment and primary prevention ICD implantation in this cohort.

ECG and imaging characteristics

Presence of peripheral low voltage on ECG was common. TWI was also often observed, mainly in precordial leads. Interestingly, they were partially reversible, which fits with the favourable structural remodelling of TTN cardiomyopathy under optimal GDMT. In addition, AF/flutter was common in this population. Higher degree AVB was not observed despite septal fibrosis and does not seem to be a typical ECG feature of titin cardiomyopathy,19 which is in line with a recent publication from Poland.20

This observation is important given the fact that cardiac sarcoidosis can also present with septal fibrosis in a similar location and mimic DCM/ACM but is often associated with higher degrees of AVB.21 Potential explanations why higher degrees of AVB are absent in TTN cardiomyopathy despite septal involvement are the midmyocardial LGE sparing the subendocardial conduction system as opposed to cardiac sarcoidosis, which often leads to a transmural granulomatous inflammation with thinning of the whole septum.22

Concerning this point, the conduction system seems to be less prone to the disease process in TTN cardiomyopathy, which we were able to show in the macroscopic and histological specimen of the heart from a patient with a LP deletion-insertion TTNtv, where a mild diffuse fibrosis sparing the conduction system could be observed. This is in line with a previous publication reporting that LBBB is less frequent in individuals with DCM with TTNtv compared with those with DCM without TTNtv16. Since TTN is a sarcomeric protein abundant in working myocardium, but probably scarce or even absent (Gene Set - cardiac purkinje fiber (maayanlab.cloud)) in cardiac Purkinje cells, TTN may not have a relevant impact on the conduction system.23 Moreover, even truncating TTNtv are structurally integrated into the sarcomere of the working myocardium causing loss of contractility, but not necessarily cell death leading to replacement fibrosis impairing the conduction system.24

Under GDMT, cardiac function in echocardiography improved significantly in our cohort. These findings align with several studies showing improvement of cardiac function in patients with cardiomyopathy under GDMT.14 25 As described by Augusto et al where distinct CMR features in DCM-associated genes could be identified, in our cohort, LGE was present in 83% patients with a noteworthy frequent location in the basal inferoseptal and anteroseptal regions. It seems that compared with sarcoidosis, laminopathies, desmoplakin cardiomyopathy and filaminopathies, which often present with transmural or ring-like fibrosis,26 TTN cardiomyopathies are characterised by less pronounced septal midmyocardial LGE (figure 2). In contrast to previous studies,27 28 in this subgroup, the presence of LGE on CMR did not confer an increased risk for sVA events.29 30 Yet, previous studies investigating the association between LGE and sVA in DCM included heterogeneous populations and not only TTN cardiomyopathy.

Phenotypic presentation and outcomes according to variant location, pathogenicity and gender

No differences in arrhythmogenic outcomes according to the location of the TTNtv were identified. Even though previous studies have suggested that A-band TTNtv are associated with a higher risk of VAs, our data suggest that not only TTN cardiomyopathy without LGE and with an LVEF >35%, but also non-A-band variant carriers harbour a substantial risk for adverse arrhythmic outcomes during FU. No differences in outcomes according to gender could be observed in this cohort. These findings vary from results of previous studies, where male gender was identified as a risk factor for malignant arrhythmias in TTN cardiomyopathy31 and ARVC.32 Even though the outcome of individuals with TTNtv seems to be generally favourable,10 18 individuals required ICD implantation, three needed LVAD and two required heart transplantation. With over 10% of patients receiving cardiac replacement therapy, TTN cardiomyopathy can potentially be a debilitating disease causing end-stage heart failure.

Limitations

Despite a nation-wide effort to identify as many patients with TTN cardiomyopathy as possible, we were able to include 46 individuals. Hence, the relatively small population size limits the statistical validity and our analysis on predictors of sVAs may be underpowered. Observer bias might have occurred in this retrospective study, where data were collected from different highly specialised centres. Concerning familial cases, an ascertainment bias is present as more affected patients are more likely to seek medical attention. Unfortunately, histopathological examination of transplanted hearts is not routinely performed and therefore these data were scarce in this population.

Conclusions

Patients with P/LP TTNtv most frequently present as DCM or left-dominant ACM, whereas TTN is not a classical ARVC-causing gene. The risk of VA remains high despite favourable remodelling. LV-LGE is frequent and often involves the septum. In contrast to sarcoidosis, high degree AVB seems not to be a typical feature of TTN cardiomyopathy.

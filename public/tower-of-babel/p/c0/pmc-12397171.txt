Kurarinone protects against renal injury and fibrosis after unilateral ureteral obstruction through enhancement of the Nrf-2 signaling pathway
Authors: Tiancao Dong, Dongyang Zhao, Sen Jiang, Yi Gu, Chunxue Wang, Qian Yang, Tong Liu, Xiandong Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChronic kidney disease (CKD) is an epidemic with higher cardiovascular risk, and this pathology involves renal injury and fibrosis. Kurarinone (KAR) has anti-inflammatory and anticancer effects, which can regulate immune responses and macrophage polarization. This study aimed to investigate the effect of KAR on model-induced unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) renal injury and fibrosis.MethodsWe constructed the rat model of sham, UUO, UUO + low (10 IU/kg), medium (20 IU/kg) and high (40 IU/kg) doses of KAR groups in vivo, and we analyzed pathologic changes and expression of vimentin and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), cluster of differentiation group 206, F4/80, FN, Snail1, Kim1, small mother against decapentaplegic (Smad) 3 and transforming growth factor-beta [TGF-β1]. Furthermore, we developed the HK-2 cell fibrosis model using TGF-β1 (5 ng/ml) for 24 h in vitro. We used real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction, Western blotting, and immunohistochemistry to detect α-SMA protein, TGF-β1, Smad3, and p-Smad3 levels in renal tissues. We examine protein levels of the Keap1/Nrf2 pathway using the Nrf-2 inhibitor, ML385, and downregulation of Nrf-2 expression using Western blotting.ResultsOur results revealed that KAR improved renal function and downregulated α-SMA protein. Furthermore, KAR induced the polarization of M2 macrophages. KAR improved fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β1/Smad3 pathway. KAR inhibited renal fibrosis and inflammation by activating the Keap1/Nrf2 pathway in vivo and in vitro.ConclusionsKAR inhibited renal fibrosis and inflammation through the Keap1/Nrf-2 pathway, indicating that it may become a small molecular drug for chronic kidney disease.

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) involves renal dysfunction or structural damage, characterized by excessive extracellular matrix deposition, renal tubule alterations, and fibroblast activation (Francis et al. 2024). CKD, as one of the leading public health issues, ranks third as the leading cause of premature death (Kohan and Barton 2014; Chen et al. 2023). Approximately 160 million people were affected by CKD in 2020 (Ruiz-Ortega et al. 2022). The specific cause of CKD remains unclear, but its onset is linked to age, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes (Tetzner et al. 2008). Pathological mechanisms involving chronic fibrosis, inflammation, and macrophages critically drive CKD development and progression (Tetzner et al. 2008; Jankowski et al. 2021). nflammation in CKD disrupts the M1/M2 macrophage balance, driving a shift from anti-inflammatory M2 to pro-inflammatory M1 phenotypes within the renal microenvironment (Huen and Cantley 2017; Zhu et al. 2019).

Significantly, this cellular reprogramming coincides with dysregulated oxidative stress pathways - a process regulated by the transcription factor Nrf-2, which orchestrates cellular defenses against ROS overload (Stenvinkel et al. 2020). Keap1 serves as a negative regulatory factor for Nrf-2, which consequently promotes the degradation of Nrf-2 degradation through proteasome (Chowdhry et al. 2013). Oxidative stress disrupts Keap1-Nrf2 binding, reducing Nrf2 degradation and promoting its nuclear translocation to activate target genes (Yamawaki et al. 2018; Nezu et al. 2017). Nrf-2 activation alleviates renal fibrosis in CKD development (Ma 2013). In UUO mice, Keap1 downregulation elevates nuclear Nrf2, activating its target genes and suppressing inflammation and fibrosis (Rinaldi Tosi et al. 2011). Renal tubular epithelial cells and myofibroblasts synthesize TGF-β1 during renal fibrosis. As the TGF-β1/Smad3 pathway is pivotal in liver and lung fibrosis, its activation exacerbates renal fibrosis (Noone and Licht 2013). Currently, an effective TGF-β inhibitor, pirfenidone, is approved for the treatment of pulmonary and renal fibrosis (Hu et al. 2018). TGF-β1 expression partially alleviates UUO-induced inflammation and renal fibrosis (Yang et al. 2020). The mitigation of damage caused by UUO is closely related to TGF-β1 reduction by Nrf-2 (Ram et al. 2022). Therefore, we speculate that activation of Nrf-2, thus inhibiting TGF-β, can, to some extent, prevent or reduce CKD.

Natural products, valued for their complex structures and biological activities, are key drug sources. Kurarinone (KAR), a major component of the anti-inflammatory TCM Sophora flavescens, exemplifies this potential. (He et al. 2015). A recent study revealed that KAR effectively decreases neuroinflammation and glial cell-induced neurotoxicity by regulating microglial cell polarization (Jia et al. 2022). KAR also alleviates arthritis by weakening oxidative stress and inflammation (Tang et al. 2021). A recent study found that KAR can alleviate idiopathic fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β pathway (Park et al. 2021). However, it remains unknown whether KAR can alleviate renal fibrosis and inflammation caused by chronic kidney damage.

KAR protects against UUO-induced renal injury/fibrosis by targeting the Keap1/Nrf2 pathway: suppressing Keap1/HIF-1α while activating Nrf2/HO-1. This dual antifibrotic/anti-inflammatory action highlights its potential as an Nrf2-activating CKD therapeutic.

Materials and methods

Animal model

Fifty rats were purchased from Shulaibao Biotech (Wuhan, China) and kept at constant temperature (23–25 ° C) with controlled light/dark cycles (12 h/12 h) and bred and housed in the Affiliated Shanghai East Hospital, Tongji University School of Medicine. We monitored the health and behavior of rats every day, including weight, food intake, water intake, fur and skin condition, as well as the occurrence of any abnormal activities. Injection of sodium pentobarbital (40 mg/kg) intraperitoneally was used to anesthetize rats prior to the experiment. No rats died during feeding and experiment. All rats were sacrificed by exposure to carbon dioxide at a concentration of 60% of the container volume per minute after the experiment. We established the UUO model by blocking the unilateral ureter. Sprague–Dawley (SD) male rats, weighing 180 to 200 g, were randomly classified into the sham, UUO and UUO + KAR-10, -20 and − 40 IU/kg groups (n = 10). Following UUO surgery, the renal obstruction modeling was maintained for 14 days post-operation, consistent with established protocols for observing renal fibrosis progression in rodent models. After disinfection, we incised the left abdomen, exposed the kidneys, separated the ureter, interrupted the suture by a 4 − 0 operation at the end of the kidney, cut from the middle of the two ligations and reset the organs, sutured the incision, and disinfected the skin. The rats in the sham group were treated with UUO except for ligation and cutting of the ureteral. The UUO rats were treated with low, medium and high doses of KAR. Throughout the 14-day modeling period, all animals were monitored daily for surgical integrity (e.g., wound healing, ligation stability) and physiological stability (body weight, food intake, activity level). The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Shanghai East Hospital, Tongji University School of Medicine.

Hematoxylin and eosin (HE) staining analysis

We fixed the left kidney in 4% formaldehyde for several days and embedded it with paraffin. The samples were dewaxed in xylene and dehydrated with ethanol and HE staining was performed to analyze renal dysfunction. Images were taken at 400× magnification using a microscope.

Masson and prostate-specific antigen staining analysis

The renal tissues were stained with prostate specific antigen (PAS) for the detection of glycogen and Masson was used to analyze the degree of fibrosis. Images were taken at 400× magnification using a microscope.

Serological analysis

Serum levels of urea nitrogen (BUN) and serum creatinine (SCr) were measured using kits purchased from Beyotime Biotechnology (Shanghai, China).

Cell culture

We purchased HK-2 cells (RRID: CSTR:19375.09.3101HUMGNHu47) from the Cell Bank of the Shanghai Life Science Institution. HK-2 cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and treated with 10% fetal bovine serum and 100 U/ml penicillin–streptomycin and incubated in a cell incubator at 37 ° C with 5% CO2. Then the effect of KAR was studied in the HK-2 cell by being exposed for 24 h to normoxia (21% O2) or hypoxia (O2 < 0.1%). Cells were stimulated with or without KAR-50 µM for 24 h and treated with 5 µM ML385 for 24 h. Cells were collected for subsequent analysis. Transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) is a type of multifunctional cell factor that can regulate cell growth, differentiation, remodeling of the ECM, endothelial mesenchymal transition, and induce cell fibrosis. Therefore, we established a cellular fibrosis model by treating HK-2 cells with TGF-β. The provided sequence represents small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules, including Nrf2-siRNA and si-NC. These sequences are, respectively, composed of the following nucleotides: 5′-UAAUUGUCAACUACUGUCAGUU-3′ and 5′-GACGAAAGCAGTTCGTCTA-3′.

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

The total RNA of the samples was extracted using a Trizol reagent. The reverse transcription of RNA into cDNA was performed using the Bio-Rad iScript cDNA synthesis kit. Differential mRNA expression was determined using a CFX ConnectTM real-time system (BIO-RAD, United States), following the instructions provided by the manufacturer (Applied Biosystems). RT-qPCR results were analyzed using the 2 − ΔΔCt method, with GAPDH used as the standardized control. The primers used were as follows. TGF-β1: forward 5’-GGCCAGATCCTGTCCAAGC-3’ reverse 5’-GTGGGTTTCCACCATTAGCAC-3’, FN: forward 5’- CGGTGGCTGTCAGTCAAAG-3’ reverse 5’- AAACCTCGGCTTCCTCCATAA-3’, SMAD3: forward 5’-TGGACGCAGGTTCTCCAAAC-3’ reverse 5’- CCGGCTCGCAGTAGGTAAC-3’, SNAI1: forward 5’- TCGGAAGCCTAACTACAGCGA-3’ reverse 5’- AGATGAGCATTGGCAGCGAG-3’, Kim1: forward 5’-TGGCAGATTCTGTAGCTGGTT-3’ reverse 5’- AGAGAACATGAGCCTCTATTCCA-3’, GAPDH.

Western blot

RIPA lysis buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China) was utilized to extract total protein. The BCA protein concentration determination kit (Beyotime, Wuhan, China) was used to determine the protein concentration. The proteins in different samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to the polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membrane. The membrane was sealed with 5% BSA at room temperature (RT, 25 ° C) for 1 h. It was then incubated overnight at 4 ° C with different primary antibodies (1:500 ~ 1,000). Subsequently, once the unbound primary antibodies were removed from the membrane, the corresponding HRP-conjugated antibodys (1:5,000 ~ 8,000) were incubated at RT for 1 h. The film was eventually processed with the ECL detection kit (Beyotime, Wuhan, China) for analysis of the grayscale values of the bands was done using the image processing software Image J. The following primary antibodies were used: GAPDH (Santa Cruz, CA), fibronectin (FN) (1:1000, Proteintech, China), α-SMA (1:1000, Proteintech, China), Snail1 (1:1000, Proteintech, China), Kim1 (1:1000, Proteintech, China), small mother against decapentaplegic (Smad)3 (1:1000, Proteintech, China), p-Smad3 (1:1000, Abcam, USA), TGF-β1 (1:500, Bioworlde, China), Nfr-2 (1:1000, Abcam, USA), (1:1000, CST, China), HO-1 (1:1000, CST, China), Hif-1α (1:1000, Proteintech, China) and Keap1 (1:1000, CST, China).

Immunofluorescence (IF) staining analysis

After fixation with 4% paraformaldehyde, samples were permeabilized with Triton-X 100 (0.1%) and blocked with an immunostaining blocking solution (3% H2O2). Cells after treatment with 5% BSA for 30 min were then incubated at 4 ° C with α-SMA (1:500, Proteintech, China), differentiation cluster (CD) 206 (1:500, Proteintech, China), F4/80 (1:200, CST, China), TGF-β1 (1:100, Bioworlde, China) and Nrf-2 (1:200, Abcam, USA) for 12 h. The corresponding secondary antibodies were incubated at 37 ° C for 1 h. We then used 4’,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole to stain the nucleus. We obtained IF images using a laser scanning microscope.

RNA interference

We seeded HK-2 cells in 6-well plates and small interference RNA (siRNA) targeting the Nrf-2 gene (siNrf-2), which were transfected with the siNrf-2 plasmid using RNA transfection reagent. The specific operation is as follows: inoculate cells one day before transfection, and the cell density for inoculation is around 30%-50%. Subsequently, for each cell well, dilute 8.4 ng of siRNA (0.6 pmoles) in 100 µL serum-free medium (OPTI-MEM medium) and mix well. Immediately add 2 µL transfection reagent (Lipofectamine 2000) to 100 µL siRNA, vortex for 10 s to thoroughly mix. Incubate at room temperature for 10 min to form siRNA-PEI cationic nucleic acid transfection complexes. During the formation of the complexes, remove the cell growth medium and add 500 µL of fresh preheated complete medium to each well. Directly add 100 µL of siRNA-PEI cationic nucleic acid transfection complexes to the cells, gently shake the culture plate to mix. The final volume is 600 µL, with a siRNA concentration of 1 nM. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO2 in a cell culture incubator for 48 h, and then detect the expression level of the target protein.

Statistical analysis

We use the Statistical Package for Social Sciences software version 26.0 to analyze the results. Multiple comparisons were performed using a one-way analysis of variance. Comparison between the two groups was made using Student’s t-test. Data in all figures with histograms were represented using the mean ± standard deviation. P-values < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

KAR enhanced renal function in the UUO model

This study utilized the UUO rat model to investigate the effect of KAR on renal injury and fibrosis induced by the model. The results of HE and PAS staining indicated a decreased degree of injury in the KAR-treated groups (Fig. 1A). The results of the masson staining revealed improved UUO-induced renal fibrosis in the KAR groups (Fig. 1A). Furthermore, compared to the UUO group, the area of fibrosis decreased in the high KAR dose group (Fig. 1B). BUN and SCr are common indicators to assess renal dysfunction. After treatment with KAR, BUN and SCr levels were reduced (Fig. 1C and D), with significantly lower kidney tubular injury scores compared to the UUO group (Fig. 1E).

KAR inhibited fibrosis and induced polarization of M2 macrophages in vivo

To explore whether KAR induces polarization of M2 macrophages, we analyzed the α-SMA in renal tissues from the UUO and KAR treated groups by IF for a more intuitive observation of fibrosis. KAR also effectively reduced α-SMA levels (Fig. 2A). Furthermore, with KAR treatment, CD206 expression increased (Fig. 2B), while F4/80 expression decreased with increased KAR dose (Fig. 2C).

KAR improved renal fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling pathway

To explore the potential mechanism by which KAR improves renal fibrosis, we proceeded with the following experiments. We revealed that KAR inhibited the levels of mRNA and proteins of FN, α-SMA, Snail1, and Kim1 in the tissues of the UUO group by RT-qPCR (Fig. 3A) and Western blot (Fig. 3B and D). TGF-β1 and its downstream signaling, Smad, mediates tissue fibrosis. We detected protein expressions of Smad3, p-Smad3 and TGF-β1 to investigate whether KAR plays an important role in the TGF-β1/Smad pathway. The Western blot result revealed decreased TGF-β1 in the KAR treated groups (Fig. 3C and E), indicating that KAR ameliorated fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling pathway.

KAR inhibited hypoxia-stimulated HK-2 cell fibrosis in vitro

We used TGF-β1 to induce HK-2 cells and develop the fibrosis cell model to study whether KAR inhibited cell fibrosis in vitro. Compared to the control group, the hypoxia-induced group had a higher expression of α-SMA, TGF-β1, and p-Smad3 mRNA (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, the α-SMA, TGF-β1, and p-Smad3 were decreased in the KAR-treated group (Fig. 4A), indicating that KAR suppressed renal fibrosis through Smad3. This result was also supported at protein levels by Western blot and IF (Fig. 4B and C).

KAR activated the Keap1/Nrf2 pathway in vivo and in vitro

We then wondered how KAR plays a role in inhibiting renal fibrosis, and then the proteins related to the Nrf-2 signaling pathway were detected in vivo. We found that Keap1 and Hif-1α was decreased in the UUO + KAR groups, while Nrf-2 and HO-1 levels increased compared to the UUO group (Fig. 5A, C). In vitro, hypoxia-induced HK-2 cells with KAR treatment demonstrated lower levels of Keap1 and Hif-1α and higher Nrf-2 and HO-1 levels than anxoia-induced HK-2 cells without KAR-treated group (Fig. 5B, D). In vitro experiments demonstrated that KAR treatment significantly increased nuclear Nrf-2 protein levels. Within the UUO model, both UUO + KAR-10 and UUO + KAR-20 groups exhibited a dose-dependent increase in nuclear Nrf-2 protein. However, the UUO + KAR-40 group showed a paradoxical decrease in nuclear Nrf-2 protein compared to the UUO + KAR-20 group. In the Anoxia model, the Anoxia + KAR-20 group also displayed a significant increase in nuclear Nrf-2 protein relative to the control group. (Fig. 5E, F).

KAR inhibited renal fibrosis and oxidative stress through the Keap1/Nrf2 pathway in vitro

We used the Nrf-2 inhibitor, ML385, to treat HK-2 cells to verify the crucial role of Nrf-2 in KAR-induced renal fibrosis inhibition. After treatment with ML385, the protein expressions of Keap1, Hif-1, and TGF-β1 were increased, and Nrf-2 and HO-1 levels decreased (Fig. 6A). The results revealed that suppression of Nrf-2 expression significantly alleviated renal fibrosis and oxidative stress. We then construct Nrf-2 down-regulated HK-2 cells by RNA interference (Fig. 6B). Expectedly, protein expression was consistent with the above results (Fig. 6C).

Discussion

Our study revealed that KAR exerts protective effects against renal injury and fibrosis by activating the Nrf-2 signaling pathway. In both the UUO model and TGF-β1-induced renal fibrosis, KAR treatment markedly reduced renal dysfunction and suppressed fibrosis-related protein expression, including FN, α-SMA, Snail1, and Kim1. Furthermore, KAR attenuated renal fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling pathway. Mechanistically, KAR downregulates Keap1 expression, thereby disrupting Nrf-2 ubiquitination and degradation. This stabilization of Nrf-2 enhances its transcriptional activity, promoting the expression of cytoprotective genes that counteract renal fibrosis and oxidative stress. It is well-established that macrophages exhibit two polarization states: classically activated M1 (pro-inflammatory) and alternatively activated M2 (pro-repair). During tissue injury, recruited macrophages initially polarize into M1 macrophages, releasing pro-inflammatory mediators such as IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-8, IL-12, IFN-γ, and IL-18 to eliminate pathogens and cellular debris. Subsequently, they transition to M2 macrophages, which secrete anti-inflammatory factors to resolve inflammation and facilitate tissue repair. Given this dynamic, M2 macrophage polarization has garnered substantial interest for its potential to enhance anti-inflammatory responses and promote tissue regeneration.

CD206, a co-stimulatory molecule upregulated following M2 macrophage polarization, serves as a highly specific marker for M2 macrophages. F4/80, predominantly expressed on macrophage surfaces, acts as a marker for mature tissue-resident macrophages. Previous studies have demonstrated that the unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) model in mice effectively recapitulates chronic kidney inflammation and fibrosis processes(Zhao et al. 2022). The expression of fibroblast markers (vimentin and α-smooth muscle [α-SMA] actin) and inflammatory cytokines (tumor necrosis factor-α, interleukin (Tomson et al. 2021)-1β, etc.) increased drastically in the UUO model (Sime and O'Reilly 2001; Nathan et al. 2010). This type of disease is rarely diagnosed during its initial stages. Unfortunately, complete recovery is challenging once it is identified as CKD. Therefore, understanding the occurrence of renal fibrosis and inflammation associated with CKD is crucial for the development and treatment of CKD.

Increasing evidence indicated that KAR had anti-inflammatory and anticancer effects. KAR can regulate immune responses (Kim et al. 2013) and promoted M2 microglia polarization (Jia et al. 2022). Our results indicated that KAR improved renal function, inhibited fibrosis, and induced M2 macrophage polarization in the UUO model, strongly indicating the crucial regulatory role of KAR in the renal fibrosis process. However, the molecular mechanisms or pathways of how KAR regulated M2 macrophage polarization remain to be further explored.

TGF-β, a crucial factor in tissue fibrosis, can directly activate Smad signaling to promote the expression of genes related to fibrosis, such as FN, α-SMA, Snail1, and Kim1 (Hu et al. 2018). An efficient TGF-β inhibitor, pirfenidone, was approved for the treatment of pulmonary and renal fibrosis (Wang et al. 2022). Additionally, Smad3 played a pathogenic role in fibrosis and inflammation (Wu et al. 2021). Recent studies revealed that P144 improved renal fibrosis by inhibiting the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling and modulated macrophage polarization (Li et al. 2022). The research found that the TGF-β/Smad3 pathway regulated renal fibrosis through epigenetic regulation, which had clinical translational implications for targeted therapies in fibrosis (Lin and Wu 2020). Hirudin, a polypeptide structure, has been reported to decrease renal fibrosis by regulating the TGF-β1/Smad signaling (Yang et al. 2020). To investigate whether KAR suppresses the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling pathway in the UUO model, we examined Smad3 and TGF-β1 expression levels. Consistent with pathway activation, α-SMA, p-Smad3, and TGF-β1 expression were significantly upregulated in both UUO and hypoxia-stimulated groups compared with the sham group. KAR treatment markedly attenuated renal fibrosis progression in both experimental models. These findings demonstrate that KAR ameliorates renal fibrosis through inhibition of the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling pathway.

The Keap1 / Rhf-2 signaling pathway mainly regulated anti-inflammatory gene expression and inhibited inflammation progression (Ahmed et al. 2017; Liu et al. 2022; Huang et al. 2020; Alsharif et al. 2022). Previous studies demonstrated a significant increase in anti-inflammatory M2 macrophage population following KAR treatment. The current research validated the link between KAR administration and Nrf-2 activation using both in vivo and in vitro models. The Keap1/Nrf-2 pathway modulates multiple anti-inflammatory genes and plays a crucial role in mediating antioxidant responses. Notably, treatment with CPUY192018—a specific inhibitor of Keap1-Nrf-2 protein–protein interaction—significantly enhanced Nrf-2 protein expression and promoted its nuclear translocation(Lu et al. 2019). Furthermore, shenkang injection and its three anthraquinones ameliorated renal fibrosis by simultaneously targeting the Keap1/Nrf-2 pathway (Luo et al. 2021). Activation of Nrf-2 specific to hepatocytes controlled fibrogenesis and carcinogenesis in steatohepatitis (Mohs et al. 2021). Previous studies have revealed that bardoxolone decreased TGF-β1-associated renal fibrosis through Nrf-2/Smad7 (Song et al. 2019). Our data also indicated that KAR can down-regulate Keap1 induced by hypoxia, thus increasing the expression of Nrf-2 and its downstream protein HO-1. These results allowed us to indicate Nrf-2 as a promising drug target. More importantly, the combination of the Keap1-Nrf-2 protein–protein interaction inhibitor and KAR would doubly inhibit Nrf-2 in renal fibrosis due to the absence of a specific Nrf-2 inhibitor (Ge et al. 2017; Magesh et al. 2012). Furthermore, our results revealed that KAR inhibited renal fibrosis through the Keap1 / Nrf2 pathway in HK-2 cells. However, the KAR regulation mechanism in Nrf-2 needs deep exploration, which will provide more clinical treatment options.

Conclusion

This study revealed that KAR reduced UUO-induced renal fibrosis. KAR also facilitated M2 macrophage polarization to reduce the inflammatory response. In terms of mechanism, KAR activated Nrf-2 expression to alleviate renal fibrosis and inflammation, indicating that KAR is a small molecular drug for CKD.

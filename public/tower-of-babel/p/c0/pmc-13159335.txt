Evaluating PERFAMLI and CHA₂DS₂-VASc scoring systems in predicting left atrial thrombosis in non-valvular atrial fibrillation
Authors: Xuejuan Ma, Xingdi Dai, Zizheng Wang, Jing Wang, Lijin Pu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveThis study aimed to assess the predictive performance of the PERFAMLI scoring system for identifying left atrial (LA) or left atrial appendage (LAA) thrombosis in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF), and to compare its diagnostic utility with that of the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score.MethodsA retrospective, single-center study was conducted involving 518 patients with NVAF who underwent transesophageal echocardiography (TEE). Patients were categorized into a thrombus group (n = 96) and a non-thrombus group (n = 422). Clinical data were collected to calculate PERFAMLI and CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to compare the discriminatory ability of both scoring systems.ResultsThe area under the ROC curve (AUC) for the PERFAMLI score was 0.892 (95% CI: 0.852–0.931), significantly higher than that of the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score, which was 0.658 (95% CI: 0.595–0.722, p < 0.01). At an optimal cutoff of 4 points, the PERFAMLI score demonstrated a sensitivity of 87.50% and a specificity of 74.88%, yielding a Youden’s index of 0.62. In contrast, the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score showed a comparable sensitivity (86.46%) but substantially lower specificity (24.88%) at a cutoff of 2 points. Analysis of an expanded patient cohort (n = 1,015) showed consistent findings, with the AUC for PERFAMLI remaining stable at 0.881.ConclusionIn patients with CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores indicating low thromboembolic risk (< 2 points) but PERFAMLI scores suggesting a higher likelihood of atrial thrombosis (≥ 4 points), the PERFAMLI scoring system may provide complementary information to support individualized clinical evaluation.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia and is strongly associated with an elevated risk of stroke and systemic thromboembolism. Notably, approximately 90% of thrombi in patients with AF originate from the left atrial appendage (LAA) [1]. The CHA₂DS₂-VASc scoring system remains widely used for stroke risk stratification in patients with AF; however, its primary endpoint is clinical stroke, and it does not differentiate thrombus location or reliably predict the presence of LAA thrombosis [2]. To address these limitations, the PERFAMLI scoring system was developed, incorporating eight predictive variables: permanent AF, left ventricular ejection fraction, red blood cell count, plasma fibrinogen concentration, age, mitral regurgitation, left atrial (LA) diameter, and interventricular septal thickness [3]. The present study aimed to evaluate the predictive validity of the PERFAMLI score (Table 1) for identifying LA thrombosis in a large cohort of patients with non-valvular AF and to compare its performance with that of the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score (Table 2), thereby supporting more informed clinical decision-making and individualized risk assessment.

Unlike CHA₂DS₂-VASc, which is primarily designed to estimate long-term stroke risk, PERFAMLI specifically focuses on predicting LA and LAA thrombosis—key pathophysiological precursors of cardioembolic events. Rather than serving as a replacement for existing stroke risk models, PERFAMLI is intended as a complementary tool to refine thrombotic risk evaluation and assist in selecting patients who may benefit from transesophageal echocardiography or tailored anticoagulation strategies.

Methods

Study design and population

This retrospective, single-center study included patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) who were hospitalized between January 2022 and January 2024. All enrolled patients underwent transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) to assess for the presence of LA thrombosis.

Inclusion criteria

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met all of the following criteria:

(1) Age ≥ 18 years; (2) Diagnosis of atrial fibrillation confirmed through clinical history, presenting symptoms, and diagnostic evaluation; (3) Underwent TEE during the index hospitalization; (4) Availability of complete clinical and imaging data.

Exclusion criteria

The following exclusion criteria were applied:

(1) Valvular AF, including patients with mechanical or bioprosthetic valve replacement or mitral stenosis; (2) Use of anticoagulation therapy prior to hospital admission; (3) Presence of severe heart failure; (4) Hematologic disorders; (5) Autoimmune diseases; (6) Malignancies; (7) Active infections; (8) Incomplete medical records.

Diagnosis of AF

The diagnosis of AF was established in accordance with the criteria set forth in the 2020 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Atrial Fibrillation and the 2023 Chinese Society of Cardiology (CSC) AF Management Guidelines [4]. A definitive diagnosis required one of the following: (1) Absence of clearly discernible P waves on a standard 12-lead electrocardiography (ECG); or (2) A single-lead ECG recording of ≥ 30 s showing persistent absence of organized atrial activity, accompanied by irregular RR intervals, in the absence of atrioventricular conduction block.

Diagnosis of LA or LAA thrombosis

Thrombi within the LAA were identified as echogenic intracardiac masses meeting all three of the following echocardiographic criteria: (1) clearly defined borders; (2) visualization in at least two orthogonal echocardiographic planes; and (3) echogenicity distinct from the surrounding cardiac structures. Masses fulfilling only two of these criteria were classified as suspected thrombi [5].

Spontaneous echo contrast (SEC) was diagnosed based on the presence of smoke-like echoes of variable density that appeared and disappeared within the left atrium or LAA. LAA sludge was defined as a dynamic, viscid, layered echodensity without a discrete mass, visualized consistently throughout the cardiac cycle [6] (Fig. 1).

Data collection and scoring

Baseline clinical characteristics, laboratory test results, and echocardiographic findings were collected retrospectively. PERFAMLI and CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores were calculated for each patient using the corresponding clinical and diagnostic parameters.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software, version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Measurement data with normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and comparisons between groups were conducted using the Student’s t-test.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was used to assess the diagnostic performance of each scoring system. The Youden’s index was calculated to determine the optimal cutoff value for each model.

Although ROC analysis demonstrated good discriminatory ability, calibration analysis and reclassification metrics, such as net reclassification improvement and integrated discrimination improvement, were not conducted. These additional evaluations should be considered in future prospective studies to further assess the reliability and clinical utility of the model.

A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Patients in the thrombus group were significantly older than those in the non-thrombus group (66.15 ± 9.42 vs. 57.34 ± 13.77 years; p < 0.001). In addition, the thrombus group exhibited larger LA diameter (43.54 ± 6.12 vs. 35.98 ± 4.30 mm), increased interventricular septal thickness (11.68 ± 1.82 vs. 10.46 ± 1.74 mm), and higher fibrinogen levels (3.36 ± 2.21 vs. 2.65 ± 0.78 g/L). All between-group differences were statistically significant (p < 0.05) (Table 3).

Diagnostic performance of PERFAMLI in the initial cohort (n = 518)

In the initial cohort, the PERFAMLI score demonstrated an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of 0.892, significantly outperforming the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score (AUC = 0.658; p < 0.01) (Fig. 2). At an optimal cutoff value of 4 points, the PERFAMLI score achieved a sensitivity of 87.50% and specificity of 74.88%. In comparison, the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score yielded a similar sensitivity (86.46%) but markedly lower specificity (24.88%).

Validation in an expanded dataset (n = 1,015)

In the expanded cohort of 1,015 patients, the diagnostic performance of the PERFAMLI scoring system remained stable, with an AUC of 0.881 (Fig. 3). This finding supports the consistency and generalizability of the model in a broader patient population.

The PERFAMLI score outperformed CHA₂DS₂-VASc in terms of specificity, accuracy, PPV, NPV, and Youden’s index. While PERFAMLI showed marginally lower sensitivity and slightly higher rates of missed and incorrect diagnoses, it demonstrated superior overall diagnostic utility for the detection of LA thrombosis in patients with NVAF.

Discussion

The PERFAMLI scoring system was developed based on previously identified independent risk factors for LA thrombogenesis, including persistent atrial fibrillation, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) < 50%, erythrocyte count ≥ 4.8 × 10¹²/L, fibrinogen concentration ≥ 4 g/L, age ≥ 67 years, mitral regurgitation, LA diameter ≥ 39 mm, and interventricular septal thickness ≥ 12 mm. These variables and their corresponding threshold values were selected through prior multivariable analyses and ROC curve optimization, and are supported by established pathophysiological evidence [3]. In the present study, these predefined cutoffs were applied without re-estimation, thereby minimizing the risk of overfitting.

Validation in a large NVAF patient cohort demonstrated that the PERFAMLI score outperformed the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score in predicting LA or LAA thrombosis, yielding an AUC of 0.892 (95% CI: 0.86–0.92) compared to 0.658 (95% CI: 0.61–0.70) for CHA₂DS₂-VASc (p < 0.001).

The clinical utility of the PERFAMLI scoring system can be considered from three key perspectives: its basis in pathophysiological mechanisms, its relevance to individualized clinical decision-making, and its methodological strengths and limitations. The score integrates eight independently validated predictors, each of which is mechanistically associated with the development of LA thrombosis.

Persistent atrial fibrillation

Persistent atrial fibrillation was present in 79.2% of patients in the thrombus group, compared with 32.9% in the non-thrombus group (p < 0.001). Sustained AF contributes to both electrical and structural remodeling of the atria. Electrophysiological alterations, such as deceleration of conduction velocity and stabilization of reentrant circuits, are accompanied by structural changes including atrial dilatation. These processes promote blood stasis and endothelial dysfunction, consistent with the pathophysiological components of Virchow’s triad [7].

In addition, patients with long-standing AF often demonstrate significantly reduced LAA emptying velocities (< 20 cm/s), a hemodynamic abnormality associated with an estimated 3.2-fold increase in the risk of thrombus formation [8].

Reduced LVEF

The mean LVEF in the thrombus group was 59.19 ± 12.77%, significantly lower than that observed in the non-thrombus group (67.21 ± 6.63%; p < 0.001). Impaired systolic function may contribute to thrombus formation within the left atrium by elevating left ventricular filling pressures, leading to atrial dilation and blood stasis. Prior studies have indicated that patients with LVEF < 50% face an approximately 2.3-fold increased risk of thromboembolic events [9].

Elevated erythrocyte count

Patients in the thrombus group demonstrated higher mean erythrocyte counts (4.89 ± 0.55 × 10¹²/L) compared with those in the non-thrombus group (4.69 ± 0.32 × 10¹²/L; p < 0.001). An elevated red blood cell count may reflect a hyperviscous state, which promotes thrombus formation by increasing blood viscosity and enhancing platelet aggregation [10].

LA enlargement and interventricular septal hypertrophy

Patients in the thrombus group exhibited significantly larger LA diameters (43.54 ± 6.12 mm vs. 36.81 ± 4.95 mm) and greater interventricular septal thickness (11.68 ± 1.82 mm vs. 9.45 ± 1.23 mm) compared to the non-thrombus group, with both differences reaching statistical significance (p < 0.001). These structural abnormalities are commonly associated with impaired left ventricular diastolic function, elevated atrial filling pressures, and diminished flow velocities in the left atrium and appendage. Hemodynamic studies have demonstrated that LAA emptying velocities below 20 cm/s are linked to a nearly threefold increase in thrombotic risk [11].

Mitral regurgitation (MR) controversy

The relationship between mitral regurgitation and LA thrombus formation remains a subject of debate. One study involving patients undergoing radiofrequency ablation for AF identified MR, detected via preoperative TEE, as a predictive factor for LA thrombosis in those with non-valvular AF [12]. Conversely, a Chinese cohort study reported a lower prevalence of MR (38.7% vs. 61.2%) and smaller regurgitant areas (2.1 ± 1.3 cm² vs. 3.8 ± 2.1 cm²) in patients with thrombus compared to those without [13].

Findings from the present study were more consistent with the former, showing a higher frequency of LA thrombus among patients with echocardiographically confirmed MR. However, detailed quantitative assessment of MR severity, such as regurgitant volume and vena contracta width, was not systematically performed, limiting the ability to fully evaluate its contribution to thrombotic risk.

Limitations of CHA₂DS₂-VASc scoring system

The CHA₂DS₂-VASc score is a well-established and widely used tool for estimating overall stroke risk in patients with AF. It incorporates common clinical risk factors associated with both cardioembolic and atherosclerotic cerebrovascular events. Its ease of use and reliance on readily accessible clinical history contribute to its practicality in routine care.

However, the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score was not specifically developed to predict LA or LAA thrombosis. Consequently, its performance in identifying patients with imaging-confirmed thrombus is limited, particularly in cases where thrombotic risk arises primarily from atrial structural remodeling and hemodynamic disturbances, rather than from conventional vascular risk factors [14]. In clinical practice, patients with NVAF and CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores ≥ 2 are often prescribed anticoagulation, even when the underlying stroke risk may involve non-cardioembolic mechanisms unrelated to atrial thrombosis.

In the present study, the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score demonstrated suboptimal diagnostic performance for detecting LA thrombosis, with an AUC of 0.658 and low specificity (24.88%). Notably, 75.12% of patients with CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores ≥ 2 had no detectable thrombus on transesophageal echocardiography, indicating a high false-positive rate and limited specificity for thrombus-oriented risk stratification.

Moreover, the CHA₂DS₂-VASc model does not include key anatomical or hemodynamic indicators, such as LA diameter or LVEF, which are directly linked to blood stasis and thrombus formation. In contrast, the PERFAMLI score incorporates such parameters, including LA diameter and interventricular septal thickness, and demonstrated superior diagnostic performance in this cohort. Specifically, PERFAMLI achieved higher sensitivity (82.4% vs. 61.7%) and specificity (76.1% vs. 24.9%) for identifying LA/LAA thrombi.

Nevertheless, a key limitation of the current study must be acknowledged: diagnostic classification was based on the presence of LA or LAA thrombus as a surrogate endpoint, rather than on clinical stroke outcomes. This surrogate-based design may introduce selection bias and limit generalizability. Future prospective validation studies incorporating longitudinal stroke outcomes are warranted to further assess the prognostic and therapeutic utility of the PERFAMLI scoring system.

Clinical implications of the PERFAMLI scoring system

(1) Anticoagulation optimization

In patients with a CHA₂DS₂-VASc score < 2 but a PERFAMLI score ≥ 4, the likelihood of underlying atrial thrombosis may be underestimated by conventional stroke risk stratification. In such discordant cases, the PERFAMLI score may assist clinicians in identifying patients who warrant closer evaluation or further imaging, such as TEE, prior to making anticoagulation decisions.

(2) Risk-stratified surveillance

Patients with PERFAMLI scores ≥ 4 may benefit from targeted TEE screening to detect LA or LAA thrombus, whereas those with scores < 4 may require less frequent invasive monitoring. This risk-adapted approach may improve the efficiency of diagnostic workflows and reduce unnecessary procedures. Based on current utilization estimates, adopting PERFAMLI-guided triage could potentially decrease TEE-related costs by approximately 32%.

(3) Clinical feasibility

The PERFAMLI scoring system is composed entirely of variables routinely assessed in standard clinical practice, including transthoracic echocardiography and complete blood count. Its reliance on readily available and non-specialized diagnostic tools enhances its feasibility for integration into primary care settings and broad applicability in resource-limited environments.

Importantly, the PERFAMLI score may be particularly useful for identifying patients with low CHA₂DS₂-VASc scores who nevertheless exhibit elevated thrombotic risk. In this subgroup, traditional stroke-based scoring may underestimate the need for further evaluation. In such scenarios, PERFAMLI may support more individualized decisions regarding anticoagulation therapy and the necessity for additional imaging.

Limitations and future directions

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, LA or LAA thrombus was used as the primary endpoint, serving as a surrogate rather than a definitive clinical outcome such as ischemic stroke or systemic embolism. Although LA/LAA thrombus is strongly associated with thromboembolic risk and is widely accepted as an imaging marker of cardioembolic potential, it does not fully account for the multifactorial nature of clinical stroke. As such, caution is warranted when extrapolating thrombus-based risk stratification to long-term clinical outcomes.

Second, this was a retrospective, single-center study conducted in an exclusively Asian patient population, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. The observed incidence of LA thrombosis (18.5%) was notably higher than the 5–10% typically reported in community-based AF cohorts, likely reflecting selection bias inherent to hospitalized populations. Furthermore, certain threshold values used in the PERFAMLI scoring system, such as interventricular septal thickness (≥ 12 mm) and age cutoffs, may be specific to the study population and require validation in more ethnically diverse cohorts [15].

Third, long-term follow-up data on clinical endpoints, including ischemic stroke and systemic embolism, were not available. As a result, the present analysis could not directly evaluate the relationship between PERFAMLI scores and subsequent thromboembolic events, underscoring the need for cautious interpretation of its prognostic implications.

Future studies should aim to conduct prospective, multicenter validation of the PERFAMLI scoring system using definitive clinical outcomes across varied populations. Validation across multiple ethnicities will be essential to determine its applicability in broader clinical settings. Additionally, integration of emerging biomarkers, such as NT-proBNP and D-dimer, and the use of advanced predictive models, including machine learning–based algorithms, may further enhance the precision of thromboembolic risk assessment in patients with NVAF.

Conclusion

The PERFAMLI scoring system demonstrated superior predictive accuracy for LA thrombosis in patients with NVAF compared with the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score. As a thrombus-specific risk stratification tool, PERFAMLI may serve as a valuable adjunct to traditional stroke-based models by offering additional, mechanism-focused insight to guide individualized clinical decision-making.

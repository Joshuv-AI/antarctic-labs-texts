Policy instruments facilitate China’s COVID-19 work resumption
Authors: Pengjun Zhao, Qiyang Liu, Tianyu Ma, Tingting Kang, Zhengzi Zhou, Zhengying Liu, Mengzhu Zhang, Jie Wan
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SignificanceIn the aftermath of COVID-19, the pressing challenge for governments worldwide is reigniting their labor forces amid economic downturns. China’s unique containment strategies and systematic work resumption efforts offer a crucial lens for addressing this global concern. By scrutinizing human mobility patterns of 320 million workers and sifting through half a million policy documents, our study reframes work resumption as a multifaceted behavioral transition influenced by capacity, motivation, and strategic policy choices. We highlight that the harmony of these elements—rather than their isolated presence—holds the key to accelerating postcrisis workforce revitalization, a takeaway that holds implications for recovery strategies globally.

Since the report of a cluster of SARS-like pneumonia cases in December 2019, COVID-19 has developed into a pandemic that has devastated most of the world. By the end of 2022, more than 600 million cases of COVID-19 had been reported, with over 1 million deaths globally. China, as the first outbreak center of the pandemic, implemented a slew of nonpharmaceutical containment measures, including lockdowns, curfews, village isolations, travel restrictions, school closures and remote teaching, and public transport shutdowns (1, 2). These containment strategies later found resonance in worldwide policy responses (3, 4).

However, as these measures took effect, curtailing virus spread (5–7), they also dramatically reshaped societal functions. This was particularly pronounced in China, where COVID lockdowns coincided with the Lunar New Year, when the populace traditionally migrates away from their work locations (8). Postcontainment, the challenge of workforce remobilization loomed large, prompting subnational governments to initiate policies fostering work resumption. After the end of the Chinese New Year national holiday, the resumption of work gradually commenced on February 3, 2020. However, at this time, the focus in all prefectures remained on COVID-19 prevention by maintaining level I public health emergency responses (Fig. 1). Starting from February 21, 2020, regions began gradually adjusting the level of public health emergency responses to level II or III, signaling that substantial work resumption had taken place. Under the guidance of the Central Government to promote work resumption, power was decentralized to allow provincial, prefectural, and county-level authorities to formulate work resumption policies based on their specific circumstances, making them highly flexible to enact or loosen restrictions and implement a variety of work resumption stimulus. While these policies were instrumental in achieving near-normal work resumption rates by April 17, 2020, a comprehensive understanding of which strategies optimized postpandemic labor market recovery remains elusive.

Our study aims to dissect the effectiveness of these strategies. Harnessing expansive cellphone location data from one of the largest telecommunication providers in China and textual data for about 500,000 policy instruments, we seek to determine the configurations most beneficial for prefectures, depending on their socioeconomic and administrative capacities.

Our investigation is grounded in a theoretical framework, the Fogg behavior model (FBM). The FBM offers a systematic lens through which to understand the nuances of behavioral changes, having been instrumental in deciphering behavioral shifts linked to COVID, such as adherence to preventive measures and vaccine acceptance (9, 10). This model posits that for any desired behavior to manifest, three concurrent elements are requisite: a) capability to exhibit the behavior, b) ample motivation, and c) suitable triggers nudging the behavioral transition (11). As illustrated in Fig. 2, there exists an interplay between motivation and capability. Highly motivated individuals may engage in a behavior even when it is challenging, and vice versa. However, diminished motivation combined with limited capability markedly decreases the likelihood of the targeted behavior’s manifesting.

We use a conceptual framework for analyzing the effectiveness of different work resumption approaches. This framework clusters variables into four main categories: (A) the capacity to change behavior collectively from lockdown to work resumption; (B) motivations to resume work, measured by administrative inclination for strict COVID-19 confinement during the national WRP; (C) triggers designed to facilitate the reopening, measured by the number of policies issued by local authorities; and (D) the impacts of these policy instruments, measured by the cumulative return-to-work rate (CRR). For details on variable selection, see the “Materials and Methods” section.

In our study, we interpret prefectures as individual behavioral entities, extrapolating the FBM to comprehend the collective shift from lockdown to work resumption because the FBM emphasizes the role of triggers in instigating behavior. In the context of post-COVID work resumption, many triggers, such as policies on lockdown easing or financial stimuli, might be less discernible or variable at the individual or firm level but are strikingly evident at the prefectural level. Moreover, within the Chinese context, prefectures, as administrative divisions, have historically been focal points for sociopolitical decision-making and localized policy implementation. In many respects, they possess autonomy, fine-tuning national directives to better fit their local realities (12). This autonomy parallels the notion of individual agency in the FBM.

Also, by focusing solely on individuals or firms, we run the risk of myopia—capturing only fragmented snapshots of the larger picture. Individual behaviors and firm decisions are often influenced by immediate personal or profit-driven motivations, potentially sidelining broader socioeconomic, administrative, and policy factors. Prefectures, in contrast, embody aggregated behaviors and decisions, encompassing broader community dynamics, administrative directives, and infrastructural considerations. Thus, a prefecture-focused approach offers a more comprehensive understanding of the multifaceted dynamics at play. It contextualizes how localized socioeconomic conditions, combined with administrative proclivities, interact with policy triggers to influence work resumption, revealing patterns that might otherwise remain obscured in a granular, individual, or firm-centric analysis.

Prefectures, seen through this lens, exhibit varied capabilities—encapsulated by socioeconomic and administrative capacities—and motivations influenced by local administrative inclinations toward pandemic management. Policy measures, in this framework, act as catalysts, nudging prefectures toward work resumption. To this end, we formulate our research hypotheses:Hypothesis 1: The socioeconomic and administrative capacity, COVID-19 containment strategy, and policy instruments must synchronize for a prefecture to reach a high COVID-19 work resumption rate (CRR).Hypothesis 2: Different types of subnational policies can generate heterogeneous impacts on the CRR of different sectors.Hypothesis 3: Policy instruments at different stages have different impacts on CRR.

Materials and Methods

Ragin’s QCA has been widely applied in the social sciences (13–15), and it is tailor-made for this study because of its ability to deal with complex complementarities and nonlinear relationships between local capacities and different types of policy interventions (16–18). QCA starts from the premise that causation is not easily unraveled because causes are rarely associated with the outcomes of interest in isolation from other causes, and specific factors may have different and even opposite effects depending on the context (19). In the case of postcrisis recovery, it is vital to examine how local capacities and policy instruments combine to generate high-low work resumption outcomes instead of a net effects estimation of whether each independent policy instrument is associated with work resumption. Drawing on Boolean algebra and set-theoretic approaches (20), QCA has advantages over other methodologies as it can determine complex causality that cannot be addressed by conventional methods for causality and the generalizability of case study results that can never be achieved by traditional qualitative techniques. Moreover, since the high/low rate of work resumption may be due to different combinations of local fiscal and administrative capacities and postcrisis recovery policy packages, QCA can better deal with causal asymmetry. In this study, we adopted the fsQCA technique.

Policy Data.

We collected 1,085,039 policy instruments from 36,392 documents issued by provincial-, prefecture-, and county-level governments from February 1 to April 10, 2020. After document skimming, we selected 472,804 pieces of policy relevant to COVID-19 containment and work resumption. Selected policies were then classified to establish a week-by-week time-series policy database. A grounded theory approach (21) was adopted to conduct policy document analysis via NVivo12, and then, a general inductive approach (22) was used to check the reliability of the results. Qualitative coding is the core process of these analyses (SI Appendix, Appendix A). First, Liu and Ma independently completed two rounds of open coding. In this stage, we worked closely with policy documents, trying to name the emergence of a core category from a specific segment of the data as new “codes.” After open coding, we conducted two rounds of axial coding. Axial coding is the process of relating codes we generated in the open coding stage to each other. Through the axial coding process, we developed an integrated set of policy instruments by weaving fractured notions into different categories. Then Liu and Ma independently used the list of categories to review the policy documents to confirm that the categories could accurately represent policy instruments and to explore the interactions between categories. The research team generated a list of 697 codes (nodes in NVivo) and 24 theoretical codes (parent nodes in NVivo). We used work resumption as the core to guide our selective coding. Finally, one theme that embodies the administrative capacity of the prefectures and three general categories of policy instruments (i.e., fiscal, employment, and procurement policies) emerged from the analysis. We then matched keywords in the policy documents to the 24 theoretical codes and allocated each piece of policy to the three categories of policy instruments based on its corresponding keywords. We used the number of policies under each category of policy instrument to measure the intensity of policy triggers that facilitate work resumption.

The extent to which a prefecture is able to resume work is measured by its socioeconomic and administrative capacity. Post-COVID-19 recovery requires a strong local economic base and the strength and effectiveness of a local authority in its policymaking and implementation processes (23). In this study, we consider prefectural socioeconomic capacity as the local economic base to cope with the COVID-19-related recession. It is measured by using the GRP per capita. Administrative capacity reflects the strength and effectiveness of a local authority in its policymaking and implementation process. It is measured by the number of policies that reinforce administrative efficiency. We did this because it can directly reflect local authorities’ capacity to respond to COVID-19, unlike other indicators such as public expenditure.

We adopted the most applicable procedures to guarantee the credibility and consistency of the policy document analysis, including independent parallel coding and checks on the clarity of categories (SI Appendix, Appendix B). The trustworthiness of our analyses is further checked in two ways. We calculated the kappa value using the Coder Comparison Queries of NVivo to test coding consistency. The kappa value was 0.81 for the first round of coding and 0.86 for the second round, indicating perfect consistency. Our open and axial coding results were further compared with COVID-19 economic stimulus packages issued by other countries, ensuring our clustering of policy instruments and their corresponding keywords.

Return-to-work Data.

The return-to-work rates were computed via big data analyses. We used anonymized human mobility trajectory information from 320 million mobile phone users across China. The raw location data are generated when cellphone users connect to cellular networks with trigger events, such as mobile communication and internet usage. In addition, users’ location information is periodically recorded (every 30 min), as is the ID of the cellular tower to which it connects. Human mobility trajectory information is from a series of geopositioning points for each cellphone which can be obtained by using the triangulation positioning technique. To identify users’ workplaces, we take the following steps. a) We select frequent cellphone users with enough records in both 2019 and 2020. To avoid the impact of students and retired individuals on the results, we have removed users younger than 22* and older than 60. b) We extract each user’s stay points as places where the individual’s moving distance is less than 500 m within 30 min. c) We impose the space-time criterion on the clean data to identify pre-COVID-19 workplace location information. The pre-COVID-19 workplace is defined as the 500 m * 500 m grid where the user spent more than 6 h in the daytime for at least 15 weekdays in a month before the outbreak of COVID-19 (December 2019). d) We then performed spatial overlay analyses between a 500 m * 500 m grid and AOI (area of interest) spatial data with sectoral attributes. Through these analyses, we identified people working in 11 main sectors (86%) of the total labor force (SI Appendix, Appendix C). Only two main sectors are missing in our analyses: the agriculture and construction sectors. We did not consider the agriculture sector because farmers were not subject to work resumption, and workers in the construction sector usually have unstable workplace locations, making it difficult to accurately identify their work resumption using cellphone location data. We conducted a paired-samples t test to compare the total number of individuals in each sector whom we identified with the corresponding number of employees in each sector from the official statistical yearbook, indicating that our identification of workplace location is generally accurate (SI Appendix, Appendix D).

We collected 320 million users’ location information from February 17 to April 10 in 2019 and 2020 to estimate people’s returns to work. The time span covered the national WRP, including the working days in 8 wk (a total of 40 d). We counted the number of people who returned to the locations of their workplace before the outbreak of COVID-19. The work resumption rate was then calculated as the ratio of the number of people who worked at a certain 500 m * 500 m grid between February 17 and April 12, 2020, to that on December 2019. CRR counts individuals who had returned to the identified pre-COVID-19 workplace location at least once from February 17 to the day CRR is calculated.

COVID-19 Confinement Strictness Index.

To measure the inclination for strict COVID-19 confinement in policy documents, we used a pretrained neural network of a word-embedding model to transform the digitized text into computable high-dimensional word vectors to represent the meaning of words (24). Word embedding models have become an emerging computational approach in the social sciences, as word vectors in well-trained embeddings represent semantic information corresponding to how people understand them (25–27). Such an approach assumes that words that share similar contexts in the text have similar meanings, while words that appear only in different and disconnected contexts have lower semantic similarity. At the same time, since vectors are computable, we can average the vectors of all words in a document to get the vector of this document. Thus, the administrative inclination for strict COVID-19 confinement can become encoded in the distributional semantics of language as policy documents focusing on COVID-19 confinement are more likely to have a higher similarity to words like lockdown, while policies that stimulate business reopening are more likely to have a higher similarity to words like production.

In this study, we used 200-dimensional doc2vec vectors derived from the Tencent AI Lab Embedding Corpus for Chinese Words and Phrases (ai.tencent.com/ailab/nlp/en/embedding.html), which contains more than 12 million training vectors for Chinese words. We take the average normalized result of all word vectors in a document as the document vector, and constructed a policy inclination dimension based on multiple pairs of antonyms representing policy extremes—work resumption→ and COVID containment→ . By projecting the document vector onto the axis we constructed, the level of policy inclination of document semantics can be calculated.

Analytical Process.

A multilevel analysis was conducted in this research, including a path analysis, a time series analysis, an examination of causal asymmetry at high and low levels of work resumption, sectoral analyses, and a robustness test for large-N QCA. The software package fsQCA4.0 was used to standardize the data and conduct the analyses. All the data were computed to fuzzy-set calibration (0, 0.5, 0.95). The minimum case frequency benchmark (≥1) and raw consistency benchmark (≥0.8) were set to calculate a truth table, and further, the proportional reduction in inconsistency was applied to filter the truth table. Utilizing the truth table, we conducted a necessity conditions analysis, and a standard analysis without setting any subjective selection for the causal conditions, and then to obtain parsimonious solutions, intermediate solutions, and complex solutions. Reasonable and well-documented intermediate solutions are often a better choice than complex solutions for reporting and interpreting in QCA research. Thus, we used the intermediate solutions to determine the number of configurations and their inclusion conditions, and we used the parsimonious solutions to determine core conditions for a given configuration. Accordingly, we plotted the results of each analysis.

A path analysis is the foundation for this research to identify the configurational paths for the causal analysis over the 8 wk, from which we developed a time series analysis to distinguish the configurational paths for each week. We also examined the causal asymmetry by computing the dependent variable to fuzzy-set calibration (0, 0.5, 0.95) for high mobility and setting this variable negative for low mobility. We then conducted configuration analysis for the 11 main sectors. QCA was designed for midsize Ns (10 to 50 cases), but it is being increasingly applied to larger samples. Our research is a typical large-N QCA with 355 cases. Focusing on parameter sensitivity and measurement error, we conducted multiple robustness tests, including randomly deleting cases, changing calibration anchors, changing frequency thresholds, and testing the results against SEM and OLS regression models.

The consistency index and coverage index are key indicators used in fsQCA research. Consistency refers to the extent to which all cases share one or more given conditions that lead to the outcomes. Generally, when the consistency is over 0.8, it means that more than 80% of cases meet the consistency conditions, and X denotes a sufficient condition. If the consistency is over 0.9, then X denotes a necessary condition. Coverage refers to the extent to which these given conditions (or the combinations of conditions) could explain the occurrence of outcomes. Returning to Work after the First Wave of COVID-19 in China.

We examined anonymized individual trajectory data from over 400 million cellphone users across 355 cities in China, comparing data from December 2019 (before the pandemic) to that of February-April 2020 (during the national Work Resumption).

Despite stringent mobility restrictions during the WRP, the return-to-work rate substantially increased. As the gears of large-scale work resumption started turning, a surge of workers flooded back, culminating in a peak during the WRP’s third week. Remarkably, the return-to-work rate soared from 31.1% on February 17, 2020, to an impressive 51.7% by March 6, 2020. Although this momentum slowed slightly thereafter, by early April, the landscape was unmistakably transformed, with over 60% of individuals firmly back at their previous workstations.

The tale of China’s CRR is a mosaic of diverse narratives (Fig. 3): The CRR of Wuhan, the pandemic’s epicenter, languished below 20% until March 24, 2020. Yet, in a striking turn, it soared to 41.0% within the next 2 wk. Meanwhile, in neighboring Hubei cities, the grip of stringent mobility rules was evident, with significant work resumption only gaining momentum post-March 16. And just a month later, their CRR touched 64.3%. Elsewhere, the scenes were even more dramatic. Cities nestled in the rustic northeastern heartlands and the bustling southeastern coast witnessed a swifter resurgence in work resumption, showcasing China’s resilience and dynamism.

The hotspot analyses revealed a shift from an encircling pattern to a one-side enclosure pattern of the clustering of prefectures with high CRRs (Fig. 4). During the initial 3 wk, this encircling pattern enveloped about 40% of prefectures. However, as the weeks passed, the southern coastal hotspots began to fade. By week 8, their radiance had dwindled, covering just 31.1% of the area. In contrast, the coldspots remained consistently present, casting a shadow over roughly 29% of the regions during the WRP. Two primary clusters with low CRRs emerged—one meandering from the central heartlands, marked heavily by confirmed cases, to the eastern shores. Another colder trail was etched along the western fringes. The former, however, waned, contracting from a broad 44.13% in week 1 to a more contained 29.51% by week 8. The latter sprawled like tendrils from the northwest to the southwest. As Southeast Asia reported a surge in COVID-19 cases post-mid-March, the border regions, akin to vigilant guardians, fortified their defenses to thwart imported cases. This pivot meant that local authorities perhaps transitioned their attention from work resumption to reinstating COVID-19 protective measures.

The weekly average return-to-work rate (WORR) displayed distinct patterns (SI Appendix, Appendix F). Prefectures fell into three main clusters based on their WORR trends over 8 wk postresumption. The clusters were 63.0% in the first, mostly from wealthier northwest regions, the Bohai Economic Rim, and less competitive southern areas; 25.5% in the second, scattered throughout China; and 11.5% in the third, primarily from COVID-19 hotspots like Hubei and Zhejiang, north-west borders, and major cities in the Pearl River Delta. The first cluster peaked by the second week then stabilized at a low rate. The second cluster saw steady growth with a brief third-week dip. The third started slow but surged after the fourth week.

Policy Instruments for Work Resumption.

Three distinct types of policy instruments during the WRP were identified (Materials and Methods): fiscal, employment, and procurement policies (for examples of different types of policies, see SI Appendix, Appendix G; for the spatial diffusion of policy instruments, see SI Appendix, Appendix H). China’s subnational fiscal stimulus can be unfolded into four dimensions: a) direct fiscal transfers to firms, households, and individuals aimed at substituting income erosion; b) fiscal transfers aimed at reducing fixed production costs; c) tax exemptions and reductions; and d) government purchases of goods and services (28). Employment policies mainly addressed safety, security, and interregional mobility issues of employees. The most pervasive policy instruments were a) policies protecting workers from infection such as developing procedures for employees to report symptoms of COVID-19, b) policies mitigating the job insecurity of rural-urban migrant workers such as free skill training programs, and c) policies directly overcoming interregional travel restrictions such as government-subsidized company-chartered cars/buses. When essential items were scarce, two main procurement policies were implemented to navigate the COVID-19 crisis: the procurement of raw materials for the manufacturing sector and the supply of urgently needed medical equipment, especially personal protective equipment (PPE).

Our solutions performed well for both coverage and consistency dimensions. Table 1 indicates a 0.63 coverage and 0.91 consistency for high work resumption, meaning that the solution accounts for 63% of prefectures with high work resumption rates, and 91% of cases within these configurations show high work resumption. For low work resumption, the figures are 0.94 for coverage and 0.70 for consistency, illustrating that our configurations effectively capture both high and low work resumption rates.

Furthermore, we find that the inclination for strict COVID-19 confinement plays a varied role across sectors. In the hotel and catering sector, its absence helped stimulate work resumption. For manufacturing, strictness in COVID-19 confinement did not significantly impact resumption rates. Conversely, in the education sector and government agencies, a strict COVID-19 confinement inclination may even foster work resumption.

Our week-by-week analyses (Table 3) reveal a three-phase path to high work resumption. In the first phase (weeks 1 to 3), the presence of social-economic capacity is the core condition for high work resumption rates, combined with administrative capacity and all three types of policy instruments. A strict COVID-19 confinement inclination is vital in week 2 but counterproductive in week 3. In the second phase (weeks 4 to 5), work resumption hinges on administrative capacity, procurement policies, and the absence of strict COVID-19 confinement. Social-economic capacity, employment, and fiscal policies become less relevant. As relatively lower (0.26 and 0.25, respectively) overall solution coverages imply, this phase sees diverse governmental approaches. In the third phase (weeks 6 to 8), the lack of stringent COVID-19 restrictions, combined with socio-economic factors, administrative capacity, and the implementation of employment policies, play a crucial role. To generate a high work resumption rate, these four shared conditions need to be supplemented with either fiscal or procurement policies.

We confirmed the robustness of these results in three tests (SI Appendix, Appendix K). First, we randomly deleted 10%, 15%, and 20% of the observations and reran the pooled analyses. We obtained five of the seven original configurations for high work resumption and four of the five original configurations for low work resumption. Second, we modified the calibration anchors for all conditions using the direct method to the 20th, 50th, and 80th percentiles and reran the pooled analysis for our main sample. Minor changes were observed regarding the kind of neutral permutations as well as the number of solutions, but the core conditions and the interpretation of the solutions remained stable. Moreover, the same results for all configurations were replicated, but one subconfiguration was reduced for high work resumption. Third, we changed the analysis thresholds by increasing the case frequency from 1 to 2 and then from 1 to 3 and reran the pooled analysis for our main sample. The number of solutions did not substantially reduce when the case frequency cutoff was set at 2, and the results were again very similar. The solutions were significantly simplified when the case frequency cutoff was 3, but the remaining configurations were still identical to our main analysis, and the most different configuration H1 was logically substitutable.

While most previous studies utilized fuzzy-set QCA (fsQCA) for small samples, our large sample enables a comparison between fsQCA and more conventional approaches such as structural equation modeling (SEM) and ordinary least squares (OLS) regression. As outlined in SI Appendix, Appendix L, SEM shows significant direct effects of socioeconomic capacity and procurement policies on work resumption, with administrative capacity’s indirect effects through various policies. OLS results align with SEM, demonstrating that socioeconomic capacity and procurement policies directly impact the work resumption rate. Significant interaction effects were found between administrative capacity and procurement policies, but collinearity issues limited further exploration of the combinations of interactions simultaneously. Thus, both SEM and OLS offer only partial insights into the causal conditions of work resumption.

Discussion

Natural and public health crises often result in decreased human mobility, leading to profound social and economic consequences such as dwindling government revenues and rising poverty and inequality (29). Therefore, revitalizing the labor force during postcrisis recovery becomes imperative. China’s response to the COVID-19 pandemic stands as a seminal example of effective labor force rejuvenation postcrisis. This positioned China as a crucible for evaluating various policy interventions aimed at stimulating work resumption across its diverse local governments. We crafted a theoretical framework based on the FBM model to delve into the nuances of COVID-19 work resumption. Utilizing a comprehensive prefecture-level dataset, which encompasses mobility patterns of 320 million workers and an analysis of approximately 500,000 policy documents, we rigorously tested three hypotheses.

Low work resumption rates, as depicted in the configurations, underline the repercussions of lacking ability, motivation, or triggers. More than the mere presence of these three factors, the synergy between them is pivotal for prefectures to stimulate work resumption effectively. Our findings challenge the notion that socioeconomic and administrative capacities always work hand in hand, enhancing the prefectural ability to cope with COVID-19. Prefectures boasting strong socioeconomic capacities often have the resilience to align with rigorous COVID-19 restrictions, even with minimal administrative interventions. In contrast, those with weaker socioeconomic foundations necessitate proactive government intervention and an amplified drive to propel work resumption. These findings reaffirm our first hypothesis: To achieve a high CRR, achieving a high CRR demands a harmonized interplay between socioeconomic prowess, administrative capacity, strategic COVID-19 measures, and well-orchestrated policy instruments.

Remarkably, while implementing an array of policy interventions concurrently tends to dampen work resumption rates, a tailored approach emphasizing a specific policy type can effectively galvanize work resumption, especially when harmonized with the distinct socioeconomic and administrative capacities of each prefecture. Our analysis reveals a nuanced tapestry of factors influencing high work resumption, in stark contrast to the more uniform drivers underpinning low work resumption. Even though our insights into low work resumption carry substantial explanatory heft—evident from the elevated solution coverage scores—they span across a mere five configuration variants. In contrast, high work resumption sprawls over seven configurations, underlining its complex, multifarious nature.

Our findings lend credence to our second hypothesis, emphasizing that different types of subnational policies can generate heterogeneous impacts on the CRR across sectors. While many developed nations lean on fiscal policies as a primary remedy to counteract economic downturns associated with COVID-19 (30), our results indicate a more nuanced scenario in China. Specifically, fiscal policies seem to predominantly benefit very limited, underdeveloped prefectures, showcasing pronounced efficacy in sectors like wholesale and retail. Although fiscal policies also appear in some configurations for sectors such as the mining and manufacturing sectors, they can only serve as supplementary policies to other core policies, such as procurement policies for the mining sector and employment policies for the manufacturing sector. This again suggests that a one-size-fits-all approach may not resonate as effectively across China’s variegated landscape.

Among different types of fiscal instruments, payroll tax cuts comprised the most salient part of China’s COVID-19 fiscal stimulus package (31), especially in underdeveloped prefectures that enacted much less direct fiscal transfers and government purchases of goods and services due to their limited socioeconomic capacity. Payroll tax cuts were especially effective in underdeveloped prefectures because their economies depend heavily on small and micro-sized enterprises (32). However, due to the household registration system,† the number of rural–urban migrants who engage in productive activities that are not taxed in more economically developed prefectures is much greater than that in underdeveloped prefectures (33), which may considerably reduce the reach of benefits among enterprises in those prefectures. Moreover, more economically developed prefectures tended to enact more fiscal transfer instruments, which may alleviate the cashflow pressure of SMEs, most of which were estimated as able to survive no more than 3 mo under the COVID-19 strike (34, 35). However, our results suggest that such instruments could not effectively facilitate work resumption.

Employment policies, given their pivotal role in fostering work resumption, especially within the realms of manufacturing and business services, gain heightened significance when looked at within the context of economically thriving coastal manufacturing hubs in China (SI Appendix, Appendix M). These areas, being hubs of China’s export-oriented industries, faced acute challenges due to global demand shocks and disruptions to the global supply chain. To facilitate work resumption, the Chinese government crafted a multifaceted suite of employment policies. COVID-related employment policies contain a variety of instruments, primarily targeting two groups: skilled workers, who were vital for the technological and quality standards of the industries, and rural–urban migrant workers, who form the backbone of manual labor in urban areas (23, 36). Skilled workers, typically having more specialized roles, often faced the anxiety of being rendered irrelevant due to rapid technological shifts or the prolonged absence from their workplace. Policies for this cohort, therefore, focused on retention and reskilling. Training programs were introduced to ensure they were abreast with technological advancements, and fiscal incentives were provided to companies to retain and attract such talent during the turbulent period. Rural–urban migrant workers faced a different set of challenges. For one, the interregional transport service shutdowns significantly impacted their ability to return to cities. Additionally, the pandemic-induced economic downturn meant that many of them faced job insecurity. To address these challenges, governments initiated policies such as government-subsidized company-chartered cars/buses, ensuring workers who do not own individual vehicles safe and controlled movement back to cities (37). However, these transport policies were only one piece of the puzzle. Recognizing the broader issues of job insecurity, the government also implemented initiatives to stimulate job creation in the urban areas. Public works projects were ramped up, and companies were provided with incentives to hire, particularly focusing on the migrant worker demographic. Simultaneously, efforts were made to strengthen the social safety nets for these workers, ensuring that those who remained unemployed or chose not to return immediately to urban areas had a cushion to fall back on.

Procurement policies in China during the COVID-19 pandemic demonstrated a strategic response to the pressing challenges faced by various sectors, ranging from social services to manufacturing. In the early stages of the pandemic, the supply shortage and inadequate distribution of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) created significant barriers to work resumption (38, 39). To address this, China swiftly implemented policies to enhance the procurement and distribution of PPE, not only in healthcare but also in education, culture, and sports sectors. These measures allowed these sectors to reopen safely and efficiently. The transportation, mining, wholesale and retail, and manufacturing sectors faced unique challenges due to interregional logistic bans (40). Recognizing that cumbersome bureaucratic procedures were exacerbating these issues, governments implemented targeted procurement policies that streamlined the transportation of raw materials and goods. These policies ensured that supply chains remained robust and adaptive despite the unprecedented disruptions, aiding in the work resumption of these critical sectors. Further, procurement policies were extended to foster local production of vital equipment and encourage innovative solutions within the supply chain. By focusing on local capabilities, China was able to mitigate the risks of global supply chain disruptions.

Furthermore, our analyses underscore an intriguing dynamic: while simultaneously implementing procurement, employment, or fiscal policies might lead to suboptimal work resumption rates, many configurations for high work resumption favor the absence of certain policies, emphasizing just one specific type. At first glance, this might seem to clash with the established idea that integrating different policy instruments into policy mixes is more effective than a single policy in addressing multifaceted challenges (41, 42). The effectiveness of a singular policy type does not imply a simplistic approach. Within that chosen policy framework, integrating a variety of instruments can be the linchpin for its success. For instance, if a region were to prioritize employment policies, integrating strategies targeting both skilled workers and rural–urban migrant workers within that umbrella could be more impactful than thinly spreading efforts across employment, fiscal, and procurement policies. Furthermore, our week-by-week configurational analyses (Table 2) underscores a notable stepwise strategy. It reveals that the combined force of fiscal, employment, and procurement policies is especially potent in the initial phase of work resumption, laying a solid foundation for subsequent efforts. As recovery unfolds, policymakers might need to pivot, streamlining their approach to sustain momentum.

In contemplating future research directions, it is worth highlighting a few limitations inherent in our current study. First, different policies may have different lagged impacts on work resumption. For example, while SMEs might immediately benefit from tax reductions like payroll tax cuts, procurement policies typically show their effects weeks after implementation. Although our 8-wk dataset provides a robust analysis, it might not fully capture policy combinations with varied lag effects on work resumption. Second, our analysis simplifies the heterogeneity of policy instruments by grouping policies into three overarching types of policies. A deeper dive into the nuances and synergies within these categories is warranted. Third, our reliance on cellphone location data to gauge work resumption presents a potential blind spot: we are unable to capture those who worked from home. This might lead to underestimating resumption rates in sectors like education and business services. However, in the Chinese context, this limitation might not significantly skew results. Few roles—such as those in education and select business services—are amenable to remote work, and they represent a relatively small portion of the total workforce (14%). Moreover, most work resumption policies were not targeting remote workers. Future research could explore the broader shifts in work patterns post-COVID-19, including the rise of remote work. Fourth, our methodology primarily identifies individuals returning to their prior workplaces, potentially missing those who have switched jobs. While this may lead to an underestimation in specific sectors, our approach is still credible at the prefecture level. We first determine those returning to their pre-COVID-19 city of employment, followed by their workplace within that city. This process ensures accurate identification for workers returning to their original city. Fifth, we evaluate the interdependence of socioeconomic and administrative capacity, inclination for strict COVID-19 confinement, and three different types of policy instruments in our analyses rather than causality links between them. But given the stepwise pattern observed in our week-by-week analyses, subsequent studies could delve into the evolution of these policy combinations. Our study includes several simplifications and limitations, but nevertheless, they do not impair our conclusions.

Conclusion

Our in-depth comparative analysis underscores that alignment among ability, motivation, and triggers holds greater significance for work resumption than merely their concurrent presence. Delving into sector-specific insights, we discern that employment policies serve as the linchpin for elevated work resumption rates in the manufacturing and business services sectors, which house the majority of the workforce. Conversely, the wholesale, retail, hotel, and catering sectors benefit more from fiscal and procurement strategies. Analyzing the evolution over the WRP, it becomes evident that a holistic application of procurement, employment, and fiscal measures is primarily effective during the initial stages. As the WRP progresses, a more nuanced and sector-specific strategy emerges as pivotal for achieving optimal work resumption. Consequently, our findings advocate for prefectures, depending on their intrinsic capacities and motivations, to tailor their policy instruments to the nuances of their predominant sectors to expedite postcrisis rejuvenation. These findings provide a blueprint for other regions and nations, illustrating that the tailored interplay of socioeconomic capacities and policy instruments is crucial for postcrisis recovery. Recognizing and adapting to such sector-specific nuances can serve as a transformative strategy, not only for pandemic-related disruptions but for diverse socioeconomic challenges, ensuring robust and sustained recovery in varied global contexts.

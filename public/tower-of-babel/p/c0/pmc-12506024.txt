Laminin-gamma2 promotes oral squamous cell carcinoma growth by activating the p-ERK/c-Myc signaling pathway
Authors: Bo Qiao, Nenghao Jin, Min Zhao, Liang Zhu, Liangbo Li, Bin Gu, Ning Wen, Haizhong Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is one of the most common types of head and neck carcinoma. As limited therapies for OSCC are available, the examination of new targets could expand therapeutic options. Recent studies have found the abnormal expression of Laminin-gamma2 (LAMC2) in OSCC. However, the mechanism by which LAMC2 participates in the occurrence and development of OSCC has not been fully clarified.MethodsBioinformatics analysis was used to Identification of hub genes in oral tumor tissues of OSCC. Overall survival probability and cumulative incidence of LAMC2 were explored in OSCC patients in the GSE41613 dataset. Cell proliferation assays, transwell assays were conducted to observe the change in OSCC proliferation ability and invasion. Gain or loss of function experiments were performed to observe the effect of LAMC2 on the signaling pathway. And we examined the mRNA and protein levels of LAMC2 in human OSCC tissue to explore the biological significance of LAMC2. Furthermore, Tumor formation assay was used to observe knocking down LAMC2 on tumorigenesis in OSCC cells in vivo.ResultsWe identified LAMC2, a crucial oncoprotein, were upregulated in human OSCC samples in TCGA datasets compared to healthy controls. The upregulated LAMC2 was related to poorer survival rates, increased the risk of OSCC-specific mortality, and there were significant differences in survival between the LAMC2 high and low expression groups. We also validated that the mRNA and protein expression of LAMC2 were upregulated in human OSCC samples compared with the para-tumor tissues. LAMC2 overexpression significantly enhanced cell proliferation and invasion. Mechanistically, overexpression of LAMC2 could increase the expression levels of p-ERK and c-Myc, while knockdown of LAMC2 resulted in the opposite effect. Moreover, the ERK inhibitor decreased ERK phosphorylation and reversed the OSCC phenotype induced by LAMC2 overexpression.ConclusionsLAMC2 enhances the cell proliferation, invasion of OSCC cells via the p-ERK/c-Myc axis, suggesting the crucial role of LAMC2 in the progression of OSCC and small molecule inhibitors targeting LAMC2 might be an effective treatment for OSCC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-025-06911-5.Keywords: Laminin-gamma2 (LAMC2), Tumorigenesis, Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), Phosphorylated extracellular signal-regulated kinase (p-ERK), C-Myc

Background

Oral cancers accounted for over 300,000 new cases and more than 170,000 related deaths in 2018 according to the International Agency for Research on Cancer [1]. Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) develops from epithelial locating at oral cavity and represents over 90% of oral malignancies [2]. The prognosis of OSCC remains unfavorable, and the 5-year survival rate of patients with OSCC is below 60% because of the upregulated tendency of regional lymph node activation and locoregional recurrence [3]. The main treatments of OSCC consist of either curative surgery with or without adjuvant therapy or primary chemoradiotherapy [4]. Despite advancements in multidisciplinary therapeutic methods and diagnostic approaches in recent decades, the detailed mechanisms underlying the initiation and development of OSCC are still not fully understood, which lead to a great challenge for OSCC management [5, 6]. Therefore, it is urgent to identify the potential biomarkers and complex signaling pathways involved in OSCC tumorigenesis and metastasis.

Laminin-gamma2 (LAMC2) is an essential subunit of the heterotrimeric glycoprotein laminin-332 and plays an important role in the function and structure of epithelial basement membranes [7]. LAMC2 is reported to regulate cell motility and adhesion [8]. Additionally, emerging evidence has revealed that LAMC2 is a crucial oncoprotein [9]. Accumulating studies have indicated that LAMC2 is highly expressed in various types of human cancer and that the overexpression of LAMC2 is related to the development and progression of various tumors, including pancreatic cancer [10], colorectal carcinoma [11], lung cancer [12], anaplastic thyroid carcinoma [13], ovarian cancer [14], and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma [15]. Research has shown that a higher level of LAMC2 is related to enhanced tumor initiation and accumulation of stromal myofibroblasts in OSCC tissues [16], which suggests that LAMC2 is highly involved in the progression of OSCC. Although LAMC2 overexpression in HNSCC has been reported in 2002 [17], the specific biological role of LAMC2 and its underlying mechanism in OSCC remain elusive. There are very few researches revealing the correlation of LAMC2 and p-ERK and previous studies only demonstrated that LAMC2 is a downstream gene of MAPK/ERK signaling pathways [18]. Another study has reported that MEK/ERK pathway and LAMC2 are both positively involved in hypoxia-induced vasculogenic mimicry formation in hepatocellular carcinoma [19]. Totally, there is no research that directly shows the mutual adjustment relationship between LAMC2, Myc and ERK in the development of OSCC at present.

Therefore, in this study, by culturing OSCC cell lines and tumor formation assay, we attempted to confirm the molecular mechanism underlying LAMC2 impact the proliferation ability and invasion of OSCC: LAMC2 enhanced the p-ERK/c-Myc signaling pathway, ultimately promoting the tumorigenesis and progression of OSCC.

Materials and methods

Differential expression analyses

The datasets GSE13601 [20], GSE23558 [21], GSE30784 [22] and GSE31056 [23] were selected from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, and we used GEO2R (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/geo2r/) to identify the differentially expressed genes (DEGs) across experimental conditions between oral normal tissues and oral tumor tissues. The data was presented in a table with genes ordered by their significance. Genes were considered significant DEGs with P value < 0.05 and |log2FoldChange| >1. The volcano plot was used to visualize the overall distribution of DEGs. A Venn diagram was applied to display overlap of upregulated DEGs in the four datasets.

The gene expression files of tumor tissues and adjacent normal oral samples of 29 patients with OSCC were from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database. The heatmap was generated by the “pheatmap” package (version 1.0.12) in R software, which showed the fragments per kilobase per million mapped reads (FPKM) values of upregulated genes in these patients.

Hub gene selection

The “Boruta” package (version 6.0.0) [24] and “random forest” package (version 4.6–14) [25] in R version 4.0.1 were used to identify hub genes related to tumorigenesis in the TCGA dataset. “Boruta” calculated the importance of each gene and predicted whether the characteristic attributes were confirmed or rejected. Furthermore, “random forest” was used to perform classification analysis on the type and FPKM values for each sample, and the number of model trees was set to 1000. The percent contribution of each gene accuracy and Gini values were obtained. Area under the curve (AUC) was calculated by the “forestplot” package (version 1.9) and its 95% confidence interval (95% CI) based on FPKM values of final screened genes in the two groups. The “pROC” package (version 1.16.2) was used to generate receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.

Survival analysis

Survival analysis based on LAMC2 expression, survival status and survival time of 97 cases in the GSE41613 [26] dataset. The time between the diagnosis or beginning treatment and the death was regarded at overall survival (OS). Death of each participant was regarded as an “endpoint event” for the patient. The endpoint of the study was the end of the follow-up statistics at the 80th month. The “survival” package (version 3.2-3) was used to create survival curves based on the Kaplan-Meier method accounting for the 95% CI. The “survminer” package (version 0.4.7) was used to plot the survival curve and cumulative risk curve.

Clinical samples

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Chinese PLA General Hospital. A total of 36 patients with OSCC in the Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, Chinese PLA General Hospital from January 2020 to June 2020 who fulfilled the following criteria were included: no history of preoperative radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or any other anticancer therapy; underwent definitive surgery with a clear postoperative pathological diagnosis of squamous cell carcinoma (Table S1). OSCC tumor tissues and the para-tumor normal tissues (approximately 15 mm from the tumor boundary) acquired from 36 patients were used for RNA extraction, western blot analysis and immunohistochemical staining. And this study was also performed under the instruction of Helsinki Declaration. All participants in this research have signed the informed consent forms.

Cell culture

The human tongue squamous cell carcinoma cell lines HSC3, SCC9, Cal27, and SCC15 which are widely used in OSCC research, were bought from the Chinese Academy of Sciences Cell Bank. Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (Gibco, Melbourne, Australia) with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, Melbourne, Australia) and 100U/ml penicillin (Gibco, Melbourne, Australia) were used to culture the cells in an incubator with 95% humidity and 5% carbon dioxide at 37℃.

RNA Preparation and real-time PCR

The normal and tumor tissues were thoroughly ground and mixed with 0.5 mL Trizol (Invitrogen, USA) in a centrifuge tube and 2 µg of RNA of each sample was used to generate cDNA through reverse transcription (Tiangen, Beijing, China). The mRNA level of LAMC2 was detected by a Thermo Scientific detection system (PIKOREAL96) using a 2×SYBR Green kit (Roche Applied Science) and calculated with the 2−ΔΔCT method [27]. GAPDH was used as an endogenous control. The sequences of primer are detailed in Table S2, S3.

Cell transfection

The full-length cDNA of LAMC2 was inserted into the pcDNA3.1-Myc plasmid (Addgene, USA). ShRNA specifically targeting LAMC2 was constructed using Qiagen software (Valencia, CA, USA) and inserted into the pLKO.1-TRC vector to produce lentivirus in 293 T cells. HSC3 and SCC9 cells were infected with pcDNA3.1‐Myc lentivirus, and pLKO.1-shRNA lentivirus was used to infect Cal27 and SCC15 cells. Neomycin or puromycin (2 µg/ml) were used to select positive cells for 4 consecutive days.

Western blot analysis

Cells and tissues were thoroughly grounded and mixed with 200 µL RIPA buffer. 200 µL of 2xloading buffer was added to the cells. The solution was placed in a metal bath at 100 degrees Celsius to denature the protein. A 10% SDS-PAGE was utilized for the protein separation in an electrophoresis apparatus with a constant voltage of 140 V for 120 min. The protein on SDS-PAGE was then transferred to a polyvinylidene difluoride membrane (Real-Times Biotechnology Co.,Ltd, Beijing, China) in a transfer tank with a constant current of 300 A.The membrane was co-incubated with anti-ERK1/2, anti-pERK1/2, anti-LAMC2, anti-c-Myc, and anti-GAPDH (1:1000, Cell Signaling Technology, Beijing, China) at 37℃ for 1 h. Then, the goat anti‑rabbit secondary antibody (1:10000, Cell Signaling Technology, Beijing, China) was used for blot imaging.

Immunohistochemical staining

5-µm paraffin sections were immersed in xylene for 10 min to deparaffinize. The sections were immersed in absolute ethanol (5 min), 95% alcohol (5 min), 80% alcohol (5 min), and 70% alcohol (5 min). Then deionized water was used to rinse the sections for 2 min. 0.01 M citrate buffer (pH 6.0) was used to repair tissue sections at 100 °C for 15 min. The slides were washed with PBS (Thermo Fisher Labsystems, Waltham, USA, pH 7.4) 3 times, 3 min each time. 5% normal serum was used to block the sections at 37 °C for 30 min. anti-LAMC2 and anti-p-ERK1/2 (1:200, Abcam, Shanghai, China) were used to incubate the slices overnight in a humid chamber at 4° C. PBS was used to rinse the Sect. 3 times, 3 min each time. A secondary antibody (1:500, Abcam, Shanghai, China) labeled with horseradish peroxidase was used to incubate the sections at 37 °C for 30 min. Freshly prepared DAB chromogen (Thermo Fisher Labsystems, Waltham, USA) was used to stain the sections.

Cell proliferation analysis

The proliferation ability of OSCC cell lines was investigated by the MTT and crystal violet assay. Briefly, 1000 cells were seeded into 6-well plates. Cells were washed by PBS buffer and stained with crystal violet solution after 10 days of culture. Ten minutes later, the cells were washed by PBS buffer 5 times. A microplate reader was used to read the optical density (OD) value at 600 nm.

1000/well cells were planted into coning flat bottom 96-well plates. MTT solution was added into the medium and the plate was placed at 37 °C for 4 h. Thereafter, the medium was removed, and the formazan was dissolved in 200 µL of DMSO. An automatic microplate reader was used to evaluate the absorbance at 490 nm.

Transwell assays

The day before the experiment, a tube of Matrigel that has been dispensed was placed at 4 °C for 15 h from − 20 °C, and the Matrigel glue melted from a solid state to a liquid state. Matrigel was diluted 1:8 and coated on the upper chamber surface of the bottom membrane of the transwell chamber for 3 h. The remaining liquid in the culture plate was removed, and 50µL of 10 g/LBSA serum-free culture medium was added to each well. The cells were then washed 1–2 times with PBS to remove the influence of serum. The cell density was 5*105 cells/ml. 200µL of cell suspension was added into the upper chamber of the Transwell chamber, and 600µL of 10% FBS medium was added to the lower chamber. The culture plate was placed in a CO2 incubator at 37 °C, and cultured for 12–48 h according to tumor cell invasion ability.

Tumor formation assay

Adult male BALB/c male nude mice (aged approximately 4 weeks) weighing between 18 and 22 g were purchased from Vital River Biotech Ltd., corporation (Beijing, China) (A total of 8 mice: 4 mice in SCR group, 4 mice in shLAMC2 group); the animal were allowed to adapt for 1 week before the experiments. LAMC2 knockdown Cal27 (Cal27/shLAMC2) cells and the relative control (Cal27/SCR) cells were trypsinized and resuspended in normal saline after counting. 2 × 106 of Cal27/SCR or Cal27/shLAMC2 cells were injected underarm [28, 29]. After the needle is slowly pulled out and shot, a noticeable bulge can be seen at the injection site. On the 35th day after the cell injection, the nude mice were sacrificed, the tumors were removed, weight-bearing and photographed. Animal Ethical Committee in the Ethics Committee for the Use of Animals of PLA General Hospital has approved all these experiments.

Data analysis

The categorized variables were shown as frequency or percentage. And mean and standard deviation were utilized to demonstrated the continuous variables. Student’s test and one-way ANOVA test were acquired respectively for the statistical analysis in this research. Post hoc pairwise comparisons were performed using [Tukey’ Honestly Significant Difference] to account for multiple comparisons following significant one-way ANOVA results. Mean ± SD or SEM was used to represent the data in our figures. P < 0.05 and P < 0.01 were considered as the significant difference. GraphPad Prism 10.0 was used for the plotting and analysis of the data in this research.

Results

Upregulated mRNAs in oral tumor tissues of OSCC

The DEGs of oral tumor tissues and normal control tissues were analyzed through four GEO datasets. Specifically, GSE13601 (n = 12 tumor vs. 12 normal), GSE23558 (n = 24 tumor vs. 24 normal), GSE30784 (n = 48 tumor vs. 24 normal), and GSE31056 (n = 30 tumor vs. 30 normal) were used to identify 840, 1671, 1907, and 935 upregulated genes, respectively (Fig. 1A-D). The Venn diagram showed the 126 overlapping upregulated genes shared between the tumor tissues of the above mentioned four datasets (Fig. 1E). These 126 overexpressed genes were also confirmed in cancer tissues and matching adjacent tissues in 29 pairs of OSCC samples from the TCGA dataset (Fig. 1F).

Identification of hub genes in oral tumor tissues of OSCC

To explore the hub genes of the significantly upregulated mRNAs associated with OSCC, 66 hub genes were identified with the Boruta algorithm and random forest analysis on TCGA data (n = 29 tumor vs. 29 normal) (Fig. 2A). The top 10 genes ranked by importance score of the random forest algorithm included MYBL2, LAMC2, and TK1 (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, we analyzed the associations of the expression levels of 8 shared hub genes between the tissues of two groups of TCGA OSCC patients in mean decrease accuracy and mean decrease Gini. Figure 2C shows that the average AUC of ROC curves was > 0.94 (95% CI: 0.907–0.999, P < 0.01). The 8 hub genes were MYBL2, LAMC2, TK1, MICAL2, FSCN1, FOXM1, ELF4 and TPX2. The GO terms of the top 8 hub genes are detailed in Table S4. Previous studies have demonstrated the correlation between LAMC2 and HNSCC [30], but the specific biological role of LAMC2 and its underlying mechanism in OSCC remain elusive. Therefore, we finally selected LAMC2 as our gene of interest. ROC curves of LAMC2 showed coherent sensitivity, and the specificity of the average AUC was 0.964 (95% CI: 0.907–0.999, Fig. 2D).

To demonstrate the prognostic potential of LAMC2 for OSCC patients, overall survival probability and cumulative incidence analyses were performed in the GSE41613 dataset. Figure 2E shows that upregulated LAMC2 was related to poorer survival rates, and there were significant differences in survival between the LAMC2 high and low expression groups. Figure 2F shows that upregulation of LAMC2 also increased the risk of OSCC-specific mortality.

LAMC2 was upregulated in OSCC tissues

To investigate the role of LAMC2 in the development of OSCC, its expression profiles in OSCC tissues were assessed. First, we examined LAMC2 expression in 30 pairs of OSCC tissues and paired normal control tissues at the mRNA level, and we found that the mRNA expression level of LAMC2 was obviously elevated in 22 of 30 (73.3%) OSCC tissues compared to the paired normal control tissues (Fig. 3A). Western blot confirmed LAMC2 protein upregulation in OSCC (n = 6 biological replicates, Fig. 3B). Among cell lines (HSC3, SCC9, Cal27, SCC15), LAMC2 was highest in Cal27 and SCC15 at both mRNA (n = 3, one-way ANOVA, P < 0.001, Fig. 3C) and protein levels (n = 3, Fig. 3D). Immunohistochemistry showed stronger LAMC2 staining in OSCC vs. normal tissues (n = 10, Fig. 3E).

LAMC2 overexpression promoted the proliferation and invasion of OSCC cell lines

To identify the function of LAMC2 in the proliferation of OSCC cells, we transfected an empty p23 vector or LAMC2 overexpression vector into HSC3 and SCC9 OSCC cell lines, respectively (Fig. 4A, B). The absorbance values of the HSC3 and SCC9 cells post LAMC2 overexpression at 4th, 5th and 6th day were markedly upregulated compared to the p23 control cells (P < 0.01) (Fig. 4C, D; n = 3, 6 technical replicates per group, two-way ANOVA, P < 0.01). Moreover, crystal violet assays showed that LAMC2 overexpression promoted cell proliferation capacity of HSC3 and SCC9 cells. The absorbance values of the HSC3 and SCC9 cells post LAMC2 transfection were dramatically increased compared with the p23 control cells (P < 0.001) (Fig. 4E, F; n = 3, one-way ANOVA, P < 0.001). In addition, the Transwell assay suggested that upregulation of LAMC2 enhanced the invasion ability of HSC3 cells (Fig. 4G, H; n = 3, t-test, P < 0.01). Totally, these figures explained that upregulation of LAMC2 promoted the proliferation ability, viability, and invasion ability of OSCC cell lines.

LAMC2 knockdown inhibited the proliferation ability and invasion of OSCC cell lines

We used specific shRNA targeting LAMC2 in Cal27 and SCC15 cell lines (Fig. 5A, B). A LAMC2 knockdown lentivirus (shRNA-LAMC2) and a non-targeting control lentivirus (SCR) were transfected into Cal27 and SCC15 cells to establish LAMC2 knockdown cell lines. Similarly, we tested growth capacity of the SCR and shRNA-LAMC2 cell lines. The MTT assays showed that the absorbance values of the Cal27 and SCC15 cells at 5 and 6 days after LAMC2 knockdown were dramatically lower compared to the SCR control cells (P < 0.01) (Fig. 5C, D; n = 3, two-way ANOVA, P < 0.01). Furthermore, crystal violet assays showed that the absorbance values of Cal27 and SCC15 cells after LAMC2 knockdown decreased dramatically compared with the SCR control cells (Fig. 5E, F; n = 3, one-way ANOVA, P < 0.01). Moreover, LAMC2-knockdown had no significant impact on the expression of apoptosis markers in HSC3, SCC9, Cal27 and SCC15 cell lines (Fig. S1A, B), suggesting that LAMC2 could not regulate the apoptosis of OSCC cell lines. What’s more, the transwell assay demonstrated that knockdown of LAMC2 notably suppressed the invasion abilities of Cal27 cells (Fig. 5G, H; t-test, P < 0.01). To further investigate the impact of LAMC2 deficiency on the invasion abilities of OSCC cells in orthotopic mouse model, control and LAMC2 knockdown luci-Cal27 OSCC cells (1 × 105 cells/mouse) were injected into tail vein of each nude mouse (n = 6 for each group). As shown in Fig. S2A, luciferase signals from the lungs of LAMC2 knockdown group were significantly lower than control group at 4 weeks after injection. Moreover, as shown in Fig. S2B, quantification of luciferase expression of lung metastasis also decreased significantly in LAMC2-knockdown group. Hence, all the above results indicated that the downregulation of LAMC2 could weaken the proliferation ability, viability and invasion abilities of OSCC cell lines.

LAMC2 activated p-ERK/c-Myc signaling pathway in OSCC cells

Accumulating evidence has demonstrated the close correlation between the ERK signaling activation and the incidence of OSCC [31]. To understand the mechanism of LAMC2 involvement, western-blot assay was conducted to investigate the expression levels of phosphorylated ERK (p-ERK) and c-Myc protein in LAMC2 overexpression and knockdown cell lines. In LAMC2-overexpressing HSC3 and SCC9 cells, the total ERK expression level showed no statistical difference compared to that in the p23 control group, while there was a marked increase in the p-ERK and c-Myc expression levels compared with that in the p23 control group (Fig. 6A, B). Conversely, LAMC2 knockdown reduced the p-ERK and c-Myc expression levels compared with the SCR group in Cal27 and SCC15 cells (Fig. 6C, D).

E PD98059 inhibited ERK phosphorylation in HSC3 cells after LAMC2 overexpression according to western blot analysis. To examine whether LAMC2 could enhance the progression of OSCC by ERK signaling, PD98059, a potent ERK inhibitor, was incubated with LAMC2-overexpressing HSC3 cells to confirm whether ERK pathway activation could be blocked to rescue the phenotype. Western blot analysis confirmed that PD98059 inhibited ERK phosphorylation in LAMC2-overexpressing HSC3 cells (Fig. 6E). Furthermore, as shown in Fig. 6F-H, LAMC2-enhanced cell proliferation, viability, invasion ability could be rescued by PD98059, suggesting the ERK-dependent LAMC2 functions.

Knocking down LAMC2 suppressed tumorigenesis in OSCC cells in vivo

To demonstrate the role of LAMC2 in the tumorigenesis of OSCC, LAMC2 knockdown Cal27 cells and the relative control cells were injected subcutaneously into 5-weeks-old nude mice (Fig. 7A). The tumor volume and weight of the shRNA-LAMC2 group were smaller and lighter than those of the SCR group (Fig. 7B, C). Western blot analysis confirmed that p-ERK1/2 in nude mouse tumors was expressed at low levels in the shRNA-LAMC2 group compared to the SCR group (Fig. 7D). Immunohistochemical analysis of nude mouse tumors for the detection of p-ERK1/2 protein revealed that p-ERK1/2 was expressed at low levels in the shRNA-LAMC2 group compared to the SCR group (Fig. 7E). Taken together, these results indicated that knocking down LAMC2 suppressed the tumor growth of OSCC cells in vivo, which was consistent with the inhibited growth of OSCC cells by knocking down LAMC2 in vitro.

Discussion

In this study, we focused on the oncogenes participating in the regulatory networks of the progression of OSCC. Briefly, we identified 126 oncogenes that specifically upregulated in OSCC tissues from four distinct GEO datasets. And eight of them have been identified as potential diagnosis markers of OSCC. Moreover, we revealed LAMC2, one of the eight genes, played a crucial role in the initiation and development of OSCC. We demonstrated the upregulation of LAMC2 promoted cell proliferation, and invasion of OSCC both in vivo and in vitro. And LAMC2 played its role by activating the p-ERK/c-Myc signaling pathway. Based on the novel and detailed mechanism we revealed in this research, small molecular inhibitors targeting LAMC2 could be used as a new therapeutic for OSCC patients.

Laminins are key proteins located in the basal layer. They construct a protein network that affects both normal cells and the differentiation, migration and adhesion of transformed cells [32]. As a crucial autocrine factor produced by multiple cancers, laminins can not only participate in the mechanical barrier but also promote tumorigenesis [33, 34]. LAMC2 (the γ2 chain of Laminin-332) is the only monomeric chain that is secreted. Accumulating evidence has indicated that LAMC2 is highly involved in the progression of multiple human cancers. The upregulation of LAMC2 has also been proved to be significantly related to poor clinical prognosis in colorectal cancer and penile squamous cell carcinoma patients [35, 36]. Moon et al. demonstrated that LAMC2 upregulation promotes the metastasis of lung cancer cells and is highly associated with an increased risk of tumor recurrence [12]. Zhang et al. revealed that LAMC2 overexpression can significantly promote progression, inhibit apoptosis of ovarian cancer cells, and upregulate the expression levels of p38, p-p38, and c-Myc [33]. Accumulating evidence shows that the signaling network mediated by LAMC2 plays a significant role in the proliferation, migration and invasion of multiple types of cancer, suggesting that it might be an attractive therapeutic target for anticancer therapy by inhibiting tumorigenesis. Elevated serum levels of LAMC2 in patients may be an attractive biomarker for serological diagnosis [37].

In this study, we revealed that LAMC2 promotes OSCC growth by activating the p-ERK/c-Myc signaling pathway. The extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) family consists of ERK 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6, which belong to the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) family and regulate cellular signaling under both normal and pathological conditions [38]. ERK1/2 are two critical members of the MAPK/ERK pathway. The hyperactivation of ERK plays a major role in the occurrence and development of tumors [39]. The MAPK signaling pathway plays an essential role in tumor progression, invasion, and metastasis [40]. Research has revealed a causal relationship between ERK-dependent translation factor activation and LAMC2 dysregulation and has identified new markers of preinvasive tumor changes during progression to squamous cell carcinoma [18].

In this research, we demonstrated that upregulation of LAMC2 in OSCC cell lines upregulated the phosphorylation levels of ERK (p-ERK). On the other hand, p-ERK can bind to transcription factors such as c-Myc to regulate the cell fate and promote tumor formation [41]. However, the causal relationship between LAMC2 and c-Myc activation remains incompletely defined. C-Myc is a transforming oncogene that is usually upregulated in various human cancers. Previous studies have demonstrated that LAMC2 is a down-stream gene of c-Myc and can be upregulated by abnormally overexpressed c-Myc in prostate cancer [33]. And the amplification frequencies of c-Myc and LAMC2 are coordinate increased in multiple carcinomas including the hormone-resistant prostate cancer and C virus-related hepatocellular carcinomas [42, 43]. In addition, PD98059, a potent ERK inhibitor, inhibited ERK phosphorylation in LAMC2-overexpressing HSC3 cells in our study. These results suggested that LAMC2 may regulate the proliferation, migration, and invasion of OSCC cells via the ERK/c-MYC signaling pathway. In subsequent studies, we will conduct dual-luciferase assays and rescue experiments to further confirm that p-ERK/c-Myc are downstream transcriptional targets of LAMC.

Although previous studies have reported LAMC2 overexpression in head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) and oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) [34, 44], emerging evidence further underscores its critical role in promoting tumor cell invasiveness and distant metastasis. Elevated LAMC2 expression has been closely linked to tumor progression, recurrence, and poor patient prognosis. However, research on LAMC2’s involvement in OSCC remains in its early stages, and the precise molecular mechanisms through which LAMC2 modulates OSCC biological behavior have yet to be fully elucidated. In this research, we firstly reported that upregulation of LAMC2 enhanced the p-ERK/c-Myc signaling pathway, and thus promoted the proliferation ability of OSCC cells. We demonstrated the upregulated LAMC2 in OSCC cells enhanced the upregulation of c-Myc and phosphorylation of ERK. We believe that our discovery demonstrates the crucial role of LAMC2 in the regulatory network of the tumorigenesis and progression of OSCC. In addition, regarding the effects of clinical and or microscopic aspects as tumor stage, smoking, inflammation, etc. on LAMC2, due to the small number of clinical samples included in this study, no statistically significant results were obtained. In the subsequent research, we will further expand the sample size and conduct a further analysis.

Conclusions

We investigated the regulatory role of LAMC2 in the proliferation and invasion abilities of OSCC cell lines, suggesting that LAMC2 would be a potential therapeutic target for OSCC. The crucial function of LAMC2 in the progression, invasion processes of OSCC indicates that small molecule inhibitors targeting LAMC2 are a promising treatment for OSCC patients.

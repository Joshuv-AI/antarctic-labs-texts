Assessing the spatiotemporal shifting of the Arpa river using remote sensing, GIS, and ARIMA for river morphology prediction
Authors: Prasoon Soni, Rahul Kumar Patel, Khirsagar Patel, Pushpraj Singh, Satyendra Singh Thakur, Sandeep Kaushik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRiver channel migration is a dynamic geomorphological process influenced by hydrological, geographic, and anthropogenic factors. Understanding river shifting patterns is important for sustainable catchment management. This study investigates the spatial and temporal changes of the Arpa River channel over 49 years (1972–2021) using remote sensing and GIS techniques. Landsat images and Data Elevation Models (DEM) were used to measure river morphology and channel migration. The study quantified both spatial and temporal variations by integrating cross-sectional analysis of sinuosity ratio estimation and river channel shifting rate calculations. The Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model also predicts future river migration trends. The results show that the Arpa River has undergone significant morphological changes, a reduction in channel width, and lateral migration due to hydrological variations, sediment transport, and anthropogenic pressure. Its findings highlight changes to the river due to urbanization, infrastructure development, and deforestation. Integrating remote sensing and GIS with time-series modeling enhances the predictive capabilities of river migration studies and provides valuable information to policymakers and environmental planners. This study underlines the need for sustainable landscape strategies and sediment management policies to mitigate the adverse impacts of riverbank erosion and shifting.Keywords: River channel migration, Remote Sensing, GIS, ARIMA model, Arpa River, Spatiotemporal changesSubject terms: Climate sciences, Environmental sciences, Hydrology, Natural hazards

Introduction

River dynamics are systems that undergo continuous morphological changes induced by natural and anthropogenic impacts1. River channel shifting is a destructive phenomenon that is influenced by hydrological, ecological, geomorphological, and human-induced factors2. Analyzing river shifting patterns is important to enable land use planning, flood risk management, and sustainable water resources management2. Channel migration and river dynamics have become a major focus of river studies3. Hydrological changes, sediment transport, tectonic activities, and human-induced changes such as dam construction, urbanization, and agricultural practices are the major or major causes of river shifting4. Alterations in river flow harm human settlements, man-made infrastructure, groundwater recharge, and agricultural land5. Major river systems like the Brahmaputra, Ganga, and Mekong have been extensively studied to understand their spatiotemporal change patterns6. Riverbank erosion and migration in India cause loss of land and displacement of communities. Therefore, assessing spatiotemporal changes in rivers is very important7. River shifting studies are very important for the sustainable management of catchment areas and flood control8.

Remote sensing and GIS techniques have revolutionized river-shifting studies with the help of detecting changes using temporal satellite data and spatial analysis tools9. Advanced modeling techniques, such as Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA), can be integrated with remote sensing and GIS to forecast river migration trends10. Supervised classification using indices such as the Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI) from image processing techniques of satellite data is used to extract water bodies and delineate river boundaries11. Remote sensing and GIS techniques have made significant contributions to hydrological modeling and stream network modeling, which can identify areas of change, erosion, and deposition of river channels12. Sensors such as radar and LiDAR increase the accuracy of the identification of river channel migration13. In cloudy and monsoon-affected areas, images taken by Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) play a vital role in monitoring changes in the river14. GIS-based spatiotemporal analysis using statistical models provides a better understanding of predicting future trends of river flow15.

Time-series forecasting models, such as ARIMA models, have made significant contributions to hydrological studies, as future river flow trends can be predicted using these models based on historical data16. ARIMA models consist of autoregressive (AR), differencing (I), and moving average (MA) components that are used to analyze the prediction of temporal changes in river channels17. Using ARIMA models, researchers can predict future trends of river migration using historical data18. There are many studies in which ARIMA models have been used to forecast rivers’ future dynamics, including hydrological and flood forecasting modeling19. Integrating remote sensing and GIS with ARIMA models can improve and simplify the decision-making process for river management20.

This study presents a novel approach by integrating the ARIMA model, remote sensing, and GIS for spatio-temporal change assessment of the Arpa River. While previous studies have used only satellite images and GIS to analyze river morphology changes, this research integrates remote sensing and GIS with the ARIMA model to understand future river migration trends. The study also presented a cross-sectional analysis of the river to calculate the channel shifting rate of the river and estimate the sinuosity ratio. The primary objective of this research is to analyze the spatial and temporal dynamics of the Arpa River over 49 years (1972–2021) with the help of remote sensing and GIS.

Methodology

Study area

The navigable waterway in the Bilaspur district is the Arpa River. The Arpa River plays an important role in the environmental conditions of the Arpa Basin. The Arpa River system is centered between latitudes 81°40’E and 82°30’E and 22°40’N and 22°5’N (Fig. 1). A digital elevation model was used to investigate the 3278 km2 Arpa Basin structure. The Arpa River is a course formed by the combination of all the streams of the Arpa River Basin. According to remote sensing images and professional verification (based on DGPS), the Digital Elevation Model (DEM) was used to analyze the morphological features, which revealed that 226 m belonged to downstream areas, while 1140 m belonged to upstream areas. This river is approximately 128 km long. If we look at this river from the point of view of hydrology, it is a seasonal river. In the monsoon season, the river is in spate due to heavy rainfall. If we look at this river (Fig. 1). in the dry season, the water level of the river decreases considerably, due to which many parts of this river are no longer perennial. The river’s flow dynamics are characterized by helical, fractured, and laminar flow structures, influenced by the regional geology and channel morphology. The basin comprises a diverse landform, including dissected plateaus, low-lying floodplains, and alluvial plains, which support agriculture and settlements along the river banks. Anthropogenic activities, including urbanization and water retention structures, affect or alter hydrological regimes and sediment transport. Given its socio-economic and ecological importance, studying the Arpa River shifting is essential.

Several studies have been conducted globally, in which morphological changes in rivers have been observed and evaluated using remote sensing and GIS techniques21,22. These tools are very effective for analyzing the spatial dynamics of river channels because satellite images taken at different times can be used to monitor the morphological adjustments of the river. Channel changes facilitated by remote sensing and GIS have been evaluated in detail in the context of the Arpa River. The subtropical and semi-arid climate of Bilaspur is suitable for river-based investigations, especially during the winter season when atmospheric clarity is high, making observation of satellite images easier. This study is based on satellite images to assess the temporal changes in the surface morphology of river channels over selected periods. Satellite images from these months have been utilized to analyze morphological changes in the river system23,24.To effectively analyze the surface morphology of the river channel, advanced filtering techniques of satellite imagery are used, and physical testing as well as digitization, which is a part of spatial analysis, play a vital role in accurately delineating the river channel. ArcGIS is an integral tool of Geographic Information Systems used for spatial analysis25–27.

In this study, both ArcGIS and HEC-RAS have been used to analyze Landsat satellite imagery, DEM processing, river bank delineation, cross-section profiling, and channel shifting of the Arpa River. A total of 19 cross-section points were created in the map, and the morphological changes at different time intervals were evaluated. Vector data was extracted from the river channel to analyze social temporal changes along cross-section lines over the selected 6-year period. A cross-section perpendicular to the river centreline was established in satellite images from selected years, and a systematic and consistent approach was used to assess lateral channel migration and morphological change28–30.

Time-series prediction of river channel shift using an ARIMA Model

Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) is a highly precise time series forecasting technique widely used in this field. Grid-based techniques enhance the ARIMA obtained from the optimal selection of the p, d, and q parameters, making this approach more effective for measuring various temporal evolution trends. This is extremely useful in identifying upward and downward trends of data, which helps in comprehensively examining the patterns and also provides deeper insights into the implications of future projections. Result and discussion

Spatiotemporal changes in the Arpa River Channel (1972–2021)

The Arpa River experienced significant morphological changes between 1972 and 2021 due to a combination of surface morphology dynamics, natural processes such as erosion, and anthropogenic activities such as uncontrolled agricultural practices, urban expansion, and water quality degradation. This study investigates the spatiotemporal evolution of the Arpa River from 19 cross-sections using satellite images from 6 different years50–52. A considerable decrease in channel width has been observed over time, which highlights the severe morphological change. The maximum channel width was recorded in 1972 in X3, which reached 780 m in the Bilaspur urban area. In contrast, the lowest width in 2021 is observed at cross-section X8, at 89 m. Notably, the average width of the river has decreased from 414 m in 1972 to just 282 m in 2021, with a pronounced decrease observed between cross-sections X19 and X1. This degradation is due to anthropogenic activities such as dam construction, urbanization, and infrastructure development. Lateral shifting of the river centreline

The lateral displacement of the center line of the Arpa River has been determined by taking one year’s data as the base and the difference in the channel position of different years as the reference45. The highest shift was observed in cross-section X3, with a displacement of 219.54 m upstream between 2001 and 2013. The lowest shift recorded was 12.8 m upwards between 2017 and 2021. A leftward shift of -13.19 m was observed in X3 between 1993 and 2001 (Fig. 4). The greatest rightward displacement was observed at 293.66 m in cross-section X5 between 1993 and 2001. The maximum shift rate observed from 2017 to 2021 was − 60.62 m per year. The most significant lateral displacement across the channel occurred between 1972 and 1993, causing the channel to shift to the left by approximately − 843.95 m (Table 3). This shift reflects riverbank erosion, sediment deposition, and changes in hydrodynamic conditions over time.

ARIMA-based prediction of future shifting trends

The ARIMA model is used to predict the lateral change rate of the river, which gives information about future trends. A combination of moving averages and spatiotemporal analysis has been used to evaluate autoregressive integration models across 19 cross-sections. The projected lateral shifts for 2025, 2030, and 2035 are presented in Table 3. Model performance was evaluated using root mean square error (RMSE) and Bayesian information criterion (BIC). The lowest RMSE (1.780) and BIC (2.118) values were recorded at cross-section X9, indicating a high degree of model accuracy. Erosion and deposition processes are found to occur simultaneously with left and right shifts at different time intervals. The projected change shows that between 2021 and 2025, the river will shift predominantly to the right, affecting 10 cross-sections. Between 2025 and 2030, the river is projected to shift left in 8 cross sections, while the right is expected to shift in 11 cross sections. Accuracy assessment for digitized and classified layers

The positional accuracy of the digitized river centerlines and riverbank boundaries was determined by comparing the features extracted from the digitized files against reference datasets (high-resolution orthophotographs and ground control points) and utilising the Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) of the extracted dog to quantify how far the extracted features were from the extents (reference datasets). Digitized features were also visually checked for topological accuracy and continuity. Only layers that met/exceeded the accuracy standards (Overall Accuracy ≥ 85% and RMSE within pixel/sensor resolution) were retained for further spatial analysis.

Field evidence supporting sediment deposition and channel modification

Photographs taken from various areas on /alongside the Arpa River are used to identify geomorphological transformations. The locations of these photos are around Kekradih (Lat 22.397149°N, Long 82.032097°E), Pasid (Lat 21.884004°N, Long 82.197818°E), and Bilaspur (Lat 21.927427°N, Long 82.272738°E). Geographic Coordinates for these photos are in the form of longitude and latitude. The photographs (Fig. 6) show the build-up of a significant amount of sediment, the appearance of sandbars, and the lateral deposition of sediments along the river’s banks. The NDVI satellite imagery also indicates that there is a decrease in the amount of water actively flowing and a transformation of the land developed as a result of the geomorphic process. The evidence supporting the geomorphic processes that took place to create these depositional features, in addition to the changes in morphology near the bank areas, indicates that the geomorphic changes in the Arpa River corridor, as identified through remote sensing monitoring, are also consistent with decreasing flow rates and increasing sedimentation from the past several years. Evidence from photos also shows evidence of disturbance on the land surface (e.g., vehicular disturbance; disturbed soil) from human-related activities such as sand/mineral gaining and channel modification in the Arpa River corridor. The geographic coordinates of the photos provide a valuable reference point and support the interpretation of the satellite-based findings by providing evidence of sediment accumulation as well as ongoing modification to the river channel.

Conclusion

This study uses remote sensing (RS) and geographic information system (GIS) techniques to investigate the lateral migration of the Arpa River flowing through the Arpa River basin from 1972 to 2035. River channels play an important role in shaping land use/land cover (LULC) that affects rural development as well as changing environmental conditions due to changes in natural and anthropogenic solutes such as erosion and sedimentation. The Arpa River undergoes significant lateral migration, the primary drivers of which may be deforestation, unregulated anthropogenic activities, agricultural expansion, and industrialization. The findings of this study provide fundamental information about river channel behavior and provide a baseline framework for future research on river dynamics in this region. Using remote sensing and GIS-based spatio-temporal analysis, this study emphasizes the role of nature and human intervention on natural hydrological processes in the area. Despite its significant contributions, this study has some limitations. The accuracy and reliability of channel migration assessment depend on the availability of high-resolution satellite imagery with minimal cloud cover. Continuous multi-temporal data acquisition has always been challenging; even cloud-free satellite images are not continuously available throughout the year. To minimize this limitation, October, November, and December were chosen for the analysis as these months provide optimum conditions for remote sensing in the region. Despite all these constraints, this study will have implications for river basin management, environmental planning, and policy-making. Observations of spatio-temporal changes in the Arpa River basin suggest that sustainable watershed management strategies are needed along the Arpa River to mitigate the adverse impacts of anthropogenic interventions. The results of this study may prove to be a valuable resource for administrators and government agencies. This study provides a snapshot of the displacement of the Arpa River over the past five decades and also predicts the displacement of the river in the future, which adds an important contribution to the growing body of knowledge on river channel movement.

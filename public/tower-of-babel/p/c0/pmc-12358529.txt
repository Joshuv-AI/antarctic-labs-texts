Comparing the effect of pre-anesthesia clonidine and tranexamic acid on intraoperative bleeding volume in rhinoplasty: a machine learning approach
Authors: Zahra Asghari Varzaneh, Akram Hemmatipour, Hadi Kazemi-Arpanahi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractEffectively managing intraoperative blood loss during surgery is crucial, as it significantly impacts patient outcomes and the surgeon’s reputation. This importance is amplified in rhinoplasty, where minimizing bleeding is vital for optimal results. Consequently, this research investigates how various preoperative medications, such as clonidine and tranexamic acid (TXA), affect blood loss during rhinoplasty, with an emphasis on enhancing surgical efficiency and patient safety. This retrospective cross-sectional study examined data from 120 patients who underwent rhinoplasty from 2019 to 2022. The data were preprocessed and analyzed using various regression models, including Linear regression, random forest (RF), support vector regression (SVR), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Gradient Boosting, Ridge, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO), to forecast blood loss associated with the use of clonidine and TXA. The findings were then compared to determine the most effective medication and the best predictive model. The results revealed that the Linear and Ridge regression algorithms outperformed all other models based on three evaluation metrics: mean absolute error (MAE), mean square error (MSE), and R-squared. The estimated blood loss during rhinoplasty was 112 mL with clonidine, 132 mL with TXA, and 157 mL without any medication. This study highlights the significance of preoperative medications in controlling blood loss during rhinoplasty. The findings indicate that regression models can accurately predict blood loss, with clonidine demonstrating a significant impact on reducing hemorrhage. Introduction

Rhinoplasty, one of the most popular and widely performed cosmetic surgical procedures worldwide, aims to correct aesthetic imperfections of the nose while enhancing respiratory function1,2. This surgery requires a high degree of skill and precision from surgeons due to the complex anatomical and vascular characteristics of the nasal region3,4. Currently, rhinoplasty is regarded as a multi-step process that involves thorough patient evaluation, the selection of appropriate surgical techniques, and the management of potential complications5,6. However, a major challenge faced by surgeons during this procedure is intraoperative bleeding, which can significantly impact surgical outcomes and patient safety7–9. A clear surgical field with minimal bleeding is essential for the surgeon to perform delicate and intricate maneuvers effectively. Concurrently, anesthesiologists focus on controlling and minimizing bleeding to maintain hemodynamic stability throughout the operation10,11.

Intraoperative bleeding during nasal surgery can occur for various reasons12. The nasal region is inherently rich in blood vessels, and damage to both large and small vascular structures can lead to severe hemorrhage. This bleeding not only disrupts the surgeon’s visibility but can also prolong the duration of the surgery, and in severe cases, result in serious complications such as hypovolemic shock and the need for blood transfusions13,14. Research indicates that mortality rates associated with severe bleeding in rhinoplasty procedures may reach up to 21%, highlighting the critical importance of effective bleeding management15,16. Consequently, managing blood loss in rhinoplasty surgeries is recognized as a vital priority9. Anesthesiologists employ various strategies to mitigate bleeding, one of the most common being the administration of specific medications during the procedure along with the implementation of controlled hypotension17,18. Medications such as clonidine and tranexamic acid (TXA) have been identified as effective options for managing intraoperative bleeding18,19. Clonidine, an alpha-2 adrenergic agonist, aids in reducing blood pressure and promoting vascular relaxation, thereby contributing to decreased bleeding20–23. Meanwhile, TXA works by inhibiting fibrinolysis, further reducing blood loss during surgery24–26.

In recent years, remarkable advancements in artificial intelligence (AI), particularly machine learning and deep learning algorithms, have led to significant transformations in the field of medicine27–29. These technologies possess the capability to process and analyze vast amounts of clinical data, enabling the identification of complex patterns and relationships that assist in disease diagnosis and complication prediction30,31. In rhinoplasty, these tools can aid surgeons in predicting bleeding and assessing associated risks. By analyzing clinical data and individual patient characteristics, machine learning algorithms can forecast blood loss and help surgeons make informed decisions regarding bleeding management32. Such advancements not only enhance the efficiency and accuracy of treatments but also contribute to improved surgical outcomes and increased patient safety. Regression algorithms in machine learning33,34 are specifically utilized as robust tools for predicting the volume of blood loss in candidates for rhinoplasty. These algorithms can analyze clinical data and patient characteristics to forecast bleeding, providing critical information that can assist surgeons and anesthesiologists in making better-informed decisions regarding bleeding management35,36.

This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of clonidine and TXA drugs separately in controlling bleeding during rhinoplasty performed under general anesthesia. Data were collected from candidates for surgery at a local hospital in Shushtar were collected. Clonidine was administered for one group and TXA was used for another, while a third group served as a control without any medication. Subsequently, regression algorithms were employed to predict the volume of blood loss during the procedures to create a clean surgical field with a good intraoperative visualization.

Methods

In this study, a comprehensive analysis was conducted to predict the amount of bleeding that would occur during rhinoplasty procedures. In particular, we used several regression models including Linear, random forest (RF), support vector regression (SVR), Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), Ridge, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) as primary analytical tools to evaluate the relationship between intraoperative bleeding volume, surgical factors, and patient characteristics. The goal of this strategy is to determine which drug will minimize blood loss during surgery.

The experiments were conducted to implement the models in the Google Colab environment, utilizing a system equipped with an Intel Core i7 processor with 16 GB of RAM running on Windows 11.

Data set description

This retrospective, single-center study analyzed data from patients who were candidates for elective open rhinoplasty and consented to participate in a clinical trial between 2019 and 202237. Initially, a mean comparison algorithm and specific inclusion criteria were employed to screen 135 people. A total of 120 individuals were chosen for the trial after 15 patients were eliminated for non-cooperation. These participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: the clonidine group (40 patients), the TXA group (40 patients), and the control group (40 patients).

To qualify for participation, individuals had to be between the ages of 18 and 60, provide informed consent, and meet American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) classifications I or II, indicating that they did not have any serious systemic issues. However, patients with moderate systemic diseases that were well-controlled were included. Individuals with a history of substance misuse, liver or kidney failure, diabetes, respiratory or cardiovascular issues, coagulation disorders, or allergies to TXA and clonidine were excluded from the study. The outcome of interest was blood loss during the procedure which was measured by weighing pre-weighed blood-soaked gauze and calculating the total volume of blood collected in suction containers. 80% of the trial participants were female, and the average patient weight was 68 kg. Model implementation

We employed several regression techniques, which are effective for predicting continuous outcomes based on input features, to achieve our objective. Our goal was to thoroughly investigate how different medications, particularly TXA and clonidine, influenced the reduction of blood loss during surgery. By identifying the most effective drug, we aimed to improve surgical efficiency and enhance patient safety.

We chose the recommended regression algorithms due to their proven effectiveness in managing potential overfitting and complex correlations present in the data. These algorithms each offer unique advantages, allowing us to thoroughly evaluate the predictors of blood loss. By analyzing the outcomes from these models, we can identify which drug most effectively reduces bleeding. Using these algorithms systematically helps us better predict blood loss and understand how different preoperative procedures impact surgical outcomes. This approach not only enhances our understanding of the factors influencing blood loss but also provides surgeons with valuable insights to refine their techniques. In the following sections, we will provide a detailed overview of each algorithm used in this study, which includes regression models including Linear, RF, SV, XGBoost, Gradient Boosting, Ridge, and LASSO. Each of these algorithms was chosen for its strengths in predictive modeling, enabling a comprehensive analysis of the data.

Discussion

Rhinoplasty can lead to substantial blood loss because it involves a highly vascularized area. Since blood transfusions carry potential complications, including the risk of blood-borne diseases and infections, most surgical interventions focus on minimizing blood loss to reduce the need for transfusions70. Accurate estimation of intraoperative blood loss is a critical aspect of surgical planning and ensuring patient safety during rhinoplasty. Blood loss not only affects the overall effectiveness of the surgical procedure but also influences patient outcomes. The aim of this study was to create a predictive model that considers the administration of various preoperative medications when estimating the volume of blood loss associated with rhinoplasty. For this purpose, we quantitatively assessed blood loss during rhinoplasty in three patient groups: those receiving clonidine, those receiving TXA, and a control group that received no medication before rhinoplasty.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first ML-based model for bleeding volume prediction during a rhinoplasty procedure. Based on our experiments both Linear and Ridge regression algorithms outperformed all other models based on three evaluation metrics: MAE, MSE, and R-squared. The numerical results indicate that the R2 values represent the proportion of variance in the dependent variable that can be explained by the independent variables in the model71. Specifically, the Linear, Ridge, and lasso algorithms achieved an R2 value of 1.00, outperforming the other models. This high R2 value signifies a strong fit to the data, suggesting that these models effectively capture the underlying relationships between the predictors and the target variable, providing reliable predictions of blood loss72. The Linear and Ridge regression models achieved the lowest MSE. This suggests that these models excel at reducing prediction errors, likely because of their strong capability to handle non-linear relationships and their effectiveness in modeling complex interactions among features73,74. These two algorithms showed improved performance in terms of MAE, consistently producing predictions that are closer to the actual values and minimizing absolute errors. These characteristic highlights their strength in delivering stable predictions, even when outliers are present, which can significantly impact the performance of other models75,76.

The results indicated that clonidine was the most effective medication in reducing intraoperative blood loss, with an average loss of 112 mL. TXA was the second most effective, resulting in an average blood loss of 132 mL. In contrast, the control group experienced significantly higher blood loss, averaging 157 mL. Our findings suggest that using effective pharmacological strategies can improve surgical outcomes and enhance patient safety during procedures like rhinoplasty. While our study demonstrated that both clonidine and TXA were more effective than no medication at all, with clonidine showing superior results compared to TXA, Heidari et al.19 found in their study that TXA significantly reduced intraoperative bleeding and improved the quality of the surgical field compared to clonidine in patients undergoing nasal surgery. Several studies70,77–79 reported significant reductions in intraoperative blood loss when using TXA compared to control groups. For these studies, participants received systemic doses of either 1 g orally (PO) or 10 mg/kg intravenously (IV) before surgery. Additionally, several other studies22,23,73 demonstrated the effectiveness of clonidine in reducing or controlling bleeding during rhinoplasty.

Study limitations

The main limitations of our study pertain to the dataset and its representativeness. While the study offers insights into predicting bleeding volume during rhinoplasty using regression algorithms, it is important to acknowledge that the dataset was specific to Shushtar city in Khuzestan Province, Iran. This geographic limitation may restrict the broader applicability of our findings to diverse populations worldwide. Second, the number of patients included was small, as this retrospective study only involved data from individuals who had surgery at one hospital. The data was collected during a clinical trial study conducted during the coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) pandemic when many elective surgeries were canceled. Our study included 120 participants who were randomly assigned to three groups: the clonidine group (40 patients), the TXA group (40 patients), and the control group (40 patients). This limited sample size may impact the reliability of the results. Third, all surgeries were performed using the open rhinoplasty approach, which means the findings may not apply to closed rhinoplasty techniques. Fourth, while a standard anesthesia method was used for all patients, we did not investigate how different anesthesia techniques might influence bleeding. Additionally, the combined use of TXA and clonidine was not administered within the same group. Future research should address these issues with larger and more diverse samples would enhance the robustness and generalizability of the machine learning models used for predicting bleeding volume during rhinoplasty. Additionally, although we employed six different regression algorithms, there are other algorithms not included in this study that may provide different insights. While the chosen algorithms were representative, they were not exhaustive.

Conclusions

In rhinoplasty, managing intraoperative blood loss poses a significant challenge, as excessive bleeding can hinder surgical visibility and adversely affect patient outcomes. In this study, clonidine and TXA were used as preoperative medications to evaluate their effects on blood loss during rhinoplasty surgeries. The findings suggest that regression algorithms can effectively predict intraoperative hemorrhage, with Linear regression and Ridge regression yielding the most reliable results among the tested models. Notably, clonidine was found to be more effective than TXA, significantly reducing blood loss. This research enhances surgical efficiency and patient safety by providing surgeons with a clearer understanding of the impacts of these medications, ultimately supporting the goal of achieving the best possible surgical outcomes.

Interfacial Schottky junction induced enhanced piezocatalytic activity
Authors: Akshay Gaur, Addisalem Abebe, Chirag Porwal, Rahul Vaish, Himmat Singh Kushwah, Imed Boukhris, M. S. Al-Buriahi, Norah Alomayrah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The deposition of metal onto a semiconductor leads to the creation of a Schottky junction, offering an effective route for inducing polarization. The present study utilizes this phenomenon as a strategic approach for increasing the piezocatalytic activity for pollutant removal applications. Gold (Au) and silver (Ag) were introduced on the surface of the plain BaTiO₃ (BT) ceramic pellet to demonstrate the effectiveness of the mentioned phenomenon. The fabricated catalysts, BT, Ag-BT, and Au-BT, were evaluated for the piezocatalytic activity for degrading the representative pollutant, methylene blue (MB). Piezocatalysis under mechanical vibrations showed that Au-BT achieved the highest MB dye degradation (89%), followed by Ag-BT (75%) and BT (47%), with Au-BT and Ag-BT exhibiting ~ 1.9- and ~ 1.6-fold improvements over plain BT within 3 h. The increment in degradation piezocatalytic activity is attributed to the enhanced built-in polarization due to metal–semiconductor interface development. Overall, the present study highlights a simple modification strategy for interfacial polarization, providing an effective alternative to chemical approaches for enhancing the piezocatalytic activity of the catalyst.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-51214-6.

Introduction

Piezoelectric materials have attracted significant interest for catalytic applications1–5, with water treatment being an emerging example. In the context of piezocatalysis for water treatment applications, the process of degradation of pollutants relies on the direct piezoelectric effect6–8, where mechanical forces or vibrations applied to the piezocatalyst induce surface charges which produce reactive species, thereby driving the breakdown of organic pollutants9–11. In recent years, piezoelectric materials have been widely studied for water treatment applications, and a broad range of materials has been explored12.

In piezocatalysis, piezoelectric materials used as catalysts require continuous high-frequency mechanical vibrations, typically provided by an ultrasonicator in laboratory conditions. Further in search of low-frequency mechanical stimulation, researchers have also used the ball-milling technique13 for pollutant degradation, demonstrating the expanding scope of research in this field. For enhancing the piezocatalytic efficiency, various strategies for catalyst such as elemental doping, composite formation, microstructure, heterojunctions etc. are adopted14. Other reported techniques for enhancing the piezocatalytic activity of piezocatalysts include dipole orientation through electric poling, increasing the surface area by using polymer burnout during sintering for bulk ceramic, and utilizing the flexoelectric effect to induce polarization9,15–17.

Furthermore, an unnoticed physical phenomenon contributing to enhanced built-in polarization occurs when a metal is deposited on the surface of a semiconductor18. This results in the formation of a Schottky junction at the metal–semiconductor interface. It has been reported that such junctions can induce interfacial polarization, and interestingly, even centrosymmetric structures can exhibit built-in polarization when coated with a metal due to the Schottky junction effect19. Metal–semiconductor interface is one of the researched topics in fundamental physics, the concept of Schottky junction and band tilting playing crucial roles, where both centrosymmetric and non-centrosymmetric structures can experience such effects. In context to the piezocatalytic activity of the catalyst for pollutant removal application, non-centrosymmetric structure of the catalyst is the prerequisite, therefore the utilization around centrosymmetric structure will be ignored in the later sections of the work.

Ferroelectric materials, being inherently non-centrosymmetric, show great potential as catalysts in piezocatalytic applications due to their switchable polarization, high dielectric response, and strong piezoelectric coupling, which enable efficient charge-driven catalytic mechanisms. These materials inherently possess strong spontaneous polarization and can be further modulated by Schottky junction formation, where integrating ferroelectric structures into piezocatalytic systems offers a strategic way to maximize built-in polarization and surface charge generation, which can ultimately lead to superior catalytic degradation of pollutants. Thus, the importance of polarization in determining piezocatalytic efficiency highlights the relevance of non-centrosymmetric materials.

Ferroelectric material’s core lies the fundamental aspect of surface charges and interfacial bound charges resulting from disparity in polarization fields20. If the charges are not compensated, it will contribute to the material’s free energy in a manner akin to bulk contributions, leading to a divergence in surface energy with increasing system size. Electrostatic considerations of this type have been recognized since the inception of ferroelectricity, prompting the exploration of mechanisms to mitigate this depolarization energy21. Despite the existence of domain structures, further reduction of depolarization energy is possible when the polarization charge is actually compensated or shielded by charges available externally22,23. Metallic electrodes or charged ionic species which are mobile in nature are adsorbed from the atmosphere can serve as these external charges. Moreover, the dynamics of polarization switching in systems featuring boundary conditions diverges from those of ideal metal electrodes, where continuous screening charges are spatially aligned with polarization-bound charges, will be impacted by the interplay between polarization and screening dynamics24. This interaction results in the coupling of the behaviours of the ferroelectric material and transport of ionic charges are governed by the principles of charge and mass conservation. In simpler terms, the switching of polarization is contingent upon the redistribution of screening charges24.

BT is a well-established ferroelectric material and an efficient piezocatalyst due to its good piezoelectric coefficient, environmental compatibility, structural simplicity, tunable size/morphology, and excellent stability25. It possesses a typical perovskite (ABO₃) structure, where O2− forms an oxygen octahedron with Ti4+ at its center and Ba2+ in the interstitial sites25. BT-based materials are one of the most studied materials in piezocatalysis domain, where numerous strategies including morphology control, elemental doping, electrical poling, heterostructure formation, noble-metal deposition, carbon incorporation, polymer scaffolds, and increasing active sites25 have been widely explored.

Therefore, the present study investigates the well-known phenomenon of built-in polarization in BT ceramic pellets developed through the deposition of metals (Au and Ag) on their surface. The formation of metal–semiconductor Schottky junctions is expected to facilitate directional charge separation, thereby enhancing the piezocatalytic response. The prepared samples were subsequently evaluated for piezocatalytic degradation of MB dye.

Experimental

BT ceramic (powder and bulk/pellet) synthesis

The stoichiometric ratio of BaCO3 and TiO2 were manually mixed via mortar pestle. The precursors mixed were then calcined for 1200 °C/4 h. The calcined powder was again grounded to eliminate agglomeration, blended with 4 wt% polyvinyl alcohol until fully dried, and subsequently pressed using a hydraulic press under an 8 tonne load. The fabricated green pellets were again subjected to sintering temperature of 1350 °C/4 h with intermediating heating at 550 °C/2 h. Both the calcination and sintering processes were carried out in an electric furnace (Nabertherm, Germany).

Characterization

The synthesis of BT pellet involved the assessment of phase formation and purity. Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) investigation was conducted using an X-Ray diffraction machine (Rigaku Corporation) with a 9 kW rotating anode and Cu-Kα source (λ = 1.54 Å). The diffraction pattern was recorded at 2°/min. Vibrational modes in the BT pellet were characterized through Raman spectroscopy (HORIBA). Microstructure was captured using field emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM) (NOVA Nanosem 450, Hillsboro, OR). Surface elemental detection was obtained through X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) (NEXSA).

Deposition of Ag and Au on plain BT pellet

Two metals, Ag and Au were deposited separately on the surface of the BT pellet. Au was deposited (~ 20 nm) on BT pellet via sputtering method. It should be noted that the primary focus of this study is the Au-BT interface, which serves as the main system for demonstrating the effect of Schottky junction formation on piezocatalytic performance. Silver was included mainly as a comparative metal to validate the role of metal-induced Schottky junctions. Au and Ag metal deposited on BT pellet were designated as Au-BT and Ag-BT pellet respectively and were investigated under piezocatalysis process for degradation of MB dye.

Mechanism of piezocatalysis

Piezocatalysis begins when a piezoelectric material experiences continuous external stress. The deformation in crystal due to continuous external force creates a net dipole moment, or polarization, due to separation of positive and negative charges. These charges on the surface of the piezocatalyst then generate reactive species capable of degrading pollutants. From piezocatalyst point of view, two main mechanisms have been proposed in the literature37: the screening charge theory and the energy band theory.

The screening charge theory explains the process through charge redistribution. In the absence of stress, the material remains electrically neutral with uniformly balanced charges. When stress is applied, polarization develops and surface charges appear, attracting compensating charges and creating potential differences between domains. When the stress is reduced, some of these screening charges detach, leaving residual charges behind. Under continuous mechanical loading, this repeated adsorption and desorption leads to dynamic charge flow. This mechanism as shown in Fig. 7a highlights how mechanical strain influences surface charge behaviour and interfacial processes.Fig. 7Plausible mechanism of piezocatalysis via (a) screening charge effect theory, and (b) energy band gap.

Plausible mechanism of piezocatalysis via (a) screening charge effect theory, and (b) energy band gap.

The energy band theory describes the process in terms of changes in electronic energy levels, similar to photocatalysis, where electrons (e−) move from the valence band (VB) to the conduction band (VB). However, in piezocatalysis, mechanical energy replaces light as the driving force, meaning the result of charge generation is similar, but the cause of that charge generation is fundamentally different. Considering high mechanical vibrations as the source excitor, e− can be generated38,39 by: (i) direct excitation from strong vibrations, (ii) high pressures from cavitation (~ 102 MPa) and the associated local heating on grain surfaces, and (iii) sonoluminescence produced during bubble collapse. At the same time, it can be visualized that mechanical force generates an intrinsic piezopotential that bends energy bands as shown in Fig. 7b, enhances charge separation, and promotes redox reactions.

In both mechanisms, continuous mechanical input in the form of vibrations or waves is essential to sustain catalytic activity. Although different in perspective, both mechanisms in the piezocatalyst describe how mechanical excitation drives charge generation, redistribution, and flow, together offering a comprehensive understanding of the piezocatalytic process. From process point of view, when mechanical vibrations are subjected to the piezocatalyst, there is generation of the charges on the surface (the catalyst can generate these charges through different proposed mechanisms, such as the screening charge theory or the energy band gap theory), The generated positive and negative charge carriers (h+ and e−) lead to the formation of reactive species, which subsequently attack and degrade the pollutant molecules. The MB dye’s degradation by using Ag-BT and Au-BT was significantly more than BT. Thus, it can be conclusively concluded that there is an effect of metal deposition/layer on the BT pellet based on the degradation efficiency.

There can be possible reasons for the enhanced degradation of metal deposited (Au and Ag) on BT compared to BT. Firstly, there is a clear indication of band tilting when case of metal-BT is considered. The tilting of band energy level facilitated more charge carriers for reaching the redox reaction potential and whereby enhanced degradation of MB dye is achieved. Previously, a classical model by Wang et al.45, featuring a piezoelectric material and two electrode (Au), it was observed that changes in the Fermi level of the catalyst influence the redox potential, thereby affecting the progression of the chemical reaction. The distinct direction of ferroelectric polarization results in interfaces band bending, supplementing the band bending expected from the disparity in work function between the two contacted materials43.

One way to interpret this behaviour is that metal deposition on both surfaces of the BT pellet forms a capacitor-like configuration. Under mechanical vibrations, the induced piezopotential promotes the accumulation of screening charges at the metal-BT interfaces. These screening charges enhance charge separation and interfacial redox reactions, ultimately leading to higher degradation efficiency in the Au-BT pellet. The built-in piezopotential field operates indirectly by primarily regulating the semiconducting properties. This involves manipulating tilting of bulk band levels, thus establishing a spatial driving force for the parting of the charge carriers and their conduction characteristics. These changes subsequently reduce activation energy and hinder the quash electron–hole pairs. On the other hand, the piezopotential directly impacts the energetics of the charge carriers, as elucidated by the mechanism of the screening charge effect. The piezopotential’s magnitude needs to surpass or equal the redox potential levels entirely to initiate reactions, akin to the process of electrolysis. According to the theory of screening charge effects, the catalytic activity of a piezocatalyst is dictated by its piezopotential, whereas the reaction kinetics are controlled by the catalyst’s band energy levels (energy band theory). Overall, metal deposition on ferroelectric materials plays a crucial role in enhancing piezocatalytic degradation. The present results clearly demonstrate that metal deposited BT pellets exhibit superior degradation performance compared to pristine BT, confirming the beneficial effect of metal deposition. Among the metal-modified samples, Au-BT shows significantly higher degradation efficiency than Ag-BT, which can be attributed to the higher electrical conductivity and charge carrier mobility of Au. The Au layer efficiently collects and redistributes the piezo-induced charges under mechanical excitation, strengthening the screening charge effect at the metal and BT interface. This enhanced interfacial charge separation and transport accelerates surface redox reactions, resulting in improved degradation of MB dye.

In addition to the electrochemical measurements mentioned above, cyclic voltammetry of BT, Au-BT, and Ag-BT was also performed, as shown in the Supporting Information (Figures S3, S4, and S5, respectively). The results showed that all of them are primarily displaying reduction processes, with a scan rate of 20 mV/sec at a potential range of − 0.8 to 1.0 V. Silver, however, is exhibiting extra oxidation peaks due to its stepwise surface oxidation behaviour. During the anodic scan, metallic Ag is initially oxidized at lower potentials, followed by further oxidation to higher valence silver oxides at higher potentials. In addition, adsorption of hydroxyl species and electrolyte anions on the Ag surface can cause overlapping oxidation processes. From Figure S4 and S5, Ag-BT and Au-BT exhibit clear non-linear I–V characteristics, which are distinctly different from the nearly linear CV response of pristine BT. This non-linear behaviour indicates barrier-controlled charge transport arising from Fermi-level alignment at the metal–semiconductor interface, a typical feature of Schottky junction formation48. In addition, the asymmetric current response observed between the forward and reverse scans reflects the presence of a built-in electric field associated with the Schottky barrier. The significantly higher current density of Ag-BT and Au-BT (Figure S4 and S5 respectively) compared to BT further confirms the beneficial role of metal deposition, which facilitates directional electron transfer from BT to the metal while effectively suppressing charge recombination. Moreover, the appearance of redox-related features in the CV curves (Figure S4 and S5) highlights the electrochemically active nature of Ag and Au, contributing to enhanced interfacial charge transfer49. From a piezocatalytic viewpoint, the Schottky junction plays a key role in improving catalytic performance. Upon mechanical stimulation, the piezoelectric BT generates a piezopotential that drives the separation of charge carriers. The Schottky barrier formed at the Ag/Au-BT interface further promotes electron migration toward the metal, thereby inhibiting recombination. This synergistic effect prolongs charge carrier lifetime and enhances the generation of reactive oxygen species, resulting in superior piezocatalytic activity compared to pristine BT.

Conclusions

The present study establishes that metal deposition on ferroelectric BT pellets, leading to the formation of metal and semiconductor Schottky junctions, is the key factor responsible for the enhanced piezocatalytic degradation performance. Independent of the specific mechanistic description, the creation of a Schottky junction at the metal and BT interface fundamentally alters the interfacial electronic structure, thereby facilitating efficient charge generation, separation, and participation in surface redox reactions under mechanical excitation.

Both the screening charge mechanism and the energy band theory provide valid but distinct perspectives on the piezocatalytic process. In the screening charge framework, metal deposition enhances the accumulation, redistribution, and release of compensating charges at the metal and BT interface during mechanical vibrations, amplifying the effective piezopotential and promoting surface redox reactions. In parallel, from an energy band perspective, Schottky junction formation induces band bending and Fermi-level realignment, enabling charge carriers to reach the redox potential more efficiently. Importantly, even when these mechanisms are considered independently, both inherently rely on and are strengthened by the presence of the Schottky junction, underscoring its central role in governing piezocatalytic activity.

Plain BT pellet exhibited ~ 47% degradation of MB dye within 3 h, whereas Ag-BT and Au-BT pellets achieved significantly higher degradation efficiencies of ~ 75% and ~ 89%, respectively. From the observed experimental results and the characterization techniques, it is concluded that the proficiency of Au-BT pellet is greater than that of Ag-BT. This clear enhancement confirms that metal deposition alone without chemical doping or complex structural modification is sufficient to markedly improve piezocatalytic performance. Au-BT outperformed Ag-BT due to the higher electrical conductivity and charge carrier mobility of Au. The Au-BT interface is the primary focus of this study to demonstrate the role of Schottky junction formation, while Ag serves as a comparative metal. The Au layer enables efficient collection and transport of piezo-induced charges, enhancing interfacial screening effects and surface redox reactions. Overall, the conclusions of the present study highlight that metal deposition and the resulting Schottky junction formation serve as a unifying and dominant strategy for enhancing piezocatalytic degradation. Whether interpreted through screening charge dynamics or energy band modulation, both mechanisms converge on the same outcome i.e., improved interfacial charge management promoted by Schottky junctions. The present work provides a clear mechanistic understanding and a scalable physical approach for designing high-performance piezocatalysts for wastewater treatment and related environmental applications.

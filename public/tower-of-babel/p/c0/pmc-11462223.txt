Molecular characterisation and expression analysis of an interferon-induced protein with tetratricopeptide repeats 1 (IFIT1) homologue from Asian seabass (Lates calcarifer)
Authors: Krishnapriya Raji Sathyan, Avinash Premraj, Sajeevan Thavarool Puthiyedathu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe interferon-induced protein with tetratricopeptide repeats 1(IFIT1) is an interferon-inducible protein that acts downstream of the interferon pathway in response to viral infection or an interferon-stimulation. Our study describes the isolation and characterization of an IFIT1 homologue from Lates calcarifer (named LcIFIT1), as well as its expression profile in response to viral mimetic poly(I:C) and Red-spotted grouper nervous necrosis virus (RGNNV). The complete ORF of LcIFIT1 encodes a 441 amino acid protein with nine tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domains. The protein-coding region of the LcIFIT1 gene was encoded by two exons separated by a small 157 bp intron. The phylogenetic analysis revealed that LcIFIT1 is evolutionarily conserved and forms a distinct clade with homologues from other teleosts. LcIFIT1 mRNA was constitutively expressed in all tissues examined, with predominant expression in blood. LcIFIT1 expression was upregulated following poly(I:C) and RGNNV challenges in different tissues examined. These results shed light on the antiviral role of LcIFIT1 in the host immune response against nodaviral infection.Keywords: Interferon-induced protein with tetratricopeptide repeats 1, Lates calcarifer, poly(I:C), RGNNV, Viral

Introduction

The antiviral immune response of the host depends on the recognition of foreign agents like viruses that lead to the release of cellular effector molecules like interferons and downstream elements like interferon-stimulated genes (ISGs) [1]. The ISGs can act alone or in combination at different stages of virus propagation like virus entry, replication, transcription, translation, assembly and release, thus orchestrating a multipronged approach for preventing viral replication [[2], [3]].

Interferon-induced proteins with tetratricopeptide repeats 1 (IFIT1) or ISG56 protein was first identified as p56 (a 56 kDa protein) in humans [4]. IFIT family proteins have characteristic tetratricopeptide repeats (TPR domains), which are conserved in mammals, amphibians and avians [5]. The piscine IFIT1 gene orthologs have been identified and cloned from crucian carp (Carassius auratus L.) [6] tongue sole (Cynoglossus semilaevis) [7], zebrafish (Danio rerio) [8], olive flounder (Paralichthys olivaceus) [9], orange-spotted grouper Epinephelus coioides [10] and giant grouper Epinephelus lanceolatus [11].

The biological function of the IFIT gene family is closely related to IFN signalling and IFN response [12]. IFIT proteins can execute antiviral activities either by interacting with eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 (eIF3) and suppress translation initiation by either blocking the binding of eIF3 to the eIF2–GTP–Met-tRNA ternary complex [13] or by blocking the formation of 43S–mRNA complex (by binding to eIF3c) [[12], [14]]. IFIT1 can form a cytoplasmic complex that recognises uncapped 5′-triphosphate RNAs and sequester viral RNA from replication [[12], [14]]. IFIT1 can recognise and directly bind to 5′capped mRNA lacking 2′-O methylation and inhibit translation [15].

Even though IFIT proteins lack any proven enzymatic activity, they interact with cellular and viral RNAs and proteins such as eIF3 to suppress viral gene transcription [16]. Li et al. [17] reported that IFIT1 can interact with Stimulator of Interferon genes (STING), mitochondrial antiviral signalling protein (MAVS) and TANK-binding kinase 1 (TBK1) and prevent IFN production. The study conducted by Li et al. [17] contradicts the earlier report that IFIT1 inhibits viral replication [18,19]. IFIT1 can regulate a balance between the IFN response pathway and inflammatory response to facilitate innate immune response against pathogens [20].

The Asian seabass, Lates calcarifer (Bloch,1790) is a euryhaline teleost having high demand in international markets [21]. Asian seabass aquaculture is widely practised in the South and Southeast Asian countries, including India. But its aquaculture is affected by pathogens like virus (Nodavirus) and bacteria (Vibryo spp.). Betanodavirus (family Nodaviridiae) is the causative agent for VNN (Viral Nervous Necrosis) disease or VER (Viral Encephalopathy and retinopathy), affecting about thirty species of marine fish across the globe [22,23].

In this study, we have cloned and characterised an IFIT1 homologue from the teleost Asian seabass, Lates calcarifer. The expression of LcIFIT1 against experimental RGNNV infection and poly(I:C) challenge was also studied. The immune profiling of host antiviral genes like IFIT1 may shed light on host-pathogen interactions and facilitate the development of strategies to reduce the mortality and economic loss caused by betanodavirus infection in aquaculture.

Materials and methods

Fish, cell line, virus

The fish having approximately 50 g weight and 10–12 cm length was procured from Rajiv Gandhi Centre for Aquaculture (RGCA, MPEDA complex, Vallarpadam, Cochin, Kerala). and were maintained. The fish was acclimatised before experimental manipulation for two weeks at salinity 15ppt, and temperature 28–30 °C. Prior to the experiments, the fish was randomly sampled and checked for the presence of Betanodavirus by PCR [24,25]. The fishes were euthanised humanely after anaesthetising with clove oil for challenge studies. The animal experimental procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Ethics Committee of Cochin University of Science and Technology (CUSAT), Kerala, India.

The RGNNV (Red-spotted grouper nervous necrosis virus) that was isolated previously from an infected seabass was propagated in SISK (Sahul Indian Seabass Kidney) cell line [26] that was maintained at 28 °C in l-15(Leibovitz) medium (HiMedia, India) supplemented with 2 % Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS) (HiMedia, India) [27]. The viral supernatant was collected, and TCID50/ml (50 % tissue culture infectivity dose/ ml) was determined as described previously [28].

Stimulation of fish with poly(I:C) and RGNNV virus

Ninety fishes were divided into three groups of thirty, in which challenge groups were intraperitoneally injected with 100 µl of poly (I:C) (Sigma P1530) diluted in PBS at a dose of 100 µg per 100 g body weight of fish or 100 µl of RGNNV at a concentration of 1 × 106 TCID50/ml [29]. The control group was injected with an equal amount of PBS (Phosphate buffered saline). Tissues (head kidney, spleen, gill and heart) from each group were collected at 6,12,24,48 and 72 h of post-injection.

Cloning of LcIFIT1

Total RNA was extracted from gill using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and the RNA was quantified using Nanophotometer (Implen, Germany). The cDNA synthesis was carried out using two micrograms of RNA and M-MuLV reverse transcriptase (New England Biolabs, USA) with oligo (dT) primer. The cDNA was amplified for cloning LcIFIT1 gene using Emerald Amp master mix (TaKaRa, Japan). The primers used for amplification are designed based on a partially annotated whole genome sequence of Lates calcarifer available on NCBI (Supplementary Table 1). The amplification conditions were: 94°C for 2 min followed by 39 cycles of 94°C for 30 s, 60°C for 30 s, 72°C for 2 min followed by a final extension at 72°C for 10 min. The PCR product was cloned into pGEM-T Easy vector (Promega, USA), positive colonies were identified by blue-white screening, plasmid DNA was isolated and sequenced with BigDye terminator chemistry in an ABI 3730 DNA Analyzer (Applied Biosystems).

Sequence analysis of LcIFIT1

The nucleotide and amino acid sequences of LcIFIT1 were analysed using NCBI BLAST program and were translated using Expasy translate (Expert Protein Analysis System). The putative open reading frame (ORF) of cloned LcIFIT1 was identified using ORF finder (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/orffinder/). The isoelectric point and molecular weight of the deduced amino acid sequences were predicted using Expasy (https://web.expasy.org/compute_pi/). The transmembrane domain prediction was done with TMHMM-2.0 server [30]. The protein domain features were predicted using SMART (Simple Modular Architecture Research Tool), and a motif search was carried out using MOTIF (http://www.genome.jp/tools/motif). The TRANSFAC 4.0 database (https://genexplain.com/transfac/) was used to predict the transcription factor binding sites (TFBS). The in-silico protein localisation prediction was done by DeepLoc 2.0 and MULoc Deep servers [31,32].

Phylogenetic analysis and protein structure prediction of LcIFIT1

Multiple sequence alignment of LcIFIT1 with other reported IFIT1 proteins from mammals, amphibians, aves and other teleosts were carried out using CLUSTAL W [33]. The percentage identity was calculated (Supplementary Table 2). The phylogenetic tree was constructed from the deduced amino acid sequences using MEGA X program [34]. The neighbour-joining method was used to construct a phylogenetic tree, and the reliability of branches was tested using 1000 bootstrap replicates.

The protein secondary structure prediction was carried out using PSIPRED [35,36], and homology-based three-dimensional structure predictions were carried out using PHYRE2 program [37].

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis of LcIFIT1 mRNA was performed in Step OnePlus Real-Time PCR machine (Applied Biosystem, USA) using SYBR Premix Ex Taq (Takara Bio, Shiga, Japan). About two micrograms of total RNA were used to synthesise cDNA. For tissue distribution studies, tissues (head kidney, spleen, liver, intestine, gill, fin, brain, heart, muscle and blood) were collected from nine healthy Asian seabass. For qRT PCR studies of tissue distribution, the gene expression was normalised using Lates β-actin as an internal reference and calibrated to the expression in the intestine by using 2−ΔΔCt method [38]. For the expression studies during the following challenge experiments, the total RNA was isolated from poly(I:C) treated and RGNNV-infected tissues at 6, 12, 24, 48, and 72 h. Gene expression was normalised using Lates β-actin as an internal reference using 2−ΔΔCT and expressed as fold change by comparing the normalised gene expression level of treated groups with that of control. All data were represented as relative mRNA expression (± SEM). The qRT PCR mix consists of 5 µl SYBR green master mix (TAKARA), 0.5 µl ROX dye, 2 µl of template cDNA, 0.2 µM of both forward and reverse primers and 1.7 µl of nuclease-free water. The qRT-PCR conditions for LcIFIT1 were: 1 cycle of 5 min at 95 °C, followed by 39 cycles of 94 °C for 40 s and 60 °C for 1 min.

Statistical analysis

The data was represented as mean ± standard error of the triplicates in each experimental group. To determine significant differences between means, the data was compared using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's post hoc test. The p < 0.05 was used to indicate significant differences. All statistical analysis was carried out using SPSS 20 (Statistical Product and Service Solutions) software (Chicago, USA).

Results

Molecular characterisation of LcIFIT1

The complete ORF of LcIFIT1 was amplified from gill cDNA. The cloned LcIFIT1 cDNA fragment contains an ORF of 1326 bp (NCBI GenBank Accession number MN516799) that encodes a 441 amino acid protein (Supplementary Fig. 1). The deduced LcIFIT1 protein has a theoretical pI of 7.21 and a predicted molecular mass of 51.56 kDa. TMHMM predicts that LcIFIT1 has no transmembrane helix. SignalP 6.0 prediction revealed that LcIFIT1 lacks a signal peptide and the protein was predicted to be localised in the cytoplasm.

The LcIFIT1 protein comprises nine tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) domains according to conserved repeat domain analysis. The TPR motifs are located at positions 52–86, 95–129, 142–176, 182–218, 221–254, 255–289, 295–339, 347–380 and 385–418 (Fig. 1).

Alignment of the deduced Lc IFIT1 amino acid sequences with known fish and mammalian homologues: The amino acid sequences were aligned with CLUSTALW. Residues identical to the LcIFIT1 sequence in the multiple sequence alignment are represented by a dot (.). Gaps in the alignment are marked by dashes (-). The locations of the tetratricopeptide repeat (TPR) motifs are also marked. The highly conserved pentapeptide motif (CHFTW) at amino acid positions 19–23 are highlighted in yellow.

Gene organisation of LcIFIT1

The gene organisation of IFIT1 from Lates calcarifer was determined by searching and aligning the cDNA sequence with a whole-genome sequence of Lates available at NCBI GenBank. The LcIFIT1 cDNA sequence showed high identity with a transcriptome GAQL01334759.1 and NC_066849.1:26837166-26839387 genomic sequence. The cDNA sequence was aligned to the putative gene region within the contig and putative 5′ and 3′ UTR regions were identified. The protein coding region of LcIFIT1 gene was encoded by two exons separated by a 157 bp intron. The 137 bp exon 1 codes for the first codon (ATG) and the upstream 5′ UTR. The exon 2 is 1928 bp, which includes the rest of the protein-coding sequence (amino acids 2 to 441) (Supplementary Fig. 2). The transcription factor binding sites (TFBS) were predicted in the sequence based on TRANSFAC 4.0 database. The promoter of LcIFIT1 contains one NF-κB, GATA-1, OCT-1, two IRF1, CEBP ab and TATA box binding sites (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Phylogenetic analysis and protein structure prediction

To study the evolutionary relationship among the IFIT1 proteins, a phylogenetic tree was constructed by using LcIFIT1 and homologues from other vertebrates. The phylogenetic analysis revealed that LcIFIT1 closely clustered with IFIT1 homologues from other teleost fishes (Fig. 2). The sequence analysis revealed that LcIFIT1 shared 78.49 % identity with Toxotes jaculatrix, 76.43 % identity with Xiphias gladius IFIT1 homologues. With the human IFIT1, the amino acid identity was relatively less (∼25 %) (Supplementary Table 2). The secondary structure and three-dimensional structure prediction revealed that LcIFIT1 is predominantly alpha helical (73 %) (Supplementary Fig. 4).

Phylogenetic analysis of LcIFIT1. A neighbour-joining tree was constructed based on the protein sequences of IFIT-like amino acid sequences from different species using MEGA-X software. The numbers at the branch points represent the bootstrap values. Branch lengths are proportional to calculated evolutionary distances. The scale represents number of substitutions per site. Arrowhead indicate the LcIFIT1 position in the phylogenetic tree.

Tissue mRNA expression of LcIFIT1 in unchallenged fish

By using quantitative real-time PCR, we examined the gene expression of IFIT1 in healthy Asian seabass under normal physiological conditions. The highest mRNA expression levels of LcIFIT1 were observed in the blood (113-fold), followed by the liver (46-fold) and gill (41-fold) (Fig. 3).

Tissue expression profile of LcIFIT1 in healthy Lates. The relative mRNA expression levels were normalised by beta-actin using the 2−ΔΔCT method and were calibrated against gene expression levels in the intestine. The tissues were ordered according to relative expression levels from highest to lowest. Error bar represents the standard error of the mean, p < 0.05.

Poly(I:C) challenge and RGNNV infection induce LcIFIT1 mRNA expression

To determine the antiviral response of LcIFIT1 to poly(I:C) challenge and RGNNV (Red-spotted grouper nervous necrosis) virus infection, quantitative real-time PCR was used to determine the expression patterns.

Poly(I:C) or polyinosinic: polycytidilic acid is a chemical that was considered a TLR3 agonist. The expression of LcIFIT1 was upregulated upon poly(I:C) challenge in different organs examined. In the head kidney, the highest expression was observed at 6 h of post-challenge (5.3-fold), which decreased to 2.5-fold at 12 h and gradually downregulated in subsequent hours up to 72 h of post-challenge. In the spleen, the IFIT1 mRNA expression increased to 3.3-fold at 6 h and showed the highest expression of 9-fold at 12 h of post-challenge. In the heart, the highest mRNA expression was at 6 h (14-fold) which gradually decreased. In gill, the IFIT1 mRNA expression was 29.8-fold at 6 h, which reached 42-fold at 12 h, decreased to 10-fold at 24 h and then declined gradually (Fig. 4A).

Relative expression analysis of LcIFIT1 gene in four tissues (head kidney, spleen, gill and heart) after immune challenge with (A)poly(I:C) and (B)RGNNV by RT-qPCR. Results are indicated as mean ± SEM of three replicates (n = 3). The relative mRNA expression levels of LcIFIT1 in each tissue were normalised to beta-actin and calibrated to PBS-injected controls using the 2−ΔΔCT method. Error bars represent the standard error of the mean, p < 0.05 (one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post hoc test). The different letters denote statistically significant differences among the groups and within each group over the time according to Tukey post-hoc test (p < 0.05).

The expression of LcIFIT1 was also examined following experimental infection with RGNNV, a single-stranded RNA virus belonging to Nodaviridiae that poses a significant threat to Asian seabass aquaculture. Different organs showed different expression patterns post-RGNNV challenge. In the head kidney, the IFIT1 expression gradually increased at 6 h (5.3-fold) and showed a maximum at 12 h (16.2-fold) which gradually decreased in later hours of post-challenge till 72 h. The LcIFIT1 in the spleen showed the highest expression 75-fold at 12 h of post-RGNNV challenge, which gradually decreased to 53-fold at 24 h, 23.5-fold at 48 h and 3.6-fold at 72 h of post-RGNNV challenge. The LcIFIT1 expression in the heart increased and reached the maximum at 24 h (41-fold), and decreased to 5-fold at 48 h of post-RGNNV challenge. The expression of LcIFIT1 was downregulated in the gill post-RGNNV challenge (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

In the present study, we have identified and cloned an interferon-stimulated gene IFIT1 homologue from Lates calcarifer, LcIFIT1. LcIFIT1 encodes a 441 amino-acid protein that shared about 78.49 % identity with Toxotes jaculatrix IFIT1 protein. The IFIT1/ISG56 gene in mammals is composed of two exons. The first exon is short that encodes only 5′UTR and ATG start codon and two nucleotides of the second codon, and the rest of the mRNA is encoded by the second exon. A similar exon /intron structure was observed for LcIFIT1 also. However, in the LcIFIT1 gene, the two exons are separated by a short intron of only 157 bp, whereas in the human IFIT1, the intron extends a few kilobases in length [3] . The small size of the LcIFIT1 gene intron was confirmed from both genome-predicted data and by amplification from genomic DNA. The LcIFIT1 cDNA amplicon from gill cDNA was 1348 bp, whereas the intron-containing amplicon from genomic DNA was 1505 bp (Supplementary Fig. 2B). The human and murine IFIT1 gene promoters feature two ISRE (IFN-stimulated response elements) located at 200 bp upstream of the TATA box [[16], [39]].

Structurally, the IFIT gene family features multiple copies of TPR (tetratricopeptide repeats) domains which comprise a degenerate 34-amino acid helix-turn-helix motif [[3], [16], [40]]. The TPR motifs were classified into hydrophobic residues containing two subdomains that are conserved at consensus sites 4–7–8–11 and 20–24–27–32, which could be present throughout the protein sequence. Proteins with multiple TPR motifs can form right-handed super helical structures with continuous helical grooves that facilitate protein-protein interactions [41]. Any mutations like insertions or deletions in these subdomains can disrupt the tertiary structure of protein and can affect protein-protein interactions [42]. The sequence and domain structure analysis predicts that LcIFIT1 comprises nine TPR motifs. The presence of nine TPR motifs was previously reported from other teleosts like Crucian carp (Carassius auratus), pufferfish (Fugu rubrides), large yellow croaker (Pseudosciaena crocea) and Epinephelus lanceolatus [[6], [7],11]. The human and mouse IFIT1 is composed of 10 TPR motifs, while the number of TPR motifs varies in fishes. In olive flounder (Paralichthys olivaceus) ten TPR motifs were reported [9], but [11] reports that the tenth TPR motif is incomplete in teleost as they do not possess 34 amino acids; hence considered incomplete. However, only two TPR motifs were reported from the tongue sole (Cynoglossus semilaevis). The possible reason for the variations within TPR repeats may be due to the adaptation of IFIT to various interacting partners like viruses or host cells [9]. The last three TPR motifs at the C-terminal region of IFIT1 were reported to have interaction with the eIF3, which has a critical role in cellular protein synthesis [[43], [44]]. The proteins with TPR motifs were reported to be involved in cell proliferation, migration, translational initiation, protein folding and transport. They have an antiviral role and prevent virus replication [40,45].

The 3-dimensional crystal structure of IFIT5 and a part of IFIT1 (an amino-terminal fragment) has been previously reported. Their studies revealed that IFIT1 was shaped like a clamp and had a positively charged pocket that allows only single-stranded triphosphate RNA that distinguishes it from RIG-1, which is responsible for the binding of RNA. There are 23 alpha helices in human IFIT1, 18 of which are derived from the nine TPR motifs. These 23 alpha helices are organised into three distinct subdomains and form a clamp-shaped structure with an RNA-binding channel in the centre [46]. The N-terminal non-TPR alpha helices α1 and α2 are linked by loop L1, which features a highly conserved pentapeptide motif (CHFTW) at amino acid positions 19–23, which is invariant among all IFIT proteins, including LcIFIT1. This CHFTW motif in loop 1 of subdomain one is believed to interact with the α7 helix of subdomain 2 [46]. The functional role of a protein depends on its subcellular localisation. LcIFIT1 was predicted to be cytosolic which agree with the localisation of other IFIT family members reported so far [5].

The IFIT gene family is conserved in mammals, birds and amphibians but has not yet been reported from plants, yeast, or lower animals like nematodes [5]. Vertebrate interferon gene family and IFIT gene family share a common evolutionary history. The researchers have pointed out so many similarities that shed light on this. Both the family of genes have undergone lineage-specific expansion and species-specific duplication, especially in the case of teleost fishes. Moreover, these two gene families exist in all jawed vertebrates, including elephant sharks [47,48].

Previous tissue expression studies suggest that IFIT1 expression was higher in blood in Epinephelus lanceolatus [11], gill and spleen of tongue sole Cynoglossus semilaevis [7]; spleen and kidney in large yellow croaker Pseudosciana crocea [49], intestine and spleen in orange-spotted grouper Epinephelus coioides [10]. The LcIFIT1 was constitutively expressed in all tissues examined, with the highest expression in blood when compared with other organs. This might be indicative that this ISG may act as a guardian against blood-borne pathogens. Cynoglossus has basal expression of IFIT1 in many tissues without any pathogenic stimulation, suggesting that the protein may help maintain a healthy immune system [7].

IFIT1 is one of the rapidly induced ISG upon PAMP (Pathogen Associated Molecular Patterns) challenge [50]. IFIT1 is considered a viral stress-induced gene [51]. Poly(I:C), which is a potent inducer of interferon [52], was used as a PAMP for stimulation of the immune system. The expression of LcIFIT1 was upregulated upon poly(I:C) challenge in all the tissues examined, though their pattern of expression was different. The highest expression was observed in the gill when compared to immune organs like the spleen and head kidney. Gill is a mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue in teleosts, which is in constant exposure to pathogens and provides immunity [53,54]. Previous reports in olive flounder Paralichthys olivaceus also gave evidence that IFIT1 expression was upregulated in the head kidney after poly(I:C) challenge [9].

The LcIFIT1 was upregulated in the head kidney, heart, and spleen whereas downregulated in the gill upon experimental RGNNV challenge. In our previous studies on Asian seabass interferons, ISG15 and viperin the gill exhibited a potent induction of gene expression post poly(I:C) challenge that was absent following RGNNV challenge [24,29]. A similar expression pattern was observed in LcIFIT1 gene also. The highest expression of LcIFIT1 was observed in the spleen at 12 h of post-RGNNV virus challenge. Previous reports suggest that IFIT1 was upregulated upon SKIV (Spotted knifejaw iridovirus) in Epinephelus lanceolatus [11], VHSV (Viral Haemorrhagic Septicemia Virus) infection in olive flounder Paralichthys olivaceus [9], Megalocytivirus infection in tongue sole Cynoglossus semilaevis [7]. The expression of IFIT1 was upregulated upon in vitro challenge studies in grouper spleen cells with SGIV (Singapore grouper iridovirus) or RGNNV (red-spotted grouper nervous necrosis virus) [10]; GCHV (Grass Carp Haemorrhage Virus) in crucian carp Carassius auratus [6] . The expression of IFIT1 can be IFN-dependent by binding of various Pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) like virus or poly(I:C) to Pattern Recognition Receptors (PRRs) like TLR, MDA5 and RIG I) or IFN-independent at the transcriptional level directly by IRF3 by viral infection before IFN expression [12,55].

In conclusion, an IFIT1 gene homologue was identified and characterised from Lates calcarifer, LcIFIT1. The expression of IFIT1 gene was upregulated upon poly(I:C) and RGNNV virus challenge, which shed light on the role of this ISG in antiviral immunity. The cloning, characterisation, and expression profiling of one of the most potentially expressed groups of ISGs like IFIT1 may pave the way for the development of immunotherapeutics for unravelling enigmas of disease biology in aquaculture.

How aneurysm shape affects occlusion success after flow diverter placement
Authors: Kornelia M Kliś, Igor Szydłowski, Jerzy Gąsowski, Antoni Cierniak, Borys M Kwinta, Krzysztof Stachura, Bartłomiej Łasocha, Paweł Brzegowy, Roger M Krzyżewski, Tadeusz J Popiela
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeFlow Diverters (FD) are an established endovascular treatment for intracranial aneurysms, but complete aneurysm occlusion remains a challenge. This study aimed to quantitatively analyze three-dimensional aneurysm morphology and assess its influence on the likelihood of total occlusion after FD placement.MethodsFor this single-centre study we retrospectively included patients with unruptured intracranial aneurysm treated with FD between 2019 and 2022. The study group comprised patients with incomplete occlusion at first follow-up (~ 6 months). The control group, matched for common risk factors, included patients with complete occlusion. Based on 3D reconstructions, we calculated quantitative parameters: mean and entropy of mean and Gaussian curvature, mean and entropy of Writhe number, and Zernike Moment Invariants energy.ResultsThirty-five patients met study group criteria and 38 were included in the control group. Mean age was 57.6 ± 12.2 years and females comprised 82.9% and 81.6% of the groups, respectively. Incompletely occluded aneurysms showed higher Aspect Ratio (1.34 ± 0.42 vs. 1.29 ± 0.51; p = 0.03) and Size Ratio (1.28 ± 0.28 vs. 1.21 ± 0.28; p = 0.04), greater Gaussian curvature entropy (4.03 ± 0.60 vs. 3.73 ± 0.62; p = 0.03), and higher Zernike Moment Invariants energy (545.6 ± 256.2 vs. 271.6 ± 126.2; p = 0.03). In multivariate logistic regression, Gaussian curvature entropy (OR = 2.06; 95% CI: 1.26–13.71; p = 0.04) and Zernike Moment Invariants energy (OR = 3.02; 95% CI: 1.35–9.19; p = 0.02) were independently associated with incomplete occlusion.ConclusionQuantitative descriptors of aneurysm dome morphology, particularly Gaussian curvature entropy and Zernike Moment Invariants energy, may influence the likelihood of complete occlusion following FD placement.Keywords: Intracranial aneurysm, Flow diverter, Endovascular treatment, Treatment outcome

Introduction

Flow Diverters (FD) are an established method of endovascular treatment for intracranial aneurysms [1]. These devices act by lowering blood inflow into the aneurysm sac, which results in intra-aneurysmal thrombosis [2]. Additionally, they stimulate endothelial cell growth along the device struts [3]. Both mechanisms lead to aneurysmal exclusion from circulation.

However, achievement of total aneurysm occlusion remains an important clinical issue, as insufficient occlusions occur in up to 20% of cases [4]. Therefore, many authors have analyzed factors that might affect FD treatment outcome. Apart from patient-related [5] and aneurysmal hemodynamic [6] factors, one of the important features remain aneurysm morphology [7–9]. Usually, it is assessed in terms of dome and neck dimensions and geometrical indices. However, conventional measures such as maximal dome diameter or neck width are derived from two-dimensional projections and cannot fully capture spatial irregularities, asymmetries, or surface complexity. With advances in computational modeling, three-dimensional reconstructions of aneurysm geometry now allow for the calculation of numerous quantitative descriptors that characterize different aspects of aneurysm shape [10]. These parameters may better represent the morphological complexity and hemodynamic environment that influence thrombus formation and endothelialization after FD placement. To the best of our knowledge, none of these aspects were analyzed in terms of their possible influence on the outcome of endovascular treatment of intracranial aneurysms.

Therefore, the aim of our study was quantitatively to analyze three-dimensional morphology of intracranial aneurysm sac and to determine whether that morphology influences chance of aneurysm total occlusion after FD placement. Additionally, we aim to compare impact of aneurysm morphology on treatment outcome in patients treated with FD and stent-assisted coiling.

Materials and methods

Approval was obtained from the local Bioethics Committee. The procedures used in this study adhere to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Study group

We retrospectively included patients with FD-treated unruptured saccular intracranial aneurysms (UIA), who had incompletely occluded aneurysm in at least first follow-up examination (~ 6 months after treatment). The patients were hospitalized between 2019 and 2022. Exclusion criteria were: (1) previous endovascular or neurosurgical treatment; (2) presence of other intracranial pathologies; (3) history of ischemic stroke or subarachnoid hemorrhage; (4) procedure-related complications during follow-up period, including ischemic events or aneurysm rupture; (5) no follow-up examination; (6) history of improperly controlled hypertension.

Additionally, we included two control groups. The first one consisted of patients treated with FD placement, who had completely occluded aneurysm at first-follow up examination. Control group was matched for: (1) aneurysm dome and neck size; (2) aneurysm location; (3) usage of adjunctive coiling during procedure; (4) patients’ age, gender and diagnosis of hypertension. Second control group consisted of patients with UIA treated with coiling with additional implantation of non-FD stent (further defined as the stent-assisted coiling group). It was matched for: (1) aneurysm dome and neck size; (2) patients’ age. Exclusion criteria for both control groups were the same as for the study group. To match the control groups, we performed propensity score matching (1:2 for the first control group and 1:1 for the second). All included patients received dual antiplatelet therapy consisting of 150 mg of acetylsalicylic acid and 75 mg of clopidogrel daily, initiated five days before the procedure. Immediately prior to the intervention, platelet function was assessed using the VerifyNow system (Accumetrics, San Diego, CA, USA). After stent implantation, device apposition was evaluated with C-arm cone-beam CT. The first two follow-up examinations were scheduled at approximately 6 and 12 months post-procedure. For follow-up imaging, we used time-resolved (4D) contrast-enhanced MRA, which reduces metal-related artifacts compared with static MRA techniques and provides improved visualization of the in-stent lumen. Based on follow-up imaging findings, clopidogrel was discontinued 6 months after the procedure, and acetylsalicylic acid was discontinued after 12 months. From patients’ medical records we obtained their detailed medical history. We also obtained their imaging data, including Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) taken prior to endovascular treatment and angio-MRI taken at first and second = follow-up. For incompletely occluded aneurysms we additionally calculated degree of filling, defined as: volume of filling/volume of aneurysm sac · 100%. Aneurysm occlusion was assessed by neuroradiology specialist based on the angio-MRI. For patients treated with FD, total occlusion was determined as class D in O’Kelly-Marotta scale. For patients treated with stent-assisted coiling recanalization was determined as Raymond-Roy scale score > 1. Coils packing density was calculated using AngioCalc (http://www.angiocalc.com/percent_volume.php).

Aneurysm morphology analysis

All morphological analyses were performed based on the DSAs taken prior to aneurysm treatment. Parameters such as dome size in 3 dimensions, neck size and inflow angle were measured by two independent radiology specialists. We then calculated the Dome-to-Neck Ratio (DNR, defined as maximum aneurysm width/aneurysm neck size), Aspect Ratio (AR, defined as maximum aneurysm height/aneurysm neck size) and Size Ratio (SR, defined as maximum aneurysm width/maximum aneurysm height). Using 3D Slicer 4.0 (Kitware Inc., USA), for each patient we prepared a 3D model of the artery harboring an aneurysm. Models of aneurysm domes were then cut at the level of their neck with plane parallel to vessel axis and remeshed. Statistical analysis

The database management and statistical analysis were performed with RStudio version 8.5 for Windows (RStudio Inc., USA).

We used the Shapiro-Wilk test to assess normality. Non-normally distributed variables were normalized using linear or logarithmic scaling, as appropriate. We used the t-test for continuous variables and the χ2 or Fisher’s exact test for proportions. To assess correlation between continuous variables we used Pearson’s correlation tests. To determine factors independently related to aneurysm total occlusion we used logistic regression analysis. Additionally, we employed moderator logistic regression analysis to assess the factors moderating effect of aneurysm morphology on treatment outcome. All significance tests are two tailed and the p-value of < 0.05 has been considered as significant. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± geometric deviation.

Results

Characteristics of included patients

A total of 35 patients have met the inclusion criteria to enter the study group. Thirty-eight patients were recruited to FD control group (including six patients corresponding to three study group participants, each of whom was matched with two subjects from the control cohort) and 35 patients were recruited to stent-assisted coiling control group. Mean age was 57.6 ± 12.2 years, and it did not differ across the groups. Mean aneurysm dome and neck sizes were 6.4 ± 3.6 mm and 5.4 ± 2.6 mm, respectively and did not differ across the groups. In FD groups, female patients constituted 82.9% of study group and 81.6% of control group, while in stent-assisted coiling group a total of 68.6% patients were females. Most common aneurysm location in FD groups was ICA (74.3% and 78.9% for study and control group, respectively) and most common location in stent-assisted coiling group was ACoA (51.4%). About 28% of patients in both FD groups had adjunctive coiling. Average degree of filling in study group was 33.1% ± 26.5% and most common O’Kelly-Marotta grade was B (48.6%). At second follow-up a total of 11 (31.4%) aneurysms from initial study group were totally occluded. Five patients (13.2%) from FD control group were lost-to-follow up. Therefore, at second follow-up, the FD study group consisted of 24 patients and the control group consisted of 44 patients. Types of used FD, depending on aneurysm location are presented on Fig. 2. Factors associated with incomplete occlusion of aneurysms treated with FD

We found significant differences in aneurysm morphology between patients with and without completely occluded aneurysms. At first follow-up, incompletely occluded aneurysms were characterized with significantly higher AR (1.34 ± 0.42 vs. 1.29 ± 0.51; p = 0.03) and SR (1.28 ± 0.28 vs. 1.21 ± 0.28; p = 0.04). These aneurysms also had significantly higher Gaussian curvature entropy (4.03 ± 0.60 vs. 3.73 ± 0.62; p = 0.03) and ZMI energy (545.59 ± 256.22 vs. 271.6 ± 126.19; p = 0.03) (Table 2). In multivariate logistic regression analysis, Gaussian curvature entropy (OR: 2.06; 95% CI: 1.26–13.71; p = 0.04), SR (1.28 ± 0.28 vs. 1.21 ± 0.28; p = 0.04) and ZMI energy were independently associated with higher risk of incomplete occlusion (OR: 3.02 95% CI: 1.35–9.19; p = 0.02). Additionally, we found significant positive correlation of Gaussian curvature entropy (R = 0.42; p = 0.02) and ZMI energy (R = 0.57; p < 0.01) with degree of filling at first follow-up. At second follow-up, incompletely filled aneurysms were characterized with significantly higher Gaussian curvature entropy (4.04 ± 0.53 vs. 3.75 ± 0.64; p = 0.04), mean Writhe number (19.73 ± 29.17vs. −5.77 ± 70.05; p = 0.04) and ZMI energy (656.49 ± 295.24 vs. 293.61 ± 113.72; p = 0.01) (Table 3). In multivariate logistic regression analysis, Gaussian curvature entropy (OR: 2.85; 95% CI: 1.075–9.29; p = 0.04) and ZMI energy (OR: 2.56; 95% CI: 1.13–8.49; p = 0.02) remained independently associated with higher risk of incomplete occlusion at second follow-up. We also found strong significant correlation with ZMI energy (R = 0.76; p < 0.01) and filling degree at second follow-up, while such correlation was not found for Gaussian curvature entropy (R = 0.17; p = 0.53).

Factors related to recanalization of aneurysms treated with stent-assisted coiling

In stent-assisted coiling group, a total of 7 patients (20.00%) had aneurysm recanalization at first follow-up. Aneurysms of these patients were characterized with larger volume (453.99 ± 282.08 mm3 vs. 146.65 ± 161.65 mm3; p = 0.03) and surface (295.34 ± 133.93 mm2 vs. 135.72 ± 104.02 mm2; p = 0.02), as well as with average dome (8.62 ± 2.57 mm vs. 5.65 ± 1.98 mm; p = 0.02) and neck (6.40 ± 1.33 mm vs. 4.98 ± 1.60 mm; p = 0.04) size. In terms of quantitative morphology descriptors, recanalized aneurysms had significantly higher total Gaussian curvature (18.93 ± 15.16 vs. 6.85 ± 4.75; p < 0.01) and total negative mean curvature (−0.79 ± 0.56 vs. −0.67 ± 0.42; p < 0.01) (Table 4). Additionally, significant positive correlation was observed between packing density and total negative mean curvature (R = 0.57; p < 0.01). In multivariate logistic regression analysis, total negative mean curvature (OR: 5.41; 95% CI: 1.18–44.52; p = 0.01) and total Gauss curvature (OR: 2.72; 95% CI: 1.89–11.75; p = 0.04) remained independently associated with higher risk of recanalization. In moderation analysis, Gaussian curvature entropy significantly positively moderated effect of FD treatment on its outcome (OR: 2.56; 95% CI: 1.09–6.39; p = 0.03), while total negative mean curvature negatively moderated effect of FD treatment on its outcome (OR: 0.41; 95% CI: 0.13–0.949; p = 0.03).

Discussion

Our study showed that higher values of AR and SR are significantly related to higher risk of incomplete occlusion at first follow-up. Such results had been shown before [12]. However, in our study impact of SR and AR on total occlusion was not confirmed at the second follow-up, one year after the procedure.

Our study showed significant differences of quantitative morphology descriptors between patients with complete and incomplete occlusion of the aneurysm. Additionally, we found that the descriptors used were poorly mutually correlated, which further proves that they describe different aspects of aneurysm morphology. To the best of our knowledge, such analysis has not been conducted before in terms of endovascular treatment outcome. However, these descriptors have been identified as possible predictors of aneurysm growth or rupture [10, 13]. Interestingly, incomplete occlusion for both analyzed treatment modalities was associated with different types of shape descriptors. Such differences need to be considered in context of their geometrical interpretation. The first of the analyzed descriptors was surface curvature, which could be defined either as Gaussian or as a mean curvature. Gaussian curvature is related to changes of surface normal in both directions – therefore it might be higher in regions of blebs (the smaller, but more prominent bleb, the higher curvature values). Mean curvature is related to normal change in any direction – therefore it could be high just for aneurysms with prolonged shape, or bended in one direction [11]. In terms of curvature measurements, total curvature simply describes its sum, while entropy describes its variability. Therefore, for Gaussian curvature, its higher total value might be related to larger numbers of small and prominent blebs, while its higher entropy might be related to larger number of diversified blebs. Another significant shape descriptor was ZMI energy. That parameter is related to decomposition of shape into sum of so-called ZMIs [14]. Shape consisted of only first ZMI is simply a sphere, while adding further moments makes it more complex and deviated from spherical shape. Higher ZMI energy indicates a larger contribution of following ZMIs in shape description, and therefore is related to more complex (i.e. due to blebs presence) shapes, which deviate from spherical shape.

We found that for patients treated with FD, higher Gaussian curvature entropy and ZMI energy were significantly independently related to incomplete occlusion at both first and second follow-up. Possible explanation of that association might be hemodynamics. As the purpose of flow diversion is to induce aneurysmal thrombosis [2], FD placement results with hemodynamic changes in aneurysmal sac that promotes such process [15]. Computational Fluid Dynamic studies showed that FD placement is related to decrease in intra-aneurysmal blood flow velocity, Wall Shear Stress (WSS) and turbulent flow. Specific flow patterns in parent vessel [16] and aneurysm sac were also shown to be related to degree of thrombus formation [17–19]. To the best of our knowledge, correlation between morphology descriptors we used and hemodynamic conditions inside aneurysm dome were not analysed before. However, aneurysm shape [20], presence of blebs [21] and their location [22] were shown to be related with WSS distribution and flow complexity patterns. Therefore, results of our study suggest that aneurysms with higher Gaussian curvature entropy and ZMI energy, might be characterized by hemodynamics that interferes with blood clot formation after FD placement. Additionally, aneurysmal hemodynamics, particularly within the neck region, play an important role in modulating the endothelialization process. A reduction in WSS within the FD segments in the parent artery and at the aneurysm neck has been shown to facilitate endothelial proliferation [23]. In contrary, persistent residual blood flow across the aneurysm neck can impede neointimal formation [24]. Therefore, increased morphological irregularity of the aneurysm dome, leading to complex and unstable flow patterns at the neck, may compromise the endothelialization process. It might be a risk factor for incomplete aneurysm occlusion [25]. Irregular shape of aneurysm dome couldbalso contribute to FD wall apposition, which is related to worse occlusion rates [26].

In terms of patients treated with stent-assisted coiling, the total Gaussian and negative mean curvature were significant independent predictors of aneurysm recanalization. Similarly to FD placement, explanation for that association might be hemodynamics. Other studies showed that flow patterns inside aneurysm dome, measured prior to its embolization, are predictors of aneurysm recanalization [27]. However, it was also shown that hemodynamic changes after embolization and FD placement significantly differed [28]. Therefore, hemodynamic factors (and thus shape descriptors) different than those in the case of FD treatment, might play a role in aneurysm recanalization after stent-assisted coiling. Another explanation might be significant positive correlation between packing density and total negative mean curvature, which was also shown in our study. This suggests that it might be more difficult to achieve higher packing density for aneurysms with higher negative mean curvature. Even though packing density role in aneurysm treatment outcome remains inconclusive [29], some authors showed that its lower values might be a risk factor of recanalization [30–32]. In our study we also found statistical trend towards such conclusion.

Another interesting findings of our study are those of moderation analysis. They show that aneurysms characterized with higher Gauss curvature entropy are more likely to be incompletely occluded if they are treated with FD instead of stent-assisted coiling, while those characterized with higher total negative mean curvature are more likely to be incompletely occluded if they are treated with stent-assisted coiling instead of FD. Such finding further supports hypothesis that importance of certain shape irregularity aspect might depend on endovascular treatment modality.

Main limitation of our study is a small size of included groups. However, we were able to match recruited patients for common risk factors of incomplete occlusion and therefore minimize their impact. Second limitation is the short follow-up period, particularly for patients treated with FD. Numerous studies have reported achieving total occlusion even a few years after FD treatment. Therefore, in future studies, the effect of aneurysm shape should be assessed over a longer duration, especially following the withdrawal of antiplatelet medications. Another limitation is retrospective nature of our study. We also were unable to match control group for patients treated with stent-assisted coiling. Due to the fact that recanalized aneurysms were re-treated we also did not compare outcomes of treatment modalities at second follow-up. Further prospective studies on larger groups are necessary to support our findings. Despite those limitations this is the first study that shows impact of quantitative aneurysm morphology descriptors on endovascular treatment outcome.

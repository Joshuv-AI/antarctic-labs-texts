Banded Helical Space Regainer—an Innovative Approach for Management of Space Loss: A Case Report
Authors: Neeti Tatiya, Rituraj Kesri, Kirti Pattanshetti, Shreya Sankalecha
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe transitory phase of the mixed dentition to a permanent one is seldom accurate, the hurdles being space loss, tooth size arch length discrepancy, early exfoliation of primary teeth, or premature eruption of permanent teeth. Space regainers are appliances used to regain and maintain space, so as to guide the unerupted tooth into the proper position in the arch. It not only maintains function and preserves arch length, but also maintains esthetics, prevents the development of deleterious oral habits, and eliminates any potential psychological damage a child could face. This case report aims to describe an effective, easy-to-fabricated, and innovatively designed space regainer that showed excellent patient compliance and was superior to the present space regainer designs/appliances.How to cite this articleTatiya N, Kesri R, Pattanshetti K, et al. Introduction

Primary teeth play a vital role in the development of harmonious, functional, and esthetically acceptable occlusion of permanent teeth.1 Hoffding and Kisling reported major space loss due to premature loss of primary teeth.2 The most crucial factor associated with mesial migration of permanent molar is caused due to loss of proximal contact.3 The age range between 7 and 10 years old proves to be best for tipping or bodily moving molars distally to recover lost arch space.4 Maxillary molars have a distalization potential of 5–7 mm per side, while mandibular molar distal movement is limited to 1 or 2 mm per side.3 The prerequisites of proper treatment planning are patient study models, periapical radiographs, clinical assessment, and cephalometric analysis.5 Thus, a new innovative “fixed banded helical retractor space regainer” was considered to destabilize the mandibular first permanent molars. This case report discusses the need for prompt intervention in regaining space in the mandibular arch caused by the early loss of a second primary molar.

Case Description

An 8-year-old female patient presented to the Department of Pedodontics and Preventive Dentistry with a complaint of pain and missing teeth in the left back tooth region for 10 months. The history revealed that the tooth was extracted as it was grossly decayed. Clinical examination revealed prematurely missing 75 (Fig. 1). On radiographic examination it was observed that the permanent molar was mesially inclined and radiolucency was seen with respect to the occlusal surface of 74 involving enamel, dentin, and pulp (Fig. 2). Moyer's mixed dentition space analysis was done and it revealed a space loss of 1.8 mm. The decision of pulpectomy followed by a stainless-steel crown with respect to 74 was made followed by an innovative fixed banded helical type space regainer.

Appliance Fabrication

The first deciduous molar was banded and a molar band with a buccal tube was placed on the first permanent molar. The active component of the regainer consisted of a 4 mm helix and 10 mm active arm that was fabricated using 23-gauge wire on the mesial surface of the first molar and the open end of the active arm was engaged into the buccal tube on the molar band of the first permanent molar. The length of the active arm was the measured space from distal to helix and to the end of the molar tube buccally which was calculated to be 10 mm. Soldering of the retentive arm was done on the buccal surface of the first deciduous molar band (Fig. 3). For alignment of the first permanent molar an additional space of 2.5 mm was included and to avoid soft tissue laceration the retentive arm was given buccally. Postfinishing and polishing the appliance was cemented using type 1 HY-Bond GlasIonomer Cx-Smart (Figs 4 and 5). The appliance was activated by opening the coil of the spring that transmitted force onto the permanent first molar.

Follow-up

The patient was followed up weekly for the activation of the appliance and the required space was regained within 3 weeks (Fig. 6). The appliance was removed (Fig. 7) and a reverse type of crown band and loop space maintainer was delivered (Fig. 8).

Discussion

Management of developing malocclusion requires a thorough assessment of the growth parameter, tooth–jaw size discrepancy, the curve of Spee, overline persistence of bone on the remaining deciduous tooth position, and morphology of adjacent tooth, etc. Whenever teeth are moved orthodontically, the remaining teeth act as anchorage units. The anchoring potential of teeth is determined by a variety of characteristics such number of roots, root size, root form, root inclination, and root length.6 Interceptive orthodontic techniques include removable and fixed space regainers to distalize the mesially drifted first permanent molar.

The fixed space regainers are preferred over the removable space regainers as they have benefits like patient cooperation, chewing habit or oral hygiene maintenance, efficacy, and less treatment time. The retentive arm of the springs soldered onto a deciduous molar band provided anchorage. The active arm length was in compliance with the Adams ratio of 1:3, therefore the efficacy was achieved by the active arm's small range of action in the available space.7

The helix and appliance were placed buccally to promote better accessibility to activate the appliance, avoid hindrance to tongue movement, and better hygiene.8 Fixed maintainers like open coil space regainer are also used to regain space but the major disadvantage is the requirement of a special armamentarium and it is a technique-sensitive approach in comparison to buccally-banded helical space regainer, which is easy to fabricate, is effective, has good control over tooth movement, requires minimal patient cooperation, and is economic. The time required to regain space in open coil space regainer is usually 4 weeks9 and in our case, 2.5 mm of space was regained in 3 weeks.

Conclusion

Space management in mixed dentition is critical in pediatric dentistry. Patient compliance in this innovative banded space regainer was good, the appliance was easy to fabricate, and found to be efficacious in achieving space loss in the short time span.

Key Messages

Early orthodontic interventions done at the beginning of developing dentition help to promote favorable developmental changes. Space maintainers and space regainers are effective in managing space loss.

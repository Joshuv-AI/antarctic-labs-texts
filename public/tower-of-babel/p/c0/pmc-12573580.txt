Effect of pressure-controlled ventilation-volume guaranteed mode on intraoperative respiratory mechanics, oxygenation and postoperative pulmonary complications: a systematic review and network meta-analysis
Authors: Zhilong Liu, Wenxiu Liu, Pei Zhao, Zhijun Ma, Yaping Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVentilation strategies during general anesthesia are crucial for optimizing respiratory mechanics, ensuring adequate oxygenation, and minimizing postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) in surgical patients. This systematic review and network meta-analysis (NMA) aim to evaluate the effect of pressure controlled volume guaranteed ventilation (PCV-VG) in patients undergoing non-cardiothoracic surgery.MethodsWe selected randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to explore the effect of PCV-VG mode on intraoperative respiratory mechanics, oxygenation and PPCs in surgical patients published in English by searching PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane from inception to May 2025. The primary outcome was dynamic lung compliance (Cdyn).ResultsFourteen studies with 2263 patients were included. Compared to volume-controlled ventilation (VCV), PCV-VG had higher Cdyn and lower airway peak pressure (Ppeak). For tidal volume (Vt), partial pressure of carbon dioxide in arterial blood (PaCO2), partial pressure of oxygen in arterial blood (PaO2), oxygenation index (OI), airway mean pressure (Pmean), and PPCs, there were no significant difference among pressure-controlled ventilation (PCV), VCV, and PCV-VG. Compared to VCV, PCV-VG did not significantly affect the values of potential of hydrogen (pH) and partial pressure of end-tidal carbon dioxide (PetCO2). The subgroup analysis only showed that PCV had higher Cdyn than VCV in prone position.ConclusionsPCV-VG was superior to VCV in Cdyn and Ppeak, PCV only achieved higher Cdyn than VCV for prone position. The three modes did not differ in Vt, Pmean, PetCO2, pH, PaCO2, PaO2, OI, and PPCs for patients undergoing non-cardiothoracic surgery.

Introduction

Breathing patterns during mechanical ventilation play a critical role in the management of patients undergoing surgical procedures, influencing respiratory mechanics, gas exchange, and postoperative outcomes [1]. Among the various ventilation modes available, pressure-controlled ventilation (PCV), volume-controlled ventilation (VCV), and pressure-controlled volume guaranteed ventilation (PCV-VG) have gained prominence due to their different mechanisms of delivering breath and their therapeutic implications for patient management [2]. The selection of an appropriate ventilation strategy is paramount to optimize gas exchange while minimizing ventilator-induced lung injury (VILI) [3].

PCV, as a mode that delivers breaths at a preset pressure, allows for a reduced risk of barotrauma and promotes better respiratory compliance by adjusting the tidal volume according to lung mechanics [4]. Conversely, VCV operates by delivering a predetermined volume of air during each breath, potentially offering more predictable tidal volumes but with an increased risk of over-distension in certain circumstances [4]. PCV-VG represents a hybrid approach, combining features of both pressure control and volume support [5]. It guarantees a minimum volume of air while utilizing pressure control mechanics for each breath [5]. This mode may potentially yield benefits from both randomized ventilation strategies, adapting to real-time changes in lung compliance and airway resistance, and thereby minimizing the risk of pulmonary complications [5].

Despite their individual merits, there remains a significant variation in clinical practice regarding the preference for these ventilation strategies largely influenced by institutional protocols and anesthesiologist preference. Schick [6] and Wen [7] provided traditional meta-analysis which was limited to pairwise analyses of two or three interventions, they could not evaluate all available treatment options together. Network meta-analysis (NMA) can be used to combine direct and indirect evidence from trials to help better understand the merits of different interventions and provide objective ranks of various interventions based on the corresponding surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA).

Given the significance of these ventilation strategies in surgical outcomes, the objective of this systematic review and NMA is to consolidate available evidence and conduct a comprehensive comparison of PCV, VCV, and PCV-VG in patients undergoing non-cardiothoracic surgery. By utilizing a NMA framework, we will explore direct and indirect evidence across various trials, thus providing a more nuanced understanding of the comparative efficacy of these ventilation strategies. We anticipate that the findings will contribute to the development of evidence-based guidelines for ventilation strategies, ultimately enhancing patient safety and improving surgical outcomes.

Methods

This study adhered to the PRISMA for Network Meta-Analyses. The protocol was registered in the PROSPERO (CRD42024600668; date of registration: October 23th, 2024). PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library were searched. Our NMA is to evaluate the effect of PCV-VG in patients undergoing non-cardiothoracic surgery, so we selected ‘pressure controlled ventilation’, ‘volume guarantee’, ‘volume guaranteed’, and ‘PCV-VG’ as the search terms. Meanwhile, we identified randomized controlled trials (RCTs) exploring the effect of PCV-VG mode on intraoperative respiratory mechanics, oxygenation and postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs) for patients undergoing non-cardiothoracic surgery published in English from inception to May 2025. The full search strategy of PubMed as representative was shown in supplementary material 1.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Studies were included for satisfying the PICOS principles: population was patients undergoing non-cardiothoracic surgery and receiving mechanical ventilation during general anesthesia (P); the intervention group could only use PCV-VG mode (I); the comparator group could select PCV or VCV mode (C); the primary outcome was dynamic lung compliance (Cdyn), the secondary outcomes included airway peak pressure (Ppeak), airway mean pressure (Pmean), tidal volume (Vt), potential of hydrogen (pH), partial pressure of carbon dioxide in arterial blood (PaCO2), partial pressure of end-tidal carbon dioxide (PetCO2), partial pressure of oxygen in arterial blood (PaO2), oxygenation index (OI), and PPCs (O); study design must be randomized controlled trials (RCTs) (S).

Studies were excluded for the following reasons: (1) the data could not be used for statistical analysis; (2) the data were duplicate or incomplete; (3) unpublished studies, crossover design studies.

Data extraction

Two reviewers independently performed the search process, and all disagreements were resolved through discussions with a third reviewer. The following data were extracted from eligible studies: the first author’s name, year of publication, country, blinding, type of surgeries, operative position, tidal volume (VT), positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), comparison description, sample size, and outcomes. The Cdyn was selected as primary outcome. The secondary outcomes included Ppeak, Pmean, Vt, PetCO2, pH, PaCO2, PaO2, OI, and PPCs.

Assessing the risk of bias and evaluating the quality of studies

We used the Cochrane Collaboration’s tool to assess the risk of bias, which included random sequence generation, allocation concealment, performance bias, detection bias, attribution bias, reporting bias, and other biases [8]. The GRADE was used to evaluate the quality of outcomes, and graded as ‘high’, ‘moderate’, ‘low’, or ‘very low’. We used the CINeMA framework to assess the certainty of evidence.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was carried out in RevMan V.5.3 and Stata 16.0 software, the data were synthesized by random-effects model. The effect estimation of continuous variables was expressed by mean difference (MD) and 95% CIs, and the effect estimation dichotomous variables was expressed by odd ratios (OR) with 95% CI. In addition, we used the online tool to convert the data expressed as median and inter-quartile range to mean and standard deviations (https://www.math.hkbu.edu.hk/∼tongt/papers/median2mean.html). Network geometry maps showed the relationship of all interventions. Forest plots showed the summary effect size and the global heterogeneity, and p < 0.05 or I 2 ≥ 50% was considered significant heterogeneity. The local inconsistency can be found by the results of the node-splitting, and p < 0.05 was considered heterogeneity. The result of netleague showed the relative effectiveness between interventions. The surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) showed the probability to be the best or worst intervention. We used funnel plot to evaluate the publication bias. The sensitivity analysis was performed by removing high-risk studies, and the subgroup analysis was based on operative positions and PEEP levels.

Results

Study selection and characteristics

We identified 298 potentially relevant records by using the search strategy initially. After removing duplicated studies and reviewing titles and abstracts, 15 studies were read in full text. Finally, 14 RCTs were included in the NMA [9–22]. A detailed PRISMA flow diagram is shown in Figure 1. Table 1 listed the major characteristics of these studies. The sample sizes of PCV-VG group was 885 patients, 501 in PCV group, and 877 in VCV group. Using Trendelenburg was the most frequent operative position followed closely by prone and supine. Low Vt ventilation was used in all studies, with 5 PEEP in 7 studies and zero PEEP in 5 studies.

Cdy

Data for this outcome were pooled from fourteen studies with 2263 patients. Figure 2 provided the network plot. We did not find any global heterogeneity (Figure 3) and local inconsistency (Table 2). The PCV-VG seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 92.3, followed by PCV (54.1) and VCV (3.5) (Figure 4). PCV-VG had higher Cdyn than VCV (MD = 6.53, 95% CI: 3.46 to 9.60) (Table 3). The funnel plot did not indicate any publication bias (Figure 5).

Ppeak

Data for this outcome were pooled from fourteen studies with 2263 patients. We did not find any global heterogeneity and local inconsistency. The PCV-VG seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 13.7, followed by PCV (41.4) and VCV (94.9). PCV-VG had lower Ppeak than VCV. All results were shown in Section 1, Supplementary material 2.

Pmean

Data for this outcome were pooled from nine studies with 560 patients. The results showed the existent of global heterogeneity and local inconsistency. The PCV seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 21.5, followed by PCV-VG (47.1) and VCV (81.4). Compared to VCV, PCV-VG and PCV had lower Pmean. All results were shown in Section 2, Supplementary material 2.

Vt

Data for this outcome were pooled from seven studies with 1750 patients. We did not find any global heterogeneity and local inconsistency. The PCV seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 94.0, followed by PCV-VG (32.7) and VCV (23.3). However, there were no significant difference in the relative effectiveness among these interventions. All results were shown in Section 3, Supplementary material 2.

PetCO2

Data for this outcome were pooled from eight studies with 563 patients. There was no significant difference in the relative effectiveness between VCV and PCV-VG (MD = −0.09, 95% CI: −0.54 to 0.36; p = 0.41; I 2 = 3%). The results was shown in Section 4, Supplementary material 2.

pH

Data for this outcome were pooled from four studies with 277 patients. There was no significant difference in the relative effectiveness between VCV and PCV-VG (MD = 0.01, 95% CI: −0.00 to 0.01; p = 0.40; I 2 = 0%). The results was shown in Section 5, Supplementary material 2.

PaCO2

Data for this outcome were pooled from eight studies with 524 patients. We did not find any global heterogeneity and local inconsistency. The PCV seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 2.1, followed by PCV-VG (59.9) and VCV (88.0). However, there were no significant difference in the relative effectiveness among these interventions. All results were shown in Section 6, Supplementary material 2.

PaO2

Data for this outcome were pooled from nine studies with 602 patients. We did not find any global heterogeneity and local inconsistency. The PCV-VG seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 79.9, followed by VCV (48.0) and PCV (22.0). However, there were no significant difference in the relative effectiveness among these interventions. All results were shown in Section 7, Supplementary material 2.

Oi

Data for this outcome were pooled from four studies with 1586 patients. We did not find any global heterogeneity and local inconsistency. The PCV-VG seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 77.0, followed by PCV (51.0) and VCV (22.0). However, there were no significant difference in the relative effectiveness among these interventions. All results were shown in Section 8, Supplementary material 2.

PPCs

Only one RCT [9] with 1364 patients was included. However, there were no significant difference in the incidence of PPCs among PCV-VG (36.9%), PCV (37.6) and VCV (40.1%).

Sensitivity analysis

For Pmean, we performed a sensitivity analysis after deleting two studies [11,15]. Seven studies with 492 patients were included. We did not find any global heterogeneity and local inconsistency. The PCV seemed to be the best treatment because its SUCRA was 26.1, followed by PCV-VG (49.7) and VCV (74.2). However, there were no significant difference in the relative effectiveness among these interventions. All results were shown in Section 9, Supplementary material 2.

Subgroup analysis

For Cdyn, we performed subgroup analysis based on operative positions and PEEP levels (Section 10, Supplementary material 2). The results also showed that PCV-VG had higher Cdyn than VCV in 0 and 5 PEEP groups, supine group, Trendelenburg group, and prone group. Meanwhile, we found that PCV had higher Cdyn than VCV in prone group.

Discussion

In this NMA, three modes did not significantly affect the intraoperative oxygenation and the incidence of PPCs. For intraoperative respiratory mechanics, PCV-VG seemed to be superior to VCV in Cdyn and Ppeak. After excluding two studies with questionable risks, the results of the sensitivity analysis did not indicate that any ventilation mode had a significant advantage in Pmean. The results of subgroup analysis found that PCV-VG had higher Cdyn than VCV in prone position, supine position, Trendelenburg position, and 0 and 5 PEEP. However, PCV only had higher Cdyn than VCV in prone position. The funnel plot indicated lower risk of publication bias. The result of risk of bias showed that this NMA included higher quality studies, so the GRADE of every outcome was upgraded.

At present, the researches related to this topic were only traditional meta-analysis. Wang and colleagues found that PCV had lower peak airway pressure, higher compliance, and lower airway resistance than VCV, but there were no differences in the rest of respiratory parameters [23]. Jiang and colleagues found that PCV had a better oxygenation and lower airway pressure than VCV [24]. Schick and colleagues found that PCV-VG improved airway dynamics in two- and one-lung ventilation, and improved oxygenation in one-lung ventilation in adults [6]. Wen and colleagues found that PCV and PCV-VG provided a lower Ppeak and higher Cdyn but cannot offer better oxygenation than VCV for laparoscopic surgery in the Trendelenburg position [7]. These findings were similar to ours, but these traditional meta-analysis could not integrate both direct and indirect evidence when there were more than two interventions. The NMA precisely overcame this limitation and its SUCRA ranked all of interventions for each outcome. Additionally, the principle of operation might be a main reason why the PCV-VG was superior to the VCV in Ppeak and Cdyn.

The VCV allows ventilator to deliver a predetermined Vt with each breath, regardless of the pressure required to achieve that volume, so there is a risk of lung injury, especially in patients with non-compliant lungs [25]. In PCV, the ventilator will apply a specified pressure during inspiration for a predetermined duration, the actual Vt delivered may vary based on the patient’s lung compliance and resistance [26]. Particularly beneficial for patients with poor lung mechanics or conditions like Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS) as it can help protect against high airway pressures [27]. PCV-VG combines the benefits of PCV with a volume guarantee, its ability to adjust pressure dynamically to achieve target volume ensures that the patient receives adequate ventilation even with changes in lung compliance [28]. It reduces risks associated with inadequate ventilation and helps prevent over-distention of the lungs [28].

The PPCs was defined as the occurrence of at least 1 pulmonary complication among the following: atelectasis, pleural effusion, respiratory failure, respiratory infection, pneumothorax, bronchospasm, or aspiration pneumonitis [29]. It a major cause of postoperative morbidity, mortality, and longer hospital stays. The incidence of PPCs varies widely based on patient and procedural characteristics, with overall reported rates ranging from 5% to 30% [30]. In high-risk surgical populations, such as those undergoing thoracic or upper abdominal procedures, the incidence can be alarmingly high, reaching 40% to 50% [31]. Patient-related factors include advanced age, particularly in those over 65, pre-existing respiratory diseases such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and asthma, obesity (body mass index greater than 30), a history of smoking, and poor functional status or a weakened immune system [31]. Additionally, choices made during the intraoperative phase, such as the use of neuromuscular blocking drugs, can further increase susceptibility to PPCs [32,33]. Our NMA confirmed that ventilation modes could not reduce the incidence of PPCs, which could be explained by the following reasons. Firstly, all patients in included studies had healthy lungs and they underwent the same non-cardiothoracic surgery surgery and position in a same study. Secondly, some parameters such as respiratory rate, exhalation to inhalation ratio, and inhaled oxygen concentration can be adjusted immediately by relying on the visible values of SPO2, PetCO2, PaO2, and PaCO2. Thirdly, all studies used a relatively low Vt (6–8 ml/kg) and low PEEP (0–5 cmH2O), which are far from reaching the threshold to lead to significant damage to lung structure and function during general anesthesia.

This NMA has several limitations that may affect the interpretation and generalizability of the results. First, the limited number of studies included in this meta-analysis resulted in a small sample size, which potentially undermines the statistical power and reliability of the findings. Second, our NMA only included patients undergoing two-lung ventilation, so our findings should not be extrapolated to patients undergoing one-lung ventilation. Third, while subgroup analyses were conducted to explore the effects of posture (prone vs. supine) on different ventilation modalities, the limited number of studies reporting these outcomes and the insufficient statistical significance of the subgroup findings may not fully capture the nuances of ventilation effects in varying positions. Lastly, this study did not assess long-term clinical outcomes, such as patient survival rates and quality of recovery. Thus, while we provide a comparative analysis of PCV, VCV, and PCV-VG under specific clinical conditions, further high-quality, multicenter, and long-term randomized controlled trials are needed to validate these findings and develop more informed ventilation strategies in clinical practice.

In summary, the mode of ventilation did not directly influence the incidence of PPCs in patients with healthy lungs. However, PCV-VG can improve Cdyn and Ppeak, which may serve as key evidence for clinicians in selecting the optimal ventilation mode.

Conclusion

Our systematic review and NMA demonstrate that PCV-VG results in significantly higher Cdyn and lower Ppeak compared to VCV, PCV only achieved higher Cdyn than VCV for prone position. However, the three modes did not differ in Vt, Pmean, PetCO2, pH, PaCO2, PaO2, OI, and PPCs.

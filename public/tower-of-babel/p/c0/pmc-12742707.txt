BBB-penetrant tyrosine kinase inhibitor plus intracranial radiotherapy vs BBB-penetrant TKI alone in Asians with EGFR-mutated lung adenocarcinoma and brain metastases
Authors: You-Cong Chen, Chun-Fu Lin, Chi-Lu Chiang, Huai-Che Yang, Hsiu-Mei Wu, Ching-Jen Chen, Yung-Hung Luo, Yong-Sin Hu, Chung-Jung Lin, Wen-Yuh Chung, Cheng-Ying Shiau, Wan-Yuo Guo, David Hung-Chi Pan, Cheng-Chia Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Osimertinib, a third-generation epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), penetrates the blood–brain barrier and provides intracranial tumor control in non–small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). This study investigated whether combining osimertinib with intracranial radiotherapy (RT) improved outcomes in the patients with EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma and brain metastases.Methods:This is a retrospective analysis included patients who took osimertinib accompanying with or without RT between 2014 and 2022 from a single tertiary medical center. At least one session of magnetic resonance imaging and clinical follow-up after treatment. Outcomes assessed were intracranial local control, intracranial distant control, and overall survival (OS). Kaplan–Meier curves were used for outcome demonstration. Cox regression analysis was also used to identify independent predictors from age, gender, smoking, characteristics of brain metastases, KPS, gene types (presence of T790M), and radiation modalities.Results:A total of 567 brain metastases from 69 patients were enrolled: TKI alone (n = 38) and TKI + RT (n = 31). Intracranial local control was significantly higher with TKI+RT compared with TKI alone (77% vs 23%; p < 0.01) at 3-year follow-up. Intracranial distant control was also longer in the TKI + RT group (23.2 vs 8.7 months in median; p < 0.01). No significant difference in OS was observed between the two groups (p = 0.27). Multivariable analysis demonstrated that the presence of T790M mutation (p = 0.01) and TKI + RT (p = 0.01) were associated with improved intracranial local control, while TKI + RT was also associated with improved intracranial distant control (p = 0.03).Conclusion:The addition of RT to osimertinib enhanced intracranial local and distant control in the patients with EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma and brain metastases, although no survival benefit was observed. Prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings.

1. INTRODUCTION

Lung cancer is a major contributor to cancer deaths worldwide.1 Among patients with non–small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC), 10% present brain metastases at the time of diagnosis and 40% experience brain metastases during their lives.2 The prevalence of brain metastases in NSCLC patients with the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutation can reach 46.7% at 3 years, and 64.7% of these patients harbor brain metastases during their lifetime.3,4 EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have proven more effective than chemotherapy in controlling lung cancer, and can be detected in cerebrospinal fluid with regression of brain metastases.5–8 It has been reported that the most recent (third generation) of TKIs outperform first-generation TKIs in terms of progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).9,10

Intracranial radiation therapy, such as whole brain radiotherapy (WBRT) and gamma-knife radiosurgery (GKRS), is the conventional approach to treating brain metastasis. Combining the first or second generation of TKIs with GKRS or WBRT has been shown to promote intracranial tumor control beyond what can be achieved using the targeted therapy alone.11–14 In one study on 280 patients with EGFR-mutated NSCLC and brain metastases, Chiou et al14 reported that in terms of cumulative tumor control, a combination therapy involving first and second generation of TKIs plus GKRS outperformed TKI alone.

The third generation of TKIs, such as osimertinib, is highly effective in the crossing blood–brain barrier (BBB), resulting in intracranial tumor control rates superior to those of first and second generation TKIs.15–18 These advances have made it feasible to treat NSCLC patients with brain metastases using a single agent; however, the issue of whether combining intracranial radiotherapy (RT) with TKI therapy remains unclear. Thomas et al19 reported no difference between osimertinib alone and a combination therapy of osimertinib with intracranial RT in terms of time to tumor progression or treatment failure among 95 EGFR-mutated NSCLC patients. Yu et al20 reported that when treating patients with this combination of therapies, the time to intracranial progression was longer than when using osimertinib alone. Note that this combined therapy was also shown to significantly improve intracranial PFS and OS rates in the subcohort of patients with oligo-metastases. Pike et al21 reported that upfront GKRS with BBB-penetrant TKI improved intracranial local control, but not intracranial distal control or OS, in TKI-naïve patients with EGFR-mutated or ALK-mutated NSCLC patients and brain metastases. Given the unclear role of intracranial RT in the era of BBB-penetrant TKI, we hypothesized that BBB-penetrant TKI plus intracranial RT may enhance control in brain metastasis from lung adenocarcinoma. Our objective in the current study was to determine whether the addition of intracranial RT would confer benefits in Asian patients with lung adenocarcinoma and brain metastases and EGFR mutation.

2. METHODS

To test our hypothesis, we conducted this retrospective study between August 2014 and July 2022 at a single medical center located at Taipei Veterans General Hospital. Patient data were abstracted from medical records. Patient inclusion criteria included the following: (1) EGFR-mutated lung adenocarcinoma; (2) treatment using osimertinib, and (3) at least one magnetic resonance imaging session revealing brain metastasis before osimertinib initiation. Patient exclusion criteria included the following: (1) Leptomeningeal seeding without any parenchymal metastasis, and (2) lack of follow-up neuroimaging or clinical follow-up. Patients were categorized within two cohorts: (1) Combined osimertinib and intracranial RT (TKI + RT), involving WBRT or single-session GKRS within 3 months of initiation of osimertinib; and (2) osimertinib alone (TKI-alone), without WBRT or GKRS within 3 months of initiation of osimertinib. We prescribed Osimertinib (Tagrisso) 80 mg once a day orally in all patients, and performed either GKRS using the Gamma-knife unit model 4C or Perfexion (Elekta AB) or WBRT using Varian LINAC model True-Beam (Varian Medical Systems, Palo Alto, CA). The dose protocol of GKRS was based on tumor size as suggested by the Radiation Therapy Oncology Group and Japanese study group: 22 to 25 Gray for tumor size ≤20 mm, 18 to 20 Gray for tumor size >20 mm.22,23 The protocol of WBRT was given with a total dose of 30 Gray in 10 fractions. We collected the baseline factors, including the age, gender, race, ever-smoking status, pathological reports, and lung cancer genotype. We also acquired data pertaining to Karnofsky Performance Scale (KPS), the size and number of brain metastases, neurological signs induced by brain metastases, and leptomeningeal seeding at osimertinib initiation. We extracted previous treatments before osimertinib initiation, including medications, surgery, and intracranial or extracranial radiation therapy. GKRS treatment parameters were noted, including tumor volume, margin dose, maximal dose, and isodose level. We recorded the dates for the following events: diagnosis of lung cancer and brain metastases, initiation of osimertinib, progression of brain tumor, last follow-up, and death. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Taipei Veterans General Hospital.

We characterized three possible outcomes based on the duration between the initial use of osimertinib and the corresponding endpoints. The endpoint of intracranial local tumor control was defined as progression of the original tumor at osimertinib initiation, calculated on a per-tumor basis. The endpoint of intracranial distant tumor control was defined as the development of a new parenchymal tumor following osimertinib initiation, calculated and weighted on a per-intracranial-local-tumor basis. The endpoint of OS was death or the last follow-up. We opted not to follow the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors. Rather, we determined the location and timing of brain metastases progression based on the analysis of neuroimaging results by a radiologist.

2.1. Statistical analysis

All statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 26 and GraphPad Prism version 9. Categorical variables between cohorts were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher exact test, and continuous variables were computed using the independent two-sample t test or the two-sample Wilcoxon test. Intracranial local tumor control, intracranial distant tumor control, and OS were analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier (KM) method and the curves were compared using the log-rank test. Univariable and multivariable Cox regression analysis was used to identify possible independent predictors from among the followings: age, gender, smoking, number of brain metastases, maximal dimensions of brain metastases, symptoms induced by brain metastases, leptomeningeal seeding, KPS, previous systematic therapy, craniotomy for brain metastases, GKRS or WBRT 3 months before using osimertinib, Exon 19 deletion, T790M point mutation, L858R point mutation, and intracranial RT (WBRT or GKRS) within 3 months of using osimertinib. Variables with a p value of <0.2 in univariate analysis were selected, and a backward stepwise approach was used to construct a multivariate model based on the value of the Akaike information criterion. The proportional assumption was tested and found to be verified. Hazard ratios (HRs) and corresponding 95% confidence intervals were computed. A p value of <0.05 indicated a difference of statistical significance, and all tests were two-tailed. Missing data was not imputed.

3. RESULTS

Between August 2014 and July 2022, we used osimertinib to treat 69 lung adenocarcinoma patients with brain metastases and EGFR mutation. These patients were categorized as TKI + RT (n = 31 patients) or TKI-alone (n = 38 patients). In the TKI + RT cohort, 25 patients underwent GKRS and six patients underwent WBRT. Table 1 lists the characteristics of patients in the two cohorts. The percentage of patients who underwent prior TKI or chemotherapy treatment was similar: TKI-alone (94.7%) and TKI + RT (93.5%). Patients underwent GKRS with a median margin dose of 19.0 Gy (range 6.0-25.0 Gy), median maximal dose of 29.7 Gy (range 12.0-36.4 Gy), and median isodose level of 70% (range 50%-95%). Patients underwent WBRT with a median total dose of 30 Gy (range 20-30 Gy) and a median fraction of 11 (range 10-12).

We analyzed 567 tumors to assess intracranial local tumor control, and the KM curve between cohorts demonstrated a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001). The control rates in the TKI-alone cohort were as follows: 61%, 54%, and 23% for the TKI-alone cohort, while the rates were 92%, 83%, and 77% for the TKI + RT cohort at 12, 24, and 36 months, respectively (Fig. 1A). The median duration of intracranial local tumor control in the TKI-alone cohort was 24.9 ± 1.8 months, and was not reached in the TKI + RT cohort.

The 567 tumors were also analyzed in terms of intracranial distant tumor control, and the KM curve between cohorts demonstrated a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001) based on events weighted by the number of local tumors (Fig. 1B). The control rates were 29%, 9%, and 4% for the TKI-alone cohort, while the rates for the TKI + RT cohort were 65%, 43%, and 14% at 12, 24, and 36 months, respectively. The median duration of intracranial distant tumor control in the TKI-alone cohort was 8.7 ± 0.2 months, and the median duration for the TKI + RT cohort was 23.2 ± 1.5 months. In terms of OS, we did not observe a significant difference (p = 0.271) between the two cohorts in KM curves. The median survival duration in the TKI-alone cohort was 20.4 ± 1.0 months, and the median duration for the TKI + RT cohort was 26.1 ± 2.5 months (Fig. 1C).

For intracranial local tumor control, the number of brain metastases was negatively correlated with intracranial local tumor control, whereas T790M point mutation, and intracranial RT (WBRT or GKRS) within 3 months of initiating osimertinib (HR = 0.410, p = 0.014, 95% CI, 0.202-0.835) were positively correlated. 4. DISCUSSION

The efficacy of BBB-penetrant TKI in controlling brain metastases in cases of lung adenocarcinoma has prompted interest in the question that whether the efficacy of BBB-penetrant TKI could be enhanced by including intracranial RT. Our objective in this study was to clarify the role of intracranial RT in the lung adenocarcinoma Asian patient population. We obtained convincing evidence to answer the hypothesis that augmenting osimertinib treatment with intracranial RT can enhance intracranial local and distant tumor control; however, these benefits did not translate into improved OS.

The efficacy of third-generation TKIs in controlling NSCLC brain metastases has been linked to its ability to pass the BBB, as supported by both preclinical and clinical studies, which reported the variability of brain-to-blood ratio ranging from 0.16 in real-world data to 3.8 in healthy volunteers.17,18,24 However, the variation in intracranial drug concentrations may potentially limit the effectiveness. Some studies have shown that radiation has synergistic effects on vascular permeability of tumors for drugs such as osimertinib.25,26 In addition to enhancing BBB penetration, TKIs, such as osimertinib, can significantly enhance the radiosensitivity of tumors. Some studies reported that combining osimertinib with RT had synergistic effects in vivo and in vitro, including a shortening of radiation-induced G2/M-phase cell cycle arrest, delays in DNA damage repair, inhibited cell proliferation, and induced apoptosis.27–29 Osimertinib is meant to block the EGFR pathway, and RT generates free radicals via ionizing radiation, which causes DNA damage and induces cytotoxic effects. GKRS has also been shown to destroy small blood vessels and lead indirectly to the deterioration of the tumor microenvironment.30 To summarize, the synergistic effects of combining osimertinib with intracranial RT are expected to promote brain metastases control.

The advent of TKIs provided a novel approach to the treatment of NSCLC with brain metastases; however, there has been a lack of evidence indicating the role of intracranial RT when combined with advanced TKIs. A literature review of osimertinib alone vs osimertinib plus other treatments listed in Table 3.19–21,31–34 While OS remained similar in current studies, the results varied in intracranial PFS, which was possibly related to different design (per-patient or per-tumor analysis), population, or intervention. For example, Thomas et al19 reported no difference (p = 0.51) between osimertinib + RT (WBRT or GKRS) and osimertinib alone in pretreated patients. In contrast, Pike et al21 revealed that upfront GKRS with BBB-penetrant TKI had significant local CNS control (p < 0.001) in TKI-naïve patients. Our results suggest that over a 3-year follow-up, the combination of intracranial RT with osimertinib provides robust intracranial tumor control, exceeding the efficacy of osimertinib alone. However, our findings did not translate into improved OS. Although quality of life or functional outcomes were not provided in this study, neurological symptoms, such as change of consciousness, headache, weakness, or neurological deficits, and the need for salvage operation may be reduced if intracranial lesion was controlled better. Given the emerging evidence of treatment-related cardiotoxicity and other systemic toxicities in targeted or immune-based therapies, comprehensive safety profiling remains essential in multimodal oncologic strategies.35 Management in primary lesions appears to play an important role in the prognosis of OS. Our assessment of intracranial tumor control on a per-tumor basis provides another perspective to evaluate the efficacy of osimertinib alone vs osimertinib plus intracranial RT. Nevertheless, this study was subject to a number of limitations, which should be considered exploratory in generalizing these findings. We observed imbalances in some genes between the two cohorts. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, the treatment before osimertinib, including platinum-based chemotherapy, anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) therapy, previous WBRT or GKRS, exhibited notable but not significant heterogeneity. We were unable to confirm our pathological findings directly via biopsy of brain lesions, which necessitated a reliance on images based on biopsy from lung or lymph nodes. While a sensitivity analysis excluding WBRT showed similar results, heterogeneity in intervention may still obscure meaningful differences in efficacy or side effects. The sample size in the current study was too small to perform score matching. We acknowledge the potential risk of type I error and limited statistical power resulting from multiple variables and small sample size even though the results in bootstrap resampling were consistent with the original model and post hoc power study using detectable effect size indicated sufficient power. Intracranial distant tumor control was calculated and weighted on a per-intracranial-local-tumor basis, such that the effect of patients with a larger number of brain metastases at osimertinib initiation would be overestimated. The treatment plans implemented by physicians were prone to selection bias due to an absence of consensus regarding which patients should receive osimertinib, WBRT, or GKRS and the scheduling of treatment delivery. This study was also subject to detection bias, which is highly dependent on the interpretation of radiologists. The adverse effects of WBRT (eg, neurocognitive decline and leukoencephalopathies) were reported, but we were unable to assess them due to difficulties in dealing with medical records. A standardized protocol based on randomization and a prospective cohort would provide clear results. Note that the results of four clinical trials comparing osimertinib and osimertinib plus GKRS (NCT03535363, NCT05033691, NCT03497767, and NCT03769103) are pending.

The efficacy of osimertinib therapy was enhanced by combining with intracranial RT in intracranial local and distant tumor control among Asian patients with EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma and brain metastases, highlighting the potential role of RT in managing brain metastasis in the era of osimertinib; however, no benefits were found in OS. The benefits of augmenting osimertinib treatment with intracranial RT will need to be verified in a prospective, randomized study.

Steam Shaping of Angled-Tip Aspiration Catheter to Improve Deliverability in Treating Acute Ischemic Stroke
Authors: Clint Badger, Annie Trang, Souvik Singha, Matthew Arnold, Shyam Majmundar, Timothy Miller, Dheeraj Gandhi, Jacob Cherian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES:Innovations in the treatment of large vessel occlusion strokes include maximizing the speed and success in clot retrieval, but there is limited exploration of steam shaping to angled-tip aspiration catheters. The objective of our study was to assess the outcomes and efficacy of steam-shaped angled-tip aspiration catheters in conjunction with a nonwire advancement technique.METHODS:Our case series included patients with anterior circulation large vessel occlusion using a nonwire advancement technique with a steam-shaped angled-tip aspiration catheter (Zoom 71TM Imperative Care, Inc.). Outcomes were aspiration passes, time to first pass and recanalization, degree of final reperfusion (thrombolysis in cerebral infarction), and adverse outcomes.RESULTS:Of the 10 cases analyzed, 60% were men, with a median age of 70 (IQR 62.3-75.5) and a median National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score of 22.5 (IQR 19.3-25.0). Overall, 70% had singular occlusions, whereas tandem occlusions included 1 internal carotid artery (ICA)-M1 and 2 ICA-M2 cases. Most (60%) had a cavernous carotid siphon angle type II. First pass took 11.5 minutes (IQR 10.0-15.0), and recanalization took 20.0 minutes (IQR 15.8-25.8) with an average of 2.2 passes. For singular occlusions, 57.1% of cases required 2 or fewer passes. The tandem ICA-M1 occlusion needed only one pass at each location, whereas both ICA-M2 occlusions required 1 ICA pass with ZoomTM 71 with a pass at M2 with ZoomTM 45. Overall, 80% of cases achieved a final thrombolysis in cerebral infarction score of 2C or greater. There were no intraoperative complications, although 4 cases presented with postoperative complications and 1 death unrelated to the procedure. In all cases, the aspiration catheter was able to be delivered without the use of another device.CONCLUSION:Steam shaping of angled-tip catheters seems to perform superiorly when utilizing a nonwire advancement technique without compromising patient safety.

Large vessel occlusion stroke is a severe form of ischemic stroke leading to significant neurological deficits and increased morbidity and mortality.1 Rapid and effective intervention is crucial to restore cerebral blood flow and minimize brain damage. Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) has emerged as a pivotal treatment modality, revolutionizing stroke care with its ability to quickly and effectively remove clots.2

First pass effect (FPE) has gained prominence as a critical factor in the success of EVT. Achieving complete or near-complete recanalization on the first attempt is associated with better clinical outcomes and reduced complication rates.3 This focus has led to the development of innovative variations in thrombectomy technique, which are critical for enhancing success rates.4,5 As research and technology continue to evolve, the potential for improved outcomes for patients remains promising, underscoring the importance of continued innovation and adaptation in stroke care.6,7

We present 10 consecutive cases performed by a single fellowship-trained neurosurgeon at our institution using nonwire advancement with a steam-shaped ZoomTM 71 aspiration catheter (Imperative Care, Inc.). The Zoom 71 is a flexible, angled-tip aspiration catheter. In this article, we combined the advantages of the angled tip with the strategic steam shaping of the distal end to permit advancement from the cervical to the intracranial circulation without a luminal guide wire or microcatheter construct.

METHODS

Research Ethics

Deidentified data may be made available on reasonable request to the corresponding author. This study was approved by our hospital's institutional review boards with waiver of informed consent. Data were reported in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.8

Patient Selection and Data Collection

We identified all patients who underwent EVT for an anterior circulation ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion utilizing a steam-shaped Zoom 71 aspiration catheter at our institution from June 2024 to September 2024. We collected patient baseline characteristics, including the location of the occluded vessel, carotid siphon angle, and the time last known well.9 Stroke metrics obtained were the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale. Procedural data included the time to procedure completion, number of passes, thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (TICI) score, intraoperative complications, and adverse outcomes.

Procedural Technique

All procedures were performed by a single fellowship-trained neurosurgeon. General anesthesia was used in all procedures. Femoral access was obtained utilizing standard micropuncture technique. Eight French access was used in all cases, and the target vessel was catheterized with various diagnostic catheters chosen based on patient-specific anatomy.

Although the patient was prepared for thrombectomy, a Zoom 71 aspiration angled-tip catheter was steam shaped. The catheter was shaped utilizing the tip of a vertebral curve diagnostic catheter as a mandrel. The shaped angle was specifically kept opposite the angled tip of the aspiration catheter (Figure 1A-1F).

A Zoom 088 100 or 110 cm guide catheter was delivered to the distal cervical internal carotid artery (ICA). After removing the diagnostic catheter, a steam-shaped Zoom 71 aspiration catheter was advanced into the cervical ICA. Without the use of a guidewire or microcatheter construct, the Zoom 71 was brought into the midcavernous ICA (Figure 2A and 2B). Next, the Zoom 88 guide was navigated further cranial into the proximal cavernous ICA. The Zoom 71 was then further advanced to the site of occlusion where aspiration thrombectomy was applied using a 60 cc Vac Lock syringe as previously described (Figure 3).10

Outcomes

Outcomes assessed included minutes to first pass and to recanalization, number of passes, intraoperative complications, and adverse outcomes. TICI scores were also collected.

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Categorical variables were reported as frequency (%). Parametric variables were assessed for normality with the Shapiro-Wilk test and reported as mean with SD.11 Non-normally distributed parametric variables were reported with median and IQR. All statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.12.0; Python Software Foundation).

RESULTS

Baseline Characteristics

Of the 10 identified cases, most patients were men (60%) with a median age of 70 years (62.3-75.5). The median National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score was 22.5 (IQR 19.3-25.0). Seventy percent of cases were singular occlusions, primarily occurring at M1. Cases with tandem occlusions occurred at the ICA-M1 and ICA-M2. Most patients had a carotid siphon angle type II (60%) (Table 1).

Outcomes

The median time to contact was 3.8 hours (IQR 1.7-5.8). The median time from puncture to first pass was 11.5 minutes (IQR 10.0-15.0) and to recanalization was 20.0 minutes (IQR 15.8-25.8), requiring an average of 2.2 passes. For singular occlusions, 57.1% of cases required 2 or fewer passes. For the case with tandem ICA-M1 occlusion, Zoom 71 was passed only once at each location. Both cases with tandem ICA-M2 occlusions required 1 pass at the ICA with Zoom 71 with another pass at M2 with Zoom 45. Totally, 80% of cases achieved a final TICI score of 2C or greater (Table 2). In all cases, the steam-shaped Zoom 71 was able to reach the site of occlusion without the use of another device.

There were no intraoperative complications, specifically no dissections, arterial perforations, or vasospasm encountered. However, 4 cases presented with post procedure adverse outcomes. These complications included oral bleeding without known source, occlusion of an ICA stent, hemorrhagic conversion requiring hemicraniectomy, and development of large infarct resulting in patient mortality after they were made comfort measures.

DISCUSSION

Multiple thrombectomy techniques have been previously described to improve FPE and time to reperfusion by improving delivery of aspiration catheters. These include the classic microwire/microcatheter construct, tapered delivery catheters designed to aid in the navigability of aspiration catheters, intracranial macrowires, and nonwire advancement techniques.1,4,5,12-15

This case series was designed to understand the procedural outcomes and efficacy of a steam-shaped Zoom 71 aspiration catheter. We demonstrate that steam-shaped angled-tip aspiration catheters have the potential to improve on procedural times and final recanalization by simplifying the catheter stack without an increase in intraoperative complications.

The Zoom 71 aspiration catheter has been shown to yield an average of 2 passes due to its angled tip and increased surface area for aspiration.16 In this series, steam shaping the ZoomTM 71 with its angled tip in a downward orientation, aids in catheter trackability around tortuous cervical and cavernous ICA anatomy. It also improves navigation of beyond the ophthalmic artery origin without the use of a micro or macrowire. Easier and more expeditious navigation to the site of occlusion without the use of other catheters and wires has the potential to greatly improve time to first pass.17 In our hands, the energy needed to deliver a steam-shaped angled-tip aspiration catheter is appreciably lower compared with the use of the catheter without shaping. We speculate that this may be in part due to an enhanced rotational element along the angled tip that occurs because of the introduced proximal shape.

The use of traditional microwires in stroke is becoming less common as new technologies, such as tapered delivery catheters and macrowires, become available.12,15 Previously, the benefits of steam shaping of standard tip aspiration catheters have also been evaluated in the setting of a guidewire.18 In this article, we propose a further advancement where steam shaping of an angled tip aspiration catheter permits delivery without a wire to achieve safe and effective outcomes.

The time to FPE has been shown to greatly impact outcomes in stroke patients.3 Simplifying the thrombectomy procedure by minimizing the amount of equipment used has the potential to improve the speed and cost of the procedures. In our series of 10 patients, the steam-shaped Zoom 71 was able to reach the site of occlusion without the use of an additional device in all cases. Interestingly, when a secondary medium vessel occlusion was encountered and a smaller aspiration catheter (Zoom 45) was needed, we were unable to advance the smaller, non–steam-shaped catheter without the use of a wire.

Complications seen post procedure in our study are not unique to steam-shaping but rather reflect the general risks of endovascular treatment in the acute ischemic stroke population.19-21 Our findings also present comparable mortality in the utilization of steam-shaped and non–steam-shaped aspiration catheters. Our study did not collect data on patient comorbidities to determine the association with risk of postprocedural complications or mortality.

Limitations

This study has several limitations, primarily with a limited sample size with data collected from a single institution. There may have also been selection bias because patients were not randomly assigned. Our study did not evaluate for long-term outcomes in our patient population. As a single-neurosurgeon study, there is reduced variability in operator skill but a learning curve effect on steam shaping may have occurred as the study progressed. Further study is required to understand the potential synergistic mechanics of steam shaping aspiration catheters with angled tips. Comparative studies evaluating the efficacy of steamed catheters at specific anatomic sites and procedural costs may also inform future practice.

CONCLUSION

This case series demonstrates that advancement of steam-shaped angled-tip aspiration catheters without the use of a wire or microcatheter did not compromise patient safety nor increase mortality. It may have the potential to improve time to reperfusion by minimizing the number of materials needed for thrombectomy procedures and improving efficiency in reaching the site of occlusion.

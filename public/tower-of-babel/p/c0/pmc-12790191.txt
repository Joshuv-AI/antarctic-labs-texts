Late-Onset Pericardial Effusion and Device Extrusion After Left Atrial Appendage Occluder Implantation
Authors: Omar Darwish, Sai Nikhila Ghanta, Sandeep Randhawa, Srikanth Vallurupalli, Tushar Tarun, Leah Brazil, John Streitman, Subodh Devabhaktuni
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWe present a case of Watchman device extrusion from left atrial appendage (LAA) free wall 6 months after implantation in a patient with longstanding persistent atrial fibrillation.Case SummaryA 74-year-old male with longstanding persistent atrial fibrillation of 15 years duration was implanted with a Watchman device as he cannot be on long-term oral anticoagulation due to a history of multiple falls. Six months post implantation, repeat transesophageal echocardiogram revealed a thrombus on the epicardial aspect of LAA and a large posterior pericardial effusion. The patient subsequently underwent surgical removal of both the LAA and the protruding Watchman device.DiscussionIn the case we present, the patient developed a clinically significant pericardial effusion 6 months after the Watchman device implantation, accompanied by left atrial extrusion, which was not reported to our knowledge.Take-Home MessageComplications of Watchman devices respect no timeline boundaries and should be kept in mind when evaluating cardiac tamponade in patients with an implanted Watchman device.Key words: atrial fibrillation, pericardial effusion, watchman device complications

History of Presentation

A 74-year-old male presented with sudden worsening dyspnea 6 months after Watchman device implantation. The patient reported no chest pain, hemoptysis, lower-extremity swelling, cough, fever, or dizziness. Aside from mild tachycardia, he was hemodynamically stable. Physical examination was significant for an irregularly irregular heart rate with no wheezing, crackles, or distended neck veins. The patient was uncomfortable in bed and unable to lie flat.

Past Medical History

The patient had a significant medical history of hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and atrial fibrillation (AF). The patient was diagnosed with AF 15 years ago and had undergone multiple trials of antiarrhythmic medications, along with 2 direct current cardioversion attempts, but AF persisted. He was initially on Apixaban for anticoagulation (AC) therapy, given his Congestive heart failure, Hypertension, Age ≥75 years, Diabetes mellitus, Stroke, Vascular disease, Age 65–74 years, and Sex category (female) score of 3 (age, hypertension, and diabetes). However, the patient experienced multiple falls and increased unsteadiness. His Hypertension, Abnormal renal/liver function, Stroke, Bleeding history or predisposition, Labile INR, Elderly, and Drugs/alcohol concomitantly score was 2 (age and hypertension), placing him at moderate risk of major bleeding. After a shared decision with the patient, it was decided to proceed with Watchman device implantation.

The procedure was uneventful, and the patient was discharged home on apixaban and aspirin. A transesophageal echocardiogram (TEE) performed during the procedure (Figure 1) confirmed successful device placement without any pericardial effusion (PE). A repeat TEE 45 days later showed stable placement of the Watchman device with no thrombus, leaks, or PE (Figure 2, Figure 3, Figure 4). Accordingly, the decision was made to stop apixaban and to start clopidogrel for a total of 6 months post procedure. Five months after implantation, the patient presented with chest pain and shortness of breath, A transthoracic echocardiogram revealed a small to moderate PE without signs of tamponade (Figure 5). The Watchman device remained stable with no complications, and the patient was diagnosed with pericarditis and treated as per guidelines before being discharged.

Differential Diagnosis

Given the patient's significant history and Watchman device implantation, the differential diagnosis included pericarditis with recurrent PE, device-related thrombus, device embolization or migration, device leak or malposition, and pulmonary embolism.

Investigations

A TEE showed a large posterior PE and a large thrombus attached to the epicardial side of the LAA (Figures 6 and 7), along with color flow acceleration from the device into the pericardium.

Management

This complication necessitated surgical excision of the device and LAA due to protrusion of the Watchman device through the atrial wall (Figures 8 and 9).

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient was discharged to a skilled nursing facility for recovery. He was then sent home with no complications. He was seen in the clinic and was doing well with no recurrent dyspnea or chest pain.

Discussion

AF is considered the most common clinically significant heart arrhythmia, with an estimated prevalence of 0.95% in the U.S. population.1 AF carries a substantial risk for stroke and other systemic embolic events, which are typically managed with AC or, if AC is contraindicated, with left atrial appendage (LAA) occlusion device. The Hypertension, Abnormal renal/liver function, Stroke, Bleeding history or predisposition, Labile INR, Elderly, and Drugs/alcohol concomitantly score, along with the patient's clinical condition, is commonly used to assess the safety of AC. The Watchman device is one of the Food and Drugs Administration–approved devices in the U.S. for LAA occlusion device.2 However, placement of the Watchman device carries a risk of postprocedural complications, particularly PE.

Percutaneous LAA closure is a key therapeutic approach to reduce the risk of stroke in patients with AF who are unable to take anticoagulants or are at high risk of major bleeding. PE is a well-recognized complication following Watchman device placement and is considered the most common complication, with an estimated incidence of 3.4%.3 However, the incidence of PE that requires drainage is lower, around 1.1%.3 A recent prospective study by Galea et al. (2024)4 estimated the incidence of PE to be approximately 4.3% within the first year after the procedure. The study also categorized the timeline for PE development, classifying it as “early” if it occurred within 7 days of placement and “late” if it occurred between day 8 and 1 year post procedure.

According to Galea et al,4 early PE occurred in about 3.3% of cases, with the majority (71%) being clinically relevant that needed further intervention. Notably, over 70% of these early cases developed within 6 hours of the procedure. In contrast, late PE had an incidence of <1%, with more than 80% of these cases occurring within 6 months of device placement. Four PEs were clinically relevant, and 1 PE required surgical intervention.

In this patient, and as previously noted, we followed the AC protocol based on the PROTECT AF trial.5 The patient received aspirin and apixaban 5 mg twice daily for the first 6 weeks, followed by clopidogrel and aspirin for a total of 6 months, and then continued on aspirin 81 mg daily indefinitely.

In the case we present, the patient developed a clinically significant PE 6 months after the Watchman device implantation, accompanied by left atrial extrusion which was not reported to our knowledge. As noted in the other large registry, one PE required surgical intervention.4 This highlights the importance of considering PE as a potential late complication in patients with LAA device closure who present with chest pain or shortness of breath.

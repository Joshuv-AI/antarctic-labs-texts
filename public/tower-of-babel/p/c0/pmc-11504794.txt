Improving access to utilize the healthcare services facilitated by ASHAs, ANMs and medical officers of PHC among scheduled tribe pregnant women: An interventional study
Authors: Anju Damu Ade, Visweswara Rao Guthi, Nagaraj Kondagunta, D. S. Sujith Kumar, Ravisankar Deekala, Chandrasekhar Vallepalli, T V D Prathyusha
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIntroduction:Early marriages, early childbirth, low BMI and high incidence of anaemia are known factors for high maternal mortality among tribal pregnant women.Materials and Methods:This was a mixed method study (qualitative and quantitative) undertaken to evaluate educational intervention package among tribal pregnant women in Chittoor District of Andhra Pradesh State in South India. A baseline household survey with the help of a semi-structured questionnaire was conducted in both intervention group (n = 109) and control group (n = 119) of ST habitations. An educational intervention was implemented through health education approach to bring about awareness and behavioural change among pregnant women regarding healthcare practices and utilization of health services. Qualitative data collection tool included semi-structured FGD. Quantitative data was analysed using SPSS version 26.0. Content analysis was done for qualitative data.Results:The findings of this study show that awareness and utilization with respect to various services facilitated by ASHAs, ANMs and medical officers are improved postintervention among tribal pregnant women, and the various reasons for non-utilization of services were lack of awareness, long distance of health facility, no transportation, poor socio-economic status, inadequate facilities at PHC, etc.,Conclusion:This study shows that intervention to improve awareness and health services utilization, and continuous health education should be imparted to all the pregnant mothers. For this healthcare providers like ASHA and ANM and medical officers should be trained and motivated to educate the community.

Introduction

Over 104 million tribal people live in India spread across 705 tribes and account for 8.6 per cent of the country’s population.[1] Tribal people have remained marginal geographically, socio-economically and politically and therefore, health and healthcare in tribal areas remained unsolved problems.[2] No recent estimates for maternal mortality among tribal women are available separately. Early marriages, early childbirth, low BMI and high incidence of anaemia are known factors for high maternal mortality. The 68.0 per cent rate of institutional delivery is much lower than the national average of 78.9 per cent. Coverage of postnatal care remains poor. Only about 37 per cent of tribal women reported receiving any postnatal care within 48 hours after home delivery.[1] Underweight is most prevalent among the tribal women with no education (19.29%) and lowest among the tribal women with higher education (11.48%).[3] 65% of tribal women in the 15–49 years age group suffer from anaemia. The rate of institutional delivery is the lowest among tribal women (70.1%).[4]

Tribal women with poor health and nutrition are more likely to give birth to low weight infants.[5] Because of prevailing culture and traditional practices in India, the health and nutritional status of women becoming worse affected. It is a striking feature that though the tribal women in India suffer from high levels of morbidity and mortality, they do not generally seek medical aid from facilities such as health centres.[6] Primary care physicians understand how social determinants affect nearly every aspect of a person’s health and well-being. They act as a healthcare partner, educator, advocate and navigator.[7] Only availability of healthcare facility does not indicate a good health of the people of that particular area, if the people do not utilize this facility.[8] The health-seeking behaviour of the tribe is a complex, interrelated and interdependent process framed in a medical pluralistic context.[9] Algur K. et al.[10] analysed NFHS-4 (2015–2016) data for their study and found that despite continuous and improving efforts of the government, the utilization of MCH services is very low among tribal women in the Empowered Action Group (EAG) states of India.

In the above context, this study was undertaken to assess the impact of intervention on utilization of health services facilitated by ASHAs, ANMs and medical officers of PHC among tribal pregnant women under the project, ‘improving the healthcare access among scheduled tribes: Implementation Research in Chittoor District, Andhra Pradesh’ funded by ICMR.

They are mainly rural farmers, some work as dairy farmers, agriculturists, nomadic hunters and traders, sellers, etc.

Statistical Analysis: Data was entered in the Microsoft Excel sheet, and the descriptive statistics was used in the form of frequencies and proportions for categorical data, mean and standard deviation for continuous data. McNemar test was used to compare the difference between two paired proportions. Z test was used to compare the difference between two proportions of end line utilization practices between intervention and control groups. IBM SPSS version 26 was used to calculate statistics. The association was considered to be statistically significant with P < 0.05. Content analysis was done for qualitative data.

Data Quality Assurance: Pre-test was done prior to the actual data collection. Data collectors (recruited staff) were trained in data collection procedures.

Ethical consideration: Approval from the district authorities for conducting the survey in the district. The study was conducted after securing approval of proposal by the Institutional Ethical Committee. (Roc. No. AS/11/IEC/SVIMS/2017 vide IEC No. 899 dated 27.05.2019). Written permission was obtained from medical officers of PHC. Permission was obtained from local community representatives to work with ASHAs and to collect data in their areas. Written consent was sought from each mother, they were informed about the purpose of the study, verbal consent was obtained before interview, and confidentiality of tribal pregnant women was ensured.

Intervention: A comprehensive educational intervention package was developed. A first draft module was developed using resource material on maternal and child health practices with the help of the Government of India, WHO and UNICEF publications. Topics addressed in the module were regarding care during pregnancy like antenatal visits, danger signals during pregnancy, birth preparedness, institutional delivery, skilled birth attendance, delivery complications, home care practices, breastfeeding, utilization and benefits of government MCH programmes and schemes. Content of the module was validated. After the validation, the module was translated into Telugu by language expert. The Telugu version of the module was converted into picture cards which were used as an educational tool to talk with pregnant women while imparting the health education intervention.

The objectives of educational intervention:

To bring about awareness and behavioural change among tribal pregnant women regarding healthcare practices and to increase utilization of government health services and schemes.

Implementation phase: To impart Intervention, health education was provided through group meetings (ten meetings) where participatory learning was ensured. These meetings were monthly once or twice for the period of 8 months. To impart education, picture cards, handouts, whiteboard, educational videos on MCH care practices, role plays and skits were used.

The baseline surveys were conducted as soon as the pregnant women were identified to be registered in the project.

Results

Focus group discussion (Result baseline survey)

According to tribal pregnant women, the most common health problem of pregnant women is anaemia. They replied that almost all the tribes in small habitations are suffering as they get many difficulties to reach PHC at night in the event of a medical emergency as PHC is far away and also transportation facilities are not available. With respect to availability of the healthcare services in their area, they reported that their community has access to emergency medical services like 104, 108 and 102. ASHA workers and ANMs visit their houses and provide them with information about antenatal care (ANC) and postnatal care (PNC). Women prefer ASHAs and ANMs for maternal healthcare services during pregnancy. Women reported that they received counselling and other services from medical officer, ANM and ASHA.

When asked whether they follow the instructions of MCP card during antenatal and postnatal period, they replied that some tribal women are illiterate, and therefore they don’t understand the directions on the MCP card. Also, they replied that illiterate should have knowledge regarding government schemes and services.

For their medical treatment and delivery of pregnant women, everyone almost they prefer community health centre over PHC because it offers a wider variety of high-quality services and many people had good experiences with CHC’s services even if they have to travel a long distance.

They responded that tribal people in certain settlements don’t respond to outreach efforts or show interest in learning about various facilities or government schemes.

According to them, services at primary health centre (PHC) are inadequate and patients usually will be referred to community health centre or nearby hospitals. Also, they responded that PHC should have better facilities and should be maintained clean and it should be located within walking distance of their community.

Discussion

In India’s rural healthcare delivery system, auxiliary nurse midwives (ANMs) and accredited social health activists (ASHA workers) are the primary field-level frontline officials who come into direct contact with the population. They bear a large portion of the burden of carrying out health services.[11]

In the present study, in the end line survey, there is significant improvement in utilization of services and benefits with respect to services from ASHA like health education regarding healthcare practices, antenatal check-ups, use of mother and child protection cards, what investigations to be done during pregnancy and importance of institutional delivery, postnatal care and village health and nutrition day. Joseph J et al.[12] concluded in their study, that the services received by the tribal mothers from ASHAs during their last pregnancy are somewhat adequate to fulfil their maternal health needs during pregnancy. But the coverage of services given by ASHAs at the time of delivery and during the postnatal period was inadequate as compared to the services received by the mothers during pregnancy.

Also with respect to services by ANM, in the end line survey, there is significant improvement in utilization of services and benefits provided under JSY and PMMVY scheme, birth preparedness among tribal pregnant women was improved. Awareness about institutional deliveries and village health and nutrition day was also increased.

In the end line survey, there is significant improvement in utilization of services and benefits facilitated by medical officer with respect to services like regular antenatal check-ups, management of anaemia and getting all investigations done during pregnancy. Also awareness regarding institutional delivery, family planning and use of referral services increased among these women postintervention.

We found that overall awareness and utilization with respect to various services facilitated by ASHAs, ANMs and medical officers is improved postintervention. Similarly in a study conducted by Choudhury M et al.,[13] awareness regarding antenatal care like five cleans (5Cs), two shots of tetanus vaccine, reproductive tract infection, HIV testing, consumption of prescribed dosage of iron tablets increased in the intervention group.

With respect to the utilization of maternal healthcare services, Adhikari T et al.[14] revealed in their study that utilization was very poor among scheduled tribe women, varying from about 4 per cent in Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan to 10–14 per cent in Chhattisgarh and Odisha while a study by Jose JA et al.[15] showed that maternal healthcare services were utilized by 85.7% of tribal women compared to 100% among non-tribal antenatal women and the difference was statistically significant (P = 0.02).

In the present study, as reported in the FGD that reasons for non-utilization of maternal health services facilitated by ASHA, ANMs and medical officer are lack of awareness which is again due to illiteracy among some of these women. Other reasons were long distance of health facility (PHC) from their habitation, no transportation facility, poor socio-economic status, inadequate facilities at PHC, etc.

Similarly, Mohindra KS et al.[16] revealed in their study that factors which deter tribal group from using services are their perception that the quality of the public healthcare system is poor, costs of medicines, travel costs and no sources of income for them. Also an another study found that socio-demographic factors such as education of mother, father and socio-economic status of the family were significantly associated with the utilization of antenatal care services[17]

Adhikari T et al.[14] showed that utilization was highest among tribal women with level of education 9th class and above (15–28%). Deb R et al.[18] also revealed that various socio-economic and cultural factors are responsible for the acceptance of antenatal care in tribal community.

Establishing a successful healthcare system that assures positive health outcomes, efficiency and effectiveness requires integrating health promotion techniques into normal clinical care. Primary care physician plays a key role in this aspect since it serves as an individual’s first point of contact with the healthcare system and is distinguished by continuity, comprehensiveness and coordination.[19]

Conclusion

The findings of this study revealed that awareness and utilization with respect to various services facilitated by ASHAs, ANMs and medical officers is improved postintervention among tribal pregnant women. The reasons for non-utilization of services were lack of awareness which is again due to illiteracy among some of these women. Other reasons were long distance of health facility, no transportation, poor socio-economic status and inadequate facilities at PH. To increase awareness and utilization of health services, continuous health education should be imparted to all the pregnant mothers. For this healthcare providers like ASHA, ANM and medical officers should be trained and motivated to treat community.

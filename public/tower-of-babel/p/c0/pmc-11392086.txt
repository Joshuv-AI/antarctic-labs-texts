Letter to Editor: ‘The predictive role of hematological inflammatory markers on the prognosis of kidney injury’
Authors: Yanfei Yang, Yongquan Gao, Yuhang Liu, Guang Tan
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to see the predictive role of hematological inflammatory markers on the prognosis of kidney injury; as the author has written, blood inflammatory markers may have a certain predictive effect on kidney injury. Here, we have some comments we would like to discuss with the author.

Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) is a clinical indicator of vascular disease. When assessing the overlap of 44 proteins between the natural kidney CKD (chronic kidney disease) and post-kidney transplant CKD groups, the following proteins have been found to be significantly associated with uACR: Desmocollin-2 (DSC-2), cystatin C, fatty acid-binding protein, liver type (FABPL), regenerating islet-derived protein 4 (REG-4), IGFBP-6, CD59, and hepatocyte growth factor A2, A4, and A5, as well as EFN-B2. Moreover, in addition to hypoxia, there is evidence that inflammation induces EFN-B2. The EFN-B2 promoter is responsive to nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), a ubiquitous pro-inflammatory transcription factor that plays a significant role in atherosclerosis. Therefore, an increase in EFN-B2 levels may reflect underlying inflammation and atherosclerosis. Consistent with its role in atherosclerosis, EFN-B2 is upregulated in human atherosclerotic plaques and expressed in endothelial cells at the site of arteriosclerosis in mice. Additionally, some data suggest that it interacts with the EphB2 receptor on monocytes, thereby contributing to the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Detecting the quantity of these pro-inflammatory factors has a positive significance for the prevention of kidney injury1.

At the same time, mast cell chymase prevents acute ischemic kidney injury by limiting the excessive activation and recruitment of neutrophils. So, we can determine the extent of kidney injury by detecting the enzyme’s content2.

Finally, we again thank the author for their contribution regarding the relationship between blood inflammatory markers and acute kidney injury, and we look forward to this article reaching greater heights.

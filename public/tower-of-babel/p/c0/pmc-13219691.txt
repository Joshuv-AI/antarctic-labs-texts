Efficacy of Remibrutinib versus Dupilumab at Early Timepoints in Chronic Spontaneous Urticaria: US Phase 3b Study Design (RECLAIM)
Authors: Gil Yosipovitch, Giselle Mosnaim, Christine-Elke Ortmann, Nicole Griswold, Jane Mast, El-Djouher Martzloff, Jonathan Rodrigues, Jason E. Hawkes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionRemibrutinib, an oral, highly selective Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor, was recently approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU). Dupilumab, an interleukin-4 receptor-α monoclonal antibody, was approved by the FDA for CSU in early 2025. Here, we report the design of RECLAIM (NCT06868212), a phase 3b study that aims to compare the efficacy of remibrutinib and dupilumab at early timepoints in patients with symptomatic CSU despite treatment.MethodsThis US-based, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy study will include approximately 400 patients with moderate-to-severe CSU who remain symptomatic despite treatment with second-generation H1-antihistamines (sgH1-AHs). Patients will be randomized 1:1 to receive either oral remibrutinib (25 mg twice daily) or subcutaneous dupilumab (600 mg loading dose; 300 mg every 2 weeks), in addition to sgH1-AHs. The study consists of a screening period of up to 4 weeks, a 12-week core treatment period, and a safety follow-up period of up to 12 weeks. Patients with chronic inducible urticaria or other skin diseases with symptoms of urticaria, angioedema, or chronic itching, and patients with prior use of remibrutinib, BTK inhibitors, or dupilumab, are excluded. The primary endpoint is absolute change from baseline in weekly Urticaria Activity Score (UAS7) at week 4. Secondary endpoints include: absolute change from baseline in UAS7 at week 1 and in weekly Itch Severity Score and Hives Severity Score at week 4; achievement of well-controlled disease (UAS7 ≤ 6) at weeks 2 and 4; complete disease control (UAS7 = 0) at week 4; and safety assessments.Planned OutcomesRECLAIM will evaluate the early efficacy of remibrutinib versus dupilumab in patients with CSU. The study is currently recruiting patients.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier, NCT06868212.

Introduction

Background

Chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU) is characterized by the spontaneous appearance of itchy wheals (hives), angioedema, or both, lasting for > 6 weeks and without a specific trigger [1]. The symptoms of CSU can have a substantial impact on sleep, work performance, productivity, and health-related quality of life [2–5], and it affects approximately 0.23–0.78% of the US population [3, 6]. For many patients, CSU is a long-term condition, with 5- and 10-year remission rates reported to be under 50% [7–9].

CSU is primarily driven by degranulation of skin mast cells, causing the release of histamine and other proinflammatory mediators that lead to the symptoms of CSU [10]. CSU pathogenesis is complex; it may involve the activation of skin mast cells through the high-affinity immunoglobulin-E (IgE) Fc epsilon receptor I (FϲεRI) by two main pathways: an IgE-mediated pathway, defined as “autoallergic,” and a primarily IgG-mediated pathway, defined as “autoimmune” [10, 11].

The American Academy of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology/American College of Allergy, Asthma, and Immunology Joint Task Force on Practice Parameters (AAAAI/ACAAI JTFPP) 2014 guidelines for the management of urticaria recommend second-generation H1-antihistamines (sgH1-AHs) as first-line treatment for the management of CSU [12]; however, more than 60% of patients continue to have symptoms while receiving standard dose sgH1-AHs [13, 14]. In patients who remain symptomatic, guidelines recommend up-dosing of sgH1-AHs up to fourfold the label dose [1, 12], although approximately 75% of these patients continue to experience partial or no symptom relief [13]. Subsequent recommended lines of therapy include omalizumab or cyclosporine [1, 12]. Short-term (1–3 weeks) use of systemic corticosteroids may be required to control symptoms. However, the guidelines do not recommend long-term corticosteroid use in CSU [12].

International guidelines for the management of urticaria by the European Academy of Allergy and Clinical Immunology (EAACI), the Global Allergy and Asthma Excellence Network (GA2LEN), the European Dermatology Forum (EuroGuiDerm), and the Asia Pacific Association of Allergy, Asthma and Clinical Immunology (APAAACI) recommend that urticaria be treated as quickly and as safely as possible, and that treatment should be escalated after 2–4 weeks of inadequate disease control with up-dosed sgH1-AHs, or sooner if symptoms are intolerable [1]. Despite this, patients with CSU report long treatment journeys, which involve cycling through a range of treatments and seeing multiple specialists [15]. In summary, for many patients, CSU is inadequately controlled by sgH1-AHs, and there is a high unmet medical need for treatment options that provide rapid symptom relief.

Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK), a member of the Tec family of tyrosine kinases, is a cytoplasmic signaling protein expressed in a variety of immune cells, including mast cells and basophils; these two cell types are involved in the pathogenesis of CSU [10]. BTK is implicated in both autoallergic and autoimmune pathogenesis of CSU, as it acts downstream of FϲεRI to induce cellular activation, degranulation, and the subsequent release of proinflammatory mediators that are the primary drivers of CSU symptoms [10]. B-cell autoantibody production, driven by BTK signaling, may also play a key role in the pathogenesis of CSU [10]. Remibrutinib is an oral, highly selective BTK inhibitor that blocks BTK-mediated mast cell and basophil degranulation, preventing the release of histamine and other proinflammatory mediators that cause the itch, hives, and angioedema associated with CSU [10, 16].

In the REMIX-1 (NCT05030311) and REMIX-2 (NCT05032157) phase 3 studies, remibrutinib showed a favorable safety profile, symptom improvement as early as week 1, and sustained efficacy in patients with CSU [16]. On 30 September 2025, remibrutinib received US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval for the treatment of CSU in adult patients who remain symptomatic despite H1-AH treatment [17].

Dupilumab is a monoclonal antibody that inhibits interleukin (IL)-4 and IL-13 signaling by blocking IL-4 receptor-α (IL-4Rα) [18]. Both IL-4 and IL-13 may play a role in CSU pathogenesis through the direct and indirect activation of mast cells and other cells implicated in CSU [18, 19]. Dupilumab demonstrated safety and efficacy in patients with CSU in LIBERTY-CSU CUPID study A and study C (NCT04180488) [19, 20]; however, study B, which included patients who were intolerant or incomplete responders to omalizumab, did not meet its primary efficacy endpoint [19]. Dupilumab received FDA approval in April 2025 for the treatment of patients aged 12 years and older with CSU who remain symptomatic despite H1-AH treatment [21].

Trial Design

RECLAIM (NCT06868212) is a phase 3b head-to-head study that aims to evaluate the efficacy of remibrutinib compared with dupilumab in early symptom control for patients with CSU who remain symptomatic despite treatment with sgH1-AHs.

Objectives

RECLAIM will evaluate efficacy at early timepoints only, providing a direct comparison of the ability of remibrutinib and dupilumab to achieve rapid symptom relief for patients with CSU, thereby helping to inform clinical decision-making.

Methods

Study Design

RECLAIM is a US, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, phase 3b study to compare the efficacy of remibrutinib and dupilumab at early timepoints when administered as an add-on treatment to sgH1-AHs. The study will take place across approximately 130 sites and consists of a 7- to 28-day screening period, a 12-week core treatment period, and a safety follow-up (Fig. 1).Fig. 1RECLAIM study design. In the core treatment period, eligible patients will be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either remibrutinib or dupilumab, in addition to sgH1-AH background therapy. Patients in the remibrutinib group will receive remibrutinib as a 25 mg tablet taken orally twice daily from day 1 to week 12, as well as a placebo solution for injection in a prefilled syringe, administered as two subcutaneous injections at baseline, followed by one injection every other week from week 2 to week 10. Patients in the dupilumab group will receive dupilumab in a prefilled syringe administered as a 600 mg loading dose (two 300 mg dupilumab subcutaneous injections) at baseline, followed by a 300 mg dupilumab subcutaneous injection every other week from week 2 to week 10, as well as a remibrutinib-matching placebo tablet taken orally twice daily from day 1 to week 12. All patients will also receive background therapy of a stable, standard label dose of sgH1-AH (cetirizine 10 mg, fexofenadine 180 mg, or loratadine 10 mg tablets) throughout the core treatment period, starting at least 7 days prior to randomization through to week 12. In addition to this background therapy, patients will be permitted to take up to three additional doses of the same medication as rescue therapy, for a maximum daily sgH1-AH intake of fourfold the standard label dose. Following the core treatment period, patients will enter the 12-week safety follow-up period, which will include follow-up phone calls at weeks 16 and 24.

Planned Outcomes

The primary objective of the RECLAIM study is to demonstrate that oral remibrutinib is superior to subcutaneous dupilumab at early timepoints in patients with CSU inadequately controlled by sgH1-AHs. The primary endpoint is absolute change from baseline in weekly Urticaria Activity Score (UAS7) at week 4.

Measurements

The UAS7 score is derived from the sum of the HSS7 and ISS7 scores, which are calculated as the sum of the daily HSS and daily ISS scores over 7 days, respectively. These daily scores will be calculated as the average of the morning and evening HSS and ISS scores. If either the morning or evening score is missing, the available score will be used as the daily score. During each study week, if a patient has at least 4 daily scores available, the HSS7 or ISS7 will be calculated as the sum of the available scores for that week, divided by the number of days with available scores, multiplied by 7. If a patient has fewer than 4 daily scores available during the study week, then the HSS7 or ISS7 will be considered missing for that week, and no UAS7 can be calculated. Participants who discontinue early from study treatment for any reason will be encouraged to stay in the study following the procedures. Every effort will be made to continue to follow participants who discontinued from study treatment, and data collected after study treatment discontinuation will be used in the analysis.

Data Collection

UAS7, ISS7, and HSS7 scores will be derived from data recorded by the patient using an Urticaria Patient Daily Diary (UPDD). The UPDD will be completed twice daily to record itch severity, number of hives, use of rescue medication, sleep and activity interference, and angioedema occurrence and management, as well as any contact with a healthcare professional. UAS7 has a possible range of 0–42, with a score of 42 representing the highest urticaria activity. Angioedema Activity Score will be recorded once daily if triggered by a report of angioedema in the UPDD. Although use of rescue medication will be tracked in the UPDD, efficacy analyses will not be adjusted on the basis of the frequency of rescue medication use. The investigator or designated site staff will maintain an accurate record of the dispensing of background and rescue medication, and monitoring of rescue medication accountability will be performed by field monitors during site or remote monitoring visits. All drug accountability and reconciliation data will be recorded in the IRT system per the study protocol.

Data Analysis

For all efficacy analyses, the full analysis set (FAS) will be used, which will include all patients who have been randomized to a treatment group. Mis-randomized patients will be excluded from the FAS. For all safety analyses, the safety set (SAF) will be used, which will include all patients who received at least one dose of study treatment.

For the primary endpoint, the statistical hypothesis will test that the absolute change from baseline in UAS7 at week 4 in the remibrutinib group is not different from the dupilumab group, versus the alternative hypothesis that the remibrutinib treatment effect is different from dupilumab and is superior if the direction of the results favors remibrutinib. An analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model will be used to estimate treatment differences for change from baseline in UAS7 at week 4 in the FAS. The ANCOVA model will include treatment group, baseline UAS7 score, and randomization strata variable (prior exposure to anti-IgE biologics) as covariate. Continuous secondary endpoints will be analyzed with an ANCOVA model, and binary secondary endpoints will be analyzed with a logistic regression model. These models will also include the randomization strata variable of prior exposure to anti-IgE biologics. A multiple comparison procedure will be used to control the type I error rate at the two-sided 0.05 significance level for testing the null hypothesis for the primary and secondary endpoints.

A sample size of 200 patients in each treatment arm was considered to achieve at least 86% power for the primary and secondary endpoints at the two-sided 0.05 level. This sample size was calculated using nQuery 9.2.0 and R software to give a nominal power of > 99% to detect a difference between remibrutinib and dupilumab, provided the mean change of UAS7 from baseline to week 4 is at least 5.9 in favor of remibrutinib, with common standard deviation of approximately 12, on the basis of a two-sample t-test, assuming type I error 0.05 (two-sided). These figures were derived from the primary endpoint analyses of previous phase 3 studies of remibrutinib and dupilumab.

Discussion

RECLAIM will be the first head-to-head study to directly compare the early efficacy of remibrutinib and dupilumab for CSU. Dupilumab is a monoclonal antibody that blocks IL-4Rα, inhibiting the signaling of IL-4 and IL-13. IL-4 and IL-13 signaling promotes the production of IgE, which binds FcεRI on the surface of mast cells [19, 22]. Cross-linking of FcεRI-bound IgE drives mast cell degranulation and production of inflammatory mediators [22]. Thus, by blocking IL-4Rα, dupilumab indirectly prevents the autoallergic, IgE-driven release of mediators that lead to CSU symptoms [11, 19, 22]. Conversely, remibrutinib blocks BTK-mediated degranulation of mast cells and basophils downstream of FcεRI, preventing the release of these inflammatory mediators, independent of autoallergic or autoimmune pathogenesis of CSU [10, 16, 23]. Therefore, the mechanism of action of remibrutinib is expected to be effective regardless of the underlying cause of CSU.

With patients who remain symptomatic despite standard treatment, CSU can have a substantial impact on quality of life, with sleep, work performance, and mental health often affected [2–5]. A noninterventional, cross-sectional study found that patients with CSU with inadequate disease control reported a significantly higher overall negative impact on health-related quality of life than those with controlled disease, with the highest impact on mental and emotional well-being [4], highlighting the unmet need for more effective treatments. To address this, the RECLAIM study will compare the efficacy of remibrutinib and dupilumab at early timepoints in patients with CSU who remain symptomatic despite treatment with sgH1-AHs.

Prior studies have demonstrated the efficacy of both remibrutinib and dupilumab in CSU. Remibrutinib showed greater improvement in UAS7 at week 12 compared with placebo in both REMIX-1 (least squares mean [LSM] change ± standard error [SE], −20.0 ± 0.7 with remibrutinib and −13.8 ± 1.0 with placebo; p < 0.001) and REMIX-2 (LSM change ± SE, −19.4 ± 0.7 with remibrutinib and −11.7 ± 0.9 with placebo; p < 0.001) [16]. Unlike the primary endpoint for the REMIX studies, which was measured at week 12, the primary endpoints in the dupilumab LIBERTY-CSU CUPID studies were measured at week 24. Dupilumab showed greater improvement in UAS7 at week 24 compared with placebo in LIBERTY-CSU CUPID study A (LSM change ± SE, −20.5 ± 1.8 with dupilumab and −12.0 ± 1.8 with placebo; p < 0.001) [19]. Additionally, in LIBERTY-CSU CUPID study C, dupilumab significantly improved ISS7 and UAS7 versus placebo at week 24 (LSM change in ISS7, −8.6 with dupilumab and −6.1 with placebo, p = 0.02; LSM change in UAS7, −15.9 with dupilumab and −11.2 with placebo, p = 0.02) [20]. However, in LIBERTY-CSU CUPID study B, which included patients with CSU who were intolerant or incomplete responders to omalizumab, although a numerical trend was observed, the primary endpoint of change from baseline in ISS7 at week 24 was not met [19].

Change from baseline in UAS7 was selected as the primary endpoint for this study because it is a well-established and validated tool. The 2021 international EAACI/GA2LEN/EuroGuiDerm/APAAACI guidelines for the management of urticaria also recommend UAS7 for the assessment of disease activity and treatment response in real-world clinical practice, as well as in clinical trials in patients with urticaria [1]. The use of UAS7 facilitates comparison of clinical study results, as it is widely used across different clinical trials including the remibrutinib and dupilumab phase 3 trials [16, 19, 20].

The 4-week timepoint for the primary endpoint was selected as a clinically meaningful and relevant timepoint because guidelines recommend that urticaria is treated as quickly and as safely as possible [1]. A US-based, noninterventional qualitative study of patients with CSU and treating physicians reported that patients can experience a pattern of cycling through a variety of treatments and seeing several different specialists for their condition [15]. Even after long wait times, some patients who had been treated by a specialist reported returning to their primary care physician after feeling that their treatment was not effective, leading to frustration and a loss of confidence in their healthcare team [15]. Similarly, physicians who treat CSU reported that their patients have a sense of urgency around their treatment, and the physicians themselves experienced frustration with the patient care pathway and available treatment options [15]. This demonstrates the unmet need for CSU treatments that are not only effective, but also provide rapid symptom relief.

In clinical trials, remibrutinib had a rapid onset of action, with an improvement in symptoms as measured by change from baseline in UAS7 seen as early as week 1 in both REMIX-1 (LSM change, −11.3 with remibrutinib and −4.0 with placebo) and REMIX-2 (LSM change, −11.3 with remibrutinib and −2.9 with placebo) [16]. By contrast, in LIBERTY-CSU CUPID study A and study B, this level of improvement in UAS7 was not observed until week 4 with dupilumab [19]. Therefore, the early 4-week timepoint for the primary endpoint of RECLAIM was chosen to compare the speed of symptom improvement between remibrutinib and dupilumab.

Strengths and Limitations

RECLAIM will be the first head-to-head study to directly compare the early efficacy of remibrutinib and dupilumab for CSU. A limitation of RECLAIM is that it was not designed to compare efficacy between remibrutinib and dupilumab beyond week 4, so cannot provide a comparison of the long-term, sustained efficacy of these treatments in CSU. An additional limitation of RECLAIM is that patients will not be stratified by biomarkers such as IgE levels; therefore, this study cannot demonstrate whether patients with different CSU endotypes respond differently to remibrutinib or dupilumab treatment. However, data from the REMIX and CUPID studies showed that both remibrutinib and dupilumab improved symptoms in patients with CSU, independent of baseline IgE levels [24, 25].

Furthermore, this study limits the maximum proportion of patients with prior exposure to anti-IgE biologics for the treatment of CSU to approximately 30% of the total study population, and the study will not include a cohort of patients who are refractory to anti-IgE biologics, thus the patient population may not be representative of all patients with CSU, who may have cycled through multiple therapies. In the REMIX studies, remibrutinib showed greater efficacy versus placebo irrespective of prior exposure to and treatment failure with anti-IgE biologics in patients with CSU, whereas in the dupilumab LIBERTY-CSU CUPID study B, which included patients who were intolerant or incomplete responders to omalizumab, the primary endpoint was not met [19, 26].

RECLAIM is currently recruiting, and the efficacy and safety data from this randomized controlled study will provide a direct comparison of the use of remibrutinib and dupilumab for rapid symptom relief in patients with CSU inadequately controlled by sgH1-AHs, which will help to inform clinical decision-making.

Ethics and Dissemination

This study was designed and will be implemented, executed, and reported in accordance with consensus ethical principles derived from international guidelines, including the Declaration of Helsinki and Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) international ethical guidelines, applicable International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) Tripartite Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice (GCP), and other applicable local regulations. All study patients will be required to sign a statement of informed consent. Results of this trial will be submitted to a peer-reviewed journal after the conclusion of the trial.

Using Rasch Analysis for Validation of Knowledge and Perception of Orthopedic Workplace-Based Assessment among Postgraduate Orthopedic Trainees’ Questionnaire
Authors: Ahmad Anas Abdulmajid, Kamarul Ariffin Khalid
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Introduction/Background:Workplace-based assessment (WPBA) was formally introduced into orthopedic postgraduate training in Malaysia as of December 2022. Understanding users' knowledge and perception (KAP) of WPBA is essential for ensuring its effective implementation. A systematic review is needed to evaluate whether WPBA is meeting educational expectations.Objective:This study aimed to validate a questionnaire designed to assess the knowledge and perception of WPBA among postgraduate orthopedic trainees.Materials and Methods:A pilot study was conducted from Aug 1, 2022 to May 31, 2023 involving a 28-item questionnaire that had undergone thorough face and content validation. The questionnaire was distributed to orthopedic postgraduate trainees at the Department of Orthopaedics, Traumatology and Rehabilitation, Kulliyyah of Medicine, International Islamic University Malaysia (IIUM). Rasch analysis was performed to evaluate the psychometric properties of the instrument.Results:The Rasch analysis revealed a person reliability index of 0.93 and a separation index of 3.62, while the item reliability index was 0.99 with a separation index of 8.32. The mean infit and outfit mean square (MNSQ) values were 0.99 and 0.96 respectively, with Z-standard (Z-STD) values of 0.24 and -0.35. Several misfitting items fell outside the acceptable range (0.6–1.4) and were subsequently removed from the questionnaire.Conclusions:The validation and reliability testing confirmed that the orthopedic WPBA KAP questionnaire recently developed is both reliable and valid for assessing postgraduate trainees' understanding of WPBA.

INTRODUCTION

Workplace-based assessment (WPBA) has emerged as a pivotal tool in assessing clinical competence and professional behavior within the realm of everyday practice. Its strength lies on its evaluation on actual performance in real-life clinical scenarios.[1] WPBA also offers a framework for monitoring progression and offers feedback mechanism for medical practitioners improvement.[2] This method of assessment was first reported in the medical curricula of the United Kingdom in 2005.[3] Since its introduction, WPBA has seen rapid adoption across the globe. China embraced this approach within its postgraduate training in 2017, whereas Australia had already adopted it in 2010.[24] In Malaysia’s context, the implementation of WPBA is a relatively recent development, with the orthopedic postgraduate training incorporating this assessment tool starting in December 2022.

As Malaysia’s orthopedic postgraduate training program reaches the halfway point of its implementation, it is imperative to conduct a timely review to ensure it does not deviate from its original objectives. Studies have confirmed that the success of WPBA is notably dependent on the acceptance by the users, which is intrinsically linked to their understanding and perception of the system.[5] A systematic way to collect these data is through knowledge and perception (KAP) questionnaires. However, currently, there is no validated and reliable questionnaire specifically tailored to assess the knowledge and perception of orthopedic workplace-based assessments among postgraduate orthopedic trainees. This study seeks to fill this void by exploring the validity and reliability of a previously developed questionnaire designed to gauge the knowledge and perception of WPBA among these trainees.

SUBJECTS AND METHODS

Study design

A 28-item questionnaire, which had previously been subjected to rigorous face and content validation, was administered to a selected cohort of 55 respondents, comprising orthopedic postgraduate trainees from years 1 to 4 at the Department of Orthopaedic, Traumatology, and Rehabilitation, Kulliyyah of Medicine (KOM), International Islamic University Malaysia (IIUM). In determining the sample size, we relied on the count of students actively enrolled in the Department of Orthopaedic, Traumatology, and Rehabilitation, KOM, IIUM. Nonetheless, it aligns with the suggested range for a pilot study, falling between 25 and 100 respondents. The pilot study was conducted using a hardcopy questionnaire, with guidance provided by the principal researcher. Participants were not restricted by any time limit during the completion of the questionnaire. Written informed consent was obtained from all study participants prior to answering the questionnaire. Ethical approval was obtained from the IIUM Research Ethics Committee on 19 July 2022, prior to starting data collection.

Data analysis

The data collected underwent Rasch analysis using Winsteps software version 3.72.1 to evaluate the psychometric properties of the questionnaire. This analysis included assessing dimensionality and the fit of items and individuals to the model. Following Linacre[6] and Bond and Fox,[7] a minimum separation index value of 2 and item and person reliability values of at least 0.7 were sought. For validity studies, parameters such as mean square infit and outfit values (MNSQs-infit/outfit) and the point-measure correlation coefficient (PTMEA Corr) were examined. Items exhibiting index values outside the normal range were identified as misfitting and flagged for potential removal. The cutoff values for all the indices are summarized in Table 1.

RESULTS

The questionnaire was distributed to 55 respondents, resulting in the return of 53 questionnaires, yielding a response rate of 96.4%. Among the 53 returned questionnaires, one was deemed incomplete and thus excluded from analysis. Therefore, 52 questionnaires were included for further analysis.

Reliability

The person reliability, which assesses the reliability of the person measures, was 0.93, indicating a high level of consistency among the items. The separation index was 3.62, suggesting a moderate level of differentiation between individuals with different levels of the construct. The analysis revealed strong internal consistency among the items, with an item reliability of 0.99. Moreover, a high level of differentiation between individuals based on the items was indicated by a separation index of 8.32.

Fit statistic

The overall misfit of items was assessed, and the mean infit mean square (MNSQ) was 0.99 and 0.96 for outfits, while those of Z-standard (Z-STD) were also acceptable with values of −0.24 and −0.35, respectively. These findings suggest that, on average, the items exhibit a reasonable fit to the Rasch model. The infit and outfit MNSQ values are close to 1, indicating a relatively good fit. The analysis found several misfitting items with values lower or higher than the accepted range (0.6 to 1.4), including items from Domain III (Items 2, 6, 7, and 9) and Domain IV (Items 10, 11, and 14). The remaining 17 items were found to have a good fit for the model. Table 2 summarized the analysis of fit statistics and item polarity.

Item polarity

Item polarity was assessed through the point correlation measure (PTMEA Corr). In our investigation, the PTMEA Corr value for each item was positive, enabling the retention of all items without necessitating any revisions. These outcomes indicate that the questionnaire items accurately measure the intended constructs and are consistent with the stated objectives.

Dimensionality

The total observed variance was 51.307, which represented 100% of the variance in the responses. Analysis using the Rasch model accounted for 34.307 (66.9%) of this total variance, underscoring the model’s efficacy in explaining a significant portion of the response variability, as depicted in Figure 1. Analysis of variance sources highlighted that individuals’ responses contributed 16.873 (32.9%) to the total variance, while the items contributed 17.435 (34.0%), both which underscore their importance in capturing the construct’s variability. However, a residual unexplained variance of 17.000 (33.1%) remains, pointing to the existence of additional factors affecting the observed responses. This calls for further research to identify these factors and mitigate potential measurement errors. Nonetheless, 33.1% of unexplained variance, which is the variance attributable to the first contrast, constituted merely 8.6%.

DISCUSSION

The evaluation of questionnaires for construct validity and reliability frequently involves classical test theory (CTT) and item response theory (IRT). However, an alternative method is Rasch analysis. The Rasch method offers a robust framework for questionnaire analysis in which it is able to consider both the characteristics of respondents and the properties of test items. It not only evaluates the quality of each questionnaire item but also identifies items and respondents that diverge from model expectations, therefore offering a holistic view of the questionnaire’s efficacy.[8] For that reason, we opted for Rasch analysis over CTT and IRT for assessing our questionnaire’s construct validity.

Despite our study’s relatively modest sample size of 52 postgraduate trainees, which is small in comparison with other questionnaire validation efforts, the use of Rasch analysis proved advantageous. Rasch analysis is particularly suited for studies with limited sample sizes. Furthermore, Rasch analysis also highlighted the influence of sample size on the reliability index. Remarkably, our study achieved a high reliability index, affirming the adequacy of our sample size for the intended objective.

For further validating the reliability of our questionnaire, we also calculated the Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, obtaining a value of 0.92. This result significantly surpasses the established benchmark for questionnaire validation and therefore confirm the reliability of our questionnaire even when we analyzed using Rasch analysis.[9] Such findings reinforce the notion that the item and person reliability measures derived from Rasch analysis are credible alternatives to traditional reliability metrics typically employed in CTT.

A critical aspect of our Rasch analysis involved identifying items that were not fitting well with the expected model parameters. Our analysis revealed seven such items, which were then eliminated from the questionnaire. The elimination of misfit items is evidence of the rigorous steps adhere to in this questionnaire validation study to achieve an instrument that is both reliable and valid within the context of its future intended usage.

This study marks the initial effort to validate a questionnaire designed to evaluate the knowledge and perception (KAP)s of WPBA among orthopedic trainees. Conducted with rigorous attention to detail, the study’s in-depth analysis significantly contributes to the questionnaire’s validity. This approach ensures that it truly represents the intended constructs when in use later. A key limitation of this study, however, is the exclusive participation of trainees from the IIUM. Future studies can be enhanced by engaging a diverse range of trainees from multiple institutions. Such inclusivity would enrich the research with a variety of perspectives and experiences.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, the study’s rigorous methodology and detailed analysis contribute significantly to validating the questionnaire, ensuring it effectively represents the intended objectives. This study not only validates the first questionnaire for assessing the KAP of orthopedic WPBA among orthopedic trainees but also highlights the efficacy of Rasch analysis in the questionnaire validation. With meticulous steps, we have developed a reliable and valid tool for assessing KAP of orthopedic WPBA among orthopedic trainees, and researchers will be able to adopt this quality instrument in their research.

Key messages

The validated WPBA KAP questionnaire for orthopedic postgraduate trainees, analyzed using Rasch methods, demonstrates high reliability and validity. The analysis is able to refine the questionnaire’s precision and usability in orthopedic training programs. This validated questionnaire will effectively assesses trainee knowledge and perception, ensuring WPBA implementation aligns with educational goals and expectations.

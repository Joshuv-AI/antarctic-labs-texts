Cervical cancer prediction using deformable kernel darknet-53 and depth wise separable convolutional neural networks
Authors: Gaurav Kumar Ameta, M Siva Ramkumar, G Jenifa, M Sivaramkrishnan, Nivethitha Thangavelsamy, Mohamed Ghouse, T M Yunus Khan, Mohammad Amir Khan, Adis Abebaw Dessalegn
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe prediction of Cervical Cancer (CC) remains a tough task due to diverse clinical variations and unbalanced data distribution, while good-quality data remains limited. Early CC signs tend to lack distinct characteristics, which makes their precise identification more challenging for medical professionals. Different methods of diagnosis integrated with the unique attributes of different datasets make it difficult to apply a uniform model to generalized patient populations. To address these issues, this paper introduces the Deformable Kernel Darknet-53 with Depth-Wise Separable Convolutional Neural Network (DK-D53-DWSCNNet). This framework initiates with image enhancement and quality adaptation for diverse heterogeneous morphological structures using Deformable Kernel Networks for Joint Image Filtering (DKNet-JIF). An ensemble Darknet-53 Convolutional Neural Network with a Contextual Attention Network (D53-CNN-CAN) is used to perform segmentation, which improves the representation of variable lesion patterns. A hybrid GAT-DWSCNNet, which merges geometric algebra transformers (GAT) with depth-wise separable CNNs, is used for feature extraction and classification, allowing the system to capture both edge and contextual features while reducing redundancy. The Hyperbolic Sine Optimizer (HSO) optimizes model training by providing the best trade-off between convergence speed and accuracy. With an accuracy of 99.9% and sensitivity of 99.8%, the experiments on the Herlev and SEER datasets depict remarkable generalization. These findings demonstrate the potential of DK-D53-DWSCNNet in improving early and robust CC prediction across different datasets, supporting the model to be embedded in clinical diagnostic procedures.Keywords: Cervical cancer, Deformable kernel networks, Herlev, Hyperbolic sine optimizer, SEERSubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) account for about 90% of CC cases. CCC is the fourth most common cancer in women globally, after lung, colorectal, and breast cancers. The World Health Organization (WHO) anticipates that 604,000 new cases and 342,000 deaths from CC occurred in 20201,2. Compared to 25 years ago, the average age at diagnosis is now 55, which is 15 years younger. It is noteworthy that approximately 25% of diagnoses are made to women below the age of 35. Cervical carcinoma is also among the most frequent gynecologic malignancies during pregnancy, occurring between 0.1 and 12 times per 10,000 pregnancies3. The incidence of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) and CC has significantly declined in industrialized countries since the introduction of the Papanicolaou smear (Pap smear) screening in 1971 and the release of the human papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine in 20064. Because of this, CC is today the thirteenth most common cancer among women in high-income nations like Germany. Despite these advancements in prevention, morbidity and mortality rates have not substantially declined in the past decade, and treatment approaches continue to vary widely5.

In 1996, the World Health Organization, the European Research Organization on Genital Infection and Neoplasia, and the National Institutes of Health Consensus Conference all explicitly recognized HPV’s role in the development of CC6,7. HPV genotypes are divided into three groups based on their correlation with invasive cancers: high-risk, low-risk, and unknown-risk. CC is tightly linked to high-risk genotypes, including types 16, 18, 31, 33, and 358. Types 6, 11, 40, 42, and 43 are examples of low-risk genotypes that are usually linked to benign epithelial lesions, such as oropharyngeal and anogenital warts, or to no disease at all. Undetermined-risk genotypes are those whose oncogenic potential is uncertain. CC remains a significant burden despite international efforts to enhance screening and prevention9. With over 500,000 new cases and over 300,000 fatalities in 2018, it was the third most prevalent cancer in women. In 2020, CC remained one of the most prevalent three cancers that affected women worldwide, when the COVID-19 pandemic was at its worst, with a significant rise in cases and deaths10.

CC mortality and incidence vary significantly by geography. In East Africa, the incidence rate is 40.1 per 100,000 women, while in West Asia, it is 4.1 per 100,00011. East Africa has the highest mortality rate at 28.6 per 100,000 women, while Australia and New Zealand have the lowest at 1.6 per 100,000. Limited access to primary preventive programs, especially in LMICs, is a significant contributing cause to these discrepancies12,13. More than half of WHO member states had HPV vaccination programs in place as of mid-2020, and the worldwide immunization rate was still well below the advised level. Furthermore, in many areas, early screening facilities for the identification of precancerous cervical lesions are still insufficient14. The creation of customized therapeutic interventions, the discovery of early molecular indicators, and the investigation of the vaginal microbiota’s impact on HPV clearance are current approaches meant to enhance early detection and treatment. One promising but underutilized method of improving CC screening among emerging technologies is the incorporation of artificial intelligence (AI), especially in regions where there is a lack of qualified medical personnel.

Challenges & problem statement

The application of AI and deep learning (DL) to medical images seems to be a great emerging medical research field. CNNs, as well as transformer-based architectures, have been successful in skin lesion detection, lung nodule classification, and breast cancer histopathology. Nonetheless, cervical cytology faces three challenges that limit the direct utilization of these methods15:

Diverse data heterogeneity sensitivity: Variations in staining, illumination, and acquisition protocols, like those seen in Herlev and SEER datasets, pose challenges that CNNs often cannot overcome. This hinders the overall generalization of the models.

Novelty and contribution

The limitations discussed above demonstrate the need for designs that integrate adaptive feature extraction, relevant attention, and low computational demand to increase robustness in various datasets and lessen the need for manual preprocessing. To address these limitations, we introduce the DK-D53-DWSCNNet model. The proposed model is unique because it integrates four different modules:

DKNet-JIF: Improves preprocessing by adjusting to diverse imaging distortions and complex cell shapes.

D53-CNN-CAN: Enhances segmentation by focusing on key context features and reducing irrelevant information.

The use of deformable kernels during the preprocessing stage of the DKNet-JIF provides greater flexibility to different cell shapes as well as imaging artifacts. The D53-CNN-CAN segmentation module makes lesion morphologies through their contextual features, facilitating better representation with selective attention. In the final stage, GAT-DWSCNNet utilizes depth-wise separable convolutions alongside attention-based geometric algebra features to eliminate unnecessary information, maintain control over model complexity, and improve robustness across different datasets. Each of these considerations improves the model’s ability to generalize over different datasets, as proven by the results on Herlev and SEER. The model combines separate data processing methods into one system, helping the system achieve a better balance between accuracy and generalization. The model’s performance was tested on the Herlev cytology dataset and SEER clinical data to verify the experimental results, with an accuracy of 99.9% and a sensitivity of 99.8%. The results are superior in comparison to the results obtained with CNN, transformer, and ensemble-based methods.

Literature survey

To improve CC prediction in this area, numerous studies have been carried out using neural network techniques. Tan et al.16 presented that Pap smear images can be used to classify CC using deep CNN models. Without the need for segmentation or manually created features, using the Herlev dataset, this work developed DL algorithms for the automatic diagnosis of CC. Using transfer learning in Google Colab through Keras, thirteen pre-trained CNN models were assessed. DenseNet-201 achieved the highest accuracy in the seven-class classification task. The main benefit was effective performance at low computational cost. The scale of the dataset and the models’ reliance on transfer learning as opposed to domain-specific feature learning, however, were the method’s limitations.

Deo et al.17 have presented CerviFormer, a cross-attention and latent transformer-based CC classification approach based on pap smears. Using the Herlev and Sipakmed datasets, this study introduced CerviFormer, a Transformer method based on cross-attention that can be used to identify CC in Pap screening images. It effectively used a small latent Transformer module to process large-scale inputs with few architectural assumptions. Its binary classification accuracy on the Herlev dataset was 94.57%. Strong efficiency and adaptability were benefits. Nevertheless, the model’s intricacy and possible requirement for substantial computer resources would restrict its application in clinical settings with limited resources.

Bhavsar et al.18 have presented TransPapCanCervix: An Improved Ensemble Model for Classifying CC Based on Transfer Learning. The 1,140 single-cell images from the Herlev dataset were used in this study to classify squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) using both individual and ensemble DL models, XceptionNet, DenseNet121/169, InceptionResNet, and ResNet50/101, among others. K-fold cross-validation validated the model’s resilience. The approach provided accurate SCC classification with up to 98% accuracy. Improved diagnostic performance and dependability were benefits; nevertheless, high model complexity and processing demands could be disadvantages that affect real-time clinical application.

Kalbhor et al.19 have presented feature transfer and learning methods for CC detection using CNNs. This study used the Herlev dataset to automatically classify Pap smear cells using two DL algorithms. The accuracy of ResNet-50, which employed machine learning (ML) classifiers using models that have been trained as feature extractors, was 92.03%. The second used fine-tuning in conjunction with transfer learning, and GoogleNet achieved 96.01% accuracy. Benefits include increased diagnostic precision and flexibility with sparse data. Reliance on pre-trained models and the possibility of over-fitting while fine-tuning are drawbacks, though, necessitating rigorous validation and significant processing power.

Popescu et al.20 have presented ML classifiers and fuzzy min-max networks of neurons that were hybridized with DL models that have been trained for the identification of CC. This research proposed a hybrid method for Pap smear image detection that integrated ML classifiers, a fuzzy min–max neural network, and DL methods using the Herlev and Sipakmed datasets. Pre-trained models such as GoogleNet, ResNet-18/50, and AlexNet were employed for feature extraction. The Herlev dataset shows good performance from ResNet-50. The method used fuzzy logic to improve interpretability and accuracy. However, because of the integration of numerous architectures and tuning procedures, complexity and training time could be disadvantages.

Chen et al.21 have presented a single-center validation of a customized prognostic prediction technique for high-grade endocrine CC and an investigation of the SEER database. To forecast a patient’s cancer-specific survival in CHGNEC, including SCNEC and LCNEC cases, this study created a predictive nomogram using the SEER dataset. Age, cancer stage, T1, N0, and surgery were found to be important predictive variables. Strong predictive performance was demonstrated by external validation using clinical data, with AUC values as high as 0.85. Benefits include more individualized treatment planning and better risk classification. However, the limited external validation cohort and possible inter-institution variability in clinical data were drawbacks.

Shi et al.22 have presented an enhanced prognosis accuracy with a SEER-based method for forecasting both general and cancer-specific mortality for individuals with cervical adenocarcinoma. This work used the SEER dataset to design and evaluate predictive nomograms that were to forecast overall survival (OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) for people with cervical adenocarcinoma (CA) at 1, 3, and 5 years. Cox regression and LASSO (Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator) analysis were used to identify significant characteristics such as age, TNM stage, SEER stage, grade, and tumor size. Strong predictive accuracy was demonstrated by the nomograms (C-index up to 0.832). Although there were benefits, such as accurate risk classification, generalizability may be constrained by retrospective data and possible selection bias.

Xie et al.23 have presented the research that utilized a SEER database analysis to develop nomograms that estimated the diagnostic outcome and prognosis for women with CC combined with second primary malignancies. The research developed prognosis and diagnosis nomograms for CC patients with second primary malignancies by using SEER data from 2000 to 2019. Analysis using logistic and Cox regression identified essential clinical elements, which included patient age along with FIGO stage, as well as therapeutic approaches. The prognostic model demonstrated satisfactory accuracy when predicting 5-year overall survival rates, while the diagnostic nomogram demonstrated good accuracy with an AUC value reaching up to 0.851. The system helps to create better follow-up decisions as well as patient-specific risk evaluations, but limitations exist because of its retrospective nature and restricted population coverage.

Shan et al.24 have presented a SEER-based investigation of the nomogram, prognostic variables, and incidence of cervical carcinoma with lung metastases. This study built a visual nomogram for overall survival prediction at three and five years by analyzing CC lung metastasis patients from the SEER database (2010–2015) to find survival statistics and prevalence with prognostic factors. The methods used for variable selection included Cox regression and Kaplan-Meier analysis, which established grade, surgery, and treatment order as important variables. A high predictive accuracy level was shown through the nomogram’s performance, which exceeded 0.93 AUC. The model contains data limitations from retrospective studies, along with underreporting of variables associated with metastasis as its main weaknesses, although it provided benefits for planning patient-specific treatments.

Tong et al.25 have used the SEER database to study the treatment of uterine cancer in patients who received radiation therapy for CC. The research relied on the SEER database to study the treatment patterns of uterine cancers that developed following CC treatment with radiation therapy. Both univariate and multivariate Cox regression studies showed that patients who had chemotherapy in addition to surgery had a higher overall survival rate. Proof through Kaplan-Meier analysis also demonstrated these findings. The surgery-plus-chemotherapy group had the highest results, but radiotherapy and chemotherapy by themselves only provided modest benefits. The study’s shortcomings include retrospective bias and the absence of clinical variables outside the purview of SEER, even though it provides insightful recommendations for individualized care.

In recent research in medical and applied DL, attention-based models as well as heterogeneous models have been given major focus. Along with them, efficient backbones and preprocessing specific to the domain have been proven to improve both accuracy and robustness. As has been observed in long short-term memory-variational encoder (LSTM-VAE) models that perform over complex cyber-physical data, VAE-based models that use hybrid models of signal decomposition and recurrent autoencoders are strong in temporal as well as structural latent feature extraction26. Sequence models together with hybrid signal processing front ends (such as Hilbert–Huang Transform (HHT) + LSTM-VAE + Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU)) purvey multi-domain feature extraction that lessens false positives in noisy data as well as improves sensitivity to minute abnormalities27.

In the same manner, combining classical transformations with smart deep neural network (DNN) classifiers (e.g., Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) + DNN) is instrumental in distinguishing between various disturbances and intrusive patterns. This brings the importance of domain-knowledge-driven pre-processing that explicitly captures the features for subsequent deep classification, as shown in28. The DCSSGA-UNet proposed in29 combined the DenseNet201 and attention mechanisms to improve segmentation accuracy. The DenseNet201 model forms the basis of the encoder, which uses transition blocks and dense convolutions to extract global features at multiple scales. The decoder follows the standard U-Net architecture, while the application of semantic guidance attention (SGA) and channel spatial attention (CSA) modules helps to selectively enhance important features and reduce unnecessary ones, effectively bridging the semantic gaps.

The hybrid CNN-transformer models can be explainable fused using EfficientNet/ResNet backbones and vision transformer (ViT) (EFFResNet-ViT), which not only outperforms the previous methods in terms of classification but also provides interpretability to the models with Grad-CAM. Grad CAM helps the healthcare professionals visualize the reasoning behind the model’s decisions, which in turn, enhances the reliability of deployment in clinical environments30.

As with many other medical imaging tasks, DL has been applied for the prediction of CC with multitask datasets such as Herlev and SEER serving as benchmark tests. While automated models report accuracies of over 95%, there are still significant gaps in methodology, interpretability, and robustness of models. Multi-CNN frameworks and hybrid classifiers attained 94–97% accuracy on Herlev images, which is similar to the performance of classical CNNs and ensemble methods on cytology datasets. Their performance, however, is negatively impacted when evaluated on external datasets and is further downgraded due to overfitting and sensitivity to staining variations.

While EFFResNet-ViT30 and similar models provide Grad-CAM visualization that allows clinicians to see areas that influenced decisions, such approaches are expensive to compute and need a lot of training data. There is a need for lighter explainable models that can be deployed in low-resource clinical settings. Integrating cytological images with clinical or demographic data (such as HPV type, age, and survival data) has been examined in a variety of studies. For instance, models trained on the SEER database incorporate risk factors with image-derived features for prognosis prediction. Although these approaches show promise, they typically face challenges in dealing with multi-modal data and adapting to new cohorts.

In CC datasets, class imbalance is a strongly pronounced problem with benign cases far outpacing malignant cases. Researchers have used SMOTE-based oversampling, GANs for synthetic image generation, and focal loss for imbalance handling. Although these methods improve the minority-class sensitivity, they tend to increase the computational cost or generate unrealistic samples. Recent state-of-the-art models further illustrate the advantages of integrating CNNs with transformers, attention modules, or domain-specific preprocessing techniques. These include DCSSGA-UNet29 for segmentation, FFT + DNN28 for classification in the frequency domain, and the combined signal-processing and recurrent models26]– [27.

The framework proposed, DK-D53-DWSCNNet, addresses the above-mentioned issues by adopting deformable kernels for adaptive preprocessing, using contextual attention for more accurate segmentation, employing depth-wise separable convolutions for lighter-weight feature extraction, and applying the HSO for balanced training of imbalanced datasets. Image acquisition

Herlev contains a collection of Pap smear images acquired from a Danish hospital that features data labels that identify CC cases. The dataset contains different classes of squamous cell carcinoma in addition to glandular lesions and normal tissue examples. The National Cancer Institute operates the SEER dataset, which serves as a complete source for United States cancer data with demographics and clinical and survival reports. The database enables epidemiological research and prognosis examination by offering extensive records across various cancer forms with specific information about CC. Both noise reduction and contrast normalization methods in image pre-processing practices allow for precise image analysis and classification of high-quality images.

The raw cytology images are likely to contain noise, blur, uneven illumination, staining, and other distractors that mask the useful diagnostic markers, making preprocessing pivotal in CC image analysis. The shapes and textures of cervical cells are highly variable, and the shapes of fixed kernels used by traditional filtering methods are inadequate in capturing and adapting to them. This drawback leads to a reduction in the sharpness of the cell borders and has a negative impact on subsequent segmentation and classification.

To resolve these challenges, a new framework that incorporates DKNet-JIF was introduced. Deformable kernels differ from traditional convolution kernels in that they do not have static sampling locations but instead adapt their receptive fields and sample positions for the image regions they are processing. This allows DKNet-JIF to better capture and refine image features such as irregular nuclear boundaries, subtle cytoplasmic textures, and overlapping cell regions. Joint filtering further merges spatial and semantic information, eliminating unnecessary noise and maintaining crucial edges. By enhancing boundary sharpness, correcting illumination artifacts, and improving structural consistency, DKNet-JIF produces high-quality preprocessed images that better reflect the true morphology of cervical cells. This significantly facilitates accurate segmentation in the subsequent D53-CNN-CAN module and improves overall model robustness, especially when dealing with heterogeneous datasets such as Herlev and SEER.

Segmentation using D53-CNN-CAN

The D53-CNN-CAN is used to segment the pre-processed Herlev and SEER Datasets to efficiently locate and identify disease-affected areas. For the task of segmentation and feature extraction, Darknet-53 was chosen as the visual backbone because it balances representational depth, multi-scale feature maps, and inference speed well. Knowing Darknet-53 to be the backbone of YOLOv3, it generates intermediate feature maps that are rich and available at multiple spatial resolutions. These feature maps are crucial for segmentation and for feeding the contextual attention modules, known as CAN. This obviates the need for very deep or computationally expensive encoders. In contrast to ResNet variants, Darknet-53 provides dense, high-resolution feature maps and requires fewer architectural changes for integration with deformable kernels and attention heads. When compared to EfficientNet and lightweight ViT variants, Darknet-53 is immediately compatible with existing convolutional segmentation heads and generates feature maps that retain fine spatial information. This enabled to create an ensemble D53-CNN that (a) works seamlessly with DKNet-JIF preprocessing, (b) allows CAN-style attention to be added at multiple stages, and (c) is computationally efficient for both training and deployment.

Geometric algebra transformer

Standard self-attention mechanisms are designed to function with scalar or vector embeddings and are especially effective in capturing lengthy dependencies. Though effective, they often overlook other specific features, such as the geometric relations in cervical cytology—nuclear orientation, relative angles, aspect ratios, and multichannel structural interactions. The GAT enhances attention by capturing local patches and features as multivector objects, allowing the incorporation of rotation, reflection, and scale relationships into the learned representation. By dividing ordinary convolution into depth-wise and point-wise operations, DWSCNNet increases efficiency while preserving feature extraction accuracy. Using the HSO, the GAT-DWSCNNet weight parameter is optimized for classification.

In CC prediction, it is important to extract features with great accuracy because lesions in their earliest stages only exhibit minor changes in structure that are easy to miss using conventional convolution methods. The proposed framework’s hybrid method of employing GAT along with DWSCNNet ensures that local details and a wider perspective are both considered. The GAT method enhances the default attention systems by integrating geometric relationships between nuclei, cytoplasm, and their surrounding tissue, thus enabling the model to focus on their relationships. The model can then analyze and extract features that are scale- and orientation-independent and that describe cell shape and its pixel-in-context structural surroundings better than methods that depend solely on pixels. By integrating geometric algebra operations, the transformer could bring out discriminative patterns in structure that may not be easily visible and that can help differentiate patterns, for example, an irregular nuclear outline or an abnormal cytoplasmic ratio. All these patterns have been proven to be critical features in CC diagnosis. By separating standard convolution into depth-wise and point-wise convolution, the DWSCNNet module enhances the computation efficiency and reduces the redundancies. This efficient design decreases the chance of overfitting and lowers model complexity, which makes it possible for the framework to handle high-resolution medical images effectively. When DWSCNNet is used alongside GAT, the extracted features are guaranteed to be rich both semantically and computationally. The combination of the GAT and DWSCNNet frameworks robustly accomplishes the task of feature extraction and classification, which widens the scope on which the model can be applied, all while preserving high accuracy and sensitivity.

Discussion

The usage of HSO yielded performance improvements uniformly when compared to classic optimizers like Adam, RMSProp, and SGD. Training with HSO improved classification accuracy by 1.6–2.4% across both datasets and lowered convergence time by around 18%. The DK-D53-DWSCNNet trained on the Herlev dataset with HSO optimizer reached 99.9% accuracy and 99.8% sensitivity; without HSO, accuracy was 97.8%. In the case of the SEER dataset, the model that was based on HSO managed to obtain an accuracy of 99.6%, doing better than Adam at 97.2%, RMSProp at 96.8%, and SGD at 95.9%. Such gains in performance showcase the impact that the optimizer has on balancing the training process and improving the model’s ability to generalize.

The DK-D53-DWSCNNet model proposed in this paper, which uses HSO, clearly outperforms existing methods for CC detection. Traditional methods of detection, such as classical CNN or ensemble classifiers, usually perform at an accuracy of 92–96% on the Herlev dataset, and their performance drops on other datasets. Attempts at more modern hybrid or transformer-based DL methods have gone as far as attempting 97–98% accuracy, still having performance issues. The model, on the other hand, exceeds these methods with an accuracy of 99.9%, maintains a sensitivity of 99.8%, and continues to perform well on the SEER dataset, showcasing adaptability across datasets. This is made possible by the HSO integrated into the model, which, due to improved optimization dynamics, helps the model to generalize well and is capable of learning effective features while avoiding overfitting from the diverse data sources.

The results demonstrate that the proposed DK-D53-DWSCNNet model significantly outperforms existing techniques across multiple evaluation metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, for both the Herlev and SEER datasets. It exhibits the lowest error rate (0.1%) and fastest computational time (0.2 s), highlighting its efficiency and accuracy in classifying various cancer types. Statistical analysis using the Independent Samples T-Test and Wilcoxon Rank-Sum Test further supports the superiority of the proposed method, with consistently lower p-values compared to other models. These findings suggest that the proposed model is not only effective but also computationally efficient, making it a promising solution for cancer classification tasks32–36.

Conclusion

In this research, a DK-D53-DWSCNNet framework was proposed, which incorporates deformable kernel preprocessing, contextual attention–guided segmentation, geometry-aware transformers, and depth-wise separable convolutions with HSO-based optimization for the accurate prediction of CC. The newly developed model outperforms existing ones on both the Herlev and SEER datasets, having a cross-validated accuracy of over 99.6% besides high sensitivity and specificity results. In contrast to current CNN- and transformer-based models, the proposed framework achieves better generalization on different datasets and enhanced minority-class detection due to the imbalance mitigation methods. Nonetheless, there are certain limitations to consider, including: Annotations on the Herlev dataset are few and might not represent the clinical cases adequately. The SEER dataset is imbalanced, and despite the application of class weights and SMOTE oversampling, the chance of overfitting still exists. Also, although external hold-out testing has been done, validation on other large and independent datasets, as well as in actual clinical workflows, is required before it can be implemented. The framework has the potential to handle the challenges faced by cytopathologists, such as:

In future, the research will address: (i) validation on larger, multi-institutional datasets to verify robustness, (ii) furthering multimodal fusion to include more clinical risk factors, and (iii) creation of explainable visualizations that fit into clinical decision support tools. Overall, the DK-D53-DWSCNNet model distinctively outperforms previous methods in accuracy, generalization, and efficiency metrics. However, it should be used alongside expert cytopathologists as a diagnostic tool rather than as a stand-in for them, particularly because it has not yet been verified in prospective clinical trials.

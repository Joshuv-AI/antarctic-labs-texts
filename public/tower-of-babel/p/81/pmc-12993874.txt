Questions regarding the reduction of the incidence of stage 4 breast cancer in Europe
Authors: Philippe Autier
Published: 2026

Cardoso and colleagues concluded that breast screening may have lowered the incidence of stage 4 breast cancers in several European countries, highlighting average annual percent changes (AAPCs) down to −6.16 (95% CI, −8.28 to −4.00) after screening introduction.1 This conclusion is overstated. First, for all countries, only 6–10 year data after screening introduction were used for trend analyses. Second, the −6.16 AAPC was from Portugal (South), where 20% of stage were unclassified or unknown. Breast cancer patients with missing data on stage have a poorer survival,2 in part because they may be diagnosed with more advanced-stage cancer. Also, only 4–6% of breast cancers are detected at stage 4. Therefore missing data may partly explain the drops in incidence rates before of after screening introduction.

Stage-specific analyses are contestable because staging procedures have changed over time. Since screening is about detecting cancers before they become large or spread outside the breast, it is preferable to consider stage 2–4 cancers altogether. In The Netherlands, 428,171 invasive breast cancers, have been registered from 1989 to 2023,3 with only 1.4% missing data on stage.1 Breast screening started in 1989 with a persistent participation rate of around 80%. After 34 years of screening, the incidence rate of stage 2–4 cancers (n = 249,568) has remained quite stable over time (Fig. 1).

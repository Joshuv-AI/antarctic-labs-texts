Research topic detection in scientific articles using a hybrid BERT integrated telescopic vector tree model with emperor penguin enhanced NSGA II optimization
Authors: Keerthi Krishnan, K S Easwarakumar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA vast number of research articles are published as a result of the enormous expansion in scientific study. An effective search from this collection of articles is made possible by using the correct search terms. Novice researchers find it challenging to search for articles that are pertinent to their areas of interest and to select the relevant keywords. Numerous studies suggest that optimization algorithms are effective in improving topic identification and detection. This provides researchers a valuable tool to help them traverse the deluge of research publications and keep abreast of the most recent developments in their field. By combining a TV-Tree (Telescopic Vector Tree) and Hybrid BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) with EPO (Emperor penguin optimization) enhanced NSGA - II (Nondominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm-II), this paper suggests a novel method for identifying research topics. The Hybrid BERT model combines the strengths of BERT and other machine learning algorithms to improve topic detection accuracy, while the TV-Tree based system provides an efficient and scalable framework for organizing and retrieving research topics. This method seeks to attain state-of-the-art performance in research topic detection by utilizing the advantages of both approaches, allowing for more effective and efficient categorization and retrieval of research papers. In order to determine the most important research subjects across a range of disciplines, this study suggests a revolutionary topic identification technique. This system uses sophisticated algorithms to examine enormous volumes of data in order to identify areas of interest and trending themes. Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach in detecting prominent research topics, leading to potential accuracy improvements and significantly outperforming baseline models. This provides valuable insights for researchers, academics, and industry professionals seeking to stay updated on the latest developments in their fields.Keywords: Research topic detection, Hybrid BERT model, Telescopic vector -tree, NSGA - II optimization, Text analysis, Multi-objective optimizationSubject terms: Computer science, Software

Introduction

Research topics detection and analysis plays an important role in determining the direction of advancement in almost all disciplines in research and development. This offers valuable insights into recent trends, assisting researchers in staying updated and focused on emerging areas of interest. Notably, the volume of information in the form of research publications has been increasing, and this has, in turn, resulted in the introduction of many online scientific repositories such as various open archives by several universities, Digital Bibliography & Library Project (DBLP), author home pages, etc. These pools contain a vast collection of research articles in various fields such as medicine, library science, natural sciences, and other applied fields. Specifically, ongoing research activities in the field of Computer Science (CS) is on the rise globally. As a result, a large volume of research articles is published every year. The field of computational research has expanded to encompass various new and interesting domains, including social network analysis, text mining, information retrieval, data science, and beyond. Furthermore, cutting-edge fields like blockchain, artificial intelligence, machine learning, natural language processing, the Internet of Things (IoT), and cybersecurity are becoming more well-known and present new opportunities for study and development. Furthermore, the increasing importance of big data analytics, cloud computing, and autonomous systems is also driving growth and development in this field. Thus, naive researchers find it difficult to identify recent research trends in their areas of research interest.

New researchers need to systematically analyze the research articles published in various journals and top conferences to understand the current research trends in their research area.So, there is a need for developing a system which provides them with current research topics in their research domain specifically and refine their literature search by using these topics as search phrases. This work proposes a Hybrid BERT with TV-tree based approach to identify the most influential research subjects from a vast collection of research articles because it is time-consuming for academics to look for trending research topics. These research topics can be used as keywords while performing literature search and thus narrow down to the exact topic of interest thereby saving a lot of time in identification. This work aims to develop an analysis model that utilizes text data from scientific research articles to build a topic model. A multimodal topic modeling approach is proposed to extract topic labels from text data, leveraging the relationship between textual content and underlying research themes. The proposed topic model is trained to model the distribution of text data and extract meaningful representations. Using a hybrid NSGA-II and EPO algorithm, topics are extracted from the text data vocabulary, and pre-trained models are employed to identify patterns and train the model to identify topics, uncovering the underlying themes and relationships within the scientific research articles.

Building a TV-tree

TV-trees51 are one of the data structures for indexing high dimensional data and is used for document retrieval, where documents are represented as a multi-dimensional vector. Pushpa et al.52 shown that the time taken to retrieve documents using TV-trees are less when compared to documents taken from a web document database. A salient feature of TV trees is that it allows efficient selection of a small number of dimensions as active dimensions from the high dimensional vector. The ability of TV-trees to dynamically extend and decrease the feature vectors is another significant benefit. A node in a TV-tree, like in an R-tree, indicates a region that is enclosed by its center and radius. The tree’s leaf nodes are where objects are clustered, while the parent nodes hold the description of each object’s minimum bounding region (MBR). Recursively, parent nodes are clustered until the root is created.

A TV-tree is denoted by TV (m, Nc, δ)53, m is the dimension of the data being stored, Nc is the maximum number of children allowed for each node and δ is the maximum number of active dimensions. The leaf nodes stores distinct research articles and hence all leaf node clusters are disjoint which can be considered as inner level clusters. After constructing the TV-tree, we focus only on the root and first level nodes whose cluster labels give the influential or trendy research topics. We utilize a benchmark dataset of research articles, such as the arXiv dataset (available online: https://arxiv.org/archive/), which provides a large collection of scientific papers in various fields.

Evaluation

The pre-existing categories and tags assigned to research articles in the arXiv dataset serve as a ground truth for evaluating the labels of clusters formed by these articles. The cluster labels of root and first level internodes are verified against the pre-existing labels. The evaluation metrics used are Precision, Recall, Accuracy, and F-measure.

This paper investigates the integration of language transformers, specifically Hybrid BERT model with the TV-Tree based framework, into topic modeling frameworks to improve topic detection accuracy. Discussion

To better understand the performance and real-world applicability of the proposed Hybrid BERT integrated Telescopic Vector Tree (TV-Tree) model with Emperor Penguin Enhanced NSGA-II Optimization (EPO-NSGA-II), we performed a qualitative analysis focused on the interpretability, coherence, and practical relevance of the identified research topics. This analysis complements the quantitative results by providing insight into how meaningful and accurate the generated topics are from a human perspective. We extracted and analyzed several topic clusters from a benchmark corpus of scientific articles across multiple domains, including Computer Science, Life Sciences, Environmental Studies, and Engineering. Each topic cluster was generated by the proposed model and evaluated for semantic coherence and domain relevance. To assess how understandable and meaningful the generated topics are from a human standpoint, we conducted a coherence-by-human-evaluation study. Three domain experts in each field rated 50 randomly sampled topic clusters on a Likert scale (1 to 5) for coherence and semantic clarity, providing a human-centered evaluation of topic interpretability. Additionally, topical diversity was assessed quantitatively by computing the Intra-Cluster Cosine Similarity and Jaccard Distance between keywords within and across clusters. The average cosine similarity within clusters was 0.83, indicating strong internal consistency, while the average Jaccard distance across clusters was 0.71, reflecting substantial external diversity. Together, these metrics confirm that the proposed model generates topic clusters that are both semantically coherent and topically distinct—key qualities for effective research topic detection.

The qualitative analysis demonstrates that the proposed Hybrid BERT + TV-Tree + EPO-NSGA-II model generates semantically coherent, interpretable, and domain-relevant topic clusters. The high human coherence scores, structural clarity, and strong alignment with expert-curated taxonomies affirm the model’s real-world applicability. It serves not only as a technical advancement but also as a practical tool to empower researchers, especially those new to a field, by simplifying literature discovery and trend identification. To validate the scalability and computational efficiency of the proposed Hybrid BERT integrated Telescopic Vector Tree (TV-Tree) with Emperor Penguin Enhanced NSGA-II (EPO-NSGA-II) framework, we conducted a detailed system complexity evaluation. This included runtime benchmarks, memory usage analysis, and scaling performance across progressively larger datasets. The goal was to ensure that the system maintains acceptable performance as it scales to millions of scientific articles, a common requirement in real-world academic search and recommendation engines. Runtime scales approximately linearly with data size due to the parallelized architecture and efficient TV-Tree structure. BERT encoding remains the most computationally intensive step but benefits from GPU acceleration. The system remains memory-efficient considering the complexity of models used. Memory usage scales sub-linearly in the TV-Tree and optimization stages, due to effective pruning and parallel partitioning. While the proposed method is comparable in runtime to modern models, it offers superior topic coherence and lower memory footprint—a key requirement for deploying at scale.

The system complexity evaluation confirms that the proposed model is highly scalable and computationally efficient. It maintains strong performance on datasets up to 5 million articles, with optimized memory usage and near-linear runtime scaling. This positions the model as a practical solution for large-scale academic databases, digital libraries, and research discovery platforms. The deployment of automated topic detection systems in academic research, such as the proposed Hybrid BERT + TV-Tree + EPO-NSGA-II model, raises important ethical and bias-related concerns. The training datasets often exhibit language, disciplinary, and citation biases—favoring English-language and STEM publications while underrepresenting humanities and non-Western scholarship—potentially leading to skewed topic detection. Additionally, pretrained language models like BERT may carry latent societal biases, including gender and racial associations, which can influence topic clustering and interpretation. Ethical risks include reinforcing dominant paradigms, marginalizing lesser-known research, and misleading novice researchers who may over-rely on machine-generated topics. To address these issues, strategies such as incorporating diverse and balanced datasets, using domain-specific embeddings, including confidence scores, and ensuring transparency in topic generation are essential. Responsible deployment requires a commitment to fairness, interpretability, and oversight to prevent perpetuating systemic inequities within the academic ecosystem.

Conclusion

A system for detecting top research topics in a particular research domain is proposed in this paper. Research articles published in the top journals and conference proceedings are taken for analyzing the proposed system. Each research article is converted to a vector representation and Telescopic Vector (TV) trees are used for storing these high dimensional vectors. A hybrid NSGA-II and EPO algorithm can be employed for feature extraction in research topic detection by leveraging the strengths of both techniques. Initially, EPO can be used to extract a diverse set of features from the research articles, utilizing its global optimization capabilities. Then, NSGA-II can be applied to refine and select the most relevant features, optimizing multiple objectives such as feature relevance, redundancy, and dimensionality. The hybrid approach allows for efficient exploration of the feature space and effective selection of the most informative features, leading to improved research topic detection accuracy.The active dimensions of the internal nodes of TV tree is used to retrieve frequently occurring topics in the respective child nodes. The region represented by each internal node represents a cluster of similar articles. The clusters created are disjoint but the set of topics belongs to each cluster are not distinct. The top research topics identified by the system is analyzed and the results shows that the performance of the system is good and reduces search time as well. This study demonstrates the effectiveness of the Hybrid BERT with TV-Tree based system in detecting and retrieving research topics with high accuracy and efficiency. The suggested method offers a reliable and scalable solution for research topic detection and retrieval by utilizing the advantages of EPO and NSGA-II, Hybrid BERT, and TV-Tree. This allows researchers to swiftly and effortlessly find pertinent studies and further their fields’ understanding.

Racial and Ethnic Differences in Adoption of Mitral Valve Transcatheter Edge‐to‐Edge Repair Over a Decade in the National Veterans Affairs Healthcare System
Authors: Balakrishnan Pillai, Tariku J Beyene, Guson Kang, Daniel Amponsah, Paul A Heidenreich, Celina M Yong
Published: 2024

Mitral valve transcatheter edge‐to‐edge repair (mTEER) has been shown to be a safe and effective treatment for patients with primary mitral regurgitation (MR) at prohibitive surgical risk and as a lifesaving adjunct to medical therapy in the population with heart failure with secondary MR. 1 , 2 However, there are incomplete data regarding the racial and ethnic differences in mTEER access, quality, and outcomes during its first decade of adoption in the United States. The national Veterans Affairs (VA) Healthcare System provides a unique opportunity to study this evolution in care without the influence of insurance coverage and ability to pay, which are often cited as barriers to novel procedure use among minoritized populations. 3 , 4

We conducted a retrospective study of the nationwide VA Healthcare System to identify differences in mTEER procedure use and outcomes by race and ethnicity from January 1, 2013 to December 31, 2022. We used International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision (ICD‐9) and Tenth Revision (ICD‐10) codes to identify all mTEER recipients and annual mTEER uptake by self‐identified race or ethnicity (non‐Hispanic White [White], non‐Hispanic Black [Black], Hispanic, with groups comprising <2% excluded from analysis due to small numbers) indexed to 100 000 VA patients of the same race or ethnicity with an MR diagnosis in the same year (Figure). Those with missing race (5.5%) were excluded. Patients with a concomitant diagnosis of infective endocarditis, mitral stenosis, surgical mitral valve repair, or surgical mitral valve replacement were excluded. A multivariate logistic regression model was used to determine likelihood of receiving mTEER by race or ethnicity (reference: White patients), adjusting for age at MR diagnosis, year of MR diagnosis, Charlson comorbidity index score, heart failure admission in the preceding year, distance to nearest mTEER center, and year of mTEER procedure. A Cox‐proportional hazard model was used to determine whether time from MR diagnosis to mTEER procedure varied by race and ethnicity. To evaluate trends over time, mTEER uptake was regressed on year, racial or ethnic group, and their interaction, from which fitted lines with P values were generated. Finally, a separate multivariate logistic regression model was used to determine the relative risk of 1‐year mortality after mTEER by race or ethnicity (reference: White patients), adjusting for age at MR diagnosis, Charlson comorbidity index score, and year of mTEER procedure. This study was approved by the Stanford Institutional Review Board and the VA Research and Development Committee. Requirement for informed consent was waived. Data from this study will be made available upon reasonable request.

Linear trendlines demonstrate the rate of mTEER uptake regressed on year, racial or ethnic group, and their interaction. Notably, mTEER procedure use increased significantly more over time in White patients compared with Black and Hispanic patients (P=0.045 and <0.01, respectively). MR indicates mitral regurgitation; mTEER, mitral valve transcatheter edge‐to‐edge repair; and TEER, transcatheter edge‐to‐edge repair.

There were 458 patients who received the mTEER procedure (mean age 73.4±9.69 years; White 81.8%, Black 12.3%, Hispanic 2.9%, Asian or Pacific Islander 1.7%, Native American 1.2%; male 96.9%; mean Charlson comorbidity index score 4.7±3.13). White, Black, and Hispanic patients differed significantly in their age, Charlson comorbidity index score, distance to mTEER center, and heart failure admission in the preceding year (adjusted covariates). The overall rate of mTEER usage increased over the decade studied (P<0.01); however, this increase was higher among White patients relative to the Black patients (P=0.045) and Hispanic patients (P<0.01) (Figure). Compared with White patients, Black patients and Hispanic patients were less likely to receive mTEER (adjusted odds ratio [AOR], 0.64 [95% CI, 0.42–0.97] and AOR, 0.43 [95% CI, 0.21–0.89], respectively). Quality of care was not significantly different between White, Black, and Hispanic patients (median time from MR diagnosis until mTEER was 208 [160–283], 396 [232 641], and 287 days [155 730], respectively, log‐rank P=0.5). White, Black, and Hispanic patients had low 1‐year mortality (18%, 12%, 11%, respectively). Likelihood of 1‐year mortality after mTEER was not significantly different across groups (relative to White patients: Black patients AOR, 0.53 [95% CI, 0.19–1.87], Hispanic patients AOR, 0.53 [95% CI, 0.06–5.16], respectively).

This study demonstrates 2 important observations. First, there has been significantly lower uptake of mTEER among minoritized patients across the VA Healthcare System—upending the expectation that elimination of insurance barriers would reduce disparities in access and pointing to factors beyond insurance that may disproportionately affect minoritized veterans. Second, we did not observe statistically different outcomes after mTEER by race or ethnicity, though wide CIs in the small sample of Hispanic patients preclude definitive conclusions in this group. Our findings among Black VA patients mirror non‐VA studies showing similar mortality by race. 4 , 5

Our study has limitations. Due to limitations of ICD coding, we assume similar prevalence of severe MR and primary versus secondary MR by race or ethnicity. Although we used time to treatment as a measure of quality, we were unable to assess how this was achieved. Additionally, our study focused on procedures performed within the VA system and did not assess veterans referred elsewhere. Although racially and ethnically diverse, the veteran population is predominantly male. We adjusted extensively for sociodemographic and comorbid conditions, but additional confounders may persist.

Despite similar quality of care and outcomes after mTEER in the VA, even immunity from insurance barriers was insufficient to overcome the trailing adoption of mTEER among minoritized patients, highlighting the need to explore the complex barriers to care beyond insurance status to close gaps among minoritized groups.

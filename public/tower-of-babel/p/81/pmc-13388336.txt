Dapagliflozin Inhibits Thrombin Induced ADAM17 Phosphorylation and Downstream ERK/p-c-Jun-mediated CTGF Induction to Prevent Neointimal Hyperplasia
Authors: Ting-Lin Yen, Po-Ching Chou, Chia-Ti Tsai, Yi-Ling Chen, Chieh-Yu Chen, Yi-Fan Chien, Wen-Chin Ko
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeVascular restenosis following angioplasty remains a major clinical challenge driven by excessive vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) migration, proliferation, and fibrosis. Thrombin, a potent signaling molecule beyond its coagulative function, promotes neointimal hyperplasia. Dapagliflozin, a sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, has been shown to exert cardiovascular protection independent of its glucose-lowering effects, yet its role in thrombin-mediated fibrotic signaling remains unclear.MethodsCultured A10 vascular smooth muscle cells were used to evaluate thrombin- stimulated A Disintegrin And Metalloprotease 17 (ADAM17), ERK1/2, c-Jun, and connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) by immunoblotting and migration analyses. Enzymatic activity (ADAM17 and thrombin) assays were tested by cell-free system kit. Pharmacological inhibition of ADAM17 by TAPI-1 and extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) by U0126 were employed to delineate pathway hierarchy. An in vivo rat carotid artery balloon injury model was used to assess the effects of dapagliflozin on neointimal formation.ResultsThrombin stimulation markedly increased ADAM17 phosphorylation, ERK1/2 and c-Jun activation, and CTGF expression in A10 cells. Dapagliflozin significantly suppressed these responses in a concentration-dependent manner without affecting thrombin enzymatic activity. Introduction

Vascular restenosis following angioplasty or mechanical injury remains one of the most persistent complications in cardiovascular intervention, resulting in recurrent ischemia and the need for repeated revascularization [1, 2]. The underlying pathological process, neointimal hyperplasia, is driven by the excessive proliferation and migration of vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs) and by maladaptive extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling within the arterial wall [3]. Although advances in stent design and antiproliferative coatings have improved clinical outcomes, a significant proportion of patients still develop restenosis, emphasizing the unmet need for pharmacological strategies that target the molecular drivers of vascular remodeling and fibrosis.

Connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) is a central mediator of vascular fibrosis and neointimal formation. In the injured vessel wall, thrombin is abundantly generated through activation of the coagulation cascade [4], and signals primarily through protease-activated receptors (PAR-1 and PAR-4) expressed on endothelial cells, platelets, and VSMCs [5]. Thrombin stimulation promotes VSMC proliferation, migration, and ECM synthesis [6], while inducing pro-fibrotic growth factors such as transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), and CTGF [7–9]. Elevated CTGF expression has been consistently observed in atherosclerotic [10, 11] and restenotic arteries lesions [12], where it contributes to VSMC phenotypic switching from a contractile to a synthetic state, thereby enhancing migratory and proliferative capacity [13–16]. Through these mechanisms, CTGF serves as a critical downstream effector linking thrombin-driven inflammatory signaling to pathological vascular remodeling.

A disintegrin and metalloproteinase 17 (ADAM17) has emerged as an important upstream regulator of thrombin-induced fibrotic signaling. Thrombin stimulation has been shown to increase ADAM17 expression and activation [17], facilitating the proteolytic shedding of membrane-bound ligands such as TNF-α and HB-EGF [18], and receptors that amplify inflammatory and proliferative responses within the vascular wall [19]. Phosphorylation of ADAM17 represents a key regulatory step that enhances its protease activity [20], enabling downstream activation of signaling pathways such as extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK)1/2 and c-Jun [17]. These kinases in turn drive transcriptional upregulation of CTGF, positioning ADAM17 as a molecular hub that couples extracellular thrombin stimulation to intracellular fibrotic gene expression. Given its established involvement in cardiac fibrosis and vascular remodeling, ADAM17 represents an attractive therapeutic target for limiting pathological ECM accumulation and neointimal growth.

Dapagliflozin, a sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor originally developed for glycemic control in type 2 diabetes, has demonstrated robust cardiovascular (DAPA-HF) and renal (DAPA-CKD) benefits that extend beyond glucose lowering, significant reductions in hospitalization and mortality among patients with or without diabetes [21–24]. Emerging evidence indicates that dapagliflozin exerts anti-oxidative, anti-inflammatory, and anti-fibrotic effects in cardiac and vascular tissues, possibly through SGLT2-independent pathways involving modulation of intracellular ion transporters, mitochondrial metabolism, and inflammatory signaling [25–27]. However, whether dapagliflozin directly influences thrombin-induced ADAM17 activation and its downstream ERK/c-Jun/CTGF cascade remains unknown.

Given that ADAM17 plays a pivotal role in vascular remodeling and that thrombin is a major physiological activator of this enzyme, understanding how dapagliflozin modulates this pathway may reveal a novel mechanism underlying its vasculoprotective effects.We therefore hypothesized that dapagliflozin suppresses thrombin-induced ADAM17 phosphorylation, leading to attenuation of ERK and c-Jun activation and reduced CTGF expression.

In this study, we investigated the mechanistic basis of dapagliflozin’s vascular protection using both in vitro cultured VSMCs models and in vivo balloon injury experiments. We aimed to delineate whether dapagliflozin’s effects are independent of SGLT2 and to identify its specific molecular targets within the thrombin-ADAM17-ERK/p-c-Jun-CTGF cascade. Elucidating this pathway may not only broaden our understanding of dapagliflozin’s pleiotropic actions but also open new therapeutic avenues for the prevention of restenosis and vascular fibrosis.

Materials and Methods

Chemicals and Reagents

Dapagliflozin (#11574) was obtained from Cayman Chemical (MI, USA). Thrombin derived from bovine plasma (#T4648) and DMSO (#D8418) were purchased from Sigma–Aldrich (St. Louis, MO, USA). Cell culture reagents, including Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM + GlutaMAX, #10569-010), fetal bovine serum (FBS, #10437-028), penicillin–streptomycin (#15140-122), non-essential amino acids (NEAA, #11140-035), and TrypLE (#12604-021), were supplied by Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA). The phospho–c-Jun primary antibody (RRID: AB_726900, #ab32385, 1:1000) and anti-c-Jun antibody (RRID: AB_731602, #ab40766, 1:1000) were obtained from Abcam (Cambridge, UK). Antibodies targeting phospho-JNK (RRID: AB_331659, #9251, 1:1000), total JNK (RRID: AB_2250373, #9252, 1:1000), phospho-ERK1/2 (RRID: AB_390779, #4695, 1:1000), total ERK1/2 (RRID: AB_2315112, #4370, 1:1000), and GAPDH (RRID: AB_561053, #2118, 1:3000) were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology (Danvers, MA, USA). The CTGF antibody (RRID: AB_10917259, sc-365970, 1:1000) was acquired from Santa Cruz Biotechnology (Dallas, TX, USA). The cleaved PAR-1 (Ser42) antibody (RRID: AB_10744166, #SAB4500823, 1:1000) was obtained from Sigma–Aldrich. The ADAM17 antibody (#GTX31632, 1:1000) was purchased from GeneTex (Irvine, CA, USA). The phospho-ADAM17 (Thr735) antibody (RRID: AB_2816411, #PA5-104938, 1:1000) and total PAR-1 antibody (RRID: AB_2900674, #PA5-116040, 1:1000) were sourced from Thermo Fisher Scientific. Dapagliflozin was dissolved in DMSO and kept at -20 °C until use.

Animals

Male Wistar rats (20–25 weeks old, 450–550 g) were obtained from BioLASCO (Taipei, Taiwan). All procedures were reviewed and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Cathay General Hospital (CGH-IACUC-113-019). Experiments were performed following the 3Rs principles, the National Research Council’s Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (8th edition), and ARRIVE recommendations to ensure high ethical standards. Animals were examined before enrollment, and those presenting infection, inflammation, or neurological abnormalities were excluded. Surgical interventions were conducted under 2.5% isoflurane anesthesia, and analgesics were administered post-operatively to reduce discomfort. Throughout the study, animals were closely observed for unusual symptoms, and predefined humane endpoints were applied when necessary. Rats were randomly allocated to three groups: a sham-operated control (n = 6), a vehicle group receiving DMSO (n = 6), and a dapagliflozin-treated group (1.4 mg/kg/day for 7 days; n = 6). At the end of the 14-day protocol, animals were euthanized using CO₂ at a displacement rate of 50% of chamber volume per minute, consistent with NIH ARAC recommendations for rodent euthanasia. Dapagliflozin was administered using an implanted osmotic minipump at the time of balloon injury. The pump was loaded with a predefined amount of dapagliflozin sufficient to provide continuous drug release for 7 days and was not removed thereafter. This dosing strategy was designed to target the acute post-injury phase characterized by inflammatory activation and vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation. Neointimal formation was evaluated on day 14 following injury.

Cell Cultivation

The A10 vascular smooth muscle (VSM) cell line (RRID: CVCL_0130), originally established from the thoracic aorta of embryonic rats, was sourced from the Bioresource Collection and Research Center (BCRC), Taiwan. Cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with GlutaMAX, 10% fetal bovine serum, non-essential amino acids (0.1 mM), sodium pyruvate (1 mM), and a standard antibiotic mixture (100 U/mL penicillin G and 1 mg/mL streptomycin). Cultures were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator containing 5% CO₂. Depending on the experimental application, cells were seeded into 6-cm dishes for protein expression studies by Western blotting and RT-PCR.

Cell Drug Treatment Protocol

For cell-based experiments, A10 vascular smooth muscle cells were subjected to a defined drug treatment protocol prior to analysis by real-time RT-PCR and immunoblotting. Cells were pretreated with dapagliflozin (1–10 µM) or vehicle control (DMSO) for 90 min. When pharmacological inhibitors were used, cells were pretreated with the ADAM17 inhibitor TAPI-1 or the ERK inhibitor U0126 for 60 min before thrombin stimulation. Following pretreatment, cells were stimulated with thrombin (1 U/mL) to induce downstream signaling responses.

Cells were harvested at specific time points after thrombin stimulation according to the signaling events being assessed. At 3 min, cells were collected to evaluate ADAM17 phosphorylation (p-ADAM17) and cleaved PAR-1 expression. At 5 min, cells were harvested to assess phosphorylation of JNK and ERK1/2. At 10 min, p-c-Jun expression was examined. For assessment of CTGF responses, cells were collected 2 h after thrombin stimulation to analyze CTGF protein and mRNA expression levels. All treatment durations and sampling time points were kept consistent across experiments to ensure comparability.

Real-Time RT-PCR Analysis

A10 cells (2.5 × 10⁴) were plated in 6 cm culture dishes and exposed to either the vehicle control (0.05% DMSO) or dapagliflozin at concentration of 10 µM. and RNA extracted using the NucleoSpin RNA kit (Macherey-Nagel, Düren, Germany, #740955.5) and stored at -80 ℃. One µg of total RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems™, Thermo Fisher Scientific, #4368814). Samples were quantified using QuantiNova PCR Kits (Qiagen, Hilden, #208252) by using the Step One Plus™ Real-Time PCR

System (Applied Biosystems™, Thermo Fisher Scientific, ). The nucleotide sequences of the primers used for amplification were as follows: for CTGF, sense 5’-CAAgCTgCCCgggAAAT-3’ and antisense 5’-CggTCCTTgggCTCATCA-3’; for GAPDH (as internal control), sense 5’-AgACAgCCgCATCTTCTTgT-3’ and antisense

5’-CTTgCCgTgggTAgAgTCAT-3’ ; for SGLT2, sense 5’-TgAgTggAATgCgCTCTTTg-3’ and antisense 5’-gAggCATggTAATCACTCCg-3’.

Immunoblotting

A10 cells (2.5 × 10⁴) were seeded into 6-cm plates and treated with either vehicle (0.05% DMSO) or dapagliflozin at 1, 5, or 10 µM. Following incubation, cells were harvested and lysed in RIPA buffer supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). Protein concentrations were determined, and equivalent protein amounts from each sample were separated by SDS–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking, membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with the appropriate primary antibodies. Protein detection was achieved using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL), and images were captured with a UVP ChemStudio imaging platform (Analytik Jena). Densitometric analysis of the immunoblots was performed using Image-Pro Plus 4.5 (Media Cybernetics, MD, USA), and quantified values were plotted to compare relative protein expression among groups.

Wound Healing Assay

A10 cells (1 × 10⁴ per insert) were seeded into Ibidi® culture inserts (#80366, Regensburg, Germany) and allowed to grow at 37 °C until reaching full confluence. To create a wound area [28], the inserts were gently lifted with sterile forceps to generate a defined cell-free gap. Cells were then treated for 24 h with either vehicle (DMSO), thrombin (1 U/mL), or thrombin in combination with dapagliflozin (10 µM). Phase-contrast images of the wound area were obtained immediately after insert removal (0 h) and again after 24 h to evaluate cell migration. The extent of gap closure was measured, and wound width was quantified using NIS-Elements image analysis software.

Animal Model of Balloon Angioplasty

Balloon injury of the right common carotid artery in male Wistar rats was performed following established methodologies [28, 29]. In brief, animals were anesthetized with 2.5% isoflurane, and a 2 F Fogarty embolectomy catheter (#120602F, Edwards Lifesciences, Irvine, CA, USA) was inserted through the external carotid artery and advanced into the common carotid artery. The balloon was inflated to distend the vessel and then withdrawn toward the entry site; this maneuver was carried out three times before catheter removal. Immediately post-procedure, rats received an intraperitoneal injection of vehicle (DMSO) or dapagliflozin (0.7 mg), followed by continuous delivery of dapagliflozin for 7 days (1.4 mg/kg/day) using an ALZET® osmotic pump (Cupertino, CA, USA). 14 days after angioplasty, animals were euthanized with CO₂ and perfused transcardially with PBS. The injured carotid segments were excised, embedded, and sectioned at 5 μm thickness for hematoxylin and eosin staining. Images were acquired using an Echo Revolve microscope (Echo, San Diego, CA, USA), and morphometric parameters-including external and internal elastic laminae, lumen area, and neointimal thickness-were quantified with ImageJ 1.53a (NIH, Bethesda, MD, USA). The intima-to-media (I/M) area ratio was calculated using the formula: (IEL area - lumen area)/(EEL area - IEL area) [29]. Dapagliflozin was administered during the acute post-injury phase (days 0–7) to modulate early inflammatory and cellular responses, followed by a drug-free recovery period (days 7–14) prior to tissue collection, consistent with our previous experimental design [28].

Statistical Analysis

Quantitative data are presented as the mean accompanied by the standard deviation (SD), with the number of experimental replicates specified for each dataset. To examine differences across multiple experimental conditions, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied. In cases where ANOVA identified significant discrepancies among groups, Tukey’s post hoc analysis was utilized to perform pairwise comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value below 0.05. All statistical evaluations were conducted using the GraphPad Prism software, version 6 (GraphPad, San Diego, California, USA).

Result

Effect of Dapagliflozin on Thrombin-Induced CTGF Expression in A10 Cells

To determine whether dapagliflozin affects thrombin-induced CTGF expression in vascular smooth muscle cells, we employed an in vitro A10 cell model. Stimulation with thrombin (1 U/mL) markedly increased both CTGF protein and mRNA expression levels in A10 cells (Fig. 1A and B). However, pretreatment with dapagliflozin (10 µM) significantly attenuated thrombin-induced CTGF expression at both the protein and transcript levels (Fig. 1A and B). As shown in Fig. 1A, dapagliflozin suppressed CTGF protein expression in a concentration-dependent manner (1, 5, and 10 µM).

Effects of dapagliflozin on thrombin-induced CTGF expression in A10 vascular smooth muscle cells. A10 cells were pretreated with dapagliflozin (1–10 µM) or vehicle (DMSO) for 90 min, followed by stimulation with thrombin (1 U/mL). Cells were harvested 2 h after thrombin stimulation for analysis of CTGF protein expression by immunoblotting (A) and CTGF mRNA expression by quantitative RT-PCR (B). GAPDH was used as an internal control. Data are presented as mean ± SD. p < 0.01 vs. DMSO (indicated by **); p < 0.01 or p < 0.001 vs. thrombin + DMSO (indicated by ## or ###, respectively). n = 3 independent experiments

Together, these findings demonstrate that dapagliflozin exerts a clear inhibitory effect on thrombin-induced CTGF expression in A10 cells. This suppression may underlie the ability of dapagliflozin to mitigate thrombin-driven fibrotic signaling and collagen accumulation in vascular smooth muscle cells. Nevertheless, the detailed molecular mechanisms responsible for this inhibition remain to be elucidated and warrant further investigation.

Role of Dapagliflozin in Thrombin-Induced Signaling Pathways

Given that thrombin acts as a potent stimulus for CTGF expression in A10 vascular smooth muscle cells, we next investigated whether its canonical receptor, protease-activated receptor 1 (PAR-1), is affected by dapagliflozin treatment. As shown in Fig. 2A, thrombin stimulation markedly increased the activated form of PAR-1 (cleaved PAR-1) in A10 cells. Interestingly, pretreatment with dapagliflozin (10 µM) significantly suppressed the thrombin-induced cleavage of PAR-1, suggesting that dapagliflozin may interfere with PAR-1 activation and its downstream signaling.

Cells were harvested at 3 min to assess cleaved PAR-1 expression (A), at 5 min to evaluate phosphorylation of JNK and ERK1/2 (B, C), and at 10 min to assess c-Jun phosphorylation (D) by immunoblotting. Data are shown as mean ± SD. **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001 vs. DMSO group; #P < 0.05, ##P < 0.01, ###P < 0.001 vs. thrombin + DMSO group. n = 3 independent experiments. Uncropped Western blot images corresponding to this figure (2B and D) were provided in the Supplementary Materials (Sup. Figure 3)

Previous studies have reported that the PAR-1/JNK/c-Jun signaling axis plays a critical role in mediating thrombin-induced CTGF expression [9]. To determine whether dapagliflozin modulates this pathway, we examined the phosphorylation of JNK following thrombin stimulation. Unexpectedly, dapagliflozin (10 µM) did not attenuate thrombin-induced JNK phosphorylation (Fig. 2B), indicating that its inhibitory effects on CTGF expression are unlikely to occur through the conventional PAR-1/JNK signaling cascade. This observation prompted us to explore alternative signaling mechanisms that might account for dapagliflozin’s action.

Since previous studies have demonstrated that thrombin can activate the ERK1/2 pathway to induce downstream fibrotic responses [17], we next assessed whether dapagliflozin affects ERK1/2 activation under thrombin stimulation. As shown in Fig. 2C, pretreatment with dapagliflozin markedly reduced the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 compared with thrombin stimulation alone. A similar inhibitory pattern was observed for c-Jun phosphorylation, suggesting that dapagliflozin effectively disrupts the ERK1/2–c-Jun axis.

To determine whether the effects of dapagliflozin in A10 cells were mediated by SGLT2, we assessed SGLT2 mRNA expression by quantitative RT-PCR. As shown in Supplementary Table 1, SGLT2 transcript levels were barely detectable in A10 vascular smooth muscle cells, indicating minimal endogenous SGLT2 expression in this cellular model.

Moreover, total PAR-1 protein expression was not significantly altered by thrombin, dapagliflozin, or ADAM17 inhibition (Supplementary Fig. 1), indicating that changes in cleaved PAR-1 reflect receptor activation rather than differences in receptor abundance.

Taken together, these findings suggest that dapagliflozin may not regulate CTGF expression through the canonical PAR-1/JNK/c-Jun signaling cascade. Instead, it appears to modulate an alternative ERK1/2–c-Jun–dependent pathway, possibly through interference with an as-yet unidentified upstream ligand or receptor involved in thrombin-induced signal transduction, which in turn may modulate the activation state of PAR-1.

Dapagliflozin Functions as an Inhibitor of ADAM17 Phosphorylation

Previous studies have demonstrated that thrombin stimulation increases both the expression and phosphorylation of ADAM17 [30] and c-Jun, suggesting that ADAM17 may serve as a proximal mediator linking thrombin signaling to downstream fibrotic responses. To verify this, we examined the phosphorylation of ADAM17Thr735 in A10 vascular smooth muscle cells. As shown in Fig. 3A, thrombin stimulation induced a time-dependent increase in ADAM17Thr735 phosphorylation, confirming that ADAM17 is activated upon thrombin exposure.

We next assessed whether dapagliflozin modulates ADAM17 activity. To determine whether dapagliflozin directly affects ADAM17 protease activity, a cell-free ADAM17 Fluorogenic Assay Kit (#78000, BPS Bioscience, CA, USA) was performed using recombinant ADAM17. Dapagliflozin was added directly to the reaction mixture, and ADAM17-mediated substrate cleavage was quantified in the absence of thrombin or cellular components. We observed that dapagliflozin inhibited ADAM17 protease activity in a concentration-dependent manner (1, 5, and 10 µM) (Fig. 3B). Consistently, immunoblot analysis revealed that dapagliflozin pretreatment markedly suppressed thrombin-induced ADAM17Thr735 phosphorylation in A10 cells (Fig. 3C). To exclude the possibility that this inhibition resulted from a direct reduction of thrombin enzymatic activity, we performed a Thrombin Activity Assay (Abcam, Cambridge, UK), which confirmed that dapagliflozin did not affect thrombin activity (Fig. 3D).

Collectively, Fig. 3 distinguishes between thrombin-induced ADAM17 phosphorylation in cells and the direct effect of dapagliflozin on intrinsic ADAM17 enzymatic activity assessed in a cell-free system. These results indicate that thrombin triggers ADAM17Thr735 phosphorylation, leading to the activation of downstream signaling cascades-potentially involving ERK1/2, c-Jun, and CTGF-as previously reported. Both enzymatic and phosphorylation analyses consistently demonstrate that dapagliflozin acts as a direct inhibitor of ADAM17 activation, thereby potentially modulating thrombin-driven fibrotic signaling in vascular smooth muscle cells.

ADAM17 Plays a Critical Role in the Thrombin-Induced ERK1/2/c-Jun/CTGF Signaling Pathway

TAPI-1 is a well-characterized TACE (ADAM17) inhibitor that blocks the shedding of several membrane-bound proteins. To clarify the role of ADAM17 in thrombin-induced signaling, we employed TAPI-1 (10 µM) to examine its involvement in the ERK1/2/c-Jun/CTGF pathway. Because PAR-1 is the principal receptor mediating thrombin signaling in vascular smooth muscle cells, we first investigated whether ADAM17 inhibition affects thrombin-induced PAR-1 activation. As shown in Fig. 4A, thrombin (1 U/mL) markedly increased the cleaved form of PAR-1, whereas pretreatment with TAPI-1 significantly reduced PAR-1 cleavage. These results indicate that ADAM17 inhibition attenuates thrombin-induced PAR-1 activation, suggesting a functional association between ADAM17 activity and PAR-1 cleavage.

Cells were harvested at 3 min to assess cleaved PAR-1 expression (A), at 5 min for ERK1/2 phosphorylation (B), at 10 min for c-Jun phosphorylation (C and E), and at 2 h for CTGF protein expression (D and F). We next evaluated the effect of ADAM17 inhibition on downstream signaling events. Under thrombin stimulation, treatment with TAPI-1 significantly decreased ERK1/2 and c-Jun phosphorylation (Fig. 4B and C), indicating that ADAM17 activity is required for activation of these kinase pathways. Consistently, Western blot analysis revealed that TAPI-1 markedly suppressed CTGF protein expression induced by thrombin (Fig. 4D).

To verify the hierarchical organization of this pathway, we used U-0126 (10 µM), a selective ERK1/2 inhibitor, and examined its effect on c-Jun phosphorylation and CTGF expression. As shown in Fig. 4E, inhibition of ERK1/2 effectively reduced both c-Jun phosphorylation and CTGF induction, confirming that ERK1/2 functions upstream of c-Jun and CTGF in thrombin-mediated signaling. However, pharmacological inhibition of ERK with U0126 significantly reduced, but did not completely abolish, thrombin-induced CTGF expression, indicating that CTGF induction is partially dependent on ERK signaling.

Importantly, total PAR-1 protein expression was not significantly altered by thrombin, dapagliflozin, or ADAM17 inhibition (Supplementary Fig. 2), indicating that changes in cleaved PAR-1 (Fig. 4A) reflect receptor activation rather than differences in receptor abundance.

Collectively, these results demonstrate that ADAM17 activation plays an essential role in mediating the thrombin-induced ERK1/2/c-Jun/CTGF signaling cascade, thereby linking extracellular thrombin stimulation to downstream fibrotic gene expression and pathological vascular remodeling.

Dapagliflozin Attenuates VSMC Migration and Reduces Neointimal Formation After Vascular Injury

After characterizing the cellular effects of dapagliflozin in vitro, we next evaluated its functional impact on vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) migration and restenosis. VSMC migration is a central event driving vascular remodeling during atherosclerosis progression and post-angioplasty restenosis [31]. As shown in Fig. 5A and B, treatment with dapagliflozin (10 µM) significantly inhibited VSMC migration compared with the DMSO vehicle control.

Cell migration was assessed using a wound-healing assay, and images were acquired immediately after insert removal (0 h) and after 24 h. (B) Quantification of wound closure from the migration assay. (C) Representative hematoxylin and eosin–stained cross-sections of rat common carotid arteries collected 14 days after balloon injury. Rats received vehicle or dapagliflozin (1.4 mg/kg/day via osmotic pump) treatment during the acute post-injury phase (days 0–7). To validate these findings in vivo, a balloon injury model was established in the common carotid artery of Wistar rats (n = 6 per group). In DMSO-treated animals, balloon injury induced prominent neointimal thickening and luminal narrowing (Fig. 5C). In contrast, rats receiving dapagliflozin (1.4 mg/kg/day, 7 days) exhibited a marked reduction in neointimal hyperplasia, as reflected by a significantly lower intima-to-media (I/M) ratio (Fig. 5D).

In this study, dapagliflozin delivery was limited to the first 7 days after balloon injury by the drug capacity of the osmotic pump, while the device itself remained implanted. This approach allowed selective pharmacological targeting of the early inflammatory and proliferative phase that initiates neointimal formation, followed by a drug-free recovery period. Assessment at day 14 enabled evaluation of sustained structural effects resulting from early intervention rather than continuous drug exposure.

Collectively, these findings indicate that dapagliflozin suppresses VSMC migration and neointimal formation, thereby supporting its potential therapeutic role in mitigating pathological vascular remodeling and restenosis following vascular injury (Fig. 6).

Discussion

The present study demonstrates that dapagliflozin inhibits thrombin-induced ADAM17 phosphorylation and downstream ERK1/2–c-Jun–CTGF signaling in vascular smooth muscle cells, thereby attenuating cell migration and neointimal formation following vascular injury. These findings reveal a previously unrecognized, SGLT2-independent mechanism by which dapagliflozin exerts anti-fibrotic and vasculoprotective effects. By suppressing ADAM17 activation-a key molecular hub linking thrombin signaling to vascular remodeling, dapagliflozin may represent a novel therapeutic strategy to prevent restenosis and pathological vascular fibrosis .

ADAM17 has emerged as a critical regulator of cardiovascular remodeling through its role in the proteolytic release of membrane-bound growth factors and cytokines, including TNF-α and HB-EGF. Excessive ADAM17 activation contributes to maladaptive tissue responses, promoting fibroblast proliferation, ECM deposition, and inflammation. In the myocardium, ADAM17 overexpression has been implicated in cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis, both of which contribute to the progression of heart failure. The ability of dapagliflozin to suppress ADAM17 phosphorylation and activity, as observed in this study, suggests that similar mechanisms could mitigate myocardial fibrosis and improve ventricular function. These findings parallel clinical evidence from large randomized trials such as DAPA-HF, which demonstrated that dapagliflozin significantly reduces hospitalization and mortality in patients with heart failure, regardless of diabetic status [22, 32]. Thus, the anti-fibrotic effects observed in vascular tissue may reflect a broader cardioprotective mechanism that extends to myocardial remodeling [33].

Given the broad biological functions of ADAM17 beyond vascular remodeling, including its involvement in inflammatory and viral-entry pathways, it is also important to consider its interaction with the ACE2 axis. ADAM17 is known to cleave the ectodomain of angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2), generating soluble ACE2 (sACE2) and reducing membrane-bound ACE2. This process disrupts local renin–angiotensin balance and promotes vascular inflammation and fibrosis. Importantly, elevated sACE2 levels have been associated with increased SARS-CoV-2 binding and enhanced viral spread, potentially worsening COVID-19 outcomes [34]. Therefore, the suppression of ADAM17 activation by dapagliflozin observed in this study may help preserve membrane ACE2 expression, attenuate RAS dysregulation, and limit excessive sACE2 formation. Such an effect could provide additional vascular and cardioprotective benefits, particularly under conditions of viral or inflammatory stress.

Thrombin acts as both a coagulation factor and a potent signaling molecule driving vascular inflammation and fibrosis. Its primary receptor, protease-activated receptor 1 (PAR-1), mediates thrombin-induced VSMC activation and CTGF expression. Our results show that inhibition of ADAM17 with TAPI-1 attenuated thrombin-induced PAR-1 cleavage, suggesting that ADAM17 activity may influence PAR-1 activation. Although PAR-1 has long been recognized as the canonical mediator of thrombin [35] signaling, the functional relationship between PAR-1 and ADAM17 appears may be bidirectional according our results. It remains uncertain whether ADAM17 functions strictly downstream of PAR-1 or if it contributes to receptor recycling or sustained PAR-1 activation through the shedding of specific ligands. Future studies using PAR-1 knockout or siRNA-silenced models will be essential to delineate this signaling hierarchy and confirm the interplay between PAR-1 and ADAM17.

Our mechanistic experiments using both dapagliflozin and the ADAM17 inhibitor TAPI-1 consistently demonstrated suppression of ERK1/2 and c-Jun phosphorylation as well as CTGF expression. These results identify ADAM17 as an upstream regulator of the ERK/c-Jun/CTGF axis in thrombin-stimulated VSMCs. The present study expands these findings by demonstrating that dapagliflozin effectively disrupts this signaling cascade. Beyond the ERK/c-Jun/CTGF axis, ADAM17 is also known to regulate multiple pro-fibrotic signaling pathways, including TGF-β [36] and EGFR-dependent [37] cascades. Both pathways are involved in smooth muscle cell proliferation and ECM remodeling. It is therefore plausible that dapagliflozin, through inhibition of ADAM17 phosphorylation, simultaneously suppresses several convergent fibrotic networks. This pleiotropic modulation could explain the broad anti-fibrotic effects observed in different cardiovascular tissues and highlight ADAM17 as a unifying therapeutic target for fibrotic disease. Thus, the drug may block both the direct thrombin-ADAM17 pathway and secondary growth factor receptor activation, providing a dual mechanism of fibrotic suppression. This integrated inhibition likely accounts for the strong attenuation of neointimal hyperplasia observed in the balloon injury model. Despite of ERK signaling plays a major role in thrombin-induced c-Jun activation and CTGF expression, the incomplete suppression of CTGF by ERK inhibition suggests the involvement of additional ERK-independent pathways. Accordingly, the inhibitory effects of dapagliflozin on CTGF expression are likely mediated through modulation of multiple signaling nodes rather than exclusive ERK blockade.

Although this study focused on vascular smooth muscle cells, ADAM17-mediated signaling is also crucial in cardiac fibroblast activation [38] and myocardial fibrosis [30]. Given that the cellular context differs between the vasculature and myocardium, the regulation and impact of ADAM17 inhibition may not be identical. For instance, ADAM17 aggravates cardiomyocyte apoptosis induced by doxorubicin and inhibition of ADAM17 may ameliorate doxorubicin-induced cardiomyopathy [39]. Future studies using cardiac-specific models or fibroblast cultures will be necessary to determine whether dapagliflozin’s suppressive effects on ADAM17 are conserved in myocardial tissue. Demonstrating this parallel mechanism would establish a direct mechanistic link between its vascular and myocardial benefits, enhancing translational significance.

The translational relevance of these findings extends to both diabetic and non-diabetic populations [22, 40]. Dapagliflozin has demonstrated robust cardiovascular and renal benefits in patients irrespective of their glycemic status. Oxidative stress and inflammation are known to enhance ADAM17 activation via redox-sensitive kinases such as PKC [41] and p38 MAPK [42]. In diabetic individuals, the drug’s capacity to reduce hyperglycemia, oxidative stress, and inflammation likely contributes to secondary suppression of ADAM17 activation. In contrast, under non-diabetic conditions, the direct inhibition of thrombin-ADAM17-ERK signaling may represent the dominant protective mechanism. This dual mode of action may explain why dapagliflozin shows consistent cardiovascular benefit beyond glucose control, reinforcing the concept that their therapeutic efficacy stems from both metabolic and non-metabolic mechanisms [43].

Despite these novel insights, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the direct molecular target of dapagliflozin within the thrombin-ADAM17 signaling cascade remains to be identified. Whether the drug interacts directly with ADAM17 or modulates upstream kinases regulating its phosphorylation requires further biochemical investigation. Second, our findings were primarily based on in vitro and short-term (14 days) in vivo models; therefore, validation in chronic vascular injury or heart failure models will be necessary to confirm long-term translational relevance. Finally, further studies using genetic silencing or overexpression of ADAM17 and PAR-1 could clarify whether the observed effects are mediated through a direct inhibitory interaction or secondary signaling modulation.

It should be noted that the concentration of dapagliflozin used in the present in vitro experiments (10 µM) exceeds reported systemic plasma levels in clinical settings, with a Cmax of approximately 215 nM [44]. This higher concentration was selected to enable mechanistic interrogation of ADAM17-dependent signaling in vascular smooth muscle cells. Importantly, for the prevention of neointimal hyperplasia, local drug delivery strategies such as drug-eluting stents [45] may achieve substantially higher tissue concentrations than systemic administration, rendering the concentrations used in this study potentially relevant in a localized vascular context. Importantly, the minimal SGLT2 expression detected in A10 cells further supports that the inhibitory effects of dapagliflozin on ADAM17-dependent signaling observed in this study are unlikely to be mediated by SGLT2.

Taken together, our findings provide compelling mechanistic evidence that dapagliflozin suppresses the thrombin–ADAM17–ERK/c-Jun–CTGF signaling cascade, thereby limiting VSMC activation, fibrosis, and neointimal hyperplasia. By targeting a central fibrotic signaling axis, dapagliflozin may offer therapeutic advantages not only in preventing vascular restenosis but also in mitigating myocardial fibrosis and remodeling in heart failure. This study highlights ADAM17 as a key molecular target for future cardio-protective therapies and underscores the expanding role of dapagliflozin as a pleiotropic agent in cardiovascular disease management.

Conclusion

In summary, this study identifies ADAM17 as a critical mediator of thrombin-induced vascular fibrosis and reveals that dapagliflozin directly inhibits ADAM17 phosphorylation and protease activity, thereby disrupting the ERK1/2–c-Jun–CTGF signaling cascade. These effects result in reduced VSMC migration and neointimal formation, providing a mechanistic explanation for dapagliflozin’s vasculoprotective and anti-fibrotic properties. The findings suggest that dapagliflozin, beyond its role as an SGLT2 inhibitor, may serve as a potential therapeutic strategy to prevent restenosis and cardiovascular fibrosis in both diabetic and non-diabetic conditions. Further studies are warranted to explore its translational potential in chronic vascular injury and heart failure models.

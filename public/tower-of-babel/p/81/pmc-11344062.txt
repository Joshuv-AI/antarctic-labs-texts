An experimental and spectroscopic investigation on pongamia pinata as liquid dielectrics for rural micro grid under various load conditions
Authors: Lalith Pankaj Raj Nadimuthu, Nisha Sathiya Moorthy, Kirubakaran Victor, Mariprasad Thenkaraimuthu, Baseem Khan, Ahmed Ali
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe transformer mineral oil is generally hydrocarbon-based and is not environmentally friendly, so it holds a significant share in environmental pollution. As an alternative to conventional transformer oil, plant-based insulation oil has been investigated globally in the past few decades. Even though vegetable oils are considered an environmentally viable alternative to mineral oil, the extensive utilization of vegetable oils could create a threat to Indian food security. The present work focuses on using Pongamia Pinnata oil (PPO) as a significant alternative to conventional transformer oil (CTO) in distribution transformers. The effectiveness of the proposed PPO is experimentally verified using thermal studies and electrical studies through a low-level distribution transformer of 1 kVA. A comparative analysis was carried out between the proposed and conventional oil regarding physical, chemical, and thermal properties. Also, scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and Energy Dispersive X-ray spectroscopy analysis have been carried out for fibreglass cloth insulation material with PPO and CTO to ensure the intrinsic structural strength of the insulation material. The structural strength ensures the bonding and life span of the insulation in the transformer. The cost–benefit analysis is also favourable for the proposed oil as a better green liquid dielectric for transformers.Keywords: Pongamia pinnata oil, Green liquid dielectric, Spectroscopy analysis, Thermal ageing, Degradation studies, Distribution transformerSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Electrical and electronic engineering, Energy infrastructure

Introduction

In India, the last mile quality and uninterrupted access to electricity is a challenging task. Rural electrification is vital to the quality of livelihood and financial development of human beings. According to the World Sustainable Energy Goals Report, 840 million people still lack electricity access globally1,2. In the global arena of developing countries, hybrid renewable energy penetration is essential to achieve rural electrification3,4. The government of India initiated Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana (DDUGJY) to develop rural electrification rapidly. The demand for various power infrastructure requirements is significant for rapid rural electrification in remote rural India. The microgrid has significant scope for quality and uninterrupted electricity access in rural India. The renewable integrated microgrid has considerable potential for rapidly accelerating rural electrification5,6. The demand for various power infrastructures like distributed renewable power generation and low voltage distribution transformers for effective source integration is increasing in the microgrid network. The government of India initiated a framework on the National Smart Grid Mission (NSGM) in 2015 to achieve the target of developing and deploying smart grids in India7. These key initiatives led to the Integrated Power Development Scheme (IPDS) development by the Ministry of Power to strengthen the sub-transmission and distribution network. The demand for distributed transformers has rapidly increased based on government schemes and policies. Therefore, the manuscript primarily focused on green insulation for low-level distribution transformers through non-edible (i.e., green liquid dielectrics) oils.

In 1890, a pioneer liquid-cooled transformer was disseminated in Germany. In ancient and modern power systems, transformers are top-ranked in effective power transfer. Based on the duty application, 50–2500 kVA capacity transformers are classified as distribution infrastructure transformers. Nevertheless, the capacity of the transformer and the insulation hold a noteworthy contribution towards life expectancy. The quality and health of the transformer insulation are the deciding factors of the transformer'slifespan8. Insulation degradation (or) deterioration is pointedly related to the operational temperature of the transformer.

The solid and liquid insulation failure is the fundamental cause for transformer failure (or) breakdown. About 70% failure rate is shouldered by liquid insulation, while solid insulation's tensile strength holds 50% of the failure rate. A liquid di-electric ensures the two primary functions of insulation medium and cooling the transformer. The physicochemical and electrical properties of conventional transformer oil (i.e., mineral oils) are highly suitable for cost-effective transformer application. Nevertheless, the flash point, fire point and dielectric strength of CTO are low, which are considered as the demerits of CTO.

Pongamia pinnata (PP) is a rich oilseed-producing tree with significant renewable energy potential9. Climate change mitigation is ensured through their nitrogen-fixing property. Annual yield is estimated at 90 kgs of PP oilseeds per tree with a potential harvest capacity of 9000 kg/ha. The overall oil composition of the seed is estimated as 30–40% of its total weight. Fast-growing and drought-resistant are the significant properties of the Pongamia tree. Initially, PP oil is un-utilized and unhandled effectively. Currently, the biofuel industry identified PP as a feasible source of liquid dielectric oil for the transformer application. Additionally, the quantum of mineral oils extracted from fossil fuels is rapidly decreasing.

In the USA, eco-friendly FR3 and highly biodegradable liquid dielectrics are established in the transformer application. In addition, sunflower, coconut, palm, rapeseed, and canola oils are investigated by researchers for ASTM standards. However, the oil mentioned above is edible, which threatens the nation's food security. An alternative, abundantly available and non-edible oil (i.e., pungamia pinata oil) is proposed than FR3 mainly due to the disadvantages of the FR3 over the proposed PPO. Malaysian researchers came up with the results according to the MS 2322:2010 standards, as FR3 has a higher moisture content, high dissipation factor, higher neutralization value, and high pour point10.

In this paper, various experimental studies (i.e., thermal and electrical aspects) and intrinsic structural strength (i.e., Scanning Electron Microscopy and Energy Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy Analysis) were carried out to ensure the feasibility of green insulation (i.e., Pongamia pinata oil-PPO) in low-level distribution transformer applications to accelerate the rural electrification sustainability.

Literature review

The literature review mainly focuses on achieving a cost-effective green liquid dielectric insulation alternative to replace conventional transformer oil for cooling and insulation purposes. Here, several literature studies are summarized as the microgrid is the feasible technology for effective rural electrification of last-mile electrical connectivity where the conventional power grid is not economically viable. The microgrid has a significant potential for distributed energy resource integration. Here, cost-effective and lossless power generation and distribution are achieved through the low-voltage transformer with a smaller capacity range of 62.4 kVA. Therefore, the microgrid network architecture requires a huge quantum of low-voltage transformers. Hence, the demand for solid and liquid dielectrics has increased drastically to develop and deploy microgrids in rural India.

Several biodegradable liquid dielectrics are existing in the marketplace. Nevertheless, it could create a massive threat to the food security of the country. Therefore, green liquid dielectrics from non-edible seeds are proposed for the effective cooling and insulation of the transformer. The aged edible oil has a greater breakdown strength than conventional mineral oil11. Because the level of sludge generation in edible oils is less than in mineral oil, breakdown (or) splitting of these sludge contents could rapidly occur in the mineral oil and degrade the insulation life11. A long-duration accelerated thermal ageing study was executed for 5000 h with a data sampling rate of 500 h to obtain the effective Dielectric Dissipation Factor (DDF) according to IEC 60247 specifications. Also, various thermal zones are maintained at 140 °C, 120 °C, 100 °C and 80 °C temperatures12. Based on the investigation, 0.0025 DDF was observed at the less than 100 °C operational temperature, whereas a greater than 100 °C DDF value was observed as 0.0165, which exceeded the standard limit. The ionic mobility improved because of a higher temperature, which enhanced the conduction losses to a greater extent. The viscosity loss and oxidation are prime factors for conduction losses in the liquid dielectric oils13.

It ensured in identification of the voids, cracks, ruptures, and agglomeration on the surface area of insulation under investigation24. Also, it has better solid insulation properties than other comparable insulations26,27. Also, the thermal ageing studies are validated through the degree of polymerization (DP) and levelling of the degree of polymerization (LODP) and summarized that 150–200 is the best suitable value for transformer application28. Artificial thermal ageing investigations were executed on cellulosic insulation materials with vegetable and mineral oil at 130 °C, 90 °C and 130 °C temperatures then, observed the surface degradation through SEM analysis29,30. The aged and unaged solid transformer insulations (i.e., kraft paper and pressboard) obtained degradation rates upto 1.22% and 17.85% during the 100 h period31,32. A comparative analysis has been carried out between mineral oil and ester oil through the systematic thermal ageing process (i.e., X-ray diffraction, XRD, and SEM analysis) and observed the less degradation of effect on the ester oil, which supports better performance of the transformer33.

Electrical properties analysis of the proposed green dielectrics

Three types of oil samples are prepared to investigate the ageing effect on the breakdown strength: a fresh oil sample, a thermally stressed oil sample without a Mica sheet, a fiberglass composite, a lanthanoid sheet called a catalyst, and a third sample with a catalyst. The oil sample aging procedure was conducted using a hot air oven set at 110 °C for a duration of 2 months. The pongamia pinata oil sample test results were related through CTO. In accordance with IS 6792-2008, the Nutronics Oil Test Kit conducts the Break Down Voltage (BDV) test23. At first, adjust the space between the two hemispherical electrodes to 2.5 mm. prior to pouring the oil into the test cell, clean it with the same oil. Then, the test cell is filled with the oil. Afterward, adjust the voltage and apply it to the electrode. The voltage rises at a rate of 2 kV/s. Took measurement five times with a two-minute time delay to improve the accuracy.

According to IS 6103-2006 standards, measure the dielectric strength (i.e., dielectric dissipation factor) of the oil sample using a Schering circuit test and a null indicator oscilloscope41. Initially, pour oil samples onto three terminal test cells, forming capacitance and isolating oil that behaves like a dielectric medium. First, raise the temperature to 100 °C and then reduce it to 90 °C. The measurement was then carried out every five days’ time intervals to investigate effect of aging on the oil samples.

Two types of samples are used to investigate the impact of Partial Discharge Inception Voltage (PVID) on liquid dielectrics, such as CTO and PPO. The electrode combination needle to the plane electrode is used, and its radius is 20 µm. The gap spacing between the adjacent electrodes is maintained at 30 mm. The gap spacing and radius of the electrode are crucial factors for partial discharge phenomena on the insulation materials. Here, to measure the PD pulse burst using a narrow bandwidth detector and digital oscilloscope. The effect of partial discharge on the liquid dielectrics following statistical operators is used, such as the magnitude of PD, Skewness, and Kurtosis. The last two factors are very important used to estimate the data distribution within the range.

Spectroscopy analysis of the proposed green dielectrics:

The spectroscopic investigation is significant in understanding the microstructure and degradation factor of materials. The physical, chemical, thermal, and electrical parameters initially validate the insulation properties. However, due to the shortfall of accuracy and insulation failure rate in the transformer application, the spectroscopic analysis has been put forth as a proactive approach to understanding the intrinsic behaviour pattern of the proposed insulation material with the standard material42. Generally, cellulosic solid insulation contains 3–4% lignin, 90% cellulose, and 6–7% hemicellulose composition. Degradation of solid insulation occurs continuously due to the operation of the transformer (electrical and thermal stress), and their impact on the structural changes needs to be analyzed appropriately to overcome the transformer failure rate43. The systematic thermal ageing process is carried out, and micro-cracks and voids are observed in samples44,45. Therefore, SEM and EDX analysis (i.e., physiochemical analysis46,47) has been carried out through systematic laboratory procedures, and results are reported.

The EDX analysis is primarily to quantify the Changes in the Elemental Composition, formulation of new elemental compounds and of the samples (Fresh and Aged) to understand their elemental degradation characteristics. Also, the effect of the metal materials of the transformer indicates the degradation of oil solid insulation materials based on the acid number of the composite material48. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) of the PPO sample is carried out using TGA Q500. The TGA curve is used to identify the percentage degradation of sample weight with respect to time or temperature. In addition, Derivative Thermogravimetric Curve (DTG) illustrates the rate of change in weight with respect to time. These EDX, TGA, and DTG analyses are highly pertinent to the proposed green dielectric oil degradation studies.

Results of electrical properties

The result shows that the average breakdown strength of Fresh Conventional Transformer Oil (FCTO) is 44.6 kV, and that of Fresh Pongamia Pinata Oil (FPPO) is 53.6 kV, as illustrated in Fig. 10. Figure 11 highlights that the thermally aged CTO sample has an average BDV of 54 kV, while a PPO sample has an average BDV of 60 kV. The breakdown strength of a thermally aged oil sample is higher than that of a fresh oil sample, which is due to the aging process reducing the moisture content in the oil samples. The average breakdown strength of Conventional Transformer Oil with Catalyst (CTOC) is 56 kV, whereas that of Pongam Pinata Oil with Catalyst(PPOC) is 67 kV, as illustrated in Fig. 12. The result shows that the breakdown strength of PPOC is higher than that of CTOC. Therefore, the PPO is feasible to utilize in HV applications. This is because PPO contains a higher amount of unsaturated fatty acids, resulting in a high viscosity that reduces particle movement within the oil samples. Hydrocarbon compounds, the primary chemical constituent of mineral oil, react with oxygen to generate aging by-products in the oil sample, which can freely circulate within the oil because of its low viscosity, which reduces breakdown strength.

The experimental analysis reveals that the dielectric dissipation factor of oil samples is gradually increasing with thermal ageing, due to the dissociation of oil molecules, which in turn reduces fluid flow resistivity. Hence, oil molecules freely move within the oil samples, enhancing oil conductivity. Interestingly, throughout all sampling periods, the dielectric dissipation factor of POOC is consistently lower than that of CTOC. This is due to a CTO composite of carbon molecules in the range of 15 to 40 per molecule, composed of C–C and C-H bonds. The aging effect easily breaks these molecules. In contrast, the proposed pongamia oil has a triglyceride structure that has more than seventy percent unsaturated fatty acids. Moreover, the viscosity of POOC with CTOC is higher. This led to a decrease in POOC's conductivity to match that of CTOC. POOC's resultant dielectric dissipation factor is low and illustrated in Fig. 13.

Dielectric dissipation factor of PPO and CTO Oils.

The statistical investigation of CTO and PPO using skewness and kurtosis over various ageing periods gives significant insights into their behaviour under ageing conditions. Mineral oil has quite low skewness values, ranging around 0.02, with no notable growth or decreasing trend detected over the aging period. This suggests a rather symmetric distribution of data throughout the ageing process. The kurtosis values for mineral oil are slightly increasing, ranging from 1.45 to 1.68. This indicates that as mineral oil ages, the tails of the distribution increase heavier, implying more extreme values in the data.

Conventional transformer oil (CTO) has both positive and negative skewness values, with negative values slightly higher in absolute terms and tabulated in Table 5. However, there is no discernible tendency in these changes. Positive kurtosis values gradually grow with time, but negative kurtosis values vary without a clear pattern, showing fluctuation in the data's peakedness. Pongam oil, which has been considered a substitute for mineral oil, has comparable statistical behaviour. The skewness values for pongam oil are equally low, about 0.0186, with no notable trend, indicating a symmetric distribution over time. The kurtosis readings for pongam oil increase significantly from 1.35 to 1.57, indicating that it, like mineral oil, tends to have heavier tails with age.

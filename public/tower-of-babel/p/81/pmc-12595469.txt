Monocyte-derived exosomal periostin driven by histone lactylation contributes to retinal neovascularization
Authors: Wei Shen, Zheng Nie, Mengzhu Wang, Haorui Zhang, Yongxuan Liu, Huimin Zhu, Wen Zhou, Lin Zhang, Min Zhao, Hao Zeng, Mai Peng, Yukun Zhou, Jiawei Zhao, Shikun Wang, Guangping Gao, Yuxin Jiang, Wenbin Zou, Xi Shen, Liqiang Wang, Hongyuan Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SignificanceNeovascularization in retinopathy is a major cause of irreversible vision loss. While local hypoxia and ischemia in the retina are well-established pathogenic factors driving retinal neovascularization (RNV), the contribution of systemic factors remains poorly understood. We demonstrated that enhanced histone lactylation in blood monocytes of patients with proliferative diabetic retinopathy, as well as in monocytes exposed to high glucose conditions or hypoxia, regulates exosomal periostin (POSTN) levels. The elevated exosomal POSTN promoted RNV by stabilizing hypoxia-inducible factor -1 alpha. Targeting glycolytic monocytes offers a promising new therapeutic strategy for inhibiting neovascularization in retinopathy.

Neovascularization in retinopathy, such as proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) and retinopathy of prematurity (ROP), is a major cause of irreversible vision loss (1). Local hypoxia and ischemia in the retina are well-known pathogenic drivers that increase the levels of angiogenic growth factors, promoting retinal neovascularization (RNV) (2, 3). Intravitreal antivascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) injections, laser photocoagulation, and vitreoretinal surgery therapies have improved the outcomes of PDR and ROP (4–6). However, these therapies meet the challenges of drug resistance and damage to the neuroretina (7, 8). The limitations may arise from their failure to consider the systemic factors that drive RNV.

Exosomes are secreted by nearly all cell types and play a crucial role in intercellular communication (9). Peripheral blood exosomes circulate throughout the body, influencing the status of various tissues and organs (10). Circulating exosomes can stimulate angiogenesis in tumors, myocardial infarction, age-related macular degeneration, and other diseases (11–13). Recent studies indicate that plasma exosomes contribute to retinal microvascular damage in the early stage of diabetic retinopathy (14). However, the role of circulating exosomes during the progression of RNV remains unclear.

Peripheral monocytes originate from myeloid progenitors in adult bone marrow and can migrate into tissues where they differentiate into macrophages (15). Monocytes and macrophages make up the key components of the mononuclear phagocyte system (16). Research has shown that the mononuclear phagocyte system is a key source of blood-derived extracellular vesicles (17). In pathological conditions, monocyte and macrophage-derived exosomes have been found to promote angiogenesis (18, 19). Increased monocyte glycolytic activity has been observed in various diseases, including infections, tumors, diabetes, and trained immunity (20–23). Elevated lactate levels resulting from enhanced glycolysis can boost monocyte histone lactylation, promoting angiogenic gene transcription (24–26). However, the specific role of exosomes derived from high-glycolytic monocytes in the regulation of RNV is yet to be fully understood.

Periostin (POSTN) is a secreted protein involved in various biological processes, including cell adhesion, fibrosis, and angiogenesis (27, 28). Genetic ablation of Postn impairs both physiological revascularization and pathological neovascularization in the mice’s retinas (29), whereas the underlying molecular mechanism remains unclear. POSTN is well known to activate integrin-focal adhesion kinase (FAK) signaling (30). Exosomal POSTN derived from cardiac explant-derived progenitor cells has been shown to activate FAK, thereby promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation (31). Recent studies suggest that POSTN also localizes to the cytoplasm and nucleus of endothelial cells (ECs), where it stabilizes hypoxia-inducible factor -1 alpha (HIF-1α) to increase production of angiogenic growth factors (32, 33).

In the present study, we found that exosomal POSTN levels were elevated in the blood plasma of PDR patients. Monocyte-derived exosomal POSTN increased under high glucose (HG) and hypoxia conditions, and conditional deletion of Postn in myeloid cells attenuated RNV. Moreover, POSTN levels in monocytes were regulated by histone lactylation. Inhibition of histone lactylation, through myeloid-specific deletion of hexokinase 2 (Hk2) or treatment with oxamate, reduced exosomal POSTN levels and suppressed RNV. Mechanistically, exosomal POSTN promoted the stabilization of HIF-1α and enhanced the production of angiogenic growth factors. Interestingly, metformin treatment reduced exosomal POSTN levels derived from monocytes and mitigated RNV in mice. Our findings highlight that high-glycolytic monocytes contribute to RNV, with exosomal POSTN acting as a key mediator.

Results

Circulating Exosomal POSTN Promotes the Growth of ECs.

Circulating exosomes have been reported to play a role in the progression of diabetic retinopathy during its early stages (14). However, their roles in the PDR and pathological angiogenesis are still elusive. Here, we isolated the exosomes from the blood plasma of patients with and without PDR. The clinical characteristics of patients were summarized in SI Appendix, Table S1. Exosomes from these two groups showed no differences in morphology as both exhibited typical cup-like shapes under the transmission electron microscope (TEM) (Fig. 1A). Nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) showed that both exosomes exhibited an average size of approximately 110 nm (Fig. 1B). Meanwhile, the known exosomal markers were assessed using immunoblotting. The result indicated the presence of CD9, CD63, CD81, and TSG101 in these vesicles, while calnexin, an endoplasmic reticulum marker, was not detected (Fig. 1C). These data suggested no physical differences between the exosomes isolated from the two groups. Next, the effects of these exosomes on ECs were evaluated. The results indicated that, compared to the control group, exosomes isolated from the blood plasma of PDR patients significantly enhanced the proliferation, migration, and sprouting of ECs (Fig. 1 D–F).

To identify potential bioactive proteins, we performed proteomics with exosomes isolated from the blood plasma of control or PDR patients. There were nine significantly upregulated proteins in exosomes isolated from the blood plasma of PDR patients (Fig. 1G). POSTN was specifically focused on because it is reported to play a role in angiogenesis (34, 35). The increased levels of POSTN were confirmed using blood plasma exosomes from 30 additional control or PDR patients (Fig. 1H and SI Appendix, Fig. S1A and Table S2). Given the known association of POSTN with epithelial–mesenchymal transition, we examined exosomal POSTN levels in PDR patients with and without epiretinal membranes (ERM) and found no significant (ns) difference between the two groups (SI Appendix, Fig. S1B). RNV is also a key feature in the retinas of infants with ROP (1). Notably, elevated exosomal POSTN levels were observed in the plasma of ROP infants (SI Appendix, Fig. S1C and Table S3). These findings suggest that increased exosomal POSTN levels are associated with neovascularization in retinopathy.

We then determined the source of elevated circulating exosomal POSTN. Our data demonstrated that POSTN was detectable in monocytes, neutrophils, and lymphocytes, but undetectable in red blood cells and platelets. Furthermore, we observed elevated POSTN levels in monocytes, but not in neutrophils and lymphocytes, isolated from the blood of PDR patients (SI Appendix, Fig. S1D). Then, the expression of POSTN in monocytes was further assessed using bone marrow–derived monocytes and RAW 264.7 (a mouse macrophage cell line) cells. HG significantly increased POSTN expression in monocytes and RAW 264.7 cells but had no effect on POSTN expression in ECs (C166), pericytes (Human retinal pericyte cells, HRPC), and smooth muscle cells (SMC) (SI Appendix, Fig. S1E). Elevated POSTN levels were observed in exosomes derived from monocytes and RAW 264.7 cells treated with HG (Fig. 1I and SI Appendix, Fig. S1F). We next isolated and cultured monocytes from the blood of PDR patients and control subjects. Elevated levels of exosomal POSTN were detected in monocyte-derived exosomes from PDR patients (Fig. 1J). Compared to control exosomes, these exosomes significantly promoted EC proliferation, migration, and sprouting (Fig. 1 K–M and SI Appendix, Fig. S1 G–I). Collectively, these data indicate that monocytes contribute to increased exosomal POSTN levels in the plasma of PDR patients and secrete high amounts of exosomal POSTN under high glucose conditions.

The oxygen-induced retinopathy (OIR) mouse model is widely used to study pathological neovascularization in retinopathy, which partially mimics the pathological process of ROP and PDR (7, 36). The induction process of OIR in mice was presented (SI Appendix, Fig. S2A). Mice at P7 are put into cages of hyperoxia, and 5 d later, the mice are returned to room air at P12. Relative hypoxia induces pathological angiogenesis in retinopathy, which peaks at P17. The expression of POSTN in monocytes isolated from the bone marrow of OIR mice at P17 was elevated compared to control mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S2B). Exosomal POSTN levels were elevated in the plasma of OIR mice compared to control mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S2C). Plasma-derived exosomes from OIR mice enhanced the proliferation, migration, and sprouting of ECs (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 D–F). Further data showed that hypoxia increased POSTN expression in monocytes (SI Appendix, Fig. S2G). Consistent data were acquired in exosomal levels of POSTN (SI Appendix, Fig. S2H). Exosomes isolated from hypoxia-treated monocytes promoted the proliferation, migration, and sprouting of ECs (SI Appendix, Fig. S2 I–K). These data suggest that the OIR mouse model is a suitable system for investigating the role of exosomal POSTN in RNV.

Myeloid Deletion of Postn Suppresses Pathological Angiogenesis in Retinopathy.

To better reveal the role of monocyte-derived exosomal POSTN, we sorted monocytes from the bone marrow of myeloid Postn-deleted mice to perform subsequent experiments. Postnfloxed/floxed (Postnfl/fl) mice were used and intercrossed with Lyz2-CreERT2 transgenic mice to generate myeloid Postn-deleted mice (PostnΔMΦ). Immunoblot data showed significantly decreased expression of POSTN in the monocytes (monocyteΔPostn) isolated from PostnΔMΦ mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S3A). Treatment with HG was unable to increase POSTN levels in monocyteΔPostn (Fig. 2A). Consistent data were acquired in exosomal POSTN levels (Fig. 2B). HG-treated monocyteΔPostn-derived exosomes were unable to increase the proliferation, migration, and sprouting of ECs compared to HG-treated monocyteWT-derived exosomes (Fig. 2 C–E). Furthermore, we used a coculture system to evaluate the crosstalk between monocytes and ECs under HG conditions. The data showed that ECs cocultured with monocyteΔPostn exhibited decreased proliferation compared to ECs cocultured with monocyteWT (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 B and C).

Activation of retinal myeloid cells has been reported to promote neovascularization in retinopathy (37). Immunofluorescence staining revealed elevated POSTN protein levels in monocyte-derived macrophages within the neovascular tufts of OIR mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 D and E). To further validate this finding, we isolated retinal macrophages by flow cytometry and assessed POSTN expression at the transcript level. Consistently, POSTN mRNA levels were significantly higher in retinal macrophages from OIR mice compared to those from control mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S3 F and G). To evaluate the role of myeloid-derived POSTN in the progression of RNV, we assessed oxygen-induced retinopathy in PostnΔMΦ and PostnWT mice. The area of RNV was significantly reduced in PostnΔMΦ mice compared with that in PostnWT mice (Fig. 2 F and G). However, the avascular area showed ns change in the retina of PostnΔMΦ mice and PostnWT mice (Fig. 2 F and H). The proliferation of ECs in the retina of OIR was then assessed using 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) staining. The result showed that myeloid-specific deletion of Postn suppressed the proliferation of ECs significantly (Fig. 2 I and J). Taken together, these data indicate that monocyte-derived POSTN contributes to RNV, although the potential involvement of macrophages derived from infiltrating retinal monocytes cannot be ruled out.

Monocyte Histone Lactylation Regulates Exosomal POSTN Levels.

The molecular mechanism governing POSTN levels in monocytes is then investigated. The monocytes in the blood of PDR patients usually are exposed to HG, which can alter their metabolic status. Metabolomics analysis showed that monocytes from the blood of PDR patients exhibited increased glycolysis compared with the nondiabetic group (Fig. 3A and SI Appendix, Fig. S4A). The glycolysis product, lactate, was elevated in monocytes isolated from the blood of patients with PDR (Fig. 3 A and B). The increased lactate levels were further confirmed in monocytes isolated from the blood of PDR patients using a chemical kit (Fig. 3C). Recently, lactate has been intensively studied for its role in the epigenetic regulation of gene expression through histone lactylation (26). Our data showed that histone lactylation, especially H4K8 lactylation (H4K8la), was upregulated in monocytes isolated from the blood of PDR patients (Fig. 3D and SI Appendix, Fig. S4B and Table S4). Consistent data were acquired in mouse monocytes treated with HG (Fig. 3 E–G).

To determine whether monocytes H4K8la controls the expression of POSTN, we performed ChIP-qPCR analysis. The data indicated the direct binding of H4K8la to promoter regions of Postn. Increased binding of H4K8la to Postn was observed in monocytes isolated from the blood of PDR patients (Fig. 3H). The result of qPCR further confirmed the upregulation of Postn in monocytes (Fig. 3I). Consistent data were acquired in mouse monocytes treated with HG (Fig. 3 J and K).

We then used myeloid-specific Hk2 deletion mice to confirm monocyte’s histone lactylation in governing the expression of Postn. Hk2floxed/floxed (Hk2fl/fl) mice were intercrossed with Lyz2-CreERT2 transgenic mice to generate myeloid Hk2-deleted mice (Hk2ΔMΦ). Immunoblot data showed significantly decreased expression of HK2 in monocytes (MonocyteΔHk2) isolated from Hk2ΔMΦ mice (SI Appendix, Fig. S4C). Compared to the control group, reduced glycolysis and lactate production were observed in monocyteΔHk2 (SI Appendix, Fig. S4 D and E). The upregulated H4K8la and POSTN levels induced by HG were reversed in MonocyteΔHk2 under HG conditions (Fig. 3 L and M). Meanwhile, the levels of exosomal POSTN were also reversed in monocyteΔHk2 treated with HG (Fig. 3N).

To better confirm the role of histone lactylation in regulating exosomal POSTN, we used oxamate to inhibit lactate production in monocytes. The inhibitor effectively reduced H4K8la and POSTN levels in monocytes under HG conditions (SI Appendix, Fig. S4 F and G). Moreover, the elevated exosomal POSTN levels in high-glucose-treated monocytes were also reversed by oxamate (SI Appendix, Fig. S4H).

Inhibition of Monocyte Glycolysis Reduces Pathological Angiogenesis in Retinopathy.

Next, we used Hk2ΔMΦ mice to evaluate the impact of glycolysis inhibition on pathological angiogenesis in retinopathy. We observed a significant reduction in the area of RNV in Hk2ΔMΦ mice compared to Hk2WT mice (Fig. 4 A and B). However, the avascular area remained unchanged (Fig. 4C). The proliferation of ECs in the retina of OIR was assessed using EdU staining. The data showed that myeloid-specific deletion of Hk2 suppressed the proliferation of retinal ECs significantly (Fig. 4 D and E).

We next evaluated the effect of intraperitoneal oxamate administration on histone lactylation in monocytes and RNV. The data showed that blood plasma lactate levels and monocyte histone lactylation were reduced in OIR mice treated with oxamate compared to the control group (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 A and B). Further data showed that oxamate could reduce the area of RNV in OIR mice, whereas it did not affect the avascular area (SI Appendix, Fig. S5 C–E). These results further support the involvement of monocyte histone lactylation in the progress of RNV. However, as oxamate is not a monocyte-specific inhibitor, its potential effects on glycolysis in other cell types cannot be ruled out.

Additionally, we assessed the effect of myeloid deletion of Hk2 on ECs. Exosomes derived from HG-treated monocyteΔHk2 were unable to enhance the proliferation, migration, and sprouting of ECs (Fig. 4 F–H). Furthermore, we used a coculture system to evaluate the crosstalk between monocytes and ECs under HG. The data showed that ECs cocultured with monocyteΔHk2 exhibited reduced proliferation compared to ECs cocultured with monocyteWT (SI Appendix, Fig. S5F).

Exosomal POSTN Reverses the Vascular Changes after Myeloid Hk2/Postn Deletion.

To further confirm the role of exosomal POSTN in ischemic retinopathy, we isolated the exosomes of HG-treated mouse monocytes. Then, we assessed their effects on RNV in OIR mice. First, the effects of exosomes on RNV were assessed in Hk2ΔMΦ mice. The area of RNV was larger in exosomes-treated Hk2ΔMΦ mice compared to untreated Hk2ΔMΦ mice (Fig. 5 A and B). However, the avascular area showed no differences between these two groups (Fig. 5C). Meanwhile, the proliferation of ECs in the retina was assessed using EdU staining. The result indicated that the proliferation of ECs was significantly increased in the retinal vasculature of exosome-treated Hk2ΔMΦ mice (Fig. 5 D and E). Then, the effects of these exosomes on ischemic retinopathy were studied in PostnΔMΦ mice. These exosomes enhanced the RNV and ECs proliferation in the retina of PostnΔMΦ mice without affecting the avascular area (Fig. 5 F–J). These data highlight the vital role of exosomal POSTN in RNV.

Suppressing Monocyte Glycolysis with Metformin Reduces Exosomal POSTN and Attenuates RNV.

Metformin is the first-line drug for type 2 diabetes (T2D) (38). In addition to its glucose-lowering effects, metformin also plays a vital role in antiaging, antitumor, and cardiovascular protection (38–40). Notably, metformin can decrease glycolysis levels of monocytes during trained immunity (20). Here, we showed that metformin inhibited the glycolysis of mouse monocytes treated with HG and reduced lactate levels (Fig. 6 A and B). Metformin treatment reduced H4K8la, POSTN, and exosomal POSTN levels in monocytes under HG conditions (Fig. 6 C–E). Notably, PDR patients who received oral metformin exhibited reduced levels of exosomal POSTN protein. (Fig. 6F and SI Appendix, Table S5). We also evaluated the effect of sulfonylureas or insulin on exosomal POSTN levels and found ns difference between patients who received these treatments and those who did not (SI Appendix, Fig. S6 A and B and Table S5). It is demonstrated that intravitreal injection of metformin can reduce RNV in mice by abrogating the senescence-associated secretory phenotype of the retina (41). Systemic administration of metformin at 300 mg/kg through intraperitoneal injection nearly eliminated all neovascular tufts but caused significant weight loss in the pups (SI Appendix, Fig. S6 C–F). A dose of 200 mg/kg had no impact on body weight and was therefore chosen for the study (SI Appendix, Fig. S6G). Here, our results showed that systemic administration of metformin attenuated RNV and inhibited EC proliferation (Fig. 6 G–K). These data reveal a potential role of metformin in delaying PDR progression.

Exosomal POSTN Contributes to Retinal Angiogenesis through HIF-1α Stabilization.

Next, the mechanism underlying exosomal POSTN-induced RNV was investigated. POSTN is known to be one of the ligands and regulators of the integrin/focal adhesion kinase (FAK) pathway (42). Exosomal POSTN is reported to activate the phosphorylation of FAK in cardiomyocytes (31). Thus, we first assessed the effect of monocyte-derived exosomal POSTN on FAK signaling in ECs. Interestingly, HG-treated monocyte-derived exosomes did not activate FAK in ECs (SI Appendix, Fig. S7A). Exosomes isolated from the blood plasma of PDR patients were also unable to activate the phosphorylation of FAK in ECs (SI Appendix, Fig. S7B). The data implied that exosomal POSTN contributes to retinal angiogenesis through mechanisms other than the classical integrin/FAK pathway.

Recent research demonstrates that POSTN is detected both in the cytoplasm and nucleus of human pulmonary artery endothelial cells (32). Intracellular POSTN facilitates the stabilization of HIF-1α (32). Here, we found that protein levels of HIF-1α were elevated in ECs incubated with exosomes isolated from the blood plasma of PDR patients (Fig. 7A). Meanwhile, increased protein levels of HIF-1α were observed in ECs incubated with HG-treated monocyte-derived exosomes (Fig. 7B). Exosomes isolated from monocyteΔHk2 or monocyteΔPostn were unable to increase the level of HIF-1α in ECs (Fig. 7B and SI Appendix, Fig. S7C). These results indicate that exosomal POSTN might determine the EC cell fate by regulating the protein levels of HIF-1α.

Interestingly, we found that exosomal POSTN mainly translocated into cytoplasm and nucleus after incubation with ECs for 24 h, suggesting a potential role of exosomal POSTN in intracellular regulatory function (Fig. 7C). Consistent with the data of immunoblot, increased HIF-1α staining was observed in ECs after the treatment of exosomes isolated from HG-treated monocyteWT (Fig. 7C). Then, we forced the expression of Flag-POSTN in 293T cells using adenovirus and sorted the secreted exosomes. The protein levels of exosomal POSTN were much higher than the exosomes isolated from control 293T cells (SI Appendix, Fig. S7D). The results showed that Flag-tagged exosomal POSTN localized in the cytoplasm and nucleus in ECs and facilitated the stabilization of HIF-1α (Fig. 7D). Coimmunoprecipitation (co-IP) assay with anti-Flag antibody or control antibody IgG indicated the physical interaction of POSTN and HIF-1α in ECs (Fig. 7E). Luciferase assays further confirmed that exosomal POSTN stimulated the Hif-1α-responding reporter driving luciferase activity (Fig. 7F and SI Appendix, Fig. S7 E and F).

Next, bulk RNA sequencing was performed to identify potential angiogenic genes that exosomal POSTN and HIF-1α regulated. There were 54 upregulated genes that were controlled by HIF-1α and involved in angiogenesis, and the top 10 significantly upregulated genes were presented (Fig. 7G and SI Appendix, Fig. S7G). VEGFA and glucose transporter type 1 (GLUT1) are focused as they are well known to regulate angiogenesis (43, 44). These genes’ mRNA and protein levels were confirmed after treatment with HG-treated monocyte-derived exosomes (Fig. 7 H–K). In comparison, exosomes isolated from monocyteΔHk2 or monocyteΔPostn could not increase these genes’ mRNA and protein levels in ECs (Fig. 7 H–K).

To further validate the role of HIF-1α in exosomal POSTN-mediated angiogenic activity, we inhibited HIF-1α using both siRNA and a pharmacological inhibitor. Knockdown of Hif-1α by siRNAs reversed the exosome-induced upregulation of angiogenesis-related genes (Fig. 7 L and M and SI Appendix, Fig. S8A). Additionally, treatment with exosomes failed to restore the PX-478-suppressed proliferation, migration, and sprouting of ECs (SI Appendix, Fig. S8 B–D). In the OIR model, PX-478 treatment significantly reduced retinal neovascular tufts, and exosome administration failed to rescue these vascular changes (SI Appendix, Fig. S8 E–G). Taken together, these data suggest that exosomal POSTN stabilizes HIF-1α and upregulates HIF-1α-controlled angiogenic genes to promote RNV.

Discussion

The role of local hypoxia, ischemia, and angiogenic molecules in the retina has been well studied during the progression of RNV. In contrast, the role of systemic factors is less investigated. Here, we revealed that the exosomes from the blood plasma of patients with PDR promoted the proliferation of ECs, and the protein levels of exosomal POSTN increased. POSTN is known to promote angiogenesis during physical and pathological conditions (28, 32). The data suggest that circulating exosomal POSTN may play a role in the progression of RNV. Exosomes are complex carriers containing proteins, lipids, RNAs, and metabolites that mediate intercellular communication (9). Exosomes enriched with IgG and exosomal circular RNA-cPWWP2A are implicated in microvascular damage during early diabetic retinopathy (14, 45). Moreover, exosomal miRNA-9-3p and lncRNA LOC100132249 have been reported to promote angiogenesis in PDR (46, 47). These findings suggest that beyond POSTN, other circulating exosomal cargos may also contribute to PDR, warranting further investigation to elucidate their roles in the observed effects.

Monocytes are innate blood cells that maintain vascular homeostasis (48). Recent research suggests that monocytes, in addition to regulating innate immunity, regulate angiogenesis (49–52). Previous works reveal that monocyte-derived macrophages are adjacent to ECs and promote pathological neovascularization in the retina of OIR mice (37, 53). Additionally, exosomes derived from macrophages are reported to promote angiogenesis (18). However, the role of exosomes in the crosstalk between monocytes and ECs remains unclear. Our results showed that monocyte-derived exosomal POSTN levels were increased under HG and hypoxic conditions. Deletion of Postn in myeloid cells resulted in decreased exosomal POSTN levels and impaired angiogenesis growth. Notably, the supplement of exogenous exosomal POSTN partially reversed the vascular defects caused by myeloid Postn deletion. These data imply a vital role of monocyte-derived exosomal POSTN in RNV.

Enhanced glycolysis in monocytes is observed in trained immunity, vascular regeneration, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, and tumors (25, 54, 55). Recent investigations reveal that upregulated glycolysis in monocytes and macrophages contributes to myocardial infarction angiogenesis and RNV (24, 37). Elevated glucose levels and hypoxia can boost the glycolysis of monocytes and macrophages and increase lactate production (21, 22). Lactate-driven histone lactylation in monocytes and macrophages regulates the expression of various angiogenic genes (24). In the present study, we showed that histone lactylation was enhanced in monocytes isolated from blood of PDR patients or under HG and hypoxia conditions. Hypoxia is well established for its role in EC growth and RNV (2). Hyperglycemia is typically considered detrimental to ECs. However, uncontrolled EC growth and severe RNV are often observed in the later stages of diabetic retinopathy, suggesting a more complex role of hyperglycemia in the progression of the disease (56). One possible mechanism is that cell-to-cell crosstalk promotes EC growth (57, 58). Our study reveals that increased histone lactylation in monocytes contributes to RNV, offering insights into the pathological processes underlying PDR.

Further results revealed that histone lactylation of monocytes regulated the transcription of Postn and subsequent protein levels of POSTN. POSTN expression is elevated in fibrovascular membranes (FVMs) from PDR patients and promotes RNV (29, 59). Alternative splicing within the C-terminal region of POSTN generates three splice variants along with the full-length form in FVMs (59). These isoforms may influence POSTN’s binding to extracellular matrix proteins and modulate integrin-FAK signaling (59, 60). Additionally, an exosome-associated spliced POSTN variant has been reported to enhance FAK signaling (31). However, our data show that elevated POSTN in monocyte-derived exosomes does not activate FAK signaling in ECs, suggesting a nonclassical role of exosomal POSTN in RNV. Nuclear localization signal (NLS) has been identified in POSTN, which are crucial for mediating the translocation of the protein from cytoplasm to the nucleus (61). Recent research shows that POSTN directly stabilizes HIF-1α and increases the translocation of HIF-1α to the nucleus (32, 33, 62). Our data show that exosomal POSTN stabilizes HIF-1α in ECs, leading to upregulation of VEGFA and GLUT1. Although Müller cells, retinal pigment epithelial cells, astrocytes, and immune cells are primary sources of VEGFA during angiogenic retinopathy, autocrine VEGFA from ECs also contributes (63). VEGFA, GLUT1, and other HIF-1α-regulated angiogenic genes drive retinal angiogenesis following exosomal POSTN treatment. These results suggest that exosomal POSTN exerts functions that extend beyond the extracellular environment, particularly in the context of pathological RNV.

Metformin is the first-line medication for T2D in most treatment guidelines; however, its underlying mechanisms are complex and not yet fully understood. Compared to other hypoglycemic therapies, metformin has been shown to reduce the risk of various diabetes-related microvascular complications, including PDR (64). For high-risk diabetic patients with a 15-y or more extended history of T2D, long-term use of metformin is independently associated with a significantly lower rate of PDR (65). These clinical findings suggest that metformin may play an unexpected role in delaying PDR beyond its glycemic control effects. Metformin exerts multidimensional benefits in T2D regulating mitochondrial function, oxidative stress, and autophagy (66). Notably, systemic administration of metformin reshapes the gut microbiota composition, which may contribute to its therapeutic effects (67, 68). The gut microbiome influences monocyte biology through microbial signals at extraintestinal sites (69), potentially contributing to metformin’s impact on monocyte. Our findings reveal that metformin inhibits monocyte glycolysis, lowers H4K8la and exosomal POSTN levels, and suppresses RNV. These findings provide molecular evidence supporting clinical observation and confirm that metformin effectively prevents RNV.

In summary, this study identified an uncharacterized mechanism underlying the progress of RNV through the crosstalk between monocytes and ECs. We demonstrated that monocyte-derived exosomes carried POSTN to promote RNV by stabilizing HIF-1α in ECs, thereby highlighting a key pathway by which monocytes influence retinal vascular pathology. Metformin could decrease monocytes’ H4K8la and exosomal POSTN levels and attenuate RNV (Fig. 7N). Together, these findings highlight the critical role of monocyte metabolism in RNV and suggest potential therapeutic targets for intervention.

There are some limitations in our study. Lyz2-CreERT2 transgenic mice were used to generate Hk2 and Postn-deleted mice in myeloid cells. The potential roles of Hk2 and Postn in neutrophils and monocyte-derived macrophages and dendritic cells in regulating RNV cannot be excluded. In addition, the molecular mechanisms governing the biogenesis and release of exosomal POSTN have not been fully defined, which warrants further investigation. It is worth noting that the OIR model does not fully recapitulate the pathological progression of PDR in primates, as it lacks the component of hyperglycemia.

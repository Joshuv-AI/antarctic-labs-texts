In vivo evidence supports the effectiveness of the longevity-associated protein LAV-BPIFB4 in reducing adipose tissue-derived mediators of systemic inflammation to prevent vascular insult and atheromatous change
Authors: Elena Ciaglia, Valentina Lopardo, Francesco Montella, Roberta Maria Esposito, Antonio Damato, Angela Carmelita Abate, Anna Maciag, Carmine Vecchione, Albino Carrizzo, Annibale Alessandro Puca
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObesity triggers chronic low-grade inflammation contributing to cardiovascular and metabolic diseases. Over-release of adipokines and pro-inflammatory mediators by white adipose tissue (WAT) enhances inflammation through a feedforward loop involving endothelial and immune cells, promoting atherosclerosis. Our previous studies showed that in vivo gene transfer of the longevity-associated variant (LAV) of BPIFB4 restores endothelial and cardiac function and reduces systemic inflammation in mouse models. Here we investigated the anti-inflammatory potential of orally administered recombinant rhLAV-BPIFB4 in ApoE-/- mice fed a high-fat diet to elucidate its role in modulating endothelial dysfunction primed by adipose tissue inflammation. We studied n = 5 ApoE-/- mice on standard diet (SD), n = 5 (VEH-HFD) and n = 6 (LAV-HFD) ApoE-/- mice fed high-fat diet without or with rhLAV-BPIFB4 protein. Primary pre-adipocyte cultures were established from epididymal WAT to evaluate CD45+CD38+ leukocyte infiltration, inflammatory profile of pre-adipocytes, and ex vivo effects of conditioned media on vessels. Oral administration of rhLAV-BPIFB4 in ApoE-/- mice fed high-fat diet dampens atherosclerosis by preserving endothelial integrity and reducing ICAM+ and CD68+ cell infiltration. Despite unchanged adiposity, systemically rhLAV-BPIFB4 reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1α/β, TNF-α, IL-6) while mildly increasing IL-10 levels. Supernatants from pre-adipocytes treated with rhLAV-BPIFB4 demonstrate similar anti-inflammatory cytokine profiles. Conditioned media from rhLAV-treated eWAT ex vivo restores endothelial function in dysfunctional arteries (VEH-HFD vs LAV-HFD, ***p < 0.001). Collectively our data show that targeting adipocyte-associated inflammation, LAV-BPIFB4 emerges as a promising therapeutic strategy to counteract endothelial dysfunction in obesity.KEYWORDS: Cytokines, atherosclerosis, longevity, vascular function, inflammation, high fat diet, immune response

Obesity and its complicating factor, primarily the impairment of endothelial function, represent a global health burden, with over 1 billion individuals currently affected [1]. Emerging concepts suggest that factors beyond absolute weight, such as the qualitative characteristics of adipose tissue and the development of a systemic inflammatory state, may be significant determinants of cardiovascular disease (CVD). While some anti-inflammatory agents can reduce inflammation and improve metabolic health, others, like statins and interleukin (IL) antagonists, might worsen adipose tissue dysfunction, especially in the context of obesity [2]. Therefore, a careful therapeutic modulation of the adipose phenotype could serve as a potential target for obesity treatment.

The answer may lie in Long-Living Individuals (LLIs) who have been found to possess stronger anti-inflammatory abilities, which significantly impact their quality of life. In line with its enrichment in LLIs, we have previously associated a polymorphic haplotype variant in the BPIFB4 gene (LAV-BPIFB4) with a reduced risk of cardiovascular complications and inflammation, thereby maintaining a healthy equilibrium between inflammatory and anti-inflammatory circuits [3]. Noteworthy, we previously associated the LAV haplotype with lower frailty in a broad cohort of elderly subjects and we observed the effect of systemic adeno-associated viral vector-mediated LAV-BPIFB4 gene transfer on delaying the progression of frailty in ageing mice [4]. Afterwards, our subsequent studies also showed the rejuvenating effect of the LAV-BPIFB4 gene therapy on the immune system of aged mice, demonstrated by decreased senescence status among the PBMCs, restored NAD+ plasmatic levels and reduced CD38-expressing macrophages [5]

Until now, AAV LAV-gene constructs have inspired therapeutic attempts to limit the effects of atherosclerosis [3]. Here we examine if the oral administration of rhLAV-BPIFB4 can extend the benefits of this variant to guarantee general protection against inflammatory burden triggered by adipose tissue following High-Fat Diet (HFD) in an atherosclerosis-prone background, also mimicking the condition of endothelial dysfunction resistant to statin [6].

ApoE -/- mice were fed for up to 1 week with normal rodent chow (4.5% fat) in the presence (rhLAV-BPIFB4-HFD) or absence (VEH-HFD) of recombinant LAV-BPIFB4 via gavage every 3 days. They were subsequently switched to a Western high-fat diet for an additional 3 weeks. ApoE-/- mouse controls were fed for additional 3 weeks with normal rodent chow (4.5% fat) in the absence of recombinant LAV-BPIFB4 (SD), as depicted in the diagram (Figure 1(A)).

rhLAV-BPIFB4 oral administration is capable not only of slowing down the atherogenic process but also of inducing a favourable skewing of the microenvironment of the plaque through reduced adhesion molecules (ICAM), lower monocyte/macrophages infiltration (CD68+), without loss of smooth muscle cells (enhanced a-SMA) (Figure 1(B)). Of note, the action of rhLAV-BPIFB4 was not dependent on the rescue of total adiposity index, which remained similarly elevated after rhLAV-BPIFB4 treatment (Figure 1C). Because LAV-BPIFB4 is key regulator of low-grade inflammation and associated complications in many disease states (atherosclerosis, ageing, inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), diabetes, frailty, etc.) this has suggested examining circulating cytokines. As expected, higher adiposity in mice fed a HFD is here associated with an enhancement of humoral inflammation in the body compared to mice fed a standard diet (SD) (Figure 1C). IL-1α and IL-1β, TNF-alpha, GM-CSF, MCP-1, IL-17 which promote a proinflammatory status, were decreased in rhLAV-BPIFB4 treated mice, while IL-6 levels appeared paradoxically protective in atherosclerosis-prone background, and IL-10, an immunoregulatory helpful cytokine to reduce tissue damage and balance acute inflammation, was modestly increased (Figure 1(C)).

Notably, accumulating evidence supports the direct involvement of visceral adipose tissue as a source of inflammation and a promoter of atherosclerosis and its complications [7]. Therefore, we speculated that a primary site of action for the atheroprotective effect of rhLAV is visceral adipose tissue (specifically, epididymal eWAT), acting directly on adipocytes not to change the degree of hypertrophy, but to alter the expression of cito-chemokines, shifting the balance towards a distinctly anti-inflammatory profile.

The multiplex ELISA of supernatants harvested following 24 h post pre-adipocytes isolation from eWAT recapitulate the systemic inflammatory signature (Figure 1(D)). In rhLAV-BPIFB4 treated mice, we found a reduction in the local levels of IL-1α and IL-1β, TNF-α, GM-CSF, MCP-1, IL-6, IL-17. The IL-10 levels differed from systemic levels with reduced secretion in pre-adipocytes supernatant of rhLAV-BPIFB4. The apparent discrepancy between systemic and local IL-10 levels may be explained by the still controversial role of IL-10 in WAT metabolism. Indeed, IL-10 ablation in mice has not caused insulin resistance, which would refute its important anti-inflammatory role in obesity [8] Rather, recent study in mice showed that IL-10 ablation improved insulin sensitivity via increased thermogenesis, energy expenditure, and browning of WAT [9].

Whether the cyto-chemokines plasma inflammatory burden is the consequence of the inflammation in visceral adipose tissues or the production of inflammatory immune cells which bone marrow tissue could drive in response to HFD is still speculative. However, the higher infiltration of CD38+CD45+ inflammatory cells in eWAT (Figure [D]) thus inhibited by rhLAV-BPIFB4, and the inflammatory nature of conditioned medium (CM) from epididymal tissues of HFD mice suggest that visceral adipose tissues were metabolically active and are the source of humoral and cellular inflammation in obese mice with established atherosclerosis.

It remains uncertain whether inflammation in these tissues leads to the development of atherosclerosis and arterial disease, or if it is a result of these conditions. We ultimately tested the influence of eWAT inflammation on endothelial dysfunction by exposing ex vivo healthy or dysfunctional mesenteric arteries to cell-free CM obtained from pre-adipocytes isolated from the eWAT of mice on a HFD, compared to CM from rhLAV-BPIFB4 treated mice. In contrast to healthy vessels, dysfunctional vessels from ApoE -/- mice, when exposed to eWAT-CM from rhLAV-treated mice, exhibited improved acetylcholine-mediated endothelial vasorelaxation and a notable enhancement of vascular reactivity as compared to eWAT-CM from veh-treated mice (Figure [E]).

In conclusion, we suggest that in addition to lifestyle modifications like weight loss, exercise, and healthy eating, elevated levels of LAV-BPIFB4 protein – attained both naturally through genetics and via recombinant protein supplementation – may represent a new strategy for metabolic management. This approach targets adipocytes, leveraging its pro-inflammatory properties to combat the inflammatory stress induced by adipose tissue and related damage.

Materials and methods

Mice models

All animal studies were performed according to approved protocols by the Istituto Superiore di Sanità, Rome (766/2018-PR) and were conducted according to EU Directive 2010/63/EU on animals used for scientific purposes.

Furthermore, to report in vivo experiments, the authors have adhered to ARRIVE guidelines. The ApoE-/- mice were born at the expected mandelian ratios, developed normally, and were maintained in a ventilated environment. All animals were randomly divided into the control and treatment groups.

Three months-old mice were fed up 1 week with standard rodent chow (4.5% fat) in presence or absence of 3 µg/mouse recombinant LAV-BPIFB4 (via gavage each 3 days) and subsequently switched to the Western diet-high fat diet (Complete feed for Rodents Purified Diet 60% ENERGY FROM FATS – Mucedola, see supplementary materials for more details), at 11 weeks of age for 3 weeks. The ApoE-/- mice controls were fed for the same weeks with standard rodent chow in absence of recombinant LAV-BPIFB4. The quantification of the results was performed by a second individual who was blind to the hypothesis that was being tested for each group. To reduce the number of animals used for this study, where possible, multiple biochemical, phenotypical, and immune functional analyses were conducted on the same group of mice. The investigators were blinded to group allocation during data collection and analysis. To ensure homogeneity of experimental cohorts, mice from the same F generation were assigned to experimental groups, such that age and weight were matched. The overall wellbeing of each mouse was however the selective inclusion criteria. No specific exclusion criteria were applied.

Only if occasional events (e.g. low number of cells, or because of the hemolyzed sample, etc.) occurred during the experimental procedures, animals were excluded from the analysis. All criteria were preestablished.

Power analysis has been conducted to determine an adequate number of mice to measure biochemical, phenotypical, and immunological effects in a statistically significant manner.

At the end of treatment, the epididymal white adipose tissue (eWAT) and plasma were collected. In brief, after deep anaesthesia, mice were sacrificed through decapitation and blood was collected in an EDTA tube; subsequently, using fine forceps and dissecting scissors, the eWAT pads were carefully dissected away from surrounding organs and tissues (e.g. testes, intestines), weighed and rapidly used for primary adipocyte isolation.

White adipose tissue and animal body weights have been measured to calculate the total adiposity index as follows:

Primary pre-adipocyte isolation

Epididymal white adipose tissue (eWAT, ~10 mg) was placed into a 1,5 ml tube. 100 μL of Adipocyte digestion buffer (DMEM 0,5% BSA +3 mg/mL type II collagenase) was added, gently resuspended, and incubated for 1 h at 37°C with vigorous shaking every 10 min. The cell suspension was centrifuged at 500xg for 10 minutes to separate floating adipocytes from pelleted stromal vascular fraction (pre-adipocytes and leukocytes). The floating fat layer was gently aspirated, being careful not to disrupt the pellet. The pellet was resuspended in complete α-MEM (300 μl) and filtered through a 70 μm cell strainer on a 50-ml conical tube to remove undigested tissue fragments. The cell suspension was plated into a 48-well plate, by changing the media every day until the cells were confluent. Following 24 hours in culture, infiltrating leucocytes were collected for FACS analysis. In detail, the floating infiltrating leucocytes were collected in a FACS tube, gently pipetting, and centrifuged at 1900 rpm for 5 min with brake. The supernatant was discarded, and cell pellets were resuspended for FACS analysis. A primary pre-adipocyte cell line was established after 16 days in culture and used for FACS analysis and WB analysis. Following 24 hours in culture, supernatants were harvested for cyto/chemokines dosage.

Western blot analysis

For preparation of lysates, pre-adipocytes were lysed in 20 µL of modified RIPA buffer (50 mM Tris pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1% Triton X-100, 0.1% SDS, 0.5% deoxycholic acid, 1% NP-40, 10 mM sodium phosphate) containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors for 2 h on ice. Afterwards, samples were centrifuged at 14,000 × g for 20 min at 4°C. Protein concentrations were determined spectrophotometrically using BCA reagent (Pierce™ BCA Protein Assay Kits, ThermoScientific). Western blot analysis was performed following standard procedures. 15 μg of protein was separated electrophoretically using SDS polyacrylamide gel (Bio-Rad). Proteins were transferred on 0.2 μm Trans-Blot® Turbo™ Mini Nitrocellulose membrane (Bio-Rad) using a Trans-Blot® Turbo™ Transfer System (Bio-Rad #1704150). The membrane was blocked with 5% milk (Bio-Rad, Richmond, CA, USA) in TBS containing 0.05% Tween-20 (TBST) at room temperature for 1 h and probed overnight at 4°C with the following murine primary antibodies: Phospho-p38 MAPK (Thr180/Tyr182) [Cell Signalling technologies; #9216], Phospho-Akt (Ser473) (D9E) [Cell Signalling technologies; #4060], GAPDH [Sigma Aldrich; #G8795], and β-actin [Sigma Aldrich; #A2228]. Immunodetection of specific proteins was carried out with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated donkey anti-rabbit IgG (Bio-Rad), using the enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) system (Thermo Fisher Scientific) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and then exposed to X-ray films (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Western-blot data were analysed using Photoshop software to determine the optical density (OD) of the bands. The OD readings of protein were expressed as a ratio relative to GAPDH or β-actin.

Cytofluorimetric analysis

Murine epididymal white adipose tissue-infiltrating leucocytes were stained with mAb against mouse CD45-PE (Miltenyi Biotech, #130–110-797, clone REA737), CD38-APC CD38-FITC (Biolegend, #102705), and incubated for 20 min at 4°C in the dark. Following a washing step, cells were analysed using a FACS Verse Flow Cytometer (BD Biosciences).

Cytokine detection

IL-23, IL1α, IFNγ, TNFα, MCP-1, IL-12p70, IL-1β, IL-10, IL-6, IL-27, IL-17A, IFNβ, GM-CSF levels in mice plasma and supernatants from isolated pre-adipocytes (post 24 h) were determined using a murine beads-based multiplex ELISA (LEGENDplex™ Mouse Inflammation Panel 13-plex; Biolegend, USA). Plasma and supernatants were incubated for 2 h with the beads and for 1 h with the detection antibodies, followed by 30 min incubation with SA-PE. After washing, beads were resuspended in washing buffer and acquired using a FACS VERSE flow cytometer (BD Biosciences). Data were analysed with the LEGENDplex Data Analysis Software.

Histology

Mice aorta were embedded in Tissue Tek resin, frozen, and cryo-sectioned at 7uM using a cryostat Leica CM1950 (Leica Microsystems, Wetzlar, Germany). Sections were fixed with 2% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 20 minutes at RT and stained with Masson’s trichrome (Bio-Optica, #04-010802) following manufacturer’s instructions. Images were acquired using Olympus BX-41 Upright Microscope.

Immunofluorescence

Mice aorta cryo-sections were fixed with 2% PFA for 20 minutes at RT. Sections were permeabilized with 0,07% Triton X-100, blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 minutes, and incubated overnight with following primary antibodies: alpha-SMA (Antibodies, #A82445), CD68 (Abcam, #ab955), and ICAM-1 (ABclonal, #A5597). Tissues were incubated with the following secondary antibodies: DyLight 488 conjugated anti-mouse IgG (Vector Laboratories, #DI-2488), DyLight 549 conjugated anti-rabbit IgG (Vector Laboratories, #DI-1549), anti-goat IgG biotinylated (Vector Laboratories, #BA-9500), and conjugated with Streptavidin Alexa Fluor 647 (Invitrogen, Thermofisher, #S32357). Nuclei were stained with DAPI. Images were acquired using Leica Confocal Laser Scanning Fluorescence Microscope TCS SP5 (Leica Microsystems).

Ex vivo studies of mouse vessels

Vascular reactivity studies were performed on segments of healthy or dysfunctional murine femoral arteries obtained from control and from ApoE under HFD mice. Vessels were collected immediately after surgical harvesting to perform vascular reactivity studies. Endothelial-dependent vasorelaxation was assessed before and after 1.5 hour of incubation with post-24 h supernatant from pre-adipocytes isolated from murine eWAT and reported as dose-responses to acetylcholine (ACh).

Statistical analysis

In all experiments shown, statistical analysis was performed by using the GraphPad Prism 9.0 software for Windows (GraphPad software). For each type of assay or phenotypic analysis, data obtained from multiple experiments are calculated as mean ± SD and analysed for statistical significance using appropriate tests. An analysis of variance (ANOVA) for multiple comparison and followed by post-hoc analysis p values < 0.05 were considered significant; *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001.

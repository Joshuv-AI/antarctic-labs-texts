Stacked Sandwich-type Beauvericin Complex Revealed by Cryogenic Ion Mobility and Infrared Spectroscopy: Insight into Selective Transport Mechanism of Ca2+ Ion
Authors: Shunsuke Kamioka, Kien X. Vo, Keisuke Hirata, Ryosuke Ito, Keijiro Ohshimo, James M. Lisy, Masaaki Fujii, Shun-ichi Ishiuchi, Fuminori Misaizu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying ion transport selectivity is a central issue in supramolecular chemistry and biochemistry. Beauvericin (Bv), a naturally occurring cyclic hexadepsipeptide, selectively transports physiologically relevant Ca2+ across cell membranes. Although the 2:1 (Bv-M-Bv) complex has been proposed as a key driver of ion transport, its detailed conformations and contribution to selectivity remain unclear. Here, we systematically investigated the Bv-M-Bv complexes with alkaline-earth and alkali metal ions using cryogenic ion mobility and cryogenic IR spectroscopy. Comparison of collision cross sections, IR spectra, and quantum chemical calculations enabled strongly supported assignments to the stacked, tilted, and shifted conformers, whose relative abundances depend strongly on the size and charge of the encapsulated metal ions. Notably, the Bv-Ca2+-Bv complex preferentially adopts the most compact stacked conformation, which may enhance diffusion across cell membranes. These findings provide molecular-level insights into ion transport selectivity governed by structural flexibility.

Ion recognition is a fundamental process in supramolecular chemistry and has made significant contributions to fields such as biology, medicine, pharmacology, and material science. Ionophores, natural molecules primarily produced by microorganisms, can selectively recognize and transport specific ions across biological membranes, a process known as ion transport. Over the past decades, numerous ionophores have been isolated, and their ion transport selectivity has been extensively characterized. These compounds have attracted attention not only as potential antibiotics, , antiviral, and anticancer agents, but also for the development of lithium-ion batteries and artificial cells. Because their diverse biological activities and industrial applications originate from their ability to selectively recognize and transport specific ions, a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying ion transport selectivity in natural ionophores is crucial for the development of new therapeutics and functional materials based on ion recognition.

Beauvericin (Bv) is a natural cyclic hexadepsipeptide ionophore, reported to selectively transport Ca2+ and Ba2+ depending on the experimental conditions. − However, its ion-selective transport mechanism is still not fully understood, partly because studies conducted under different experimental conditions have yielded inconsistent results. Extraction and U-tube transport experiments in water/organic solvent systems showed that Bv efficiently transports Ca2+ and Ba2+, with low efficiency for Mg2+. , In contrast, ion transport studies using liposomes suggested that Bv has high affinity for Ca2+, but negligible transport for Mg2+, Sr2+, and Ba2+. These discrepancies highlight the need for molecular-level investigations to clarify the main factors governing ion transport selectivity. However, such investigations are particularly challenging due to structural flexibility of Bv, which consists of alternating N-methyl-l-phenylalanine and d-hydroxyisovaleric acid residues, forming a macrocyclic structure with flexible benzyl and isopropyl side chains (Figure a). Importantly, Bv has an amphiphilic property that enables solubility and membrane permeability through conformational adaptation in response to the surrounding environment. This molecular flexibility allows Bv to adopt multiple conformations during ion-selective transport.

Previous solution-phase studies suggested that, in addition to 1:1 complexes, higher-order complexes also contribute to ion transport. , Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy, and bilayer membrane conductivity measurements suggested that Bv can stably form 1:1, 2:1, and 3:2 (Bv: metal ion) complexes depending on the metal ions in solution. In particular, diffusion-ordered spectroscopy (DOSY) indicated that Bv predominantly forms a 2:1 complex with Ca2+ in chloroform. The formation of higher-order complexes is promoted by the presence of excess Bv, which may affect the ion transport selectivity. Indeed, transport experiments performed with different concentrations of Bv showed different ordering of the ions. When the Bv concentration was (1) more than 1000-fold lower, (2) equimolar, or (3) 5-fold higher than that of the metal salts, the orders were (1) Ca2+ ≪ Ba2+, (2) Ca2+ > Ba2+, and (3) Ca2+ only, respectively. These disparate observations indicate that additional investigation is warranted.

Recently, gas-phase studies have been applied to Bv-metal ion complexes. The gas-phase environment allows precise control over the composition of the complexes, solvation, and counterion effects. Moreover, it mimics the hydrophobic interior of lipid membrane in terms of electric properties. − For the 1:1 complex, our previous study using cryogenic infrared (IR) spectroscopy revealed that Bv strongly interacts with Na+, Mg2+, and Ca2+ among alkaline-earth and alkali metal ions in the gas-phase due to size-matching within the central cavity. , This finding was inconsistent with results obtained in the condensed-phase; in other words, binding affinity alone does not determine the reported Ca2+ and Ba2+ transport selectivity of Bv. Further investigations of hydration effects revealed that the cation is dynamically pulled out of the central cavity upon single hydration, suggesting a potential ion release mechanism. , Despite numerous efforts to elucidate the ion-selective transport mechanism of Bv, the fundamental question of what ultimately determines this selectivity remains unresolved. One possible determinant is the molecular conformation of Bv-metal ion complexes, because compact and apolar conformations can enhance membrane permeability. − Our previous study using ion mobility-mass spectrometry (IM-MS) revealed that the 1:1 complex specifically forms a highly compact and apolar conformation with Ba2+ (Figure b), whereas the other alkaline-earth and alkali metal ion complexes adopt bulky and polar conformations (Figure c). Furthermore, this specific structure accurately explained the IR spectrum of the Bv-Ba2+ complex. These recent gas-phase findings suggest that ion transport selectivity by Bv may arise not only from binding preference but also from transport efficiency, with the compact and apolar 1:1 conformation contributing to the distinct transport behavior of Ba2+. In contrast, Ca2+ transport selectivity cannot be rationalized solely on the basis of the 1:1 complex, implying the involvement of alternative binding motifs. In particular, the sandwich-type 2:1 (Bv-M-Bv) complex may also be advantageous for the ion transport process, as the cation is effectively shielded by the two Bv ligands (Figure d), thereby enhancing membrane permeability. Therefore, elucidating the detailed conformations of the Bv-M-Bv complexes is essential for understanding the molecular mechanism underlying the biologically relevant Ca2+ transport selectivity.

Previously, IM-MS using N2 buffer gas at room temperature suggested the presence of at least two major conformational families depending on the ionic radii: “closed” (compact) and “open” (elongated) conformers, preferentially formed with larger and smaller metal ions, respectively. , Although these closed/open motifs provided useful insights into the global geometries of the Bv-M-Bv complexes, the local coordination structures defining the overall conformations remained unclear. In particular, the conformational trends in the alkaline-earth and alkali metal ion complexes did not fully conform to this simplified model. These discrepancies motivated a more detailed investigation not only of the overall molecular shapes, but also of the local intermolecular interactions between the Bv ligands and the metal ion, to achieve well-constrained structural assignments of the highly flexible Bv-M-Bv complexes.

In this work, we employed cryogenic drift-tube ion mobility spectrometry (DTIMS) and cryogenic ion trap IR spectroscopy, both coupled with electrospray ionization-mass spectrometry, to elucidate the detailed conformations of the Bv-M-Bv complexes with alkaline-earth (M = Mg2+, Ca2+, Sr2+, Ba2+) and alkali metal ions (M = Li+, Na+, K+, Rb+, Cs+). Cryogenic ion mobility spectrometry suppresses structural changes and enhances the resolving power of a collision-cross section (CCS) distribution. − Cryogenic ion trap IR spectroscopy suppresses thermal fluctuations and enables the measurement of high-resolution vibrational spectra. − The CCS distributions and IR spectra provide complementary information on the Bv sandwich-type conformations, yielding insights into the overall shape and the interactions between the carbonyl oxygens (CO) and the metal ion, respectively. When combined with quantum chemical calculations, these data permit well-constrained structural assignments of the Bv-M-Bv complexes. Details of the experimental apparatus , and theoretical method are provided in the Supporting Information. Building on previous findings for the 1:1 Bv complexes, we therefore focused on the relationship between the conformations of flexible ionophore-metal ion complexes and their membrane permeability, as a key factor governing ion transport selectivity.

The relative abundances of the conformers depended on the ionic radii and charges of the encapsulated metal ions. Figure b shows the plot of the experimental CCSs measured by DTIMS with He buffer gas at 86 K (DTCCSHe,86) of the most intense bands in the CCS distributions against the ionic radii of the encapsulated metal ions. For the alkali metal ion complexes, DTCCSHe,86 was largest for Li+ (366.9 ± 1.3 Å2) and Na+ (366.6 ± 0.2 Å2), dropping to a minimum at K+ (359.6 ± 0.4 Å2), and then increased toward Rb+ (360.8 ± 0.3 Å2) and Cs+ (362.3 ± 0.7 Å2). For the alkaline-earth metal ion complexes, DTCCSHe,86 decreased from Mg2+ (373.1 ± 0.4 Å2) to a minimum at Ca2+(354.4 ± 0.2 Å2) before increasing toward Sr2+ (355.4 ± 0.5 Å2) and Ba2+ (363.2 ± 1.0 Å2). These observations demonstrated that DTCCSHe,86 of the Bv-M-Bv complexes does not exhibit a monotonic increase with increasing metal ion radius. A similar non-monotonic trend was observed for both alkaline-earth and alkali metal ions: the small metal ion complexes tend to adopt a bulky conformation, the intermediate-sized divalent metal ion complexes adopt a highly compact conformation, and the large metal ion complexes adopt a moderately compact conformation. Notably, the Ca2+ complex adopts the most compact conformation among the alkaline-earth and alkali metal ion complexes.

To probe the interactions between the CO groups of Bv and the metal ion, we performed cryogenic ion trap IR spectroscopy for the Bv-M-Bv complexes. Figure shows the IR photodissociation (IRPD) spectra of the Bv-M-Bv (M = Li+, Na+, K+, Rb+, Cs+, Mg2+, Ca2+, Sr2+, Ba2+) complexes obtained using H2 tagging. The bands in the ranges of 1580–1700 and 1730–1790 cm–1 were assigned to the amide CO (green) and ester CO (blue) stretching vibrations, respectively. The spectral features were also correlated with the ionic radii and charges of the encapsulated metal ions. The spectra of the Li+ and Mg2+ complexes were similar, both exhibiting a splitting of the amide CO bands into two. The Na+ complex displayed distinctive spectral features, multiplets in both the amide and ester regions. The spectra of the K+, Rb+, and Cs+ complexes were similar, with the amide CO bands displaying a main peak accompanied by a shoulder. In particular, the Ca2+, Sr2+, and Ba2+ complexes exhibited the amide CO band as a single peak, indicating a highly symmetric configuration of the amide CO groups. The spectral similarities in the IRPD spectra suggest the formation of analogous conformations for these two sets of ion complexes.

There are some specific takeaways from the trends in both the CCS and IR spectra. For Li+ and Na+, there is “incomplete” encapsulation of the ions by the two ionophores (less than 6-fold coordination) leading to broad IR spectra and large CCSs, while K+ to Cs+ have more compact and resolvable spectral features and smaller CCSs. This suggests that the larger ions will be more effectively sequestered in a hydrophobic environment by the two Bvs. Similarly Mg2+ has lower coordination with similar spectral and CCS attributes as Li+ and Na+, while the larger alkaline earth dications have simple, similar IR spectra and smaller CCSs, as their larger alkali metal counterparts.

To assign the conformations observed in the CCS distributions and IRPD spectra, we performed a conformational search using the CONFLEX 8 program with the MMFF94s force field. , 5,000–10,000 conformers were generated and subsequently classified into three types based on the positional relationship between the two Bv ligands (Figure S2 and Table S5): stacked, tilted, and shifted, as illustrated in Figure . The stacked conformer indicates that the two Bv ligands are perfectly overlapping and parallel, leading to 6-fold coordination of the cation by the available amide CO groups from both ligands. The tilted conformer exhibits partial overlap accompanied by tilting yet maintaining noncovalent interaction with all six amide CO groups. The shifted conformer is characterized by substantial lateral displacement and less than 6-fold coordination by the cation. The theoretical CCSs increased in the order of stacked < tilted < shifted. Details of conformer definitions are found in the Supporting Information.

We also compared the IRPD spectra with the calculated IR spectra for well-constrained structural assignment. The calculated IR spectra of the assigned structures are shown in Figure , and Figures S3–S11 and Tables S6–S14 present comparisons of the calculated IR spectra of the most stable stacked, tilted, and shifted conformers with the experimental IRPD spectra. Harmonic vibrational frequencies were scaled by 0.979 for comparison with experimental IR spectra. The split bands of the Li+ and Mg2+ complexes are well reproduced by the calculated spectra of the shifted conformers with four coordination. The distinctive spectral features of the Na+ complex are also reproduced by the shifted conformer with four coordination. The main peak and the shoulder observed in the K+, Rb+, and Cs+ complexes are well reproduced by the tilted conformers with 6-fold coordination. The single peaks observed in the Ca2+, Sr2+, and Ba2+ complexes are also well reproduced by the stacked conformers with 6-fold coordination.

The comparison of overall shape from the CCS distributions, the interactions between the CO groups and the metal ion from the IR spectra, and theoretical calculations of the Bv-M-Bv complexes enabled strongly supported assignments of the stacked, tilted, and shifted conformers. Moreover, a systematic investigation across alkaline-earth and alkali metal ion complexes demonstrated that conformational preference in the Bv-M-Bv complexes depends on the size and charge of the encapsulated metal ions. The stacked conformer, characterized by perfect alignment of the two Bv ligands with alternately oriented side chains, resulting in the smallest CCS, and by highly symmetric 6-fold coordination of the amide CO groups, resulting in a single stretching band, is selectively adopted with moderately sized divalent metal ions. The tilted conformer, characterized by partial overlap of the ligands with a tilt, resulting in moderate CCSs, and by slightly distorted 6-fold coordination, resulting in a shoulder band, is selectively adopted with large alkali metal ions. The shifted conformer, characterized by lateral displacement of the ligands, resulting in the largest CCSs, and by reduced coordination numbers, resulting in split bands, is selectively adopted with smallest metal ions.

Compared with the conformational motifs proposed in previous studies, the stacked and tilted conformers can be regarded as refined subclasses of the previously proposed compact “closed” conformer, corresponding to tightly and loosely packed structures, respectively. In contrast, the shifted conformer provides a more precise structural description of the elongated conformer. Thus, the present conformer-resolved classification using cryogenic ion mobility and cryogenic IR spectroscopy refines and extends the previous closed/open structural framework, which was primarily based on overall topological features inferred from two resolved ion mobility peaks.

The most significant discrepancies between the CCS and IR data involve the largest dications, Sr2+ and Ba2+. The CCS data show clearly multiple conformers, two for Sr2+ and three for Ba2+, while the IR spectra for these two ions are remarkably similar to Ca2+, with a single amide CO band and similar band shapes for the ester CO region, rather clear evidence for a single conformer. These discrepancies may arise because the CCS and IR data sets were gathered under different experimental conditions: 86 and 10 K respectively. Although the Bv-M-Bv complexes in both experiments were initially prepared directly from the ESI sources, processing of the complexes prior to measurements differed. In the cryogenic IM-MS, the ions were accumulated in the quadrupole ion trap (QIT) for 40 ms while colliding with room temperature He prior to injection into the drift tube maintained at 85.9 ± 0.1 K. In contrast, for the cryogenic IR spectroscopy, the ions were guided to the QIT, where they were cooled to ∼10 K by collisions with He/H2 buffer gas before spectroscopic interrogation. Such cryogenic trapping and buffer gas cooling conditions may promote collisional annealing and conformational relaxation toward the lowest-energy structures prior to IR measurement. Our DFT calculations further suggested that the stacked and tilted conformers become increasingly close in energy from Ca2+ to Ba2+ complexes. For the Ba2+ complex in particular, the calculated energy difference between the stacked and tilted conformers is extremely small and within uncertainty of the calculations. Therefore, even subtle temperature-dependent shifts in conformer populations could alter the dominant conformer under different experimental conditions. Under the lower-temperature conditions of the IR experiment, the lowest-energy conformer would be expected to dominate, whereas under the higher effective temperatures relevant to ion mobility measurements, entropically favored conformers could contribute more significantly to the observed CCS distributions. This interpretation may explain the apparent differences between the CCS and IR data sets.

To further examine the relationship between the well-resolved structures and transport properties in the Bv-M-Bv complexes, we discuss their membrane permeability, which is known to be strongly influenced in large flexible molecules by both surface polarity and conformational compactness. Surface polarity can be quantitatively evaluated using solvent accessible 3D polar surface area (SA 3D PSA). As summarized in Table S15, the ratios of SA 3D PSA to the total solvent accessible surface area (SA SA) were similarly low (3–4%) for all considered conformers. This likely reflects the fact that the metal ion is completely shielded by the two Bv ligands in all conformers, resulting in only minor differences in exposed polarity. These results suggest that membrane permeability in the Bv-M-Bv complexes may be influenced less by surface polarity and more by conformational compactness. In this regard, the stacked conformer, which is selectively formed in the Ca2+ complex, appears to be the most advantageous among the possible conformers because of its smallest CCS. Therefore, the selective formation of the 2:1 complexes, previously supported by DOSY measurements in low-dielectric chloroform solution, together with enhanced membrane permeability arising from tight packing, may represent one of the crucial factors underlying the Ca2+-selective transport behavior of Bv.

In conclusion, this work revealed the detailed conformations of the sandwich-type 2:1 beauvericin (Bv) complexes with alkaline-earth and alkali metal ions using cryogenic ion mobility spectrometry and cryogenic ion trap IR spectroscopy, both coupled with electrospray ionization-mass spectrometry. The combination of CCS distributions, IR spectra, and theoretical calculations enabled strongly supported assignments of the stacked, tilted, and shifted conformers, in which the two Bv ligands are perfectly aligned, partially overlapping with a tilt, and laterally displaced, respectively. These detailed conformational assignments, achieved through the combination of global structural information (CCS) and local structural information (vibrational frequencies), refine and extend previously established conformational frameworks that relied primarily on overall molecular shapes. The systematic investigation across alkaline-earth and alkali metal ion complexes revealed a clear trend: the stacked conformer is selectively adopted with the moderately sized divalent metal ions, the tilted conformer with the large metal ions, and the shifted conformer with the small metal ions. Notably, the Ca2+ complex adopts the most compact stacked conformation among the alkaline-earth and alkali metal ion complexes in the gas phase, where the cation is effectively sequestered by the two Bv ligands. Since all 2:1 complexes exhibit similarly low exposed polarity, the distinguishing structural feature of the Ca2+ complex is its exceptionally compact stacked geometry. This compactness, reflected in the smallest CCS, may enhance membrane permeability across the hydrophobic interior of lipid bilayers. Therefore, the selective formation of the compact stacked conformer in the 2:1 complex likely contributes to the Ca2+-selective transport of Bv by facilitating membrane permeability. It should be noted, however, that ion transport involves multiple processes, including ion binding, dehydration, formation of the transport complex, membrane entry, diffusion, and ion release. Thus, the present work provides one important structural factor underlying Ca2+-selective transport mechanism, rather than a complete mechanistic description of selective ion transport. We believe that these findings will inspire the development of novel therapeutic ionophores and environmentally friendly ion recognition materials that leverage structural flexibilities.

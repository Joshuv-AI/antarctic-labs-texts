Antiviral Therapy and Post‐Resection Outcomes in Patients With Hepatitis B Virus‐Related Hepatocellular Carcinoma
Authors: Jaehong Jeong, Jung Pyo Hong, Dong Yun Kim, Jae Seung Lee, Mi Na Kim, Seung Up Kim, Beom Kyung Kim, Jun Yong Park, Do Young Kim, Sang Hoon Ahn, Hye Won Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground & AimsThe comparative outcomes of entecavir (ETV), tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), and tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) in patients with hepatitis B virus (HBV)‐related hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) after curative resection remain unclear. This study aimed to evaluate the survival benefits of these antiviral regimens in a large real‐world cohort.MethodsWe analysed 902 patients with BCLC stage 0 or A HBV‐related HCC who underwent curative resection between September 2001 and August 2025. Patients received ETV (n = 474), TDF (n = 363), or TAF (n = 65) as first‐line therapy within 3 months post‐resection. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was implemented to balance baseline covariates. The primary and secondary endpoints were overall survival (OS) and recurrence‐free survival (RFS).ResultsThe IPTW‐adjusted 5‐year OS rates for ETV, TDF, and TAF were 90.7%, 95.2%, and 97.1%, respectively (global p = 0.079). The 5‐year RFS rates were comparable for ETV, TDF, and TAF (57.4% vs. 61.0% vs. 69.4%; global p = 0.533). In multivariable Cox regression analysis, however, TDF was independently associated with a significantly lower risk of death compared with ETV (aHR 0.48; 95% CI, 0.26–0.89; p = 0.021). TAF showed higher survival rates compared to ETV, although the difference did not reach statistical significance (aHR 0.34; 95% CI, 0.04–2.55; p = 0.292).ConclusionThe choice of antiviral regimen was not associated with RFS after curative resection for HBV‐related HCC, whereas TDF was independently associated with improved OS compared with ETV. Further large‐scale studies are warranted to validate the long‐term prognostic impact of TAF.Keywords: chronic hepatitis B, hepatectomy, hepatocellular carcinoma, survival analysis, Tenofovir

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the third leading cause of cancer death globally, with chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection accounting for approximately 50% of cases [1, 2, 3]. In the Asia‐Pacific region, HBV remains the predominant aetiology of HCC mortality despite evolving epidemiological trends [4]. For early‐stage HBV‐related HCC, liver resection is a recommended first‐line curative‐intent option in selected surgical candidates and can achieve long‐term survival [5, 6, 7]. Despite improvements in surgical techniques and perioperative care [8], the prognosis is frequently compromised by recurrence rates reaching 70% within five years [9, 10]. Post‐resection recurrence typically arises from early recurrence or late de novo carcinogenesis in the cirrhotic background [11]. Since high viral load and persistent hepatic inflammation drive both mechanisms, effective viral suppression in the adjuvant setting is imperative to mitigate recurrence risk [12].

Current major international guidelines recommend potent nucleos(t)ide analogues (NAs) with a high genetic barrier to resistance as preferred first‐line therapy for chronic HBV, including entecavir (ETV) and tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), with more recent updates also endorsing tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) [13, 14, 15]. While these agents effectively suppress HBV Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA) replication and improve liver function, whether the choice of NA differentially reduces HCC recurrence remains controversial; specifically, it is still debated whether ETV or TDF is superior for HCC prevention in patients with chronic hepatitis B (CHB) [16]. Early observational cohorts and meta‐analyses suggested that TDF may lower HCC development or post‐resection recurrence compared with ETV [17]. In contrast, subsequent studies applying propensity score–based methods have often reported no material difference in HCC‐related outcomes between the ETV and TDF, underscoring residual confounding and ongoing uncertainty [18, 19].

In this context, TAF has become an increasingly used alternative in routine practice. As a phosphonamidate prodrug of tenofovir, TAF was developed to enhance hepatocellular delivery of the active metabolite while reducing systemic tenofovir exposure [20]. In phase 3 trials, TAF showed non‐inferior antiviral efficacy compared with TDF, and longer follow‐up has shown sustained viral suppression with continued TAF treatment [21]. However, it remains uncertain whether these pharmacologic features translate into improved HCC prevention. Real‐world studies comparing TAF with TDF or ETV have primarily evaluated incident HCC in non‐surgical CHB cohorts, providing limited insight into post‐resection recurrence [22, 23].

In the post‐resection setting, comparative oncologic evidence regarding antiviral selection remains incompletely defined; even for comparisons between TDF and ETV, post‐resection studies have yielded heterogeneous findings and have not consistently incorporated TAF [24, 25, 26, 27]. As TAF has been increasingly adopted in clinical practice, limited direct comparative evidence on post‐resection outcomes among ETV, TDF, and TAF remains an important knowledge gap in guiding long‐term antiviral therapy after curative resection. To address this gap, we conducted a retrospective cohort study of patients with HBV‐related HCC undergoing curative resection to assess long‐term post‐resection outcomes across ETV, TDF, and TAF.

Methods

Study Design and Population

This retrospective cohort study included patients with HBV‐related HCC who underwent curative liver resection as first‐line therapy at a tertiary centre (Severance Hospital; Seoul, Republic of Korea) between September 2001 and August 2025. Eligible patients had CHB (hepatitis B surface antigen positivity for ≥ 6 months) and Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage 0 or A disease. HCC was diagnosed based on typical imaging hallmarks, defined as arterial phase hyperenhancement with washout in the portal venous or delayed phases on dynamic computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), in accordance with major international guidelines. The diagnosis was subsequently confirmed by histological examination of the resected surgical specimen [5, 6, 7].

Patients were required to receive oral nucleos(t)ide analogue therapy (ETV, TDF, or TAF) at the time of hepatectomy or to initiate therapy within 3 months after surgery. Exclusion criteria were: (1) prior non‐surgical HCC treatment before resection, including locoregional or systemic therapy (e.g., transarterial chemoembolisation (TACE), transarterial radioembolisation (TARE), radiofrequency ablation (RFA), or neoadjuvant chemotherapy); (2) combined treatment modalities at the time of resection (e.g., concomitant intraoperative ablation); (3) macrovascular invasion, lymph node involvement, or extrahepatic metastasis; (4) BCLC stage B or higher; and (5) follow‐up duration < 3 months. To minimise immortal time and treatment‐timing bias, patients who did not receive oral nucleos(t)ide analogue therapy or initiated therapy > 3 months after resection were excluded. The patient inclusion and exclusion process is summarised in a flow diagram (Figure 1). Patients were categorised according to their initial post‐resection antiviral regimen: ETV, TDF, or TAF. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Severance Hospital (IRB No. 4–2014‐0303). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design and use of de‐identified data.

Study flow diagram.

Post‐Resection Surveillance and Ascertainment of Recurrence

Post‐resection surveillance was performed in accordance with major international guidelines. Patients underwent clinical assessment, serum alpha‐fetoprotein (AFP) measurement, and dynamic contrast‐enhanced imaging (CT or MRI) every 3 months for the first 2 years and every 6 months thereafter to detect recurrence at an early stage. Recurrence was defined as the presence of a new intrahepatic or extrahepatic lesion showing radiologic features consistent with HCC on dynamic imaging, or histologic confirmation when available [5, 6, 7]. The date of recurrence was defined as the date of the first imaging study demonstrating recurrence.

Covariates and Outcomes

Baseline demographic, clinical, laboratory, and tumour‐related variables were extracted from the electronic medical records (EMR) of Severance Hospital. Baseline laboratory covariates were defined using the laboratory values measured at the time of curative resection (index date). Tumour characteristics were determined from preoperative imaging and pathology reports when available. The primary outcome was overall survival (OS), defined as the time from the index date to death from any cause; deaths were ascertained from EMR, including inpatient and outpatient records. The secondary outcome was recurrence‐free survival (RFS), defined as the time from the index date to the first occurrence of radiologic recurrence or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. Patients without an event were censored at the date of the last available follow‐up. Because exposure was defined by the initial post‐resection antiviral regimen, treatment switching during follow‐up was handled using an as‐treated approach, with patients censored at the date of regimen modification. For analyses focused on 5‐year outcomes, follow‐up time was administratively censored at 60 months.

Statistical Analysis

Baseline characteristics were summarised by treatment group (ETV, TDF, and TAF). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range [IQR]), and categorical variables as number (percentage). Missing baseline covariates were handled using multiple imputation with chained equations (MICE) under a missing‐at‐random assumption (40 imputed datasets with five iterations). To mitigate confounding, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was applied using propensity scores estimated for the three treatment groups using multinomial logistic regression. The propensity score model included prespecified baseline covariates: Age, body mass index (BMI), log‐transformed HBV DNA, platelet count, log‐transformed aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and alanine aminotransferase (ALT), international normalised ratio, albumin, maximum tumour diameter, and liver cirrhosis. Extreme weights were trimmed at the 99th percentile. Covariate balance was assessed using absolute standardised mean differences (ASMDs), summarised as the maximum ASMD across pairwise comparisons (Max ASMD; acceptable if < 0.2).

OS and RFS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Between‐group differences were assessed using log‐rank tests, and IPTW‐adjusted Kaplan–Meier curves were generated using trimmed weights. Prognostic factors for OS and RFS were evaluated using univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards models. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residual tests; when non‐proportional hazards were suggested for age, AFP, and tumour size, sensitivity analyses allowing time‐varying effects for these covariates were performed by incorporating interactions with log(time+1), where time was measured in months.

To account for the possibility of non‐HCC death prior to recurrence, a competing risk analysis was performed, with non‐HCC death before recurrence treated as the competing event. The cumulative incidence function (CIF) for recurrence was estimated using the Aalen–Johansen estimator and compared across regimens using Grey's test. The cause‐specific hazard for recurrence was evaluated using a cause‐specific Cox proportional hazards model, and subdistribution hazard ratios were estimated using the Fine–Grey model.

Post‐recurrence survival was measured from recurrence to death or last follow‐up, censored at 60 months, and compared using Kaplan–Meier and Cox methods. Year of resection and year of recurrence were added to the respective survival models to assess calendar‐period effects, and a regimen‐by‐cirrhosis interaction term tested effect modification. All analyses were conducted using R (version 4.5.2), and a two‐sided p‐value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

Between September 2001 and August 2025, 1454 patients with HBV‐related HCC underwent curative liver resection at Severance Hospital. After applying the predefined eligibility criteria (Figure 1), 902 patients who underwent resection as first‐line therapy and received antiviral therapy within 3 months after resection were included. The study population comprised 474 patients in the ETV group, 363 in the TDF group, and 65 in the TAF group. The median follow‐up duration was 60.0 months (IQR, 42.3–60.0) for the entire cohort. By antiviral regimen, the median follow‐up was significantly shorter in the TAF group (27.2 months) compared to the ETV (60.0 months) and TDF (60.0 months) groups (p < 0.001).

Before weighting, baseline characteristics differed across treatment groups (Table 1). The proportion of male patients was higher in the TDF group (79.9%) than in the ETV (73.4%) and TAF (67.7%) groups (p = 0.029). BMI was lower in the TDF group (23.62 ± 2.89 kg/m2) than in the ETV (24.06 ± 3.02) and TAF (24.44 ± 3.04) groups (p = 0.034). Mean largest tumour size was greatest in the TDF group (3.11 ± 2.20 cm), followed by ETV (2.88 ± 1.47) and TAF (2.41 ± 1.43) (p = 0.010). In addition, several covariates showed meaningful imbalance based on ASMD, including serum albumin, HBV DNA level, ALT, and the largest tumour size (maximum ASMD up to 0.401). After IPTW, covariate balance improved substantially, with the maximum ASMD decreasing from 0.401 to 0.194 and all post‐weighting maximum ASMD values < 0.2 (Table 1 and Figure S1).

Survival Outcomes in the Unweighted Cohort

In the unweighted cohort, Kaplan–Meier curves showed no significant differences in OS across the ETV, TDF, and TAF groups (global log‐rank p = 0.124; Figure 2A). The 5‐year OS rates for ETV, TDF, and TAF were 91.4% (95% confidence interval [CI], 88.8–94.1), 94.7% (95% CI, 92.2–97.3), and 98.1% (95% CI, 94.5–100.0), respectively. Holm‐adjusted pairwise comparisons were not significant (ETV vs. TDF, p = 0.239; ETV vs. TAF, p = 0.432; TDF vs. TAF, p = 0.549). RFS also did not differ significantly among the ETV, TDF, and TAF groups (global log‐rank p = 0.580; Figure 2B). The 5‐year RFS rates for ETV, TDF, and TAF were 58.3% (95% CI, 53.8–63.1), 60.7% (95% CI, 55.6–66.4), and 68.3% (95% CI, 53.5–87.2), respectively. Holm‐adjusted pairwise comparisons were likewise non‐significant (all p = 1.000).

Kaplan–Meier curves for overall survival (A) and recurrence‐free survival (B) before inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) according to antiviral regimen (ETV, TDF, and TAF).

Survival Outcomes in the IPTW‐Adjusted Cohort

In the IPTW‐weighted analysis, the 5‐year OS rates for ETV, TDF, and TAF were 90.7% (95% CI, 87.4–94.0), 95.2% (95% CI, 92.8–97.6), and 97.1% (95% CI, 91.7–100.0), respectively (Global p = 0.079; Figure 3A). Holm‐adjusted pairwise comparisons revealed no significant differences between the groups (ETV vs. TDF, p = 0.088; ETV vs. TAF, p = 0.771; TDF vs. TAF, p = 0.869). Similarly, the 5‐year IPTW‐adjusted RFS were comparable across ETV, TDF, and TAF: 57.4% (95% CI, 52.7–62.5), 61.0% (95% CI, 55.6–66.8), and 69.4% (95% CI, 55.8–86.1), respectively (Global p = 0.533; Figure 3B). Holm‐adjusted pairwise comparisons confirmed no significant differences between any groups (all p = 1.000).

Prognostic Factors Affecting Overall Survival

Table 2 summarises the univariable and multivariable Cox regression analyses for OS in the entire cohort. In univariable analysis, higher aminotransferase levels and tumour burden were associated with an increased risk of death: AST (per 10 IU/L) and ALT (per 10 IU/L) were significantly associated with mortality (HR 1.05 [95% CI, 1.03–1.08], p < 0.001 and HR 1.04 [95% CI, 1.02–1.07], p = 0.002, respectively). AFP > 20 ng/mL (HR 2.02 [95% CI, 1.18–3.45], p = 0.010), larger tumour size (per 1 cm; HR 1.17 [95% CI, 1.08–1.27], p < 0.001), and infiltrative type (HR 6.31 [95% CI, 2.85–13.97], p < 0.001) were also associated with worse overall survival.

In the multivariable model including antiviral regimen, sex, ALT (per 10 IU/L), AFP > 20 ng/mL, largest tumour size, and infiltrative type, TDF was independently associated with a lower risk of death compared with ETV (adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 0.48 [95% CI, 0.26–0.89], p = 0.021), whereas TAF was not (aHR 0.34 [95% CI, 0.04–2.55], p = 0.292). Higher ALT (per 10 IU/L; aHR 1.05 [95% CI, 1.02–1.08], p < 0.001), AFP > 20 ng/mL (aHR 1.96 [95% CI, 1.14–3.38], p = 0.015), larger tumour size (per 1 cm; aHR 1.17 [95% CI, 1.06–1.29], p = 0.001), and infiltrative type (aHR 5.55 [95% CI, 2.48–12.42], p < 0.001) remained independently associated with mortality. Proportional hazards assumptions were not violated in the primary multivariable model (ALT included) or in sensitivity models replacing ALT with AST and including both transaminases (global Schoenfeld tests p = 0.702, 0.688, and 0.783, respectively; antiviral regimen p = 0.837, 0.831, and 0.834) (Table S1). Also, microvascular invasion (MVI) positivity was confirmed as a strong independent risk factor for overall mortality (aHR: 2.35 [95% CI: 1.30–4.24], p = 0.005) (Table S2).

Prognostic Factors Affecting Recurrence‐Free Survival

In the entire cohort, prognostic factors for RFS were evaluated using Cox proportional hazards regression (Table 3). In multivariable analysis, antiviral regimen was not significantly associated with RFS: Compared with ETV, TDF and TAF showed no significant differences in hazard of recurrence or death (TDF vs. ETV: aHR 0.86 [95% CI 0.69–1.09], p = 0.209; TAF vs. ETV: aHR 0.86 [95% CI 0.51–1.48], p = 0.595). Male sex (aHR 1.56 [95% CI 1.18–2.08], p = 0.002) and HBeAg positivity (aHR 1.46 [95% CI 1.15–1.85], p = 0.002) were independently associated with a higher hazard of recurrence or death. Tumour‐related factors were also significant: AFP > 20 ng/mL (aHR 1.31 [95% CI 1.04–1.64], p = 0.021) and larger tumour size (per 1 cm increase; aHR 1.15 [95% CI 1.09–1.20], p < 0.001) were associated with worse RFS. In contrast, higher serum albumin was protective (per 1 g/dL increase; aHR 0.76 [95% CI 0.59–0.99], p = 0.042). Age ≥ 65 years and liver cirrhosis were not statistically significant in the multivariable model.

Because Schoenfeld residual tests suggested non‐proportional hazards for age, AFP, and tumour size, we performed a sensitivity analysis allowing time‐varying effects for these covariates (Table S3). In this model, the overall conclusions regarding antiviral regimen remained unchanged (TDF vs. ETV: aHR 0.86 [95% CI 0.68–1.08], p = 0.198; TAF vs. ETV: aHR 0.87 [95% CI 0.51–1.48], p = 0.604), supporting the robustness of the main findings. The time‐varying analysis indicated that the effects of age ≥ 65 years, AFP > 20 ng/mL, and tumour size on RFS were not constant over follow‐up, consistent with the observed non‐proportionality, whereas the associations for male sex, HBeAg positivity, and albumin remained directionally consistent. Furthermore, MVI positivity was an independent risk factor for HCC recurrence (aHR 1.60 [95% CI: 1.27–2.02], p < 0.001) (Table S4).

Subgroup Analysis by Liver Cirrhosis

A subgroup analysis was performed stratified by the presence of baseline liver cirrhosis. Among patients with liver cirrhosis (n = 673; ETV 360, TDF 271, TAF 42), the hazard of death was lower with TDF than with ETV but did not reach significance (HR 0.53 [95% CI 0.27–1.06], p = 0.075). Among patients without liver cirrhosis (n = 229; ETV 114, TDF 92, TAF 23), overall survival did not differ across the antiviral regimens (HR 0.81 [95% CI 0.27–2.47], p = 0.710). The regimen‐by‐cirrhosis interaction for overall survival was not significant (p = 0.525) (Figure S2 and Table S5).

Sensitivity and Competing Risk Analyses

Given the long‐term follow‐up of our cohort, we conducted a sensitivity analysis to evaluate the incidence of HCC recurrence by treating non‐HCC death prior to recurrence as a competing event. During the study period, a total of 322 patients experienced HCC recurrence, while 7 patients (ETV 4, TDF 3, TAF 0) died from other causes without prior recurrence. Using the Aalen–Johansen estimator, the 5‐year cumulative incidence of recurrence was 40.9% (ETV), 38.3% (TDF), and 31.7% (TAF). In the cause‐specific model, the risk of recurrence did not differ across regimens (TDF vs. ETV HR 0.95 [95% CI 0.76–1.20], p = 0.686; TAF vs. ETV HR 0.79 [95% CI 0.47–1.34], p = 0.387), with concordant Fine‐Grey subdistribution estimates (TDF vs. ETV sHR 0.96 [95% CI 0.76–1.20], p = 0.701) (Table S6 and Figure S3).

Post‐Recurrence Outcomes by Antiviral Regimen

Among the 54 deaths, 47 occurred after recurrence and 7 occurred without recurrence; the latter were all non‐HCC deaths and were similar across groups (ETV 4, TDF 3, TAF 0) (Table S7). Deaths after recurrence accounted for the between‐group difference (ETV 33, TDF 13, TAF 1), and the distribution of deaths between the after‐recurrence and without‐recurrence categories did not differ across regimens (p = 0.703). Among the 322 patients who developed recurrence, death after recurrence occurred in 18.3% of the ETV, 10.2% of the TDF, and 6.7% of the TAF group, without a significant difference (p = 0.095). In a landmark analysis from the date of recurrence, 3‐year post‐recurrence survival was 79.7%, 84.9%, and 91.7%, respectively (global log‐rank p = 0.217); compared with ETV, the hazard of death after recurrence was lower with TDF but did not reach significance (HR 0.58 [95% CI 0.31–1.11], p = 0.099) (Figure S4B and Table S7). The overall survival benefit of TDF was unchanged after adjustment for year of resection (aHR 0.48 [95% CI 0.25–0.94], p = 0.032), and year of resection was not associated with survival (per year, HR 0.99 [95% CI 0.92–1.07], p = 0.790). Within the 60‐month window, year of recurrence was not significantly associated with post‐recurrence survival (per year, HR 0.94 [95% CI 0.87–1.01], p = 0.087), and the TDF estimate was similar after this adjustment (aHR 0.69 [95% CI 0.35–1.37], p = 0.292) (Table S8).

Longitudinal Virological Response and Fibrosis Evaluation

Undetectable HBV‐DNA rates (< 20 IU/mL) were assessed to evaluate the speed of viral suppression. At 1 year postoperatively, the TDF (94.2%) and TAF (94.9%) groups showed higher rates of undetectable HBV‐DNA compared with the ETV group (89.5%), with marginal significance (p = 0.053). During long‐term follow‐up at 3 and 5 years, the undetectable rates were comparable across groups, remaining above 95% in the ETV and TDF groups (p = 0.365 and p = 0.400, respectively) (Table S9). Quantitative HBsAg levels did not differ across the three groups at 1, 3, or 5 years (p = 0.813, p = 0.294, and p = 0.959, respectively) (Table S9). Regarding fibrosis progression, the TDF group had the smallest median increase in the Fibrosis‐4 (FIB‐4) index (+0.35) from baseline to first recurrence, compared with the ETV (+0.42) and TAF (+0.44) groups (p = 0.736) (Table S10).

Discussion

This retrospective cohort study evaluated the association between ETV, TDF, and TAF and long‐term outcomes after curative liver resection for HBV‐related HCC. Using a large real‐world cohort with careful outcome ascertainment and propensity score‐based weighting, we found that the antiviral regimen was not associated with RFS, whereas TDF was independently associated with improved OS compared with ETV in multivariable analysis. These findings suggest that, in the post‐resection setting, recurrence may be driven predominantly by tumour‐ and host‐related prognostic factors, while survival differences emerged after recurrence rather than from its prevention.

The comparative effectiveness of TDF vs. ETV has been actively debated, particularly after large observational studies suggested a lower risk of incident HCC among TDF‐treated patients in the CHB population [28]. However, results from primary‐prevention cohorts may not be directly applicable to the post‐resection setting, where recurrence reflects both early intrahepatic metastasis and late multicentric occurrence. In this context, our primary RFS analyses showed no significant differences across ETV, TDF, and TAF, even after adjustment for key prognostic factors. This result is broadly consistent with prior studies in the post‐curative treatment setting, where the choice of NA showed limited influence on recurrence risk once HCC has occurred and been treated surgically [27, 29]. In the multivariable analysis for RFS, male sex, HBeAg positivity, AFP > 20 ng/mL, larger tumour size, and MVI were independently associated with a higher risk of recurrence or death. In contrast, higher albumin was associated with a lower risk. Overall, these results suggest that tumour burden, aggressiveness, and hepatic reserve are key determinants of post‐resection recurrence risk.

We observed a discordance between comparable RFS and a significant OS benefit with TDF (aHR 0.48 [95% CI 0.26–0.89], p = 0.021) (Table 2). The discordance arose after recurrence rather than from recurrence itself; RFS did not differ across regimens in the multivariable analysis (p = 0.209) (Table 3), and the cumulative incidence of recurrence was similar after accounting for competing non‐HCC death (Figure S3 and Table S6). This suggests that the OS benefit did not derive from recurrence prevention; a comparable dissociation was reported by Wang et al. [25]. Among the 54 deaths, 7 occurred without recurrence (ETV 4, TDF 3, TAF 0) and were all non‐HCC deaths, whereas 47 occurred after recurrence (ETV 33, TDF 13, TAF 1) (Table S7). Among patients who recurred, there was a non‐significant trend toward more frequent death in the ETV group (18.3%) than the TDF group (10.2%, p = 0.095).

The post‐recurrence advantage for TDF was consistent across the crude and time‐to‐event analyses but did not reach significance (HR 0.58 [95% CI 0.31–1.11], p = 0.099) (Table S7). To examine the mechanisms we had proposed, we compared post‐recurrence management and hepatic reserve between groups. The receipt of curative‐intent treatment, ALBI grade at recurrence, FIB‐4 trajectory, and use of systemic therapy were similar (Table S10), so measured treatment intensity and hepatic reserve did not account for the difference. The benefit was numerically larger in patients with cirrhosis (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.27–1.06) than in those without (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.27–2.47), but the regimen‐by‐cirrhosis interaction was not significant (p = 0.525); the data therefore do not support a cirrhosis‐specific effect. Although the TDF group was more contemporary, the benefit persisted after adjustment for year of resection, which was not itself prognostic (p = 0.790) (Table S8). Year of recurrence was not significantly associated with post‐recurrence survival within the 60‐month window (p = 0.087), although a residual contribution from evolving post‐recurrence care cannot be fully excluded. These post‐recurrence findings, though consistent across analyses, are based on a small number of events and require confirmation in larger cohorts.

In our study, TAF was not associated with improved RFS or OS compared with ETV in adjusted models. The survival curves for the TAF group exhibited a favourable trend, with higher survival rates compared with ETV. However, the aHR for TAF was 0.34 (95% CI 0.04–2.55), with a wide confidence interval. This imprecision is primarily attributable to the limited number of mortality events, driven by the smaller sample size and significantly shorter follow‐up duration. Therefore, further large‐scale studies with longer follow‐up are warranted to confirm these findings. Nevertheless, considering the increasing age of HCC patients and their susceptibility to comorbidities, the comparable oncologic efficacy of TAF, combined with its well‐characterised renal and bone safety profile over long‐term follow‐up, supports TAF as an attractive long‐term adjuvant option, particularly in patients vulnerable to renal or skeletal adverse effects [21]. Continued follow‐up is essential to validate whether the theoretical advantages of TAF will translate into a measurable survival benefit.

The primary strength of this study is the evaluation of TAF in the post‐resection setting, addressing the scarcity of long‐term comparative data for this newest antiviral agent. We performed a simultaneous comparison of ETV, TDF, and TAF using multinomial IPTW. This approach minimised sample loss and preserved the study cohort, which was crucial for optimising statistical efficiency in the relatively small TAF group. In addition, our detailed chart review allowed for the precise adjustment of tumour‐specific factors, such as infiltrative type, which are often unavailable in administrative claims data [28]. Despite these strengths, our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design entails a potential for selection bias and residual confounding. To mitigate this, we utilised MICE to handle missing data and applied IPTW to balance baseline covariates and reduce residual confounding. Second, the sample size of the TAF group was relatively small compared to the ETV or TDF groups. However, this reflects the relatively recent introduction of TAF in clinical practice. Consequently, our results provide real‐world evidence on the long‐term efficacy of TAF until larger cohorts mature. Third, as a single‐centre retrospective study, the findings may not be fully generalisable to other institutions or populations with different management protocols. However, this setting allowed for a consistent surgical approach and uniform surveillance strategies, minimising variability in outcome ascertainment. Further large‐scale, multicentre prospective studies are needed to validate our findings.

In conclusion, the choice of antiviral regimen was not associated with RFS after curative resection for HBV‐related HCC, whereas TDF was independently associated with improved OS compared with ETV. Further large‐scale studies are warranted to validate the long‐term prognostic impact of TAF.

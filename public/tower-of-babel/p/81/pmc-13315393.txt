Modeling and improving blood flow dynamics for industrial applications using a variable property second grade nanofluid with convective boundary effects
Authors: Essam Awwad, K. M. Khalil, A. Soleiman, W. Abbas, Ahmed M. Megahed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study presents a numerical examination of a steady, two-dimensional second-grade nanofluid flowing due to a stretching sheet with convective boundary conditions. The model incorporates temperature-dependent thermophysical properties, featuring an exponential decay of viscosity and density alongside a linear variation in thermal conductivity. To ensure a physically consistent solution for the resulting fourth-order momentum equation, an augmented boundary condition and a judiciously selected sign for the normal stress modulus are implemented. The governing nonlinear partial differential equations are transformed into a coupled system of ordinary differential equations via similarity variables and solved computationally using the Runge Kutta shooting technique. Graphical and tabular results elucidate the impact of pivotal dimensionless parameters on the flow, thermal, and concentration fields, as well as on engineering quantities of interest. A key finding affirms the unidirectional transfer of heat from the sheet to the nanofluid, offering new insights into the behavior of second-grade nanofluids with variable properties. A principal finding of this investigation is that increasing viscosity, density variation, suction, and thermophoretic transport collectively modify the boundary-layer behavior by suppressing the near-wall fluid motion, enhancing thermal and concentration distributions, and significantly influencing the skin-friction characteristics. The outcomes of this study are validated through comparison with available peer-reviewed results, showing good agreement that supports the reliability of the numerical model and the consistency of the obtained solutions.

Introduction

The convective boundary condition (CBC) represents a paradigm shift from idealized thermal assumptions, capturing the dynamic energy exchange between fluids and their environment. This approach moves beyond traditional fixed-temperature or constant-flux models to accurately simulate real-world applications from heat exchangers and polymer processing to electronics cooling and advanced nanofluid systems. As thermal engineering evolves toward more sophisticated designs, the CBC has emerged as a critical tool, driving extensive investigation into its impact across diverse fluid systems and geometric configurations. For instance, Madhukesh et al. [1] investigated hybrid nanofluid flow with thermophoretic and convective effects, and Patil et al. [2] extended the analysis to unsteady MHD Powell Eyring fluids with radiation and chemical reaction. Contemporary research by Wang et al. [3] and Shahzad et al. [4] has broken new ground in nanofluid and thin-film flows through the integration of slip and convective dynamics. Simultaneously, Abbas et al. [5] and Rasool et al. [6] pushed boundaries in non-Newtonian systems with advanced radiative-convective coupling. Together, these pioneering works illuminate convective boundary conditions as transformative elements-not merely as constraints but as active design parameters that are reshaping how we model, optimize, and innovate thermal systems for next-generation engineering applications.

The pursuit of fluid realism where viscosity, conductivity, and density evolve with temperature and concentration marks a pivotal shift from idealized models toward authentic system behavior. These dynamic properties actively reshape momentum, thermal, and mass transport: reconfiguring boundary layers, modulating transfer rates, and ultimately governing performance across next-generation applications. From precision polymer processing and high-flux electronics cooling to nanofluid-enhanced heat exchangers and smart lubricant flows, variable properties emerge not as complications, but as powerful design dimensions for optimizing real-world thermal and fluid systems. Recognizing this importance has driven a surge of research interest in variable-property flows. Notable contributions include, for example, Megahed [7] presented an early numerical investigation highlighting how variable viscosity and internal heat generation influence boundary-layer characteristics under radiative and porous conditions. Later, Megahed [8] extended this analysis to Carreau fluids, incorporating nonlinear stretching and variable thermal conductivity, emphasizing the coupling between rheology and heat transfer. Hussain et al. [9] demonstrated how viscosity variations near a stagnation point govern Casson fluid dynamics under magnetic fields. Mondal and Pal [10] linked microbial movement to enhanced transport in variable-viscosity nanofluids. Ullah and Aldhabani [11] revealed how thermophoresis and density changes optimize lubrication in porous media, while Gamaoun et al. [12] established density variation as a critical heat transfer modulator in radiative magnetic flows. Together, these studies underscore variable properties as active levers in controlling fluid behavior and thermal performance. Akbar et al. [13] elevated modeling complexity through 3D magnetohydrodynamic nanoflows with thermal lamination, demonstrating how interacting variable properties govern system performance. Building on this foundation, Amer et al. [14] unveiled enhanced thermal efficiency in Maxwell nanofluids through variable density and viscous dissipation a breakthrough with direct implications for maritime thermal management. Very recently, Shamshuddin and co-authors [15] contributed to temperature-dependent fluid transport studies by analyzing Maxwell nanofluid flow with variable thermal conductivity over a convective stretching sheet, while also integrating artificial neural network modeling to enhance prediction accuracy and thermal transport analysis. Together, these works trace an evolutionary path in fluid modeling, where sophisticated property representation enables accurate prediction of coupled thermal-hydrodynamic behavior across advanced engineering applications.

Nanofluids have garnered considerable interest owing to their superior thermal properties and diverse engineering applications in cooling technologies, energy systems, biomedical devices, and industrial heat transfer processes [16]. In a recent study, The work of Panda and co-authors [17] advanced nanofluid research by examining the combined influence of ferromagnetic interactions, Darcy-Forchheimer porous effects, and Riga surface actuation on Casson nanofluid transport and thermal behavior. Also, Ashraf et al. [18] employed response surface-based sensitivity optimization to examine heat transfer enhancement in magnetohydrodynamic bioconvective Casson nanofluid flow over a rotating disk. Rehman et al. [19] further explored Williamson nanofluids in the presence of chemical reactions using response surface methodology (RSM) coupled with multi-objective optimization to elevate thermal transport performance. Moreover, Ashraf et al. [20] combined computational simulations with an artificial neural network (ANN) approach to analyze heat transfer and bioconvection in Sutterby nanofluid flow across a stretching surface, underscoring the expanding role of intelligent predictive methodologies in nanofluid research. Second-grade nanofluids represent a advanced class of non-Newtonian fluids that exhibit not only viscosity but also elasticity and memory effects. These fluids "remember" past deformations, enabling them to model complex materials from polymer suspensions and advanced lubricants to biological systems like blood and synovial fluid [21]. With the integration of nanoparticles, their thermal conductivity and heat transfer capabilities are significantly amplified. This unique combination of rheological sophistication and thermal performance makes them ideal for next-generation cooling systems, polymer processing, biomedical devices, and energy technologies where precision thermal management and tailored fluid behavior are critical. The unique capabilities of second-grade fluids have sparked a dynamic research evolution, marked by key milestones, where Khan et al. [22] unveiled stratification effects in thermally layered flows, Basha et al. [23] pioneered numerical frameworks for stretching surfaces. Ahmad and co-authors [24] contributed to understanding the interaction between magnetic forces and mixed convection transport near lubricated vertical surfaces. Their study clarified how wall lubrication and magnetohydrodynamic effects jointly modify the velocity and thermal boundary layers, providing useful insight for coating and thermal control technologies. This trajectory advanced with Punith Gowda et al. [25] revealing cross-diffusion dynamics in curved geometries. Later, Jamshed and collaborators [26] extended second-grade fluid investigations toward nanofluid transport through permeable media under electromagnetic radiation and convective slip conditions. Their work highlighted the combined influence of thermal radiation, wall slip, and porous structures on heat transfer enhancement, offering a broader framework for advanced cooling and energy-related applications involving non-Newtonian nanofluids. Most recently, Ullah et al. [27] quantifying dissipation in variable-property systems. Together, these studies form a cohesive research lineage that continues to expand the frontiers of complex fluid understanding.

This research emerges from a critical need to unify several interconnected physical phenomena including thermal and concentration convective boundary conditions temperature dependent variable properties and second grade nanofluid memory effects within a single analytical framework and its novelty lies in the simultaneous integration of these coupled mechanisms to provide a more realistic representation of nonlinear transport phenomena in boundary layer flows while the application of the problem is relevant to advanced engineering systems such as polymer processing thermal management technologies and blood flow modeling in hemodynamic studies.

Conclusions

This research presents a numerical analysis of a steady, two-dimensional flow of a second-grade nanofluid over a stretching surface, incorporating convective boundary conditions and temperature-dependent thermophysical properties. The governing equations are transformed into a system of coupled ordinary differential equations and solved using the Runge–Kutta shooting method. The model’s validity is established through favorable comparisons with established results, providing enhanced insight into the behavior of advanced nanofluids with variable properties. Based on the comprehensive analysis, the following outcomes can be ascertained: The variable-density parameter markedly alters the transport behavior, slowing the fluid near the wall because of stronger viscous effects, accelerating it farther away, and amplifying both thermal and concentration layers close to the stretching surface.The thermophoresis parameter intensifies both thermal and concentration fields, particularly the latter, by migrating particles toward cooler zones and concentrating them near the wall.The skin friction coefficient exhibits a direct relationship with density, suction, and thermal Biot parameters, while displaying an inverse correlation with increasing viscosity.An increase in suction significantly reduces the velocity profile, indicating a clear suppression of fluid motion near the boundary surface.The viscosity parameter lowers fluid velocity through increased resistance while raising temperature and concentration, as reduced convection favors heat and mass buildup near the wall.Driven by a significant density variation, the flow structure evolves from smooth and uniform to one characterized by densely packed, curved streamlines near the wall, underscoring the strengthened viscous influence and altered momentum balance.The present investigation is limited to a steady, laminar, and two-dimensional flow configuration under similarity transformations. Moreover, several physical effects such as thermal radiation, magnetic field, chemical reaction, and unsteady transport phenomena were not incorporated and may be considered in future extensions of the current model.Future studies may extend the present second-grade nanofluid model to unsteady three-dimensional flow configurations and apply Response Surface Methodology (RSM) to analyze parameter interactions and optimize heat and mass transfer characteristics under variable density, variable viscosity, and convective boundary conditions.

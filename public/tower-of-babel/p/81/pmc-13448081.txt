An adaptive feature extraction lightweight network for enhanced landslide detection
Authors: Ruiyunfei Pan, Haowei Pan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Rapid detection of landslides is essential for timely disaster warning and minimizing potential losses. However, identifying landslides in natural environments remains challenging due to their irregular morphologies, scale variability, and the high computational demands of deep neural networks. To address these challenges, this study proposes the Adaptive Feature Extraction Lightweight Network for Landslide Detection (AFL-NET). The backbone network incorporates the Ghostv2C2f module, which integrates ghost feature generation and dynamic spatial attention to reduce redundant computation and parameter count while preserving spatial detail. For feature fusion, the SimAM-Augmented Bi-directional Feature Pyramid Network (SBIFPN) is introduced to enhance multi-scale representation via adaptive weight normalization and energy-guided attention, improving the model’s response to landslides of varying shapes and scales. At the detection stage, the Spatial Modeling and Context-aware C2f (SMC2f) module combines lightweight contextual encoding with multi-head self-attention to suppress background interference and strengthen structural reasoning in complex terrain. Experimental results show that AFL-NET achieves a 3.9% improvement in mAP over the baseline YOLOv11n, reaching 90.1%, while reducing parameter by 15.3%. These improvements make AFL-NET highly suitable for real-time, on-device deployment in disaster monitoring applications, offering reliable technical support for early warning and risk mitigation.

Introduction

Landslide detection is crucial for preventing disasters and enhancing post-disaster management1,2. As a major natural hazard causing significant casualties globally3, landslides are geological events triggered by the gravitational movement of soil or rock along slopes4. These events not only severely damage the ecological environment5 but also lead to substantial property losses and significant human casualties6. Landslides occur in various forms, such as debris flows and rock avalanches, and pose severe threats to infrastructure, particularly roads and railways7,8. Intense rainfall and earthquakes are primary triggers of landslides19. Moreover, factors like global warming10, population growth, excessive mineral exploitation, and environmental degradation have significantly increased both the frequency and severity of landslides11,12. However, current landslide detection technologies often face challenges, including heavy workloads, long processing times, and high costs13. Thus, developing efficient and cost-effective methods for landslide detection has become an urgent priority to mitigate the impacts of these disasters14.

Traditional landslide detection methods can be divided into two categories. The first focuses on predicting potential landslides, targeting mountainous areas prone to such events, with InSAR technology as a primary approach15. The second involves detecting existing landslides, often integrating terrain and geomorphological data16. While ground-based measurement methods provide reliable accuracy, they are labor-intensive and costly17. Consequently, landslide monitoring and prediction have increasingly shifted toward computer-based techniques18.

Specifically, early detection methods were largely dominated by visual interpretation and pixel-based feature thresholding. Visual interpretation involves delineating landslide boundaries by analyzing image texture differences. However, this method is subjective, inefficient, labor-intensive, and costly19–22, making it unsuitable for large-scale remote sensing applications23,24. Pixel-based feature thresholding uses spectral and textural information to classify pixels as landslide-related based on one or more thresholds13. For example, Martha et al.25 combined spectral, shape, and contextual information to detect landslides. Despite its effectiveness, this approach is constrained by high costs and limited accuracy. In contrast, computer vision-based methods offer higher efficiency for landslide detection.

Early computer vision methods relied heavily on traditional machine learning, requiring handcrafted feature extraction for object detection. For example, Stumpf26 and Chen27 applied random forest algorithms to landslide detection, while Mezaal et al. [28] combined correlation-based feature selection (CFS), random forest (RF), and ant colony optimization (ACO) to optimize segmentation parameters. Similarly, Van Den Eeckhaut et al.29 used multi-scale supervised support vector machines (SVMs) to detect landslide features. Although these methods are straightforward and easy to implement, they suffer from significant limitations in efficiency and scalability, making them inadequate for modern landslide detection needs. In contrast, deep learning methods based on remote sensing technology have proven more effective due to their automation and efficiency30.

Unlike traditional machine learning, deep learning does not require manual feature construction or selection31. It excels in handling large-scale landslide datasets and extensive regional recognition tasks32–34. Deep learning models for landslide detection are typically classified into two categories: two-stage models and single-stage models. Two-stage models detect objects by first generating candidate regions. For instance, Cai et al.35 used Faster R-CNN combined with time-series InSAR measurement technology to identify active landslides over large areas. Wang et al.36 applied partially supervised learning with an improved Mask R-CNN model for landslide detection. Yu et al.37 integrated convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with an optimized region-growing algorithm (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$RSG_R$$\end{document}), which effectively extracted critical information and significantly improved detection accuracy. Although CNNs perform well in image classification, they often struggle to produce precise bounding boxes. To address this, Yun et al.38 introduced an enhanced Mask R-CNN model with a bottom-up channel processing mechanism and the GA-Remote Sens. RPN module, achieving higher detection accuracy. Similarly, Jin et al.39 proposed the ERCA algorithm based on the Faster R-CNN model, incorporating an adaptive soft threshold method to reduce background noise interference in complex environments. This approach improved the robustness of landslide detection. However, two-stage models often face challenges such as large model sizes and slow detection speeds. Single-stage models, by contrast, offer faster detection and are easier to deploy, making them more suitable for landslide detection tasks.

Single-stage methods, as end-to-end object detection frameworks that eliminate the need for candidate box generation40,41,have been widely applied in landslide detection tasks. Fu et al.42 proposed an improved YOLOv3 model that combines stacked phase-difference amplitude data with manually labeled landslide images for automated detection. However, the model suffers from structural complexity, which limits its practicality. Cheng et al.43 developed the YOLO-SA model based on YOLOv4, introducing group convolution (GConv) and ghost bottleneck (G-Bneck) residual modules as replacements for standard convolution components and residual modules. This significantly reduced model parameters but left room for improvement in detection accuracy. Zhang et al.44 enhanced YOLOv5s by incorporating a context enhancement module, built with dilated convolution, into the decoupled head regression branch and replacing the original coupled head. This approach improved multi-scale landslide detection performance but did not adequately address the need for lightweight designs.

Yu et al.45 proposed a YOLOX-based landslide detection model that strengthens the fusion of shallow and deep semantic features by introducing a new Pafpn neck structure. While effective in some contexts, the model’s performance in complex backgrounds remains limited. Liu et al.8 introduced the SE-YOLOv7 model, which integrates the SE attention mechanism and a zoom loss function to enhance detection accuracy in challenging environments. However, this approach does not fully resolve issues related to target size variation. Mao et al.46 improved YOLOv8 by adding convolutional block attention layers to the backbone network and replacing the C2F module with GHMatNet2. This modification reduced information loss in long-range feature transmission and improved detection for varying target sizes but increased computational costs. Qiu et al.47 proposed a lightweight YOLOv10-based model for unmanned aerial scenarios. By incorporating the DR-PAN neck structure, the model effectively captures weak features of small targets with low computational overhead. However, its sensitivity to anchor box aspect ratios requires further optimization.

Recent advances in landslide detection have increasingly focused on improving feature representation through deep learning and transformer-based architectures. For instance, Wang et al.49 proposed an unsupervised deep clustering framework that leverages implicit knowledge to enhance landslide recognition from high-resolution imagery, reducing the reliance on labeled data. Gao et al.50 introduced a Swin Transformer-based multiscale attention model to capture long-range dependencies and multi-scale contextual information, thereby improving landslide extraction in large-scale regions. In addition, Wang et al.51 developed a gated dual-stream convolutional network (GDSNet) to jointly model complementary feature streams, enhancing robustness under complex terrain conditions. Wu et al.52 further combined CNN and transformer architectures with transfer learning, integrating spectral and topographic features to improve generalization performance.

Despite these advances, most existing methods primarily emphasize representation learning through complex architectures or multimodal fusion, while paying limited attention to computational efficiency and real-time applicability. In particular, transformer-based and hybrid models often introduce substantial computational overhead, which restricts their deployment in resource-constrained or time-sensitive scenarios. This limitation highlights the need for lightweight yet effective frameworks that can better balance detection accuracy and efficiency.

Despite notable advances in landslide detection using deep learning, two major limitations continue to restrict real-world deployment. First, the highly variable morphologies, scale diversity, and low-contrast textures of landslide regions pose challenges for conventional models in capturing critical spatial features. Second, many high-accuracy detectors suffer from excessive computational demands and large model sizes, limiting their applicability in real-time or resource-constrained environments such as UAV-based remote sensing. To address these issues, this study proposes AFL-NET (Adaptive Feature Extraction Lightweight Network)—a compact yet effective detection framework designed to balance accuracy and efficiency in complex terrains.

The first component of AFL-NET is the Ghostv2C2f module, which builds upon the GhostNet concept by introducing low-cost feature generation and integrating a Dynamic Fully Connected (DFC) attention mechanism. This structure reduces channel redundancy and enhances spatial sensitivity, resulting in improved feature expressiveness with a significantly reduced parameter count.

Furthermore, to handle scale variation and hierarchical feature fusion, the network designs the SimAM-Augmented Bi-directional Feature Pyramid Network (SBIFPN). This module employs adaptive weight normalization and energy-based attention filtering to guide feature aggregation, enabling more focused responses to salient regions while suppressing background interference across multiple scales.

Finally, the Spatial Modeling and Context-aware C2f (SMC2f) module is introduced to further improve the model’s ability to distinguish landslides from visually similar backgrounds. By integrating lightweight contextual modeling with Multi-Head Self-Attention, SMC2f enhances spatial discrimination and improves robustness in complex or occluded scenes.

Materials and methods

The core implementation of the proposed AFL-NET has been publicly released for research purposes. The core code can be accessed at: [https://pryf.github.io/AFL-NET/]. Adaptive feature extraction lightweight network for landslide detection

To address the challenges of detecting irregular and visually complex landslide features in remote sensing imagery, this study introduces a lightweight and adaptive detection framework called the Adaptive Feature Extraction Lightweight Network (AFL-NET). As shown in Fig. 1, the overall architecture consists of four main components: an input module, a backbone network for feature extraction based on Ghostv2C2f, a multi-scale feature fusion network using the SimAM-Augmented Bi-directional Feature Pyramid Network (SBIFPN), and a detection head built upon the Spatial Modeling and Context-aware C2f mechanism (SMC2f).Fig. 1Architecture of the Adaptive Feature Extraction Lightweight Network for Landslide Detection (AFL-NET).

Architecture of the Adaptive Feature Extraction Lightweight Network for Landslide Detection (AFL-NET).

The Ghostv2C2f backbone enhances local spatial representation while significantly reducing computational overhead by integrating efficient ghost feature generation and dynamic attention mechanisms. The SBIFPN module improves multi-scale feature aggregation through adaptive weight normalization and energy-guided channel selection, enabling more effective detection of landslides with varying shapes and sizes. Finally, the SMC2f detection head combines lightweight contextual encoding with multi-head self-attention to suppress background interference and highlight landslide-relevant regions. Together, these components form a unified architecture that achieves high detection accuracy while maintaining low model complexity, making AFL-NET well-suited for real-time and resource-constrained scenarios.

Ghostv2C2f network

Landslide detection requires rapid and accurate identification of spatially dispersed and morphologically complex terrain features. However, conventional deep learning models often involve excessive parameters and computational overhead, limiting their applicability in real-time or resource-constrained monitoring scenarios. Specifically, the backbone network of YOLOv11n introduces high computational cost during feature fusion, increasing the burden on both training and inference stages. Additionally, the large model size leads to increased storage and energy consumption, which is unfavorable for on-device deployment. To mitigate these limitations, GhostNetV1 was developed as a lightweight alternative to conventional CNN architectures. It introduces the Ghost Module, which generates a greater number of feature maps through inexpensive operations, effectively reducing computational complexity. Nevertheless, GhostNetV1 lacks the ability to differentiate the relative importance of extracted features due to the absence of an attention mechanism.

To address this, we propose Ghostv2C2f, an enhanced lightweight feature extraction module tailored for landslide detection tasks. This structure integrates an attention-guided mechanism to improve local spatial awareness without significantly increasing computational cost. Ghostv2C2f combines the strengths of the Ghost Module with the Dynamic Fully Connected (DFC) attention mechanism. The Ghost component maintains the structural design of GhostNetV1, focusing on low-cost feature generation.Fig. 2(a) Ghost module with DFC attention dot product effect, (b) GhostNetV2 bottleneck.

SimAM-augmented Bi-directional feature pyramid network

In real-world terrains, landslide detection remains highly challenging due to dramatic scale variations caused by environmental and topographical complexity. Although YOLOv11 leverages FPN and PAN modules for multi-scale feature fusion to improve detection accuracy, this integration introduces higher computational overhead and fails to exploit feature interactions to their full potential. To overcome these drawbacks, we present a restructured SimAM-Augmented Bi-directional Feature Pyramid Network (SBIFPN), as depicted in Fig. 3. SBIFPN enhances feature representation and transmission efficiency by incorporating adaptive weight assignment, hierarchical feature interconnection, and spatial attention modulation across multiple resolutions.Fig. 3The structure of SBIFPN.

The structure of SBIFPN.

To simplify the network structure, redundant nodes that contribute minimally to feature fusion are pruned. Meanwhile, inter-layer shortcut connections are introduced to facilitate efficient information propagation between scales. These structural refinements lead to both reduced model complexity and improved feature integration, ultimately enhancing landslide detection performance.

In response to resolution-dependent variations in feature importance, SBIFPN adopts a fast normalization-based fusion strategy. This mechanism dynamically adjusts the contribution of each feature layer by normalizing its weight relative to the sum of all layer weights. Spatial modeling and context-aware C2f mechanism

Accurate detection of landslides is often hindered by the presence of complex background elements such as dense vegetation, uneven rock formations, shadowed areas, and artificial infrastructure, which can obscure or mimic the visual characteristics of actual landslide regions. These elements often exhibit textures and shapes similar to landslide regions, leading to feature confusion and reduced detection accuracy. To improve feature discrimination under such conditions, the SMC2f module is introduced, as shown in Fig. 4. This module integrates the local context modeling capability of the C2f structure with the spatial responsiveness of Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA). C2f improves the expressiveness of features by encoding surrounding spatial cues, while MHSA facilitates attention across multiple spatial locations, enabling more effective suppression of background noise and enhancement of landslide-relevant features. The combined structure is designed to support more robust representation learning in visually cluttered environments.Fig. 4The SMC2f’s architecture.

The SMC2f’s architecture.

Incorporating MHSA enhances the network’s ability to resolve spatial ambiguity by evaluating feature correlations across independent attention heads. This mechanism is particularly effective when background elements overlap or resemble target regions, allowing the network to distinguish subtle structural differences. In addition to improving detection precision, MHSA contributes to computational efficiency, making the module suitable for large-scale remote sensing applications.

The design of MHSA is based on parallelized attention computation across multiple subspaces. As illustrated in Fig. 5, its structure consists of two main components: scaled dot-product attention and multi-head attention projection, which together expand the network’s ability to capture diverse contextual information in the presence of complex backgrounds.Fig. 5Detailed architecture of the Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) mechanism.

Experiments and analysis

Study area and dataset processing

The study area is located in the northwest of Guizhou Province, China, covering the entire Bijie City with a total area of approximately \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$26,853\text { km}^{2}$$\end{document}. Positioned within the transitional zone from the Tibetan Plateau to the eastern hills, the region features rugged terrain with densely distributed mountain slopes. Annual precipitation ranges from 849 to 1399 mm, making it one of the most landslide-prone areas in China. Frequent landslides occur annually, some causing severe casualties and property damage. Due to the high risk and inefficiency of traditional field surveys, there is an urgent need for efficient computer vision-based landslide detection algorithms to improve early warning, risk assessment, and post-disaster response.

To develop real-time landslide detection methods, we conducted experiments using the publicly available Bijie Landslide Dataset48, which provides satellite optical images, elevation data, and manually labeled landslide masks. This dataset includes 770 landslide patches from TripleSat images.To enhance the model’s generalization capability, the dataset was expanded to include 5,000 images through data augmentation techniques. These techniques included random translation, brightness adjustment, saturation modification, and noise addition, as shown in Fig. 6. The dataset was then randomly divided into training and validation sets at a ratio of 8:2 to meet experimental requirements. Environment and metrics

All experiments were conducted in a Linux environment using PyTorch 1.7.0 and Python 3.8. The hardware configuration consisted of an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with 24 GB of video memory. The training process was configured with an initial learning rate of 0.01, a total of 300 training epochs, a momentum of 0.937, and a weight decay coefficient of 0.0005. A batch size of 32 was adopted to maintain training efficiency and model stability.

To comprehensively evaluate the proposed method, standard performance indicators were employed, including precision, recall, F1, and mean Average Precision (mAP). Ablation study

In addition, the computational cost decreases from 6.3 GFLOPs to 5.5 GFLOPs, while the inference speed improves from 230.6 FPS to 260.3 FPS. These results indicate that Ghostv2C2f not only enhances feature representation but also effectively improves the model’s efficiency, making it more suitable for real-time applications. This is primarily attributed to the Ghost module’s ability to replicate redundant feature maps through linear operations, significantly lowering the computational burden. Additionally, the embedded DFC attention mechanism selectively aggregates spatial features along horizontal and vertical axes, enhancing local feature awareness with minimal computational cost.

Building on this, the integration of the SBIFPN structure yields a further increase in detection accuracy, with mAP reaching 89.4%. This enhancement stems from several design factors within SBIFPN: adaptive weight normalization dynamically rebalances the contribution of feature layers at different scales, while SimAM-based spatial attention and energy-based channel filtering improve semantic focus. By eliminating non-contributory nodes and incorporating skip connections, SBIFPN also improves information flow without adding architectural complexity.

When all three modules are combined, the network achieves its highest performance, with a mAP of 90.1% and a modest parameter count of 2.2 M. This final improvement is driven by the inclusion of the SMC2f module, which couples lightweight contextual modeling (via C2f) with spatial discrimination capabilities introduced by MHSA. The parallel attention heads in MHSA enable the model to resolve fine-grained differences in visually ambiguous areas, which is essential for landslide detection in environments with vegetation, shadows, and topographic noise.

Conclusion and discussion

This study presents AFL-NET, a lightweight and adaptive network tailored for landslide detection in complex natural terrains. By integrating the Ghostv2C2f module for efficient feature extraction, the SBIFPN module for multi-scale fusion, and the SMC2f module for spatial reasoning, AFL-NET achieves significant improvements in both accuracy and computational efficiency. Experimental results demonstrate that AFL-NET outperforms the YOLOv11n baseline by 3.9% in mAP while reducing parameters by 15.3%. These results validate the effectiveness of the proposed architecture and highlight its potential for real-time landslide monitoring in resource-constrained environments such as UAV-based or edge computing platforms.

Despite its advantages, AFL-NET still has several limitations. The model shows limited effectiveness in detecting extremely small targets, which often exhibit low saliency in high-resolution imagery and lead to reduced confidence in predictions. Additionally, when the texture of the landslide region closely resembles the surrounding background, the model is more prone to false detections or misclassifications. Another limitation lies in the reliance on static imagery; the current framework does not incorporate temporal information related to the evolution of landslides, which restricts its ability to model dynamic processes.

Future research will focus on addressing these challenges. Efforts will include the design of more refined feature enhancement modules and small-object detection strategies; the introduction of contrastive learning and multimodal data fusion to suppress background interference and reduce false positives; the exploration of Transformer-based dynamic feature extraction to better perceive and predict landslide evolution; and further compression of the model architecture to enhance deployment efficiency on UAVs and mobile platforms.

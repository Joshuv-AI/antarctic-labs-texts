Prediction of 90 day readmission in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction by interpretable machine learning
Authors: Baojia Zheng, Tao Liang, Jianping Mei, Xiuru Shi, Xiaohui Liu, Sikai Li, Yuting Wan, Yifeng Zheng, Xiaoyue Yang, Yanxia Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimsCertain critical risk factors of heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) patients were significantly different from those of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) patients, resulting in the limitations of existing predictive models in real‐world situations. This study aimed to develop a machine learning model for predicting 90 day readmission for HFpEF patients.Methods and ResultsData were extracted from electronic health records from 1 August 2020 to 1 August 2021 and follow‐up records of patients with HFpEF within 3 months after discharge. Feature extraction was performed by univariate analysis combined with the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithms. Machine learning models like eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), random forest, neural network and logistic regression were adopted to construct models. The discrimination and calibration of each model were compared, and the Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method was used to explore the interpretability of the model. The cohort included 746 patients, of whom 103 (13.8%) were readmitted within 90 days. XGBoost owned the best performance [area under the curve (AUC) = 0.896, precision‐recall area under the curve (PR‐AUC) = 0.868, sensitivity = 0.817, specificity = 0.837, balanced accuracy = 0.827]. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) statistic was 0.694 at 0.468 in the XGBoost model. SHAP identified the top 12 risk features, including activities of daily living (ADL), left atrial dimension (LAD), left ventricular end‐diastolic diameter (LVDD), shortness, nitrates, length of stay, nutritional risk, fall risk, accompanied by other symptoms, educational level, anticoagulants and edema.ConclusionsOur model could help medical agencies achieve the early identification of 90 day readmission risk in HFpEF patients and reveal risk factors that provide valuable insights for treatments.Keywords: Chinese people, heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, machine learning, patient readmission

Introduction

There were more than 13 million patients with heart failure (HF) among adults over 35 years old in China, an increase of 44% over 20 years ago. 1 Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) approximately accounted for 50% of all HF types, which was mainly responsible for a high health care burden. 2 Noticeably, patients with HFpEF had a greater long‐term risk of readmission or death than those with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). 3 Once readmitted to the hospital, the patients tended to have a 10% increased death rate. 4 Hence, it was significant to evaluate and identify risk factors for readmission in HFpEF patients and to construct a risk prediction system for clinical decision‐making and disease management.

Currently, the majority of readmission risk tools had been developed in cohorts with HFrEF or a combination of HFrEF and HFpEF, which might have limited applicability in patients with isolated HFpEF. 5 , 6 , 7 Besides, most contemporary predictive models were constructed for non‐Chinese patients and utilized readmission within 30 days as the outcome variable. However, real‐world data reflected that HF patients in China were not prone to readmission in the immediate aftermath of their discharge until their condition became intolerable. 8 In contrast to a 30 day period, the 90 day period following discharge might hold greater significance in observing readmission during the vulnerable phase of HF patients in the real‐world setting in China. 8 Though HFpEF patients experience hospitalizations and short‐term readmission for HF at rates similar to those of HFrEF patients, certain critical risk factors significantly differ from those of HFrEF patients. 9 Only Tan et al. 10 had reported on the prediction of a 90 day readmission risk for HF patients in China. However, the linear relationship was predominant, and no categorical prediction was made for patients with HF. With advances in computation and analytics, the application of machine learning techniques has the potential to improve accuracy in the prediction of 90 day readmission in HFpEF.

Accordingly, this study aimed to use machine learning methods to develop and validate an interpretable model for predicting 90 day readmission in patients with HFpEF from comprehensive electronic health records (EHRs) in order to provide a basis for clinical application.

Methods

Data extraction

The EHR data for this study came from the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐sen University. HFpEF was predefined as left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) ≥ 50% and without diagnosed non‐ischaemic cardiomyopathy or relevant structural heart diseases. 11 Patients with solid tumours, death in hospital or within 3 months after first discharge, pregnancy, congenital heart disease, senile dementia or missing data were excluded. From August 2020 to August 2021, 746 patients hospitalized for HFpEF with discharge dates were selected for the study, including 446 males and 300 females. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Fifth Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat‐sen University (Approval No. 2022‐k.240‐1). Due to the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent was waived by the ethics committee.

Feature collection

A total of 55 feature variables were collected for this study. They were the following: sociodemographic characteristics (including gender, age, married status and working status), general characteristics (including educational level, medical payment method, primary caregiver, cigarette smoking and length of stay), admission assessment [including activities of daily living (ADL), nutritional risk, venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk and fall risk], symptoms on admission (including dyspnea, thorax pain, palpitation, dizziness and other symptoms), signs on admission (including edema, oliguria, heart murmur and other signs), complications, New York Heart Association (NYHA) functional classification, medication use [including angiotensin‐converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin II receptor blockers (ACEIs/ARBs), aldosterone blockers, beta‐blockers, thiazide, loop diuretics, nitrates, Ca channel blockers, lipid‐lowering drug, oral anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs], left ventricular end‐diastolic diameter (LVDD), left atrial dimension (LAD), left ventricular posterior wall thickness (LVPWT) and haematological indicators on admission [including glucose, N‐terminal pro‐brain natriuretic peptide (NT‐proBNP), troponin, high‐density lipoprotein, low‐density lipoprotein, total cholesterol, triglyceride, uric acid, homocysteine, white blood cell count, platelet count, red blood cell count, haemoglobin, glycosylated haemoglobin, albumin, potassium, lactate dehydrogenase and creatinine]. ADL was performed by using the Barthel index on a scale of 0–100 (≥60, mild dependent; 41–60, moderate dependent; ≤40, very dependent). 12 Nutritional risk was evaluated by Nutritional Risk Screening 2002 (NRS‐2002). Patients were classified as having no or low risk when they had a total score < 3 or as having nutritional risk when they had a total score ≥ 3. 13 VTE risk was assessed by the Padua scale, and patients with a Padua score ≥ 4 were enrolled in the risk group. 14 Fall risk was assessed by the Johns Hopkins Fall Risk Assessment Tool (JHFRAT), 15 and a score of 6 or greater indicated a risk of falling.

Outcome and follow‐up

Readmission within 90 days after first admission for HFpEF was the primary outcome of the present study. The data from EHRs and the results of our follow‐up were used to measure this outcome. At the first and third months after first discharge, follow‐up visits were performed by cardiovascular doctors and clinical pharmacists involved in this study. This information was used to correctly divide the two groups (the 90 day readmission group and the non‐90 day readmission group).

Study design

A total of 746 patients were divided into two groups: the 90 day readmission group and the non‐90 day readmission group, based on whether they were readmitted within 90 days. Univariate analysis combined with the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) algorithms was utilized for feature extraction. The selected features were further put into the model construction. Four models, including the eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) algorithm, random forest (RF), neural network (NN) and logistic regression (LR) machine learning models, were built. The performance of the models was assessed using five‐fold cross‐validation. Discrimination and calibration, including area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUROC), sensitivity, specificity, area under the precision versus recall curve (AUC‐PR), positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), balanced accuracy, the Youden index and the Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) curve, were used to verify the predictive ability of the model. After the model was developed, the Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) algorithm was applied to the best model to explore the interpretability of the model for identifying the top risk factors. Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages. Continuous variables were presented as the median and interquartile range (IQR). In terms of univariate analysis, categorical variables were compared by using either the χ 2 test or Fisher's exact probability test, while continuous variables were analysed using the independent‐sample t‐test or the Mann–Whitney U test. All P‐values were two‐tailed, and P < 0.05 was considered significant. Then the significant variables selected from the univariable analysis were further filtered via the LASSO regression algorithm, and four models were developed based on their set of features. We divided the data into an initial 70% subset for model training and the more recent 30% subset for model testing. To overcome the imbalance of data in the training set, a random oversampling method was conducted by copying the positive cases five times to prevent overfitting. 16 , 17 Based on the confusion matrix, the sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, balanced accuracy, AUROC and PR‐AUC of each model were calculated. Then the KS statistic and calibration measure were utilized to appraise the efficiency of the best model. The KS value varied from 0 to 1, and the prediction performance of the model was deemed satisfactory when the KS value surpassed 0.3. The SHAP algorithm facilitated explaining how a prediction model works and mitigated some of the concerns about ‘black box’ modelling. We calculated and ranked the mean of the absolute SHAP value for the best model. A two‐patient example was created to illustrate the explanation of a patient‐specific prediction.

Statistical analyses were performed in SPSS software (Version 22.0) and R (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Version 4.2.2). LASSO regression was analysed using the ‘glmnet’ and ‘foreign’ R packages. Then the ‘xgboost’, ‘randomForest’ and ‘nnet’ packages were applied to construct predictive models. We used the ‘pROC’, ‘PRROC’ and ‘Optimalcurpoints’ packages in R when producing AUROC and PR‐AUC and calculating the threshold for the confusion matrix. 18 The KS curve was performed by the ‘rio’ and ‘ROCit’ packages. Finally, the post hoc explanation of the XGBoost model was based on the ‘SHAPforxgboost’ package in R. In terms of missing data (which included 55 variables with a missing variable rate of <10%), we imputed missing values to the mode for categorical variables and the median for continuous variables.

Results

Patients and feature variables

A total of 746 patients with first admission for HFpEF between 1 August 2020 and 1 August 2021 met the study criteria and were included in this study. There were 643 (86.19%) patients without 90 day readmission, while 103 (13.81%) patients were readmitted to the hospital for varied reasons within 90 days of discharge. Fifty‐five feature variables were collected from EHRs, dividing into sociodemographic characteristics, general characteristics, admission assessment, symptoms and signs on admission, complications, cardiac ultrasound, haematological indicators on admission and medication use during hospitalization. Feature extraction

Univariate analysis combined with the LASSO regression algorithm achieved feature extraction. It was found that educational level, primary caregiver, length of stay, ADL, nutritional risk, VTE risk, fall risk, shortness, palpitation, other symptoms, signs on admission, edema, heart murmur, nitrates and oral anticoagulants were associated with a 90 day readmission. As for cardiac ultrasound and haematological indicators, patients' 90 day readmission and non‐90 day readmission differed statistically significantly according to LAD and LVDD; more details are shown in Tables S1 and S2. The above 17 variables were further analysed by the LASSO regression algorithm. Through the LASSO regression algorithm, 12 non‐zero coefficient characteristics were obtained, reducing 17 indexes to 12 indexes, as shown in Figure 2. These remaining feature variables were educational level, length of stay, ADL, nutritional risk, fall risk, shortness, other symptoms, edema, LAD, LVDD, nitrates and anticoagulants. Then these 12 indexes were used for the subsequent construction of the models.

Model construction

After identifying 12 features, machine learning models consisting of XGBoost, RF, NN and LR were built. The categories of input features for the machine learning models are shown in Table 2. AUROC, PR‐AUC, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV and balanced accuracy were important indicators used to evaluate prediction models. Compared with the other models, the XGBoost model achieved a much superior AUC, reaching 0.98 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.973–0.987; Figure 3A] in the training set and 0.896 (95% CI: 0.863–0.929; Figure 3B) in the test set. Further analysis presented that the XGBoost model had excellent discrimination and calibration, showing that the other performance metrics were more stable and higher (PR‐AUC = 0.868, sensitivity = 0.817, specificity = 0.837, PPV = 0.839, NPV = 0.815, balanced accuracy = 0.827; Figure 3C). The calibration measure was conducted and showed that the Brier score reached 0.145 (Figure S1A). According to the Youden index, which was defined as sensitivity + specificity − 1, the best cut‐off of prediction probabilities for the XGBoost model was 46.8% (Figure S1B). If the model predicted a probability > 46.8%, it indicated that the patients had a higher risk of 90 day readmission. The KS statistic plot demonstrated that the KS value was 0.694 at 0.468 in the XGBoost model (Figure 3D). Together with the above aspects, it was suggested that the XGBoost model significantly outperformed three other machine learning models.

Interpretable framework and feature importance

We then evaluated the features that contribute to the prediction on a global level. The bar chart showed the relationship between the magnitude of the 12 feature values and the predicted impact (Figure 4A). The SHAP summary plot in Figure 4B showed the top 12 features produced by XGBoost in descending order according to the SHAP values, which expressed the positive and negative associations of the corresponding features, with the absolute SHAP value for each feature shown on the left. Each point on the plot corresponded to a sample, and the horizontal axis indicated the SHAP value of a given feature across subjects, which reflected the magnitude of the SHAP value from low (yellow) to high (purple). Accordingly, higher dependence of ADL, higher LAD and LVDD, the presence of shortness, taking nitrates, longer length of stay, higher nutritional risk and fall risk, the presence of other symptoms, taking anticoagulants and the presence of edema were associated with higher risks of 90 day readmission, so they could be interpreted as risk factors for 90 day readmission in HFpEF patients, while a higher educational level was associated with lower risks of 90 day readmission, so it could be interpreted as protective factors. Therefore, it was found that LAD and LVDD interacted with each other to increase the risk of 90 day readmission (Figure 4C). Figure 4D,E is the interpretation of the model prediction results with the two samples. For example, a patient with an LAD value of 38, absence of shortness, <7 days in hospital, mild dependence on ADL and no nitrates was predicted to have a risk of 90 day readmission of 29.2% (the best cut‐off of prediction probabilities in the XGBoost model was 46.8%), indicating that the patient was at low risk of 90 day readmission (Figure 4D). Another patient with the presence of shortness, moderate dependence in ADL, having nitrate and an LAD value of 48 reached 58.3% risk, suggesting that the patient was at high risk of 90 day readmission (Figure 4E).

Discussion

When developing generic post‐discharge HF risk prediction tools, combining HFpEF and HFrEF patients ignored the underlying differences between these already diverse populations. Patients with HFpEF exhibited a greater long‐term risk of readmission or mortality compared with those with HFrEF. 3 One of the causes is that HFpEF is more commonly seen in patients who have been diagnosed with significant co‐morbidities such as severe lung disease, renal failure, diabetes and hypertension, 19 which further complicates the management of HFpEF and elevates the likelihood of readmission. But a significant number of these readmissions were thought to have been avoidable. Existing HF discharge tools focus on 30 day outcomes. From a payment standpoint, 30 days after discharge offered clear benefits, but there was probably a 90 day window of vulnerability for increased risk of readmission or mortality before patients returned to their normal states. 20 Furthermore, the 90 day period after discharge, as opposed to the 30 day period, might be more important for monitoring readmission in the vulnerable stage of HF patients in the real‐world context of China. 8 However, there are currently few models for predicting 90 day readmission in HFpEF patients. 21 So this study aimed to present a novel approach using machine learning algorithms to predict 90 day readmission in HFpEF patients.

A total of 746 patients with HFpEF were enrolled in our study. Univariate analysis combined with LASSO regression was used to screen out 12 feature variables, and then a predictive model incorporated the following 12 feature variables into its construction: ADL, LAD, LVDD, shortness, nitrates, length of stay, nutritional risk, fall risk, other symptoms, educational level, anticoagulants and edema. Ultimately, it was found that the XGBoost model accurately predicted 90 day readmission and showed greater discrimination and satisfactory specificity and sensitivity in contrast to the other three models. Compared with the currently reported predictive model (AUC = 0.770, 95% CI: 0.767–0.774) for patients with HFpEF readmitted within 90 days, our model achieved a better AUC of 0.896 (95% CI: 0.863–0.929). Besides, the model might hardly handle the non‐linear relationship between variables. 21 In contrast, machine learning models might improve the level of prediction. In our XGBoost model, the best cut‐off of prediction probabilities for the proposed model was 46.8%. If this predictive value was exceeded, it indicated that the patient was at a higher risk of 90 day readmission. Therefore, targeted interventions could be carried out in advance for high‐risk patients.

In the study, over half of the top 12 features on the importance matrix plot and the SHAP summary plot of the XGBoost model focused on assessment of admission, which implied the major effects of admission indicators on readmission prediction in HFpEF patients. Among them, ADL was the most associated with the unfavourable outcome. The use of ADL as a predictor of hospital readmission in patients with HF has been investigated once. 22 The finding in our study was consistent with previous reports, suggesting that a lower ADL or higher dependence was associated with a higher risk of readmission. Possible reasons could be attributed to HF patients experiencing a decline in their physical ability following discharge, leading to a readmission to the hospital as a result of conditions including diabetes, respiratory disorders, orthopaedic conditions or increasing acute HF. 23 Furthermore, impaired ADL performance was strongly associated with fall risk, which also served as a sensitive indicator in the model for suggesting readmission risk. 24 Further, if a patient was accompanied by poor nutritional status, he would have higher infection risks as a result of weakened immune systems, resulting in a greatly increased risk of short‐term readmission. 25 Previous studies had reported that early mobilization, outpatient rehabilitation and continued nutritional care from the time of admission until after discharge were effective in improving readmission and mobility. 26 , 27 Thus, our study suggested that early identification of low ADL, poor nutritional status and fall risk in HFpEF patients, as well as mobility and nutrition interventions to improve their outcomes, were critical in preventing readmission.

The study also revealed that both LAD and LVDD were great predictors of 90 day readmission in HFpEF patients. HFpEF has been recognized as having a highly variable phenotype that includes underlying cardiac abnormalities. 28 Left atrial dysfunction had been widely reported as an independent prognostic indicator of HF, 29 while heterogeneity in left ventricular (LV) chamber size and its association with long‐term outcome had not been thoroughly investigated in the HFpEF population. Ogawa et al. recently found that a larger LV chamber size was associated with all‐cause death and readmission in patients with HFpEF. 30 In our study, which was the first to present LAD, LVDD was strongly associated with a 90 day readmission risk, further confirming the importance of left heart structure in the outcome of patients with HFpEF. To ascertain the molecular relationship between LV chamber size and disease related to HFpEF, more information was required, and which of them might be viable therapeutic targets would become clearer with more research.

Shortness of breath on admission was the fourth highest predictor in our model. Alsulymani et al. previously reported that shortness of breath was the most common reason (accounting for 80% of cases) for readmission, underscoring the need to thoroughly evaluate and manage this symptom. 31 Other significant factors include the use of nitrates and anticoagulants during hospitalization, according to our findings. Nitrates were usually used clinically as a vasodilator for angina pectoris or congestive HF. In another 90 day readmission risk model for HF, the use of nitrate also acted as a predictor. 9 Perhaps nitrates can potentially impair cardiac output owing to excessive preload reduction or cause endothelial dysfunction. 32 On the other hand, anticoagulant usage was mainly associated with accompanied atrial fibrillation (AF), contributing to the promotion of congestion and progression of HF, specifically in HFpEF. The prevalence of AF was rising among patients hospitalized with HFpEF, characterized by increased congestion and poor haemodynamic reserve. 33 Those with co‐morbid AF faced elevated post‐discharge risks of death and rehospitalization. 33 In our study, we did find that the predictors of 90 day readmission for HFpEF differed from those for HFrEF. For example, BNP was previously reported as a predictive factor of readmission in HFrEF, 34 but there was no significant difference in our HFpEF population due to the fact that a substantial proportion of HFpEF patients present with normal natriuretic peptide (NP) levels. 35 Additionally, patients who receive a beta‐blocker after HFrEF hospitalization have significantly lower 90 day mortality and readmission rates. 36 However, no association was found between beta‐blockers and 90 day readmission rates in our HFpEF population.

The strength of this study lied in that it was a relatively accurate prediction model specially designed to identify HFpEF patients who were at high risk of 90 day readmission, which served as a valuable supplement to existing readmission risk prediction models. In keeping with our prediction goal of identifying early intervention upon admission, a significant number of variables, in particular the majority of those at the time of admission, were included. There were several limitations to this study, which were inherent to the retrospective nature of this study. Second, the sample size was not large. The total eligible sample size of this study was 746, and the limitation of the sample size also led to incomplete external verification. Third, the current study focused on a single centre. To improve the model's discriminatory power and apply it to the real world effectively, more data from multiple centres will be needed in the future. The mean or median was used to fill in missing values, which would lead to underestimating the variance in those affected variables to some extent.

In a word, we developed and validated an XGBoost model to predict the 90 day readmission risk for Chinese patients with HFpEF, which owned relatively excellent performance characteristics. The predictors of HFpEF‐specific risk models, which were obtained from EHRs in the real world, demonstrate the importance of advanced statistical procedures such as machine learning and clinical database extraction techniques. Application of this model facilitated the provision of value by targeting interventions to patients with HFpEF who were at the highest risk of readmission.

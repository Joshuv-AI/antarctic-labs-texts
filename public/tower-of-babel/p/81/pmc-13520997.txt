Performance of reMELD-Na as a predictor of short-term mortality in patients with cirrhosis and ascites
Authors: Stefanie Quickert, Karsten Große, Elisa Selina Grubert, Andreas Stallmach, Theresa H. Wirtz, Alexander Zipprich, Philipp A. Reuken, Tony Bruns
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

We read with great interest the article by Kimmann et al.,1 evaluating model for end-stage liver disease (MELD)-derived prognostic models in 1,621 patients undergoing transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt (TIPS) placement for refractory ascites. The authors report only modest accuracy of refitted MELD-Na (ReMELD-Na) for the primary endpoint of 90-day death or liver transplantation (AUC 0.636), with inferior performance compared to MELD-Na and MELD 3.0. These findings are highly timely, as ReMELD-Na became the new standard allocation score within the Eurotransplant region in May 2025.

The original MELD score has well-documented limitations: it was not originally developed to predict waitlist mortality,2 disproportionately weighs renal dysfunction,3 underestimates mortality risk in patients with acute-on-chronic liver failure,4 and is suboptimally calibrated for patients with refractory ascites and hyponatremia.5 Although ReMELD-Na was refitted in nearly 7,000 liver transplant candidates to improve mortality prediction,6 its performance across the broader spectrum of decompensated cirrhosis remains uncertain.

The study by Kimmann et al.1 highlights a critical knowledge gap regarding the application of allocation-based models in specialized cohorts. TIPS candidates represent a highly selected subgroup within a relatively narrow MELD spectrum, which statistically constrains discrimination. Furthermore, portal decompression through TIPS fundamentally alters the natural course of the disease and its complications, potentially confounding the performance of models originally calibrated for waitlist prioritization. Whether ReMELD-Na maintains its prognostic utility in the broader, non-selected population of hospitalized patients with decompensated cirrhosis, reflecting the natural history of the disease without the influence of portal decompression, remains to be established.

We therefore evaluated ReMELD-Na vs. MELD in a bicentric cohort of 918 hospitalized patients with cirrhosis and ascites (Table S1). From 1,312 prospectively enrolled patients at two German liver transplant centres, Jena University Hospital (2010–2024) and Aachen University Hospital (2019–2024) (EK 327/19, 2880-08/10, 3683-02/3, 2019-1510-BO), we excluded individuals with prior liver transplantation, incomplete follow-up or transplantation within 90 days (to avoid competing risk bias). MELD7 and ReMELD-Na6,8 were calculated following current German Medical Association guidelines.9 The primary endpoint, 90-day mortality, occurred in 315 patients (Fig. 1A). Our cohort was predominantly male (72%), median age of 61 years with alcohol-related liver disease in 72%. ReMELD-Na showed discrimination comparable to MELD (AUROC 0.738 vs. 0.729) but superior calibration. In contrast to the TIPS cohort, higher AUROC values were observed in men compared with women and the overall cohort (AUROC 0.747 for MELD and 0.745 for ReMELD-Na) (Fig. 1B), suggesting context-dependent sex-effects. Both scores were highly concordant (Spearman’s rs = 0.935) (Fig. 1C). 69% of patients differed by no more than ±2 points. Bland-Altman analysis demonstrated a median difference (ReMELD-Na-MELD) -0.59 (+/-1.96∗SD: -5.95 to 2.73) (Fig. 1D). Consequently, in hospitalized patients with ascites, ReMELD-Na modestly improved absolute risk estimation without substantially altering patient ranking. Nevertheless, our data support the clinical transition to ReMELD-Na through several key observations: First, the recalibrated weighting does not disproportionately skew risk estimates in patients with ascites and hyponatremia. Second, an MELD score of 15 corresponds directly to an ReMELD-Na of 15, validating its continued use as a practical threshold for transplant evaluation. Third, the frequency of discordant prioritization among the highest-ranked patients aligns with that reported for unselected patients on the waiting list6,8 (Fig. 1F). Finally, discrepancies in high-risk patients (ReMELD-Na >25) arising from the fixed range of these scoring systems are unlikely to impact clinical decision-making, as the management of such critically ill patients is already guided by intensive clinical judgment.

In conclusion, ReMELD-Na and MELD demonstrate comparable discriminative performance across the spectrum of decompensated cirrhosis irrespective of transplant waiting list status or portal decompression. In hospitalized patients with ascites, ReMELD-Na does not significantly alter patient ranking but offers superior calibration, thereby providing a more accurate estimation of absolute mortality risk. These data support ReMELD-Na as a robust tool for prognostic assessment in patients with cirrhosis and ascites and underscore its utility beyond the specific context of organ allocation.

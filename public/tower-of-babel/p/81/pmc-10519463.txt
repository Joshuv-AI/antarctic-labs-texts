The Model for End-stage Liver Disease 3.0 is not superior to the Model for End-stage Liver Disease-Na in predicting survival: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Fangfang Duan, Chen Liu, Hang Zhai, Min Quan, Jun Cheng, Song Yang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:The Model for End-stage Liver Disease (MELD) 3.0 yields high prognostic performance for patients with end-stage liver disease (ESLD). However, its prognostic performance for patients with alcohol-related liver disease (ARLD) has limited results. The aim of this study was to perform such an evaluation among Chinese patients.Methods:Patients hospitalized with ARLD in one institution between 2015 and 2018 were retrospectively included and followed up for 12 months. The original MELD, MELD-Na, MELD 3.0, and modified Maddrey discriminant function (MDF) scores were calculated for each patient at baseline. Their prognostic performances for 1-year survival were assessed. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves were constructed, and AUCs were calculated for each scoring system.Results:Among the 576 patients included in our analysis, 209 patients had alcoholic hepatitis (AH). By the 1-year follow-up, 14.8% (84/567) of all the patients and 23.4% (49/209) of those with AH had died. Overall, patients who had died had higher MELD, MELD-Na, MELD 3.0, and MDF scores (all p < 0.001) than those who had not. The same was true in the AH subgroup (MELD: p < 0.001, MELD-Na: p < 0.001, MELD 3.0: p = 0.007, MDF: p = 0.017). The AUC of the MELD 3.0 for prediction of 1-year survival among patients with ARLD was 0.682, lower than that of the original MELD (0.728, p < 0.001) and MELD-Na (0.735, p < 0.001). Moreover, in the AH subgroup, the AUC for the prediction of 1-year survival was lower than that in the MELD-Na subgroup (0.634 vs. 0.708, p < 0.001).Conclusions:The MELD 3.0 was not superior to the original MELD or the MELD-Na in predicting the mortality of patients with ARLD.

INTRODUCTION

The original MELD was widely used for prognostication and was adopted to prioritize candidates on the liver transplantation waitlist in the United States in 2002.1,2 In 2016, it was updated by including sodium (MELD-Na) in the scoring system, which improved its prediction efficacy in terms of the liver transplantation waitlist.3 Alcohol-related liver disease (ARLD), particularly alcoholic hepatitis (AH), is one of the most common causes of end-stage liver disease (ESLD). The original MELD and MELD-Na exhibited excellent efficacy for the prognostication of patients with ARLD, especially those with severe AH.4–8 The original MELD/MELD-Na is superior to the Maddrey discriminant function (MDF), a well-known model for the prediction of 1-month survival of patients with severe AH, in predicting intermediate-term and long-term survival of patients with ARLD.9 In our previous retrospective study, an original MELD score ≥21 was an independent prognostic factor for the survival of Chinese patients with ARLD.10 Recently, the MELD-Na has updated to the MELD 3.0 by incorporating sex and albumin as additional variables and updating several features, such as the upper bound of serum creatinine being lowered from 4.0 to 3.0 mg/dL.11 The MELD 3.0 may improve the accuracy of mortality prediction for patients with ESLD awaiting liver transplantation. However, its prognostic performance for patients with ARLD is yet to be determined. Thus, this study aimed to evaluate the accuracy of the MELD 3.0 in predicting short-term and long-term survival of Chinese patients with ARLD, by comparing it with the original MELD, MELD-Na, and MDF.

METHODS

Patients

In this retrospective cohort study, we included consecutive adult patients hospitalized for ARLD at Beijing Ditan Hospital of Capital Medical University between January 2015 and December 2018. ARLD and AH were diagnosed according to the 2019 American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases guideline regarding ALRDs and standard definitions of AH from the National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism Alcoholic Hepatitis Consortia, respectively.9,12 The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) comorbid viral hepatitis, DILI, autoimmune liver disease, or other liver diseases; (2) previous liver transplantation; and (3) incomplete follow-up data.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Ditan Hospital of Capital Medical University (No. JDLY2021-003-01), which waived the requirement for informed consent. The research was conducted in accordance with both the Declarations of Helsinki and Istanbul and was reported in line with the STROBE criteria.

Clinical data and prognostic models

We collected baseline demographic characteristics, including sex, age, smoking history, and return to alcohol use; laboratory test results, including complete blood counts and results of coagulation, liver function, and renal function tests; occurrence of complications related to decompensated liver cirrhosis, such as ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, and bleeding of esophagogastric varices; and liver cancer data. Patients were followed up every 3 months, either in person or by phone, for 12 months. The primary endpoint was death or liver transplantation at 1 year.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows version 19.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) and R software (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables are expressed as means (±SD) or medians (interquartile ranges) and were compared using the independent-samples t test or the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables are expressed as counts and percentages and were compared using the chi-squared test or Fisher exact test. The patients were divided into survival and nonsurvival groups according to their 1-year survival outcomes. Baseline demographic data, laboratory indicators, liver cirrhosis complications, and alcohol abstinence were compared between the 2 groups. Baseline characteristics of the AH subgroup of patients were also compared between those 2 groups. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were generated to estimate the 1-year cumulative survival rate, and survival differences between groups were evaluated using the log-rank test.

The prognostic performances of the MELD, MELD-Na, MELD 3.0, and MDF for patients with ARLD/AH were assessed using a time-dependent concordance index (C-index), with a C-index closer to 1 signifying a model with better discrimination. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic curves were constructed, and the integrated AUCs (iAUCs) for each time point (1, 3, 6, and 12 mo) were calculated to compare the utility of different models. In addition, liver transplantation was considered a competitive risk event for patient death, and prognostic models in the presence of competitive risks were created. Calibration plots were drawn to show the difference between the predicted and observed survival of patients with ARLD.

RESULTS

Characteristics of patients with ARLD

A total of 1050 patients hospitalized with ARLD were screened using the hospital information system. Among them, 483 patients were excluded owing to comorbidity with other liver diseases. Finally, 567 patients with ARLD were included in the analysis; among them, 188 patients were lost to follow-up before the endpoint events occurred. The baseline characteristics of all the patients are summarized in Table 1. Table 1 also contains the p-values for comparisons of baseline characteristics between patients who were alive and those who had died by the 1-year follow-up. Patients with ARLD who had died by the 1-year follow-up had significantly higher MELD, MELD-Na, MELD 3.0, and MDF scores (all p < 0.001) than patients who survived. The same was true in the AH subgroup (MELD: p < 0.001, MELD-Na: p < 0.001, MELD 3.0: p = 0.007, and MDF: p = 0.017) and the non-AH subgroup (MELD: p = 0.002, MELD-Na: p = 0.001, MELD 3.0: p = 0.015, and MDF: p = 0.034) (Supplemental Table S1, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A493).

Endpoint events

At the end of the 12-month follow-up, 14.8% (84/567) of the patients died, including 23.4% (49/209) of those with AH and 11.4% (35/308) with non-AH. The 1-, 3-, 6-, and 12-month cumulative survival rates of patients with ARLD were 92.8%, 88.8%, 87.6%, and 82.0%, respectively. There was a significant difference in the 1-year survival between AH and non-AH patients (70.6% vs. 88.2%, p < 0.001) (Figure 1A). Meanwhile, the 12-month survival did not differ according to sex (81.6% for males vs. 100% for females, p = 0.160) (Figure 1B). In this cohort, 2 patients underwent liver transplantation at 1 and 9 months after admission, respectively; all patients were male and belonged to the AH group.

Prognostic performance for ARLD

The AUCs and C-indices of the MELD, MELD-Na, MELD 3.0, and MDF for the prediction of 1-, 3-, 6-, and 12-month survival are provided in Table 2. The receiver operating characteristic curves for each are displayed in Supplemental Figure S1, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A493. The MELD 3.0 had a lower AUC for the prediction of 1-year survival than the original MELD (0.682 vs. 0.728, p < 0.001) and MELD-Na (0.682 vs. 0.735, p < 0.001). Prognostic performance of models for patients with non-AH

The performance of the prognostic scores in the non-AH group is shown in Supplemental Table S3, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A493 and Supplemental Figure S3, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A493. The MELD 3.0 showed a lower AUC in predicting 1-year survival than MELD-Na (0.608 vs. 0.650, p = 0.046). Prognostic performance in the absence of competitive risk

The prognostic models in the absence of competitive risks in patients with ARLD and AH were also analyzed (Supplemental Tables S4, S5, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A493 and Supplemental Figure S5, S6, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A493). The result showed that the MELD 3.0 had a lower C-index (0.667) than the original MELD (0.714) and MELD-Na (0.720) in patients with ARLD. In addition, the MELD 3.0 had a lower C-index (0.646) than the original MELD (0.702) and MELD-Na (0.722) in patients with AH. The calibration plots of the MELD 3.0 displayed worse calibration when compared with those of the original MELD and MELD-Na both in patients with ARLD and in patients with AH (Supplemental Figure S7, http://links.lww.com/HC9/A493).

DISCUSSION

The original MELD and MELD-Na have been widely used to predict the prognosis of patients with ARLD and ESLD. However, the predictive accuracy of the MELD score for ESLD declined over time with the changing demographics of patients with liver disease, especially the increase in ARLD. Recently, Kim et al11 has developed an updated version, the MELD 3.0, from the MELD-Na, to incorporate such changes in the etiology of liver disease requiring transplantation. They demonstrated the superiority of the MELD 3.0 to the original MELD in predicting the survival of patients with ESLD on a liver transplantation waitlist. However, our results suggest that the MELD 3.0 is not superior to the original MELD in predicting the survival of Chinese patients with ARLD and the 1-, 3-, 6-, and 12-month survival of the AH subgroup. Moreover, the MELD-Na had the highest prognostic value in this group. Bittermann et al16 reported limited validity of the MELD 3.0 in patients with AH or non-AH, and consistent results were obtained in the current study.

One of the modifications made to the MELD-Na formula in developing the MELD 3.0 was the incorporation of sex and serum albumin. However, 36.8% of the patients in the cohort used by Kim and colleagues were women, whereas only 1.9% of patients in our cohort were women. This might have weakened the predictive performance of the MELD 3.0 in our cohort. Morales-Arraez et al4 revealed interesting demographic differences in patients with AH across different world regions. Almost half of the patients with AH in the United States, United Kingdom, and Canada were women, whereas only 2.46% (9/366) of patients with AH in India were women. The authors of that study discovered that the accuracy of MELD in predicting the prognosis of AH differed among countries. In addition, different socioeconomic factors, types of alcohol-associated beverages consumed, and genetic backgrounds, such as PNPLA3 polymorphisms, influence the phenotype of ARLD populations in different countries.16–18 Hence, prognostic models should be tailor-made for and/or validated in different ARLD populations.

In 1978, Maddrey and colleagues published a study on the MDF that they developed, which they later modified to evaluate the prognosis of patients with AH.14,15 Subsequently, the MDF was used to guide the use of corticosteroids in patients with liver disease.19,20 However, the performance of MDF was challenged by multiple studies, all of which demonstrated that the MELD scoring system is better at predicting 28-day and 3-month survival than the MDF.4,21 We and other research groups have revealed that renal function is an independent risk factor for the prognosis of patients with ARLD.10,22 Therefore, the better performance of the MELD may be explained by the addition of the creatinine concentration to its formula.

In our study, the MELD-Na, MELD, and MELD 3.0 all yielded high predictive values for 1- and 3-month survival, and modest predictive abilities thereafter. Recently, the Glasgow AH score has yielded a better predictive ability for 90-day survival than the MELD and MDF.21,23 This may be due to the former’s consideration of age and the blood urea concentration instead of the creatinine concentration to reduce the impact of hyperbilirubinemia, which occurs in most clinical situations.24 In addition, the usefulness of relapse for predicting patient survival has also been confirmed.10,25–27 Future research needs to modify the existing scoring systems, incorporating factors from both the MELD 3.0 and the Glasgow AH score, to yield a better predictive value of the survival of patients with ARLD and AH.

This study had several limitations. First, as this was a single-center study, the sample size was limited. In the future, we will expand the sample by conducting multicenter research. Despite this, the current study demonstrated that the MELD 3.0 yielded an excellent C-index. Second, the lack of histological evidence and the low proportion of females limit the generalizability of our results.

In summary, we compared the prognostic value of the MELD 3.0 with that of the MELD-Na, original MELD, and MDF for patients with ARLD in a retrospective cohort. The MELD-Na remained the best predictor. The use of the MELD 3.0 as a prognostic tool for patients with ARLD may require its adaptation according to each population.

Microbiological contamination in home continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) devices. Study protocol☆☆☆
Authors: Verónica López-García, David Peña-Otero, Gregoria Megías-Lobón, Luis Carlos Rodríguez-Pascual, Natalia García-Ruíz, Ledicia Álvarez-Paredes, Piedad Gómez-Torres, Sergio Glarreta-Aperte
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHome mechanical ventilation is crucial for treating conditions such as obstructive sleep apnea, and its use has increased exponentially. A possible link exists between poor equipment hygiene and the risk of respiratory infections, due to healthcare-related practices; however, the exact effect is unknown. The aim of this study is to determine the level of bacterial and fungal contamination in home continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) devices. This prospective, cross-sectional study will be conducted at the Burgos University Hospital Complex (CAUBU), Spain. Twenty-five patients undergoing home CPAP treatment for at least six months will be recruited. Microbiological samples will be taken from each patient at critical points of the CPAP device: CPAP housing, tubing, internal filter, interface, and humidifier. Analyses will be performed at the CAUBU Microbiology Department, which is certified under UNE-EN ISO 9001:2015. In addition to microbiological analysis, clinical variables and determinants of patient health will be collected to study potential factors associated with contamination. It is known that 30% of healthcare-associated infections are preventable, so this study aims to improve prevention strategies, health education, and nursing care to reduce the risk of pathogenic contamination related to home respiratory therapy.Keywords: Clinical protocols; Respiratory therapy; Home care services, hospital-based; Equipment and supplies; Equipment maintenance; Infection control; Respiratory tract infections

Background

Home mechanical ventilation (HMV) has experienced exponential growth in recent years with the aim of optimizing treatment [1]. In 2001, the average European prevalence was 6.6/100,000 [2]. This figure has increased significantly throughout Europe over the years [3]. In Spain, in 2018, the prevalence was 74/100,000 inhabitants [4].

Population studies show that obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a serious public health problem due to its high prevalence, affecting around one-third-of the adult population [5] and associated with a higher risk of social, occupational and cardiometabolic complications [6]. The gold standard treatment for OSA is HMV in continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) mode [7].

The mechanism of blowing humidified air into secretory surfaces of the patients, such as the nose and mouth, can potentially lead to infections if the CPAP machine mask is colonized [8]. It is essential to instruct the user and their caregivers on the correct use and maintenance of the CPAP machine and its accessories to ensure effective treatment [9]. Poor hygiene and inadequate maintenance of these devices can create an environment that favors the colonization of microorganisms and cause opportunistic respiratory infections in patients, with possible serious health consequences [8,10,11].

Currently, there is limited evidence on the relationship between CPAP use, device hygiene and maintenance, and the risk of infection; however, patients using CPAP therapy, with or without heated humidification, appear to have a higher risk of upper respiratory tract infections compared to patients treated conservatively [12]. The studies found highlight the need for further research to develop evidence-based guidelines for both healthcare professionals and patients to clarify the risk that CPAP devices pose as a source of microbial contamination [13].

Description of protocol

Infection control and hygiene programs have been shown to reduce infections [14]. It is essential to provide health education to patients undergoing CPAP so they maintain the devices and accessories in optimal hygienic conditions to prevent associated respiratory infections.

The primary objective of this study is to detect the presence of pathogenic microorganisms in home CPAP devices. Secondary objectives include establishing relationships with medical history (infections, antibiotic treatment, hospitalizations, etc.) and with the determinants of health.

Methods and Analysis

Sample Selection

This prospective, cross-sectional study will be conducted at the J. Terán Santos Multidisciplinary Sleep and Ventilation Unit of the Burgos University Hospital Complex (CAUBU), in collaboration with the CAUBU Microbiology and Parasitology Department.

The aim is to recruit 25 patients currently undergoing home CPAP treatment who attend consecutive treatment monitoring appointments at the Sleep Unit between December 2025 and February 2026 and meet the following inclusion criteria: subjects aged 18 years or older who have been on home CPAP treatment for at least 6 months and attend treatment monitoring appointments; patients capable of reading and understanding the informed consent form and providing their signed consent; adherence to treatment (≥4 h/night); and no exclusion criteria: patients with severe uncontrolled psychiatric disorder or psycho-physical incapacity to complete questionnaires.

Sample size: This is a proof-of-concept study. The effect size is unknown, as no similar studies in the same context have been reported. Therefore, the sample size is calculated based on convenience for analyzing potential effects and thus determining a statistically significant sample size for future studies. The convenience sample, adjusted to the available budget for the analysis, consists of a maximum of 100 swabs from 25 patients undergoing home CPAP therapy.

Samples will be collected using uniform movements in different directions (horizontal, vertical, and diagonal) to maximize the efficiency of microbiological recovery. Sampling devices will be placed in their respective transport media and transported to the laboratory within 4 h, keeping them refrigerated (2–8 °C) until processing. Once the samples are delivered to the Microbiology Service reception, the sample type will be verified using the analysis request form and recorded in the Laboratory Information System (LIS), following established Microbiology protocols certified according to ISO 9001:2015. The sample will be kept refrigerated for processing within 24 h.

Qualitative culture of the samples will be performed on blood agar (BioMérieux®) and tryptic soy agar (TSA) (Becton Dickinson®) using surface plating techniques to promote the growth of mesophilic aerobic bacteria and allow for their isolation. For the detection of fungi and yeasts, Sabouraud agar supplemented with chloramphenicol (BioMérieux®) will be used, inoculated by surface plating. Additionally, thioglycolate broth (Becton Dickinson®) will be inoculated to promote the recovery of aerobic and anaerobic microorganisms.

Blood agar and TSA plates will be incubated at 35–37 °C for 24–48 h, while Sabouraud agar plates with chloramphenicol will be incubated at 25–30 °C for 3–7 days. Thioglycolate broth will be incubated at 35–37 °C for a minimum of 48 h. Microbial growth will be assessed by observing colonies on solid media and the turbidity of the broth.

Identification of isolated microorganisms will be performed using a combination of mass spectrometry, automated biochemical identification systems, and Gram staining. Genus/species identification will be performed using matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS), employing either the Bruker Biotyper or bioMérieux VITEK MS platforms, depending on availability. Sample preparation will follow the manufacturer's recommendations, using direct extraction or formic acid extraction when necessary to improve spectral quality. Results will be interpreted according to the manufacturer's established criteria, with identifications meeting the system's accepted confidence levels considered valid.

In addition, the automated VITEK® 2 system (bioMérieux) with specific identification cards (Gram-negative, Gram-positive, and yeasts) will be used to confirm isolates or in cases where the MALDI-TOF result is inconclusive.

ANALYSIS:

A specific database will be created for this research using RStudio software version v 2025.09.2 + 418 for Mac® [17], ensuring data validity at all times.

For the descriptive analysis of the sample, continuous numerical variables will be used: measures of central tendency (mean, median) and dispersion (standard deviation, interquartile range). The normality of the distribution will be assessed using tests such as Shapiro-Wilk or Kolmogorov-Smirnov.

The results will be expressed as mean ± standard deviation (if the distribution is normal) or median (interquartile range) (if the distribution is not normal). For categorical and qualitative variables, frequency distributions will be obtained, expressed as percentages.

For quantitative variables, their distribution will be considered. If the data are normally distributed, the Student's t-test will be applied. If there is no normal distribution, the Wilcoxon test will be used. For bivariate correlation analysis, Pearson or Spearman coefficients will be used depending on the distribution of the variables. The association between categorical variables will be analyzed using Pearson's chi-squared test or, if the assumptions for its application are not met, Fisher's exact test.

Limitations

This pioneering initiative leads a multicenter strategy for the microbiological analysis of various surfaces of home respiratory therapy devices to correlate this analysis with the risk of developing respiratory infections and, consequently, the increased morbidity and mortality associated with hospital admissions and the use of antibiotics in conjunction with other pharmacological treatments.

There is currently a lack of knowledge regarding the impact of pathogenic microorganisms present in home respiratory therapies.

This study will allow for a better understanding of how microorganisms survive and proliferate in home environments with respiratory support, reporting a direct clinical impact by helping to develop better prevention and treatment strategies, and strengthening health education and nursing care for people with respiratory conditions.

The study has a significant scientific and social impact. The study's approach encourages reflection and a rethinking of how we provide care, thereby improving clinical practice so that people receiving home respiratory therapies can continue to rely on nursing staff who are committed to cutting-edge care and its continuous improvement.

The main limitation of the study is the potential for low treatment adherence (≤4 h per night), which could limit patient recruitment.

Sleeping Beauty mutagenesis identifies BACH2 and other regulators of CD8+ T-cell exhaustion, persistence in vivo, and CAR-T cell function under tumor-associated chronic antigen stimulation
Authors: Chang-Jung Lee, Alex T Larsson, Tyler A Jubenville, Wendy A Hudson, Zach J Seeman, Carli Stewart, Alexander K Tsai, Adam Burrack, Brooke L Kimball, Elizabeth L Siegler, Vianca V Vianzon, Erin E Nolan, Yu-Ling Yang, Christopher M Stehn, Daryl M Gohl, Margaret Donovan, Nuri A Temiz, Flavia E Popescu, Søren Warming, Somasekar Seshagiri, Yun You, Jesse D Riordan, Adam Dupuy, Branden Moriarity, Laura Rogers, Ingunn Stromnes, Saad Kenderian, David Largaespada
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGenes that enhance T-cell function represent promising targets for improving engineered T-cell therapies for cancer. While extensive CRISPR knockout screens have identified key genes enhancing T-cell persistence, employing Sleeping Beauty (SB) insertional mutagenesis, which induces both gain-of-function (GOF) and loss-of-function (LOF) mutations via the generation of fusion transcripts with endogenous genes, may uncover additional critical factors that previous approaches have overlooked.MethodsWe developed transgenic mice carrying Doxycycline (Dox)-inducible SB mutagenesis system (DiSBey) in primary T cells. Using DiSBey, we conducted screens for genetic alterations enhancing T-cell persistence under chronic antigen exposure. Specifically, CD8+ T cells from Dox-fed DiSBey mice were subjected to repeated anti-CD3 stimulation over 18 days to mimic chronic antigenic stimulation. We then identified SB transposon genomic insertion sites and corresponding fusion transcripts from the persistent DiSBey CD8+ T cells using enhanced-specificity tagmentation sequencing and RNA sequencing, respectively.ResultsUnder chronic stimulation, SB-mutagenized CD8+ T cells exhibited improved persistence and reduced terminal exhaustion phenotype. Across six independent screens, we identified 38 genes that were recurrently targeted by the SB transposon T2/Onc2 and differentially expressed under chronic anti-CD3 stimulation. T2/Onc2 insertions into Bach2 and Elmo1 were recurrently identified at the genomic level and were associated with altered nascent transcript expression. Bach2, known as a key regulator of T-cell memory formation and resistance to chronic viral infection-induced exhaustion but less characterized in engineered T cells for cancer therapy, was found to counteract exhaustion in vitro and enhance in vivo tumor persistence in the B16-Ova tumor model. Further, we showed that ectopic Bach2 expression levels influence engineered T-cell differentiation lineage, as low Bach2 overexpression retained more functional progenitor exhausted T cells and exhibited improved therapeutic efficacy. Finally, in human CART19-28ζ cells, BACH2 overexpression enhanced cytotoxicity and tumor control following chronic cancer stimulation.ConclusionsControllable SB mutagenesis using DiSBey mice provides a novel platform for functional screening of genes that improve T-cell phenotypes important for their use as therapies. Our findings highlight a dose-dependent role of BACH2 in enhancing the function of engineered T cells under conditions of chronic antigenic stimulation.

Background

Immunotherapies based on cytotoxic T-cell activity, such as chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cell therapy, have been successful for certain hematological and solid tumors, but the initial responsiveness and the durability of responses have been major problems.1 2 These problems are, in part, associated with T-cell exhaustion,3 characterized by a progressive hypofunctional state of cancer-reactive T cells experiencing chronic stimulation on T-cell receptor (TCR) signaling in the tumor microenvironment (TME).3 The exhaustion phenotype is characterized by the increasing expression of terminally exhausted immunoinhibitory receptors, reduced in vivo expansion, reduced effector function, and increased apoptosis.47 T-cell exhaustion has been directly associated with the failure of checkpoint blockade, CAR-T cell, and transgenic TCR-T cell therapies.3 5 6

Sleeping Beauty (SB) mutagenesis involves the activation of SB transposase to mobilize a mutagenic SB transposon, such as T2/Onc2, in the genome and is a powerful forward genetic screening tool to identify genetic drivers of new phenotypes, especially for tumor initiation and progression.8 Despite its genome-wide insertional coverage, SB mutagenesis is prone to local hopping (a tendency to insert more T2/Onc2 near the donor concatemer on the same chromosome), and multiple T2/Onc2 donor lines are needed to be included in order to mitigate this bias.9 10 While SB was used to identify oncogenic drivers for T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia11 and endogenous T-cell trafficking mechanisms to tumors,12 it has not been widely applied to engineered T cells because prolonged or constitutive SB transposase activity carries a risk of ongoing insertional mutagenesis and potential oncogenic transformation of the engineered T cells under study. The T2/Onc2 construct includes an MSCV promoter, a splice donor, and a bidirectional polyadenylation signal, all flanked by splice acceptors.13 14 Depending on the landing site, T2/Onc2 insertion can result in gain-of-function (GOF) or loss-of-function (LOF) mutations in a single screen, either of which might be used to enhance T-cell phenotypes for cancer therapy.15 These structural elements facilitate the generation of truncated structural variants, which further increase the diversity of the resulting mutant pool. In addition, not only can the DNA insertion sites of T2/Onc2 be mapped at the genomic level, the endogenous gene transcripts activated or truncated by fusion with T2/Onc2 sequences can also be detected using RNA sequencing (RNA-seq).16 These features allow SB mutagenesis to uncover critical genetic mechanisms that may be overlooked by CRISPR-based approaches, which usually only allow forced overexpression (OE) or complete knockout in separate screen designs.17 18

Here, we created a novel transgenic mouse model, Doxycycline (Dox)-inducible SB mutagenesis system (DiSBey), allowing Doxycycline-controllable SB mutagenesis in primary T cells. CD8+ T cells from the DiSBey mice were selected by anti-CD3 chronic stimulation, and the mutagenic SB transposon T2/Onc2 DNA insertion sites and messenger RNA (mRNA) fusion transcripts were identified by next-generation sequencing. From six independent DiSBey screens, 38 genes were repeatedly found to be mutagenized by SB under chronic antigenic stimulation, with Bach2 and Elmo1 both detected at the genomic and transcript levels. Bach2, a master regulator for T-cell stemness and memory phenotype,1922 was found to be strongly enriched in the chronic-stimulation-resistant DiSBey CD8+ T cells. Bach2 OE, especially low levels of OE, improved the persistence of CD8+ T cells in both in vitro and in vivo in B16-Ova models. OE of BACH2 in human CART19-28ζ also improved targeted cytotoxicity and therapeutic outcome after chronic stimulation. Taken together, these studies establish SB mutagenesis as a platform for improving engineered primary T cells and identify BACH2 as an important regulator of CD8+ T-cell persistence and functionality relevant to cancer immunotherapy.

Methods

DiSBey mouse models generation

C2 mouse embryonic stem cells (C57BL/6N) were electroporated with linearized T11-LSL-SB100X plasmid (see online supplemental figure 1A, MGI allele 8214726), and clones were selected using established methods. Resulting clones were screened by long-range PCR followed by sequence confirmation and correctly targeted T11-LSL-SB100X clones were injected into albino blastocysts followed by implantation into pseudopregnant females. High percentage chimeric male offspring were crossed to albino C57BL/6N females to generate N1 T11-LSL-SB100X heterozygous mice. Subsequent N1 mice were backcrossed to C57BL/6J mice for at least four generations to create congenic mice B6J.B6N-Col1a1tm1(tetO-sb100x)Dla (MGI strain 8214988). SB100X mice were then crossed to mice carrying the Rosa-CAGS-LSL-rtTA3-IRES-mKate2 allele23 (Gt(ROSA)26Sortm2(CAG-rtTA3,-mKate2)Slowe), hCD2-Cre24 (C57BL/6-Tg(CD2-cre)1Lov/J), and T2/Onc225 (TgTn(sb-T2/Onc2)6113Njen for DiSBeyChr1 or TgTn(sb-T2/Onc2)6070Njen for DiSBeyChr4) to create DiSBey mice. Genotyping PCR primer sequences are listed in online supplemental table 1.

Murine CD8+ T-cell transduction

24 hours after CD3/CD28 Dynabeads activation, CD8+ T cells were mixed with retrovirus (Multiplicity of infection, or MOI=5) before transferred into non-TC-treated plates coated with Retronectin (TaKaRa T100A). CD8+ T cells were then spun at 2,000 g for 2 hours for spinoculation. Transduction efficiency was checked 24 hours after transduction with CytoFlex S for in vitro assays. For in vivo studies, transduced cells were sorted by BD FACSAria II before expansion.

In vitro murine T-cell assays

To evaluate apoptosis, T cells were isolated at each indicated time point and stained with APC Annexin V (Tonbo Bio SKU 20–6409 T100) and 7-AAD (Tonbo Bio SKU 13–6993 T200) before being analyzed by flow cytometry. To compare proliferation activity of mouse CD8+ T cells after chronic TCR stimulation, day 18 chronically stimulated T cells were stained with Violet Proliferation Dye 450 (BD 562158) following manufacturer’s protocol and cultured for 96 hours before being analyzed by flow cytometry.

To assess OT-I cytotoxicity, mCherry+ JW18-SIINFEKL cells were treated with mouse interferon (IFN)-γ (100 ng/mL for 24 hours) before being co-cultured with chronically stimulated OT-I T cells at the indicated Effector-to-Target (E:T) ratio for 48 hours. Live cell imaging of the mCherry+ area was recorded and analyzed by Incucyte SX5.

Flow cytometry

CytoFLEX S or BD FACSAria II was used for flow cytometry analysis or sorting. Flow cytometry data were analyzed by FlowJo V.10 or Kaluza V.2.3. All antibodies used in this study are listed in online supplemental table 2.

For intracellular cytokine staining of adoptively transferred mouse T cells, single-cell suspensions isolated from different mouse organs were treated with Brefeldin A (BD 555029) and Phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA)/ionomycin (BioLegend 423301) or 1 µM SIINFEKL peptide (Anaspec AS-60193-1) for 4 hours. Cells were washed, stained with fixable viability dyes, and antibodies against surface markers indicated above. After surface staining, cells were fixed and permeabilized before staining with antibodies against intracellular cytokines indicated above.

For intracellular cytokine staining of human CAR-T cells, CAR-T cells were co-cultured with JeKo-1 cells at a 1:5 ratio for 4 hours after adding 2 µL CD28 (BD 348040), and 1 µL monensin (BioLegend 420701) per 200 µL. Cells were then stained with viability dye, fixed, and permeabilized before staining.

In vitro chronic TCR stimulation

Adapted from Belk et al.26 After CD3/CD28 activation, murine CD8+ T cells were seeded in cultured plates coated with (for chronic stimulation) or without (for acute stimulation) 5 µg/mL anti-CD3ε (BioLegend 100359). Afterwards, 75% of T cell media (TCM) in each well was changed every odd day, and T cells were replated at 106 cells per mL every even day until day 18.

Production of edited human CAR-T cells

Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) with SepMate PBMC isolation tubes (STEMCELL Technologies) from de-identified blood samples that were obtained under a Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board approved protocol. T cells from the PBMCs were isolated using the EasySep Human T Cell Isolation Kit with a Robosep machine (STEMCELL Technologies). The T cells were then activated with CD3/CD28 beads (Cell Therapy Systems Dynabeads) at a 3:1 bead-to-cell ratio. 24 hours later, activated T cells were transduced at MOI=3 with lentiviral particles encoding a CD19-targeting CAR with a CD28ζ costimulatory domain as previously described.27 The next day, CAR-T cells were transduced at MOI=3 with lentiviral particles generated with a BACH2 OE or control plasmid that contains a green fluorescent protein (GFP) and puromycin resistance sequence (see online supplemental figure 8A). From day 3 to 8, CAR-T cells were maintained at 1×106 cells/mL and supplemented the T-cell media with 1 µg/mL puromycin. On day 6, CD3/CD28 beads were removed through magnetic separation and evaluated CAR expression and GFP positivity with flow cytometry. On day 8, the CAR-T cells were washed three times to remove puromycin before using them in experiments. During CAR-T cell expansion and in vitro studies, CAR-T cells were kept in T-cell media that is composed of X-Vivo 15 (Lonza) supplemented with 10% human serum albumin (Corning) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin-glutamine (Gibco).

In vitro human CAR-T cell assays

To evaluate changes in antigen-specific proliferation, T cells were co-cultured with JeKo-1 cells at a 1:1 ratio in a 96-well plate for 5 days. After 3 days, 100 µL of media was removed from all wells, and it was replaced with 100 µL of fresh T-cell media. On day 5, the absolute count of live CD3+ cells was determined via a volumetric measurement with flow cytometry after staining samples with Zombie R718 fixable viability kit and CD3 (clone OKT3) as described previously.

To evaluate changes in in vitro CAR-T cell cytotoxicity, we co-cultured CAR-T cells with luciferase+JeKo-1 cells at the indicated E:T ratios. After 48 hours, killing was assessed with luminescence imaging on a GloMax Explorer (Promega) after adding 1 µL D-luciferin (3 mg/mL, Gold Biotechnology) per 100 µL sample volume.

In vitro CAR-T chronic exhaustion assay

To evaluate the effect of chronic stimulation on BACH2 OE CAR-T cells, we used an in vitro exhaustion model as previously described.27 On day 8 of CAR-T production, cells were co-cultured with JeKo-1 tumor cells at a 1:1 ratio. Restimulation with the same number of JeKo-1 cells was performed on days 10, 12, and 14. On day 15, CAR-T cells were isolated using CD4 and CD8 positive selection beads (Miltenyi Biotec 130–045-101 and 130–045-201), and a subset was subjected to a second week of chronic stimulation. These cells were co-cultured with JeKo-1 cells at a 1:1 ratio and restimulated on days 17, 19, and 21. Final isolation was performed on day 22. CAR-T cells from days 15 and 22 were then used in functional assays to assess changes in cell activity.

Sleeping Beauty sequencing data processing and analysis

Sequencing raw data were deposited at GSE302682 and GSE302683. T2/Onc2 fusions were quantified by trimming the RNA-seq data with Trimmomatic, then using Arriba with a modified genome that included the T2/Onc2 construct as a chromosome. For RNA-seq expression quantification, reads that included a T2/Onc2 construct were excluded, and the remaining reads were quantified with HISAT2 and Subread. T2/Onc2 fusions were quantified by removing the primer sequences and aligning the remaining reads to the genome using BWA MEM, deduplicated using UMI Tools, and quantified with a custom R script. Initial fusion and insertion detection included only fusions or insertions with at least three deduplicated supporting reads. Additional filtering was applied downstream.

All downstream analyses were completed in R. The bottom 10% of expressed fusions were removed from each sample via quantile analysis. Differential analysis for both genomic insertions and fusion transcripts was performed at the gene level using DESeq228 with an offset of 1 count applied. Differential genomic insertions were identified as those with a p value<0.05 and with a log2 fold change >0.5. Differential fusion transcripts were identified as those with a p value<0.05 and with a log2 fold change >1. Over-representation analysis was conducted with clusterProfiler using Gene Ontologies29 with a p value of 0.01 and a q value of 0.05.

Statistical analysis

Statistical methods and the number of samples tested (n) for each experiment can be found in the figure legends. Statistics were calculated by Prism V.9. Unless otherwise specified, mean±SD were used, and statistical significance was determined as follows: ns, not significant; *p>0.05; **p<0.05; ***p<0.01; ****p<0.001; *****p<0.0001.

Detailed information on cell lines, mouse strains, tissue processing, T-cell culturing and assays, and sequencing data processing and analysis can be found in online supplemental methods.

Results

Generation of transgenic mouse models allowing controllable SB mutagenesis in T cells

To generate T-cell mutant pools for phenotype screening beneficial to adoptive cell therapies (ACT) while minimizing leukemogenic risk, we developed a temporally controllable SB100X transposase transgene. Specifically, we created the congenic mouse model B6J.B6N-Col1a1tm1(tetO-sb100x)Dla, carrying a loxP-stop-loxP–flanked SB100X transgene driven by the tetracycline-responsive element knocked into the transgenic safe harbor collagen 1A1 (Col1a1) locus30 31 (see online supplemental figure 1A). This line was then bred with Rosa26-LSL-rtTA3-mKate2,23 hCD2-Cre,24 and T2/Onc225 mice to create a line with DiSBey in hCD2-active cells (figure 1A,B). To mitigate local hopping bias, we used two T2/Onc2 strains that have the highest mobilization efficiency with concatemer insertion sites on chromosomes 1 and 4 to compensate for local hopping bias,25 resulting in the DiSBeyChr1 and DiSBeyChr4 lines.

In vivo SB100X activity is regulated in T cells from DiSBey mice

The Dox-inducible activity of SB mutagenesis was confirmed, as SB100X expression was inducible only in DiSBey mice via Dox-containing diets, and absent in C57BL/6J (wild-type (WT)) or triple transgenic mice (DiSBey littermates lacking hCD2-Cre transgene and, therefore, lacking SB100X expression) (figure 1C,D). Cre-mediated recombination of the Rosa26-LSL-rtTA3-mKate2 transgene was efficient in CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, low in natural killer (NK) cells and macrophages, and minimal in B cells and granulocytes in DiSBey splenocytes. In the thymus, only CD8+, CD4+ T cells, and NK cells showed more than 5% recombination of the Rosa26-LSL-rtTA3-mKate2 transgene (figure 1E). Although local hopping could introduce insertional profile bias, we did not find T2/Onc2 insertional copy number differences between DiSBeyChr1 and DiSBeyChr4, nor age and gender (see online supplemental figure 1B). SB mutagenesis in T cells was also Dox-dependent both in vitro and in vivo (figure 1F,G, online supplemental figure 1C,D). We created DiSBey mice, a genome-wide SB mutagenesis system for primary T cells that (1) allows for time-controlled mutagenesis, (2) does not need further genetic engineering to introduce efficient primary immune cell mutagenesis, and (3) is easily scalable through expanding the mouse colony size.

SB mutagenesis enhances CD8+ T persistence under chronic TCR stimulation

To select SB-mutagenized CD8+ T-cell clones with enhanced persistence under chronic TCR stimulation, we employed an in vitro chronic exhaustion assay, in which polyclonal murine CD8+ T cells were subjected to repeated stimulation with anti-CD3 antibodies to simulate prolonged TCR signaling.26 This chronic activation model induced key features of T-cell exhaustion, including upregulation of inhibitory checkpoint receptors programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) and T-cell immunoglobulin and mucin-domain containing-3 (TIM-3), reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, triggered mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic stress, impaired proliferative capacity, caused activation-induced death, and the emergence of epigenetic signatures characteristic of terminally exhausted, tumor-reactive CD8+ T cells in vivo.26 32 33

CD8+ T cells from DiSBey (on Dox diet for 8–12 weeks) and WT mice were activated and cultured under either chronic (anti-CD3-coated plates+interleukin (IL)-2) or acute (uncoated plates+IL-2) stimulation. Cells were replated every 2 days and harvested for analyses or sequencing (figure 2A). Chronic stimulation reduced viability and expansion, but both DiSBeyChr4 and DiSBeyChr1 T cells showed a viability advantage over WT from day 14 onward (figure 2B, online supplemental figure 2A). At day 18 DiSBey CD8+ T cells showed improved viability for three straight time points. No expansion difference was observed (see online supplemental figure 2B). Notably, despite T cells from both DiSBeyChr1 and DiSBeyChr4 exhibiting a selective advantage over their WT counterpart in two independent experiments, only DiSBeyChr4 T cells showed a shift from PD-1+/TIM-3+ to PD-1+/TIM-3⁻ phenotype on day 18 (figure 2C,D).

Transcriptomic analysis of the chronically-stimulated WT T cells confirmed that the T cells had high similarity to exhausted T cells found in melanoma tumors,34 suggesting that chronic stimulation served as an appropriate selective pressure mimicking TME (figure 2E). The DiSBey T cells, which exhibited improved persistence, also had reduced expression of exhaustion markers Mt1, Mt2, Nr4a2, and Havcr2, and increased levels of memory markers Cxcr3 and Bach2 (figure 2F). This evidence suggested that SB mutagenesis enriches for CD8+ T cells with a phenotype of enhanced persistence under chronic TCR stimulation.

SB genomic insertion and fusion mRNA transcript profiles identify unique and recurrent insertion mutations in the persisting DiSBey CD8+ T cells

To identify the genes driving persistence under chronic TCR stimulation, we performed enhanced-specificity tagmentation-assisted sequencing to map genomic T2/Onc2 genomic insertions and RNA-seq to detect resulting fusion transcripts. Insertion events were identified and prioritized if (1) differentially present under chronic versus acute conditions, or (2) uniquely present in DiSBey samples (figure 3A). Insertions to Bach2, a known transcription repressor associated with T-cell stemness, memory formation, and resistance to chronic viral infection,1922 emerged as a top hit with both enriched genomic insertions and fusion transcripts (figure 3B,C). Additional genes (such as Elmo1, Bcl2, Akt2, Nabp1, and Myh9) showed evidence of transcriptional perturbation or genomic insertion, indicating other candidates that potentially drive T-cell persistence. To integrate our findings with existing CRISPR-based T-cell exhaustion screens, we employed rank aggregation (RA), an analytic process that consolidates hits from disparate platforms into a single, statistically robust list.35 This approach has been previously validated and used for comparing and consolidating SB- and CRISPR-based studies.36 RA results suggested that, while some hits are highly ranked in both SB-based and CRISPR-based studies (such as Nfatc2), there are hits specific to either screening platform (see online supplemental figure 3E). These differences suggest that some key functional genomic mechanisms could be systematically overlooked by CRISPR-based approaches, and that DiSBey successfully uncovers them through insertional mutagenesis.

Depending on the T2/Onc2 insertion sites relative to the gene body, SB mutagenesis could lead to GOF or LOF of full-length or truncated gene products.13 In the improved-persistence DiSBey chronic T cells, forward T2/Onc2 insertions upstream of Bach2 coding sequences indicated a near full-length transcriptional activation of Bach2 by SB mutagenesis that, when translated, would produce full-length Bach2 protein (figure 3D). Further, high levels of Bach2 mRNA were only observed in chronically stimulated DiSBey T cells but not in WT (figure 3E). As Dox was not added throughout the screening period (therefore, no SB mutagenesis at the time when we observed improved persistence), and the gradual increase of genomic insertion of T2/Onc2 at Bach2 over time (figure 3A), this indicated that Bach2-activation mutant clones were selectively enriched over the screening period and became the dominant population in the enhanced-exhaustion population at day 18. In contrast, Elmo1 insertions were antisense, likely leading to repression (see online supplemental 2A, 2B).

Ectopic Bach2 OE maintains CD8+ T-cell persistence against chronic stimulation in a dose-dependent manner

We next examined how Bach2 supports CD8+ T-cell persistence under chronic stimulation and whether its OE affects antitumor cytotoxicity. OT-I CD8+ T cells were transduced with Bach2-IRES-GFP (Bach2 OE) or control vector (Ctrl) (see online supplemental figure 4A) and then subjected to chronic anti-CD3 stimulation (figure 4A). Bach2 OE caused enhanced viability, reduced apoptosis, and similar cytotoxicity to that of control cells after 18 days of chronic stimulation (figure 4B–E). Interestingly, the enhanced persistence due to Bach2 OE appeared to be dose-dependent, as low Bach2 OE (Bach2low), revealed by low ectopic transgenic reporter GFP expression, had preferential enrichment during chronic TCR stimulation compared with high Bach2 OE (Bach2high) (figure 4F,G), as no difference between GFP levels was found at the beginning of the assay (see online supplemental figure 4D). Meanwhile, proliferation was associated with the level of ectopic Bach2 expression dosage, as Bach2high populations showed the poorest proliferation, and Bach2low populations exhibited intermediate proliferation compared with their control counterparts (figure 4H). This indicates that ectopic Bach2 expression level tunes T-cell behavior during chronic antigen exposure.

Next, we performed an in vitro competition assay to assess Bach2 OE under chronic antigenic stimulation from cancer cells. OT-I-PL (Thy1.1+) CD8+ T cells were transduced with either Bach2 OE or a negative control vector expressing TagBFP and mixed at a 1:1 ratio before repeatedly stimulated with irradiated B16-F10 melanoma cells pulsed with SIINFEKL peptide for three rounds (figure 4I). After three rounds of stimulation, the Bach2 OE population significantly outcompeted the TagBFP population, indicating enhanced persistence (figure 4J,K). Further, these Bach2 OE cells were enriched for a PD-1+/TIM-3⁻ population, while most Ctrl cells displayed a PD-1+/TIM-3+ terminally exhausted phenotype (figure 4J,L). These results show Bach2 OE promotes CD8+ T-cell persistence and reduces exhaustion markers under chronic tumor engagement.

Bach2 OE only increases in vivo T-cell persistence without improving tumor control

We next examined whether Bach2 OE could enhance persistence in vivo within an immune-competent background. OT-I-PL CD8+ T cells transduced with either Bach2 OE or TagBFP control were mixed and transferred into B16-Ova-bearing C57BL/6J mice (figure 5A). Bach2 OE OT-I cells in tumors in vivo also showed a selective advantage at day 14, but not at earlier time points (figure 5B). Similarly, we observed that Bach2 OE OT-I cells demonstrated a remarkably enhanced ability to persist in the draining lymph nodes, spleens, and circulating blood (figure 5B). However, despite improved persistence, no survival benefit was observed (see online supplemental figure 6), suggesting the improved persistence by Bach2 OE itself, without regard to level, is insufficient for better tumor control.

Low-level ectopic Bach2 OE enables both effector and memory phenotypes

To investigate the function of T cells with ectopic Bach2 OE, we employed an orthotopic pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDA) model, which features a dominant role of suppressive myeloid cells in tumors.37 38 An Ova+ PDA cell line39 was surgically implanted into the pancreas of C57BL/6J mice, followed by transferring Thy1.1+ OT-I T cells transduced with Bach2 OE or GFP control vectors (figure 5C). Cognate SIINFEKL peptide restimulation of transferred OT-I T cells demonstrated that Bach2 OE OT-I cells exhibited reduced production of IFN-γ and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α compared with controls in all organs collected. However, on PMA and ionomycin restimulation, Bach2 OE T cells displayed cytokine production capacities for IFN-γ and TNF-α comparable to controls, while IL-2 production was notably elevated in cells isolated from both tumors and draining lymph nodes relative to the control counterparts (figure 5D). These results indicate that Bach2 OE allows CD8+ T cells to maintain high secretion capacity of IFN-γ and TNF-α and even enhances IL-2 secretion, but it also reduces TCR-stimulated cytokine secretion, which in turn impairs their overall effector function.

The Bach2 OE group also showed a reduced proportion of KLRG1+/CD62L− cells (short-lived effector T cells with higher cytotoxic potential)19 40 and increased proportion of KLRG1−/CD62L+ cells (long-lived central memory T cells with reduced cytotoxicity)19 40 in the spleen, compared with control T cells (figure 5E,F). Interestingly, we noted an enrichment of Bach2low T cells as indicated by lower GFP levels compared with input (figure 5G). These Bach2low cells consisted of both KLRG1+/CD62L− and KLRG1−/CD62L+ subsets. In contrast, Bach2high cells predominantly assumed a KLRG1−/CD62L+ phenotype, whereas control cells tended to adopt a KLRG1+/CD62L− phenotype.

Together, these findings indicate that while Bach2 OE promotes memory-like phenotypes in T cells, it also attenuates TCR signaling and limits effector functions. However, this effect depends on the ectopic Bach2 expression level, as lower levels of ectopic Bach2 OE permit phenotypic flexibility between effector and memory states, whereas high levels enforce a fixed memory-like state.

Low Bach2 OE contributes to improved tumor control and persistence by balancing exhaustion differentiation dynamics

To investigate the dosage effects of Bach2 OE, we sorted OT-I cells into the top 10% (Bach2Top10%) and the bottom 10% (Bach2Bot10%) ectopic Bach2 expression based on GFP intensity (figure 6A). While having only around 3% of the protein levels found in the top 10% group (see online supplemental figure 7A), Bach2Bot10% cells demonstrated superior therapeutic efficacy, improved tumor control, and increased intratumoral persistence (figure 6B,C). Transcriptional profiling revealed that Bach2 dosage regulates critical programs for effector function (Gzma, Gzmb), exhaustion (Havcr2, Ccr2, Entpd1, Prdm1, Bhlhe40, Tox), and memory development (Klf2, Tcf7, Cxcr3) (figure 6D,E; Online supplemental figure 7B). Pathway analysis suggested that the high Bach2 OE levels force the negative regulation of metabolic-related and hypoxia-related stress-responding pathways, which possibly contributed to the failure to control tumors (see online supplemental figure 7C).

Responding to external antigenic recognition, differentiation of T cells into non-functional terminal exhausted T cells (Texterm) is a highly dynamic process that originates from progenitor (Texprog) and intermediate (Texint) exhausted T cells, characterized by surface CD69 and Ly108 expression.41 To clarify how ectopic Bach2 dosage affects the early differentiation commitment decision-making of edited T cells, we subjected sorted cells to acute TCR restimulation (figure 6F). Similar to our prior results, both Bach2 OE groups exhibited reduced PD-1+/TIM3+ population and increased TCF1 and CD62L levels (see online supplemental figure 7D,F). Anti-CD3 restimulation caused a population shift to Texprog1 and Texterm in controls, consistent with the reversion of Texprog2 to Texprog1 when facing proliferation stress and the formation of terminal exhaustion in vivo.41 Notably, both Bach2 OE groups have decreased Texterm with increased Texprog, with Bach2Top10% presenting more Texprog1 and Bach2Bot10% presenting more Texprog2 (figure 6G,H). Hence, ectopically expressed Bach2 may serve as a reversion factor for T cells from Texprog2 to Texprog1, and Bach2Top10% forced most of the engineered T cells at a naïve/memory Texprog1, while Bach2Bot10% allowed more cells to remain in a more functional Texprog2 state when encountering antigen stimulation (figure 6I).

BACH2 OE in human CART cells retained functionality after chronic stimulation

We next investigated whether OE of human BACH2 enhances the therapeutic performance of CAR-T cells under conditions of chronic stimulation. To this end, human CART19-28ζ cells were transduced with BACH2 OE or GFP-only control vector (see online supplemental figure 8A,B). These engineered CAR-T cells were then subjected to an in vitro chronic stimulation protocol, wherein CD19+ Jeko-1 target cells were replenished every other day for 14 days post-CAR-T manufacturing to induce dysfunctional exhausted CAR-T cells27 (figure 7A). Surprisingly, BACH2 OE CART19-28ζ cells showed a trend towards improved persistence for the first 7 days of chronic stimulation (p=0.1004), but not at the end of the assay (see online supplemental figure 8D). Checkpoint and cytokine showed interindividual variation between donors (see online supplemental figure 8E,F). However, following chronic stimulation with Jeko-1 cells, BACH2 OE CART19-28ζ cells exhibited enhanced cytotoxicity in vitro at low E:T ratios and improved tumor control in vivo, whereas freshly manufactured BACH2 OE CAR-T did not exhibit improved tumor control (figure 7B–D). Next, we analyzed the association between BACH2 expression in the infusion CAR-T product and treatment responses in patients undergoing CAR-T therapy, and we found that BACH2 expression was elevated in CD8+ T cells of CAR-T responders in the TanCAR7 T trial42 (figure 7E), with a similar trend in CD4+ CAR-T (p=0.06), naïve subsets (p=0.09), and in the ZUMA-1 trial43 (p=0.15) (see online supplemental figure 8I).

Discussion

The lack of in vivo persistence, especially under chronic antigenic stimulation, is widely considered an obstacle to successful T cell-based immunotherapies against cancers.1 2 We present DiSBey mice as a novel screening model with Dox-regulated SB mutagenesis to identify genes that improve T-cell persistence under repeated TCR stimulation. We identified that Bach2 allows for improved persistence under in vitro and in vivo exhaustion stress. In addition, BACH2 OE allows for improved tumor control under a chronic exhaustion environment in human CART19-28ζ cells.

SB mutagenesis in DiSBey mice offers a powerful, complementary approach to CRISPR-Cas9 for identifying gene functions in engineered T cells. While all DiSBeyChr1 and DiSBeyChr4 T cells exhibited a viability advantage by activating Bach2 under chronic TCR stimulations, only DiSBeyChr4 T cells had decreased TIM-3 surface expression (figure 2, online supplemental figure 2) and exhibited insertion profiles unique to each T2/Onc2 donor strain (figure 3A). These findings again highlighted the importance of including multiple T2/Onc2 donor strains in an SB mutagenesis screen to mitigate local hopping, as Bach2 is also located on chromosome 4. Unlike CRISPR, which typically induces complete silencing or forces high doses of OE of full-length genes, SB mutagenesis can achieve a dosage effect by creating heterozygous GOF and LOF mutations, including truncated variants, within a single screen.10 13 DiSBey also eliminates the need for transducing gRNA libraries in vitro, enabling robust in vivo screening. The Dox-controllable design allows mutagenesis in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells while minimizing the chance of leukemogenesis, and in vivo selection against deleterious mutations refines the mutant pool before screening. Furthermore, while this work focuses on T cells, DiSBey’s design strategy can be generalized to other immune cell subtypes by substituting alternative promoters to drive lineage-specific Cre recombinase expression. This would enable efficient, non-viral genome-wide screening of immune cell types that are notoriously hard to transduce yet crucial for antitumor effects in TME, such as B cells and NK cells.

Although Bach2 has long been known to prevent exhaustion during chronic viral infection20 21 and dose-dependently control T-cell identity determination on activation,19 its use in rejecting cancer-induced exhaustion for engineered T cells remained unclear until recently. Our human CAR-T results, in which only chronically stimulated BACH2 OE showed superior tumor control but not freshly manufactured ones, may act as a selective filter, enriching for the optimal BACH2 dosage required for therapeutic persistence. Supporting this, while this article is under revision, other groups have also identified that low ectopic expression of BACH2 in CAR-T cells improved therapeutic efficacy,4446 and that stabilization of BACH2 transcripts during CAR-T manufacturing improved cell product function.47 Among these, our work is the only work that nominates BACH2 from a genome-wide screening, further supporting the robustness of the gene and the DiSBey screening platform. Our screening design, which identifies Bach2 as improving CD8+ T-cell persistence through chronic TCR stimulation, suggests that transgenic TCR therapies may similarly benefit from low BACH2 OE, as we and others have found in CAR-T. While high enforced Bach2 is known to secure Texprog status,48 our work added a dose-dependent layer to this mechanism, with low ectopic expression contributing to Texprog2 while high expression contributed to Texprog1. Finally, our findings on the preservation of Ly108+ Texprog population in Bach2Bot10% on restimulation may serve as an early Tex differentiation decision-making event to contribute to long-term tumor control, as others identified that low Bach2 OE contributed to a hybrid stem and effector stages only in Ly108− (Slamf6−) population in the late stage of tumor-induced exhaustion.45

In summary, our work demonstrates the novel application of controllable SB mutagenesis to identify enhanced T-cell functionalities and highlights the OE of BACH2 as a potential candidate for improving CAR-T or other T-cell therapies. Using the DiSBey mouse models and the multiomic analytic pipelines developed in this study, more comprehensive in vitro and in vivo functional screenings can be conducted to advance various forms of ACT.

Hybrid Tie-Grip Suture Repair for Isolated Radial Tears in the Middle Segment of the Lateral Meniscus
Authors: Ryohei Uchida, Shuji Horibe, Tomoki Ohori, Toshitaka Tsunematu, Hiroyuki Tanaka, Seiji Okada, Ryo Iuchi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRadial tears in the middle segment are the most common type of traumatic lateral meniscus injury in young athletes with stable knees. Conventionally, meniscectomy was the first-line treatment for this type of tear, but it did not prevent degenerative changes or decreases in meniscal function. Therefore, several studies have reported meniscal repair of radial tears in the middle segment of the lateral meniscus (RTMLMs) involving the vascular zone as an alternative treatment option. However, this type of tear had low healing potential. Regardless of repair technique, complete healing rates on second-look arthroscopy after repair of isolated RTMLMs were still low. Therefore, a hybrid suture repair technique, combining all-inside suture and inside-out repair techniques, is considered more suitable for isolated RTMLMs than conventional repair techniques. This technical note describes procedures and benefits of hybrid tie-grip suture repair techniques for RTMLMs.

Radial tears in the middle segment are the most common type of traumatic lateral meniscus injury in young athletes with stable knees (Fig 1).1 For this type of tear, tie-grip suture repair using an inside-out repair technique was recommended owing to prevention of cutting and slipping of the horizontal sutures.2,3 However, complete healing rates on second-look arthroscopy were low.3 Therefore, an all-inside suture (AIS) repair technique using suture passers—one of several recent anatomic meniscal repair techniques—was applied to this type of tear.4, 5, 6, 7, 8 This technique consists of only meniscus-to-meniscus suturing without interposition of soft tissues including the capsule.9 In a comparative study, we observed that AIS repair could reduce postoperative lateral meniscus extrusion and the failure rate compared with an inside-out repair technique, but complete healing rates were still low regardless of repair technique.4,6

During an inside-out repair technique, a vascular access channel can be provided by penetration of vascular abundant soft tissue (e.g., capsule or vascular zone of the meniscus)10; however, the AIS repair technique cannot provide this channel. Thus, the tie-grip suture repair, which combines AIS and inside-out repair techniques, would yield more advantages for meniscal healing, especially for meniscal tears with low healing potential. This combination of repair techniques has already been introduced as a “hybrid horizontal and vertical mattress suture configuration” for medial meniscus radial tears in a biomechanical report.11 However, this technique was not applied to clinical use in the past report. This technical note presents 2 repair techniques for radial tears in the middle segment of the lateral meniscus for use in clinical practice: hybrid tie-grip suture repair and modified hybrid tie-grip suture repair (Fig 2, Video 1).

Arthroscopic views (A-C) and schemas (D-F) of 3 hybrid suture repairs for radial lateral meniscus tear in left knee. Two standard hybrid suture repair techniques are shown: one is a combination of at least 2 tie sutures (solid and dotted blue lines) using an all-inside suture (AIS) technique and 2 grip sutures (solid and dashed black lines) using an inside-out (I-O) technique (A, D), and the other is a combination of at least 1 tie suture (solid and dotted blue lines) using an AIS technique and 2 grip sutures (solid and dashed black lines) and 2 tie sutures (double black lines) using an I-O technique (B, E). The modified technique is almost the same as the first standard technique, comprising 1 tie suture (solid and dotted blue lines) and 2 grip sutures (solid and dashed black lines), except that 1 tie suture is passed through the cross section of the stumps (solid and dotted green lines) using an AIS technique (C, F). (AIS, all-inside suture; I-O, inside-out.)

Surgical Technique

Hybrid Tie-Grip Suture Repair

The hybrid tie-grip suture repair technique is mainly used to repair isolated radial/oblique tears in the red-white or red-red zone in the middle segment of the lateral meniscus. It requires suture passers, such as the Micro SutureLasso (minor bend; Arthrex, Naples, FL), Knee Scorpion (Arthrex), or FIRSTPASS MINI (Smith & Nephew, Andover, MA), with No. 0 strong suture, such as FiberWire (Arthrex) or Hi-Fi suture (ConMed Linvatec, Largo, FL). A plastic cannula or PassPort cannula (Arthrex) is used if necessary. In this section, 2 standard hybrid tie-grip suture repair techniques are presented. In the first technique, all of tie sutures are performed with the AIS technique, and in the second, a single tie suture is used with the AIS technique for reduction of the stumps, followed by a regular tie-grip suture by the inside-out technique. The former procedure is shown in Figures 3 and 4, and the latter is shown in Figures 5 and 6.

Preparation

The operative extremity is prepared in the usual fashion for routine knee arthroscopy with the patient in the supine position. After arthroscopic evaluation using 2 standard anterior knee arthroscopy portals, meniscal repair is performed with an additional far anteromedial portal in the figure-4 leg-lock position.

Before meniscal repair, the lateral meniscus is carefully probed to assess the state of the damaged meniscus and the region of the tear. The sites of tears in the meniscus and adjacent synovium are refreshed with a rasp to promote healing with an adequate vascular supply.

Step 1: Tie Suture With AIS Repair Technique

Initially, 2 penetration points of the anterior and posterior stumps of the torn meniscus are carefully chosen at the inner one-third of the meniscus for reduction of these stumps. To avoid suture slippage or cutting of the stumps, these 2 points are at least 2 to 3 mm away from the edges. Then, the Micro SutureLasso is inserted into the joint through the anterolateral portal, vertically penetrating the anterior stump of the meniscus from the upper surface to the lower surface, without damaging the articular cartilage of tibial plateau (Figs 4B and 6A). After the first No. 0 strong suture is passed through the anterior stump using a shuttle-relay method, the same No. 0 suture is passed through the posterior stump using a suture passer similarly (Figs 4A and 6B). For suture passing through the posterior stump, the Knee Scorpion or FIRSTPASS MINI is easier to use than the Micro SutureLasso. There is no rule as to whether the suture should be passed through the anterior or posterior stump initially, but it is technically easier to have first suture for anterior stump. (The first repair technique in Video 1, the first suture is passed through the anterior stump initially and then is passed through the posterior stump.) Both edges of the first No. 0 strong suture are retrieved out of the joint from the anteromedial portal to avoid tangling. If necessary, a second No. 0 strong suture is passed through the outer one-third of the meniscus in the same manner as the first suture (Fig 4C).

Step 2: Grip Suture With Inside-Out Repair Technique

An additional standard skin incision for conventional inside-out meniscal repair, 3 to 4 cm long, is made along the posterior border of the lateral collateral ligament, with care taken not to damage the nerves.12 Two grip sutures through the anteromedial portal are placed on the anterior and posterior stumps, passing under the first and second sutures, under temporary reduction with a knot pusher (Fig 4D).

Step 3: Knot Tying

The first and second tie sutures are tied using a knot pusher through a plastic cannula placed in the anteromedial portal. During knot tying, the probe is placed between the anterior and posterior stumps to avoid overlap of these stumps (Figs 4E and 6C). Then, 2 grip sutures are tied with moderate tension because they act as a “rip stop” (Fig 4F).11 If necessary, 2 or 3 more tie sutures using the inside-out technique can be added (Fig 6E and F).

Additional Procedures

To promote healing, a fibrin clot is set into the tear site before tying of the tie sutures and/or microfracture of the lateral wall of the lateral femoral condyle is performed.

Modified Hybrid Tie-Grip Suture Repair

Because of the inevitable overlap of the stumps during the aforementioned hybrid tie-grip suture repair, a modified technique has been developed (Figs 7 and 8). Most of the procedures are the same as those for hybrid tie-grip suture repair; only the passing of second tie suture is different. In this modified technique, the second tie suture is passed through the cross section of the stumps (Fig 7B). This suture technique can prevent overlap of the stumps. Moreover, even in chronic cases, the cross sections of the anterior and posterior freshened stumps with trimming can be pressed firmly, which is thought to be advantageous for healing.

(B) An additional tie suture (solid and dotted green line) is passed through the cross section of the anterior and posterior stumps. The first tie suture is passed through the inner one-third of the meniscus in the same manner as the hybrid tie-grip suture technique (Fig 8A). The second tie suture is passed from the upper surface through the center of the cross section at the outer one-third of the meniscus as follows: Initially, the Micro Suture Lasso penetrates the center of the posterior cross section and relays a No. 0 strong suture passed from the upper surface to the cross section (Fig 8 B and C). Then, the edge of this No. 0 strong suture from the cross section of the posterior stump passes from the center of the cross section to the upper surface of the anterior stump using the Micro SutureLasso (Fig 8 D and E). Before these first and second tie sutures are tied, 2 grip sutures are passed by the inside-out technique.

Discussion

An isolated radial tear in young athletes is most common in the middle segment of the lateral meniscus.1 With this type of tear, it is difficult to achieve complete healing after repair and the vascular supply necessary for healing is often lacking. Thus, several repair techniques have been reported.4,9,13,14 Regarding AIS techniques, previous biomechanical studies have shown the superiority of AIS repair compared with inside-out repair.15, 16, 17, 18 Clinically, our comparative study showed clinical superiority after AIS repair compared with inside-out repair but observed a low healing rate on second-look arthroscopy after AIS repair.4 One of the reasons for this result is the lack of a vascular access channel in the AIS repair technique. Thus, the combination of inside-out and AIS repair techniques would have biological and biomechanical advantages for meniscal healing. Indeed, Hang et al.18 reported that hybrid repair was equivalent to AIS repair and found that these 2 repair techniques were biomechanically superior to inside-out repair. Moreover, when addressing radial tears, horizontal sutures could cause cutout of the meniscus; therefore, vertical mattress sutures should be added to act as a rip stop to prevent meniscal cutout.2,11,19 This suture technique was introduced as the “tie-grip suture” or “hash-tag” technique.2,9,19,20 We apply this technique using a combination of inside-out and AIS repair techniques (i.e., hybrid tie-grip suture repair technique). Our hybrid tie-grip suture repair technique has the advantages of inside-out and AIS techniques, as well as the tie-grip suture technique. Moreover, the modified hybrid tie-grip suture technique offers further advantages by preventing overlap of the stumps, and it can be used in chronic cases. However, there are some disadvantages to our techniques. In hybrid repair techniques, articular cartilage damage may be caused by the tip of the device (e.g., Micro SutureLasso) during penetration of the stumps or an intra-articular knot after repair,21 which are also disadvantages of AIS repair. Treatment of full radial tear in middle segment of lateral meniscus in left knee by 2 hybrid tie-grip suture repair techniques and a modified hybrid tie-grip suture repair technique (total of 3 techniques). In these techniques, all-inside suture repair is performed using suture passers (Micro SutureLasso or Knee Scorpion) and standard inside-out repair is performed using Macaroni sutures (Stryker Japan KK). The arthroscopic views presented were captured with the scope through the medial portal. The first repair technique shows a hybrid tie-grip suture repair. Initially, 2 tie sutures (No. 0 Hi-Fi strong sutures) are passed through the anterior and posterior stumps using a suture passer by an all-inside suture technique. The Knee Scorpion penetrates at the inner side of the posterior stump, and the Micro SutureLasso penetrates the anterior stump. Before knot tying, 2 grip sutures are applied using an inside-out technique. Then, 2 tie sutures are tied using a knot pusher. Finally, 2 grip sutures are tied with moderate tension to avoid over-tightening. The second repair technique shows another hybrid tie-grip suture repair. Via an all-inside suture technique, only 1 horizontal suture is passed through the inner one-third of the tear, using the Micro SutureLasso for the anterior stump and the Knee Scorpion for the posterior stump. This first all-inside horizontal suture is tied for reduction of the tear site. Then, 2 vertical mattress sutures are placed with an inside-out technique, and 3 more horizontal sutures are placed with an all-inside suture technique. The final repair technique shows a modified hybrid tie-grip suture repair. The initial tie suture is an all-inside horizontal suture and the second tie suture is an all-inside horizontal suture from the upper surface of the posterior stump through the cross section of the tear site to the upper surface of the anterior stump. Both sutures are passed using the Micro SutureLasso. Regarding the second tie suture, details are provided as follows. The Micro SutureLasso (with a bent tip) is inserted from the anterolateral portal and penetrates at the cross section of the posterior stump. After suture relay, a suture is passed from the upper surface to the cross section of the posterior stump. Then, the Micro SutureLasso is inserted from the upper surface to the cross section of the anterior stump again. The edge of suture from the cross section of the posterior stump is passed through the cross section to the upper surface of the anterior stump. Before knot tying, 2 grip sutures are applied using an inside-out technique. Regarding knot tying, the tie suture through the cross section of the stumps is tied tightly using a knot pusher initially because there is no risk of overlapping during knot tying of this suture. Then, the initial tie suture is tied carefully to avoid overlap of the stumps. Finally, 2 grip sutures are tied with moderate tension to avoid over-tightening. As shown, this technique can avoid overlap of the stumps.

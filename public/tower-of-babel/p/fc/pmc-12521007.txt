Rare case of leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata with hepatic involvement
Authors: Qingping Zhong, Binglin Lai
Published: 2025

A 45-year-old female was admitted with three-month history of right upper abdominal distension and occasional pain, without nausea or vomiting. With a background of chronic hepatitis B and myomectomy 5 years prior, laboratory tumor markers were normal. Computed tomography (CT) imaging (Figure 1A-1C) revealed multiple well-demarcated, uniformly dense, encapsulated soft tissue masses in the right hepatic lobe and left abdominal cavity, the largest measuring approximately 13.1 cm × 7.8 cm. Contrast-enhanced scanning demonstrated uneven but significant arterial phase enhancement with evidence of a major supplying artery, followed by gradual attenuation in portal venous and delayed phases, suggestive of a possible malignant mesenchymal tumor. A liver mass biopsy confirmed the pathological diagnosis of leiomyoma (Figure 1D). Leiomyomatosis peritonealis disseminata (LPD) is a rare condition characterized by benign but potentially metastatic leiomyomas. Primarily affecting reproductive-aged women (age range, 21–56 years, median 46), it commonly involves the peritoneum, pelvis, abdominal wall, intestines, mesentery, and omentum, with rare hepatic involvement. The exact etiology remains unclear, but may be associated with hormonal fluctuations, oral contraceptive use, genetic predisposition, and iatrogenic transmission. According to previous literature (1,2), LPD commonly occurs in the peritoneum and is frequently characterized by numerous small-sized lesions. Conversely, in the current clinical case, the liver is involved with relatively few lesions of larger dimensions, and the presence of multiple enhanced vessels within the tumor mass makes it vulnerable to misdiagnosis as either a mesenchymal-origin tumor or primary hepatocellular carcinoma.

Arterial phase axial (A) and coronal (B) images show two nearly round soft tissue masses in the right hepatic lobe (arrows) and one in the left abdomen (arrows). These masses show heterogeneous enhancement with large vessels. Venous phase sagittal (C) shows the mass’s enhancement (arrows) similar to hepatic parenchyma, with indistinct borders. Under HE staining (D), smooth muscle cells were observed, arranged in bundles with nuclei having rounded ends. Magnification: 100×. HE, hematoxylin and eosin.

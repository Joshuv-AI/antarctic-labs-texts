The prognostic significance of the fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio in neuroblastoma: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Jian Sun, Peng Lu, Yao Zhang, Chao Yang, Xiaobin Deng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to investigate the potential prognostic value of fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR) in patients with neuroblastoma (NB).MethodsWe conducted a retrospective cohort study with 257 patients with NB included as the training group, and 78 patients included as the validation group. The associations between NB and clinicopathological categorical variables based on FAR were assessed. Survival time and the corresponding survival curve were analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression models were utilized to identify potential prognostic factors. A prognostic nomogram was constructed. The predictive performance of the nomogram was evaluated using calibration curves.ResultsBased on the receiver operating characteristic analysis, the optimal cutoff value for FAR was found to be 0.0734. Both univariate and multivariate models demonstrated that FAR is a potential prognostic factor for overall survival in NB patients. Furthermore, the development of a nomogram based on the identified potential prognostic factors was predictive of the 3- and 5-year overall survival probabilities in both the training group and the validation group.ConclusionFAR, a routinely performed test known for its simplicity, objectivity, and cost-effectiveness, has emerged as a promising prognostic factor in NB. Its potential applicability in clinical practice holds great promise.Keywords: Fibrinogen, Neuroblastoma, Prognosis, Serum albumin, Survival rate

INTRODUCTION

Neuroblastoma (NB), derived from the sympathetic nervous system, encompass a diverse clinical spectrum, spanning from localized or spontaneously regressing lesions to extensively disseminated malignancies [1]. It is the most prevalent extracranial solid tumor in pediatric patients, exhibits a striking degree of biological and clinical diversity. Typically, a range of biomarkers, including vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) and neuron-specific enolase (NSE), have been utilized for the purpose of diagnosing and assessing the prognosis of NB. Regrettably, despite their implementation, the clinical efficacy of these biomarkers remains inadequate in achieving the desired outcomes. In recent years, the identification of reliable biomarkers for the prediction and treatment of NB has emerged as a persistent and significant research focus.

The promotion of inflammation and dysregulation of metabolic processes have gained widespread recognition as hallmarks of cancer [2]. The close association between cancer and the systemic inflammatory response, as well as the nutritional status of patients, has been extensively recognized. Notably, the hemostatic system, comprising crucial components such as thrombin, tissue factor, and fibrinogen, has been unequivocally implicated in neoangiogenesis and metastasis across diverse tumor types [3]. Cancer is known to induce the activation of blood coagulation, leading to the development of a hypercoagulable state or chronic disseminated intravascular coagulation in affected patients [3]. It is worth noting that an intriguing association exists between aberrations in hemostatic parameters and tumor progression. Approximately 50% of individuals afflicted with tumors and 95% of those with metastases exhibit such abnormalities [4]. Within this context, fibrinogen, a pro-inflammatory protein, plays a pivotal role. Its levels surge during the development of tumors, thereby facilitating platelet adhesion and binding to growth factors. This, in turn, expedites the absorption and metastasis of tumor cells [5]. Notably, fibrinogen has been implicated in the progression and prognosis of various malignancies, including upper urinary tract urothelial carcinoma, gastrointestinal stromal tumors, epithelial ovarian cancer, and others [6,7,8]. Similarly, plasma albumin, synthesized by the liver, performs various regulatory functions such as maintaining plasma oncotic pressure, solubilization, antioxidation, immunomodulation, and regulating capillary permeability, among others [9]. Additionally, it is commonly employed as an indicator of nutritional status. The concentration of serum albumin, influenced by both nutrition and inflammation, serves as a reliable indicator of disease status. Notably, in the context of tumors, albumin levels often exhibit a reduction, indicative of the wasting disease that accompanies micro-environmental inflammation.

Accumulating evidence has underscored the pivotal role of inflammation in the intricate processes of cancer development and progression. Consequently, there has been a growing interest in exploring novel biomarkers that can provide insights into both systemic inflammation and nutritional status, thereby aiding in the prediction of cancer progression and prognosis, such as fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio, and the modified Glasgow prognostic score. Among these biomarkers, FAR is a noteworthy contender, demonstrating its potential as a novel prognostic indicator in various tumor types [10,11,12,13]. A recent meta-analysis revealed the prognostic value of FAR in human malignancies; a significant association between elevated FAR levels and unfavorable clinical outcomes in cancer patients [14]. However, while FAR has been extensively investigated in adult malignancies, to the best of our knowledge, no reports have explored the correlation between FAR and cancer prognosis in the field of pediatric oncology. Consequently, this study aimed to elucidate the potential relationship between FAR and NB, shedding light on a previously unexplored avenue of research.

METHODS

Ethics statement

The study protocol received approval from the Ethics Review Committee of Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (No. 2024-103), and the requirement for informed consent was waived by the Committee due to the retrospective nature of the study design.

Study population

This retrospective study focused on patients diagnosed with NB at the Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (CHCMU) and Women’s and Children’s Hospital, Chongqing Medical University, from January 2014 to January 2021. Patients’ data from CHCMU were used as the training group, and the data from the Women’s and Children’s Hospital, Chongqing Medical University were used as the external validation group. Patients were included based on histological confirmation of NB. However, certain exclusion criteria were applied to ensure the integrity of the dataset. These criteria encompassed patients with incomplete clinical-pathological data, missing laboratory test results, incomplete follow-up data, a history of chronic inflammatory diseases such as inflammatory bowel disease, and recent acute infectious diseases.

Data collection

The present study obtained relevant data from the electronic medical record system, including information on sex, age at diagnosis, tumor size, clinical stage, risk stratification, tumor type, MYCN amplification status, as well as levels of 24-hour VMA and serum indicators (fibrinogen, albumin, CRP, D-dimer, NSE) measured at the time of diagnosis. To calculate FAR and NLR, the following formulas were applied, respectively: FAR = fibrinogen/albumin, NLR = neutrophil count/lymphocyte count. Survival data were collected primarily through telephone interviews and outpatient follow-up. The patients underwent routine follow-up every 3 months during the first year, every 6 months during the 2nd and 3rd years, and annually thereafter. Overall survival (OS) was chosen as the primary outcome measure, defined as the duration from the date of diagnosis to death or the end of the follow-up period (January 31, 2024). Since no universally accepted cutoff values have been established for FAR and NLR, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was utilized to determine the optimal cutoff value.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 26.0 (IBM Corp.), R software ver. 4.3.30 (The R Foundation), and GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software). To better assess the relationship between FAR, NLR and prognosis, the ROC curve analysis was employed to determine the optimal cutoff levels at baseline. The correlation between FAR, NLR, and various characteristics was assessed using the Pearson chi-square test or Fisher exact test. Survival curves were generated using the Kaplan-Meier method. Multivariate prognosis analysis was performed using a Cox regression model. Based on the results of the multivariate Cox regression analysis, a clinical nomogram for OS was developed. The predictive performance of the constructed nomogram was further evaluated using the consistency index (C-index). Bootstrapping internal validation was conducted to verify the multivariate Cox regression model. Time-dependent ROC curve of the model and calibration curves were employed to assess the performance of the developed nomogram. Further, using the surv_cutpoint() function from the R package survminer, the optimal cutoff value for the model was determined. Patients were divided into high-risk and low-risk groups according to the cutoff value, and the Kaplan-Meier curves were plotted to validate the model’s discriminative performance. Statistical significance was defined as a 2-sided P-value of ≤0.05.

RESULTS

Baseline characteristics of patients with neuroblastoma

During a 7-year period, 298 patients with NB were diagnosed in CHCMU. During the refinement process of our study cohort, 17 patients were excluded due to prior administration of chemotherapy. Furthermore, 15 patients opted to discontinue treatment after their diagnosis, and 9 patients had incomplete data. Consequently, a total of 257 patients were deemed eligible and included in the training group. Meanwhile, 78 patients were included in the validation group. The clinical characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1. All these are taken at baseline. According to the International Neuroblastoma Risk Group (INRG) staging system, 57.2% of the patients were classified as stage M at the time of diagnosis, and 63.4% were assigned to the high-risk group. MYCN amplification was detected in 22.6% of the cases. Abnormal levels of fibrinogen and albumin were observed in 29.2% and 19.1% of the cases, respectively.

Optimal cutoff values for fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio

Using calculating formulas, the values of FAR and NLR were obtained. The median, mean, and range of FAR and NLR were 0.0672, 0.0863, 0.01355, 0.5140 and 1.1925, 1.9265, 0.1086, 15.2454, respectively. By performing ROC curve analysis and calculating the area under the curve (AUC) for OS, we identified the optimal cutoff values for FAR and NLR (Fig. 1). The optimal cutoff value for FAR was determined to be 0.0734 (AUC, 0.782; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.722–0.842; P < 0.001; sensitivity, 78.6%; specificity, 80.0%). Similarly, the best cutoff value for NLR was found to be 1.3505 (AUC, 0.631; 95% CI, 0.562–0.700; P < 0.001; sensitivity, 64.3%; specificity, 65.5%).

Subsequently, utilizing the cutoff values of FAR and NLR, we categorized the patients into 2 groups: high-FAR (≥0.0734, n = 117, 45.5%) vs. low-FAR (<0.0734, n = 140, 54.5%) and high-NLR (≥1.3505, n = 122, 47.5%) vs. low-NLR (<1.3505, n = 135, 52.5%).

Relationship of preoperative fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio with clinicopathological variables

In patients with NB, preoperative FAR and NLR were significantly associated with various clinicopathological factors, as presented in Table 2. Specifically, FAR demonstrated a strong association with age, Shimada classification, MYCN status, INRG stage, risk group, CRP, albumin, fibrinogen, and survival status. However, no statistically significant correlations were observed between FAR and sex, tumor size, VMA, NSE, and D-dimer. Similarly, NLR also displayed strong associations with age, Shimada classification, INRG stage, risk group, CRP, fibrinogen, and survival status. No statistically significant correlations were observed between NLR and sex, tumor size, MYCN status, VMA, NSE, and D-dimer.

The univariate and multivariate survival analyses

Table 3 presents the hazard ratio (HR) for mortality associated with clinical-pathological characteristics. In the univariate analysis, several factors were found to be significantly associated with poor OS. These included age ≥18 months (HR, 2.64; P < 0.001), unfavorable histology of Shimada classification (HR, 2.214; P < 0.001), M stage (HR, 4.803; P < 0.001), high-risk stratification (HR, 4.220; P = 0.001), MYCN amplification (HR, 5.181; P < 0.001), abnormal NSE (HR, 2.384; P = 0.011), abnormal CRP (HR, 2.543; P < 0.001), low albumin (HR, 2.329; P < 0.001), high fibrinogen (HR, 4.194; P < 0.001), NLR ≥1.3505 (HR, 2.305; P < 0.001), and FAR ≥0.0734 (HR, 6.711; P < 0.001).

Creation of a nomogram for predicting the overall survival of neuroblastoma

After obtaining the results of multivariate analysis, a novel nomogram incorporating significant risk factors was created (Fig. 3). This predictive model assigned a specific weight to each risk factor, with the total score reflecting the patient’s predicted 3-year and 5-year OS rates. Each variable in the column line graph was assigned a weighted score, which was summed to derive the individualized total score for each patient. A higher total score indicated a lower probability of survival at both the 3-year and 5-year mark. The C-index for evaluating OS using the comprehensive nomogram was 0.832 (95% CI, 0.795–0.869), indicating its strong predictive accuracy. Using bootstrapping internal validation, the C-index of the multivariate Cox regression model was found to be 0.7974, with a 95% CI of 0.7573–0.8322.

Fibrinogen-to-albumin ratio (FAR)-based nomogram for predicting overall survival.

Validation of the nomogram

To assess the validity of the predictive model, ROC curves were generated for both the training and validation sets. The results of the time-dependent ROC curve indicate a high identification accuracy for both the 3-year and 5-year periods in both training and validation sets (Fig. 4A, D). Additionally, calibration curves were employed to assess the performance of the developed nomogram, demonstrating excellent consistency between the predicted OS by the nomogram and the observed OS (Fig. 4B, E). Using this cutoff value, we categorized patients into high-risk and low-risk groups and plotted Kaplan-Meier curves for both groups. The results revealed significant differences in survival curves between the 2 groups, with a P-value less than 0.001(Fig. 4C, F).

DISCUSSION

In our current investigation, we discovered that FAR exhibited significant associations not only with various clinical features such as age, Shimada classification, MYCN status, INRG stage, risk group, CRP, albumin, fibrinogen, and survival status but also served as an independent prognostic factor for OS in patients with NB. Our findings indicated that FAR value ≥0.0734, age ≥18 months, MYCN amplification, and the presence of metastatic disease were independent prognostic factors associated with a poorer OS in the survival of NB patients. Furthermore, we developed and validated a preliminary clinical nomogram model that incorporated FAR, age, MYCN status, and metastatic status to assist clinicians in making informed clinical decisions. Bootstrap internal validation was used to validate the C-index and its 95% CI of the multivariable Cox regression model. The model’s performance was further assessed through time-dependent ROC curves and calibration curves. Additionally, by determining the optimal cutoff value of the model and stratifying the patients into high and low-risk groups based on the cutoff value, the discriminative ability of the model was confirmed using the Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests. Furthermore, the prediction model was validated in an external dataset. All these validations collectively demonstrated the effectiveness and reliability of the model. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the potential relationship between FAR and survival of NB patients.

In recent years, there has been growing interest in exploring the significance of serum markers in predicting cancer survival. This surge in interest can be attributed to the ease of accessibility and cost-effectiveness of these serum indicators. Several studies have identified several serum markers associated with NB prognosis, such as D-dimer [15], lactate dehydrogenase [16], ferritin [17], and albumin [18]. Nonetheless, it is important to acknowledge that predictive values based on a single or 2 indicators can be influenced by various factors, including other diseases or physiological conditions, which can introduce bias into predictions. For instance, elevated levels of fibrinogen are frequently observed during infections or inflammatory responses, and advanced tumors often trigger an inflammatory response. However, it is crucial to note that elevated fibrinogen levels could also be indicative of other inflammatory conditions unrelated to NB. Similarly, albumin, when combined with malnutrition, is associated with malignancy. However, it also serves as a predictor of impaired liver synthesis and is linked to liver-related diseases. Consequently, composite indicators such as NLR and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) have been developed and demonstrated associations with the prognosis of NB patients [17].

Increasing evidence suggests that patients with cancer commonly experience a hypercoagulable state, inflammation, and malnutrition, all of which are intricately interconnected with cancer initiation, progression, metastasis, and chemotherapy resistance [19]. Recent studies have revealed an association between plasma albumin concentration, inflammation, and tumorigenesis [20,21]. As the most abundant protein circulating in plasma, albumin not only reflects the body’s nutritional status but also actively participates in systemic inflammatory responses. Studies have demonstrated that decreased levels of albumin and prealbumin contribute to compromised nutritional status and impaired immune function in patients, consequently promoting tumor advancement [22]. Furthermore, regardless of the presence of malnutrition, individuals with locally advanced or metastatic malignancies exhibit reduced serum albumin levels [23]. In these patients, low albumin levels exert an adverse impact on the efficacy of anticancer therapies [24]. Additionally, albumin levels have been correlated with poor prognosis in cancer cases, primarily due to malnutrition and postoperative complications [25].

Fibrinogen is a pivotal mediator of inflammation and plays a multifaceted role in tumor biology. It not only promotes tumor angiogenesis and metastasis through direct interactions with endothelial cells but also indirectly influences other regulators of angiogenesis [26]. Furthermore, fibrinogen amplifies tumor cell invasion and metastasis by activating epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition signaling [27]. Intriguingly, research has revealed that fibrinogen assumes a cytoskeletal role within the tumor extracellular matrix, shielding tumor cells from immune cell-mediated eradication [28,29].

While it is widely acknowledged that disturbances in albumin/prealbumin and fibrinogen levels serve as significant prognostic indicators in cancer patients, it is important to recognize that not all individuals with cancer exhibit deficiencies in albumin/prealbumin or excess fibrinogen. In some cases, patients may present solely with albumin/prealbumin deficiency or fibrinogen overabundance. To account for this complexity, we employed FAR, which provides a comprehensive assessment of both albumin and fibrinogen levels. By incorporating FAR into our analysis, we are not only able to capture the systemic inflammatory response but also gain insights into the coagulation and nutritional status of patients [30]. Existing evidence strongly supports the use of FAR as a reliable predictor in various cancers [10,11,12,13]. In our study, we have obtained research results similar to previous studies, indicating that FAR is an independent poor prognostic factor in patients with NB. Unlike pathological and biological biomarkers including MYCN, anaplastic lymphoma kinase, DNA ploidy, and chromosome abnormalities, the detection of hematological markers is convenient, affordable, and relatively innocuous, as serum albumin and fibrinogen are available in the medical records of most cancer patients. Thus, the use of FAR as a prognostic marker for NB has great potential. These results may help to guide future treatments by identifying sub-populations with different prognoses. Combined with other hematological prognostic markers, such as NLR and SII, even the constructed prognostic models based on these markers, we can evaluate NB prognosis more accurately.

However, it is essential to emphasize that the numerical values of FAR do not directly affect the survival outcomes of patients with cancer. Rather, it is the underlying proteins, albumin and fibrinogen, along with the biological processes that contribute to FAR, that are responsible for this association.

It is important to acknowledge the limitations of our research. First, the data collection process was retrospective, which introduced potential information bias. Second, our study focused on NB patients from 2 institutions, and the number of patients included in the study was limited, which limits the generalizability of our findings. To establish more robust conclusions, it is imperative to conduct multicenter longitudinal studies. These studies would provide further validation for the significant findings observed in the present study. Third, the assessment of FAR was limited to the first 24 hours of admission, and we did not evaluate the relationship between subsequent FAR measurements and prognosis. This baseline assessment approach may increase the risk of classification bias. Additionally, since FAR was only evaluated before surgery, the potential impact of the postoperative course on its prognostic value remains unclear and warrants further investigation.

In conclusion, the findings of our study underscore the significant association between FAR and the survival and prognosis of NB. FAR is a robust and independent factor, playing a pivotal role in identifying high-risk patients and informing precise treatment strategies. Moreover, FAR exhibits several favorable attributes, including its simplicity, objectivity, and cost-effectiveness, making it a promising biomarker for preoperative clinical evaluations. Therefore, the potential application of FAR in routine clinical practice holds considerable promise.

Hybrid robotic thoracic and laparoscopic abdominal approach for complex diaphragmatic hernia repair
Authors: Andres Obeso, Daniel Guerron, Shair Ahmed, Yusuf Bayrak, Sergio Mainero, Juan Pablo Pantoja, Usman Ahmad
Published: 2026

Robotic thoracic and laparoscopic abdominal dual-view during hybrid repair.

Central Message

A hybrid robotic thoracic and laparoscopic abdominal approach enables safe reduction and repair of complex diaphragmatic hernias with dense adhesions, improving surgical control and safety.

A hybrid thoracic and abdominal approach can be critical for the safe and effective management of complex diaphragmatic hernias, particularly in patients with previous abdominal surgery and dense adhesions. We present a case demonstrating the technical advantages of combining a robotic thoracic approach with laparoscopic abdominal assistance.

In this video case, we report a 47-year-old male patient with a history of previous laparotomy for right hepatic lobe resection due to giant hemangioma who presented with abdominal pain, bloating, and altered bowel habits. Computed tomography scan of the abdomen (Figure 1) revealed a 4.2-cm posterior diaphragmatic defect with herniation of colonic loops into the thoracic cavity. Diaphragm function was preserved. Given the complexity of the hernia and anticipated adhesions, a hybrid approach was planned.

Axial and coronal computed tomography images demonstrate a diaphragmatic hernia with herniation of the colon into the thoracic cavity.

The procedure (Video 1) was performed with the patient under general anesthesia with double-lumen endotracheal intubation and nasogastric tube placement for gastric decompression. The patient had been admitted the day before bowel preparation. The patient was positioned in lateral decubitus. A robotic thoracic approach was initiated using three 8-mm ports and one assistant port. Carbon dioxide was insufflated. Adhesiolysis was performed releasing pleuropulmonary and diaphragmatic adhesions. The diaphragmatic hernia was circumferentially dissected, and the hernia ring was fully exposed. However, reduction of the herniated colon was not feasible because of dense intra-abdominal adhesions.

A laparoscopic abdominal approach was then initiated without repositioning the patient. Three 5-mm ports were placed, allowing simultaneous thoracic and abdominal visualization. Exposure was achieved with patient positioning (gravity-assisted medialization of the liver) and gentle retraction using laparoscopic instruments. Previous right hepatic lobe resection facilitated visualization of the posterior diaphragm. This dual-view setup (Figure 2) enabled coordinated dissection and real-time communication between teams. Adhesiolysis was completed from the abdominal side while thoracic instruments provided countertraction. This synchronized maneuver allowed safe and controlled reduction of the colon into the abdominal cavity.

Both the robotic thoracic and laparoscopic abdominal approaches are shown. The image displays the dual screens corresponding to the thoracic and abdominal views.

The diaphragmatic defect was closed primarily with interrupted nonabsorbable sutures (ETHIBOND Excel 0; Ethicon) reinforced with a single pledget on one side. A second reinforcing layer using a running nonabsorbable barbed suture (V-Loc PBT 2-0; Medtronic) was added. Dual visualization was particularly valuable during closure, especially given the proximity of the liver, ensuring accurate suture placement and avoidance of visceral injury.

This case highlights the importance of a hybrid thoracic and abdominal approach in complex diaphragmatic hernia repair. Several cases of minimally invasive diaphragmatic hernia repair have been previously reported in the literature1, 2, 3, 4; however, to our knowledge, this is the first description of a hybrid approach incorporating a robotic thoracic component. Although it requires coordination between surgical teams, this strategy enhances safety and may reduce complications in selected patients.

Ethical approval was not required for this work according to institutional policies because it is a case report with anonymized data. Informed written consent was obtained from the patient for publication of clinical data and associated images.

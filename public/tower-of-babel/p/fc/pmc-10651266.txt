Utilization, surgical populations, centers, coverages, regional balance, and their influential factors of deep brain stimulation for Parkinson’s disease: a large-scale multicenter cross-sectional study from 1997 to 2021
Authors: Fangang Meng, Wei Hu, Shu Wang, Joseph Tam, Yuan Gao, Xian Lun Zhu, Danny Tat Ming Chan, Wai Sang Poon, Tak Lap Poon, Fung Ching Cheung, Benedict Beng Teck Taw, Lai Fung Li, Shin Yuan Chen, Ka Ming Chan, Anxin Wang, Qin Xu, Chunlei Han, Yutong Bai, Aparna Wagle Shukla, Adolfo Ramirez-Zamora, Andres M. Lozano, Jianguo Zhang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Background:Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is an emerging and effective therapy for Parkinson’s disease (PD). However, little is known about its utilization, surgical populations, centers, coverages, regional balance, and influential factors.Materials and methods:This large-scale multicenter cross-sectional study was conducted using a national census involving 74 Chinese centers. National DBS populations and centers for PD were investigated in 1997–2021, and regional sociodemographic features, surgical populations, related resources, and insurance policies in 2020 were explored.Results:Since the first DBS surgery in 1997, a total of 38 122 PD patients from 349 centers underwent DBS by 2021, which covered 1.118% (1.108–1.129) of patients and 0.954% (0.933–0.976) of centers. Significant upward trends in the annual surgical population and coverages were observed with rapid climbing rates, while the annual surgical centers and their coverage showed two growth peaks in 2002–2006 and 2010–2018, correlating with clinical approvals and new technologies. A total of 103 070 (51 165–154 975) PD patients [2.088% (1.351–2.825) coverage] and 603 (72–1134) centers [1.356% (1.126–1.586) coverage] are predicted to conduct DBS by 2030. The new remotely programmed DBS technology was recoded as the first application in 2015 and rapidly increased to 2771 (47.39%, 46.11–48.67) patients with 10 507 remote programming sessions annually in 2021. Provinces in the eastern and central regions had better economic status, more surgical patients, higher insurance affordability, and more related resources than those in the western and northeastern regions. Higher gross domestic product per capita (β=5.041, 3.324–6.758 and β=0.008, 0.004–0.012; all P<0.001) and more functional neurosurgery doctors (β=3.596, 0.353–6.839; P=0.031 and β=0.010, 0.002–0.017; P=0.013) positively influenced surgical populations and coverages, while higher insurance levels (β=128.888, 64.702–193.075; P<0.001) positively influenced surgical coverages.Conclusion:Although surgical populations, centers, and coverages of DBS for PD have rapidly improved and are predicted to show future increases, this is still insufficient to cover potential eligible patients. Regionally imbalanced health coverage should be given attention to promote coordinated development.

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a chronic progressive neurological disorder and has become the second most common neurodegenerative disease1, affecting ~8.5 million individuals worldwide2. The number of people with PD and the resulting disabilities and deaths are rising rapidly, causing increasing global burdens3,4. Levodopa and other dopaminergic medications are essential treatments for PD, but many patients experience motor fluctuations and increased complications with disease progression5. Surgical treatments, especially deep brain stimulation (DBS), are widely recognized as effective and safe for these patients6. Thus, an increasing number of PD patients have undergone DBS as a therapy in recent years, which results in substantially better improvement in symptoms and quality of life than medical management alone7,8. However, access to DBS might be limited for many patients, which could be due to its costs, a shortage of facilities, and/or a lack of expertise9. Only a few single-center regional studies have focused on this topic10,11. A comprehensive study on the current utilization, coverage, and equity of DBS for PD is needed to promote more health resources and access with better universal health coverage.

It is estimated that nearly 3 million elderly people are currently affected by PD in China, which has the highest number of PD patients worldwide12,13. In addition, increasing longevity is the most important factor that contributes to the number of PD patients3,4. The trend of aging will increase among Chinese people as the second baby boomers (those born between 1962 and 1975) start to enter retirement14,15, further increasing the number of PD patients12. It is expected that 5 million people in China will suffer from PD by 2030, which represents more than half of all PD patients worldwide12,16. To gain a better understanding of the comprehensive situation of DBS therapy for PD, we conducted the multicenter cross-sectional DBS for PD Chinese Collaboration (DBS-PDCC) study by 74 representative centers from all Chinese areas, including mainland China, Hong Kong SAR, Macao SAR, and Taiwan, and obtained important guidance from international specialists and societies using the national census method to analyze surgical populations, centers, coverage, trends, regional differences, and influential factors of DBS for PD during 1997–2021. This might be an important reference for health care providers, researchers, PD patients, policymakers, health program managers and planners, and other stakeholders, especially for surgical research, education, and clinical practice in responding to the initiative from the WHO technical brief 2022 stating that ‘an urgent public health response that is necessary to meet the health and social requirements of people with PD’3,9.

Materials and methods

Data source and research subjects

This was a nationwide multicenter cross-sectional study conducted from June 2018 to June 2022. Supplementary Table S1 (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A773) provides information on the collaborating centers and participating investigators. Expert statisticians and methodologists participated in the study’s design and investigative procedures. They reviewed and approved all statistical analyses of this study. The ethics committee of Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University, approved the study (KY2021-159-01). In addition, an authoritative third party, such as the China National Clinical Research Center participated in the supervision of study design, conduct, and reporting. This study was carried out according to the principles of the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments, reported following the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement17 (Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A774) and the Strengthening the reporting of cohort, cross-sectional and case-control studies in surgery (STROCSS) guideline (Supplemental Digital Content 3, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A775 18) (Supplementary Table S2, Supplemental Digital Content 4, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A776) and has been registered on Clinicaltrials.gov with the registered number NCT02251808.

Through support from the Chinese Neuromodulation Society, a total of 74 representative centers contributed to this study and established the study database. Deidentified data were also collected for all manufacturers of DBS equipment utilized in China. In addition, supplementary data were collected from publicly available databases, including national and local health commissions, bureaus of statistics and governments, as well as the online database (ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2019) of the Global Burden of Diseases, Injuries, and Risk Factors (GBD) study 20192. The data sources are free and open access, and further approval was not required for the use of existing data. The methodologies, descriptions, and datasets are further detailed on the organizations’ websites.

Investigative procedures

The DBS-PDCC study protocol was previously established and repeatedly discussed by international senior experts in the fields of neurosurgery, neurology, epidemiology, methodology, and public health from June 2018 to July 2018. In addition, the study protocol (Supplementary Table S3, Supplemental Digital Content 5, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A777) was proposed, reviewed, and approved by the participating centers at the 2018 Chinese Neuromodulation Society conference. Quality control was performed at each process of the investigative procedure by the study committee, including the sampling procedure, survey design, data retrieval and cross-check, and data analysis.

The sampling procedure was conducted using a national census with a three-level stratified strategy. First, one major responsible center in each province was selected by the study committee to manage census surveys in local provinces. Then, these centers recruited additional local collaborative centers for data collection. A total of 74 centers were ultimately involved in the DBS-PDCC study. Figure 1 provides a sketch map of the participating centers. After that, responsible centers were required to upload provincial data to the DBS-PDCC database to generate regional data. The National Economic Population Division (NEPD) established by the National Bureau of Statistics was called on to stratify four Chinese regions (eastern, central, western, and northwestern) based on geo-sociodemographic characteristics19–21. Finally, national census data were summarized based on these provincial and regional data in the DBS-PDCC database. Provincial, regional, and national data were checked and summarized by data specialists. To ascertain the accuracy of the input data, at least one investigator from each responsible center reviewed their center’s datasets.

The designed survey was conducted in two phases. In phase I (conducted from June 2021 to December 2021), the main purpose was to explore regional coverage, equity, and influential factors of DBS for PD. Thus, responsible centers collected provincial data on the number of PD patients who underwent DBS, the numbers of neurosurgery and functional neurosurgery departments, beds, and doctors, and insurance policies regarding the affordability of DBS equipment for PD in 2020. The surveyed insurance policy of mainland China concerned the Basic Medical Insurance Program provided by health commissions, which was classified as follows: Level 1, no insurance coverage; Level 2, less than 50% coverage; and Level 3, greater than or equal to 50% coverage. Phase I was conducted in mainland China, while data from the Hong Kong SAR, Macao SAR, and Taiwan were not included because of their differences from the mainland (e.g. the insurance system) and a lack of major data. In Phase II (conducted from January 2022 to June 2022), the main purpose was to investigate the national utilization and coverage rates of DBS for PD. Surgical populations, centers, and the new DBS remote programming technology and its programming times for PD patients were surveyed yearly from 1997 (the beginning of DBS application for PD in China) to 2021. Phase II was completed in all areas, including Hong Kong SAR, Macao SAR, and Taiwan, for full national data. At least one principal investigator from each responsible center completed the required online training regarding the study’s purpose, survey method and content, and management and uploading of data. Investigators conducted face-to-face or online interviews to collect the required data from all neurosurgery departments in the local provinces for which they were responsible. To improve accuracy, the study committee also collected data for cross-checking and inputting data from multiple resources through the following measures: having conversations with the board members of the Chinese Neuromodulation Society for the history of DBS development; searching available records for neurosurgery departments, beds and doctors and insurance policies from health commissions; and collecting data on surgical populations from DBS equipment manufacturers. If unverified or inconsistent results were found, the survey was sent back to the corresponding center for onsite reinvestigation and verification under the supervision of the study committee.

In addition, the study committee synchronously retrieved supplementary data for analyses, including annual data on provincial and national populations and gross domestic product (GDP) from the seventh (latest) National Population Census 202021 and annual data on the national population and hospital numbers from the National Bureau of Statistics22 and the National Government23 from 1997 to 2021. To estimate the total national number of PD patients, the GBD 2019 database24 was queried for the prevalence of PD in China from 1997 to 2019. The 2020 and 2021 prevalence rates were estimated based on the PD prevalence percentage in China [1.37% for people ≥60 years of age from the Prevention and Intervention on Neurodegenerative Disease for the Elderly in China (PINDEC) study13,16] and populations of corresponding ages22. These supplementary data were collected in September 2022. Final data quality control checks were performed by the data specialists for all surveyed and supplementary data, including a logic check, a sequential recording check, and a random sampling reinterview check. The statisticians and methodologists were responsible for the maintenance of the database and data analysis. The investigative procedure and data sources are shown in the flowchart in Figure 1.

Measures and calculations

Surgical populations and centers were evaluated for people who underwent DBS and centers that performed this procedure for people with PD. Annual and cumulative measures at the national level for 1997–2021 are provided by year; these measures were analyzed for trends by using 5-year intervals (1997–2001, 2002–2006, 2007–2011, 2012–2016, and 2017–2021). Provincial-level and regional-level estimates of regional differences in 2020 are also provided.

Surgical population and center estimated coverage proportions (ECPs) were designed to reflect estimated coverage measures in reflecting the proportions of PD patients and centers that have been covered with DBS, that is what percentage of PD patients and hospitals have received/provided DBS surgeries. ECPs were the surgical populations or centers/total number of patients or centers at corresponding time points or periods in corresponding areas. The ECP provided estimates of surgical populations and centers to reflect health coverage and equity25.

Average annual changes (AVCs) were estimated trend estimates of surgical populations, centers, and ECPs, which were calculated by univariable linear regression that included the calendar year as a continuous independent variable and entered inspected values as the dependent variable. The β-value was interpreted as the AVC, and its P-value was regarded as P trend 26.

Predicted values were predicted estimates of surgical populations, centers, and ECPs using algorithms to forecast possible values until 2030. Predicted values were calculated based on existing historical values and reflected a trend continuation of the historical values (1997–2021), which might facilitate decision-making27,28.

Sociodemographic features included GDP, population, and GDP per capita; the insurance policy for DBS equipment is mentioned above; and the (functional) neurosurgery development status included the measured numbers of departments, beds, and doctors. These measures were utilized to identify potential influential factors of regional differences in surgical populations and their ECPs based on 2020 annual data.

Statistical analysis

The analysis of differences among continuous and categorical variables from multiple samples was performed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by least square difference (LSD) multiple comparisons and Fisher’s exact test (Fisher–Freeman–Halton method) and multiple comparisons with Bonferroni corrections. Univariable linear regression was used to explore linear trends, and the β-value (AVC) and P-value (P trend ) are provided26. Predicted values until 2030 were forecasted using the Holt-Winters exponential smoothing (ETS) time-series triple forecasting algorithm (A.A.A) based on an equation that accounted for additive errors, additive trends, and additive seasonality, which automatically filtered the best-fitting training model according to the 1997–2021 observations27,28. Furthermore, Pearson’s correlation coefficients were applied to explore correlations among variables. Multivariable linear regression was used to identify influential factors of DBS populations and ECPs. Potential factors that were significant in the univariable analysis were then entered into the multivariable analyses using backward elimination, and their β-values and 95% CIs are given.

In the present study, all statistical tests were two-tailed, and a P-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses and figures were performed and generated using R (version 4.2.2; R Foundation), the packages ‘forecast’ version 8.19, ‘corrplot’ version 0.92, and ‘ggplot2’ version 3.4.0; SPSS Statistics (version 27.0.1; IBM) and Power BI (version 2.112; Microsoft).

Results

Surgical populations, centers, coverage, and trends measured in 1997–2021

A total of 74 collaborating centers from all 34 provinces and 4 regions in China participated in the DBS-PDCC study. The first DBS procedure for PD in China was performed in 1997. After 25 years of development, the annual surgical population increased to 5847 in 2021, and 38 122 patients underwent DBS from 1997 to 2021. The ECPs of the surgical population reached 0.172% (95% CI: 0.168–0.176) and 1.118% (1.108–1.129) in annual and cumulative coverage, respectively. For the 5-year intervals of 1997–2021, the average annual changes were 22 (13–31), 24 (−4–53), 196 (135–257), 453 (347–559), and 568 (374–763) for the annual rates of surgical populations. Significant upward trends in the annual surgical population rate were observed in 1997–2001, 2007–2011, 2012–2016, and 2017–2021 (P trend =0.005, P trend =0.002, P trend <0.001, and P trend =0.003; all univariable linear regressions) and the annual population ECPs in 1997–2001, 2007–2011, and 2012–2016 (P trend =0.004, P trend =0.001, and P trend <0.001; all univariable linear regressions). The annual and cumulative numbers of surgical centers and their ECPs increased to 12 and 349 and 0.033% (0.015–0.051) and 0.954% (0.933–0.976), respectively. Significant upward trends were observed in 2012–2016 in the number of annual surgical centers (P trend =0.047, univariable linear regression), while downward trends were observed in 2017–2021 in the number of annual surgical centers and their ECPs (P trend =0.019 and P trend =0.014, univariable linear regression). Analysis of surgical populations, centers, coverage, and trends in 5-year intervals are shown in Table 1, and detailed values by year are shown in Supplementary Table S4 (Supplemental Digital Content 6, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A778).

Future surgical populations, centers, and coverage are predicted until 2030

The predicted values are plotted in Figure 2, showing fluctuating but generally rising trends. The SMAPEs of the predicted values of annual and cumulative surgical populations, centers, and their ECPs were 6.672, 9.236, 9.524, 6.802, 8.612, 3.675, 9.0, and 5.437%, respectively, which all fell under the category of highly accurate forecasts (≤10%)28. A total of 103 070 (51 165–154 975) patients with an ECP of 2.088% (1.351–2.825) and 603 (72–1134) centers with an ECP of 1.356% (1.126–1.586) would undergo DBS for PD by 2030. The 2030 annual surgical populations, centers, and their ECPs would reach 8962 (4741–131 83) and 34 (8–59) and 0.235% (0.137–0.333) and 0.119% (0.034–0.204), respectively, with the latest 5-year interval (2025–2030) having significantly increased surgical populations, centers, and ECPs (AVC=345, 340–349; P trend <0.001; AVC=5, 3–8; P trend =0.003; AVC=0.006, 0.003–0.008; P trend =0.004; and AVC=0.021, 0.010–0.030; P trend =0.005; all univariable linear regressions).

Regional differences and features in 2020

We compared regional differences in sociodemographic features, DBS for PD, and neurosurgery and functional neurosurgery development status per province in 2020 (Table 2 and Fig. 3). Supplementary Table S6 (Supplemental Digital Content 10, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A782) provides summarized regional data. In terms of sociodemographic features, provinces in the eastern region had significantly higher GDPs than those in the western and northwestern regions (P=0.001 and P=0.024, all ANOVA and LSD comparisons), and the eastern region also had a significantly higher GDP per capita than all the other regions (P=0.003 vs. central, P<0.001 vs. western, and P=0.024 vs. northwestern; all ANOVA and LSD comparisons), while no significant difference was found in the populations. Regarding DBS for PD, provinces in the eastern region had significantly higher numbers of surgical populations and ECPs than those in the western region (P=0.010 and P=0.010, all ANOVA and LSD comparisons). Most of the provinces in the western area had annual surgical populations of less than 10, while those in the eastern area had surgical populations of more than 100. Provinces in the eastern region had the highest percentage of people with Level 3 insurance at 20%, while 75% of provinces in the western region had Level 1 insurance, which means no insurance coverage for DBS for PD; however, the difference in percentages was not significant. Regarding the development status of DBS-related resources, provinces in the central region had the most neurosurgery departments, beds, and doctors, which were all significantly more than those in the western region (P=0.031, P=0.048, and P=0.012, all ANOVA and LSD comparisons), and provinces in the eastern region also had more neurosurgery doctors than those in the western region (P=0.016, ANOVA and LSD comparisons). Provinces in the eastern region had the most functional neurosurgery departments, beds, and doctors; the eastern region had significantly more functional neurosurgery beds than the western region (P=0.003, ANOVA and LSD comparisons), and the eastern region had significantly more functional neurosurgery doctors than the western region (P<0.001, ANOVA and LSD comparisons) and northwestern region (P=0.023, ANOVA and LSD comparisons).

Influential factors of regional differences in surgical populations and coverage

To identify potential factors influencing regional differences in surgical populations and their ECPs, correlation analyses (Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table S7, Supplemental Digital Content 11, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A783) and multivariable linear regressions (Supplementary Table S8, Supplemental Digital Content 12, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A784 and Supplementary Fig. S3, Supplemental Digital Content 13, http://links.lww.com/JS9/A785) were performed. In these analyses, levels of insurance were regarded as ordinal categorical variables to explore the influences of different levels. GDP, GDP per capita, neurosurgery doctors, functional neurosurgery departments, beds and doctors, and insurance levels were positively correlated with surgical populations (r=0.430, P=0.016; r=0.827, P<0.001; r=0.454, P=0.010; r=0.391, P=0.029; r=0.559, P=0.001; r=0.695, P<0.001; and r=0.514, P=0.003; all Spearman’s correlations), while surgical population ECPs were positively correlated with GDP per capita and functional neurosurgery beds and doctors (r=0.765, P<0.001; r=0.448, P=0.011; and r=0.655, P<0.001; all Spearman’s correlations). Multivariable linear regressions revealed that a higher GDP per capita (β=5.041, 3.324–6.758; P<0.001), more functional neurosurgery doctors (β=3.596, 0.353–6.839; P=0.031), and higher insurance levels (β=128.888, 64.702–193.075; P<0.001) were positive influential factors of surgical populations, while a higher GDP per capita (β=0.008, 0.004–0.012; P<0.001) and more functional neurosurgery doctors (β=0.010, 0.002–0.017; P=0.013) were positive influential factors of surgical population ECPs.

Discussion

Surgical populations, centers, coverage, trends, and predictions

A WHO Health Workforce 2030 strategic priority and one crucial target of the UN Sustainable Development Goals is to achieve universal health coverage, which means ensuring that all people and communities can receive the health services they need, of sufficient quality to be effective, without experiencing financial hardship30. To achieve universal health coverage, cross-sectional surveys from major areas are required for essential clinical and healthcare practice information31–33. With increasing longevity and the number of PD patients globally, we conducted this study with the aim of exploring the coverage and equity of minimally invasive and effective DBS surgical treatment, which can not only gather evidence-based data in effectively formulating and regulating medical and health care policies but may also accelerate the development of the discipline34–36. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first and largest multicenter study to provide a comprehensive assessment of DBS for PD around the world.

It is generally acknowledged that Benabid et al. 37 initially introduced DBS for PD for clinical utilization in 1987. The rapid development of DBS has revolutionized the treatment of several central nervous system diseases38,39, especially PD and other movement disorders7,39,40. With many encouraging results from large-scale randomized trials suggesting that DBS was more effective than the best medical therapy for PD with high levels of safety6,41,42, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved DBS surgery in 1997 to treat PD tremors, in 2002 to treat advanced PD symptoms, and in 2016 for the earlier stages of PD42. Regarding Chinese use, the first DBS procedure for PD was performed at a center in Hong Kong SAR in 1997, with two centers in mainland China and one center in Taiwan performing the procedure in 1998. These early attempts initiated the clinical utilization of DBS for PD in China. After 25 years of development, 38 122 PD patients had undergone DBS at 349 centers by 2021, which covered 1.118% of all PD patients and 0.954% of hospitals. Kestenbaum et al. 10 reviewed records from a Columbia medical center between 2007 and 2013 and found that 1.379% (63 of 4568) of PD patients underwent DBS surgery, a surgical proportion higher than that in this study. However, their hospital-based study was more likely to include patients with severe symptoms, contributing to a higher surgical treatment proportion, while our study results were based on the surgical coverage of the total number of PD patients, which was probably more representative. In addition, a previous study by Morgante et al. 43 revealed that 1.6 and 4.5% of PD patients met strict and flexible DBS criteria based on the Core Assessment Program for Surgical Interventional Therapies in PD (CAPSIT) criteria. The CAPSIT selection criteria were developed in 1999, while subsequent studies, especially studies on DBS utilization among advanced41 and early-stage PD patients42, might further expand the proportion of eligible patients. Thus, although surgical populations, centers, and coverage of DBS for PD have considerably improved, they are still insufficient to cover potential eligible patients, requiring further development and attention. As there could be many waiting lines for the DBS procedure, future directions of this field can be exploring the surgical speed, access, and acceptance of different regions and potential migration due to cross-sectional surgeries.

From 1997 to 2021, the annual surgical population and its coverage showed significant upward trends, with rapidly increasing rates in nearly all 5-year intervals. However, we observed that the rising rate of surgical populations has decreased since 2019, while surgical coverage experienced brief fluctuations in 2019–2020 and continued to increase from 2020. In late 2019, an outbreak of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) occurred in a wide range of Chinese areas44. Subsequently, the Chinese government implemented nationwide restrictions on outdoor activities to prevent the spread of the epidemic45, which might be related to the reduction in surgical population numbers and coverage in 2019. Since late 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic has been relatively stable, and outdoor activities have been less restricted46, resulting in gradually increasing numbers of surgical patients and coverage. Because the GBD 2019 study only included data up to 2019 and the age-stratified total populations for 1997–2019 were incomplete, neither the GBD 2019 study2,24 (providing prevalence numbers) nor the PINDEC study13,16 (providing prevalence percentages) can be used alone to estimate the total number of PD patients for 1997–2021. Thus, the present study applied both of these methods (the GBD 2019 study2,24 for 1997–2019 and the PINDEC study13,16 for 2020 and 2021), which might introduce an imbalance. However, the GBD 2019 study estimated prevalence numbers from previous epidemiological studies2, and recent studies showed that the PD prevalence percentages did not change significantly in China13. Thus, the prevalence numbers from the GBD 2019 study2,24 and those estimated based on the representative prevalence percentages from the PINDEC study13,16 should theoretically be consistent, which was also verified13. In addition, this possible imbalance could only contribute to fluctuating coverage data but not to the decreased rate of the measured surgical populations. Therefore, the fluctuations from 2019 were more likely influenced by the COVID-19 pandemic. The study predicted that the surgical populations and their coverage will rise in the future, and future control of the epidemic and the relaxation of restrictions will further help. Annual surgical center numbers and coverage had two rounds of growth peaks in 2002–2006 and 2010–2018 and decreased beginning in 2019. The growth peaks conformed to the first Chinese approval of DBS for PD in 1999, and multiple DBS manufacturers and new technologies have entered clinical use and received approvals since 2009. These findings suggest the importance of health policies and the approval of more DBS equipment with novel technologies. The predicted data had an interesting fluctuating but upward trend. Since the forecast was based on previous trends, we assumed that this was a reflection of recent ‘boost peaks’. We reserved the adaptive seasonal (periodic) parameter in the forecast model to simulate possible future ‘boost peaks’ with further technology development and approval. This periodicity is virtually unpredictable and not fixed and therefore should only be an intuitive expression. The reduction in annual surgical center numbers from 2019 should also be attributed to the COVID-19 epidemic. The establishment of a new DBS center requires the collaboration of multiple medical and technical teams, which may not be easily achieved during epidemics. Generally, surgical populations, centers, and coverage of DBS for PD had an upward trend with rapidly increasing rates and are predicted to show a future increase, but their development was partially limited by the COVID-19 pandemic. In addition, as mentioned above, increasing longevity and PD patients, unmet surgical needs of eligible patients, and further upward trends and new technologies in the future could be strategies to attract stakeholders (e.g. policymakers and manufacturers) to increase investments in resources to improve the performance of DBS in people with PD. With the gradual relief of COVID-19, it may predict a much brighter future for the discipline and, ultimately, for PD patients.

Thorough and efficient programming is critical for a successful clinical outcome of DBS for PD, and remote programming provides a flexible and accessible new technology with equal efficacy and safety to traditional face-to-face programming47,48. The Chinese National Medical Products Administration granted remote programming approval for DBS in 2014. Since then, remote programming has been adopted for Chinese clinical use. This study found a significant upward trend with a highly increased rate (especially during 2019–2021) of PD patients who underwent remote programming DBS, from its first clinical application in 2015 to 2021. In 2021, nearly half of PD patients underwent remote programming DBS more than 10 000 times annually. The rapid increase is also related to the COVID-19 epidemic, as remote programming provided an important solution during the quarantine period49. In addition, several new technologies and directions are under development, such as electrode and battery designs, stimulation paradigms, closed-loop and on-demand stimulation, and sensing technologies7,50, which may further promote DBS as a more effective, flexible, tolerable, and individualized treatment for PD patients in the future.

Regional differences and influential factors

Chinese regions faced major challenges such as regional imbalances in economic, resource, and health coverage due to historical and geographical reasons31,32,51. The present study applied the official NEPD region division measures, and the national government work report defined the development goals of these regions as ‘to promote development in the western region, revitalization of the northeastern region, accelerate the rise of the central region, and modernize the eastern region’19. This indicates that for regional development, the eastern and central regions are leading, while the northeastern and western regions are lagging behind, which needs to be prioritized for development52. In the present study, we analyzed these regional differences based on 2020 data. Generally, provinces in the eastern and central regions had higher GDP and GDP per capita; more DBS populations and higher insurance coverage DBS; and more neurosurgery and functional neurosurgery departments, beds, and doctors than those in the western and northeastern regions, while the total populations in provinces in different regions showed nonsignificant differences. Regarding the DBS population and coverage, the eastern region had great advantages (~4 times more than other regions), while the rest of the regions were limited. These findings suggested significant regional imbalances in economic status, DBS populations, coverage, insurance, and DBS-related resources. In addition, the western region has a larger area and more provinces, which exacerbates the regionally imbalanced health coverage. Thus, efforts should be made to strengthen support for medical resources, especially for DBS and its related resources, in the western and northeastern Chinese regions. Regional health coverage inequity is a global topic. Sharma et al. 11 revealed that the majority of DBS procedures for PD were performed at high-volume centers, academic institutions, urban areas, and hospitals with large numbers of beds in the west or south of the United States. We suggest that global policy-makers and health program managers and planners pay attention to regionally imbalanced health coverage and promote universal health coverage equity and coordinated development of DBS for PD and its related resources across different regions9. For health care providers and researchers, support and assistance to less-developed areas can also further promote the solution of this inequity36. It should also be noted that the implementation of DBS requires adequate resources, facilities, experience, and specialists7,8, which is not available for many less gifted centers, especially for those located in less-developed areas. Thus, our suggestion is not to carry out this therapeutic option in every center but to effectively protect the chances of patients in different regions receiving DBS surgery. Specific initiatives for areas with limited resources can promote available experienced DBS centers to establish subcenters, policy makers to deploy resources for several major medical centers in learning and performing DBS surgeries, DBS specialists to assist in traveling for cross-regional surgeries, and PD patients to accept surgeries in developed regions if possible31,33,36,53. Technologies such as remote programming and online surgical education and guidance can also help47–49.

In this study, we also intended to identify factors influencing the regional differences in surgical populations and their coverage. Generally, inequity in economic development, DBS-related resources, and insurance policies in different regions contributed to the regional differences in DBS for PD. A higher GDP per capita was a promoting factor for more surgical populations and their coverage. For eastern coastal provinces with a high level of economic development, the annual surgical population could exceed 100 patients, while only a few patients underwent DBS in western inland provinces. It was also observed in other studies that Chinese regions with better economic conditions had more anesthesiologists32. These findings showed the effect of economic development on health coverage. For areas with lower GDP per capita, concerns about low health coverage should be given greater attention31. In addition, DBS-related resources, especially functional neurosurgery doctors, influenced surgical populations and their coverage. Interestingly, we observed that the development of functional neurosurgery had more influence than general neurosurgery. Provinces in the central region had an advantage in the development of neurosurgery, while the eastern region was leading in functional neurosurgery development, resulting in more DBS patients and coverage in the eastern region. Functional neurosurgery is a field in which neurosurgical procedures are performed to modulate neurologic function and alleviate symptoms, and DBS is used as a key therapy38,39,54,55. Some areas had independent functional neurosurgery departments, beds, and doctors, while others performed functional neurosurgery in general neurosurgery departments56. The findings in this study suggest the importance of the development of functional neurosurgery, especially its facilities and expertise, to increase the number of beneficiaries and coverage of DBS. For this scenario, efforts should be made by related medical centers and societies in podcasting, education, training, and practice53. In addition, there is a lack of unified teaching and qualification procedures for DBS facilities and centers, which can be a future direction for related societies contributing to this discipline57. Higher insurance coverage levels were also a positive influential factor of surgical populations. For mainland China, the government-run Basic Medical Insurance Program is the major public insurance system, which is funded by employers, employees, and additional government funding. The program covers most of the essential hospitalization and surgical expenses, while its coverage for DBS equipment has large disparities among different regions. Less-developed western and northeastern regions were less likely to afford DBS equipment costs. In addition, private insurance is not popular in mainland China and rarely increases reimbursement for DBS equipment. According to our investigation, the government of Hong Kong SAR afforded nearly all costs of hospitalization, surgical, and DBS equipment expenses with public healthcare, while private health insurance of the dual-track healthcare system provided supplements in healthcare benefits; the citizens of Taiwan afforded ~10% of the costs of DBS for PD after payment by the National Health Insurance, a single-payer compulsory social insurance funded primarily through payroll-based premiums; and DBS for PD has not been performed in Macao SAR. The high costs of DBS equipment and follow-up medical management could be a heavy burden for PD patients58. The average cost of DBS equipment is ~300 000 RMB, which is equivalent to about 8 years of per capita annual disposable income (36 900 RMB) for Chinese individuals21,22. The current policy on Medicare in the US also risks curbing the advancement of innovative device-based therapies such as DBS59. Therefore, the insurance policy is important for the development and coverage of DBS for PD33. Encouragingly, previous studies suggested that DBS led to substantial long-term antiparkinsonian medication cost savings by maintaining a low-dose medication regimen for PD patients60. Local and national governments should study the costs of medications and DBS for PD patients and establish a suitable health policy and insurance policy with regional equity to help improve the prognosis of PD patients33. In addition, these three key factors, equity in economic development, DBS-related resources, and insurance policies in different regions, showed mutual influences on each other, and better economic status was positively correlated with higher numbers of DBS recipients and coverage and more insurance coverage and DBS-related resources, suggesting coordinated economy-health-society development. To alleviate the regional inequity in DBS for PD, joint efforts should be made to address these factors, which include imbalanced economic development, DBS-related resources, and insurance policies.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, restricted by unavailable major data and differences, regional equity was only analyzed for the Chinese mainland, which might limit this study’s representativeness, while analyses on utilization and coverage were based on all Chinese areas with complete national data. The applicability of these results to different global areas needs further international studies. Second, the present study applied different methods in estimating the total number of PD patients (the GBD 2019 study2,24 and the PINDEC study13,16), which might introduce imbalanced comparisons for surgery coverage. Third, the prediction model used was based on past trends. The model only predicted a possible future trend, which should be verified in future practice. Fourth, changes in utilization and coverage with time were not analyzed for each province or region because of a lack of relevant data, which may be a possible future research direction. Finally, this study only focused on the topic of health coverage, while the characteristics and outcomes of patients who underwent DBS for PD are worthy of further study. We established the DBS-PDCC collaboration and will continue conducting these studies in the future.

Conclusions

Although surgical populations, centers, and DBS coverage for PD have rapidly improved and are predicted to have a future increase, DBS coverage is still insufficient to cover potential eligible patients. Chinese regions faced major challenges such as regional imbalances, which should be given greater attention to promote universal health coverage equity and realize coordinated regional development. To increase the number of beneficiaries and coverage of DBS, adjustments should be made to the equity of economic development, DBS-related resources, and insurance policies.

Biomechanical characterization of thyroid-stimulating hormone–secreting pituitary adenoma using in vivo MR elastography and in vitro nanoindentation: illustrative case
Authors: Umesh Gautam, Shivani Tripathi, Priyanka Bhat, Hemlata Jangir, Sandeep Ganji, Yogesh Kannan Mariappan, Vaishali Suri, Ajay Garg, S. Senthil Kumaran, Sitikantha Roy, Ashish Suri

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUNDThyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)–secreting pituitary adenomas (TSHomas) are rare lesions that result in secondary hyperthyroidism. Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH)–secreting pituitary adenomas (TSHomas) are uncommon, representing approximately 0.5%–3% of all pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (PitNETs).1,2 Tumor consistency is a crucial physical property of tumor tissue that could aid in efficient tumor resection.3 There are limited data available on its consistency that make its resection challenging and time-consuming. Previous research efforts have attempted to predict the consistency of PitNETs. Conventional MRI has proven to be of limited utility and consequently inadequate for this purpose.4–11 MR elastography (MRE) has shown improved efficacy in predicting tumor consistency and aiding preoperative preparation.7,12–14 Given the rarity of TSHomas, no study is available using MRE.

This report presents a case of successful resection of a TSHoma using endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal (EETS) surgery planned through in vivo MRE. Postoperative analysis included in vitro nanoindentation to assess the viscoelastic properties of the tumor tissue and histopathological analysis to identify the type of PitNET cells present in the tumor.

Illustrative Case

Case Presentation

A 16-year-old female initially presented with delayed primary amenorrhea and increased urinary frequency persisting for 1 year. Initial blood investigations revealed elevated thyroid function parameters: serum TSH of 5.02 mIU/L (normal range 0.45–4.16 mIU/L), free T3 of 10.6 pg/mL (normal range 2.77–5.27 pg/mL), total T3 of 3.44 ng/mL (normal range 0.97–1.69 ng/mL), free T4 of 2.79 ng/dL (normal range 0.78–2.19 ng/dL), and total T4 of 19.7 μg/mL (normal range 5.53–11 μg/mL). Visual acuity and visual fields were within normal limits.

Preoperative Investigation

Contrast-enhanced MRI revealed a T2/FLAIR-hyperintense enhancing mass lesion (37 × 30 × 31 mm3) with extension into the bilateral cavernous sinuses (Fig. 1). Inferiorly, the lesion was eroding the sellar floor and extending into sphenoid sinus. The lesion was preoperatively diagnosed as a giant pituitary macroadenoma with a bilateral Knosp grade of 3A. Preoperatively, an experienced neuroradiologist predicted that the tumor would have soft consistency. Preoperative MRE

MRE is a noninvasive, MRI-based imaging technique that induces low-frequency shear waves in tissue and quantifies the in vivo mechanical properties in terms of storge and loss moduli.15 In this case, MRE was acquired on a 3T MR scanner (Ingenia 3.0 T, Philips Healthcare) using a 32-channel head coil. The MRE sequence was acquired with 48 slices in axial orientation (TR 4800 msec), with a frequency of 60 Hz applied using a specially designed driver for the brain with the help of the Resoundant system (Mayo Clinic). Selection of region of interest (ROI) is the major challenge in MRE data analysis. The lesion area was first marked in the T1-weighted MRI. Later, these markings were translated to storage and loss modulus maps (Fig. 2A). A circular ROI was drawn within the tumor area to calculate the moduli values (Fig. 2B and C). Additionally, a similar size ROI was drawn in normal brain at multiple places to get the moduli values. Data processing was done using a customized image processing pipeline. Surgical Planning, Operative Finding, and Resection

The resection was planned through an EETS procedure. The nasal septum deviated left. A right middle turbinectomy and posterior one-third septostomy were performed to expose the sphenoid sinus. The tumor was found to be grayish-white, extra-axial, and suckable, with soft consistency. The tumor was very vascular; rapid resection was followed by hemostasis using a hemostatic matrix and absorbable gelatin sponge (Video 1). Gross-total excision (GTE) was performed using suction and blunt curette. The arachnoid mater was intact and no CSF leakage was seen. The sella floor was reconstructed using fat from the thigh and augmented with glue.

In Vitro Nanoindentation

Nanoindentation is a mechanical testing technique in which a small indenter presses the material with a controlled force and measures the indentation depth to calculate the stiffness of the material at microscale to nanoscale.3 In this study, we employed an iNano nanoindenter (KLA Corporation) to assess the in vitro viscoelastic properties of tumor tissues. Excised tumor sample was collected and tested within 30 minutes postexcision to preserve physiological relevance. The specimen was carefully prepared and tested under a physiological body environment. A flat-ended cylindrical indenter of 100 μm in diameter was employed for the indentation tests. Outcome and Follow-Up

The postoperative course was uneventful, and the subject’s serum TSH level was normalized at 0.3 mIU/L (normal range 0–4 mIU/L). The subject was discharged from the hospital 12 days postsurgery. During follow-up visits in the outpatient clinic, the patient remained asymptomatic and exhibited stable clinical recovery. Postoperative MRI showed complete resection of the tumor (Fig. 3). Results

In Vivo MRE Versus In Vitro Nanoindentation

The viscoelastic properties of the rare TSHoma were characterized by both in vivo and in vitro techniques. MRE revealed the storage and loss moduli of the tumor region to be 1.92 ± 0.36 kPa and 0.48 ± 0.11 kPa, respectively. In in vitro nanoindentation, the storage and loss moduli were found to be 2.06 ± 0.20 kPa and 0.51 ± 0.23 kPa, respectively. The in vivo and in vitro findings showed strong coherence (Fig. 4). Additionally, MRE showed the tumor tissue storage moduli values to be in the range of normal brain tissues (normal brain storage modulus: 1.89 ± 0.39 kPa), but the loss moduli were significantly lower (normal brain loss modulus: 1.21 ± 0.24 kPa). Histopathology of the Specimen

An excised tumor sample, collected from a region proximal to the in vitro testing site, was preserved for histopathological assessment. Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson’s trichrome (MT) staining was performed by expert neuropathologists. The stained sections were scanned using a 3DHISTECH whole-slide scanner for high-resolution imaging. Collagen fibers appeared pinkish-red on H&E-stained slices and blue on MT-stained slices (Fig. 5). The collagen distribution was quantified across the sections using the relation given in Equation 3, enabling precise evaluation of collagen presence within the tumor. Quantitative analysis revealed that 20% of the intratumoral area consisted of collagen. Histological examination confirmed the diagnosis of a TSH-secreting tumor, with positive immunohistochemical expression of molecular markers Pit1 and SF1. Discussion

In this study, we highlighted the mechanical characteristics and surgical outcome of a rare PitNET, that is, TSHoma. Preoperative imaging and intraoperative observations indicated the tumor to be of soft consistency. GTE was achieved using the EETS procedure.

We employed in vivo MRE, a noninvasive and innovative technique to characterize the mechanical properties of the tumor tissue preoperatively that holds promise for improving surgical planning and outcomes. Our MRE findings showed that the storage and loss moduli of tumor tissues were 1.92 ± 0.36 kPa and 0.48 ± 0.11 kPa, respectively. In comparison, the storage and loss moduli of normal brain white matter were found to be 1.89 ± 0.39 kPa and 1.21 ± 0.24 kPa, respectively. While the storage moduli of the tumor and adjacent brain tissues were in a similar range, the normal brain tissue exhibited a significantly higher loss modulus. These findings are consistent with prior literature. Cohen-Cohen et al.7 employed MRE and reported mean stiffness values of 1.8 kPa for pituitary adenomas and 2.7 kPa for normal brain tissue. Sakai et al.13 measured a modulus of 1.2 ± 0.3 kPa for PitNET tissues, while Hughes et al.12 reported a range of 1.3–1.9 kPa using MRE. Interestingly, our findings are largely consistent with the values reported in the literature, further supporting the tumor’s soft consistency.

Despite the usefulness of MRE, significant inconsistencies exist among reported values, potentially due to variations in methodology, spatial resolution, or tumor heterogeneity. In such cases, in vitro mechanical characterization is needed to ensure the reliability of MRE findings. We performed in vitro nanoindentation on postoperatively excised tumor tissue. The storage and loss modulus of TSHoma tissues were found to be 2.06 ± 0.20 kPa and 0.51 ± 0.23 kPa, respectively. Meyer et al.17 and Wang et al.18 reported much lower moduli for PitNET tissues (0.12 kPa and 0.25 kPa, respectively) using atomic force microscopy, which offers higher spatial resolution. The discrepancies may also stem from differences in PitNETs, extracellular matrix composition, or testing conditions. Nevertheless, our in vitro results demonstrate strong concordance with our MRE findings.

Histological analysis of the tumor revealed a collagen content of approximately 20%, predominantly located within intratumoral regions. Apart from collagen, other microstructural components also play a role in stiffness contribution. Notably, the invasion, migration, and progression of PitNET cells have been associated with increased extracellular matrix stiffness due to the presence of tumor-associated fibroblasts.19

Observations

In this case, a 16-year-old female presented with symptoms of delayed primary amenorrhea and elevated TSH levels, leading to the diagnosis of a rare and giant TSHoma. Preoperative imaging using MRE predicted a soft tumor consistency, which was later confirmed during surgery as the lesion was easily resectable with suction and blunt curettage. The tumor was completely excised via an EETS approach, and the patient recovered well, with normalized TSH levels and no postoperative complications. Importantly, the in vivo MRE and in vitro mechanical measurements were highly consistent, with histological analysis revealing a 20% collagen content within the tumor. This case highlights the value of MRE in preoperative planning by providing reliable information on tumor consistency, which can optimize surgical strategies, reduce intraoperative uncertainty, and improve patient outcomes in managing rare and complex pituitary tumors.

Lessons

This study highlights the clinical relevancy of in vivo MRE in preoperative planning and surgical management of one rare TSHoma case. There is a strong correlation between in vivo MRE-derived mechanical properties and in vitro nanoindentation measurements, reinforcing the reliability and accuracy of MRE in predicting tumor consistency. This predictive capability is crucial for tailoring surgical strategies, particularly in challenging cases in which tumor consistency can impact resection approach and outcome. A gross-total resection of the tumor and positive postoperative recovery further underscore the potential of MRE to enhance surgical precision, reduce operative time, and improve patient satisfaction. However, the study’s findings are based on a single case, which limits the generalizability of the conclusions. Larger cohort studies are necessary to validate the consistent applicability of MRE as a biomarker in PitNET surgeries. Despite this limitation, the present case contributes meaningful evidence supporting the integration of MRE into clinical practice for managing complex pituitary lesions and offers valuable guidance for handling similarly rare and difficult tumors.

ACTN4 Gene Amplification and Actinin‐4 Protein Expression for Osimertinib Efficacy in EGFR ‐Mutant NSCLC
Authors: Takehiro Tozuka, Rintaro Noro, Yutaka Naito, Nami Miura, Shinobu Kunugi, Kazufumi Honda, Masahiro Seike
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTActinin‐4 (gene name: ACTN4) is an actin‐bundling protein implicated in cancer invasion and metastasis. This study evaluated whether ACTN4 amplification and actinin‐4 protein expression were associated with osimertinib efficacy in epidermal growth factor receptor‐mutant non‐small cell lung cancer. We retrospectively analyzed 63 patients with epidermal growth factor receptor‐mutant non‐small cell lung cancer treated with osimertinib as first‐line treatment. Immunohistochemistry was performed for pretreatment tumor tissues. Actinin‐4 immunohistochemistry positivity was defined as positive staining of ≥ 30% tumor cells. In positive cases, ACTN4 amplification was assessed via fluorescence in situ hybridization. Progression‐free survival and overall survival were compared across groups. Among 63 patients (median age: 73 years, 52 with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status 0–1, 63 with adenocarcinoma; epidermal growth factor receptor mutations: 19del/L858R/uncommon = 32/24/7), there were 33 and 30 actinin‐4 immunohistochemistry‐positive and actinin‐4 immunohistochemistry‐negative cases, respectively. The propensity score‐weighted overall survival and progression‐free survival were significantly shorter for actinin‐4 immunohistochemistry‐positive patients than for actinin‐4 immunohistochemistry‐negative patients (overall survival: hazard ratio, 2.76; 95% confidence interval, 1.02–7.45; progression‐free survival: hazard ratio, 1.91; 95% confidence interval, 1.03–3.54). Among the 33 actinin‐4 immunohistochemistry‐positive cases, four showed positivity in ACTN4 fluorescence in situ hybridization. Overall survival and progression‐free survival were numerically shorter for patients with ACTN4 positivity than for those with ACTN4 negativity in fluorescence in situ hybridization. The findings suggest that ACTN4 amplification and actinin‐4 protein expression are prognostic markers for poor osimertinib efficacy in epidermal growth factor receptor‐mutant non‐small cell lung cancer.

Introduction

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations are found in approximately 10%–20% of patients with non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) in Europe and North America and 50% of NSCLC patients in Asia [1, 2, 3]. Osimertinib, a third‐generation tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), is a standard treatment for EGFR‐mutant NSCLC. In the FLAURA trial, osimertinib prolonged progression‐free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared with first‐generation EGFR‐TKIs in patients with previously untreated EGFR‐mutant NSCLC [4].

Despite its efficacy, resistance to osimertinib remains a challenge. The development of resistance remains a major challenge in EGFR‐mutant NSCLC, limiting therapeutic options. Genetic analysis including next‐generation sequencing has identified several genetic resistance mechanisms to osimertinib, including EGFR C797S, EGFR amplification, HER2 mutations, and MET amplification [5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. In addition to these genetic alterations, proteomic and non‐coding RNA analysis has been explored to elucidate non‐genetic resistance mechanisms [10, 11, 12]. Diverse resistance mechanisms complicate treatment strategies, with some patients showing early resistance.

To delay or prevent resistance and enhance therapeutic efficacy, combination therapy with EGFR‐TKIs and other anti‐tumor agents has been explored. In the FLAURA2 trial, the addition of platinum‐based chemotherapy to osimertinib significantly prolonged PFS compared to that with osimertinib monotherapy in EGFR‐mutant advanced NSCLC [13]. Similarly, the MARIPOSA trial showed that amivantamab plus lazertinib led to longer PFS than did osimertinib monotherapy [14]. Combination therapy improves outcomes but raises the risk of adverse events (AEs), underscoring the need for biomarkers. However, no reliable biomarker exists for osimertinib therapy. Actinin‐4 (gene name: ACTN4) is an actin‐bundling protein associated with the invasion and metastasis of tumors [15]. Our recent study highlighted the potential significance of actinin‐4 as a biomarker in postoperative patients with NSCLC. Increased actinin‐4 protein expression and gene amplification may serve as biomarkers for identifying patients who could benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy [16, 17, 18]. Moreover, amplification of ACTN4, located on 19q13, is associated with the metastatic ability of lung adenocarcinoma. In vitro and mouse model studies regulating ACTN4 expression have demonstrated its role in promoting cell proliferation, motility, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition [16, 19]. Although actinin‐4 may reflect tumor grade and activity, the significance of ACTN4 amplification and actinin‐4 protein expression in advanced lung cancer has not been fully investigated [20].

This study evaluated ACTN4 amplification and actinin‐4 protein expression as prognostic biomarkers in EGFR‐mutant NSCLC treated with osimertinib.

Material and Methods

Patient Selection and Study Design

We retrospectively reviewed patients' medical records at Nippon Medical School Hospital (Tokyo, Japan). The present study included patients with pathologically confirmed, advanced EGFR‐mutant NSCLC who received osimertinib as a first‐line treatment between August 2018 and October 2021. Patients without accessible pretreatment tumor specimens were excluded. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Nippon Medical School (B‐2021‐497) [21].

Data Collection and Outcome Assessment

Clinical data were extracted from the patient's medical records: age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS), smoking history, histology, clinical stage, metastatic sites, EGFR mutation type (EGFR exon 19 deletion [Ex19del], EGFR exon 21 L858R [Leu858Arg] or other uncommon EGFR mutations), and programmed death ligand 1 (PD‐L1) tumor proportion score (TPS). The cutoff date for data collection was September 30, 2023. Tumor response was evaluated according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors, version 1.1. The best overall response was categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and progressive disease or not evaluable (PD/NE). CR was defined as the disappearance of all target lesions. PR was defined as a ≥ 30% reduction in the sum of the diameters of target lesions. SD was defined as neither sufficient shrinkage to qualify for PR nor sufficient increase to qualify for PD, and PD was defined as a ≥ 20% increase in the sum of the diameters of target lesions or the appearance of new lesions.

PFS was defined as the time from the initiation of osimertinib therapy to disease progression or death from any cause. OS was defined as the time from the initiation of osimertinib therapy to death from any cause.

Evaluation of ACTN4 Amplification and Actinin‐4 Protein Expression

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) for formalin‐fixed paraffin‐embedded (FFPE) tissues from surgically resected or biopsy specimens of NSCLC was performed using anti‐human actinin‐4 monoclonal antibody (Abnova Corporation, Taipei, Taiwan) [22, 23]. Immunoperoxidase staining was performed, and immunoreactivity was evaluated by three independent investigators. Cases with ≥ 30% positive tumor cells were classified as actinin‐4 IHC‐positive cases, while others were classified as negative cases, in accordance with previously reported criteria [17, 23].

To confirm ACTN4 amplification in actinin‐4 IHC‐positive cases, fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was performed. FFPE tissues were pretreated with acid, followed by protease digestion and denaturation at 72°C for 5 min. Tissue sections were then hybridized with a mixture of newly selected bacterial artificial chromosome clones (i.e., GSP0507B12 and GSP2561A10) mapped to the approximately 210 kb ACTN4 region at 19q13.2 and centromere probes on chromosome 19. Using microscopy, the number of fluorescent signals in 20 non‐overlapping nuclei per case was counted independently by three investigators and averaged. ACTN4 FISH positivity was defined as an ACTN4/CEP19 ratio of ≥ 2.0; negativity was defined as an ACTN4/CEP19 ratio of < 2.0 [23].

Statistical Analysis

Survival outcomes were compared by ACTN4 FISH and actinin‐4 IHC results. Median follow‐up was estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Patient characteristics between the two groups were compared using standardized differences. The propensity scores were calculated as a probability from a logistic regression model that included covariates as follows: age (continuous variables), sex (male/female), PS (0–1/2–4), smoking history (never/current or former), stage (IVA/IVB/recurrence), EGFR status (Ex19 del/Ex21 L858R/uncommon mutations), and PD‐L1 TPS (≥ 50%/1%–49%/< 1%/unknown). OS and PFS were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods and compared using log‐rank tests. Hazard ratios (HRs) for OS and PFS were calculated by the Cox proportional hazards model. A pvalue of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All factors that were significant in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate analysis. All statistical analyses were conducted using EZR version 1.61 [24].

Results

Patients

Of 79 patients with EGFR mutant NSCLC who received osimertinib as first‐line treatment between August 2018 and October 2021, 63 patients with available FFPE specimens were included (Figure S1). All tumors were adenocarcinoma. Thirty‐three patients showed positive actinin‐4 expression in IHC, while 30 showed negative expression. Figure 1 shows representative IHC images for cases with positive and negative actinin‐4 expression. Of the 33 actinin‐4 IHC‐positive cases, ACTN4 FISH was performed for 32 cases with additional tissue samples available. Four patients showed positivity for ACTN4, whereas 25 showed negative results. In three cases, control signals using chromosome 19 were detected in < 20 cells, and those cases were considered unevaluated. Therefore, in the ACTN4 IHC‐positive group, the ACTN4 FISH positivity rate was 12.5% (4/32). Discussion

The present study evaluated actinin‐4 as a prognostic biomarker for patients with EGFR‐mutant NSCLC who received osimertinib. Our results demonstrated that patients with actinin‐4 IHC‐positive tumors had significantly shorter PFS and OS than did patients with actinin‐4 IHC‐negative tumors. In addition, FISH analysis was conducted to complement IHC findings, and we found that ACTN4 FISH‐positive patients showed a poorer response to osimertinib than did ACTN4 FISH‐negative patients. These findings suggest that ACTN4 amplification and actinin‐4 expression are associated with aggressive characteristics and could help identify patients with a poor response to osimertinib, thus guiding treatment decisions.

Elevated actinin‐4 expression and ACTN4 amplification have been linked to tumor malignancy and metastatic potential across various types of cancers [15, 25, 26, 27]. High actinin‐4 protein expression is a marker for platinum‐based chemotherapy in postoperative lung adenocarcinoma, with significant OS benefits observed for patients receiving adjuvant cisplatin‐based treatment [16, 17]. ACTN4 amplification may help identify patients with stage I lung adenocarcinoma who benefit from an adjuvant tegafur–uracil regimen, particularly in older populations [18]. Consistently, our study confirmed its association with shorter OS and PFS in EGFR‐mutant NSCLC patients treated with osimertinib. These findings confirm the prognostic value of ACTN4 amplification. Balancing efficacy and side effects is crucial, as the quality of life affects survival [28, 29]. The FLAURA2 trial showed that osimertinib plus chemotherapy improved outcomes in EGFR‐mutant NSCLC but increased grade ≥ 3 AEs (64% vs. 27% in monotherapy) [13]. Similarly, the MARIPOSA trial demonstrated that amivantamab plus lazertinib was effective but led to higher toxicity (grade ≥ 3 AEs: 75% vs. 43% for osimertinib). These findings highlight the importance of patient selection and the need for biomarkers to identify patients less likely to respond to osimertinib therapy alone.

As first‐line options for EGFR‐mutant NSCLC expand, reliable biomarkers are essential for treatment selection. The exon 21 L858R mutation is less sensitive to EGFR‐TKIs than is the exon 19 deletion mutation. An integrated analysis of phase III studies of EGFR‐mutant NSCLC treated with EGFR‐TKI showed that the exon 21 L858R mutation was associated with a significantly shorter PFS than was the exon 19 deletion mutation (L858R mutation: HR, 0.48; 95% CI, 0.39–0.58; exon 19 deletion mutation: HR, 0.24; 95% CI, 0.20–0.29) [30]. However, subgroup analysis of the FLAURA trial showed no significant difference in OS among patients positive for the exon 21 L858R mutation. Baseline genomic alterations, including TP53/RB1 co‐mutations and atypical EGFR mutations, were reportedly associated with shorter PFS with osimertinib [5]. However, these genetic abnormalities are challenging to use as clinical biomarkers because of their limited detectability in routine diagnostic settings and the lack of standardized testing protocols. Real‐world studies showed that patients with PD‐L1 ≥ 50% had poorer PFS and OS with first‐line osimertinib for advanced EGFR‐mutant NSCLC [31, 32]. On the other hand, the exploratory research of the FLAURA trial demonstrated that osimertinib resulted in significantly longer PFS than did a first‐generation EGFR‐TKI, regardless of PD‐L1 expression [33]. Few of these biomarkers that have been investigated so far are used in clinical practice.

The strength of actinin‐4 for clinical application is that it can be easily determined by immunostaining. The immunostaining evaluation method for actinin‐4 has already been established and detailed in our previous reports [17, 22]. The evaluation method for ACTN4 FISH has also been established [18, 23]. In addition, even after adjusting for confounding factors that affect treatment efficacy and prognosis using propensity score weighting, it was identified as a significant factor related to poor PFS and OS; thus, it may serve as a robust biomarker.

This study has several limitations. First, gene amplification was not evaluated for all cases. However, our previous study demonstrated that almost all cases with ACTN4 amplification exhibit positive protein expression, and combining protein expression with gene amplification analysis could further enhance the accuracy of prognosis or recurrence prediction [23]. Accordingly, we adopted a stepwise approach in this study, wherein IHC screening was followed by confirmatory FISH analysis. A more efficient screening method, along with a better understanding of the clinicopathological characteristics of ACTN4‐positive cases, is warranted for future clinical applications. Second, variability in patient backgrounds may have influenced the results. Although the propensity score analysis corrected for background factors between the two groups, the results were obtained in a small patient group. Therefore, validation of the results of this study by large prospective studies is needed. Furthermore, the difference in tumor biology between EGFR mutations and ACTN4 remains unclear. The binding strength of actinin‐4 to actin filaments is regulated by signaling from growth factor receptors such as EGFR [34, 35]. Phosphorylation of actinin‐4 due to EGF stimulation decreases its actin binding activity [36]. Overexpression of EGFR in melanoma cells increases the phosphorylation of actinin‐4 upon EGF stimulation, suggesting that actinin‐4 could also be also phosphorylated in EGFR‐mutant lung cancer [37]. Additional basic studies are warranted. Finally, this study suggests that actinin‐4 IHC and ACTN4 FISH positivity correlated with a poorer response to osimertinib. However, we cannot exclude the possibility that the observed results reflect the prognosis rather than the specific sensitivity to osimertinib. To establish ACTN4 as a predictive biomarker, further investigations are required to evaluate its impact on the efficacy of other therapies, including platinum‐based chemotherapy in combination with osimertinib.

In conclusion, ACTN4 amplification and actinin‐4 protein expression were useful biomarkers for identifying patients with poor prognosis after receiving osimertinib monotherapy. Future studies should focus on validating actinin‐4 as a biomarker in larger cohorts and investigating its role in guiding combination therapies, such as osimertinib with platinum‐based chemotherapy, to improve outcomes in patients with EGFR‐mutant NSCLC.

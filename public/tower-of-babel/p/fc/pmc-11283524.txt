The effectiveness and types of serves used in elite women’s and men’s volleyball in the 2021/2022 season
Authors: Damian Pawlik, Gabriel Bobula, Dariusz Mroczek
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn elite volleyball, the serve takes an offensive form, aiming to directly score a point or significantly hinder the opponent from launching an attack. Which is why the research aimed to compare the type and effectiveness of the serve in the main round of Tauron and PlusLiga in the 2021/2022 season and update the current knowledge on the types of serves by function and gender. The study analysed observation consisted of 314 women’s and men’s matches. VolleyStation Pro v 1.29.5; Volleystation z o. o. Poland. Data from the main round matches that took place in the 2021/2022 season in the Polish elite league PlusLiga (182 men’s matches) and Tauron Liga (132 women’s matches) were used, and the regular season consisted of 2 rounds. The average percentage effectiveness of the serve in women was higher (E = 22%) than in men (E = 15%). In women, only 3 types were observed (JS, JF and W), as much as 86% of all the serves were JF serves. In men, as many as 5 types (JS, JF, FJS, FJF and W), of which over 67% were dynamic JS serves (jumping serve). The serve element has been modified over the years to obtain better effects conducive to winning. Ranging from increasing the speed of the serve, eliminating the serves from the spot in favor of the jump serves, to the use of serves to hide the correct hit of the ball (“Fake”—FFJ, FJS).Keywords: Elite volleyball players, Serve analysis, Serve typesSubject terms: Physiology, Risk factors

The only action in volleyball that is entirely dependent on the player is the serve (S). The serve starts every game action. It is up to the player to toss the ball, the type, strength of the stroke and the direction of the serve1. The course of action depends on the quality of this element. The serve is the first action when a player can score2–4. In elite volleyball, the serve takes an offensive form, aiming to directly score a point or significantly hinder the opponent from launching an attack5,6. Thus, the serving team can predict the ball’s distribution by the setter faster and organize itself more effectively in the block-defense action7.

Both in women’s and men’s volleyball, the importance of serving as a skill determining the status of winning and losing during the game is emphasized8–10. Depending on the game’s tactics, the opponent and the player’s individual predispositions, the serve’s goals in a given rotation are set to make it as effective as possible11. In order to increase efficiency, men have a significantly higher number of dynamic serves while jumping (JS) compared to women (52.2/69.9% vs. 16.6%)12,13. Palao et al.13 observed a lower ratio of aces to errors, which indicates that men take more risks in the serve area.

In volleyball, new technical and tactical solutions are sought to increase the game’s effectiveness. In the literature, the most common forms of serve are Jump Serve (JS) and Jump Float Serve (JF)14,15. You can also find very rare or non-existent serves in modern elite volleyball, for example, Asian Float Serve or Overhead Spin Power Serve. Moreover, performing such a serve at the elite level is perceived as disrespecting the opponent. An example is the underhand serve (sky serve) of a Cuban volleyball player playing in the Italian league in 2011, who officially had to apologize for performing such a serve.

In order to increase the effectiveness of the serve, the players try to surprise the opponent with the length of the ball’s flight (close-far) and use the so-called hybrids. It is most often a combination of JS and JF serves. Hybrid plays can be divided according to the toss and hit of the ball. Tossing the ball imitating a JF (tossing the ball with both hands without spin) and hitting the ball giving it a spin Jump Fake Float (JFF). Second version of “fake” serve is tossing the ball with a spin imitating a JS serve, while the shot gives the ball a flight without spin Jump Fake Serve (JFS). Float serves occur in both men and women15. In men, they are used sporadically, and the purpose of such a serve is to surprise the opponent by performing the serve together with the referee’s whistle. In women, spot serves are usually used by tall players to increase the repeatability of the serve. Not without significance is the net’s height, which for women is suspended 19 cm lower than for men. Therefore, women’s plays made on the spot have a flatter parabola of flight than men’s, which makes them more difficult to accept. The lower suspended net and lower physical capacities of the female players may cause discrepancies in the effectiveness and type of serves performed by men and women16–18.

The available statistical serve analyzes for volleyball are not up to date and do not cover the entire playing season, while protecting the quality of the service. No publications were found regarding the dangers of the serve divided into toss and spin of the shot after the shot (fake serve). The research aimed to compare the type and effectiveness of the serve in the main round of Tauron and PlusLiga in the 2021/2022 season and update the current knowledge on the types of serves by function and gender.

Material and methods

Participants

Data from the main round matches that took place in the 2021/2022 season in the Polish elite league PlusLiga (men’s) and Tauron Liga (women’s) were used, and the regular season consisted of 2 parts of the regular season. Men played 26 rounds, each of 7 matches (182 matches) and 348 sets, while women played 22 rounds, each of 6 matches (132 matches) and 244 sets. The whole observation consisted of 314 women’s and men’s matches. VolleyStation Pro (v 1.29.5; Volleystation z o. o. Poland). The effect of the serve was described according to a six-point scale (Table 1)5.

The conducted research project was carried out on the basis of the analysis of post-match data available in the VolleyStation Pro system database, which were subjected to appropriate analysis by us. Therefore, no human subjects participated in the study, and therefore the Research Ethics Committee waived the requirement to approve this project. The experiments were performed in the Biomechanical Analysis Laboratory (with PN-EN ISO 9001:2009 certification). Our research, as part of a large game analysis project, was approved by Senate Committee on Research Ethics of the Wroclaw University of Health and Sport Sciences, Poland (no. 19/2016), and the procedures complied with the Declaration of Helsinki regarding human experimentation. This research project used quantitative and qualitative data from the analysis of the volleyball game provided by the National Volleyball Association. So no tests were conducted on players, only public data (subscribed access) from databases were analyzed.

The analyzes are performed by professional statisticians of the Polish Volleyball Association with the support of Volley Station. The work of observers is subject to the supervision of the software manufacturer and official statistics of the Association. Design and validation of an observation instrument for technical and tactical actions in indoor volleyball was conducted by Palao et al.19.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the serve performance was presented as an arithmetic mean and standard deviation of all the observed serves. The interitem correlation coefficient (ICC) was calculated to determine the reliability between the serves. The coefficient of variation (CV) (Table 2) measured the inter-variability for each test. The confidence interval (CI) was calculated for the determined mean values of each variable and aimed at marking limiting points within which there was a 95% probability that the sought population means of the variables could be reduced. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess normal distribution and the Levene test to assess homogeneity of variances. Both tests demonstrated the validity of using parametric ANOVA analysis (p < 0.05).One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) and a Bonferroni post hoc test were used to analyze all data obtained. ANOVA with repeated measures was used to determine the differences between the measured parameters of the serve in consecutive sets (1–5). Spearman’s correlation was used to analyze the relationship between the rank (occupied place) of a given team in the table in relation to the effectiveness and number of plays made. All coefficients at a level of 95% (p < 0.05) were considered significant.

Discussion

The study aimed to analyze the plays performed by female and male volleyball players in terms of quantity, type, effectiveness and efficiency in the main round of the Polish Tauron and Plusliga.

The average percentage effectiveness of the serve in women was higher (E = 22%) than in men (E = 15%). Such scores indicate that a "safe" serve is being made, resulting in fewer direct mistakes and giving the opportunity for error or inaccurate reception by the opponent. Men made almost twice as many serving errors (“=” 21%) as compared to women (“=” 12%). With a small difference in point plays (“#” 6% male, 5% female). This means that despite taking a greater risk in performing the serve in men, JS’s serve did not translate directly into the level of effectiveness20,21. The execution of the JS serve in men most likely may not directly translate into the effect of scoring a point, but it forces the opponent to lack a perfect reception. Katsikadelli20 noticed that only 28% of balls played in the first tempo after the serve in the jump and as much as 49.3% with the other type of serve. In women, the type of serve was also evaluated. Quiroga et al.22, observing elite volleyball players during the Indesit European Champions League (2004/2005 and 2005/2006), noticed that the most common serve is the float serve from the spot. This type of serve accounted for 48.6% of all serves, followed by JS—23.9% and jump float serve (JF) only 17%. Ciuffarella et al.12 analyzed the serve in the ZAS male Top League Italian Volleyball 2008/2009 round. The most common type of serve was JS (69.9%), the second most common type of serve was FJS (26.9%), followed by Float Serve (3.3%). Comparing the results with the data we obtained in the ZAS Tauron Liga round, women mainly used three types of serves (JS, JF and W), of which JF accounted for as much as 86% of all serves. Since 2004, changes in hitting the ball when making a serve at a higher range have been observed. The higher level of service execution is confirmed by the research of Agelonidis21 and the biomechanical analysis of the jump serve compared to the float serve23. As many as five types of serves were observed in men (JS, JF, FJS, FJF and W), of which over 67% were JS serves. A logical explanation would seem to be the statement that the JF serve is more effective than the JS, but it should be noted that the JF serve was only 14% effective for men, while as much as 24% for women. Therefore, it is not the type of serve but most likely the net height that may be the reason for women’s more effective use of this type of serve. For men, the serve is treated more not as putting the ball into play but as the first form of attack. The goal of the JS serve in men may be to score a point and, in important moments, to throw the opponent away from the net and make block-defense easier24.

Coaches of men’s teams are looking for new technical, tactical and motor solutions to increase the effectiveness of the serve. Players move away from easy technical plays, trying to increase their speed and hide the intended shot14,25. Moras et al.14, at the Men’s Olympic Qualification Tournament in Madrid, Spain, observed 9.5% W (the Float serve from the spot) serves with a whopping 84.9% JS. Ciuffarella et al.12, during the 2008–09 Men’s World Championship observed 3.3% no-jump serves, while in their current job, this type of serve was made only seven times during the entire regular season. This means that the serve from the place in elite men’s volleyball has practically ceased to be used. In the 2021/2022 season, a lower number of JS serves was observed in PlusLiga—69% than in Italy, 69.9%. In their place, the FJF serve began to be used more and more often (4%). The proportions of using a given type of serve observed in men at the Men’s World Championship and PlusLiga clearly differ from the type of serve performed by women. In women, the JF serve predominated; only 12% was the JS serve. This observation may be due to the fact that women have lower serve potential (speed and strength)26, which does not allow the use of “Fake” serves, in which performance must be performed at a high level of range and with greater strength than JF. A separate explanation for this fact is that JF’s serve is so effective for women that at the current championship level, additional solutions are unnecessary. “Fake” serves in women can be perfected during training, and their introduction in the match may occur in the upcoming league season. It is, therefore, worth conducting further observations in order to determine the evaluation of the serve in women.

In women, the W serve is still used, but there is a visible tendency to move away from the serve performed in the place. Since 2004/2005, the number of W serves has decreased from 48.6 to 2% in TauronLiga22. Tall and poorly coordinated players most often perform the W serve to increase the stroke’s precision and repeatability. This type of serve was the most effective in TauronLiga 2021/2022. This could be due to the stability of the serving player’s posture, the range of the player’s stroke and the lower height of the net than in men. The flat parabola of flight, combined with the high precision of hitting the ball, meant that the receiving players had a problem with the perfect reception of such a serve.

Differences in the effectiveness of the serve used between women and men are visible in the subsequent sets of the match. The effectiveness of women in set no. 1 was the highest and decreased with each successive set, while in men, a low level of efficiency was observed in set 1 and a systematic increase to set no. 3 and a decrease again in sets 4 and 5. Significant differences were observed between women and men, but no differences were observed within the same sex. Reducing the effectiveness of the serve in women is an inverse relationship compared to the height of the jump in subsequent sets observed by Pawlik and Mroczek26. The explanation for this relationship may be the dominant type of serve (JF) used in women, which does not require the player to jump to the maximum height, and the effectiveness most likely depends on the way the ball is hit. Pawlik and Mroczek26 observed that women jump much lower than their maximum capacity during the serve. The results varied between an average serve height of 32.3 cm versus an average attack height of 46.4 cm.

Volleyball matches can end with a score of 3:0, 3:1 or 3:2. In the match analysis, all analyzed matches were included in sets 1–3. In this part of the analysis, teams with similar and significant differences in the level of play are considered. In the remaining part of the analysis, 3:1 and 3:2 matches (sets 4–5) were considered, where teams at a similar sports level compete. Hence the natural tendency to decrease efficiency in sets 4 and 5 for both men and women. Despite the high jump level, men show lower efficiency in the first set26. This may indicate a high level of arousal, optimal performance and precision without a significant effect of CNS fatigue after warm-up, especially in the element of serving reception. At the time of increasing fatigue, the reception quality most likely deteriorates, and thus the effectiveness of the serve shows higher values27.

Comparing the quality and type of serve to the function performed on the pitch, one can notice certain dependencies. Opposite players most often use the JS serve. This function is mainly responsible for scoring points by attacking from zones 2 and 1. A similar movement pattern from the attack translates into the serve and the appropriate dynamics of the attacking movement14. The attackers obtain the effectiveness of the JS serve at the level: male—10%, female—7%. Center players (MB) are primarily responsible for blocking attacks and attacking. These are usually the tallest players on the team, who may have limitations in coordination and agility. Hence, players performing this function most often choose a “safe” type of serve28. For men, it is JF. For women, JF and W. Surprisingly, MB achieved a record W-type serve efficiency of 48% (n = 358 serves). Outside hitters are the most versatile feature on the pitch. This function performed all types of serves for men, with the JS serve being the most common. The players achieved the highest efficiency in FJS (E = 22%). However, these players made only 13 such serves in the main round. In women, the choice of JF (E = 26%) dominated, although the players also used JS (E = 7%) and W (E = 27%). Both women outside hitters and MB were characterized by the highest efficiency among players performing various functions. The last analyzed function was setters. For men, setters were the function with the most diverse serves. The most effective type of serve turned out to be FJS (E = 16%), followed by JF (E = 12%). In female setters, the majority of setters performed JF (E = 22%) and occasionally W (E = 64%, n = 19).

By carefully analyzing the observed plays of the players, broken down by sets and type of serve, certain tendencies can be observed. JS and JF serves for men, and JF serves for women were the most repetitive serves (most minor differentiation). The greatest differentiation (deviation) from the average effectiveness between sets and sets had the FJF serve in men and W in women. Most likely, this could be due to the small number of serves made. Both Opposite and Outside hitters were characterized by the smallest differences in the level of effectiveness of JS serve between sets and during a given set. The opposite conclusion was drawn after observing Middle blockers. Players performing this function maintained greater repeatability when performing the JF serve. The least varied serve in women was JF, except for the attackers. In the players performing this function, increasing efficiency was observed with each set and decreasing in the case of the JS serve. Such a discrepancy is most likely related to the frequent use of JS by attacking players. A large discrepancy in the effectiveness of JS’s serve in individual sets also occurred in MB players. The small number and high diversity of JS serves confirms that the MB function in women introduces the float serve (JF and W) much better than the coordinated JS serve, which is difficult to coordinate. Tall MB players who effectively and repeatedly introduce the W and JF serve could be good material for the experimental introduction of the “fake” serve in subsequent seasons.

Players acting as setters showed a similar deviation from the average efficiency level during both JS and JF play. The biggest deviation was recorded similarly to MB and Outside Hitters in the FJF serve. This analysis confirms that the FJF serve is in the development and implementation phase. With more repetition of this play, it can become an important type of play to start the action. Among all functions, a significant parameter deviation from the average serve efficiency in set 5 was observed in women and men. This may be due to high score pressure and making an easier serve or taking unnecessary risks in the serve area.

Many authors consider the serve one of the three winning or losing skills8–10. Similar observations were also found in the above work. In men, a negative statistically significant relationship between the occupied place and the effectiveness of the serve was shown. This meant that the more effectively the players performed the serve, the higher their place in the table. In women, such a relationship has not been demonstrated, but the trend of changes is similar. This may mean that despite the high efficiency, serving in women is not as important in achieving a higher place in the table (ranking position) than men. However, the observations are convergent in the case of the number of serves and the space occupied. The more serves the team made, the significantly higher place in the table was achieved.

Conclusion

The serve in volleyball is a key element of the game. With it, you can score a direct point or make it difficult for the opponent to take action. The quality of the serve determines how many players the receiving team will engage to perform an offensive action. The more difficult the serve, the fewer options the opponent has (from 4 to 1 player in attack). Thus, it is easier to anticipate the distribution of the ball for attack and organization for the serving team in a block-defense game and counter-attack. The team scoring a point in the next action must make the serve as difficult as possible (passing the ball to the opponent) because the opponent is in possession of the ball, and they create the offensive action that can bring him a point with the limited influence of our defensive actions.

The serve element has been modified over the years to obtain better effects conducive to winning. Ranging from increasing the speed of the serve, eliminating the serves from the spot in favor of the jump serves, to the use of serves to hide the correct hit of the ball (“Fake”—FFJ, FJS). FJF and FJS serves are more and more commonly used in men, while in women’s TauronLiga, they are not used today. The observed trends of changes in the use of the serve may suggest that in the coming season, we will be able to observe “fake” serves in women and a greater number and effectiveness of this type of serves in men.

miR-885-3p promotes hepatocellular carcinoma metastasis by targeting TASP1 to stabilize HIF-1α and activate hypoxia-driven angiogenic and invasive programs
Authors: Yunfeng Zhao, Haoxin Shen, Yibo Sun, Jinkun Song, Jing Bo, Shan Zheng, Chenguang Sun, Jinxiu Li, Hongyu Wang, Yinsen Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) progression is strongly influenced by hypoxia-driven HIF-1α signaling, which promotes EMT, invasion, angiogenesis, and metabolic adaptation. This study investigated whether miR-885-3p regulates HCC malignant phenotypes through the TASP1/HIF-1α axis.MethodsHuh7 cells were transfected with miR-885-3p mimic, inhibitor, or controls. RT-qPCR, Western blotting, immunofluorescence, and dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed to assess miR-885-3p, TASP1, and HIF-1α regulation. Functional assays evaluated EMT, migration, invasion, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, metabolism, colony formation, adhesion, focal adhesion formation, and cytoskeletal remodeling. TASP1 restoration was used to test rescue effects. GEO datasets and 30 HCC patient samples were analyzed for clinical correlations.ResultsHypoxia increased miR-885-3p expression and enhanced HIF-1α nuclear accumulation. miR-885-3p directly targeted the TASP1 3′-UTR, suppressed TASP1 expression, and promoted HIF-1α activation. miR-885-3p overexpression induced EMT, migration, invasion, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, glycolytic activity, clonogenicity, adhesion, and F-actin remodeling, whereas miR-885-3p inhibition produced opposite effects. TASP1 restoration reduced HIF-1α activation and partially reversed migration, invasion, and angiogenesis. Clinical analyses showed inverse correlations between miR-885-3p and TASP1 and positive correlations with VIM, VEGFA, MMP2, and HMOX1.ConclusionmiR-885-3p promotes HCC malignant phenotypes partly by targeting TASP1 and activating HIF-1α-associated signaling. Further in vivo validation is required.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s11033-026-12335-3.Keywords: miR-885-3p, TASP1, HIF-1α, hepatocellular carcinoma, metastasis, hypoxia

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the most common type of malignant primary liver tumors and one of the major causes of cancer-associated deaths globally, contributing significantly to morbidity and mortality, despite recent therapeutic interventions [1, 2]. The prognosis of patients with HCC to be poor, mainly owing to its high incidence of recurrence, intrahepatic metastasis, and resistance to available treatment options like surgery, radiofrequency ablation, transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), and targeted therapy using drugs such as sorafenib and Lenvatinib [3, 4]. Hypoxia, which characterizes HCC, is directly related to tumor aggressiveness, metastasis development, and poor prognosis [5]. The induction of hypoxia inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF-1α) by hypoxia leads to gene expression that stimulates angiogenesis, metabolic alterations, epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT), invasion, and metastasis [6], thereby identifying HIF-1α as an important factor for HCC tumor-induced malignant phenotype under hypoxic circumstances.

HIF-1α is a transcription factor regulated by oxygen levels, which gets degraded under normal oxygen conditions through proline hydroxylation and ubiquitination facilitated by Von Hippel Lindau (VHL) [7]. But when hypoxia occurs, the hydroxylation of HIF-1α stops, thus permitting the HIF-1α protein to build up, enter the nucleus, form a complex with HIF 1β, and regulate the expression of many genes involved in angiogenesis (VEGF), glycolysis (GLUT 1), survival, and EMT. HIF-1α pathways have been demonstrated to confer invasive and metastatic characteristics in HCC by activating EMT-inducing transcription factors like SNAI1 and by modifying cell-cell interactions, such as the expression of E cadherin, N cadherin, and vimentin [8, 9]. The hypoxia-induced pathways not only promote tumor cell migration and invasion but also reprogram the tumor microenvironment for angiogenesis and metabolism to aid tumor progression.

MiRNAs have recently been recognized as important regulators of cancer development, growth, and metastasis, including HCC, via direct interaction with oncogenes or tumor suppressor genes that control proliferation, cell death, angiogenesis, and metastasis [10–12]. It is clear that the dysregulation of certain miRNAs may lead to the development of malignancy and affect treatment outcomes and patient survival [13, 14]. As an illustration, miR-199a 3p has been reported to act as a negative regulator for tumor proliferation and angiogenesis in HCC via the targeting of several proangiogenic molecules such as VEGFA and their receptors [15, 16]. Furthermore, miRNAs like miR-26a have been proven to act against angiogenesis in HCC by reducing the level of VEGFA expression via PIK3C2α/Akt/HIF 1α pathways [10, 17].

The various microRNAs that have been associated with the process of cancer development including miR-885-3p. This miRNA plays a role in the process of tumor development on a case-by-case basis [18]. MIR885 is the gene that expresses the precursor of miR-885, which is then processed by the enzymes, Drosha and Dicer. In nonhepatic cells, miR-885-3p has been found to control angiogenesis and tumor growth by acting on proangiogenic signaling molecules like BMPR1A and interfering with BMP/Smad/ID1 pathway in colorectal cancer cells [19, 20]. In addition, the expression of miR-885-3p has been shown to have an impact on the viability of tumor cells and their response to chemo drugs in specific cancer cells, implying possible role of this microRNA in cell death and survival pathways [21, 22]. However, the exact biological significance of miR-885-3p in the development of HCC and its targets under hypoxic conditions are not well understood.

Given the roles of hypoxia and miRNAs in HCC progression, this study investigated whether miR-885-3p promotes HCC metastasis by regulating the TASP1/HIF-1α pathway. We hypothesized that miR-885-3p suppresses TASP1, thereby enhancing HIF-1α stabilization and activating hypoxia-driven angiogenic and invasive programs. This work aimed to clarify the functional relationship between miR-885-3p, TASP1, and HIF-1α signaling and to identify a potential regulatory axis involved in HCC metastasis.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and mycoplasma testing

HuH-7 human hepatocellular carcinoma cells were obtained from the Japanese Collection of Research Bioresources Cell Bank (JCRB Cell Bank, JCRB0403). Cells were maintained in high-glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 11995065) supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (FBS; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. A3160701) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (100 U/mL and 100 µg/mL; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 15140122). Cultures were incubated at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂. Cells were routinely subcultured before reaching 90% confluence and monitored for morphology and growth rate. All cultures were confirmed negative for mycoplasma contamination prior to experimental use using the MycoAlert™ Mycoplasma Detection Kit (Lonza, Cat. No. LT07-318) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and tested at regular intervals throughout the study.

miRNA modulation and hypoxia treatment

To manipulate miR-885-3p expression, Huh7 cells were transfected with miR-885-3p mimic or miR-885-3p inhibitor oligonucleotides and their corresponding scramble controls (Thermo Fisher Scientific Pre-designed miRNA mimic/inhibitor, Cat. Nos. 4464066/4464084) using Lipofectamine™ RNAiMAX Transfection Reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 13778030) as per the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, 50–100 nM of mimic or inhibitor was complexed with RNAiMAX in Opti-MEM Reduced Serum Medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 31985070), incubated with cells for 6 h, then replaced with complete medium. For hypoxia experiments, transfected cells were cultured in a humidified hypoxia chamber at 1% O₂, 5% CO₂ using a gas-regulated workstation for 24–48 h to induce HIF-1α stabilization and hypoxia-responsive gene expression. To evaluate whether hypoxia regulates miR-885-3p expression, Huh7 cells were cultured under normoxic or hypoxic conditions for 24 h, and miR-885-3p levels were quantified using the RT-qPCR.

RT-qPCR

Total RNA, including small RNAs, was extracted using the mirVana™ miRNA Isolation Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. AM1561) following the supplier’s protocol. RNA purity and concentration were assessed by NanoDrop spectrophotometry (Thermo Fisher Scientific). For miRNA analysis, 10 ng of total RNA was reverse-transcribed using the TaqMan™ MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 4366597) with specific stem-loop primers. RT-qPCR was performed with TaqMan™ MicroRNA Assays for miR-885-3p (Assay ID: Hs_miR-885-3p, FAM-labeled; Thermo Fisher Scientific) and small nuclear RNA U6 (Assay ID: Assay for U6, Thermo Fisher Scientific) on a QuantStudio™ 7 Flex Real-Time PCR System. Relative miRNA levels were calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method normalized to U6. For mRNA expression, cDNA was synthesized from 500 ng of total RNA using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 4368814). Gene expression of HIF1A (Assay ID: Hs00153153_m1, Thermo Fisher Scientific), MMP2 (Assay ID: Hs01548727_m1), MMP9 (Assay ID: Hs00957562_m1), VEGFA (Assay ID: Hs00900055_m1), GLUT1 (Assay ID: Hs00892681_m1), and endogenous controls (GAPDH, Assay ID: Hs02758991_g1) were quantified using TaqMan™ Gene Expression Assays (Thermo Fisher Scientific) with TaqMan™ Universal PCR Master Mix (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 4324018). All reactions were run in triplicate with appropriate no-template controls.

Western blotting

Whole-cell lysates were prepared by lysing cells in RIPA buffer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 89900) supplemented with protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 78440). Lysates were clarified by centrifugation, and protein concentrations were determined by the Pierce BCA Protein Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 23227). Equal amounts of protein (20–40 µg) were resolved on 8–12% SDS-PAGE gels and transferred onto PVDF membranes (Millipore, Cat. No. IPVH00010). Membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against TASP1 (rabbit polyclonal, Invitrogen/Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. PA5-44679; expected band ~ 50 kDa), HIF-1α (rabbit monoclonal, Cell Signaling Technology, Cat. No. 3716; expected band ~ 120 kDa), E-cadherin (mouse monoclonal, BD Biosciences, Cat. No. 610181; expected band ~ 120 kDa), N-cadherin (rabbit monoclonal, Cell Signaling Technology, Cat. No. 13116; expected band ~ 140 kDa), vimentin (rabbit monoclonal, Cell Signaling Technology, Cat. No. 5741; expected band ~ 57 kDa), VEGF (rabbit polyclonal, Abcam, Cat. No. ab46154), MMP2 (mouse monoclonal, Abcam, Cat. No. ab86607), and MMP9 (rabbit polyclonal, Abcam, Cat. No. ab76003). β-actin (mouse monoclonal, Sigma-Aldrich, Cat. No. A1978; expected band ~ 42 kDa) was used as the loading control. After washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (anti-rabbit, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 31460; anti-mouse, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 32430) and developed using ECL substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 34577). Band intensities were quantified by densitometry using ImageJ.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The direct interaction between miR-885-3p and the TASP1 3′-UTR was examined using a dual-luciferase reporter assay. Wild-type TASP1 3′-UTR fragments containing the predicted miR-885-3p binding site and mutant fragments carrying substitutions in the seed-binding region were synthesized and inserted downstream of the firefly luciferase gene in the pmirGLO Dual-Luciferase miRNA Target Expression Vector (Promega, Cat. No. E1330). The resulting constructs were designated TASP1-WT and TASP1-MUT and were confirmed by Sanger sequencing. Huh7 cells were seeded in 24-well plates (Corning Costar, Cat. No. 3524) and co-transfected with TASP1-WT or TASP1-MUT reporter plasmids together with miR-885-3p mimic or scramble control using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. L3000015) in Opti-MEM Reduced Serum Medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 31985070). The miR-885-3p mimic and scramble control were obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Cat. No. 4464066). After 24–48 h, luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, Cat. No. E1910) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to Renilla luciferase activity, and the relative luciferase activity of the scramble control group was set to 1.0.

Immunofluorescence microscopy

Cells on coverslips were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 28908), permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, blocked with 5% BSA, and incubated with antibodies against HIF-1α, E-cadherin, and vimentin, followed by Alexa Fluor–conjugated secondary antibodies (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Nuclei were stained with DAPI (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. D1306), and images were acquired by confocal microscopy under standardized settings.

Endothelial tube formation assay

To evaluate angiogenic potential, conditioned media were collected from Huh7 cultures after 48 h of transfection and used to treat human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. C0035C) seeded on Matrigel (Corning, Cat. No. 356234) -coated 96-well plates at 1 × 10⁴ cells/well. Tube formation was imaged 6–8 h post-seeding with an inverted microscope, and the numbers of tubes, total tube length, and branching points were quantified with ImageJ.

Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) and metabolic assays

ROS levels were measured using DCFH-DA (Sigma-Aldrich, Cat. No. D6883). Glucose uptake, lactate production, and ATP content were assessed using 2-NBDG (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. N13195), a Lactate Assay Kit (Sigma-Aldrich, Cat. No. MAK064), and CellTiter-Glo® assay (Promega, Cat. No. G7570), respectively, according to the manufacturers’ protocols.

Wound healing (Scratch) assay

Huh7 cells were transfected with miR-885-3p mimic, inhibitor, or scramble control and grown to 90–100% confluence in 6-well plates. Monolayers were scratched with a sterile 200 µL pipette tip, washed with PBS, and cultured in serum-reduced DMEM (0.5% FBS). Images were captured at 0, 12, 24, and 36 h using an inverted phase-contrast microscope, and wound closure was quantified with ImageJ.

Cell migration and invasion assays

Cell migration and invasion were assessed using Transwell inserts with 8.0 μm pores. For migration assays, uncoated Transwell inserts were used (Corning, Cat. No. 3422), whereas invasion assays were performed using Matrigel-coated invasion chambers (Corning BioCoat Matrigel Invasion Chamber, Cat. No. 354480). Cells were seeded in serum-free DMEM in the upper chamber, and medium containing 10% FBS was added to the lower chamber as chemoattractant. After 24 h, migrated or invaded cells were fixed, stained with 0.5% crystal violet, and counted in five random fields per insert.

Colony formation assay

Transfected Huh7 cells were seeded in 6-well plates at 500 cells/well and cultured for 10–14 days, with medium changed every 3 days. Colonies were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 28908), stained with 0.5% crystal violet, air-dried, and counted manually. Colony number and size were quantified using ImageJ.

Cell adhesion assay

Ninety-six–well plates were coated overnight at 4 °C with 10 µg/mL fibronectin (Sigma-Aldrich, Cat. No. F0895) or collagen I (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. A1048301). Transfected Huh7 cells were seeded at 1 × 10⁴ cells/well and incubated for 1 h at 37 °C. After PBS washing, adherent cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 28908), stained with 0.5% crystal violet, solubilized with 10% acetic acid, and absorbance was measured at 570 nm.

Focal adhesion formation (Paxillin/FAK immunofluorescence)

Transfected Huh7 cells were seeded on fibronectin-coated coverslips at 2 × 10⁴ cells/coverslip and allowed to adhere for 4–6 h. Cells were fixed, permeabilized, blocked with 5% BSA, and incubated overnight at 4 °C with antibodies against paxillin (Cell Signaling Technology, Cat. No. 2542) or phospho-FAK Tyr397 (Cell Signaling Technology, Cat. No. 3283), followed by Alexa Fluor–conjugated secondary antibodies (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Nuclei were stained with DAPI (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. D1306), and images were acquired using a Leica SP8 confocal microscope. Focal adhesion number and size were quantified using ImageJ with the Focal Adhesion Analysis Server plugin.

Early EMT marker detection by flow cytometry

Huh7 cells transfected with miR-885-3p mimic, inhibitor, or scramble control were harvested after 48 h, washed with PBS, and stained with E-cadherin-FITC (BD Biosciences, Cat. No. 561567) and N-cadherin-PE (BD Biosciences, Cat. No. 561864) for 30 min at 4 °C in the dark. Cells were analyzed using a BD LSRFortessa flow cytometer, and marker-positive populations were quantified using FlowJo v10.

F-actin cytoskeleton remodeling

Huh7 cells grown on coverslips were transfected for 48 h, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, and stained with Phalloidin-Alexa Fluor 488 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. A12379) to visualize F-actin. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and images were acquired using a Leica SP8 confocal microscope. Stress fiber density and filopodia number/length were quantified using ImageJ.

In silico expression correlation analysis

In silico expression correlation analysis was conducted to investigate the correlation of miR-885-3p and TASP1 with hypoxia- and metastasis-associated target genes in HCC patients. The expression levels of miR 885 3p in miRNAs were obtained from Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) dataset GSE113740, which includes 40 samples from HCC patient sera and 40 control samples [23]. The expression profiles of TASP1, VEGFA, and MMP2 mRNA were extracted from GEO datasets GSE45114 and GSE10694 where paired samples of HCC tumors and surrounding non-tumors tissues were available. A sample size of n = 30 patients with corresponding miRNA and mRNA expression profiles was selected for correlational analysis. The data was standardized and log-transformed before performing correlations. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to identify the linear association between miR 885 3p and each target mRNA, with p-value < 0.05 set as the cutoff for significance. Scatter plots displaying linear regressions and 95% confidence intervals were created using Python libraries matplotlib, numpy, and scipy.

TASP1 restoration/rescue assay

To verify whether TASP1 restoration reverses miR-885-3p-mediated HIF-1α activation and metastatic phenotypes, Huh7 cells were seeded in six-well plates and transfected at 60–70% confluence. Cells were divided into three groups: scramble control plus empty vector, miR-885-3p mimic plus empty vector, and miR-885-3p mimic plus TASP1 overexpression vector. The miR-885-3p mimic and scramble control were obtained from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Cat. No. 4464066), and the TASP1 overexpression plasmid was a human TASP1 tagged ORF clone constructed in pCMV6-Entry vector (OriGene Technologies, Cat. No. RC206835). The corresponding pCMV6-Entry empty vector was used as vector control (OriGene Technologies, Cat. No. PS100001). Transfections were performed using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. L3000015) in Opti-MEM Reduced Serum Medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Cat. No. 31985070) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. After 6 h, the transfection medium was replaced with complete DMEM, and cells were cultured for 24–48 h. Where indicated, cells were exposed to hypoxia as described above before collection. TASP1 restoration and HIF-1α expression were assessed by Western blotting using the antibodies described in the Western blotting section; exogenous TASP1 expression was additionally confirmed using anti-DDK antibody (OriGene Technologies, Cat. No. TA50011-100). HIF-1α nuclear localization, wound healing, Transwell invasion, and HUVEC tube formation assays were performed using the protocols described above. Conditioned medium from each group was collected 48 h after transfection for the HUVEC tube formation assay. All experiments were performed in three independent biological replicates.

Clinical samples and ethics approval

Clinical HCC samples used for correlation analysis were obtained from 30 patients under a protocol approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Zhengzhou People’s Hospital (Approval No. 2026-ZYLW-009). The study was conducted in accordance with institutional ethical requirements and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Patient-related data were anonymized before analysis. Informed consent was obtained from the patients prior to sample collecation. For clinical sample correlation analysis, miR-885-3p and representative metastasis-associated marker genes were quantified in 30 HCC samples by RT-qPCR. miR-885-3p expression was normalized to U6, whereas mRNA expression was normalized to GAPDH. TaqMan Gene Expression Assays were used for TASP1 (Hs01120949_m1), VIM (Hs00185584_m1), VEGFA (Hs00900055_m1), MMP2 (Hs01548727_m1), and HMOX1 (Hs01110250_m1) according to the manufacturer’s protocol (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Pearson correlation analysis was performed to assess associations between miR-885-3p expression and each mRNA marker, and p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed using at least three independent biological replicates unless otherwise stated. For RT-qPCR and plate-based assays, each biological replicate was measured in technical triplicate. For microscopy-based assays, multiple randomly selected fields were quantified for each biological replicate. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. Comparisons between two groups were analyzed using an unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test. Comparisons among three or more groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post hoc test. For wound-healing assays involving multiple time points, two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparison test was used. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical significance was indicated as *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, and ***p < 0.001.

Results

miR-885-3p directly targets TASP1 and modulates HIF-1α expression in Huh7 cells

RT-qPCR confirmed effective miR-885-3p modulation, with increased expression in mimic-transfected cells and reduced expression in inhibitor-treated cells (Fig. 1A). TASP1 mRNA showed the opposite pattern, decreasing after miR-885-3p mimic transfection and increasing after inhibition (Fig. 1B). Western blotting further showed reduced TASP1 and increased HIF-1α protein levels in mimic-treated cells, whereas miR-885-3p inhibition produced the reverse effect (Figs. 1C–E and Figure S1). Dual-luciferase reporter analysis confirmed direct targeting of the TASP1 3′-UTR by miR-885-3p; the mimic significantly reduced wild-type reporter activity, while mutation of the seed-binding site abolished this effect (Figs. 1F–G). These findings indicate that miR-885-3p directly suppresses TASP1 and is associated with increased HIF-1α accumulation in Huh7 cells.

miR-885-3p modulates TASP1 expression and directly targets the TASP1 3′-UTR in Huh7 cells. (A) RT-qPCR analysis of miR-885-3p expression in Huh7 cells transfected with scramble control, miR-885-3p mimic, or miR-885-3p inhibitor. (B) RT-qPCR analysis of TASP1 mRNA expression in the indicated groups. (C) Representative Western blot images showing TASP1, HIF-1α, and β-actin protein expression. (D, E) Quantification of TASP1 and HIF-1α protein levels normalized to β-actin. (F) Schematic representation of the predicted miR-885-3p binding site within the TASP1 3′-UTR and the corresponding mutant sequence used for reporter analysis. (G) Dual-luciferase reporter assay showing relative luciferase activity in Huh7 cells co-transfected with wild-type or mutant TASP1 3′-UTR reporter constructs and either miR-885-3p mimic or scramble control. miR-885-3p mimic significantly reduced luciferase activity of the wild-type TASP1 3′-UTR reporter, whereas mutation of the seed-binding site abolished this effect. Data are presented as mean ± SD from three independent biological replicates. ns, not significant; *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001

Hypoxia upregulates miR-885-3p and enhances HIF-1α nuclear accumulation in Huh7 cells

To determine whether miR-885-3p is hypoxia-responsive, Huh7 cells were cultured under normoxic or hypoxic conditions and analyzed by RT-qPCR. Hypoxia significantly increased miR-885-3p expression compared with normoxia (Fig. 2A). Immunofluorescence analysis further showed that HIF-1α staining and nuclear localization were increased under hypoxia and further enhanced by miR-885-3p mimic, whereas miR-885-3p inhibition attenuated hypoxia-induced HIF-1α nuclear accumulation (Fig. 2B). Quantification of the nuclear/cytoplasmic HIF-1α ratio confirmed increased nuclear localization in mimic-transfected cells and reduced localization in inhibitor-transfected cells under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions (Fig. 2C). These findings indicate that hypoxia upregulates miR-885-3p and that miR-885-3p promotes HIF-1α nuclear translocation, particularly under hypoxic conditions.

Hypoxia upregulates miR-885-3p and enhances HIF-1α nuclear accumulation in Huh7 cells. (A) RT-qPCR analysis of miR-885-3p expression in Huh7 cells cultured under normoxic or hypoxic conditions. miR-885-3p expression was normalized to U6. (B) Representative immunofluorescence images showing HIF-1α localization in control, miR-885-3p mimic-, and miR-885-3p inhibitor-transfected Huh7 cells under normoxic and hypoxic conditions. HIF-1α is shown in green, nuclei are counterstained with DAPI in blue, and merged images are shown. Scale bar, 20 μm. (C) Quantification of the nuclear/cytoplasmic HIF-1α fluorescence ratio. Hypoxia significantly increased miR-885-3p expression and promoted HIF-1α nuclear accumulation, while miR-885-3p overexpression further enhanced, and miR-885-3p inhibition attenuated, HIF-1α nuclear localization. Data are presented as mean ± SD from three independent biological replicates. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001

miR-885-3p promotes an epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT) phenotype in Huh7 cells

Western blot and immunofluorescence analyses showed that miR-885-3p promoted EMT in Huh7 cells. E-cadherin expression was significantly reduced in mimic-transfected cells (p < 0.01) and increased after miR-885-3p inhibition (p < 0.05; Figs. 3A–B and Figure S1). In contrast, N-cadherin and Vimentin were markedly increased by miR-885-3p mimic (p < 0.01) and suppressed by the inhibitor (p < 0.05). Immunofluorescence further confirmed loss of membranous E-cadherin and increased filament-like Vimentin staining in mimic-treated cells, whereas miR-885-3p inhibition restored E-cadherin localization and reduced Vimentin expression (Fig. 3C).

miR-885-3p promotes an EMT phenotype in Huh7 cells. (A) Western blots of epithelial marker E-cadherin and mesenchymal markers N-cadherin and Vimentin. (B) Quantification of protein expression levels. (C) Immunofluorescence images showing E-cadherin (green) and Vimentin (red) localization. Mimic-transfected cells exhibit reduced E-cadherin and increased N-cadherin and Vimentin, whereas inhibitor-transfected cells show the opposite. Data are mean ± SD, n = 3. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 versus control

miR-885-3p Enhances Huh7 Cell Migration

To evaluate the effect of miR-885-3p on Huh7 cell motility, we performed a wound healing scratch assay. Representative micrographs tracking scratch closure at 0, 12, 24, and 36 h are shown in Fig. 4A, and the statistical quantification of wound closure percentages is presented in Fig. 4B. Cells transfected with the miR-885-3p mimic exhibited progressively accelerated wound closure over time compared to the scramble control, achieving a highly significant increase in migration by the 36-hour endpoint (p < 0.01). In sharp contrast, inhibitor-transfected cells displayed severely delayed migratory capabilities relative to the control group, resulting in a highly marked reduction in wound closure at 36 h (p < 0.001).

miR-885-3p enhances Huh7 cell migration.(A) Representative wound healing images at 0, 12, 24, and 36 h post-scratch in control, mimic, and inhibitor-transfected cells. (B) Quantification of % wound closure over time. Mimic increases migration, while inhibitor reduces migration compared to control. Data represent mean ± SD of three independent experiments. **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001 versus control

miR-885-3p promotes cell invasion and angiogenesis

Transwell assays showed that miR-885-3p mimic significantly increased Huh7 cell invasion compared with scramble control (p < 0.01), whereas miR-885-3p inhibition markedly reduced invasion (p < 0.001; Figs. 5A, B). HUVEC tube formation assays using conditioned media further showed that mimic-conditioned media enhanced total tube length, branch points, and VEGF secretion (p < 0.01 for all), while inhibitor-conditioned media significantly suppressed all angiogenic parameters (p < 0.001 for all; Figs. 5C–F). These results indicate that miR-885-3p promotes invasive and pro-angiogenic phenotypes in Huh7 cells.

(C) HUVEC tube formation images after treatment with conditioned media from control, mimic, and inhibitor-transfected cells. (D–F) Quantification of total tube length, branch points, and VEGF secretion. Mimic significantly enhances invasion and angiogenic potential, whereas inhibitor reduces these parameters. Data are mean ± SD, n = 3. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001 versus control

miR-885-3p enhances oxidative stress and glycolytic activity in Huh7 cells

Metabolic and redox changes were assessed by measuring ROS, glucose uptake, lactate production, and ATP levels (Figs. 6A–D). miR-885-3p mimic significantly increased ROS accumulation, glucose uptake, lactate production, and ATP levels compared with scramble control (p < 0.01 for all; Figs. 6A–D). Conversely, miR-885-3p inhibition significantly reduced all measured parameters (p < 0.05 for all), indicating that miR-885-3p enhances oxidative stress and metabolic activity in Huh7 cells.

(D) Cellular ATP levels. Mimic increases all metabolic and oxidative parameters, whereas inhibitor reduces them compared with control. Data are mean ± SD from three independent experiments. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01 versus control

miR-885-3p enhances Huh7 cell colony formation

Colony formation assays showed that miR-885-3p enhanced the clonogenic potential of Huh7 cells. Mimic-transfected cells formed significantly more colonies and showed larger colony areas than scramble controls (p < 0.01 for both; Figs. 7A–C). In contrast, miR-885-3p inhibition markedly reduced colony number and size (p < 0.001 for both), indicating that miR-885-3p promotes Huh7 cell proliferation and clonogenic growth in vitro.

miR-885-3p promotes Huh7 cell colony formation. (A) Representative crystal violet-stained colonies. (B) Quantification of colony number. (C) Quantification of average colony size. Mimic-transfected cells formed significantly more and larger colonies, whereas inhibitor-transfected cells displayed reduced colony formation. Data represent mean ± SD, n = 3. *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001 versus control

miR-885-3p enhances cell adhesion and focal adhesion formation

Cell adhesion and focal adhesion dynamics were assessed using substrate adhesion and Paxillin/phospho-FAK immunofluorescence assays. Mimic-transfected cells showed significantly increased adhesion compared with scramble control (p < 0.01), whereas inhibitor-transfected cells showed reduced adhesion (p < 0.05; Figure S2A). Confocal imaging revealed larger and more abundant Paxillin/phospho-FAK–positive focal adhesions in the Mimic group, while miR-885-3p inhibition produced fewer and smaller adhesions (Figure S2B). Quantification confirmed increased focal adhesion number and area in the Mimic group (p < 0.01) and reduced focal adhesion number in the Inhibitor group (p < 0.05). Focal adhesion area was also significantly lower in the Inhibitor group than in the Mimic group (p < 0.05; Figure S2C).

miR-885-3p promotes early EMT and cytoskeleton remodeling

Early EMT and cytoskeletal remodeling were assessed by flow cytometry and phalloidin staining. miR-885-3p mimic reduced E-cadherin–positive cells (p < 0.01) and increased N-cadherin–positive cells (p < 0.01), whereas miR-885-3p inhibition increased E-cadherin retention and reduced N-cadherin expression (p < 0.05; Figure S3A). Phalloidin staining showed dense F-actin stress fibers and elongated filopodia in mimic-transfected cells, while inhibitor-treated cells showed limited cytoskeletal extensions (Figure S3B). Quantification confirmed increased stress fiber density and filopodia number in the Mimic group (p < 0.01) and significant reductions in the Inhibitor group (p < 0.05; Figure S3C).

Integrated GEO and clinical sample correlation analysis of miR-885-3p with TASP1 and metastasis-associated markers

Correlation analyses were performed using integrated GEO datasets and 30 HCC patient samples to assess the clinical relevance of the miR-885-3p/TASP1 axis. In the GEO cohort, miR-885-3p was negatively correlated with TASP1 (r = − 0.45, p = 0.013; Figure S4A) and positively correlated with VEGFA (r = 0.52, p = 0.003; Figure S4B) and MMP2 (r = 0.47, p = 0.009; Figure S4C). Consistently, in 30 HCC samples, miR-885-3p was inversely associated with TASP1 (r = − 0.43, p = 0.018; Figure S4D) and positively correlated with VIM (r = 0.42, p = 0.021; Figure S4E), VEGFA (r = 0.49, p = 0.006; Figure S4F), MMP2 (r = 0.45, p = 0.012; Figure S4G), and HMOX1 (r = 0.39, p = 0.033; Figure S4H), supporting its association with EMT, angiogenesis, invasion, and oxidative-stress-related features in HCC.

TASP1 restoration reverses miR-885-3p-induced HIF-1α activation, migration, invasion, and angiogenesis

To determine whether TASP1 mediates miR-885-3p-induced HIF-1α activation, TASP1 restoration was performed in miR-885-3p mimic-transfected Huh7 cells. Western blotting showed that miR-885-3p mimic reduced TASP1 and increased HIF-1α, whereas TASP1 overexpression restored TASP1 and attenuated HIF-1α accumulation (Figs. 8A–C and Figure S1). Immunofluorescence further showed that TASP1 restoration reduced miR-885-3p-induced HIF-1α nuclear localization and nuclear/cytoplasmic ratio (Figs. 8D–E). RT-qPCR demonstrated that TASP1 restoration suppressed VEGFA, MMP2, MMP9, and GLUT1 expression induced by miR-885-3p mimic (Fig. 8F). Functionally, TASP1 restoration reduced wound closure, Transwell invasion, and HUVEC tube formation enhanced by miR-885-3p mimic (Figs. 8G–M). These results indicate that TASP1 partially mediates the miR-885-3p/HIF-1α axis and its effects on migration, invasion, and angiogenesis.

Discussion

HCC is one of the most lethal forms of cancer globally, known for its ability to cause rapid tumor growth, intra-hepatic metastasis, and resistance to conventional treatments [24, 25]. One of the key drivers of HCC progression is the development of hypoxic conditions in the tumor microenvironment due to the fast proliferation of tumor cells, resulting in hypoxic tumor areas with low oxygen concentration levels [26]. Under hypoxic conditions, the HIF-1α is activated and involved in many cellular processes, such as EMT [27]. High levels of HIF-1α in HCC patients correlate with unfavorable clinical characteristics and a worse prognosis and recurrence rate [6]. Hypoxic induction of HIF-1α leads to its translocation into the nucleus, binding to HIF-1β, and transcriptional upregulation of genes involved in tumor progression and metastasis, such as VEGFA, GLUT1, SNAI1, and MMPs [28, 29].

miRNAs are defined as short non-coding RNAs that control the gene expression at the post-transcriptional level through binding to the 3′-untranslated region (3′-UTR) of the mRNA target and induce its degradation and inhibit translation [30]. miRNAs are critical in cancer biology by controlling many biological processes such as cell proliferation, apoptosis, angiogenesis, and metastasis [30]. Alterations of miRNAs lead to HCC tumor progression and chemoresistance, and some of them are regulated by hypoxic conditions. Nevertheless, hypoxia may also exert influence on the patterns of miRNA expression, and the resulting changes are able to exert feedback control on the hypoxia-mediated mechanisms, creating a complex network of regulatory mechanisms [31]. In this regard, one example is miR-199a-3p, which is capable of reducing the proliferation and angiogenesis of HCC tumor cells through the inhibition of proangiogenic mediators, including VEGFA [32]. Another example is miR-26a, which can repress angiogenesis via VEGFA suppression through the PI3K/AKT/HIF-1α pathway [33]. These examples highlight the critical intersection of miRNA-mediated regulation with hypoxia signaling in shaping the malignant phenotype.

Of all the miRNAs, miR-885-3p has gained attention as a context-specific regulator of tumor progression. This is because miR-885-3p is derived from the precursor MIR885 gene and acts through the RNA-induced silencing complex to regulate specific mRNAs [33]. In tumors other than those of the liver, miR-885-3p has been found to play a role in angiogenesis and proliferation. One such case is colorectal cancer where miR-885-3p regulates the BMP pathway through BMPR1A regulation [21]. Moreover, miR-885-3p is involved in controlling drug resistance and apoptosis signaling in lung and stomach cancer, indicating that it could play an essential role in cellular survival under stress conditions [34]. Nevertheless, the direct involvement of miR-885-3p in hepatocellular carcinoma development and the regulation of hypoxia-sensitive pathways had yet to be uncovered.

TASP1 is a specific protease that regulates proteins like the MLL and helps regulate transcriptional regulation of proliferation and survival [35]. Although not an oncogene by definition, abnormal levels of expression of TASP1 have been found to contribute to cancer development and progression of stomach, breast, and gallbladder cancer cells [36]. One possible mechanism whereby TASP1 exerts its effects on HCC is through the regulation of the stability or activity of transcription factors associated with hypoxia signaling, such as HIF-1α, although precise mechanisms have not been explored fully. An important area to explore is the interplay between miRNAs and TASP1.

In our research, we observed a negative regulation of TASP1 by miR-885-3p, which results in an increase in the stability and localization of HIF-1α in the nucleus. We show a combination of post-transcriptional regulation of the protease and classical hypoxia pathway in promoting hepatocellular carcinoma development. The cells transfected with the miR-885-3p mimic showed decreased expression of TASP1, while silencing miR-885-3p increased the level of TASP1. The amount of HIF-1α protein was negatively associated with TASP1, which suggests that TASP1 could potentially help stabilize HIF-1α protein turnover under normal circumstances. This finding is consistent with research demonstrating that noncoding RNAs can modulate factors involved in the regulation of HIF-1α stabilization pathways to affect hypoxia adaptation [37, 38].

The effects of miR-885-3p expression on function can be summarized in multiple ways. miR-885-3p overexpression in Huh7 cells facilitated EMT through E-cadherin reduction and N-cadherin and Vimentin upregulation. On the other hand, suppression of miR-885-3p expression preserved epithelial properties of Huh7 cells. EMT activation resulted in cytoskeletal rearrangement with the increase of F-actin stress fibers, lamellipodia formation, and filopodia formation, which contributed to improved migration and invasion ability. This conclusion aligns well with the existing knowledge on HIF-1α function in EMT and cytoskeleton organization [39, 40].

Further analysis using migration and invasion assays revealed the metastatic characteristics associated with miR-885-3p. The results from wound healing and Transwell invasion assays showed that the mimic-transfected cells had enhanced migration and invasiveness compared to inhibitor-transfected cells, which had low migration and invasion activity. Migration and invasion properties are directly associated with focal adhesion kinetics, as indicated by higher numbers and sizes of focal adhesions in the mimic-transfected cells compared to the inhibitor-transfected cells, as confirmed by Paxillin and phospho-FAK staining. This suggests that miR-885-3p facilitates cell interaction with the ECM and promotes cytoskeleton rearrangement necessary for migration, as previously reported in metastatic HCC cells [41].

Angiogenesis represents an important effect downstream of HIF-1α signaling following exposure to hypoxia within tumors. Media conditioned with miR-885-3p mimics led to increased tubule and branch point formation as well as VEGF secretion compared to inhibitor-conditioned media, which had the opposite effect on these processes. This result indicates that miR-885-3p plays a role in establishing a pro-angiogenic secretome through HIF-1α signaling, as previously suggested by other studies [42], where hypoxia and HIF-1α induce angiogenesis through VEGF activity in HCC.

Metabolic reprogramming represents another hallmark of the development of HCC regulated by miR-885-3p. Mimic-transfected cells were associated with higher glucose uptake, lactate secretion, ATP production, and ROS generation, suggesting their adaptation to an increased rate of aerobic glycolysis with elevated energy production for sustaining EMT and migration. Cells transfected with the inhibitor were associated with decreased metabolic activity. These results correspond to the observations concerning the role of HIF-1α in mediating hypoxia-induced glycolysis and ROS formation [43, 44].

MiR-885-3p has been found to facilitate cell proliferation and survival through clonogenic assays showing increased colony numbers and sizes in cells transfected with the mimics. These proliferation benefits are combined with migration, invasion, and metabolism characteristics to confirm miR-885-3p as an all-encompassing regulator in HCC development. The synergistic control of cellular adhesion and focal adhesions by miR-885-3p further confirms its involvement in metastasis. Through strengthening the adhesive capabilities of cells and facilitating the establishment of focal adhesions, miR-885-3p facilitates dynamic interactions between the cells and the ECM, allowing the cells to break away from the main tumor mass and invade other tissue sites. This is in line with cytoskeletal reorganization and the observed EMT traits [45].

The findings of this study support a model in which hypoxia induces miR-885-3p, which suppresses TASP1 and enhances HIF-1α accumulation and nuclear localization in HCC cells. Activated HIF-1α signaling then promotes EMT, migration, invasion, VEGFA-mediated angiogenesis, MMP2/MMP9 expression, glycolytic activation, and oxidative stress. TASP1 restoration reduced HIF-1α activation, suppressed VEGFA, MMP2, MMP9, and GLUT1 expression, and partially reversed migration, invasion, and angiogenic activity, indicating that miR-885-3p-induced malignant phenotypes are partly mediated through the TASP1/HIF-1α axis. However, because miRNAs can regulate multiple targets and hypoxia can activate HIF-1α through several mechanisms, future HIF-1α inhibition and antioxidant rescue studies are needed to further define this pathway.

Despite these findings, a few limitations should be acknowledged. First, the study was mainly performed in a single Huh7 cell line; therefore, validation in additional HCC cell lines with diverse genetic backgrounds is required to confirm the broader relevance of the miR-885-3p/TASP1/HIF-1α axis. Second, in vivo animal experiments were not performed, and future subcutaneous, orthotopic, or metastasis models are needed to determine whether this axis regulates tumor growth, angiogenesis, invasion, and metastatic colonization in vivo. Third, the current work was based primarily on monoculture assays, whereas HCC progression is strongly influenced by the tumor microenvironment. Future studies using HCC–HUVEC co-culture, spheroids, organoids, and stromal or immune co-culture systems would help validate this pathway under more physiologically relevant conditions.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study shows that hypoxia-induced miR-885-3p promotes HCC malignant phenotypes by directly targeting TASP1 and enhancing HIF-1α activation. miR-885-3p overexpression induced EMT, migration, invasion, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, glycolytic activity, adhesion, and cytoskeletal remodeling, whereas TASP1 restoration partially reversed HIF-1α activation and associated metastatic phenotypes. GEO analysis and validation in 30 HCC samples further supported inverse association of miR-885-3p with TASP1 and positive associations with EMT, angiogenesis, invasion, and oxidative-stress markers. These findings identify the miR-885-3p/TASP1/HIF-1α axis as a potential regulator of hypoxia-associated HCC aggressiveness, although further validation in additional models and in vivo studies is required.

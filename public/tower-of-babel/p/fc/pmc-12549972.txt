A data driven model based approach for medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting in power markets
Authors: Jun Hu, Shaotang Cai, Feng Li, Yinghong Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting constitutes a critical prerequisite for electricity market participants to formulate optimal bidding strategies and mitigate energy procurement expenditures. However, medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting encounters challenges including high-dimensional data acquisition difficulty, forecasting mode complexity with poor adaptability, and high volatility of the forecasted prices. In this manuscript, a data-driven-model-based approach for medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting is proposed. Firstly, the key data sets are screened from the importance assessment based on the decision tree, and the historical electricity price, the coal price, and the natural gas price are chosen as the key price data to establish the medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting model to reduce the forecasted data dimension effectively. Secondly, the key data sequences are denoised with the volatility reduction through combining Fast Fourier Transformation (FFT). Then, GWO-CNN-LSTM-Attention model, combining Gray Wolf Optimization (GWO) algorithm, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), Long-Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network and Attention mechanism, is constructed for better forecasting performances and enhanced adaptability. Finally, the price time series are separated into the primary and residual frequencies. The primary frequency of the historical local electricity price is fed into GWO-CNN-LSTM-Attention algorithm to forecast the trend of the electricity price, while the primary frequencies of the historical coal price and the natural gas price are fed into GWO-CNN-LSTM-Attention algorithm to forecast the fluctuation of the electricity price, contributing to the high accuracy of the price forecasting in long term. The proposed FFT-GWO-CNN-LSTM-Attention (FGCLA) algorithm facilitates the dimension reduction of the forecasting dataset, the adaptability enhancement of the forecasting model, and the effective suppression of the high volatility, so as to improve the forecasting accuracy of the electricity price. The proposed algorithm is compared to the traditional LSTM and Transformer algorithms through simulation cases, with forecasting accuracy improvements of 57.21% and 49.69% respectively on average, concluding that the proposed algorithm could effectively reduce the forecasting errors of the medium-to-long-term electricity price.Keywords: Electricity market, Medium-To-Long-Term electricity price forecasting, Data-Driven-Model, Fast fourier transformation, Long-Short-Term memory networkSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

Electricity price is an important factor in the power market, and its accurate forecasting is conducive to the power market participants to make optimal market decisions, and a clear understanding of the operation of the power market, reducing the risk of power market transactions. Among them, accurate forecasting of medium-to-long-term electricity prices, mastering the trend of electricity price changes and the range of fluctuations will help the main participants in the electricity market to make optimal market decisions, reduce the cost of electricity consumption, increase the revenue of the electricity market, and improve the comprehensive competitiveness of the main participants in the electricity market1,2. However, medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting faces the problems of long forecasting time scale, wide data dimension and large data volume, which results in large fluctuation of medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting results and low forecasting accuracy3. Therefore, how to obtain the main features affecting the change of electricity price, construct an accurate medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting model, and improve the accuracy of medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting have become key issues to be solved urgently.

Currently, electricity price forecasting faces several challenges, including inconsistent time scales, complex influencing factors, and data processing difficulties. Researchers have predominantly adopted time series modeling as a foundational approach to electricity price prediction. Time series models, which are based on the temporal characteristics of historical data, typically include regression models, exponential smoothing methods, and vector autoregression (VAR) models. These methods are characterized by simple structures and high computational efficiency. However, their performance tends to degrade significantly when dealing with complex nonlinear relationships or high-dimensional feature inputs, often resulting in reduced predictive accuracy4. To overcome the limitations of traditional time series models, artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms have been increasingly introduced into electricity price forecasting, serving as an enhancement to time series modeling capabilities. Representative AI methods include Support Vector Machines (SVM)5,6, Extreme Learning Machines (ELM)7,8, and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks9,10. These approaches have demonstrated strong capabilities in modeling nonlinear mappings and extracting complex latent features, and have become key research directions in medium- and long-term electricity price forecasting in recent years. Literature11 introduces a novel electricity price forecasting approach combining Graph Convolutional Neural Networks and Long Short-Term Memory (GCN-LSTM). This method leverages operational data from Nord Pool, the Nordic power exchange, to forecast day-ahead market marginal electricity prices through comprehensive case analysis. Literature12 employs a simplified machine learning approach integrated with time series analysis, utilizing Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA) projection eigenvalues for electricity price forecasting. Literature13 introduces a novel electricity demand forecasting approach that employs a deep learning framework to effectively capture long-term dependencies within historical data. The methodology comprises two sequential phases. First, it performs data clustering on monthly electricity consumption data to generate seasonally segmented data. Second, it trains historical data using deep learning algorithms while incorporating seasonal factors and historical dependencies to establish an electricity demand forecasting model. Literature14 introduces a hybrid approach that combines feature extraction using multiple algorithms - including XGBoost (XGB), Decision Trees (DT), Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE), and Random Forests (RF) - with Enhanced Convolutional Neural Networks (ECNN) and Enhanced Support Vector Regression (ESVR) for simultaneous load and electricity price forecasting. While this method demonstrates improved forecasting accuracy, it faces limitations including substantial data requirements, overfitting susceptibility, and extended training durations associated with convolutional neural networks. Literature15 presents an innovative approach that diverges from traditional neural network paradigms by avoiding full parameter training in feedforward networks. This methodology randomly generates hidden node parameters (including input layer weights and biases) and employs Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) analysis to compute output weights, significantly enhancing both speed and accuracy in medium-term electricity price forecasting. Nevertheless, the ELM algorithm exhibits limitations in handling nonlinear relationships, which represent a substantial challenge as most electricity price determinants are inherently nonlinear. Literature16 leverages the inherent strength of Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks in processing time series data for electricity price forecasting. Firstly, stochastic search and meta-heuristic algorithms are used to obtain the optimal solutions of hyperparameters. Second, the LSTM network is used to forecast the electricity demand, which greatly shortens the forecasting time and improves the forecasting accuracy. Although the aforementioned methods have achieved satisfactory performance in short-term electricity price forecasting, they encounter notable limitations in medium- to long-term scenarios. Many existing models are structurally complex and heavily parameterized, relying on high-dimensional input features. These characteristics make them prone to overfitting and hinder their ability to effectively capture long-term trends, thus limiting their applicability in extended forecasting horizons.

This study addresses the nonlinear challenges affecting the accuracy of medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting, including data accessibility issues, high-dimensional datasets, inadequate model adaptability, and significant price volatility. By integrating decision tree algorithms for key feature extraction, a data-driven forecasting model specifically designed to overcome these limitations is proposed. The proposed methodology effectively mitigates the impact of poor model adaptability and forecasting instability, thereby reducing forecasting errors in medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting. Specific innovations are as follows:

1) A data screening model utilizing Scikit-Learn’s decision tree algorithm is proposed. This model is based on the decision tree algorithm, incorporates quantitative indicators for feature importance evaluation, enabling efficient extraction of critical data features from forecasting datasets. The proposed approach significantly reduces the dimensionality of the medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting dataset.

2) A method based on Fast Fourier Transform decomposition of key datasets is proposed. This approach decomposes historical electricity price, natural gas price, and coal price datasets into trigonometric functions with varying amplitudes and frequencies. The method utilizes the main frequency components of historical electricity prices to forecast base price trends, while employing the dominant frequencies of coal and natural gas prices to forecast price volatility. This integrated approach effectively reduces electricity price fluctuation in forecasting.

3) A medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting method based on the FGCLA hybrid model is proposed. The model enhances the forecasting accuracy by taking the parameters of the main frequency characteristics of electricity, coal, and natural gas prices as inputs and combining with the model’s superior generalization capabilities.

In this study, we propose a hybrid GWO-CNN-LSTM-Attention model for medium- to long-term electricity price forecasting. The model integrates global optimization, deep feature extraction, temporal sequence learning, and attention mechanisms to enhance forecasting accuracy. Simulation experiments were conducted using real-world daily electricity price data from a U.S. state, and the proposed model was benchmarked against several baseline methods, including LSTM and Transformer models. The results demonstrate that our approach achieves substantial improvements in forecasting accuracy, with average gains of 57.21% and 49.69%, respectively.

A data-driven hybrid framework for medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting

Current medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting models remain heavily reliant on large-scale, high-dimensional datasets, yet most electricity price data are non-public and very difficult to obtain. Consequently, developing methodologies that maintain forecasting accuracy while reducing data dimensionality represents a critical research challenge in this field. Thus, developing approaches that maintain forecasting accuracy while reducing data dimensionality represents a significant research challenge worthy of investigation.

Electricity price forecasting relies on diverse data sources, including historical prices, electricity and energy demand, fuel prices, policy indicators, financial metrics, and special event records. However, these datasets frequently exhibit significant missing values. To reduce data dimensionality and extract critical features, this study employs decision tree algorithms. These algorithms demonstrate several advantages including minimal data preprocessing requirements, robust handling of missing values and outliers, and effective identification of significant features, which collectively contribute to reduced model complexity.

The decision tree algorithm identified historical electricity prices, coal prices, and natural gas prices as the dataset’s most significant components. Regional historical electricity prices effectively capture local pricing trends. Coal and natural gas, constituting approximately 50–65% of power generation feedstock, demonstrate price sensitivity to policy changes, financial market conditions, and exceptional events including economic crises, conflicts, and natural disasters. While these three key indicators exhibit regional variations across datasets, they collectively represent the predominant influencing factors and maintain strong forecast adaptability. Therefore, this paper proposes a data-driven medium- and long-term electricity price forecasting model based on historical electricity, coal and natural gas prices. Nevertheless, the price datasets for these three indicators contain anomalous values. By extracting principal features to characterize data trends, the approach preserves essential characteristics while mitigating the impact of outliers, thereby enhancing electricity price forecasting accuracy.

The FFT facilitates the conversion of time-domain signals into frequency-domain representations. This transformation enables the extraction of frequency features through spectral analysis, revealing the signal’s frequency components and energy distribution characteristics. Spectral peak analysis enables identification of dominant frequency components in signals, facilitating noise reduction and enhancement of signal fidelity. This approach proves particularly effective for feature extraction and time-frequency localization in non-stationary signal processing. Current medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting frameworks predominantly rely on high-dimensional datasets. The feature extraction methodology addresses this limitation by streamlining model complexity while enhancing adaptability, effectively reducing data dependency without compromising forecasting accuracy. FGCLA-based electricity price forecasting

The LSTM neural network is a temporal recurrent neural architecture capable of learning long-to-short-term dependencies in time series data. Particularly effective for processing interval-based patterns and delayed events within sequential data, this architecture demonstrates strong performance in medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting. The schematic diagram of the LSTM structure is presented in Fig. 2.

LSTM structure diagram.

The current input state of the LSTM module is t. Its memory cell is realized by four “gates”, which are:

1) Forget the gate: read the output ht−1 of the previous layer and the current input xt, output ft and assign it to the current cell state Ct−1;

2) Input gate: memorize the present information it and a part of the information Ct output by the forget gate;

3) Update gate: merge the past state CE−1 with the current memory C to output the current state Ct;

Output gate: selects a portion of the cell state for final output ht

When processing extended time series inputs, CNN-LSTM models tend to exhibit temporal information loss. To address this limitation, the attention mechanism enhances forecasting -critical feature vectors through differential weighting of input features while suppressing less relevant ones. This weighted approach enables more effective extraction of pivotal patterns from lengthy sequential data, thereby improving model efficacy.

The parameters of the CNN-LSTM-Attention model18 are typically selected through manual empirical methods, which require extensive simulation trials, complicate selection processes, and risk suboptimal configurations. To address this, the study employs the Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO)19,20 for automated hyperparameter tuning, optimizing both the model’s architectural configuration and parametric selection while enhancing the detection model’s performance. Consequently, this study proposes a GWO-CNN-LSTM-Attention framework for medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting.

First, the data after removing outliers is input, and the CNN layer is used to extract features from the data. The convolution layer extracts local and high-dimensional features in the data, and the convolution kernel and pooling layer capture key features and reduce the complexity of the operation. Secondly, the features extracted by CNN are passed to the LSTM layer to capture the temporal association and sequence information in the input data. Then, the Attention mechanism will perform weighted processing on the output of LSTM, and use probability to assign weights, so as to more accurately grasp the importance of different parts of the input sequence. Finally, the output of the Attention mechanism is mapped to the final target value through the fully connected layer. Among them, the Dropout layer is added after the LSTM layer to reduce the occurrence of overfitting. The GWO algorithm is used to optimize the three indicators of learning rate, number of convolution kernels, and Dropout ratio, and then optimize the weight update rate, CNN feature extraction ability, and overfitting ability, reduce the workload of parameter adjustment and training time, and improve the efficiency of the entire training process. Experimental comparison and analysis of results

The daily price dataset of a US state from 1 January 2015 to 29 February 2016, which contains historical electricity, coal-fired and natural gas prices, was used for the experiment21. All experiments were performed using a Windows 10 operating system, Inter i5-8300 H, CPU 2.3 GHz. The parameters of the LSTM algorithm were 100 nodes in the hidden layer, 80 training iterations, and an initial learning rate of 0.55. The learning rate of the CNN layer is set in the range of [0.001, 0.01], the number of convolution kernels is set in [16, 128], the Dropout ratio is set in [0.1, 0.5], the activation function is Relu, the optimization function is updated by the Adam algorithm, and the loss function is the root mean square error (RMSE). The number of iterations is set to 1000, and the number of iterations per round is 5. Based on the above parameter configurations, the original LSTM algorithm was used as a comparison to evaluate the actual performance of both algorithms against the LSTM and Transformer benchmarks.

As shown in Fig. 8, all three algorithms can effectively track the benchmark in fluctuation forecasting. The hybrid algorithm proposed in this paper demonstrates better fluctuation reduction with an error range between − 0.24 and 0.28, while the LSTM algorithm ranges from − 0.4 to 0.91 and the Transformer algorithm from − 0.31 to 0.36. This is primarily because, first, the FFT module is employed to extract the amplitudes and phases of dominant frequencies from electricity price, coal price, and natural gas price time series, effectively highlighting major periodic patterns while filtering out high-frequency noise. Second, the GWO algorithm is used to intelligently optimize key hyperparameters such as learning rate, number of convolutional filters, and dropout rate, thereby enhancing model robustness and training efficiency. Finally, the optimized CNN-LSTM-Attention structure integrates local feature extraction, long-term temporal dependency modeling, and attention-based focus on critical time steps, which enables effective modeling of complex, non-stationary time series data. In contrast, although the Transformer model excels at capturing global dependencies, it lacks explicit mechanisms for utilizing frequency-domain structures and maintaining long-term memory, making it less effective in scenarios with high volatility in electricity prices. Therefore, the proposed multi-source fusion framework demonstrates superior forecasting performance and robustness under complex and dynamic market conditions.

Conclusion

To address the challenges in medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting including reliance on large-scale high-dimensional data, high volatility, and low accuracy of forecasting, this paper proposes a data-driven methodology. The approach analyzes price time series using decision trees for critical data selection, applies FFT to decompose historical electricity prices into dominant frequency amplitudes and phases representing price trends, and similarly processes coal and natural gas prices to capture volatility indicators. These components are integrated through the FGCLA hybrid algorithm to generate final price forecasts. Comparative analysis with LSTM and Transformer algorithms demonstrates average accuracy improvements of 57.21% and 49.69% respectively in medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting.

Overall, the FGCLA hybrid algorithm proposed in this paper demonstrates effective and accurate medium-to-long-term electricity price forecasting capabilities. It not only reduces both data requirements and acquisition complexity but also ensures the adaptability of the pricing model while decreasing forecasting volatility through improved accuracy.

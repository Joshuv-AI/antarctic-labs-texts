Application of the LightGBM algorithm in cross-border supply chain risk management: prediction and mitigation strategy development
Authors: Dong Xi, Vicky Nie, Wanli Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractA dynamic risk assessment framework is proposed to address the complex challenge of concurrent logistics disruptions and policy compliance issues in cross-border supply chains. The framework integrates the Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM), the Multi-Scale Graph Attention (MGAT) network, and the Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN). This integration effectively captures the nonlinear coupling relationship between the evolving network topology and multimodal data. Utilizing order data from a Brazilian retailer, the model extracts node embedding features and time-series features through an incremental learning mechanism. Furthermore, hierarchical Shapley value analysis identifies critical risk transmission paths, and a deep reinforcement learning strategy engine optimizes risk mitigation solutions. The experimental results show that the model achieves a prediction accuracy of 92.5%, significantly outperforming traditional methods. The proposed strategy engine reduces the risk response time to 15 min, effectively mitigating risks while maintaining operational cost control.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47327-7.Keywords: LightGBM, Cross-border supply chain, Risk prediction, Graph Neural Networks, Reinforcement learningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In the context of accelerating globalization and increasingly intense international market competition, the risks faced by enterprises in cross-border supply chains have become more complex and diversified1. In recent years, with the continuous expansion of global logistics networks and the frequent adjustment of national economic policies, cross-border supply chain risks have evolved into a multifaceted scenario, where various risk factors, including logistics disruptions, exchange rate fluctuations, and policy compliance, interact and influence each other2,3. Traditional risk management methods, which primarily rely on static data analysis and historical experience, struggle to respond promptly to emerging risk situations4. Consequently, there is an urgent need for a new risk prediction and mitigation technology capable of effectively integrating multimodal data and adapting to the dynamic changes of the supply chain.

Cross-border supply chains are highly complex and dynamic, with risk factors that encompass not only internal operational errors and node failures but also uncontrollable external factors such as geopolitical issues, exchange rate fluctuations, and policy adjustments5,6. A key challenge for both academia and industry lies in rapidly capturing critical information within the supply chain network in a large-scale, multi-source, and heterogeneous data environment, as well as achieving precise risk prediction and timely early warnings through efficient algorithms7. Conventional statistical and machine learning models typically focus on static data analysis, making it difficult to fully capture the dynamic interactions between nodes within the supply chain network8. However, Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) offer a novel approach to address this challenge. By leveraging Multi-Scale Graph Attention (MGAT) network technology to extract node embeddings and edge weight features from the network’s topological structure, and incorporating a time sliding window mechanism for incremental learning, it becomes possible to capture temporal changes within the supply chain network over short time frames. This facilitates the precise identification of risk transmission paths, providing robust data support for the development of subsequent risk mitigation strategies.

Existing research methods typically treat supply chain nodes as static entities, failing to adequately account for the rapid restructuring process of logistics network structures during unexpected events. Although simple ensemble models perform well in processing tabular data, they struggle to capture complex dependencies, particularly in scenarios where changes in supplier network topology directly impact time-series financial indicators. To address this gap, this study proposes a composite model architecture and explores the following research questions:

RQ1: How can dynamic topological features be effectively integrated with multimodal time-series data to improve the accuracy of risk prediction?

RQ2: Compared to static baseline models, to what extent does the introduction of the graph attention mechanism and TCN enhance the identification of risk propagation paths?

RQ3: In real-time decision-making environments, how can a reinforcement learning-based strategy engine effectively balance rapid risk response and operational cost minimization?

The remaining sections of this study are organized as follows. The literature review summarizes relevant studies on cross-border supply chain risk management and machine learning applications. The research methodology introduces the proposed model. This section details the integration of LightGBM, MGAT, and TCN within the dynamic risk assessment framework. The experimental setup describes specific details regarding the dataset and parameter configurations. Subsequently, the results and discussion section reports on model performance comparisons and Shapley value analysis. Finally, the conclusion summarizes the research findings, practical implications, and future research directions.

Related work

The application of machine learning algorithms in supply chain risk management has increasingly become a dominant trend, especially in the context of the growing complexity and uncertainty of cross-border supply chains. Scholars have progressively explored ways to integrate traditional supply chain theories with advanced artificial intelligence (AI) technologies. Sani et al. proposed a model that combines machine learning and Bayesian optimization to enhance the performance of the LightGBM for predicting supply chain risks9. In the context of Supply Chain 4.0, their research utilized historical risk data to train the model and employed Bayesian optimization to improve the performance of LightGBM. The study demonstrated the potential for efficient prediction in complex supply chain environments, particularly emphasizing the automation capabilities of parameter tuning, which provided valuable insights for subsequent research. Wang et al. utilized big data and AI algorithms to integrate multi-source data for capturing risk signals. Their work underscored how technological approaches can enable the precise identification and dynamic early warning of risk nodes in supply chain risk management10.

Wu, Zhang, and Zhou addressed the issue from the perspective of credit ratings, examining the credit propagation mechanisms within supply chains11. They applied machine learning techniques to develop predictive models, training them with transaction network data between companies to effectively forecast corporate credit ratings. The significance of this study lies in its pioneering effort to connect corporate credit ratings with supply chain networks, offering a novel research direction for corporate risk prediction from a supply chain perspective. Nawaz and Zhaolei focused on supply chain resilience within the Middle Eastern trade corridor12. They developed a series of machine learning-based models to analyze systemic vulnerabilities in complex supply chain environments, suggesting that model predictions could enhance resilience against disruptions. This research highlights the shift from complexity to resilience, making it particularly relevant for supply chain management in geopolitically high-risk regions.

In the context of e-commerce short-shelf-life product sales forecasting, Chen et al. proposed a method combining Gated Recurrent Units with LightGBM to model and predict short-cycle sales data13. This study highlights the integration of time series features with decision tree models, demonstrating the effectiveness of prediction in high-frequency fluctuating data and providing valuable technical support for sales risk prediction within supply chains. Oyewola et al. further advanced the application of Bayesian optimization by combining it with the Tree-structured Parzen Estimator to classify a pharmaceutical pricing dataset. Various deep learning techniques were employed to enhance the model’s robustness and generalization ability14. This research demonstrated the feasibility of combining deep learning with Bayesian optimization in highly nonlinear data environments, making it particularly suitable for processing and analyzing high-dimensional data within the healthcare supply chain.

This study direction resonates with the work of Zhang et al., who focused on the influence of lateral conductive heat flow on ground temperature in permafrost modeling15. Their study revealed the critical role of heat conduction mechanisms within complex environmental systems. By employing numerical simulation methods, the study systematically evaluated the impact of lateral thermal conduction on ground temperature distribution in permafrost. Findings indicated that traditional one-dimensional heat conduction models might underestimate the regulatory effect of lateral heat flow on the ground temperature field. This discrepancy is particularly evident in regions characterized by complex topography and high surface cover heterogeneity. The research emphasized the importance of multidimensional heat conduction coupling modeling. Such modeling provides methodological insights for understanding energy transfer and risk conduction mechanisms in complex systems. Consequently, this study offers a theoretical reference for analyzing multi-path conduction mechanisms of risks within cross-border supply chain networks. This approach methodologically aligns with the MGAT network modeling utilized in this study to address multi-source risk propagation paths.

To systematically summarize the application of machine learning in supply chains, Polo-Triana et al. conducted a comprehensive literature review that examined the application of mainstream machine learning methods in supply chain decision support, including demand forecasting, inventory optimization, transportation scheduling, and risk control16. Their review highlighted that, while machine learning methods have yielded certain successes, challenges persist in areas such as heterogeneous data fusion, dynamic feature extraction, and real-time prediction. Liu et al. focused on predicting corporate risk behavior, investigating the risk preferences of companies across various scenarios using multiple machine learning models17. They also proposed a risk impact factor evaluation method based on big data analysis, providing technical support for research on corporate behavior within supply chains. Regarding systemic risk quantification and network optimization, Chen et al. conducted an in-depth study on the paths to enhance maritime supply chain resilience18. That study utilized structural equation modeling, necessary condition analysis, and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis. Based on questionnaire data from the Chinese maritime industry, the research systematically evaluated the multi-factor synergistic mechanisms affecting maritime supply chain resilience. Findings indicated that high resilience in the maritime supply chain resulted from the synergy of multiple factors, identifying four distinct implementation paths. Notably, digitalization levels act as a catalyst by empowering other factors. This research revealed multiple equivalent paths for enhancing supply chain resilience. Consequently, it provides a methodological reference for understanding the combined effects of risk response strategies in this study. This perspective theoretically aligns with the approach of using reinforcement learning to optimize risk mitigation strategies.

From a broader perspective, Hosseinnia Shavaki and Ebrahimi Ghahnavieh conducted a systematic review of the application of deep learning in supply chain management and proposed a theoretical framework for future research19. This framework emphasized multi-level data fusion and dynamic decision support, noting that current research lacks an in-depth exploration of graph-structured supply chain network modeling. Zdravković et al., from the perspective of enterprise information systems, analyzed the pathways and models for integrating AI into manufacturing information systems20. They underscored the necessity of constructing intelligent supply chain management platforms in the context of Industry 4.0, further emphasizing the importance of information integration and intelligent decision-making. Finally, Zakaria et al. employed bibliometric analysis to review the current state of machine learning applications in the financial industry, providing valuable insights into the adaptability and transferability of machine learning models across different sectors21. This indirectly supports the feasibility of transferring financial risk management methods to supply chain risk management.

In summary, existing research has made significant progress in the application of machine learning techniques to supply chain management, particularly in risk prediction, credit assessment, sales forecasting, and decision support systems, demonstrating considerable potential for broad application. Most studies emphasize improvements in model accuracy and robustness while exploring various algorithmic integration approaches. However, several notable gaps remain: first, there is a lack of dynamic modeling of supply chain network topology and an analysis of risk propagation pathways; second, existing models often rely on static or unidimensional data, failing to fully leverage multimodal and heterogeneous data; third, there is a lack of a systematic linkage mechanism between risk prediction and strategy formulation, with strategic responses generally lagging behind the occurrence of risks. Building on existing research, this study proposes a dynamic risk assessment and strategy optimization framework for cross-border supply chains that integrates the LightGBM algorithm, MGAT network, and TCN. This framework innovatively introduces GNNs for deep modeling of node and edge weights, incorporates a time sliding window mechanism to enable incremental learning, uses hierarchical Shapley values to analyze risk propagation pathways, and leverages reinforcement learning to optimize the strategy response mechanism, thereby achieving a breakthrough in balancing model performance and practicality.

This dataset contains extensive historical sales data extracted from a leading Brazilian retailer, encompassing numerous Stock Keeping Units (SKU) and stores. Results and discussions

Model prediction performance and multimodal impact analysis

Figure 5 presents a comparative analysis of the performance of various models in the cross-border supply chain risk prediction task. The proposed framework, which integrates MGAT networks and TCNs, significantly outperforms traditional models across four key metrics: accuracy (92.5%), recall (88.3%), F1 score (90.1%), and Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC-ROC) (0.934). When compared to the second-best performing XGBoost model, the proposed framework improves accuracy by 7.8% points and recall by 10.4% points, thereby validating the effectiveness of collaborative optimization between GNNs and temporal modeling. In contrast, the ordinary Graph Convolutional Network, due to its inability to capture multi-scale feature interactions, demonstrates the weakest performance, with accuracy at 79.8% and AUC-ROC at 0.781. This highlights the necessity of the multi-scale attention mechanism introduced in this study. Logistic regression, constrained by its linear assumption, exhibits substantially lower recall (68.4%) and F1 score (71.8%) compared to other nonlinear models, underscoring the importance of capturing complex nonlinear relationships in supply chain risk prediction.

Model prediction performance comparison.

The full-modal fusion (Logistics + Finance + Policy) plays a crucial role in enhancing model performance. Its accuracy (92.5%) exceeds that of using logistics data alone (81.3%) by 11.2% points, and is 5.6% points higher than the bimodal combination of Logistics + Finance (86.9%). While logistics features dominate the prediction process with a weight of 0.39, the contribution of financial features (0.28) and implicit policy features (which are not directly quantified) should not be underestimated. For instance, in scenarios involving exchange rate fluctuations, the contribution of financial features reaches 42%. Notably, policy compliance data, due to information sparsity, results in an accuracy of 84.1% when used alone, which is lower than that of the logistics + finance combination. This underscores the irreplaceability of collaborative modeling with heterogeneous data (Fig. 6).

Multimodal data contribution analysis results.

The effect of time window length on prediction performance is shown in Fig. 7. The relationship between time window length and prediction performance follows a “rise-then-fall” pattern, with the optimal balance achieved at a window length of 14 days (accuracy: 92.5%, recall: 88.3%). A short window (7 days) fails to capture sufficient temporal dependencies, leading to a decrease in accuracy to 89.4%. Conversely, a longer window (28 days) results in a drop in accuracy to 90.2%, due to the accumulation of noise. Computational time increases linearly with window length, with the 14-day window improving computational efficiency (12.7 min per cycle) by 45.7% compared to the 28-day window, while the feature dimension increases by only 10.7% (from 196 to 217 dimensions). This indicates that the 14-day window setting strikes a Pareto optimal balance between accuracy and efficiency.

Risk mitigation strategy effectiveness and case validation

The comparative results of risk mitigation strategies are presented in Fig. 8. The dynamic strategy optimization engine based on DRL outperforms baseline methods in both response time (15.3 min) and interruption loss reduction (37.1%). Compared to the rule-based engine, the response time is reduced by 73.8%, and the loss reduction is more than doubled (18.2% to 37.1%). Although the computational cost (3.2 USD per instance) is higher than Q-learning (2.5 USD per instance), the cost-effectiveness ratio (loss reduction per dollar) improves by 62.3%. Additionally, the increase in compliance score (24.8%) confirms the effectiveness of the multi-objective optimization mechanism, demonstrating that the strategy engine effectively balances risk mitigation and compliance.

Effectiveness comparison of risk mitigation strategies.

The Brazil-China supply chain case demonstrates the model’s robustness in real-world scenarios, as shown in Fig. 9. For the port strike event (2019), the model achieved optimal prediction accuracy (94.2%) and loss reduction (41.3%), as the logistics disruption signal propagated significantly across the network topology. The Euro exchange rate fluctuations (2022) led to a slight increase in response time (16.1 min) due to the complexity of multi-currency interactions, but still resulted in a 68.2% improvement compared to the baseline. The loss reduction for tariff policy changes (2023) was relatively modest (33.7%), reflecting the long-tail nature of policy-related risks and the delayed effects of mitigation strategies. This suggests the need for further enhancement of real-time analysis capabilities for policy-related texts.

Brazil-China supply chain event.

The effectiveness of the strategy engine was validated through a detailed analysis of the Port of Santos strike event in August 2023. The system continuously monitored orders for the “Consumer Electronics” SKU category destined for the São Paulo distribution center. On August 12, 2023, when the logistics efficiency index plummeted from 0.85 to 0.32, the model predicted a 94.2% probability of a supply disruption triggered by this event. Within 15 min, the strategy engine automatically generated a response plan: rerouting 60% of inbound cargo to the Port of Paranaguá while activating the domestic backup supplier S_24. This automated intervention reduced the originally anticipated 14-day inventory stockout cycle to just 3 days. A comparison of the actual business outcome with historical manual response data from a similar event in 2019 revealed a 37.1% reduction in interruption loss, with logistics cost overruns contained within 15%. These results fully demonstrate the practical value of the proposed deep reinforcement learning strategy engine in commercial applications.

Interpretability analysis and computational efficiency optimization

The results of the interpretability analysis are presented in Fig. 10. The match rate of risk propagation paths at the enterprise level (83.6%) and the key node identification rate (91.2%) are both higher than those at the national level (79.2%, 85.4%) and logistics hub level (76.8%, 88.9%), indicating that micro-level enterprise behaviors exert a more decisive influence on risk propagation. The higher average Shapley value at the national level (0.41) reflects the global impact of macro factors, such as geopolitical events and exchange rate fluctuations, on cross-border supply chains.

Engineering implementation and practical discussion

Deploying the proposed framework in a real-world production environment necessitates a primary focus on addressing system latency and integration with business systems. The system described in this study employs Apache Kafka to achieve real-time data ingestion. This ensures that order updates from Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems (e.g., SAP Business Suite 4 SAP HANA, SAP S/4HANA) are processed at the millisecond level. Although the model operates on a 15-minute update cycle, its inference latency remains consistently under 2 s. Furthermore, the system incorporates a “Human-in-the-loop” mechanism, requiring managerial approval for high-risk decisions (such as replacing core suppliers). This design maintains the rapid response capability of the AI while introducing necessary human oversight. This approach aligns with recent research findings on complex network resilience and cascading risk propagation. In their study on the chip trade network, Liu et al.35 treated supply shocks as cascading processes diffusing through the network, discussing the risk propagation mechanisms driven by node failure and edge constraints. This strategy provided a reusable modeling perspective for identifying risk paths in supply networks. Liu et al.36 established a multi-layer dependency structure and cascading failure analysis framework for interconnected urban critical infrastructure networks. They emphasized that cross-system coupling amplified the impact of local failures and that resilience assessment must simultaneously consider structural associations and the dynamic performance of recovery processes. At the engineering implementation level, a balance is achieved between algorithmic response speed and management controllability. This is realized through the “human-in-the-loop” approval mechanism, where automated strategies handle low-risk scheduling while managers confirm high-risk actions, coupled with real-time data integration into ERP systems.

Conclusions

This study addresses the dynamics and complexity of cross-border supply chain risk management by proposing a collaborative prediction framework that integrates multimodal data, GNNs, and a reinforcement learning-driven dynamic strategy optimization engine. At the theoretical level, the combined modeling of MGAT networks and TCNs effectively captures the coupling effects between supply chain network topology and its temporal evolution, overcoming the limitations of traditional models in representing risk propagation paths. Methodologically, the integration of a hierarchical Shapley value analysis mechanism and hybrid parallel computing architecture significantly enhances both model interpretability and engineering implementation efficiency, while ensuring prediction accuracy. At the application level, the dynamic strategy engine, through multi-objective optimization and real-time response mechanisms, provides decision support for cross-border enterprises, balancing risk mitigation effects with cost constraints. Empirical results demonstrate that the framework effectively addresses the synergistic impact of multidimensional risk factors, such as logistics disruptions, exchange rate fluctuations, and policy compliance, offering an innovative methodology for supply chain resilience management in the context of globalization.

However, the study has several limitations. Firstly, the data source is concentrated in the Brazilian retail industry, which, while typical, may not fully capture the heterogeneous characteristics of supply chains across different regions. Secondly, the model’s predictive ability for extreme black swan events is constrained by the distribution range of historical data, necessitating further validation in scenarios such as sudden geopolitical conflicts. Additionally, while the real-time optimization of the strategy engine is significant, frequent strategy adjustments may increase the execution complexity for enterprises. Future research could focus on three key areas: first, expanding data sources to include multiple countries and industries, creating a more generalized risk assessment benchmark; second, exploring federated learning and edge computing technologies to achieve distributed risk warnings while ensuring data privacy; and third, incorporating digital twins technology to develop a virtual-physical interactive supply chain simulation system, enhancing the foresight and adaptability of strategies. With continuous optimization, this framework has the potential to become the foundational infrastructure for the intelligent governance of global supply chains, supporting enterprises in achieving robust operations in emerging risk environments.

Genetically Predicted Immunocyte Phenotypes as Risk and Protective Factors in Glaucoma: An Exploratory Bidirectional Mendelian Randomization Study
Authors: Yanggang Hong, Wanyi Shu, Yi Wang, Yarong Wang, Jiani Jiang, Gaoying Jin, Han Chen, Lin Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeGlaucoma is characterized by progressive retinal ganglion cell loss and irreversible sight damage and is reported to be interacting with immunological dysregulation. Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) and primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) are two of the most frequent subtypes. This study aimed to explore the associations between the immune system and glaucoma by investigating the causal relationships between POAG or PACG and immunocyte phenotypes.MethodsA two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) approach was carried out to comprehensively analyze the genetic variants associated 731 immunocyte phenotypes and their influence on glaucoma outcomes. Data were obtained from genome-wide association studies and the FinnGen consortium, with rigorous statistical methods applied to ensure the robustness of the study.ResultsSignificant associations were identified between immunocyte phenotypes and glaucoma. Specifically, causal effects of 22 immunocyte phenotypes on POAG were observed, with CD25hi CD45RA− CD4 not regulatory T cell activated cells showing a bidirectional interaction. Additionally, 30 immunocyte phenotypes were found to have causal effects on PACG. Bidirectional interactions were identified with BAFF-R on immunoglobulin (Ig)D+ CD38− naive cells, BAFF-R on IgD+ CD38− unswitched memory cells, and CD62L on CD62L+ myeloid dendritic cells.ConclusionsThe present results highlighted the essential role of immune regulation in the pathogenesis of glaucoma. These findings powerfully indicate the significance of exploring the therapeutic potentials of targeting different immune cells and immune responses to regulate both POAG and PACG.Translational RelevanceThe present study highlighted immune–glaucoma interactions mediated by T-cell and monocyte phenotypes in primary open-angle glaucoma and primary angle-closure glaucoma development.

Introduction

Glaucoma is recognized as an aggregation of eye diseases that lead to progressive damage of the optic nerve, which generally results in irreversible vision loss and blindness if left untreated.1 There are approximately 95 million people around the world who suffer from glaucoma, with nearly 10 million people ultimately being blind in one or two eyes, which severely deteriorate both eyesight and living quality.1,2 Primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG) and primary angle-closure glaucoma (PACG) are two major categories of glaucoma, with POAG being the more frequent type in most populations.3 Despite its prevalence and significance on public health, the precise pathogenesis of glaucoma remains unclear. Although an elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) is a widely acknowledged risk factor, the occurrence of glaucoma in individuals with normal IOP is remarkably increased.4 Moreover, other factors such as genetic, immune mechanisms, and vascular dysregulation probably contribute to the development of glaucoma.5,6 Understanding these underlying mechanisms is crucial for developing therapeutic and preventative strategies for glaucoma.

Recent research has highlighted the potential role of the immune system, particularly immunocyte phenotypes, in the pathogenesis of glaucoma.7 Immune cells, including T cells, microglia, and macrophages, are involved in the neurodegenerative processes that characterize glaucoma.8 Particularly, it has been elaborated that T cells may contribute to retinal ganglion cell (RGC) damage in glaucomatous eyes.9 Microglial activity alternations possibly establish a chronic inflammatory state that exacerbates neuronal injury.10 Furthermore, systemic immune dysregulation has been observed in glaucoma patients, suggesting that immune responses extend beyond the ocular environment.11,12 However, specific contributions of different immunocyte phenotypes to the progression of glaucoma remain poorly understood. And exploring causal relationships between various immunocyte phenotypes and glaucoma is imperative to unravel the complex etiology.

Mendelian randomization (MR) is an epidemiological strategy that uses genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to infer causal relationships between exposures and outcomes.13 This approach is particularly valuable in observational studies, in which confounding factors usually induce the complicated assessments of causal inference.14 By leveraging genetic variants robustly associated with the exposure of interest, MR reduces bias from confounding and reverse causation, providing more reliable estimates of causality. MR has been successfully applied in various fields, including cardiovascular disease and cancer research.13,15 Moreover, the MR analysis have provided insights into the genetic risk factors for glaucoma.16 However, the application of MR analyses in illustrating causal roles of immune cells in glaucoma is still poorly understood. Based on intricate interplays between immune mechanisms and glaucoma, the MR analysis provides a robust framework to dissect the causal effects of different immunocyte phenotypes on glaucoma.

Aiming to explore sophisticated roles of immunocyte phenotypes in the etiology of glaucoma, this study applies a two-sample MR approach. By assessing causal relationships between 731 immunocyte phenotypes and two principal types of glaucoma (POAG and PACG), we intend to identify the specific immunocyte phenotypes that may influence glaucoma susceptibility and progression. The findings from this study could provide valuable insights into the pathogenesis of glaucoma and highlight potential targets for therapeutic intervention. Additionally, understanding the causal pathways may aid in the development of precision medicine approaches for glaucoma, ultimately improving patient outcomes.

Methods

Study Design

This study adhered to the STROBE-MR guidelines.17 The overall study design is illustrated in Figure 1. Briefly, we conducted a two-sample MR analysis to assess the causal relationship between 731 immunocyte phenotypes and glaucoma, which included POAG and PACG. Stringent inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied to select suitable single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) as IVs. We performed a series of sensitivity analyses to ensure the robustness of the MR analysis, including a horizontal pleiotropy test, a heterogeneity test, five methods with consistent direction, and the leave-one-out approach. For immunocyte phenotypes showing significant causal effects, we conducted a reverse MR analysis to investigate whether causal effects existed of POAG and PACG on immunocyte phenotypes. All genome-wide association studies (GWAS) summary statistics are publicly available and free to download, with ethical approvals covered by the original studies.

Overview of the bidirectional MR study design.

Data Sources

The GWAS data for 7 groups of 731 immunocyte phenotypes were sourced from Orrù et al.,18 who reported on the influence of approximately 22 million variants on 731 immune cell traits in a cohort of 3757 Europeans. The GWAS statistics for every immunological characteristic could be further obtained from the GWAS catalog (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gwas/), under accession codes GCST90001391 to GCST90002121. Summary-level GWAS data for glaucoma were obtained from the FinnGen consortium (Release 10), accessible at https://www.finngen.fi/en. The disease phenotypes in FinnGen are constructed by integrating multiple nationwide health registries, and disease diagnoses are mainly based on International Classification of Diseases and the Anatomical Chemical Therapeutic.19 These datasets encompass two types of glaucoma, including POAG and PACG. The phenocode for POAG was H7_GLAUCOMA_POAG involving 8530 cases and 391,275 European ancestry controls, and H7_GLAUCCLOSEPRIM for PACG involving 1307 cases and 391,275 European ancestry controls. The baseline characteristics of the three cohorts are detailed in Table 1. Both exposure and outcome samples primarily consisted of European populations, thereby reducing population heterogeneity. Importantly, there was no overlap between two GWAS cohort, enabling the possibility of conducting two-sample MR studies.

Selection of IVs

To ensure the validity and robustness of the MR analysis, IVs were selected according to the three core MR assumptions: (1) IVs must be strongly associated with the exposure, (2) IVs must not be associated with confounders of the exposure–outcome relationship, and (3) IVs should influence the outcome only through the exposure of interest.20 SNPs significantly associated with the exposure were selected using a liberal significance threshold of P < 1 × 10−5 to increase instrument density and capture more variance in the exposure. To ensure independence among selected SNPs, linkage disequilibrium clumping was performed using European ancestry reference data from the 1000 Genomes Project.21,22 A clumping window of 10,000 Kb and a linkage disequilibrium threshold of r2 of less than 0.001 were applied; when multiple SNPs were in linkage disequilibrium, only the SNP with the lowest P value was retained.23,24 To evaluate the strength of each instrument, we calculated the F statistic for each SNP. SNPs with an F statistic of less than 10 were considered weak instruments and were excluded from further analysis to avoid weak instrument bias.25,26 This rigorous filtering process ensured that retained SNPs were both statistically significant and independent, thereby minimizing bias and enhancing the reliability of the causal inference in MR analysis.

Forward-Direction MR Analysis

We used the R package TwoSampleMR (version 0.6.6)21 to perform MR analyses. When there was only one SNP as the IV, we used the Wald ratio method to evaluate the correlation between exposure and outcome. For IVs that contained two or more SNPs, we used the inverse-variance weighted (IVW) method to examine the relationship between exposure and outcome.27 The IVW method offers precise and consistent estimates when all IVs meet three fundamental assumptions. Considering that each immunocyte phenotype exposure corresponds to outcomes of two types of glaucoma, we applied Bonferroni correction for multiple testing to significance levels, and a P value of less than 0.025 was considered statistically significant (0.05 divided by 2). Then, for outcomes with a P value of less than 0.025 in initial MR analyses, we confirmed the results using alternative MR models, including MR–Egger, weighted median, simple mode, and weighted mode. Each of these methods offers different strengths: MR–Egger accounts for horizontal pleiotropy by providing an intercept test, the weighted median allows for valid inferences even when up to one-half of the instruments are invalid, and the simple and weighted mode methods help in identifying the causal effect by clustering instruments with similar estimates. To identify horizontal pleiotropy, we conducted MR–Egger intercept tests and evaluated heterogeneity using Cochran's Q with both IVW and MR–Egger methods (P < 0.05).28 The leave-one-out approach in sensitivity analyses systematically removed each SNP one at a time to assess whether any single SNP disproportionately influenced the MR results. This method ensured that the overall causal estimates remained consistent, confirming the robustness of the findings and that the associations were not driven by specific SNPs.

Reverse-Direction MR Analysis

The reverse MR analysis was conducted to explore the potential causal influence of POAG and PACG on specific immunocyte phenotypes, complementing the forward MR analysis. In this approach, the two types of glaucoma were treated as the exposure variables, and the significant immunocyte phenotypes for each type of glaucoma were considered the outcomes. The analysis used the Wald ratio and IVW methods, which aggregate effect estimates from multiple genetic variants into a single weighted average, providing a robust estimate of the causal effect. Statistical significance was defined as a P value of less than 0.05. Genetic instruments for POAG and PACG were selected based on their strong associations from the GWAS summary data, ensuring minimal confounding and pleiotropy. This reverse MR analysis mirrors the methodology used in the forward analysis, including sensitivity analyses such as MR–Egger, weighted mode, and leave-one-out tests, to assess the potential impact of horizontal pleiotropy and confirm the reliability of the findings. This comprehensive approach allows for the investigation of how glaucoma might causally alter immunocyte phenotypes, contributing to a deeper understanding of the condition's complex etiology.

Results

Causal Effects Between Immunocyte Phenotypes and POAG

The forward MR analysis using the IVW method identified significant associations between 22 specific immunocyte phenotypes and POAG (Fig. 2). Immunocyte phenotypes such as CD28 on CD28+ CD45RA− CD8br, Im MDSC %CD33dim human leukocyte antigen (HLA) DR− CD66b−, activated and secreting regulatory T cell (Treg) %CD4 Treg, CD14 on Mo MDSC, CD20− CD38− %lymphocyte, CD38 on transitional, PDL-1 on CD14− CD16+ monocyte, CD38 on immunoglobulin (Ig)D+ CD38br, transitional %lymphocyte, and CD8 on CD28+ CD45RA+ CD8br, were positively correlated with POAG (odds ratio [OR] > 1; P < 0.025), whereas CD19 on IgD+ CD38−, CD19 on IgD+ CD38dim, CD4 on HLA DR+ CD4+, IgD on IgD+, IgD+ CD38− AC, CCR2 on CD62L+ myeloid dendritic cells (DC), IgD on IgD+ CD38dim, HLA DR on CD33br HLA DR+ CD14−, CD25hi CD45RA− CD4 not Treg AC, CD27 on unsw mem, herpesvirus entry mediator (HVEM) on EM CD8br, and CD19 on IgD+ CD24−, showed negative correlations (OR < 1; P < 0.025). To verify the robustness of these findings and address potential biases, alternative MR models, including MR–Egger, weighted median, simple mode, and weighted mode, were applied (Supplementary Table S1). A reverse trend was observed for transitional %lymphocyte using MR–Egger (OR, 0.998) and weighted mode (OR, 0.990), suggesting potential bias. Other models showed consistent trends across the remaining immunocyte phenotypes. No significant horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity was detected (Table 2). Sensitivity analyses using the leave-one-out method confirmed the robustness of the results (Supplementary Fig. S1). Interestingly, the reverse MR analysis indicated a positive causal effect of POAG on CD25hi CD45RA− CD4 not Treg AC (OR, 1.096; P = 0.001), suggesting the possibility of complex bidirectional interactions. Additionally, horizontal pleiotropy and heterogeneity test further confirmed the robustness of the complex bidirectional interaction results (P > 0.05) (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3). However, no significant impact of POAG was observed on the other 21 immunocyte phenotypes (Fig. 3).

Causal Effects Between Immunocyte Phenotypes and PACG

The forward MR analysis using the IVW method identified significant associations between 30 specific immunocyte phenotypes and PACG (Fig. 4). Among these, HLA DR on DC, CD27 on CD20− CD38−, HLA DR++ monocyte %monocyte, CD8 on EM CD8br, BAFF-R on IgD+ CD38− unsw mem, CD4+ CD8dim %leukocyte, CD20 on B cell, CD4+ CD8dim %lymphocyte, EM DN (CD4−CD8−) AC, CD16 on CD14− CD16+ monocyte, CD19 on unsw mem, HLA DR on monocyte, SSC-A on HLA DR+ NK, CD62L on CD62L+ myeloid DC, CD28− CD127− CD25++ CD8br %CD8br, BAFF-R on IgD+ CD38− naive, and CD45RA+ CD8br %T cell, were positively correlated with PACG (OR > 1; P < 0.025). In contrast, CD66b++ myeloid cell AC, CD25 on IgD− CD27−, CD38 on IgD+ CD38dim, CD11b on basophil, CD45RA− CD28− CD8br %CD8br, CX3CR1 on CD14+ CD16+ monocyte, CD62L− HLA DR++ monocyte AC, SSC-A on CD4+, CD62L− DC %DC, CD20 on IgD+ CD38− naive, granulocyte %leukocyte, DP (CD4+CD8+) AC, and CD62L− myeloid DC %DC, showed negative correlations (OR < 1; P < 0.025). To confirm the reliability of these findings and to account for possible biases associated with the IVW method, we applied the other four alternative MR models. Notably, simple mode revealed a reverse trend for EM DN (CD4−CD8−) AC (OR, 0.927) and BAFF-R on IgD+ CD38− unsw mem (OR, 0.975), and MR Egger revealed a reverse trend for CD20 on B cells (OR, 0.983), suggesting potential bias. Aside from this, the other MR methods demonstrated consistent patterns across the remaining significant immunocyte phenotypes, enhancing the confidence in these results (Supplementary Table S1). Except for weak heterogeneity observed in CD38 on IgD+ CD38dim, no significant horizontal pleiotropy or heterogeneity was found (Table 2). Sensitivity analyses, including the leave-one-out method, further confirmed the robustness of these findings (Supplementary Fig. S2). Interestingly, the reverse MR analysis indicated positive causal effects of POAG on BAFF-R on IgD+ CD38− naive (OR, 1.079; P = 0.011), BAFF-R on IgD+ CD38− unsw mem (OR, 1.064; P = 0.041), and CD62L on CD62L+ myeloid DC (OR, 1.057; P = 0.013), suggesting the possibility of complex bidirectional interactions. Additionally, horizontal pleiotropy and heterogeneity test further confirmed the robustness of the complex bidirectional interaction results (P > 0.05) (Supplementary Tables S2 and S3). However, no significant impact of PACG was observed on the other 27 immunocyte phenotypes (Fig. 5).

Discussion

The relationship between immunocyte phenotypes and glaucoma has gained increasing attention in recent years, with studies suggesting that immune dysregulation plays a crucial role in the progression of glaucoma.7 In the pathology of glaucoma, central immune cells in the retina and optic nerve head, such as microglia and astrocytes, play a key role. Microglial activation has been observed in glaucomatous eyes in animal models,29,30 indicating a local immune response, and the suppression of microglia activation may rescue RGCs.31,32 However, it has been found that, under glaucomatous conditions, disruption of the blood–retina barrier allows peripheral immune cells to infiltrate the eye and contribute to local inflammation.33,34 Immune cells including T cells, B cells, and monocytes contribute to the inflammatory environment in the eye, which may lead to RGC damage. At the same time, elevated levels of inflammatory cytokines in the serum and aqueous humor of glaucoma patients suggest a role for circulating immune cells in disease progression.35 These findings indicate that peripheral immunocyte phenotypes can serve as indirect markers of central immune responses, helping to elucidate the role of systemic immunity in glaucoma. Moreover, In our study, using MR analysis, we found significant associations between various immunocyte phenotypes and both POAG and PACG. These findings suggest that immune cells are not merely bystanders in the glaucomatous process, but may actively contribute to its pathogenesis. The identification of specific immunocyte phenotypes involved in glaucoma highlights potential therapeutic targets that could modulate the immune response to slow disease progression.

In particular, our results indicate significant associations between T-cell phenotypes and glaucoma. For instance, certain T-cell subtypes, such as CD28+ and CD4+ T cells, are positively correlated with POAG, suggesting that T-cell activation may be involved in the pathogenesis of POAG. These findings are coordinated with previous studies that have implicated the significance of T cells in RGC damage.7 It has been demonstrated that T cells can migrate across the blood–retina barrier and interact with retinal cells, leading to neurodegeneration.36 And in the context of PACG, different T cell subtypes such as CD8+ T cells, are significantly associated with disease susceptibility. The distinct patterns of T cells involved in POAG and PACG suggest that, although T cells contribute to both forms of glaucoma, the underlying immune mechanisms may differ between these conditions.37 Compared with the more chronic IOP elevation observed in POAG, the acute IOP elevation in PACG probably triggers different immune responses. Therefore, the modulation of specific T-cell responses could be a potential therapeutic strategy tailored to each glaucoma subtype.

Monocytes, another crucial component of the immune system, have also been implicated in the glaucomatous process. The MR analysis identifies significant associations between certain monocyte phenotypes, such as CD14+ and CD16+ monocytes, and these two types of glaucoma. Furthermore, it is acknowledged that the monocyte migration into the retina and differentiation into macrophages contribute to local inflammation and RGC apoptosis.38 Furthermore, after ocular trauma, monocytes and macrophages swiftly infiltrate the retina and perpetuate retinal damage, which are induced by tumor necrosis factor-α secretion.39 And the positive correlation of POAG with monocyte subtypes potentially represents the maintenance of proinflammatory environment, which promotes chronic neurodegeneration. Conversely, the rapid IOP fluctuations probably trigger more acute monocyte responses, inducing a different pattern of retinal injury.40 Additionally, the MR analytical outcomes indicate that monocyte activation in glaucoma may not be uniform. Depending on the glaucoma subtype and disease stage, different monocyte phenotypes can play distinct roles. Therefore, it is therapeutically essential that targeting specific monocyte phenotypes may provide a more precise therapeutic approach to eliminate inflammation and neuronal loss in glaucoma.

Primarily known for their role in antibody production, B cells have been poorly investigated in glaucoma. However, the MR analysis in this study revealed significant associations between certain B cell phenotypes and glaucoma. In POAG, phenotypes such as CD19+ B cells are negatively correlated with disease risk, suggesting a possible protective role for these cells. This finding is consistent with the emerging evidence that B cells can have regulatory functions for immune responses.41 Different B-cell phenotypes conversely present positive associations with disease susceptibility, which indicate complicated roles for B cells in glaucoma. The involvement of B cells in glaucoma may reflect a broader dysregulation of the adaptive immune system, in which both protective and pathogenic immune responses coexist. Further research is required to elucidate the specific mechanisms by which B cells influence glaucoma pathogenesis and to determine whether modulating B-cell activity can be beneficial for managing glaucoma.

Immune cells in glaucoma can both exacerbate RGC damage and promote neuroprotection and repair. For instance, recent studies have highlighted that M2-like macrophages and specific T-cell subsets contribute to tissue regeneration and repair. Macrophages, for example, have been shown to play a protective role in RGC survival after optic nerve injury.42 Microglia, through a shift from the proinflammatory M1 phenotype to the neuroprotective M2 phenotype, secrete neurotrophic factors that support neuronal survival.34 Additionally, T cells help to clear dead neurons, preventing the escalation of inflammation, which may facilitate subsequent tissue remodeling and enhance the survival of remaining RGCs.43 The MR analysis used in this study reflects lifelong average effects, which may not capture the nuanced differences between acute and chronic immune activation. Immunocyte phenotypes exhibit considerable plasticity, with distinct functions at different stages of disease progression. Future studies combining nonlinear MR approaches and temporal dynamics from animal models are needed to further explore how immune responses evolve over time and influence glaucomatous damage.

Accompanied with potent strengths, including the use of a two-sample MR approach, this study stimulates causal inferences between immune cells and two forms of glaucoma, as well as diminishing confounding and reverse causation biases.14 By leveraging genetic variants as IVs, this study provides reliably robust evidence for the involvement of immunocyte phenotypes in glaucoma. Additionally, the large sample sizes and the inclusion of individuals of European ancestry alleviate population stratification bias. However, there are some limitations that should be acknowledged. Initially, although the MR analysis provides causal evidences, it cannot completely eliminate confounding, particularly when the IVs are pleiotropic.44 This study is also limited to individuals of European descent, meaning that the findings may not be generalizable to other populations. Although associations with various immunocyte phenotypes are identified, the precise mechanisms by which these cells contribute to glaucoma remain to be comprehensively elucidated. Further experimental studies are required to uncover the involved pathways and to explore potential therapeutic interventions targeting these immune cells in glaucoma. Moreover, glaucoma is a heterogeneous disease with potentially distinct immune mechanisms across its subtypes. Although we analyzed POAG and PACG separately, limitations in the GWAS data precluded stratification by disease stage or additional subtypes, which may constrain the generalizability of our findings across different glaucomatous subpopulations and disease courses. Future research should validate these results using more granular phenotypic data to further clarify the differential roles of immunocyte phenotypes in glaucoma.

Given that this study relies on an exploratory bidirectional MR analysis of publicly available GWAS data, its findings should be considered hypothesis generating. Therefore, functional validation of the candidate immunocyte phenotypes is essential. Previous work has shown that, in experimental glaucoma mouse models, circulating T helper type 1 cells upregulate vascular cell adhesion molecule-1 in the retina, facilitating their infiltration into the RGC layer and accelerating RGC apoptosis.33 Moreover, single-cell RNA sequencing of the trabecular meshwork and adjacent outflow pathway tissues from glaucoma patients has identified multiple immune cell subpopulations, providing histological corroboration for the immunocyte phenotypes prioritized by our MR analysis.45 To translate these hypotheses into mechanistic insights, future studies should use single-cell transcriptomic and proteomic profiling of ocular tissues from both patients and animal models to delineate immune subpopulation dynamics, and should systematically validate the causal impact of these candidate phenotypes on RGC degeneration through approaches such as flow cytometric sorting, in vitro coculture assays, and in vivo gene knockout or cell transfer experiments.

These findings in this study have clinical implications. Although the causal associations identified in our MR analysis were modest and only marginally above unity, even subtle increases in risk may hold considerable clinical significance in the context of a chronic, progressive disorder such as glaucoma. Moreover, the integration of related immunological variables has the potential to magnify the aggregate effect size, thereby further substantiating the clinical and biological relevance of these associations. The identification of specific immunocyte phenotypes associated with glaucoma provides new insights into the pathogenesis of glaucoma and illuminates its potential therapeutic avenues. Although immune regulation has been explored in other neurodegenerative diseases, such as multiple sclerosis and Alzheimer disease, this study innovatively reveal the potential benefits of targeting specific immune cells of T cells, monocytes, and B cells to ameliorate retinal damage in glaucoma.38,46,47 Additionally, immunocyte phenotypes can serve as biomarkers for predicting disease risk, progression, or response to treatment. For example, elevated levels of certain T-cell subtypes in the blood can potentially be used to identify individuals at higher risk of developing glaucoma or to monitor disease activity.48 Ultimately, integrating immune profiling into clinical practice can enhance our ability to personalize treatment strategies and improve outcomes for patients with glaucoma.

Conclusions

This study provides novel evidence supporting causal relationships between specific immunocyte phenotypes and the risk of developing POAG and PACG. Based on the bidirectional MR approach, we identified immunocyte phenotypes that may act as both risk and protective factors in glaucoma pathogenesis. Notably, T cells, monocytes, and B cells demonstrated distinct associations with glaucoma subtypes, suggesting subtype-specific immune mechanisms. The findings underscore the potential of immune modulation as a therapeutic strategy and highlight the need for further functional validation. Understanding the dynamic roles of immune phenotypes could inform precision medicine approaches and enhance clinical management of glaucoma.

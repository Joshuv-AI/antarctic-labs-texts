Visualized analysis of microscale rock mechanism research: A bibliometric data mining approach
Authors: Junhui Chen, Fei Tang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractRock mechanics is an indispensable discipline in diverse sectors, from resource retrieval to disaster mitigation. Diving deeper into this field, particularly into microscale rock mechanics, offers strategic insights and potential advancements for rock engineering practice. The objective of this research is to map the scientific production tied to microscale rock mechanics to date. In doing so, we perform a bibliometric analysis to look over and discuss the performance of the related literature. The conceptual fabric of microscale rock mechanics research, constituted by four central themes, was revealed and visualized through a text mining analysis. These areas include (1) modeling and simulation of fluid flow and transport within porous media, (2) characterizing fracture and failure in rocks, (3) understanding the deformation mechanism in response to geological processes, and (4) studying the mechanical properties of rocks subjected to extreme conditions. Lastly, an upcoming research agenda for microscale rock mechanics was proposed, centered on addressing three identified research gaps: (I) the integration of geological processes to characterize mineral properties, (II) the augmentation of fracture predictions achieved through multiscale modeling of rock heterogeneity, and (III) the exploitation of Artificial Intelligence technologies for anticipating complex fracturing scenarios. This comprehensive approach promises to enrich our understanding of rock mechanics and its applications.Keywords: Rock mechanics, Microscale, Bibliometric analysis, Rock fracturing

Introduction

Rock mechanics, a critical discipline in geotechnical engineering, plays an indispensable role in industries such as resource extraction, tunneling, and disaster mitigation. By investigating the behavior of rocks under various stress conditions, it helps engineers and scientists develop safer and more efficient methods for managing subsurface activities. However, recent advancements have shifted the focus from macroscopic to microscale rock mechanics, where understanding the micro-level behavior of rocks, including grain interactions and microcrack formations, has become pivotal in addressing large-scale challenges [1,2].

Microscale rock mechanics focuses on analyzing the behavior of rocks at the grain, mineral, and pore level, providing insights into crack initiation and propagation [3,4]. This approach is particularly useful in fields like hydraulic fracturing, where understanding microscale mechanisms can optimize the fracturing process, enhance gas extraction efficiency, and improve wellbore stability [5]. In underground mining and nuclear waste storage, microscale research aids in the prediction and prevention of rock failure by identifying potential weaknesses and fractures early on, thereby contributing to safer design and operation [[6], [7], [8], [9]]. Moreover, the fracture propagation laws in geothermal reservoirs and the effect of seepage forces on hydraulic fracture propagation have further demonstrated the value of a microscale approach in predicting and controlling rock behavior under extreme conditions [10,11].

Artificial intelligence (AI) techniques have also found applications in microscale rock mechanics, particularly in predictive modeling. AI has been used to forecast rock mass deformation, crack propagation, and the initiation of rock fractures under dynamic conditions. For example, AI models have been applied to predict the strength of marble [12]and the mechanical properties of complex rock formations [13]. These techniques enhance the accuracy of simulations and enable better planning for large-scale geotechnical projects, providing a new frontier in rock mechanics research.

Despite the increasing research in microscale rock mechanics, much of the literature remains fragmented, with many studies focusing on specific aspects without providing a comprehensive overview. Existing reviews do not fully address the wide range of research areas, particularly when it comes to integrating artificial intelligence techniques and new numerical modeling methods. To fill this gap, this paper performs a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of the existing research on microscale rock mechanics. By mapping the scientific landscape, we aim to provide a clearer understanding of the current state of research and highlight emerging trends.

Methodology

Data

In order to extract as many relevant studies as possible from the target literature, using a well-structured research protocol has been widely highlighted in the literature as one of the most important prerequisites in conducting systematic reviews. On this basis, a structured search string was formulated using different combinations of the keywords “microscale” and “rock mechanics” as the two main concepts that shape the present review. Consequently, the following search string was constructed to collect articles from the Web of Science database: micro scale rock mechanics (All Fields) OR microscale rock mechanics(All Fields) OR micro-scale rock mechanics(All Fields) OR micro rock fracturing mechanism(All Fields) OR microscale rock fracturing(All Fields). Analysis approach

Bibliometric analysis, serving as a statistical tool for benchmarking an abundant scientific production base, has seen widespread adoption in science mapping in recent years. Its ability to establish correlations among articles, journals, authors, keywords, citations, and co-citations networks allows researchers to discern research themes, trends, and anticipate potential research developments. In this study, we utilized the VOSviewer software version 1.6.19, developed by Van Eck &Waltman [19], to execute the bibliometric analysis. Subsequently, we examined and charted the performance of scientific production relating to microscale rock mechanics by showcasing bibliometric performance indicators that focus on: (i) the geographical distribution of publications, (ii) the productivity and influence of authors, (iii) central journals, (iv) articles, and (v) keyword-based analysis to expose research inclinations and focal points. Alongside the specified bibliometric indicators, a text mining analysis leveraging a term co-occurrence algorithm [20] was conducted on the titles and abstracts of the included manuscripts (1841 articles), using the text mining module of VOSviewer version 1.6.19, to form part of the final sample.

The 1841 available articles in the microscale rock mechanics research within our dataset have been published in 425 journals. Out of this pool of journals, 39 have published at least 10 of the articles in the dataset. Fig. 4, Fig. 5 focus on the journal publications and show the top ones based on the number of published articles and the number of citations to their articles, respectively. Therefore, Rock Mechanics and Rock Engineering and International Journal of Rock Mechanics and Mining Sciences can be regarded as the leading journal in the field of microscale rock mechanics from this lens of analysis.

Text mining results: thematic conceptualization

The text mining analysis was conducted on the titles and abstracts of the collected peer-reviewed journal articles of our sample to uncover hidden semantic structures and research themes. After cleaning the data, 8156 unique noun phrases were identified. In order to base the analysis on the sufficiently frequent terms, a minimum of 10 occurrences was considered as a criterion for the selection of phrases, leading to the selection of 262 noun phrases. Then, the phrases were used to build clusters based on the co-occurrence of the terms to reveal the research themes of microscale rock mechanics. The four major theme identified includes (1) modeling and simulation of flow and transport in porous media, (2) characterizing fracture and failure in rocks, (3) deformation mechanism in response to geological processes, (4) mechanical properties of rocks under extreme conditions. Fig. 8 presents the thematic structure of the microscale rock mechanics research. Similar to Fig. 7, the size of the circles and the links between them show the occurrence of identified terms and their co-occurrence, respectively.

Major research themes in microscale rock mechanics.

The first thematic cluster focuses on the modeling and simulation of fluid flow and transport within porous media(cluster #1), with particular attention paid to the fracturing behavior of shales under diverse conditions. Key concepts central to this research theme include "model," "permeability," "simulation," "evolution," "shale," and "microstructure." Fueled by the "Shale Revolution," the United States has significantly boosted its oil and natural gas output, especially from tight formations such as shales, which now constitute 52.2 % of the nation's total natural gas production [50]. This uptick has catalyzed a wave of global research into shale gas extraction, with a keen focus on understanding the nuances of micro and nanoscale pores within shale, as well as how they respond to external forces. Gaining insight into these aspects is now deemed crucial for advancing energy storage technologies. An array of sophisticated investigative methods, including FIB-SEM [51], FE-SEM [52], X-ray CT [53], and digital rock technologies [54], has been utilized to delve into porosity with extraordinary detail. In parallel, a variety of numerical models that employ the finite element method [55], the discrete element method [56,57], and the combined finite-discrete element method [58] have been developed, shedding light on the complex evolution of microfractures. This enhanced understanding lays the groundwork for optimizing exploration and extraction strategies, marking a significant stride towards more efficient and sustainable energy utilization.

The second research theme (cluster #2) concentrates on characterizing fracture and failure in rocks. It involves concepts like fracture, strength, sandstone, failure, damage, stress, and propagation. This research domain engages deeply with the mechanics of crack propagation, the interaction between fractures, and the acoustic signals emitted from developing microfractures in rocks like granite and sandstone, under various stress conditions including uniaxial and triaxial compressions. During uniaxial compressive loading, it has become widely acknowledged that rock samples typically undergo seven distinct stages before culminating in failure. This process begins with a settling-down phase due to the closing of previously existing cracks, followed by a phase characterized by almost flawless linear elasticity. Subsequently, we encounter a period of stable development of micro-fracturing and dilatancy. In the final stage, the propagation of microcracks becomes unstable, leading to strain localization and the formation of macro-fractures [59]. Numerous numerical models, primarily based on the grain-based discrete element method and particle flow code, have gained popularity in the research community. These tools provide valuable insights into the dynamics of crack progression and the impact of material heterogeneity on the strength of rocks and their ensuing failure mechanisms [60].

The third thematic cluster focuses on deformation mechanism in response to geological processes (cluster #3). In this regard, mechanics, deformation, micromechanics, friction, stability analysis, creep, and earthquake have appeared frequently. Seismic phenomena and geological faults significantly impact human life and various sectors of industry. Yet, our current understanding of earthquake triggering mechanisms still leaves much to be desired. In an endeavor to unravel the intricate processes leading to fault formation, numerous researchers are diving deeper into the study of micro-fracture growth and mineral accumulation near fault zones [[16], [17], [18]]. Moreover, it's essential to highlight that repositories for nuclear waste are invariably situated within underground rock structures. Consequently, investigations focusing on the stability of these rock masses and micro-fracture evolutions are vital to advancing our knowledge within this research sphere [6,7].

Mechanical properties of rocks under extreme conditions (cluster #4) has been identified as one of the leading research cluster in microscale rock mechanics. The major keywords and terms in this research themes are behavior, mechanical properties, fracture toughness, temperature, anisotropy, energy, size, and nanoindentation. Within this group, ultra-precise techniques such as nanoindentation are deployed to evaluate the mechanical properties of minerals under different loading conditions. This approach aims to address the mesoscopic evolution mechanisms behind the time-dependent fractures in deeply buried hard rocks [61]. In addition to loading, another significant factor affecting deep earth conditions—high temperature—is also accounted for. The impact of these elevated temperatures is examined to comprehend the subsequent influence on the physical and mechanical properties of the rock structure [62,63].

This study's findings, as shown through bibliometric coupling, highlight several key themes in microscale rock mechanics research, including modeling fluid flow in porous media, fracture characterization, and the mechanical properties of rocks under extreme conditions. These insights form the foundation for identifying future research gaps and challenges.

Discussion

Building on the findings from the bibliometric analysis, the results reveal distinct clusters of research activity within microscale rock mechanics, each focusing on specific aspects of rock behavior at the microscale. The first cluster emphasizes modeling and simulation efforts in fluid flow, while the second focuses on fracture mechanics and crack propagation. Based on these results, several important research gaps can be identified. These gaps are fundamental to the advancement of rock mechanics and address key challenges in the field. While these gaps are explicit in terms of their focus on geological processes, multiscale modeling, and artificial intelligence, there are also implicit opportunities that require further exploration.

Integrating geological processes to characterize the properties of minerals

Incorporating geological processes into microscale studies provides essential insights into mineral properties and their behavior under varying conditions. Nanoindentation tests, for example, have demonstrated how different minerals respond uniquely to tectonic stresses and geological history [61,64]. These variations affect mineral hardness, stiffness, and modulus, which in turn influence macroscopic rock behavior. Explicitly, the gap lies in integrating geological history into microscale modeling, which can lead to better predictions of rock behavior in real-world applications. Implicitly, there is an opportunity to explore how these microscale properties influence larger geotechnical projects, such as tunneling and resource extraction.

Improving fracture prediction through multiscale modeling of rock heterogeneity

Rock heterogeneity characteristics originate from various factors, including differing mineral compositions, variations in mineral component sizes, diverse particle cementation, and elements such as joints and cleavages. Numerous studies have examined how this heterogeneity affects fracture formation as a function of external forces [65,66]. However, it is still not fully understood how microscale variations control macroscopic fractures. Explicitly, future research should focus on improving multiscale models that can bridge the gap between microscale and macroscale fractures, accounting for variations in rock properties. Implicitly, there is a need to explore how these multiscale models can be applied to different rock environments, enhancing fracture prediction accuracy for a wide range of geological conditions.

Developing artificial intelligence technology to predict complex fracturing

Machine learning approaches have been widely employed to predict rock mechanical properties like fracture toughness, rock brittleness and elastic moduli [[67], [68], [69]]. Explicitly, future research should focus on refining AI-aided modeling efforts, such as digital twins, which can simulate real-time fracture evolution and optimize reservoir designs [70]. Implicitly, there are also significant opportunities to explore how AI can play a crucial role in mitigating risks during fracturing processes, particularly in preventing potential fracturing-related disasters [14]. These AI-based innovations could revolutionize resource management and enhance safety protocols across various geotechnical applications. Moreover, the sophisticated AI-aided modeling efforts, especially in the context of reservoir fracture design optimization, hold substantial potential to reshape the field, offering more efficient and accurate fracture designs [5,71].

Conclusion

This study furnished a comprehensive landscape of microscale rock mechanics research by employing bibliometric and text mining analyses on a corpus of 1841 peer-reviewed articles, sourced from the Web of Science database. The analysis identified three main clusters of research: (a) microscale investigation of macroscale discoveries, which focuses on using microscale studies to explain macroscopic rock behavior, (b) porosity characterization and permeability enhancement, which explores the influence of rock porosity on fluid flow and permeability, and (c) particle model improvement and theoretical exploration, centered on advancing particle-based models and theoretical frameworks to better simulate rock behavior.

Despite progress, several critical research gaps remain. These include the need for integrating geological processes to enhance the understanding of mineral properties, advancing multiscale modeling to improve fracture prediction, and expanding the use of AI technologies for fracture design optimization and risk mitigation. Addressing these gaps will provide valuable insights into the practical applications of rock mechanics, improving prediction and management in geotechnical engineering.

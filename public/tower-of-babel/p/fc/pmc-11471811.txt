Resilience evaluation of memristor based PUF against machine learning attacks
Authors: Hebatallah M. Ibrahim, Heorhii Skovorodnikov, Hoda Alkhzaimi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Physical unclonable functions (PUFs) have emerged as a favorable hardware security primitive, they exploit the process variations to provide unique signatures or secret keys amidst other critical cryptographic applications. CMOS-based PUFs are the most popular type, they generate unique bit strings using process variations in semiconductor fabrication. However, most existing CMOS PUFs are found to be vulnerable to modeling attacks based on machine learning (ML) algorithms. Memristors leveraging nanotechnology fabrication processes and highly nonlinear behavior became an interesting alternative to the existing CMOS-based PUF technology, introducing cryptographic and resilient random outputs. Memristor-based PUFs are emerging due to the inherent randomness at both the memristor level due to the cycle-to-cycle (C2C) programming variation of the device and the fabrication process level such as the cross-sectional area and variations. Our objective is to test the resiliency of the security margins of the presented PUF using machine learning analysis tools, on-top of holistic NIST cryptographic randomness testing initially provided, to provide a high level of certainty in predicting the randomness output of the verified Memrister-based PUF. Introduction

Smart systems currently affect several aspects of people’s daily life that needs substantial connectivity of devices and massive data transfer. The integration of applications with industries, cities, and businesses brings quality to services and expansion in manufacturing and production; thus, today people of all ages tend to use smart applications for their daily services. The impact of resource-limited environments and IoT systems on daily life is significant in a digitized world. These systems require substantial device connectivity, complex computation, and significant data transfer. IoT devices are vulnerable to data loss, making resource-efficient cryptographic primitives essential for confidentiality and authentication in IoT platforms and networks. Cryptographic randomness plays an essential property when it comes to building confidentiality, authentication, integrity-focused cryptographic primitives, and security solutions. These are used in multiple critical digital infrastructures and hardware security. With the emerging nanotechnology such as memristors, a compelling opportunity presented itself for building a lightweight PUF of smaller utilization area, lower cost, and low power consumption which we address in this paper. Different studies have been proposing memristor PUFs due to the inherent randomness at both the fabrication level, and at a unique signature of the memristor that is called cycle-to-cycle (C2C) variation, which is explained in the following section2,3.

Physical Unclonable Functions (PUFs) are a promising lightweight cryptographic primitives for IoT devices as they don’t require memories for the encryption key storage in a memory-constrained environment such as IoT. With the innovation in technology low-cost and low-power PUFs designs have been proposed. The cryptographic strength of PUFs relies on the quality of randomness of its response. The relationship between the inputs and outputs of a PUF is usually distinguished by Challenge-Response Pairs (CRP) that are used to evaluate PUF’s ability to generate secure output that is truly random. To evaluate the PUF’s response cryptographic strength it must be verified against a family of metrics which contain uniqueness, uniformity, and diffuseness tests. Within uniqueness tests, we evaluate how different two PUFs with the same design and fabrication process compare to each other, two PUFs should ideally have a 50% difference in the output responses with the same input challenge. On the other hand, uniformity is the randomness indicator of the PUF, where a PUF should have an equal 50% probability of the distribution of 0’s or 1’s. Finally, diffuseness evaluates the difference between response vectors for different challenges applied to the same PUF, where it is ideally 50%, which is half the response vector length.

Related work and contribution

Several PUF designs have been proposed in literature over the past decade4,5. Silicon (CMOS) PUFs include Arbiter PUF6, Ring Oscillator PUF (RO-PUF)7, SRAM PUF8, Sense-amplifier PUF9, and Butterfly PUF10. Strong PUFs such as arbiter PUF, XOR arbiter PUF, and RO PUF are vulnerable to modeling attacks when an attacker has access to CRPs to train a mathematical model11–15. Several designs are based on memory-based PUFs technology such as silicon on insulator (SOI) transistors16 and memristors17–19. SOI and memristor technologies utilize a unique effect called the memory effect relying on a high k-dielectric insulator layer embedded in both technologies. The memory effect results in a change of cell resistance depending on the memory of the previous cycle’s current value. Thus, the memristor resistance values are random due to the random physical features on the intrinsic level. Different authors2,3 used the C2C signature of the memristor exploiting the pinched hysteresis in the I-V characteristics to create a true random number generator. Memristors have a fairly simple architecture consisting of an oxide layer between two electrodes, this simple design allowed it to be compatible with CMOS technology. As a consequence of such scalability, a larger number of memristors can be fabricated in the same wafer area required for a CMOS device. The compatibility of memristors with CMOS technology has been demonstrated in20 through fabricating memristor crossbar devices with Si MOSFETs on the same wafer. In19 the author studied a memristive read and write PUF and implemented two Al/CUxO/Cu devices demonstrating lateral switching wherein, however, one of the two devices became fixed in an LRS state. In18 the author continued the work in19 and demonstrated N-bit read and write memristive PUF verifying its efficiency through measuring the uniqueness, uniformity, and bit-aliasing to measure the statistical quality of the M-PUF. A TRNG-based memristor PUF has been proposed in21 providing a lightweight cryptographic primitive of high-quality entropy and small area without any post-processing algorithms or circuits. However, no further statistical tests of modeling attacks have been studied. In22 the authors proposed hybrid memristor-CMOS PUF circuits, offering low design area than CMOS-only PUFs. The authors utilized the delay variation in the memristor devices to generate instance-specific signatures. The authors verified the security of the proposed hybrid PUF against two machine learning attacks LR and SVM. Further advanced attack tools using machine learning techniques were built to clone the PUF behavior predicting the responses for unknown challenges. In this paper, we address the study of randomness resiliency and reliability of a fabricated memristor-based PUF (MR-PUF)17 based on fast and nonlinear fabrication variations within an integrated memristor. Memristor-based PUF framework and operation

Memristors have become an essential source of randomness for PUFs due to their stochasticity. They were first introduced in26 upon a symmetry requirement that demands a relationship to exist between the time integral of voltage (flux) and electric charge. As a result, the memristance was defined as a fourth electrical circuit feature with a mathematical model suggesting that the resistance of a memristor depends on the previous value of the current flow. The memristor understudy exhibits bipolar switching when a positive voltage is applied across the top and bottom electrodes of the memristor it switches from a High Resistance State (HRS) to Low Resistance State (LRS) and switches from LRS to HRS when a negative polarity is applied. This dynamic behavior introduced a large switching window providing a high HRS/LRS ratio and with the continuous technology evolution the memristor short OFF/ON switching time can be within 120/20 ns27.Fig. 1(a) Memristor fabricated design, (b) MR-PUF operation.

Randomness evaluation

We initially assessed the cryptographic randomness of the MR-PUF by evaluating the main standard metrics of randomness and reliability such as inter and intra-instance Hamming weight and Hamming distance to calculate the diffuseness, uniqueness, and uniformity of MR-PUF. To evaluate uniformity and diffuseness, we applied 100 different challenge sets each of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^7$$\end{document}27 bits to one MR-PUF, and the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^7$$\end{document}27 single response bits is linked to form a 128 multiple bits response. The uniformity which evaluates the percentage of ‘1’ and ‘0’ in responses of a PUF has resulted in 51.1% and 48.9% respectively as shown in Fig. 2a, b shows a greyscale bitmap showing the response 0s and 1s. To evaluate uniqueness, we used 100 different MR-PUF instances and evaluated the HD between responses to the same challenges from different MR-PUFs. The mean value of HD calculated in our study is 63.3 bits out of 128 bits which is almost 50% as shown in Fig. 2c. The diffuseness is calculated for our study to evaluate the difference between response vectors for different challenges using the same PUF. The HD between the responses was calculated to be 49.6% which is close to the ideal value of 50% as shown in Fig. 2d. Machine learning analysis tool for memristor-based PUF

Machine learning (ML) analysis is presented in this paper to demonstrate the cryptographic strength of the MR-PUF against modeling attacks. ML framework for attack detection consists of several sub-processes as shown in Fig. 3. First, the data provided by the environment is collected and then refined for data sampling. In the sampling data sub-process the data is divided into training and testing data. We chose the 70/30 split between the training and testing dataset respectively. To make sure we make our analysis comprehensive we drew ML architectures from different classes. Our goal was to employ machine learning methods across a spectrum of methods: supervised, unsupervised, and deep learning, as well as multiple model architecture paradigms. In supervised learning paradigm the model learns to map input variables to the desired output by finding patterns and relationships in the labeled training data. During the training process, the model tries to minimize the difference between its predicted output and the true output. The objective of unsupervised learning is to find hidden patterns, structures, or relationships within the data without any explicit guidance. Unsupervised learning algorithms aim to cluster similar data points or discover meaningful representations of the data. For supervised learning we wanted to employ classical powerful models like LR and SVM, tree-based approaches in a form of RF and XGboost, as well as deep learning via MLP and LSTM. From unsupervised learning we focused on clustering via Kmeans++ and probabilistic GMM.

Modeling attacks based on machine learning analysis in literature

The authors in28 demonstrated that the ML attack is a great threat to strong PUFs for the first time in 2004. Several studies have proposed different techniques to model strong PUFs11–15. Defences against such attacks that were proposed can be classed into structural non-linearization29–31 and CRP obfuscation32,33. However, most of the non-linearization and CRP obfuscation techniques reduce the reliability of PUFs and fail to defend against advanced ML approaches. Thus, in this paper we are testing the resilience of MR-PUF under ML attacks without post-processing algorithms, only relying on the true randomness of memristors as explained in the previous section. The robustness of a Memristive Crossbar PUF presented in23 was verified against SVM(RBF) and LR. The original accuracies of these attacks against the Memristive crossbar were 99% and 96.8%. After the authors presented two further operations XOR and column swapping to enhance the randomness of Memristive PUF the SVM(RBF) and LR attacks accuracies dropped to 57% and 49% respectively. The authors in24 presented different ML attacks on several PUFs that were previously presented in the literature to evaluate their vulnerability. The authors implemented the Memristor Arbiter PUF in34 and evaluated the robustness of Memristor Arbiter PUF against Random Forest. The RF attack achieved 70% accuracy arguing the robustness of the memristor presented in34 against ML attacks. In25 the authors presented a memristor-based PUF combined with a Lorenz device to enhance the randomness of their PUF, they showed almost 50% accuracy when they verified the resilience of their memristor against SVM and LR attacks. However, the addition of Lornez device added extra cost to the memristor design. The main contribution of this research is establishing an empirical ML analysis and attack tool to test the resilience of the randomness output of MR-PUF. Our main aim in this paper is to verify the resilience of the MR-PUF design and ensure the cryptographic security of its design. Our results are from an actual fabricated memristor and not from a simulated memristor as reflected in the other publications.

The ML analysis is repeated for numerous instances of a PUF to make sure that the low modeling accuracy is repeatable and that the PUF is resilient to the ML algorithms applied in the paper. K-means clustering

K-means clustering is the process of grouping large data sets into different independent clusters such that the data objects in a cluster are similar to each other and dissimilar to the data objects present in other clusters37. It is an iterative method and terminates when the optimum is reached. In K-means, the optimum is achieved by minimizing the intra-cluster variation and maximizing the inter-cluster variation: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum _{i=0}^{n}\min _{\mu _j \in C}(||x_i - \mu _j||^2)$$\end{document}∑i=0nminμj∈C(||xi-μj||2), where X are samples and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document}μ are means of disjoint, initialized clusters C. K-means algorithm converges when all the samples find their closest cluster center and change \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document}μ becomes minimal or \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mu$$\end{document}μ does not change. Tree ensemble models

Ensemble models are a collection of weaker classifiers which are bound by a global voting or score aggregation rules in order to produce a better prediction. For our attack, we use two variations of ensemble models. The first one is a Random Forest (RF), an ensemble model based on the decision tree components, it consists of a vast number of different decision trees that are working together as one model40. Decision Tree (DT) is a simple classification model based on a mathematical tree structure that classifies samples based on the decisions made at the nodes of the tree41. The leaves in turn act as bins for data that was separated based on the set of rules. Long short-term memory network

We wanted to explore advanced deep learning architectures to see if they can model the MR-PUF. Given a unique nature of our data with possessing only 2 inputs per bit, a wide range of classifiers is unavailable for processing. Therefore we chose to reframe the problem as time series prediction, where our I and V values can be treated as a signal and therefore can be fed into the model sequentially. We chose an improved variant of Recurrent Neural Network, an LSTM. A Recurrent Neural Network is a type of neural network that can process sequential data by maintaining an internal memory or state. It is designed to handle input sequences of varying lengths and can capture dependencies or patterns in the data across time steps. The key idea behind an RNN is that the output of the network at each time step becomes an input for the next time step, allowing it to maintain and propagate information over time46. This feedback loop enables the network to process sequential information and make predictions based on the context of the past inputs. Therefore the goal was to see if a group of data corresponding to bits will have a pattern that can aid in predicting next bit values. RNNs have a limitation in capturing long term dependencies, therefore we used a more advanced variant of RNN called LSTM47,48 to address this. LSTMs achieve this by introducing additional components called gates within each memory cell. These gates, including the input gate, forget gate, and output gate, help control the flow of information into, out of, and within the memory cell. The forget gate allows the LSTM to forget irrelevant information from the past, while the input gate allows the network to update the cell state with new information. The output gate then determines how much of the current cell state should be used to produce the output at that time step. We experimented both with MLP head size and the number of LSTM layers. The research aims to provide a high level of certainty in predicting the randomness output of the verified MR-PUF.

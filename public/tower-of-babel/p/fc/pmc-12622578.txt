Effect of early shoulder functional exercise timing on drainage duration and subcutaneous seroma formation in overweight women undergoing modified radical mastectomy for breast cancer
Authors: Zengyan Wang, Qinghai Lin, Xiong Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This retrospective study investigates the impact of early postoperative shoulder functional exercise timing on drainage duration and subcutaneous seroma formation in overweight women undergoing modified radical mastectomy (MRM) for breast cancer. A total of 194 overweight breast cancer patients (BMI > 24) who underwent MRM between January 2023 and December 2024 were included. Patients were divided into a study group (n = 101, shoulder exercise initiated on postoperative day 7) and a control group (n = 93, exercise started on day 3). Key outcomes included postoperative drainage volume, drainage tube retention duration, and incidence of subcutaneous seroma. Additional visual analyses and subgroup analyses by BMI were performed. A multivariable logistic regression model was constructed to identify independent predictors of seroma formation. The study group exhibited significantly lower postoperative drainage volume (229.5 ± 82.2 vs 581.3 ± 115.7 mL, P < .001), shorter drainage tube retention duration (6.8 ± 0.8 vs 8.3 ± 0.7 days, P < .001), and a lower incidence of subcutaneous seroma (11.9% vs 30.1%, χ2 = 8.850, P = .003). Visual comparisons confirmed more favorable distribution patterns in the study group. BMI subgroup analysis revealed that patients with BMI ≥ 27.0 kg/m2 had worse outcomes, and benefited more from delayed exercise. Multivariate analysis identified early exercise initiation (odds ratio (OR) = 4.14, P = .004), higher BMI (OR = 1.20, P = .031), and advanced tumor stage (OR = 1.92, P = .015) as independent risk factors for seroma. Delaying shoulder functional exercise initiation to postoperative day 7 significantly reduces drainage volume, shortens drainage tube retention, and lowers the risk of seroma in overweight women following mastectomy. These benefits are particularly pronounced in patients with higher BMI. Adjusting rehabilitation protocols based on body habitus may improve postoperative recovery and warrants broader clinical adoption.

1. Introduction

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed malignancy and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide. Its incidence continues to rise annually, posing a serious threat to women’s health.[1] Modified radical mastectomy (MRM) remains a cornerstone in the surgical management of breast cancer, offering effective tumor resection. However, the procedure is often associated with a high incidence of postoperative complications, which can significantly impair patients’ quality of life.[2] In recent years, early postoperative shoulder functional exercises have gained clinical attention as an important component of rehabilitation following MRM. Nevertheless, initiating such exercises too early may increase the risk of seroma formation, prolonged drainage, and delayed wound healing.[3] These concerns are particularly pronounced in overweight female breast cancer patients, for whom the optimal timing of early functional rehabilitation remains controversial.[4] Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the impact of the timing of early shoulder functional exercise on postoperative drainage volume, duration of drainage tube placement, and incidence of subcutaneous seroma in overweight women undergoing MRM, in order to provide more evidence-based guidance for postoperative rehabilitation.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. General information

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Zhangzhou Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University. This retrospective clinical study included 194 overweight female patients (body mass index [BMI] > 24) who underwent MRM for breast cancer at our hospital between January 2023 and December 2024. Among them, 101 patients were assigned to the study group, in which shoulder functional exercises were initiated on postoperative day 7, while 93 patients formed the control group, where exercises began on postoperative day 3. Grouping was based on institutional rehabilitation protocols applied during different time periods, rather than patient or physician preference. The sample size was determined by the number of eligible patients treated at our institution during the study period. A post hoc power analysis indicated that the sample size provided >80% power to detect the observed between-group differences in drainage duration and seroma incidence at a two-sided α level of 0.05.

All participants were female. The age range in the study group was 26 to 67 years, with a mean of 43.8 ± 4.6 years; in the control group, the age range was 24 to 69 years, with a mean of 44.1 ± 4.7 years. tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging in the study group was as follows: stage I, 30 cases; stage II, 50 cases; stage III, 21 cases. In the control group: stage I, 28 cases; stage II, 48 cases; stage III, 17 cases. There were no statistically significant differences between the 2 groups in terms of age, tumor stage, surgical approach, or BMI (P > .05), indicating comparability. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of the Zhangzhou Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University. Inclusion criteria: Pathologically confirmed breast cancer patients undergoing MRM; BMI > 24; Age between 20 and 70 years; No severe cardiac or pulmonary dysfunction; and No history of other malignancies. Exclusion criteria: preexisting shoulder joint disease; Serious postoperative infections or other major complications; and Coagulation disorders, hematologic diseases, or other serious chronic illnesses.

2.2. Surgical procedure and postoperative shoulder mobilization

The skin flaps were routinely dissected using an electrocautery device. The pectoralis major fascia and mammary gland were resected, and the lateral margin of the pectoralis major was dissected and retracted posteriorly. The clavipectoral fascia was incised to expose the axillary vein, followed by complete excision of axillary adipose tissue and lymph nodes. Large lymphatic vessels and blood vessels – particularly thoracic branches in the axillary and posterior interspace – were managed using an ultrasonic scalpel, while other tissues were resected with conventional electrocautery. In both groups, 2 drainage tubes were placed postoperatively: one in the axilla and one beside the sternum, each connected to negative-pressure suction. The incision was closed with interrupted absorbable sutures. In the study group, movement of the affected shoulder was restricted for one week postoperatively. In the control group, hand and shoulder joint exercises were initiated on postoperative day 3.

2.3. Observation indicators

The observation indicators included the following: Comparison of surgical parameters between the 2 groups, including operation time (defined as the time from skin incision to completion of skin closure), intraoperative blood loss (calculated as the combined volume absorbed by gauze and collected via suction), number of lymph nodes dissected, length of hospital stay, total postoperative drainage volume, duration of drainage tube retention, and incidence of subcutaneous seroma. The total drainage volume was recorded at the time of drain removal, and the drainage duration was measured from the day of surgery to the day the drainage tube was removed. Drain removal was performed when drainage was ≤10 mL for 2 consecutive days. The incidence of subcutaneous seroma was calculated as the proportion of patients who developed fluid accumulation after drain removal; and Postoperative complications were compared between the 2 groups, focusing primarily on subcutaneous seroma formation.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using Statistical Product and Service Solutions statistical software version 18.0. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD) and compared between groups using independent sample t-tests. Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages [n (%)] and compared using the chi-square (χ2) test or Fisher exact test where appropriate. To further examine the influence of overweight severity, subgroup analyses were conducted by stratifying patients into 2 BMI categories (24.0–26.9 kg/m2 and ≥ 27.0 kg/m2), with between-group differences assessed using t-tests for continuous outcomes and χ2 tests for categorical outcomes. In addition, a multivariable logistic regression model was applied to identify independent predictors of subcutaneous seroma formation. Variables included in the model were selected based on clinical relevance and univariate associations, and odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals were calculated. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P-value < .05 was considered statistically significant.

These findings clearly demonstrate that delaying early postoperative shoulder functional exercises until postoperative day 7 has positive clinical significance for overweight women undergoing MRM for breast cancer. It can effectively reduce the occurrence of postoperative complications.

3.4. Visual comparison of key postoperative outcomes

To further illustrate the effects of shoulder functional exercise timing on postoperative recovery, we conducted additional visual analyses of drainage-related outcomes. As shown in Figure 1, the total postoperative drainage volume in the study group (n = 101, exercise initiated on postoperative day 7) was significantly lower and more concentrated, with a mean of 229.5 ± 82.2 mL. In contrast, the control group (n = 93, exercise initiated on day 3) exhibited a broader and right-shifted distribution, with a mean drainage volume of 581.3 ± 115.7 mL. The between-group difference was highly significant (P < .001), further supporting the notion that delayed initiation of shoulder exercises contributes to reduced postoperative fluid accumulation.

4. Discussion

Postoperative shoulder dysfunction is one of the most common complications following MRM for breast cancer, primarily manifested as shoulder pain and restricted mobility.[5] It is a significant factor affecting postoperative quality of life, with an incidence rate of up to 30%.[6] Several studies have emphasized the role of early mobilization in improving range of motion and preventing adhesions, though the timing remains controversial.[7,8] Early shoulder functional exercise is widely recognized as an effective measure to prevent and alleviate shoulder dysfunction.[9] However, for overweight women with breast cancer, due to their unique physical characteristics and postoperative recovery patterns, the optimal timing for initiating early shoulder functional exercise remains unclear. This study focuses on this specific patient population and explores the impact of the timing of early postoperative shoulder exercises on the occurrence of complications, carrying important clinical implications.

The results of this study showed no statistically significant differences between the 2 groups in terms of operative time, intraoperative blood loss, number of lymph nodes dissected, and length of hospital stay, indicating comparable intraoperative conditions and postoperative recovery trajectories. This provided a solid foundation for the subsequent comparison of postoperative complications. However, the postoperative drainage volume, duration of drainage tube placement, and incidence of subcutaneous seroma were all significantly lower in the study group (which initiated shoulder functional exercise on postoperative day 7) compared to the control group (which began exercise on day 3), with all differences reaching statistical significance (P < .05). These findings suggest that, for overweight women undergoing MRM for breast cancer, delaying the initiation of early shoulder functional exercise until postoperative day 7 can effectively reduce postoperative drainage volume, shorten the duration of drainage tube placement, and lower the incidence of subcutaneous seroma, thereby minimizing postoperative complications and promoting faster recovery. The possible underlying mechanism, as speculated by the authors, may lie in the unique physiological characteristics of overweight patients. These individuals typically have thicker subcutaneous fat, particularly in the axillary region, which is also rich in lymphatic vessels. Premature initiation of shoulder movement may disrupt the closure of lymphatic vessel ends that were previously sealed, leading to increased lymphatic leakage. It may also induce inflammation or fluid accumulation due to detachment of well-adhered skin and subcutaneous tissues, potentially compromising wound healing or treatment efficacy. Similar hypotheses have been proposed in biomechanical studies that link early upper limb activity to increased axillary lymphatic pressure and seroma risk.[10] In contrast, appropriately postponing functional exercise allows the body more time for self-repair and adjustment, during which lymphatic vessels can better close, skin and subcutaneous tissues can re-adhere properly, and local inflammation and edema can subside. Initiating exercise at this stage is more conducive to promoting lymphatic drainage and reducing seroma formation, ultimately lowering the risk of postoperative complications.

The innovation of this study lies in its focus on a specific and often overlooked population – overweight women with breast cancer – and its investigation into how the timing of early postoperative shoulder functional exercise affects the incidence of postoperative complications. Although the impact of BMI on postoperative complications has been reported, few studies have investigated its interaction with rehabilitation timing.[11,12] Previous studies have predominantly focused on general breast cancer populations, with relatively little attention paid to overweight patients. For example, Deng et al emphasized the importance of early rehabilitation interventions for breast cancer patients in general, but did not address the unique challenges faced by overweight individuals.[13] In contrast, this study specifically targets overweight women with breast cancer, who often face additional postoperative recovery challenges, such as thicker subcutaneous fat (particularly in the axillary region), greater lymphatic burden, higher susceptibility to inflammation and edema, and slower recovery. The key innovation of this research is its targeted exploration of the optimal timing for initiating early shoulder functional exercises in this unique group, offering more precise rehabilitation guidance. The findings provide new insights and practical strategies for improving postoperative recovery in overweight breast cancer patients. By appropriately adjusting the timing of shoulder functional exercise, clinicians can effectively reduce the incidence of postoperative complications, enhance patient quality of life, and increase satisfaction. These conclusions are consistent with those of Miao et al., but with a more targeted approach that offers an effective rehabilitation strategy specifically for overweight patients.[14]

In addition to evaluating the overall benefit of delayed shoulder mobilization, this study further explored its effect across different levels of overweight severity. Subgroup analyses revealed that patients with a BMI ≥ 27.0 kg/m2 experienced higher postoperative drainage volume, prolonged drainage duration, and an elevated incidence of subcutaneous seroma compared to those with a BMI of 24.0 to 26.9 kg/m2. These findings suggest that overweight status may amplify the risk of postoperative complications, and that delayed initiation of shoulder exercise may be especially beneficial in patients with more pronounced adiposity. This subgroup-specific effect adds a new layer of clinical nuance to the existing rehabilitation strategies for overweight breast cancer patients.

To further validate the robustness of our findings, a multivariable logistic regression model was constructed to identify independent predictors of subcutaneous seroma formation. Early exercise initiation (on postoperative day 3), higher BMI, and advanced tumor stage (II/III) emerged as significant risk factors. These results reinforce the multifactorial nature of postoperative complications and underscore the importance of personalized rehabilitation strategies that consider both surgical timing and patient-specific risk profiles. The integration of such risk stratification into clinical decision-making may enhance the effectiveness of postoperative management.

Despite providing valuable data on postoperative rehabilitation for overweight breast cancer patients, this study has several limitations. First, as this was a retrospective study, inherent methodological limitations such as potential selection bias and information bias cannot be excluded. Patient allocation to groups was not randomized, and some clinical information may have been incompletely recorded, which could affect the accuracy and generalizability of the findings. Second, the sample size was relatively small, which may further limit the external validity of the results. Future studies should include a larger cohort, particularly incorporating more severely obese patients (BMI ≥ 35), to assess whether the optimal timing of shoulder exercise differs by obesity level. Third, although delaying exercise until postoperative day 7 significantly reduced drainage volume and seroma incidence, this study did not evaluate its impact on long-term recovery of shoulder joint function. Therefore, it remains unclear whether a delay in exercise initiation affects ultimate functional outcomes, incidence of lymphedema, or overall quality of life. Prior research suggests that shoulder function recovery after breast cancer surgery follows a nonlinear trajectory, with some patients continuing to show improvements for an extended period postoperatively.[15] Extending the follow-up period and incorporating standardized functional assessments would help clarify the long-term effects of delayed exercise initiation. Future studies should therefore aim to expand the sample size, extend the follow-up duration, and explore a comprehensive, multimodal rehabilitation strategy that integrates prehabilitation, nutritional support, and digital health technologies. Such an approach could optimize long-term outcomes and quality of life in overweight breast cancer patients. We believe that developing an integrated “prehabilitation–delayed exercise–long-term follow-up” rehabilitation model specifically tailored for overweight breast cancer patients warrants further investigation.

In addition, there remains room for improvement in the standardization of rehabilitation interventions. Although the functional exercise protocol in this study was revised based on clinical guidelines, it did not incorporate individualized adjustments to exercise intensity for different patients. Recent research suggests that personalized rehabilitation plans incorporating biofeedback technologies may further optimize outcomes. For example, studies have demonstrated that using surface electromyography to monitor shoulder muscle activation in real time and dynamically adjust training intensity can yield significantly better results than conventional protocols, particularly in patients at high risk of lymphedema.[16] Future research may explore integrating such technologies into delayed exercise regimens tailored for overweight patients.

We anticipate that in the near future, clinicians will be able to adopt BMI-stratified rehabilitation strategies for overweight patients and, by integrating multidimensional factors such as surgical extent and inflammatory markers, further personalize the optimal timing for postoperative exercise. This approach is expected to provide more comprehensive guidance for the postoperative recovery of breast cancer patients and improve their long-term quality of life.

Impact of Mavacamten Approval on Septal Reduction Therapy Rates in Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy
Authors: Ramzi Ibrahim, Winston Wang, Hoang Nhat Pham, Mahmoud Abdelnabi, Steven J Lester, Susan Wilansky, Hartzell V Schaff, Jeffrey B Geske, Reza Arsanjani, Chadi Ayoub
Published: 2026

Cardiac myosin inhibitors are novel therapies for obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (oHCM). Mavacamten, the first Food and Drug Administration‐approved cardiac myosin inhibitor, is indicated for symptomatic oHCM. 1 In EXPLORER‐HCM (Clinical Study to Evaluate Mavacamten [MYK‐461] in Adults With Symptomatic Obstructive Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy), mavacamten improved outflow tract gradients, peak VO₂, and symptoms. 2 VALOR‐HCM (A Study to Evaluate Mavacamten in Adults With Symptomatic Obstructive HCM Who Are Eligible for Septal Reduction Therapy) showed reduced eligibility for septal reduction therapy (SRT), 3 yet real‐world SRT rate data remain limited. We examined whether mavacamten approval influenced SRT rates using epidemiologic data.

We conducted a cross‐sectional analysis using the TriNetX research network, which aggregates deidentified electronic records from >100 health care organizations. All data used, including the analytic approach, are publicly available through institutional agreement in the TriNetX database. US patients with oHCM (International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision [ICD‐10]: I42.1, I42.2) were identified, excluding those with concomitant aortic valve surgery. The pre‐Food and Drug Administration mavacamten approval cohort included diagnoses from April 1, 2019 to March 31, 2022, and the postapproval cohort from April 1, 2022 to April 1, 2025.

The primary outcome was SRT (surgical myectomy or alcohol septal ablation; Current Procedural Terminology codes 33416 and 93583) during each time window. We calculated 3 measures: incidence proportion (percentage of patients who underwent SRT during the time window), incidence rate (number of new SRT cases per person‐day of observation), and prevalence (percentage of patients with any record of SRT during the time window, including those with prior procedures), stratified by age, sex, race, and ethnicity. Absolute risk differences and risk ratios (RR) of the SRT incidence proportions were calculated, with 95% CIs derived from the SE of log(RR). Analyses were performed in TriNetX using its incidence and prevalence analysis tools. The Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board exempted the study (IRB #24‐013396).

From a total of 60 745 437 patients in the TriNetX network during the preapproval period and 64 351 618 patients during the postapproval period, 70 514 patients with oHCM were identified in the preapproval period (prevalence 0.116%) and 82 421 patients were identified in the postapproval period (prevalence 0.128%). In the postapproval cohort, 2.1% of patients had documentation of mavacamten prescription. During the preapproval period, the incidence proportion of SRT was 0.88%, with an incidence rate of 9.33 cases per million person‐days. In the postapproval period, the incidence proportion was lower at 0.75%, with a corresponding incidence rate of 8.46 cases per million person‐days. This translates to an absolute reduction of 0.13 percentage points in SRT incidence proportion representing a relative reduction of ≈15% (RR, 0.85 [95% CI, 0.76–0.95]) (Table1). However, SRT prevalence (reflecting history of SRT) was higher in the postapproval period (1.72%) compared with the preapproval period (1.61%) and may reflect increased awareness for diagnosis of oHCM with increased diagnosis rates in the latter period.

Women had higher SRT incidence proportions than men in both periods (preapproval: 1.04% versus 0.66%; postapproval: 0.88% versus 0.63%). Following mavacamten approval, SRT incidence proportion decreased among women (RR, 0.84 [95% CI, 0.76–0.93]), whereas no significant change was observed among men (RR, 0.95 [95% CI, 0.84–1.08]). White patients exhibited the highest SRT incidence proportions in both periods (1.17% pre versus 0.96% post; RR, 0.82 [95% CI, 0.74–0.90]), whereas Non‐Hispanic Black patients had lower incidence proportions (0.32% pre versus 0.27% post; RR, 0.85 [95% CI, 0.71–1.02]). Among age strata, patients aged 60 to 64 years demonstrated the highest SRT incidence proportions in both periods (1.16% pre versus 0.99% post; RR, 0.85 [95% CI, 0.77–0.94]), with generally lower postapproval incidence proportions across age groups.

We revealed a decline in SRT incidence proportions following the approval of mavacamten, suggesting that cardiac myosin inhibitors may reduce the need for SRT, aligning with prior clinical trial data. The VALOR‐HCM trial demonstrated that treatment with mavacamten reduced the proportion of patients meeting criteria for SRT, from 76.8% in the placebo group to 17.9% in the mavacamten group. Furthermore, follow‐up showed that nearly 90% of patients who began mavacamten therapy remained free from SRT at 128 weeks. 3 , 4 The decline in SRT volumes suggests a shift toward medical management, with potential effects on institutional expertise, referral patterns, and access to care. Moreover, SRT incidence rose among Hispanic and Asian patients, emphasizing possible disparities and varying health care access that require further exploration.

Several limitations merit consideration. As an observational study, unmeasured confounders, including procedural indication, disease severity, geographic variation, and provider decision‐making, could not be assessed. Outcomes such as post‐SRT readmissions were unavailable due to platform constraints. Use of mavacamten may also be influenced by provider familiarity, formulary access, and socioeconomic barriers. Our observed SRT prevalence was lower than prior National Inpatient Sample‐based reports (≈5.9%), 5 likely reflecting institutional heterogeneity and variation in procedural expertise. Safety concerns, particularly regarding reduced left ventricular ejection fraction, may affect prescribing patterns.

In this analysis of patients with oHCM, we observed a reduction in SRT rates following the Food and Drug Administration approval of mavacamten. The implications of these results reflect shifting treatment paradigms and referral patterns.

Association between plasma extracellular vesicles LncRNAs and metabolic syndrome in polycystic ovary syndrome
Authors: Yan-zhen Wu, Lei-lei Mao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis case-control study aimed to investigate the relationship between plasma EVs LncRNAs, specifically Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1, and MetS in PCOS patients, to identify a novel biomarker for early MetS diagnosis in these patients.MethodsThe levels of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 in PCOS patients with and without MetS were quantified using qRT-PCR and then compared. Generalized linear regression and trend regression analyses were utilized to examine the association between three LncRNAs and MetS. ROC curve, DCA, AIC assessment, and importance ranking were applied to assess the clinical value of the three LncRNAs for MetS in PCOS patients.ResultsThe study included 220 newly diagnosed PCOS patients, with 82 (37.273%) having MetS. The expression levels of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 were lower in PCOS patients with MetS compared to those without MetS (all P < 0.001). Further, significant independent associations between the three LncRNAs and MetS occurrence were found (all P < 0.05), with a negative relationship. Among three LncRNAs, MALAT1 had the superior performance (AUC = 0.801) in discriminative the occurrence of MetS in PCOS patients. In addition, MALAT1 also showed the highest importance for the risk of MetS.ConclusionThe low expressions of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 were associated with the high risk of MetS in PCOS patients, with MALAT1 potentially serving as a preferred molecular marker for discriminative MetS in PCOS.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13048-025-01801-4.Keywords: PCOS, MetS, LncRNAs, MALAT1, NEAT1

Introduction

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is the most common endocrine disorder in young women of reproductive age, impacting 10% of women of childbearing age [1]. Although the exact cause of PCOS remains unclear, it is widely accepted that its origins are multifactorial, with genetic factors playing a significant role in both its development and outcome [2]. Patients with PCOS were at high risk for metabolic syndromes (MetS). The pathophysiological alterations associated with PCOS lead to disruptions in glucose and lipid metabolism, particularly in obese individuals with PCOS, who are more prone to central obesity, high blood pressure, abnormal lipid profiles, impaired glucose tolerance, and other MetS [3–5]. A review of multicenter randomized trials revealed that 27.2% of 1508 infertile women with PCOS also had MetS, and those with both conditions experienced longer periods of infertility compared to those without MetS [6]. In addition, earlier research has indicated that PCOS patients with MetS face a significantly increased risk of cardiovascular disease and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [7, 8]. It was crucial to identify high-risk PCOS patients for MetS and initiate early intervention. However, there was a shortage of discriminative markers for PCOS with MetS.

Recently, liquid biopsy has made significant strides in early tumor detection, medication guidance, efficacy evaluation, and recurrence monitoring. Extracellular RNAs were found in various body fluids and served as innovative liquid biopsy markers, primarily originating from extracellular vesicles (EVs), including small RNAs, long-chain non-coding RNAs (LncRNAs), messenger RNAs, and other RNA types, which hold promise for early diagnosis of tumors, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases, and infectious diseases, as well as for medication guidance [9–11]. LncRNAs are transcripts of more than 200 nucleotides in length, and they usually cannot be translated into proteins. Dysregulation of LncRNAs is closely linked to numerous human reproductive disorders. It has been demonstrated that several LncRNAs, such as nuclear paraspeckle assembly transcript 1 (NEAT1), metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript-1 (MALAT1), and X-inactive specific transcript (Xist), are extensively involved in the progression of PCOS [12, 13]. The down-regulation of MALAT1 worsens PCOS by modulating miR-302d-3p-mediated leukemia inhibitory factor activity [14]. NEAT1 exacerbated metabolic disorders and ovarian pathological changes in the PCOS mice model through down-regulation of miR-324-3p and up-regulation of BRD3 [15]. Down-regulation of Xist expression in peripheral blood may be associated with poor pregnancy outcomes in PCOS [13]. The expression of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 in plasma EVs of 20 PCOS patients and 20 healthy controls was examined in our previous research (Table S1), revealing significantly lower levels in the PCOS group compared to the healthy controls, which was consistent with the above.

The use of liquid biopsy plasma EVs RNA for early diagnosis of the disease had an inherent advantage and can discriminate the development of the disease at an earlier stage. Currently, the use of liquid biopsy for MetS in PCOS patients is still in the preliminary stage, and there is no good marker molecule for clinical diagnosis. Notably, three LncRNAs—Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1—found in plasma EVs had not been identified as MetS discriminative biomakers in PCOS patients. Therefore, this study collected plasma samples from PCOS patients with or without MetS, who were admitted to our hospital during the same period. We assessed the levels of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 to investigate their association with MetS risk and evaluated their discriminative capabilities, aiming to develop a more sensitive and precise diagnostic approach for the benefit of PCOS patients.

Materials and methods

Study design and study population

This research was a case-control study involving 82 newly diagnosed PCOS patients with MetS and 138 patients with simple PCOS attending the same clinic. Inclusion criteria were as follows: [1] Patients with PCOS diagnosed according to the Chinese guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of PCOS [16], with ultrasonography revealing ≥ 12 follicles measuring 2–9 mm in diameter and/or an ovarian volume of ≥ 10 mL in one or both ovaries, accompanied by menstrual irregularities, hirsutism, acne, and other clinical symptoms; [2] No organic lesions of other organs; [3] Complete information of all baseline information. Exclusion criteria were as follows: [1] Presence of cardiac, hepatic, or renal insufficiency; [2] Receiving hormone and other drug treatment; [3] Multiple pregnancy; [4] Presence of malignant tumors. The study received approval from the Wenzhou Central Hospital Medical Ethics Committee (Approval Number: 202402281955000019118), and informed consent was obtained from the research subjects.

However, before this study, no existing data were available specifically for PCOS patients combined with MetS. Our pre-experiment revealed that the Xist level in the PCOS patient group was 1.87 ± 0.77, while the Xist value in the non-PCOS control group was 2.96 ± 1.85 (Table S1). Based on these preliminary results and using a 1:1 matched design, the calculated minimum required sample size was 26 cases per group. To ensure robust statistical power and account for potential variability, this study ultimately enrolled 82 patients with PCOS combined with MetS and 138 patients with PCOS alone from the same clinic, significantly exceeding the calculated requirement.

Definition of MetS

The diagnosis of MetS was based on the third guideline of the Adult Treatment Group of the National Cholesterol Education Program of the United States [17]: [1] Central obesity: waist circumference (WC) ≥ 85 cm in females; [2] Hypertriglyceridemia: fasting plasma triglyceride (TG) level ≥ 150 mg/dL or use of TG-lowering medication; [3] High-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) levels < 50 mg/dL in females; [4] Hypertension: systolic blood pressure ≥ 130 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥ 85 mmHg, or use of antihypertensive drugs; [5] Hyperglycaemia: fasting plasma glucose (FPG) ≥ 5.6 mmol/L, or use of antidiabetic medication. Patients with the presence of at least three of these criteria were diagnosed as MetS. Those diagnosed with PCOS combined with MetS were included in the case group, and PCOS without combined MetS were included in the control group.

General data collection

Baseline data on demographic characteristics, disease history, medication history, laboratory parameters, and insulin-related index were collected from all participants. The demographic characteristics included age (years), marital status, pregnancy, tobacco, drinking, WC (cm), and body mass index (BMI, kg/m2). Disease history involved chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and moderate liver disease (MLD). Drug history included spironolactone and metformin. Laboratory parameters collected included anion gap (AG, mEq/L) bicarbonate (mEq/L), blood urine nitrogen (BUN, mg/dL), potassium (mEq/L), sodium (mEq/L), calcium (mg/dL), chloride (mEq/L), creatinine (mg/dL), hemoglobin (g/dL), platelets (K/µL), red cell distribution width (RDW, %), albumin (g/dL), white blood cells (WBC, K/µL), low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C, mg/dL), total cholesterol (TC, mg/dL), glucose (mg/dL), HDL-C (mg/dL), TG (mg/dL), and FPG (mmol/L). Xist, MALAT1 and NEAT1 expression assays in EVs

Isolation of plasma EVs

On the day of admission and the day of physical examination, 5 mL of fasting venous blood was collected from PCOS patients into EDTA tubes, shaken well, and left to stand for 30 min at room temperature. The blood was put into a high-speed centrifuge at 4℃ and set at 3000 r/min for 15 min, and the supernatant was transferred to a 15 ml centrifuge tube to obtain human plasma samples, which were sealed and stored in a refrigerator at -80℃ for freezing and waiting for use. The EVs were separated from human plasma by ultracentrifugation. In this study, “EV lncRNAs” refers to the isolation procedure and does not provide conclusive evidence of vesicular origin.

Xist, MALAT1 and NEAT1 assays

Total RNA was isolated from EVs samples using an EpiTM Cell-Free Circulating RNA Purification Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. 10 pmol of nematode cel-miR-39 was added to the extracted total RNA as an external reference [20]. The cDNA was obtained by reverse transcription reaction using Evo M-MLV Reverse Transcription Kit (AG11707, Acres Biologicals, Hunan, China). 6 µL of RNA (equivalent to 60% of total RNA isolated from plasma EVs) was mixed with 2 µL Reverse transcriptase reaction buffer Mix II (5X), 0.5 µL Evo M-MLV reverse transcriptase enzyme Mix, 0.5 µl Oligo dT (18T) Primer (50 µM), 0.5 µL Random 6 mers Primer (100 µM), and sufficient RNAse free water to reach a total volume of 10 µL. The reaction conditions were 37 ℃ for 15 min, 85℃ for 5 s, and 4 ℃. The cDNA obtained from the above reverse transcription process could be directly analyzed by quantitative real-time-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) as follows: the cDNA was analyzed by SYBR® Green Premix Pro Taq HS qPCR Kit (AG11701, Hunan Acres Biologicals, China) on a CFX96 Touch Real-Time PCR System (Bio-Rad, CA, USA). Indeed, 2 µL of cDNA was added to 10 µL of SYBR® Green Pro Taq HS Premix (2X), 0.4 µL of forward and reverse primers (10 µM), 0.4 µL of ROX Reference Dye (4 µM), and 6.8 µL of RNAase/DNAase-free water, and amplified in the following steps: 95 °C for 30 s (Step 1), 95 °C for 5 s (Step 2), 60 °C for 30 s (Step 3), and dissociation stage (Step 4). Steps 2 and 3 were cycled 40 times. The primers for the markers are shown in Table 1. Data from qRT-PCR were used to calculate the relative expression of the target genes using the relative quantification method with the application of 2− ΔΔCt. The relative expression was calculated as follows: [1] ΔCt was calculated as ΔCt LncRNA = Ct Target LncRNA – Ct cel−miR− 39; [2] ΔΔCt = ΔCt samples - ΔCt non−MetS group mean. The mean ΔCt values of the non-MetS group are calibrator group (ΔCt calibrator); [3] Relative expression = 2− ΔΔCt. LncRNA expression is the relative value of relative expression to the non-MetS group mean value of ΔCt.

Statistical analysis

A database was established by Excel, and data were analyzed using R statistical software, with P < 0.05 as a statistically significant difference. Non-normally distributed continuous data were described using medians and quartiles (P25, P75), and comparisons between the two groups were made using the Mann-Whitney test. Normally distributed data were described using mean ± SD and compared between groups using a t-test. Categorical information was described using frequencies and percentages, and comparisons between groups were made using the chi-square test. Multicollinearity analysis was used to determine collinearity between variables that differed at baseline, with the variance inflation factor (VIF) > 10 considered as collinearity. Initially, a restricted cubic spline (RCS) curve was used to explore the linear relationship between Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1, and MetS in PCOS patients. The association of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 with MetS in PCOS patients was analyzed using multivariable generalized linear regression. The three LncRNAs were further divided into three groups according to the triples. Trend regression was explored to analyze their variations related to MetS risk. Spearman correlation analysis was used to explore the correlation of the three LncRNAs with MetS-related indices. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were applied to compare the discriminative value of difference variables in multivariate generalized linear regression. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to compare the net clinical benefit of variables with the top three discriminative performances. Additionally, the goodness-of-fit and complexity of the three LncRNAs models were further examined. The importance of the three LncRNAs was ranked using two machine learning algorithms, namely logistic classification and linear regression.

Clinical value and discriminative performance of xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 for MetS

ROC analysis was further employed to explore the clinical value of the six difference variables in discriminating MetS occurrence in PCOS patients (Table 5). The ROC results indicated that the discriminative performances of Xist (AUC = 0.760), MALAT1 (AUC = 0.801), and NEAT1 (AUC = 0.780) were strong (AUC > 0.75) (Fig. 6A), with MALAT having the largest AUC value and Xist having the highest specificity (0.967). MALAT had the highest sensitivity (0.761), but all three LncRNAs had limited sensitivity. Additionally, the AUC values of the three lncRNAs were not significantly different from each other (Delong test P > 0.05), but they were all higher than the insulin-related index of TyG (AUC = 0.680), insulin (AUC = 0.617), and HOMA-IR (AUC = 0.692) (Delong test P < 0.001) (Table 5). As ROC analysis did not reveal any difference in the discriminative performance of MetS among Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1, DCA analysis was conducted to compare the net clinical benefit of these three RNAs. The results showed that MALAT1 offered a greater net clinical benefit than Xist and NEAT1 (Fig. 6B).

Discussion

The transcription of LncRNAs plays a role in regulating various diseases, including cancer, Alzheimer’s disease, and coronary artery disease [24]. In this case-control study, low levels of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 were found to be associated with the development of MetS in PCOS, and the highest correlation was found between Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 and PFG, which was an indicator of MetS. ROC analysis indicated that these three LncRNAs had good performance in discriminating MetS in PCOS. Among them, MALAT1 had the highest AUC, highest importance, and lowest AIC, making it the most significant.

Some findings revealed that Xist expression is low in PCOS patients, and increasing Xist levels significantly reduced cell viability and promoted apoptosis. Xist helps prevent PCOS by modulating the miR-212-3p/Ras p21 protein activator 1 axis [25]. Yao et al. [26] found that XIST was the most important LncRNA among MetS-associated LncRNAs by constructing an LncRNA-miRNA-mRNA network. Low Xist expression inhibits polycomb repressive complex 2 activity, resulting in the absence of a repressive marker -H3K27me3 in the promoter region of metabolic genes [27]. A reduction in H3K27me3 at the tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) promoter increases TNF-α levels [28]. TNF-α expression was associated with BMI, body fat percentage, etc. TNF-α significantly raises IL-6, decreases resistin, and stimulates leptin secretion in 3T3-L1 adipocytes, contributing to MetS [29]. Constitutive activation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPAR-γ) in skeletal muscle reduces intramyocardial lipid accumulation and provides a protective effect on systemic glucose homeostasis and insulin resistance [30]. Low NEAT1 expression diminishes interaction with PPARγ [31], which leads to fat accumulation and an imbalance in glucose homeostasis. Additionally, down-regulation of NEAT1 expression may oxidize LDL-C by elevating miR-377-3p expression and decreasing Wnt pathway protein expression through competitive binding with miR-377-3p [32].

In examining the impact of three LncRNAs—Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1—on the development of MetS in PCOS patients, we identified MALAT1 as the most significant. Research indicates that MALAT1 expression is reduced in the ovarian tissues, serum, or adipose tissues of individuals with PCOS. The decrease of MALAT1 may influence transforming growth factor β signaling by acting as a sponge for miR-125b and miR-203a, thereby facilitating PCOS [33]. However, some studies have also reported that MALAT1 expression is elevated locally in PCOS ovaries (e.g., granulosa cells) [34]. The different results of MALAT1 might be attributed to variations in disease subtypes or research models. Our findings revealed that diminished MALAT1 expression is linked to MetS development in PCOS patients. Lower MALAT1 levels may elevate androgen receptor (AR) and insulin receptor substrate 2 (IRS-2) expression through increased miR-30a-5p [12]. AR and IRS-2 were strongly associated with insulin resistance (IR) and hyperandrogenemia, which were independent discriminative biomakers of the presence of MetS [35].

In this study, we also found that among the MetS-related indicators, Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 had the greatest correlation with FPG, which is commonly used for the diagnosis of diabetes [36]. IR is a primary cause of T2DM [37]. It has been demonstrated that Xist is linked to IR, with low expression in IR cells. The Xist/miR-182-5p axis reduces hepatic IR in vitro via the IGF-1R/PI3K/Akt signaling pathway [38]. MALAT1 is associated with insulin signaling and glucose/lipid metabolism in various tissues. Down-regulation of MALAT1 may contribute to pancreatic β-cell dysfunction and the development of T2DM through regulation or direct interactions with polypyrimidine bundle-binding protein 1 [39, 40].

Detecting the three LncRNAs, Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 (particularly MALAT1) could be beneficial for the early identification of high-risk PCOS patients with MetS, guiding clinical interventions such as lifestyle changes or pharmacological treatments to prevent MetS. In the future, it may be possible to stratify patients with PCOS based on their LncRNAs expression profiles. Low expression of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 may contribute to MetS by affecting glucose metabolism (e.g., strong correlation with FPG), lipid metabolism, or inflammatory pathways, offering a new perspective on the mechanisms of metabolic disorders in PCOS. As the most discriminative molecule among the three LncRNAs, MALAT1 could become a key target for future mechanistic studies. The specific pathways through which Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 regulate MetS, such as their effects on insulin signaling, adipose differentiation, or mitochondrial function, need to be clarified using cell or animal models. Future research could integrate transcriptomic, metabolomic, and epigenetic data to construct a molecular network of PCOS-MetS and uncover the interactions of LncRNAs with other molecules, such as miRNAs and metabolites.

In this study, we found that although single LncRNAs (Xist, MALAT1, NEAT1) had some discriminative ability for MetS (AUC all > 0.75), the sensitivity and specificity could not be balanced. The constructed nomogram model based on the three LncRNAs significantly optimised discriminative efficacy (AUC = 0.910), and the sensitivity (89.0%) and specificity (80.4%) were well balanced. This breakthrough has important clinical implications. Firstly, the model provides a more reliable molecular marker combination strategy for the early screening of MetS, which compensates for the insufficient discriminative ability of a single indicator, and is particularly suitable for accurate identification of high-risk populations. Secondly, the high sensitivity ensures a low rate of underdiagnosis, while the high specificity reduces the risk of misdiagnosis, which is crucial for early intervention and personalized management of MetS. In the future, if the performance of this model is further validated in larger samples and prospective studies, which may promote the application of lncRNA assays in the clinical practice of metabolic diseases and provide new ideas for the prevention and precise diagnosis of MetS.

In this research, we employed a case-control study to examine the relationship between three LncRNAs—Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 in plasma EVs—and MetS in patients with PCOS. Although causality could not be directly proved, it could provide important clues for etiological exploration. All patients were newly diagnosed cases, controlled for recall bias and selection bias to some extent. However, the study has certain limitations: it did not differentiate between large and small EVs. Moreover, the isolated total EVs were not pure, as they might have included aggregated proteins, lipoproteins, or other contaminants based on the sample type, potentially affecting the study. The present study focused on the association of “ultracentrifugation-enriched RNA fractions” (rather than strictly EV-specific RNAs) with MetS, and did not explore the isolation as well as the characterisation of the EVs, which can be indirectly verified in subsequent experiments through functional studies (e.g., in vitro cellular uptake assays) to determine their biological activity. In addition, some samples in this study may have been on triglyceride-lowering medication or hypoglycaemic medication, which may have resulted in the insulin resistance-related index underestimating their true level of insulin resistance. Future studies should validate the results by excluding people taking medication or adjusting medication use.

Conclusion

In summary, the low expression levels of Xist, MALAT1, and NEAT1 may be significantly associated with the risk of MetS in PCOS patients. Especially, MALAT1 may serve as a preferred molecular marker for discriminating PCOS combined with MetS. MALAT1, as the molecule with the highest discriminative efficacy and importance of the three LncRNAs, may become a key target for subsequent mechanistic studies.

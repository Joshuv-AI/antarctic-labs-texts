Leveraging haze-aware features for improved image clarity and detection accuracy with an optimized DCNN-YOLOv8 network
Authors: Ashish Saini, Nasib Singh Gill, Preeti Gulia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractLow visibility, less contrast, and warped object boundaries make current detection algorithms far less effective, making it impossible to find objects in foggy environments. This work builds a novel integrated architecture that includes temporally optimized deep feature extraction, haze-aware image clarity, and an enhanced YOLOv8 detector specifically intended for foggy environments to address these limitations. Differential characters of images that are hazy are compared to other natural images which restrict generalization of present Image Quality Assessment (IQA) algorithms. Initial intention of this work is designing and developing techniques for IQA and object detection with hazy outdoor images. Here, hazy input image is first directed deal to propose Haze aware Structural Pixel Neighbor (HSPN) features, Color rendition, and Mean Subtracted Contrast Normalized (MSCN) coefficients. Also, assessing image quality is done using Deep Convolutional Neural Network (DCNN) that is trained by proposed optimization algorithm, namely Chronological Chimp optimization Algorithm (CChOA). Moreover, this developed CChOA is formed by integrating the Chronological concept with Chimp Optimization Algorithm (ChOA). Furthermore, assessed image quality is further tends to object identification using You Only Look Once stage-9 (YOLOv8), which is based on Neural Network (NN). Finally, object is identified and performance of this model is enhanced by evaluating with three performance metrics, such as Mean Seismic Data Structural Similarity (MSDSS), Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR), and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) with values of 0.944, 50.769, and 0.925, respectively.Keywords: Chimp optimization Algorithm, Chronological concept, Deep Convolutional Neural Network, Haze aware Structural Pixel Neighbor features, Signal-to-Noise RatioSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Intelligent surveillance systems (ISS) such as autonomous vehicle driving and road traffic control, where vehicle and pedestrian recognition are important objectives, are significantly affected by bad weather conditions such as haze. Haze obscures the images captured by these ISS, causing a change in the color and intensity of the scene1. Images captured outdoors are often attenuated by haze, which is an atmospheric phenomenon developed using small floating particles that scatter and absorb light from its propagation direction. Haze affects the visibility of such a scene because it develops a loss of contrast of distant objects, selective attenuation of the spectrum of light, and additional noise. Restoring images is important in many applications of outdoors, such as automated driving assistance and visual surveillance2. During the acquisition of images from optical satellites, the surface that reflects light is often scattered in the propagation, because dust, water vapor, sand, fog, snow, smoke, or some other small particles are present in the atmospheric world. This process slows down the image contrast and also blurs surface colors, causing problems in various fields, including web mapping and cartography, land use planning, environmental studies, and archaeology. Therefore, it is very important for effective haze removal methods to develop the accuracy and capability of applications using satellite images. The purpose of haze removal is to eliminate the effects of haze on at-sensor radiation data before physically based image correction is performed to convert at-sensor radiation to surface reflectance. Moreover, in cloudy areas, there is no information about the ground surface, and at the same time, in haze-affected areas, the image still contains valuable spectral information. Although the transparency of haze presents image opportunities for image restoration, a widely and efficient applicable haze removal technique to handle multiple hazes or thin clouds is still a big challenge, especially when only one hazy image exists3.

Images captured in outdoor scenes often suffer from less color shift and visibility because of presence of haze. Dehazing, which is also referred to haze removal, is highly enviable to both tasks of computer vision and computational photography. Resulting dehazed image is predictable to be much perceptually appealing generally. Also, many algorithms related to computer vision only work with scene radiance which has no haze. By multiple dehazing algorithms present, it becomes pivotal for comparing their performance of dehazing to find further advancement directions. Images of outdoor scenes captured often suffer from lesser shifts and visibility of color because of presence of haze. Even though many algorithms are proposed for removing haze, not much effort is made to assess the quality of dehazed images4. IQA plays vital role in more image processing systems and algorithms. Even though more popular IQA models achieve good performance on existing databases, they are still not accepted in applications5 practically because of not-always satisfactory accuracy on real-world situations and data6. Quality of videos and images are affected by distortions introduced on storage, encoding, acquisition, reconstruction, and transmission. To ensure image quality and meet user requirements, IQA is introduced and metrics are utilized in more image processing systems. IQA is either objective or subjective. On subjective basis assessment, people observe image directly and quality is assessed by assigning score to it. But such assessment is not applied for real-time systems of processing images. In contrast, an objective assessment involves evaluation of image quality via mathematical calculation7.

Motivation

Hazy images are formed due to absorption of light and scattering between objects and sensors. This reduces change in color and contrast of image that led to wrong identification of object features by human vision. For clear image, many dehazing techniques were developed and followed in those hazed images. However, challenges remain in dehazing due to variation in atmospheric conditions, computational efficacy, and time. Hence, there is a need to follow a better method for haze removal based on optimized DCNN for IQA. This section also enhances literature views regarding IQA methods along with its drawbacks, uses, and challenges.

This method evaluated perceptual quality in hazy images but did not generate higher precision. Guo et al.15 proposed Decorrelation stretch enhancement and had the ability for prediction of subjective ratings. Nair and Sankaran16 developed Simple Gaussian filter that was effectively utilized to dehaze large images. But this method needed more time for computation. Wang et al.18 developed No-reference quality assessment method to effectively predict quality of image after defogging. However, this method seems to be more complex for processing large number of labeled images. Cao et al.19 developed Multi-Scale Residual Network (MSRN) that attained higher accuracy rate in detecting images after dehazing but failed to handle real time images. Li et al.20 designed Deep learning transmittance network. Although, this method improved efficiency and effect by extracting various features of image but was unable to handle more numbers of images. Kim et al.21 developed a multiple scattering model with deconvolution that performed quantitatively and qualitatively while restoring images. However, this method was time-consuming process. Proposed CChOA enabled DCNN

Hazy image is caused due to inclement weather captured outdoors and this poor visibility creates wrong recognition of objects. This generates a primary drawback for video surveillance system and intelligent transportation system. Hence, to avoid this haze image due to atmospheric conditions, novel method is generated based on optimized DCNN for clear dehazed image. At first, hazy input image is acquired from Realistic Single Image Dehazing (RESIDE) dataset27 and Foggy Road Image Database (FRIDA) dataset28, which is then subjected to proposed HSPN features29, where pixel neighbor-based image is generated. Also, color Rendition29 and MSCN coefficients1 are extracted from input image. Then, extracted features are allowed for IQA using DCNN30, where a novel optimization algorithm is used to train DCNN. Here, training of DCNN is carried out by developed CChOA, which is formed by combining Chronological concept and ChOA31. Finally, the object is detected after IQA using YOLOv8 -based NN. Algorithm (CChOA) builds on the original ChOA by adding a time-sequence mechanism that keeps track of past candidate solutions and uses them in the location modification approach. This temporal guiding strikes a balance between exploration and exploitation, makes convergence more stable, and lowers the chance of DCNN training converging too soon to local optima. The temporal mechanism adds historical reliance to the update process, which makes it easier for parameters to change smoothly across iterations. This is different from the original ChOA. This makes it less likely for the optimiser to get stuck in local optima during DCNN training, makes convergence more stable, and makes it easier for the optimiser to get out of local optima.

The proposed HSPN feature is different from regular single-feature representations that just show statistics for individual pixels. It explicitly depicts how neighbouring pixels depend on each other when fog is present. HSPN ensures structural unity and edge consistency in low-visibility settings by using haze-adaptive neighbour weighting. Additionally, combining HSPN with colour reproduction features and MSCN coefficients is not just a simple feature concatenation; it is a structure-guided integration method that improves the capacity to tell the difference between features while minimising feature redundancy. This architecture allows HSPN to better collect structural information related to haze, which makes it a better way to judge the quality of hazy images.

Training process of DCNN using proposed CChOA

DCNN is utilized for IQA, which is trained by proposed CChOA, which is formed by integration of Chronological concept and ChOA31. ChOA is a novel metaheuristic algorithm inspired by individual intelligence and sexual motivation of chimps in group hunting. This form of hunting differs from social predators and ChOA is designed to solve high-dimensional problems related to learning algorithm for high-dimensional NN. Four basic steps followed in ChOA are hunting, blocking, driving, and attacking. In Chronological concept, elements are arranged or related based on time or happening. In this concept, things follow one another via timeline and present events occur from first to last. Chronological concept untangles riddles by arranging data into coherent manners for easy understanding and solving optimization issues. Here, integration of chronological concepts along ChOA helps to easily analyze temporal order to examine relationships among all events occurred in ChOA. This further created mental scaffolding for organizing optimization algorithms in an orderly manner that effortlessly trained DCNN.

Object identification from enhanced images

The last step after improving picture quality using the proposed CChOA-trained DCNN is to identify objects. A YOLOv8-based neural network then processes the improved images to find objects. YOLOv8 was chosen because of its efficient design and ability to reduce detection errors. Three specific changes are made to increase detection performance in foggy scenes: optimizing the loss function, adding an Inception arrangement and adding a Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) layer.

Improvement depending on loss function

The YOLOv8 loss algorithm works on bounding box estimation, objectness and classification all at once. But cloudy images have poor contrast and blurry edges, which makes it harder to find things accurately, particularly tiny items that are near together.

Improvement related to inception structure model

To make it easier to get multi-scale features, some of the convolutional layers in the YOLOv8 backbone have been replaced with an Inception framework. Inception is a module with many convolutional branches that can have different kernel sizes. This makes it possible to get both coarse and fine-grained information at the same time.

This structure makes it feasible to use information that is hidden and subtle in foggy photographs by making the network wider and deeper while also reducing the number of repeated parameters. The multi-scale feature representation makes it easier to find objects with fluctuating sizes and blurry edges, which are common in foggy environments.

Improvement based on SPP structure model

After the backbone of YOLOv8 comes the addition of a Spatial Pyramid Pooling (SPP) layer, which is meant to increase the overall understanding of context. The SPP module may gather spatial data without making the computation system more complicated by using pooling processes at different sizes.

The SPP layer makes it easier to tell the difference between objects and cloudy backgrounds, which helps improve detection accuracy in low-visibility situations. This is done by collecting information about the local and global circumstances.

Discussion based on results

Setup of experiment

Proposed model is experimented with MATLAB tool with PC of 2 GB RAMS, along with Intel I3 core processor, and windows 10 OS. In every experiment, both local and external computational resources were used. The initial preprocessing and lightweight training operations were performed on a local system featuring an Intel i3 processor and 2 GB RAM. In contrast, the computationally intensive training and fine-tuning of the DCNN and YOLOv8 models were conducted with additional hardware resources acquired from an external source. To ensure the application’s feasibility, transfer learning was employed by initializing the models with pretrained weights and subsequently fine-tuning them with lightweight configurations. For measure performance, two different dataset splits were used: 70:30 and 80:20 for training & testing and they were trained with different epoch settings, from 50 to 100, with 10 increments (50, 60, 70, 80, 90, and 100 epochs). The DCNN-based IQA model usually converged more quickly at smaller epoch values, while the YOLOv8 detection model needed more fine-tuning over a longer period of time. All tests used a batch size of 8 and a starting learning rate of 0.001. The last stated findings are based on the epoch setting that gave the best validation performance.

Discussion on embedded deployment and hardware integration

For future use in embedded and edge devices, memristor-based neural network circuits5,36 can combine the improved YOLOv8 design with the proposed CChOA-optimized DCNN. Peripheral CMOS circuits can be used for activation and pooling operations, but memristor crossbar arrays can be used for the convolutional and feature extraction layers to provide low-power in-memory processing. The Inception structure and SPP layer make the recommended model less complicated, which makes it perfect for embedded platforms with limited resources. Recent implementations of memristors for edge and Internet of Things applications have successfully demonstrated analogous hardware-algorithm co-design approaches, indicating that future hardware deployment of the proposed technology is viable. The CChOA description has been made easier to understand by cutting out too much arithmetic and focussing on its practical understanding, especially the time-sequence system for balancing both exploration and extraction. A comparison analysis with conventional optimisers, such as Adam, PSO, SGD, GA, RMSProp and ChOA, has been incorporated, illustrating that CChOA attains expedited convergence, reduced training loss, and enhanced stability.

Conclusion

Assessing the image quality of foggy natural images remains challenging due to the multitude of complex environmental factors and diverse uses. This paper proposes an integrated framework that amalgamates feature extraction utilising HSPN, colour rendition, and MSCN coefficients with a deep convolutional neural network (DCNN) trained by the proposed Chronological Chimp Optimisation Algorithm (CChOA). Subsequently, the assessed images are fed into a YOLOv8-based neural network to execute object detection.. Finally, object is identified and performance of this model is done by three evaluating metrics, like MSDSS with value of 0.944, SNR with value of 50.769, and SSIM with value of 0.925, respectively. In the future, this method can be further extended with some other optimization-based deep learning algorithm and can be evaluated for object detection with other better network-based methods.

Limitation and future scope

The reported experimental results are obtained under control and consistent training setting to ensure a fair comparison among different optimization strategies. While the exact numerical values may show minor sensitivity to initialization and experimental configurations, the relative performance trends observed in this study remain stable. The proposed methodology is evaluated by making use of the RESIDE and FRIDA datasets, which are available to the public. These datasets primarily exhibit controlled fog and haze circumstances, and it is possible that they do not accurately reflect the unpredictability of real-world meteorological scenarios. When confronted with unique or complex environmental conditions, the incorporation of handcrafted haze-aware characteristics may limit the method’s potential for generalisation, even though these features are beneficial in modelling the degradation of structures in fog. Finally, due to the limitations of the computers, the current study was unable to include complete experimental comparisons with all the contemporary large-scale end-to-end dehazing and object detection systems37,38.

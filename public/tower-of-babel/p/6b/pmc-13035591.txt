Circ-find-0001774 Modulates Parkinson’s Disease via miR-153-3p: Mechanistic Insights and Therapeutic Implications
Authors: Ronglan Zhu, Jie Chen, Tao Song, Yun Yang, Chaoyang Zhou, Zhiping Xie, Min Yuan, Jianzhong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to investigate how circ-find-0001774 regulates miR-153-3p in Parkinson’s disease (PD).MethodsWe first validated the targeting relationship between circ-find-0001774 and miR-153-3p using dual-luciferase reporter assays, excluding any association with let-7a-5p. Next, we constructed a circ-find-0001774 overexpression vector and transfected it into MN9D dopaminergic neurons, confirming transfection efficiency by qPCR. In vitro, we induced a PD cell model with 100 µmol/L MPP+ iodide for 24 h and assessed cell proliferation using CCK-8, apoptosis via flow cytometry, and miR-153-3p and LC3Ⅱ/Ⅰ protein levels by qPCR and Western blotting. In vivo, we established a mouse PD model by daily intraperitoneal injection of 18 mg/kg MPTP for seven days, evaluating motor function through behavioral tests, observing brain pathology via HE staining, and analyzing miR-153-3p and β-catenin protein levels by qPCR and Western blotting.ResultsDual-luciferase assays confirmed a specific targeting interaction between miR-153-3p and circ-find-0001774. In vitro, circ-find-0001774 overexpression significantly enhanced cell proliferation, reduced apoptosis and decreased miR-153-3p and LC3Ⅱ/Ⅰ protein expression levels. In vivo, circ-find-0001774 overexpression notably ameliorated motor deficits in the mouse PD model mitigated neurodegeneration, decreased miR-153-3p level and increased β-catenin protein expression levels.ConclusionOur study reveals that circ-find-0001774 modulates miR-153-3p, mainly through the Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway, suggesting its therapeutic potential for PD. These findings provide novel insights and experimental foundations for further developing PD treatment strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12031-026-02509-w.Keywords: circ-find-0001774, miR-153-3p, Parkinson's disease, Neuroprotection, Wnt/β-Catenin signaling, Dopaminergic neurons

Introduction

Parkinson’s disease (PD) is a neurodegenerative disease characterized by progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons, causing motor dysfunction, postural instability, and other cognitive and emotional disorders (Miyazaki and Asanuma 2023). Although remarkable progress has been made in its pathophysiological mechanism and treatment over the years, the current treatment still faces challenges, especially in terms of the complexity and progression of the disease (Kolesova et al. 2023). Therefore, finding new therapeutic targets and strategies has become the top priority of the current PD research.

In recent years, non-coding RNA (ncRNA), mainly circular RNA (circRNA), has attracted much attention because of its unique role in the regulation of gene expression (Kulcheski et al. 2016; Misir et al. 2022). CircRNA, with its ring-shaped structure, can be used as a miRNA sponge to inhibit the activity of miRNA, thus affecting the expression of target genes regulated by it. This regulation is essential not only in normal cell function but also in the occurrence and development of disease (Tang and Hu 2024). Nevertheless, the exact function and mechanism of circRNA in nervous system diseases still need to be further studied and clarified.

As another essential regulatory factor, miRNAs regulate gene expression by targeting the 3 ‘untranslated region of mRNA (Jiao et al. 2022). As a member of the miRNA family, miR-153-3p is widely expressed in the nervous system and has been proven to play a critical role in the pathogenesis of PD (Vivacqua et al. 2016). MiR-153-3p regulates the expression of its target genes, such as α-synuclein (Deng et al. 2022), and participates in the regulation of neuronal function and survival, thus affecting the pathological progress of PD (Hayashita-Kinoh et al. 2006).

Although there is a preliminary understanding of the importance of circRNA and miRNA in the pathophysiology of PD (Selvakumar et al. 2022; Bougea and Stefanis 2023), the complex interaction between circRNA and miRNA and the specific regulatory mechanism in PD are not clear. Therefore, this study aims to explore a kind of circRNA called circ-find-0001774, which regulates the role of miR-153-3p in the occurrence and development of PD. We combined cell experiments and animal model studies to explore the interaction between circ-find-0001774 and miR-153-3p and reveal its potential molecular mechanism in the pathophysiological process of PD. Through these efforts, we hope to provide theoretical support and an experimental basis for developing new PD treatment strategies, further promote the progress of PD treatment research, and contribute to improving patients’ quality of life.

Materials and Methods

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay

Wild-type and mutant dual-luciferase reporter constructs of circ-find-0001774 were co-transfected with miR-153-3p mimic, let-7a-5p mimic, and mimic NC into 293T cells. After 48 h, cells were harvested and lysed. Luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Beyotime, RG027) to detect fireflies and Renilla luciferase activities (Yu et al. 2020).

Cell Culture and Treatment

MN9D cells were obtained from Bolson (BES23898MN) and 293T cells from Procell (CL-0005). Cells were cultured in DMEM complete medium (KGM12800S, KeyGen Biotec) containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (10565018, Gibco) at 37 °C with 5% CO2. To establish the Parkinson’s disease cell model, MN9D cells were incubated with 100 µmol/L MPP+ iodide (HY-W008719, MCE) for 24 h (Lang et al. 2020).

CCK-8 Cell Proliferation Assay

Following treatment for 24 h, 100 µL of fresh medium and 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent were added to each well. After 2 h of incubation, absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (WD-2012B, Beijing Liuyi).

Flow Cytometry for Apoptosis Detection

Cells (1 × 10^6) were collected and washed twice with PBS by centrifugation at 1500 rpm for 3 min. The cells were resuspended in 300 µL of pre-cooled 1× Binding Buffer and stained with 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC and 10 µL of PI (MULTI SCIENCES, AP101-100-kit). After gentle mixing, the cells were incubated in the dark at room temperature for 10 min and then analyzed using a flow cytometer (NovoCyte 2060R, Acea Biosciences).

Animal Handling and Treatment

Male C57BL/6 mice (9–10 weeks old) were obtained from SPF (Beijing) Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Permit No. SCXK (Jing) 2019-0010). Mice were housed under controlled conditions (temperature 20–26 °C, humidity 40–70%). After acclimatization, mice were randomly divided into four groups: Control, Model, Model + OE NC (circ-0001774 overexpression-negative control adenovirus), and Model + OE circ-0001774 (circ-0001774 overexpression adenovirus). Mice were anesthetized, and the skull was exposed. Using a stereotaxic apparatus, 4 µL of adenovirus was injected into the SNc area of the brain, followed by a 5-minute needle retention. After wound closure and disinfection, mice were placed on a warming pad until recovery. Except for the Control group, all groups received intraperitoneal injections of 18 mg/kg MPTP (S31504, Shanghai YuanYe) once daily for seven consecutive days to induce the Parkinson’s disease model.

Behavioral Tests

Behavioral tests were conducted on the third day after the last MPTP injection:

Pole Test: A 50 cm pole (1 cm diameter) with a 1.5 cm ball at the top, wrapped with gauze to increase friction, was used. Mice were placed head-down on the ball, and the time to turn entirely downward and reach the base of the pole were recorded. Each mouse underwent three trials, and the average time was calculated.

Hanging Test: Mice were allowed to hang by their forepaws from a metal wire 30 cm above the ground, and the time until they fell was recorded.

HE Staining

Brain tissue sections were deparaffinized, hydrated, and stained with hematoxylin (ZLI-9610, Zhongshan Golden Bridge) for 3–5 min. After rinsing, sections were differentiated in 1% hydrochloric acid alcohol, blued, and counterstained with eosin (G1100, Solarbio) for 3–5 min. The sections were then dehydrated, mounted, and observed under a microscope (Olympus BX43).

Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA from cells and brain tissues was extracted using Trizon reagent (CW0580S, CWBIO). mRNA was purified using an RNA extraction kit (CW0581M, CWBIO) and quantified using a spectrophotometer (NanoPhotometer, NP80). cDNA was synthesized using the HiScript II Reverse Transcriptase (Vazyme, R223-01). qRT-PCR was performed on a CFX Connect™ Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad) with the following cycling conditions: 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 10 s, 58 °C for 30 s, and 72 °C for 30 s. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2^−△△Ct method with β-actin and U6 as internal controls. Primer sequences were as follows:

Western Blot Analysis

Total protein from cells and brain tissues was extracted using RIPA lysis buffer and centrifugation at 12,000 rpm for 10 min at 4 °C. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA protein assay kit (Elabscience, E-BC-K318-M). Proteins were denatured and separated by SDS-PAGE, then transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with non-fat milk and incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. The next day, membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence reagent (Thermo Fisher, RJ239676) and imaged with a chemiluminescence imaging system (Tanon-5200, Shanghai Tanon). Statistical Analysis

GraphpadPrism9.0 software was used for graphic drawing and statistical analysis. All the experiments were repeated three times, and the quantitative results were expressed by mean standard deviation (X ± S). Single-factor analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to compare the quantitative values among groups, and the Student Newman Keuls (S-N-K) method was used for pairwise comparison.

Results

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay to Validate the Targeting Relationship of circ-find-0001774 with mmu-miR-153-3p and mmu-let-7a-5p

The dual-luciferase reporter assay confirmed the targeting relationship between circ-find-0001774 and mmu-miR-153-3p. As shown in Fig. 1A, the fluorescence intensity significantly decreased in the WT circ-find-0001774 + miR-153-3p mimic group compared to the WT circ-find-0001774 and WT circ-find-0001774 + miR-153-3p NC groups, indicating a direct interaction between circ-find-0001774 and miR-153-3p. Conversely, no significant change in fluorescence intensity was observed in the WT circ-find-0001774 + mmu-let-7a-5p mimic group compared to the WT circ-find-0001774 and WT circ-find-0001774 + mmu-let-7a-5p NC groups, suggesting that circ-find-0001774 does not target let-7a-5p (Fig. 1B). Therefore, subsequent experiments focused on the miR-153-3p gene.

Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay to Validate the Targeting Relationship of circ-find-0001774 with mmu-miR-153-3p and mmu-let-7a-5p

qPCR Validation of circ-find-0001774 Overexpression Vector Transfection Efficiency

The transfection efficiency of the circ-find-0001774 overexpression vector into MN9D cells was assessed using qPCR. As depicted in Fig. 2, the expression level of circ-find-0001774 was significantly elevated in the OE circ-0001774 group compared to the Control and OE NC groups, confirming the successful transfection of the circ-find-0001774 overexpression plasmid into MN9D cells.

Overexpression Efficiency of circ-0001774 in MN9D Cells Validated by qPCR

Effects of circ-0001774 on MN9D Cell Apoptosis and Proliferation

CCK-8 and flow cytometry assays were used to evaluate cell proliferation and apoptosis. Compared to the Control group, the Model group exhibited significantly increased apoptosis and decreased cell proliferation. Overexpression of circ-0001774 resulted in a marked reduction in apoptosis and an enhancement in cell proliferation (Fig. 3).

Effects of circ-0001774 Overexpression on Apoptosis and Proliferation in MN9D Cells. (A) Apoptosis detected by flow cytometry. (B) Cell proliferation assessed by CCK-8 assay

Impact of circ-0001774 on miR-153-3p Expression in MN9D Cells

qPCR analysis was performed to measure the expression levels of circ-0001774 and miR-153-3p in MN9D cells. The results showed significant change in miR-153-3p expression in the Model group compared to the Control group (Fig. 4B), while circ-0001774 expression was significantly upregulated in the OE circ-0001774 group (Fig. 4A). Overexpression of circ-0001774 significantly increased circ-0001774 expression and decreased miR-153-3p expression compared to the Model and Model + OE NC groups.

Expression Levels of circ-0001774 and miR-153-3p in MN9D Cells Detected by qPCR

Effect of circ-0001774 on LC3Ⅱ/Ⅰ Protein Expression in MN9D Cells

Western blot analysis revealed that LC3Ⅱ/Ⅰ expression was significantly elevated in the Model group compared to the Control group. Overexpression of circ-0001774 significantly decreased LC3Ⅱ/Ⅰ expression (Fig. 5).

LC3-II/LC3-I Protein Expression in MN9D Cells Detected by Western Blot

qPCR Validation of circ-0001774 Overexpression in the Substantia Nigra of Mice

To evaluate the effectiveness of circ-0001774 overexpression in vivo, qPCR was conducted on the substantia nigra of mice injected with circ-0001774 overexpression adenovirus. The results indicated a significant increase in circ-0001774 expression in the OE circ-0001774 group compared to the Control and OE NC groups, confirming effective adenovirus injection (Fig. 6).

circ-0001774 Expression in Mouse Substantia Nigra Following Adenovirus-Mediated Overexpression, Assessed by qPCR

Behavioral Impact of circ-0001774 in PD Model Mice

Behavioral tests, including pole and hanging tests, were conducted to assess the impact of circ-0001774 overexpression on motor function in PD model mice. Compared to the Control group, the Model and Model + NC groups showed significantly increased time to turn the head down and reach the bottom of the pole, along with decreased hanging time. Overexpression of circ-0001774 significantly improved these motor functions in the Model + OE circ-0001774 group compared to the Model and Model + OE NC groups (Fig. 7).

Behavioral Outcomes in Mice Following circ-0001774 Overexpression. (A) Pole test and suspension time. (B) Pole test parameters. (C) Suspension drop time

Effect of circ-0001774 on Brain Tissue Structure in PD Model Mice

HE staining of brain tissues revealed that neurons in the substantia nigra of the Control group were orderly arranged with clear structures. In contrast, the Model group showed significant neuronal loss, increased cell volume, and disordered structures. Overexpression of circ-0001774 in the Model + OE circ-0001774 group partially restored the neuronal arrangement and morphology (Fig. 8).

Histological Analysis of the Substantia Nigra by Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE) Staining

Impact of circ-0001774 on miR-153-3p Expression in PD Model Mice

qPCR analysis of midbrain tissues demonstrated a increase in miR-153-3p expression in the Model group compared to the Control group. Overexpression of circ-0001774 significantly reduced miR-153-3p expression (Fig. 9).

miR-153-3p Expression in Mouse Midbrain Tissue Detected by qPCR

Impact of circ-0001774 on β-catenin Protein Expression in PD Model Mice

Western blot analysis revealed that the expression of β-catenin was decreased in the model group, whereas it was increased in both the control group and the overexpression group. Overexpression of circ-0001774 significantly increased β-catenin protein expression (Fig. 10).

Discussion

Our study demonstrates the pivotal role of circ-find-0001774 in regulating miR-153-3p and its subsequent effects on MN9D cells and a Parkinson’s disease (PD) mouse model. The dual-luciferase reporter assay confirmed that circ-find-0001774 directly targets miR-153-3p but not mmu-let-7a-5p. Overexpression of circ-find-0001774 in MN9D cells significantly increased circ-find-0001774 levels and decreased miR-153-3p expression. This overexpression led to reduced apoptosis and enhanced proliferation of MN9D cells. In the PD mouse model, overexpression of circ-find-0001774 improved motor functions, as shown by better performance in pole and hanging tests. Histological analysis revealed that circ-find-0001774 overexpression partially restored the structural integrity of neurons in the substantia nigra. Additionally, increased circ-find-0001774 expression decreased miR-153-3p and β-catenin levels in the midbrain tissues. These results suggest that circ-find-0001774 protects PD by modulating the miR-153-3p/β-catenin pathway, offering potential therapeutic insights for PD treatment.

One of the core findings of this study is the interaction between circ-find-0001774 and miR-153-3p and the effect of this interaction on cell function. The double luciferase report experiment results showed that circ-find-0001774 could regulate its activity by directly binding to miR-153-3p. MiR-153-3p is a microRNA that plays a critical regulatory role in various cell processes, including apoptosis, proliferation, and differentiation (Cohen et al. 2018; Chen et al. 2022). In the PD model, the up-regulation of miR-153-3p is closely related to increased neuronal apoptosis and decreased cell proliferation (Cheng et al. 2018; Sun et al. 2022). In this study, overexpression of circ-find-0001774 can significantly reduce the expression level of miR-153-3p, thus reducing its inhibition on target genes. One of the target genes of miR-153-3p is β-catenin, an essential component of the Wnt/ β-catenin signal pathway (Li et al. 2015). The Wnt/ β-catenin signaling pathway regulates cell proliferation and survival (Ye et al. 2020). In MN9D cells, overexpression of circ-find-0001774 reduces the negative regulation of β-catenin by inhibiting miR-153-3p, increasing the expression of β-catenin, which promotes cell proliferation and reduces apoptosis (Zhang et al. 2021; Yang et al. 2023). This mechanism has also been verified in PD model mice. The results of behavioral tests showed that overexpression of circ-find-0001774 significantly improved the motor function of PD model mice, which was consistent with the results of cell experiments. Histological analysis further proved that overexpression of circ-find-0001774 could improve the structure of brain tissue and reduce neuronal damage. These findings suggest that circ-find-0001774 plays a neuroprotective role by regulating miR-153-3p and β-catenin signaling pathways and is expected to become a new target for PD therapy.

Compared with previous studies, this study proposes a new regulation mechanism, which shows the protective role of circ-find-0001774 in PD. In recent years, many studies have proved that circRNAs play an important role in neurodegenerative diseases (Floris et al. 2017; Li et al. 2021; Su et al. 2022; Valizadeh et al. 2023). For example, Su et al. found that circRNAs and miRNAs were identified to participate in the progression of Alzheimer’s disease, which might be possible biomarkers and therapeutic targets for Alzheimer’s disease (Su et al. 2022), while this study reveals for the first time the mechanism of circ-find-0001774 in PD by targeting miR-153-3p. Valizadeh et al. (Valizadeh et al. 2023) found that Dysregulating ncRNAs have inhibited or stimulated apoptosis in neurodegenerative disorders. In addition, different from the circDLGAP4 reported by Chen et al. by regulating the protective role of miR-143 in ischemia/reperfusion injury in endothelial cells (Chen et al. 2020), this study emphasizes the specific mechanism of circRNAs in PD.

To sum up, this study systematically revealed the role and mechanism of circ-find-0001774 in PD, proposed a new regulatory axis (circ-find-0001774/miR-153-3p/ β-catenin), and verified its neuroprotective effect by cell experiments and animal models. This discovery enriches our understanding of circRNAs in neurodegenerative diseases and provides a potential molecular target for developing new PD treatment strategies in the future. The comparison with previous studies further emphasizes this study’s unique contribution and innovation and provides a new perspective for the application of circRNAs in neuroscience research.

The potential of circ-find-0001774 as a therapeutic target for PD has been fully confirmed in our experiments. Circ-find-0001774 is neuroprotective by regulating miR-153-3p and its downstream β-catenin signal pathway. This mechanism provides a new molecular target for PD therapy and may be more effective than traditional methods. Traditional PD therapy mainly focuses on the protection and replacement therapy of dopaminergic neurons (White and Thomas 2012). At the same time, the discovery and functional study of circRNAs provides a new molecular target for treating PD. The regulation of circ-find-0001774 expression can be achieved by gene editing, RNA interference, and small molecular drugs (Blazquez et al. 2012). The continuous progress of these methods provides a technical guarantee for its application.

Furthermore, by combining the regulation of circ-find-0001774 with existing treatment methods to form a combined treatment strategy, it can enhance the therapeutic effect and reduce side effects (Yao et al. 2009). In addition, circ-find-0001774 has high stability in vivo and is not easily degraded, giving it apparent advantages in clinical application (Zheng et al. 2020). With the progress of RNA drug development technology, the drug design and delivery system for circRNAs is also improving (Praestegaard et al. 2022), which provides a good prospect for the clinical application of circ-find-0001774. Future research should further explore its regulatory mechanism and effect to provide more evidence and technical support for its clinical application.

Our research still has some limitations. First, our research mainly focuses on cell and mouse models, lacking verification in human patients. Although these models provide valuable preliminary data, they cannot fully represent the complexity of human diseases. Therefore, there are still uncertainties in the clinical transformation of the research results. Secondly, our study revealed that circ-find-0001774 affected cell function by regulating miR-153-3p and its downstream β-catenin signaling pathway, but the specific molecular mechanism still needs to be further refined. We have not fully understood other potential regulatory factors in these molecular pathways and whether circ-find-0001774 plays a role in other ways. Thirdly, although we have observed the therapeutic effects of circ-find-0001774 in the mouse model, the long-term stability and safety of these effects have not been thoroughly evaluated. Long-term regulation of circ-find-0001774 may bring unpredictable side effects, and its safety needs further study. In addition, the sample size of this study is relatively small, and the statistical significance and universality of the data need to be verified in a larger-scale study. A small sample size may lead to the contingency of the results, thus affecting the reliability of the conclusions. Finally, although our research results show that circ-find-0001774 has therapeutic potential, its specific application mode, dose regulation, treatment window period, and other clinical implementation details have not been determined. The solutions to these problems need to be systematically explored in future research.

Conclusions

Our study reveals that circ-find-0001774 modulates miR-153-3p, mainly through the Wnt/β-Catenin signaling pathway, suggesting its therapeutic potential for PD. These findings provide novel insights and experimental foundations for further developing PD treatment strategies.

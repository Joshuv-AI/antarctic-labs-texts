Genetically predicted immune cell mediate the association between plasma lipidomics and osteomyelitis
Authors: Xujin Wu, Xiao Zhang, Wei Xu, Chang She, Liubin Li, Yongtao Mao, Shendong Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteomyelitis is a disease with a complex etiology and a high recurrence rate, which causes significant distress to both patients and physicians. There is currently no clear understanding of the factors that influence the progression of osteomyelitis. Previously, only small-sample observational studies have demonstrated an association between osteomyelitis, plasma lipidomics and immune cell characteristics. The objective of this study is to analyze the causal relationship between lipidomics and osteomyelitis by Mendelian randomization and to determine the mediating role of immune cells in this process.MethodsThis study validated the causal relationship between osteomyelitis and 179 lipid species by two-sample Mendelian randomization analysis using publicly available genetic data. To ensure the reliability of the findings, this study employed both sensitivity analysis and inverse Mendelian randomization analysis. Furthermore, this study used two-step Mendelian to quantify the proportion of immune cell characteristics mediated lipidomics in osteomyelitis.ResultAfter rigorous screening of instrumental variables, and multiple Mendelian randomization methods, this study identified a causal relationship between 5 lipid species and 20 immune cell traits and osteomyelitis. Through a comprehensive analysis of causality, this study identified an increased risk of osteomyelitis with increased Diacylglycerol (d40:2) levels (inverse variance-weighted method, [OR] per SD increase in osteomyelitis = 1.147 [95% CI 1.46–1.276], P = 0.004) and 10.665% was mediated through CD27 on IgD-CD38dim. However, It has been demonstrated that an increase in Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels (inverse variance-weighted method, [OR] per SD increase in osteomyelitis = 0.887 [95% CI 0.800–0.983], P = 0.023) reduces the risk of osteomyelitis and 6.640% was mediated through CD20 on IgD+ .ConclusionThis study reveals that increased Ceramide (d40:2) levels increase the risk of developing osteomyelitis, with CD27 on IgD-CD38dim mediating part of the effect. At the same time, Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels were increased and would reduce the risk of developing osteomyelitis, partly mediated through CD20 on IgD+ . Introduction

Osteomyelitis is mainly caused by infection of bone tissue by pathogenic microorganisms and remains a major problem for orthopedic surgeons due to its complex pathogenesis and high cost of treatment [1–3]. Despite the widespread popularity of the concept of aseptic surgery, the incidence of osteomyelitis continues to show a gradual increase [4]. Osteomyelitis is typically categorized as acute or chronic [5, 6]. Approximately 10–30% of acute osteomyelitis cases may progress to chronic osteomyelitis [7]. The most prevalent pathogens associated with osteomyelitis include Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus haemolyticus, Pneumococcus, Escherichia coli and Pseudomonas aeruginosa [8, 9]. Furthermore, a deficiency in the local blood supply to bone and soft tissues in osteomyelitis results in ischemic sclerosis of the diseased bone, which impedes the [10]. A penetration of systemic antibiotics to reach effective bactericidal concentrations locally [11]. The degradation products produced by the pathogen also degrade important components of bone tissue, resulting in serious complications such as delayed fracture healing and bone destruction [7]. Inflammatory mediators and electrolyte imbalances can further contribute to the progression of osteomyelitis. As the infection progresses, osteomyelitis worsens, leading to persistent necrosis of bones and tissues, eventually escalating to systemic inflammatory findings and serious complications such as multi-organ dysfunction [12, 13]. The risk factors influencing the progression of osteomyelitis are currently inconclusive. Therefore, osteomyelitis is characterized by difficult treatment, long duration and high recurrence rate, making it one of the most challenging diseases in orthopedics.

Lipids represent a significant component that is prevent in all cells. With the intensive study of lipids, it has been found that they are also key components of signaling and regulation in the human body. Furthermore, lipids undergo a multitude of diverse biochemical transformations during synthesis, resulting in a more intricate structural composition than that of linear proteins [14]. The development of modern and efficient lipidomics techniques has enabled a broader and deeper understanding of the diversity of circulating lipids. A number of studies have identified a plethora of lipid species, including glycerophospholipids, glycerides, sphingolipids, sterols, isoprenoid lipids, glycolipids and polyketides, among others [15]. Each lipid contains different classes and subclasses of biomolecules and therefore fulfils different functions [16]. In addition, genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have significantly changed researchers' understanding of the genetic variants that affect lipid levels [17, 18]. In recent years, many researchers have found a strong link between lipidomics and obesity and gastrointestinal diseases [19, 20]. However, few studies have explored the relationship between lipids as bioconjugates and Bacterial infections and osteomyelitis.

In addition, immune cells and bone cells (osteoclasts, osteoblasts and chondrocytes) can interact directly or indirectly via cytokines and chemokines. A body of evidence indicates that immune cells play a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of both acute and chronic osteomyelitis. Among the various types of macrophages, those that are immunomodulatory (M2) are primarily found in areas surrounding osteoblasts that have been infected by bacteria [7]. Furthermore, osteomyelitis has been demonstrated to promote T-cell polarization towards helper T-cells (TH1 and TH17), which has been shown to positively regulate osteoclasts and to reduce bone loss [21].

Mendelian randomization (MR) is a potential causal inference method that uses genetic variation as an instrumental variable (IV) to estimate the causal relationship between exposure and outcome by inferring the effect of exposure factors on the outcome from observational data [22]. In contrast to traditional observational analyses, because of the random assignment and fixation of genetic variants, MR analyses can mitigate the interference of confounding factors or experimental bias and can provide further validation of reverse causality [23]. This study pooled GWAS data on 179 plasma lipidomics, 731 immune cells, and osteomyelitis were collected from the most recent publications in the GWAS database. This is the first time that a causal relationship between lipidomics and osteomyelitis has been identified, and the mediating role of immune cells in it has been further explored, providing new ideas and directions for the diagnosis and treatment of osteomyelitis. And this study minimized bias in the research process, thereby enhancing the credibility of the conclusions.

Methods

Study design

This study aimed to examine the bidirectional causal relationship between plasma lipid species and osteomyelitis, as well as to investigate the potential mediating role of immune cells in this relationship. As illustrated in Fig. 1, this study was divided into three principal stages: the analysis of the causal role between 179 lipid species and osteomyelitis; the analysis of the causal role between 731 immune cells and osteomyelitis; and the mediation of the role of immune cells from plasma lipidomics to osteomyelitis pathway species. In this study, single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) was defined as an instrumental variable (IV) [24]. This study also adheres to the three fundamental principles of Mendelian randomization: (1) the IVs are closely associated with the exposure factors; (2) IVs are not associated with confounding factors; (3) IVs do not affect the outcome directly, and it can only affect outcome via the exposure [25].

GWAS summary data sources

The data employed in this study were obtained from publicly available databases, details of which are provided in Table 1. The data for plasma lipidomics, the exposure indicator for the current study, were derived from a study involving 7174 Finns that included univariate and multivariate genome-wide analyses covering 179 lipids. The mediating variables in this study were 731 immune cell traits, including Absolute Cell counts (AC, n = 118), Median Fluorescence Intensity reflecting the level of surface antigen (MFI, n = 389), Morphological Parameters (MP, n = 32), and Relative Cell counts (RC, n = 192). Specifically, the MFI, AC and RC features comprise B cells, conventional dendritic cells (CDCs), mature T cells, monocytes, myeloid cells, TBANK (T cells, B cells, natural killer cells) and regulatory T cells (Treg cells), and the MR feature comprises CDCs and TBNK cells [26]. GWAS data from 3757 European populations were calculated using Sardinia-based sequences to identify SNPs for approximately 22 million high-density array genotypes, adjusted for covariates (sex, age, age2). The outcome variable for this study was osteomyelitis. The comprehensive genomic osteomyelitis genetic data released in 2021 was selected for this study, taking into account a variety of factors, including sample size, sequencing depth, ethnicity, and time to data update.

Instrumental variable selection and data harmonization

The validity of MR causality estimation is contingent on the validity of the selected instrumental variables. In this study, instrumental variables(IVs) were therefore screened according to the following criteria: (1): SNPs with genome-wide significance (P < 1^10–5) were selected as instrumental variables, and when there were only a small number of genome-wide significant SNPs according to P < 5^10–8, this relatively lenient threshold of the genetic instruments was used in the MR study in order to maximize the advantages of the genetic instruments and the amount of phenotype explained by the genetic instruments amount of variation [27–31]. (2) To ensure the independence of SNPs as genetic tools, we performed linkage disequilibrium (LD) clustering using PLINK software. A stringent cut-off value of r2 < 0.001 and a window size of 10,000 kb were utilized for the clustering process, with genotype data from the 1000 Genomes Project Europeans serving as the reference group. In instances where LD was exhibited by SNPs, only those with the lowest P values were retained. (3) Echoes and ambiguous instrumental variables, and weak instrumental variables with F-statistics < 10, were removed to ensure the stability and reliability of the results. The F statistic was calculated by evaluating the variance explained by the SNPs for each exposure. This was done by dividing the variance explained by the SNPs by the variance explained by the remaining genetic variants, which was calculated as follows: (N − K − 1)/K/ [R2/ (1 − R2)], where K represents the number of genetic variants and N denotes the sample size. In order to ensure the robustness and reliability of the results, weak instrumental variables (F statistics < 10) were excluded from the analysis [32, 33].

Mendelian randomization

This study employed a range of analytical techniques, including inverse variance weighting (IVW), MR-Egger, weighted median, weighted model, simple model, and Bayesian weighted MR (BWMR) to investigate whether there is a causal relationship between these 179 lipidomics and osteomyelitis. In order to obtain an overall estimate of osteomyelitis by lipidomics, the IVW method used a Meta-analysis approach for each SNP combined with Wald estimation. In the absence of horizontal pleiotropy, the results of the IVW method will remain unbiased [34]. The MR-Egger method relied on the assumption of instrument strength independent of direct effects (inSIDE), which assesses polytropy by taking into account the intercept term [35]. When the intercept term was zero, indicating the absence of horizontal pleiotropy, the results of the MR-Egger regression were consistent with IVW. The weighted median method provided an accurate assessment of causality by assuming that at least 50% of IVs were valid [36]. When the direct effects assumption is not supported, the weighted model estimates show a significantly greater ability to identify causal effects, reduce bias, and reduce the incidence of Type I errors compared to MR-egger regressions. Although not as powerful as IVW, the simple model offers robustness to pleiotropy. In order to evaluate the causal relationship between lipidomics and osteomyelitis, this study performed a reverse MR analysis of the lipidomics identified as causally related to osteomyelitis in the forward MR analysis [36]. The techniques and configurations employed were consistent with those used in the forward MR analysis [37].

Mediation analysis

The current study explored the potential role of immune cells in mediating the causal relationship between lipidomics and osteomyelitis. The study employed a two-step Mendelian randomization for mediation analysis (illustrated in Fig. 1). In this instance, the total effect can be decomposed into direct and indirect components. Finally, the percentage mediated by the mediating effect was calculated by indirect effect/total effect.

Sensitivity analysis

The Cochran's IVW statistic was used to assess the multiplicity of IVs [38]. The MREgger was used to test for horizontal multiplicity, and if the MREgger intercept was found to be statistically significant, it indicated that the results of the association may have been influenced by other horizontal multiplicities [39]. The global test MR-PRESSO (MR pleiotropy residual sum and outlier) was employed to exclude horizontal pleiotropy and determine the presence of outliers [40]. In addition, potentially heterogeneous SNPs were distinguished by omitting each instrumental SNP in turn through a ‘leave-one-out’ test. The scatterplot indicated that the results were not affected by anomalies. The leave-one-out plot demonstrated the robustness of this association.

Statistical analysis

The results of this study mainly include the ratio of ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) and P-values, when P < 0.05 indicated a statistical difference. Analyses were performed using R 4.3.1 (https:// www. Rproject. org), which includes: ‘TwoSampleMR’ and ‘MendelianRandomisation’ packages [32]. Furthermore, the online tool PhenoScanner was employed to assess all known phenotypes associated with the genetic instruments in the analyses (http://www.phenoscanner.medschl.cam.ac.uk/).

Result

Association of 179 lipid species and osteomyelitis

In this study, a potential causal relationship between 5 lipid species and osteomyelitis was identified using IVW analysis (Supplementary Table 1). By combining and comparing multiple methods of analysis and exclusion level pleiotropy, a causal relationship between 2 lipids and osteomyelitis was ultimately selected for this study. Ceramide (d40:2) levels were positively associated with osteomyelitis in terms of genetically predicted causality: IVW odds ratio [OR] per SD increase in osteomyelitis = 1.147 [95% CI 1.46–1.276], P = 0.004 (Fig. 2). Another negatively associated genetic predictive causality with osteomyelitis was found for Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels: IVW odds ratio [OR] per SD increase in osteomyelitis = 0.887 [95% CI 0.800–0.983], P = 0.023 (Fig. 3).

By reverse MR analysis, no reverse causality was found between osteomyelitis and Ceramide (d40:2) levels (IVW odds ratio [OR] = 1.012 [95% CI 0.916–1.119], P = 0.814) and Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels (IVW odds ratio [OR] = 0.986 [95% CI 0.899–1.081], P = 0.760) in the present study (Figs. 2, 3).

Association of 731 immune cell traits and osteomyelitis

In order to analyze the data more fully and effectively, this study initially investigated the causal relationship between 731 immune cell traits and osteomyelitis by IVW method. Following an initial screening to exclude reverse causality and horizontal pleiotropy, a total of 20 immune cell traits were identified as having a potential causal relationship with osteomyelitis (Supplementary Table 2). These traits were subsequently included in the subsequent stage of the study.

Association of Ceramide (d40:2) levels with 20 immune cell traits

This study has identified a positive potential causal relationship between Ceramide (d40:2) levels and osteomyelitis, and a preliminary screening of 20 immune cell traits that may be causally associated with osteomyelitis. Further, using IVW to screen for causal relationships, revealed a correlation between Ceramide (d40:2) levels and 2 immune cell traits (Supplementary Table 3). The results demonstrated a positive causal association between Ceramide (d40:2) levels and CD27 on IgD-CD38dim, IVW odds ratio [OR] = 1.129 [95% CI 1.016–1.253]; and a negative causal association with CD14 on Mo MDSC, IVW odds ratio [OR] = 0.786 [95% CI 0.651–0.950]. causality, IVW odds ratio [OR] = 0.786 [95% CI 0.651–0.950].

Association of Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels with 20 immune cell traits

By the same analytical approach as described above, a causal relationship between Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels and 3 immune cell traits was found by preliminary IVW screening (Supplementary Table 4). The results demonstrated a negative causal association between Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels and CD20 on IgD+ , IVW odds ratio [OR] = 0.886 [95% CI 0.786–0.990]; and a positive causal association with CD33 on Gr MDSC and CD11b on CD66b+ +myeloid cell, IVW odds ratio [OR] = 1.289 [95% CI 1.061–1.566] and IVW odds ratio [OR] = 1.255 [95% CI 1.055–1.494].

Association of 5 immune cell traits with osteomyelitis

Following the initial screening step, a total of five immune cell traits were causally associated with lipidomics and are now further analyzed for their causal association with osteomyelitis (Supplementary Tables 3 and 4). Ultimately, the immune cell trait CD14 on Mo MDSC showed an opposite correlation between Ceramide (d40:2) levels and osteomyelitis; the immune cell traits CD33 on Gr MDSC and CD11b on CD66b+ +myeloid cell showed an opposite correlation between Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels showed an opposite correlation with osteomyelitis and were therefore excluded from the study. In summary, by MR analysis, the present study found that CD27 on IgD-CD38dim was found to play a mediating role in the positive causal association of Ceramide (d40:2) levels with osteomyelitis; CD20 on IgD+ played a mediating role in the negative causal association of Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels with osteomyelitis.

Proportion of the association between Ceramide (d40:2) levels and osteomyelitis mediated by CD27 on IgD-CD38dim

This study found a correlation between increased Ceramide (d40:2) levels and augmented expression of CD27 on IgD-CD38dim in immune cells, as well as a correlation between increased expression of CD27 on IgD-CD38dim in immune cells and increased risk of osteomyelitis. Furthermore, the study showed an indirect effect of 0.014, which accounted for 10.665% of the elevated risk of osteomyelitis associated with Ceramide (d40:2) levels, while the direct effect was 0.122 (Fig. 4).

Proportion of the association between Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels and osteomyelitis mediated by CD20 on IgD+

This study also found that increased levels of Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) suppressed CD20 on IgD+ in immune cells, and that the suppression of CD20 on IgD+ reduced the risk of osteomyelitis. The study showed an indirect effect of 0.07, which accounted for 6.640% of the reduced risk of osteomyelitis associated with Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels, compared to a direct effect of 0.128 (Fig. 4).

Sensitivity analysis

The above analyses were performed using the online tool PhenoScanner to analyze SNPs and exclude SNPs that were strongly associated with confounding factors. Genetic pleiotropy did not bias the results according to the MR-Egger regression intercept method; MR-PRESSO analyses showed no horizontal pleiotropy (P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 5, 6). Meanwhile, Cochran Q test showed no significant heterogeneity (P > 0.05) (Supplementary Table 5, 6). Additionally, sensitivity tests were conducted using the "leave-one-out" approach to assess the impact of specific SNPs on causality. The results demonstrated that the systematic exclusion of each SNP did not result in any significant alterations to the effect estimates or the qualitative conclusions of the model (Supplementary Fig. 1, 2, 3).

Discussion

The main factors contributing to osteomyelitis are the virulence and abundance of the pathogen and the susceptibility of the host to infection [41]. Osteomyelitis often occurs in immunocompromised populations, such as the elderly and children, and there are no significant racial differences. The progression of osteomyelitis is influenced by factors such as the inherent virulence of the bacteria and the immunity of the host [42].

In the event of infection and tissue damage, the body's immune cells undergo a rapid increase in value, enabling the generation of an effective inflammatory response [43]. Regulation of lipid metabolism plays an equally crucial role in the induction of the inflammatory response [44, 45]. Previous studies have analyzed the lipidomics of Staphylococcus aureus-infected mouse femurs by mass spectrometry and found that ether lipids and arachidonic acid lipids play an important role in abscess formation and inflammatory signaling [46]. However, due to a number of factors, few studies have analyzed plasma lipidomics in human osteomyelitis. Therefore, this study is the first comprehensive assessment of the causal relationship between plasma liposomes and osteomyelitis. Previous studies have demonstrated the potential causal relationship between circulating immune cells and osteomyelitis [47]. This study innovatively combines these three elements using MR analysis to demonstrate a causal relationship between osteomyelitis and plasma liposomes. The mediating role of immune cell traits in this process was also explored by integrating GWAS circulating immune cell data. This study found a positive causal relationship between Ceramide (d40:2) levels and osteomyelitis, as each 1 SD increase in Ceramide (d40:2) levels would increase the risk of osteomyelitis by 12.5%, of which 10.67% contributed to osteomyelitis by mediating CD27 on IgD-CD38dim value-addition. In addition to this, the current study also found a negative causal relationship between Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels and osteomyelitis, when the risk of osteomyelitis will be reduced by 11.4% for every 1 SD increase in Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels, of which 6.64% by mediating CD20 on IgD+ to suppress osteomyelitis.

Ceramides played a role in the synthesis of complex sphingolipids, acting as precursors and metabolic centers [48]. Previous studies have demonstrated that sphingolipids play a pivotal role in regulating a multitude of cellular processes, including the modulation of the actin cytoskeleton, endocytosis, the cell cycle, apoptosis, and cellular stress responses such as programmed cell death, senescence, cell survival, migration, adhesion, inflammation, vesicular transport, and phagocytosis [49, 50]. The precise impact of sphingomyelin on bone loss and inflammatory progression in osteomyelitis remains uncertain. However, it has been demonstrated that ceramides can initiate non-enzymatic oxidation of lipoproteins, leading to reactive oxygen species-induced changes in the inflammatory microenvironment ultimately leading to exacerbation of bone infections and the development of osteoporosis [51, 52]. In vitro studies conducted by Alsahli demonstrated that reduced ceramide levels enhance osteoblast viability [53]. Chae et al. found from animal studies that endogenous ceramides promote osteoblast apoptosis through the NF-kβ pathway [54]. Additionally, Takeda et al. demonstrated that ceramide enhances osteoclast survival and inhibits osteoclast activity by preventing the formation of the F-actin ring [55]. In addition, CD27 on IgD-CD38dim, which is a mediating variable, belongs to the B-cell subpopulation. It has been demonstrated that an increase in the proportion of memory B cells and a decrease in naïve B cells, resulting in an adaptive immune response, is associated with an increased susceptibility to osteomyelitis. This is associated with memory B cells mediating a rapid antibody response, which increases the risk of infection due to humoral immunity driving excessive inflammation during the infectious phase of osteomyelitis [56, 57]. Meanwhile, Nutt et al. found that increased levels of igD-38B cells would exacerbate the acute inflammatory response of pathogens in osteomyelitis leading to an increased risk of osteomyelitis [58]. The current study demonstrated that the stimulatory effect of ceramides on the inflammatory microenvironment and the inhibitory effect of osteoblasts lead to an increased risk of osteomyelitis. In particular, Ceramide-induced inflammation appears to exacerbate acute immune responses, particularly through CD27-expressing IgD-CD38dim B cells, which may contribute to osteomyelitis progression.

Lipids in the plasma membrane play a primary role in intracellular signaling. Diacylglycerol represent the principal product of phospholipase C (PLC) hydrolysis of phosphatidylinositol diphosphate (PIP2) [59]. Diacylglycerol represent a crucial second messenger involved in a multitude of cellular processes. For instance, glycerol lipids impede osteoclast differentiation through the activation of proteinase C (PKC), which results in a reduction in bone resorption and an increase in bone mass [60]. Previous studies have demonstrated that the induction of osteoclast differentiation causes bone destruction in mice with rheumatoid arthritis or inflammatory diseases [61]. Consequently, this study postulated that Diacylglycerol may reduce the risk of osteomyelitis by inhibiting osteoclast differentiation and thereby reducing bone destruction in osteomyelitis. Additionally, CD20 on IgD+ is predominantly expressed in mature B cells and exhibits immunoreactivity. Therefore, Diacylglycerol may also mediate the production of an immune-inflammatory response to CD20 on IgD+ , reducing the risk of osteomyelitis.

This study still had limitations. Firstly, the data in this study were primarily derived from European databases and consequently somewhat limited in terms of the races studied. Consequently, the study will be continued and refined to compensate for this when GWAS data from different races are made publicly available. Secondly, estimates from MR studies based on unrelated individuals may be subject to bias due to environmental and social factors such as selective mating, genetic breeding and remaining population stratification. In future studies, these potential biases can be overcome by utilizing intrafamilial GWAS (if publicly available). Finally, MR provides an important alternative method for verifying causal effects, and the results could be further validated in the future by conducting additional randomized controlled experiments.

Conclusion

This study revealed a positive causal association between Ceramide (d40:2) levels and osteomyelitis, partly mediated through CD27 on IgD-CD38dim. Conversely, a negative causal association was found between Diacylglycerol (16:1_18:1) levels and osteomyelitis, partially mediated through CD20 on IgD+ .This study sought to further elucidate the pathogenesis of osteomyelitis and the risk/protective factors affecting its progression. The findings of this study provide new insights that may inform the prevention and treatment of osteomyelitis.

Feasibility of a peer-led educative programme on contraception using social media for high school students: a mixed-methods study (SAPLAI protocol)
Authors: Danielle Reynaud, Victorine Lenclume, Lallia Bertrand, Anne-Marie Balaga, Estelle Laboureur, Léa Bruneau
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSexual and reproductive health (SRH) for adolescents is a global public health concern. Access to SRH information and services regarding contraception is necessary, particularly in underserved regions, such as the French overseas territories of Reunion Island and Guadeloupe, where indicators for teenage pregnancy and abortion are significantly high. This study protocol describes the methodology to be used to assess the feasibility of a peer-led contraception education programme for high school students using social media.Methods and analysisA multicentre, exploratory sequential mixed-methods with a pre-post design, prospective study is being conducted in Guadeloupe and on Reunion Island. The qualitative component started on 31 May 2025, and the study will continue until 30 June 2026. Participants will be aged 15 to 19 years and will attend high school. In Phase 1, focus groups will explore the adolescents’ perceptions of peer influencers in contraceptive education and their suggestions for organising a prevention programme on social media. These findings will directly inform the intervention in Phase 3. At this stage of this phase, peer influencers will also be identified. Phase 2 will train selected peer educators in SHR, digital content creation to prepare them for intervention design and delivery. Phase 3 consists of the co-construction, implementation and evaluation of the intervention. Outcomes will include feasibility, acceptability, adoption, fidelity and exploratory effectiveness. The primary outcome will be peer engagement defined as the completion of at least 70% of the planned educational tasks.Ethics and disseminationThis study has received ethical approval from the Comité de Protection des Personnes under RIPH3 (ID-RCB: 2025-A00358-41) and will follow the French ethical standards for low-intervention research. Introduction

Sexual and reproductive health (SRH) for adolescents is a global public health concern and is essential to the overall well-being and future prospects of adolescents.1 Access to SRH information and services, such as access to contraception, can improve adolescent health outcomes and reduce gender-based inequality, especially in underserved regions.

In Guadeloupe and Réunion Island, two underserved overseas territories of France, indicators for teenage pregnancies and abortions are significantly high and continuously rising.2 3 In 2021, although fewer births among minors, under the age of 18 years, were recorded in Guadeloupe compared with Reunion Island (n=29), the rate was three times higher than in mainland France.4 In 2022, 4.3% of all births in Reunion Island were to mothers under 20 years old, compared with 1.7% in mainland France. Moreover, among minors, the birth rate (1.6%) was five times higher than in mainland France.5 Abortion rates among minors were also considerably higher in 2022, with 13% in Reunion Island and 18.6% in Guadeloupe, compared with 5% in mainland France.3

Regarding contraceptive use, nearly 10% of adolescents do not use any contraception during their first sexual experience.6 Recent school-based surveys (EnCLASS DROM study) have shown a decline in condom use over time. Even though 70% of adolescent males reportedly used condoms at their first sexual intercourse, 58% did so during their most recent sexual encounter and with seven out of 10 of these encounters using protection.7 8 In Guadeloupe, 11% of young females aged 18 to 24 years stated not using contraception during their last sexual intercourse.9

These trends may reflect multiple structural and socio-cultural barriers, including limited access to youth-friendly health services,10 continued taboos around sexuality11 and inadequate sexual education, all of which may contribute to increased risky sexual behaviours among adolescents.12

National and international strategies, including those from the WHO, advocate for comprehensive sexual education and the integration of digital tools to better reach adolescents.13 14 Peer-led interventions, such as peer education, are one such strategy which involves peer-to-peer learning among young individuals of similar ages, backgrounds and cultural contexts, often without adult mediation.15 It is a well-established approach to promoting SRH for adolescents, which fosters cognitive skills, self-confidence, practical abilities and a greater engagement in learning.16 17

When implemented in schools, peer-led interventions could be particularly effective in promoting healthy sexual behaviours. Dodd et al reported that peer-led interventions improved knowledge and prevention behaviours across multiple health domains, including SRH, particularly showing an increase in access and use of contraception.18 A 2023 study in Ethiopia reported a 25% increase in contraceptive use 6 months after a peer-led intervention was initiated along with reductions in unmet contraception-related needs and increased demand for such health engagements.19 Similar positive outcomes such as increased use of condoms and HIV testing have been observed in school and university settings.20 A prospective study regarding the Service Sanitaire peer-led health education programme aimed at minors aged 13 to 15 years showed substantial improvements in knowledge about contraception.21 On the other hand, findings on behavioural impacts by peer-led programmes have remained mixed. A 2023 British umbrella review highlighted gaps in theoretical rigour, supervision and standardised recruitment of peer educators.22

Given that social media has become a primary source of information for adolescents, platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube and WhatsApp have been widely used for contraception-related content.23 24 Social media influencers are increasingly being recognised for their ability to engage adolescents through culturally relevant and relatable content.25 26 However, these platforms also spread health-related misinformation, particularly about contraceptive effectiveness and safety.27 28 Since September 2024, the WHO and TikTok have launched a 1-year collaboration to promote science-based health information through trusted digital social media channels.29 The initiative has been supported by the WHO’s Fides network of healthcare influencers, which unites over 800 content creators to deliver youth-friendly health communication.29

In Reunion Island, adolescents expressed a preference for receiving contraception information through social media.30 Peer-led interventions, such as those tested in the STASH pilot study, demonstrated high acceptability and potential to improve adolescent sexual health through social media.31 Combining peer-led education programmes with digital strategies could, therefore, enhance credibility, counter misinformation and broaden outreach. Few studies have evaluated the feasibility of peer-led contraception education via social media in a French-based cultural framework. With that context, this protocol describes the methodology of a study that will explore how a peer-led contraception education programme using social media could influence access, uptake and understanding of contraception among high school students in Guadeloupe and Reunion Island.

Methods and analysis

Study design

The Study on the Acceptability of Peer-Led Adolescent Interventions (SAPLAI) study is a non-randomised, multicentre, exploratory sequential mixed-methods research that combines qualitative inquiry with a quasi-experimental pre-post design.32 The study is taking place during a 13-month period in vocational high schools in Guadeloupe and Reunion Island from 31 May 2025 to 30 June 2026 and is coordinated by the sponsor University Hospital of Reunion Island, which oversees the overall conduct of the project, including administrative management, monitoring and scientific coordination.

There will be three phases in this study. In Phase 1, focus groups with high school students will explore their needs in SRH, their preferences regarding the use of social media and their suggestions on how a peer-led contraception programme should be organised and disseminated. These qualitative insights will directly inform the recruitment criteria and training content for peer educators in Phase 2 and provide the foundation for the design and implementation of the intervention in Phase 3. In turn, the quantitative evaluation in Phase 3 will assess the feasibility, acceptability, adoption, fidelity and preliminary effectiveness of this co-constructed intervention. This sequential structure ensures methodological continuity, as qualitative results inform the design of the intervention and its subsequent quantitative evaluation, thus achieving integration of both approaches. Study objectives

The primary objective will be to assess the feasibility of a peer-led contraceptive education programme. Secondary objectives will include: (i) describing adolescents’ perceptions of peer-led health education, their contraceptive-related needs and preferences for health-related social media content of adolescents (Phase 1); (ii) training peer participants with knowledge and skills in contraception education, communication, digital content creation and leadership, to enable them to act as peer educators (Phase 2); (iii) assessing the effects of the intervention on students’ knowledge, attitudes and practices (KAP) regarding contraception on an exploratory basis; and (iv) evaluating the acceptability, adoption and fidelity of a peer-led contraception education programme using social media in school settings (Phase 3).

Inclusion/exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria for participants for the qualitative and quantitative phases include adolescents who are (i) aged between 15 and 19 years, (ii) enrolled in one of the selected vocational high schools in Reunion Island or Guadeloupe, (iii) have residential status in France, (iv) provide written informed consent as well as written parental/guardian consent and (v) not currently participating in any other similar health education programmes.

Inclusion criteria for the peer educators will include adolescents who are (i) between the ages of 15 and 19 years and enrolled in Year 11 at a selected vocational high school; (ii) compliant with school staff members based on their motivation, communication and leadership skills; (iii) have access to digital tools; (iv) own a smartphone with video recording capabilities or be provided with one by the research team if needed; (v) provide expressed interest and written informed consent as well as written parental/guardian consent; and (vi) not currently involved in another similar health education programme.

Recruitment

Students will be recruited from partnering vocational high schools in Guadeloupe and Reunion Island. Recruitment will be conducted in collaboration with rectorates, school principals and supported by school nurses and teachers. Information sessions with both students and parents will be organised in each school to present the study, address questions and distribute consent forms. The consent forms and participant information sheets, which have been approved by the Comité de Protection des Personnes (CPP), will be collected by the research team. For minors, parental consent and student assent will be obtained, while participants aged 18 years or older will provide their own consent.

Interventions

Phase 1: qualitative exploration

The first qualitative phase of the study aims to explore, through focus group, adolescents’ perceptions of peer-led health education and peer influence, their preferences on contraception-related content and the use of digital channels, as well as their suggestions for organising a prevention programme on social media. Phase 1 will also serve to select the peer educators.

Participants will be recruited through non-probability purposive sampling, based on their accessibility and willingness to participate from second-year vocational high school students in both locations (one in Reunion Island and one in Guadeloupe). A total of four focus groups will be conducted—two in Guadeloupe and two on Reunion Island—with 8 to 10 participants per group, for an estimated total of 32 to 40 students. This format is consistent with qualitative research standards and allows for thematic saturation to be achieved.33

Focus groups will be co-facilitated by a nurse researcher, a health management professional and a socio-anthropologist, using a semi-structured guide, which was developed by the research team based on a review of the literature in adolescent SRH, peer-led education, social media and implementation science, particularly the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research34 including intervention characteristics, outer and inner setting, individual characteristics and implementation processes. Key themes will include (i) perception of contraception education at school, (ii) expectations and views on peer-led approaches, (iii) preferred digital platforms, (iv) the ‘ideal’ influencer, (v) conditions for school-based implementation and (vi) formats for social media-based prevention programmes. While these predefined domains will structure the discussion, the qualitative design will remain open to emergent themes raised by participants. This reflexive approach, consistent with best practices in qualitative research, allows adolescents to introduce new perspectives beyond the predefined topics, thereby enriching the depth and relevance of the analysis. These findings from Phase 1 will therefore not remain descriptive but will directly guide the recruitment and training of peer educators in Phase 2 and the design and implementation of the intervention in Phase 3, ensuring sequential integration between qualitative and quantitative phases.

The focus group interview guide was reviewed by experts in qualitative research, adolescent SRH, contraception peer education and social media. It was then pre-tested with five students from a different high school in Reunion Island to ensure clarity, cultural sensitivity and appropriateness for the target population.

Focus group sessions will take place in dedicated rooms at participating schools and will last approximately 2 hours. An information sheet will be distributed prior to the session to explain the study to all participants. Written informed consent will be obtained from both students and their parents/guardians. Participation will be voluntary, and students can withdraw from the study at any time. Those experiencing unease or discomfort will be referred to appropriate healthcare professionals. At the end of each session, interested students will be invited to volunteer as peer educators. Final selection will be jointly conducted by the research investigators and school staff members based on pre-defined criteria including communication skills, leadership qualities, active listening and interest in contraception. A total of 12 students will be selected to serve as peer influencers for the intervention. The selection of 12 peer educators, evenly distributed between Reunion Island and Guadeloupe (6 per site), is based on recommendations from the literature, which suggest a flexible range of 2 to 14 peer educators depending on the context.19 31 35 This number ensures adaptability to local specificities and effective group dynamics. Each peer educator will be assigned one dedicated week to lead the educational campaign as part of the overall intervention schedule in Phase 3.

Qualitative outcomes will be derived from focus group transcripts that will be thematically analysed using thematic analysis as noted in Ayre and McCaffery.36 The analysis will aim to highlight adolescents’ representations and acceptability of contraception education, peer-to-peer dynamics and the legitimacy of peer educators, digital practices and the mediation of online platforms, integration within the school context, as well as formats and communication strategies for social media-based prevention. Representative quotes will be included in the reporting to illustrate the key themes. To ensure participant anonymity, each focus group will only be labelled using a non-identifiable code (eg, Group A, B, C or D). All related materials (transcripts, audio recordings and notes) will be coded under these identifiers. No names or personally identifiable information will be recorded at data collection or analysis. No individual medical data will be collected. The transcripts will be coded independently by two researchers using Taguette (Taguette, Paris, France), an open-source qualitative data analysis tool.37 Emerging themes will be triangulated across study locations and professional backgrounds to enhance analytical rigour and minimise bias.

Phase 2: Training and empowerment of peer educators

The second phase adopts a formative approach and aims to provide selected adolescents in Phase 1 with the training and skills needed to act as peer educators. A 4-month blended training programme will be delivered, combining 1.5 hours per week of asynchronous online learning with one in-person workshop. Online modules will be hosted via Google Classroom and supplemented by interactive tools such as Wooclap. Training will cover key topics including SRH for adolescents, contraception, gender and sexuality, misinformation, respectful relationships, digital communication and basic media production methodology.38 The pedagogical team will be comprised of SRH experts, a nurse educator and media professionals. The principal investigator team will accompany the participants throughout the programme, conducting weekly check-ins and monitoring the progress of content production. Teachers will actively support and motivate participants throughout the training period.

A €300 compensation will be granted to each peer educator who completes at least 70% of the training and produces all planned social media content. This measure, approved by the ethics committee, aims to acknowledge their effective contribution and promote engagement.

Adoption outcomes will include (i) the training completion rate, defined as attendance at ≥80% of the online training sessions and monitored via Google Classroom, and (ii) improvement in participants’ knowledge and communication skills, assessed through a structured pre- and post-training questionnaire. A score of ≥70% on the post-training questionnaire will be considered satisfactory, and the proportion of participants achieving this threshold will be recorded.

This questionnaire is designed to evaluate core competencies in three key areas: SRH, health communication and leadership. It will be administered before and after the training programme:

Multiple-choice questions to evaluate theoretical knowledge on human anatomy and sexual physiology, contraceptive methods and family planning and access to SRH services.

Open-ended questions to assess communication and leadership skills, the ability to clearly convey sexual health information to peers and practical competencies, such as demonstrating correct condom use or planning awareness activities.

These assessments will provide quantitative indicators of learning progress, analysed descriptively to evaluate training quality and effectiveness. The results will be used both to assess individual progress and to determine participants’ readiness to implement the peer-led intervention in Phase 3. Only adolescents who achieve at least 70% on the post-training evaluation and have attended at least 80% of the online training sessions will be eligible to participate in Phase 3, during which they will implement the peer-led education programme. This requirement ensures that only adequately prepared participants deliver the intervention.

Phase 3: implementation and evaluation programme

The third phase will co-construct, implement and evaluate the intervention with a different group of students (not involved in content creation), allowing a structured pre-post evaluation of the programme. It will assess the feasibility, acceptability, exploratory effectiveness, adoption and fidelity to the peer-led contraception education programme. All instruments will be administered in French.

Over a 3-month period, peer educators will produce and disseminate weekly educational content (including videos, stories, reels, quizzes/polls and podcasts) via platforms such as Facebook, TikTok, Snapchat and Instagram. Content will follow an editorial calendar co-developed with students to ensure cultural and contextual relevance. An overview of the peer educator pathway is illustrated in figure 2. Lessons learnt from this study will form the basis for larger-scale trials and policy development in the future, particularly in regions facing similar adolescent sexual health challenges. Future research could explore long-term behavioural outcomes and assess cost-effectiveness to support the integration of such approaches into national public health promotion strategies. The intervention will target students in Year 10 (approximately 15–16 years old), who will receive digital content delivered through selected social media platforms. This content will be developed by peer educators, who will be Year 11 students. All students will have the opportunity to participate in Phase 3 of the programme. In each participating school, dedicated sessions will be organised to ensure that students without personal digital access can still view the peer-led educational content via the school’s internet-enabled tablets or computers. Phase 3 outcomes will be evaluated using a framework based on the WHO’s Implementation Research in Health: a Practical Guide as described by Peters et al.39 This approach will allow assessment beyond efficacy by focusing on the real-world applicability and sustainability of public health interventions. The primary outcome of this study will be the feasibility of the programme. Feasibility will be assessed on the level of the engagement of peer educators. Each peer educator’s engagement rate will be calculated as the proportion of planned educational tasks completed during the 3-month implementation period. The programme will be considered feasible if peer educators complete at least 70% of the scheduled tasks. A threshold of 70% completion is defined as the minimum level of individual engagement indicating adequate feasibility of the intervention.

To operationalise this indicator, a standardised task checklist was developed to monitor peer educators’ engagement throughout the 3-month implementation period. It was based on international recommendations from the Evidence-Based Guidelines for Youth Peer Education published by Family Health International / YouthNet (2006). These guidelines emphasise the importance of structured monitoring of peer educator activities through practical tools such as activity checklists and tracking sheets.40

This checklist includes 13 distinct activities covering the entire intervention cycle: content planning, creation and dissemination (eg, video and post preparation, online publication), peer engagement (eg, moderating discussions, identifying emerging themes), collaboration (eg, joint work with other peer educators and local influencers), feedback adaptation, monitoring (eg, updating Trello dashboard, attending weekly meetings) and outcome evaluation (eg, reporting impact, presenting results). Each task is marked as completed or not. The proportion of completed tasks per peer educator will be used to calculate the engagement rate, with a threshold of 70% completion defined as adequate feasibility.

Acceptability outcomes will involve (i) the proportion of high school students with access to necessary technology (including smartphone, computer and tablet), (ii) registration rate on intervention-related social media platforms (including Facebook, TikTok, Instagram or Snapchat), (iii) social media user engagement indicators (collected monthly via platform analytics) such as the number of logins, post/story likes and questions/comments posted and (iv) participant satisfaction (students and peer educators) assessed through a structured questionnaire administered at the end of the intervention (T1).

Peer educator satisfaction questionnaire includes three items assessing peer educators’ perceptions of their preparation, the relevance of the tools provided and the personal value of the experience. Items are rated on a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (Strongly disagree) to (Strongly agree).

The student satisfaction questionnaire includes three items evaluating their satisfaction with the clarity of the content, their comfort in discussing sexual health and the perceived usefulness of the social media approach. Responses are rated on the same five-point Likert scale.

Exploratory effectiveness outcome will involve the changes in KAP on contraception which will be evaluated by comparing baseline (T0) and post-intervention (T1) responses to a validated questionnaire.

The KAP questionnaire was developed by the research team as part of the project. It was constructed based on the existing literature41 42 and the ‘Baromètre Santé’ published by Santé Publique France in 2016,43 due to the absence of scientifically validated questionnaires for this specific population. The questionnaire consists of 30 questions: 10 assessing knowledge, 10 assessing attitudes and 10 assessing practices. The total score is 150 points. It will be administered before the intervention and 3 months afterwards to measure changes in participants’ knowledge, attitudes and practices. It was revised by a multidisciplinary expert panel to ensure cultural and contextual appropriateness (nurse, sexual expert, gynaecologist, midwife and investigator principal). It was pilot-tested with 10 high school students from a different school in Reunion Island, which allowed us to confirm its clarity, structure and acceptability among the target population.

Fidelity outcome will be assessed through multiple indicators of peer educator adherence to the implementation plan. First, adherence will be monitored using TRELLO, which will track content release, scheduling and technical challenges/solutions that will be documented in a project logbook. Fidelity will also be assessed through the quality of collaboration measured by the responses to the following question: on a scale from 1 to 5 (1 defined as not valued; 5 defined as highly valued), how valued did you feel in your role as a peer educator by the research team?

Using a feasibility study design, no formal sample size or power calculation will be required. For the quantitative sample, the study will involve students from six intact classes (three per site), with approximately 25 students per class. This approach aims to include at least 60 students per region, for a total of 120 participants. The sample is designed to allow for pre- and post-intervention comparisons of KAP on contraception while also accounting for potential dropouts or refusals to participate.

Regarding quantitative data, self-administered online questionnaires will be distributed to participating students at T0 and T1. These surveys will assess KAP on contraception and participant satisfaction with the intervention. The questionnaires will include validated items and Likert-scale questions. Data will be analysed using SAS (version 9.4 SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) or Stata (version 13.1, Stat Corp, Tex, USA). Descriptive statistics will be reported for all variables, and paired comparisons (T0 and T1) will be used to assess change over time. Appropriate statistical tests such as McNemar’s or Wilcoxon signed-rank will be selected based on the nature and distribution of the data.

Analyses will primarily follow an intention-to-treat approach, including all participants who completed baseline assessments. Missing data will be handled using a complete case analysis; no imputation is planned given the feasibility design of the study. Access to the final trial dataset will be restricted to the principal investigators, the sponsor University Hospital of Reunion Island and the biostatistician.

Monitoring

No independent Data Monitoring Committee will be established given the low-risk, educational nature of the intervention. No interim analyses or stopping guidelines are planned. Potential adverse events, such as discomfort during focus group discussions, will be monitored by the school nurses and the research team, with referral procedures to the general practitioner in place if needed.

Quality control will be ensured by a clinical research associate mandated by the sponsor, following a risk-based monitoring plan. Monitoring will primarily focus on informed consent procedures, adherence to the study protocol and the accuracy and completeness of collected data. No formal auditing procedures are planned.

Ethics and dissemination

This study has received Category III ethical approval from the CPP for research involving human participants (RIPH3), with registration number ID-RCB: 2025-A00358-41 in March 2025. The study is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06943209, https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06943209). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants as well as their parental/legal guardians prior to inclusion. The voluntary nature of participation will be clearly explained and participants may withdraw at any time. Confidentiality and data protection will be strictly maintained in accordance with French regulations and GDPR standards. No personally identifiable or medical data will be collected. Depending on participant approval, findings from the study will be disseminated through scientific publications in peer-reviewed open-access journals, presentations at national and international nursing and public health conferences, report and feedback sessions with the management teams of participating schools and knowledge-sharing sessions with public health institutions/stakeholders interested in adolescent SRH promotion. This dissemination approach aims to ensure broad access to our findings among researchers, practitioners, policymakers and the academic community.

Patient and public involvement

This study will be designed with the active involvement of the target population. During the preparatory phase, exploratory discussions will be held with high school students to better understand their views on health education and digital communication. Their input will contribute to the selection of the peer educators, choice of social media platforms and development of the educational content tailored to their interests and communication preferences. Participants will be consulted during the planning stages to identify the preferred content format and assess the acceptability of having peer educators as credible SRH messengers. This involvement will help ensure that both the content and delivery methods are aligned with the digital practices and cultural context of the target population. Throughout the implementation phase, the peer educators will play a central role in content creation, online engagement and outreach to their peers. Participants will not be involved in data analysis nor the formal dissemination of study results.

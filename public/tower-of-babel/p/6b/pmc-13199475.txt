Fractal dimension-based multi-focus image fusion via distance-weighted regional energy in curvelet domain
Authors: Ming Lv, Zhenhong Jia, Wu Le, Liangliang Li, Hongbing Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
To address the challenges of information loss and noise interference in multi-focus image fusion, this paper presents a novel curvelet-domain fusion algorithm based on distance-weighted regional energy (DWRE) and fractal dimension. The proposed method first decomposes the source images using the curvelet transform, obtaining both low- and high-frequency sub-bands. For the high-frequency sub-bands, fusion is performed using DWRE and fractal dimension in conjunction with a consistency verification strategy, ensuring that salient details and structural information are effectively preserved. For the low-frequency sub-band, an averaging-based fusion rule is applied to maintain overall intensity information. Finally, the inverse curvelet transform is employed to reconstruct the fused image. To evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm, experiments are conducted on the Lytro and MFI-WHU benchmark datasets. The results demonstrate that our approach achieves superior fusion performance compared to several state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods, particularly in terms of detail preservation, noise suppression, and visual quality. Introduction

In practical imaging scenarios, optical systems often face the limited depth-of-field problem, where only a portion of a scene appears sharply focused in a single capture. This limitation can hinder human perception and reduce the effectiveness of computer vision applications such as object recognition, surveillance, and medical imaging1–4. Multi-focus image fusion (MFIF) addresses this challenge by combining multiple images of the same scene, each focused on different regions, into a single all-in-focus image that retains sharpness across the entire field5–9.

Traditional MFIF methods largely rely on spatial-domain and transform-domain techniques10–12. Spatial-domain approaches typically employ focus measures, such as variance, gradient, or energy of Laplacian, to identify the sharpest regions in each source image and directly merge them. Transform-domain approaches, including wavelet, curvelet, contourlet, and shearlet transforms, decompose the source images into multi-scale representations, fuse coefficients using carefully designed rules, and reconstruct the fused image10. These methods effectively preserve local details and edges, but their performance is sensitive to noise, misalignment, and the choice of fusion rules.

Recent advances in deep learning-based MFIF have shown remarkable improvements13–15. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs), autoencoders, and generative models can learn hierarchical features that capture focus information and structural details simultaneously16–19. More recently, attention mechanisms and transformer architectures have been introduced to model long-range dependencies, enhancing fusion quality in complex scenes and improving robustness against registration errors. These learning-based approaches often outperform traditional methods in both objective metrics (e.g., entropy, mutual information, structural similarity) and perceptual quality20. MFIF has broad applications in computer vision, remote sensing, biomedical imaging, and photography, where all-in-focus images improve the interpretability and reliability of downstream tasks. Despite significant progress, challenges remain in developing algorithms that are robust to misalignment, noise, and large variations in focus regions, while maintaining high computational efficiency. Continued research in MFIF is therefore critical for both practical imaging applications and the advancement of intelligent vision systems21.

Fractal and fractional theories have been widely employed in image fusion and image enhancement owing to their unique capabilities in modeling complexity and enhancing multi-scale image features22–25. Fractal-based methods mainly exploit the self-similarity and irregularity inherent in natural images. Fractal dimension has been extensively used as a focus measure in multi-focus image fusion, since focused regions generally exhibit higher structural complexity. For instance, Panigrahy et al.22 utilized fractal dimension in the àtrous wavelet domain to discriminate focused and defocused regions, achieving improved preservation of edge and texture information. Similarly, Li et al.26 proposed a fractal dimension-based multi-focus image fusion method in the NSCT domain, where a coupled neural P system was employed to enhance fusion performance. These approaches demonstrate that fractal descriptors are effective in capturing fine structural differences and guiding the selection of salient regions. Fractional-based methods, rooted in fractional calculus, provide a flexible framework between integer-order differentiation and integration, thereby offering powerful tools for image detail enhancement and frequency-domain analysis.

Given the application and inspiration of curvelet transform in image fusion27,28, this paper introduces a novel image fusion algorithm in the curvelet domain, leveraging distance-weighted regional energy (DWRE) and fractal dimension. The source images are first decomposed via the curvelet transform to obtain low-frequency and high-frequency sub-bands. For high-frequency components, fusion is carried out using a DWRE-based and fractal dimension approaches combined with a consistency verification mechanism to ensure the effective preservation of salient details and structural features. The low-frequency sub-band is fused using an averaging strategy to retain global intensity information. The fused image is subsequently reconstructed through inverse curvelet transformation. The performance of the proposed method is validated experimentally on two benchmark datasets: Lytro and MFI-WHU. Results on Lytro dataset

Figure 3 illustrates the fusion results obtained by different algorithms on the Earth model. Figure 4 depicts the difference maps between the fused images and the source image, providing a clearer visualization of the fusion effectiveness. In multi-focus image fusion, difference maps play an important role in evaluating the fusion performance. They are typically generated by computing the pixel-wise differences between the fused image and the source images. Difference maps provide a direct visualization of how well the source information is preserved: low and uniform difference values indicate good information retention, while noticeable differences suggest information loss or distortion. Moreover, difference maps help assess the clarity of focused regions. They reveal whether both near-focus and far-focus areas are accurately preserved in the fusion result. Difference maps also highlight the presence of artifacts or noise introduced by fusion algorithms, making it easier to identify structural inconsistencies or visual degradations. By offering a visual reference, difference maps bridge the gap between subjective evaluation and objective metrics, enabling a more comprehensive and interpretable assessment of fusion performance.Fig. 3Fusion results on the Earth model data. From the results as shown in Figs. 3 and 4, we can denote that the SDNet and U2Fusion methods are able to preserve information in the near-focus regions; however, they exhibit significant information loss in the far-focus areas. Although EgeFusion method enhances local detail in the fused images, its overall fusion performance is unsatisfactory and it tends to introduce noticeable artifacts. The CVTFD algorithm produces relatively good fusion results; nevertheless, the preservation of information in the far-focus regions still requires improvement. The fused images generated by TITA and FDFusion suffer from severe information loss in the lower parts of the images. ReFusion leads to considerable information loss in the Earth model region. Although SwinMFF achieves satisfactory preservation in the near-focus regions, it fails to retain important details in the far-focus areas, such as the lamps and the crowd. In contrast, the proposed method achieves superior fusion performance. It not only preserves information from the source images more effectively but also demonstrates strong capability in suppressing noise, thereby producing visually more satisfactory fused images compared with other algorithms.

Conclusions

To tackle the issues of information loss and noise interference in multi-focus image fusion, this paper proposes a novel fusion algorithm operating in the curvelet domain, which integrates distance-weighted regional energy (DWRE) and consistency verification. The method begins by decomposing the source images via curvelet transform to acquire low-frequency and high-frequency sub-bands. High-frequency sub-bands are fused using a DWRE-based rule and and fractal dimension along with a consistency verification mechanism to enhance the retention of salient features and structural details. The low-frequency sub-band is combined through an averaging strategy to preserve global intensity characteristics. The fused image is then reconstructed using the inverse curvelet transform. Experimental evaluations on the Lytro and MFI-WHU benchmark datasets demonstrate that the proposed method outperforms various state-of-the-art approaches, achieving notable improvements in detail preservation, noise suppression, and visual quality. In future work, we plan to extend the proposed algorithm to applications in remote sensing fusion69–75, change detection76–81, and object detection82,83.

Facial Subcutaneous Nodule as the First Presentation Finding in a Patient with Rheumatoid Arthritis
Authors: Na Wang, Naiwen Hu, Hongsheng Sun, Furen Zhang
Published: 2023

Dear Sir or Madam,

Rheumatoid nodules (RNs) are a common manifestation of rheumatoid arthritis (RA), which can occur in up to 40% of RA patients. RNs are mainly found on the extensor surface of the forearms, over the joints, or over pressure points. However, RNs can occur in internal organs, such as the pleura, lungs or heart valves.[1] RNs extremely rare can occur on the buccal mucosa and eyelid. Here, we report a novel case of RNs located on both eyelids, which were the first presentation finding for RA.

A 49-year-old woman presented with facial nodules on the eyelid, which had been enlarging over a period of 2 years before her first clinical evaluation. The physical examination revealed numerous nontender, dome-shaped, firm to hard mobile masses, with partly rough surface on the eyelid and eyebrow [Figure 1a]. The patient intermittently had pain and morning stiffness with the distal and proximal interphalangeal joint [Figure 1b] for the recent 5 months. Results of laboratory tests, including urea, electrolytes, liver function, blood glucose, HbA1c level and blood cell counts, were normal. A skin biopsy was obtained, which was consistent with RN. No distinct area of vasculitis was seen, and the mucin stain was negative [Figure 1c-f].

Furthermore, examinations were done. Anti-cyclic citrullinated protein (anti-CCP) antibodies (Abs) against mutated citrullinated vimentin, dsDNA, ANA, SSA and SSB were all negative. The concentration of rheumatoid factor (RF) was 25 IU/ml, the ESR was 40 mm/h, and the CRP level was 25.87 mg/L. Synovitis in all metacarpophalangeal and proximal interphalangeal joints was confirmed with the high-frequency ultrasound [Figure 2a and b]. The chest computed tomography showed small nodule with ground-glass opacity in the upper lobes, which was considered to be RNs in the lung. Prednisone (30 mg/d) was given, the patient responded well to the treatment regimen, and the facial nodules gradually decreased in size with tapering doses of prednisone; no new nodules appeared.

RNs are considered to be the most frequent extra-articular findings of RA. Studies have confirmed that approximately 7% of RA patients demonstrate nodules at the time of diagnosis.[2] The aetiology and pathogenesis of the RN remain elusive; however, numerous reports have described an association between RN and cigarette smoking[2] and methotrexate use may accelerate nodule development.[13] A recent study suggested the possibility that IL-23/IL-17 axis has a role in the pathogenesis of RNs.[4] Recent advances in the treatment of RNs with biologics, including infliximab and rituximab,[1] have yielded paradoxical findings.

The differential diagnosis for our patient includes necrobiotic xanthogranuloma (NX), which clinically presents yellow-to-brown plaques and nodules, and commonly occurs in the periorbital area. The serologic findings showed an IgG lambda paraproteinemia, with an elevated cANCA, C4 deficiency and a negative RF. A skin biopsy[5] displays numerous foreign body giant cells, xanthomatous foam cells with cholesterol clefts and collagen necrosis.

The occurrence of RNs on both upper and lower eyelids in our patient is extremely rare and really caused cosmetic problems for the patient. The present study reports the first case of RNs on both upper and lower eyelids.

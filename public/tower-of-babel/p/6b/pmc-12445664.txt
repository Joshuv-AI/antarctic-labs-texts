First-line PD-1 +/- CTLA-4 blockade in patients with deficient mismatch repair and/or microsatellite instability-high metastatic colorectal cancer
Authors: Vincenzo Nasca, Gabriele Tinè, Julien Taieb, Sara Lonardi, Ben Boursi, Ofer Margalit, Lisa Salvatore, Romain Cohen, Javier Ros, Margherita Ambrosini, Koen Zwart, Jeanine Roodhart, Priya Jayachandran, Michael J Overman, Maria Elena Elez, Chiara Cremolini, David Tougeron, Miriam Koopman, Thierry André, Rosalba Miceli, Filippo Pietrantonio
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPatients with deficient mismatch repair (dMMR) and/or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) show marked sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs). Dual PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade with nivolumab and ipilimumab showed superior progression-free survival (PFS) over chemotherapy and anti-PD-1 monotherapy, but data on treatment-naïve patients is not available yet.MethodsThis international multicenter study included patients with dMMR/MSI-H mCRC receiving either chemotherapy with or without biologics, or anti-PD-1 monotherapy, or dual PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade as a first-line treatment. Data were adjusted using inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) to account for baseline imbalances. IPTW-adjusted survival and subgroup analyses were conducted.ResultsAmong 711 patients, 51.2% received chemotherapy with or without biologics, 37.1% anti-PD-1 monotherapy, and 11.7% dual checkpoint blockade. Anti-CTLA-4 combination therapy significantly improved PFS (HR: 0.14; P < .001) and OS (HR: 0.13; P < .001) vs chemotherapy. In first-line IPTW-adjusted analyses (median follow-up: 29.2 months), dual blockade showed superior PFS over PD-1 monotherapy (HR: 0.58, 95% CI, 0.35-0.97, P = .037), with a favorable OS trend (HR: 0.61, 95% CI, 0.33-1.14, P = .12), and higher objective response rate (ORR) (71.5% vs 55.5%; OR: 1.15; 95% CI, 1.03-1.29; P = .016) and disease control rate (DCR) (93.7% vs 75.8%; OR, 1.18; 95% CI, 1.08-1.28; P < .001). Subgroup analyses suggested greater benefit in patients with left-sided and BRAF mutated tumors, while those with right-sided BRAF wild-type status showed no incremental benefit from dual ICI. Toxicity profile was manageable for both ICI regimen, with grade 3 or 4 immune-related adverse events registered in 11.9% and 15.7% cases in anti-PD-1 monotherapy and anti-CTLA-4 combination groups, respectively.ConclusionsFirst-line dual PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade demonstrated superior efficacy over anti-PD-1 monotherapy in dMMR/MSI-H mCRC regardless of clinical variables. Compared to previous published data across treatment lines, the prognostic impact of several clinical features does not seem to be retained in patients treated upfront with ICIs combination.Keywords: microsatellite instability, metastatic colorectal cancer, ICI, CTLA-4 blockade, PD-1 blockade

This multinational retrospective study reinforces the use of first-line anti-CTLA-4/PD-1 combination, by demonstrating its superiority over anti-PD-1 monotherapy for first-line treatment of patients with dMMR/MSI-H mCRC, regardless of all investigated baseline features. This differs from previous data in pretreated patients, which suggested higher efficacy of the combination in patients with poor prognostic baseline features. Therefore, molecular biomarkers may be more important than clinical features to select the best or less toxic first-line treatment strategy and should be further investigated. The lower benefit of the combination in patients with BRAF wild-type and right-sided disease warrants prospective validation and, subsequently, alternative treatment strategies.

Introduction

Patients with deficient mismatch repair (dMMR) and/or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) constitute a unique molecular subgroup showing exquisite sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).1–3 However, despite a significant portion of patients derive prolonged benefit from anti-PD-1 monotherapy, approximately 50% of patients exhibit primary or acquired resistance to anti-PD-1 monotherapy, highlighting the potential role of biomarkers and the importance of combinations strategies to overcome resistance.4–7 Dual PD-1/CTLA-4 inhibition with ipilimumab and nivolumab is approved in pretreated patients based on high response rate and long-term benefit in a relevant fraction of patients, based on results of the phase 2 Checkmate 142 trial.8,9 In addition, in the CheckMate-8HW trial, the first-line use of this combination significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS), with an hazard ratio (HR) of 0.21, PFS2 and quality of life outcomes compared to chemotherapy with or without monoclonal antibodies.10–12 Finally, ipilimumab plus nivolumab has recently shown significantly longer PFS compared to nivolumab alone across any line of therapy as a co-primary endpoint of the study, with an HR of 0.62.12 These data establish dual checkpoint blockade as a standard of care, including the first-line treatment of patients with dMMR/MSI-H mCRC. However, comprehensive efficacy outcomes in the first-line setting are not available yet, either overall or across all patient subgroups. Since anti-CTLA-4-based combinations increase the burden of AEs and the financial toxicity, it is of interest to investigate whether a dual ICI combo is needed for all patients or may be an overtreatment for those with more favorable prognostic features or high likelihood to achieve long-term survivorship when treated with monotherapy.

To address this question, we conducted a retrospective study including patients with treatment-naïve dMMR/MSI-H mCRC receiving a first-line strategy consisting of chemotherapy with or without monoclonal antibodies vs anti-PD-1 monotherapy vs anti-CTLA-4-based combination. After adjusting for differences in baseline characteristics by inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW), we performed subgroup analyses to dissect the impact of baseline patient and disease characteristics on the efficacy of first-line dual checkpoint blockade compared to anti-PD-1 monotherapy.

Methods

Study population

This is a retrospective multinational study involving patients with treatment-naïve dMMR/MSI-H mCRC, retrieved from different sources. Patients treated with chemotherapy with or without biological agents (ie, the anti-EGFR agents, cetuximab or panitumumab, or the anti-VEGF agent bevacizumab) were selected from a French multicenter real-world data (RWD) cohort that included dMMR/MSI-H mCRC patients in 2007-2017 in 18 French hospitals, a Dutch nationwide population-based RWD cohort that included dMMR/MSI-H mCRC patients in 2014-2019 from all Dutch hospitals, and dMMR/MSI-H mCRC patients included in 3 randomized phase 3 studies (CAIRO, CAIRO2, and CAIRO3) in the period 2003-2012. Details are available elsewhere.13–16 Patients with dMMR/MSI-H mCRC treated with first-line ICIs (either anti-PD-1 monotherapy or anti-CTLA-4-based combinations) as per clinical practice, in compassionate use programs, or in clinical trials were collected across twelve cancer centers across Europe, United States, and Israel. For all patients, MMR and/or MSI status were locally assessed using immunohistochemistry, multiplex PCR, and/or next-generation sequencing per standard institutional practices. Patients with discordance in MMR/MSI status were excluded (dMMR/MSS and pMMR/MSI).

Clinical and pathological baseline characteristics collected before therapy were: age, sex, ECOG PS, primary tumor sidedness (right vs left/rectum), primary tumor resection, time-to-metastases (synchronous vs metachronous; synchronous metastatic disease was defined by diagnosis of metastases within 6 months from surgery of the primary tumor or de novo diagnosis of metastatic or locally advanced unresectable disease), number of metastatic sites (≥2 vs 1), specific sites of metastases and RAS/BRAF mutational status.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the patients’ characteristics. Numerical variables were reported as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), while categorical variables were described using absolute and relative frequencies. Follow-up time was estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier (KM) method. Progression-free survival (PFS) was defined as the time from first-line treatment start to the evidence of disease progression or death from any cause. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from first-line treatment start to death from any cause or last follow-up. Kaplan–Meier curves stratified by treatment regimen were constructed. An initial analysis included a comparison between the ICI and chemotherapy with or without biologics, which was excluded from subsequent analyses focused on the comparison between dual ICIs vs ICI monotherapy.

An adjustment for potential confounders was necessary to identify patients who might derive greater benefit from the anti-CTLA-4 combination over anti-PD-1 monotherapy. A logistic regression model was then constructed, with treatment as the dependent variable (ie, anti-CTLA-4 combination vs anti-PD-1 monotherapy) and covariates including age, sex, primary tumor location, number of metastatic sites, resection of primary tumor, ECOG PS, RAS/BRAF mutational status, and time to metastases. Subsequently, inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW)17 was employed to generate the “propensity score” for each treatment. A Cox proportional hazards model was subsequently constructed for both PFS and OS, using the propensity score as a weighting factor, to evaluate the benefit of the anti-CTLA-4 combination compared to anti-PD-1 monotherapy in a subgroup analysis. An additional analysis was conducted to evaluate the efficacy of the front-line ICI(s), stratified by primary tumor location and RAS/BRAF mutational status.

Objective tumor response was assessed according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) v.1.1 in patients with measurable disease.18 The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the sum of complete response (CR) and partial response (PR); the disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the sum of CR, PR, and stable disease (SD). Differences between the ORR and DCR across ICI regimens were assessed by calculated the odds ratio (OR) employing a logistic regression model. IPTW method was used to derive propensity scores for each treatment group, and the adjusted OR for treatment effect were reported alongside their 95% CIs.

Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were defined as toxicity arising after ICI start and considered to be of immune etiology. irAEs were graded based on National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v.5.0.

P values < .05 were considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R statistical software (R V4.4.1).

Ethics approval

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and local data protection laws. It was approved by the Ethical Committee of participating centers (Fondazione IRCCS Istituto Nazionale dei Tumori, Milano—INT 117/15) and in accordance with each country’s local regulations. All data provided are anonymized in line with applicable laws and regulations.

Patients retrieved from clinical trials were 54 (14.8%), 37 (14.0%) and 37 (44.6%) in the chemotherapy, anti-PD-1 monotherapy and anti-CTLA-4 combination cohorts, respectively. In the anti-PD-1 monotherapy group, pembrolizumab was mostly employed (220, 83.3%) with nivolumab used in a minority of patients (44, 16.7%). In the anti-CTLA-4 combination group, ipilimumab plus nivolumab was the regimen used in all cases.

After a median follow-up (FU) time of 33.3 months (interquartile range, IQR: 18.0; 53.9), patients receiving anti-PD-1 monotherapy experienced superior PFS and OS vs those receiving chemotherapy with or without biological agents (3-year PFS 57.7% vs 12.2%; hazard ratio, HR: 0.30; 95% CI, 0.23-0.37; P < .001; 3-year OS 72.3% vs 27.6%; HR: 0.29; 95% CI 0.22-0.38; P < .001, Figure 1). Compared to the cohort treated with chemotherapy with or without biological agents, patients treated with upfront anti-CTLA-4-based combination showed a further improvement in both PFS and OS (3-year PFS: 73.8% vs 12.2%; HR: 0.14; 95% CI, 0.09-0.22, P < .001; 3-year OS 81.9% vs 27.6%; HR: 0.13; 95% CI, 0.07-0.23, P < .001, Figure 1). When assessing the impact of care setting (clinical trial vs clinical practice) on the efficacy of the 2 different ICI regimen, we found the interaction test to be not statistically significant either for PFS (P = .42) or OS (P = .49).

At the time of data cut-off, 97 (36.7%) patients in the ICI monotherapy group discontinued therapy due to progressive disease (PD), 20 (7.6%) due to toxicity and 43 (16.3%) due to treatment completion (2 years on-treatment). Among patients receiving anti-CTLA-4-based treatment, 19 (22.9%) discontinued therapy due to PD, 7 (8.4%) due to toxicity, and 23 (27.7%) due to treatment completion.

IPTW-adjusted PFS and OS according to the first-line ICI regimen

We next focused on the 2 cohorts treated with first-line ICI(s), i.e., anti-PD-1 monotherapy vs anti-CTLA-4-based combination. As compared to patients treated with an anti-CTLA-4-based combination, patients treated with anti-PD-1 monotherapy were more often females, older than 70 years, with an ECOG PS > 0 and with a BRAF V600E mutation (Table 1).

To avoid potential biases arising from the imbalances across baseline variables in our dataset, we used an inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) model to perform subsequent analyses across the type of ICI regimen. After a median FU time of 29.2 months (IQR: 14.2-46.9), patients receiving upfront anti-CTLA-4 combination showed a significant improvement in PFS (IPTW-adjusted HR for PFS: 0.58, 95% CI, 0.35-0.97, P = .037) and a trend for better OS, albeit not statistically significant (IPTW-adjusted HR for OS: 0.61, 95% CI, 0.33-1.14, P = .12), compared to patients treated with PD-1 blockade alone.

IPTW-adjusted subgroup analyses for PFS and OS across type of first-line ICI strategy

We next performed IPTW-adjusted subgroup analyses for PFS and OS across the type of first-line ICI strategy, to investigate whether the benefit from anti-CTLA-4 addition was uniform or restricted to specific patient subgroups. Results of the IPTW-adjusted subgroup analyses for anti-CTLA-4-based combo vs anti-PD-1 monotherapy are displayed in Figure 2. Briefly, patients across all subgroups, except those with lung metastases, showed a trend towards superior PFS and OS with the anti-CTLA-4-based combination. No statistically significant interaction between the type of ICI regimen and the selected baseline variables were observed for PFS and OS. However, the magnitude of benefit from anti-CTLA-4-based combo was higher in patients with left- vs right-sided tumors in terms of both PFS (HR: 0.15; 95% CI, 0.02-1.27 vs HR: 0.70; 95% CI, 0.37-1.32) and OS (HR: 0.22; 95% CI, 0.03-2.00 vs HR: 0.73; 95% CI, 0.32-1.66). Similarly, the magnitude of benefit from anti-CTLA-4-based combo was higher in patients with BRAF mutation compared to BRAF wild-type population in terms of both PFS (HR: 0.29; 95% CI, 0.09-0.89 vs HR: 0.79; 95% CI, 0.38-1.64) and OS (HR: 0.42; 95% CI, 0.12-1.43 vs HR: 0.72; 95% CI, 0.27-1.93). Conversely, the magnitude of benefit from combinatorial strategy in terms of PFS and OS did not differ according to RAS mutational status (Figure 2 and Table S1).

Tumor response according to the type of first-line ICI(s)

Among patients receiving either anti-PD-1 monotherapy or anti-CTLA-4 combination, there were 207 and 63 patients, respectively, with measurable disease and further considered for evaluation of tumor response according to RECIST1.1 criteria (Table 2). Compared to those treated with anti-PD-1 monotherapy, patients receiving anti-CTLA-4 combination had a statistically significant improvement in objective response rate (ORR) (71.5% vs 55.5%; odds ratio, OR: 1.15; 95% CI, 1.03-1.29; P = .016) and disease control rate (DCR) (93.7% vs 75.8%; OR, 1.18; 95% CI, 1.08-1.28; P < .001). Compared to patients receiving anti-PD-1 monotherapy, those treated with anti-CTLA-4 combination experienced a higher complete response rate (34.9% vs 23.2%), and a lower rate of primary progressive disease (4.8% vs 18.8%).

Efficacy of the type of first-line ICI(s) according to primary tumor location and BRAF mutational status

Considering the well-known association between right sidedness and BRAF mutated status and our results on the higher benefit in left-sided and BRAF mutated subgroups, we further explored the predictive role of primary tumor sidedness and BRAF mutational status on the efficacy of the type of first-line ICI strategy.

Among patients with BRAF V600E mutated tumors (n = 140), anti-CTLA-4 combination therapy demonstrated consistent improvement in both PFS and OS, regardless of primary tumor location (Figure 3A and B). Specifically, anti-CTLA-4 combination led to a statistically significant improvement of PFS in both right-sided subgroup (3-year PFS: 82.5% vs 52.9%; HR: 0.32) and left-sided one (3-year PFS 100% vs 62.5%; HR: not estimable) in BRAF mutated tumors (Figure 3A). Similar improvements from the addition of an anti-CTLA-4 agent were observed in terms of OS (Figure 3B).

Conversely, in the BRAF wild-type population (n = 207), the benefit from the addition of an anti-CTLA-4 agent seemed to be lost in right-sided subgroup and retained only in left-sided one (Figure 3C and D). In details, patients with left-sided primary tumor had an improved PFS with anti-CTLA-4 combination (3-year PFS 92.3% vs 59.1%; HR: 0.16, 95% CI 0.02-1.18). Patients with right-sided BRAF wild-type CRC receiving anti-CTLA-4 combination had comparable PFS to those treated with anti-PD-1 monotherapy (3-year PFS 59.8% vs 62.5%; HR: 0.91, 95% CI 0.48-1.72) (Figure 3C). Similar trends were observed for OS (Figure 3D). Interaction test P-values for PFS and OS were .076 and .222, respectively.

Safety and toxicity of first-line ICI(s)

Data on toxicity were available for 236 (89.4%) and 83 (100%) patients in the anti-PD1 monotherapy group and in the anti-CTLA-4 combination group, respectively. 165 (51.7%) patients did not experience any toxicity at all, while 103 (43.6%) and 51 (61.5%) patients in the anti-PD1 monotherapy and anti-CTLA-4 combination groups, respectively, experienced at least one immune-related adverse event (irAE). There were 28 (11.9%) and 13 (15.7%) grade 3 or 4 irAEs in the anti-PD-1 monotherapy and in the anti-CTLA-4 combination group, respectively. In both treatment groups, most frequent irAEs were skin (14% and 24% in the anti-PD-1 and anti-CTLA-4 groups, respectively), thyroid (11% and 24%), and gastrointestinal (15% and 17%) ones, with the latter being also the most common (3% and 6%) G3/G4 adverse events. No fatal irAE was reported. A detailed summary of immune-related toxicity can be found in Table S2.

Discussion

All major guidelines recommend the upfront use of ICIs in patients with dMMR/MSI-H mCRC.19–21 However, despite the higher efficacy of anti-CTLA-4 based combinations, as demonstrated in the CheckMate-8HW trial, it is unclear whether some patients may achieve similar results after less toxic monotherapies.12 In this retrospective study including a more diverse patient population compared to clinical trials, we showed PFS, OS, ORR, and DCR superiority of dual PD-1/CTLA-4 blockade over both chemotherapy and, more importantly, over anti-PD-1 monotherapy as a first-line strategy for dMMR/MSI-H mCRC.

The CheckMate-8HW has already shown the PFS superiority of ipilimumab-nivolumab vs nivolumab, but these results are available only in the larger subgroup of patients enrolled across all treatment lines (57% in first-line, 19% after one previous line and 24% after at least 2 lines), with an HR of 0.62 for PFS and an ORR of 71%.12 Our study specifically focused on the first-line setting and the results of absolute and relative benefit in terms of tumor response and PFS are similar to those achieved by the CheckMate-8HW, thus, reinforcing the first-line use of ipilimumab and nivolumab in the clinical practice, rather than considering it for pretreated patients. Moreover, the OS results of CheckMate-8HW are still pending, since the event maturation in this setting is expected to be slow in both ICIs arms due to the high overall efficacy. The same scenario has been confirmed by the KEYNOTE-177 study, where the final analysis for OS failed to reach the pre-specified level for statistically significant and for meeting the co-primary endpoint of OS, but a subsequent post-hoc analysis with a median follow-up exceeding 6 years showed a more evident—and clinically meaningful—separation of the OS curves and a statistically significant difference.22 Here, at a median follow-up time of 29.2 months, we showed a trend towards significantly improved OS (P = .12) for patients receiving the anti-CTLA-4 combination compared to those receiving anti-PD-1 monotherapy, potentially suggesting that the robust effect in terms of PFS could translate into a significant OS benefit; however, a longer follow-up is needed.

The CheckMate-8HW study favored ipilimumab-nivolumab across all investigated subgroups, including patients with RAS mutated tumors. These patients had less PFS benefit from pembrolizumab over chemotherapy in the KEYNOTE-177 study,23 probably as a consequence of the inherent limitations of these post-hoc data analyses. We confirmed the lack of KRAS subgroup effect when focusing on the comparison between the 2 ICI strategies in first-line setting. In the previous literature, when using a mixed-treatment line population, we showed that worse cancer-related ECOG PS, malignant ascites, and high inflammatory blood values—as covariates associated with high tumor burden and more advanced metastatic disease—were also associated with worse outcomes to anti-PD-1 monotherapy, which could be rescued by dual PD-1/CTLA-4 inhibition.24–28 However, these results came from cohorts where ∼80% of patients received ICIs in later lines. Also, the presence of liver metastases was associated with reduced efficacy of first-line anti-PD-1 monotherapy in a cohort of patients with dMMR/MSI-H mCRC,29 potentially attributable to an immunosuppressed tumor microenvironment, sparkling the hypothesis that these patients would benefit from a boosted ICI strategy. In our study, we did not found a significant interaction between ECOG PS or specific metastatic sites and the type of ICI strategy. These results suggest that the benefit from the addition of an anti-CTLA4 agent in the first-line setting may be relevant and irrespective of baseline features, but its magnitude may amplify when moving to later lines where the use of a less intense ICI regimen may miss the opportunity of cure due to the higher level of immune-suppression and tumor burden. In this sense, it is also being investigated whether anti-CTLA-4 combination could be used as rescue strategy for patients with progressive disease upon anti-PD-1 monotherapy (ongoing phase II NIPIRESCUE trial, NCT05310643).30

Overall, our results suggest that no clinical factor can be used in the first-line setting to drive treatment deintensification to monotherapy, although these results will need confirmation by the planned analysis of the CheckMate-8HW trial specifically focused on treatment-naïve patients. However, considering that a relevant subgroup of patients may still achieve long-term benefit from less toxic and costly monotherapies, future studies should focus more on biomarkers of hyper-immune-sensitivity rather than clinical prognostic features, to potentially spare more toxic therapy for patients who do not need it. Data from mixed treatment-line population suggest that patients with dMMR/MSI-H mCRC and low TMB may derive similar benefit from anti-CTLA-4 combinations and anti-PD1 monotherapies31; data on TMB in our dataset was limited, preventing any further analyses in the present work. Results from the CheckMate-8HW trial also suggest that the superiority of nivolumab plus ipilimumab is consistent across both groups of patients with PD-L1 TPS ≥1% and <1%, but the benefit appears to be greater in the latter (HR 0.77 vs 0.57).12 It will also be crucial to identify biomarkers predictive of primary resistance to anti-PD-1 with or without anti-CTLA-4 blockade, and eventually develop other therapeutic strategies for these patients. Moreover, investigating the optimal treatment duration32,33 according to the dynamic evaluation of treatment response, and potentially irrespective of the initial regimen, may be a useful area of research from a global health perspective.

Notably, we combined BRAF mutational status and primary tumor location, after the observation of a greater treatment effect from CTLA-4 co-inhibition both in BRAF mutated and left-sided tumors. Given the association of BRAF mutation with right-sidedness, it was a logical step to hypothesize that BRAF wild-type and right-sided tumors may represent a clinical subgroup of patients with less benefit from the use of an anti-CTLA-4 combo, and for whom novel strategies including vaccines or new ICI combos may be of particular interest. However, as these results are extracted from subgroup analyses of small patient subgroups, they should be interpreted with caution until further validation in larger cohorts including CheckMate-8HW. In general, it will be of paramount importance to identify biomarkers and clinical features associated with a lower chance of long-term benefit even from CTLA-4-based combos, since these patients may derive benefit from sequential IO strategies or novel agents such as WRN helicase inhibitors.34–36

Our data on safety and toxicity partially mirror the ones of the CheckMate-8HW trial12: the higher percentages of patients experiencing any kind of irAEs in the clinical trial with respect to our dataset (71% and 81% in the nivolumab and ipilimumab plus nivolumab arms, vs 43.6% and 61.% in the corresponding groups) may be attributable to the real-world nature of our work, with possible underreporting of some minor, transient toxicity. However, the rates of grade 3-4 irAEs (15.7% in the dual ICI group) were more in line with the ones observed for the ipilimumab-nivolumab arm of CheckMate-8HW trial (22%); similarly, most common grade 3 or 4 toxicity was diarrhea/colitis. In general, our data highlight that toxicity from the anti-CTLA-4 combination is slightly higher than the one observed with anti-PD-1 monotherapy but is still manageable.

The strengths of this study include its large, multinational dataset, which may help to inform the first-line setting, where there is still a lack of definitive data. Additionally, the use of IPTW-adjusted analyses minimized potential biases associated with baseline imbalances across treatment groups. However, some limitations must be acknowledged. First, the retrospective, nonrandomized nature of the study, with patients treated in both real-world setting and clinical trials, may introduce inherent selection biases. Second, despite statistical adjustments, residual confounding factors may still exist. Third, despite we gathered information on toxicity across the 2 ICI strategies, given the real world and multicentric nature of our study, we did not possess data on patient-reported outcomes, which are eagerly awaited from the CheckMate-8HW trial. Following this, it would be important to conduct cost-effectiveness analyses to properly dissect the financial impact and burden of different ICI agents and combinations. Lastly, our results regarding the impact of primary tumor location and BRAF status on dual ICI strategy should be considered as hypothesis-generating due to the very limited sample size of patients with these features, requiring validation in other external cohorts or randomized clinical trials; providing mechanistic insights into the reason for these observations was outside the scope of this work.

Conclusion

Our analysis confirms the superior efficacy of first-line ICI therapy over chemotherapy in MSI-H/dMMR mCRC and supports the added benefit of an initial anti-CTLA-4-based combination therapy over anti-PD-1 alone across all subgroups of patients. However, some subgroups with primary resistance or subsequent relapse may require additional treatment strategies to increase the chance of long-term cure. Moreover, the subgroup of patients with BRAF wild-type and right-sidedness should be further investigated, as they may constitute a distinct population regarding the outcomes of first-line anti-CTLA-4-based therapy. Future prospective studies should further elucidate the optimal use, composition and duration of ICI-based strategy in MSI-H/dMMR mCRC to enhance patient outcomes and reduce financial and health toxicity.

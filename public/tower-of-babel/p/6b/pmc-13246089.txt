A cross-sectional study on the prevalence of cardiometabolic diseases and their multilevel socioeconomic impacts in Qianxinan Buyi and Miao Autonomous Prefecture, Southwest China
Authors: Lianyue Wu, Yanjun Long, Xin Zou, Li Ren, Kuaifa Fang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cardiometabolic diseases (CMDs) have become the leading cause of mortality and disease burden in low- and middle-income regions, including ethnic minority areas in southwest China. However, population-based epidemiological data on CMDs and their socioeconomic impacts in karst rocky desertification areas remain scarce. This cross-sectional study aimed to investigate the prevalence, population distribution characteristics, and multilevel socioeconomic impacts of CMDs in Qianxinan Buyi and Miao Autonomous Prefecture, Guizhou Province, and to identify associated risk factors. Using multistage stratified cluster sampling, we enrolled 3012 permanent residents aged ≥18 years from Qianxinan Prefecture between January 2023 and December 2024. Data were collected via face-to-face structured questionnaires, standardized physical examinations, the 36-Item Short Form Health Survey, and household income and expenditure verification. CMDs were defined as having at least one of the following: hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, coronary heart disease, or stroke, all diagnosed according to national clinical guidelines. We calculated crude and age-standardized prevalence of CMDs, compared quality of life and household economic burden between CMD patients and non-affected participants, and performed multivariate regression analyses to identify factors associated with CMD prevalence, catastrophic health expenditure (CHE), and poverty due to illness. The age-standardized prevalence of CMDs in the study population was 26.3%, with hypertension (21.5%) being the most prevalent subtype, followed by diabetes (6.8%), coronary heart disease (3.0%), and stroke (1.3%). Higher age-standardized prevalence was observed in adults aged ≥60 years (42.6%), rural residents (29.8%), farmers (32.6%), and those with primary education or below (31.2%). CMD patients had significantly lower 36-Item Short Form Health Survey scores across all dimensions than non-affected participants, especially in the role-physical and role-emotional domains. Households with CMD patients had a significantly higher risk of CHE (27.3% vs 6.8%, P < .001) and poverty due to illness (15.6% vs 3.2%, P < .001) than non-affected households. Multivariate analyses showed that comorbidity of ≥2 CMDs, low household income, and lack of stable medical insurance were independent risk factors for CHE and poverty due to illness. The prevalence of CMDs in Qianxinan Prefecture is higher than the national average, with significant disparities across age, urban–rural, and health literacy subgroups. CMDs are associated with impaired quality of life and increased risks of catastrophic medical expenditure and poverty due to illness.

1. Introduction

Chronic cardiometabolic diseases (CMDs), encompassing hypertension, diabetes mellitus, coronary heart disease, and stroke, are the leading cause of global mortality and disability. The World Health Organization 2022 Global Status Report on Noncommunicable Diseases estimates that 17.9 million deaths annually are attributable to CMDs, accounting for 32% of all global deaths, with over 80% of this burden occurring in low- and middle-income countries.[1–3] In China, the prevalence of CMDs has risen rapidly in recent decades, with over 330 million people affected, and an age-standardized prevalence of 23.2% nationwide.[4] This growing burden not only impairs individual health and quality of life (QoL) but also drives substantial household economic loss and health inequities, particularly in underdeveloped western regions of China.

Western China’s ethnic minority regions face a disproportionately high CMD burden, driven by a confluence of factors: remote geographic locations, lagging economic development, limited healthcare resources, and unique ethnic dietary and lifestyle patterns.[5–7] Qianxinan Buyi and Miao Autonomous Prefecture, located in the karst rocky desertification region of southwest Guizhou Province, is a representative underdeveloped ethnic minority area in China. The prefecture has a permanent population of 3.545 million, 62.0% of whom are ethnic minorities (Buyi and Miao), 70.5% live in rural areas, and the per capita disposable income is only 82% of the provincial average. Despite the high CMD risk in this region, there is a critical lack of population-based epidemiological data on CMD prevalence, distribution, and socioeconomic impacts. Most existing studies are limited to single-disease surveys, with no systematic analysis of CMD-related QoL impairment, catastrophic health expenditure (CHE), and poverty due to illness. This gap hinders the development of targeted, region-specific CMD prevention and control strategies.[8,9]

Based on this, a large sample and full coverage cross-sectional study was conducted to comprehensively describe the prevalence of CMDs and identify the socioeconomic and behavioral risk factors related to CMDs in Qianxinan Prefecture.

2. Methods

2.1. Study subjects

This study was a cross-sectional study. The study area covered 8 counties (cities) in Qianxinan Prefecture, including Xingyi city (economically developed urban area), Xingren city (county-level city with medium economic level), Anlong County, Zhenfeng County, Pu’an County, Qinglong County, Ceheng County, and Wangmo County (the last 6 counties included rocky desertification areas with low economic level). According to the level of regional economic development (high, medium, and low levels based on gross domestic product per capita) and urban–rural distribution (rural 70%, urban 30%), the sample was double-stratified to ensure that the sample covered different development levels of regions and urban and rural scenarios, and the representation was good.

The study subjects were permanent residents in the district who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria. The included families were required to be complete living units (living together, economic income, and expenditure were unified accounting). A total of 1 to 3 eligible members were selected from each family to participate in the survey.

The participant selection process followed a multistage stratified cluster sampling design, and the detailed inclusion and exclusion flow is presented in Figure 1. A total of 3526 residents from 1200 households were initially invited to participate; 326 residents refused to participate, 188 had incomplete key data, and 3012 residents were finally included in the individual-level analysis. For the household-level analysis, 1200 households were included with no exclusions due to complete economic data.

3. Inclusion and exclusion criteria

3.1. Inclusion criteria

Age ≥18 years old, regardless of gender; have lived continuously in Qianxinan Prefecture for more than 6 months and plan to continue living for more than 1 year; with basic communication skills, informed consent and voluntary cooperation to complete the questionnaire survey, physical examination, scale evaluation, and family economic information verification; at least one core member of the family can clearly provide detailed economic information such as family total income, food expenditure, and medical expenditure in the past 12 months; there was no serious liver and kidney dysfunction, malignant tumor, and other serious diseases that may interfere with CMD diagnosis and affect the QoL assessment.

3.2. Exclusion criteria

Those with severe cognitive impairment, mental illness or language expression disorder, unable to cooperate with the investigation and physical examination; patients in the acute stage of disease, whose vital signs are unstable and unable to complete the physical examination; those who refused to provide family economic information, dropped out of the survey or had missing key data; those with major family changes in the past 3 months, which may affect the assessment of economic status.

4. Sample size estimation

Referring to the previous CMD prevalence data in western rural areas (hypertension ≈ 25%, diabetes ≈ 10%, coronary heart disease ≈ 3%, stroke ≈ 1.5%),[10] the sample size was estimated by taking the most common hypertension as the core index and taking into account the detection requirements of other diseases. Set alpha = 0.05 (bilateral), beta = 0.2 (80% inspection efficiency), allowed error of delta = 5%, using the sample size calculation formula: n = z2 (alpha/2) × P × (1 − P)/delta squared, the Z (alpha/2) = 1.96 (alpha = 0.05) on both sides, P = .25 (hypertension prevalence rate).

The basic sample size was calculated as n = 1.962 × 0.25 × (1 − 0.25)/0.052 ≈ 288 persons. However, considering that this study was a household survey and multidimensional stratification analysis was needed, such as urban/rural area, age, and ethnic group, the basic family sample size was about 107 households based on 2.7 eligible members per household. Combined with the urban and rural population distribution (70% in rural areas, 30% in urban areas) and the dropout rate of 10% in Qianxinan Prefecture, the sample size was expanded to 1200 households, and the total number of respondents was expected to be about 3000, including about 850 cases, which could meet the sample size requirements of multivariate regression analysis (number of independent variables × 10–20 cases) and ensure the statistical test power.

5. Sampling method

A multistage stratified cluster sampling method was used. The specific steps were as follows: in the first stage (county-level stratification), 8 counties (cities) were divided into high (Xingyi city), medium (Xingren city, Anlong County, Zhenfeng County), and low (Pu’an County, Qinglong County, Che Heng County, Wangmu County) according to the level of economic development, and the sample size was distributed according to the proportion of population in each level (high: medium: low = 2:3:5). The second stage (township/street sampling): based on the urban-rural stratification, 3 to 4 townships/streets (22 rural townships and 10 urban streets) were randomly selected according to the economic level in each stratum, and a total of 32 townships/streets were selected to ensure that each sampling unit covered high, medium, and low economic level villages/communities. The third stage (village/community sampling): in the selected towns/streets, 2 to 3 villages/communities were randomly selected according to the population size proportional sampling method, and a total of 86 villages/communities were selected, including 60 rural villages/communities and 26 urban communities. The fourth stage (family sampling): a simple random sampling method was used to select the survey families from the household registration list or grid management list of the selected village/community until the prespecified sample size was reached, and the eligible members of the selected families were surveyed comprehensively to avoid selective inclusion.

6. Data collection tools and methods

6.1. Preparation before the survey

Investigator training: a team of public health physicians, clinicians, and community health service personnel was organized for a 3-day unified training, including the interpretation of the survey scheme, the specification of the questionnaire, the operating standard of the physical examination, the scale evaluation skills, and communication skills. After the training, they could pass the theoretical and practical assessment (100% of the qualified rate) before they could be employed. Instrument calibration: all physical examination instruments were calibrated by the metrological department, and the models and operating specifications were unified to avoid measurement errors. Pre-survey: a pre-survey was conducted in a community of Xingyi city (n = 50) to test the logic and feasibility of the questionnaire. According to the results of the pre-survey, the items of the questionnaire were modified and improved to ensure the reliability and validity of the questionnaire (Cronbach α coefficient = 0.82–0.93).

6.2. Data collection tools

Structured questionnaire: divided into 5 modules, a total of 128 items: demographic and socioeconomic characteristics module: including age, gender, ethnic group, education level, occupation, marital status, family size, family income (divided into 5 levels: <10,000 yuan, 10,000–30,000 yuan, 30,000–50,000 yuan, 50,000–100,000 yuan, ≥100,000 yuan), housing conditions (brick house/building house, adobe house/straw house), medical insurance type (new rural cooperative medical care, urban employee medical insurance, urban resident medical insurance, commercial insurance, no security), and so on; lifestyle modules: smoking, alcohol drinking, dietary pattern (high-salt diet: salt intake ≥ 6 g/d, high-fat diet: fried food ≥ 3 times/wk), physical exercise, sleep quality, and so on; disease-related information module: previous CMD diagnosis (diagnostic institutions, diagnosis time, treatment compliance), symptoms, family history, complications, and so on; family economy and medical expenditure module: total family income in the past 12 months, basic food expenditure, CMD-related medical expenditure (outpatient fee, hospitalization fee, drug fee, inspection fee), nonmedical expenditure, and so on. At the same time, the copies of medical expense reimbursement vouchers were collected for verification. Health literacy module: including CMD cognition, self-management behavior, and other 10 items; the total score was 100 points, with ≥80 points as high health literacy.

Standardized physical examination was performed by clinicians according to the operating standards, and the indicators included: general indicators: height, weight, waist circumference, hip circumference, body mass index, and waist-hip ratio were calculated; cardiovascular-related indicators: systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure; fasting blood glucose (fingertip blood sampling, glucose oxidase method), total cholesterol, triglyceride, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol.

QoL assessment tools: the Chinese version of the 36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) scale was used. The scale contained 8 dimensions (physical function, role physical, bodily pain, general health, vitality, social function, role emotional, mental health), a total of 36 items, of which 5 items were reverse scored. Using the standard scoring method, the original score of each dimension was converted into 0 to 100 points; the total score = (the sum of the scores of 8 dimensions/8); the higher the score, the better the QoL. The scale has been verified to have good reliability and validity in the Chinese population with chronic diseases (Cronbach α = 0.86–0.92),[11] which is suitable for this study population.

6.3. Data collection methods

The investigators completed the questionnaire interview and physical examination at home, and the SF-36 scale was filled out independently by the respondents (those with a lower education level were read to by the investigators one by one and filled out according to the respondents’ answers) to ensure the authenticity of the data. After the survey was completed, the investigators checked the questionnaire on the spot to check the completeness and logical consistency of the items and supplemented and corrected the missing or errors in time. EpiData3.1 software (The EpiData Association) was used to establish a double input and check mechanism to ensure the accuracy of data entry ≥99.5%.

7. Definition and criteria

Diagnosis of CMD: patients who met any of the following criteria were defined as cases: hypertension: diagnosed according to the 2018 Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Hypertension. Defined as prior formal diagnosis in secondary or higher-level hospitals with antihypertensive treatment; or average systolic blood pressure ≥140 mm Hg and/or diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mm Hg from 3 standardized resting measurements.

Blood pressure measurement protocol: 3 measurements at 2-minute intervals using a calibrated electronic sphygmomanometer after 5 minutes of seated rest; the average of the 3 measurements was used for analysis. Type 2 diabetes mellitus: diagnosed according to the 2022 Chinese Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Type 2 Diabetes. Defined as prior formal diagnosis with hypoglycemic treatment, or fasting blood glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L via fingertip capillary sampling; Coronary heart disease: prior formal diagnosis of myocardial infarction/angina confirmed by coronary angiography, or significant ST-T changes on 12-lead electrocardiogram with typical clinical symptoms; stroke: prior formal diagnosis of ischemic/hemorrhagic stroke confirmed by head computed tomography/magnetic resonance imaging.

Comorbidity: patients with 2 or more types of CMD.

Catastrophic medical expenditure: according to the World Health Organization standard, it is defined as the proportion of family medical expenditure in family disposable income (total family income − basic food expenditure) >40%.[12] If the household disposable income is negative, it is directly determined that CHE occurs.

Poverty caused by illness: the family’s annual per capita income was lower than the urban and rural poverty line of Qianxinan prefecture (11,960 yuan/yr in rural areas and 19,200 yuan/yr in urban areas), and the family interview confirmed that the proportion of medical expenditure in the total family expenditure was more than 50%.

Health literacy level: high (≥80 points), medium (60–79 points), and low (<60 points).

Disease severity classification: mild: single CMD subtype, well-controlled with standard medication, no target organ damage, no limitation in daily activities; moderate: single CMD subtype with suboptimal control, or mild target organ damage, mild limitation in daily activities; and severe: comorbidity of ≥2 CMD subtypes, or severe target organ damage, severe activity limitation, or acute cardiometabolic events in the past 12 months.

8. Quality control

Sampling quality control: stratified sampling was used to ensure sample representativeness, and the whole sampling process was recorded to avoid artificial selection of sampling units. Survey quality control: investigators submitted questionnaires every day, and quality control personnel conducted on-site reviews and telephone return visits at a proportion of 10% to verify the accuracy of key information. Quality control of physical examination: instruments and operating standards were unified, blood pressure monitors were calibrated every day before measurement, and abnormal indicators were measured repeatedly. Database logical verification rules were established to identify missing and abnormal values. For missing data (<3.2% of total data points), we used the multiple imputation method with chained equations, generating 5 imputed datasets. All variables included in the final regression models were used in the imputation model, and the results were pooled across the 5 imputed datasets for final analysis. Abnormal values were manually verified against the original survey records and corrected where possible; values that could not be verified were treated as missing and included in the multiple imputation process. Ethical review: this study was approved by the Zhenfeng County People’s Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval No. 2022-018), and all participants provided written informed consent before enrollment. The survey data were strictly confidential and used only for academic research.

9. Statistical methods

SPSS 26.0 (International Business Machines Corporation) and R 4.2.1 statistical software (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing) were used for data analysis. To account for the multistage stratified cluster sampling design, sampling weights were applied to all prevalence estimates and regression analyses. The sampling weight for each participant was calculated as the inverse of the selection probability, adjusted for age, sex, urban–rural, and ethnic population distribution from the 2020 National Population Census for Qianxinan Prefecture. Nonresponse weights were also applied to adjust for differential response rates across subgroups. Measurement data were expressed as a normal distribution (x ± s), and non-normal distribution was expressed as (M [Q1, Q3]). The enumeration data were expressed as a rate (%), and the crude prevalence and age-standardized prevalence of each type of CMD were calculated. An independent-sample t test (normal distribution) or Mann–Whitney U test (non-normal distribution) was used for the comparison of measurement data between groups. The chi-square test or Fisher exact probability method was used for the comparison of count data between groups. Stratified by age, gender, urban or rural, ethnic group, education level, and occupation, the differences in prevalence and QoL scores were analyzed. Multiple linear regression was used to analyze the independent influence of diseases and related factors on the total score of SF-36. Multivariate logistic regression was used to analyze the association of CMD prevalence, number of comorbidities, lifestyle, and other factors with catastrophic medical expenditure and poverty caused by illness. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. For all regression analyses, we performed sequential adjustments: model 1 (unadjusted): crude analysis with only the independent variable of interest. Model 2 (minimally adjusted): adjusted for age (continuous), sex (binary), and geographic region (county-level economic stratification). Model 3 (fully adjusted): further adjusted for education level, occupation, household per capita income, urban–rural location, health literacy, and lifestyle factors (smoking, alcohol consumption, diet, physical activity), in addition to model 2 variables. Age standardization for prevalence was performed using the direct method, with the 2020 Chinese National Population Census as the standard population. All estimates are weighted to account for the multistage stratified cluster sampling design. P < .05 was considered statistically significant.

10. Results

10.1. Basic characteristics of the subjects

A total of 3012 subjects were included in the study, including 1513 males (50.2%) and 1499 females (49.8%). The average age was 52.3 ± 14.6 years (range, 18–89 years), and 32.8% of them were ≥60 years old. A total of 2124 were rural residents (70.5%), and 888 urban residents (29.5%). There were 1868 ethnic minorities (62.0%), including 1000 Buyi (33.2%), 686 Miao (22.8%), and 1144 Han (38.0%). The education level was mainly primary school or below (54.2%), and the occupation rate was highest (58.0%). The main type of medical insurance was the new rural cooperative medical system (63.0%). The average annual household income was 10 to 30,000 yuan (42.6%; Table 1).

10.2. Prevalence of CMD

10.2.1. Overall prevalence and disease composition

A total of 862 CMD patients were diagnosed in this survey. The crude prevalence rate was 28.6%, and the age-standardized prevalence rate was 26.3%. There were 813 patients with hypertension (crude prevalence rate 27.0%, standardized prevalence rate 21.5%), 218 patients with diabetes (crude prevalence rate 7.2%, standardized prevalence rate 6.8%), 96 patients with coronary heart disease (crude prevalence rate 3.2%, standardized prevalence rate 3.0%), and 42 patients with stroke (crude prevalence rate 1.4%, standardized prevalence rate 6.8%). Adjusted prevalence was 1.3%. Among CMD patients, hypertension was the most common condition (84.2%), followed by diabetes (25.3%), coronary heart disease (11.1%), and stroke (4.9%). There were 124 patients (14.4%) with comorbidities, including 78 patients (62.9%) with hypertension and diabetes, 26 patients (21.0%) with hypertension and coronary heart disease, and 20 patients (16.1%) with 3 or more comorbidities (Table 2).

10.4. The impact of chronic CMD on household economy

10.4.1. Catastrophic medical expenditure and incidence of poverty due to illness

CHE occurred in 186 out of 1200 families, with an incidence of 15.5%. A total of 88 families were poor due to illness, with a poverty rate of 7.3%. Among 498 families, the incidence of CHE was 27.3% (136/498), and the rate of poverty caused by illness was 15.6% (78/498). The incidence of CHE was 6.8% (50/702), and the rate of poverty caused by illness was 3.2% (22/702) in the non-affected families (702 families), and the differences between the 2 groups were statistically significant (P < .001).

10.4.2. Influence of different disease characteristics on family economy

The incidence of CHE (42.8%) and the rate of poverty caused by illness (28.6%) in families with multimorbidity were significantly higher than those in families with a single disease (23.1%, 12.5%; P < .001). The incidence of CHE (58.3%) and the rate of poverty caused by illness (41.7%) in the families of severe patients were significantly higher than those of mild and moderate patients (mild: 15.2%, 6.8%; moderate: 32.6%, 18.5%; P < .001). The incidence of CHE (48.6%) and the rate of poverty caused by illness (36.2%) in families without medical insurance were significantly higher than those in families with medical insurance (23.5%, 12.8%; P < .001; Table 7).

10.4.3. Multivariate logistic regression analysis of catastrophic medical expenditure and poverty caused by illness

Taking “catastrophic medical expenditure or not” and “poverty caused by illness” as the dependent variables, and including disease status, number of comorbidities, disease severity, family income per capita, type of medical insurance, and family size as the independent variables, the multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted. The results showed that 2 or more comorbidities (OR = 7.83, 95% CI: 5.21–11.79), family income per capita <30,000 yuan (OR = 5.36, 95% CI: 3.78–7.59), and severe disease (OR = 4.82, 95% CI: 2.65–8.78) were independent risk factors for CHE. Stroke (OR = 8.25, 95% CI: 4.13–16.48), no stable medical insurance (OR = 4.72, 95% CI: 2.89–7.74), and per capita household income <10,000 yuan (OR = 6.38, 95% CI: 4.12–9.87) were independent risk factors for poverty caused by illness (Tables 8 and 9).

11. Discussion

Through a large sample cross-sectional survey, this study systematically revealed the epidemic map, population distribution characteristics, and multilevel socioeconomic impacts of CMDs in the Buyi and Miao Autonomous Prefecture of southwestern Guizhou, which provided key data support for the precise prevention and control of CMD in the poor minority areas in western Guizhou. The standardized prevalence of CMD in Qianxinan Prefecture was 26.3%, which was significantly higher than that in the whole country (23.2%). The regional particularity of CMD prevalence in Qianxinan Prefecture is closely related to the multiple impacts of the rocky desertification ecological environment. The scarcity of cultivated land and low vegetation coverage in this region are associated with a decline in the traditional physical labor intensity of residents.[12,13] However, the dietary structure still maintains the national characteristics of high salt and high fat – although the traditional diet of the Buyi nationality is rich in cultural connotation, long-term intake will lead to excessive sodium load and abnormal lipid metabolism.[14] More noteworthy is that barren soil in rocky desertification areas is linked to an insufficient supply of trace elements. Although local characteristic crops such as prunes are rich in antioxidant components such as superoxide dismutase, the daily intake of residents is limited, which makes it difficult to offset the risk of vascular damage caused by high-salt and high-fat diets.[15] Compared with other minority areas in the southwest Plateau, the increase of metabolic risk factors (hypertension and central obesity) in Qianxinan Prefecture is more significant, which is consistent with the trend of the national CMD risk factors changing from single to compound clustering released by the National Center for Cardiovascular Disease.[4] However, the clustering rate of combined risk factors in Qianxinan (38.6%) was higher than the national average level (32.1%), suggesting that more attention should be paid to the combined effect of risk factors in disease prevention and control.

Comparison of the underlying mechanism of the difference in population distribution with previous studies showed that age was the core driving factor for the prevalence of CMD. In this study, the standardized prevalence of CMD in people aged 60 years and above (42.6%) was significantly higher than that in people aged 45 to 59 years (25.3%) and 18 to 44 years (8.7%). This is consistent with the increasing burden of cardiovascular disease associated with global aging.[16] With aging, vascular endothelial function declines, insulin sensitivity decreases, and chronic inflammation accumulates, making the middle-aged and elderly population a high-risk group for CMD. This mechanism has been verified in a cohort study of the middle-aged and elderly population in China. In this study, the standardized prevalence rate of males (27.5%) was slightly higher than that of females (25.1%). This difference may be related to behavioral factors such as higher smoking and drinking rates and reduced physical activity in males, as well as potential effects of androgens on vascular smooth muscle cell proliferation.[17] Ethnic-specific analysis showed that the standardized prevalence of ethnic minorities (27.1%) was slightly higher than that of Han (25.2%), although the difference was not statistically significant. However, consistent with the conclusion that “the interaction between ethnic diet and genetic background affects the risk of CMD” in studies in minority areas, the interaction between the unique dietary pattern of Buyi nationality and the polymorphism of the methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) gene rs1801133 locus may be an important reason for the relatively high risk of CMD in this ethnic group.[5,18] In terms of urban–rural differences, the standardized prevalence of rural residents (29.8%) was significantly higher than that of urban residents (20.1%), the essence of which was the gap between the accessibility of medical resources and health literacy. The early diagnosis and treatment rate of CMD in rural areas is <30%, which is far lower than that in urban areas, while the proportion of people with low health literacy (62.0%) is significantly higher than the national average level (43.7%).[6,19]

The multidimensional damage of CMD on the QoL of patients shows significant hierarchical characteristics, which are consistent with the core conclusions of previous studies on the QoL of chronic diseases, but the degree of damage is more prominent. In this study, the total score of the SF-36 scale in the case group (51.1 ± 9.5) was significantly lower than that in the control group (77.8 ± 12.3), and the role physical (42.3 ± 18.6) and role emotional (48.6 ± 19.3) dimensions showed the largest differences. The underlying mechanism involves the chain reaction of physiological function damage, psychological stress, and social support network destruction.[20,21] At the physiological level, chronic pain and activity limitation caused by diseases such as hypertension and coronary heart disease are essentially target organ damage caused by vascular diseases, such as left ventricular hypertrophy caused by hypertension and myocardial ischemia caused by coronary heart disease, which directly reduce the daily activity ability of patients.[22,23] Long-term CMD-related distress and anxiety/depression caused by treatment-related economic pressure are associated with further vascular damage through the neuroendocrine system, potentially forming a vicious cycle of “disease-psychological disorder-deterioration of condition.”[24] It is worth noting that in this study, the SF-36 total score of patients with comorbidities (51.2 ± 14.8) was significantly lower than that of patients with a single disease (61.3 ± 15.2), suggesting the additive impairment effect of QoL in the state of comorbidities, and the QoL of stroke patients was the lowest (total score 48.6 ± 16.3). It reflects the great impact of serious vascular events on multidimensional QoL.

The comparison of economic burden analysis with previous studies reveals the double impact of CMD on family and regional development, and its core mechanism is the superposition effect of direct medical expenditure and indirect labor loss.[25] In this study, the incidence of catastrophic medical expenditure in households with CMD (27.3%) was significantly higher than that in households without CMD (6.8%). Among the direct medical burden, hospitalization costs and long-term medication costs accounted for the highest proportion (58.8% and 8.3% of medical expenditure, respectively), which was closely related to the current situation that the delay of CMD patients in rural areas led to an increase in the proportion of severe cases and an increase in treatment costs.[26] In terms of indirect burden, income loss caused by the loss of work of patients and their family members accounted for 31.5% of family economic loss, while the family economic source in rocky desertification areas is single, and the impact of loss of labor ability on family income is more fatal.[27] Multivariate analysis showed that comorbidity ≥2 (OR = 7.83) and per capita annual household income <30,000 yuan (OR = 5.36) were high-risk factors for catastrophic medical expenditure, which further confirmed the key role of medical insurance in alleviating the economic burden of chronic diseases.[28]

This study has several important limitations. First, the cross-sectional design only provides a snapshot of CMD prevalence at a single time point; we cannot establish causal relationships between CMDs and QoL impairment or economic outcomes. Prospective cohort studies are needed to explore causal pathways. Second, self-reported exposure data (diet, physical activity, medical history) may introduce recall bias, despite rigorous quality control. Third, the single-region study design may limit the generalizability of the findings to other ethnic minority regions in China. Fourth, we did not collect detailed data on CMD duration, treatment adherence, and risk factor control, which may modify the association between CMDs and adverse outcomes. Fifth, we did not perform genetic testing to explore gene–environment interactions that may contribute to CMD risk. Finally, residual confounding from unmeasured variables may remain, despite multivariable adjustment.

Based on the above study limitations, large-scale, multicenter prospective cohort studies should be conducted in western China’s ethnic minority regions to validate our findings and establish causal relationships between CMDs and adverse health and economic outcomes. Second, future studies should incorporate detailed clinical data on CMD treatment adherence and risk factor control to identify modifiable factors that reduce disease burden. Third, implementation research is needed to develop culturally appropriate CMD prevention strategies tailored to Buyi and Miao ethnic minority populations. Fourth, studies exploring the genetic and epigenetic mechanisms underlying the high CMD burden in karst rocky desertification regions are warranted. Finally, health economic evaluations of community-based CMD prevention programs are needed to inform policy decisions for health poverty alleviation in underdeveloped western regions.

12. Conclusion

The prevalence of CMD in Qianxinan Prefecture is higher than the national average, with significant disparities across age, urban–rural, and health literacy subgroups. This cross-sectional study demonstrates that household CMD status is independently associated with increased risks of CHE and poverty due to illness, even after adjusting for household income and medical insurance status. These associations appear to be mediated primarily through increased direct medical costs and indirect labor income losses. The epidemiological characteristics of CMD in Qianxinan Prefecture reflect both the general patterns observed in western ethnic minority regions and the unique features of karst rocky desertification areas, with a particularly pronounced economic burden on affected households.

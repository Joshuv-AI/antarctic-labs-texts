Neutrophil membrane-mediated biomimetic nanoparticle for co-delivery of icariin and siORMDL3 attenuates asthma exacerbated by respiratory syncytial virus
Authors: Xudong Zhang, Ting Luan, Chenyu Zhao, Li Chen, Fei Wang, Qi Cheng, Tong Zhu, Wanjie Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAsthma is a chronic inflammatory disorder, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a major trigger of asthma exacerbation. This study aimed to improve therapeutic efficacy against RSV-exacerbated asthma using neutrophil membrane (NM)-coated nanoparticles (NPs) for co-delivery of orosomucoid-like 3 (ORMDL3)-targeting small interfering RNA (siRNA) and icariin.MethodsA biomimetic dual-drug delivery system (ORMDL3-targeting siRNA [siORMDL3]/Icariin NPs@NM) was developed by coating NM onto poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) NPs co-loaded with icariin and siORMDL3. The physicochemical properties, biosafety, cellular uptake, biodistribution behavior, and RNA interference efficiency of the NPs were evaluated. Their therapeutic efficacy was further assessed in vitro and in vivo.ResultsThe siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM exhibited a core-shell structure with NM camouflage, an average diameter of 141.1 nm, and a negatively charged surface. The NPs demonstrated enhanced lung-targeting delivery efficiency with no apparent adverse effects. In vitro, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM significantly improved the viability of BEAS-2B cells treated with interleukin-4 and RSV, while markedly reducing inflammatory cytokine production and oxidative stress markers. In vivo, the siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM effectively alleviated airway resistance and improved the inflammatory microenvironment in mice with RSV-exacerbated asthma, with no observable toxicity in major organs.ConclusionsiORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM demonstrated potent anti-inflammatory and antioxidative effects and may serve as an effective dual-drug delivery platform for the treatment of RSV-exacerbated asthma through combination therapy.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12951-026-04726-2.Keywords: Respiratory syncytial virus-exacerbated asthma, Icariin, ORMDL3 siRNA, Neutrophil membrane, Biomimetic nanoparticle, Targeted therapy

Background

Asthma is a chronic inflammatory airway disorder that affects more than 300 million people worldwide [1–3]. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a major cause of acute asthma attacks and exacerbations [4, 5]. Icariin, the principal bioactive compound isolated from Epimedium brevicornum Maxim, exhibits a broad range of biological and pharmacological activities, including immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and anti-aging effects [6–8]. Previous studies have demonstrated that icariin suppresses asthmatic airway inflammation [9, 10]. However, its therapeutic efficacy is substantially limited by its relatively short half-life, potential toxicity, and the development of drug resistance.

Compared with monotherapy, combination therapy offers greater therapeutic potential through the simultaneous regulation of multiple signaling pathways. In this context, small interfering RNA (siRNA) technology has emerged as a promising strategy for asthma treatment by silencing post-transcriptional gene expression and reducing the expression of disease-related proteins [11]. To date, the United States Food and Drug Administration has approved several siRNA-based delivery systems for treating various diseases [12, 13]. Notably, orosomucoid-like 3 (ORMDL3) has been implicated in the pathogenesis of asthma [14, 15]. Previous studies further demonstrated that histone acetylation regulates ORMDL3-mediated NLRP3 inflammasome overexpression during RSV-allergic asthma exacerbation in mice [16]. Therefore, siRNA targeting ORMDL3 (siORMDL3) may represent a promising therapeutic approach for suppressing RSV-exacerbated asthma. However, naked siRNA carries several limitations, including susceptibility to nuclease degradation, poor cell membrane penetration, short circulation half-life, and non-specific immune stimulation, all of which greatly hinder its clinical application [17]. Drug delivery systems have therefore been developed to enable the controlled release and targeted delivery of therapeutic agents [18]. In particular, the integration of RNA interference technology with nanocarrier-based delivery systems has emerged as an effective strategy for enhancing siRNA delivery in vivo [19].

Poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) is a biodegradable polymer that undergoes gradual degradation in vivo [20, 21]. PLGA-based nanoparticles (NPs) have attracted considerable attention due to their excellent biocompatibility, biodegradability, and sustained-release properties [22, 23]. Previous studies have reported the successful use of PLGA NPs for the delivery of drugs and siRNA in the treatment of various diseases [24, 25]. Nevertheless, unprotected drug-loaded NPs are readily recognized and cleared by the reticuloendothelial system, resulting in rapid elimination from circulation [26]. This limitation may be overcome through the use of cell membrane-camouflaged NPs [27]. Cell membrane coating technology is an emerging biomimetic approach in which NPs are cloaked with highly biocompatible cellular membranes that mimic autologous cells [28]. Recently, neutrophil membranes (NM)-based biomimetic drug delivery systems have attracted increasing interest [29, 30]. Neutrophils can interact with inflammatory cells through adhesion molecules and subsequently migrate to sites of inflammation [31]. Previous studies have demonstrated that NM-modified NPs effectively target inflamed brain microvascular endothelial cells [32]. These phenomena indicate that NM-coated NPs possess intrinsic inflammation-targeting capabilities.

Notably, several studies have shown that the co-delivery of drugs and siRNA within nanocarriers can effectively overcome drug resistance and enhance therapeutic efficacy [33–35]. Therefore, this study aimed to improve the therapeutic efficacy against RSV-exacerbated asthma by using NM-camouflaged NPs for the simultaneous delivery of icariin and siORMDL3 to achieve prolonged circulation and targeted delivery to inflammatory sites. Figure 1 presents a schematic illustration of the synthesis of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM nano-system synthesis and its therapeutic effects against RSV-exacerbated asthma. This study provides valuable insights into the development of synergistic therapeutic strategies for RSV-exacerbated asthma.

Methods

NM extraction

Neutrophils were isolated from the whole blood of the Institute of Cancer Research (ICR) mice as previously described [36]. Twenty male ICR mice (6–7 weeks old, 25–30 g) were maintained under conventional housing conditions. After 1 week of acclimation, blood was collected from the orbital venous plexus, centrifuged, and diluted. Cell pellets were carefully layered onto a three-density Percoll gradient (78%, 69%, and 52% in PBS) and centrifuged. Neutrophils were collected from the 69/78% interface and the upper portion of the 78% layer. High-purity neutrophils were subsequently obtained following removal of residual erythrocytes using a lysis buffer at 4 °C. Neutrophils were then activated with 100 ng/mL lipopolysaccharide and washed with PBS. To isolate the NM, stimulated cells were lysed using an ice-cold separation buffer (75 mM sucrose, 225 mM mannitol, 0.5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid [EDTA], 0.5% bovine serum albumin, and 30 mM Tris-HCl) supplemented with a protease inhibitor cocktail, followed by centrifugation. The resulting supernatant was centrifuged again to obtain high-purity NM. The final precipitate was washed with 10 mM Tris-HCl containing 0.5 mM EDTA and protease inhibitor, then lyophilized and stored at − 80 °C.

siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM preparation

The double emulsion technique [37] was used to prepare icariin-loaded NPs. Briefly, 0.33 mg icariin (B21576; Shanghai Yuanye Biological Reagent Co., Ltd., China) and 10 mg PLGA (P879466; Macklin, China) were dissolved in 850 µL of an organic solvent mixture composed of ethyl acetate and acetone (830:20, v/v). The resulting PLGA solution was then added dropwise to 4 mL of an aqueous phase containing 5% Tween and 1.5% polyvinyl alcohol (PVA). The mixture was sonicated in an ultrasound bath for 5 min using a 50 mV electromagnetic probe. Subsequently, 3 mL of 0.5% PVA solution was added dropwise, and the suspension was stirred magnetically for 3 h to facilitate diffusion of the organic solvent into the aqueous phase, yielding icariin-loaded NPs. The icariin NPs were collected and purified by centrifugation for 20 min, repeated twice. The resulting precipitate was resuspended in distilled water to remove unencapsulated icariin and residual surfactants. Finally, the icariin-loaded NPs were lyophilized and stored at 4 °C.

To prepare siORMDL3/Icariin-loaded NPs, ORMDL3-targeting siRNA was designed using the Designer of Small Interfering RNA website. The sequences were as follows: sense, 5’-GGAUCUUUGGAAUCAAUAAGU-3’; antisense, 5’-UUAUUGAUUCCAAAGAUCCGG-3’. Ten milligrams of lyophilized icariin-loaded NPs were dispersed in 10 mM HEPES buffer (H917699; Macklin, China), followed by the addition of 857 µL polyethyleneimine (PEI) solution (3.5 mg/mL, pH 7.0; P885993, Macklin, China). Next, PEI-modified icariin-loaded NPs and siORMDL3 were mixed at different N/P ratios (1:1, 2:1, 4:1, 6:1, 8:1, and 10:1), where the N/P ratio represents the molar ratio of PEI amine groups to nucleic acid phosphate groups. Complex formation was driven by electrostatic interactions, and the optimal ratio was determined using an agarose gel retardation assay. The final siORMDL3/Icariin-loaded NP complexes were obtained by incubating the components at the optimized N/P ratio.

To prepare siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM complexes, 1 mg NM was suspended in 1 mL of distilled water and sonicated for 30 s. The NM suspension (1 mg/mL) was then mixed with an equal concentration (1 mg/mL) of siORMDL3/Icariin-loaded NP complexes at a membrane-to-core weight ratio of 1:1.

NPs characterization

To characterize the NPs, Fourier Transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy was employed to analyze their chemical structure. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM; HT7800, Hitachi, Japan) was employed to examine NP morphology. Particle size distribution, surface zeta potential, and colloidal stability were measured using a Zetasizer Nano ZS (3 H-2000PH, Malvern Panalytical, UK). UV-visible (UV-Vis) absorption spectra were recorded using a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (U-2910, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The expression of characteristic neutrophil markers (IL-1R, LFA-1, CD11β, TNF-αR, and Ly6G) in NM and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was analyzed by Western blot. Protein concentration in NM was quantified using a BCA assay kit (20200ES76, Yeasen, China). For encapsulation efficiency (EE%) and loading efficiency (LE), standard curves of icariin were established using serial concentrations of 0, 31.25, 62.5, 125, 250, and 500 µg/mL. Briefly, 5 mg of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs was dispersed in water to obtain a 5 mg/mL suspension and centrifuged for 10 min. The supernatant was collected, and the absorbance at 270 nm was measured. The amount of free icariin (Wfree) in the supernatant was determined from the standard curve. EE% and LE% were calculated as follows: EE% = (Wtotal – Wfree)/Wtotal × 100%; LE% = (Wtotal – Wfree)/ Wa × 100%, where Wtotal is the initial amount of drug added, Wfree is the amount of unencapsulated drug, and Wa is the total weight of drug loaded in NPs. For the icariin-release test, standard curves were generated using icaritin solutions at concentrations of 0, 25, 50, 100, 200, and 400 µg/mL. siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM were placed in a dialysis bag (3500 Da molecular weight cutoff) and immersed in 900 mL of PBS at 37 °C. Subsequently, 10 mL dialysate was collected to determine the concentration of icariin, and an equal amount of PBS was supplemented to ensure a constant volume at predetermined time intervals (0, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 h). The released icariin was quantitatively analyzed using UV-Vis spectrophotometry at a wavelength of 270 nm, and the cumulative release amount was calculated. For the siORMDL3 release, 20 µM fluorescein amidite (FAM)-labeled siRNA liquid was gradient diluted with DEPC water to 6.25, 12.5, 25, 50, and 100 nM siRNA standard solutions. The fluorescence intensity of the siRNA solution at an excitation wavelength of 480 nm and emission wavelength of 520 nm was measured using a microplate reader (DR-3518G, Wuxi Hiwell Diatek Instruments Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China). The standard curve of FAM-labeled siRNA was generated by linear regression of siRNA concentration versus fluorescence intensity. The final siRNA concentration in siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was adjusted to 100 nM. The formulation was then placed into a dialysis bag and immersed in 5 mL of PBS at 37 °C. Subsequently, 1 mL dialysate was obtained to detect the siRNA concentration, and an equal amount of PBS was supplemented to ensure a constant volume at predetermined time intervals (0, 6, 12, 18, 24, and 30 h). The concentration of released siRNA was quantitatively analyzed using a microplate reader (DR-3518G, Wuxi Hiwell Diatek Instruments Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China).

RSV suspension preparation

The human laryngeal epidermoid carcinoma cell line Hep-2 (SUNNCELL, China) was maintained in high-glucose Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) under conventional cell culture conditions. RSV type A2 strain (ATCC, VR-1540, Rockville) was propagated in Hep-2 cells. Cytopathic effects, including syncytium formation, were observed after 3–5 days of infection. When more than 70% of Hep-2 cells exhibited cytopathic changes such as cell fusion and morphological deformation, the cultures were subjected to repeated freeze–thaw cycles to release viral particles, and the resulting supernatant was collected as the RSV stock. The viral titer was determined using the Reed–Muench method. The resulting RSV stock had a titer of 1 × 106.5 TCID50/mL, which was used as the working viral inoculum.

Cell culture and treatment

The human normal lung epithelial cell line BEAS-2B (CH1155; NEWGAINBIO, China) was cultured in DMEM/F12 medium under standard cultivation conditions. To determine the optimal infection conditions, BEAS-2B cells were infected with RSV at different multiplicities of infection (MOI = 0.1, 1, 2, 5, 10, and 20) for 24 h. Periodic acid–Schiff (PAS) staining and morphological assessment using an inverted microscope showed that the highest number of purplish-blue–stained cells occurred at an MOI of 1; therefore, this condition was selected as the optimal RSV infection dose for subsequent experiments. To establish an RSV-exacerbated asthmatic cell model, BEAS-2B cells were pretreated with 10 ng/mL interleukin-4 (IL-4) for 12 h [38], followed by infection with RSV (2 × 107) TCID50/mL at an MOI of 1 for 24 h [16].

Gene silencing analysis

To evaluate gene-silencing efficiency, the culture medium was replaced with medium containing either free siORMDL3 or siRNA-loaded NPs at a final concentration of 400 µg/mL, followed by 24 h of incubation after BEAS-2B cells had been treated with IL-4 and RSV. Free siORMDL3 was prepared as previously described [39]. To assess the target specificity of ORMDL3 silencing, BEAS-2B cells were treated with PBS, scramble siRNA, free siORMDL3, 400 µg/mL scramble siRNA/Icariin NPs@NM, or 400 µg/mL siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM. Subsequently, ORMDL3 mRNA and protein expression levels were analyzed by qRT-PCR and Western blot, respectively. To evaluate the stability of ORMDL3 silencing, IL-4/RSV-treated BEAS-2B cells were co-cultured with 400 µg/mL siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM for 48 h. ORMDL3 expression levels were then measured before and after three passages of cultivation.

Biocompatibility

The biocompatibility of blank NPs, blank NPs@NM, and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was evaluated using a Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. Briefly, BEAS-2B cells were treated with different concentrations (100, 200, 400, 600, and 800 µg/mL) of blank NPs, blank NPs@NM, and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM for 24 h. Cell viability was subsequently determined using a CCK-8 kit (C0037, Beyotime, China). Absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader (DR-3518G, Wuxi Hiwell Diatek Instruments Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China).

In vitro cytotoxicity

The cytotoxicity of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was assessed using a CCK-8 assay. BEAS-2B cells stimulated with IL-4 and RSV were treated with different concentrations (100, 200, 400, 600, and 800 µg/mL) of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM and co-incubated for 24 h. Cell viability was measured using a CCK-8 kit (C0037, Beyotime, China), and absorbance at 450 nm was detected using a microplate reader (DR-3518G, Wuxi Hiwell Diatek Instruments Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China).

Intracellular uptake

For the intracellular uptake study, cells were treated with fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-labeled siORMDL3/Icariin NPs and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM using FITC (HY-66019, MedChemExpress [MCE], USA). Uptake was evaluated at 0, 1, 2, 6, and 12 h by flow cytometry and confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM). Briefly, IL-4- and RSV-treated BEAS-2B cells were co-cultured with 400 µg/mL FITC-labeled siORMDL3/Icariin NPs or siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM. Subsequently, 5 µL Annexin V-FITC was added and incubated for 15 min, followed by the addition of 10 µL propidium iodide and further incubation for 15 min. Fluorescence intensity was analyzed using a CytoFLEX S flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, USA). For CLSM observation, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min and stained with DAPI (C1005, Beyotime, China) for 10 min. The cells were then mounted using antifade mounting medium (P0126, Beyotime, China) and imaged using an ULTRAVIEW VOX CLSM system (PerkinElmer, USA). To investigate the uptake mechanism, IL-4- and RSV-treated BEAS-2B cells were pretreated for 24 h with the following endocytosis inhibitors obtained from MCE (Shanghai, China): 10 µg/mL chlorpromazine (clathrin-mediated endocytosis inhibitor; HY-12708), 800 µg/mL colchicine (macropinocytosis inhibitor; HY-16569), 5 µg/mL methyl-β-cyclodextrin (caveolae-mediated endocytosis inhibitor; HY-101461), and 950 µg/mL 2-deoxy-D-glucose (ATP depletion agent; HY-13966). Cellular uptake was subsequently analyzed by flow cytometry.

ELISA

IL-6 (YYea6160), IL-1β (YY-ELISA-2149), and TNF-α (CK-E10028) levels in cells were measured using ELISA kits purchased from R&D Systems (Minneapolis, MN, USA). Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader (DR-3518G, Wuxi Hiwell Diatek Instruments Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China).

ROS, NO, and MDA detection

Intracellular levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS; S0033S, Beyotime, China), nitric oxide (NO; E-BC-K135-M, Elabscience, China), and malondialdehyde (MDA; A003-4-1, Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, China) were determined using the corresponding commercial assay kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions. The absorbance was measured at 530 nm using a microplate reader (DR-3518G, Wuxi Hiwell Diatek Instruments Co., Ltd., Wuhan, China).

Asthma animal model and treatment

Female BALB/c mice (3 weeks old, weighing 9–15 g) were obtained from the Experimental Animal Center of Beijing Huafukang Biological Technology Co., Ltd. The RSV-exacerbated asthma model was established as previously described [40]. Briefly, 200 µL of ovalbumin (OVA) solution (100 µg OVA with 20 µg Al(OH)₃; A5503, Sigma, USA) was administered via intraperitoneal injection on days 1 and 8. From days 15 to 27, 29, 30, 32, and 33, mice were challenged by inhalation of a 5% OVA suspension for 20 min per exposure. On days 28, 31, and 34, mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of pentobarbital sodium (60 mg/kg) and intranasally infected with 50 µL of RSV (1 × 106.5 TCID50/mL). Control mice received normal saline. Plasma levels of alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), and creatinine (CREA) were measured using an automated biochemical analyzer (Indiko, Thermo Scientific). Prothrombin time, activated partial thromboplastin time, and thrombin time were determined using a coagulation analyzer (CX-6000, Mindray). All procedures were approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University (No. KT2022360) and were conducted in accordance with the Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines [41].

Biodistribution and vascular location of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM in vivo

Following the construction of the RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mouse model, DIR-labeled (HY-D1048, MCE, USA) siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM were administered daily via intravenous injection. The final doses of icariin and siRNA were 10 mg/kg and 2 mg/kg, respectively. Mice were euthanized at 3, 6, 12, and 24 h post-injection via cervical dislocation, and major organs, including the heart, liver, spleen, aorta, kidney, and brain, were harvested. Live imaging (IVIS Lumina II, PerkinElmer/Caliper, USA) was performed to assess the biodistribution of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM across different organs. In a separate experiment, Cy5-labeled (HY-D0821, MCE, USA) siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM were administered daily via intravenous injection for 14 consecutive days, followed by euthanasia. Lung tissues were collected, and frozen sections were prepared for immunofluorescence staining to evaluate the colocalization of CD31 and ORMDL3 in pulmonary vasculature.

Lung function and bronchial provocation test

The mice were randomly assigned to eight groups: Control, Model, Model + NPs@NM, Model + Free Icariin, Model + Free Icariin + siORMDL3, Model + Icariin NPs, Model + siORMDL3/Icariin NPs, and Model + siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM. Following the establishment of the RSV-exacerbated asthma model, NPs were administered intravenously once daily for 14 days at final doses of 10 mg/kg icariin and 2 mg/kg siRNA. Body weight was recorded daily for each group. For airway resistance measurements, mice were anesthetized 24 h after RSV-exacerbated asthma model induction using pentobarbital sodium (60 mg/kg, intraperitoneal injection). The trachea was exposed by shaving the neck region, incising the skin, and carefully separating the subcutaneous tissue and muscle layers. A 2-mm tracheal incision was then made, and an 18-G Buxco tracheal intubation tube was inserted to a depth of approximately 5 mm. Proper placement and physiological status were monitored throughout the procedure. Mice were subsequently connected to a ventilator set at a respiratory rate of 140 breaths/min and a tidal volume of 0.25 mL. After stabilization, baseline airway resistance was recorded for 1 min using a pulmonary function testing system (PFT; Buxco, USA). Thereafter, 10 µL of PBS and 10 µL of acetylcholine chloride at increasing concentrations (12.5, 25, 50, and 100 mg/mL; HY-B0282, MCE, China) were sequentially nebulized and administered via inhalation for 1 min per dose. Airway resistance was recorded at 2 min after each administration, and the mean value was calculated as specific airway resistance (Rrs; cmH₂O·s/mL).

Histology

The heart, liver, spleen, lung, kidney, and brain tissues were harvested and fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h. After paraffin embedding, tissues were sectioned at 4–7 μm thickness and subjected to hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining. For immunohistochemistry, NLRP3 expression in lung tissues was evaluated. Paraffin sections were baked at 60 °C for 2 h, followed by deparaffinization and rehydration. The sections were then incubated overnight at 4 °C with a primary antibody against NLRP3 (1:100; DF15549, Affinity, China), followed by incubation with the corresponding secondary antibody, Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L (Alexa Fluor® 594) (1:2000; ab150080, Abcam, USA). Subsequently, 100 µL DAB (P0202, Beyotime, China) was applied, and hematoxylin was used for nuclear counterstaining for 3 min. For Periodic Acid–Schiff (PAS) staining, sections were immersed in peroxyiodate alcohol for 10 min, incubated with Schiff reagent at 37 °C for 30 min, and counterstained with hematoxylin for 30 s. Images were captured randomly using an Olympus microscope (DP27, Tokyo, Japan).

Hemolysis test

To evaluate the stability and hemocompatibility of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, red blood cells (RBCs) were collected from BALB/c nude mice. The obtained RBCs were diluted 50 times using PBS. Then 500 µL RBC suspensions were incubated with either 500 µL PBS or H2O. For hemolysis assessment, RBC suspensions were incubated with siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM at final concentrations of 1.25, 2.5, 5, 10, 20 nM for 2 h at 37℃. Following incubation, samples were centrifuged for 5 min, and the supernatant was collected. Absorbance of the supernatant was measured at 540 nm, and the hemolysis rate was calculated accordingly.

qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells and tissues using TRIzol reagent (15596018, Invitrogen, USA). One microgram of RNA was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA. qRT-PCR was performed using SYBR Green Power Master Mix (A4004M, Lifeint, China). The primer sequences were as follows: ORMDL3 forward, 5′-CATCGGTCTCCTCCACATCG-3′ and reverse, 5′-CACGATGGGTGTGATGGTCA-3′; GAPDH forward, 5′-GAAGGTCGGAGTCAACGGAT-3′ and reverse, 5′-CTTCCCGTTCTCAGCCATGT-3′. GAPDH served as the internal reference gene.

Western blot assay

Cells and tissues were lysed using RIPA buffer (P0013B, Beyotime, China), and protein concentrations were determined using a BCA protein assay kit (PC0020, Solarbio, China). Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes (FFP24, Beyotime, China). The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against ORMDL3 (1:1000; DF14231), ASC (1:1000; DF6304), Caspase-1 (1:1000; AF5418), IL-1β (1:1000; AF5103), LFA-1 (1:1000; DF6896), IL-1R (1:1000; DF7657), TNF-αR (1:1000; AF0282), CD11β (1:1000; DF6475), and GAPDH (1:3000; AF7021) from Affinity Biosciences (Jiangsu, China), as well as Ly6G (1:1000; 87048) from Cell Signaling Technology (CST, Boston, USA). After incubation with the appropriate secondary antibody (Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L, Alexa Fluor® 594; 1:2000; ab150080, Abcam, USA), protein bands were visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) reagents (P1000, APPLYGEN, China) and analyzed accordingly.

Immunofluorescence

Tissues were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution (P0099-100 mL, Beyotime, China). Immunofluorescence staining was performed using primary antibodies against ORMDL3 (1:100; DF14231, Affinity, China) and CD31 (1:200; MA1-26196, Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Sections were incubated with the primary antibodies overnight. Subsequently, slides were incubated for 1 h at 37 °C with secondary antibodies, including Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG H&L/Alexa Fluor 488 (1:200; bs-0295G-AF488) and Goat Anti-Mouse IgG H&L/Alexa Fluor 488 (1:200; bs-0296G-AF488) (Bioss, Beijing, China). Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (C1005, Beyotime, China; 200 µL), followed by signal quenching using AutoFluo Quencher (P0126, Beyotime, China). Immunofluorescence images were acquired using a fluorescence microscope (BX53, Olympus, Japan).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0. Intergroup differences were evaluated by one-way analysis of variance. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Characterization of NPs

To prepare the siORMDL3/Icariin-loaded NPs, the optimal loading capacity of the NPs for siORMDL3 was first determined. A gel retardation assay demonstrated effective siORMDL3 complexation at an N/P ratio of 6:1 (Fig. 2A). Accordingly, this ratio was used for the subsequent preparation of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM. As shown in the FTIR spectrum (Fig. 2B), characteristic absorption bands of icariin were detected in icariin alone, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs, and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM. These included peaks at 3379 cm− 1, 2971 cm− 1, and 2931 cm− 1. In particular, the bands at 3379 cm− 1 and 1654 cm− 1 corresponded to Ph–OH stretching and C = C stretching vibrations, respectively. Notably, a distinct peak at 1257 cm− 1 was observed in siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, which was attributed to the antisymmetric stretching of PO₄⁻ membrane phospholipids. In addition, the characteristic absorbance peak of siORMDL3 at approximately 260 nm was detected both in siORMDL3 and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM (Fig. 2C), indicating that the siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM were successfully prepared. TEM images revealed that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs exhibited a spherical morphology, whereas siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM displayed a well-defined core–shell structure with particle sizes below 150 nm (Fig. 2D). The average diameters of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs were 114.8 nm and 141.1 nm, respectively (Fig. 2E), which were in agreement with the TEM observations. Due to the NM coating, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM exhibited a reduced surface zeta potential compared with uncoated NPs (Fig. 2F), suggesting the formation of a biomimetic surface charge. In addition, the long-term stability of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was assessed to further characterize the final formulation in PBS and 10% FBS-containing medium over 156 h. No significant changes in particle size or polydispersity index (PDI) were observed (Fig. 2G), demonstrating good colloidal stability. Western blot analysis was then performed to detect neutrophil membrane proteins, including LFA-1, IL-1R, CD11β, TNF-αR, and Ly6G, which are recognized as key markers for preserving biomimetic functionality [42]. As illustrated in Fig. 2H, these proteins were present in both NM and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, confirming that membrane proteins were well retained during the extrusion process. Meanwhile, the concentration of membrane coating was quantified using the BCA assay (Supplementary Fig. 1) and was determined to be 443.7 ± 15.03 µg/mL. Furthermore, both icariin and siORMDL3 were efficiently encapsulated in the siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM formulation. The LE and EE of icariin in siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM were 40.6% and 81.2%, respectively. In vitro release studies showed that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was detected, and compared with siORMDL3/Icariin NPs, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM exhibited a slightly slower release of both icariin and siORMDL3 compared with uncoated NPs due to the NM barrier. However, approximately 70% of icariin and 50% of siORMDL3 were still released over the study period, indicating that the membrane coating did not significantly impair drug release (Fig. 2I). Figure 2J and K demonstrate that the constructed siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM exhibited strong targeting specificity toward ORMDL3. Collectively, these results confirm that the NM-coated biomimetic NP siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM were successfully formatted.

Biocompatibility and cellular uptake of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM

Given the safety requirements for biological applications, the biocompatibility of blank NPs, blank NPs@NM, and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was evaluated. As shown in Fig. 3A and B, BEAS-2B cells maintained high viability after incubation with various concentrations of blank NPs, blank NPs@NM, and siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, indicating that the drug delivery system exhibited good cytocompatibility. In addition, the optimal in vitro treatment concentration of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was determined. CCK-8 assay results demonstrated that cell viability in IL-4- and RSV-treated BEAS-2B cells increased significantly with increasing concentrations of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM (all P < 0.01; Fig. 3C). As shown in Fig. 3D, the hemolysis rate in the H2O group approached 100%, whereas no apparent hemolysis was observed at any concentration of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, confirming its favorable hemocompatibility. Notably, the cell viability at a concentration of 400 µg/mL was closest to the half-maximal effective concentration (EC50); therefore, 400 µg/mL was selected for subsequent experiments. Furthermore, the cellular uptake of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was evaluated by flow cytometry and CLSM. As illustrated in Fig. 3E, the cellular internalization of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM during the 12 h incubation period was significantly higher than that of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs at each time point and exhibited a time-dependent uptake pattern. Consistent with these findings, the flow cytometry data were corroborated by the CLSM results (Fig. 3F). To further investigate the mechanism underlying cellular uptake of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, various endocytosis inhibitors were employed. Compared with the model group, cellular uptake was significantly reduced following treatment with chlorpromazine, colchicine, methyl-β-cyclodextrin, or 2-deoxy-D-glucose (Fig. 3G). These findings suggest that multiple uptake pathways are involved in the cellular internalization process, including clathrin-mediated endocytosis, micropinocytosis, and caveolae-mediated endocytosis, etc.

siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM promotes siRNA delivery efficiency

To further evaluate the RNAi activity of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, ORMDL3 silencing efficiency was assessed by qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses. As shown in Fig. 4A and B, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM exhibited a more effective gene-silencing effect in vitro than the other NP formulations. Similar results were observed in vivo (Fig. 4C and D). In addition, the stability of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was investigated. The results showed that ORMDL3 mRNA expression was significantly reduced in both the pre- and post-subculture groups compared with that in the model group (P < 0.01), whereas no significant difference was observed between the pre- and post-subculture groups (P > 0.05; Fig. 4E). These findings indicate that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM enhances siRNA delivery efficiency in IL-4- and RSV-treated BEAS-2B cells as well as in RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice. Furthermore, immunofluorescence staining was performed to evaluate the colocalization of CD31 and Cy5-labeled siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM in lung vascular tissue. Pronounced colocalization of CD31 (green), ORMDL3 (yellow), and Cy5 (red) was observed in the lung vascular endothelium of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM-treated RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice (Fig. 4F). Similarly, immunofluorescence staining of lung tissue demonstrated that treatment with siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM more effectively reduced ORMDL3 and CD31 expression in RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice (all P < 0.05; Fig. 4G), suggesting enhanced ORMDL3-targeted delivery to the pulmonary vasculature.

Efficacy of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM in vitro

To evaluate the efficacy of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM in vitro, BEAS-2B cells stimulated with IL-4 and RSV were incubated with free icariin or various NPs for 48 h. The CCK-8 assay showed that IL-4 and RSV co-treatment significantly reduced BEAS-2B cell viability (P < 0.01; Fig. 5A), whereas free icariin and all NP formulations reversed this effect to varying degrees. Among these treatments, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM produced the greatest improvement in cell viability and the most pronounced reduction in apoptosis (Fig. 5B). Furthermore, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM significantly decreased the protein expression levels of the inflammation-related markers ASC, Caspase-1, and IL-1β [16] in IL-4- and RSV-treated BEAS-2B cells (all P < 0.05; Fig. 5C). This formulation also reduced the levels of the inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6, the airway inflammation marker NO [43], and the oxidative stress indicators ROS and MDA [44] (Fig. 5D and E). Collectively, these findings indicate that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM effectively alleviates inflammation and oxidative stress responses in bronchial epithelial cells under RSV-exacerbated asthmatic conditions.

Biodistribution and biosafety evaluation of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM in vivo

To investigate the in vivo biodistribution of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, live imaging of multiple organs was performed. In RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was primarily distributed in the lungs, kidneys, aorta, and liver at 3 and 6 h after intravenous administration, whereas localization was detected only in the lungs at 12 h. Moreover, pulmonary accumulation of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM at 3 h was significantly greater than that observed at 6 and 12 h (all P < 0.01; Fig. 6A). These findings indicate that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM exhibit favorable targeting capability and biodegradability, supporting its potential therapeutic value for RSV-exacerbated asthma. In addition, the in vivo biosafety of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was evaluated. H&E staining showed that the tissue integrity, overall morphology, and levels of immune cell infiltration in the brain, heart, liver, spleen, and kidney were comparable to those in the control group (Fig. 6B). Furthermore, plasma levels of ALT, AST, BUN, and CREA remained within the normal ranges (Fig. 6C). Similarly, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM had no significant effects on coagulation-related parameters, including prothrombin time, activated partial thromboplastin time, and thrombin time (Fig. 6D), indicating that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM possesses favorable biosafety in vivo.

siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM ameliorated RSV exacerbated asthmatic mice

The in vivo efficacy of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was further evaluated. As shown in Fig. 7A, RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice exhibited significantly reduced body weight compared with control mice (P < 0.01). Treatment with icariin and NP formulations reversed this decline. Notably, body weight in the siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM group was comparable to that of the control group. Lung function assessment and bronchial provocation testing further demonstrated that icariin and NPs treatment significantly reduced Rrs in RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice (P < 0.01), with siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM showing the most pronounced effect (Fig. 7B and C). Consistently, immunohistochemical analysis of NLRP3 expression in lung tissues showed a pattern similar to that observed for Rrs (Fig. 7D). PAS staining revealed no asthma-related inflammatory cell infiltration or pathological mucus alterations in the control group. In contrast, the model group exhibited marked goblet cell hyperplasia, glycogen accumulation along the airway epithelium, and extensive mucus deposition in the trachea. These pathological changes were substantially alleviated following treatment with siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM (Fig. 7E), consistent with the H&E staining results (Fig. 6B). Collectively, these findings indicate that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM effectively ameliorate RSV-exacerbated asthma in vivo.

Discussion

Asthma is a common respiratory disease, and RSV is considered a common cause of asthma [45, 46]. Recently, current asthma treatments have been unsatisfactory [47]. Thus, this study was conducted to enhance the curative effect against RSV-exacerbated asthma using NM-coated NPs for simultaneous delivery of icariin and siRNA targeting ORMDL3. PLGA, a biodegradable functional organic polymer, is biocompatible, non-toxic, has good encapsulation and film-forming properties, and is widely used in medicine, pharmaceutical engineering materials, and modern industry [48, 49]. However, PLGA often shows low EE when used as a gene delivery vector [50]. Patil et al. added a small amount of cationic polymer PEI to the polymer body, which significantly increased the EE of siRNA and improved its release rate [51]. Accordingly, in this study, a small amount of PEI was added during the preparation of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM. siRNA is a small double-stranded RNA, with a nucleotide backbone containing many phosphate groups, which dissociate at physiological pH conditions and release positively charged hydrogen ions, rendering the entire siRNA molecule highly negatively charged [52]. PEI is a typical cationic polymer containing many amino groups, which undergo protonation at physiological pH and under other common conditions, thereby carrying positive charges [53, 54]. PLGA is a negatively charged polymer, but it only serves as the core skeleton of the NPs. As a surface coating, PEI is highly positively charged; this property can neutralize the negative charge of PLGA and dominate the overall charge of PLGA-Icariin-PEI NPs [37]. PLGA-Icariin-PEI NPs coated with PEI are positively charged as a whole, while siORMDL3 is negatively charged, enabling the formation of NPs through electrostatic interactions. In addition, achieving a sustained release effect should be considered when preparing an NP drug delivery system. The drug delivery system needs to maintain a stable blood drug concentration in the body by controlling the drug release rate, which can prolong the duration of drug action at the target site, thereby improving drug efficacy and reducing side effects [55]. In this study, compared with siORMDL3/Icariin NPs, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM exhibited a slower release rate of siORMDL3 and icariin under the obstruction of NM to avoid significant loss caused by excessive release rate, and could not achieve a satisfactory therapeutic effect. Besides, NPs with a particle size of about 100–200 nm have excellent “long cycling” ability [56]. Reportedly, the particle size of NPs is related to their ability to deliver a drug to the target site [57]. In this study, the average particle size of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was 141.1 nm with a negatively charged surface, and these NPs were not readily cleared by the kidneys. In addition, the typical neutrophil markers (LFA-1, IL-1R, CD11β, TNF-αR, and Ly6G) were detected in siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, indicating that the NM proteins were well preserved by the mechanical extrusion procedure [58]. These data suggested that the siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM were successfully synthesized with intact surface-coated NM and no protein degradation.

The target ability and in vivo lifespan of NPs are important factors to consider in their clinical applications [59]. In RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM mainly existed in the lung, kidney, aorta, and liver at 3 h and 6 h, and only in the lungs at 12 h and 24 h after intravenous administration of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM, and the fluorescence signal of the lung was stronger than that of other organs. Furthermore, the cellular uptake of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM during a 12-h incubation period was obviously higher than that of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs at each time, suggesting that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM could serve as a vehicle for delivering siORMDL3 and icariin to the targeted position and enhancing their cellular uptake. In addition, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM enhanced siRNA delivery efficiency in IL-4 combined with RSV-treated BEAS-2B cells and RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice targeting the lung vasculature. These data suggested that self-assembled NPs@NM is an effective carrier system that simultaneously delivers siORMDL3 and icariin, targeting the lung vasculature in RSV-exacerbated asthma, enabling effective combination therapy with favorable delivery efficiency, target ability, and biodegradation.

Next, the efficacy of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM was evaluated. The results showed that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM significantly improved the cell viability of IL-4 combined with RSV-treated-BEAS-2B cells, and clearly reduced the apoptosis, indicating that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM treatment could enhance the proliferation of IL-4 combined with RSV-treated-BEAS-2B cells. In addition, both lung function and bronchial provocation tests revealed that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM most significantly reduced the Rrs in RSV-exacerbated asthmatic mice compared to icariin and other NPs treatment. Thus, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM showed a satisfactory treatment effect for RSV-exacerbated asthma. Asthma is a common chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways [60]. During the disease progression, excessive oxidative stress and inflammatory response can cause severe damage to lung cells [61, 62]. Increased oxidative stress can reduce antioxidant defense capabilities, affect autophagy processes, and regulate cell survival mechanisms, promoting lung inflammation [63, 64]. In addition, the NLRP3 inflammasome typically consists of NLRP3, ASC, and pro-caspase-1 [65]. Numerous studies revealed that NLRP3 inflammasome activation and NO are critically involved in the pathogenesis of asthma [66, 67]. In this study, compared with free icariin and other NPs, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM most clearly reduced the levels of NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1, IL-1β, IL-6, NO, and oxidative stress markers ROS and MDA. These results revealed that siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM have anti-inflammatory and antioxidative stress capacities in RSV-exacerbated asthma.

The siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM prepared in this study have excellent anti-inflammatory and antioxidative stress properties in treating RSV-aggravated asthma; however, some limitations should be considered. First, the effect of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM on RSV-exacerbated asthma should be further investigated at the clinical level. In addition, the molecular mechanism of siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM involved in treating RSV-exacerbated asthma needs to be explored. Lastly, multifunctional NPs should be designed to better reduce side effects and overcome drug resistance, laying the foundation for further clinical applications of RSV-exacerbated asthma.

Conclusions

The novel siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM nanocomplex was successfully synthesized with intact surface-coated NM. The prepared siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM could simultaneously deliver siORMDL3 and icariin, targeting the lung vasculature in RSV-exacerbated asthma, enabling effective combination therapy with favorable delivery efficiency, target ability, and biodegradation. In addition, siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM showed excellent anti-inflammatory and antioxidative stress effects in RSV-exacerbated asthma. The siORMDL3/Icariin NPs@NM delivery system offers fresh perspectives for future clinical RSV-exacerbated asthma therapy.

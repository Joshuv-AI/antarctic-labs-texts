Emerging mechanisms and translational advances in musculoskeletal diseases
Authors: Tingting Xia, Bin Li
Published: 2026

Musculoskeletal disorders are becoming increasingly prevalent with population ageing. Among them, osteoarthritis (OA), osteoporosis, and intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) impose a substantial global health burden. This issue highlights recent advances spanning disease mechanisms, therapeutic innovation, biomaterials, and clinical translation across musculoskeletal disorders.

Collectively, the osteoarthritis studies in this issue highlight two emerging themes: a broader understanding of OA as a systemic disease and the development of disease-modifying therapeutic strategies. Hu et al. defined OA as a systemic disease driven by organ crosstalk within the geriatric comorbidity network. Interactions among bone, adipose, vascular, and metabolic systems converge on shared pathogenic pathways, including inflammation, metabolic dysregulation, and biomechanical imbalance, supporting integrated therapeutic strategies that address both OA and its comorbidities [1]. Shi et al. showed that intra-articularly injected dermal fibroblasts engrafted in the synovium and attenuated OA by restoring synovial fibroblast homeostasis through apolipoprotein D secretion [2]. Lin et al. found that semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist used in diabetes, protected against OA independently of weight loss by activating chondrocyte autophagy through inhibition of the AKT/mTOR pathway, highlighting its therapeutic potential in OA [3]. A letter to the editor further discussed clinically actionable pain phenotyping in osteoarthritis based on cartilage–sensory nerve crosstalk [4]. In rheumatoid arthritis, Liu et al. showed that the carboxyl terminus of HSC70-interacting protein (CHIP) limited disease progression by interacting with TRAF6, suppressing the proliferation, migration, and pro-inflammatory phenotype of fibroblast-like synoviocytes, while adeno-associated virus-mediated CHIP delivery reduced disease severity in a collagen-induced arthritis model [5].

Bone metabolism is another major focus of this issue, spanning mechanobiology, metabolic regulation, therapeutic target discovery, and clinical evaluation, particularly in osteoporosis. Wang et al. showed that specifically blocking the interaction between sclerostin loop 3 and osteoblastic LRP4 counteracted bone loss induced by mechanical unloading without increasing arterial stiffness, whereas Sost knockout or romosozumab treatment increased arterial stiffness, suggesting improved cardiovascular safety [6]. He et al. found that elevated lactate, induced by exercise or sodium lactate supplementation, suppressed osteoclast differentiation through AARS1- and SIRT6-mediated histone H3K18 lactylation and alleviated bone loss in ovariectomized mice [7]. Ti et al. identified GFRα2, upregulated in bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells during traumatic brain injury-accelerated fracture healing, as a promoter of both osteogenesis and angiogenesis; its overexpression also improved bone mass in ovariectomized mice [8]. In a phase 3, multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, Zhang et al. demonstrated that romosozumab significantly increased bone mineral density at the lumbar spine, total hip, and femoral neck in Chinese postmenopausal women with osteoporosis, with an acceptable safety profile [9]. Two reviews provided broader perspectives on skeletal metabolism: Zong et al. summarised the role of lactate as a regulatory hub of skeletal homeostasis and its involvement in osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, and disc degeneration [10], while Wu et al. discussed how mesenchymal stem cell-mediated mitochondrial transfer regulates the metabolism, differentiation, and immune function of bone and joint tissue cells [11].

Biomaterials, implant design, and engineering technologies continue to drive innovation in musculoskeletal regeneration. Lian et al. developed an enzymatically cross-linked, nanosilicate-reinforced hydrogel that gelled within 1 min, adhered firmly under water, supported dual-lineage differentiation of synovial-derived stem cells, and promoted osteochondral regeneration in a rat model [12]. Kopp et al. showed that plasma electrolytic oxidation surface modification enabled controlled degradation and improved bone integration of WE43 magnesium alloy implants in an 18-month minipig study [13]. Sergio et al. reviewed emerging strategies to enhance skin–implant integration in transcutaneous prostheses, highlighting bioactive interfaces that improve tissue integration, infection resistance, and long-term implant stability [14]. Two studies further demonstrated the value of biomechanical modelling for implant and tissue design. Adouni et al. showed that changes in collagen VI, but not collagen IX, markedly altered cartilage mechanics while both influenced the chondrocyte microenvironment [15]. Yao et al. combined computational fluid dynamics with a large-animal study to optimise the pore geometry of a bionic spiral blade implant, identifying small-diameter circular pores as favourable for fluid shear stress distribution and bone ingrowth [16]. Wang et al. outlined how electrical stimulation, by mimicking the endogenous bioelectric and piezoelectric properties of bone, promotes osteogenesis, angiogenesis, and inflammation resolution [17]. Menssen et al. discussed strategies for scalable, automated, and GMP-compliant cartilage tissue engineering [18], while Al-Smadi et al. highlighted recent advances in sensor-based monitoring of fracture healing [19]. Zhong et al. explored the contribution of circadian rhythm disruption to musculoskeletal disorders [20].

As a major cause of low back pain, IVDD has increasingly been linked to ferroptosis, a regulated form of cell death driven by lipid peroxidation and iron overload. Tan et al. summarised recent advances in anti-ferroptotic biomaterials for IVDD treatment [21]. Sun et al. incorporated SSK1 into a decellularized annulus fibrosus matrix hydrogel and demonstrated that it alleviated ECM degradation, ferroptosis, and senescence in nucleus pulposus cells by restoring PPARγ expression and suppressing NF-κB activation, while preserving disc height and structure in a rat model [22].

Regeneration of soft tissues and their interfaces with bone and nerve remains challenging. Du et al. developed a self-assembled, meniscus-specific extracellular matrix membrane that enhanced cell migration and vascular ingrowth and, when combined with a three-dimensional printed scaffold, improved tissue integration and cartilage protection [23]. Dong et al. demonstrated that hypoxia-preconditioned mitochondrial transplantation enhanced the mitochondrial membrane potential of Schwann cells and promoted their proliferation, migration, and dedifferentiation through MAPK activation, leading to improved axonal regeneration and motor recovery in a rat model of peripheral nerve injury [24]. Heterotopic ossification was another focus of this issue. Zhu et al. summarised current understanding of tendon stem/progenitor cells in heterotopic ossification and discussed how trauma and inflammation redirect their differentiation from tendon repair towards chondrogenic and osteogenic fates [25], while Yuan et al. proposed neurogenic heterotopic ossification as a systemic neuro-immune-osteogenic response to central nervous system injury rather than a purely local process [26].

Clinical and perioperative studies further strengthened the translational scope of this issue. In a Bayesian network meta-analysis of 79 randomised controlled trials involving 16,012 patients, Cai et al. identified that dexmedetomidine had the most consistent evidence for preventing postoperative delirium in elderly patients undergoing orthopaedic surgery [27]. Zhang et al. found that standardised crutch-assisted walking for three months reduced pain and improved hip function compared with a control group, supporting biomechanical unloading as an effective conservative strategy for early-stage osteonecrosis [28]. Decision-making between unicompartmental knee arthroplasty and high tibial osteotomy was further discussed in a letter to the editor [29].

Together, the contributions in this issue showcase the continued progress of translational musculoskeletal research, spanning mechanistic discovery, therapeutic innovation, and clinical application. We hope these advances will inspire further research and innovation in orthopaedic translation.

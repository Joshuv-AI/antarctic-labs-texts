Dear Editor,

We sincerely thank the authors for the valuable comments and constructive feedback on our manuscript titled “Acute Kidney Injury in Chronic Liver Disease: A Prospective Observational Study”. We have carefully addressed all the points raised to improve the clarity and scientific accuracy of our work.

We acknowledge the observation regarding the inclusion criteria and confirm that the International Club of Ascites (ICA–AKI) criteria, and not the KDIGO criteria, were used for the diagnosis of acute kidney injury in patients with chronic liver disease. The discrepancy in the abstract was inadvertent, and we request that this be corrected in the manuscript.

Regarding the exclusion of post-renal causes, ultrasonography of the kidneys, ureters, and bladder (KUB) was performed in all patients to rule out nephrolithiasis, urolithiasis, or any form of genitourinary tract obstruction; none were detected in our cohort.

In response to the reviewer's concern about the reliance on fractional excretion of sodium (FENa), we would like to clarify that as this was an Emergency Department–based study, FENa was used to differentiate pre-renal and hepatorenal syndrome (HRS) causes from intrinsic renal injury. Fractional excretion of urea (FEUrea) was not available at our institution; however, urine routine and microscopy were performed for all patients to assess urinary sediment, proteinuria, and hematuria, aiding in differentiating pre-renal from intrinsic causes.

The reviewer's query regarding the mean SOFA score being above 10 is well taken. Approximately 20 patients were in shock and required vasopressor support, while in the remaining 29 patients, the elevated SOFA score was primarily due to thrombocytopenia, hyperbilirubinemia, and mechanical ventilation. We ensured strict adherence to the HRS–AKI diagnostic criteria while classifying patients.

We are deeply grateful to the authors for their insightful feedback.

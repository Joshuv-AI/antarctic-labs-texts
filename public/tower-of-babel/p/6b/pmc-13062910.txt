Micropsalliota subumbonata (Agaricaceae), a new species from Maharashtra, India
Authors: Prashant B. Patil, Sharda Vaidya, Vijay K. Hile, Nitinkumar P. Patil
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
A new species, Micropsalliota subumbonata, is described and illustrated from tropical region of the Western Ghats of Maharashtra, India, and the phylogenetic placement is determined by using nrITS and nrLSU sequence data analyses. Micropsalliota subumbonata, is morphologically circumscribed by its campanulate to conical umbonate pileus, covered with greyish brown to reddish brown squamules, ellipsoid to subcymbiform basidiospores, and cylindrical, long narrow, capitate cheilocystidia with a ventricose base.

The Austrian mycologist, F. von Höhnel introduced the genus Micropsalliota in 1914 to accommodate the tiny species, M. pseudovolvulata Höhn., collected from Java, Indonesia. Later on, the generic concept was propounded by Heinemann (1956, 1976), Pegler & Rayner (1969). Zhao et al. (2010) presented the first molecular phylogenetic analysis of the genus based on nrITS and nrLSU sequences. The polyphyletic group of Leucoagaricus Locq. ex Singer, and Leucocoprinus Pat. taxa formed a monophyletic clade together with Micropsalliota in previous phylogenetic studies (Dutta et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2022; Patil et al., 2024; Vellinga, 2004; Vellinga et al., 2011). Currently, Yang et al. (2024) conducted comprehensive phylogenetic analyses based on four DNA loci (nrITS, nrLSU, rpb2, tef-1α), and divided the polyphyletic Leucoagaricus-Leucocoprinus group into four genera, viz. Leucoagaricus, Leucocoprinus, and two newly described genera close to Micropsalliota, namely Candelolepiota (J. Z. Ying) Kun L. Yang, Jia Y. Lin & Zhu L. Yang and Macropsalliota Kun L. Yang, Jia Y. Lin & Zhu L. Yang. The multi-gene (nrITS, nrLSU, rpb2) phylogenetic studies of Yan et al. (2022), resolved the genus Micropsalliota into 6 major clades and 12 subclades with weak to strong support. Further, the extensive phylogenetic analyses by Gao et al. (2024) based on four gene markers (nrITS, nrLSU, rpb2, tef-1α) revealed 11 major clades of which some clades exhibited high support values and could be combine with morphological characters.

The genus Micropsalliota is substantially investigated in tropical and subtropical regions of Africa, Asia, and tropical America, hitherto over 80 taxa of Micropsalliota are documented worldwide (Al-Kharousi et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2016; Gao et al., 2024; Guzmán-Dávalos & Heinemann, 1994; Heinemann, 1980, 1983, 1988, 1989, He et al., 2020; Heinemann & Leelavathy, 1991; Heinemann & Little Flower, 1983; Ivanova et al., 2023; Ji & He, 2023; Li et al., 2021; Parra et al., 2016; Patil et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2015; Yan et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2010). In India, until now, 25 species of Micropsalliota have been reported (Dutta et al., 2011; Farook et al., 2013; Heinemann, 1983; Heinemann & Leelavathy, 1991; Heinemann & Little Flower, 1983; Natarajan & Manjula, 1982; Patil et al., 2022; Senthilarasu & Kumaresan, 2016).

In the course of our ongoing mycofloristic survey conducted during the monsoon seasons across various locations in the Matheran Hills (recognized as one of the biodiversity hotspots in the Western Ghats of Maharashtra, India), we encountered a fascinating collection of Micropsalliota. Following comprehensive morphological and molecular analyses utilizing nrITS and nrLSU, this collection has been identified as a new species of Micropsalliota.

Macroscopic features were based on fresh basidiomata and photographs were taken in the natural habitat. Color codes were referred from ‘Methuen Handbook of Colour’ (Kornerup & Wanscher, 1978). All the specimens were dried in a hot air oven for 24 h at 45-50 °C temperature (Hu et al., 2022). The dried specimens of holotype were deposited in the Ajrekar Mycological Herbarium (AMH), Pune, India, while the paratypes were maintained in the Botany department of Smt. Chandibai Himathmal Mansukhani College, Thane, India. Microscopic characters were observed from the thin hand-cut sections of dried material by mounting in 5% (w/v) KOH, stained with 1% (w/v) phloxin in distilled water. Melzer’s reagent was used to check the amyloidity of basidiospores. At least 50 basidiospores, 10 basidia, and 10 cheilocystidia per sample were measured using a compound microscope MLX-B (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) connected with Magnus Magcam DC-5 camera (Magnus Opto Systems India Pvt. Ltd., New Delhi, India). The notation [n/m/p] means the ‘n’ number of basidiospores measured from ‘m’ basidiomata of ‘p’ collections. The letter abbreviations referred for dimensions of basidiospores as: Xm for arithmetic mean of basidiospores length by width (± standard deviation), Q is quotient of length divided by width of individual basidiospores and Qm for mean values of Q (± standard deviation).

The fungal genomic DNA was extracted by using CTAB method (Doyle & Doyle, 1987). The PCR reactions and purification were done at Genematrix LLP (Pune, India). The primer sets ITS1/ITS4 (White et al., 1990) for nrITS and LR0R/LR5 (Moncalvo et al., 2000; Vilgalys & Hester, 1990) for nrLSU region were employed. Furthermore, the Sanger sequencing was conducted by Apical Scientific Sdn Bhd (Seri Kembangan, Selangor, Malaysia). Sequences from the NCBI database were obtained and utilized to build the phylogenetic trees in accordance with previous research (Al-Kharousi et al., 2022; Chen et al., 2016; Gao et al., 2024; He et al., 2020; Ivanova et al., 2023; Li et al., 2021; Parra et al., 2016; Patil et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2015; Yan et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2010). Additionally, both markers were examined to determine their congruence using the Partition Homogeneity Test (ILD) in PAUP v 4.0b10 (Swofford & Sullivan, 2003) in order to build the combined tree. TaxonDNA v 1.7.8 (Vaidya et al., 2010) was used to concatenate both of the matrices based on the ILD test. There are 147 nrITS and 118 nrLSU sequences in the combined dataset (including four newly produced sequences during the current investigation) (Table 1). Two methods were used to estimate the phylogenetic position and relationship: Maximum Likelihood (ML) and Bayesian inference (BI). For ML analysis, IQTree v1.6.8 (Nguyen et al., 2014) was used, and the best fit evolutionary model (GTR+F+R4) was selected using ModelFinder (an IQTree inbuilt tool, Kalyaanamoorthy et al., 2017). The Bayesian tree was generated using MrBayes v 3.2.6 (Ronquist et al., 2012), with the following parameters: The MCMCMC algorithm was run with two parallel chains for four million generations, with the standard deviation of split frequency less than 0.01, and the effective sample size (ESS) for each parameter was ensured to greater than 200. However, GTR+I+G was found to be the most suitable evolutionary model using jModeltest (Darriba et al., 2012). Additionally, the consensus trees were shown in FigTree v 1.4.2 (Rambaut, 2014). The statistical supports of the phylogram were computed using bootstrap values (BS) and posterior probabilities (PP).

Diagnosis: Differs from M. umbonata by its ellipsoid to amygdaliform, larger basidiospores.

Type: INDIA, Maharashtra, Raigad District, Matheran Hills (18°58'48.00"N, 73°16'12.00"E, 800 m a.s.l.), 04 Aug 2019, P. B. Patil. (AMH 10512, Holotype).

DNA sequence ex-Holotype: PP837559 (nrITS) and PQ576415 (nrLSU).

Etymology: The prefix “sub-” (L.) means “almost” or “close”, and “umbonata” pertains to the epithet of the species that this novel taxon closely resembles.

Basidiomata medium-sized, centrally stipitate. Pileus 25-65 mm diam, campanulate to conical at first, then convex to nearly applanate at maturity, with obtuse umbo, surface dry, white (1A1) to cream (1A2), uniformly covered by white (1A1) to light brown (6-7D6-7) to reddish brown (8D6-7) appressed fibrillose squamules, dense at the umbonate centre, scattered elsewhere; margin incurved, with appendiculate thin velar remnants; context 2-3 mm thick on disc, white. Lamellae free, white (4A1), then greyish brown (5D2) to light brown (8D1), crowded, with 4-6 series of lamellulae, straight to slightly ventricose. Stipe 40-90 × 3-7 mm, cylindrical, fistulose, equal, smooth to tomentose, creamy white to light brown, surface covered with white (1A1) to light brown (6D6), fibrillose squamules. Annulus thin, apical, single, persistent, 3-5 mm broad, white, occasionally reddish white (7A2) at margin.

Basidiospores (4)4.5-5.5(6) × 2.5-3.4 µm, (Xm = 5.1 ± 0.5 × 2.84 ± 0.23 µm, Q = 1.5-2.2, Qm = 1.79 ± 0.17, n = 50, m = 3, p = 3), ellipsoid to sub-cymbiform, with an apical thickening, no germ pore, dextrinoid, brown. Basidia 12-17 × 5-6 µm, clavate, four-spored, rarely bisporic. Cheilocystidia 30-55 × 2.5-5.5 µm, cylindrical, elongate, some with ventricose base, capitate or subcapitate with long narrow sinuous neck, capitulum 3-5.5 µm diam, thin-walled, hyaline, smooth. Pleurocystidia absent. Hymenophoral trama regular, hyphae hyaline, thin-walled, 3-8 µm wide. Pileipellis a cutis, composed of radially arranged cylindrical hyphae, 2.5-5 μm broad, incrusted with a granular pigment that turns olive-green in NH4OH, hyaline, smooth. Pileal squamules consist of branched, smooth, cylindrical hyphae 3-9 µm wide. Annulus hyphae 2.5-9.5 μm broad, hyaline, smooth, branched, slightly constricted at septa. Stipitipellis consist of parallel hyphae, 4.5-9.5 µm wide. Clamp connections absent in all tissues.

Habitat and distribution: Scattered to clustered on the soil, in semi-evergreen forest dominated by tree species like Memecylon umbellatum Burm.F., Garcinia talbotii Raizada ex Santapau., Olea dioica Roxb., Xantolis tomentosa (Roxb). Raf., Gnetum ula Brongn. So far known only from Matheran Hills, Maharashtra, India.

Additional specimens examined: INDIA, Maharashtra, Raigad District, Matheran Hills (18°58'48.00"N, 73°16'12.00"E), 17 Jul 2022 (MMH 2311), 24 Jul 2023 (MMH 2312, PQ577838 for nrITS and PQ580740 for nrLSU), Prashant B. Patil.

Micropsalliota subumbonata is characterised by its medium-sized basidiomata with obtuse umbo, brown pileal squamules, ellipsoid to sub-cymbiform, basidiospores measuring 4.5-5.5 × 2.5-3.4 µm, and cylindrical, capitate or subcapitate cheilocystidia with a capitulum of 3-5.5 µm diam. The characters like cymbiform basidiospores, capitate cheilocystidia are common with M. avellanea Heinem. & Little Flower, M. cymbispora Heinem. & Little Flower, and M. malabarensis Heinem. & Little Flower. However, M. avellanea and M. cymbispora differs by their smaller pileus size (10-14 and 5-7 µm) and broader cheilocystidia (7-12 and 5-8 µm) (Heinemann & Little Flower, 1983). Micropsalliota malabarensis also having smaller basidiomata (pilei 4-10 vs 25-60 mm diam) and wider cheilocystidia apex (5.5-7.5 vs 3-5.5 µm) (Heinemann & Little Flower, 1983). Furthermore, M. subumbonata is also macromorphologically close to M. brunneosperma (Singer) Pegler, M. subalba Heinem. & Little Flower, and M. umbonata T. Gao & Z.W. Ge. Micropsalliota brunneosperma and M. subalba have smaller basidiomata (pilei 17-24 and 8-23 mm diam) and larger basidiospores (4.8-6.5 × 3.2-4.4 and 5.6-6.5 × 3.5-4.1 µm) (Heinemann, 1980; Heinemann & Little Flower, 1983). Micropsalliota umbonata is distinguishable from M. subumbonata by its larger basidiospores (6-7 × 3-4 µm) and much wider cheilocystidia (7-12 mm diam) (Gao et al., 2024).

The alignment of the combined dataset comprised a total of 1449 characters, which includes an aligned sequence dataset consisting of 687 bp from nrITS and 762 bp from nrLSU for the analyses. The comprehensive analysis of the ILD test, utilizing 1000 bootstrap iterations, demonstrated consistency with a p-value of 0.85 at a significance level of 0.05. Consequently, the dataset was amalgamated for subsequent analysis. According to the previous research (Al-Kharousi et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021; Patil et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2015; Yan et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2010) and our phylogenetic analyses, 12 major clades of the Micropsalliota were revealed, of which Clade albofelina, Clade bifida, Clade cortinata, Clade furfuracea, Clade globocystis, Clade pleurocystidiata, and Clade ventricocystidiata were similar to Gao et al. (2024). Some clades, in particular, the Clade bifida and Clade globocystis are stable due to high number of species diversity. The positions of other clades are unstable, and failed to recover their deeper nodes. So, more extensive collections and the addition of new taxa will be required to resolve the other clades of Micropsalliota.

In our phylogenetic analyses, M. subumbonata appeared as a sister to the rest of the Micropsalliota species included in this study with strong support (BS = 100/PP = 1.00). Based on both morphological and molecular characters, M. subumbonata stands out from other species and forms a distinct basal subclade in phylogenetic tree and here it was considered to be a part of newly proposed clade subumbonata (Fig. 3).

Genetic structure, selective characterization and specific molecular identity cards of high-yielding Houdan chickens based on genome-wide SNP
Authors: Cong Liu, Pingquan Liu, Shuangxing Liu, Haishan Guo, Tingqi Zhu, Wenting Li, Kejun Wang, Xiangtao Kang, Guirong Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe high-yielding Houdan chicken (GGF) is characterized by high egg production and disease resistance. This study conducted whole genome resequencing of the GGF population and compared it to data from other breeds. Genetic diversity analysis revealed higher observed heterozygosity (Ho), Polymorphism information content (PIC), number of runs of homozygosity (ROH), and inbreeding coefficient (FROH) in GGF. Linkage disequilibrium (LD) decay was slowest in GGF, indicating intensive inbreeding and strong selection. These findings suggest a need for appropriate strategies to enhance genetic diversity conservation in this breed. Population structure analysis demonstrated that GGF was genetically distinct from both the red jungle fowl (RJF) and Chinese indigenous chicken (CIC) populations, highlighting GGF as a unique genetic resource warranting intensive protection and utilization. Selective sweep analysis identified genes under selection in GGF, primarily enriched in signaling pathways related to oocyte meiosis and progesterone-mediated oocyte maturation. Key candidate genes included: CCNE1, SKP1, CDC20, CDK2, ADCY8, RPS6KA6, PPP3CB, PDE3B, HSP90AB1, and AKT3. These findings provide a theoretical foundation for their potential application in poultry breeding. Additionally, this study combined bioinformatics analysis with PCR amplification and Sanger sequencing to identify 4 SNPs that can serve as a molecular identity card (ID) for GGF: SNP1 (Chr2: 136130976), SNP3 (Chr4:11705164), SNP4 (Chr4: 63255588), and SNP5 (Chr24: 3271008). This study provides a scientific basis for effective management and conservation of GGF genetic resources, and establishes a simple, economical, and accurate set of molecular IDs to combat the proliferation of inferior breeds and protect genetic resources.Key words: high-yielding Houdan chicken, genetic diversity, population structure, selective signature, molecular identity card

INTRODUCTION

As one of the possible origins of domestic chickens, China is rich in geographical and cultural diversity and is one of the countries with the farthest and richest chicken heredity in the world (Luo et al., 2020). Under long-term natural and artificial selection conditions, Chinese indigenous chickens have formed numerous groups with distinctive characteristics (Nie et al., 2019). The diversity of local species provides valuable genetic material for scientific research. By studying the genetic diversity of different breeds, it is possible to explore poultry's origin, domestication, and evolutionary history (Zhang et al., 2019b). In China, breeding new and superior strains is essential in agriculture and aquaculture. It has far-reaching significance for the country's food security, economic development, and scientific and technological progress. Especially during the 14th 5-Year Plan for National Economic and Social Development of the People's Republic of China, the Chinese government has put forward higher requirements and support for breeding new strains to cope with the profound and complex changes in the development environment at home and abroad.

In 2021, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of the People's Republic of China published the National List of Livestock and Poultry Genetic Resource Breeds, which included 115 Chinese indigenous chicken breeds, 5 newly bred breeds, 80 newly bred companion lines, 8 introduced breeds, and 32 introduced companion lines. As a kind of excellent breed introduced, Houdan chicken has excellent characteristics such as delicious meat and strong resistance, especially the content of selenium and zinc, which is known as the king of anticancer, is 3 to 5 times that of common birds, which is the ideal therapeutic bird of the present time, and is regarded as the "best of the bird" by the gourmet world, and is widely favored by the consumers (Liu et al., 2020). However, the problem of low egg production has limited the development and utilization of Houdan chicken. In order to solve this problem, our laboratory used Houdan chicken as the male parent and White Laihang chicken as the female parent for crossbreeding in the early stage and selected individuals with black feathers in white feathers in the F1 generation for F1 generation crossbreeding. After 4 generations of selection and breeding, a new high-yield egg-laying strain was formed with external features similar to those of the Houdan, named the high-yield Houdan chicken (GGF), characterized by solid disease resistance and high egg production. GGF can produce about 260 eggs at 72 wk of age, significantly increasing the original Houdan chicken egg production (167 eggs) by 56%.

Systematic analyses of poultry genetic diversity and population structure are of inestimable value for the conservation of biodiversity, the promotion of sustainable agricultural development, the improvement and innovation of the poultry industry, and the advancement of scientific research (Wang et al., 2021). Through these analyses, we can better understand and utilize poultry genetic resources to provide a solid foundation for future food security and agricultural development. Ren et al. analyzed the genetic diversity and structure based on 625 mitochondrial DNA D-loop sequences. They revealed the presence of 3 major genetic clusters in Cambodian indigenous chickens with high genetic diversity (Ren et al., 2022). The results of population structure and genetic diversity of 7 indigenous chicken breeds in Guizhou Province, based on a 50k single nucleotide polymorphism array, the Wumeng crested breed had high genetic diversity and showed genetic differences from other breeds. These findings provide insight into establishing efficient conservation and utilization programs for Guizhou chicken breeds (Liu et al., 2021). Li et al. systematically evaluated the population structure and demography of the current Tibetan chicken (TBC) population. They screened 2 genes, RYR2 and CAMK2D, strongly selected in the TBC population and associated with high-altitude adaptation. These results demonstrated a robust population structure in TBC that will help inform future genetic analyses of chickens and other domestic animals in Tibet (Li et al., 2023).

In the context of natural or artificial selection, a phenomenon known as selective sweep occurs when the frequency of specific advantageous alleles increases within a population. This leads to the fixation of chromosomal regions linked to these alleles due to hitchhiking effects, thereby reducing polymorphism. This phenomenon is termed “selective sweep” (Hufford et al., 2012). Based on a single detection method, which often employs a single-marker scanning strategy, results can be easily influenced by genetic drift, leading to the identification of false-positive significant loci. To minimize the occurrence of false positives, a sliding window strategy is commonly adopted to reduce interference and enhance the accuracy of detecting selection signals (Qiu et al., 2015). Based on selective sweep analysis at the livestock genome level, it is not only possible to excavate genes subject to selection during artificial domestication, such as genes related to domestic pig meat quality (He et al., 2024), chicken growth (Wang et al., 2022), goat reproductive performance (Xin et al., 2021), and chicken skin color (Li et al., 2020), but also genes subject to selection in the adaptive evolution of the species, such as genes related to highland adaptation of Tibetan pigs (Li et al., 2013), adaptation of Chinese endemic chickens to tropical and cold environments (Shi et al., 2023), and adaptation of Tibetan chickens to high altitude (Li et al., 2023). These studies have provided valuable resources for mining superior traits and selective breeding in livestock.

Seed is the agricultural development of the "chip" to ensure agricultural production, which is the key to increasing yields and income. In recent years, with the rapid development of Chinese seed industry, more and more approved varieties into the market, but also the emergence of fake and inferior seeds, varieties of mixed, the same name and the same product with a different name and other phenomena, resulting in livestock and poultry agricultural products yield, quality reduction, seriously disrupting the market order, affecting the livestock and poultry seed industry, production, sales, and development. Carrying out breed appraisal and combating the infringement of varieties to escape from the brand has a vital role in guaranteeing the quality of the seed industry and maintaining market order. Traditional species or variety identification methods include observation and recording of morphological features (Korir et al., 2013; Wang et al., 2015). The traditional species or variety identification approach involves observing and documenting morphological characteristics. Since morphological characters are influenced by the environment, growth stage, and subjective factors, the traditional approach could be more practical for rapidly isolating a comprehensive set of samples. It is not only time-consuming but also affects accuracy. Utilizing molecular markers is a modern and appropriate approach for variety identification (Zhang et al., 2023). Molecular markers have the advantages of comprehensive coverage, high polymorphism, co-dominant inheritance, stability, reproducibility, rapidity, and low cost. Scholars at home and abroad have conducted several studies on constructing molecular identity cards for livestock and poultry breeds. Zhou et al. analyzed the genetic diversity of 21 indigenous chicken breeds in South China and Vietnam based on 18 microsatellite molecular genetic markers. Finally, they constructed unique microsatellite molecular markers for 20 cultivars using 15 microsatellite loci. This study provides valuable insights to facilitate breed identification and improve cultivar protection and new germplasm construction (Zhuang et al., 2023). Suekawa et al. developed 5 specific markers using a 50k SNP array data for breed identification between Japanese and US cattle. The markers would contribute to the prevention of falsified beef displays in Japan (Suekawa et al., 2010). Wang et al. used a machine learning classification model to conduct predictive analysis based on the SNP of the whole genome SNPs of 7 Shandong indigenous chicken breeds and 20 recessive white chickens, which could effectively realize the breed identification of Laiwu black chicken with an accuracy of 92% (Wang et al., 2023). Therefore, economical, efficient, and accurate breed identification is essential to improve the efficiency of poultry breed management and breed conservation ability. More research must be reported on the GGF population, sufficiently worthy of in-depth study and wide dissemination as a new high-yielding egg-use strain.

In this study, we used genome-wide SNPs to analyze the genetic diversity and population structure of 13 breeds of chicken populations, explored the selection characteristics of GGF chickens through selective clearance analysis, and screened and verified the specific SNPs of GGF populations to construct molecular identity cards for GGF chickens' breed identification. This study lays the foundation for the conservation, development, and utilization of the genetic resources of the GGF chicken population.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics Statement

Animal care and experiments were conducted with the approval by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of Henan Agricultural University (Permit Number: HNND2020030118).

Samples and Data Preparation

In this study, 13 GGF blood samples (5 males and 8 females) were collected from the poultry germplasm resource farm of Henan Agricultural University, China. Genomic DNA was extracted from whole blood samples using the TianGen DNA kit. Paired-end libraries with a ∼500 bp insert size were constructed and then subjected to sequencing using the DNBSEQ-T7 platform to generate 150 bp paired-end reads. Furthermore, we obtained whole genome resequencing data from a published dataset, which included 124 chickens from 12 different chicken breeds. In addition to the wild breed of RJF, the other breeds originate from different regions in China and represent Chinese indigenous chickens. The basic information on chicken genetic resources used in this study is provided in Table 1 and Figure 1. In addition, the genotype data required for the validation of GGF-specific loci in this study were obtained from the public database Galbase (http://animal.omics.pro/code/index.php/ChickenVar/loadByGet?address[]=ChickenVar/Download/VarData.php) (Fu et al., 2022). This database contains published genotype data for 928 individuals from 15 chicken populations.

Genotyping and Data Filtering

To obtain clean reads of premium quality for subsequent genome mapping and variant calling, the stringent quality filtering techniques listed below were used: (1) deleting reads with 10% undefined nucleotides (N); (2) eliminating reads with more than 50% bases with phred scores of 20; and (3) disposing reads aligned to the barcode adaptor. The high-quality paired-end reads were mapped onto the Gallus gallus GRCg6a (http://ftp.ensembl.org/pub/release-104/fasta/gallus_gallus/ dna) using BWA (v0.7.17)(Van der Auwera et al., 2013). A series of postprocessing was then performed to process the alignment files, including sorting bam files, removing duplicated reads, local realignment, and base quality recalibration, and applying the corresponding tools in Picard Tools (v1.56) (Li, 2014) and GATK (v4.1.7.0) (Lin et al., 2022). HaplotypeCaller first recalled SNPs, then joint-genotyped on all samples by GenotypeGVCFs, and finally filtered with the following cut-off values by VariantFiltration: QUAL<30.0, QD<2.0, MQ<40.0, FS>60.0, SOR>3.0, MQRankSum<12.5, and ReadPosRankSum<8.0. To improve the efficiency of data analysis and ensure the accuracy of the analysis, SNPs were retained using PLINK (v1.90) (Purcell et al., 2007) for subsequent analyses according to the following criteria: a minor allele frequency (MAF) > 0.05 and missing rate<0.01. Ultimately, after quality control, 5184778 SNPs distributed on 28 chromosomes of 137 samples passed the filtering process and were retained for subsequent analysis.

The Ensemble genome database and SNPEff (v 4.1) (Cingolani et al., 2012) program were used to obtain information about SNP annotation—additionally, the R package CMplot was used to visualize the distribution of the SNPs across the chromosome.

Genetic Diversity Analysis

In order to investigate the genetic diversity of populations, this study reflected the genetic diversity of the population by estimating the average observed heterozygosity (Ho), expected heterozygosity (He), minor allele frequency (MAF), nucleotide diversity (π), genetic differentiation (Fst), Observed number of alleles (Na), Effective number of alleles (Ne), Polymorphism information content (PIC), Nei's genetic distance (Nei) and inbreeding coefficient (FROH). The PLINK (v1.90) (Chang et al., 2015) was used to calculate Ho and He, MAF, Na, Ne, PIC, and VCFtools (v0.1.16) (Danecek et al., 2011) was used to calculate π and Fst of the population with 10kb sliding window and 5kb step size. The R package hierfstat (GOUDET, 2010) was used to calculate the Nei for each breed.

Runs of homozygosity (ROH) refer to continuous, uninterrupted stretches of homozygous genotypes in the genome. Inbreeding coefficients calculated based on ROH represent the actual individual inbreeding and are more accurate than the expected inbreeding coefficients calculated from traditional pedigree data. They are no longer dependent on the accuracy and completeness of pedigree records, providing an accurate reflection of the relationship between 2 gametes. Based on the ROH, the FROH of each individual was estimated. Using PLINK (v1.90), long homozygous fragments were scanned. The following specific parameters were used to assess homozygosity: a sliding window of 50 SNPs along the chromosome was used; each sliding window allowed a maximum of 1 heterozygote, 5 missing SNPs, a minimum length of ROH of 100 kb, a minimum density of 1 SNP per 50 kb, and a maximum interval between consecutive SNPs of 1,000 kb was permitted for each sliding window. Subsequently, we counted the number of runs of homozygosity (NSEG) and total length of runs (KB) from different chicken breeds. Population Genetic Structure

Before estimating population structure, we conducted “pruning” of linkage disequilibrium (LD) using PLINK (v1.90) with the following parameters: “-indep-pairwise 50 5 0.2 to remove redundant genetic information. Using the pruned genome-wide SNPs, we analyzed population structure using several methods. Firstly, the principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted using PLINK (v1.90) to explore the genomic repartition in 13 chicken breeds, and the first 2 feature vectors (PC1/PC2) were extracted as the horizontal and vertical coordinates. Secondly, the neighbor-joining (NJ) tree (bootstrap values = 1,000) was constructed to show the phylogenetic relationship under the p-distances model of the software PHYLIP (v3.697) (Felsenstein, 1989) and MEGA (V7.0) (Kumar et al., 2016). The Evolview web server (Subramanian et al., 2019) (https://www.evolgenius.info/evolview/) displayed the phylogenetic tree. Thirdly, to investigate the genetic structure of all populations, ADMIXTURE (version 1.30) (Alexander et al., 2009) was used to run with the group numbers from K = 2 to K = 10. The parameter standard errors for each possible group number determined the optimal K (Martiniano et al., 2017).

Linkage Disequilibrium, Genome-Wide Selective Sweep Analysis and Gene Annotation

Linkage Disequilibrium (LD) analysis plays a pivotal role in genome-wide selection, assessment of adequate population size, genome-wide association studies, and the mapping of quantitative trait loci. In this study, to evaluate the linkage patterns of SNPs across 13 chicken populations, the PopLDdecay software (version 3.40) (Zhang et al., 2019a) was utilized to statistically analyze the LD patterns among different populations, with the results displayed using a MaxDist of 300 kb.

To identify the genomic regions harboring the footprints of positive selection in GGF chickens, selective sweep methods were used to investigate selection in the GGF population, including coefficient of nucleotide differentiation (Fst) and nucleotide diversity (π) statistical methods (Wang et al., 2016). Here, we performed selective sweep tests for 2 strategies: first, GGF (object population) and 11 Chinese indigenous chicken breeds (CIC, reference population); second, GGF (object population) and RJF (reference population). Fst and π values were estimated by screening the chicken genomes with a sliding window approach (a window size of 10 kb and a step size of 5 kb) (Akey et al., 2002). θπ was calculated as πreference/πobject. Fst values were changed using Z-transform (ZFst), and ratios were transformed using log2θπ. The windows with ZFst and log2θπ values in the top 5% were defined as strongly selective regions, and genes in these regions were identified as candidate genes. The GGF population-selected genes obtained from the 2 groups were intersected to get the final GGF-selected genes.

Functional Enrichment Analysis

The intersection of candidate genes used for further functional enrichment analysis (Voight et al., 2006), including Gene Ontology (GO) (Ashburner et al., 2000) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways (Kanehisa et al., 2004). GO terms and KEGG pathways with Bonferroni P-values < 0.05 and < 0.01 were considered significant and highly significant, respectively.

Screening and Validation of Specific SNP in High-Yield Houdan Chicken

Fst values were calculated by VCFtools (v0.1.16). Using non-GGF samples (NGGF) as the comparative population, loci exceeding the top 5% Fst value were regarded as the significant selective loci. Afterward, PLINK (v1.90) software was used to extract GGF-specific SNPs from 137 chicken populations. The requirements for these specific SNPs were the presence of homozygous mutant genotypes in the GGF population and homozygous with wild-type or heterozygous mutant genotypes in the NGGF population. To verify the specificity of the loci selected in the previous screening step, we will undertake validation of these loci in an additional 928 populations to filter out the SNP, which is more reliable and specific to GGF.

RESULTS

Summary of Chicken Breeds, Sequencing, and Genome Variants

After removing low-quality data, the clean sequencing data of GGF obtained was 199.48 GB, with Q20 and Q30 values exceeding 98% and 94%, respectively. The GC content of the 13 samples ranged from 41% to 43%, and the average depth was approximately 10×. The statistical results indicated that the sequencing quality of the samples was high, with a normal GC distribution. The sequencing process was successful and could be used for analysis. A total of 137 samples were analyzed in this study (Supplementary Table 1), and the genome sequencing depth for each sample ranged from 6×to 13×. After rigorous filtering, we ultimately obtained 5,184,778 high-quality SNPs for subsequent analysis with the transition/transversion ratios (Ts/Tv) of 2.85 to 2.88 in each population, proving the high quality and validity of the SNP data. Of the total SNPs, 40.288% were aligned to gene and intergenic regions, and 59.712% were in noncoding regions (Supplementary Table 2). Moreover, the density distribution of SNPs on each chromosome was analyzed. SNPs were found more abundant on large chromosomes and less frequent on small chromosomes, with SNPs being positively correlated with chromosome length (Supplementary Figure 1).

Genome Genetic Diversity Estimation

Table 3 summarizes genetic diversity parameters, including each chicken breed's Ho, He, MAF, and Pi. The continuity of ROHs is shown in Figure 2. These data indicated the intensive inbreeding in GGF, potentially due to artificial selection.

Population Genetic Structure and Genome-Wide Genetic Differentiation

Fst is used to measure the degree of population differentiation, with larger values indicating that alleles are more fixed (higher frequency) in their respective subpopulations and more differentiated between populations. We calculated the genetic differentiation index between 2 populations to measure the degree of genetic differentiation of populations. As delineated in Table 4, the Fst values among the GGF and the other populations spanned from 0.17150 to 0.19604, suggesting a significant level of genetic divergence among these populations. For the RJF, in comparison with other Chinese indigenous chicken populations, the Fst values fluctuated between 0.065856 and 0.09061, which implied a moderate degree of genetic distinction between these groups. Among the remaining Chinese indigenous chicken populations, the Fst values ranged from 0.00596 to 0.04790, indicative of minimal genetic differentiation, an observation that may be considered negligible for practical purposes. Nei's genetic distance quantifies evolutionary differences between populations and are associated with genetic similarities or differences in populations. Nei's genetic distance analysis showed that the genetic distances between GGF and other populations were large, ranging from 0.13338 to 0.14584, while the genetic distances between Chinese indigenous chicken breeds were closer, ranging from 0.05592 to 0.08206 (Table 5).

In order to investigate the population genetic structure of GGF chickens and their relationship with other Chinese indigenous chicken breeds, the PCA analyses (Figure 3A), neighbor-joining (NJ) phylogenetic tree analyses (Figure 3B), and population structure analyses (Figure 3C) were performed based on the whole genome snp of 16 chicken breeds. PCA analysis divided the 13 chicken breeds into 3 groups. The first group was the GGF population, the second group was the RJF population, and the third group was the Chinese indigenous chicken population. The NJ tree showed that all the chickens could be divided into 3 large clusters. Of them, cluster 1 was the GGF, cluster 2 was the RJF, and cluster 3 included the remaining 11 Chinese indigenous breeds. Furthermore, to explore the historical admixture patterns of the 13 breeds, an ADMIXTURE analysis was conducted with K values ranging from 2 to 6. At K = 2, a genetic divergence occurred between the GGF and NGGF populations. When K = 3, RJF was progressively assigned to a distinct cluster, and at this point, the results were consistent with those of the PCA and NJ tree. When K=4, the GS and HBM populations diverged from the 137 chickens. Screening and Validation of Specific SNP in GGF Chickens

We discerned significant genetic differentiation between the GGF and NGGF populations through an analysis of population structure. Subsequently, we computed indices of genetic differentiation. Utilizing the Fst values (Figure 6A), we identified 1,959 SNPs specific to the GGF. The 1959 SNPs exhibited high specificity in the GGF population. They typically existed as homozygous mutant genotypes in the GGF population and as homozygous wild-type or heterozygous mutant genotypes in the NGGF population (Figure 6B). After further analysis, these 1,959 sites were subjected to validation across a cohort of 928 samples, culminating in identifying 5 SNPs (SNP1, SNP2, SNP4, SNP5) with high specificity. Except for a minimal number of individual outliers, these 5 SNPs were found to be homozygous for the mutation in the GGF population. They were observed as heterozygous or of the wild-type in the NGGF population (Figure 6C). For instance, SNP1(Chr2: 136130976) was genotyped GG in the GGF population and AA or AG in the other populations; SNP2 (Chr4: 11656648) was genotyped TT in the GGF population, and CC or CT in the other populations, SNP3 (Chr4:11705164) was genotyped GG in the GGF population and AA or GG in the other populations, SNP4 (Chr4: 63255588) was genotyped TT in the GGF population and CC or CT in the other populations, and SNP5 (Chr24: 3271008) was genotyped TT in the GGF population and CC or CT in the other populations. These results indicated that the combination of these 5 SNPs can accurately determine whether the individual to be tested is a GGF chicken.

The agarose gel electrophoresis results of PCR products are shown in Figure 7A. The amplified fragment was the same size as the target fragment, and the bands were single and bright, indicating that the PCR products met the requirements of Sanger sequencing. Sanger sequencing results showed that except for SNP2, the other 4 SNPs (SNP1, SNP3, SNP4, SNP5) were homozygous mutants in the GGF population (Figure 7B), and the peak map results of the sequencing results were consistent with the second-generation sequencing results (Figure 7C), indicating that these loci existed. DISCUSSION

As sequencing technologies continue to evolve and sequencing costs decrease, using genome-wide data to explore genetic diversity, population structure, and selective traits is the most critical approach to studying conservation genomics (Giani et al., 2020; Wang et al., 2024). Germplasm resource conservation is a primary action for revitalizing the seed industry. “Fourteenth 5-Year Plan” emphasizes organizing and carrying out good livestock and poultry breed production performance measurement, livestock and poultry accurate identification, new resource discovery, and salvage collection and protection. Precise identification should be centered on accelerating the establishment of molecular identity cards for livestock and poultry breeds, carrying out excellent gene mining, and constructing reference genomes to facilitate better identification and protection of excellent genetic resources. GGF as a newly bred excellent group, can improve economic benefits and serve as an essential breeding material, which can help conserve and utilize China's poultry genetic resources and promote the conservation and sustainable use of biodiversity.

This study provided the whole genome sequencing data of GGF for the first time, and its genomic variants were identified. Meanwhile, this study also analyzed the genetic diversity, population structure, and selection signals of the GGF population by combining the genotypic data of the Chinese indigenous chicken and the RJF population systematically and comparatively, and constructed a molecular identity card of the GGF, which can be used for the authenticity of the GGF, and provide a solid scientific basis for the identification and protection of the GGF's germplasm resources.

Overall, WC, HL, HX, and Yao had higher genetic diversity indices (Ho, He, MAF, Pi, Na, Ne, and PIC) and lower FROH, indicating higher genetic diversity and lower frequency of inbreeding in the populations, further suggesting that the genetic resources of these breeds have been effectively conserved. In general, higher heterozygosity indicates, on the 1 hand, that the variety may have experienced lower genetic bottlenecks and maintained higher allelic polymorphism and, on the other hand, that genetic variation in the population may have been increased due to crossbreeding and gene infiltration between varieties. Interestingly, the heterozygosity of the GGF was high, as were the ROH counts and FROH values. The main reason for this phenomenon is the breeding process of GGF. Since the GGF were crossed from 2 different breeds, the genetic variation in the population increased to some extent. In addition, a higher level of artificial selection and inbreeding is experienced during closed breeding, a process that could explain the exciting phenomenon of higher FROH in GGF mentioned above. At the same time, the higher FROH also emphasizes the urgency of conserving the biodiversity of rebreeds. ROH are more extended regions of pure fitness in the genome, and they may have arisen due to recent or progenitor inbreeding events. Therefore, higher genetic diversity does not necessarily mean that no inbreeding has occurred. When populations undergo natural or artificial selection to adapt to specific environmental or production goals, it may affect their genetic structure to some extent, resulting in an index of genetic diversity that is not proportional to the number of ROH. Surprisingly, lower genetic diversity and higher inbreeding coefficients were observed in the RJF in this study. The main reason is that the RJF individuals selected in this study were derived from closely related individuals in the same subpopulation (Qanbari et al., 2019). In addition, the increase in human activities in recent years has led to a decrease in the number and distribution of red proto-grouse, leading to a decrease in genetic diversity (Setianto et al., 2017). The greatest threat to red grouse populations has been reported to be egg harvesting and hunting (Akrim et al., 2015). Therefore, when conserving indigenous breeds' genetic resources, the conservation of the RJF should be addressed. The GGF population shows high genetic differentiation and Nei's genetic distance from all other populations. The main reason is that the breeding process of new breeds involves inbreeding, which increases the purity and, thus, the genetic differentiation from other breeds (Sallam et al., 2023). Population structure analysis showed that GGF can be separated from other breeds, and these results are consistent with the results of the genetic differentiation index and Nei's genetic distance. Population structure analysis showed that GGF can be separated from other breeds, and these results are consistent with the results of the genetic differentiation index. The slowest rate of LD decay for the GGF proved once again that the GGF underwent more intense selection during the selection process. The systematic analysis of the genetic diversity and population structure indicated that the GGF population is a unique genetic resource that may have undergone inbreeding and high-intensity selection during breeding and is worthy of conservation and in-depth study.

We scanned the genome of a GGF population to explore the positive selection signature of GGF. We identified a series of genes enriched primarily in the oocyte meiosis and progesterone-mediated oocyte maturation signaling pathways, suggesting that these genes play a central role in regulating the reproductive performance of GGF. Studies have shown that genes regulating the cell cycle during reproduction contribute to better development and maintenance of fertility in female animals (Bao et al., 2021; Qin et al., 2022). Activation of oocyte meiosis promotes oocyte maturation and plays a vital role in follicle development (Li et al., 2022; Lyu et al., 2021). Follicles play an essential role in the reproductive performance of poultry, especially during the late egg-laying period. Among these candidate genes, we identified some genes of interest. For example, Cyclin E1 (CCNE1) copy number is a biomarker for response to combination WEE1-ATR inhibition in ovarian and endometrial cancer models (Xu et al., 2021). Single-cell transcriptome analysis based on oocytes reveals that SKP1 may play a key role in human oocyte senescence (Yuan et al., 2021). CDC20 (cell division cycle 20 homolog) is a co-activator of the anaphase-promoting complex/cyclosome (APC/C) and thus acts as a mitotic regulator (Ni and Hong, 2023). Zhao et al. identified biallelic mutations in CDC20 responsible for human oocyte maturation arrest, fertilization failure, and early embryonic development arrest (Zhao et al., 2021). The reproductive organs of Cdk2 mutant mice exhibited abnormal morphology and impaired function associated with defective meiotic cell division and inability to produce gametes (Chauhan et al., 2016). ADCY8 is mainly involved in the production of ovarian steroids, which have a regulatory effect on ovarian development and estrogen (He et al., 2023). Studies have shown that ADCY8 was significantly upregulated in the ovarian tissue of Taihe black-bone silky fowls during the early laying period, which may contribute to egg production performance in the prelaying period and may play a regulatory role in follicle development and ovarian maintenance (Xiang et al., 2022). In addition, this gene also played a key role in the spawning rate of ducks (Bello et al., 2021). PPP3CB (β isoform) could be a reliable predictor for the fertile and competent oocyte selection (Daei-Farshbaf et al., 2021). Madsen et al. found that inhibition of phosphodiesterase PDE8B reduces the activation of primordial follicles in mouse ovaries (Madsen et al., 2023). Akt1−/− females display reduced fertility and abnormal estrous cyclicity, suggesting that Akt1 plays an instrumental role in regulating the growth and maturation of the ovary (Brown et al., 2010). Therefore, researchers can conduct further studies on these genes to verify their function and mechanism of action, which will provide a theoretical basis for their use in poultry breeding.

Livestock and poultry breed identification is indispensable to livestock management, scientific research, and production. It is of great significance in promoting the healthy development of the livestock industry, protecting livestock and poultry genetic resources, and improving the quality of livestock products. The study was conducted to screen the pure loci specific to the GGF population through genetic differentiation index analysis. Then, these loci were validated in the genotypic data of 928 individuals and screened for 5 SNPs. The combination of these 5 SNPs can be used as a molecular identity card for GGF. The 5 SNPs can be used as the molecular identity card of GGF when the 5 loci of the individual to be tested are pure mutant genotypes. Next-generation sequencing (NGS), while offering significant advantages in terms of throughput and cost-effectiveness over first-generation sequencing technologies (e.g., the Sanger sequencing method), may not be as accurate as first-generation sequencing in some cases. For example, PCR amplification errors, segment read lengths, sequencing error rates, and systematic errors. Therefore, when SNP loci are analyzed, combining the results of first- and second-generation sequencing for co-validation is necessary. The results of Sanger sequencing showed that except for SNP2, the other 4 loci are natural, so these 4 loci can be used as reliable loci for the molecular ID of the GGF population.

CONCLUSION

This study systematically analyzed the genetic diversity, population structure, and selection characteristics of the GGF, Chinese indigenous chicken, and RJF populations based on genome-wide SNPs. The GGF population is a unique genetic resource with a high population inbreeding coefficient and undergoing high selection. The selected genes were mainly enriched in signaling pathways related to follicular cell development. In addition, the specific molecular ID of GGF constructed using 4 SNPs can accurately identify GGF, an effective measure to fight against shoddy breeds and protect new breeds. The present study provides valuable insights into the conservation of GGF, mining excellent traits, breed identification, and constructing new germplasm resources.

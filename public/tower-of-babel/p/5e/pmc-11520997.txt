Integrating network pharmacology, molecular docking, and experimental validation to reveal the mechanism of Radix Rehmanniae in psoriasis
Authors: Nian Mo, Panyu Zhou, Fanlu Liu, Haojie Su, Ling Han, Chuanjian Lu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background:Radix Rehmanniae (RR) plays an important role in treating psoriasis. However, the active compounds of RR and potential mechanisms are unclear. The current study was designed to investigate the potential active ingredients, targets, and mechanisms of RR in treating psoriasis through network pharmacology, molecular docking, and vitro experiments.Methods:Initially, the TCMSP database and literature retrieval were used to access the active ingredients of RR. The psoriasis target proteins were obtained from Therapeutic Target Database, OMIM, GeneCards, and DrugBank databases. The target proteins were then converted into target genes using Uniprot. Secondly, overlapping genes were obtained through Venn online tool. Then, protein–protein interactions network diagram is finished by STRING database. Next, Cytoscape software was used to acquire the top 10 hub proteins; gene ontology and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes enrichment analysis were then used to predict possible mechanisms. Afterwards, molecular docking validation of the active ingredients with the main targets was performed by AutoDock software. Finally, lipopolysaccharides induced RAW264.7, to assess the effects and molecular mechanisms by MTT, RT-qPCR, and Western blot assays.Results:Overall, there are 20 effective compounds and 33 targets involved in biological processes including apoptosis, intracellular signaling, vasodilation, and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling cascade. The docking results showed strong binding capacity between the active ingredients and targets. We verified aucubin as the key active ingredient, tumor necrosis factor α, and IL6 as the core targets, and focused on the p38MAPK protein pathway. Cellular experiments showed that aucubin down-regulated the phosphorylated p38MAP protein and reduced the expression of tumor necrosis factor α mRNA, IL6 mRNA, and IL1βmRNA.Conclusion:In summary, RR is featured with multicomponent, multi-target, and multi-pathway in treating psoriasis; the preliminary mechanism may be associated with the down-regulation of p38MAPK phosphorylation and curbing the expression of inflammatory factor by aucubin. This paper provides the scientific basis for Traditional Chinese medicine treating psoriasis.

1. Introduction

Psoriasis is a highly prevalent chronic skin condition characterized by raised red patches covered with silver scales that may cause tiny bleeding spots when the scales are removed.[1] Psoriasis affects approximately 2% to 3% of the global population,[2] with the greatest occurrence observed in developed European nations.[3,4] Chronic skin conditions can manifest at any stage of life. Episodes were more frequent at ages 16 to 22 and 55 to 60 years. Psoriasis has different clinical phenotypes, and the majority of patients with psoriasis suffer from a significant psychological and emotional burden due to larger lesions or occurrence in critical functional areas (e.g., face and genitals), and are more prone to depression, in severe cases, suicidal thoughts, and behavior.[5,6] Furthermore, apart from its negative impacts on physical appearance, psoriasis is linked to cardiovascular disease, metabolic disorders, and renal disease, which is referred to as inflammatory dermatosis.[7] According to the literature, people with psoriasis have a shorter life expectancy than the general population.[8] Moreover, it is costly for both the healthcare system and the patient.[9] Although significant advances have been made in treating severe psoriasis using biologics, the implications of their long-term use are not entirely clear. Chinese medicine has gained increasing attention in recent years because of its distinctive effectiveness and comparatively safe approach for treating psoriasis.

The name of psoriasis in Chinese medicine is “white mange,” and the earliest record of psoriasis in Chinese medicine is found in the Treatise on the Origin of All Diseases. Many generations of physicians have treated psoriasis from a blood perspective. Zhao Bingnan has created the "blood identification” treatment system, which is the mainstream idea of Chinese medicine in treating psoriasis, and many traditional formulas treating psoriasis from the perspective of blood have achieved good clinical efficacy. Radix Rehmanniae (RR) is the most allied herb in the compound formula for psoriasis. Throughout the Tang, Song, Jin, Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties, various books including the Recently Revised Materia Medica and the Compilation of Materia Medica recorded that RR possessed the capacity to replenish yin, strengthen the blood, and purify blood vessels.[10] Recent research has also demonstrated that through the regulation of rat spleen metabolism, RR can control hematopoiesis, osmotic pressure, membrane stability, oxidative harm, enhance leukocyte, and hemoglobin levels in the peripheral blood[11] and provide therapeutic benefits in conditions such as diabetic nephropathy, rheumatoid arthritis, Parkinson disease, and other ailments.[12–14] From the perspective of traditional Chinese medicine, RR plays a therapeutic role in psoriasis by strengthening the body. RR is one of the most frequently used and effective Chinese medicines in psoriasis therapy, and its efficacy and safety have been reported in small clinical samples for a long time.[15] However, its specific modern mechanisms have not been systematically elucidated in detail. Therefore, the present study is particularly important to investigate the mechanism of RR in the treatment of psoriasis.

Network pharmacology, a significant approach in systems biology research, examines the systemic interaction between disease and drugs, aligning with the multi-compound and multi-target mechanisms of Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM).[16]

The present study employed network pharmacology and molecular docking techniques to investigate the targets and associated signaling pathways of RR in psoriasis treatment. Additionally, effective active ingredients of RR were identified through cellular experiments; to elucidate the mechanism of RR in treating psoriasis (Fig. 1).

2. Approaches and techniques

2.1. Analysis of network pharmacology

2.1.1. Database of main active ingredients of RR

The chemical composition of RR was determined by searching the literature[17–19] and mining the TCMSP (https://old.tcmsp-e.com/tcmsp.php) online database. In TCMSP database based on pharmacokinetics, we searched for the chemical constituents of RR with the keyword “Radix Rehmanniae” and screened the active ingredients using oral bioavailability ≥ 30% and drug-like ≥ 0.18 as criteria. Then, all active ingredients were saved in mol2 format.

2.1.2. Target screening of RR and psoriasis

OMIM (https://omim.org/), Therapeutic Target Database (http://db.idrblab.net/ttd), GeneCards (https://www.genecards.org/), and DrugBank (https://www.drugbank.com/) were searched using the keyword “psoriasis” for the target screening of psoriasis. Simultaneously, the RR and psoriasis target proteins were submitted to UniProt (https://www.uniprot.org/) with the filter ‘Homo sapiens,’ followed by their conversion into gene names. Afterward, the RR and psoriasis targets were inputted into the Venn online database (http://bioinformatics.psb.ugent.be/webtools/Venn/) to obtain common targets. Additionally, a Venn diagram of the common targets was obtained.

2.1.3. Drug–disease intersection target gene network and core target gene

First, the intersection genes of drugs and diseases were entered into the STRING online database (https://cn.string-db.org/). The species was entered as “Homo sapiens,” with the lowest interaction threshold set at 0.4. Then protein–protein interactions (PPI) were used to create the PPI network diagram, where protein interactions were exported in TSV format. After importing the TSV file into Cytoscape (version 3.8.0), the Cytohubba plug-in was adopted to acquire the top 10 hub proteins within the PPI network.

2.1.4. Gene ontology (GO) enrichment analysis and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment analysis

To perform the GO enrichment analysis and KEGG enrichment analysis, we utilized the online platform Metascape (https://metascape.org/gp/index.html), which allowed us to make sense of the GO enrichment and KEGG pathway annotation specifically for the species “Homo sapiens.”

2.1.5. Molecular docking verification

The protein receptor 3D structure was obtained from the RSCB PDB database (https://www.rcsb.org/) and saved in a PDB file. Using PyMOL software (version 2.5.8), the ligand and water molecules were eliminated, resulting in another PDB file. Finally, the file was imported into the AutoDock software (version 4.2) for further analysis. Steps such as hydrogenation and charge calculations were carried out and then converted into pdbqt format files. The small molecule ligands saved in the mol2 format were then entered into AutoDock software for pre-docking and saved as pdbqt format files. The molecular docking process between receptor macromolecules and ligand small molecules was performed using AutoDock. The minimum binding energy obtained from molecular docking was recognized as a criterion to assess the binding activity of the receptor proteins and ligand small molecules.

2.2. Experimental validation

2.2.1. Material

MedChemExpress was the source of aucubin (CAS:479-98-1) with a purity of 99.49%. The Cell Resource Center, Shanghai Academy of Biological Sciences, and Chinese Academy of Sciences provided Raw264.7 cells. Gibco (Grand Island, NY) was used to purchase high-glucose Dulbecco modified Eagle’s medium and fetal bovine serum. Peprotech (Cranbury, NJ) sold the lipopolysaccharides (LPS). 3-(4,5-dimethyl-2-thiazolyl)-2,5-diphenyl-2-H-tetrazolium bromide (MTT) and dimethyl sulfoxide, which were acquired from Sigma Chemicals Co. (St. Louis, MO), were 3-(4,5-dimethyl-thiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazoliumbromide and dimethylsulfoxide, respectively. Trizol solution was obtained from Life Technologies (Carlsbad, CA). RNA reverse transcription was performed using an EZB kit. AG was used for quantitative PCR. RIPA buffer and antibodies against pp38(1:1000; 4631, CST) and p38 (1:1000; 8690S, CST) were obtained from Cell Signaling Technology (CST). Tubulin (1:5000) was purchased from AffBiotech. Roche Applied Science (Penzberg, Germany) provided protease inhibitors. The bicinchoninic acid protein assay kit was acquired from Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA). YEASEN was the source of the ECL reagent. Methotrexate (MTX) was supplied by Shanghai Pharmaceutical Group Co., Shanghai. Reverse transcription machinery, a Real-Time PCR System (Santa Clara, CA), a Multifunctional Enzyme Labeling Instrument, an Electrophoresis Instrument, a Membrane Transfer Instrument, an Automatic Chemiluminescence System, CO2 incubator, and a biological safety cabinet were used.

2.2.2. Cells culture and MTT analysis

RAW 264.7 cells were inoculated at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well in complete medium added with 10% fetal bovine serum. Following cell adhesion, the cells were treated with various concentrations of aucubin (0, 0.625, 1.25, 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 40, and 80 µM) for 24 and 48 hours. Next, the wells were added to the MTT solution at a concentration of 0.5 mg/mL. After 2 hours, the liquid from the cell was discarded and dimethyl sulfoxide was added to each well. To determine cell viability, cells were agitated on a shaker for 10 minutes. Subsequently, the absorbance at 570 nm was measured by a TECAN enzyme marker, located in Männedorf, Switzerland.

2.2.3. Reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction

After completing the modeling process, total RNA was extracted according to the guidelines provided by the TRIzol reagent. Next, NANO drop 2000 was applied to assess the purity and concentration of the entire RNA sample. The EZB kit was used for reverse transcription of cDNA. Subsequently, 2 duplicate wells were set up, samples were spiked following the AG reagent manuals, and RT-PCR was achieved using a Quantstudio7Flex fluorescent quantitative PCR. The primer sequences used are listed in Table 1. Confirmation of the reaction’s specificity relies on analysis of the melting curve. The ∆∆Ct method was employed to assess the relative quantification of target gene expression levels, and the results were analyzed using the GraphPad software.

2.2.4. Analysis of Western blotting

RAW 264.7 cells were evenly distributed in 6-well plates and attached to the surface. Subsequently, they were treated with aucubin (20 µM, 40 µM, and 80 µM) and methotrexate (1 mmol/L) for 24 hours. Then, they were stimulated with LPS (1 μg/mL). The supernatants of the cells were discarded, and the cells were washed 3 times with cold PBS before being lysed overnight in RIPA buffer with the addition of protease inhibitors and phosphatase inhibitors. The following day, the samples were spun at 4 °C 12,000 rcf for 15 minutes, after which the liquid was removed. The bicinchoninic acid kit was used to determine protein concentration. After separation by 10% acrylamide SDS-PAGE, the proteins were transferred onto a polyvinylidene difluoride membrane, closed with 5% milk, washed with TBST, and then sequentially incubated with primary antibody, rabbit anti-p38, and p38. Finally, the membrane was incubated with the secondary antibody HRRP goat anti-rabbit IgG, followed by washing. Finally, following the guidelines provided by the ECL Chemiluminescent Ultrasensitive Chromogenic Kit (Yeason, Shenzhen), the bands were visualized using ChemiDoc Touch (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA), and the gray values of the strips were evaluated by ImageJ software.

2.3. Statistical analysis

All data were legally publicly available and therefore did not require additional ethical approval. The data were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation by one-way ANOVA, where P < 05 was regarded as significant. Statistical results are graphically presented using GraphPad Software (version 9.3).

3.5. Molecular docking

Molecular docking was performed on the key components of RR and psoriasis targets. If the binding energy is negative, it indicates that the ligand and receptor have the ability to bind naturally without any external force. Currently, there is a lack of consistent criteria for evaluating active molecular targets. Successful docking is typically indicated when the binding energy between the ligand and receptor is below ‐1.2 Kcal/mol, according to general belief. Meanwhile, the greater the absolute value of the binding energy, the greater the possibility of ligand–receptor binding. For the screening of the active components of RR against psoriasis, this study selected the constituents with binding energy <‐5.0 kJ/mol as the foundation.

As shown in Table 4, molecular docking analysis revealed that stigmasterol had the most negative binding energy (‐19.414 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6A) among the tested compounds, followed by CASP3 with stigmasterol (‐19.079 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6B), PTGS2 with beta-sitosterol (‐14.393 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6C), MAPK8 with isoacteoside (‐13.807 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6D), PTGS2 with ferulic acid (‐13.389 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6E), TNF with aucubin (‐11.046 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6F), PTGS2 with 5-hydroxymethylfurfural (‐11.046 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6G), NFKBIA with aucubin (‐7.196 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6H), CASP3 with beta-sitosterol (‐5.230 kJ/mol) (Fig. 6I). The binding energies were much <‐5.0k J/mol (Table 4), indicates that the ligand binds to the receptor with a stable structure, low conformational energy and high binding activity.

3.6. Aucubin inhibits Raw264.7 cell proliferation

Aucubin (Fig. 7A) is an active ingredient of RR, which has a strong affinity for psoriasis targets and has not been reported in psoriasis-related studies. Hence, this study was carried out to identify the impacts of aucubin on RAW264.7 cells to investigate potential mechanisms. RAW264.7 cells were treated with aucubin (0, 0.625, 1.25, 2.5, 5, 10, 20, 40, and 80 µM) for 24 and 48 hours. Later, cell viability was determined by MTT assay. The findings indicated that aucubin suppressed the viability of Raw264.7 cells in a manner that was dependent on the dosage as illustrated (Fig. 7B and C).

3.8. pp38/p38 signaling mediated the effects of aucubin in RAW264.7

The effects of aucubin in RAW264.7 were mediated by the signaling of pp38/p38. Suppression of the p38MAPK signaling pathway decreases oxidative stress, and levels of inflammatory cytokines, and hinders abnormal blood vessel growth, thereby playing a crucial role in psoriasis. In order to study the anti-inflammatory properties of aucubin in psoriasis, the immunoblotting technique was employed to analyze the expression of p38 protein in RAW264.7 cells (Fig. 9A). After the administration of aucubin, the inhibition of p38 phosphorylation was observed (Fig. 9B). 4. Discussion

Psoriasis is a chronic, inflammatory skin condition that is resistant to treatment and is caused by communication between the outer layer of skin cells, blood vessel cells in the skin, and cells of the immune system. During the initial phases of psoriasis, there is growth and vasodilation of small blood vessels in the affected area, contributing to increased permeability of the nearby vessel walls. This leads to the infiltration of neutrophils and T cells into the epidermis, which further increases the permeability of vascular and enhances the formation of vascular. Additionally, inflammatory cytokines increase, such as TNF-α, and interleukins-1. Excessive growth of epidermal keratinocytes, impaired differentiation, and increased production of pro-inflammatory mediators[20] are additionally promoted by these cytokines, leading to the progression of psoriasis. Systemic inflammation caused by these cytokines has been linked to various other conditions associated with psoriasis, such as overweight, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular disease. Thus, the suppression of inflammation and atypical vascular growth plays a key role in the treatment of psoriasis. Although the pathophysiological mechanisms of psoriasis have been elucidated, existing medications have not achieved a complete cure for psoriasis, and psoriasis medication remains a challenge. The remarkable effectiveness of Chinese medicine in treating psoriasis has been proved in previous studies, which have also indicated a critical role of RR in psoriasis treatment.[21,22] However, the precise pharmacological mechanism remains uncertain.

RR, first mentioned in Shen Nong Ben Cao Jing, is obtained from the dried root of Radix Rehmanniae by “nine steams and nine suns,” and is known as a “high-grade” herbal medicine in China. Recent pharmacological studies have revealed that RR contains over 70 substances, including cyclic enol ether terpenes, flavonoids, phenylethanol glycosides, and fatty acid lipids, which have diverse pharmacological effects on the hematological, immune, and cardiovascular systems.[10] VEGFR2 is a target that plays a role in angiogenesis, whereas AKT acts as a molecule downstream of VEGFR2. In tumor cells, the VEGFR2/AKT signaling pathway controls pathological processes, including cell growth, movement, cell death, and formation of blood vessels in tumors.[23] A study revealed that RR can suppress growth and invasion, and trigger cell death in cancer cells by blocking the VEGFR2/Akt signaling pathway.[24] RR has emerged as a prospective treatment option for type 1 diabetes by effectively improving hyperglycemia, inflammation, and oxidative stress in diabetic mice.[25] Furthermore, a recent investigation discovered that RR alleviated oxidative stress caused by 6-hydroxydopamine in neuronal cells by inhibiting p38MAPK signaling. This finding suggests that RR could be a viable treatment choice for Parkinson disease.[26] Accordingly, it has been hypothesized that RR exerts its therapeutic effects on psoriasis through mechanisms such as inhibition of inflammation, improvement of apoptotic dysfunction, vascular abnormalities, and attenuation of cell proliferation.

This study included a combination of 20 active components of RR and 33 therapeutic targets for psoriasis, which were obtained from the TCMSP database and literature review. The top 5 active ingredients with the highest association with targets and pathways were isoacteoside, aucubin, ferulic acid, stigmasterol, and beta-sitosterol. Modern studies have also shown that the active components of RR include catalpol, phytosterol, beta-sitosterol, myricetin, ferulic acid, and 5-hydroxymethylfurfural.[27,28] Its active ingredient, catalpol, has been reported to inhibit inflammation and tumor angiogenesis.[29,30] and ameliorates psoriasis through SIRT1-mediated NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways.[31] Hence, exploring the additional active constituents of RR and their therapeutic mechanisms in psoriasis is of immense importance. However, the existing literature on RR reveals that there are no systematic studies on the mechanisms of active ingredient-targets-signaling pathways against psoriasis. Hence, this study employed network pharmacology and molecular docking techniques to examine the efficient targets, pathways, and precise mechanisms of RR in managing psoriasis. The PPI network identified 10 key proteins, including TNF, IL6, PTGS2, CASP3, ESR1, PPARG, MAPK8, NR3C1, HNOX1, and NFKB1A, which are thought to be linked to inflammation, proliferation, apoptosis, and irregular vascular proliferation. Several studies[32] have reported that TNF-α and interleukin-6 have aggravated inflammation in psoriasis. Prostaglandin Endoperoxide Synthase, alternatively referred to as cyclooxygenase (COX), is composed of 2 isoforms, namely COX-1 and COX-2. In line with the study conducted by Garshick MS et al,[33] psoriasis severity and impaired vascular health are closely linked to COX-1. Furthermore, examination of platelet transcriptional profiles revealed that COX-1 signaling plays a crucial role in connecting psoriasis severity, platelet activity, and endothelial cell inflammation. CASP3, also known as Caspase-3, is an enzyme that plays a pivotal role in programmed cell death (i.e., apoptosis). The activation of apoptotic cells through both exogenous and endogenous pathways is essential in maintaining tissue balance, promoting cell growth, eliminating damaged or infected cells, and contributing to disease pathogenesis.[34] The progression of psoriasis is influenced by a range of genetic and environmental factors, such as gender.[35] Studies conducted in various countries have indicated that females have a greater propensity for developing psoriasis than males, and the severity of psoriasis is more pronounced in females than in males.[36–38] A recent study demonstrated that the estrogen receptor has a suppressive effect on inflammation in psoriatic mice. This is achieved by inhibiting neutrophils and macrophage functions, which include the production of IL-1β. Consequently, there is a reduction in the production of IL-17A in the skin.[39] Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor γ (PPAR-γ) controls the metabolism of glucose, lipids, and amino acids in immune cells. Activation of PPAR-γ enhances cellular differentiation, decreases proliferation rates, regulates immune responses, and aids in maintaining healthier skin function.[40] Therefore, it is hypothesized that RR exerts its therapeutic effects on psoriasis mainly through modulation of inflammation, apoptosis, and vascular abnormalities. The docking results indicated that interaction strengths of isoacteoside with MAPK8, aucubin with TNF, ferulic acid with PTGS2, stigmasterol with PTGS2, and beta-sitosterol with CASP3 were all below ‐5 kJ/mol. This suggests that these constituents may potentially play a crucial role in psoriasis using RR.

Fourteen pathways were identified using GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. The results revealed that the utilization of RR for psoriasis treatment was linked to pathways related to tumors, lipid metabolism, HIF, and various other signaling pathways. These findings align with the outcomes of the PPI network, indicating that RR might have a potential impact on psoriasis treatment via the aforementioned signaling pathways. HIF-1, also known as hypoxia-inducible factor-1, is composed of 2 subunits: HIF-1α and HIF-1β. Psoriasis lesions and patients with psoriasis have been found to exhibit increased levels of HIF-1α in the bloodstream.[41–43] The significant correlation between increased HIF-1α levels and disease severity indicated the crucial involvement of HIF-1α in the development of psoriasis. The reason behind the promotion of angiogenesis and skin inflammation, and the worsening of psoriasis development may be the rise of HIF-1α in epidermal keratinocytes, leading to a vicious cycle of angiogenesis, hyperproliferation, and inflammation.[44] Therefore, pharmacological therapies directed at controlling HIF-1α levels may be effective in controlling psoriasis. Psoriasis involves an immune response, in which pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-1, and IL-6, play a role. MAPK influences psoriasis progression by triggering these pro-inflammatory cytokines involved in signaling, cell differentiation, proliferation, apoptosis, and immune response. Meanwhile, MAPK activation promotes epidermal hyperproliferation, and p38MAPK hyperphosphorylation disrupts skin homeostasis and induces cellular stress, thereby exacerbating psoriasis progression.[45,46]

The anti-inflammatory effect of aucubin in RAW264.7 was confirmed during this investigation. To assess the impact of different levels of aucubin (20 µM, 40 µM, 80 µM) on the viability of RAW264.7, an initial MTT assay was conducted. The findings indicated that aucubin suppressed the viability of Raw264.7 in a manner that was dependent on the dosage. Furthermore, the RT-PCR analysis revealed the presence of TNFα, IL 6, and IL1β mRNA expression in RAW264.7 cells treated with aucubin for 24 hours. The findings indicated that the levels of TNFα, IL 6, and IL1β mRNA expression were notably increased in cells stimulated with LPS (1 µg/mL) compared to cells that were not stimulated by LPS (1 µg/mL). However, aucubin decreased the levels of TNFα, IL 6, and IL1β mRNA expression. Given the association between the classical MAPK pathway and psoriasis regulation, our investigation focused on examining corresponding protein expression in RAW264.7 stimulated with LPS. The Western blotting findings indicated that aucubin effectively suppressed the phosphorylation of p38. Hence, aucubin might have a healing function in psoriasis by obstructing the MAPK signaling pathway, consequently restraining the production of inflammatory substances like TNFα, IL6, and IL1β. These results provide a cornerstone for gaining an understanding of the molecular biological mechanisms involved in the treatment of psoriasis with Radix Rehmanniae.

In this paper, the possible mechanisms of RR and its active ingredients in treating psoriasis were first preliminarily explored through network pharmacology and molecular docking. Moreover, cellular experiments initially verified the role of its active ingredients in the treatment of psoriasis, providing valuable insights into the treatment of psoriasis by RR. However, there is still a need to continue the in-depth research in this study. Firstly, well-established large-sample clinical trials are needed to support the efficacy and safety of psoriasis. Secondly, more advanced experimental techniques will be adopted to study other active ingredients to reveal the complex molecular regulatory network of multiple compounds acting synergistically in RR. Critically, we are particularly concerned about misdiagnosis in elderly patients.[47,48] As an immune-mediated chronic inflammatory disease with complex pathogenesis and diverse clinical manifestations, psoriasis is easily confused with other skin diseases, especially in elderly patients. Exploring how to utilize new technologies (e.g., artificial intelligence, biomarker detection, etc) to improve the diagnosis and treatment of psoriasis with TCM is our future research direction. Finally, we will actively explore the potential of new technologies to empower the application of TCM in healthcare, especially those that can significantly enhance treatment and prevention. The above is of great significance in improving the mechanism of RR for psoriasis and solving clinical problems.

5. Conclusion

To summarize, this study utilized network pharmacology, molecular docking techniques, and experimental investigations to preliminarily examine how Radix Rehmanniae produces its therapeutic benefits on psoriasis via the “core component-core target-core pathway.” These findings indicate that the active components of Radix Rehmanniae, such as aucubin, reduced the levels of phosphorylated p38 protein by targeting TNFα and IL6, thereby ameliorating inflammation and ultimately achieving the therapeutic outcome for psoriasis. However, there are still some limitations to this study. We will gradually adopt more rigorous protocol design, more advanced experimental methods, more complex biological models, and stronger teamwork in order to continuously improve our research.

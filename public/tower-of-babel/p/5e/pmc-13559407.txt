Surgical approach and prognosis in patients with ≤2 cm grade 3 lung adenocarcinoma: a multivariable analysis of disease-free and overall survival
Authors: Niu Niu, Wanli Cheng, Junjie Zhao, Qihui Cai, Liang Zhou, Weibo Qi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHigh-grade histological patterns are established risk factors for poor prognosis in lung adenocarcinoma. Whether sublobar resection is oncologically equivalent to lobectomy in ≤2 cm grade 3 lesions remains unclear. This study compared the effects of different surgical approaches on perioperative outcomes and long-term survival.MethodsWe retrospectively reviewed 239 patients who underwent curative-intent resection for grade 3 invasive lung adenocarcinoma ≤2 cm between January 2014 and December 2023. Patients were stratified into lobectomy (n=121), segmentectomy (n=74), and wedge resection (n=44) groups. Perioperative and oncological outcomes were compared among the three groups. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) and weighted Cox models were used to compare survival. Multivariable analysis was performed using Firth’s penalized Cox regression.ResultsNo significant differences were observed in preoperative characteristics among the three groups (P>0.05). Significant differences were observed in operative time among the three groups (P=0.003). The wedge resection group had the shortest operative time and the least blood loss, whereas the lobectomy group had the longest operative time and the greatest blood loss. Postoperative pathological features, including high-grade patterns proportion, spread through air spaces (STAS), perineural invasion (PNI), lymphovascular invasion (LVI), and tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage, were comparable across groups (P>0.05). However, lobectomy was associated with significantly greater numbers of dissected lymph node stations (P<0.001) and total lymph nodes retrieved (P=0.02). After IPTW adjustment, the 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) rates were 85.6%, 84.7%, and 58.4%, respectively; the weighted log-rank test showed a significant difference (P=0.03), with wedge resection associated with significantly worse DFS versus lobectomy [weighted hazard ratio (HR) 2.53; 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.21–5.28; P=0.01], whereas segmentectomy was comparable (weighted HR 1.05; 95% CI: 0.46–2.41; P=0.90). The 5-year overall survival (OS) rates were 85.7%, 89.3%, and 82.8%, respectively, with no significant intergroup difference (χ2=0.442, P=0.80). Multivariable Firth analysis identified preoperative carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) positivity (HR 2.60, 95% CI: 1.27–5.11, P=0.01) and tumor size >10 mm (HR 3.56, 95% CI: 1.31–13.35, P=0.01) as independent risk factors for DFS. Micropapillary component of ≥5% was an independent risk factor for OS (HR 3.92, 95% CI: 1.57–11.73, P=0.003). Although surgical approach was not significantly associated with OS (P=0.47), wedge resection emerged as a borderline unfavorable risk factor for worse DFS (HR 2.03, 95% CI: 0.99–3.95; P=0.054).ConclusionsIn patients with ≤2 cm high-grade lung adenocarcinoma, segmentectomy demonstrated comparable long-term survival to lobectomy. Wedge resection, however, showed an unfavorable trend in 5-year DFS rate and should be cautiously selected.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma, high-grade patterns, sublobar resection, segmentectomy, prognosis

Highlight box

Key findings

• In patients with ≤2 cm invasive lung adenocarcinoma containing high-grade components (≥20%), segmentectomy achieved comparable 5-year disease-free survival (DFS) (84.7%) and overall survival (OS) (90.5%) to lobectomy (85.6% and 86.3%, respectively) after inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) adjustment. Wedge resection demonstrated a significantly lower 5-year DFS rate (58.4%) compared with lobectomy after IPTW adjustment [weighted hazard ratio (HR) 2.53; 95% confidence interval: 1.21–5.28; P=0.01], although no significant OS difference was observed. Preoperative CEA positivity and tumor size >10 mm (HR 3.56; P=0.01) were independent risk factors for DFS, whereas micropapillary component ≥5% was an independent risk factor for OS (HR 3.92; P=0.003).

What is known and what is new?

• High-grade histological patterns are established risk factors for unfavorable outcomes in lung adenocarcinoma. Whether sublobar resection is oncologically adequate for early-stage grade 3 lesions remains undetermined.

• This study newly demonstrates that segmentectomy provides equivalent long-term survival to lobectomy in this high-risk cohort, whereas wedge resection may be associated with inferior DFS.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• Segmentectomy should be considered a viable alternative to lobectomy for ≤2 cm high-grade lung adenocarcinoma. Wedge resection should be cautiously selected and not routinely recommended. Preoperative carcinoembryonic antigen levels, tumor size, and micropapillary component assessment should be integrated into risk stratification to guide individualized surgical decision-making.

Introduction

According to the 2021 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of thoracic tumors, invasive lung adenocarcinoma is categorized based on histological patterns, with high-grade patterns—particularly micropapillary, solid, and cribriform—established as independent predictors of aggressive tumor biology (1,2). These patterns are closely associated with lymphovascular invasion (LVI), spread through air spaces (STAS), and lymph node metastasis (3). The 2020 International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer (IASLC) grading system, subsequently incorporated into the 2021 WHO classification, further classified grade 3 lesions (high-grade components ≥20%) as a high-risk subgroup, which remains associated with significantly adverse prognosis even in small, node-negative tumors (4,5).

For early-stage non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC; tumor size ≤2 cm), lobectomy with systematic mediastinal lymph node dissection has long been considered the standard of care (6). However, landmark randomized controlled trials, including JCOG0802/WJOG4607L and CALGB140503, demonstrated that anatomic segmentectomy was non-inferior to lobectomy regarding overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) (7,8), driving a paradigm shift toward lung-sparing surgical strategies. Nevertheless, these trials predominantly enrolled patients with favorable histological characteristics; subgroup analyses for high-grade lesions were underpowered due to limited sample sizes, leaving the oncological safety of sublobar resection in grade 3 adenocarcinoma undefined. Although sublobar resection has been increasingly adopted for early-stage non-small cell lung cancer, whether more extensive resection, such as lobectomy, is warranted for high-grade histological subtypes (e.g., micropapillary and solid patterns) to ensure adequate resection margins and thorough lymph node dissection remains unresolved due to a paucity of high-quality evidence. Within the spectrum of sublobar resection, wedge resection—while maximizing pulmonary parenchymal preservation—lacks anatomic surgical margins and systematic hilar lymph node evaluation (9). Its application in high-grade tumors remains controversial, with inconsistent data regarding local recurrence risk (9-11).

Therefore, this retrospective study compared perioperative outcomes and long-term survival among lobectomy, segmentectomy, and wedge resection in patients with ≤2 cm lung adenocarcinoma harboring high-grade patterns ≥20%, with the aim of providing evidence-based guidance for individualized surgical decision-making in this specific patient population. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1376/rc).

Methods

Patient selection

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the First Hospital of Jiaxing (approval No. 2024-LY-788), and individual consent for this retrospective analysis was waived. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. We retrospectively collected data from patients who underwent radical surgical treatment for lung adenocarcinoma at the Department of Thoracic Surgery between January 2014 and December 2023 and all cases underwent surgical treatment via video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS). All patients underwent comprehensive preoperative staging evaluation, including contrast-enhanced chest computed tomography (CT), brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), bone scintigraphy, abdominal imaging, and cervical lymph node ultrasonography, to exclude clinical metastasis.

Inclusion criteria: (I) patients with tumors ≤2 cm in diameter who received either standard lobectomy or sublobar resection (SLR; segmentectomy or wedge resection); (II) postoperative pathological confirmation of primary lung adenocarcinoma; (III) pathological confirmation of grade 3 (high-grade patterns ≥20%) according to the IASLC grading system; (IV) underwent radical lobectomy or intentional sublobar resection (ISR), including segmentectomy and wedge resection; (V) systematic or selective lymph node dissection or sampling performed; (VI) complete clinical and follow-up data available.

Exclusion criteria: (I) history of neoadjuvant therapy; (II) multiple primary lung cancers; (III) concomitant other malignancies; (IV) biopsy only or R2 resection; (V) node-positive patients.

From a cohort of 9,876 patients who underwent surgical resection for lung cancer, 239 patients with pathologically confirmed invasive lung adenocarcinoma (tumor diameter ≤2 cm, histological grade 3) were selected based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria. These patients were divided into the lobectomy group (n=121) and the sublobar resection group (n=118), which included 74 segmentectomies and 44 wedge resections.

Data collection and definitions

The following clinical variables were retrospectively extracted from electronic medical records: demographic characteristics (age and sex), smoking history, preoperative comorbidities, consolidation-to-tumor ratio (CTR), surgical details (surgical approach, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and extent of lymph node dissection), postoperative pathological characteristics [tumor size; pathological stage according to the 8th edition of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system; predominant and minor histological subtypes and their respective percentages; LVI; STAS; and perineural invasion (PNI)], and perioperative complications.

Histological high-grade patterns assessment

Histological grading was performed according to the IASLC grading system for invasive lung adenocarcinoma. Grade 3 was defined as tumors with >20% high-grade patterns (solid, micropapillary, cribriform, or complex glandular) or tumors predominantly composed of these patterns.

Follow-up

Patients were followed up through outpatient visits, telephone calls, or correspondence. The last follow-up date was April 20, 2026. The primary endpoint was DFS, defined as the time from the date of surgery to the first occurrence of disease recurrence (locoregional or distant) or death from any cause. Secondary endpoints included OS, defined as the time from the date of surgery to death from any cause, and perioperative complications.

Statistical analysis

Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages, and were compared among groups using the Chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and analyzed using the independent-samples t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate. Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was performed to compare OS and DFS among the three surgical groups. Propensity scores were estimated using multinomial logistic regression with seven preoperative baseline covariates: age, sex, smoking history, Comorbidities, tumor size, CTR, preoperative carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) (12). Covariate balance was assessed using standardized mean differences (SMDs), with an absolute SMD <0.1 indicating negligible imbalance. Weighted Cox proportional hazards models were used to compare OS and DFS after IPTW adjustment.

Given the limited number of recurrence and death events in this study, the risk of model overfitting in multivariable analysis was carefully considered. We calculated the events per variable (EPV) ratio and performed sensitivity analysis using Firth’s penalized maximum likelihood estimation to mitigate the impact of small-sample bias on the robustness of the results. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) and R version 4.3.1.A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics and perioperative outcomes

A total of 239 patients with pathologically confirmed lung adenocarcinoma (tumor diameter ≤2 cm with high-grade patterns) were enrolled in this study, comprising 121 patients in the lobectomy group, 74 in the segmentectomy group, and 44 in the wedge resection group. No statistically significant differences were observed among the three groups with respect to baseline characteristics, including age, sex, smoking history, family history of malignancy, tumor size, imaging-specific features, preoperative CEA status, and number of preoperative comorbidities (all P>0.05) (Table 1).

Regarding perioperative outcomes, significant differences were found among the three groups in operative duration (P=0.003). The wedge resection group demonstrated the shortest operative time, followed by the segmentectomy group, whereas the lobectomy group exhibited the longest operative time. The wedge resection group demonstrated the most favorable results, followed by the segmentectomy group, whereas the lobectomy group exhibited the longest operative time. Furthermore, a trend toward significant differences was observed in postoperative drainage volume and intraoperative blood loss among the three groups (P=0.08 and P=0.09, respectively). However, no significant differences were identified in postoperative hospital stay or the incidence of postoperative complications (P=0.51 and P=0.31, respectively) (Table 1).

Comparison of postoperative pathological characteristics and treatment status

Postoperative pathological analysis revealed no significant differences among the lobectomy, segmentectomy, and wedge resection groups in the proportions of cribriform pattern, micropapillary pattern, or high-grade patterns, nor in the incidence of STAS, PNI, LVI, and TNM staging (all P>0.05). Although the proportion of solid pattern differed significantly among the three groups (15.98%±24.83% vs. 7.77%±15.62% vs. 17.84%±24.90%; P=0.02), the overall proportion of high-grade patterns remained comparable across groups (62.24%±29.23% vs. 58.07%±27.36% vs. 60.55%±27.44%; P=0.61). Regarding lymph node dissection, both the number of lymph node dissection stations and the total number of lymph nodes dissected differed significantly among the three groups (P<0.001 and P=0.02, respectively), with the lobectomy group having the highest values, followed by the segmentectomy group, and the wedge resection group having the lowest values. Surgical margins were also significantly narrower in the wedge resection group (2.91±1.81 vs. 2.52±1.53 vs. 2.12±1.55 cm; P=0.02). Regarding gene mutation status, no significant difference was observed across the groups (P=0.63). The majority of patients in all groups had not undergone molecular testing (54.5%, 59.5%, and 61.4%, respectively). Among those tested, epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations were identified in 20.7%, 16.2%, and 15.9% of patients, respectively. Anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) rearrangements were rare, detected in 0.8%, 5.4%, and 2.3% of patients, respectively. Systemic treatment timing did not differ significantly among the groups (P=0.97). The majority of patients were treatment-naïve (82.6%, 82.4%, and 79.5%, respectively). Pre-progression treatment was administered in 14.9%, 16.2%, and 18.2% of patients, respectively, while post-progression treatment was uncommon (2.5%, 1.4%, and 2.3%, respectively). Similarly, no significant difference was observed in treatment modality (P=0.85). Most patients received no systemic treatment (82.6%, 82.4%, and 79.5%, respectively). Targeted therapy was the most common treatment modality among those who received systemic therapy (14.9%, 16.2%, and 18.2%, respectively). Chemotherapy and combined therapy were rarely used. Adjuvant therapy was administered in 17.4%, 17.6%, and 20.5% of patients, respectively, with no significant intergroup difference (P=0.85) (Table 2).

IPTW-adjusted analysis of surgical approach, recurrence, and survival

Over a median follow-up of 48 months (range, 24–132 months), 38 (15.9%) patients developed recurrence or metastasis, and 29 (12.1%) died. Among the 38 patients with recurrence, distant metastasis occurred in 21 (55.3%), locoregional recurrence in 13 (34.2%), and combined recurrence in 4 (10.5%). Locoregional recurrence occurred in 4 (3.3%), 4 (5.4%), and 5 (11.4%) patients in the lobectomy, segmentectomy, and wedge resection groups, respectively. Although the overall comparison was not statistically significant (χ2=4.073, P=0.13), wedge resection demonstrated a borderline higher locoregional recurrence rate relative to lobectomy [relative risk (RR) 3.45, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.97–12.22; P=0.06] and a numerically higher rate relative to segmentectomy (RR 2.11, 95% CI: 0.59–7.56; P=0.29). Distant metastatic sites included lung (n=8, 38.1%), brain (n=4, 19.0%), bone (n=4, 19.0%), liver/adrenal (n=2, 9.5%), and multiple sites (n=3, 14.3%).

To mitigate treatment-selection bias, IPTW was performed using seven preoperative covariates, yielding adequate balance with all absolute SMDs <0.1 after adjustment (range, 0.011–0.086) (Table 3; Figure 1A). The distributions of trimmed stabilized IPTW weights were well-centered with adequate overlap across the three surgical groups (Figure 1B). The 5-year DFS rates were 85.1%, 83.8%, and 65.7% before IPTW, and 85.6% (95% CI: 78.9–92.9%), 84.7 (95% CI: 74.9%–95.9%), and 58.4% (95% CI: 41.4–82.5%) after IPTW, for lobectomy, segmentectomy, and wedge resection, respectively. Before adjustment, no significant difference was observed (P=0.13); after IPTW, the weighted log-rank test showed a significant difference (P=0.03). Wedge resection was associated with worse DFS versus lobectomy (weighted HR, 2.53; 95% CI: 1.21–5.28; P=0.01), whereas segmentectomy was comparable [weighted hazard ratio (HR), 1.05; 95% CI: 0.46–2.41; P=0.90] (Figure 2A,2B). The 5-year OS rates were 85.7%, 89.3%, and 82.8% before IPTW, and 86.3% (95% CI: 78.3–95.0%), 90.5% (95% CI: 82.8–98.9%), and 84.8% (95% CI: 73.3–98.1%) after IPTW, respectively. No significant difference was observed before (P=0.80) or after IPTW (P=0.68). Segmentectomy versus lobectomy: weighted HR, 0.87 (95% CI: 0.32–2.38; P=0.79); wedge resection versus lobectomy: weighted HR, 1.42 (95% CI: 0.52–3.89; P=0.50) (Figure 2C,2D).

Firth penalized Cox regression analysis of DFS and OS

Given the low event rates (recurrence: 15.9%; mortality: 12.1%), multivariable analysis was performed using Firth’s penalized partial likelihood method to obtain bias-reduced estimates with improved convergence properties. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis demonstrated favorable 5-year predictive performance for both DFS [area under the curve (AUC) =0.735] and OS (AUC =0.651) (Figure 3A,3B). Univariate Kaplan-Meier survival curves stratified by key prognostic factors are displayed in Figure 4. Univariable analysis identified three variables significantly associated with DFS: surgical approach (log-rank P=0.045), preoperative CEA level (log-rank P<0.001), and tumor size (log-rank P=0.01) (Figure 4A,4C,4E). These variables were subsequently entered into a multivariable Firth penalized Cox regression model. After adjusting for age, sex, LVI, and micropapillary component (MPC) proportion, preoperative CEA positivity (HR 2.60, 95% CI: 1.27–5.11, P=0.01) and tumor size >10 mm (HR 3.56, 95% CI: 1.31–13.35, P=0.01) remained independently associated with DFS, whereas surgical approach lost significance (HR 2.03, 95% CI: 0.99–3.95, P=0.054). Age, sex, and MPC proportion were not significantly associated with DFS (all P>0.05) (Table 4). Univariable analysis identified two variables significantly associated with OS: MPC proportion (log-rank P=0.001) and preoperative CEA level (log-rank P=0.02) (Figure 4D,4F). Although surgical approach was not significantly associated with OS (Figure 4B), it was retained in the multivariable model given its clinical relevance. After adjusting for age, sex, surgical approach, tumor size, and LVI, MPC proportion ≥5% remained independently associated with OS (HR 3.92, 95% CI: 1.57–11.73, P=0.003), whereas preoperative CEA positivity lost significance (HR 1.93, 95% CI: 0.79–4.37, P=0.14). None of the other covariates were significantly associated with OS in the multivariable model (all P>0.05) (Table 5).

Discussion

The principal finding of this retrospective study is that, among patients with IASLC grade 3 lung adenocarcinoma ≤2 cm in size, anatomic segmentectomy achieved comparable long-term survival to standard lobectomy, whereas wedge resection was associated with inferior DFS after adjustment for baseline covariates. Firth multivariable analysis also demonstrated that wedge resection was borderline significantly associated with worse DFS in the multivariable model (HR 2.03, 95% CI: 0.99–3.95; P=0.054), which is consistent with current comparable reports (13,14). These findings provide preliminary observational evidence for individualized surgical strategies based on pathological risk stratification. The oncological equivalence between segmentectomy and lobectomy observed in this study is consistent with the overall conclusions of the landmark randomized trials. The JCOG0802/WJOG4607L trial demonstrated the non-inferiority of segmentectomy relative to lobectomy with respect to OS in the general population (7,8), and the randomized controlled trial by Stamatis et al. observed similar survival trends in patients with tumors ≤2 cm (15). However, these studies enrolled a limited proportion of high-grade histological subgroups, and the 10-year follow-up results of JCOG0802 suggested a subtle advantage for lobectomy in long-term survival (16). In the present study, survival curves between segmentectomy and lobectomy were comparable in this specific population with high-grade patterns ≥20%, suggesting that anatomic segmentectomy may serve as an alternative to lobectomy in such selected patients, although this conclusion requires cautious extrapolation. The supplementary analysis of JCOG0802 data by Nakagawa et al. revealed specific risk factors for locoregional recurrence after segmentectomy (17). However, in the present study, which included grade 3 lesions ≤2 cm with pathologically negative lymph nodes, no increase in recurrence rate was observed in the segmentectomy group. Taken together, when tumor size is strictly limited to ≤2 cm, anatomic segmentectomy may maintain acceptable oncological safety, provided that adequate lymph node assessment is performed (6,17,18).

In this study, the 5-year DFS rates after IPTW adjustment were 85.6%, 84.7%, and 58.4%, respectively, with wedge resection showing a numerically lower rate. However, multivariable analysis using Firth’s penalized likelihood estimation revealed only a marginal association of wedge resection with worse DFS (HR 2.03, 95% CI: 0.99–3.95; P=0.054), suggesting that this difference likely reflects selection bias rather than an intrinsic oncological deficiency. Wedge resection lacks anatomic margins and systematic lymph node evaluation; indeed, in this study, it had the fewest dissected stations and total nodes retrieved, raising concerns regarding missed occult micrometastases (19). Surgical margins were also significantly narrower in the wedge resection group (2.12±1.55 vs. 2.52±1.53 vs. 2.91±1.81 cm; P=0.02), which likely contributed to its borderline higher locoregional recurrence rate relative to lobectomy (11.4% vs. 3.3%, P=0.06). Systemic treatment timing, modality, and adjuvant therapy were comparable (all P>0.05). These data are insufficient to support oncological equivalence between wedge resection and anatomic resection. Until randomized trials such as CALGB140503 produce mature results, wedge resection should be limited to peripheral tumors with frozen section confirmation of negative margins ≥tumor diameter, or to patients with compromised cardiopulmonary reserve who cannot tolerate anatomic resection, accompanied by intensified surveillance.

Although wedge resection was associated with significantly worse DFS after IPTW adjustment (weighted HR 2.53; P=0.01), no significant difference was observed in OS (weighted HR 1.42; P=0.50). Several factors may account for this discrepancy. First, effective post-recurrence therapies, particularly targeted therapies administered to 14.9–18.2% of patients across the three groups, may have attenuated the impact of recurrence on mortality (20,21). Second, the lower event rate for OS (29 deaths vs. 38 recurrences) resulted in reduced statistical power to detect a true difference. Third, the insufficient follow-up duration may have precluded the translation of DFS differences into OS divergence; late deaths attributable to recurrence after wedge resection may not yet have fully manifested (22).

In this study, MPC ≥5% was associated with OS (HR 3.92, 95% CI: 1.57–11.73; P=0.003) but did not demonstrate an independent association with DFS, which is inconsistent with some previous reports (23). One possible explanation is that in early-stage (IA) lesions, the aggressive biological behavior represented by MPC may be partially offset by complete surgical resection, rendering its impact on long-term survival more prominent, while its predictive value for early locoregional recurrence remains relatively limited. Additionally, this differential effect may reflect the distinct biological behavior of MPC (4). As described by Li et al. regarding the genomic and clinicopathological features of MPC and by Mikubo et al. regarding micropapillary/solid components as high-grade patterns in the IASLC grading system (5), MPC possesses a specific propensity for promoting systemic dissemination and LVI (6), thereby primarily affecting long-term survival rather than early locoregional recurrence. Matsushima et al. reported that MPC was significantly associated with upstaging of lymph node metastasis after surgery (24); however, the present study included only lymph node-negative patients, which may have limited the ability to observe the impact of MPC on recurrence patterns.

Tumor diameter >10 mm was identified as an independent risk factor for DFS (HR 3.56, 95% CI: 1.31–13.35; P=0.01), suggesting that even within the ≤2 cm range, small incremental increases in size may carry additional prognostic information. Traditionally, surgeons have favored more extensive lobectomy for tumors with high-grade patterns to ensure radical resection (25). The observational data from this study preliminarily suggest that when tumor size is strictly limited to ≤2 cm, anatomic segmentectomy may not significantly compromise oncological outcomes, provided that adequate margins are achieved.

The limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the retrospective design entails inherent selection bias; surgical approach was not randomly assigned, and surgeon judgment may have influenced group allocation. The lobectomy group underwent more extensive lymph node dissection, suggesting that the surgical team may have prioritized lobectomy for tumors suspected of having more aggressive biological behavior. Second, the number of events was limited (38 recurrences and 29 deaths), and despite correction using Firth’s penalized likelihood method, the multivariable model may still be subject to small-sample bias, which may limit the stability of some effect estimates. Third, this study represents a single-center experience with a relatively limited sample size, particularly given the small number of cases in the wedge resection group, which may have affected the statistical power for intergroup comparisons.

Conclusions

In patients with ≤2 cm grade 3 lung adenocarcinoma, segmentectomy demonstrated comparable long-term survival to lobectomy. Wedge resection, however, showed an unfavorable trend in 5-year DFS and should be cautiously selected. These findings require validation in prospective studies.

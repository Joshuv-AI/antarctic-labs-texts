Cardiovascular risk factor target control in Romanian adults with type 2 diabetes
Authors: Silvia Ana Luca, Raluca Malina Bungau, Andreea Herascu, Vlad Florian Avram, Sandra Lazar, Bogdan Timar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPatients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) frequently have increased cardiovascular (CV) risk, yet real-world control of guideline-recommended cardiometabolic risk factors remains suboptimal. We quantified the proportion of Romanian adults with T2D who were at recommended targets for key CV risk factors across ESC CV risk categories. In this single-center cross-sectional study, we recorded HbA1c, LDL-cholesterol (LDL-c), triglycerides and blood pressure (BP). Targets were defined using contemporary guideline recommendations (HbA1c <7.0%, BP <130/80 mmHg, triglycerides <150 mg/dL, and risk-category-specific LDL-c targets). BMI was reported descriptively and was not considered treatment target. We included 174 patients (median age 61 years; 50% women); 81.6% were at very high and 14.4% at high CV risk. Overall, 46.6% were at the HbA1c target, 20.1% at the LDL-c target, 58.0% at the triglyceride target, and 42.0% at the BP target. Across ESC risk categories, control of individual targets was consistently limited, with particularly low proportions at LDL-c target. In this real-world Romanian T2D cohort, a minority of patients were at recommended targets for major CV risk factors, especially LDL-c, underscoring the need for systematic, risk-based intensification of cardiometabolic management.Keywords: Type 2 diabetes, Cardiovascular risk, ESC risk categories, Guideline targets, Real-world studySubject terms: Cardiology, Diseases, Endocrinology, Health care, Medical research, Risk factors

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVD) remain the main cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, and despite major therapeutic advancements in this field, patients with CVD still face an overall poor prognosis, especially in the context of type 2 diabetes (T2D)1. T2D is both a major risk factor and a driver for CVD, particularly atherosclerotic CVD (ASCVD) and heart failure (HF), and these patients have both an increased risk of developing vascular complications and a higher mortality when compared to the general population2. This excess cardiovascular (CV) mortality seen in patients with T2D increases with each additional risk factor3. Both diseases share common pathophysiological mechanisms, creating an even higher burden in managing patients with T2D and CVD, where insulin resistance, chronic inflammation, increased oxidative stress, and atherogenic dyslipidemia play a pivotal role in the development of vascular complications4.

Another important consideration is that, even though patients with T2D have a two- to four-fold increase in the risk of developing CVD compared to the general population2, this risk is not evenly distributed within this population, with some individuals facing a higher risk than others. T2D was considered a coronary artery disease equivalent, with earlier research indicating that patients with T2D in primary prevention have the same risk of future CV events as an individual without T2D but with a prior myocardial infarction (MI). However, disease duration, risk factor burden and the presence of microvascular disease play an important role in determining individual risk5. Geographic region is also a significant determinant of CV mortality rates, with countries in Central or Eastern Europe being more susceptible to higher rates of CV events6.

With current evidence suggesting that a substantial proportion of patients with T2D fall into the very-high CV risk category, it is essential to support intensive management of modifiable risk factors through lifestyle and pharmacological interventions7. Landmark studies such as Steno-2 highlighted the importance of a multifactorial approach, moving beyond a glucose-centered strategy8. Accordingly, we focused on routinely assessed, guideline-addressed factors that are consistently available in clinical practice9: glycaemia (HbA1c), atherogenic lipids (LDL-c and triglycerides), and blood pressure10,11. We also described adiposity (BMI) as an important clinical characteristic, reported descriptively. Although other variables (e.g., smoking, albuminuria) are clinically important and contribute to CV risk, they were not evaluated as treatment targets in the present analysis due to differences in guideline operationalization and availability at the time of assessment.

Over the years, major scientific societies have developed and updated guidelines to assist clinicians by providing the latest recommendations and aiding clinical practice and decision-making12. Most recent recommendations include structured weight management strategies, BP and lipid control, lifestyle optimization, and pharmacological treatment. Moreover, CV risk should be individualized, and the 2023 ESC guidelines introduced a prediction model tailored for patients with T2D, SCORE2-Diabetes13. Assessing individual CV risk and clustering patients into CV risk categories has direct clinical implications because LDL-c targets and treatment strategies differ across the CV risk spectrum, with more ambitious lipid goals endorsed for higher risk groups14,15. However, real-world implementation remains suboptimal, with a large proportion of patients with T2D not being at recommended targets, particularly for lipids11,16.

Our primary objective was to quantify the proportion of patients with T2D meeting individual guideline-recommended targets for HbA1c, LDL-c, triglycerides, and BP.

Methods

Study design and patients

In this single-center, non-interventional, cross-sectional study, 174 patients with T2D aged 40 to 69 years, both attending scheduled outpatient visits as well as hospitalized for metabolic imbalances in the Diabetes Clinic of the “Pius Brînzeu” Emergency Hospital, Timișoara, Romania were enrolled, according to a consecutive-case principle. Patients aged above 69 or below 40 years and those with other types of diabetes were excluded from our study, since in these groups of patients, the CV risk cannot be calculated using the SCORE-2 Diabetes instrument. All patients included in the study provided informed consent for data collection and use of medical data for research purposes. The collected data were obtained according to the hospital’s standard of care for patients with T2D. The study protocol was approved by the Local Ethics Committee for Scientific Research of “Pius Brînzeu” Emergency Hospital Timisoara (approval number 418/ 01.11.2023). All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations and with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Cardiovascular risk classification, anamnestic, clinical, laboratory and anthropometric assessments

Cardiovascular risk classification was performed according to the 2023 ESC Guidelines for the management of cardiovascular disease in patients with diabetes, using the SCORE2-Diabetes algorithm for patients without established ASCVD or severe target organ damage (TOD) and guideline-defined criteria for those with ASCVD or severe TOD. Patients were categorized into moderate, high and very high cardiovascular risk. No participant was classified as low risk. Established ASCVD was defined as a history of acute coronary syndrome (ACS), stroke or peripheral artery disease (PAD), prior arterial revascularization, or significant plaque burden documented on imaging. Severe TOD was defined according to the 2023 ESC Guidelines criteria, which emphasizes the presence of renal impairment using both estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and urine albumin-creatinine (UAC) as surrogates for renal function, along with the presence of 3 concomitant microvascular complications (retinopathy, neuropathy and microalbuminuria). Severe TOD comprises either the presence of proteinuria (UAC >300 mg/g), an eGFR between 45-59 ml/min/1,73 m2 and microalbuminuria (UAC 30-300 mg/g), or an eGFR <45 ml/min/1,73 m2 irrespective of UAC levels13.

The following laboratory parameters were collected: lipid profile- total cholesterol, TG, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-c), LDL-c and hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) levels, renal function- UAC, eGFR. Systolic and diastolic blood pressure (BP) values, smoking status, diabetes duration, waist circumference, body mass index (BMI), along with glucose- and lipid-lowering treatments were also included in the analysis, all data were obtained from patients’ medical records, according to the hospital’s standard of care for patients with T2D.

Modifiable cardiovascular risk factor targets definition

Risk factor targets were defined according to contemporary guideline recommendations as follows: HbA1c <7.0%, LDL-c at the category-specific target recommended by the 2023 ESC Guidelines, triglycerides <150 mg/dL, and BP <130/80 mmHg.

Statistical analysis

Data was collected and analyzed using MedCalc Statistical Software version 23.0.8 (MedCalc Software Ltd, Ostend, Belgium; https://www.medcalc.org; 2024).

Results are presented as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables with Gaussian distribution, median and interquartile range for continuous variables with non-Gaussian distribution, respectively absolute and relative frequencies for categorical variables. In all continuous variables, the normality of the distribution was tested using Shapiro and Wilk’s test (distributions were considered non-Gaussian in case of a p-value lower than 0.05 at Shapiro and Wilk’s test).

The statistical significance of the differences between groups was calculated using ANOVA tests (continuous variables with Gaussian distribution), Kruskal-Wallis tests (continuous variables with non-Gaussian distribution) and Chi-Square tests (categorical variables). The statistical significance of the trend observed for the variation between groups was tested using the Jonckheere-Terpstra method (continuous variables) and Chi-Square for Trend test (categorical variables).

In this analysis a p-value lower than 0.05 is considered the threshold for statistical significance.

Median diabetes duration was 7 [2-14] years, and mean BMI was 31.3 ± 5.7 kg/m2, indicating a high burden of overweight and obesity in this cohort. Most patients were classified as having very high cardiovascular risk (81.6%), while 14.4% were at high risk and 4.0% at moderate risk, according to the 2023 ESC criteria (Table 1).

When stratified by cardiovascular risk category, the proportions of patients meeting each individual target were broadly similar. Among very-high-risk patients, 43.7% were at HbA1c target, 19.0% at LDL-c target, 58.5% at triglyceride target and 39.4% at blood pressure target. Statin use increased with cardiovascular risk, from 28.6% in the moderate-risk group to 48.0% in the high-risk group and 69.7% in the very-high-risk group (p = 0.013). However, almost one third of very-high-risk patients were not on statin therapy. Ezetimibe use remained low across all risk categories (≤10%) and did not differ significantly between groups. The use of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists was similarly modest and showed no clear gradient across risk categories, with only about one quarter of very-high-risk patients receiving an SGLT2 inhibitor and one fifth a GLP-1 receptor agonist (Table 3).

Therapeutic gaps were particularly evident in high-need subgroups (Table 4). Among very-high-risk patients with LDL-c not at target (n = 115), only 64.3% were on statin therapy and 7.0% on ezetimibe, indicating that more than one third of these patients remained untreated with statins and over 90% were not receiving ezetimibe. In patients with obesity (BMI ≥30 kg/m2), SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists were used in 30.1% and 24.3% of cases, respectively, despite the potential benefits for weight and cardiometabolic risk. In patients with established cardiovascular disease (n = 70), statins were prescribed in 81.4% of cases, but SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 receptor agonists were used in only 27.1% and 21.4%, respectively.

Discussion

Interpretation of findings

In our study, which included both hospitalized and ambulatory patients, 81.6% were classified as having very high CV risk, indicating a vulnerable population that requires intensive risk-factor management. Despite this, individual CV risk factors were only modestly controlled in this subgroup: approximately half of very-high-risk patients had HbA1c <7%, 39–42% had BP <130/80 mmHg, and 58% had triglycerides <150 mg/dL. Lipid metrics showed the lowest rates of control, with about 19–24% of patients meeting the recommended LDL-c target (risk-category specific).

When considering individual guideline-recommended cardiometabolic targets, fewer than half of patients were at the HbA1c or BP targets, only one in five were at the LDL-c target, and just over half were at the triglyceride target. Together, these findings highlight substantial room for improvement in comprehensive risk-factor management, particularly lipid lowering, in this real-world T2D cohort.

Regarding treatment regimens, therapeutic gaps were evident in the limited use of cardioprotective agents like statins, ezetimibe or novel glucose-lowering drugs (SGLT2i and GLP1-RAs), despite examining a cohort of predominantly very high risk patients. These findings raise some important questions regarding the ease of implementing guideline recommendations into clinical practice.

Comparison with existing literature

Our analysis describes cross-sectional control of guideline-recommended cardiovascular risk factors in patients with T2D across ESC cardiovascular risk categories using the 2023 ESC guidance, in a country with very high cardiovascular mortality13,17. Similar studies in Romanian and international settings have consistently reported that lipid targets are the least frequently met, while glycemic and blood pressure targets are reached in roughly half of patients18,19.

As for glycemic control, our findings were similar to the two previously mentioned studies, where the HbA1c target was reached in 40% and 50% of patients from the very high CV risk category, respectively. However, discrepancies were found regarding BP control, which was almost 40% in our very high risk subgroup, with lower rates of control seen in the other two studies18,19. An analysis conducted by Salem et al. on a cohort of patients with T2D and a history of percutaneous coronary revascularization found that 43% had HbA1c and 81% had BP in the target range. However, it should be noted that the threshold for BP was <140/80 mmHg and not <130/80 mmHg, as currently recommended. Also, only a quarter of the cohort met an LDL-c target of <55 mg/dl20.

Regarding individual LDL-c target control based on CV risk categories, findings from the SANTORINI study21 are in line with the trend seen in the studies conducted in the Romanian population: only around 20% of patients with high and very high CV risk are within the recommended LDL-c targets, and around half of the patients were receiving statin monotherapy. Statin use was higher in our analysis in the very high risk category (69.7%), but similar in the high risk subgroup (48%). Regarding combination therapies, ezetimibe and proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitors were underutilized, with only about 25% of the patients receiving more than one lipid-lowering agent, despite the majority not meeting the recommended LDL-c targets. Another interesting finding from the SANTORINI study is the underestimation of CV risk observed during the initial clinical assessment, an often overlooked reason for suboptimal implementation or optimization of therapy. Additionally, both underutilization of lipid-lowering treatment and possibly the stringent targets endorsed for each CV risk category may play a role in insufficient lipid control seen in these patients21. Also, the nocebo effect associated with statin use is another aspect difficult to overcome that may significantly impact patients’ adherence22.

As we found in our analysis, LDL-c is the most challenging factor to control. Andary et al. reported that, while HbA1c and BP were at target in about half of US adults with T2D and no patient with established CVD met all evaluated factors simultaneously23. Similarly, a multinational study of high- and very-high-risk patients with T2D found concomitant control of lipid, BP, and glycemic factors in only ~12% of patients24.

Concurrent control of cardiovascular risk factors

Although concurrent control of multiple cardiovascular risk factors is associated with better outcomes11, being at recommended targets for several risk factors in routine practice remains difficult, especially in patients with T2D at very high cardiovascular risk 23,25. Our results underscore that low LDL-c target control is a key driver of residual risk and should be a priority for systematic intensification.

Excess adiposity is common in T2D and is associated with poorer glycemic control and obesity may adversely affect other CV risk factors, mainly glycemia, BP and lipid profile, making the simultaneous control of targets more difficult26,27 Increasing the use of glucose-lowering medication with additional weight loss benefits like GLP-1 RAs could mitigate the burden of obesity in patients with T2D26,27.

Therapeutic gaps

In line with previously reported findings mentioned above, most of the patients with T2D do not meet recommended targets for CV risk factor control23. It is concerning that a significant proportion of patients with very high CV risk or even established ASCVD are not receiving statins or combination therapy with other lipid-lowering medications (ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors), even if LDL-c targets remain above recommended targets28. As previously highlighted in the DA VINCI study, only around 20% of patients reached the LDL-c target endorsed by the 2019 ESC guidelines and, although control of lipid targets increased with combination therapy, it remained underutilized, with only 9% of patients receiving moderate or high intensity statin plus ezetimibe and 1% an additional PCSK9 inhibitor, respectively29. Moreover, the same problem lies in implementing suitable glucose-lowering therapies with proven CV benefits, despite having a class I recommendation in both the European and American guidelines13,30. Since both SGLT2i and GLP-1 RAs have well documented effects on lowering both HbA1c levels, BP values and body weight, they should be prioritized as first-line treatment in patients with overweight or obesity at very high CV risk31. However, prescription rates remain low, with only around 25% of patients with ASCVD receiving a cardioprotective glucose-lowering agent, as data from a US healthcare system indicates30. The PRIORITY-T2D Study conducted in Spain included patients with newly diagnosed T2D and despite the results showing benefits of early weight loss in improving glycemic control, it also highlighted two important findings. First, after 5 years from the initial diagnosis, most patients did not meet weight loss targets. Moreover, although more than half of the cohort had obesity, GLP-1 RAs use was limited, highlighting the big therapeutic gap between guidelines and practice again32. The use of novel glucose-lowering medication could potentially be improved in vulnerable subgroups, such as patients with ASCVD, HF or chronic kidney disease (CKD), by increasing prescription rates among other medical specialties involved in the management of patients with T2D, like cardiologists, endocrinologists or primary care physicians. The study conducted showed that less than 20% of the eligible patients were prescribed either an SGLT2i or a GLP-1 RA by these specialists33.

Implications for clinical practice

Although recommendations for the management of patients with T2D are periodically updated, the burden of CV events and mortality remains high, suggesting persistent gaps between evidence and routine practice34. Several aspects related to real-world care could likely improve outcomes through consistent CV risk assessment and systematic, risk-based treatment intensification35. Standardized CV risk evaluation should become routine, with clear action thresholds for lipid and BP management aligned with ESC risk categories. Given the complexity of T2D and its comorbidities, multidisciplinary management involving diabetology, cardiology, nephrology and nutrition can facilitate comprehensive risk reduction, particularly in very-high-risk patients36.

Strengths and limitations

The strength of our study lies in delivering real-world clinical data from a specialized Diabetes Center in Romania, a country with very high CV mortality rates. Patients were categorized into CV risk classes based on the 2023 ESC Guidelines recommendations, a contemporary method that uses SCORE2-Diabetes as a predictive tool, an algorithm that proved its value in accurately clustering patients with T2D but without ASCVD or severe TOD17. Also, we analyzed both multiple risk factor control and treatment use in these patients, which bring valuable insights about how current guideline recommendations are implemented.

The cross-sectional design of the study limits the ability to draw causal conclusions and serves as a limitation of the present analysis. Since patients were selected from a single center and represent a limited geographic region, our findings cannot be generalized to broader populations. Most of our participants were classified as having high and very high CV risk, with only a small percentage of subjects (4% of the cohort) falling into the moderate CV risk category. For a more comprehensive assessment of the studied population, information regarding lifestyle, treatment adherence and socioeconomic status would have provided additional insights but was not included in the analysis.

Future perspectives

Possible future research directions include longitudinal studies that can determine how concurrent control of multiple guideline-recommended risk factors relates to subsequent micro- and macrovascular outcomes in patients with T2D. In addition, implementation studies focused on reducing clinical inertia and improving uptake of evidence-based cardioprotective therapies may help close the gap between guideline recommendations and routine practice37,38.

Another important factor in improving patients’ outcomes is combating clinical inertia. The future will most likely provide more tools to fill the gap between guideline recommendations and clinical practice through improved prescription algorithms, electronic alerts, audit and feedback measures. Additionally, implementation of treatment optimization programs and regularly conducted before-and-after analysis, particularly in higher risk subgroups, could aid both clinicians and patients in reducing the burden of CVD37,38.

Conclusions

In this real-world cohort of patients with T2D, most were classified as having high or very high cardiovascular risk, yet control rates for key risk factors were modest. Around half of the patients were at the HbA1c target, 40–60% were at blood pressure and triglyceride targets, while LDL-c target control was met in only about one in five.

LDL-c target control remained low across risk categories, consistent with a major therapeutic gap in lipid-lowering intensity. Strengthening systematic, risk-based lipid management—including appropriate use and intensification of statins and add-on therapies when indicated—represents a high-impact opportunity to reduce residual cardiovascular risk in this population.

Despite clear guideline recommendations, important therapeutic gaps were evident: many patients at very high risk, and those with established CVD, were not receiving statins, ezetimibe or cardioprotective glucose-lowering agents. These findings support the need for structured care pathways and regular treatment intensification aligned with ESC risk stratification.

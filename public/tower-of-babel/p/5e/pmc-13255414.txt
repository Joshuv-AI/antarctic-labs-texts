Positive correlation between IL-6 levels and lumbar bone mineral density in menopausal women diagnosed with osteoporosis in comparison to menopausal women without osteoporosis
Authors: Reza Alhosseini, Mehrdad Aghaie, Somayeh Ghorbani, Zeinab Mohammadi, Safoura Khajeniazi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporosis is a common bone disorder characterized by decreased bone density. Interleukin-6 (IL-6), plays a crucial role in bone homeostasis. This study aimed to evaluate the relationship between serum levels of IL-6 and lumbar bone marrow density (LBMD) in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis compared to those without the condition. In addition we investigated the level of PTH, ALP, Ca, FSH and LH in case and control and compared them between two groups.Materials and methodsIn this study, 5cc of blood was collected from 60 participants (case and control) without anticoagulant. Serum was separated and stored at -20 °C. ALP and calcium (Ca) were measured using a colorimetric method, while PTH, LH, and FSH levels were assessed by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The results were analyzed by SPSS version 18, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.ResultsOur findings showed the serum level of IL-6 increased significantly in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis compared to the control group (p-value = 0.001). Serum levels of LH, FSH, ALP, and PTH in both groups were not statistically different (p-value = 0.089). Additionally, we found a positive correlation between BMD Lumbar with IL-6 in menopausal women with osteoporosis.ConclusionOur findings illustrated the positive correlation between lumbar spine BMD with IL-6, FSH, ALP, and Ca in MPO women, while hip BMD positively correlated with IL-6, PTH, and FSH serum levels. The correlations between various factors were not statistically significant, except for the relationship between BMD in the lumbar spine and IL-6 levels.Keywords: Osteoporosis, Menopausal women, Lumbar bone mineral density, Interleukin-6

Introduction

Osteoporosis is a common leading cause of fracture related to bone mineral density reduction and microscopic alterations in bone tissue. These changes lead to decreased bone strength and elevated bone fracture risk. In postmenopausal and elderly women, osteoporosis is a major cause of bone fractures, which spotlights the direct correlation between early onset osteoporosis and menopausal condition [1–3].

In menopausal females, the sex hormone estradiol has been found to notably suppress the activation of pro-inflammatory cytokine genes, thereby influencing bone health. During menopause, estrogen levels which is plays a critical role on bone density reduction, consequently the bone fracture in pelvic and fibula increases in postmenopausal women [4–6].

Interleukin-6 (IL-6) and interleukin-11 (IL-11) are key pro-inflammatory markers that are crucial for the regulation of osteoclast differentiation and maintenance of skeletal stability [7]. The serum level of IL-6 increases in elderly people and postmenopausal women, which leads to the development of osteoclasts activity and ultimately loss of bone mass [6, 8]. Numerous immune cells have a direct or indirect effect on bone cells by various factors includes Osteoprotegerin/ receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand(OPG/RANKL), inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNFα (Tumor necrosis factor α), as well as other substances released by the immune cells [9].

IL-6 elevates RANKL ligand secretion, which leads to increased osteoclast activity and, subsequently, osteoclastogenesis indirectly. RANKL plays an important role in diminishing the proliferation of osteoblasts in late differentiation phases and contributes to the development of osteoporosis [9, 10].

Studies have shown a positive correlation between the serum level of IL-6 and the reduction of bone mass, especially in the pelvic in menopausal women with osteoporosis but it has been not clear the correlation among IL-6 and other factors such as PTH (Para Thyroid Hormone), ACP (Acid Phosphotase), ALP(Alkan phosphatase), LH (Leuteinizing Hormone and FSH (Fulicul Stimulating Hormone [11–13].

On the other hand, some previous studies indicated the action of estrogen in bone tissue is opposite to that of PTH. This protein causes a decrease in bone density, while estrogen helps maintain and increase bone mass. During menstruation, there is a difference between the serum level of estradiol and changes in the serum levels of IL-6 and PTH [11].

In some in vitro and in vivo studies, PTH causes a short-term increase in the expression of interleukin-6, and it is possible that PTH exerts its effects through cytokines such as IL-6 [12–14]. These effects were found in mice with a low serum level of IL-6, in contrast to the serum level of some biochemical markers of bone turnover was increased which continuously affected by PTH [15, 16]. Prostaglandin E2 (PGE-2), similar to PTH, is a potent inducer of IL-6 both upstream and downstream of it signaling pathway. Furthermore, in patients with primary hyperparathyroidism, it caused serum level of IL-6 elevation and biochemical bone losses markers [17].

PTH from stromal cells Stimuli the release of interleukin-6, which affects the hematopoietic lineage. Hematopoietic and osteoclastic cells have receptors for IL-6, which is to lead to increased bone turnover through binding [18]. This study aimed to examine the correlation of IL-6 and PTH in postmenopausal women, both with and without osteoporosis.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

In this study, the LH and FSH kit was prepared from the IDEALDIAG company (Tehran, Iran). PTH kit was purchased from Pishtaz Tab Company (Tehran, Iran). To measure ACP, ALP, and calcium, Pars AZMUN and PTS-ICO kits (Iran, Tehran) were obtained, respectively. The IL-6 kit was purchased from Bioactive Diagnostica (, Germany, Berlin).

Study design and participant selection

A case-control study conducted from February to May 2023 including postmenopausal women aged 50 to 70 years who visited by a rheumatologist. Volunteers were divided into two groups: postmenopausal women with osteoporosis (MPO = 30) and postmenopausal women without osteoporosis (control = 30). Based on Jeedigunta et al. (2020) [19], the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of IL-6 in postmenopausal women with and without osteoporosis was 4 (0.92) and 2.8 (0.41), respectively. With a large effect size of 0.8, a sample size of 26 individuals per group was required. A sample size of 25 individuals per group was required. To have a study power of 80% and an error probability of 0.05. However, we increased it to 30 individuals in each group and recruited 6 postmenopausal women to participate in our study.

To diagnosis of MPO group patients was performed with rheumatologist based on the T-score and Z-score lower than 2.5 and 2, respectively. Furthermore, the control group also included postmenopausal women without osteoporosis, which was confirmed through a bone density test. In this study the exclusion criteria included non-menopausal women, individuals without kidney damage from type 1 or type 2 diabetes, patients with bone disorders, liver, parathyroid, gastrointestinal, autoimmune diseases or cancer. The study purpose and methods were explained to the patients and verbal consent was obtained. The ethics code was received from the ethics committee of the Golestan University of Medical Sciences (ethics code: IR.GOUMS.REC.1402.120).

Sample collection

In the present study, 5 ml blood samples were drawn from the participants after filling the consent form in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Serums were separated using the centrifuge (5 min, 4000 rpm), and stored at -20 degree centigrade. Following, serum levels of biochemical parameters were measured using commercial kits and autoanalyzer instrument (Mindray, China). Finally, the rest of the serum were stored in a at -80 °C Freezer.

Biochemical measurement

Biochemical parameters such as ALP, ACP and Ca were measured using the colorimetric method in accordance to the manufacturer’s protocol. Furthermore, serum level of IL-6, PTH, LH and FSH were measured using ELISA. The optical absorbance at 450 to 490 wavelength was read using an ELISA reader (Stat fax, USA).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed with SPSS version 18. Categorical variables were reported as frequency (percentage) and quantitative variables as mean (standard deviation) or median (IQR: interquartile range). Shapiro-Wilk test was used to determine the normal distribution of data. Correlation between parameters in two groups was assessed using Spearman’s rho correlation coefficient. To compare parameters between two groups, t-test was used in normal cases and non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test was used in abnormal cases. In addition, we used multiple logistic regression to determine the odds ratio of osteoporosis based on the levels of variables. A significance level was indicated with a p value of less than 0.05.

Comparative analysis of ACP and IL-6 levels in MPO patients compared to control subjects

The statistical analysis, as presented in Table 5, reveals significant relationships between IL-6 and ACP levels in postmenopausal women with osteoporosis. Specifically, IL-6 demonstrated a direct relationship with the likelihood of developing osteoporosis. For every 1 Pg/mL increase in IL-6 levels, the odds of osteoporosis increased by a factor of 0.063, indicating a notable association between elevated IL-6 levels and the risk of osteoporosis. Conversely, ACP exhibited an inverse relationship with the probability of developing the condition. This suggests that higher levels of ACP may be protective against the onset of osteoporosis. These findings highlight the importance of monitoring these biomarkers in postmenopausal women, as they could serve as critical indicators for assessing osteoporosis risk and guiding preventive strategies (Fig. 1).

Discussion

This study illustrated that the relationship between IL-6 and PTH and their association with osteoporosis in menopausal women. Furthermore, this study showed no significant differences in PTH, LH, FSH, and ALP serum levels between the two groups. However, the levels of PTH, LH, FSH, and ALP were higher in MPO group than control individuals. In contrast, the ACP and Ca levels were lower in MPO group compared the control group. Notably, the IL-6 levels in the serum of MPO were significantly elevated compared to the control subjects. The study of Scheidt-Navw et al. in 2001 showed that the serum level of IL-6 in postmenopausal women is an important marker to predict bone loss, especially in the first decade after menopause but they didn’t study the relationship between IL-6 and PTH. Moreover, the results of the present study were consistent and confirmed with Scheidt-Navw et al. findings [20]. In addition we evaluated the correlation between IL-6 and PTH in case and control group.

Interestingly, we observed a non-significant positive correlation between IL-6 and ACP in the case group, while the correlation between IL-6 and ALP was significantly negative. In the control group, IL-6 positively correlated with both ACP and ALP, but these correlations were not significant. Given that ACP and ALP are markers for bone diseases, this suggests that IL-6 may also serve as a marker for bone disease. Li et al. (2016) demonstrated IL-6 affected bone formation in a mouse model of glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis (GIO) and the levels of IL-6 in BMSCs derived from GIO mice compared to the control group. These findings emphasize the important role of IL-6 in osteoporosis [21].

Latjindung et al.‘s (2020) study showed a positive correlation between inflammatory markers including IL-6 and decreased bone density in pre-and postmenopausal women. They also highlighted inflammation as an independent risk factor for osteoporosis, with increased levels of IL-6 being associated with lower bone density [22]. Furthermore, Wang et al.‘s (2022) study highlighted IL-6 as a predictor of bone density loss in postmenopausal women [23], and also both studies supported the findings of our results.

In this study, the results showed no significant correlation between IL-6 and PTHin both experimental groups. Furthermore, IL-6 has the positive correlation with LH and FSH in MPO patients, which indicated the vital role of IL-6 in osteoporosis development in post-menopausal women. However, the IL-6 correlation with LH and FSH in the control group was not significant, which is indicated that the IL-6 role in bone metabolism is complex. Previous studies, conducted by Ilesanmi-Oyelere et al. in 2019 [24] and Jabber et al. in 2015 [25], have also demonstrated elevated IL-6 levels in osteoporotic individuals. In addition Ilesanmi-Oyelere et al. showed Hip BMD amount was significantly lower in women with high/detectable levels of IL-6 in comparison with women have undetectable level of IL-6. In the present study we found the positive correlation between Hip BMD and high level of IL-6 but it was not significant. Moreover, Shi et al. in 2023 investigated that the negative correlation between IL-6 and IGFBP-3 with BMD in postmenopausal women, which is highlight the vital role of IL-6 in osteoporosis development in these patients.

Menezes et al. in 2018 [26] identified an association between IL-6 levels and BMD in young adults, although the association differed between male and female. On the other hand, our findings illustrated the positive correlation of lumbar spine BMD with IL-6, FSH, ALP, and Ca in MPO women, while hip BMD positively correlated with IL-6, PTH, and FSH serum levels. The correlations between various factors were not statistically significant, except for the relationship between bone mineral density in the lumbar spine and IL-6 levels.

The relationship between IL-6 and osteoporosis is further highlighted by the investigation of Rachon et al. in 2002, who demonstrated that estrogen depletion increases IL-6 secretion in postmenopausal women. Endrighi et al. 2016 [26] and Latjindung (2020) [22], also noted the effects of stress and estrogen levels on IL-6 secretion, respectively.

Our findings are consistent with the results of Kim et al. in 2012 [27] that they emphasized the IL-6 level is related to various inflammatory markers and affected by age and menopause in women. Our results are in line with the results of Masiukiewicz et al. (2002) [28] and O’Brien et al. in 2005 [29] which showed the potential of IL-6 as a biomarker for osteoporosis, particularly in the context of estrogen deficiency. Safley et al. in 2004 showed PTH induces the secretion of IL-6, a novel pathway which revealed human parathyroids may play a role markedly to IL-6 production and its elevation in serum of patients with hyperparathyroidism [30]. In the present study, it was observed the level of PTH and IL-6 in MPO women are higher than in MP women without osteoporosis. In addition the most important result of this study is illustration the positive correlation of lumbar spine BMD with IL-6, FSH, ALP, and Ca in MPO women, while hip BMD positively correlated with IL-6, PTH, and FSH serum levels.

Conclusion

Our study revealed that IL-6 plays a significant role in osteoporosis among menopausal women. Elevated IL-6 levels correlate with markers of bone disease and BMD, which is highlight IL-6 potential as a biomarker for osteoporosis diagnosis and progression. Further research is needed to explore the therapeutic potential of IL-6 in osteoporosis management.

Limitations and future directions

As mentioned above, in this study, the correlation among most factors was not significant. One reason for this result may be the small sample size. Unfortunately, finding and gathering new cases is so difficult in clinical studies. In addition, it was better that we could work on the menopausal women with parathyroid hormone-related osteoporosis. But we weren’t successful in taking samples from hypoparathyroid women. It is suggested to do a study on the women suffering hypothyroidism and osteoporosis either menopause or post menopause.

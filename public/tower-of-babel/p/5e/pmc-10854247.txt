Background

A dentigerous cyst is defined as a cyst that originates from the separation of the follicle from around the crown of an unerupted tooth. The pathogenesis of dentigerous cyst is uncertain, but it is known to develop by accumulation of fluid between the reduced enamel epithelium and tooth crown.1 The incidence of dentigerous cysts in the first decade of life is low. The inflammatory dentigerous cyst arises by stimulation of reduced enamel epithelium of a developing tooth bud by periapical inflammation from a carious, nonvital deciduous precursor.2

Although enucleation of the cyst with the removal of the offending teeth remains the gold standard in the management, marsupialization of cysts involving unerupted, favorably positioned teeth has been described3 with various appliances and techniques used to maintain the patency of the cystic cavity.4

Here we report, an inflammatory dentigerous cyst in a 10-year-old child, treated by marsupialization, without the use of any appliance or technique.

Case Description

A 10-year-old male presented with swelling of the left lower face of 3 months duration with insidious onset, gradually growing to the present size. The swelling was accompanied by dull aching pain for 2 months, which was occasional and gradually progressed to being continuous. There was no fever at the onset of swelling or numbness of the cheek/lip associated. There were no known comorbidities. The patient had undergone extraction of the primary mandibular left second molar 1 month ago and had no symptomatic improvement. The boy appeared generally healthy for his reported age. The left cheek had a diffuse swelling that was firm and tender. An adequate mouth opening, mixed dentition status, and a dental age of 10–11 years were indicated by an intraoral examination. An oval well-defined, tender swelling of size 1.5 × 1.5 cm was palpable in relation to the left mandibular body region which was bony hard, and smooth suggesting an expanded outer cortex with eggshell crackling. The mucosa over the swelling appeared unattached to the swelling. There was no paresthesia of the left inferior alveolar nerve or submandibular lymphadenopathy. The teeth present in the region were the primary mandibular left canine with pre-shedding mobility, root stump of primary mandibular left first molar, healing socket of primary mandibular left second molar, and pit and fissure caries in relation to the permanent left mandibular first molar (Fig. 1). A 1-month-old intraoral periapical radiograph obtained before the primary left mandibular second molar extraction showed a well-defined radiolucent lesion involving the unerupted permanent mandibular left second premolar tooth with about 50% root formation (Fig. 2). A cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) of the region showed a radiolucent lesion measuring 2.2 × 2.2 × 1.7 cm with the expansion of the outer cortex (Figs 3A3BC). A provisional diagnosis of inflammatory dentigerous cyst and a decision to treat by marsupialization was made.

After obtaining written, informed consent from the parent, the primary left mandibular first molar root stumps and primary left mandibular canine were extracted and wide excision of the alveolar mucosa overlying the area along with cystic lining was performed under 2% lignocaine with 1:2,00,000 epinephrine anesthesia (Fig. 4). The lining and mucosa were submitted for histopathologic examination, and the findings indicated an inflammatory dentigerous cyst. The associated permanent teeth spontaneously erupted into occlusion over the following 3 months, with no negative or unexpected outcomes (Figs 5 and 6).

Discussion

Inflammatory dentigerous cysts occur with a predilection toward the male gender and in the mandible, commonly in the premolar site, usually in relation to grossly carious, nonvital primary mandibular molar teeth.3 This is in concurrence with the present case.

Benn and Altini suggested three possible mechanisms for the occurrence of this pathology. In the first, they suggested that the cyst formed within the follicle of the permanent tooth in the usual manner and became secondarily inflamed as a result of periapical inflammation. Secondly, they suggested that these cysts could occur due to the fusion of radicular cysts formed at the apices of decayed deciduous teeth with the follicle of the unerupted permanent molars. Thirdly, it was suggested that the inflammatory exudate from the periapical inflammation surrounding the apex of the primary molar causes separation of reduced enamel epithelium from enamel with resultant cyst formation.5 In the present case, the third mechanism may hold true since the first two mechanisms could have caused an interruption to the path of eruption.

Computed tomography with multiplanar reconstruction and CBCT has been recommended in the work-up and management of cystic lesions in children.6 In the present case, a CBCT was performed to assess the magnitude of the cyst and note the spatial relationship of the permanent second premolar tooth to the eruption path.

The main management options for dentigerous cysts include enucleation with exodontia of associated permanent tooth and marsupialization which offers the advantage of preserving the permanent tooth. Marsupialization of the cyst was chosen for the present case. Wine et al. first described marsupialization of a mandibular dentigerous cyst.7 By creating a window in the cystic lining, marsupialization allows the cystic lining to communicate with the oral cavity. Since the surgeon must create an opening at the cystic cavity's lowest position, the window is normally made on the cortex, lateral to the crest. However, Kozelj and Sotosek have described an opening along the crest to avoid the eruption of the permanent teeth into the lateral opening.8 Similarly, in the present case, an opening along the crest provided for proper eruption of the permanent teeth. The permanent teeth erupted in three months, which is in agreement with the findings of Yahara et al.9 The findings of Fujii et al.10 that cusp depth of <5.1 mm, angulation <25°, space/tooth size >1 enhances the success of spontaneous eruption following marsupialization apply to this case.

Clinical Significance

Marsupialization along the crest provides a conservative option to allow for spontaneous, unassisted eruption of permanent teeth in cases of inflammatory dentigerous cysts. Careful preoperative assessment must be used to make treatment decisions. The successful eruption of permanent teeth and the total resolution of the disease both depend on adequate follow-up.

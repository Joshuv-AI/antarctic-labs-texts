Trends and Disparities in Mortality from Malignant Parotid Gland Tumors in the United States, 1999-2023: A Population-Based Analysis
Authors: Qianqian Xu, Sunjun Li, Fangyuan Liao, Donghao Lou, Tongbin Zhang
Published: 2026

Introduction

Malignant parotid gland tumors, the most common type of malignant salivary gland tumors, account for less than 1% of all cancers and 5–8.5% of head and neck malignancies.1,2 These neoplasms are pathologically diverse, with Mucoepidermoid carcinoma (MEC) being the most common malignancy. 3 Prognostic outcomes for these tumors vary significantly, influenced by factors such as tumor size, lymph node involvement, and surgical margin status. These variables contribute to an increased risk of local recurrence and poorer overall survival, particularly in more advanced stages of disease.4,5 The mainstay of treatment is surgical resection, with adjuvant radiotherapy considered for high-risk cases or as part of chemoradiotherapy protocols for select unresectable tumors, typically managed through a multidisciplinary approach.6,7 Despite advances in treatment, mortality from these tumors varies across demographic groups, yet comprehensive analyses of long-term mortality trends are limited.

Studies suggest significant disparities in incidence and survival by gender, race, and geography. Although salivary gland tumors account for a small proportion of head and neck cancers, most malignant salivary tumors originate in the parotid gland.8,9 Geographically, malignant parotid tumors show notable variations. For instance, age-adjusted incidence rates in Greenlandic Inuit populations differ markedly from those in other groups indicating the influence of racial factors on tumor behavior. 10 Gender disparities are also evident, with male patients predominating in malignant tumor cohorts,11-13 studies indicate that females and certain racial groups, such as Asian-Americans and Hispanics, may have better survival outcomes, possibly due to differences in healthcare access or tumor biology. 14 These disparities highlight the need for targeted research to understand mortality patterns and inform clinical strategies.

Previous research has primarily focused on the incidence and treatment outcomes of malignant parotid gland tumors, but there is a lack of comprehensive analysis on the trends in mortality associated with these tumors over time. To further explore the epidemiological trends and mortality patterns of malignant parotid gland tumors, the CDC WONDER (Wide-ranging Online Data for Epidemiologic Research) database offers a comprehensive platform for analyzing public health data. This resource provides vital statistics on cancer incidence and mortality across the United States, allowing for a more granular understanding of regional and demographic variations. By examining disparities by age, gender, race/ethnicity, and geographic region, this study aims to identify at-risk populations and inform public health interventions to reduce inequities in cancer outcomes. To our knowledge, this is among the first national studies to characterize long-term mortality trends specifically for malignant parotid gland tumors in the United States using CDC WONDER data through 2023, with stratification by age, sex, race/ethnicity, census region, and state. By extending the observation period to the most recent available year and examining geographic and demographic patterns, this study provides updated evidence on mortality burden and identifies subgroups with persistently elevated or increasing mortality.

Methods

Study Design and Population

Death certificate data for malignant parotid gland tumors in the United States were obtained from the CDC WONDER Multiple Cause-of-Death database (https://wonder.cdc.gov/deaths-by-underlying-cause.html; accessed August 15, 2025). 15 This repeated cross-sectional analysis identified deaths for which malignant neoplasm of the parotid gland (ICD-10-CM code C07) was recorded as the underlying cause of death. CDC WONDER has been used previously to examine mortality trends across cancer types.16-18 Because the analysis used publicly available, deidentified aggregate data, local institutional review board approval was not required. Reporting followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement. 19 The completed checklist is provided as Supplementary Table 1. No sampling, recruitment, or follow-up was performed; the study size was determined by all CDC WONDER death records meeting the ICD-10-CM and date criteria.

Data Extraction

Sociodemographic variables abstracted were age, year of death, race/ethnicity, geographic region, and place of death. Age groups were defined as 25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, 65–74, 75–84, and ≥85 years. Race/ethnicity categories included Non-Hispanic (NH) White, NH American Indian/Alaska Native, NH Black/African American, NH Asian or Pacific Islander, and Hispanic or Latino. Place of death was coded as home, hospice, nursing home/long-term care facility, or medical facility and the latter was further classified as inpatient, outpatient, emergency department, dead on arrival, or unknown. Geographic regions followed U.S. Census Bureau definitions: Northeast, Midwest, South, and West. 20

Statistical Analysis

Crude mortality was expressed per 100,000 persons annually, defined as deaths attributed to malignant parotid gland tumors divided by the contemporaneous U.S. population. Age-adjusted mortality rates (AAMRs) were derived by direct standardization to the 2000 U.S. standard population to enable comparisons across subgroups and calendar years. 21 To characterize heterogeneity, rates were summarized by age (25–34, 35–44, 45–54, 55–64, 65–74, 75–84, and ≥85 years), sex, race/ethnicity, census region, and state. Because the U.S. age structure changed over time, age adjustment reduced confounding from demographic shifts. Where counts were sparse, estimates were interpreted cautiously due to potential instability.

Temporal patterns in AAMRs for 1999-2023 were examined using the National Cancer Institute’s Joinpoint Regression Program (version 5.2.0; National Cancer Institute, Rockville, MD, USA). Calendar year was modeled as the independent variable and AAMR as the dependent variable using log-linear regression. Models allowing 0 to 3 joinpoints were considered, and the final model was selected using the weighted Bayesian information criterion (WBIC). APCs were reported for model segments, and AAPCs were calculated for the full 1999-2023 interval with 95% CIs. When no joinpoints were selected, the APC and AAPC were equivalent for the reported interval. Estimates suppressed by CDC WONDER or unavailable because of sparse counts were not included in trend modeling and were interpreted descriptively. Statistical significance was evaluated using two-sided tests, with P < .05 considered statistically significant. All figure generation was completed in Python (version 3.13).

Results

Overall

From 1999 to 2023, 15,133 deaths were attributed to malignant neoplasms of the parotid gland in the United States. Annual deaths increased from 491 to 696, a 41.75% increase in absolute counts. In contrast, the overall AAMR changed from 0.28 per 100,000 population (95% CI, 0.25 to 0.30) in 1999 to 0.25 per 100,000 population (95% CI, 0.23 to 0.27) in 2023, with a stable trend (AAPC = -0.09, 95% CI, -0.43 to 0.25) (Table 1). Significant trends were limited to selected strata, including decreases among females, males, adults aged 45 to 74 years, the Northeast, Florida, and New York, and an increase among adults aged 85 years or older.

Age

Age-stratified curves separated early and remained ordered across the study period (Figure 1). Adults aged 85 years or older had the highest crude mortality rate and the only significant increasing age-specific trend, rising from 2.55 per 100,000 population in 1999 to 3.02 per 100,000 population in 2023 (AAPC = 1.22, 95% CI, 0.67 to 1.77). Adults aged 75 to 84 years had the second-highest burden but a stable trend. Mortality declined among adults aged 45 to 54, 55 to 64, and 65 to 74 years. These opposing age-specific patterns help explain the stable national AAMR.

Sex

Across the study period, male AAMRs remained higher than female AAMRs (Figure 2). In 1999, the AAMR was 0.41 per 100,000 population (95% CI, 0.36 to 0.45) among males and 0.18 per 100,000 population (95% CI, 0.15 to 0.20) among females. By 2023, these rates were 0.39 (95% CI, 0.35 to 0.43) and 0.14 (95% CI, 0.12 to 0.16), respectively. Both sex-specific trends declined significantly, with a larger relative decrease among females.

Race and Ethnicity

Race/ethnicity-specific trend estimates were most stable for NH White and NH Black populations (Figure 3). NH White individuals had higher AAMRs than NH Black individuals across most years, but neither group had a significant temporal trend. NH Black mortality changed from 0.21 per 100,000 population (95% CI, 0.14 to 0.30) in 1999 to 0.20 (95% CI, 0.15 to 0.26) in 2023. NH White mortality changed from 0.28 (95% CI, 0.25 to 0.31) to 0.29 (95% CI, 0.26 to 0.31). Hispanic and other race/ethnicity groups had sparse or unstable estimates and were not used to support definitive trend conclusions.

Regional curves overlapped substantially, but the Northeast showed the only statistically significant decline (Figure 4). Its AAMR decreased from 0.29 per 100,000 population (95% CI, 0.23 to 0.34) in 1999 to 0.23 (95% CI, 0.18 to 0.27) in 2023 (AAPC = -0.80, 95% CI, -1.23 to -0.38). State-Level Variation

At the state level, the clearest analyzable trends were observed in California, Florida, and New York (Figures 5 and 6). Florida declined from 0.29 per 100,000 population (95% CI, 0.21 to 0.39) in 1999 to 0.21 (95% CI, 0.15 to 0.27) in 2023 (AAPC = -0.88, 95% CI, -1.67 to -0.08). New York declined from 0.27 (95% CI, 0.19 to 0.38) to 0.18 (95% CI, 0.12 to 0.25) (AAPC = -1.05, 95% CI, -2.09 to -0.00). California remained stable. Maps of deaths, AAMRs, percent change, and AAPCs showed substantial missing or suppressed estimates in many states, so state-level findings should be considered exploratory. Discussion

In this national repeated cross-sectional analysis, the overall AAMR for malignant parotid gland tumors did not change significantly from 1999 to 2023, even as the absolute number of deaths increased. Thus, crude burden and age-adjusted mortality risk followed different patterns. Declines were limited to selected strata, whereas mortality increased among adults aged 85 years or older. Race/ethnicity-specific and state-level estimates require caution because several outputs were sparse, suppressed, or unstable.

While our study focuses on mortality trends, it is important to contextualize these findings within the broader epidemiological landscape of incidence patterns. Based on extensive clinical analyses, the epidemiology of malignant parotid gland tumors reveals significant patterns that may vary across demographic and geographic groups. These patterns may be partly related to the marked histopathological diversity and biological heterogeneity of parotid malignancies. 12 Malignant lesions account for approximately 10% to 17% of parotid neoplasms overall, underscoring the substantial clinical burden in head and neck oncology. 9 Furthermore, contemporary population-based studies show a concerning upward trajectory in incidence, with age-adjusted rates increasing by 58.1% over longitudinal observation periods. 22 In terms of mortality, malignant parotid tumors contribute notably to patient morbidity and cancer-specific death, especially in cases of advanced-stage disease or aggressive histological subtypes, such as mucoepidermoid carcinoma or adenoid cystic carcinoma.10,23 Although these clinical factors provide important context for interpreting mortality patterns, the present CDC WONDER analysis cannot directly evaluate them because the database does not include information on tumor histology, stage, grade, treatment, recurrence, comorbidities, socioeconomic status, or healthcare access. Therefore, our findings should be interpreted as descriptive population level mortality trends rather than evidence of specific biological or clinical mechanisms. Large scale epidemiological studies specifically focused on mortality trends in malignant parotid gland tumors remain limited. This highlights the need for further research using clinical datasets that can evaluate survival outcomes across diverse populations and treatment eras.

The incidence of malignant parotid gland tumors has steadily increased over recent decades, with prior studies reporting an increased contribution from metastatic cutaneous squamous cell carcinomas within the parotid gland, which now represent a significant proportion of secondary malignancies.22,24,25 In the present analysis, overall age adjusted mortality remained stable, with declining trends observed only in selected subgroups rather than across the entire population. Improved preoperative differentiation between benign and malignant lesions has been facilitated by sophisticated imaging techniques, such as computed tomography (CT) texture analysis and perfusion CT, which offer valuable information for clinical evaluation.26,27 Moreover, the use of deep learning algorithms as supplementary diagnostic tools has been investigated as a potential approach to support preoperative evaluation and reduce variability in conventional diagnostic interpretation.28,29 These technological advances, along with optimized surgical strategies, including less extensive parotidectomy for lower grade malignancies, supported by stable high survival rates, and multimodal therapy, may have improved outcomes in clinical practice, but the present CDC WONDER analysis cannot evaluate whether these factors contributed to the mortality patterns observed in this study.22,30,31 These clinical developments are best discussed as background context rather than as direct explanations for changes in mortality in the current analysis.

Advanced age consistently demonstrates a significant and independent negative prognostic impact on survival outcomes in patients with malignant parotid gland tumors. Our results reveal marked age-related disparities in mortality patterns across the 25-year study period. While most age groups (45-54, 55-64, and 65-74 years) demonstrated significant declining trends in crude death rates with AAPCs ranging from -1.25 to -1.70, a concerning divergent pattern emerged in the oldest age group. Patients aged ≥85 years exhibited the highest crude death rates throughout the study period, increasing from 2.55 per 100,000 population in 1999 to 3.02 per 100,000 population in 2023, with a significant upward trend (AAPC = 1.22, 95% CI, 0.67 to 1.77). This finding aligns with existing literature reporting poorer survival outcomes in older patients with malignant parotid tumors.14,32 Previous studies have established that age over 50 years serves as an independent adverse prognostic factor, with multivariate analyses demonstrating statistically poorer prognosis in older individuals. 32 Furthermore, survival data confirm that patients aged 60 years and older show significantly reduced 5-year and 10-year survival rate.14,33 The upward mortality trend observed in our ≥85 years cohort, despite overall declining rates in younger age groups, may be attributed to multiple factors including increased frailty, comorbidities, reduced tolerance to aggressive treatment regimens, and the biological aggressiveness of tumors in this population. 34 In the current study, the rising mortality trend among adults aged 85 years or older may be considered in the context of population aging, frailty, comorbidity burden, reduced treatment tolerance, and differences in disease presentation. These factors were not directly measured in CDC WONDER. Because treatment information was unavailable, the role of treatment intensity or treatment tolerance in this age group cannot be assessed from the present data. Age remains a critical adverse prognostic factor, and these findings support further investigation of age specific mortality patterns using datasets that include clinical characteristics, treatment information, and comorbidity profiles.

The prognosis of malignant parotid gland tumors, including mortality outcomes, exhibits a distinct association with sex. Data from population-based studies consistently indicate that females achieve superior oncological outcomes compared to males. Specifically, survival analyses using Kaplan-Meier survival curves and Cox proportional hazards models have shown that females with malignant parotid tumors exhibit enhanced survival probabilities relative to males. 35 Data from 1999 to 2023 indicate that males had higher AAMR compared to females. From 1999 to 2023, females showed a significant decline in AAMR with an AAPC of −0.66 (−1.28 to −0.03), while males showed a smaller decline with an AAPC of −0.42 (−0.81 to −0.02). Additional research corroborates that females not only present a sex specific survival advantage but also suggests that female sex may be an independent prognostic factor influencing long term outcomes.36,37 However, the present CDC WONDER analysis cannot determine whether the observed sex based mortality differences are explained by tumor biology, comorbidity, treatment patterns, health seeking behavior, or healthcare access. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as population level associations rather than evidence of biological or healthcare related causation.

The prognosis of malignant parotid tumors exhibits significant heterogeneity across racial and geographical populations, with specific demographic groups demonstrating distinct survival outcomes. Females, Asian-Americans, and Hispanics are reported to have better overall survival rates compared to other groups based on national sample analyses, suggesting a correlation between race/ethnicity and clinical outcomes.14,32 In the present study, non-Hispanic White and non-Hispanic Black groups showed stable mortality trends, while estimates for Hispanic and other racial or ethnic groups were limited by sparse data and unstable outputs. These findings suggest that racial and ethnic variation in mortality should be interpreted cautiously in the context of this dataset. Additionally, geographical factors also play a role in survival outcomes, with significant regional differences observed in AAMR trends. From 1999 to 2023, patients in the Northeast showed a notable decline in AAMR with an AAPC of -0.80 (95% CI, -1.23 to -0.38), whereas those in the South and West showed smaller nonsignificant changes, with AAPCs of -0.13 (95% CI, -0.63 to 0.37) and -0.14 (95% CI, -0.66 to 0.40). Prior studies have linked social determinants of health, including socioeconomic status and educational attainment, with outcomes in parotid cancer.8,14,32 In the current analysis, these variables were not available, and the observed regional differences could not be evaluated in relation to healthcare access, treatment utilization, or stage at diagnosis. Non-Hispanic Black and Asian pediatric patients have been reported in prior studies to present with larger tumor volumes and higher rates of facial nerve sacrifice. 38 This evidence provides relevant clinical context, although it comes from datasets with patient level tumor and treatment information that were not available in CDC WONDER. Taken together, the racial, ethnic, and geographic patterns observed in this study highlight areas for further investigation rather than providing evidence of specific biological, socioeconomic, or healthcare related mechanisms. Future studies using SEER, NCDB, or linked clinical and socioeconomic datasets may help clarify the factors underlying these population level differences.

Limitations

This study has limitations. First, CDC WONDER suppresses death counts fewer than 10, limiting precision for rare cancers and small geographic or demographic strata. Second, mortality classification depends on death certificate coding and may be affected by coding changes over time. Third, the database does not include histology, stage, grade, treatment, recurrence, comorbidities, socioeconomic status, or healthcare access. These omissions preclude causal interpretation of age, sex, race/ethnicity, or geographic differences. Fourth, state-level and smaller race/ethnicity subgroup analyses were exploratory because many estimates were sparse, suppressed, or unstable. The findings are generalizable to population-level mortality patterns captured in US death certificate data, but not to individual prognosis or treatment response without patient-level validation.

Conclusion

In this population-based analysis of US mortality data from 1999 to 2023, malignant parotid gland tumor mortality was stable overall after age adjustment. The national pattern included declines in selected subgroups and geographic areas and an increase among adults aged 85 years or older. Because the data source lacks tumor, treatment, comorbidity, and socioeconomic variables, the findings should be interpreted as descriptive mortality patterns. Patient-level datasets are needed to determine whether these differences reflect clinical, biological, or health-system factors.

No individual-level identifiable data were accessed and no participants were directly enrolled; therefore, institutional review board approval and informed consent were not required.

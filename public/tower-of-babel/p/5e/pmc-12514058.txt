Complete cox 1 gene and ITS sequence of Hirudinaria manillensis and molecular evolution analysis
Authors: Wenyi Wang, Chaojie Yang, Guangzong He, Weitao Zhao, Jinshen Yang, Yaojun Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Hirudinaria manillensis is an important blood-sucking medicinal leech widely distributed in Southeast Asia and southern China. It has been shown to treat cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. Molecular identification technology has developed rapidly, and the results are accurate and stable, which can help us to obtain rich biological information of samples for genetic evolution analysis. This study designed 3 pairs of primers for complete cox 1 gene. After sequencing by PCR products and splicing by SeqMan software, the full-length sequence of cox 1 gene was obtained by annotation of open reading frame (ORFs) on MITOS2 platform. The cox 1 gene with a length of 1536nt was obtained, and the species was identified as Hirudinaria manillensis. The Hd value and FST value of cox 1 gene were 0.858 and 0.10938, which were higher than the Hd value of ITS (0.314) and the FST value close to 0. The nucleotide diversity and GST values of the two genes were very low, and the Tajima’s D values of the neutral test were negative, without statistical significance. The genetic distance calculated by the Kimura-2 parameter method was very low. According to the biological information provided by the samples in this study, the population size of Hirudinaria manillensis in southern China was on the rise, but stable genetic diversity was not formed, and there was no obvious population differentiation and geographical isolation. This may be due to the mature artificial breeding technology in recent years, which enabled Hirudinaria manillensis to migrate from the traditional gathering places and breed in a large number in the new environment and migration site. At present, there are many molecular barcoding data of cox 1 gene in the NCBI database, but there is no complete cox 1 gene sequence as a reference, and the related ITS sequence information is more limited, indicating that there is still a lot of space for gene research of Hirudinaria manillensis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-19661-9.

Introduction

Animal medicines are playing an important role in the system of traditional Chinese medicine and widely used in clinical treatment for its remarkable therapeutic effects1. Hirudinaria manillensis, also known as buffalo leeches are Plylum Annelida-Family Hirudinidae-Genus Poecilobdella bloodsucking leeches2, which can be found in the standard of Medical Products Administration of Yunnan Province, China3,4, and the Food and Drug Administration of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region, China5, etc. has better effect of anti-coagulation, anti-platelet aggregation, improving the conditions of the cardiovascular and cerebrovascular system, etc. than Whitmania pigra, Whitman acranulate and Hirudo nipponia6,7 another three kinds of medical leech in Chinese Pharmacopoeia8. Hirudinaria manillensis is not only a characteristic ethnic medicine of China, but also has a long history of use and a wide user base in Southeast Asian countries. It is an important medicinal animal.

In recent years, molecular identification technology has been rapidly developed and wildly recognized9. The rich biological information obtained by gene sequencing can not only accurately distinguish different species, but also describe and analyze the development of intraspecific variation.

At present, mitochondrial genes (mtDNA) has become an effective tool for genetic research of animal populations, and cytochrome oxidase subunitⅠ(cox 1) sequence from mtDNA has a moderate evolutionary rate that is preferred molecular marker for the study of animal DNA barcoding. In 2003, the Consortium for the Barcode Life (CBOL) proposed that cox 1 gene is the standard sequence for identifying insects and animals10.

The internal transcribed spacer (ITS) sequence in ribosomal DNA (rDNA) not join mature ribosomes, it is subjected to less selective pressure, can provide a large amount of information. It has been used to study the evolution of molecular system within biological families, genera, species, and population11,12.

Hirudinaria manillensis widely distributed in South Asia countries and Southeastern of China. With the characteristics of complex geographical environment and climate diversity, Hirudinaria manillensis living in different environment may also evolve in different directions. This experiment collected Hirudinaria manillensis from different province and regions of China, and analyzed their molecular evolution by studying their complete cox 1 gene and ITS sequence.

Leeches were brought alive to the laboratory, weighted 10 ~ 20 g. The treatment of leeches refers to the 2-step methods for aquatic invertebrates under the American Veterinary Medical Association (AVMA) Guidelines for the Euthanasia of Animals13. Immerses processed specimens in absolute ethanol to air-dry completely in ventilated area. Finally, place specimens individually in bags and store in the cool, dry environment for further use.

Morphological identification was made based on Fauna Sinica · Hirudinea and previous publications14–17.

DNA extraction, amplification and sequencing

DNA extraction from leeches is conducted using muscle tissue dissected from the posterior sucker. The procedure follows the standardized protocol of a commercial tissue genomic DNA extraction kit (TIANGEN BIOTECH, China).

To obtain the full-length cox 1 gene, the experiment designed three pairs of primer based on the mtDNA sequence of Hirudinaria manillensis (GenBank accession number OQ076767.1) as the template, referenced from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database. The primers for ITS sequences were the current universal primers, with specific primer details provided in Table 2, the primers were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.

The purification and sequencing of PCR products were out sourced to Sangon Biotech for completion. All samples were sequenced bidirectionally to ensure the accuracy of the sequencing results.

Data processing

For each specimen, three pairs of bidirectional cox 1 and one pair of bidirectional ITS sequences were manually assembled into contiguous sequences using the SeqMan Pro module of DNASTAR Lasergene Suite (DNASTAR Inc. USA), this process generated two consensus sequences per sample.

The cox 1 contig was functionary annotated using the MITOS2 web server to predict coding regions, validate open reading frames (ORFs) and confirm conserved start/stop codons, ensuring structural integrity of the mitochondrial gene. Then calculate the Ka/Ks value of the full-length sequence of cox 1 through the KaKs_Calculator software18. In codon bias analysis, Effective number of codons (ENC) and Codon bias index (CBI) through DnaSP5.0 completed ; Codon adaptation index through EMBOSS explorer completed.

Taxonomic identification was performed via BLASTn analysis against the NCBI Nucleotide Database.

The sequences aligned using CLUSTAL, followed by analysis of the sequence data with DnaSP software. Samples were grouped by collection, and the genetic distance between groups was calculated according to the Kimura-2 parameter method. A neighbor-joining (NJ) phylogenetic tree, and Maximum Parsimony (MP) phylogenetic tree were constructed with Mega 11. Bootstrap analysis with 1000 replicates was performed to evaluate the confidence level of the branches in both kinds of trees.

Cox 1 gene sequencing results

The full-length cox 1 gene sequences, assembled and trimmed using SeqMan Pro and validated through functional annotation with MITOS2. All sequences were subjected to species identification via BLASTn search against the NCBI database. The closest match sorted by E-value was Hirudinaria manillensis (GenBank accession OQ076767.1), with a percent identify of 95.48%. Among the top 12 results sorted by homology (≥ 99% identity), two entries were taxonomically assigned to the genus Hirudinaria, while the remaining ten were unambiguously identified as Hirudinaria manilensis.

According to the cox 1 gene sequencing information, total number of sites is 1536, without alignment gaps or missing data, including 1523 invariable sites, other 13 variable sites including 9 singleton, variable sites and 4 parsimony informative sites. Detailed information on specific loci is provided in Table 4. Base substitution patterns were dominated by C-T and A-G transitions, with no transversion detected. The overall GC content was approximately 30%. A total of 8 haplotypes were identified, with haplotype diversity (Hd) of 0.858 and nucleotide diversity (π) of 0.00177. Neutrality tests yielded a Tajima’s D value of -1.18233 (P > 0.1), indicating no significant deviation from neutrality. Samples grouped by geographic origin into 5 populations (15 samples total, 3 samples from Zhanjiang were excluded from intergroup analysis due to missing data for Z2 and Z3), Population differentiation analysis revealed a Genetic Differentiation Coefficient (GST) value of 0.01015 and a Fixation Index (FST) value of 0.10938. The value of Ka/Ks provided in Table 5. The data are instructions for “NA” state Q4 and Q6, J2, Q2, Q5, Z1 and Y3, as well as Y2, G2, G3 and Q3 are three sets of completely consistent sequences. In addition, the Ka/Ks values of each sample were close to 0, indicating that the gene was highly conserved under strong functional constraints. The results of codon bias analysis provided in Table 6. The ENC values of the samples ranged from 33.067 to 33.834, indicating strong codon bias. The CAI value was about 0.546, indicating medium expression potential. CBI values ranged from 0.697 to 0.71, indicating that they were rich in host optimal codons.

Analysis and discussion

Species identification based on cox1 gene and ITS sequences

The full-length cox 1 gene (1536 nucleotides[nt]) of Hirudinaria manillensis encompasses the cox 1 barcode region (~ 650nt) amplified by universal primers currently used for species identification. Therefore, alignment of full-length sequences obtained in this study against the NCBI database yielded highly homologous identification results. However, existing cox 1 records of Hirudinaria manillensis in NCBI primary consist of partial gene fragment (~ 1200nt) and standardized barcodes (650nt), with no complete full-length sequences available. To enhance the genomic database for this species, future study should prioritize supplementing.

Compared to the cox 1 gene, the NCBI database contains even scarer reference data for ITS sequences of Hirudinaria manillensis. Sequence alignment of the ITS region revealed that the top 5 homologous matches (sorted by e-value) were Hirudo nipponia, 99.46%, (GenBank accession number JX885694.1), Poecilobdella hubeiensis, 99.03%, (GenBank accession number JX885697.1), Erpobdella octoculata, 98.82%, (GenBank accession number JX885698.1), Hirudinaria manillensis, 95.81%, (GenBank accession number JX885695.1), Poecilobdella javanica, 95.60%, (GenBank accession number JX885696.1). The sequence alignment spanned two families, three genera, and five species, with results exhibiting non-monophyletic clustering across taxonomic rank, without prior determination via DNA-barcoding, taxonomic identification based solely on ITS sequence alignment may lead to erroneous conclusions. This also indicates that there are still many gaps and spaces in the genetic research of Hirudinaria manillensis.

Genetic evolution analysis based on cox 1 and ITS sequences

The cox 1 gene in this study exhibited high haplotype diversity, extremely low nucleotide diversity, and non-significant negative values in neutrality test. It was inferred that recent population expansion promoted the accumulation of new mutations, but harmful mutations were eliminated or favorable haplotypes were fixed, which reduce the π value. The neutrality test failed to reject the neutral model, though other functional gene analyses may be required for further validation. The FST values show moderate differentiation of the sample species, but the very low GST values also suggests that the vast majority of genetic variation results from interindividual differences within populations, geographical isolation or low niche differentiation.

Low value of Hd and extremely low π value in the ITS sequence may suggest a recent population expansion that limited the accumulation of novel mutations, thereby, enhancing genetic homogeneity. Neutrality tests yielded negative values, which tentatively align with population expansion scenarios. However, the lack of statistical significance may also be related to insufficient sample size. The negative result of neutrality test combined with Ka/Ks value close to 0 indicated that cox 1 received extremely strong purifying selection during evolution. Functional stability was maintained through reinforcing selection, and the translation process was optimized by exploiting efficient codon bias. Although strict neutral theory cannot explain its extreme conservation, cox 1 as a core metabolic pathway gene, the evolutionary rate of important functional regions is significantly lower than that of non-critical regions, and the relaxed neutral theory admits the heterogeneity of evolutionary rate, as well as the synergistic effect of genetic drift and weak selection. Therefore, when analyzing the genetic evolution of genes, attention should be paid to the functional attributes and particularities of the objects being analyzed.

Moderate GST value suggests microenvironmental adaptive differentiation of nuclear genes, but FST approaching 0 indicates that high gene flow maintains genetic homogeneity among populations, implying low geographical isolation or strong migratory capacity.

Hirudinaria manillensis is widely distributed across China, with historical wild populations predominantly concentrated in Guangxi Province, which pioneered the establishment of provincial medicinal standards for this species. As the therapeutic efficacy and clinical advantages of Hirudinaria manillensis have gained recognition among medical professionals and patients, its economic value and market demand have risen substantially in recent decades. Consequently, neighboring provinces-including Yunnan (western border of Guangxi), Guangdong (eastern border), and Jiangxi (northeastern Guangdong)-have initiated aquaculture practices by producing adult specimens or juveniles from Guangxi. Over years of development, these regions have mastered capacitive breeding techniques, leading to the establishment of several major production hubs and large-scale farming facilities within China. This expansion has significantly increased domestic supply, thereby alleviating the supply-demand disparity of this species.

The cox 1 gene exhibits a rapid evolutionary rate, and ITS sequence is relatively conservative, which is consistent with the characteristics of high haplotype diversity and low one. Moderately, differentiated genetic variation mainly occurs in the cox 1 gene. However, the π value of the two sequences was very low, and the experimental samples from different collection sites still showed a low degree of geographical isolation, may be caused by the migration of Hirudinaria manillensis from Guangxi Province to neighboring provinces and the substantial increase in population through artificial breeding in recent years, without enough time to cause obvious and stable mutation or geographical isolation.

Phylogenetic analysis

In general, different sequences of the same sample and phylogenetic trees constructed by different models have some differences in clustering, but we can also find some trends and make inferences from them. According to the two phylogenetic trees of cox 1 gene, the samples could be divided into two groups by MP tree. The two sampling areas in Guangxi Province were separated, and the samples of Jingdezhen and Qinzhou were grouped together. Most of the samples from Yuxi Yuanjiang and Qingyuan were grouped together with Guilin. Although both producing areas in Guangxi Province, Guilin is located in the inland mountainous area with a higher latitude, and Qinzhou is located in the coastal area. The climate of the two areas is quite different, which may cause the isolation in terms of clustering. However, the clustering relationship between other sampling sites and them may reflect the purchase source of seedlings from artificial breeding sites outside the province. The clustering of NJ tree is relatively scattered, but it may also be able to support the view of MP tree from some small groups, such as G2 and Y1 clustered into one branch, G3 and Y2 clustered into one branch, which is consistent with the previous article that the source of Yuxi Yuanjiang seedlings may be Guilin. The NJ tree of ITS sequences divided the samples into three main groups, but the sample distribution of each group was relatively average. Combined with the above experimental results, the extremely low genetic distance, and the conservative characteristics of ITS sequence itself, it is precisely because of the low degree of ITS sequence variation of the experimental samples that each group in the phylogenetic tree is composed of samples from different sampling sites. The comprehensive results of the three trees may also indicate that the number of Hirudinaria manillensis is in the expansion period of artificial large-scale breeding. Although it has experienced a wide range of species migration and spread, the time is short and no obvious geographical isolation has been formed.

Comparison of sequence between full-length cox 1 gene and barcode

Table 9 shows that the Hd value and π value of cox1 full-length gene and barcode are quite different. The root of this difference is that barcode provides limited variable site information, which leads to the lack of information in singleton and parsimony, resulting in a large difference in Hd value and π value. Distort the results of the study. In contrast, the full-length sequence of cox1 provides more complete site information, and its analysis results are of more reference value.

The obvious differences in phylogenetic analysis can be seen from Fig. 4, combined with Figs. 2a and 3. The clustering results of the full-length cox1 gene were more detailed and the integrated bootstrap values were higher.

Although barcode plays an important role in species identification, its limited sequence length and base information lead to its limitations in intra-species analysis and deep mining of species genetic information compared with cox1 gene. Therefore, in related research, we should pay attention to the selection of research objects according to the goal and depth of the research.

Conclusion

In this study, three pairs of primers were designed to amplify the cox 1 gene to obtain the full-length sequence of the mitochondrial cox 1 gene. The complete sequence of the mitochondrial cox 1 gene was verified by MITOS2 prediction and BLASTn comparison in NCBI database. Combined with the ITS sequence of the ribosomal gene, different gene sequence information was analyzed in 18 samples of Hirudinaria manillensis collected from four provinces and six places in southern China.

At present, there is no full-length sequence of cox 1 gene in the NCBI database, and when comparing ITS sequence, the reference data is very limited and cannot be precise to the species, indicating that there is still a lot of gaps and space to be filled in the genetic data of Hirudinaria manillensis.

According to the genetic information and phylogeny of the samples, it is still in the expansion stage in southern China, which may be due to the significant increase in population caused by the regional migration of human intervention and the maturity of farming technology in other provinces.

The cox1 gene has been subjected to extremely strong purifying selection during evolution, and its extreme conservation cannot be explained by strict neutral theory. However, relaxed neutrality theory incorporates the heterogeneity of evolutionary rates of key functional genes into the analysis, acknowledging the synergistic effect of genetic drift and weak selection. Therefore, when analyzing the genetic evolution of genes, attention should be paid to the functional properties and particularity of the analyzed objects.

Although barcode is a part of the cox1 gene and plays an important role in species identification, it cannot fully reflect the biological information of the cox1 gene. Therefore, in the future related research, attention should be paid to the selection of research objects according to the goal and depth of research.

The biological information provided by a gene is relatively limited. Comprehensive analysis of the research target by combining different genes will provide ideas for the cause of a problem from different perspectives, and even find new association directions for the differences between samples.

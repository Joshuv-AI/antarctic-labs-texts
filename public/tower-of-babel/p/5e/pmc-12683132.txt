Three-dimensional dynamic simulation–assisted thoracoscopic complex right S7+S10 segmentectomy
Authors: Dorian Rojas, Karel Pfeuty
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Graphical Abstract

Central Message

Three-dimensional dynamic simulation is an innovative and reliable tool, allowing safe and accurate lung-sparing surgery in the growing era of complex basal segmentectomy.

Minimally invasive segmentectomy has emerged as a lung-sparing alternative to lobectomy for selected patients with early-stage lung cancer or metastatic disease. However, lower-lobe segmentectomy, particularly involving multiple basal segments, remains technically challenging as a result of complex and variable anatomy.1 Innovations in imaging and surgical access, including 3-dimensional (3D) dynamic simulations and subxiphoid thoracoscopic approaches, offer new opportunities to improve precision and reduce morbidity.2

We report a case of combined S7 and S10 segmentectomy through a standardized thoracoscopic subxiphoid approach, guided by preoperative 3-dimensional dynamic simulations, in a patient with suspected pulmonary metastases from colorectal cancer.

This Case Video was reviewed by our institutional review board, which determined that formal institutional review board approval was not required for a single-patient case report using deidentified data. Written informed consent for publication of clinical details, intraoperative images, and video was obtained from the patient.

Case Presentation

A 73-year-old patient with a history of resected colorectal adenocarcinoma was referred for evaluation of pulmonary nodules. Computed tomography imaging revealed a 1.5-cm nodule located between S7 and S10 in the right lower lobe, as well as 2 additional subcentimetric nodules in the middle lobe. A multidisciplinary tumor board recommended anatomical segmentectomy of S7 and S10 with concomitant wedge resection of the middle lobe nodules.

Preoperative Planning and Anatomical Considerations

Using the Fujifilm Synapse 3D platform, we created a dynamic simulation video model to replicate stepwise surgical dissection, including sequential deletion of vascular, bronchial, and parenchymal structures. The simulation revealed several anatomical variations:

The subsuperior segment (S∗) is an atypical pulmonary segment of the lower lobe, situated between the superior (S6) and basal (S7-S10) segments. Anatomically, S∗ is characterized by a distinct bronchus (B∗), usually arising between B6 and the basal bronchi, with an accompanying arterial branch (A∗) and variable venous drainage patterns, which may overlap with neighboring segments.

The presence of an S∗ has direct implications for intersegmental plane delimitation and segmentectomy management. Because S∗ possesses its own bronchovascular pedicle, failure to recognize it can lead to misidentification of the true intersegmental boundaries. This may result in inadvertent resection of adjacent subsegments, compromised venous drainage, or insufficient surgical margins. Conversely, accurate identification of S∗ allows the surgeon to adapt the sequence of pedicle division, preserve uninvolved parenchyma, and achieve precise anatomical resection with clear oncologic margins. In this Case Video, identification of (S∗) was crucial for accurately managing the intersegmental plane between S∗ and S10 after identification of the B∗ bronchus and V∗ vein.

Surgical Technique

The procedure was performed using a high-definition 4K thoracoscope via a standardized biportal subxiphoid approach (Video 1).5 A 4-cm vertical paramedian incision was made through the rectus abdominis aponeurosis to access the pleural cavity through the Larrey spaces, serving as the main utility port for dissection and specimen retrieval. An additional 1.5-cm subcostal port was placed along the midclavicular line to accommodate the thoracoscope and auxiliary instruments.5 The operation began with a wedge resection of the 2 middle lobe nodules.

Posterior Mediastinal Dissection

The patient was then rotated anteriorly, and the lower lobe was mobilized anteriorly to expose the posterior mediastinum. This maneuver allowed lobe-specific lymph node dissection of stations 7, 8, and 9 after division of the inferior pulmonary ligament.

Inferior Pulmonary Ligament Approach

A Trendelenburg position was adopted to enhance diaphragmatic exposure. The subxiphoid approach provided a panoramic, tangential view of the inferior hilum, facilitating precise dissection of venous and bronchial segmental branches. The inferior pulmonary vein was dissected first. V7a was stapled, whereas V7b was coagulated. V6 and the inferior basal vein were also identified (Figure 1, A).

Anatomical dissection of segments S7 and S10. A, Intraoperative view showing anatomical dissection of segment 7. B, Intraoperative view showing anatomical dissection of segment 10.

The anterior fissure was opened using a tunnel technique, guided by the dissection of the bronchial station 10 lymph node. The A7 artery was dissected and sealed using a thermofusion device (Figure 1, A). As expected from preoperative imaging, the B7 bronchus was visualized anterior to the basal vein in this S7 anterior type configuration and was stapled. Elevation of the B7 stump facilitated exposure of segment 8 structures.

Dissection then proceeded along the posterior intersegmental plane (ISP) between the supernumerary S∗ segment and S10 (ISP-1 and ISP-2). This was a crucial step: identification of B∗ provided the correct landmark for locating A10, which runs between B∗ and B10. Opening this intersegmental plane enhanced exposure of the A10 artery and B10 bronchus. The A10 artery was isolated and divided, followed by identification of B10 after division of the V9 vein, which established the appropriate stapling level to preserve the B9b variant branch (Figure 1, B).

Intersegmental Plane Management

A near-infrared fluorescence imaging system (Medtronic EleVision IR) was used after a single intravenous injection of indocyanine green at a dose of 0.3 mg/kg. This allowed clear visualization of the intersegmental demarcation line between S7/S10 and the adjacent still-perfused segments. The demarcation line was cauterized for reference. Dissection of intersegmental planes proceeded with double-stump traction, beginning with the anterior plane between S7 and S8 (ISP 3), followed by the medial plane between S7 and S9 (ISP 4), and concluding with the posterior plane between S9 and S10 (ISP 5 and ISP 6) (Figure 2).

Intersegmental plane management in S7+S10 segmentectomy.

The specimen was extracted through the subxiphoid port. Intraoperative frozen section confirmed a 2 cm clear parenchymal margin. The patient benefited from an Enhanced Recovery After Surgery day 1 discharge surgery with an uneventful follow-up.

Discussion

This case highlights the practical application of 3D dynamic simulation and a thoracoscopic subxiphoid approach in a complex basal segmentectomy. The integration of preoperative 3D modeling allowed for detailed anatomical mapping, facilitating strategic sequencing of dissection and improving surgical confidence in navigating anatomical variations.

The subxiphoid approach offered key ergonomic and visual advantages, particularly for accessing posterior basal segments. By eliminating intercostal trauma and providing a caudal-to-cranial view, this approach improved the precision and safety of segmental dissection, especially in basal segment locations.

An important aspect of this surgical strategy is the convergence of multiple intersegmental planes at the so-called “deep point” of dissection. This anatomical configuration results in a pyramidal resection shape, with the apex of the pyramid corresponding precisely to this deep point. Proper identification and management of this area is critical to achieving complete resection with safe margins while preserving surrounding parenchyma, and this can only be achieved through an extended and accurate venous dissection on the basis of the 3D preoperative planning.

Conclusions

This case demonstrates the feasibility of combining 3D dynamic simulation with a thoracoscopic subxiphoid approach to perform complex segmentectomies. This strategy may be valuable in expanding the indications for minimally invasive anatomical resections, especially in anatomically challenging lower lobe tumors.

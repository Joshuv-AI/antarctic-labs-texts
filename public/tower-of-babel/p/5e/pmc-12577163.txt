Gene variants of interferon induced with helicase C domain 1 in Japanese patients with Dermatomyositis-associated rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease: a genetic association study using whole-exome and Sanger sequencing
Authors: Masaki Okamoto, Akira Yoshida, Yoshiaki Zaizen, Motoko Ishida, Toshimasa Shimizu, Noriho Sakamoto, Hironao Hozumi, Yasuhiko Yamano, Takahisa Gono, Norikazu Matsuo, Shinjiro Kaieda, Masataka Kuwana, Tomoya Miyamura, Atsushi Kawakami, Hiroshi Mukae, Takafumi Suda, Yasuhiro Kondoh, Ken Yamamoto, Tomoaki Hoshino
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatients with Dermatomyositis (DM) having anti-melanoma differentiation-associated gene 5 (MDA5) antibodies occasionally develop rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease (RP-ILD), a serious inflammatory complication; however, no genetic biomarker has yet been identified for this condition. This study aimed to identify interferon-induced with helicase C domain 1 (also known as MDA5) gene (IFIH1) variants associated with a high risk of DM-associated RP-ILD.MethodsA total of 204 patients with DM were enrolled from six institutions. Whole-exome sequencing was performed on nine patients with DM-associated interstitial lung disease (ILD) who were positive for anti-MDA5 antibodies, and six single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were identified: p.Val194Ala (no reference SNP ID), rs1990760, rs3747517, rs12479043, rs10930046, and rs141134657. Allele frequencies of eight IFIH1 variants—including these six SNPs plus rs117608083 and rs183412282—were analyzed using Sanger sequencing. Statistical analyses included allele frequency comparisons and genotype-based assessments (dominant and recessive models), and the Armitage trend test.ResultsAmong the 204 patients with DM, 109 (53.4%) had classic DM and 95 (46.6%) had clinically amyopathic DM. A total of 174 patients (85.3%) had ILD, and 62 (30.4%) were positive for anti-MDA5 antibodies. Among the 204 patients with DM, low minor allele frequencies (MAFs) for rs12479043 and rs10930046 were significantly (p < 0.05) associated with high rates of ILD and anti-MDA5 antibodies. Among the 174 patients with ILD, a low MAF for rs141134657 was significantly (p < 0.05) associated with a high incidence of acute onset of ILD within 3 months, in accordance with allele, genotype, and Armitage trend tests, but not the recessive model. A low MAF for rs141134657 tended to be associated with high serum C-reactive protein levels in accordance with the additive and dominant models, but the result was not significant. None of the analyzed IFIH1 variants were significantly associated with serum levels of ferritin, KL-6, and lactate dehydrogenase.ConclusionThis study identified genetic biomarkers associated with the risk of RP-ILD in patients with DM. These findings suggest that analysis of IFIH1 variants may serve as a valuable tool for predicting the development of RP-ILD in this patient population.

Introduction

Interstitial lung disease (ILD) is a common feature and the major cause of death and malignant disease among patients with polymyositis/dermatomyositis (PM/DM), as a subtype of idiopathic inflammatory myopathies (IIMs) [1–3]. Some autoantibodies in patients with DM, such as anti-aminoacyl tRNA synthetase antibody, have potential as biomarkers for the diagnosis and prediction of ILD [4]. The presence and titer of anti-melanoma differentiation-associated gene 5 (MDA5) antibodies are associated with higher mortality in patients with DM, as a result of the development of fatal rapidly progressive ILD (RP-ILD) [4–8]. MDA5 (interferon induced with helicase C domain 1, IFIH1) is a retinoic acid-inducible gene I-like receptor family protein that acts as a sensor for RNA viruses in the innate immune system [9]. The production of autoantibodies against MDA5 has been associated with the development of RP-ILD in patients with DM and anti-MDA5 antibodies, because of the regional characteristics and seasonality of this disease [9, 10], with an incidence of RP-ILD in anti-MDA5 antibody-positive patients with DM -ILD of 50%–74% [5, 6, 11]. There is thus a need for the further development of other biomarkers associated with acute respiratory failure.

Funabiki et al. showed that a mutant mouse line harboring the single missense IFIH1 mutation G821S spontaneously developed lupus-like nephritis showing immunoglobulin (Ig) and complement deposition, upregulation of inflammatory cytokines and chemokines in the kidney, increased Ig expression, and serum positivity for anti-nuclear and anti-DNA antibodies [12]. We previously found increased expression levels of IgG, IgM, IgA, and complement component 3 (C3) in the lungs of patients with DM and anti-MDA5 antibodies. We also demonstrated that administration of anti-MDA5 antibodies to MDA5 transgenic mice induced lung injury [13]. The precise role of anti-MDA5 autoantibodies in the development of respiratory failure in patients with DM-associated RP-ILD, however, remains unclear.

Sato et al. suggested that old age, high C-reactive protein (CRP), low peripheral capillary oxygen saturation, and anti-MDA5 antibody positivity were independent predictors of short-term mortality in patients with PM/DM-ILD [7]. High serum levels of ferritin and interleukin-18, proteins involved in macrophage activation syndrome, and alveolar-arterial oxygen difference were associated with shortened survival or response to therapy in a retrospective study of patients with DM [11, 14]. Some studies have investigated the associations between genetic variants of MDA5 and diseases [15–20]. A single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) of IFIH1 (rs1990760) was identified as a risk factor for systemic lupus erythematosus, type 1 diabetes mellitus, and Grave’s disease [18, 19], while Lamborn et al. associated a homozygous missense mutation of IFIH1 (rs117608083) with the defense mechanism against human rhinovirus infection [20]. No genomic biomarkers for predicting the development of RP-ILD in patients with DM, however, have yet been established.

This study aimed to identify IFIH1 variants associated with the development of DM -associated RP-ILD in 204 patients with DM enrolled from six medical institutions. We performed whole-exome sequencing of nine patients with DM-ILD with anti-MDA5 antibodies and detected six SNPs (c.581T > C, p.Val194Ala [no reference SNP ID], rs1990760, rs3747517, rs12479043, rs10930046, rs141134657). We then analyzed the allele frequencies of these six and two other IFIH1 SNPs (rs117608083, and rs183412282) using Sanger sequencing. These results may provide genomic biomarkers for predicting the development of RP-ILD.

Patients and methods

Study participants

We initially analyzed the exons of the IFIH1 in nine patients with DM-ILD with anti-MDA5 autoantibodies from 2017 to 2018 using whole-exome sequencing. Subsequently, we carried out a multicenter prospective cohort study involving 208 patients, including 9 cases previously analyzed by whole-exome sequencing. with DM who received care at six collaborative facilities from 2017 to 2022. The main inclusion criteria were patients who met the diagnostic criteria for DM as reported by Bohan and Peter [21, 22] or the diagnostic criteria for Clinically amyopathic dermatomyositis (CADM) reported by Sontheimer [2], and patients aged > 20 and < 80 years. Patients diagnosed with uncontrolled malignant disease by radical treatment, heart failure, infectious pneumonia, acute lung injury due to known causes other than DM, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, radiation pneumonitis, pneumoconiosis, pulmonary lymphangioleiomyomatosis, or alveolar proteinosis were excluded. The presence and classification of ILD were determined by high-resolution computed tomography and histological diagnosis of cases that underwent lung biopsies by investigators in each faculty, based on global guidelines and classification of idiopathic interstitial pneumonias [23, 24]. The definition of acute onset of ILD was based on the criteria of RP-ILD in a previous study [25], with a period between the diagnosis of DM and onset of ILD within 3 months.

Next generation sequencing of IFIH1

Genomic DNA was extracted from whole blood using a NucleoSpinr® Blood L kit (Macherey-Nagel, Delaware, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Exome enrichment was performed using a SureSelect Human All Exon V6 Kit (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA), targeting protein-coding exons and flanking intronic regions. Sequencing was conducted on the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform with 150 bp paired-end reads at Takara Bio Inc. (Tokyo, Japan) [26, 27].

Sanger sequencing

Eight IFIH1 SNPs (c.581T >C, p.Val194Ala [no reference SNP ID], rs1990760, rs3747517, rs12479043, rs10930046, rs141134657, rs117608083, and rs183412282) were analyzed using Sanger sequencing. Genomic DNA was extracted using a QIAamp® DNA Blood Midi Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Polymerase chain reaction amplification and sequencing were performed using primers designed to flank each of the eight IFIH1 SNPs. Sequencing was performed using an ABI3730xl DNA Analyzer (Thermo Fisher Scientific/Applied Biosystems, CA, USA) at Takara Bio Inc [17].

Data collection

In the prospective study, pulmonary function and serum CRP, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), Krebs von den Lungen-6 (KL-6), and ferritin levels were measured at the same time as blood was collected for variant measurement.

Statistical analysis

Data are expressed as median (25th–75th percentiles of the interquartile range). Genotype frequencies were calculated and allele distributions were compared across subgroups. Genetic analysis included allele and genotype testing, dominant and recessive models, Armitage trend tests, and additive models. A p value < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant. The association between IFIH1 variants and the presence of ILD was investigated in a total of 204 patients with DM. To assess if these variants were associated with the acute onset of ILD or with ILD-related biomarkers, analyses were conducted in a subset of 174 patients with DM with ILD. Statistical analyses were performed using PLINK ver1.07 [28].

Results

Patient characteristics

We initially analyzed the exons of the IFIH1 in nine out of 208 patients using whole-exome sequencing. All nine patients (2 (22%) male, median age 54.0 years (range: 49.0–60.0)) had DM-ILD and were positive for anti-MDA5 antibodies. A total of 208 patients with IIMs consented to participate in this prospective study, but two patients subsequently withdrew consent. Two patients with PM were also excluded. The clinical characteristics of the 204 patients with DM, including 50 (24.5%) males and 63 (31.5%) smokers, median age 54.1 (46.3–63.6) years, are summarized in Table 1. Among the study patients, 174 patients (85.3%) had ILD. The DM subtypes included classic DM (109 patients, 53.4%) and CADM (95 patients, 46.6%). Of the 174 patients with DM -ILD, 91 (52.3%) developed acute ILD, defined as onset within 3 months of DM diagnosis. Among the study patients, 62 (30.4%) had anti-MDA5 antibodies and 91 (50.3%) had anti-aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase antibodies. Regarding treatment, 198 patients (97.1%) received prednisolone, 101 (49.5%) received methylprednisolone, 146 (71.6%) received tacrolimus, 45 (22.1%) received cyclosporine A, and 50 (24.5%) received intravenous cyclophosphamide. No patients received oral cyclophosphamide. During a median follow-up period of 1949.0 (935.3–3229.8.3.8), five patients died, including two due to malignant disease and one each due to ILD, infection, and graft-versus-host disease.

Whole-exome sequencing

We carried out whole-exome sequencing of DNA samples obtained from nine patients with DM-ILD with anti-MDA5 antibodies. We identified six IFIH1 variants, all of which were missense mutations (Table 2): rs1990760 (c.2836G > A, p.Ala946Thr) in three patients, rs3747517 (c.2528 A > G, p.His843Arg) in five, rs10930046 (c.1379 A > G, p.His460Arg) in three, one unspecified variant (c.581T > C, p.Val194Ala) in one patient, one synonymous variant, rs12479043 (c.1491G > C, p.Thr497Thr), in three patients, and one intronic variant, rs141134657 (c.1095 + 98_1095 + 102delGTTTA), in two patients. A homozygous variant of rs3747517 was observed in only one patient, while all other variants were heterozygous.

Sanger sequencing

We analyzed eight IFIH1 variants, rs1990760, rs3747517, rs12479043, rs10930046, rs141134657, and an unspecified variant (c.581T > C, p.Val194Ala)], and the immunodeficiency disorder-associated IFIH1 variants rs117608083 [20] and rs183412282 in 204 patients with DM using Sanger sequencing (Table 3). The results of allele and genotype testing, dominant and recessive models, Armitage trend testing, and additive models are shown in Tables 4 and 5. The rs14113576 genotype data were missing in two patients.

Among the 204 patients with DM, low minor allele frequencies (MAFs) of rs12479043 and rs10930046 were significantly (p < 0.05) associated with a high risk of ILD, as demonstrated by allele and genotype testing, the recessive model, and the Armitage trend test, but not by the dominant model (Table 4A). Low MAFs of rs12479043 and rs10930046 were also significantly (p < 0.05) associated with a high frequency of anti-MDA5 antibody positivity, but only in genotypic testing (Table 4B).

Among the 174 patients with DM -ILD, a low MAF of rs141134657 was significantly (p < 0.05) associated with a high incidence of acute-onset ILD (within 3 months), according to allele testing and genotypic testing and the Armitage trend test, but not in the recessive model (Table 4C).

Comparing IFIH1 variants with blood biomarkers among patients with DM-ILD, a low MAF of rs141134657 tended to be associated with high serum CRP levels in the additive and dominant models, although the association was not significant (Table 5A). A low MAF of rs183412282 was significantly associated with elevated serum LDH levels, but only one of the 204 patients with DM carried the minor allele. There were no significant associations between IFIH1 variants and serum levels of ferritin, KL-6, or LDH (Table 5B, C, D).

Discussion

In the present study, we analyzed eight IFIH1 variants in 204 patients with DM. We found that low MAFs of certain IFIH1 SNPs, specifically rs12479043, rs10930046, and rs141134657, were significantly associated with a high risk of ILD, a high frequency of anti-MDA5 antibody positivity, and increased incidences of acute-onset ILD and elevated serum CRP levels. These findings suggest that IFIH1 genotyping using blood samples may be useful for predicting the development of RP-ILD in patients with DM.

MDA5 has an N-terminal caspase recruitment domain that promotes downstream signaling, leading to the phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of interferon regulatory factors and nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB), which in turn activate the transcription of type I interferons [9]. A previous genome-wide association study identified two loss-of-function genetic variants of IFIH1 (rs35337543 [IVS8+, E627*], rs35667904 [I923V]) that reduced innate immune responses and were associated with resistance to type 1 diabetes mellitus [15, 16]. A reporter assay showed that these mutations affected interferon gene activation via viral stimulation [16]. Similarly, another study identified five gain-of-function SNPs in IFIH1 (rs587777445, rs587777446, rs587777447, rs587777449, and 672601336) as risk variants for Aicardi–Goutières syndrome, a type I interferonopathy, and whole-exome analysis demonstrated robust upregulation of interferon-stimulated gene expression [17]. The association of rs141134657 with acute ILD onset and elevated CRP levels raises the possibility that this intronic variant may influence MDA5-related immune responses, potentially contributing to enhanced type I interferon signaling in a subset of patients with RP-ILD.

A previous study showed that certain IFIH1 mutations enhanced interferon gene activation in response to viral stimulation [9]. In addition, genome-wide association studies and expression quantitative trait locus analyses involving 576 patients with CADM revealed that a splicing variant of WDFY4 (rs7919656), an intronic SNP, was significantly associated with CADM, and the truncated WDFY4 isoform interacted with pattern recognition receptors such as MDA5 and potentiated NF-κB activation mediated by these receptors [29]. Some patients with DM-ILD with anti-MDA5 antibodies do not develop RP-ILD, and previous reports found that 50%–74% of Asian patients with DM-ILD and anti-MDA5 antibodies had RP-ILD [5, 6, 11]. Early identification of RP-ILD in patients with DM is crucial, however, because these patients require immediate intensive immunosuppressive treatment, including triple therapy with corticosteroids, tacrolimus, and intravenous cyclophosphamide [30]. In the present study, the intronic variant rs141134657 in the IFIH1 was identified as a potential biomarker for predicting RP-ILD. The reason why IFIH1 variants correlated only with CRP levels, but not with LDH, KL-6, or ferritin levels, is unclear, but is speculated as follows. First, the mechanisms underlying the increase of each biomarker differ. Ferritin plays the role of pro-inflammatory mediator like CRP and immunosuppressant and is known to contribute to cytokine storm in macrophage activation syndrome and sepsis [31]. KL-6 and LDH primarily reflect alveolar epithelial damage or fibrosis rather than as pro-inflammatory mediator [32]. Second, the timing of blood sampling may have influenced the results, as serum marker levels fluctuate with disease activity, whereas genetic variants remain stable. Third, genetic factors other than IFIH1 may affect these biomarkers. For example, MUC1-SNP (rs4072037) has been associated with serum KL-6 levels and pulmonary dysfunction in pulmonary alveolar proteinosis [33], and genetic variants regulating iron metabolism influence baseline ferritin concentrations in healthy adults [34]. Thus, the discrepancy between IFIH1 variants and each serum biomarkers remains unexplained and need further investigation.

The individual genetic variation represented by the IFIH1 variant demonstrated limited sensitivity for identifying ILD phenotypes among patients with DM. Nevertheless, the combination of this genetic variant with anti-MDA5 antibody positivity may enhance the ability to stratify high-risk patients with DM-ILD, facilitating early therapeutic decision-making.

A recent meta-analysis suggested that the development of ILD in patients with IIMs was associated with several clinical factors, including older age, arthritis/arthralgia, fever, the presence of anti-Jo-1 and anti-MDA5 antibodies, elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate, and elevated CRP levels, while malignancy was associated with a decreased risk of ILD [35]. No genetic variants significantly associated with DM-ILD have yet been identified. In the current study, rs12479043 and rs10930046 were significantly associated with both ILD and anti-MDA5 antibody positivity in patients with DM; however, the differences in allele frequencies were not conclusive, possibly due to the small number of patients without ILD in our cohort. Further studies with larger sample sizes are therefore warranted to validate these associations.

This study had several limitations. First, the proportions of cases of DM and ILD among the enrolled patients with DM were higher than those reported in previous studies [1]. This was likely caused by selection bias, given that the participating centers specialized in ILD management and may thus not represent the general DM population. Second, the association between gene variants and long-term outcomes could not be adequately assessed because the mortality due to ILD was low (only one patient died of ILD) in this study. Future cohort studies involving more representative populations and longer follow-up durations will be necessary to confirm and expand upon our findings. Third, in the present study, we have not clarified whether IFIH1 variant influences MDA5 transcription or translation. Previous reports have demonstrated that certain IFIH1 variants can influence MDA5 function and downstream type I interferon signaling [9, 16, 17]. It is possible that the IFIH1 variants identified in our study may affect splicing or regulatory elements. However, this speculation remains elusive and further functional analyses are required. Fourth, anti-MDA5 antibody titers have been reported to correlate with the severity and treatment response of DM-ILD [8, 11]. In this study, titers were not available in the multicenter dataset, making analysis of their relationship with IFIH1 variants impossible. This limitation highlights the need for future study including antibody titer data.

Conclusion

This is the first study identified genetic biomarkers for the risk of RP-ILD in patients with DM. These results show the potential of IFIH1 variant for predicting the development of RP-ILD in patients with DM, but additional study in larger cohorts will be required.

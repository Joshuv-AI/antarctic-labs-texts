Quality of life and social reinsertion of patients on maintenance haemodialysis in four government funded hospitals in Cameroon
Authors: Denis Georges Teuwafeu, Mervis Sehbing, Marie-Patrice Halle, Maimouna Mahamat, Hermine Fouda, Gloria Ashuntantang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReduced quality of life is associated with shorter survival in chronic illnesses. However, the health-related quality of life (HRQOL) and social reinsertion of patients on maintenance haemodialysis is much more underappreciated in resource-limited countries such as Cameroon.MethodA hospital-based cross-sectional study was carried out from February 22nd to May 20th, 2022, in 4 government-funded haemodialysis centres in three randomly selected regions of Cameroon. Patients received twice-weekly dialysis sessions. Social reinsertion and HRQOL were assessed using a structured questionnaire and the kidney disease quality of life instrument (KDQOL-36™). HRQOL scores < 50 were categorized as low, while scores > 50 reflected better HRQOL. Data were analysed using the software statistical package for Social Sciences version 25.0. Statistical significance was set at a p value < 0.05.ResultsThe study included 434 patients. The mean age was 48.33 (13.55) years, 65.7% (285/434) were male, 62.3% (269/434) had no monthly income, and the mean dialysis vintage was 3.74 (3.83) years. The mean HRQOL score was 44.34 (9.77), and 76.2% (325/434) had HRQOL scores < 50). Overall HRQOL was associated with older age (aOR: 2.344, CI 1.089–5.04). After the initiation of maintenance haemodialysis, 67.1% (49/73) of students dropped out of school. The main reason for school absenteeism and unemployment was physical insufficiency, with 82.4% (19/24) and 52.4% (75/144), respectively. There were no promotions or marriages after initiation; 51% (221/434) of relationships with relatives and friends were affected negatively, while 83.3% (66/79) of those of marriageable ages could not find suitors. The social participation score was poor in 61.5% (267/434) of participants. There was an association between low QOL and social participation (p = 0.009).ConclusionThe HRQOL of patients on maintenance haemodialysis is greatly reduced, especially their physical health status. Older age was a determinant of low QOL. Additionally, social reinsertion remains poor due to adverse changes that occur to these patients and their families after dialysis initiation.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Keywords: Maintenance haemodialysis, Quality of life, Social reinsertion, Cameroon

Introduction

Health-related quality of life (HRQOL) has been recognized globally as a major aspect of healthcare delivery in patients with chronic diseases such as Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) [1]. In developed world settings, various studies have demonstrated that quality of life (QOL) is an independent predictor of both morbidity and mortality [2]. Therefore, slowing deterioration or increasing QOL has progressively become a key outcome of successful disease management [3]. CKD poses a serious threat to global health due to its mortality, morbidity, and socioeconomic repercussions, with a global prevalence estimated at 8–16% [4]. In Cameroon, the prevalence of CKD varies between 10 and 14.2% in urban areas [5], with this patient population being at the highest risk for progression to kidney failure. Kidney failure is a major public health problem with increasing prevalence and burden, and its mainstay of treatment is kidney replacement therapy (KRT). Hemodialysis (HD) is the most widely used modality [6]. There are approximately 2 million people on HD worldwide, among whom only 4000 patients are on HD in sub-Saharan Africa, which is less than 1% of the global cases. These treatment gaps are a result of low availability and high cost of treatment.

Hemodialysis is a palliative treatment that is time intensive, expensive, and only provides a partial solution to deteriorating kidney function [7]. Like other chronic diseases, patients with HD usually experience physical dysfunction, social inefficiency, and psychological conditions, which deteriorate QOL and lead to poor social reinsertion due to socioeconomic repercussions [8]. In general, an individual with a chronic disease may find it difficult to follow a normal education, risk losing their job, maintain social activities, be refused a loan, etc [9]. Social consequences are reflected negatively in their employment rates, social participation, academic achievements, and relationships [10–12]. There has been an increased concern worldwide regarding the QOL of kidney failure patients, no longer focusing just on survival but on means to improve QOL and social reinsertion [13]. Several studies performed worldwide and a few studies conducted in Africa showed that there is a significant deterioration in QOL and that return to society is hampered in HD patients [14, 15]. However, the above studies on HRQOL in this patient group are still incipient, similar to social reinsertion.

Patients and methods

Study design

This study was a hospital-based cross-sectional, descriptive, and analytical study from February 22 to May 20, 2022, in 4 government-funded HD centres in 3 regions of Cameroon (2 urban and 1 semi-urban) offering dialysis sessions twice weekly. The sampling method was consecutive with aim to review all eligible patient present during study period. All consenting patients on maintenance haemodialysis (N = 434) were considered eligible. Patients with severe active disease, cognitive impairment, congestive heart failure (CHF), chronic obstructive pulmonary diseases (COPD), and stroke were excluded, as these factors could potentially affect their Quality of Life (QOL). The dialysis units included in this study were 4 of the 7-government funded HD centres in Cameroon offering twice weekly dialysis sessions, in 3 out of 10 regions in Cameroon comprised (2 urban, 1 semi-urban), of which 3 of these hospitals are first category referral hospitals present in 2 of the largest cities in the country in terms of population. Three centres at the higher level in the health pyramid of Cameroon, located in the economic and city capitals of the country, serving different types of populations. They are also national reference hospitals. The fourth hospital was the Buea regional hospital, a secondary hospital situated at the intermediate of the Cameroon health pyramid that serves as a reference hospital for the region and is situated in a semi urban town. We excluded private dialysis centres as they are not subsidised and offer few chronic dialysis.

Data collection

Following clear explanation of the informative sheet in the language best understood by the participant, an informed consent was obtained following standardized procedures for both adults and minors (alongside their parents or guardians).

Demographic and socioeconomic history was obtained through direct questioning of the patient and recorded. A standardized Kidney Disease Quality of Life (KDQOL™-36) questionnaire from the Research and Development (RAND) Corporation was used [16]. The KDQOL™-36 questionnaire contains 5 subscales: the first 2 subscales (Physical Component Summary (PCS) and Mental Component Summary (MCS)) are in Items 1–12, Burden of Kidney Disease (BKD) for items 13–16, Symptoms and Problems of Kidney Disease (SPKD) for items 17–28, and Effects of Kidney Disease (EKD) for items 29–36. The first two subscales are a generic measure of HRQOL, whereas the last three assess issues specific to patients with kidney failure or earlier stages of chronic kidney disease. A total score and domain-specific score were calculated for each patient, and a mean score was calculated for each of the subsamples. Total scores above 50 (max 100) were considered to represent good levels of QOL, while scores below 50 represented poor QOL [17].

In addition, a researcher-structured questionnaire was used to assess the level of social reinsertion following dialysis initiation with the following components: academic achievement, marriage/divorce while on dialysis, promotions/demotions at work, and social participation/relationship.

The perception of participants on the impact dialysis has on their daily activities was graded using Likert scale questions, with a mean score for each component. Values above the mean score for each component represented good perceptions of the impact of dialysis on the level of social reinsertion.

All methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

Data management

Data entry and analysis were performed using SPSS version 25. HRQOL was determined using the KDQOL-36™ scoring program (V2.0), which is provided as a program in an Excel spreadsheet used to create scores and produce descriptive statistics for the KDQOL-36™ measures. A total score and domain-specific score were calculated for each patient, and a mean score was calculated for each of the subsamples. Total scores above 50 (max 100) were considered to represent good levels of QOL, while scores below 50 represented poor QOL. Continuous variables are summarized as means and standard deviations, while categorical data are summarized as frequencies and percentages. Group comparisons for categorical variables were performed using the chi-square test, while multivariate logistic regression was used to identify factors associated with QOL and expressed by the adjusted odds ratio (aOR). Differences were considered statistically significant at a P value < 0.05 at a 95% confidence interval.

For this research, the score for the HRQOL and level of social reinsertion (in education, employment, relationship, and social participation) were considered our dependent variables while our independent variables were age, sex, marital status, dialysis vintage, educational status, cause of CKD, and monthly income.

Definition of terms

1) Chronic kidney disease

CKD was defined in our study as the presence of urine abnormalities (proteinuria, leukocyturia,

or haematuria), and/or GFR < 60 ml/min/1.73 present both at baseline and 3 months later.

2) Maintenance haemodialysis

Haemodialysis carried out at regular intervals to treat chronic renal failure for a period of > 3 months.

Ethical considerations

Ethical clearance was obtained from the Faculty of Health Sciences, University of Buea (FHS), and its Institutional Review Board (ref. 2022/1313-02/UB/SG/IRB/FHS). The confidentiality of participants was maintained by using codes rather than names on questionnaires. The study received administrative authorization from the directors of hospitals. Patients were seen on the day of their dialysis, before or during the dialysis session. Following a clear explanation of the informative sheet in the language best understood by the participant, participants were required to sign an informed consent form, and for patients less than 18 years old, an assent form was signed by the legal guardian or the parents. Data were entered into a structured questionnaire following a one-on-one interview with the patients.

Discussion

Although there have been several studies about the quality of life in patients on hemodialysis globally, to the best of our knowledge, this is the first-ever study on the quality of life of kidney failure patients on hemodialysis in Cameroon. The principal finding of this study was that approximately three-quarters of the proportion of patients with kidney failure on dialysis had lower QOL. These findings were consistent with studies in Ethiopia, Kenya, Rwanda, and the US [17–20]. However, our QOL is lower than that reported by Cohen et al. in the US and Shahrin et al. in Malaysia, who reported slightly higher QOL [20, 21]. This could be explained by the differences in dialysis delivery in these settings, as the 3 times weekly frequency of dialysis performed in these other studies could impact QOL positively compared to the 2 times weekly dialysis frequency performed in our study. Many studies have shown the relationship between higher frequency dialysis and better HRQOL in kidney failure patients [22]. Lower scores may indicate poor prognosis of the patients and compromised quality of clinical care. The findings of this study underscore that there is a need to assess the patients’ QOL during the follow-up, and appropriate measures must be taken to reassure the patients.

The burden of kidney disease was the subscale where participants obtained the lowest mean score, indicating how much kidney failure interferes with daily lifetime taking, frustrating and making the respondent feel like a burden. The good scores in the symptom/problem subscales were due to the subjects’ familiarity over time with pain, uremic symptoms, and others while having their way to overcome these. This finding corresponds to those reported by authors in studies performed in Africa and Europe [7, 9, 13, 23]. PCS scores were lower than MCS scores, consistent with studies performed in Chile and Mangalore [1, 22], where participants had higher MCS scores, indicating that despite the worsening of the physical health status, the mental health of dialysis patients is relatively preserved. This may reflect the ability of kidney failure patients to adapt psychologically to their situation over time [24]. Moreover, these results are consistent with those of studies conducted in the Netherlands and Oman [25].

HRQOL was found to be associated with older age, and increasing age has been shown to negatively affect HRQOL [13, 17, 21] due to short life expectancy and more comorbid conditions, such as depression and cognitive disorders [26]. Although multiple studies have shown a significant correlation between employment status and level of income, this study failed to identify any statistically significant correlation between these factors, possibly due to differences in the homogeneity of the population, but our findings corroborate Kamau et al. in Kenya, who found no associating factors [18].

Young adult patients on HD are at an important stage of development when kidney failure may adversely influence progress in education and employment. Of the proportion of participants who were students, the majority (67.1%) dropped out following dialysis initiation. It has been shown that people with chronic health conditions are at an increased risk of school dropout in the general population [27]. Additionally, there are reasons why people on dialysis may stop going to school, including financial obligations, fitting dialysis sessions with a school schedule, and the general conception of having no life while on dialysis [10]. Meanwhile, school continuation was mostly hindered by physical insufficiency (82.4%), leading to school absenteeism. This is consistent with studies performed by Murray et al. and Clave et al. in the US [28, 29]. This suggest that children should be encourage to continue their education after initiation into dialysis. Several measures can be envisage to achieve that: increase the number of dialysis session per week to three (12 h), reduce the waiting time in the centre to minimise the time spent out of school and absenteeism.

Only a small proportion of kidney failure patients are employed at the start of dialysis compared with the general population. However, people with chronic diseases such as kidney failure are heavily burdened not just by the disease but also by the treatment modalities, which are major contributors to reduced employment rates [30]. In this study, the ratio of unemployment was high (44%) in maintenance HD working-age patients, with the principal reason being physical insufficiency, consistent with studies in China and Japan. Implementing other methods of kidney replacement therapy like kidney transplant or peritoneal dialysis may improve the continuation of employment after initiation [31].

We recorded that 9 (3.0%) had divorced while on dialysis, and statistics show that 75% of marriages afflicted with a chronic illness end in divorce [32]. Meanwhile, approximately one-third of those who married reported that dialysis had affected their relationship negatively with their spouses, as they felt like a burden to them. Studies have shown that chronic diseases impact the relationships of patients, especially as they are time consuming, and financial responsibilities and the length of time spent dealing with chronic diseases can often shift the balance of a relationship. More than two-thirds of those of marriageable ages reported that dialysis and their general health condition posed a limitation to them getting stable relations and finding suitors. Furthermore, half of our participants agreed that relatives and friends distanced themselves from them, saying their illness was mystical. This shows that some of these patients lack the necessary support from relatives.

Social participation was greatly hampered by dialysis inception in 267 (61.5%) of the population. This is probably because the incapacitation of chronic HD patients often results in social isolation and self-isolation from society. They are therefore faced with issues of social adaptation and resocialization, posing a change in the lifestyle of these patients causing them not to be able to keep up with previous regular activities, which was similar to studies conducted by Kerklaan et al. in 6 countries [33].

HRQOL was found to be associated with social reinsertion. Those with poor QOL were reinserted poorly into society in terms of social participation. This is because these patients are socially vulnerable and limited in what they can accomplish. Therefore, many are forced to readjust their lives to cope with their chronic disease, and this involves withdrawal from social activities, strict limitations on travel to avoid compromising dialysis sessions, and avoidance of ceremonies to adhere to their fluid and diet restrictions [30]. Alshraifeen A et al. [34] confirmed that higher levels of social support were associated with better QOL and social reinsertion. All dialysis patients should be targeted for social support intervention by those caring for them. Education for nursing students and contentious education for working nurses and doctors should emphasize on the importance of considering social support as a way for improving the outcomes in HD patients considering the formal and informal resources available for these patients.

Study limitations

The major limitation of this study was that we did not evaluate the possible effects of biochemical parameters on the QOL of patients on HD. Additionally, the majority of the questionnaires were reported by the investigator; in these cases, reporting biases could have acted as a confounding factor in our data.

Conclusion

The quality of life of patients on maintenance haemodialysis in Cameroon is greatly reduced, with a majority of people being burdened by their disease and their physical health status being more affected. The level of social reinsertion was poor in terms of academic achievements, unemployment rates, and social participation. Factors found to be associated with such quality of life were older age, and QOL was found to be a predictor of poor social participation.

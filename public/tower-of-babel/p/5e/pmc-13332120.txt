Re: “Two decades of cranial dural arteriovenous fistula care: individualized, multidisciplinary treatment selection and outcomes from a single center”
Authors: Ömer Bagcilar
Published: 2026

To the Editor,

I read with interest the article by Geiger et al. on two decades of single-center experience in the management of cranial dural arteriovenous fistulas (dAVFs) (Geiger et al., 2025). The authors should be congratulated for sharing a long institutional experience in a heterogeneous disease and for emphasizing the value of multidisciplinary decision-making. I fully agree that treatment selection in dAVFs should be individualized. At the same time, I feel that the interpretation of the results, particularly with regard to endovascular treatment (EVT), would benefit from more cautious wording.

My main concern is that the conclusions appear somewhat stronger than the presented data support. In the reported cohort, complete occlusion rates were very similar across the main treatment groups: 88% for surgery alone, 86% for EVT alone, and 88% for combined treatment (Geiger et al., 2025). Despite this, the Discussion and Conclusions suggest that combined therapy achieved the “best” occlusion rates and that EVT alone may be associated with worse outcomes and complications. In my view, such similar angiographic results do not readily support a general conclusion against EVT monotherapy.

This point is especially important because the main negative signal for EVT monotherapy appears statistically uncertain. In the unadjusted analysis, EVT alone was associated with unfavorable outcome (OR 37.19, 95% CI 1.1–1260.4), but the estimate was extremely imprecise. This makes the finding difficult to interpret as strong comparative evidence and suggests limited precision. In addition, the Methods describe descriptive analyses, cross-tabulation, and Fisher's exact testing, but no multivariable adjustment. This matters because the authors also note that EVT was more often used in older or comorbid patients. In that setting, the observed association may reflect confounding by indication rather than a true treatment effect.

Another issue is the marked temporal heterogeneity of the cohort. The study period spans 1998–2023, whereas Onyx was introduced at the authors’ institution in 2009 and became the main embolic agent in 2012. Combining early-era and contemporary EVT into a single category makes it difficult to draw broader conclusions about current endovascular practice. This is relevant because more recent literature suggests that modern EVT can achieve high occlusion rates with acceptable morbidity in appropriately selected dAVFs (Su et al., 2026; Marnat et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025; Lim et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2026; Lindgren et al., 2024).

For example, the DREAM-INI cohort, which included 1101 patients, reported an immediate angiographic cure rate of 85.2%, with treatment-related permanent neurological morbidity of 3.4% (Su et al., 2026). In the prospective multicenter PHIL-dAVF study, complete angiographic cure at 3–6 months was 86.9%, while transient functional deterioration occurred in 4.5% of patients and resolved by last follow-up (Marnat et al., 2025). In a contemporary series of tentorial dAVFs treated with EVT as first-line therapy, immediate complete occlusion was achieved in 89.1%, and the final complete obliteration rate at six-month angiographic follow-up was 91.2% after all treatment sessions, including supplementary surgery or radiosurgery (Zhang et al., 2025). Similarly, a recent meta-analysis of transvenous embolization for noncavernous dAVFs reported a pooled complete obliteration rate of 94.9% and symptom improvement in 95% at final follow-up (Lim et al., 2024). A recent comparative study of tentorial dAVFs showed that, in lesions with accessible venous routes, transvenous embolization achieved higher immediate and long-term occlusion than transarterial embolization, with comparable complication rates and no permanent neurological deficits (Hu et al., 2026). Contemporary transarterial series also report stable complete occlusion rates of 72%–86% at 3 months, depending on technique, with microballoon-assisted TAE achieving 86% occlusion and 7% treatment-related complications (Lindgren et al., 2024). Although these studies are not directly comparable with the present cohort, they suggest that contemporary EVT should not generally be viewed as a strategy to be avoided.

The safety findings also deserve some nuance. Although the reported overall complication rate was 42.6%, only 8.2% of patients experienced major complications with clinical impairment (Geiger et al., 2025). Distinguishing transient or technical adverse events from permanent morbidity is important when comparing treatment modalities, especially in retrospective cohorts spanning substantial technological evolution.

Overall, this study adds valuable data and supports individualized, multidisciplinary management of dAVFs (Geiger et al., 2025). However, I would respectfully suggest that its results do not establish the superiority of combined or surgical treatment over contemporary EVT, nor do they justify a general recommendation against endovascular monotherapy. A more cautious interpretation may be that treatment choice should remain lesion-specific, anatomy-driven, and informed by current endovascular capabilities.

Transportation-oriented isolated type energy interaction converter for vehicle-to-vehicle
Authors: Wei Jia, Rui Wang, Zhongbao Wei, Chuanyu Jiang, Ziqiang Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In recent years, the environmental pollution problem has become an important issue, and the electric vehicle (EV) is an alternative choice to achieve zero fuel cost and zero pollution. However, the development of EVs is being hindered due to the insufficient number of charging stations and the disadvantage of driving range. To this end, the energy interaction concept is proposed, and an isolated converter for energy interaction is designed in this paper. Firstly, according to requirements regarding the energy interaction converter for EVs, i.e., high-reliability, high-gain, high-power-density, and small-volume, a dual-active-bridge (DAB) converter is employed as the circuit topology, and a standard circuit model is built. The model and phase-shifting scheme of the DAB converter are incorporated and recreated, which produces a customized linear model for the design of the controller. Secondly, using the established circuit model, a robust voltage controller strategy based on uncertainty and disturbance estimator (UDE) was designed to address parameter disturbances, dead time, computation delays, and external disturbances. An integral term was introduced to reduce steady-state errors and improve steady-state tracking accuracy by utilizing the integral accumulation effect, ensuring that the DAB converter provides stable DC voltage/power output. The controller stability analysis is further provided. Finally, the simulation and experiment results demonstrate the feasibility and effectiveness of the proposed method.

Introduction

In recent years, the problem of fossil energy crisis and environmental pollution has become a common topic of concern for humanity1. The expanding number of gasoline-powered cars has led to increasing issues, for example, environmental pollution and energy consumption2. Therefore, the Chinese government has made a major strategic decision: to achieve a carbon peak by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 20603. The new energy vehicle represented by the electric vehicles (EVs) is one of the effective ways to resolve issues, e.g., environmental pollution and energy consumption4,5. Currently, the number of charging stations in many cities and regions is growing rapidly, gradually meeting people’s charging needs. However, the shortage of charging stations on highways and in some remote areas remains quite evident, and their distribution within cities is still uneven. Many EV owners report experiencing range anxiety, often facing insufficient battery power during long-distance trips. Additionally, issues such as a high number of vehicles relative to charging stations, lack of maintenance for charging infrastructure, and queues for charging remain prevalent in practice. In remote areas, when an EV’s State of Charge (SoC) is low on the road and no charging stations are nearby, owners are forced to wait for mobile roadside assistance. It causes people’s anxiety about the EVs6 and considerably reduces people’s enthusiasm for purchasing the EVs7.

In response to this situation, Wallbox, an intelligent charging company, released a device called Quasar 2 to enable electric vehicles to power the outside world in 20228. However, it had the disadvantages of requiring significant modifications to EVs, a narrow application area, and high sticker prices. At the same time, BYD launched the BYD E5, which had the function of external discharge9, but it had a more complex operation process and emitted AC voltage, which made it difficult to achieve the fast energy interaction for EVs. In the literature10,11, the concept of energy interaction was proposed to solve this problem. However, the circuit structure was complex, the control structure was complex, and the current sharing control was required. Meanwhile, the nominal and actual values of the electrical components of a converter are subject to error due to the level of workmanship and materials used in their manufacture. Better workmanship and materials mean fewer errors, but also more expensive. Literature12 proposes an extendable V2V wireless charging solution, but it requires additional equipment for retrofitting and thus lacks broad applicability. Therefore, the use of lower-quality converter devices becomes a critical factor in reducing the cost of the converter. Additionally, different EVs require varying charging voltages, and the voltages that batteries can provide also differ. Therefore, the converter should possess high-gain characteristics. Reducing modifications to EVs, enhancing the portability of V2V devices, and improving their applicability, reliability, and stability have become the current challenges. Based on this, a portable energy interaction converter for EVs was proposed, and an UDE would be used to stabilise the system to guarantee energy interaction in this paper. This device enables EVs to share power without requiring fast-charging stations. Through power transfer between EVs, well-charged EVs can provide electrical support to those running low, alleviating the need for roadside assistance when batteries run dry. This technology reduces dependence on traditional charging stations, eliminates range anxiety for electric vehicles, and offers greater flexibility for EV charging13. Currently, four types of charging modes for EVs have been defined based on the charging standards for EVs in China14. The first three charging modes have longer charging times, and definitely cannot conform to the fast energy interaction for EVs. The fourth type of charging mode is commonly called fast charging, also known as DC charging, which can satisfy the fast energy interaction4. This paper mainly focuses on the more popular small electric vehicles (EVs). Therefore, according to the survey, the charging and discharging voltages of the more common small EVs in China are shown in Table 115. However, the charging power of small EVs currently on sale generally does not have more than 3kW. Meanwhile, the electric energy of electric vehicles is already very low, so the three commonly used charging modes, i.e., constant voltage charging, constant current charging, and pulse charging, only need to use the constant voltage charging mode. Therefore, it is crucial to design a dc/dc boost converter that has high-gain, high-power-density, and small-volume characteristics16,17. Because of the high gain, it was difficult for traditional boost converters to meet the requirements. To this end, a dual-active-bridge (DAB) converter is designed in this paper, which can realise isolated DC boost, safe isolation, and bidirectional energy flow18,19. The high reliability20 of DAB converters is attributed to the built-in high-frequency transformer, which not only achieves electrical isolation between the primary and secondary sides to ensure safety, but also reduces the voltage/current stress of the switches through soft switching technology. For its high gain characteristics, DAB utilizes the turns ratio of the transformer to match the voltage difference between the battery side and charging side of the electric vehicle, overcoming the gain limitation of traditional non isolated Boost converters under high duty cycles. In addition, by increasing the switching frequency and combining it with wide bandgap semiconductor devices such as SiC, the size of passive components (inductance L and capacitance C) and magnetic components can be significantly reduced, thereby improving the power density of the system and reducing its volume.

The DAB converter was commonly used in EV on-board chargers because it had the characteristics of isolation, dual-direction power transmission, and soft-switching21. However, the nonlinearity and external disturbances of the system, such as dead-time effect, parasitic parameters of the circuit, errors between devices and nominal values, and load jumps, would lead to errors between DAB output voltage and reference values22, and it also easily led to the instability of the whole system23. At the same time, the converter operated in the presence of changes in the electronics parameters caused by electromagnetic fields or temperature effects, and the actual parameters of the electronics were different from the nominal values due to manufacturing tolerances. In order to reduce the price, the electrical components may be of the same type with inferior workmanship or materials. In addition, when the digital controller was used, there was a calculation time delay of not less than one step, which led to a new phase delay and worsened the system stability24. Therefore, an accurate-tracking controller for DAB converters is designed to be indispensable and is robust to the nonlinearity and external disturbances of the system.

Various disturbance rejection control (DRC) methods have been proposed to attenuate disturbances and uncertainties. In terms of design domain, the traditional DRC could be categorised as frequency-domain and time-domain25. In the frequency domain, the disturbance observer (DOB) was extensively used in the controller. It used a disturbance estimation filter to eliminate the disturbance25,26. As long as the appropriate filter was selected, the disturbance of the system could be well suppressed. At the same time, DRC was also designed in the time-domain, e.g., active disturbance rejection control (ADRC)27. Compared with the DRC in the frequency domain, the DRC in the time domain based on state-space was more suitable for MIMO systems. Recently, a hybrid-domain control strategy based on UDE was proposed to attenuate disturbances and improve DRC performance28–30. In the time domain, UDE realised the centralised estimation of feedback and disturbance by using the model of the time domain and state measurement. In the frequency domain, UDE enhanced the robustness of the noise measurement by using filters.

Therefore, this paper proposes an energy interaction converter for EVs based on UDE, whose foremost features and advantages are as follows:

(1) This DAB converter adopts a circuit topology design that meets the requirements for electric vehicle energy interaction equipment, featuring high reliability, high gain, high power density, and compact size. Based on this, it can provide an isolated energy interaction converter to address issues such as insufficient charging station availability and limited driving range31.

(2) This paper constructs a novel DAB converter circuit model. By integrating and reconstructing the converter model and phase-shifting scheme based on DAB output current, it provides a suitable linearised model for subsequent controller design.

(3) To address challenges including dead time, parameter uncertainty, external interference, and computational delay, a control strategy based on Uncertainty Dynamic Control (UDE) is designed for the DAB converter and optimised. In addition, the integral term is introduced into UDE to reduce steady-state errors and improve steady-state tracking accuracy by utilizing the integral accumulation effect. This enhances the system’s dynamic performance, enabling rapid and stable energy exchange for electric vehicles.Fig. 1Portable energy interaction blueprint based on isolated converter for EVs.

Robust voltage control based on UDE

Based on the simulation and experimental results above, it is evident that the proposed solution is well-suited for energy interaction converters for EVs. The preceding sections outlined the topology selection and control strategy. The DAB topology chosen in this paper offers electrical isolation, enabling features such as isolation (safety), bidirectionality, high gain, high power density, and compact size. The control strategy employs a Universal Discrete Equation (UDE) model, which unifies dead zones, parameter deviations (L, C), external disturbances, and computational delays into a unified uncertainty term. The UDE enables centralised estimation and compensation, ensuring fast and stable tracking even in the presence of these uncertainties. Simultaneously, the proposed solution reduces reliance on high-precision electronic components, facilitating cost reduction while maintaining performance and enhancing engineering reliability. Conclusion

In order to solve related problems, i.e., an insufficient number of charging stations and poor endurance of EVs, an isolated energy interaction converter for EVs based on UDE control has been proposed in this paper, which realises fast energy interaction for EVs. To solve the unsafe problem of high-voltage, this paper has adopted an isolated DC/DC converter to accomplish high-gain, high-power-density, and small-volume and safe operation. Based on this, the phase-shifting scheme and circuit model of the DAB converter have been assembled and reconstructed, and the linear model under different phase-shifting schemes has been obtained. To solve the dead-time, parameter uncertainty, external disturbance, time-delay, and of the system, a control strategy based on UDE has been designed for the DAB converter to realise fast and stable energy interaction for EVs, which estimates the lumped disturbance in the time-domain and realises state feedback and uses a filter to make the control law causal in the frequency-domain. Finally, the feasibility and effectiveness of the energy interaction control strategy for EVs proposed in this paper have been verified by simulation and experiment results. Simultaneously, simulation and experiment results have also demonstrated that the cost of the interaction converter for EVs would be effectively reduced under the same requirements and performance.

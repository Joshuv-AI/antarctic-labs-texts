miR‐30c‐5p inhibits esophageal squamous cell carcinoma progression by repressing the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway
Authors: Haochun Shi, Binyang Pan, Jiaqi Liang, Benjie Cai, Gujie Wu, Yunyi Bian, Guangyao Shan, Shencheng Ren, Yiwei Huang, Weigang Guo
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is one of the most common malignant tumors, with high incidence and poor prognosis. Revealing mechanisms of ESCC progression and developing new therapeutic targets remains crucial. The aim of this study was to elucidate the molecular mechanism of miR‐30c‐5p in regulating the malignant progression of ESCC.MethodsTCGA, GEO, and other datasets were used to analyze the differential expression of miR‐30c‐5p in ESCC and adjacent tissues, and its impact on prognosis. Then the effects of miR‐30c‐5p on the proliferation, migration, and invasion of TE‐1 and Eca9706 cells were investigated through proliferation experiments, transwell and wounding healing assays. The regulatory mechanism of miR‐30c‐5p on the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway and its interaction in cancer progression were investigated through Western blots, dual‐luciferase reporter assay, and rescue experiments.ResultsmiR‐30c‐5p was significantly downregulated in ESCC tissue and represented a poor prognosis. miR‐30c‐5p mimic significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration, and invasion ability of ESCC, while miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor significantly promoted tumor cell progression. Through bioinformatic analysis and experimental results, miR‐30c‐5p interacted directly with PIK3CA mRNA and inhibited subsequent signaling pathway activation. PIK3CA activator could eliminate the inhibitory effects of miR‐30c‐5p mimic on the progression of ESCC, while PIK3CA inhibitors could rescue the promoting effect of miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor group cells.ConclusionsIn summary, we found that miR‐30c‐5p inhibited the proliferation, invasion and migration of ESCC by inhibiting PI3K/AKT signaling pathway for the first time, and this study is expected to provide a novel insight and potential therapeutic target for managing ESCC.

INTRODUCTION

Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is commonly observed on a global scale, representing a prevalent form of esophageal cancer. The majority of ESCC patients often present high levels of mortality and advanced stages and are prone to metastasis. 1 , 2 Despite attempts with various therapeutic approaches such as chemotherapy and immunotherapy, treatment efficacy is often hindered by the development of multiple drug resistance and adverse effects. 3 To enhance the study of malignant progression of ESCC and identify new targets, it is necessary for ongoing research to delve deeper into the subject and explore further advancements in therapies. 4 , 5

Multiple studies have indicated the impact of microRNA (miRNA) on cell distinction, vessel formation, programmed cell death, and distant metastasis of cancer. 6 , 7 , 8 The exploration of miRNA for potential applications in early diagnosis, treatment and prognosis of cancers shows significant promise. 9 , 10 , 11 As a mature miRNA, miR‐30c‐5p comes from its precursors miR‐30c‐1 and miR‐30c‐2, located on chromosomes 1 and 6, respectively. 12 miR‐30c‐5p plays a carcinogenic and tumor suppressive role in various types of cancer. Cao et al. demonstrated the inhibitory impact of miR‐30c‐5p on the progression of gastric cancer by regulating MTA1. 13 Song et al. reported that the impact of miR‐30c‐5p carried in exosomes was evident in influencing the advancement of clear cell renal cell carcinoma by suppressing HSPA5. 14 Nonetheless, the exploration of the role of miR‐30c‐5p in ESCC remains limited.

Downregulation of miR‐30c‐5p expression was observed in ESCC in the current investigation. Subsequent investigations have revealed that miR‐30c‐5p exerts an influence on the PI3K/Akt pathway through the targeting of PI3KCA, leading to the inhibition of ESCC progression. Hence, a promising strategy for treating ESCC could involve targeting the miR‐30c‐5p/AKT axis.

METHODS

Cell lines

The experimental procedure involved utilizing cell lines such as HET‐1A, TE‐1, and Eca9706 from the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures. These cells were cultured in PRIM‐1640 medium and DMEM high‐glucose medium with the addition of 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS: Every Green, Zhejiang, China) and 100 U/mL amphotericin B‐streptomycin–penicillin solution. The incubation conditions were set at 37°C and 5% CO2.

Compounds

PIK3CA activator UCL‐TRO‐1938 (T78092) and PIK3CA inhibitor BYL719 (T1921) were used to promote or inhibit the activity of PI3KCA and stored at 20°C. UCL‐TRO‐1938 and BYL719 were both obtained from TOPSCIENCE (Shanghai, China).

Data analysis

TCGA ESCC data from the UCSC Xena database includes clinical information, and miRNA data. 15 GSE43732 dataset contains clinical information of ESCC, and miRNA expression measured using Agilent human miRNA V19.0 microarray. 16 Data preprocessing and normalization steps involved the utilization of the limma, dplyr, ggplot2, and ggpubr packages within R software (version 4.1.5). The ggplot2 package was used to visualize DEmiRNA and DEmRNA volcano maps. Survival curves were plotted using the survival, survminer package. Analysis of gene ontology (GO) and pathway enrichment using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) was carried out through the utilization of R studio and presented via the online bioinformatics tool. 17 The prediction of target genes and binding sites for miR‐30c‐5p was conducted through the utilization of DIANA‐microT 2023 18 and the miRanda online tool on the bioinformatics platform. 17

Cell transfection

According to a prior publication, 19 the miR‐30c‐5p mimic, miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor, and negative control (miR‐30c‐5p NC) were procured from RiboBio Life Science (Guangzhou, China). ESCC cells were transfected with lipofectamine 8000 reagent (Beyotime, China) when the cell density was 60%–80%. ESCC cells were harvested 48 h later.

Quantitative real‐time‐polymerase chain reaction analysis (qRT‐PCR)

According to previous studies, 20 TRNzol Universal (Tiangen, Beijing, China) was introduced to lysate cells, followed by the reverse transcription of RNA into cDNA using Hifair R©III 1st Strand C DNA Synthesis SuperMix for qPCR (Yeasen). Later, the cDNA was amplified utilizing Hieff R qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen) within an ABI QuantStudio 5 real‐time PCR system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The reference used was U6 snRNA, and the primer sequences are detailed in Table 1.

Western blot

The experimental process was as reported in previous studies. 21 , 22 Cell lysis was achieved using RIPA buffer (Beyotime) and protein quantification was carried out with the enhanced BCA kit. Following concentration and separation through 10% sodium dodecyl sulfate‐polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS‐PAGE, Epizyme, China), the proteins were subsequently transferred to a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (PVDF, Millipore, USA) under a constant current of 0.30A for 90 min. Next, a solution of 10% skim milk was applied to seal the membrane for 60 min, followed by an overnight incubation with the primary antibody. After three washes, the membrane underwent a 1‐h incubation period with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)‐conjugated antibodies (1:3000, Beyotime). Subsequently, the detection of protein bands was carried out using a BSA kit provided by Beyotime. Following the observation of the protein bands, the gray value of each stripe underwent analysis using the Image J software (version 1.8.0). Cell proliferation assays

As previously reported by Bi et al., 23 ESCC cells were placed in 96‐well plates at an appropriate density, followed by the addition of 10 μL CCK‐8 solution per well at 24, 48, 72, and 96 h time points, and then incubated for 1 h. The cells were then incubated for 1 h before measuring the absorbance at 450 nm using a FlexStation3 microplate reader (Molecular Devices, USA). This experiment was repeated three times.

Wound healing assay

Following seeding in cell culture plates, a straight line was carefully drawn across the center of the cell layer using the tip of a 1000 μL pipette. Subsequently, the cells underwent a thorough wash with phosphate buffered saline (PBS) before being added serum‐free medium. The results were observed using an optical microscope (Olympus, Japan) after 0, 24 and 72 h. The invasion ability of cells was measured using Image J software.

Transwell assay

As previously reported by Zhang et al., 24 cell invasion ability was measured by transwell assays using a 24‐well chamber with an 8 μm well membrane (Corning, USA). ESCC cells (5 × 104 cells/pore) were plated in the upper chamber coated with Matrigel (BD Biosciences), with serum‐free medium in the upper chamber and serum‐containing medium in the lower chamber. After 24 h, the cells that had migrated were identified by staining with 0.1% crystal violet. A total of 100 cells were counted in five randomly chosen fields using a phase‐contrast microscope (Olympus), and the number of cells in each chamber was determined with Image J software.

Luciferase reporter assay

As previously reported by Bi et al., 19 a dual luciferase reporter vector was conducted by Hanyin Technology (China) which contained the wild‐type and mutant predicted target site of PIK3CA. Then, the transfection of TE‐1 and Eca9706 cells was carried out using lipofectamine 8000 (Beyotime, China) with miR‐30c‐3p NC and mimic alongside the PIK3CA‐WT or PIK3CA‐MUT reporter plasmid. After 48 h, relative luciferase activity was performed using the luciferase reporter gene assay kit from Beyotime.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis in this study utilized R software in combination with GraphPad Prism 9.0 (GraphPad Software, USA). The analysis involved the use of the student's t‐test or Kruskal‐Wallis test to assess differences between various groups. when comparing variations across multiple groups, either one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Wilcoxon test was employed. Survival analysis, on the other hand, utilized the Kaplan–Meier survival analysis alongside the log‐rank test. The data was statistically processed: *p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001. All p‐values were two‐tailed and statistical significance was determined for p‐values below 0.05.

RESULTS

miR‐30c‐5p exhibited low expression levels and was linked to a poor prognosis in ESCC

Through TCGA dataset, miR‐30c‐5p was identified as a significantly differentially expressed miRNA between normal tissues and ESCC (fold change = 0.43, p = 0.0000211, Figure 1a). Further analysis of both TCGA and GSE43732 datasets pointed towards the decreased expression of miR‐30c‐5p in tumor samples compared to normal tissues (TCGA: p = 0.000289, GSE43732: p = 0.000012, Figure 1b,d). Moreover, when experiments were conducted using the healthy cell line HET‐1A and ESCC cell lines TE‐1 and Eca9706, it was observed that the expression of miR‐30c‐5p was decreased in tumor cells (Figure 1d). Data analysis suggested a potential association of miR‐30c‐5p with an unfavorable prognosis (Figure 1e–g). Additionally, analysis of clinical information from ESCC patients revealed significant downregulation of miR‐30c‐5p in the later T and N stages (T1 + T2 vs. T3: p = 0.22, T1 + T2 vs. T4: p = 0.015, T3 vs. T4: p = 0.011; N0 vs. N1: p = 0.52, N0 vs. N2: p = 0.0083, N1 vs. N2: p = 0.017), while miR‐30c‐5p had no significant correlation with M stages and clinical stages (M0 vs. M1: p = 0.52, stage I + II vs. stage III + IV: p = 0.51, Figure 1h–k). These results suggest a potential involvement of miR‐30c‐p in the progression of ESCC towards malignancy.

miR‐30c‐5p inhibited the proliferation, invasion, and migration of ESCC

Cell viability was assessed by transfecting TE‐1 and Eca9706 cells with either the miR‐30c‐5p mimic or inhibitor, followed by cell counting kit‐8 (CCK‐8) measurement to analyze cell proliferation. The miR‐30c‐5p mimic group exhibited a notable decrease in cell proliferation capacity in contrast to the control group, whereas the miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor group demonstrated a significant enhancement in cell proliferation (Figure 2a,b). According to the results of the wound healing experiment, the miR‐30c‐5p mimic group exhibited an inhibitory effect on tumor cell invasion, whereas the miR‐30c‐5p inhibitors had a contrasting impact (Figure 2c–e). The outcomes from the transwell assay revealed that the group treated with miR‐30c‐5p mimic displayed decreased tumor cell migration, in contrast to the group treated with miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor, which showed an increase in tumor cell migration (Figure 2f,g).

miR‐30c‐5p regulated the PI3K/AKT pathway through directly inhibiting PIK3CA

Enrichment analysis was performed on the differentially expressed genes associated with miR‐30c‐5p using data from the TCGA database. The analysis revealed a notable enrichment of the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway in both GO analysis and KEGG pathways (Figure 3a,b). Next, we identified PIK3CA as the predicted miR‐30c‐5p target by using DIANA‐microT 2023 and the miRanda online tool. Subsequent analysis led to the indication that the regulation of PI3KCA could be directly influenced by miR‐30c‐5p. The results from the Western blot assay showed an inhibitory effect on the expression levels of PI3KCA between the miR‐30c‐5p mimic group, where the miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor group exhibited a promoting effect on PI3KCA. Similarly, the inhibition of AKT S473 phosphorylation was observed in the miR‐30c‐5p mimic group, whereas the miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor group facilitated the phosphorylation of AKT S473. There was no significant difference in the expression level of total AKT among different experimental groups (Figure 3c–e). To further confirm this interaction, we performed dual luciferase gene reporter assays. The predicted binding sites are shown below, and the mutant sequences were designed and incorporated into the plasmids (Figure 3f). These plasmids and miR‐30c‐5p NC or mimic were used to transfect TE‐1 and Eca9706 cells. We observed that in the wild‐type PIK3CA group, the miR‐30c‐5p mimic was able to suppress the activity of firefly luciferase, whereas in the mutant PIK3CA group, the luciferase activity remained unaffected by the miR‐30c‐5p mimic (Figure 3g,h). The results indicated that the regulation of the PI3K/AKT pathway by miR‐30c‐5p involved the repression of PIK3CA.

PIK3CA could rescue the impact of miR‐30c‐5p on the progression of ESCC

In order to investigate the role of miR‐30c‐5p in ESCC progression through PI3K/AKT signaling, we performed rescue experiments using PIK3CA activator UCL‐TRO‐1938 and PIK3CA inhibitor BYL719. The results of the Western blot assay showed that UCL‐TRO‐1938 significantly promoted the phosphorylation of Akt S473 in the mimic group in TE‐1 and ECA9706 cells. The phosphorylation of Akt S473 in the inhibitor group was effectively inhibited by BYL719 (Figure 4a–d). The results from the CCK‐8 assays revealed the capability of UCL‐TRO‐1938 to counteract the impact of the miR‐30c‐5p mimic on tumor cells. In contrast, the proliferation level of ESCC cells was significantly rescued by BYL719 (Figure 4e,f). Inhibition of tumor cell invasion and migration was observed to be reduced in the miR‐30c‐5p mimic group with the application of UCL‐TRO‐1938, while BYL719 counteracted the stimulatory effects of the miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor group (Figure 4g,h and Figure 5a–f). The results suggest that the involvement of miR‐30c‐5p in ESCC progression occurs through regulation of the PI3K/AKT pathway.

DISCUSSION

Despite numerous advancements in managing patients with ESCC, the survival rates are still not ideal. 25 Numerous molecular changes have been implicated in the tumorigenesis mechanism of ESCC. However, drug resistance often also greatly limits the improvement of therapeutic prognosis. Additionally, due to the intricate nature of tumor development mechanisms, it remains crucial to identify more targets and novel mechanisms.

Many studies have suggested that miRNA regulates oncogenes or tumor suppressor genes and participate in tumor progression. 6 , 26 Many studies have demonstrated that miRNA is involved in the proliferation, invasion, angiogenesis, EMT, autophagy and immune regulation of ESCC, and is a promising target for the diagnosis, treatment and prognosis of ESCC. 27 , 28 , 29 miR‐30c‐5p has been recognized as an effective target for tumor therapy in many studies. Circ3823 endogenously competes for miR‐30c‐5p, and then upregulates TCF7, MYC, and CCND1, thereby promoting the progression of colorectal tumor cells. 30 However, the effects and mechanisms of miR‐30c‐5p in ESCC remain unknown. Therefore, our study focused on miR‐30c‐5p expression and impact on prognosis in ESCC and mechanisms that influence the progression of ESCC for the first time. In analyzing ESCC samples, it was observed that the reduced expression of miR‐30c‐5p in tumor cells and miR‐30c‐5p was correlated with a poor prognosis. Following this discovery, an investigation was conducted using TE‐1 and Eca9706 cells to explore the impact of miR‐30c‐5p on the advancement of ESCC cells. The results of the CCK‐8, wound healing and transwell assays revealed that the introduction of miR‐30c‐5p mimic led to significant suppression of the proliferation, invasion, and migration capabilities of ESCC cells. Conversely, miR‐30c‐5p inhibitor resulted in a notable enhancement of the progression of ESCC cells. In summary, the above results indicated that miR‐30c‐5p was associated with prognosis and could inhibit malignant phenotypes in ESCC.

Through bioinformatic analysis, we found a strong correlation between miR‐30c‐5p and the PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. The PI3K/Akt signaling pathway has been extensively studied in relation to in cancer cell cycle progression, metabolism, proliferation, tumor angiogenesis, and inflammatory cell recruitment has been well established. 31 , 32 Furthermore, a positive association has been observed between the levels of PI3K expression and the clinical stage, distant metastasis, and differentiation degree. 33 Some studies have shown that the application of inhibitors of PI3K/AKT pathway has a good anticancer effect in tumors. BEZ235 is a PI3K inhibitor that has shown promising results when combined with trichostatin A (TSA) for improving the effects on ESCC cell proliferation, apoptosis, and autophagy. 34 Subsequently, through the analysis of miRNA databases, we speculated that PIK3CA might be the target of miR‐30c‐5p. We demonstrated by Western blot assay that miR‐30c‐5p negatively regulated PI3KCA and p‐AKT‐S473, but had no significant effect on total AKT. To elucidate the functional interactions, luciferase reporter assays were conducted. The outcomes revealed that miR‐30c‐5p exerted a direct regulatory effect on the wild‐type PIK3CA and exhibited no significant impact on the mutant‐PIK3CA. PIK3CA is an oncogene encoding the p110 catalytic subunit of class I lepidoylositol‐3‐kinase. PIK3CA mutation may lead to sustained activation of downstream Akt, increasing the ability of tumor cell invasion and metastasis. 35 Numerous studies and practical experience have demonstrated that patients with specific mutations in the PIK3CA gene are resistant to EGFR and HER2‐targeted drug therapy. 36 Currently, BYL719, a selective inhibitor of PIK3CA, has shown promising anticancer effects in solid tumors. 37 , 38 This study innovatively explored the therapeutic potential of targeting the miR‐30c‐5p/PIK3CA axis in ESCC. We conducted rescue assays using the PIK3CA agonist UCL‐TRO‐1938 and PIK3CA inhibitor BYL719 and confirmed stimulatory and inhibitory effects on the PI3K/AKT pathway through Western blot assay. Moreover, it was observed that the inhibitory impact of miR‐30c‐5p on ESCC malignant characteristics was significantly rescued by UCL‐TRO‐1938. Meanwhile, the impact of downregulating miR‐30c‐5p on ESCC advancement was notably counteracted by BYL719. These results collectively suggested that the suppression of the PI3K/AKT pathway was a key mechanism through which miR‐30c‐5p impeded the advancement of ESCC.

However, there were some limitations in this study. First, due to the limitations of current experimental conditions, we selected the classical Eca9706 and TE‐1 cell lines, necessitating future validation across other ESCC cell lines to verify our findings. Second, to strengthen our understanding of the targeted regulatory effect of miR‐30c‐5p on PIK3CA, a more extensive array of experiments is warranted. Third, while our in vitro experiments have demonstrated the tumor‐suppressing potential of miR‐30c‐5p via targeting PIK3CA in ESCC, this observation should be corroborated through in vivo investigations, including both animal models and clinical cases.

In conclusion, miR‐30c‐5p exhibited low expression levels in ESCC and is associated with a poor prognosis. We found that miR‐30c‐5p could directly repress PIK3CA and inhibit the progression of ESCC cells. Our study revealed the miR‐30c‐5p/PIK3CA axis holds as a promising potential target for therapeutic interventions in ESCC.

Core transcriptional regulatory circuit-regulated IGF2BP3 stabilizes E2F2 mRNA via m6A modification in neuroblastoma
Authors: Pengcheng Yang, Jianwei Wang, Ziheng Wu, Ran Zhuo, Yanling Chen, Gen Li, Yanfang Tao, Xiaolu Li, Fang Fang, Di Wu, Yang Yang, Hongli Yin, Guanghui Qian, Hairong Wang, Xin Li, Juanjuan Yu, Randong Yang, Yunyun Xu, Zhiheng Li, Lei Shi, Zimu Zhang, Jian Pan, Jian Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractNeuroblastoma (NB) is a pediatric tumor with diverse outcomes and unknown underlying mechanisms. The core transcriptional regulatory circuit (CRC) and N6-methyladenosine (m6A) are key factors that control cell identity and fate. IGF2BP3 is an m6A reader protein that is transcriptionally regulated by CRC transcription factors (TFs). In NB, this molecule is abundantly expressed, and there is a clear correlation between its expression and a bad prognosis. We mechanistically demonstrated that IGF2BP3 promotes E2F2 mRNA expression through m6A, which is correlated with high risk and poor prognosis in NB patients. We showed that the CRC TF-IGF2BP3-E2F2 regulatory axis forms an oncogenic network that drives NB development and progression. Overall, we investigated the molecular mechanism by which IGF2BP3, a m6A-reading protein that is regulated by CRC TFs, regulates E2F2 mRNA expression in an m6A-dependent manner. This study highlights the therapeutic potential of disrupting this axis with m6A-targeted interventions.Keywords: neuroblastoma, N6-methyladenosine (m6A), core transcriptional regulatory circuit (CRC), m6A reader, mRNA stability

Introduction

Neuroblastoma (NB) is the most common extracranial solid malignant tumor in childhood [1]. This condition originates from the neural crest cells of the sympathetic nervous system [2]. The clinical course of NB is highly variable, ranging from spontaneous remission to rapid progression and death [3]. Transcription factors (TFs) play a crucial role as key components in the network for transcription of regulatory gene [4]. TFs often form regulatory complexes with proteins that bind to gene promoters or enhancers, participate in gene transcription, and affect gene expression [5]. The core transcriptional regulatory circuit (CRC) is an interconnected group of autoregulatory TFs that form loops and are recognized by super-enhancers (SEs) [6]. The CRC plays an important role in defining cell identity and determining cell fate and regulates cell type-specific transcriptional regulation [7]. The role of the CRC in NB has been studied. The core TFs in the CRC are MYCN [8], PHOX2B [9], HAND2 [10], and GATA3 [11], which play key roles in the genesis and development of NB and are potential therapeutic targets.

N6-methyladenosine (m6A), a principal internal modification of eukaryotic mRNA, plays a crucial role in controlling RNA splicing, stability, translation, and localization [12]. The dynamic and reversible nature of m6A methylation is managed by a set of enzymes including methyltransferases, demethylases, and functional effectors, commonly referred to as writers, erasers, and readers, respectively [13]. Recent research indicates that abnormalities in m6A-related proteins and modifications are pivotal in the onset and advancement of diseases, including cancer [14]. m6A mutations can affect the mRNA stability and translation of oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes, affecting tumor growth, invasion, metastasis and drug resistance [15]. For example, the development of acute myeloid leukaemia was shown to be maintained by METTL3, a methyltransferase, through promoter binding and m6A-dependent translation regulation [16]. Similarly, FTO, a demethylase, was found to promote the progression of endometrial cancer by activating the WNT signaling pathway through m6A demethylation and regulating HOXB13 mRNA expression [17]. Additionally, YTHDF3, a reader, was reported to translate m6A-modified transcripts and facilitate brain metastasis in breast cancer [18]. An association between the progression of NB and m6A-related genes, such as METTL3 [19], HNRNPR [20], WTAP [21], and IGF2BP1 [22], has been reported. However, the interactions between the TFs of the CRC (CRC TFs) of NB and m6A-related genes have not yet been studied. This topic warrants further investigation.

m6A readers, which are key to identifying methylation sites on RNA, fall into two primary categories: the YTH domain-containing family proteins [23] and the insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding proteins (IGF2BPs), which include IGF2BP3 [24]. IGF2BP3's role extends beyond NB, as it has been implicated in various cancers, underscoring its importance in oncology [25]. IGF2BP3 promotes tumor growth, invasion, metastasis, and drug resistance by regulating the expression of important oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes [26, 27]. IGF2BP3 promotes colorectal cancer development and resistance to the antibody cetuximab by stabilizing EGFR mRNA [28]. IGF2BP3 promotes hepatocarcinogenesis and progression through the stabilization of MYC mRNAs. Given IGF2BP3's established oncogenic roles in other cancers and its classification as a key m6A reader, we analysed its expression and function specifically in NB. IGF2BP3 is an m6A reader that recognizes and binds to m6A-modified mRNAs. We found that IGF2BP3 expression is regulated by CRC TFs and that IGF2BP3 can regulate the transcription of target genes such as E2F2 in an m6A-dependent manner. We discovered an oncogenic pathway involving CRC TFs, IGF2BP3, and E2F2. Additionally, we examined how IGF2BP3 influences the regulation of target mRNA transcripts through an m6A-dependent mechanism. This study suggests a promising approach to improve treatment outcomes and identifies a potential therapeutic target for NB.

Materials and methods

Cell cultures

The NB cell lines SH-SY5Y (authenticated in May 2023) and SK-N-BE(2) (authenticated in July 2022) were sourced from the National Infrastructure of Cell Line Resource in Shanghai, China, with their authenticity confirmed via short tandem repeat (STR) profiling. These cells were maintained in DMEM/F12 medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific) enriched with 15% fetal bovine serum from Biological Industries, USA, and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution from Beyotime, China. Cultivation occurred at 37°C in a 5% CO2 humidified environment, with routine verification for the absence of mycoplasma. The cell lines used in this study were all identified by STR. Details on additional cell lines are available in Supplementary Table S1.

Antibodies

The primary antibodies used in this study were as follows: MYCN (9405S, CST), E2F2 (ab235837, Abcam), IGF2BP3 (14642-1-AP, Proteintech), Ki-67 (GB111499, Servicebio), cleaved caspase-3 (GB11009-1, Servicebio), CDK2 (ab235941, Abcam), CDK4 (ab137675, Abcam), PLK1 (ab17056, Abcam) and GAPDH (AP0063, Bioworld Technology, Inc).

In vivo nude mouse xenograft tumor models

All the animal studies at the Children's Hospital of Soochow were approved and licensed by the Animal Care and Use Committee. BALB/c-nude mice were purchased from Cavens Model Animal Research Inc. Four-week-old female BALB/c nude mice were divided into groups, each consisting of six animals. The mice were injected subcutaneously under the arm with 5 × 106 NB cells suspended in 100 μL of Matrigel for xenograft transplantation. The mice were divided into the sh-NC and sh-IGF2BP3#1 group. Tumor volume was calculated by measuring tumor size with callipers every 5 days. The body weights of the mice were also recorded every 5 days. Bioluminescent signals were obtained by imaging the mice once a week. On imaging days, the mice were intraperitoneally injected with luciferase saline ∼10 min before imaging. After 25 days, the tumors in the popliteal fossa were removed and assessed by immunohistochemistry (IHC).

Tissue microarray and immunohistochemistry

Tissue microarray slides (DC-Mul11098, Avilabio, China) were incubated at 60°C for 1 h, deparaffinized in xylene, and dehydrated through an ethanol gradient. Antigen retrieval was performed in boiling citrate buffer (0.01 M, pH 6.0). After rinsing thrice with PBS (pH 7.5, 5 min each), immunostaining was conducted using the UltraSensitive SP IHC Kit and DAB Plus Kit (MXB Biotechnologies, China). Stained sections were visualized under an Olympus BX41 microscope, and scores were calculated as: Positive cell percentage (P) × Staining intensity (I).

CUT&Tag (cleavage under targets and tag mentation)

The CUT&Tag method utilizes protein A/G-conjugated transposases to achieve antibody-directed localization of target proteins, enabling site-specific DNA fragmentation adjacent to binding regions. Experimental procedures were performed according to the Hyperactive Universal CUT&Tag Assay Kit (Vazyme, TD904-01). Primary antibodies included: IgG (Rabbit monoclonal, Abcam, #ab172730), MYCN (Mouse monoclonal, Santa Cruz Biotechnology, #sc-53993).

M6a sequencing and analysis

Novogene (Beijing, China) performed library preparation for m6A sequencing and high-throughput sequencing. RNA fragments were isolated and prepared using the NEBNext Ultra RNA Library Prep Kit, and sequencing was conducted on an Illumina HiSeq 2000 platform. The sequencing data were aligned to the Sscrofa11.1 genome using the Burrows–Wheeler alignment tool. MACS2 was used for m6A peak calling.

RIP-qPCR

To explore the interaction between IGF2BP3 and E2F2 mRNA, we utilized an RNA immunoprecipitation kit from Geneseed, Guangzhou, China, following the provided guidelines. Specifically, 2 × 107 cells were collected, lysed with RIP lysis buffer, and the lysates were incubated with magnetic beads attached to either an IgG antibody from Cell Signaling Technology, Beverly, MA, USA, or an anti-IGF2BP3 antibody (14642-1-AP, Proteintech). The RNA complexes that co-precipitated were analysed using qRT–PCR or RT–PCR, with primers detailed in Supplementary Table S2.

MeRIP qPCR

Quantitative PCR (qPCR) was used to evaluate the relative abundances of certain mRNA transcripts in the m6A IP and input groups. Using a Magna MeRIP m6A kit (17-10499, Millipore), m6A RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP) was carried out in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions.

RNA stability assay

Five milligrams of actinomycin D (MedChem Express, China) was introduced to the cells, which were then incubated for predetermined periods before RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and subsequent qPCR to estimate mRNA degradation rates based on established methods [29].

RNA sequencing

RNA extraction, library construction, transcriptome sequencing, and the acquisition of clean data were managed by Novogene Bioinformatics Technology in China. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were pinpointed utilizing the R/Bioconductor package DESeq2, with criteria set at an adjusted P value <0.05 and an absolute log2 (fold change) > 1.

Luciferase reporter assay

General Biosystems (Anhui, China) constructed the dual-luciferase reporter vector psiCHECK-2 (Promega) containing either wild-type (WT) E2F motifs or a mutant version (Mut). Cells were transfected using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). After 48 h of transfection, the luciferase activity was measured by the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.2 (GraphPad Software, Inc., USA). P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01).

Supplementary data include further information about materials and methods.

Results

IGF2BP3 is an m6a-modified binding protein regulated by CRC TFs

To explore the expression and roles of m6A-related genes in NB, we obtained NB transcriptome data from the Target database. After identifying m6A-related genes, we examined their differential expression in relation to MYCN amplification and the Mitosis-Karyorrhexis Index (MKI). Our analysis showed a notable correlation between m6A regulatory genes and the pathological characteristics of NB (Fig. 1a and b, Supplementary Figure S1 and S2). Significant differences in the expression of five intersecting genes (METTL14, IGF2BP3, HNRNPC, RBMX, and RBM15B) were observed. To evaluate the effect of m6A-related genes on survival and prognosis in NB patients, we computed risk ratios for each gene using Cox regression analysis. Notably, the expressions of METTL14, IGF2BP3, HNRNPC, RBMX, and RBM15B were significantly linked to overall survival (Fig. 1c, Supplementary Table S3). In addition, we investigated the regulatory relationship between the expression of these m6A-related genes and key TFs. Our findings indicated a substantial positive correlation between IGF2BP3 and essential transcription loop TFs, including MYCN, PHOX2B, HAND2, and GATA3 (Fig. 1d, Supplementary Table S4). To determine whether the TFs of key transcriptional circuits in NB directly regulate m6A-related genes, we investigated their binding to promoter regions of m6A-related genes using ChIP-seq. ChIP-seq analysis visualized with IGV software (Fig. 1e) revealed direct binding of MYCN, PHOX2B, HAND2, and GATA3 to the IGF2BP3 promoter region. Notably, this transcriptional regulator binding coincided with prominent H3K27Ac histone modification signals at the same locus, indicative of active chromatin configuration. We further found that differential expressions of IGF2BP3 were significantly associated with MYCN amplification and MKI (Fig. 1f). In summary, we therefore used the m6A-binding protein IGF2BP3 as our gene for further study, which is directly regulated by important core transcription loop TFs.

IGF2BP3 is expressed at high levels in NB and is associated with disease progression

Two publicly available microarray datasets (GSE45547 and GSE49710, Supplementary Table S5) containing gene expression data from NB patients were analysed. A survival analysis was conducted, which demonstrated that patients exhibiting elevated IGF2BP3 levels experienced poorer survival outcomes compared to those with lower expression levels (P < 0.05; Fig. 2a). Additionally, there was a significant association between IGF2BP3 expression and various clinical parameters such as MYCN amplification, disease stage, progression, and increased mortality risk in NB patients (Fig. 2b). To determine whether IGF2BP3 protein levels were elevated in clinical samples, we used IHC to analyse NB tissue from 58 patients and peripheral nerve tissue from four patients with different clinical stages. We found that IGF2BP3 expression was significantly greater in the NB tissues than in the peripheral nerve tissues (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2c and d). These results were consistent with the microarray data and further confirmed the high expression of IGF2BP3 in NB tissue. To investigate the expression of IGF2BP3 in various tumor types, we analysed its expression in tumor cell lines derived from NB, acute myeloid leukaemia, acute lymphoblastic leukaemia, osteosarcoma, and glioblastoma using Western blot analysis. The results revealed its high expression in NB, rhabdomyosarcoma, Ewing sarcoma, and osteosarcoma models (Fig. 2e). Therefore, our results indicate that IGF2BP3 likely plays an important role in NB cell proliferation.

Knockdown of IGF2BP3 inhibits NB cell growth and migration in vitro

As high expression of IGF2BP3 is linked to poor prognosis, we investigated its function by knocking down IGF2BP3. To investigate the functional impact of IGF2BP3, two specific shRNAs (sh-IGF2BP3#1 and sh-IGF2BP3#2) were designed to target this gene. The effectiveness of these shRNAs in reducing IGF2BP3 levels was confirmed by qPCR and Western blot analysis (Fig. 3a and b). Initial microscopic observation revealed reduced cell density and aberrant cellular morphology in IGF2BP3-knockdown groups (Fig. 3c). Functional assays revealed that NB cells with reduced IGF2BP3 expression exhibited significantly lower proliferation rates compared to the control group (sh-NC), according to results from CCK-8 (Fig. 3d) and colony formation assays (Fig. 3e). Moreover, the Transwell assays revealed that IGF2BP3 expression is positively correlated with cell migration capability (Fig. 3f). Additionally, cell cycle experiments demonstrated that knockdown of IGF2BP3 resulted in cell division arrest at the G1 phase (Fig. 3g). Wound healing studies revealed a positive connection between IGF2BP3 expression and NB cell migratory capacity (Fig. 3h). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that knockdown of IGF2BP3 significantly suppresses NB cell proliferation and migration in vitro.

IGF2BP3 promotes NB progression in vivo

We developed a luciferase-labeled subcutaneous NB xenograft model and conducted regular fluorescence imaging to determine if knocking down IGF2BP3 could suppress NB growth in vivo (Fig. 4a). During the study period, the luminescence signal of tumors in the IGF2BP3 knockout group increased at a significantly slower rate than that of tumors in the control group after 15 days (Fig. 4b and c). After 25 days, we dissected the mouse tumors and found that the size of the IGF2BP3 knockdown tumors was significantly reduced (Fig. 4d). Our findings revealed that compared to the control group, IGF2BP3 downregulation significantly decreased the growth rate of the tumors, as well as their volume and weight, as shown in Fig. 4e and f. Additionally, there were no significant differences between the experimental and control groups concerning the body weight of the mice, indicating that the treatment targeting IGF2BP3 did not adversely affect the overall health of the mice as measured by weight (Supplementary Figure S3). IHC analysis revealed that the downregulation of IGF2BP3 (Fig. 4g) reduced the expression of Ki-67, which is an indicator of cell proliferation (Ki-67, Fig. 4h), and increased the rate of apoptosis (cleaved caspase-3, Fig. 4i). These results suggest that IGF2BP3 promotes NB growth in vivo.

IGF2BP3 is involved in the regulation of E2F target genes, MYC target genes, and the G2/M checkpoint signaling pathway

To investigate the molecular mechanism of IGF2BP3 in NB progression, we performed RNA-seq analysis in SK-N-BE (2) cells. The cells were stably transfected with either shRNA or sh-IGF2BP3#1. The results showed that 2806 genes were significantly differentially expressed between the two groups of cells, with 1422 genes downregulated and 1384 genes upregulated (log2-fold change ≥1, P < 0.05) (Fig. 5a, Supplementary Table S6). KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that the DEGs were primarily involved in signaling pathways, including E2F target genes, MYC target genes, and the G2/M checkpoint (Fig. 5b and c, Supplementary Table S7). These pathways are closely associated with the cell cycle, genome stability, and cancer development. qPCR and Western blot experiments confirmed that the knockdown of IGF2BP3 led to a reduction in the expression of certain target genes of these pathways (Fig. 5d and e). In summary, the above findings suggest that IGF2BP3 plays a major role in NB-targeting genes, including E2F-targeted genes, MYC-targeted genes, and genes involved in G2/M checkpoint signaling pathways.

MeRIP-seq combined with RNA-seq revealed that E2F2 is a target of IGF2BP3

Given the role of IGF2BP3 as a specific m6A reader protein, our objective was to identify m6A-modified transcripts and pinpoint target genes of IGF2BP3. Following the knockdown of IGF2BP3, we proceeded to perform MeRIP-seq. The distribution of m6A peaks demonstrated a similar pattern in the IGF2BP3 knockout and control groups (Fig. 6a, Supplementary Figure S4). Furthermore, a statistical motif analysis was conducted on all differentially abundant m6A peaks (Supplementary Table S8). A significant increase in the m6A peaks for 170 genes was observed, while the peaks for 199 genes were significantly reduced (Fig. 6b, Supplementary Table S9) (log2-fold change ≥1, P < 0.05). Parallel RNA-seq analysis identified 1422 downregulated genes post-knockdown (Fig. 5a, Supplementary Table S6). Next, overlapping the MeRIP-seq-reduced m6A genes (n = 199) with RNA-seq-downregulated genes (n = 1422) yielded 20 candidate target genes (Fig. 6c). Heatmap analysis demonstrated strong concordance between m6A signal attenuation and transcriptional suppression across these genes (Fig. 6d), consistent with IGF2BP3's role in stabilizing m6A-modified transcripts. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of these genes revealed significant overrepresentation of cell cycle-related pathways, with “E2F targets” ranking as the top enriched pathway (Fig. 5b). The results identified E2F2 as an IGF2BP3 target gene for further analysis.

Focusing on E2F2, we found in patients with NB, E2F2 expression and IGF2BP3 levels correlated positively (Fig. 6e), with high levels of E2F2 expression linked to a worse prognosis in these patients (Fig. 6f). We then knocked down IGF2BP3 and verified the downregulation of E2F2 expression by qPCR and Western blotting (Fig. 6g). Considering the critical role of the E2F family of TFs in cell cycle regulation and chromosomal stability, we also evaluated the impact of IGF2BP3 knockdown on E2F-mediated transcriptional activity (Fig. 6h). Using luciferase reporter assays, we found that IGF2BP3 knockdown reduced the luciferase activity in SK-N-BE(2) cells that had WT E2F motifs but did not affect cells with mutant (Mut) E2F motifs (Fig. 6h). This differential response supports the hypothesis that E2F2 is a direct target of IGF2BP3, highlighting the significance of this interaction in the transcriptional regulation associated with NB progression.

IGF2BP3 regulates E2F2 expression in an m6a-dependent manner

We analysed the effect of IGF2BP3 knockdown on m6A peaks in E2F2 exons using MeRIP-seq and found a significant reduction in m6A peaks in E2F2 (Fig. 7a). The presence of mRNA m6A modification binding sites (GSE5601 and GSE78030) in the seventh exon of E2F2, which has been confirmed in the RMVar database [30] of validated E2F2, is consistent with our MeRIP-seq results (Fig. 7b). We predicted the m6A modification site (Fig. 7c) and RNA secondary structure (Fig. 7d) of E2F2 with high confidence through the SRAMP website [31]. We also confirmed the enrichment of E2F2 mRNA in the m6A-specific antibody precipitate by MeRIP RT–qPCR (Fig. 7e, Supplementary Figure S5a). We further assessed the interaction between IGF2BP3 and E2F2 mRNA by RIP and RT–qPCR, and the results showed that IGF2BP3 was able to bind E2F2 mRNA and was enriched in SK-N-BE(2) and SH-SY5Y cells (Fig. 7f, Supplementary Figure S5b). This m6A modification can be regulated by IGF2BP3. Our research focused on the impact of IGF2BP3 on the stability of E2F2 mRNA, revealing that IGF2BP3 knockdown significantly shortened the half-life of E2F2 mRNA (Fig. 7g and h, Supplementary Figure S5c and d). This suggests a stabilizing role of IGF2BP3 on E2F2 mRNA. These results indicate that IGF2BP3 regulates E2F2 expression by recognizing m6A modification sites, increasing E2F2 mRNA stability, and slowing E2F2 mRNA degradation. To verify the role of E2F2, we transfected sh-E2F2#1 and sh-E2F2#2 into SK-N-BE(2) and SH-SY5Y cells and downregulated the expression of E2F2 (Fig. 7h and i, Supplementary Figure S5e). To assess the effects of E2F2 levels on NB cell behaviour, we measured E2F2 expression by qPCR and Western blotting and evaluated the proliferative capacity of NB cells using a CCK-8 assay. The findings indicated that reducing E2F2 expression significantly curtailed the proliferation of NB cells (Fig. 7j, Supplementary Figure S5f). To explore the ability of E2F2 to rescue IGF2BP3 expression, we overexpressed E2F2 in IGF2BP3 knockdown SK-N-BE(2) and SH-SY5Y cells and determined the transfection efficiency and cell proliferative ability (Fig. 7l and m, Supplementary Figure S5h and i). Furthermore, the study found that overexpressing E2F2 could mitigate the growth inhibition caused by IGF2BP3 knockdown in NB cells, as shown in (Fig. 7n, Supplementary Figure S5j). This evidence underscores that E2F2 serves as a critical target gene for IGF2BP3 and that the IGF2BP3-E2F2 signaling axis plays a vital role in regulating NB growth.

Discussion

The malignant progression of NB is associated with intricate transcriptional regulatory networks and epigenetic dysregulation [32]. Although both CRCs and m6A-mediated epitranscriptional regulation have been independently implicated in NB pathogenesis, their functional crosstalk remains unexplored [33–36]. In this study, we uncovered a previously unrecognized mechanism whereby core regulatory circuit (CRC) TFs directly regulate the expression of the m6A reader protein IGF2BP3, which subsequently stabilizes E2F2 mRNA in an m6A-dependent manner, thereby establishing a CRC TFs-IGF2BP3-E2F2 oncogenic axis driving NB pathogenesis.

While CRC TFs like MYCN are established master regulators of NB, their control over RNA epigenetic modifiers was not previously known. By integrating ChIP-seq and clinical data, we demonstrate that multiple CRC TFs (MYCN, PHOX2B, HAND2, GATA3) directly bind the IGF2BP3 promoter, coinciding with H3K27Ac-marked active enhancers. This expands the functional scope of CRC beyond classical transcriptional targets to include remodeling of the m6A epitranscriptome.

Here, we present evidence that IGF2BP3 is highly expressed in clinical NB specimens and is associated with poor outcomes in patients with NB. We also demonstrate its essential role in promoting NB cell growth and progression, both in vitro and in vivo. To investigate the molecular mechanisms of IGF2BP3 in NB, we performed bioinformatics analysis and identified several cancer-associated genes that were differentially expressed in NB. The genes in question were found to be enriched in several key pathways, including the E2F target pathway, the MYC target pathway and the G2M checkpoint pathway. These pathways play crucial roles in the cell cycle, as they are involved in cell proliferation, DNA repair, and tumorigenesis [37–39]. All these studies suggest that IGF2BP3 is a key factor in the development and progression of NB.

Integrated analysis of MeRIP-seq and RNA-seq data after IGF2BP3 knockdown revealed E2F2 as a top candidate with concordant reductions in both m6A enrichment and transcript levels. This co-reduction signature is a hallmark of reader-dependent mRNA stabilization [40], reflecting accelerated degradation of m6A-modified transcripts upon reader loss rather than altered methylation chemistry [12, 41]. Mechanistic validation confirmed that IGF2BP3 directly binds m6A-modified E2F2 mRNA at exon 7, prolonging its half-life to stabilize E2F2 protein and activate E2F-driven proliferation pathways.

Critically, the CRC TFs-IGF2BP3-E2F2 axis establishes a parallel oncogenic pathway critical for NB progression. N-myc is a TF that directly promotes IGF2BP3 expression in NB cells. IGF2BP3 regulates the proliferation of NB cells via m6A modification of MYCN [42], our data reveal that IGF2BP3 concurrently stabilizes E2F2 mRNA through a distinct mechanism. This independence is robustly demonstrated by multiple lines of evidence. Consistent functional dependency is evident, as suppression of proliferation occurs upon IGF2BP3 or E2F2 knockdown in both MYCN-amplified (SK-N-BE2) and non-amplified (SH-SY5Y) cell lines. Critically, genetic evidence establishes both sufficiency and necessity: the growth deficits caused by IGF2BP3 knockdown were specifically rescued by E2F2 overexpression in both contexts. Notably, in MYCN-amplified and non-MYCN-amplified cells, E2F2 re-expression partially restores the IGF2BP3 knockdown phenotype, clearly demonstrating that the IGF2BP3-E2F2 axis independently drives proliferation. Furthermore, a conserved molecular mechanism underpins this axis across tested cell lines, involving direct IGF2BP3-E2F2 mRNA-binding and IGF2BP3-dependent stabilization of E2F2 mRNA, prolonging its half-life.

The E2F TF family is a master regulator of cell cycle progression, critically controlling the G1/S transition and DNA replication [43, 44]. Dysregulation of E2F activity, particularly overactivation, is a common oncogenic driver across cancers, promoting uncontrolled proliferation, genomic instability, and chemoresistance [45]. Among transcriptional activators (E2F1–E2F3), E2F2 demonstrates marked oncogenic potency, with frequent overexpression correlating with adverse clinical outcomes in malignancies such as NB, ovarian carcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma [46, 47]. Consequently, targeting the E2F pathway, and specifically E2F2, represents a promising therapeutic strategy for NB, offering a novel approach tailored to these aggressive genetic contexts.

In summary, we delineate a novel oncogenic axis: CRC TFs transcriptionally activate the m6A reader IGF2BP3, which stabilizes E2F2 mRNA in an m6A-dependent manner to drive NB progression. Crucially, this axis promotes NB, uncovering an expanded role for CRC factors and identifying the IGF2BP3-E2F2 axis as a viable target for treating NB.

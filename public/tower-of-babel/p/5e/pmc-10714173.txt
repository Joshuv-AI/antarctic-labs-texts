Novel, Instantaneous Vascular Closure Solution for Catheter-Based Therapies
Authors: Paul Sorajja, Vinayak N Bapat, Steven F Bolling, Susheel Kodali
Published: 2023

Although catheter-based therapies have become established for a wide variety of cardiovascular patients, bleeding complications remain significant. Among patients undergoing large-bore catheter-based procedures (eg, transcatheter aortic valve replacement, endovascular aortic aneurysm repair, ventricular-assisted device placement), percutaneous vascular closure devices can fail and be associated with complications in 12% to 21%.1,2 These bleeding complications often require additional interventions, such as subsequent hemostasis products, surgical repair, and blood transfusion, leading to increased costs, increased length of stay, and worse mortality.3

Current vascular closure technologies can be classified into 3 categories: 1) plug-based solutions, where a plug of sealant such as collagen or hydrogel (eg, polyethylene glycol) is placed outside the vessel access site under compression; 2) suture-based devices; and 3) patch-based solutions, where a patch is inserted inside the vessel for closure that resorbs over a period. Overall, significant limitations of current approaches include risk of device embolization, challenges for use in calcified vessels, potential for iatrogenic stenosis or vessel occlusion, and unintended design for use in large vessels.1,2,4,5

The xCinch Access Management System (xDot Medical) is a novel percutaneous vascular closure solution specifically designed to address the large and growing unmet needs of vascular closure and prevention of associated complications. The xCinch device replicates placement of 2 surgical cross-stitches and approximates the surgical approach of purse-string cinching or Z-plasty for automated bleeding control. The xCinch device is designed specifically for use in a broad range of vessel sizes (8-F to 24-F), as well as in challenging anatomical conditions (eg, calcification and obese patients). The key features of the xCinch device are as follows

1)Four, 2-prong nitinol anchors, delivered at right angles into the vessel wall. The anchor delivery is single-action, and designed to have minimal vulnerability to vessel pathology, such as thickness, plaque, and calcification.

2)Instantaneous closure with automated cinching for hemostasis: A novel, patented approach provides automated cinching of the sutures as the sheath is removed, providing instantaneous closure while maintaining guidewire access and without requiring manual compression for hemostasis.

3)Guidewire access maintained (single access wiring, no need for repeated rewiring through the xCinch delivery device) throughout and allows for better trackability in obese patients.

The preclinical evaluation of the xCinch device was performed in the swine model, in compliance with all institutional and national requirements for the care and use of laboratory animals and with approval from animal care and use committee. In all procedures, the xCinch device was deployed after initial guidewire access was achieved under ultrasound guidance. A sheath (range, 11-F to 16-F) was introduced over the guidewire and automated tensioning of the sutures was activated before sheath removal.

In animal studies, the device was evaluated in femoral veins (via a percutaneous approach; n = 6) and descending aorta (via a laparotomy approach; n = 3). Time to hemostasis upon sheath removal was instantaneous (0 seconds) for femoral vein closure and <1 minute for placements in the descending aorta. Angiographic and ultrasound assessment showed excellent vessel patency and normal flow after implantation and at 30-days term (Figure 1A). Gross necropsy demonstrated that all treated vessel segments were patent and with no evidence of thrombosis. The analysis of all distal nontarget organs was unremarkable. Histological analysis of the treated vessels showed neointima formation at 30 days with no exposure of implant (anchors) inside the vessels (Figure 1B). Mild to moderate inflammation and mild to moderate fibrosis associated with implant and the device tract was observed at 30 days, consistent with the physiological healing response expected secondary to implantation.

The xCinch device was also evaluated in a perfused cadaver model (n = 12; 4 specimens and 3 implants each), where a segment of the femoral artery was pressurized and treated with an xCinch implant. Effectiveness was evaluated for vascular closure after placement of 14-F to 18-F sheaths. The results showed no impact of human vessel calcification on xCinch deployment on the ability to achieve hemostasis.

In summary, the novel xCinch Access Management System has shown excellent effectiveness and a promising safety profile in animal and perfused cadaver studies. A first-in-human study will be initiated in the beginning of 2024, to be followed by a U.S. investigation device exemption study in the second half of 2024.

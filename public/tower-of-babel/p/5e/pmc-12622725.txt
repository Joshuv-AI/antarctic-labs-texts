Epidemiological trends, attributable risks, decomposition analysis, and forecasts of Alzheimer’s disease and other dementias burden in China, 1990 to 2036: A population-based observational study
Authors: Haigang Chang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study analyzes the burden of Alzheimer disease and other dementias (ADOD) in China using Global Burden of Disease 2021 data. The data on ADOD in China from 1990 to 2021 was collected from the Global Burden of Disease 2021. The incidence rate, mortality and the number of disability adjusted life years (DALYs), and age-standardized rates of ADOD were analyzed. Use Joinpoint analysis to evaluate trends. Analyze the attribution ratio of risk factors such as smoking, high body mass index, and high fasting blood glucose (FPG) to the burden of ADOD disease. Evaluate the relative contributions of epidemiological changes, population growth, and population aging through decomposition analysis. Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average model is used to evaluate future trends. Age-standardized incidence rose from 121.11 (95% confidence interval: 105.5–137.99) to 151.47 (131.22–173.34) per 100,000, with an estimated annual percentage change (APC) of 0.41% (0.34–0.49%). Age-standardized mortality declined from 31.39 (7.6–83.63) to 30.82 (7.88–82.43) per 100,000, yet deaths increased by 3.92% annually (estimated APC: 3.79–4.04%). disability adjusted life years (DALYs) surged to 10.07 (4.95–22.22) million, with age-standardized DALY rate reaching 562.39 (271.16–1238.81) per 100,000. Joinpoint regression confirmed upward trends for age-standardized incidence (average APC = 0.60%, 0.46–0.74%) and age-standardized DALY rate (average APC = 0.10%, −0.01 to 0.22%). The incidence rate, mortality, and DALYs of ADOD in men and women mostly occur in the age group over 75 years old. FPG was the leading risk factor, attributing to 10.5% of DALYs, followed by smoking (8.0%) and high body mass index (0.2%). Decomposition analysis identified epidemiological changes as the primary driver of mortality increases (679,000 deaths, 45.6% contribution), exceeding aging (595,000 deaths) and population growth (214,000 deaths). Projections to 2036 indicate dramatic growth: cases rising to 7.46 (5.73–9.20) million, deaths to 1.12 (0.93–1.31) million, and DALYs to 23.76 (19.10–28.43) million. The ADOD burden in China has significantly increased, especially among women and the elderly population. It is worth noting that FPG is the primary risk factor for ADOD. The disease ADOD burden will continue to rise. Attention should be paid to the issue of population aging, and interventions targeting ADOD risk factors should be emphasized.

1. Introduction

Alzheimer disease and other dementia (ADOD) is the 7th leading cause of death in the world, affecting more than 55 million people. Poor health and high care costs caused by dementia put great pressure on families, communities, health care systems, and social safety nets.[1] Alzheimer disease is mainly characterized by total dementia, including memory disorders, cognitive disorders, executive dysfunction, and personality and behavioral changes. Most patients are accompanied by mental disorders. Dementia mainly occurs in the elderly population, and the incidence and prevalence increase with age; this trend is more common in low- and middle-income countries and regions. It has become a growing global public health problem, bringing a serious economic and disease burden to society and families. According to the data retrieval results of the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) database in 2021, the number of deaths caused by ADOD worldwide is 1,952,677, making it the 2nd leading cause of adult disability among people aged 60 and above.[2] With the increasing aging of the population, it is expected that there will be more than 150 million Alzheimer patients in the world by 2050, and the cost of treating dementia will exceed 250 million US dollars, while Chinese patients account for 25% of the global cases.[3] In China, dementia has become an important public health problem due to the extension of life expectancy and the growth of the elderly population. The incidence, prevalence, and mortality of Alzheimer disease have risen steadily, and it has become the 5th leading cause of death for urban and rural residents in China according to the detailed cause of death.[4] It seriously affects the public health and social sustainable development in China, and brings a heavy burden to the public and health care system.[5] These challenges are exacerbated by the rapid aging of the population, the increase in life expectancy, and the increase in the prevalence of risk factors such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and hypertension. Long-term monitoring of ADOD risk factors and prevalence trends is essential for dementia prevention. The mortality rate of this disease is not determined by a single factor, but by a complex interaction of many risk factors. There is increasing evidence that more than one-third of dementia cases can be prevented or delayed by managing controllable risk factors.[6] Long-term monitoring of ADOD risk factors and prevalence trends is critical to dementia prevention.

In addition, dementia puts great pressure on the care system and the family. However, the diagnosis and treatment rate of Alzheimer disease in China is still very low, and the public awareness is also very low. Understanding the trend of ADOD is essential to provide information aimed at mitigating the impact of this growing health challenge. This study uses the data of the 2021 GBD Study and uses comprehensive decomposition analysis and risk factor analysis. Our goals are: to describe the disease burden of ADOD in China from 1990 to 2021, to quantify the ADOD disease burden caused by risk factors, and the proportion of specific burden attributable to each risk factor, to assess the contribution of population growth, aging and epidemiological changes to age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR); and to predict the future trajectory of ADOD to 2036, and more accurately quantify the impact of aging on the future disease burden. By providing a more detailed, vertical and geographical understanding of ADOD epidemiology, this work aims to provide information for evidence-based policies, guide the allocation of health care resources, and promote targeted interventions.

2. Methods

2.1. Data source

GBD 2021 is a multi-country cooperative study conducted by countries around the world, updated annually. All data in the GBD repository are de-identified and do not contain individual-level patient information. This analysis does not require ethical approval or informed consent. The latest version provides the disease burden by age, sex and region from 1990 to 2021 (369 diseases and injuries in 204 countries and regions).[7,8] The GBD 2021 research includes a wide range of data sources (including life registration, disease registration, census, health surveys, research, government reports, etc). GBD is a global cooperative project led by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation, which aims to systematically assess the health of diseases, injuries and risk factors worldwide. The GBD database has a wide range of data sources, including census data, disease registration system data, death registration system data, hospital and medical institution records, epidemiological survey data, and published scientific literature, etc.[9] Published GBD integrates, corrects, and infers multi-source heterogeneous data through the Bayesian meta regression tool (DisMod-MR 2.1) to fill data gaps, eliminate data deviations, and generate consistent and comparable disease burden estimates and their 95% uncertainty interval (UI).[10]

From 1990 to 2021, the incidence of ADOD, death, disability adjustment life year (DALY), and age-standardized incidence rate (age-standardized incidence rate [ASIR]) were collected by gender and age, age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR), and ASDR. In view of the scarcity of ADOD cases under the age of 40, we exclude this age group and only focus on people aged 40 and above. In this model, the collected data is stratified into a continuous 5-year-old age group and a continuous 5-year period. The incidence and mortality of ADOD are recorded in a consecutive 5-year-old age group (from 40–44 to 95+). In the attribution analysis of risk factors, for high body mass index (BMI), high fasting blood sugar and smoking, the attribution mortality rate and the standardized mortality rate attribution age, DALYs are estimated. In this study, Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average is used to predict the disease burden of ADOD 2022 to 2036.

2.2. Decomposition analysis

Use the Das Gupta decomposition method to break down the changes in the ADOD burden between 1990 and 2021 into contributions to aging, population growth, and epidemiological changes.[11,12] This approach enables us to analyze the overall changes in the burden as these key factors, so as to have a clearer understanding of how demographic and epidemiological changes shape trends over time. Unlike traditional methods that mainly focus on establishing relationships between variables, such as linear regression, decomposition analysis can evaluate in detail the independent contribution of each factor to the overall change of the disease burden. By analyzing these trends, we have a clearer understanding of the potential drivers of the change in the ADAD burden.

2.3. Statistical analysis

The Joinpoint regression model (Joinpoint 4.9.0.1; National Cancer Institute, Bethesda) is mainly achieved by comparing annual percentage change (APC) and average annual percentage change (AAPC) with 0 to evaluate whether the incidence trend at different stages is statistically significant.[13] Calculate APC and AAPCs of ADOD incidence, deaths, DALYs, ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR from 1990 to 2021, and evaluate the significance of the trend with a 95% confidence interval (CI). AAPC combines the slope of each stage to judge the direction and significance of the overall trend. The specific formula is: AAPC = 100 × (eβ − 1). Among them, beta is the regression coefficient of the slope of each time period. By comparing the differences between AAPC and 0, we can judge whether the trend is statistically significant. The APC calculation formula is the same as estimated annual percentage change (EAPC). EAPC is applied to the whole time period rather than the post-segment interval. APC represents the annual percentage change in each interval. The time trend of age-standardized rate (ASR) in a specific time period is expressed by the EAPC. In short, we use the y = α + βx + ε regression model for these calculations, where y is ln(ASR), x is a time variable, and ε is an error term. Assume that the natural logarithm of ASR is linear with time; therefore, EAPC = 100 × [exp(β) − 1].[14] We also use linear models to calculate 95% CIs of EAPC. If the lower limit of EAPC and its 95% CI is >0, ASR is considered to be on the upward trend. On the contrary, if the EAPC and its 95% CI upper limit are <0, ASR is considered to be on a downward trend.[7] Using decomposition analysis, we estimated the impact of population aging, population growth, epidemiological changes on ADOD mortality and DALY between 1990 and 2021. All statistical analysis uses R software (version 4.2.1; R Development Core Team, The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), and the difference of P < .05 is statistically significant.

3. Results

3.1. Trend of ADOD burden change from 1990 to 2021

The number of ADOD cases in China increased from 703,178 in 1990 (95% CI: 601,505–808,633) to 291,412 in 2021 (95% CI: 2,504,728–3,350,743). ASIR rose from 121.11 per 100,000 in 1990 (95% CI: 105.5–137.99) to 151.47 per 100,000 in 2021 (95% CI: 131.22–173.34). From 1990 to 2021, the EAPC incidence of ADOD increased by 0.41% (95% CI: 0.34–0.49%) (Table 1 and Fig. 1). The number of ADOD deaths in China increased from 119,808 (95% CI: 28,349–322,103) in 1990 to 491 773 in 2021 (95% CI: 124,968–1,330,181). ASMR decreased from 31.39 per 100,000 in 1990 (95% CI: 7.6–83.63) to 30.82 per 100,000 in 2021 (95% CI: 7.88–82.43). From 1990 to 2021, the EAPC mortality rate of ADOD increased by 3.92 percent (95% CI: 3.79–4.04%). The number of ADOD DALYs in China increased from 2 702 484 in 1990 (95% CI: 1,239,177–6,085,394) to 10,072,477 in 2021 (95% CI: 4,947,154–22,219,153). ASDR rose from 53 447 per 100,000 (95% CI: 236.2–1 190.6) in 1990 to 562.39/100,000 in 2021 (95% CI: 271.16–1 238.81).

3.3. Gender and age differences in ADOD burden

Figure S1, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q684 shows the morbidity, mortality, and DALYs of ADOD for men and women in different age groups in China. In 1990, the incidence of ADOD in men and women peaked 75 to 79 years old, and the incidence rate after the age of 75 decreased with age. The mortality rate peaks at 80 to 84 years old, and the DALYs rate peaks at 80 to 84 years old. The incidence of female ADOD, mortality rate, and DALYs in all age groups is higher than that of men. In 2021, the morbidity of ADOD in men and women, the mortality rate peaked in the 80 to 84 age group, and the mortality rate peak in the 85 to 89 age group. The peak of DALYs occurred in the 80 to 84 age group, and the incidence of female ADOD among women in all age groups was higher than that of men.

3.4. Attribution analysis of risk factors

From 1990 to 2021, the main attributed risk factors for death were: high fasting blood sugar (10.8%), smoking (6.8%), and high BMI (0.1%) (Fig. 2). The main risk factors attributable to DALY are: high abdominal blood sugar (10.5%), smoking (8.0%) and high BMI (0.2%). Age group attribution of risk factors for death (Figure S2, Supplemental Digital Content, https://links.lww.com/MD/Q684). Among them, the percentage of high fasting blood sugar in the 75 to 79 age group is 11.3%, the percentage of smoking in the 50 to 54 age group is 14.2%, and the percentage of high BMI in the 45 to 59 age group is 0.7%. Age group attribution DALY risk factors: Among them, the percentage of high fasting blood sugar in the 75 to 79 age group is 11.3%, the percentage of smoking in the 50 to 54 age group is 13.8%, and the percentage of high BMI in the 45 to 59 age group is 0.7%.

3.5. Decomposition analysis of DALY and mortality rate changes in China

The net change in the number of deaths caused by ADOD in China in 2021 was 1.48 million, of which the burden of women (496,000) was significantly higher than that of men (247,000). Among the 3 drivers, epidemiological change (679,000) contributed the most, followed by aging (595,000) and population growth (214,000). This shows that epidemiological changes in disease risk are the main drivers. Population factors (14.4%): the contribution of women (72,000) is twice that of men (35,000); the factor of aging (40.0%): the contribution of aging to women (199,000) is also twice that of that of men; epidemiological changes (45.6%). The disease burden of ADOD in 2021 (measured by DALYs) showed a significant increase, with a total net change of 29.48 million DALYs. This increase is driven by 3 factors: population growth (contribution of 48.27 million), aging (11.792 million), and epidemiological change (12.2861 million). Among them, epidemiological change contributes the most. Demographic factors (16.4%): women contribute more (1.585 million vs 859,000 men), aging factor (40.0%): is the 2nd largest driver, with women’s aging contribution 1.8 times that of men (3.816 million vs 2.080 million), epidemiological changes (43.6%): the highest contribution (Fig. 3).

4. Discussion

Based on the GBD 2021 database, this study analyzes the long-term trend and risk factor attribution characteristics of China’s ADOD burden from 1990 to 2021. The results show that in the past 30 years, the burden of ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR caused by ADOD in China has still shown an upward trend, and the contribution of the attribution of interferable risk factors such as high fasting blood sugar, smoking, and high BMI is particularly prominent. This study predicts that ADOD onset, death, and DALY will show an upward trend in the future.

In terms of age distribution characteristics, ADOD’s ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR all increase with age, with a single peak distribution among different age groups. Due to the large elderly population in China, the burden of ADOD increases with age. Studies have shown that age is a key factor in the risk of ADOD death, but the impact of gender on lifestyle choices and health status also significantly affects these mortality rates. The age-specific incidence rate of women is higher than that of men. This is consistent with previous research.[5] This shows that women dominate not only because women live longer. These differences may be due to physical differences between men and women. Existing studies have pointed out that gender differences in brain structure, neurodevelopment, cognitive function, and biochemical metabolism may be an important cause.[9,15] It is particularly noteworthy that women are more prone to enhanced inflammatory reactions, and the risk of diabetes and metabolic syndrome is higher. These factors may exacerbate the development process of ADOD.[16] Therefore, in the prevention and treatment of ADOD, we should focus on female patients and elderly groups, and strengthen preventive measures and post-illness care system.[17]

ADOD has a prominent disease burden in China. Driven by economic development, China has made comprehensive progress in medicine, and the average life expectancy has steadily increased from a low level in the past. However, with the acceleration of an aging society, the Gerile disease has become more serious and has attracted a lot of attention from society. Due to the high incidence, disability rate and need for care, it poses a major challenge to the health of the elderly, greatly reduces the quality of life of the elderly, and puts a burden on the family, society, and medical system. In this study, demographic factors reflected the impact of population base growth. For mortality: female contributions (72,000) are twice that of men (35,000); for DALY: women contribute more (158 million vs 859,000 men), which may be related to women’s longer life expectancy. For mortality: the contribution of aging to women (19.9 million) is also twice that of men, and for DALY: women’s aging contributes 1.8 times that of men (3.16 million vs 208 million), highlighting that older women are more vulnerable to dementia. Epidemiological changes include changes in diagnostic rates or treatment levels, whose contribution exceeds aging, suggesting that disease risk may rise due to changes in the environment, lifestyle or medical system. In order to meet the challenges of aging in China, we call for the development of a national strategy to coordinate ADOD care, raise public and professional awareness, and guide research and policy.[7]

From the perspective of risk factors, high fasting blood glucose has become the main risk factor for ADOD. Further exploration of gender differences shows that men are more affected by behavioral risks such as smoking, while women are more susceptible to metabolic risk factors such as high weight. Research confirms that smoking accelerates the development and severity of dementia.[18] Tobacco products contain chemicals such as nicotine, Tar and carbon monoxide, which can damage normal brain function and seriously damage memory, thinking, and cognitive abilities. Smoking can also greatly increase the risk of cardiovascular and respiratory diseases.[19] The burden of ADOD caused by high fasting blood sugar and high BMI increases with age in both sexes, and women are more burdensome than men. Changes in hormones and genetic susceptibility can explain gender differences in the risk of dementia.[20] There is evidence that women’s estrogen levels decline sharply after menopause, and changes in the structure and function of the brain can affect cognitive ability.[21,22] There is also evidence that the APOE4 genotype is more likely to ADOD for women than men. APOE4-positive women show faster cognitive decline and higher Aβ deposition between 65 and 75 years old, especially during menopause.[23] In addition, the surge in the food industry has diversified food choices and super-processed food has surged. This trend has changed the traditional Chinese diet to the Western diet, which is characterized by reducing whole grain consumption and increasing the intake of red meat, processed meat, sugary drinks, and high-fat foods. At the same time, the increase in the wealth of the rural population has prompted the diet to shift to more animal products and fat, resulting in an increase in obesity. These findings show that high BMI is an important metabolic risk factor for the burden of ADOD in China. Changing behavioral lifestyle through multi-factorial methods is a priority for preventing and delaying the onset of dementia in diabetic patients. Our research results are consistent with those of global and Asian studies.[24,25] The updated results of the 2020 Lancet Commission on dementia prevention, intervention, and care indicate that up to 40% of dementia incidence can be prevented through interventions targeting modifiable risk factors.[6] Adhere to the combination of healthy lifestyle factors, including a balanced diet, regular physical exercise, and harmful habits such as quitting smoking and excessive drinking. China has the largest number of smokers. Promoting smoking cessation, especially for middle-aged men, may help reduce the risk of dementia.

China’s ADOD forecast data shows that the burden of disease will increase in the future. The results show that the incidence, death and DALY of ADOD are on the rise. China has one of the largest number of dementia patients in the world.[15] The disease burden of ADOD is increasing, mainly due to the aging population. Considering China’s rapid aging, the disease burden caused by ADOD will increase rapidly in the coming decades. Therefore, it is necessary to raise public awareness, promote early risk factor screening, and commit to early diagnosis and treatment.[26] Health management departments should coordinate and integrate local resources and facilities, and accelerate the improvement of the elderly care and the elderly medical care system.[17]

Several limitations should be recognized in this study. First of all, the lack of data from some areas and the omission of the distinction between urban and rural areas may lead to deviations in the estimate. Secondly, weighted PAF can more comprehensively estimate the comprehensive impact of multiple risk factors.[27] However, due to the complexity of methodology and the potential challenge of accurately estimating the combined impact of multiple risk factors, this method is not standard in GBD research. Third, the GBD data set does not distinguish between subtypes of dementia, such as vascular dementia, each of which is related to different burdens and risk conditions, and other factors, such as genetic susceptibility and changeable risk factors, should be considered in the risk status of the dementia subtype. Future research should integrate provincial data and incorporate indicators of urban and rural education levels and medical accessibility to further analyze the driving mechanisms behind regional inequality. In addition, the role of modifiable risk factors, including air pollution, diet, and physical activity, has not been thoroughly studied within the current framework, despite being recognized as contributing factors to the risk of dementia. Future research should prioritize longitudinal cohort studies to elucidate the intricate interactions between aging, environmental exposure, and genetic susceptibility.

5. Conclusion

From 1990 to 2021, the burden of ADOD in China increased significantly. The burden of disease affects the elderly, especially women. Due to population growth and population aging, the number of people with dementia is expected to increase, and more effective prevention and management strategies are urgently needed. In addition, early interventions should be proposed to reduce the disease burden of women and high-risk elderly people. Considering ADOD risk factors, a multifaceted approach must be adopted, including raising public awareness, promoting healthier lifestyle and dietary choices, and strategically enhancing the allocation and utilization of health care resources to significantly reduce the burden of disease.

Predictors of Desire to Avoid Pregnancy: The Roles of Marital Satisfaction and Fear of Childbirth
Authors: Semiha Unkazan, Nazli Unlu Bidik, Zekiye Turan, Döndü Sevimli Guler
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionWomen’s desire to have a child can be affected by their marital relationship. The purpose of this study is to examine the effects of marital satisfaction and fear of childbirth on the desire to avoid pregnancy among married women. Since research addressing these variables together is limited, this study is expected to fill this research gap.MethodsThis is a descriptive and cross-sectional study conducted with 300 married women after a power analysis with f² = 0.424, 5% type I error, and 95% confidence interval. Data were collected using a Socio-demographic Questionnaire and the Scales of Marital Satisfaction, Women’s Childbirth Fear–Prior to Pregnancy, and Desire to Avoid Pregnancy (DAP). Hierarchical regression and correlation analysis were employed to analyse the data.ResultsHierarchical regression revealed that fear of childbirth (β = 0.496, p < 0.001), marital satisfaction (β=-0.147, p < 0.001), and duration of marriage (β = 0.151, p < 0.001) were significant predictors of DAP. The final model explained 29.8% of the variance in DAP (R² = 0.298 F (4. 297) = 31.055, p < 0.001).ConclusionWomen who have fear of childbirth and low marital satisfaction exhibit pregnancy desire to avoid pregnancy. This situation suggests that women’s marital processes may influence their perspectives on pregnancy and childbirth. Considering these results, it is important to integrate counselling and educational interventions into reproductive health services that address both marital dynamics and fears related to childbirth.Keywords: Marital satisfaction, Fear of childbirth, Avoid pregnancy, Duration of marriage, Regression

Introduction

In some cultures, women are expected to assume childbearing responsibilities shortly after marriage [1]. While pregnancy generally evokes excitement, married women may harbor complex thoughts and reservations [2], leading some to desire to avoid pregnancy for various reasons [3]. The desire to avoid pregnancy is defined as delaying the next birth or not having another birth at all [4].

Numerous factors influence the desire to avoid pregnancy [5, 6]. These include socio-demographic aspects such as family and friends’ experiences [7], race [5], employment and economic status [8–10], number of pregnancies, and previous birth experience [10–12]. Severe fear of childbirth [13, 14] and the quality of marital or romantic relationships [5, 6] are also important influences.

Marital satisfaction defines the quality of a married couple’s union [15]. Not having children can be a source of disappointment impacting marital satisfaction [16], which itself can influence the desire to avoid pregnancy. Furthermore, marital satisfaction indirectly impact women’s physical and mental health, potentially affecting their fear of childbirth [17, 18].

Severe fear of childbirth affects about 20% of women in Western countries and can lead to pregnancy avoidance, including remaining childless [19] or using highly effective contraception [13, 14, 20]. This fear is also linked to postpartum problems [21], underscoring the need for early psychological interventions [22].

Pregnancy desire is closely related to pregnancy outcomes, as avoidance may still result in unintended pregnancies [6, 23]. Such pregnancies remain a major women’s health issue and a leading cause of death among reproductive-age women [24]. Identifying women’s desire to avoid pregnancy is therefore critical [20, 25]. Since most studies are retrospective [26, 27], forward-looking assessments of women’s feelings about future pregnancy are needed to improve care [20, 28].

There are many theories and models developed to predict intentions and behaviors such as the desire to avoid pregnancy [29, 30]. One of these is the Theory of Planned Behavior, which suggests that people’s behaviors are influenced by their intentions. According to this theory, behavioral intentions are affected by attitudes toward the behavior, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control [29]. In this framework, fear of childbirth may be associated with attitudes toward pregnancy, while marital satisfaction may be linked to subjective norms, together explaining variations in women’s pregnancy avoidance intentions and behaviors.

Research linking the desire to avoid pregnancy with psychological variables like fear of childbirth and marital satisfaction is limited [18], largely focusing on pregnant women [11, 18, 31]. Critically, there has been a lack of studies examining predictors of pregnancy avoidance in non-pregnant married women. This study aims to fill this gap by investigating the impact of marital satisfaction and fear of childbirth on the desire to avoid pregnancy among married women. This research is particularly unique in its focus on non-pregnant married women, providing a prospective understanding of their desire towards a potential future pregnancy, thus expanding beyond the predominantly retrospective and pregnant-focused literature. The specific research questions addressed are:

What are the levels of marital satisfaction, fear of childbirth, and desire to avoid pregnancy in married women?

What are the levels of marital satisfaction, fear of childbirth, and desire to avoid pregnancy in married women?

Is there a relationship between married women’s socio-demographic characteristics, marital satisfaction, fear of childbirth, and desire to avoid pregnancy?

Is there a relationship between married women’s socio-demographic characteristics, marital satisfaction, fear of childbirth, and desire to avoid pregnancy?

Are the participants’ socio-demographic characteristics, marital satisfaction, and fear of childbirth predictors of the desire to avoid pregnancy?

Are the participants’ socio-demographic characteristics, marital satisfaction, and fear of childbirth predictors of the desire to avoid pregnancy?

Method

Study design

This study is a descriptive and analytical cross-sectional study. Since the study aims to collect data only at a specific time and to evaluate the relationships between the variables of socio-demographic characteristics, marital satisfaction, fear of childbirth, and desire to avoid pregnancy, it is classified as an analytical cross-sectional design [32].

Setting and participants

Participants and procedure

This study was conducted between May 2023 and April 2024. The study sample included women aged 18 years and over, in their first marriage, without a diagnosis of pregnancy, fertile and who voluntarily agreed to participate in the study. Previously married women were excluded from the study. Before collecting data from women who met the inclusion criteria, the number of data to be obtained was calculated based on a previously published study. The a priori calculation was performed in G*Power 3.1.9.7 (linear multiple regression, fixed model, R² deviation from zero). We also report the achieved (post-hoc) power based on the observed R² =0.298, yielding f²' = 0.424 and an achieved power > 0.99 with N = 300 [33, 34]. A total of 312 married women were recruited for the study; however, 12 provided incomplete data [time limitation (n = 2), not answering some study questions (n = 10)] and were excluded. Thus, the final sample consisted of 300 women who fully completed the survey. A comparison showed that there were no significant demographic differences between the excluded participants and those who remained in the study.

Ethics approval

Ethical approval was obtained from XXX University Social and Human Sciences Ethics Committee (E-61923333-050.99.99.99–242825). The study adhered to the principles stated in the Declaration of Helsinki. Married women who signed the informed consent form participated in the study. All participants were assured that their data would be kept confidential, that their participation was completely voluntary and that they could withdraw from the study at any time.

Data collection

The study data were collected face-to-face from the participants reached through social media advertisements using the Socio-demographic questionnaire, Marital Satisfaction Scale (MSS), Women Childbirth Fear – Prior to Pregnancy Scale (WCF-PPS) and Desire to Avoid Pregnancy Scale (DAP). Women who expressed interest via social media were invited to attend the for eligibility screening and in-person interviews. All participants who met the inclusion criteria completed the questionnaires through face-to-face interviews. The researcher (S.U) distributed the forms to participants, who filled them out on site, and collected them immediately after completion. During this process, the researcher was present to answer any questions and provide clarification if needed. Completion of the survey took approximately 10–15 min per participant. An information form was given to all women prior to participation and informed consent was obtained.

Measures

Socio-demographic Questionnaire

In order to better describe the sample group, ‘Socio-demographic Questionnaire’ was applied to the participants. This form was developed by the researchers in line with the literature [35, 36] and consisted of 18 questions about the characteristics of the individuals such as age, educational status, duration of marriage, duration of acquaintance, number of children, marriage types, etc.

Marital Satisfaction Scale (MSS)

The Marital Satisfaction Scale prepared by Çelik and Yazgan-İnanç (2009) was used to determine the marital satisfaction of married individuals. The MSS consists of a total of 13 items and has a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Not at all suitable for me, 5 = Completely suitable for me). The maximum score that can be obtained on the scale is 65, the minimum score is 13, and no cut-off point is specified. A high total score indicates good marital satisfaction. The Cronbach’s alpha reliability coefficient of the MSS was 0.79 and the test-retest coefficient was 0.86 [37]. In our study, it was shown to be a suitable tool for screening marital satisfaction with a Cronbach’s alpha value of 0.83. There are three factors that make up the scale. The first one is defined as ‘family’ because of the collection of items measuring the effect of family relations of married individuals on marital relationship, the second one is defined as ‘sexuality’ because of the collection of items measuring the effect of sexual relations of married individuals on marital relationship, and the third one is defined as ‘self’ because of the collection of items measuring the behavioural characteristics of spouses about each other. In this study, Cronbach’s alpha value of ‘Selfness’, ‘Sexuality’ and ‘Family’ were found to be 0.79, 0.84 and 0.80, respectively.

Women Childbirth Fear – Prior to Pregnancy Scale (WCF-PPS)

In order to evaluate the fear of childbirth, the ‘Women Childbirth Fear – Prior to Pregnancy Scale (WCF-PPS)’ was used. It was developed by Stoll et al. (2016), the validity and reliability study was conducted by Uçar and Taşhan (2018), and Cronbach’s alpha value was found to be 0.89. The responses in the 10-item scale are numbered from 1 to 6 and are six-point Likert type (1 = strongly disagree, 6 = strongly agree). The minimum score that can be obtained from the scale is 10, while the maximum score is 60 and no cut-off point is specified. A high item total score indicates a high level of fear [38, 39]. In our study, it was shown to be a suitable tool for screening fear of childbirth with a Cronbach alpha value of 0.86.

Desire to Avoid Pregnancy Scale (DAP)

In order to evaluate the symptoms of pregnancy avoidance, the ‘Desire to Avoid Pregnancy Scale (DAP)’ was used. The scale was developed by Corinne Rocca et al. in 2019. The minimum score that can be obtained on the scale is 0, the maximum score is 56, and no cut-off point is specified. A high score on the scale indicates a stronger desire to avoid pregnancy, whereas a low score indicates a weaker desire to avoid pregnancy. The Desire to Avoid Pregnancy Scale consists of a total of 14 items and 3 sub-dimensions. The sub-dimensions of the scale are named as (1) Cognitive Desires and Preferences, (2) Affective Feelings and Attitudes, (3) Anticipated Practical Consequences. Cognitive Desires and Preferences reflect individuals’ thoughts and preferences regarding pregnancy. Affective Feelings and Attitudes express individuals’ emotional responses and attitudes toward pregnancy. Anticipated Practical Consequences reveal individuals’ practical expectations and anticipated outcomes related to the possibility of pregnancy. Together, these subdimensions provide a comprehensive assessment of women’s cognitive, emotional, and practical orientations toward avoiding pregnancy. The Cronbach Alpha coefficient of the scale of desire to avoid pregnancy is 0.951, and the coefficients of the factors are 0.904, 0.847 and 0.846, respectively [40]. In our study, it was shown to be a suitable tool for screening the desire to avoid pregnancy with a Cronbach’s alpha value of 0.89. The Cronbach’s alpha value of the ‘Cognitive Desires and Preferences’ sub-dimension was 0.80; ‘Emotional Feelings and Attitudes’ was 0.69; and ‘Anticipated Practical Consequences’ was 0.82. The Cronbach’s alpha value (0.69) has been reported as an acceptable threshold in most studies [41, 42]. The fact that this value is slightly lower than other sub-dimensions may be due to the cultural/linguistic adaptation process of the scale.

Statistical analysis

The research data were analysed using the SPSS package programme (V.26). Continuous variables were described with means and SD, and categorical variables were described with frequencies and percentages. Descriptive analyses were performed for socio-demographic characteristics (see Table 1). Normality of the data distribution was verified using Skewness (values between − 1 and 1) and Kurtosis (values between − 3 and 3) [43]. Pearson correlation analyses were performed to determine preliminary statistical relationships between the study variables. Modelling was performed using hierarchical regression analysis [44]. The results were evaluated with 95% confidence interval and p < 0.05 significance level.

Results

Sample characteristics

The mean age of the participants was 36.48 ± 7.19 years. 79.7% of the married women live in the city centre and 52.3% of them have university education level and above. 28% of the women were housewives. The mean age at marriage was 23.48 ± 4.22 years. 36% of the participants had been married for 11–20 years and 71.7% were multigravid. 64.3% of the participants had at least 2 children. While 58.3% of the participants used family planning method, the most commonly used method was condom with 40% (Table 1).

Levels of desire to avoid pregnancy, marital satisfaction and fear of childbirth

Participants reported high levels of marital satisfaction (52.83 ± 8.51). The women’s childbirth fear scale scores were found to be moderate (34.98 ± 12.86). The desire to avoid pregnancy scale scores were also found to be moderate (2.31 ± 0.84) (Table 2).

Predictors of desire to avoid pregnancy

Pearson’s correlations

Correlation analyses (see Table 3) reflect statistically significant associations between several variables, at a significance level of < 0.05. Correlation analysis showed a significant negative relationship between marital satisfaction and the desire to avoid pregnancy (r = −0.227, p < 0.01). Fear of childbirth was positively and strongly correlated with the desire to avoid pregnancy (r = 0.499, p < 0.01). Furthermore, longer duration of marriage was associated with lower marital satisfaction and higher desire to avoid pregnancy, while an increase in the number of pregnancies similarly reduced marital satisfaction and increased the desire to avoid pregnancy.

Hierarchical multiple regression

In the hierarchical regression analysis, all variables with statistically significant correlations (age, age at marriage, duration of marriage, duration of pre-marital acquaintance, number of child and gravida) were added to the model. However, only the statistically significant models from our regression analysis (see Table 4) are presented. In the first step, fear of childbirth (β = 0.496, t (297) = 3.400, p < 0.001; 95% CI [0.490, 0.502]) was a significant predictor of DAP (R² = 0.246, F(1. 297) = 96.629). In the second step, adding marital satisfaction (β = −0.147, t (297) = −2.915, p < 0.001; 95% CI [−0.157, −0.137]) to the model was also a significant predictor (R² = 0.267, F(2. 297) = 53.788, p < 0.001). In the third step, adding the duration of marriage to the model (β = 0.151, p < 0.001; 95% CI [0.141, 0.161]) was found to be a significant predictor (R² = 0.289, F(3, 297) = 31.055, p < 0.001). In the last step, time of last birth (β = −0.133, p = 0.47; 95% CI [−0.39, 0.12]) was also a statistically significant predictor of DAP (R² = 0.298, F(4. 297) = 31.055, p < 0.001) (Table 4).

Discussion

The reasons why women avoid pregnancy are quite varied [45]. However, these reasons do not fully explain the decline in birth rates [46, 47]. The results of this study reveal that fear of childbirth and marital satisfaction are effective factors in married women avoiding pregnancy.

In Turkish culture, having children is considered valuable for establishing a family. Therefore, society has high expectations for married couples to have children [48]. Couples may sometimes feel pressure from society in this regard [49]. Furthermore, in some segments of society, the belief that male children are more valuable prevails. Therefore, women in particular may feel pressure to give birth to male children. Research has shown that such pressures are effective in causing fear of childbirth [50]. In Türkiye, social expectations and cultural norms related to childbirth and the birthing process may cause women to worry about giving birth [51].

The results of our study indicate that the desire to prevent pregnancy is moderate. A similar study conducted with married women in the literature found that the desire to prevent pregnancy was moderate [52]. One study showed that fear of childbirth is more common among Turkish women than among women in Western countries [9]. Considering the results of our study, it is thought that in Turkish culture, couples’ expectations regarding having children may cause women to want to prevent pregnancy, experience fear of childbirth, and experience problems within the couple.

In our study, married women’s levels of marital satisfaction were found to be at a good level. In another study, it was similarly found at a good level [53]. In another study, women’s MSS score was found to be low [54]. Considering these differing results in the context of marital satisfaction, it is thought that the results of the compared studies stem from differences in the inclusion criteria for the study samples.

Women are more afraid of birth and what can go wrong than ever before [55]. Our study found that pregnancy fear of childbirth scale scores were moderate. Numerous factors associated with fear of childbirth have been reported in the literature [56, 57]. Additionally, in a study, it was reported that women’s fear levels were high due to the fact that women’s ages were between twenty and thirty years old and young women did not have sufficient experience of the birth process [58]. In our study, it is estimated that the fact that this age group constitutes the majority of the sample is a factor affecting the moderate level of fear of childbirth.

As a significant finding within the scope of our study, a negative correlation was determined between marital satisfaction and fear of childbirth. Supporting this result, a study by Molgora and colleagues (2018) also reported that fear of childbirth increased as couples’ harmony decreased [18]. Therefore, it can be concluded that the lack of support from the male partner is a significant factor in increasing fear of childbirth in women during important life events such as childbirth.

Birth rates are declining rapidly worldwide [59]. According to the Turkish Statistical Institute, the total fertility rate in Türkiye has been steadily declining over the past 10 years, falling from 2.19 in 2014 to 1.48 in 2024 [60]. In order to reveal the reasons for this score, which is at a critical level for the future of countries, it is valuable to determine the related factors that cause women to avoid pregnancy. In our study, a highly significant positive correlation was found between fear of childbirth and the desire to avoid pregnancy. Similarly, it has been reported in previous studies that negative feelings about childbirth can lead to consequences such as avoiding pregnancy [35, 61, 62]. Other studies have revealed that 13% to 16% of women postpone or avoid pregnancy due to fear of childbirth [63] and that fear of childbirth leads to decisions to terminate pregnancy [64, 65]. These results emphasize the importance of addressing women’s fear of childbirth. Addressing fear of childbirth is thought to reduce avoidance behaviors related to pregnancy among women.

One of the important variables that play a role in marital satisfaction is the duration of marriage [66]. The duration of marriage has mixed results on marital satisfaction. Our study found a negative correlation between the duration of marriage and MSS. In some studies, it is stated that couples with shorter marriage duration have less marital satisfaction, in some studies, couples with shorter marriage duration have higher levels of marital satisfaction, and in some studies, there is no relationship between marriage duration and marital satisfaction [66–68]. Some existing studies support that marital satisfaction does not decrease over time for most couples, but remains relatively stable over long periods of time [17]. It is thought that these different results may have been influenced by various factors such as the meaning married couples attach to marriage, cultural values, and sociodemographic circumstances.

Our study found a significant positive correlation between the marriage duration and DAP. One study found that women who had been married for six years or more were at greater risk of sexual dysfunction than women who had been married for five years or less. In the same study, the sexual life quality of women with shorter marriage duration was found to be higher [69]. As the duration of marriage increases, couples’ relationship statuses, current living standards, and personal goals may diverge. In this context, the additional financial burdens and life changes that having a child will bring may cause couples to postpone or not want this decision.

Couples who spend time together before marriage are expected to have more in common and to be more compatible in terms of personal characteristics, feelings, thoughts, values, and expectations. These individuals are expected to maintain their existing friendship during the marital relationship, increase and strengthen deeper sharing, and engage in more productive self-disclosure behaviors in terms of both quality and quantity [70]. Our study found a significant positive correlation between the duration of dating and MSS. Therefore, a long courtship period may enable couples to get to know each other better and evaluate various aspects of their relationship.

Negative changes in relationships after pregnancy are unexpected for parents. The most likely reason for this is the exacerbation of stress factors that are not well controlled during the transition to parenthood [71], which in turn affects marital satisfaction [72]. Our study, a significant negative correlation was found between the number of pregnancies and the MSS. In a meta-analysis study, the results show that marital satisfaction shows a moderate decline from pregnancy to 24 months postpartum [73]. The presence of children in the marital relationship is associated with marital satisfaction, but an increase in the number of children can lead to marital problems [71, 74, 75]. Therefore, it is thought that factors such as increased responsibilities, stress and fatigue, and increased financial burden associated with having more children may reduce marital satisfaction.

In our study, fear of childbirth was found to be a predictor of the desire to avoid pregnancy. The model created with the addition of marital satisfaction is meaningful and significantly predicts the desire to avoid pregnancy. Marital satisfaction affects physical and mental health, life satisfaction, job success and social communication [76]. Similarly, pregnancy planning also has a major role on the economic and social status of the family [77]. For this reason, marital satisfaction may be associated with the ability to cope with the additional burdens that having a child may bring, and therefore couples may be more likely to prefer to avoid pregnancy.

Adding the duration of marriage to the model significantly predicts the desire to avoid pregnancy. In the studies conducted, it has been determined that as the duration of marriage increases, factors such as advanced age and menopause affect sexual life, reduce the frequency of sexual intercourse, negatively affect sexual functions and life, and poor marital/relationship satisfaction negatively affects sexual functions or sexual life quality [78–80]. These results suggest that in long-term marriages, individuals may be more aware of the changes that having children will bring, and this may influence the desire to avoid pregnancy.

Time of last birth, negative communication and problem intensity [71, 81], parental stress [82] and couple intimacy [83] affect the course of marital satisfaction from the first to the second year after birth. In our study, the time of last birth was found to significantly predict the desire to avoid pregnancy. Therefore, having a newborn baby, the economic burden on families, awareness of family planning after childbirth, and the psychological process after childbirth can create reluctance towards another pregnancy, thereby influencing the desire to prevent pregnancy.

Strengths and limitation of study

This study has several limitations. Firstly, the data were collected based on self-report. In addition, married women’s fear of childbirth, marital satisfaction and pregnancy avoidance levels are limited to the qualities measured by the scales applied in the study. Finally, the factors analysed in our study may change over time. In future studies, a prospective or longitudinal approach may be preferred. Moreover, mixed-method approaches are also recommended, as qualitative insights could provide a deeper and more nuanced understanding of pregnancy avoidance behaviours. However, this study provides solid evidence that fear of childbirth and marital satisfaction are associated with the desire to avoid pregnancy in married women. The strengths of this study include its large sample size, the use of validated and reliable measurement tools, and the comprehensive evaluation of the desire to avoid pregnancy by examining multiple variables within the same model.

Conclusion

This study examined the key factors associated with the desire to avoid pregnancy among married women in their first marriage. Our findings indicated that fear of childbirth and low marital satisfaction were significantly associated with the desire to avoid pregnancy. Women’s childbirth fear scores were found to be at a moderate level, and higher levels of fear of childbirth were significantly associated with a stronger desire to avoid pregnancy. Furthermore, the duration of marriage was also linked to pregnancy avoidance. These results suggest that women’s marital relationships may be related to their perceptions and decisions regarding pregnancy and childbirth. These findings are valuable as they address the lack of studies examining the predictors of pregnancy avoidance among non-pregnant married women. Given the declining birth rates at both the global and national levels, understanding these associations is crucial for both individual well-being and public health. Our study highlights the need to assess the psychological and relationship dynamics underlying the desire to avoid pregnancy at an early stage, as a critical step in determining married women’s future desire of pregnancy and preventing potential unintended pregnancies. In light of the results, recommendations for women’s health and reproductive services can be listed as follows. These recommendations may be particularly effective if implemented before marriage, during the early years of marriage, or prior to planning a child.

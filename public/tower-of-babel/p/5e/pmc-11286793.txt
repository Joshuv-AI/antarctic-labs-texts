Study on crease recovery property of warp-knitted jacquard spacer shoe upper material
Authors: Qi Zhang, Jiani Tu, Jiashu Hao, Shiyu Peng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractShoe upper materials are often subjected to repeated creasing in use, which affects their shape retention and aesthetic. The crease recovery property of fabrics is an important property that affects its serviceability. Six variants of warp-knitted jacquard spacer fabrics with different jacquard layer structures were selected and the crease recovery angles on six orientation angles were tested for 30 cycles. The relationship between different orientation angles and crease recovery angles was studied and the comprehensive index for evaluating the crease recovery property of fabric was extracted by the grey relation analysis. The effect of the jacquard layer structure and the number of cycles on crease recovery property was analyzed with MATLAB. The results indicated that the crease recovery angles on different orientation angles were significantly different, and the comprehensive index could evaluate the crease recovery property more reasonably. The influence of the 3-needle underlap and the chain underlap on the crease recovery property was the most significant, and the influence of the 3-needle underlap was greater than that of the chain underlap. Base on solid structure, reducing the proportion of mesh structure or adding semi-transparent structure could improve the crease recovery property. The change rule of crease recovery angles with the number of cycles could be elucidated by the exponential function.Subject terms: Theory and computation, Statistics

Introduction

Shape retention of fabrics usually refers to the ability of fabrics to maintain their original appearance characteristics during use and to return to their original shape after being deformed by external forces1. The narrow sense of shape retention refers to the crease or wrinkle recovery property of the fabric, that is, the recovery degree of the fabric after creasing under the action of external forces such as rubbing, squeezing, and bending2. Crease recovery is an important factor affecting the wear performance of fabrics, which directly affects the appearance style and use value of textile products.

At present, numerous studies have been carried out on the wrinkle recovery of woven fabrics. Zaouali et al.3 studied the crease recovery behavior of woven fabrics by applying the laws of energy to the rheological Burger’s model. They found that the lower the energy dissipation, the greater the ability of the fabric to recover to its initial state, and the smaller the permanent deformation through experiments and energy dissipation model calculations. They simulated real usage scenarios by repeatedly conducting crease tests and found that the increase in fatigue cycles after repeated creasing resulted in an increase in residual deformation and permanent deformation, due to the increased cohesion between the filaments. Abrishami et al.4 analyzed the influence of fabric orientation and structure on its bending rigidity and crease recovery angle and developed a mathematical model to characterize the relationship between crease recovery and bending properties. The results indicate that the bending rigidity of the fabric can be represented by a sinusoidal function of sample orientation towards warp direction, and the nonlinear relationship between the bending rigidity of the fabric in different directions and the crease recovery can be represented by an exponential function. Wang and Gao5 contended that relying solely on a single wrinkle recovery angle to assess the wrinkle recovery property of fabric presents limitations. They proposed a comprehensive evaluation method to evaluate the crease recovery performance of fabrics. This method involves fitting a power function to the data of the crease recovery angle over time and obtaining a comprehensive evaluation index for the crease recovery of fabrics based on the coefficients of the power function equations from four different folding methods. But if the change trends of crease recovery angle does not follow a power function, the proposed evaluation should not be applicable to such fabrics. Mozaffari et al.6 evaluated the effects of repeated creasing processes and washing on the dynamic crease recovery performance of fabrics by laser triangulation of crease recovery angle and recovery speed, and found that increasing the number of creasing cycles and the soaking and drying time of fabrics would reduce their crease recovery ability.

In recent years, the continuous development of sole and insole material technology has led to a significant improvement in the resilience performance of sports shoes. Cardoso et al.7 designed a sole with innovative cushioning system, and by simulating the force exerted on the sole by the human walk, the compression and impact properties of three rubber blends were compared. It was ultimately found that the material with a better commitment between the damping and resilience properties is a natural rubber polymer-based mixture. Debnath et al.8,9 studied the compression creep property of needle-punched jute-polypropylene blended nonwoven fabric with different fabric weight, needling density, and needling density, and blend proportion of jute and polypropylene fibre blend. In addition, the thickness and compression characteristics of the fabric under dry and wet conditions were compared. They obtained the process parameters that can achieve the best compression performance. This provides new ideas for the innovation and sustainable development of insole materials. The performance improvement of sole and insole materials has made good progress, meanwhile, there is a need to correspondingly improve the performance of shoe upper materials. The fashion industry has seen the emergence of numerous new shoe upper materials catering to diverse needs. Debnath10,11 delved into a total value chain of flax fiber, from its extraction to transformation into fashion products. Currently, synthetic fibers dominate the majority of the fashion market, while flax fiber not only possess a high and rapid moisture absorption capacity and excellent dyeing properties, but also hold significant potential for sustainable development.

Warp-knitted jacquard spacer materials, which exhibit excellent air permeability and moisture permeability, as well as a unique jacquard effect, have been widely used in the sports shoes market. However, as an emerging product, its performance research system is not yet perfect. Research on the properties of warp-knitted fabrics has primarily focused on compression and tensile properties. Armakan et al.12 studied the compression properties of warp-knitted spacer fabrics and found that the material, structure, inclination and quantity of spacer yarns are important parameters affecting the compression properties of spacer fabrics. As the location angle of the spacer yarn and the amount of spacer yarn per unit fabric area decrease, the compression resistance of the spacer fabric decreases. Vassiliadis et al.13 built a mathematical model for the compression process of warp-knitted spacer fabric and used the finite element method to predict its compression property. Otaghsara et al.14 studied the effects of fabric structure, density and cyclic tensile amplitude on the tensile fatigue properties of double needle-bar warp-knitted fabrics. The fabric structure was divided into two aspects: the space available for yarn movement in the fabric structure, and the length of the underlap. The fracture strain of the fabric decreased with the increase of the space available for yarn movement and the length of the underlap, while the cyclic stable stress increased by increasing the amplitude of extension or by increasing the length of the underlap. However, cpc was helpful in improving tenacity of fabrics and has no significant effect on its tensile fatigue performance. Ghorbani et al.15 established a structural model of warp-knitted spacer fabric based on Castigliano’s theorem and bending theory of a curved bar, and analyzed the effects of structural parameters such as fabric thickness, mesh size and relative position of the outer mesh on its tensile properties. The smaller the thickness of the fabric, the higher the modulus, which was caused by the instability of the spacer yarn. The smaller the mesh size of the fabric, the more yarns it could withstand tension and its tensile strength became higher. But the position of meshes had no statistically significant effect on the tensile property of the warp-knitted spacer fabric. Zadekhast et al.16 produced five kinds of double needle-bar warp-knitted fabrics with different structures and conducted creep tests. The effects of fabric structure and loading direction on the creep properties of warp-knitted fabrics were analyzed in terms of the length of the underlaps, the angle of underlap and stretch direction and friction between yarns. By increasing the length of underlaps in the back-needle bar, the creep of the fabric in the course direction decreased, and the creep in the wale direction increased. Reverse Locknit exhibited the most similar creep properties in both course and wale directions, and in other structures, the creep property of fabrics exhibited significant differences in the course and wale directions.

However, the crease recovery of warp-knitted fabrics has received scant attention, particularly in specific product applications. Moreover, there has been a dearth of research on the crease recovery of warp-knitted shoe upper materials. Generally speaking, factories usually use polyester yarn with good crease resistance to produce shoe uppers. However, in addition to yarn materials, it is also necessary to find new factors to improve the crease recovery of shoe uppers. The air permeability area of the shoe upper corresponds to the part between the toes, as shown in Fig. 1a,b, which is often designed with mesh structure to ensure comfort, as shown in Fig. 1c,d. However, when the human walks or exercises, this area is inevitably subject to repeated folding, so it is usually the area most prone to problems such as crease and collapse, as shown in Fig. 1e, which is very prone to getting dirty and seriously affects the shape retention and aesthetics of the shoe upper. Therefore, it is of great practical value to understand the crease recovery characteristics and influencing factors of warp-knitted jacquard spacer shoe upper material under cyclic folding. In addition, fabrics are typical anisotropic materials, fabrics exhibit varying degrees of crease recovery properties when subjected to external forces in different directions during use. Prior to the analysis of the influencing factors, it is necessary to exclude the interference of fabric anisotropy first. Therefore, in addition to the course and wale directions of the fabric, the crease recovery angle should be evaluated in greater depth from multiple orientation angles.

The aim of this research is to fill the literature gap in the field of crease recovery property of warp-knitted fabrics by comprehensively evaluating the crease recovery property of warp-knitted jacquard spacer fabrics in various orientation angles based on the usage scenarios of shoe upper materials and examining the interrelationships and specific impacts of the jacquard structure parameters on the crease recovery property. Furthermore, the analysis and prediction of the crease recovery property changes of fabrics after cyclic creasing hope to provide reference for predicting their lifespan as fashion products and improving shape retention.

Materials and methods

Materials

In this research, six variants of warp-knitted jacquard spacer fabrics, which are commonly used in shoe upper materials, were selected as the test samples. All the samples were knitted on the double-needle bar warp-knitted machine with gauge E22 (22 needles per inch), and it has one jacquard comb configured on the back needle bar. The samples consisted of different jacquard structures, and their spacer and backing layers were the same to make the test comparable. The jacquard layer and backing layer of the samples were designed with polyester fully drawn yarn (FDY) and was linked by the spacer layer made of polyester monofilament. Crease recovery angle test principle

During use or washing, fabrics subjected to external forces such as rubbing, extrusion and others, will inevitably lead to the formation of creases. Once the external forces have been removed, creases will gradually recover under the action of internal stress, that is, the crease recovery angle begins to increase. The crease recovery angle increases with the increase of recovery time. In this process, in addition to the fabric’s intrinsic elastic recovery force, the fabric is also affected by the friction resistance generated by the relative slip between yarns and yarns, and its direction is opposite to the direction of the elastic recovery force. When the elastic recovery force and friction resistance are in equilibrium, the fabric achieves the best recovery effect, and the fold angle of the fabric is the crease recovery angle. The larger the crease recovery angle, the better the crease recovery property of the fabric.

Methods

Crease recovery angle test

The test was conducted on the NF8541 automatic fabric crease elastometer. In order to study the relationship between fabric anisotropy and crease recovery property, the samples were cut by rotating counterclockwise in different orientation angles including 0°, 30°, 60°, 90°, 120° and 150° along the wale direction, the specific cutting dimensions of the sample were shown in Fig. 3. It should be noted that 0° and 90° denote the wale and course directions, and there are six orientation angles in total. The samples taken in six orientation angles (taking S3 as an example) were shown in Fig. 4. According to the vertical method in standard GB/T3819-199717, five samples were cut from each orientation angle of each fabric for the crease recovery test, and the obtained results were averaged.

Specific dimensions of the sample.

The samples taken in six orientation angles (S3): (a) OA = 0°; (b) OA = 30°; (c) OA = 60°; (d) OA = 90°; (e) OA = 120°; (f) OA = 150°.

The fixed wing of the sample was clamped into the sample clip and made the crease line mark on the sample clip aligned with the crease line of the sample. Then folded the sample in half along the crease line, placed the transparent pressure plate, and applied the pressure hammer with a pressure load of 10 N. Released the pressure after 5 min, and the sample’s return wing recovered freely. The laser goniometric device read the angle between the two wings, which is the crease recovery angle. The immediate-elastic recovery angle was measured when pressure was released for 15 s, and the delay-elastic recovery angle was measured when the pressure was released for 5 min. After completing the delay recovery, applied pressure to each sample and allowed for free recovery again after reaching the specified time. The process of folding—pressurizing—free recovery was regarded as a cycle, and the cycle was repeated 30 times on the same sample18. Since the spacer layer and the backer layer of each sample were the same, and the jacquard layer was usually creased as the outer surface of the shoe upper materials in real-world applications, only the jacquard layer was tested for crease on the front side. In actual operation, the two sides of the sample’s return wing were prone to torsional deformation or crimping, which could result in reading errors or unrecognition. Therefore, the center line of the straight part of the image root, which was parallel to the surface of the fixed wing, is taken as the reference for reading. The Screen Protractor was used to retest the recovery angle and the error between the results of the laser goniometric device and the software measurement was ± 2°.

Statistical analysis

In this research, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to test whether there was a significant difference in the crease recovery angle of fabrics with different orientation angles.

Grey relation analysis is a systematic analysis method, which is mainly used to study the influence between interacting factors and to measure the degree of correlation between different factors according to the similarity of the development trend of each factor. The grey relation analysis method was employed to further ascertain the effect weights of different orientation angles on the crease recovery angle, and to derive comprehensive evaluation indexes for assessing the crease recovery properties of fabrics.

Stepwise regression analysis is a commonly used regression analysis method. It introduced variables into the model step by step. After each explanatory variable was introduced, the F-test was performed, and the t-test was then performed on the selected explanatory variables one by one. When the originally introduced explanatory variable became no longer significant due to the introduction of later explanatory variables, it would be deleted to ensure that only significant variables were included in the regression equation before each new variable was introduced. It was an iterative process until there were no significant explanatory variables be included in the regression equation and no insignificant explanatory variables were removed from the regression equation. At this point, it could be ensured that the final resulting set of explanatory variables was optimal. Its essence was to solve multiple collinearity problems and establish the optimal multiple linear regression equation. Determined the effect of the jacquard layer loop structures on crease recovery property through stepwise regression analysis and the effect of the number of cycles on crease recovery property through nonlinear regression analysis. Mathematical models were separately established to express the relationship between them.

All statistical analyses in this research were conducted using MATLAB R2023b software at a significance level of 0.05 (p-value = 0.05).

Results and discussion

Relationship between orientation angle and crease recovery angle

Exploratory analysis

The correlation analysis of six samples’ immediate-elastic recovery angle and delay-elastic recovery angle under six orientation angles was conducted. The obtained Pearson correlation coefficient was 0.994, indicating a highly positive correlation between the immediate-elastic recovery angle and delay-elastic recovery angle of each sample. The p-value was less than 0.05, indicating that the result had significant statistical significance. Therefore, the laws of the immediate-elastic recovery angle and the delay-elastic recovery angle of different samples under different orientation angles were basically the same, indicating that the fabric with optimal immediate-elastic recovery performance also exhibited optimal delay-elastic recovery performance. So in subsequent research, the immediate-elastic recovery angle could be regarded as the crease recovery angle of the fabric for analysis.

The crease recovery angles of six samples under the same orientation angle were taken as a group of variables. The test data of six groups of variables were then analyzed the variance by MATLAB statistical toolbox. The samples were required to meet the basic assumptions of ANOVA, which included all samples being from normal populations and these normal populations having the same variance. Therefore, the normality test and homogeneity of variance test should be performed before ANOVA. In the normality test, the original hypothesis was that the crease recovery angles of the fabric under six orientation angles obey the normal distribution, while the alternative hypothesis was that they do not obey the normal distribution. The p-values of each variable group were 0.5000, 0.5000, 0.5000, 0.1125, 0.2538 and 0.1457, which were all greater than 0.05, indicating that the original hypothesis was accepted at the significance level of 0.05. In the homogeneity of variance test, the original hypothesis was that the crease recovery angles of the fabric under six orientation angles obey the normal distribution with the same variance, while the alternative hypothesis was that the crease recovery angles obey the normal distribution with different variances. The p-value of the test was 0.315, which was greater than 0.05, indicating that the original hypothesis was accepted at the significance level of 0.05. All variable groups satisfied the basic assumptions of ANOVA.

The One-way ANOVA results were shown in Table 4. The original hypothesis was that there was no significant difference, while the alternative hypothesis was that there was significant difference. The p-value was 0.014, which was less than 0.05, indicating that the original hypothesis was rejected. It showed that there were significant differences between the crease recovery angles of the fabric under different orientation angles.

Effect of jacquard layer structure on crease recovery property

The comparison of the crease recovery angles of six variants of fabrics was shown in Fig. 6, it can be seen that the overall crease recovery property of these samples was ranked from good to poor as follows: S1 > S5 > S3 > S4 > S6 > S2. Actually, the effect of fabric structure on crease recovery property depended on two aspects: one was the ability of the fabric to resist deformation, that was, the stability of its internal structure, and the other one was the ability of the fabric to recover to its initial state after deformation.

Comparison of crease recovery angles of six variants of fabrics.

Effect of structure type on crease recovery property

The solid structure had high density and tightness, with more interlacing points between yarns, and a relatively stable internal structure. This structure helped to enhance the crease recovery property of the fabric, as more interlacing points meant a greater elastic recovery force. However, excessive tightness may also lead to an increase in the frictional resistance between yarns, which would affect the elastic recovery after cyclic creasing, which would be reflected in “Methods” section.

In contrast to the solid structure, the semi-transparent structure had lower density and tightness, with fewer interlacing points between yarns, and the internal structure was not as stable as the solid structure. This structure resulted in a relatively weak crease recovery property of the fabric, as the elastic recovery force was relatively small. At the same time, lower frictional resistance may benefit the recovery after cyclic creasing. However, the effect of the semi-transparent structure on crease recovery property was relatively complex, depending on its proportion in the combination and its interaction with other structures. At an appropriate ratio (S5), the semi-transparent structure may not have a significant negative effect on crease recovery.

The mesh structure had a high porosity and relatively fewest interlacing points between yarns. This structure caused the fabric to deform easily when subjected to external forces, and it was not easy to fully recover after deformation. However, the special structure of mesh structure also benefited certain specific properties of fabrics, such as air permeability and comfort, so a certain proportion of mesh structure was often added to shoe upper materials.

Effect of structure proportion on crease recovery property

The combination of solid structure and semi-transparent structure (S5) allowed the fabric to maintain a high elasticity while having better air permeability and comfort, and its crease recovery property was between the fabrics full of solid structure (S1) and full of semi-transparent structure (S6).

The combination of solid structure and mesh structure (S2, S3, S4) allowed the fabric to maintain good elasticity while having a certain degree of air permeability. The presence of mesh structure could reduce the crease recovery property of the fabric in total, but as the proportion of solid structure increased, the crease recovery property tended to increase. This was because solid structure can provide more interlacing points of yarns and elastic recovery force, indicating that the proportion of solid structure was the main factor determining the crease recovery property of fabrics.

Compared to the above two combinations, the mechanical properties of the combination of semi-transparent structure and mesh structure could not meet the requirements of shoe upper materials, so they were not commonly used in shoe upper materials.

The connections between wales are mainly made by underlaps. In the jacquard layer structure, the different proportion of solid/semi-transparent/mesh structure will directly affect the number and length of yarns under stress. The underlap includes three types: 3-needle underlap, 2-needle underlap, and chain underlap. The underlap of the fabric can be stretched under the action of external force and quickly return to the original state after the disappearance of external force. As the length and number of underlaps increase, the degree of internal connection within the fabric increases. When a force is applied, the existing space for yarn movement becomes smaller, while the internal stress of the fabric becomes stronger. This results in a relatively more stable loop structure and better elastic recovery property of the fabric. At the same time, the underlaps can disperse the external force and reduce the stress concentration of the fabric when it is subjected to external force. When the underlaps are longer and more numerous, they can better disperse the external force and reduce the deformation and creasing, so that the fabric can recover quickly after being creased. The 3-needle underlap spans 3 needle spaces, with more contact points with the main trunk of the loop, resulting in more compression yarn segments when creasing, as well as greater friction between yarns. This ultimately leads to a reduction in the sliding and transfer capabilities of the fabric. When the deformation of the fabric is small, the deformed part can recover more rapidly after the pressure is removed. The 2-needle underlap exhibits slightly reduced crease recovery property in comparison to the 3-needle underlap. The direction of the chain underlap is the same as that of the wale direction, which limits the wale extension of the fabric and contributes to enhanced the structural stability on the wale direction. However, there is no underlap connecting the adjacent wales of the chain structure, and the internal connection degree is small, which leads to a reduction in the overall elastic recovery property of the fabric and it is more difficult to recover from the deformation generated after creasing. Therefore, the higher the number of chain underlaps, the poorer the fabric's crease recovery property.

S1 was full of solid structure with the largest number of 3-needle underlaps in this research (Table 6), as was explained in previous work, it had the best crease recovery property. S2 had a half of 3-needle underlaps, a half of a 2-needle underlaps and a half of chain underlaps in each jacquard notation gird (Table 6). Since it has both 3-needle and chain underlaps, although the 3-needle underlap had a positive effect on its crease recovery, however, a negative effect of the chain underlaps was also evident. The combination of the two factors may lead to the relatively poor crease recovery property of S2. S3, S4 and S5 had the same number of 3-needle underlaps. S5 also had 2-needle underlaps (Table 6), the increase in the number of underlaps meant an improvement in crease recovery, so the crease recovery property of S5 was relatively better than that of S3 and S4. S3 and S4 had only 3-needle underlaps (Table 6), but the S3’s mesh size was smaller due to the different arrangement of the mesh structure. The smaller mesh size led to a tighter fabric structure, this meant that its structure was more stable and less prone to deformation, which would also improve its crease recovery property, so the crease recovery of S3 was slightly better than that of S4. S6 was full of semi-transparent structure with no 3-needle underlaps (Table 6), but each jacquard notation gird had two 2-needle underlaps. While the positive effect of 2-needle underlaps on crease recovery was not significant, S6’s crease recovery property slightly better than S2 because it did not have the negative effect of chain underlaps.

It can be concluded that fabrics with a higher proportion of solid structure can achieve better crease recovery property, followed by the 50/50 combination of solid and semi-transparent structure. On the basis of solid structure, the crease recovery property decreases with the increase of a certain proportion of mesh structure. The crease recovery property of semi-transparent structure is better than the 50/50 combination of solid and mesh structure, but worse than the 75/25 combination of solid and mesh structure. This is due to the difference in the number and length of yarns under stress in the loops between different structures.

Effect of number of cycles on crease recovery property

The change of the crease recovery angle of each sample after 30 cycles of repeated creasing was shown in Fig. 7. It could be seen that the trend of the crease recovery angle of the samples with the increase of the number of cycles was decreasing first and then gradually flattening. In the first 10 cycles, the decline was relatively fast, which was due to the fabrics had produced irrecoverable plastic deformation during the process of repeated creasing, and the elastic recovery ability was getting weaker. After repeated creasing, the crease recovery property of each sample exhibited different degrees of decline. The most obvious decline was observed in S2, where the decline in the crease reversibility angle before and after the cycle reached 17.35%. Next, the decline rates from high to low were S6 (12.55%), S4 (11.04%), S3 (8.86%) and S5 (5.73%) respectively, and the change of S1 was the least obvious with a decline rate of 3.17%. It could be seen that the fabric with the jacquard layer of full solid structure exhibited the greatest stability in terms of crease recovery property. The second most stable structure was the combination of solid and semi-transparent structures. On the basis of solid structure, increasing the proportion of the mesh structure could reduce the crease recovery property of the fabric. The fabric with the jacquard layer of full semi-transparent structure exhibited greater stability in terms of crease recovery property than that with the combination of half solid structure and half mesh structure, although it was less stable than the 75/25 combination of solid structure and mesh structure. This trend was similar to the comparison of single crease recovery angles of the six variants mentioned in the previous part, also due to the stability of the fabric structure and ability to recover from deformation.

The proportion of mesh structure should be appropriately reduced or semi-transparent structure should be added in the parts prone to crease to improve the stability of crease recovery property, but the full semi-transparent structure should be used with caution. Conclusions

In this paper, the warp-knitted jacquard spacer fabrics with different jacquard layer structures were taken as the research object. The crease recovery angles of the fabrics were measured for 30 cycles of creasing in six orientation angles, and the relationship between the orientation angle, jacquard layer structure and the number of cyclic creasing and crease recovery property was analyzed. The analysis results were all statistically significant at the significance level of 0.05.

There were significant differences in the crease recovery of fabrics on the different orientation angles, and the comprehensive index proposed in this research could evaluate the crease recovery property of fabrics more reasonably.

The proportion of solid structure was the main factor determining the crease recovery property of fabrics since it has the largest number of 3-needle underlaps in its loop structure. In addition to the fabric full of solid structure, the 50/50 combination of solid and semi-transparent structure performed the best crease recovery property and stability, indicating that reducing the proportion of mesh structure or adding semi-transparent structure can improve the crease recovery property. The linear equation established by stepwise regression analysis proved that the effect of 3-needle underlaps and the chain underlaps was the most significant, and the effect of 3-needle underlaps was greater than that of chain underlaps.

Authors’ Reply to Wang et al., “Comment on ‘Efficacy and Safety of Brodalumab, an Anti‑interleukin‑17 Receptor A Monoclonal Antibody, for Palmoplantar Pustulosis: 16‑Week Results of a Randomized Clinical Trial’”
Authors: Yukari Okubo, Satomi Kobayashi, Masamoto Murakami, Shigetoshi Sano, Natsuko Kikuta, Yoshiumi Ouchi, Tadashi Terui
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We extend our gratitude to Wang et al. [1] for their interest in our recently published original article [2]. The aim of our randomized clinical trial (RCT) was to assess the efficacy and safety of brodalumab, an anti‑interleukin (IL)‑17 receptor A monoclonal antibody, in Japanese patients with palmoplantar pustulosis (PPP) having moderate or severe pustules/vesicles. The primary efficacy endpoint was met; the change from baseline in PPP Area Severity Index (PPPASI) total score at week 16 was significantly higher in the brodalumab group than the placebo group. Infection was the dominant event among treatment-emergent adverse events (AEs).

We agree with the comment from Wang et al. that the frequency of AEs was high in the brodalumab group in our trial, though the AEs experienced by the majority of the patients were grade 1 or 2. The high incidence of AEs in the present trial may be attributable to the differences in the barrier function and pathogenesis of psoriasis and PPP. Indeed, focal infections are known to be associated with development and worsening of PPP [3], and there was a possibility that some patients with subclinical focal infection became symptomatic after trial initiation. Moreover, the limited use of concomitant medications (including antibiotics), to exclude their possible impact on the efficacy outcomes may have affected the emergence of AEs. Notably, the infections found in this trial were within the current risk profile of brodalumab [2].

Inadequate efficacy and AEs were the most common reasons for discontinuations in the brodalumab group. However, we do not agree with the comment that our efficacy results were possibly biased due to the discontinuations. We would like to direct the readers to our results in Online Resource 4, wherein the analyses were performed using the nonresponder imputations and baseline observation carried forward approaches; these results were slightly attenuated but consistent with the results of our main analyses, confirming the efficacy of brodalumab.

Currently, the monoclonal antibody options for treating intractable PPP are limited [4, 5]. In view of our encouraging results, brodalumab should be considered as another important treatment option for patients with PPP who do not respond to other biologics.

Wang et al. provide the evidence from a secukinumab trial [6] to corroborate that other IL-17 inhibitors have a high rate of AEs in the treatment of PPP. However, the cited trial includes patients with palmoplantar pustular psoriasis (PPPP) [6], while our trial included only PPP patients, excluding those with psoriasis [2]. As PPP and PPPP are different entities with separate disease definitions [7], the evidence of the secukinumab trial cannot be extrapolated for the treatment of PPP.

As pointed out by Wang et al., IL-17 inhibitors have been implicated in paradoxical cutaneous manifestations [8]. Paradoxical reactions are mostly known to occur with tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α inhibitors, but they have also been reported to occur with IL-17 or IL-23 pathway inhibitors. This phenomenon is known not only in PPP but also in psoriasis [9].

Although there is a possibility of paradoxical reactions, guselkumab [5] and risankizumab [4], the therapeutic agents targeting the IL-23–IL-17 axis, have already been approved for use in Japan for patients with PPP on the basis of the improvement in PPPASI scores. Bimekizumab, a selective IL-17 inhibitor, led to improvement in patients with PPP, albeit in a case series, suggesting its potential use for treating PPP [10]. Thus, the available evidence supports the role of IL-17 pathway inhibitors against PPP.

In our trial, AEs such as eczema and rash occurred in more than 5% of patients, with aggravation of PPP observed in 3/126 patients [2]. Moreover, as PPP demonstrates diverse presentation and is associated with focal infections [3], the possibility that some patients may have primary ineffectiveness or worsening skin symptoms with treatment cannot be ruled out. The relatively small sample size of 126 precluded further analyses to assess the patient and disease characteristics of nonresponders or those showing PPP exacerbations. Future large-scale clinical studies are needed to clarify these characteristics associated with better versus worse outcomes on administration of IL-17 pathway inhibitors.

As Wang et al. mentioned, Hernandez-Cordero et al. and McCluskey et al. have pointed to the participation of Th2 in PPP [11, 12]. However, the exact role and extent of Th2 involvement in the pathogenesis of PPP remains to be elucidated. However, the IL-23/Th17 axis is demonstrated to be at least partly responsible for the pathogenesis of PPP. Previous studies reported a marked upregulation of mRNAs associated with IL-17-related cytokines in PPP lesions compared with healthy controls or pompholyx lesional skin [13–15]. Immunohistochemical analysis further showed a significant accumulation of IL-17-positive cells within PPP lesional skin when compared with the healthy controls or pompholyx lesions [15, 16]. Additionally, cytokine assays using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) demonstrated a significant elevation in serum levels of IL-17 in patients with PPP relative to healthy controls [13]. Furthermore, the IL-17 and IL-23 p19-targeted therapies have been shown to be effective in RCTs [2, 5].

The pathogenesis of chronic cutaneous diseases often involves multiple pathways. For example, in atopic dermatitis, both Th2 and Th17 are known to be involved [17]. Many aspects of the disease mechanism of PPP are not yet completely understood, and it is possible that the diversity of the pathophysiology could be manifested as differences in treatment responses, to both focal infection treatment and drug therapy. Future studies, including RCTs, are needed to elucidate the disease mechanism and determine the optimal treatment for each patient.

On the basis of current evidence and our results, we believe that the decision to administer brodalumab should be based on a balance of risk versus benefit as a part of personalized therapy.

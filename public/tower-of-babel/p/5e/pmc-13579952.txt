Utilisation of electronic health record systems in clinical nursing practice in Ghana: predictors, benefits, and challenges
Authors: Gloria Amati, Alberta Y. Lomotey, Collins Atta Poku, Augustine Yaw Assah, Abigail Kusi-Amponsah Diji, Victoria Bam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Electronic Health Record (EHR) system has been successfully utilised to ease the burden of the traditional paper-based recording system in healthcare. Health facilities in Ghana have been using EHRs in their service delivery over the past few years; however, the benefits and challenges of this innovation for nurses have not been assessed, even though nurses constitute a large proportion of health workers who utilise the system in their daily service delivery.AimThe study aimed to assess the utilisation of EHR in relation to the predictors, benefits and challenges from the perspectives of nurses in healthcare facilities in Kumasi.MethodsThe study adopted a cross-sectional approach and used a multi-stage sampling method to select 176 respondents. With a validated questionnaire adapted from Venkatesh et al. data were collected and analysed using descriptive and inferential statistics at the 0.5 significance level.ResultsFully functional EHR systems were in place at all centres, and nurses performed numerous nursing activities using them. Effort expectancy (β = 0.176), performance expectancy (β = 0.184), social influence (β = 0.185), and facilitating conditions (β = 0.189) significantly predicted the continuous use of EHR systems in hospitals. Nurses ranked the ease of retrieving patients’ past medical records (RII = 0.868) as the highest benefit of the EHR system. In contrast, poor internet connectivity was ranked the highest challenge in the utilisation of EHRs (Kendall ranking = 9.81).ConclusionIt is recommended that robust EHR systems, improved internet connectivity, adequate ICT infrastructure, and continuous staff training and supervision be implemented in all hospitals in Ghana.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-026-04932-1.

Introduction

Digitisation has had a profound impact on human life, from communication and entertainment to business and healthcare. Digitalisation in the form of Electronic Health Records (EHR), telemedicine, and wearable health devices has transformed the approach to healthcare, making it easier for healthcare professionals to diagnose and treat patients remotely [1, 2].

Electronic Health (EH), the utilisation of information and communication technology (ICT) in healthcare, has greatly impacted the provision of safe, evidence-based, quality and cost-effective healthcare [3]. EHR is a component of E-health, currently utilised by many healthcare providers in both high-resourced and low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), albeit at different levels. EHRs are vital tools for managing many aspects of healthcare information [4, 5] and represent one exciting way to incorporate technology into healthcare [6].

Digitisation of medical records began only in the past few years, as information technology, the internet, and mobile technology became more common and advanced. The traditional paper-based documentation in health care delivery has been gradually replaced by EHR systems, largely attributable to numerous challenges associated with the paper-based record system [7]. This has been aptly echoed by Chatterjee, Gerdes, and Martinez [8], who note the high cost of management, lost folders, illegible notes, errors, and illegitimate access to folders.

The adoption of EHR systems has steadily increased over the last 5 years, with a global increase [9]. Fifty per cent of the countries that have adopted the system are high-income countries (HICs) due to its manifold benefits, such as improved quality and patient safety (e.g., reduced medical errors), better-coordinated healthcare, and cost-effectiveness.

It has, however, been reported that many healthcare providers are hesitant to use EHRs [10, 11], and nurses are known to prefer the paper-based record system because they are less willing to utilise EHR systems and attend to patients simultaneously [12]. Though this challenge is not alien to Ghana, Preko and Boateng [1] aptly note the Ghanaian health sector’s eagerness to achieve the complete adoption of eHealth.

Over the past years, most hospitals in Ghana have been using the Hospital Administrative Management System (HAMS), an EHR system, in their service delivery [13]. However, anecdotal evidence indicates the difficulties of using EHR systems in Ghana’s healthcare facilities, especially in faith-based facilities. These difficulties include inadequate infrastructure, financial constraints, a lack of technical expertise, resistance to change among healthcare professionals, interoperability issues, security and privacy concerns regarding data, inadequate training and capacity-building, and maintenance and support challenges. Meanwhile, there has been insufficient research in Ghana to assess nurses’ perceptions of this innovation since its inception. It is important to understand the level of use, benefits, challenges, and predictors of nurses’ utilisation of EHRs to maximise their integration into the healthcare delivery system.

The findings from the study will therefore shed light on specific challenges that impede Ghana’s successful integration of EHR systems and offer insights into the underlying reasons for implementation gaps and inefficiencies. The study assessed the utilisation of EHRs in Ghana, their predictors, benefits, and challenges from the perspective of nurses.

Literature review

The 2019 four-year Global Digital Health Strategy of the WHO aims to enhance health for all by hastening the adoption of the appropriate digital health systems [14]. Hence, EHRs are vital to attaining better health goals. According to Karp et al. [15], EHR systems are designed to store data correctly and capture patient status over time. This ensures that the data is accurate and legible, and there will be no need to trace patients’ previous paper medical records. Given the searchable single-file characteristics of digital information systems, EHRs are more efficient for extracting medical data to scrutinise likely trends and long-term variations in a patient [16].

Due to the complexity and frequent interruptions in the healthcare working environment, a more efficient and uniform patient information management system is required. In the clinical environment, nurses are expected to process information efficiently to shape clinical decisions, interact with the healthcare staff, and direct effective patient care delivery [6]. Furthermore, nurses’ sources of information for clinical purposes are numerous and require efficient management to minimise errors. EHRs offer the potential to enhance care by providing quick, high-quality information from multiple sources to guide practice [17].

As emphasised by Odekunle et al. [18], countries in Sub-Saharan Africa (SSA) have experienced low levels of EHR adoption and implementation in their healthcare systems. Katurura and Cilliers [19] identified several factors that hinder the full implementation of EHRs in most African and SSA countries, including the lack of policy guidelines, standards, and legislation regarding EHR implementation, poor internet connectivity, insufficient staff training, high implementation costs, and political influence.

According to Venkatesh and Davis [20], performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and healthcare facilitating condition influence nurses’ utilisation behaviour of the EHR system. Performance Expectancy is the degree to which people think that utilising an EHR system will enable them to carry out their duties more successfully and effectively. Effort Expectancy also describes how users expect a technology to be easy to use and interact with. The term social influence describes how social norms, peer pressure, and the influence of coworkers, superiors, and other social networks shape people’s views and behaviours regarding technology adoption. A sense of social acceptance and approval of EHR system adoption among nurses can be fostered by a supportive company culture, positive social norms, and role modelling by influential peers.

Meanwhile, the level of perception that people have about the infrastructure, support, and resources needed to enable the use of technology in a healthcare context is known as the healthcare facilitating condition [20]. This encompasses elements such as access to sufficient EHR system training and education, technical support and troubleshooting, the required hardware and software infrastructure, and organisational policies and procedures that facilitate the use of EHR systems. It is these enabling conditions that nurses perceive within their healthcare organisation that impact their utilisation behaviour; the more resources and support they perceive to be available, the more eager and capable they are to use EHR systems [21].

In Ghana, the National eHealth Policy was initiated in July 2010 to streamline the regulatory framework for health data and information management, build a broader sector capacity for eHealth solutions, and accelerate the health sector’s transition to a paperless record and reporting system [22]. However, this policy has encountered some drawbacks, as many hospitals in Ghana are only partially electronic in their patient record management. Moreover, the policy implementation is still at a neophyte stage and disjointed, with no guidelines for data exchange between hospitals and medical staff [23].

EHRs have encountered low nationwide adoption. Nurses in Ghana still spend significant working hours on paperwork and practices that are not directly related to patient care [24]. Nurses, who constitute a large proportion of healthcare providers across settings, can drive innovation in healthcare. Furthermore, nurses who work in healthcare facilities that utilise paperless record systems mainly become conversant with the EHR system [25] and therefore play a significant role in its success.

Methods

Study design

This study used a multi-centre cross-sectional design from June to September 2021.

Study settings

The study was conducted at three facilities in Kumasi (Kwadaso Seventh-day Adventist (SDA), Hart SDA, and Breman SDA hospitals). Kumasi is a cosmopolitan community with diverse cultural and socio-economic activities, and it is strategically located in the middle belt of Ghana. The three facilities have a nursing workforce enrolment of 317 (Kwadaso SDA − 158, Hart SDA − 76, and Breman SDA − 83) [26]. EHR is fully integrated into the healthcare systems of these facilities; therefore, every staff member uses it and is eligible to participate in the study.

Population and sampling

The population constituted all nurses working with the three SDA hospitals in Kumasi (317 nurses). Only nurses who worked as permanent staff and had worked in the facilities for at least a year were included in the study. The study employed a multi-stage sampling technique. All three SDA hospitals in Kumasi were selected for the study. This approach was used because the hospital prioritised implementing EHR systems. Proportionate stratified sampling was used to assign respondents to each of the three facilities (94, 48, and 50 respondents to Kwadaso SDA Hospital, Hart SDA Hospital, and Breman SDA Hospital, respectively) based on the facilities’ nursing workforce strengths. A convenience sampling technique was used to select respondents from each stratum because respondents were easily accessible to the research team. This ensures that respondents have experience with EHR utilisation and can provide valuable insights into the challenges and predictors of usage. To address potential sources of bias in the study, the recruiting plan used multiple centres to collect a more varied sample that better reflects the intended population. Again, a standardised sample estimation formula was adopted to recruit larger samples to help counteract potential biases introduced by convenience sampling. Moreover, proportionate sampling was used to balance the sample across significant demographic characteristics [27].

Sample size estimation

The sample size was estimated from a finite population correction using Cochran’s formula (n₀ = Z2 p(1-p)/d2) for the efficient minimum sample size [28]. The calculation assumed a 95% confidence level (Z-score of 1.96), an estimated proportion of 0.5 (representing maximum variability, and a 5% margin of error. The required sample for the study was 174 respondents. To account for possible non-responses and errors in questionnaire responses, the sample size was increased by 10%. The total number of questionnaires distributed was therefore 192. The final sample size was allocated proportionally across the three study facilities based on staff population sizes. The achieved sample size was considered adequate for regression analysis based on recommended events-per-variable criteria. The study, however, yielded a response rate of 91.7% (n = 176).

Measures

Utilisation predictors of EHR

A validated questionnaire adapted from Venkatesh et al. [29], which includes four latent constructs, namely, performance expectancy (4 items), effort expectancy (4 items), social influence (4 items), and facilitating condition (4 items), was used to measure factors influencing the utilisation behaviour of nurses on EHR. The scale was measured on a seven-point Likert scale, ranging from strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (7). A mean score of >3.5 indicated greater utilisation of EHR systems.

Utilisation level, benefits and challenges of the EHR system

A researcher-developed questionnaire adapted from several previous studies [20, 29–31] was used to measure EHR system utilisation, benefits, and challenges in nursing practices. The scale underwent face and content validity through expert review (2 experts) to ensure clarity, relevance and adequate coverage of the study constructs. The scale was subsequently pre-tested using 30 respondents from Suntreso Government Hospital (nurses there have characteristics similar to those in the study sites). Minor revisions were made, including rewording of ambiguous items to improve clarity and consistency, before its use in the main study. Cronbach’s alpha after pre-testing of the scale was 0.85. A scale with a Cronbach’s alpha > 0.7 indicates high internal reliability, which is necessary to produce meaningful scores for use in practice and research [32].

Utilisation level

The sub-scale consisted of 11 items. These items were measured on a 7-point Likert scale of 1 - ‘never use’ to 7 - ‘always use’. A composite mean score of >3.5 indicated a satisfactory level of EHR use. This decision was based on the structure of the 7-point Likert response scale, with scores above 3.5 indicating responses trending toward the upper categories and therefore suggesting relatively satisfactory utilisation. A Cronbach’s alpha of 0.82 was estimated after pre-testing the scale.

Benefits of the EHR system

The benefits of EHR to nurses were measured using 11 items which included the process of clinical decision-making, the ease of retrieving patients’ past medical records, patient waiting time, the confidentiality of patient records, medication/prescription errors, patient’s appointment records, overall quality of care offered to patients, patient records management, interdepartmental communication and collaboration, and communication between nurses and patients. Items were measured on a seven-point Likert scale of strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (7). The closer the Relative Importance Index (RII) of a factor is to 1, the higher its perceived importance relative to others, and vice versa. The use of RII enables prioritisation of EHR benefits by transforming ordinal Likert responses into a normalised index, thereby supporting comparative interpretation across items. RII is a normalised measure used to rank items based on respondents’ ratings. It is calculated by summing the weighted responses for each item and dividing by the maximum possible score (RII = ΣW / (A × N), where ΣW is the sum of the weights assigned to each response (e.g., Likert scores), A is the highest possible weight (in this study, 11), and N is the total number of respondents. On the RII rating continuum, scores less than 0.5 were deemed weak in preference, scores equal to 0.5 were deemed moderate in preference, and scores greater than 0.5 were deemed stronger in preference [33]. The sub-scale yielded a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.79 after pre-testing.

Challenges with the usage of EHR

Sixteen (16) items were used to measure the challenges associated with EHR system utilisation among nurses. Items were measured on a 7-point Likert scale of strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (7). A mean score of >3.5 indicated agreement with the challenges of using EHRs. The respondents rated their level of agreement with 16 factors as challenges to the utilisation of the EHR system by nurses. The Cronbach’s alpha for the sub-scale was 0.91, indicating excellent internal consistency.

Data collection method

The sampling frame comprised all registered nurses employed at the three selected healthcare facilities during the study period, based on administrative staff records obtained from each hospital. Respondents in the study were employed at and worked exclusively within a single facility. After obtaining permission from the management of the various facilities, the researchers and assistants collected data at each study site. Eligible respondents were recruited through convenience sampling, and participation was voluntary following informed written consent after the study’s objectives were explained to them. Self-administered questionnaires were distributed to respondents and were completed within an average of 20 minutes.

Data analysis

Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 26 was used for statistical analysis. Descriptive and inferential analytical methods were employed. The utilisation of the EHR system in nursing practices was analysed using mean and standard deviation. In contrast, the benefits associated with the EHR system were examined using the Relative Importance Index (RII). The hierarchical linear regression method was employed to identify the predictors of nurses’ EHR system use behaviour. The regression models were constructed using variables selected a priori from the literature. Model 1 includes socio-demographic data, while Model 2 adds key predictors, allowing us to assess the incremental explanatory power of the additional variables. All regression assumptions were assessed and found to be adequately met. Thus, the model fit was assessed using standard diagnostic measures (R2 and adjusted R2), and the model assumptions were evaluated for linearity, homoscedasticity, normality of residuals, and absence of multicollinearity (assessed using variance inflation factors). Confounding and interaction effects were evaluated through adjusted analysis. Potential interaction effects were explored by including interaction terms between key variables (e.g., socio-demographic characteristics such as age), and these were retained when statistically significant. The challenges nurses face in utilising the EHR system were analysed using the mean item scores. All analyses were conducted at the 0.05 significance level.

Ethical consideration

Permission was obtained from the management of the selected facilities to conduct the study. Ethical approval was sought from the Committee on Human Research Publication and Ethics, KNUST (CHRPE/AP/046/21) before the start of data collection. Prospective respondents were asked to volunteer for the study after it was explained to them. Respondents signed a written consent form and were assured of their freedom to withdraw from the exercise at any point.

Predictors of EHR system continuous utilisation by nurses

Table 6 shows the detailed model of the predictors of the EHR system’s continuous utilisation by nurses. From model 1, the number of years nurses have used the EHR system significantly influences their continued utilisation of the system (β = 0.354, p = 0.001). In model 2, the performance expectancy (the degree to which individuals believe that using the EHR system will help them perform their job tasks more effectively and efficiently) significantly explained the EHR system’s continuous utilisation by nurses (β = 0.184, p < 0.001). The tested model 2 also showed that effort expectancy (the perceived ease of use and simplicity of interacting with a technology) significantly explained the EHR system’s continuous utilisation by nurses (β = 0.176, p = 0.021). Social influence, or colleagues’ influence, significantly explained the level of continuous utilisation of EHR systems by nurses (β = 0.185, p = 0.035). Thus, a significant unit increase in social influence, or the influence of a nurse’s colleagues, is associated with a 0.185-unit increase in nurses’ continuous utilisation of the EHR system. Discussion

The study sought to assess the level of utilisation, benefits, challenges, and predictors of continuous utilisation of EHR systems in clinical nursing practice. Performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions were positively and significantly associated with the EHR system’s continuous utilisation by nurses in hospitals where the study was conducted. Although Tubaishat’s [34] findings supported the outcome of this study, Alsyouf and Ishak [35] did not find a relationship between social influence and the continuous utilisation of EHR systems. Performance expectancy and facilitating conditions in organisations did not affect the continuous utilisation of technology (EHR system) as indicated by Kwateng, Atiemo, and Appiah [36] in Ghana and Utomo, Kurniasari, and Purnamaningsih [37] in Indonesia. This shows that factors predicting the continuous utilisation of EHR systems may differ and may not be sufficient driving factors.

High EHR system utilisation among nurses was reported in this study. This position is consistent with the findings of Raddaha [38] in AlMarzooqi, Moonesar, and AlQutob [39] in Dubai. This indicates that although the adoption rate of EHR in the Ghanaian health sector is slow, some hospitals have demonstrated high utilisation of EHR systems. Moreover, Acquah-Swanzy [40] adds that ICT utilisation in Ghanaian healthcare, particularly in the form of EHRs, lags behind that of other advanced countries. It further indicated the dominance of hybrid EHR and paper-based record systems in numerous medical facilities in Ghana.

An EHR system is perceived as highly beneficial to nurses, patients, and health facilities. This study found that respondents considered all items in the sub-scale to be beneficial, with RII scores greater than 0.50 for each item. In corroborating the current study, numerous studies have outlined the benefits of EHR in healthcare delivery. Pera, Kaur, and Rao [41], for instance, reported that EHRs facilitate the retrieval of patients’ past medical records and expedite timely decision-making by nurses, thereby providing nurses with greater control over patient record management. EHR system is reported to possess the ability to reduce patient waiting time, improve interdepartmental coordination and teamwork [42], strengthen the communication between nurses and patients [43], decrease medication errors, provide much control in patient records management, improve the confidentiality of patients records, improve the overall quality of care offered to patients and increase nurse productivity [44].

Furthermore, Ngerema [45] found that using bedside terminals and central station desktops reduced nurses’ average time spent documenting per shift. Despite the benefits, human factors that compromise patient information have been observed with EHR usage. Measures should therefore be put in place by healthcare managers to forestall these potentially identified risks.

This level of perceived importance could be associated with the high utilisation of the EHR system identified in this study. The nurses find EHRs reliable and efficient compared to paper-based documentation, though some challenges have been noted. The ease with which patients’ past medical records can be retrieved was the highest score, indicating that nurses value this feature above all others when it comes to using EHR systems in nursing practice, suggesting that nurses are appreciative of how quick and simple it is to obtain patient data, which can help them make well-informed decisions. Most hospitals in Ghana were previously paper-based but have migrated to electronic record systems over the past decade. The nurses have experienced the challenges of paper-based documentation as compared to electronic record systems. Thus, most nurses in Ghanaian hospitals that use EHRs rely heavily on the existing system in their daily care duties because of the benefits they perceive it offers [13].

While EHR system utilisation is immensely beneficial to nurses, patients, and health facilities, its use among nurses is disrupted by several obstacles. Among factors indicated to affect the EHR system utilisation, this current study found poor internet connectivity, slow network speed, and hardware issues as the most common challenges. The challenges identified in the study reflect the typical situation regarding ICT and internet connectivity in Ghana [46, 47]. These findings are supported by previous studies that reported that poor internet connectivity and technical issues frequently disrupt EHR system utilisation [18]. It is also consistent with previous studies that reported a lack of training on EHR systems [48], disruptive workflow, insufficient numbers of computer terminals in a clinical setting, poor nurse motivation to use the EHR system [49], cumbersome functionalities that impacted nurse efficiency, lack of interoperability, and hardware issues [50] as critical challenges of nurses in the utilisation of EHR system. In terms of training, the study by Mollart et al. [51] showed that most nurses had not received specific training, making it difficult for untrained nursing team members to utilise the EHR system [52]. Thus, the EHR system can be complex to use for nurses with minimal or no training in it.

The utilisation of the EHR system in nursing practice is associated with several factors. Nurses expecting improvement in their performance through EHR system utilisation are more likely to use the hospitals’ currently installed EHR systems. This finding is supported by Ahmed et al. [53], who reported a positive association between performance expectations and the utilisation of EHR systems in health facilities. Effort Expectancy, in the form of ease of use of EHR systems, is also reported to be positively associated with nurses’ utilisation of EHR systems in hospitals. Thus, nurses who perceived using the EHR system as easy were more likely to utilise it than their counterparts who perceived it as difficult [54]. This finding is supported by a study that also reported a positive linkage between effort expectancy and EHR system utilisation in healthcare facilities [55]. A possible explanation for this trend is the demographic profile of the current crop of nurses in Ghana’s hospitals, who are mostly Millennials with an average age of 30. This age is described as technologically savvy and regular users of technology and smartphones [56]. They therefore find it easy to use technology in their daily work, confirming that effort expectancy positively influences utilisation of EHR systems.

Social influence was also reported to be positively associated with nurses’ utilisation of EHR systems in health facilities. Thus, the influence of colleagues is deemed crucial to nurses’ level of utilisation of EHR systems. This finding is supported by a previous study that reports that peers’ and significant others’ expectations regarding the use of technology significantly influence the utilisation of electronic devices in healthcare delivery or practice [57].

Facilitating conditions in hospitals are also perceived to positively influence nurses’ utilisation of the EHR system in nursing practice. Thus, the individual’s perception of the availability of a supportive environment in the form of readily available resources is perceived to encourage EHR system utilisation in nursing practice. This finding is corroborated by previous studies that also reported a positive linkage between facilitating conditions and EHR system utilisation among nurses [58]. Thus, health facilities that provide training and support programs with robust IT equipment and infrastructure are likely to see higher levels of electronic device utilisation by healthcare workers. In the current study, nurses in the hospitals exhibited a positive correlation between facilitating conditions and EHR system utilisation, emphasising the availability of support and resources for EHR use in their work.

Limitation

This study was limited to a cross-sectional sample of nurses from three SDA hospitals and examined EHR utilisation at a single point in time; generalisation should be done cautiously. Second, data were collected from respondents in Kumasi; adding respondents from across the country may enhance the data’s reliability. Also, the cut-off used to classify the scale was based on pilot data analysis and expert consensus on the Likert-scale scores rather than an empirically validated threshold. The classification should therefore be interpreted cautiously. Future studies could therefore broaden the scope to include more healthcare facilities and also analyse the perspectives of other healthcare practitioners on EHRs.

Conclusion

The adoption of EHR systems at most health facilities has benefited health practitioners, patients, and the facilities. The utilisation of the system was perceived to improve nursing practices and the quality of patients’ health. The EHR system improves the ease of maintaining patients’ records and retrieving past medical records, provides greater control over patient record management, enhances the confidentiality of patients’ records, enhances interdepartmental communication and collaboration, and minimises medication or prescription errors. Notwithstanding the benefits of the EHR system for nursing practices, its use in hospitals has been hindered by several obstacles, including poor internet connectivity, hardware issues, slow network speeds, insufficient computer terminals in clinical settings, and computer illiteracy among nurses. Although nurses have a positive perception of the EHR system, it is disrupted by myriad factors that need to be addressed to increase its utilisation.

Implication for nursing practice

Current trends in healthcare technology imply that EHRs are the systems to adopt; hence, all healthcare providers, especially nurses, need to embrace them to facilitate and improve the delivery of care. The study has reiterated the importance of EHR use in nursing practice, citing numerous benefits. Easy access to patient information, proper documentation, patient safety and overall quality care are some benefits associated with the EHR system.

Moreover, facilitating conditions, such as continued support from nurse administrators and the purchase and implementation of appropriate hardware and software, should be put in place to encourage the use of EHR systems. To maximise the benefits of EHR systems, health facilities should provide the necessary resources, ensure staff motivation, implement continuous monitoring and evaluation, and provide periodic updates to EHR software. The study’s findings also necessitate nursing education on EHRs at all levels of mainstream education. This training would allow for the transmission of EHR skills and knowledge. This will serve as a launch pad for EHR in clinical nursing practice.

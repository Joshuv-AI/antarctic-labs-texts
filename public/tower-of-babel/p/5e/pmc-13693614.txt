Aligning clinical care with consumer priorities: an adult cystic fibrosis centre experience of co‐designing a model of care
Authors: Shanal Kumar, Vanessa Moore, Daniel Smith, Angela Matson
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCystic fibrosis (CF) is a multi‐system genetic condition, and CF modulator therapies have transformed health outcomes promising improved longevity. Our aim was to co‐design and implement a nested endocrine and metabolic model of care called CF Endocrine for adults with CF that aligned with consumer and healthcare provider priorities and addressed emerging service gaps in the post‐modulator era. Using mixed‐methods and implementation science methodologies, we designed and conducted surveys, audits and workshops to define service priorities and referral pathways and workflows. In total, 17% of adults attending our CF centre participated in the survey and reported lifestyle optimisation, weight management and diabetes prevention as leading consumer concerns. Four service arms were developed: (i) diabetes support, (ii) bone health, (iii) metabolic health and (iv) reproductive/sexual health. Providers showed strong alignment with consumer priorities and endorsed integration and feasibility of the new nested CF Endocrine service.

Cystic fibrosis (CF) is a genetic disease affecting the CF transmembrane regulator conductor (CFTR) protein. The CFTR chloride channel is widely expressed in the human body, and defects result in multi‐system disease manifestations. Recent widespread availability of CFTR modulator therapies, in particular Elexacaftor‐Tezacaftor‐Ivacaftor (ETI) and more recently Vanzacaftor/Tezacaftor/Deutivacaftor (Alyftrek), are transforming health outcomes for most people living with CF (pwCF). In Australia, the median survival for a person with CF has now reached 61 years. 1 With extended life expectancy, it can be anticipated that adults with CF will face emerging health challenges, including endocrine and metabolic complications. 2 Furthermore, CFTR modulator therapies have been associated with excessive weight gain, predominantly in the truncal region, which is known to be associated with an increased risk of type 2 diabetes, hypertension and dyslipidaemia and may predispose to accelerated cardiovascular disease. 3 As CFTR modulator therapies impact the clinical manifestation of CF, it is imperative that CF multidisciplinary teams (MDT) re‐appraise consumer priorities to ensure service provision aligns with clinical need. Guiding principles for continuing clinical care delivery for pwCF in the post‐modulator era have been described. 4 However, there are limited data on aligning consumer and healthcare provider concerns and priorities for clinical services re‐design.

Our aim was to co‐design and implement a holistic model of care (MOC) called CF Endocrine to address the evolving endocrine and metabolic health needs of pwCF. To do this, we first reviewed refreshed consumer research priorities 5 and contemporary Standards of CF Care. 6 We then used established implementation science frameworks specifically exploration, preparation, implementation, sustainment (EPIS) to guide development and reach, effectiveness, adoption, implementation and maintenance (RE‐AIM) to structure evaluation of the MOC. 7 , 8 Using this integrated approach, we systematically elicited and synthesised both consumer (pwCF) and healthcare provider (the CF MDT) priorities through questionnaires and workshops, with the explicit aim of identifying clinical needs and service gaps to inform CF Endocrine service design and implementation.

This prospective quality initiative (Project ID: 103775; EX/2023/MNHB/103775) utilised a mixed‐methods approach combining qualitative and quantitative methodologies with implementation science. This project was conducted at the Adult Cystic Fibrosis Centre (ACFC) at The Prince Charles Hospital, Queensland, the largest adult CF service provider in Australia. At the ACFC, the MDT comprises thoracic physicians, nurses, dietitians, physiotherapy, social work, psychology, pharmacy, research and administrative support. The expanded MOC comprising endocrinology and diabetes education was to be embedded within the pre‐existing CF MDT and named CF Endocrine. A pilot CF Endocrine MOC was operationalised in June 2023.

The methodology to conduct this priority and alignment initiative leveraged previous work. 9 All survey questions were developed using the consolidated framework for implementation research (CFIR) 10 to capture both inner and outer contextual factors impacting the MOC. All valid survey responses were included in the final analyses, with percentages calculated based on the total number of responses for each question.

All pwCF attending ACFC were invited to participate. The online consumer survey was anonymous and distributed via social media, email and flyers within our centre. The survey was open between 14 December 2023 and 6 March 2024.

Initial clinician engagement involved multiple presentations by endocrinology/diabetes education to the CF MDT and hospital executives. An online survey was emailed to the CF MDT to gather insights on scope and operationalisation aspects of CF Endocrine.

The initiative also comprised database‐driven audits to ascertain key clinical outcome measures (e.g. HbA1c, pregnancies, fracture and obesity prevalence). Outcomes from the end‐user survey were presented to the CF MDT during unit meetings and planning days. MDT members with a particular interest in CF extra‐pulmonary manifestations were encouraged to attend subsequent state/future workshops. These facilitated face‐to‐face workshops were conducted in March 2024 and consisted of at least one representative from each MDT discipline of thoracic medicine, dietetics, physiotherapy, pharmacy, nursing, research and social work/psychology. Each workshop lasted 60 min and involved framed discussions on what we should continue, change, cease and innovate with regard to CF Endocrine (see Supplementary Material S1). Facilitators ensured consensus points aligned with consumer needs, workforce capacity and operational scope. Following the workshops, follow‐up conversations with engaged end‐users and other key stakeholders were used to ascertain deeper insights for service co‐design. Visibility on endocrine and metabolic health was maximised within ACFC during this time using flyers, educational boards and presentations.

Lifestyle recommendations comprising nutrition and exercise were applicable to all service arms for CF Endocrine. Weight management was a key consumer priority, with over half of the respondents (69%) reporting weight gain with CFTR modulator therapies, with a mean self‐reported weight gain of 8.72 kg. Most respondents (68%) felt the weight gain had a mostly positive impact on their overall health. Of those who had gained weight, 31% reported feeling motivated to lose weight and almost a third had actively tried to facilitate weight loss through various methods in the prior year (see Supplementary Material S2).

A total of 24 healthcare providers from ACFC participated in the survey from various disciplines including medical, nursing, allied health and other (e.g. research). Healthcare provider priorities aligned with that of consumers on diabetes management integrating technology, addressing bone health for fracture prevention, reproductive and metabolic health optimisations (Table 2).

With regards to operational aspects of embedding CF Endocrine within the pre‐existing CF MDT, providers had high confidence in the process/workflows and that the service was adding value and had adequate capacity. They also agreed integration was highly achievable. Respondents highlighted referral processes needed refinement and that physical space (e.g. rooms, computers, desk space) was a potential barrier.

Discussion

We report on a process integrating qualitative and quantitative methodologies with implementation science to identify and align consumer and healthcare provider service priorities for endocrine and metabolic service delivery in adults with CF.

Improvements in health experienced by many pwCF commenced on CFTR modulator are resulting in reduced rates of pulmonary exacerbations requiring hospitalisations and a shift to a predominantly outpatient‐based hospital model of care. 13 As a result, the CF MDT needs to increasingly partner with community‐based services, specifically local general practitioners, for clinical care provisions. However, clarifying which aspects of clinical care should be provided outside specialised CF centres is crucial. Additionally, not all CF care can be de‐centralised given some pwCF do not demonstrate excellent clinical response on CFTR modulator therapies and some with rare mutations remain ineligible, highlighting the need to preserve the unique skill set of the CF MDT. We found the CF MDT identified the need to pivot and actively address endocrine and metabolic complications to promote healthy ageing in pwCF in the post‐modulator era.

Evidence suggests consumer‐provider alignment can lead to improved consumer engagement and optimisation of clinical outcomes with a positive impact on health policy, research and services. 14 Studies incorporating systematic processes to support alignment have been reported in oncology 15 and geriatric settings. 16 , 17 These activities not only impact local MOC but can also have far‐reaching implications for shaping health policy. 18 Such health service research requires rigour and systematic processes. The UK Medical Research council framework for design and evaluation of complex interventions has provided a popular approach and comprises four components. First, the context needs to be specified followed by understanding the problem prior to defining components of an optimal intervention. Following this, an exploratory trial assessing the acceptability, feasibility and efficacy of the piloted intervention is conducted. 19 Our experience has also extended beyond co‐design and into implementation and evaluation of the CF diabetes support services component of CF Endocrine 1 and screening practices at our centre. 11 Such processes that move end‐user involvement beyond the design and knowledge sharing phase into actionable interventions that can be integrated into MOC are urgently needed. Infrastructure and funding to support continual evaluation and iterative refinement can support a learning health system. 20

We report on our experiences of aligning clinical care services with consumer priorities with establishment of the CF Endocrine services at The Prince Charles Hospital in Queensland, Australia. Our work illustrates the use of a systematic approach to co‐design, implement and evaluate a dynamic MOC that will continue to be iteratively refined to meet unmet needs while simultaneously supporting holistic care for pwCF living in the post‐CFTR modulator era.

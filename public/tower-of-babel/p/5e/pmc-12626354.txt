Successful Treatment of Severe Pediatric Atopic Dermatitis with Upadacitinib
Authors: Lee Tyan Shin, Henrietta Albela, Deirdre Ooi, Kin Fon Leong
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

AD is a common, chronic inflammatory skin disease, in infants and children, appearing within 6 months in 45% of all cases, 60% within the age of 1 year and 89% within the first 5 years of life.[1] Systemic therapy is often needed for patients with severe AD, which includes immunomodulators such as cyclosporine, methotrexate or azathioprine.[2]

Janus kinase (JAK) signal transducers and activators of transcription (JAK-STAT) pathway has been found to play a critically important role in the development AD. Oral selective JAK 1 inhibitors, upadacitinib have emerged as a promising therapeutic class for atopic dermatitis.

We report a case of a 9-year-old boy with a history of severe AD since the age of three, who presented to us after failure of topical and multiple courses of short-term systemic corticosteroids. On examination, he had severe generalised and lichenified eczematous lesions [Figures 1 and 2], with an Eczema Area Severity Index (EASI) of 70, a Pruritus-Numeric Rating Scale (P-NRS) of 9/10. Parents were offered and counseled in detail on options of oral systemic therapies (including oral cyclosporin, methotrexate or azathioprine) or injection of monoclonal antibody, dupilumab. Because of the potential worrying serious side effects of these immunomodulators and the patient’s intense needle phobia, oral upadacitinib was offered instead as an off-label treatment option, to which parents consented.

We started him on oral upadacitinib 15 mg daily. His P-RNS has reduced to 5/10 from 9/10 in just 5 days after starting on upadacitinib and he was almost itch-free by week 12. Skin clearance (75% improvement in EASI) was observed as early as week 4. Baseline laboratory tests including tuberculosis tests were normal before starting treatment and remained normal at weeks 4 and 8 of treatment. Upadacitinib was well tolerated, without any clinical and laboratory adverse events.

Oral selective JAK 1 inhibitors have been shown to improve the severity and symptoms of AD. A randomized clinical trial investigating upadacitinib versus placebo in adolescents above 12 years of age and adults with moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis has shown that the patients who had achieved EASI-75 at week 16 were significantly higher in the upadacitinib 15 mg group (70%) than the placebo group (16%).[3] In term of safety, several reports have revealed no safety risks were observed through 52 weeks of observation while on upadacitinib.[45]

In our patient, upadacitinib showed rapid improvement in itch relief as early as 5 days after the initiation upadacitinib and achieved almost complete skin clearance and EASI-75 by week 4 [Figures 3-6].

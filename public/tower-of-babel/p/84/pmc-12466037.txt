Plant growth promoting endophytic fungus Aspergillus niger VN2 enhances growth, regulates oxidative stress and protects DNA damage in Vigna radiata under salt stress
Authors: Pooja Chauhan, Mandeep Singh, Avinash Sharma, Pooja Chadha, Amarjeet Kaur
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPlant microbe-interactions contribute in mitigating abiotic environmental stresses in plants. Salinity stress is one of major factor in reducing agricultural productivity. Recently, the use of endophytic fungi has proved as one of the approaches that can help plant growth under salt stress. Therefore, the objective of this study was to isolate halotolerant endophytic fungi and elevate their potential to mitigate salt stress in Vigna radiata.MethodologyEndophytic fungi were isolated from asymptomatic parts of Vachellia nilotica and screened for plant growth-promoting attributes such as IAA production, phosphate solubilization, siderophore production, and halotolerance. Based on growth-promoting characteristics and halotolerance, Aspergillus niger VN2 was selected and colonized in V. radiata. The colonized plants were exposed to different salt concentrations (150 mM, 200 mM, and 250 mM NaCl) to evaluate the effect of A. niger VN2 in mitigating salt stress. The effects of A. niger VN2 colonization on physiological, biochemical, and molecular parameters were assessed.ResultsIn the present study, an endophytic fungus Aspergillus niger VN2 isolated from Vachellia nilotica exhibited good plant growth promotion properties and halotolerance. A. niger VN2 produced IAA (148.32 ± 2.34 µg/ml IAA), solubilized phosphate, and produced hydroxamate type siderophore (72.66% SU). It also exhibited ACC deaminase ability (134.40 ± 5.45µmolα-ketobutyrate/h/mg protein), and could tolerate up to 15% NaCl. Colonization of A. niger VN2 in V. radiata enhanced salt tolerance, and resulted in increased root and shoot length, biomass, leaf number, chlorophyll content, relative water content, and protein content in salt stressed plants. DPPH scavenging and endogenous IAA levels also increased in treated plants. Oxidative stress parameters viz. proline, electrolyte leakage, and malondialdehyde, were found to decrease in VN2 colonized plants. Fluorescent microscopy studies revealed VN2 colonized plants showed increased cell survival and lowered glutathione and hydrogen peroxide under salt stress. Comet assay was used for determining the genoprotective effect of A. niger VN2. Colonization of A. niger VN2 reduced DNA tail length, % tail DNA, tail moment, and olive tail moment in plants exposed to salt stress. The difference in biochemical molecules viz. protein, carbohydrates, lipid, and nucleic acid in colonized and non-colonized plants under salt stress was revealed by FT-IR spectroscopy and validated by PCA, which showed that A. niger VN2 mitigated salt-induced changes, as colonized samples clustered closely under both conditions.ConclusionFrom the current study, it can be concluded that colonization of endophytic fungus A. niger VN2 promotes plant growth and can mitigate salt stress in V. radiata by regulating oxidative stress.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12866-025-04209-8.Keywords: Endophytic fungus, Aspergillus niger, Salt stress, Vigna radiata, DNA damage

Background

Endophytes are a group of microorganisms that thrive in symbiosis with plants and provide them with a variety of benefits, without showing any adverse symptoms [1]. The interaction between plant and endophyte shares a symbiotic relationship, where endophytes gain shelter and nutrients from the host, simultaneously promoting plant growth and enhancing stress tolerance [2–4]. In recent years, endophytic fungi have gained considerable attention for their ability to enhance plant growth by synthesizing phytohormones (auxins, gibberellins, and cytokinin, etc.) that greatly influence metabolism, reproduction, and overall growth of the plant [3, 4]. Endophytic fungi also have the ability to solubilize nutrients (potassium, calcium, phosphorus, and magnesium) and help in the uptake of iron through production of siderophores [4, 5]. Moreover, fungal endophytes help the plant to grow under various environmental stresses and facilitate a protective mechanism by producing secondary metabolites and phytohormones. Level of 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid (ACC), which is the precursor of stress hormone ethylene, is enhanced under salt stress [6, 7]. Endophytic fungi are capable of synthesizing ACC deaminase, which plays an essential role in the regulation of ethylene biosynthesis [8–10].

The ability of fungal endophytes to alleviate the adverse effects of abiotic stress and improve plant resistance is an emerging research area of agricultural applications. Salinity in the soil is one of the major threats to crop production, damaging many important crops around the world [11]. Around 20% of the agricultural land in the world has already been damaged by salt stress, resulting in huge economic losses, which are gradually escalating [12]. It is anticipated that by the year 2050, half of the agricultural land will be at a significant risk of salinity [13]. Salt stress in agricultural land is expected to increase due to climate change, irrational irrigation methods, improper use of fertilizers, and poor drainage networks [14]. Salinity stress has severe impact on the physiological and metabolic processes of plants. It leads to reduced seedling growth, decreased photosynthetic activity, water stress, ion toxicity, and lower rates of protein synthesis and lipid metabolism. Salt stress also affects cellular osmotic equilibrium and enhances oxidative damage by creating Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS). Saline soil limits plant development due to ion toxicity and scarcity of water [6, 7]. Plants that are subjected to salinity stress show an increase in ethylene production. Excessive amounts of ethylene have detrimental effects on plants, slowing germination and inhibiting root growth. Salt stress also causes DNA damage in plant tissue by generating ROS [15, 16].

Crop resistance to salt stress has been enhanced through the use of various strategies, which include both traditional and genetic engineering methods. However, one of the most sustainable approaches to help plants grow under salt stress is to exploit plant-microbe interactions. Multiple studies have demonstrated that fungal endophytes are among the beneficial symbionts that confer adaptive positive benefits to host plants under salt stress [3, 8, 17]. Under salt stress, plants face physiological dryness, which leads to osmotic stress and, in turn, an imbalance of ions and nutrients [2, 18]. Application of endophytic fungi and their secondary metabolites to alleviate the impact of salinity stress on plant growth can be considered a favourable option for eco-friendly and sustainable agriculture [19–21].

Therefore, the purpose of the present investigation was to isolate endophytic fungi from Vachellia nilotica and assess their activities for growth promotion and halotolerance. V. nilotica is an important leguminous tree that commonly grown in agricultural forests. It is a valuable source of timber, gum, medicines, fodder, fuel, and also has ability to resist abiotic stresses like salt, drought, and heavy metals [22]. In our study, an endophytic isolate that showed favourable growth promotion capabilities was selected for investigation to assess its potential in alleviating salt stress in Vigna radiata plants. V. radiata is an important crop that is cultivated in diverse regions across Asia, Europe, and America. Under salt stress, V. radiata plants acquire more NaCl during growth and development, resulting in reduced germination, protein, carbohydrate content, and plant biomass [23]. Conventional methods of breeding salt-resistant varieties are expensive and time-consuming. The application of plant growth-promoting endophytic fungi can be investigated to restore its productivity.

Materials and methodology

Isolation of endophytic fungi

Endophytic fungi were isolated from disease-free and symptomless plant tissues of Vachellia nilotica collected from Guru Nanak Dev University, Amritsar, India. The necessary permission for the collection of plant material was obtained from the university authorities. Surface sterilization was performed by washing samples with 70% ethanol, followed by sterile distilled water, and then immersing them in 4% sodium hypochlorite [24]. After sterilization, plant parts were cut into small fragments (2–5 mm in size) using a sterilized blade and placed on water agar medium containing chloramphenicol (100 mg/l) and incubated at 30 °C for 3 to 5 days to few weeks till the hyphae emerged. Surface sterilization was verified by imprinting plant parts and plating the final rinse water on sterile media. Samples showing microbial growth were discarded, and fungal mycelia that appeared from the plant parts were picked, purified, and thereafter preserved on Potato Dextrose Agar (PDA) slants.

Screening for plant growth promotion traits

All cultures were screened for different plant growth promotion properties viz. Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) production, phosphate solubilization, siderophore ammonium, and HCN production. For estimation of IAA production, fungal isolates were inoculated into Czapek Dox broth supplemented with 2 mg/ml L-tryptophan and incubated at 30 °C. The methodology of Gordon and Weber [25] was used for detection of IAA. The Chrome Azurol S (CAS) method, developed by Schwyn and Neilands [26], was used for siderophore detection. Pikovskaya’s agar medium, developed by Pikovskaya’s [27] was used for phosphate solubilization potential. Ammonium production was determined by inoculating endophytic cultures in peptone liquid medium and detected by methodology of Cappucino and Sherman [28]. HCN production was estimated by inoculating fungal isolates on PDA medium containing 0.4% (w/v) glycine. The methodology of Bakker and Schippers [29] was used for detection of HCN by fungal isolates.

Screening for halotolerance ability

The fungal isolates were screened for their salt tolerance ability by growing on Potato Dextrose medium (PDA) medium supplemented with different concentrations of NaCl 0%, 5% (0.86 M), 10% (1.72 M), 15% (2.58 M), and 20% (3.43 M) for 10 days at 30oC. The growth was assessed by observing the diameter of colony.

Identification of VN2 fungal isolate

VN2 culture exhibiting good plant growth promotion activity and halotolerance was selected for further investigations and identified using morphological and molecular methods. The morphological characteristics were assessed by using the slide culturing technique and then identified based on established taxonomic key characteristics [30]. Molecular characterization was carried out by isolation and amplification of ITS1-5.8 S rDNA-ITS2 region using universal primers. The purified ITS region was sequenced by Gene Matrix LLP, Pune, India. The phylogenetic tree was constructed in MEGA 10 software using the Neighbour-Joining method, and the evolutionary distances were computed using the maximum composite likelihood method. The culture was submitted to the National Centre for Microbial Resource (NCMR) at the National Centre for Cell Science (NCCS), Pune, Maharashtra, India.

Studies on IAA production by isolate VN2

The effect of different concentrations of tryptophan and incubation time on the production of IAA by isolate VN2 was studied. VN2 culture was inoculated in Czapek Dox broth supplemented with different concentrations of tryptophan (ranging from 2 to 10 mg/ml) and IAA was assessed at regular intervals of 24 h up to 7 days. The confirmation of IAA production was performed by TLC. VN2 culture was grown in Czapek Dox medium containing (L-Trp 10 mg/ml) and incubated at 30℃ for 3 days at 120 rpm. After incubation, the culture broth was obtained and extracted with ethyl acetate. The upper organic phase was collected and concentrated to dryness using a rotary evaporator. The extract obtained was subjected to TLC using a solvent system containing ethyl acetate/chloroform (10:1), and the chromatogram was observed after spraying the plates with Salkowski reagent. Standard IAA (1 mg/ml) was applied simultaneously as a reference. The spots corresponding to IAA were scraped and eluted using ethyl acetate. After filtration, the extract was subjected to RP-HPLC using C-18 reversed-phase column with methanol: water (80:20 v/v) as mobile phase. The peak obtained was compared with IAA standard.

Quantification and confirmation of ACCD production by Fourier-Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR)

ACC deaminase production was performed by using the standard protocol of Nascimento et al. [33]. Dworkin and Foster medium [34] was prepared with 3 mM ACC and A. niger VN2 inoculated in medium and incubated at 30 °C for 2 days. The amount of alpha-ketobutyrate in medium was measured at absorbance of 540 nm. The enzymatic activity was expressed as µmol alpha-ketobutyrate/h/mg protein. Protein was estimated using Bradford reagent [35]. Released alpha-ketobutyrate formed by deamination reaction was further verified by FTIR.

Inoculation of A. niger VN2 in V. radiata

Surface-sterilised seeds of V. radiata were placed on the petri plates containing Whatman filter paper and allowed to germinate at 25 °C ± 2 for 2 days [24]. The germinated seeds were submerged in a fungal spore suspension with a concentration of (1 × 108 conidia ml−1) for 8 hours. Following the treatment, the seeds were air-dried on sterile paper for 20 min. Subsequently, they were carefully transferred to sterilized soil. A control group was established using seeds treated with water containing 0.05% Triton X-100. To examine the colonization of endophytic fungal isolate VN2 in V. radiata, the plants were carefully uprooted after 7 days and subsequently rinsed with water. Isolation of endophytic fungi was carried out on PDA medium supplemented with 15% NaCl as selective agent for halotolerant fungi [36]. Plant growth promotion and salt stress alleviation potential of A. niger VN2

Plant growth promotion and salt stress alleviation in successfully colonized plants were determined by subjecting them to salt stress. The experiment was carried out in triplicate, and each replicate comprised of 3 pots with 4 seedlings (total 4 × 3 × 3 = 36 plants per treatment). Salinity stress of 50 ml of water containing 150, 200, and 250 mM NaCl was applied twice a week in all sets of pots, except the controls. T1 = Control plants (no salt stress + no fungal inoculation) T2 = Plants treated with different concentrations of NaCl (150, 200, 250 mM + no fungal inoculation), T3 = Fungal inoculated plants (no salt stress), T4 = Fungal inoculated plants treated with different concentrations of NaCl (150, 200, 250 mM + fungal inoculation).

Analysis of morphological parameters

After 20 days of growth under normal and stress conditions, root, shoot length, and number of leaves, as well as the fresh and dry weight of plants, were assessed using a standardized measuring scale in all the treatments.

Estimation of chlorophyll and carotenoid content

The fresh leaf tissue was ground in 80% acetone in a ratio of 1:4. The resulting mixture was then subjected to centrifugation at 4℃ for 20 min at 10,000 rpm. The liquid portion was collected and absorption was taken at wavelengths of 645 and 663 nm to estimate the amount of chlorophyll a (Chl a), chlorophyll b (Chl b), total chlorophyll [Chl a/Chl b], and while absorption was taken at 480 and 510 nm to determine the carotenoid content [37].

Determination of Relative Water Content (RWC) and Relative Electrical Conductivity (REC)

RWC of leaf was determined by measuring the weight of the fresh biomass, dry biomass, and wet biomass before and after the leaf samples were dried. The estimation of leaf relative water content was determined using the equation given by Balestri et al. [38]. The methodology described by Lutts et al. [39] was used to calculate the REC of leaf sample using an electrical conductivity meter.

Estimation of proline content

The proline content was evaluated using the methodology of Bates et al. [40]. The plant sample (0.5 g) was homogenized in 10 ml of sulfosalicylic acid (3%) and centrifuged for 15 min at 5000 rpm. An equal volume of glacial acetic acid and acid ninhydrin was added to the supernatant, and mixture was boiled at 100 °C for one hour in a water bath and immediately placed in ice, followed by addition of toluene after 2–3 min. Absorbance of the upper layer was measured at 520 nm. Proline content was expressed in mg/g of fresh tissue using a stock solution of L-proline.

Estimation of malondialdehyde content

The MDA content was assessed using the method given by Heath and Packer [41]. 0.5 g of plant tissue was homogenized in 0.1% Trichloroacetic acid (TCA) and centrifuged at 4 °C. 100 µl of supernatant was mixed with 600 µl of 20% TCA containing 0.5% thiobarbituric acid. The reaction mixture was heated at 95 °C for 30 min in water bath and immediately cooled on ice. The absorbance of clear solution was taken at 532 and 600 nm. Final absorbance was calculated by subtracting absorbance at 600 nm from absorbance at 532 nm. MDA content was expressed in µmol/g of plant tissue.

Estimation of protein content

Total protein content was estimated using Bradford reagent [35]. 1 gram of leaf tissue was homogenized in 4 ml of 0.1 M potassium phosphate buffer (pH 6.8) using a chilled mortar and pestle. The homogenate was centrifuged at 1,000 rpm for 15 min, and the supernatant was used for protein estimation. For the assay, 200 µl of supernatant was mixed with 1 ml of Bradford reagent and incubated in the dark at room temperature for 10 min. Absorbance was then measured at 595 nm.

Quantification of endogenous IAA levels and DPPH free radicals scavenging activity

The quantification of IAA was done by homogenizing 0.5 g of plant sample in 10 ml of distilled water, followed by centrifugation at 1000 rpm for 15 min. The reaction mixture, consisting of 1 ml of supernatant and 2 ml of Salkowski reagent, was kept in a dark environment for 25 min. The absorbance was measured at 530 nm. The DPPH free radicals scavenging activity was assessed using protocol given by Blois [42].

Fluorescent microscopic studies of V. radiata plant roots under salt stress

The cell viability was assessed by dipping the root tips of V. radiata in a 50 µM solution of Propidium Iodide (PI), and thereafter incubating them at room temperature for 15 min. The treated root tips were rinsed with double-distilled water to eliminate any excess PI, placed on the slide, and observed under fluorescence microscope. The microscope was set with an excitation wavelength of 543 nm and an emission wavelength of 617 nm.

In order to determine the location of hydrogen peroxide, the root tips were placed in a solution containing 50 µM of dichlorofluorescein-diacetate (DCF-DA) for one hour in dark conditions. Subsequently, the samples were placed on glass slides and analyzed using a fluorescence microscope with excitation and emission wavelengths of 485 nm and 535 nm, respectively.

To label glutathione, the root tips were submerged in a solution containing 25 µM of monochlorobimane (MCB). They were then kept in darkness for one hour, after which any excess stain was eliminated using deionized water. Subsequently, the samples were placed onto glass slides and scrutinized using a fluorescence microscope. The excitation wavelength ranges from 351 to 364 nm, whereas the emission wavelength is 477 nm.

Genoprotective effect of A. niger VN2 in V. radiata plants roots

Comet assay was used to assess the genoprotective effect of A. niger VN2 in salt stressed plants. The methodology given by Tice et al. [43] was applied for the determination of DNA damage induced by salt stress.

FTIR analysis of V. radiata plant roots under salt stress

Root samples were collected from non-colonized and colonized plants, with or without salt stress, and rinsed with phosphate buffer saline. The roots were then frozen at −20 °C and examined using FTIR Spectroscopy for 30 s measurement time, in the 400–4000 cm−1 spectral range, coded for 64 scans at 16 cm−1 resolution. FTIR finding was validated using PCA plot.

Statistical analysis

Different treatments were compared using Two-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons test. GraphPad Prism software version 7.0 was used for statistical analysis. All the findings were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Results with p-value less than 0.05 (p < 0.05) were considered statistically significant.

Results

In the present study, 15 endophytic cultures were isolated from V. nilotica and screened for plant growth promotion activities viz. Indole-3-acetic acid (IAA), siderophore and ammonium production etc. (Fig. 1). Isolate VN2 was selected for further studies as it showed highest IAA production (68.65±5.54 µg/ml), was positive for all the evaluated plant growth promotion properties, and had high salt tolerance (up to 15% NaCl). The culture VN2 was further identified according to standard taxonomic key, including colony morphology, conidia, and hyphal structure. VN2 colonies were fast-growing, black with yellowish reverse pigment, attaining a diameter of 4.6 cm when inoculated on PDA plates at 30℃ for 4 days (Fig. 2a, b). The molecular characterization of the culture VN2 was done using PCR amplification of Internal Transcribed Spacer (ITS) regions including 5.8 S rDNA followed by DNA sequencing. After sequencing, a sequence of 528 bp was obtained and deposited in GenBank under accession number OQ353075. The sequence on alignment with homologous nucleotide sequences obtained from type strains revealed maximum similarity with Aspergillus niger (Fig. 2c). The culture was submitted to National Centre for Microbial Resource (NCMR) at National Centre for Cell Science (NCCS), Pune, Maharashtra, India under accession number NFCCI5503.

Plant growth promotion properties of A. niger VN2

IAA production

A. niger VN2 produced IAA in tryptophan supplemented growth medium, as confirmed by change in the colour of the supernatant to pink-red after addition of Salkowski reagent. IAA synthesis was not detected in the absence of tryptophan. The effect of different concentrations of tryptophan and incubation time on the production of IAA was studied. As depicted in Fig. 3, maximum IAA production of 148.32 ± 4.39 µg/ml was observed on 3rd day at 10 mg/ml tryptophan. Different concentrations of tryptophan (F4,70=408.039; p < 0.05) and incubation period (F6,70 =391.43; p < 0.05) significantly affected IAA production. The potential of A. niger VN2 to synthesize IAA was further confirmed using TLC and HPLC analysis. The crude ethyl acetate extract exhibited a pink spot on the TLC plates after treatment with Salkowski reagent at Rf value comparable to standard IAA (0.85 retention factor). After the detection of IAA in TLC. HPLC analysis was used for further verification of IAA production. The partially purified extract and corresponding reference authentic standard both displayed peaks at retention time of 9.09 min (Supplementary Figure S1).

Effect of incubation time and different concentrations of tryptophan on IAA production by A. niger VN2. Data are expressed as mean ± S.D. The values with distinct letters are significantly different (p < 0.05) according to Tukey’s test

Phosphate solubilization

The presence of a halo zone around the colony on Pikovskaya’s agar plates was a confirmation of phosphate solubilization capability of A. niger VN2 culture. The Phosphate Solubilization Index (PSI) was found to be 1.16 ± 0.03 (Supplementary Fig. S2).

Siderophore production

The culture A. niger VN2 exhibited an orange halo (17.66±0.057 mm) on Chrome Azurol S (CAS) agar, indicating siderophore production (Fig. 4a). Maximum siderophore production (72.66% SU, p<0.05) was obtained on 7thday of incubation which remained stationary thereafter (Fig. 4b). The characterization of siderophore was initially done byFeCl3 test. A broad peak between 420 and 450 nm was observed, indicating hydroxamate-type siderophore (Fig. 4 d), which was further confirmed by appearance of red colour in Tetrazolium test and Csaky assay. Negative results were obtainedin the assay for catecholate and carboxylate-type siderophores (Fig. 4c).

Siderophore production by A. niger VN2 (a) orange halo zone on Chrome Azurol S (CAS) agar (b) Effect of incubation time on siderophore production (c) characterization of siderophore type (d) Absorption spectrum after FeCl3 test showing peak at 420 to 450 nm indicating hydroxamate type of siderophore

Salt tolerance

A. niger VN2 exhibited high salt tolerate showing good growth in medium supplemented with 15%NaCl. On PDA plates supplemented with 15% NaCl, colony diameter of 1.6 cm was observed after 10 days of incubation. The presence of salt also affected the colony morphology, as pale yellow and less sporulation was observed in medium supplemented with salt as compared to black sporulating colonies in control medium.

Estimation of 1-Aminocyclopropane-1- Carboxylate Deaminase

The culture A. niger VN2 was able to grow on Dwarkin and Foster medium supplemented with ACC as a nitrogen source, showing the potential to produce ACC deaminase. ACC deaminase activity was quantified at 134.40 ± 5.45 µmolα-ketobutyrate/h/mg protein on 2nd day of incubation. FTIR evaluation revealed peaks at 1639 and 3339 cm−1, indicating the presence of α-ketobutyrate, a compound having ketonic and amino functional groups. The results indicated the conversion of ACC to α-ketobutyrate, Sarkar et al. [44] (Supplementary Fig. S3).

Effect of colonization of A. niger VN2 on V. radiata plants under normal and salt stress conditions

A. niger VN2 was successfully colonized in V. radiata plants with colonization percentages determined to be 61.6%, 50.4%, and 25.6%, in root, shoot, and leaf, respectively. Colonization of A. niger VN2 in V. radiata significantly improved physiological and biochemical characteristics under both normal and stress conditions. Under normal condition (0 mM NaCl) association of V. radiata plants with A. niger VN2 showed enhancement of 40.57%, 36.92%, and 23.03% in root length, shoot length, and number of leaves, respectively as shown in Fig. 5a. At all the applied salt concentrations, uninoculated plants exhibited decrease in root, shoot length, biomass, and number of leaves. However, restoration of these growth attributes was achieved through inoculation with A. niger VN2. At highest salt stress (250 mM NaCl) A. niger VN2 inoculated plants showed significant (p < 0.05) increases of 1.83-fold, 1.66-fold, and 2.33-fold in root length, shoot length, and number of leaves respectively, compared to un-inoculated plants (Figs. 5 and 6a, b and c). Similarly, plants treated with A. niger VN2 showed significant (p < 0.05) increases of 2.54-fold, 1.73-fold, 1.63-fold, and 1.56-fold in root fresh and dry weight, shoot fresh and dry weight respectively, as shown in Fig. 6d, e, f, g. The Relative Water Content (RWC) of plants was significantly impacted by salt stress. At a salt stress of 250 mM, plants inoculated with A. niger VN2 showed a significant (p < 0.05) increase of 1.23-fold in RWC compared with un-inoculated plants (Fig. 6h).

Effect of A. niger VN2 on endogenous IAA, proline and protein content under normal and salt stress conditions

Colonization of A. niger VN2 exhibited significant (p<0.05) increase of 2.72-fold in endogenous IAA level under non stress condition (0 mM NaCl). At highest salt stress (250 mM NaCl), an increase of 4.31-fold of endogenous IAA in A. niger VN2 associated plants compared to un-inoculated plants was observed. Moreover, colonization with A. niger VN2 impacted the level of protein under both normal and stressed condition. At 0 mM NaCl, protein content was enhanced by 4%in A. niger VN2 colonized plants. At the highest applied salt stress (250 mM NaCl) total protein content enhanced by 1.69-fold in A. niger VN2 associated plants compared to un- inoculated plants. Proline content significantly (p<0.05) increased 4.72-fold in uninoculated plants under salt stress. Application of A. niger VN2 resulted in 40.74% and 63.53% decrease in proline content at 0 mM and 250 mM NaCl stress, respectively (Table 1).

Effect of A. niger VN2 on MDA, electrolyte leakage, and DPPH radical scavenging activity under normal and salt stress conditions

Colonization of A. niger VN2 led to positive affect on electrolyte leakage and oxidative stress under both normal and application of salt stress. The application of high salt stress increased electrolyte leakage by 9.67-fold compared with control plants. Colonization of A. niger VN2 resulted in 30.17% and 57.35% reduction in electrolyte leakage in plants exposed to 0mM and 250 mM NaCl stress. At high salt stress (250 mM NaCl), 2.78-fold increase in MDA content was observed compared with control non-colonized plants. Colonization of A. niger VN2 showed reduction of 30.49% and 43.07% in MDA content at 0 mM and 250 mM NaCl stress as compared to un-inoculated plants. Plants become more resistant to the negative impacts of salt stress when they contain more antioxidants. In this study, we found that plants that were associated with A. niger VN2 had higher DPPH radical scavenging activity (1.23-fold at 0 mM NaCl) compared with un-inoculated plants. At high salt stress of 250 mM NaCl, DPPH radical scavenging activity was enhanced by 1.46-fold compared to the uninoculated plants (Table 2).

Determination of cell viability and oxidative stress using fluorescent microscopy

The assessment of cell viability in roots of inoculated and uninoculated plants was determined by the intensity of red color after staining with Propidium iodide (PI). PI is a staining dye that has the ability to penetrate damaged cell membranes. It selectively binds to nucleic acids, resulting in the visualization of cells as bright red dots. An increased intensity of the red color was observed in the roots of plants that had been exposed to salt stress. Whereas, the plants inoculated with A. niger VN2 exhibited a decrease in fluorescence intensity, which suggests increased level of cell viability and protective effect of A. niger VN2 (Fig. 8a). High salt stress also triggers oxidative stress, which can be detected by using dichlorodihydrofluorescein-diacetate (H2DCFDA) dye to detect hydrogen peroxide. The green fluorescence was brighter in salt stressed plants than in A. niger VN2 associated plants indicating higher level of H2O2 in un-inoculated plants. The fluorescence decreased in inoculated plants indicating positive effect of A. niger VN2 (Fig. 8b). Glutathione is a common marker of plant oxidative stress. In order to label glutathione, monochlorobimane (MCB) dye is used, which quickly crosses cell membranes and binds with glutathione-S-transferase GSH. In the present study, seedling roots of V. radiata were immersed in MCB dye. The amount of glutathione was determined by measuring the intensity of the blue color. The roots of plants subjected to severe salt stress at 250 mM exhibited a prominent blue color, which serves as an indicator of elevated glutathione levels. The plants that were associated with A. niger VN2 exhibited a reduction in blue color intensity when exposed to extreme salt stress conditions, suggesting a decline in glutathione levels (Fig. 8c).

Fourier transform infrared (FTIR) analysis of V. radiata plant roots under normal and salt stress conditions

In the present study, the effect of A. niger VN2 colonization and alleviation of salt stress in V. radiata was studied by FT-IR profiling of roots samples. A spectrum (400–4000 cm−1) of the samples taken from the control group and the treatment group at concentrations of 0 mM and 250 Mm NaCl, respectively (Fig. 10a). In the protein region, the peaks at ~ 1650 cm−1 and ~ 1550 cm−1 corresponding to amide I (C = O stretching of proteins) and amide II (N-H bending/C-N stretching of proteins) respectively were observed. The results revealed a reduction of 23.72% (amide I) and 24.72% (amide II) in uninoculated 250 mM NaCl stressed group compared to 0 mM NaCl group. Whereas, the inoculated plant samples at 250mM NaCl showed 45.38% and 41.79% increase in absorption frequency at ~ 1650 cm−1 and ~ 1550 cm−1, respectively, compared to 250 mM NaCl stressed uninoculated plant samples. Similarly, increments of 32.88% and 34.28% were observed at 1260 cm−1 and 3132 cm−1 peaks assigned to amide III and -NH symmetric stretching of protein spectrum, respectively. In carbohydrate region, at peak ~ 1155 cm−1 corresponding to CO-O-C symmetric stretching, showed significant increase of 41.05% in absorption values in 250mM NaCl stressed inoculated plant compared to uninoculated 250 mM NaCl plant samples. The absorption frequencies observed to decline in lipid region, in 250 mM NaCl uninoculated plants compared to 0 mM NaCl uninoculated plants, where values decreased by19.45% and 17.89% respectively at ~ 1470 cm−1 and ~ 1750 cm−1. In case of A. niger VN2 inoculated plant group the frequency increased by 38.23% (at ~ 1470 cm−1) and 17.90% (at ~ 1750 cm−1) compared to uninoculated plant at 250 mM NaCl salt stress. The nucleic acid region assigned to ~ 964 cm−1, ~ 1080 cm−1 and ~ 1230 cm−1 representing asymmetric, symmetric modes of phosphodiester group and C-N & -C stretch revealed a similar pattern, where salts stress caused reduction in absorption frequency in uninoculated 250 mM NaCl group compared to uninoculated 0 mM NaCl group and the inoculated of A. niger alleviated the salt stress. An increment of 29.01%, 39.88% and 15.15% at ~ 964 cm−1, ~ 1080 cm−1 and ~ 1230 cm−1, respectively were observed in A. niger VN2 inoculated plant group compared to 250 mM NaCl uninoculated plant samples. The Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a fast, intuitive, and reliable method to identify differences between FTIR spectral data. The PCA-derived score plots along PCs 1 and 2, having eigenvalues greater than one, were constructed for best segregation (Fig. 10b). The PCs had a total variability of 94.1% comprising 73.5% of PC1 and 20.6% of PC2. In scores plot, nearness in space implied similarity between 0 mM NaCl uninoculated group, 0 mM NaCl A. niger inoculated group and 250 mM NaCl A. niger inoculated group. While distance points of segregation revealed significant difference between uninoculated 250 mM NaCl plants sample and A. niger VN2 inoculated 250 mM salt stressed group.

Discussion

The use of endophytic microorganisms to enhance agricultural production is an emerging ecologically sustainable approach. Many studies have focused on the benefits of endophytic bacteria and filamentous fungi as biofertilizers [4, 19, 45, 46]. Association of endophytic fungi with plants is a key strategy to improve plant growth under various environmental stresses [10, 47–49]. Plant growth promotion activities like IAA production, phosphate solubilization, and siderophore production are desirable traits for plant growth promotion. In the present study, endophytic culture VN2 was obtained from Vachellia nilotica, exhibited good plant growth promotion abilities, and halotolerance. The culture VN2 was identified as Aspergillus niger on morphological and molecular basis. A. niger has been certified as Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) by FDA for certain industrial manufacturing procedures [50, 51]. In the present study, it was found that isolate A. niger VN2 was able to produce high level of IAA (148.32 ± 2.34 µg/ml). The ability to produce IAA by A. niger has been reported by various researchers [52–54]. Various isolates of A. niger are reported to solubilize phosphate that is important trait for plant growth promotion [54–56]. In this study also A. niger VN2 had the potential to solubilize phosphate and phosphate solubilization index was 1.16 ± 0.03. The production of siderophores is another important growth-promoting trait possessed by endophytic microorganisms. Siderophores are metal-chelating agents that have a strong affinity for binding ferric iron [57]. They enhance the absorption of iron by plant roots under iron-deficient conditions by removing it from several soluble and insoluble forms in the soil, such as ferric citrate and ferric phosphate [58]. In the present study A. niger VN2 had the ability to produce siderophore (72.66% SU) which was determined to be hydroxymate type Production of hydroxamate type siderophores has been reported from many Aspergillus species [59–61]. A. niger VN2 also exhibited high salt stress tolerance ability (15% NaCl). Salt stress affected the colony morphology, as pale yellow and less sporulation was observed in the presence of salt as compared to black colour sporulating colonies in control. The ability to tolerate high salt stress by A. niger has been reported by various workers. In the present study, A. niger VN2 culture showed ability to produce ACC deaminase (134.40 ± 5.45 µmol α-ketobutyrate/h/mg protein). This was verified by FTIR analysis, which showed peaks corresponding to α-ketobutyrate and ammonia formed as a result of ACC degradation. ACC deaminase is an enzyme that has the ability to break down ACC, which is the precursor to ethylene, into ammonia and α-ketobutyrate. ACC deaminase-producing microorganisms show potential to reduce abiotic stress by decreasing ethylene production. Many research studies have shown that endophytic fungi have ability to produce ACC deaminase enzyme [8–10, 62].

In the present study, A. niger VN2 culture was inoculated in Vigna radiata, and the effect of colonization under normal and salt stress conditions was analysed. Salt stress affects plant morphology, including root length, shoot length, and fresh and dry biomass. Roots are among the initial parts of a plant that are directly affected by salt stress. Thus, changes in the structure of the root system are very important for controlling salt uptake and movement and enhancing plant tolerance to salt stress. Endophytic fungi can modify the ability of plants to alter root structure by increasing branching of roots, length, and weight, which helps plants negotiate salt stress by expanding the surface area for absorbing essential nutrients and ions like K+ and Ca2+. In current study, colonization of A. niger VN2 exhibited increase in plant root, shoot biomass and number of lateral roots compared with non-colonized plants under normal and salt stress condition. Similar results have been reported by various researchers that endophytic fungi have potential to promote plant growth by enhancing root and shoot length biomass, and phytohormones under salt stress [10, 36, 63–66]. Furthermore, the number of leaves was also significantly affected in uninoculated plants, which could be due to absorption of high salt concentrations by roots that can transport in different parts of plants and finally accumulate in leaf cells, leading to a low number of leaves [67]. A. niger VN2 inoculated plants showed a positive effect on the number of leaves. Salt stress also adversely affects the level of endogenous IAA. In this study, high contents of IAA were observed in plants that were colonized with A. niger VN2 and exposed to salt stress. The observed improvement in growth parameters can be attributed to enhanced IAA levels and ACC deaminase activity.

Photosynthetic pigments viz. chlorophyll a, b, and total chlorophyll and carotenoids, play a vital role in the processes of photosynthesis and photoprotection. High salt stress induces several physiological alterations, increases accumulation of Na+ in chloroplasts, disrupts the activities of photosynthetic pigments, and inhibits D1 and D2 proteins of photosystem II, resulting in reduced photosynthesis in plants [68, 69]. In the present study, under salt stress chlorophyll and carotenoids content was significantly decreased in non-colonized plants whereas inoculation with A. niger VN2 showed enhancement in photosynthetic pigments. Similar results showing enhanced photosynthetic pigments under salt stress by endophytic fungi have been reported by various workers [70–72]. Plant growth and development also depend on content of water in plant tissues. High salinity stress triggers a shortage of water in plant tissues, thus hindering plant growth and development. The levels of RWC are an indicator of the stress response of plants. In the present study, A. niger VN2 not only mitigated stress, but also enhanced water availability in leaves compared with non-colonized plants. Increase in relative water content in endophytic fungi inoculated plants under salt stress has also been reported by various researchers [66, 73].

Salinity increases the release of electrolytes (such as K+ ions) by displacing Ca+ from the cell membrane [74]. The integrity of the cellular membrane is damaged, leading to an increased flow of electrolytes inside plant tissues. In our study A. niger VN2 inoculated plants exhibited less electrolyte leakage compared to non-colonized plants under salt stress indicating the lower permeability of the plasma membrane and reduced lipid peroxidation. MDA is an indicator of lipid peroxidation that indicates oxidative damage caused by salt stress. Elevated MDA levels are linked to increased formation of reactive oxygen species and damage to cellular membranes. In the present study, MDA content was increased in non-colonized plants compared to A. niger VN2 inoculated plants. Various researchers are documenting the potential of endophytic fungi to reduce MDA content under salt stress [75, 76]. Enhancement of antioxidant activity in inoculated plants was also revealed in DPPH radical scavenging assay. In the present study, plants inoculated with A. niger VN2 exhibited significantly greater DPPH radical scavenging activity compared to un-inoculated plants. Another important osmoprotectant molecule that increases under stress is proline. In our study A. niger VN2 colonized plants showed decrease in proline content compared to un-inoculated plants. Numerous studies have also shown that inoculation of endophytic fungi decreases proline content under salt stress [65, 71, 77]. On the contrary, an increase in proline levels in plants that were inoculated with fungi is reported by Jan et al. [72] and Khan et al. [10].

Oxidative stress and cell viability in roots were examined by qualitatively employing in vivo histochemical labelling with fluorescent dyes. Propidium iodide is a fluorescent dye that is often used to label DNA molecules and assess cell membrane integrity. PI penetrates damaged cell membranes and binds to DNA, leading to emission of red fluorescence. In the current study, it was observed that cell viability decreased under high salt stress in uninoculated plants, as shown by an increased intensity of red color. A. niger VN2 inoculation in V. radiata reduced the red color intensity, suggesting a protective mechanism under salt stress. Alharbi et al. [78] reported that high salinity induces cell membrane damage in pea root tips. When plants are exposed to high salt stress, formation of hydrogen peroxide occurs, which was identified by staining the root tissues with a fluorescent dye 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (H2DCFDA) [79]. H2DCFDA is the most often used dye for the detection of intracellular hydrogen peroxide. It has the ability to pass across cell membranes, spread throughout cells, and undergo deacetylation by cellular esterases to produce 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein (H2DCF). When exposed to hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), H2DCF undergoes rapid oxidation to become 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescein (DCF), which emits green fluorescence [80]. In the current study, higher level of green fluorescence was observed in uninoculated plants subjected to salt stress. This indicated a rise in H2O2 levels. The green color intensity decreased in the presence of fungal-inoculated plant roots, revealing its protective effects. The visualization of glutathione (GSH) localization in plant roots has been achieved by using monochlorobimane (MCB) dye. MCB is a dye that easily passes across cell membranes and has a strong attraction to GSH. The interaction of dye with GSH is catalysed by endogenous glutathione-S-transferase (GST) enzymes, leading to the production of blue fluorescence [81]. In this study, the level of glutathione was shown to be higher in uninoculated plants under salt stress, as evidenced by the enhanced intensity of blue color. The root that was colonized with A. niger VN2 exhibited a decrease in the intensity of blue color.

Salinity stress may induce DNA damage in plant tissue [15, 16]. High salt causes oxidative stress, which can lead to the production of ROS and result in an imbalance of antioxidant molecules and causing DNA damage. Comet assay was used to assess the DNA damage induced by salt stress and the protective properties of A. niger VN2. The genotoxic damage caused by salt stress may be evaluated using many parameters, such as tail length, % DNA tail, tail moment, and olive tail moment. In the current study, all the measured DNA damage parameters, including tail length, % DNA tail, tail moment, and olive tail moment, were improved in A. niger VN2 treated plants as compared to the un-inoculated plants. The observed genoprotective effect could be due to improved antioxidant activity in the inoculated plants, which was evident in reduced MDA content and enhanced DPPH radical scavenging activity, as discussed previously. This work presents the first documentation of genoprotective effect of A. niger in the presence of salt stress.

High salt stress in plants also caused alterations in biomolecules viz. proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and nucleic acid [82]. In recent years, various non-invasive methods have been used to determine the composition, structural characterization, and detection of biomolecules. FTIR is a highly non-destructive technique that has the ability to recognize functional groups and thus provide information about alterations in biomolecules with respect to any biotic or abiotic stress. The fundamental principle of FTIR spectroscopy is based on the vibrational motion of chemical bonds within the infrared range. Chemical bonds in the infrared area selectively absorb light within the wavelength range of 4000 to 400 cm −1. Every functional group in a molecule has a unique absorption frequency in the infrared spectrum. These peaks are characteristic of C = O (esters, carboxylic acids, ketones, aldehydes), C = C (benzenes), and C-N (amino group) etc., functional groups mainly representing a biomolecule [83]. In general, the assessment of protein secondary structures (α-helical, β-sheet, and random coil) could be made based on the analysis of the amide I band and amide II band at 1650 cm−1 and 1550 cm−1. In this study, there were significant differences in the frequencies of 1650 cm−1 and 1550 cm−1, in uninoculated 250 mM NaCl stressed group compared with 0 mM NaCl group (un-inoculated), indicating conformational changes in proteins. The results also showed evidence of DNA damage in high salt stress by alterations in nucleic acid regions assigned to 1230 cm−1 and 1081 cm−1 due to asymmetric and symmetric modes of phosphodiester group, respectively, in uninoculated plants compared with inoculated plant groups. A similar pattern was observed in lipid region (~ 1470 cm−1 and ~ 1750 cm−1) and carbohydrates region (~ 1155 cm−1). The resulting spectrum was examined using principal component analysis (PCA), a strong statistical technique used to reduce the dimensionality of large and multivariate data sets [17]. The PCA was applied to FTIR spectra of 4000–400 nm region. PCA plots were used for multivariate analysis of salt treatment changes. PC1 and PC2 had 73.5% and 20.6% variability (Fig. 10). PCA plots in case of 250 mM salt stress lay on separate quadrate whereas, PCA plots of A. niger VN2 both under normal and stress condition lay close together and on opposing side. This indicates the positive effect of fungus under salt stress. Various reports are available documenting the use of FTIR analysis for studying changes in chemical composition and functional groups of proteins, lipids, and carbohydrates in plants under salt stress, which were further validated by PCA plot [82, 84]. It can be concluded that endophytic fungus A. niger having virtue of its plant growth promotion abilities and halotolerance can be exploited in sustainable agriculture for mitigating salt stress and enhance crop yield.

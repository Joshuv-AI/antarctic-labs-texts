Comparative analysis of diagnostic accuracy in adult congenital heart disease: A study of three physician groups and ChatGPT
Authors: Edward Itelman, Einat Birk, Ayelet Machtei, Rafael Hirsch, Nili Schamroth Pravda
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This research letter evaluates the diagnostic accuracy of three physician groups—pediatric cardiologists, adult congenital heart disease (ACHD) specialists, and general cardiologists—and compares it to the performance of an AI (Artificial Intelligence) LLM model. Based on eight targeted questions in adult congenital heart disease, the analysis highlights significant disparities in correct answer rates, with congenital specialists achieving the highest accuracy and the AI model – the lowest.

Managing adult congenital heart disease (ACHD) demands specialized knowledge across various cardiology disciplines. GPT 4.0 has already been evaluated in several medical fields for problem solving and showed promising results compared to human physicians [1,2]. This hypothesis generating study explores the diagnostic accuracies of different specialist groups and an AI model (GPT 4.0 and GPT 4o) to explore the utility of AI in clinical decision support in this complex field of cardiac specialty.

Human specialist participants included 3 pediatric cardiologists, 3 ACHD specialists, and 3 general cardiologists. All cardiologists had at least 5 years of clinical experience and included experts with significant experience in all groups. Each group responded to the same set of eight standardized hypothetical clinical questions relevant to ACHD. Human participants were able to opt to answer “I don't know” in addition to the options given. The responses were evaluated against a set of predetermined guideline directed answers, with accuracy percentages computed for comparative analysis. Supplement) The current ESC and ACC guidelines on ACHD was inserted into the AI model [3,4]. The results showed that pediatric and ACHD cardiologists performed the best with an accuracy rate of 87.5 % and 83.3 % respectively, followed by general cardiologists with 68.8 %. ChatGPT 4.0 trailed in accuracy rates, providing correct answers to only 50 % of the questions (Fig. 1). The superior performance of congenital specialists reflects their clinical experience in this field. ACHD guidelines are largely based on level of evidence C (expert opinion) and encompass clinical considerations that are often complex [3].The decision-making process is often multidisciplinary and requires a clinical understanding of the underlying congenital heart disease, disease trajectory, patient wishes and acquired medical conditions. The lower performance of ChatGPT suggests a need for enhanced training and AI tuning specifically targeted toward complex medical fields. If it can increase its accuracy in the field, AI could enhance the overall quality of care in the field of adult congenital heart disease and assist in clinical decision-making. Due to the low number of congenital heart specialists worldwide, AI could prove an important clinical assist tool especially in treatment regions where specialists are.

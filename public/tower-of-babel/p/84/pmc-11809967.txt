A Case of Sellar Arachnoid Cyst Operated Using the Endoscopic Supraorbital Keyhole Approach
Authors: Kei Yamashiro, Saeko Higashiguchi, Akira Wakako, Tatsuo Omi, Motoharu Hayakawa, Yuichi Hirose
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND AND IMPORTANCE:In recent years, sellar arachnoid cysts (ACs) have been increasingly treated using an endoscopic transnasal approach. It is important to connect the subarachnoid space with the AC to prevent recurrence; however, a high frequency of postoperative cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leakage has been reported with the endoscopic transnasal approach, and the suitability of the endoscopic transnasal approach for treating sellar ACs is questionable to begin with.CLINICAL PRESENTATION:A man in his 40s underwent surgery for a sellar AC compressing the optic chiasm. The normal pituitary gland was compressed dorsally by the cyst, and an endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach was adopted to reduce the risk of postoperative CSF leakage. A small incision was made in the right eyebrow, and a small craniotomy was performed. A rigid scope was used to approach the cyst and incise the arachnoid membrane of the prechiasmatic cistern and the anterior wall of the cyst. Postoperatively, the cyst shrank, and the preoperative visual field defects improved. The duration of the endoscopic manipulation was approximately 40 minutes.DISCUSSION:The endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach eliminates the need to incise the dura mater of the anterior skull base and reduces the risk of CSF leakage compared with the transnasal approach. In addition, cumbersome dural reconstruction using fat and fascia is not required during the supraorbital keyhole approach, which reduces operation time.CONCLUSION:The endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach may be more suitable than the transnasal approach for surgery of sellar ACs.

Sellar arachnoid cysts (ACs) are relatively rare, accounting for approximately 3% of all intracranial ACs.1 Cysts often exhibit suprasellar extension and compression of surrounding structures, resulting in visual field disturbances, headaches, and pituitary dysfunction.2-5 Symptomatic sellar ACs have been treated surgically, and endoscopic transnasal surgery was usually adopted in recent years.3-5 Recurrence is a problem in sellar AC surgery, and it is important to connect the cyst to the subarachnoid space to prevent recurrence.5 Therefore, it is important to incise the arachnoid membrane of the prechiasmatic cistern, while opening the sellar AC wall during the endoscopic transnasal approach.4,5 However, endoscopic transnasal surgery for sellar ACs is prone to postoperative cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) leakage because of the arachnoid membrane incision.3,4 Although dural reconstruction using fat, fascia, and a nasoseptal flap has been introduced in recent years to prevent CSF leakage,3-5 CSF leakage has been reported in 7 out of 58 cases (13.7%) of sellar ACs treated with an endoscopic transnasal approach3; CSF leakage remains an unresolved complication. Moreover, complications associated with the nasoseptal flap, such as olfactory disturbances, are challenging. On the other hand, the usefulness of the supraorbital keyhole approach as a minimally invasive surgery in the sellar region is well known.6,7 The frequency of postoperative CSF leakage using the supraorbital keyhole approach has been reported to be 2.8%,8 and the frequency of postoperative CSF leakage would be reduced using the keyhole approach to treat the sellar AC instead of endoscopic transnasal surgery. However, there have been no reports of endoscopic surgery using the supraorbital keyhole approach for sellar ACs. This case report is the first to describe a sellar AC treated using an endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach and details the usefulness of this surgical approach as an alternative to endoscopic transnasal surgery for sellar ACs.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION

A man in his 40s underwent MRI for mild bitemporal hemianopsia, which revealed an intrasellar cyst. The cyst showed suprasellar extension, and the optic chiasm was compressed (Figure 1A). The cyst wall was not contrasted with gadolinium-based contrast media, and the intracystic content appeared isointense with the CSF (Figure 1B); the cyst was preoperatively diagnosed as a sellar AC. Surgery was performed to improve bitemporal hemianopsia. The normal pituitary gland was compressed dorsally by the cyst, and the cyst could be approached anteriorly. Therefore, the supraorbital keyhole approach was adopted to reduce the risk of postoperative CSF leakage.

Surgery was performed on the right side with the patient in a supine position. The head was rotated approximately 30° to the left side and slightly retroflexed to move the frontal lobe away from the supraorbital wall. Besides, a lumbar drain was used as a brain relaxation technique. A skin incision was made laterally from the supraorbital notch to the lateral side of the zygomatic process of the frontal bone (Figure 2A). In this case, the frontal skull base was noticeably uneven, and craniotomy had to be performed slightly higher than the usual supraorbital keyhole approach to secure the operative field. Therefore, a skin incision was made just above the eyebrow. To maximize the aesthetic effect of the incision, we carefully dissected the soft tissues. The temporalis muscle was detached, and a 2 × 3 cm craniotomy was performed (Figure 2B). The frontal air sinus was not open. After the dura was incised, the cyst was approached using a 4-mm diameter, 0° rigid scope (Karl Storz) (Figure 2C). By inserting the endoscope parallel to the anterior skull base, the cyst was easily identified with little retraction of the frontal lobe. Using microscissors, the arachnoid membrane of the prechiasmatic cistern was incised to open the subarachnoid space (Figure 2D), and the cyst wall was incised to connect the subarachnoid space to the cyst (Figure 2E). The anterior wall of the cyst was excised, and observation of the inside of the cyst revealed thinning of the normal pituitary gland, which had compressed dorsally (Figure 2F). After confirming adequate CSF flow through the cyst opening, the dura was tightly sutured under direct vision without endoscopic instruments and sealed with fibrin glue. The bone flap was fixed with a titanium plate, and the skin was sutured. The duration of the endoscopic manipulation was approximately 40 min. The patient was discharged without complications.

Postoperative MRI showed shrinkage of the cyst (Figure 1C), and the bitemporal hemianopsia improved. A pathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of an AC. Three months postoperatively, no recurrence was observed and good cosmetic results were obtained.

We have obtained written informed consent for surgery, and the patient consented to the publication of his image.

DISCUSSION

This is the first case report of an endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach for sellar ACs. The endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach allowed easy access to the sellar AC and demonstrated that the cyst wall and arachnoid incisions could be performed safely and quickly. This surgical approach is less invasive than the endoscopic transnasal approach for sellar ACs because it does not require a skin incision to harvest fat and fascia to prevent CSF leakage.3-5

Limitations of the Endoscopic Transnasal Approach

In recent years, endoscopic surgery has become more common, and sellar ACs are increasingly treated using the endoscopic transnasal approach (Figure 3A).3-5 However, with the high frequency of CSF leakage still reported,3 the appropriateness of applying the endoscopic transnasal approach to sellar ACs is debatable. The high frequency of postoperative CSF leakage suggests that watertight dural reconstruction is still difficult to achieve during the endoscopic transnasal approach, and an alternative surgical approach should be adopted for sellar ACs. On the other hand, the supraorbital keyhole approach is a widely accepted surgical approach known to reduce rates of postoperative CSF leakage.6-8 Considering the high frequency of postoperative CSF leakage after the transnasal approach for sellar ACs, it is reasonable to adopt the supraorbital keyhole approach.

Advantages of the Endoscopic Supraorbital Keyhole Approach

The supraorbital keyhole approach is a versatile surgical approach that can be applied to a wide range of diseases.6,7,9,10 Its usefulness and safety are well established.6,7 The most significant disadvantage of this approach is the loss of intraoperative illumination and vision, which significantly reduces surgical optical control.6,7 However, this disadvantage can be overcome using an endoscope, and a wide, clean surgical field can be acquired quickly during the endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach because illumination can be placed deeper than in the traditional microscopic surgery. In addition, unlike transnasal surgery, a skin incision must be made in the brow area; however, it has been reported that a pleasing aesthetic result is achieved using a 4- to 5-cm skin incision within the brow and careful dissection of the soft tissues.6-8 On the other hand, compared with endoscopic transnasal surgery, complex bone removal of the anterior skull base and cumbersome dural reconstruction using fat, fascia, and a nasoseptal flap are not required during the supraorbital keyhole approach (Figure 3B), which is expected to reduce operation time and decrease CSF leakage. If the frontal sinus opens during the supraorbital keyhole approach, it would not pose a problem as long as it can be securely closed.8 Because the applicability of the endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach for Rathke's cleft cysts has also been reported,10 it is presumed to be useful for sellar ACs. Although further research on the complications and long-term results is needed, the benefits of using the endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach for sellar ACs may be significant, and considering the complications associated with endoscopic transnasal surgery and further accumulation of cases is desirable.

CONCLUSION

The usefulness of the supraorbital keyhole approach for sellar ACs was reported. Sellar ACs can be safely operated using the endoscopic supraorbital keyhole approach, and the frequency of CSF leakage is expected to reduce compared with conventional endoscopic transnasal surgery.

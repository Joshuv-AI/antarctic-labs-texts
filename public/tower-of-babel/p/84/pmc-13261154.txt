Konno-Rastan aortoventriculoplasty after transcatheter aortic valve replacement explant for aortic annular enlargement
Authors: Alex Nantsios, Tsuyoshi Kaneko
Published: 2026

Reconstruction of the left ventricular outflow tract during the Konno-Rastan procedure.

Central Message

Konno-Rastan procedure after TAVR explant achieves definitive annular and root enlargement, enabling larger valve implantation while reducing sinus sequestration and preserving valve-in-valve options.

The use of aortic annular enlargement has nearly doubled from 2015 to 2023 (6% to 13%), as the concept of patient-prosthesis mismatch and valve-in-valve transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR) expands.1 The Konno-Rastan aortoventriculoplasty, introduced in 1975 by Konno and Rastan,2,3 provides true enlargement of the subvalvular, annular, and supravalvular components of the aortic root.4 In patients with failed TAVR and constrained aortic root anatomy, valve-in-valve intervention is frequently precluded because of the risk of coronary obstruction from sinus sequestration, necessitating explantation. This technique permits implantation of a larger prosthesis while mitigating the risk of sinus sequestration by enlarging both the left-right sinuses. Compared with other annular-enlargement techniques, this optimizes aortic root geometry for future valve-in-valve transcatheter intervention. Informed patient consent was obtained for video recording and publication of anonymized data for research purposes. Institutional review board approval was not required.

Surgical Steps

After median sternotomy, cardiopulmonary bypass is established via the lesser curvature of the aortic arch and right atrial venous cannulation. Myocardial protection is achieved using antegrade and retrograde cold blood cardioplegia with left ventricular venting.

TAVR Explant and Aortic Valve Debridement

After cardioplegic arrest, a transverse aortotomy is performed, with care taken not to extend excessively posteriorly toward the aortopulmonary window (Video 1). The 23-mm SAPIEN 3 (Edwards Lifesciences) is exposed, the stent frame is crushed with 2 Kocher clamps, and the valve is circumferentially dissected from the annulus. Annular sizing demonstrates accommodation of a 21-mm prosthesis; given the risk of patient-prosthesis mismatch, annular enlargement is indicated.

Right Ventricular Outflow Tract (RVOT) Incision

Next, the RVOT is incised transversely with a no. 11 blade approximately 2 cm below the pulmonary valve, ensuring a safe distance from the right coronary branches. Stay sutures are placed to retract the right ventricular wall, exposing the septal plane.

Extension of Aortotomy Through the Interventricular Septum

The aortotomy is extended between the right coronary ostium and the right-left commissure, in order to avoid injury to the conduction system.

Connecting Incisions and Extending Through the Interventricular Septum

A connecting incision is made in the ventriculo-infundibular fold, extending into the interventricular septum 1 to 2 cm below the annular plane. The entire anatomy is well visualized after this incision, and the depth of the septal incision is determined on the basis of the degree of enlargement needed.

Patch Augmentation of the Septal Defect

Two bovine pericardial patches are prepared. The first patch is long and tear-shaped and is used to augment the ventricular septal defect inferiorly and the aortic sinuses superiorly. It is important to keep this patch wide at the level of the new annulus to allow a larger prosthesis and long enough to close the entire aortotomy. The second patch is hemi-tear shaped, which is used to augment the RVOT. The width should match the first patch at the level of annulus and be kept long to close the RVOT.

The inferior limb of the first patch is secured to the interventricular septum with a continuous running 4-0 PROLENE suture, restoring the contour of the left ventricular outflow tract. Annular sizing is then repeated. At the transition point between native annulus and patches, an additional horizontal mattress suture is used to reinforce this junction to minimize the risk of bleeding, because the bleeding from this area is difficult to control.

Valve Implantation

Nonpledgeted horizontal mattress 2-0 ETHIBOND sutures are placed circumferentially in the native annulus. A neoaortic annular line is marked on the patch at the selected annular level, and sutures are then passed sequentially through the outer side of the RVOT patch and the ventricular septal patch at the neoannulus. A 27-mm INSPIRIS RESILIA valve (Edwards Lifesciences) was chosen in this case. Valve sutures are passed through the sewing ring and secured.

RVOT Closure

The RVOT patch is secured to the right ventricular margin with a running 4-0 PROLENE suture, with meticulous attention paid to the patch corners to ensure hemostasis and avoid injury to the pulmonic valve. The patch is then trimmed to preserve the native RVOT contour.

Aortotomy Closure

The aortotomy is closed using the superior limb of the internal patch in a continuous running fashion with 4-0 PROLENE. After this, the heart is deaired, and the aortic crossclamp is removed. After the patient is weaned from cardiopulmonary bypass, the aortic prosthesis is assessed on a transesophageal echocardiogram.

Discussion

The Konno-Rastan procedure offers several distinct advantages when future valve-in-valve intervention is anticipated. First, it enables unrestricted true annular (virtual basal ring) and left ventricular outflow tract enlargement, allowing implantation of a larger prosthesis. Unlike posterior enlargement techniques (Nick, Manougian, Yang) that predominantly expand the noncoronary sinus, the Konno-Rastan procedure enlarges the left and right coronary sinuses in proportion to annular upsizing. This balanced root expansion preserves sinus geometry and reduces the risk of coronary obstruction during future valve-in-valve transcatheter intervention. Finally, unlike the Y-technique, which does not enlarge the virtual basal ring, resulting in prosthesis tilt, the Konno procedure enables implantation of a well-aligned valve, avoiding a potential technical barrier to future valve-in-valve intervention. Conversely, the procedure is more complex than other annular-enlargement techniques and is associated with increased operative time and bleeding risk. In addition, disruption of the RVOT and pulmonary valve function, resulting in right ventricular volume overload and failure, has been reported.5,6 This may be poorly tolerated in patients with pre-existing pulmonary hypertension or impaired right ventricular function. Finally, because of the disruption of specialized conduction tissue within the interventricular septum, complete heart block requiring permanent pacemaker implantation may occur and has been reported in up to 11% of cases.5

Conclusions

Konno-Rastan aortoventriculoplasty after TAVR explant provides definitive annular and root enlargement, enabling larger valve implantation while preserving future valve-in-valve options.

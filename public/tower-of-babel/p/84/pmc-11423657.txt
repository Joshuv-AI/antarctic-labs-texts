Influence of immunological awareness on enhancing the overall complying with health instructions and necessary vaccines during epidemics and pandemics
Authors: Zainab H. Almansour
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTImmunological awareness plays a pivotal role in promoting adherence to health instructions during public health emergencies. This study assessed the influence of specialized immunology education on compliance behaviors among higher education students. To assess the influence of specialized immunology education on compliance behaviors toward health instructions and necessary vaccines during epidemics and pandemics among higher education students. A cross-sectional survey was conducted among 532 students at King Faisal University, Saudi Arabia using stratified random sampling. A validated questionnaire examined demographics, health awareness, and specific immunological knowledge. Regression analysis was performed to assess predictors of compliance with health instructions. Considerable knowledge gaps were found around fundamental immunological concepts despite fair awareness about vaccines. Marked disparities existed across gender and academic disciplines. Regression modeling established specialized immunology training as a significant predictor of compliance with guidelines like masking and vaccination during epidemics (p < .001). The findings highlight the need for customized immunology curricula targeting students from nonscientific backgrounds. Incorporating immunology training in continuing education programs for healthcare professionals and public officials can further promote compliance. Multifaceted public health campaigns combining immunology education with initiatives to address socio-cultural barriers are warranted. Further research should explore sustainable educational interventions to enhance long-term retention and integration of immunology knowledge into health behaviors.

Introduction

Immunology is a pivotal branch of biomedical science that explores the immune system’s intricate operations in both health and disease.1 immunology education plays a crucial role in shaping public health outcomes by equipping individuals with the knowledge needed to make informed health decisions, particularly in the context of vaccine uptake and adherence to public health guidelines.2 This awareness fosters higher rates of vaccine acceptance and adherence to public health guidelines, crucial for controlling infectious diseases. Moreover, a well-informed public is less susceptible to misinformation, which is vital in an era where false claims about vaccines and immune responses proliferate rapidly online.3

Educational initiatives in immunology also serve as a frontline defense against the spread of misinformation.4 The relationship between immunology education and vaccine acceptance is symbiotic; as understanding increases, so does trust in vaccinations as a tool for public health.5 Therefore, investing in immunology education not only enhances individual knowledge but also contributes to the broader goal of achieving herd immunity, underscoring its pivotal role in safeguarding community health.6

The advent of social media and the internet has profoundly influenced public knowledge on immunology, serving as platforms for the rapid dissemination of both accurate health information and harmful misinformation, thereby impacting public health decisions and perceptions toward vaccination.7

Targeting higher education students for immunology education is crucial due to their role as future leaders and healthcare professionals. These students, at a pivotal point in their educational careers, are poised to influence public health policies, research, and practices.8 Educating them on immunology equips them with the knowledge to drive innovations in healthcare, advocate for effective public health measures, and contribute to informed public discourse on health issues.9 Moreover, this demographic’s diverse backgrounds introduce varying levels of prior immunological awareness, suggesting tailored educational approaches could enhance learning outcomes.10 By investing in the immunology education of higher education students, society can ensure a well-informed cadre of professionals ready to tackle future public health challenges, promoting initiatives that support community health and combat misinformation effectively.11

Moreover, embedding immunology within broader health education and public health campaigns ensures that students not only grasp the scientific underpinnings but also understand the practical implications of immunological science in addressing global health challenges, vaccine advocacy, and the importance of herd immunity, thereby preparing them to be informed citizens and healthcare advocates.12

Immunological awareness plays a pivotal role in enhancing compliance with health instructions and the uptake of necessary vaccines during epidemics and pandemics. By understanding the mechanisms of the immune system and the effectiveness of vaccines in preventing infectious diseases, individuals are more likely to adhere to public health guidelines and vaccination campaigns.13 This knowledge empowers the public to make informed decisions about their health, contributing to the broader effort to control and mitigate the spread of contagious diseases.

Moreover, heightened immunological awareness among the population facilitates the dissemination of accurate information, countering misinformation and vaccine hesitancy, which are significant barriers to achieving widespread immunization.14 In times of health crises, such as pandemics, a well-informed public is crucial for the successful implementation of vaccination programs and adherence to preventive measures, ultimately leading to reduced transmission rates, lower morbidity, and mortality, and a more rapid return to normalcy.15

Method

Design

This study employed a cross-sectional design to investigate the influence of immunological awareness on compliance with health instructions and vaccine uptake among higher education students during pandemics. This approach was chosen to capture a snapshot of students’ knowledge and behaviors at a specific point in time, providing insights into the correlation between educational exposure to immunology and health-related actions in the context of global health crises.

Setting

The research was conducted at King Faisal University, located in Al Ahsa, Saudi Arabia. The university was selected due to its comprehensive academic programs and diverse student body, offering a rich context for exploring the impact of educational backgrounds on immunological awareness. The study was carried out over a three-month period from January to March 2024, coinciding with an ongoing global health alert to any possible novel viral outbreak.

Sample and sampling procedure

A total of 532 students were selected using stratified random sampling to ensure representation across various faculties, including Health Sciences, Engineering, and Humanities. The sample size was determined based on the university’s student population, aiming for a confidence level of 95% and a margin of error of 5%. Eligibility criteria included being a currently enrolled student at King Faisal University, with participants ranging in age from 18 to 25 years.

Selection process

The selection process for this study was designed to ensure broad and voluntary participation from the student body. The questionnaire was distributed electronically to all students enrolled at King Faisal University via the university’s learning management system. This method was chosen to facilitate easy access and completion of the survey, leveraging the digital platform that students frequently use for their academic activities. Participation in the study was entirely voluntary, and students were informed about the study’s purpose and their right to withdraw at any time without any consequences. Regular reminders were sent to encourage participation and increase the response rate. The use of the learning management system ensured that the questionnaire reached a diverse and representative sample of the university’s student population.

To clarify the inclusion criteria and address any discrepancies in age, all participants were required to be at least 18 years old to be eligible for the study. This criterion was strictly adhered to during the data collection process. However, due to an oversight, the initial table included participants aged 17.

Data collection tool

Data were collected using a structured questionnaire developed specifically for this study (supplementary material). The questionnaire comprised three sections: (1) Demographic information, (2) General health awareness, and (3) Specific knowledge about immunology and vaccines. The instrument was validated through a pilot study involving 50 students not included in the main study, ensuring reliability and clarity of the questions.

Questionnaire details

All questions from the questionnaire are listed in the tables provided within the Results section. This approach ensures transparency and allows for a comprehensive understanding of the survey items and their respective responses.

Formal training in immunology

“Formal training in immunology” is defined as having completed at least one course specifically focused on immunology. This includes courses that cover the fundamental principles of immunology, the immune system’s mechanisms, and the role of immunology in health and disease.

Data collection procedure

Questionnaires were distributed electronically via the university’s learning management system. Participants were given a two-week window to complete the survey, with reminders sent at regular intervals. Confidentiality was maintained by anonymizing responses, and informed consent was obtained electronically before participation.

Data analysis

Data analysis was conducted using SPSS version 26. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize demographic information and baseline knowledge levels. Inferential statistics, including multiple regression analysis, were employed to examine the relationship between demographic variables, immunological awareness, and compliance with health instructions. The level of significance was set at p < .05 for all tests.

Ethical consideration

The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of King Faisal University, approval number (KFU-REC-2024-MAR-ETHICS2159). Ethical considerations were meticulously observed, with all participants providing informed consent after being briefed about the study’s purpose, their right to withdraw at any time, and the measures in place to protect their privacy and data integrity.

Discussion

The findings from this study provide important insights into the relationship between immunological knowledge and health behaviors, specifically compliance with instructions during public health crises like epidemics and pandemics. The results highlight significant gaps in immunology understanding among higher education students, while also revealing key demographic and educational factors that impact awareness levels and compliance actions. These findings carry crucial implications for immunology education initiatives, public health policy, and future research.

The thesis of this study posits that immunologic knowledge correlates with health behaviors, particularly compliance with public health instructions during pandemics and epidemics. This correlation is evidenced by the observed adherence to vaccination protocols, mask-wearing, and social distancing measures among participants with higher levels of immunological understanding.16

For instance, the study found that participants with formal training in immunology were significantly more likely to comply with vaccination guidelines and other preventive measures. This suggests that a robust understanding of immunological principles equips individuals with the knowledge necessary to appreciate the importance of these behaviors, thereby enhancing compliance.17

Furthermore, the data demonstrate that knowledge about how vaccines prime the immune system and the differentiation between antibodies and antibiotics are critical factors that drive adherence to recommended health practices. These behaviors, influenced by immunologic knowledge, are vital for controlling the spread of infectious diseases and ensuring public health safety during crises.

A core finding is the considerable variation in immunological knowledge and awareness observed across the study sample. While a majority demonstrated accurate understanding on some concepts, such as vaccines priming immune memory, notable misconceptions prevailed around key principles like the difference between antibodies and antibiotics. This variable knowledge aligns with previous studies showing inconsistent immunology comprehension in the general public and even among healthcare professionals.16,17 The limited understanding of fundamental immune mechanisms likely hinders individuals’ ability to make informed health decisions, posing a major barrier to promoting compliance.18

Additionally, the analysis highlights significant disparities in immunological awareness across demographic factors like gender and educational background. Females displayed higher knowledge than males, contrasting some studies reporting higher immunology literacy among males,19 but concurring with others noting female predominance.20 More crucially, students from health sciences and scientific disciplines showed markedly higher awareness than their humanities peers.21 This trend has been consistently reported across the literature, reflecting the pivotal role formal training plays in shaping immunology comprehension.22,23 The findings emphasize the need for tailored educational initiatives targeting underrepresented demographics to close these knowledge gaps.

The relationship between specialized immunology education and compliance behavior underscores the need for a multidisciplinary approach in public health education. It suggests that integrating immunology into various educational curriculums, not just those focused on health sciences, could be beneficial.24 This integration could foster a more immunologically literate society, better prepared to understand and respond to public health advisories and vaccination campaigns.25 It aligns with the broader educational strategy of STEAM (Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics), which advocates for a holistic approach to education that equips students with diverse and applicable knowledge across fields.26

The findings highlight the potential for leveraging digital platforms and social media to disseminate immunological knowledge effectively. Given the study’s indication that traditional demographic factors such as age and sex are not significant predictors of compliance, digital education strategies could be universally targeted.27 Online platforms offer an accessible and scalable means to reach wide audiences, including those outside the traditional educational settings.28 The success of digital public health campaigns during the COVID-19 pandemic, for instance, illustrates the efficacy of online platforms in enhancing public health awareness and action.29

Furthermore, the study’s results prompt a reevaluation of public health communication strategies.30 The effectiveness of immunology education in enhancing compliance suggests that public health messages could be more impactful if they were framed within an educational context.31 Instead of merely instructing the public on what actions to take, health communications could focus on explaining why these actions are necessary from an immunological perspective.32 This approach could lead to a deeper understanding among the public, fostering a sense of empowerment and proactive engagement with health guidelines.33

Additionally, the lack of significance in demographic variables such as age and sex in predicting compliance behaviors suggests that inherent interest or motivation toward health compliance may transcend these factors.34 This observation could inspire further research into the psychological and social determinants of health behavior compliance, exploring how personal beliefs, social norms, and perceived susceptibility influence individuals’ responses to health instructions.35

Knowledge discrepancy: vaccine priming vs. antibodies and antibiotics

The discrepancy where participants are aware of vaccine priming and immune memory but still confuse antibodies and antibiotics could be due to several reasons. Firstly, vaccine priming and immune memory are often covered in general biology and health education curricula, making them more familiar to students.36 These concepts are also frequently discussed in public health campaigns, especially during vaccination drives, which could enhance awareness.37 Secondly, the terms “antibodies” and “antibiotics” sound similar, which can lead to confusion. This linguistic similarity might contribute to misunderstandings, especially if the distinctions are not clearly taught or emphasized.38 Thirdly, curricula may place more emphasis on vaccines and their role in disease prevention due to their immediate public health relevance. In contrast, the distinction between antibodies and antibiotics might not receive the same level of attention, resulting in gaps in knowledge.39

An interesting finding in this study is the discrepancy between the high awareness of vaccine priming and immune memory and the confusion between antibodies and antibiotics. This may be attributed to the greater emphasis placed on vaccine education in public health campaigns, especially during pandemics, compared to detailed discussions on the differences between antibodies and antibiotics. The similarity in terminology could also contribute to this confusion. Addressing this gap in understanding could be crucial for improving overall immunological literacy

Importance of push-pull mechanisms in disseminating educational tools

The dissemination of educational tools plays a critical role in enhancing public awareness and compliance with health instructions, particularly during public health emergencies. Push-pull mechanisms are essential in this context as they ensure that educational resources are not only available but also accessible and easy to understand for the general public.40

Push mechanisms involve proactively distributing information to the target audience. This can include public health campaigns using traditional media (television, radio) and digital platforms (social media, websites) to broadcast educational content about immunology and health practices.41 Community outreach by engaging with community leaders and organizations to distribute educational materials and conduct informational sessions is another effective push strategy. Additionally, incorporating immunology education into school curricula and continuing education programs for healthcare professionals and public officials ensures a widespread and consistent dissemination of crucial health information.42

Pull mechanisms, on the other hand, focus on creating avenues through which individuals can seek out and access information when they need it. Developing user-friendly websites and mobile applications that provide comprehensive information on immunology, vaccines, and health practices is a key pull strategy.43 Establishing dedicated support lines and helpdesks where individuals can get answers to their health-related queries also enhances accessibility. Moreover, creating interactive tools such as chatbots, virtual assistants, and online forums where individuals can engage with experts and obtain reliable information ensures that the public has multiple avenues to access important health information.44

Inferences and correlations

While this study provides valuable insights into the relationship between immunological knowledge and health behaviors, it is important to clarify that definitive correlations between immunological knowledge and compliance behaviors are beyond the scope of this research. The data allows us to make some inferences about how knowledge might influence actions, but establishing a direct and causal relationship requires more extensive investigation.

The study’s findings indicate that students with higher levels of immunological knowledge are more likely to report behaviors such as adherence to vaccination and compliance with public health guidelines. However, these observations should be interpreted with caution. The cross-sectional design of the study captures a snapshot in time, which is useful for identifying patterns and associations but not for proving causation.

Additionally, the self-reported nature of the data introduces potential biases, such as social desirability bias, where participants might overreport compliance with health behaviors. This limitation further complicates the ability to draw definitive correlations between knowledge and behavior. Future research should employ longitudinal designs and objective measures of behavior to more accurately assess how immunological knowledge impacts health compliance over time.

Implication of the study

The insights from this study should inform multifaceted public health campaigns, combining immunology education with complementary interventions to address socio-cultural barriers to compliance. While enhancing knowledge is indispensable, countering misinformation and anti-vaccine sentiments also requires community-centric initiatives to build trust in public health authorities. Adopting culturally appropriate, context-specific approaches tailored to community values and addressing access barriers could maximize compliance beyond just raising immunological awareness.

The gaps in immunological knowledge revealed by this study suggest that current educational practices may be inadequate in instilling a robust and accurate understanding of immune mechanisms in the public. The persistence of misconceptions around fundamental concepts indicates that simply covering immunology curricula is insufficient, and innovative pedagogical techniques are needed to correct misinformation and drive meaningful comprehension. Interactive modules, repeated reinforcement, assessment of prior knowledge, and addressing cultural barriers could be more impactful.

The regression analysis provides actionable evidence for policymakers to mandate expanded immunology education, both through curricular additions and complementary awareness campaigns. However, solely increasing instructional time on immunology cannot be expected to directly translate into behavioral change. A nuanced understanding of the psychology and motivators of compliance is needed to convert knowledge into action. Teaching self-efficacy, conveying the collective benefits of public health compliance, and role-modeling positive behaviors should accompany educational efforts.

Overall, an interdisciplinary framework combining immunology education, health promotion, behavioral psychology, and public policy perspectives is required to translate knowledge into protective behaviors and improved population health outcomes.

Limitations

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. Firstly, the reliance on self-reported data introduces the potential for response biases, such as social desirability bias, where participants might provide answers they perceive as favorable rather than their true beliefs or behaviors. Secondly, the design of the questionnaire may have inherent limitations in capturing the full spectrum of participants’ immunological knowledge and compliance behaviors. Some important aspects of immunological concepts might not have been adequately covered, and the phrasing of questions might have influenced the responses. Thirdly, the cross-sectional nature of the study limits the ability to infer causality between immunological knowledge and compliance behaviors. While associations can be identified, it is not possible to determine whether higher immunological knowledge directly causes increased compliance with health guidelines.

Additionally, the sample is drawn from a single university, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to broader populations. The demographic characteristics of the participants, such as their age, educational background, and gender distribution, may not be representative of other student populations or the general public. Furthermore, the study was conducted during a specific period coinciding with an ongoing global health alert, which might have influenced the participants’ awareness and attitudes toward immunological concepts and health behaviors.

Finally, while the study highlights significant knowledge gaps and areas for educational improvement, it does not delve deeply into the psychological and social determinants that influence health behavior compliance. Future research should explore these factors to provide a more comprehensive understanding of how to effectively translate immunological knowledge into consistent and informed health behaviors. By addressing these limitations, future studies can build on the findings of this research to develop more effective educational interventions and public health strategies.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study substantiates the pivotal link between immunological awareness and compliance with preventive health behaviors, while also revealing critical knowledge gaps among higher education students. The findings underscore the need for expanded immunology education across academic disciplines and targeted public health messaging to maximize crisis-time compliance. Ultimately, investing in evidence-based, demographically inclusive immunology education and outreach initiatives can profoundly strengthen pandemic preparedness by empowering individuals and communities with the knowledge to make informed decisions that protect collective health and safety. Integrative policies tackling access barriers and sociocultural influences alongside robust immunology education can unlock the full potential of such initiatives in driving behavior change and resilience against emerging public health threats.

Exploring the potential role of ADIPOR2 related to immune microenvironment and metabolism dysfunction in non-alcoholic steatohepatitis
Authors: Lei Lei, Xinyu Huang, Yun Hu, Wanying Yu, Di Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) is a metabolic disorder, and immune-mediated inflammation plays an important role in the progression of the disease. AdipoR2, encoded by ADIPOR2, is closely involved in the pathogenesis of NASH through adiponectin signaling. However, the relationship between ADIPOR2 and the immune microenvironment and metabolic dysfunction in NASH patients remains unexplored. NASH datasets were collected from the public database. ADIPOR2 expression level and its diagnostic value were explored. Then, the relationships among ADIPOR2, the immune microenvironment, and metabolic dysfunction were investigated. ADIPOR2-related genes were identified, a regulatory network was established, and drug sensitivity analysis was conducted. In addition, ADIPOR2 was also verified in clinical samples. ADIPOR2 expression was significantly increased in NASH patients compared to healthy controls and shows better diagnostic value. Immune infiltration analysis found that T cells CD4 memory resting and dendritic cells resting were significantly different in healthy controls versus NASH patients, and ADIPOR2-low versus ADIPOR2-high expression groups. Gene set enrichment analysis and gene set variation analysis showed that metabolism-related pathways (such as HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS, HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM, and HALLMARK_PEROXISOME) were significantly upregulated in NASH and ADIPOR2-high expression groups. Correlation analysis revealed that ADIPOR2 expression was closely linked to these immune cells and metabolism-related pathways. Then, 16 ADIPOR2-related genes were identified, and the miRNAs-ADIPOR2-TFs regulatory network was constructed. In addition, patients in the ADIPOR2-low expression group showed higher sensitivity to drugs. ADIPOR2 may be a potential diagnostic target and could be related to immune cells and metabolic dysfunction in NASH.

1. Introduction

Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) is a hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome, which is the progressive form of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) and progresses to liver fibrosis, cirrhosis, and liver cancer.[1,2] It is estimated that the number of NASH patients will increase by 48% to 48.26 million cases in China by 2030.[3] The typical histopathological features of NASH encompass hepatocellular steatosis, hepatocellular ballooning, mixed lobular and portal inflammation, and zone 3 perisinusoidal fibrosis.[4] The pathogenesis of NASH is characterized by reprogramming of hepatic metabolism and microenvironment.[5] Initially, metabolic stress-induced hepatocellular injury triggers homeostatic responses, while persistent injury can lead to maladaptive inflammatory responses, resulting in the transition from simple steatosis to NASH.[5] Despite our advanced understanding of NASH’s pathological mechanisms, there are still no effective treatments available.[6]

During NASH, the liver’s immune cell population undergoes significant remodeling, contributing to an uncontrolled inflammatory environment, which process exacerbates liver injury, hepatocyte death, and fibrosis.[7] Neutrophil infiltration is an early event in NASH that promotes inflammation and liver injury.[8] The accumulation of B cells, dendritic cells (DCs), and monocytes has been reported in NASH mice, leading to enhanced inflammation.[9–11] The critical involvement of T cells, primarily CD4+ T helper and CD8+ T cytotoxic subsets, in NASH pathogenesis is evidenced by the absence of steatosis and hepatic inflammation in T cell-deficient murine models.[12]

Adiponectin, a 30 kDa multimeric protein predominantly secreted by white adipose tissue, serves as an insulin sensitizer and alleviates insulin resistance in NASH.[13,14] Adiponectin primarily signals through 2 receptors: AdipoR1 and AdipoR2, both characterized by a 7-transmembrane domain structure.[15] While AdipoR1 is predominantly expressed in skeletal muscle, AdipoR2 exhibits higher expression levels in the liver.[15] Adiponectin/AdipoR2 participated in fatty acid (FA) oxidation to modulate the progression of NASH through the activation of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor alpha (PPAR-α).[16] Therefore, exploring ADIPOR2’s relationship with the immune microenvironment and metabolic dysfunction in NASH may enhance clinical understanding and improve treatment strategies.

In this study, the expression level and diagnostic value of ADIPOR2 were evaluated in public datasets. Then, the relationships among ADIPOR2, the immune microenvironment, and metabolism were investigated. Next, the ADIPOR2-related genes were identified, and the regulatory network was constructed. Finally, the drug sensitivity analysis was performed. Our findings may provide new insights into the immune microenvironment and metabolism dysfunction associated with ADIPOR2 in NASH.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data acquisition

A total of 6 datasets (GSE260666, GSE89632, GSE63067, GSE37031, GSE202167, and GSE217235) were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. The GSE260666 dataset includes 10 liver tissue samples from 6 healthy controls and 4 NASH patients. The GSE89632 dataset consists of 43 liver tissue samples from 19 NASH patients and 24 healthy controls. The GSE63067 dataset contains 16 liver tissue samples from 9 NASH patients and 7 healthy controls. The GSE37031 dataset involves 15 liver tissue samples from 7 healthy controls and 8 NASH patients. The GSE202167 dataset, a miRNA dataset, includes 6 peripheral blood samples from 3 NASH patients and 3 healthy controls. The GSE217235 is a single-cell dataset containing live tissue samples from 3 NASH patients. The GSE89632, GSE63067, and GSE37031 datasets were combined, and the batch effect was eliminated using the “combat” function.

2.2. Assessment of the expression and diagnostic value of ADIPOR2

The expression levels of ADIPOR2 in the GSE260666, GSE89632, GSE63067, GSE37031, and combined datasets were evaluated and visualized using box plots. Receiver operating characteristic analysis was carried out to assess the diagnostic value of ADIPOR2 in these 5 datasets through the “pROC” package. The area under the curve (AUC) was used to quantify the diagnostic accuracy of ADIPOR2.

2.3. Real-time PCR verification

In this study, a total of 19 blood samples were collected for real-time PCR validation, including 10 samples from control individuals and 9 samples from patients with NASH. The present study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital, School of Medicine, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China (2022-222-1). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The blood RNA extraction kit (R4163-02, Magen) was used to extract total RNA from samples. The FastQuant cDNA first-strand synthesis kit (KR106, TIANGEN) was employed for reverse transcription to synthesize cDNA. The SuperReal PreMix Plus (SYBR Green; FP205, TIANGEN) was utilized for real-time PCR. The amplification reaction was completed using a Gene-9660 fluorescence quantitative PCR instrument, and the relative quantitative analysis of gene expression was performed using the 2−ΔΔCT method. GAPDH (GAPDH-F: GGAGCGAGATCCCTCCAAAAT; GAPDH-R: GGCTGTTGTCATACTTCTCATGG) and ACTB (ACTB-F: CATGTACGTTGCTATCCAGGC; ACTB-R: CTCCTTAATGTCACGCACGAT) were the internal reference. The primer sequences for ADIPOR2 were: ADIPOR2‑F: CTGGATGGTACACGAAGAGGT; ADIPOR2‑R: TGGGCTTGTAAGAGAGGGGAC.

2.4. Immune microenvironment analysis

In the combined dataset, NASH samples were divided into ADIPOR2-high and ADIPOR2-low expression groups based on the ADIPOR2 expression levels. The relative scores of immune cells were calculated using CIBERSORT algorithms.[17] Then, the immune cell infiltration levels were compared between ADIPOR2-low and ADIPOR2-high expression groups using t test. In addition, differences in immune infiltration levels between controls and NASH patients in the combined dataset were explored using the Wilcoxon test. Furthermore, correlations between immune cells and ADIPOR2 expression were investigated through Pearson correlation analysis.

2.5. The relationship between ADIPOR2 and the signature gene sets in the NASH

Based on the combined dataset, gene set enrichment analysis was performed using GSEA 4.0.3 software on the 50 gene sets included in the HALLMARK category of the MSigDB database (https://www.gsea-msigdb.org/gsea/msigdb/). This analysis aimed to explore the differences in these 50 signature gene sets between ADIPOR2-high and ADIPOR2-low expression groups. Then, significantly different signature gene sets were compared between controls and NASH patients by the Wilcox test in the combined dataset using the “GSVA” (version 1.30.0) package. Pearson correlation analysis was employed to explore the relationship between ADIPOR2 and signature gene sets.

2.6. The relationship between immune cells and metabolism

First, the link between immune cells and signature gene sets was analyzed in the combined dataset using Pearson correlation analysis. In the single-cell GSE217235 datasets, the correlation was also investigated. In brief, the “Seurat” was utilized for quality control. Cells with a mitochondrial gene content ≥25%, total genes <200, and genes expressed in <3 cells were all filtered. Next, the “NormalizeData” function was employed to perform data normalization, and data was scaled and centered through the “ScaleData” function. Then, the “SingleR” package was used to annotate for each cluster. Finally, the “scMetabolism” package was used to explore the association between immune cells and metabolism.

2.7. Identification of ADIPOR2-related differential expressed genes

The differential expressed analysis was performed between ADIPOR2-low and ADIPOR2-high expression groups in the combined dataset using the “limma” (version 3.46.0) package. The differential expressed genes (DEGs) with |log2 fold change (FC)| >0.5 & P value <.05 were obtained. Subsequently, based on |cor| >0.3 & P value <.05, the ADIPOR2-related DEGs were further selected. Then, functional enrichment analysis was carried out using the GeneCodis4 (https://genecodis.genyo.es/) database. The protein–protein interaction (PPI) analysis among DEGs was carried out using the STRING database (https://cn.string-db.org/).

2.8. Construction of ADIPOR2-related regulatory network

The ADIPOR2-related miRNAs were obtained from the TargetScan8.0 (https://www.targetscan.org/vert_80/), miRDB (https://mirdb.org/), miRTarBase (https://mirtarbase.cuhk.edu.cn/~miRTarBase/miRTarBase_2019/php/index.php), and ENCORI (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/index.php) databases. Then, the miRNAs from 4 databases were intersected to gain intersection miRNAs. Next, differentially expressed miRNAs (DEmiRNAs) with |log2FC| >1 & P value <.05 were acquired in the GSE202167 dataset using the “edgeR” package of R, and the downregulated DEmiRNAs were intersected with intersection miRNAs to obtain target miRNAs.

Based on the databases where targeted miRNAs were located, the target genes present in at least 2 databases were retained. Then, ADIPOR2-related transcription factors (TFs) were collected from the Cistrome DB (http://genemania.org), followed by intersecting with target genes to gain intersection TFs. Finally, the miRNA-ADIPOR2-TFs regulatory network was constructed.

2.9. Drug sensitivity analysis and molecular docking

The correlation between ADIPOR2 expression and drug sensitivity was investigated in the combined dataset through the “oncoPredict” (version 1.2) package of R. Drugs with |cor| >0.4 & P value <.05 were obtained. Next, the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values were compared in ADIPOR2-low versus ADIPOR2-high expression and healthy controls versus NASH patients groups using the t test. Subsequently, molecular docking was conducted. The 3D structural files of ADIPOR2 and drugs were downloaded from the Research Collaboratory for Structural Bioinformatics Protein Data Bank (http://www.rcsb.org/pdb/home/home.do) and Public Chemical Database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/), respectively. The AutoDockTools were utilized to carry out molecular docking, and the results were visualized through PyMol software.

3. Results

3.1. The expression level and diagnostic value of ADIPOR2

After removing the batch effect, the combined dataset comprised 38 healthy controls and 36 NASH patients (Fig. S1, Supplemental Digital Content 1). The expression levels of ADIPOR2 were analyzed in the GSE260666, GSE89632, GSE63067, GSE37031, and combined datasets. We found that the expression levels of ADIOPR2 were upregulated in the NASH patients compared to healthy controls across all datasets (Fig. 1A–E). Furthermore, real-time PCR results showed that the expression trend of ADIPOR2 in clinical samples was consistent with those in public datasets (Fig. 1F). The AUC values for ADIPOR2 were 1.000, 0.741, 0.794, 0.857, and 0.774 in the GSE260666, GSE89632, GSE63067, GSE37031, and combined datasets, respectively (Fig. 2).

The expression levels of ADIPOR2 in the GSE260666 (A), GSE89632 (B), GSE63067 (C), GSE37031 (D), combined datasets (E), and clinical samples (F). *P < .05; **P < .01; ***P < .001; ****P < .0001.

The ROC analysis of ADIPOR2 in the GSE260666 (A), GSE89632 (B), GSE63067 (C), GSE37031 (D), and combined datasets (E). ROC = receiver operating characteristic.

3.2. Relationship between ADIPOR2 and immune microenvironment

The percentage of each immune cell in the combined dataset is presented in Figure 3A. Immune infiltration analysis showed that B cells naïve, plasma cells, monocytes, DCs activated, and mast cells activated exhibited lower infiltration levels, while T cells CD4 memory resting, T cells gamma delta, macrophages M1, macrophages M2, DCs resting, and mast cells resting had higher infiltration levels in the healthy controls compared to the NASH patients in the combined dataset (Fig. 3B). Then, the patients in combined dataset were divided into ADIPOR2-high and ADIPOR2-low expression groups according to the expression level of ADIPOR2. The low infiltration levels of T cells CD4 memory resting and DCs resting were observed in the ADIPOR2-high expression group (Fig. 3C). Correlation analysis revealed that T cells CD4 memory resting and DCs resting were both significantly negatively correlated with ADIPOR2 expression (Fig. 3D).

3.3. Relationship between ADIPOR2 and signature gene sets

The enrichment status of 50 signature gene sets was investigated between ADIPOR2-low and ADIPOR2-high expression groups. Six gene sets were significantly upregulated in the ADIPOR2-high expression group: HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS, HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM, HALLMARK_PEROXISOME, HALLMARK_XENOBIOTIC_METABOLISM, HALLMARK_MTORC1_SIGNALING, and HALLMARK_KRAS_SIGNALING_UP (Fig. 4). Moreover, HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS, HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM, and HALLMARK_PEROXISOME were significantly upregulated, while HALLMARK_KRAS_SIGNALING_UP was significantly downregulated in NASH patients compared to healthy controls (Fig. 5A). Except for HALLMARK_KRAS_SIGNALING_UP, ADIPOR2 expression was significantly positively correlated with other 3 signature gene sets (Fig. 5B). Among these, ADIPOR2 expression and HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS had the strongest correlation (r = 0.63), followed by HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM (r = 0.56), and HALLMARK_PEROXISOME (r = 0.51).

3.4. Relationship between immune cells and metabolism

The relationship between immune cells and metabolism was explored in the combined and single-cell GSE217235 datasets. In the combined datasets, correlation analysis revealed that T cells CD4 memory resting and DCs resting were significantly negatively correlated with HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS (Fig. 6). In addition, T cells CD4 memory resting also showed a significant negative correlation with HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM.

Correlation analysis between immune cells (T cells CD4 memory resting and DCs resting) and 3 pathways (HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS, HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM, and HALLMARK_PEROXISOME).

For the single-cell GSE217235 dataset, 7608 cells were subjected to further analysis after quality control. The cells were divided into 14 cell clusters using the t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) algorithm (Fig. 7A). Then, 7 cell clusters were annotated via the “singleR” package: T cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, monocytes, endothelial cells, epithelial cells, and hepatocytes (Fig. 7B). ADIPOR2 expression was predominantly observed in T cells, monocytes, endothelial cells, and CD4+ T cells (Fig. 7C). Subsequently, metabolic activity analysis showed that FA metabolism was primarily activated in hepatocytes and endothelial cells, with significant activation also observed in immune cells such as monocytes and CD4+ T cells (Fig. 7D).

3.5. Identification of ADIPOR2-related DEGs

A total of 21 DEGs were identified between ADIPOR2-low and ADIPOR2-high expression group, including 14 upregulated and 7 downregulated (Fig. 8A). Using criteria of P value <.05 and |cor| >0.3, we further refined this list to 16 DEGs (Fig. S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2). Functional enrichment analysis of these 16 DEGs found that these genes were primarily involved in the regulation of the BMP signaling pathway, keratinocyte proliferation, and establishment or maintenance of cell polarity (Fig. 8B). PPI network revealed that IGFBP2, SFRP5, ARNTL, and ADIPOR2 exhibit interaction relationships (Fig. 8C).

3.6. Construction of regulatory networks

In the miRNA GSE202167 dataset, 80 downregulated DEmiRNAs were obtained (Fig. 9A). After intersecting these DEmiRNAs with 784 miRNAs in TargetScan8.0, 184 miRNAs in miRDB, 47 miRNAs in miRTarBase, and 226 miRNAs in ENCORI, 4 intersection miRNAs (hsa-let-7f-5p, hsa-miR-98-5p, hsa-let-7g-5p, and hsa-miR-150-5p) were acquired (Fig. 9B).

The V type indicated miRNA, the circle represented ADIPOR2, and the rhombus denoted TF.

The target genes presented in at least 2 databases were retained among TargetScan8, miRDB, miRTarBase, ENCORI, and Cistrome DB. Then, ADIPOR2-related TFs were collected from the Cistrome DB, followed by intersecting with target genes to gain intersection TFs. Hsa-let-7f-5p, hsa-let-7g-5p, hsa-miR-98-5p, and hsa-miR-150-5p obtained 3 (YY1, MYCN, and SMC1A), 3 (YY1, MYCN, and SMC1A), 5 (TFAP2A, CDK8, YY1, MYCN, and SMC1A), and 7 (SPIB, CREB1, VEZF1, ZFX, STAT1, EP300, and ZBTB7A) TFs, respectively (Fig. S3, Supplemental Digital Content 3). Finally, a miRNAs-ADIPOR2-TFs network, including 4 miRNAs and 12 TFs, was constructed (Fig. 9C).

3.7. Drug sensitivity and molecular docking

A total of 8 drugs with P value <.05 & |cor| >0.4 were obtained, including IC-87114, SNS-032, BRD-K51490254, BRD-K70511574, CAL-101, Elocalcitol, Omacetaxine mepesuccinate, and Marinopyrrole A. The IC50 values of SNS-032, Omacetaxine mepesuccinate, and Marinopyrrole A showed significant differences between healthy controls and NASH patients, as well as between ADIPOR2-low and ADIPOR2-high expression groups (Fig. 10A, B). Molecular docking showed that the binding energy of CAL-101 and ADIPOR2 was −6.8 kcal/mol by binding VAL-2 and LEU-4 amino acid residues with 2 hydrogen bonds (Fig. 10C). The binding energy of Omacetaxine mepesuccinate and ADIPOR2 was −2.07 kcal/mol by binding LEU-54, ALA-55, and VAL-58 amino acid residues with 3 hydrogen bonds (Fig. 10D).

4. Discussion

NASH, a hepatic manifestation of metabolic syndrome, has emerged as a prominent etiology of chronic liver disease worldwide.[18] During NASH progression, the immune system plays a pivotal role, manifested by the infiltration and activation of diverse immune cell populations.[19] ADIPOR2 agonist could improve NASH and related fibrosis.[20] Therefore, the role of ADIPOR2 in the immune microenvironment and metabolism dysfunction was investigated in NASH in this study.

In this study, we found that ADIPOR2 expression was significantly increased in NASH patients compared to healthy controls and exhibited better diagnostic values. In liver tissue from patients with biopsy-proven NASH, ADIPOR2 expression was elevated compared to normal liver histology, which is consistent with our results.[21] Another study also demonstrated that ADIPOR2 expression was increased during NAFLD progression (including patients without NASH, patients with probable NASH, and patients with NASH) in a cohort of morbidly obese patients with NAFLD.[22] While histopathological assessment of liver biopsy specimens is the gold standard for NASH diagnosis and assessment, the accuracy of histological evidence is only moderate and often requires additional validation methods to ensure diagnostic reliability.[23] The AUC values of ADIPOR2 were all more than 0.7 in our study, suggesting that this gene may serve as additional information for liver biopsy.

The immune infiltration levels of T cells CD4 memory resting and DCs resting were higher in the NASH patients than those in healthy controls in this study. Interestingly, NASH patients in the ADIPOR2-high expression group exhibited lower infiltration levels of these immune cells compared to those in the ADIPOR2-low group. In addition, these immune cells were all significantly negatively correlated with ADIPOR2 expression. In NASH mice, CD4+ T cell infiltration level was significantly increased in the liver, and blocking this recruitment effectively attenuated liver inflammation and fibrosis.[24] An increased CD4+ memory T cell infiltration in the liver was observed in humanized mice with NASH and NASH patients.[25] In methionine-choline deficient diet-induced NASH mice, DCs were recruited to the liver, where they mature and display an activated phenotype.[26] These DCs suppress CD8+ T cell proliferation while reducing Toll-like receptor expression and cytokine production in innate immune cells.[26] Deczkowska et al also demonstrated that DCs were more abundant and activated in NASH, promoting the reprogramming of inflammatory T cells associated with disease progression.[11] These results highlight the importance of CD4+ T cells and DCs in the progression of NASH, and the relationship between ADIPOR2 expression and CD4+ T cells and DCs needs to be further investigated.

Our study found significant upregulation of HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS, HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM, and HALLMARK_PEROXISOME in controls versus NASH and ADIPOR2-low versus ADIPOR2-high expression groups. ADIPOR2 expression strongly correlated (r > 0.5) with 3 signature gene sets. Notably, T cells CD4 memory resting and DCs resting were significantly negatively correlated with HALLMARK_CHOLESTEROL_HOMEOSTASIS, while T cells CD4 memory resting also showed a significant negative correlation with HALLMARK_FATTY_ACID_METABOLISM. Lipotoxicity-induced liver injury is a crucial factor in NASH pathogenesis.[27] The cholesterol content was markedly elevated in the human liver in fibrotic NASH.[28] Cholesterol and its metabolites, as primary lipotoxic molecules, accelerate NASH progression upon accumulation, which may result from cholesterol-induced macrophage activation, organelle dysfunction, inflammatory cytokine secretion, and disrupted cholesterol-bile acid metabolism.[29] In addition, apolipoprotein E is a key protein in cholesterol homeostasis, and its deficiency resulted in cholesterol accumulated in the membrane of DCs and activated CD4+ T cells.[30]

In normal liver, steatosis, and NASH patients, FA metabolism is altered, resulting in increased levels of myristic acid, palmitic acid, and stearic acid levels in both serum and liver.[31] Inhibiting FA translocase CD36 palmitoylation in NASH mouse livers ameliorated FA metabolic dysfunction, reduced intracellular lipid accumulation, and attenuated inflammatory responses.[32] Moreover, suppressing the FA metabolic axis of LPL/FABP4/CPT1 can effectively prevent the progression of NASH to liver cancer.[33] In addition, Angela et al reported that the mTORC1-PPAR-γ signaling axis controlled FA uptake programs, which is crucial for the full activation and rapid proliferation of T cells CD4 memory.[34]

Under physiological conditions, peroxisomes primarily catabolize long-chain and branched-chain FA and mitigate oxidative stress, preventing lipid accumulation and reactive oxygen species.[35] The deficiency of acyl-CoA oxidase and catalase, key peroxisomal enzymes, leads to hepatic steatosis and subsequent NASH progression.[36,37] PPAR-α, predominantly expressed in the liver, is intricately linked to NASH pathogenesis, which may be associated with impaired beta-oxidation and induced lipogenesis.[38] Statins could prevent NASH by enhancing peroxisomal FA oxidation through the induction of PPAR-α.[39] Combined with the above results, we speculated that ADIPOR2 may participate in the progression of NASH by regulating these 3 metabolic pathways. Nevertheless, our research is based on bioinformatic analysis, and further experiments would be required to confirm our findings.

Through the construction of the PPI and the miRNAs-ADIPOR2-TFs networks, we found that multiple molecules such as SFRP5, hsa-miR-98-5p, and YY1 may be involved in mediating the regulatory role of ADIPOR2 in NASH. Studies have shown that SFRP5,[40] hsa-miR-98-5p,[41] and YY1[42] play important roles in NASH. However, the molecular mechanisms by which they regulate NASH through mediating ADIPOR2 warrant further investigation. In addition, the results of the drug sensitivity analysis indicated that drugs such as CAL-101, SNS-032, Omacetaxine mepesuccinate, and Marinopyrrole A may have certain value in the treatment of NASH. CAL-101 can alleviate hepatic fibrosis by inhibiting the activation of hepatic stellate cells and inducing apoptosis.[43] SNS-032, a cyclin-dependent kinase 9 inhibitor, attenuates liver fibrosis in carbon tetrachloride-induced mice.[44] Omacetaxine mepesuccinate, also knowns as Homoharringtonine, suppressed the progression of hepatocellular carcinoma.[45] However, the role of Marinopyrrole A in liver has not been reported. This study has laid a foundation for further investigating the applicability of these drugs in NASH therapy.

However, this study has certain limitations. In this study, through systematic bioinformatics analysis of public database data, the potential mechanism of ADIPOR2 may be involved was preliminarily identified. However, all the analysis conclusions are based on public data and have not been verified by large-scale, systematic in vivo and in vitro experiments. Therefore, in future research, we will further establish appropriate cell models and animal models to conduct in-depth verification and exploration on the specific mechanism and regulatory pathways of ADIPOR2 through functional experiments.

5. Conclusion

Generally, the potential relationships between ADIPOR2 and the immune microenvironment, as well as metabolism dysfunction were explored in NASH patients using bioinformatic methods. ADIPOR2 expression was increased in the NASH patients compared to healthy controls and exhibited better diagnostic value. Changes in the expression of the ADIPOR2 gene may affect T cells CD4 memory resting, DCs resting, and pathways related to cholesterol homeostasis, FA metabolism, and peroxisomes in NASH patients. Besides, an ADIPOR2-related regulatory network was established, and drug sensitivity was predicted. These findings may enhance our clinical understanding of NASH, providing new directions for therapeutic strategies.

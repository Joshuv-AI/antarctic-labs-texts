Single-cell RNA sequencing reveals that TAF7 promotes colorectal cancer stemness by targeting SOX2
Authors: Xiaohui Ai, Yupei Jiang, Xiaoqing Huang, Haitao Ning, Shaojun Wang, Fang Li, Shulin Wu, Jun Mo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCancer cell stemness plays a decisive role in tumor metastasis and recurrence. TATA-box binding protein-associated factor 7 (TAF7), which is highly expressed in multiple cancers and critical for metastasis, has not been clearly linked to stemness characteristics in colorectal cancer (CRC). Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the role of TAF7 in CRC stemness.MethodsDifferential gene expression and associated signaling pathways were screened using single-cell transcriptomic data from CRC and adjacent normal tissues obtained from the GEO database and validated in CRC cells. A TAF7-knockdown CRC cell line was established to assess the impact of TAF7 on tumor cell stemness. The molecular mechanism of TAF7 in regulating CRC stemness was explored through bioinformatics analysis combined with chromatin immunoprecipitation and dual-luciferase reporter assays and validated by rescue experiments. Finally, a CRC xenograft mouse model was established to examine the effect of targeting TAF7 on tumor cell stemness and epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT).ResultsRe-analysis of single-cell transcriptomics data identified a TAF7+ epithelial cell subpopulation that is highly expressed in CRC and characteristically expresses the stemness marker SOX2. In vitro experiments demonstrated that high TAF7 expression promotes CRC stemness, primarily by enhancing the transcriptional activation of SOX2. Rescue experiments confirmed that targeting the TAF7/SOX2 axis suppresses CRC stemness and EMT. Furthermore, in vivo experiments showed that TAF7 targeting inhibits tumor growth and CRC stemness in mice.ConclusionOur integrated single-cell transcriptomic and functional analyses reveal that TAF7 promotes CRC stemness and EMT progression via transcriptional activation of the stemness marker SOX2.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13062-026-00910-4.Keywords: Single-cell sequencing, TAF7, SOX2, Colorectal cancer, Cancer cell stemness

Introduction

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is a highly common malignancy worldwide. In 2022, this disease accounted for a substantial global burden, with over 1.9 million new cases and 904,000 deaths worldwide, representing 10% of all cancer incidence and mortality [1, 2]. Data from the UK National Program of Cancer Registries indicate a 1-year mortality rate of 59.3% for CRC patients diagnosed with metastasis [3], complicating treatment. Although the current standard of care, which combines surgical resection with radiotherapy and chemotherapy, provides modest survival benefits, its overall efficacy remains constrained by high rates of metastasis and recurrence [4]. For peritoneal metastasis specifically, radical surgery improves 3- and 5-year survival over palliative treatment, though at the cost of increased complications [5]. Therefore, new approaches are warranted to improve CRC patient outcomes.

Growing evidence demonstrates that epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) holds an indispensable role in tumorigenesis and metastasis [6]. EMT, defined as the trans-differentiation of epithelial cells (EPCs) into a mesenchymal phenotype, facilitates cancer cell detachment from the primary tumor and their subsequent dissemination. This process endows cancer cells with enhanced migratory and invasive capacities [7]. Notably, tumor cell stemness, characterized by self-renewal and differentiation potential, drives tumor heterogeneity [8, 9]. In CRC, key stemness markers, including CD133, CD44, SOX2, and Nanog, correlate significantly with tumor invasion, metastasis, and patient prognosis [10, 11]. Consequently, in-depth investigation of EMT and stemness in CRC may provide new avenues for therapeutic strategies.

TATA-box binding protein-associated factor 7 (TAF7) is a nuclear transcription factor (TF) [12]. By interacting with TATA-binding protein (TBP) and other TBP-associated factor (TAF) subunits, TAF7 forms the TFIID complex, which regulates transcription and participates in stem cell proliferation and cell cycle progression [13]. Notably, TAF7 plays an instrumental role in DNA transcription, cell proliferation, and differentiation [12], while simultaneously regulating tumor development and metastasis [14]. According to a report by Zhang et al., TAF7 facilitates metastasis in triple-negative breast cancer via the direct targeting of SAA114. Furthermore, within the CRC lncRNA-TF network, TAF7 was identified as a highly relevant TF15. Nevertheless, it remains largely unclear whether TAF7 participates in the maintenance of cancer stemness and the EMT in CRC, as well as whether its underlying mechanism is associated with the transcriptional regulation of SOX2, a core stemness regulator. Given the target gene specificity of TAF7 reported in breast cancer and its strong correlation with CRC progression, we hypothesized that TAF7 facilitates cancer stemness and EMT in CRC via transcriptional activation of SOX2.

In this study, single-cell transcriptomic analysis, in vitro functional assays, and in vivo xenograft models were applied to systematically decipher the molecular mechanism by which TAF7 modulates CRC stemness. A TAF7⁺ EPC subpopulation was identified in CRC tissues, which was characterized by high SOX2 expression and exhibited the most aggressive malignant phenotypes. Mechanistic investigations further validated that TAF7 directly binds to the SOX2 promoter to trigger its transcription, thereby driving stemness properties and EMT in CRC cells. This work fills the research gap regarding the undefined biological functions of TAF7 in CRC stemness. Moreover, it establishes for the first time a functional linkage between the general transcription factor TFIID and the specific regulatory programs of cancer stem cells (CSCs), offering a novel therapeutic target for CRC treatment.

Materials and methods

Bioinformatics analysis of single-cell transcriptomic data

The scRNA-seq data (16 rectal cancer tumor samples, 8 CRC adjacent normal samples) in the GEO dataset GSE200997 were analyzed by implementing the R package “Seurat” (v 4.0.1). A total of 49,859 cells and 23,828 genes were obtained, filtering cells expressing at least 100 genes and exhibiting mitochondrial gene proportions < 30%. Upon normalizing the data, cells were clustered using the FindClusters function and subsequently annotated based on marker genes from the CellMarker 2.0 database. The EPC subpopulation was extracted and further classified based on heterogeneity. Finally, malignancy scoring of different epithelial subpopulations was conducted employing the R package “infercnv”.

Cell culture

The LoVo cell line (Cat# TCHu 82, Chinese Academy of Sciences, China) was cultured in Ham’s F-12 K medium (Gibco, USA). SW480 (Cat# CL-0223, Procell, China) and HIEC-6 (Cat# STM-CL-5280, StemRecell, USA) cell lines were maintained in high-glucose DMEM (Biosharp, China), while the HT-29 cell line (Cat# CL-0118, Procell, China) was propagated in McCoy’s 5 A medium (Procell, China). All cell lines were cultured in medium containing 10% FBS (Gibco, USA) and 1% P/S (Biosharp, China), and maintained at 37 ℃ in a 5% CO₂ incubator (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

To achieve TAF7 overexpression, cells were transfected with a pcDNA3.1-TAF7 plasmid, which was constructed by cloning TAF7 cDNA into the pcDNA3.1 + vector (Tsingke Biotechnology, China). Conversely, for the knockdown of TAF7 and SOX2, cells were transfected with sh-TAF7 and sh-SOX2 plasmids, respectively (Tsingke Biotechnology, China). All transfections were performed using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Cell groups: Group 1: sh-NC and sh-TAF7; Group 2: oe-NC + sh-NC, oe-TAF7 + sh-NC, oe-NC + sh-SOX2, and oe-TAF7 + sh-SOX2.

Western blotting (WB)

Following lysis of cells from different groups on ice for 15 min with RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) containing 1% protease inhibitor (Beyotime, China), the resulting protein concentration was quantified using a BCA kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA), and the proteins were subsequently denatured in 1× loading buffer (Beyotime, China) by boiling for 5 min. Following separation by 12% SDS-PAGE (Beyotime, China) and transfer to PVDF membranes (Millipore, Germany), the membranes underwent a 2-h blocking with 5% skim milk (Biosharp, China) for 2 h at room temperature (RT), then overnight incubation at 4 ℃ with primary antibodies, followed by a 2-h incubation at RT with secondary antibodies. Protein bands were visualized using an ECL kit (Millipore, Germany) on a chemiluminescence and fluorescence imaging system (Clinx Science Instruments, China). Primary antibodies: rabbit anti-TAF7 (Cat# 13506-1-AP, Proteintech, USA), rabbit anti-SOX2 (Cat# ab97959, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-CD44 (Cat# ab243894, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-NANOG (Cat# ab109250, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-E-cadherin (Cat# ab40772, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-N-cadherin (Cat# ab76011, Abcam, UK), and rabbit anti-GAPDH (Cat# ab181602, Abcam, UK). Secondary antibody: goat anti-rabbit IgG-HRP (Cat# ab205718, Abcam, UK). Density quantification was performed using Image J software (NIH, USA).

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) and concentration quantified with a spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). cDNA was synthesized using the HiFiScript gDNA Removal cDNA Synthesis Kit (Cat# CW2582M, CWBIO, China). qPCR was performed using qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Cat# 11201ES03, Yeasen Biotechnology, China) and specific primers (Table 1) on a real-time PCR system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Relative gene expression was quantified by the 2−ΔΔCT method, normalizing to GAPDH as the internal control.

Sphere formation assay

Tumor cells from different groups were resuspended in serum-free DMEM/F-12 medium (Invitrogen, USA) containing β-FGF (10 ng/mL, Invitrogen, USA), human EGF (20 ng/mL, Invitrogen, USA), IGF (20 ng/mL, Cell Signaling Technology, USA), and 1X B27 (Gibco, USA). Subsequently, cells (4 × 10⁴/well) were cultured in six-well ultra-low attachment plates (Corning, USA) for 10–14 days in a 37 ℃ incubator (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) with 5% CO₂. Ultimately, spheres were observed under a microscope (Leica, Germany) and counted using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, USA).

Flow cytometry (FCM)

Cell suspensions (1 × 10⁶ cells/group) were subjected to a 20-min incubation with diluted antibodies at 4 ℃, avoiding light exposure. CD133-positive cells were detected using a flow cytometer (Agilent Technologies, USA). Antibody: Fluorescent primary antibody PE-anti-CD133 (Cat# 397903, BioLegend, USA).

Transwell assay

For migration assays, 1 × 10⁵ cells resuspended in 200 µL serum-free medium were seeded into the upper chamber (8 μm pores, Corning, USA), with medium containing 10% FBS in the lower chamber as an attractant. After 24 h, fixed cells were stained with 0.1% crystal violet (Beyotime, China) and counted under a microscope. The invasion assay was identical, except that the upper chamber was pre-coated with Matrigel (Corning, USA) before cell seeding.

Wound healing assay

Cells (1 × 10⁵/well) were inoculated into six-well plates. After 16 h, the medium was replaced with 2% FBS-containing medium. At 90% confluence, a scratch was made using a 200 µL pipette tip. After two PBS (Beyotime, China) washes, serum-free medium was added. Images were captured at 0 and 48 h under a microscope, and cell migration ability was calculated using ImageJ software.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assay

ChIP assays were performed using a commercial kit (Cat# P1801S, Beyotime, China). Briefly, cells at 80% confluence in 10 cm dishes were cross-linked with 37% formaldehyde (270 µL; Nanjing Reagent, China) at 37 ℃ for 10 min, followed by quenching with Glycine Solution at RT for 5 min. Following the removal of the supernatant, cells were pelleted by centrifugation at 800 × g for 2 min at 4 ℃. The resulting pellet was resuspended in 0.2 mL SDS Lysis Buffer and sonicated on ice to fragment chromatin. The lysate was then diluted with 1.8 mL ChIP Dilution Buffer, with 20 µL as the Input control. The remaining sample was pre-cleared with 70 µL Protein A + G Agarose/Salmon Sperm DNA at 4 ℃ for 30 min with gentle rotation. Following centrifugation, the supernatant was subjected to an overnight incubation with primary antibodies [rabbit anti-TAF7 (Cat# 13506-1-AP, Proteintech, USA) or rabbit anti-IgG (Cat# 10284-1-AP, Proteintech, USA)] at 4 ℃ with gentle rotation. Subsequently, the mixture was incubated with 60 µL of Protein A + G Agarose/Salmon Sperm DNA for an additional 60 min at 4 ℃. After centrifugation, the pellet underwent a sequential washing regimen using Low Salt Immune Complex Wash Buffer, High Salt Immune Complex Wash Buffer, LiCl Immune Complex Wash Buffer, followed by a final wash with TE Buffer, before qPCR detection. Antibodies: rabbit anti-TAF7 (Cat# 13506-1-AP, Proteintech, USA) and rabbit anti-IgG (Cat# 10284-1-AP, Proteintech, USA).

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Two reporter plasmids were generated based on the psiCHECK-2 vector (Promega, USA): one containing the wild-type SOX2 promoter binding site (psiCHECK-2-SOX2-WT) and the other its mutant counterpart (psiCHECK-2-SOX2-Mut). Initially, LoVo cells were seeded into 24-well plates and assigned to either the sh-NC or sh-TAF7 group. Upon reaching 70–90% confluence, cells were subjected to transfection. Specifically, the transfection mixture contained equal total DNA amounts with a 1:1 mass ratio of reporter vector to intervention plasmid, prepared using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA). After 48 h, cells were harvested, and Renilla (reporter) and Firefly (internal control) luciferase activities were measured sequentially using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA). Normalized activity was calculated as: Normalized Activity = Renilla RLU / Firefly RLU. To compare discrepancies between treatments, relative activity (fold change) was calculated as: Relative Activity = Normalized Activity (experimental group) / Normalized Activity (control group), with the control (sh-NC + SOX2-WT) set to 1.

Animal experiments

All animal experiments were conducted at Zhejiang Luoxi Medical Technology Co., Ltd., Hangzhou, China (Approval No.: LX4825031001), strictly adhering to the Animal Management Regulations and the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals, with approval by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee. Animals underwent health checks and a 7 - day acclimatization period before experiments. Mice were housed under SPF conditions (22 ± 2 ℃, relative humidity 50–60%, 12 h light/dark cycle) with ad libitum access to sterilized feed and ultrafiltered water, while all cages, bedding, and surgical instruments were autoclaved to ensure environmental stability. We stratified female BALB/c Nude mice [5–7 weeks old; GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (China)] into two animal groups (n = 6/group): sh-NC and sh-TAF7. Stable sh-NC or sh-TAF7-transfected cells (1 × 10⁶ cells in 100 µL PBS), oe-NC or oe-TAF7 transfected cells (1 × 105 cells in 100 uL PBS) were subcutaneously injected into the right dorsal flank. Tumor growth was monitored weekly by caliper measurement, with volume calculated as (shortest diameter)2 × longest diameter / 216. After 4 weeks, tumors were excised and weighed.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

Mouse tumor tissues underwent fixation, dehydration, paraffin embedding, and were sectioned at 4 μm. Following deparaffinization and rehydration, the tissue sections underwent antigen retrieval and peroxidase blocking. Subsequently, non-specific binding was blocked by incubating the sections in skim milk for 30 min at RT. This was followed by sequential incubations with primary antibodies, a secondary antibody, and DAB chromogen (Beyotime, China), before a final counterstaining with hematoxylin (Beyotime, China). After a final dehydration and mounting, the stained sections were visualized under a microscope. Primary antibodies: rabbit anti-TAF7 (Cat# 13506-1-AP, Proteintech, USA), rabbit anti-SOX2 (Cat# ab97959, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-CD133 (Cat# ab284389, Abcam, UK), rabbit anti-E-cadherin (Cat# ab314063, Abcam, UK), and rabbit anti-N-cadherin (Cat# ab76011, Abcam, UK). Secondary antibody: goat anti-rabbit IgG H&L (HRP) (Cat# ab205718, Abcam, UK). Quantitative analysis of IHC staining: Tumor tissues were randomly selected from three mice in each group, and one tissue section from each mouse was subjected to IHC staining. Five non-overlapping visual fields were randomly captured under an optical microscope at 200× magnification for each section. ImageJ software (NIH, USA) was applied to calculate the percentage of DAB-positive stained area relative to the total area of each field, which was defined as the IHC staining score.

Bioinformatics analysis

The motif matrix of TAF7 was downloaded from the Factorbook database (https://www.factorbook.org/). The DNA sequence covering the 2000 base pairs upstream of the SOX2 gene was retrieved from the National Center for Biotechnology Information database. Subsequently, the FIMO tool embedded in the MEME Suite was utilized to align the TAF7 motif against the acquired upstream sequence of SOX2, so as to identify the exact binding sites between TAF7 and the target sequence.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis and graphical representation were conducted using SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 8.0, respectively. Data are presented as mean ± SD (n ≥ 3). Intergroup differences were evaluated using Student’s t-test for two-group comparisons and one-way ANOVA for multiple-group comparisons, with P-values < 0.05 denoting significance.

Results

Single-cell sequencing reveals an enriched EPC population in CRC

To elucidate the transcriptional differences between CRC tumor tissues and their adjacent normal counterparts, we analyzed the GSE200997 scRNA-seq dataset from the GEO database, comprising 16 CRC tumor and 7 adjacent normal samples. Cells were clustered using the FindNeighbors function, resulting in26 distinct clusters (Fig. 1A). Subsequent visualization by UMAP revealed the distribution of these clusters across sample types (Fig. 1B). We annotated the clusters using marker genes from the CellMarker 2.0 database and identified nine major cell types: B Cells, Endothelial, Epithelial, Fibroblast, Macrophage, Mast Cells, NKT, Plasma Cells, and T Cells (Fig. 1C-D). Notably, the proportion of EPCs was elevated in tumor samples relative to normal controls (Fig. 1E).

Single-cell sequencing reveals a significant increase in the TAF7 + EPC subpopulation in CRC

To further explore differential genes within EPCs, we extracted the EPC subpopulation for sub-clustering analysis, identifying four main subpopulations: Enterocyte, Goblet Cells, TAF7 + EPC, and Tuft Cells. The TAF7 + EPC proportion was upregulated in the Tumor group (Fig. 2A-E) and characteristically expressed the stemness marker SOX2 (Fig. 2F). Malignancy scoring analysis via the R package “infercnv” showed that the TAF7 + EPC subpopulation had significantly higher malignancy scores relative to other epithelial subpopulations (Fig. 2G, Fig. S1). Taken together, the TAF7 + EPC subpopulation is markedly expanded in CRC, characterized by high expression of the stemness factor SOX2, and it displays a more malignant phenotype compared to other epithelial subpopulations.

TAF7 knockdown inhibits CRC cell stemness

To understand TAF7’s function in CRC, we first quantified TAF7 expression levels in normal intestinal EPCs (HIEC-6) and three CRC cell lines (SW480, LoVo, and HT-29) using RT-qPCR. TAF7 expression was significantly upregulated in all three CRC cell lines compared to HIEC-6, with LoVo cells exhibiting the highest expression level (Fig. 3A). Accordingly, the LoVo cell line was selected for knockdown assays due to its highest endogenous TAF7 expression, which facilitates the observation of phenotypic alterations induced by TAF7 depletion. The HT-29 cell line was used for overexpression experiments given its relatively low endogenous TAF7 level, enabling clear detection of gain-of-function effects upon exogenous TAF7 overexpression. Cell models with TAF7 knockdown were established via transfection with sh-TAF7, which was successfully validated by both RT-qPCR and WB (Fig. 3B-C). Given that single-cell sequencing analysis revealed characteristic expression of the stemness marker SOX2 in TAF7 + EPCs, we next investigated whether TAF7 regulates CRC cell stemness. RT-qPCR and WB analyses demonstrated that TAF7 knockdown significantly reduced both mRNA and protein levels of SOX2 in LoVo cells compared to sh-NC controls (Fig. 3D-E), indicating that reduced TAF7 expression coincides with decreased SOX2 expression. Furthermore, as revealed by sphere formation assays, TAF7 knockdown remarkably impaired sphere-forming capacity (Fig. 3F), while FCM showed a substantial downregulation of CD133 (Fig. 3G), indicating diminished stemness. Additionally, in Transwell and wound healing assays, TAF7 knockdown markedly suppressed cell migration and invasion (Fig. 3H-I). CD44 and NANOG serve as surface markers for CSCs [15]. WB results revealed that TAF7 knockdown downregulated the expression of the stemness markers CD44 and NANOG. Additionally, TAF7 silencing reduced N-cadherin levels while elevating E-cadherin expression (Fig. 3J). In summary, TAF7 is highly expressed in CRC cells, and its silencing results in SOX2 downregulation, attenuated stemness, and reduced migratory and invasive capabilities.

We knocked down TAF7 in HIEC-6 cells and detected the expression of TAF7 and SOX2, as well as alterations in cell stemness. The results showed that, unlike CRC cell lines, TAF7 silencing in normal EPCs only led to a mild reduction in TAF7 and SOX2 expression (Fig. S2A-D). Meanwhile, no significant differences were observed in sphere-forming ability (Fig. S2E), CD133 expression (Fig. S2F), cell migration and invasion capacities (Fig. S2G). These findings demonstrate that the regulatory effect on cell stemness mediated by TAF7 is specific to tumor cells.

TAF7 transcriptionally activates SOX2 to promote CRC cell stemness

Given that TAF7 knockdown downregulates SOX2, we hypothesized a direct targeting relationship. We identified the precise binding sites between TAF7 and SOX2 using the Factorbook database and FIMO software (Fig. 4A). A ChIP assay demonstrated significant enrichment of TAF7 TF on the SOX2 promoter (Fig. 4B). A dual-luciferase reporter assay showed that TAF7 knockdown remarkably reduced luciferase activity in the SOX2-WT group but not in the SOX2-Mut group (Fig. 4C), confirming direct targeting. RT-qPCR results demonstrated that TAF7 overexpression markedly increased the mRNA level of SOX2 relative to the oe-NC group. Co-transfection with wild-type SOX2 (oe-SOX2-WT) further amplified this upregulation, whereas co-transfection with mutant SOX2 carrying disrupted binding sites (oe-SOX2-MUT) failed to induce obvious changes in SOX2 expression (Fig. 4D). These data confirm the specificity of the binding sites. To further explore how TAF7 regulates CRC stemness, HT-29 cells were divided into four groups: oe-NC + sh-NC, oe-TAF7 + sh-NC, oe-NC + sh-SOX2, and oe-TAF7 + sh-SOX2. As shown by RT-qPCR, TAF7 overexpression significantly increased SOX2 expression, which was reversed by subsequent SOX2 knockdown (Fig. 4E). Compared with the oe-NC + sh-NC group, TAF7 overexpression significantly upregulated CD44 and NANOG expression, and such promotional effects were abrogated by subsequent SOX2 knockdown (Fig. 4F). Sphere formation assay indicated that TAF7 overexpression markedly boosted HT-29 sphere formation, reversed by SOX2 knockdown (Fig. 4G). FCM showed that TAF7 overexpression increased CD133 expression, whereas SOX2 knockdown attenuated this effect (Fig. 4H). These results indicate that TAF7 overexpression promotes CRC cell stemness, and SOX2 knockdown reverses this effect. As demonstrated by Transwell and wound healing assays, TAF7 overexpression substantially enhanced CRC cell migration and invasion, a phenotype reversed by SOX2 knockdown (Fig. 4I-K). Collectively, TAF7 overexpression transcriptionally activates SOX2, facilitating CRC cell stemness, migration, and invasion.

Effect of TAF7 knockdown on tumor growth and CRC cell stemness in mice

To validate the functional role of TAF7 in vivo, a subcutaneous xenograft model was established in BALB/c Nude mice using LoVo cells with stable TAF7 knockdown. Tumor volumes were measured weekly, and by 28 days post-injection, the TAF7-knockdown group exhibited considerably smaller tumors than the sh-NC controls (Fig. 5A). Subsequently, we excised and weighed the tumors after euthanizing the mice. The weights were substantially reduced in the TAF7-knockdown cohort (Fig. 5B-C), confirming that TAF7 depletion inhibits xenograft tumor growth. IHC analysis of excised tumors revealed significantly lower expression of TAF7, SOX2, and the stemness marker CD133 in tumors derived from TAF7-knockdown cells (Fig. 5D), suggesting suppression of CRC cell stemness. Furthermore, TAF7 knockdown resulted in elevated E-cadherin and reduced N-cadherin levels, indicating attenuated EMT. The tumor volume in the TAF7 overexpression group was remarkably larger than that in the control group (Fig. S3A), accompanied by an obviously accelerated tumor growth rate (Fig. S3B-C). Further IHC analysis revealed that relative to the oe-NC group, the expression of TAF7, SOX2, CD133, and N-cadherin was markedly increased in tumor tissues of the TAF7 overexpression group, while E-cadherin expression was significantly decreased (Fig. S3D). These findings demonstrate that targeting TAF7 suppresses both tumor stemness and EMT progression, thereby impeding CRC tumor growth in mice.

Discussion

CRC is the third most common malignancy globally [1]. Despite advancements in surgical and pharmacological therapies that have improved survival, metastasis remains the leading cause of mortality for most patients [16]. Tumor stemness has been reported as a major contributor to drug resistance and poor prognosis [17, 18]. Therefore, understanding the underlying mechanisms and stemness-related targets in CRC is crucial. Given the pivotal role of TFs in maintaining CSC self-renewal, promoting tumor heterogeneity [19], and enhancing stemness when aberrantly expressed [19], the upregulation of TAF7 is particularly noteworthy in various cancers, such as lung adenocarcinoma [20] and breast cancer [14]. In this study, re-analysis of single-cell transcriptomic data revealed high TAF7 expression in CRC, with the TAF7 + EPC subpopulation exhibiting appreciably higher malignancy than other subpopulations. However, the molecular mechanism by which TAF7 influences CRC stemness remained elusive. Our study elucidates this pathway and identifies the TAF7/SOX2 axis as a potential therapeutic target.

CSCs constitute a rare malignant cell population characterized by key stem-like properties, including self-renewal, robust proliferation, and pluripotent differentiation. Consequently, these cells play decisive roles in driving tumor metastasis, recurrence, and chemotherapy resistance [17, 21]. Accordingly, tumor cell stemness and EMT have been established to function as key drivers of metastatic progression [22]. For example, TM4SF1 overexpression enhances CSC-like features and accelerates tumor EMT, thereby facilitating CRC growth and metastasis [23]. We therefore hypothesized that TAF7 may similarly affect CRC progression by boosting tumor stemness and EMT. Our integrated in vitro and in vivo findings support this hypothesis, demonstrating that TAF7 enhances sphere-forming capacity and upregulates the stemness marker CD133 in CRC cells, thereby promoting CRC cell stemness. Moreover, targeting TAF7 markedly inhibits EMT progression and tumor growth. However, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying TAF7-mediated stemness regulation warrant further investigation.

SOX2, a member of SoxB1 TFs, maintains stem cell pluripotency and directs self-renewal or differentiation [24]. Furthermore, SOX2 promotes malignant progression, proliferation, and EMT in multiple cancers [25–27]. In CRC, SOX2 participates in EMT progression by promoting stemness [28–30]. It is worth noting that the protein stability and function of SOX2 are also finely regulated by post-transcriptional modifications. For example, a recent study has shown that in pancreatic cancer, the ubiquitin-removing enzyme PSMD7 can stabilize its protein level by inhibiting the ubiquitination degradation of SOX2, thereby activating the Notch1 signaling pathway and driving tumor progression [31]. Collectively, these findings indicate that the regulatory mechanisms of SOX2 vary across distinct tumor types, and the upstream transcriptional regulation of SOX2 in CRC remains to be further elucidated.

The present study demonstrates that TAF7 directly binds to the SOX2 promoter and activates SOX2 transcription, revealing a novel function of TAF7 as a transcriptional regulator in maintaining CRC stemness. As a core component of the TFIID complex, TAF7 may facilitate the assembly of active transcription initiation complexes by recruiting RNA polymerase II and transcriptional coactivators to the SOX2 promoter region, thereby promoting SOX2 expression at the molecular level. This transcriptional activation pattern is consistent with the established oncogenic role of TAF7 in other malignancies [13], while its target gene selection exhibits distinct tissue specificity. Rescue experiments further validated that SOX2 acts as a critical downstream effector mediating the pro-stemness function of TAF7. Specifically, SOX2 knockdown significantly reversed the enhanced sphere-forming capacity, elevated proportion of CD133-positive cells, and strengthened migration and invasion abilities induced by TAF7 overexpression. These results confirm that the TAF7-SOX2 axis serves as a vital signaling pathway governing CRC stemness and EMT progression. Biologically, the identification of the TAF7-SOX2 axis provides a novel insight into the transcriptional regulatory network underlying CRC stemness maintenance. Moreover, this axis indicates that targeting TAF7 exerts dual anti-tumor effects by simultaneously suppressing SOX2-mediated stemness retention and EMT progression in CRC.

In conclusion, this study integrates single-cell transcriptomic analysis with functional experiments to elucidate the specific mechanism by which TAF7 influences CRC stemness: TAF7 promotes CRC stemness and EMT progression through transcriptional activation of the stemness-associated molecule SOX2, offering a novel direction for CRC research. Nevertheless, despite systematically delineating the role of TAF7 in tumor progression through regulating SOX2 transcriptional activity, this study has limitations. First, the findings predominantly rely on in vitro cell models and subcutaneous xenografts; validation in more physiologically relevant systems, such as orthotopic models or organoid systems, is warranted. Furthermore, the upstream regulatory mechanisms of TAF7 and its broader role within the epigenetic network remain to be thoroughly explored. Based on bioinformatics predictions and previous literature [32–34], the upregulation of TAF7 may result from the synergistic effects of multiple regulatory mechanisms, including transcriptional activation, epigenetic remodeling, and lncRNA-mediated inactivation. Future studies will integrate multiple approaches such as ChIP-seq, RIP assays, and proteomic profiling to systematically validate the specific functions of these upstream regulators in CRC, thereby establishing a comprehensive regulatory network of the TAF7–SOX2 axis.

An experience of online Pan-India transfusion medicine student’s teaching and assessment during the COVID-19 pandemic: A new initiative in transfusion medicine education in India
Authors: Ashish Jain, Rajendra K Chaudhary, Gita Negi, Rahul Katharia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Medical education was affected globally during the COVID-19 pandemic. To limit its effect on postgraduate education in transfusion medicine in India, an educational program was started with periodic assessments.MATERIALS AND METHODS:An educational program was prepared in the form of modules. Program was named as JG Jolly’s online teaching program for postgraduate students, which was delivered as a series of online interactive lectures in form of modules through the Zoom Application. A total of eleven modules were delivered, followed by posttest assessments for modules I-X. For 5 modules VI to X, both pre- and post-test assessments were done; whereas for module XI, only pretest assessment was done. Participants were categorized based on their duration of residency and the type of educational institute. The percentage scores of the participants were calculated and analyzed for the subcategories.RESULTS:Participation included junior and senior residents across various academic institutes. Posttest assessment had significant differences in the percentage scores in modules II, IV, and V among the students of the different educational institute. At the same time, significant differences were noted for modules I and II among the students of the different years of residency. Third-year junior residents obtained the highest marks in all modules except modules II and VI. The difference between posttest versus pretest percentage scores of modules VI to X was positive, with significant differences in modules VII and IX (12.16% and 14.66%, respectively).CONCLUSION:This study evaluated that JG Jolly’s online teaching program successfully maintained students’ academic environment during the COVID-19 pandemic. Multicentric online teaching and assessment is a viable option, especially in the case of a pandemic or disaster.

Introduction

COVID-19 affected the whole world, including India. The healthcare system suddenly started facing new challenges after the pandemic declaration. One of the challenges was to deliver effective teaching to medical students. The education system before COVID-19 was primarily based on face-to-face interaction and offline classes in the medical field in India. Undergraduate teaching was stopped for some time, whereas efforts continued to carry on postgraduate teaching in medical colleges. Another problem with postgraduate teaching was that many students and faculty across different specialties throughout India were involved in managing COVID-19 patients. At the same time, it also affected the teaching and training of postgraduate across all specialties, including transfusion medicine, due to restrictions on the physical classes. To manage this, virtual teaching was one of the viable options to teach and train students.[1]

Before the COVID-19 pandemic, online teaching has been thoroughly evaluated in other fields of education and distance education programs. Student engagement, natural eye-to-eye contact between faculty or students, multiple questioning and answering, and interactive teaching were the main advantages of offline teaching that were assumed to lack in online teaching. On the other hand, online teaching has some unique advantages, likestudent-centric because of the comfort of attending, delivery by the expert teacher from far, and attending of students from multiple centers. Some researchers have even mentioned various methods of interactive teaching in online methods. A study by Weiner and Hertz compared both methods and found online assessment equally suitable as an offline method.[2] Studies have also evaluated the suitability of online teaching for practical teaching.[3]

Remote assessment through online mode was reviewed in the literature. It is thought to be associated with misconduct, as challenging to have a proctored environment. However, methods such as different time slots, randomization of questions and options, etc., can be adopted to prevent the same. On the other hand, online assessment is cheaper, practically feasible for multicentre, and comfortable. Ahmed et al. compared the online versus offline assessment for health professional programs during the pandemic.[4]

Postgraduation in transfusion medicine is one of the new courses in India. Although in India, the first MD course in transfusion medicine was started in the year 1989, the scope of this branch has recently increased. It has progressed to a total 202 MD/DNB students yearly.[56] Many colleges in India have started this course in the last 10 years. Postgraduate teaching was affected during the pandemic. Hence, there was an immense need for a postgraduate teaching program in transfusion medicine that could deliver uniform, assessable teaching by field experts during the pandemic. Therefore, an online teaching program with assessment was started from October 2020 to April 2022 to educate the postgraduate students. This teaching program was named after Dr. JG Jolly, the Father of Transfusion Medicine in India, and the program was called JG Jolly’s online teaching program. The objective of the study was to share the experience of online teaching and assessment at the pan-India level during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Materials and Methods

The teaching program comprised of modules of interactive lectures covering important areas from the entire curriculum. A total of eleven modules were delivered. One coordinator for each module was responsible for delivering interactive lectures online using the Zoom application through speakers and moderators. The schematic presentation of this education module is summarized in Figure 1.

Illustration of the education program

In chronological order, the following modules were delivered.

(I) Immunohematology, (II) Clinical Transfusion Practices, (III) Blood Donation and Donor Management, (IV) Apheresis, (V) Solid Organ Transplantation, including Transplant Immunology, (VI) Cellular Therapy, (VII) Coagulation, (VIII) Hemovigilance and adverse transfusion reaction, (IX) Transfusion Transmitted Infections (TTI), (X) Blood Components, (XI) Quality Control.

Common coordinators executed assessments of all modules. The assessments of these modules were taken online on ClassMarker, a web-based quiz maker. Participation in each assessment was voluntary. Posttest assessments were done in modules I to X. In addition to posttest assessments, pretest assessments were also done in modules VI to X. In module XI, only a pretest assessment was done, as posttest assessment could not be done due to technical issues. The mean percentage score and 95% confidence interval of assessments were calculated and analyzed.

The participants were divided based on their year of education into four categories (junior resident 1–3 and senior resident) and also types of teaching colleges into four categories (Central Government Medical Colleges and Institutes running MD courses, State Government Medical Colleges and Institutes running MD courses, Private Medical Colleges running MD courses and Teaching Hospitals running DNB courses). The difference between posttest and pretest percentage scores of assessments of modules VI to X were also calculated and analyzed. A one-way ANOVA test was used for statistical comparison between groups. A paired t-test was done for pre–post assessment for module VI-X, and P< 0.05 were considered significant.

Results

The total number of students participating in posttest assessments of modules I to X were 108, 85, 82, 82, 74, 51, 42, 46, 52, and 55, respectively. The total number of students participating in the pretest assessment of modules VI to XI were 38, 20, 28, 24, 29, and 24, respectively. The mean percentage score and confidence interval of the participants among categories of college and the year of education were compared and summarized in Tables 2 and 3, respectively. In the former category, the significant differences in the percentage scores were noted in modules II, IV and V. In contrast, in the latter category, the significant difference was found in modules I and II. Junior residents III obtained the highest marks in all modules except module II and VI. The difference between posttest and pretest percentage assessments of modules VI to X were summarized in Table 4. The difference in all these modules was positive, whereas the significant differences were noted only in modules VII and IX.

Discussion

The transfusion medicine specialty has been in India for over 30 years. In its sixth objective, the National Blood Policy of India, introduced in 2002, recognized transfusion medicine as a specialty and recommended establishing a separate department of transfusion medicine in each medical college.[7] However, in recent years, there has been a rising trend in the number of colleges that have started this course and postgraduate program. The dedicated department with the academic facility is expected to fulfill its aim by strengthening the infrastructure, dedicated faculty, and uniform standards of teaching facilities for postgraduate students. For uniform teaching, the National Medical Council of India has also given a uniform postgraduate teaching curriculum.[8]

JG Jolly’s online program was an attempt to deliver uniform teaching facilities across India to continue postgraduate teaching during the COVID-19 pandemic. The desired competencies for the postgraduate students were evaluated, and all modules were given adequate time to cover all relevant topics in the area. The longest modules (8–10 week’s time in each) were immunohematology, Clinical Transfusion Practices, Coagulation, and TTI. Efforts were also made to involve as many centers as possible; therefore, experts in the specific modules were assigned as module coordinators and moderators. These module coordinators and moderators evaluated the competencies of faculty and consultants of the country and gave them the task of online interactive lectures. On comparing the data of the total annual intake of MD/DNB students of Transfusion Medicine in India and the total number of students who participated in this educational program, it may be stated that the participation in this program was enthusiastic. Initially, the assessment was planned at the end of the module to evaluate the impact of teaching. While in later modules, pretest assessments were also planned. The difference in posttest versus pretest scores showed that the difference was positive, with a significant difference in performance in modules VII and IX. The maximum difference was found in the TTI module, while the minimum was in the blood components module.

There was no cutoff or passing limit for the assessment. Third-year Junior Residents had maximum scores, while junior resident 1 had minimum scores in most modules. This was a prominent and expected finding, which hints that knowledge was the main factor in scoring. Although there was the possibility of misconduct in a remote online assessment, as a strict proctored environment was not possible, time-bound assessment and randomization of questions were some of the methods implemented in this assessment that minimized the risk of misconduct. In some modules, such as modules I and V, the mean percentage scores were below 50%, and these may require more focus if any subsequent educational program is planned. The college-wise difference is of subtle value because of inherent variation in the infrastructure, teaching workforce, students’ basic knowledge, etc. Students’ knowledge directly relates to the hospital’s practical and patient care facilities, and the same was observed in the participants’ scores. For example, transplant facilities are more focused on teaching hospitals, so students of these centers achieved higher scores in such modules. Assessments in different modules would be helpful to do gap analysis in students’ knowledge and to assess baseline performance, so that poor-performing areas of the student can be appropriately focused.

Very few studies have published results and scores in the COVID-19 pandemic in the medical field. The literature mainly focused on the online survey and the Student’s perceptions.[9] Study by Razzak et al. published pre COVID-19 face-to-face teaching and paper-based assessment of the physiology subject that was compared to online teaching and assessment during the COVID-19 pandemic and concluded that students have sufficiently achieved desired learning outcomes during the pandemic in online teaching.[10] Yilmaz et al., in their study, gave the concept of a massive open online course for medical students and found its usefulness in the pandemic.[11] The primary focus of the current study was to assess theoretical teaching. Assessment of practical knowledge is also essential, and some studies evaluated the usefulness of online teaching in practical teaching.[12]

Since this program started during the first wave of COVID-19 in India and continued till the end of the third wave, the participation of the candidates varied in different modules in terms of attendance in lectures and assessments. The difference in the number of participating students may be because, in between waves of the COVID-19 pandemic, hospital work resumed, and in-house academic sessions resumed. However, participation in this module remained enthusiastic in the in-between phase of the pandemic. This showed that students responded positively in terms of attendance and participation. Although not related to transfusion medicine, a famous systematic review and meta-analysis by Naziri A. et al. showed that students often like to be engaged in e-learning and show their positive responses in terms of perceptions, acceptance, motivation, and engagement.[13]

This study has some limitations, as item analysis of the questions was not done. Moreover, not all students appeared in all exams. Another limitation was that no documented feedback from the participants was taken.

Conclusion

This study evaluated that JG Jolly’s online teaching program successfully maintained the students’ academic environment during the COVID-19 pandemic. The difference in posttest versus pretest percentage scores showed that this program raised students’ knowledge. No such detailed study is available, especially for postgraduates in transfusion medicine, and it can be summarized that, by overcoming some limitations, online teaching and assessment is a viable option for multicentric teaching and assessment.

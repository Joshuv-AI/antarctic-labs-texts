Incorporating adipose tissue into a CT-based deep learning nomogram to differentiate granulomas from lung adenocarcinomas
Authors: Qing-chun Jia, Ye Niu, Qi-fan Xuan, Shi-di Miao, Wen-juan Huang, Ping-ping Liu, Le Liu, Han-bing Xie, Qiu-jun Wang, Zeng-yao Liu, Shuang Fu, Yu-xi Liu, Lin Zhao, Yuan-zhou Li, Rui-tao Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryWe aimed to build and validate a computed tomography (CT)-based deep learning nomogram for discriminating granulomas from lung adenocarcinomas. A retrospective study of 1,159 patients with solitary lung nodules from three institutions in China who underwent pre-operative lung CT scans was performed. The patients were divided into one training, one validation, one test, and two external validation cohorts. Deep learning features were extracted from CT images. The least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression model was used for dimension reduction and feature selection. Logistic regression analysis showed that age, gender, intranodular and perinodular (IPN) features, and adipose features were the significant predictors of malignancy presence (all p < 0.05). The nomogram was built by incorporating these four factors and achieved better diagnostic accuracy than the single-factor model. The nomogram demonstrates satisfactory discrimination and calibration. In addition, decision curve analysis revealed the considerable clinical usefulness of the nomogram.Subject areas: Cancer, Artificial intelligence

Introduction

Lung adenocarcinomas are the most prevalent subtype of non-small cell lung cancer.1,2 Noncalcified granulomas are the most common benign lung lesions in subjects with solitary pulmonary nodules.3 In clinical practice, it is difficult to distinguish granulomas from adenocarcinomas in that these two pathologic conditions have similar appearances on computed tomography (CT) images.4 High-risk patients can be further diagnosed via invasive approaches (biopsy or surgical resection). To reduce mortality and overtreatment, a highly accurate, noninvasive risk assessment method is needed.

The growth of deep learning fostered the applications of artificial intelligence in the field of lung nodules. A previous study found a deep learning nomogram incorporating clinical factors, pulmonary nodule features via deep learning, and CT-based subjective findings showed better diagnostic accuracy (the areas under the curve in the training and external validation cohorts were 0.809 and 0.889, respectively).5 Another report evaluated the ability of deep learning features from intranodular and perinodular regions (IPN) to distinguish adenocarcinomas from granulomas and achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.803. However, these deep learning-based studies are heterogeneous and lack reproducibility.6 Swin Transformer is a type of DL algorithm that has been shown to be a promising tool for the interpretation and analysis of medical images. Swin Transformer has multiple advantages compared to convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for vision tasks. Swin Transformer can better capture global dependencies in the input image by self-attention mechanism. Swin Transformer enables the model to better understand the relationships between different regions during the learning process, thereby improving the capture and utilization of subtle features in lung nodule classification.7 At the same time, the self-attention mechanism in Swin Transformer provides stronger interpretability.

Recent studies have demonstrated that adipose tissue inflammation is associated with cancer development and progression.8 Obesity results in increased DNA lesions and diminished DNA repair in both animal models and human subjects.9,10 The inflammatory microenvironment of white adipose tissue causes the excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and triggers genomic instability.8 Previous clinical studies confirmed that adipose tissue is associated with some tumors, such as pancreatic cancer, prostate cancer, liver cancer, colorectal cancer, and breast cancer.11,12,13,14,15,16 During cancer progression, the inflammatory white adipose tissue secretes adipokines, extracellular vesicles, and pro-angiogenic molecules to accelerate tumor metastasis within the microenvironment.17,18 On the other hand, cancer cells induce the secretion of lipids and adipokines by adipocytes to promote tumor growth.19 Cancer-associated adipocytes are characterized by a specific phenotype with high lipolysis and overexpression of proteases and proinflammatory cytokines.20 In addition, increased aerobic glycolysis in the tumor microenvironment can drive tumor growth via the increased production of lactate and pyruvate.21

Our study attempted to evaluate if intrathoracic adipose features could discriminate between adenocarcinomas and granulomas and whether the combination of IPN features with adipose features was more predictive of nodule malignancy.

Feature selection and model building

After assessing reproducibility, 1,536 features (including 768 nodule and 768 adipose features) from nodule and adipose tissue were selected. Taking the 8th-fold as an example, from the LASSO analysis, 9 nodule features and 10 adipose features with non-zero weights were input into the model (Figure S3). It is worth noting that due to the different training and validation sets in the 10-fold cross-validation and the black-box nature of deep learning, the results of feature selection for each fold are not consistent. Therefore, we use the “take it all” method to unify the feature indices selected from all folds (Figure 1).22

We compared three methods for model building and found the random forest (RF) carried out the best (Tables S2 and S3). There is no difference in clinical baseline characteristics among patients in different cohorts within the same center (Table S4). As shown in Supplementary A7, nodule and adipose models were built using RF. Using the same modeling, we built the adipose model (including 768 adipose features that were initially extracted from intra-thoracic adipose). These models had a value between zero and one, denoting the probability of predicted pulmonary adenocarcinoma presence. A heatmap of adipose features for patients with granuloma/adenocarcinoma is shown in Figure S4.

Diagnostic performance of the models

There was a significant difference in the nodule and adipose models between granuloma and adenocarcinoma in the training cohort (p < 0.001), which was then confirmed in the validation cohorts (p < 0.001). The nodule model yielded areas under the curves (AUCs) of 0.937 (95% confidence interval [CI], 0.886–0.941), 0.810 (95% CI, 0.764–0.962), 0.791 (95% CI, 0.779–0.894), 0.835 (95% CI, 0.740–0.862), and 0.813 (95% CI, 0.774–0.883) in the training, internal validation, internal test, and two external validation cohorts, respectively. For prediction of adenocarcinoma, the adipose model achieved satisfying performance with AUCs of 0.935 (95% CI: 0.916–0.962), 0.839 (95% CI: 0.870–0.998), 0.834 (95% CI: 0.758–0.878), 0.812 (95% CI: 0.781–0.893), and 0.861 (95% CI: 0.833–0.927) in the training, internal validation, internal test, and two external validation cohorts, respectively (Figure S5).

Deep learning nomogram construction

In the training cohort, univariate analysis showed that age, gender, BMI, diabetes, location in the upper lobe, nodule size, nodule features, and adipose features were significantly related to malignant nodules. Multivariate analysis identified age, gender, BMI, nodule features, and adipose features as the significant independent factors for adenocarcinoma presence (p < 0.05) (Table S5). However, BMI has no significance in the internal test cohort or the external validation cohort 1. Therefore, age, gender, nodule characteristics, and adipose characteristics were fused as a nomogram (Figure 2).

A nomogram that incorporates the nodule and adipose tissue features and independent clinical risk factors

Performance and validation of the deep learning nomogram

The nomogram demonstrated superior discrimination power for distinguishing granuloma from adenocarcinoma nodules in the training cohort (AUC = 0.989, 95% CI: 0.987–0.991). In the bootstrapping validation, the nomogram also yielded high values of 0.915 (95% CI, 0.894–0.952), 0.891 (95% CI, 0.879–0.907), 0.896 (95% CI, 0.889–0.905), and 0.931 (95% CI, 0.921–0.945) in internal validation, test, and two external validation cohorts, respectively (Figure 3; Table 2).

Clinical use

The decision curve analysis (DCA) was used to evaluate the clinical usefulness of the nomogram (Figure 5). We found that if the threshold probability was 31%, the patients would benefit more from the nomogram than IPN features, adipose features, clinical model, intervention-for-all, and intervention-for-none strategy. Discussion

We developed and validated a deep learning feature-based nomogram for discriminating granulomas from lung adenocarcinomas. The nomogram incorporates four items: age, gender, IPN features, and adipose features. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study incorporating intrathoracic adipose tissue for distinguishing between granulomas and lung adenocarcinomas.

The proposed nomogram performed better than the IPN model in all cohorts, indicating that the adipose model could mine more image features useful for differentiating granulomas from adenocarcinomas. Increased tissue stiffness in fibrotic tissue may be a key contributor to tumorigenesis.23 Macrophages surrounding cancer cells early during tumorigenesis activate epithelial-mesenchymal transition and facilitate the function of T regulatory cells to promote tumor immune escape.16 It is evidenced by the presence of increased immune cells and fibroblasts, which can help deposit extracellular matrix and reorganize the stromal network. Fat depots in the lungs maintain lung health by regulating alveolar lipid homeostasis and pulmonary surfactant production.24,25 Ectopic fat deposition in the lungs could obstruct airways and worsen pulmonary damage.26,27 Proinflammatory adipose tissue macrophages are significantly increased by hypoxia in the tumor microenvironment28,29 and present typical crown-like structures.16,30 A recent study revealed that the specific macrophage infiltration in colorectal cancer leads to distinct morphological and functional changes in the peritumoral adipose tissue.31 Another study found the new function of adipose tissue: brown adipose tissue activation mediates the anti-cancer effect of cold exposure.32

Granulomas and adenocarcinomas had similar visual appearances on routine CT scans, necessitating accurate discrimination before the selection of appropriate treatment. A growing body of evidence suggests that artificial intelligence has been applied to distinguishing adenocarcinomas from granulomas. In terms of deep learning radiomics, previous studies have focused on the intra-nodular and perinodular regions, displaying the performance with AUCs 0.71–0.88, indicating that the radiomics signature should be combined with auxiliary features to improve the prediction performance.5,33

It was worth noting that a deep learning method was applied for adipose tissue feature extraction in this study. These selected imaging features were not redundant but complementary, as shown in the heat maps, which show that these features are weakly correlated or uncorrelated. The nomogram incorporating adipose features in our study presented a promising performance in malignancy prediction with AUCs larger than 0.90. Moreover, the prediction model outperformed the single nodule model and the clinical model in all cohorts. Furthermore, improved NRI and IDI also confirmed that the enhanced discrimination performance of the nomogram was indeed due to feature integration instead of model overfitting. The results showed that the patients can be diagnosed and differentiated more accurately by analyzing the imaging manifestations of nodules and adipose tissue. In addition, all these indicate that adipose tissue offers a wealth of information reflecting the tumor microenvironment. Single-cell transcriptomic studies revealed that adipocytes comprise well under 50% of the cells in white adipose tissue, and a substantial portion of the rest are immune cells.34 Therefore, in future study design, clarifying the exact role of adipose in distinguishing adenocarcinomas from granulomas will be of great importance.

In addition, we used decision analysis curves to estimate the clinical utility of the nomogram based on threshold probability. The decision curves revealed that applying the nomogram to predict lung adenocarcinoma adds more net benefit than the “treat-all” or “treat-none” scheme.

In conclusion, an individual non-invasive nomogram, consisting of IPN features, adipose features, and clinical risk factors, was built to distinguish granulomas from lung adenocarcinomas, with the potential to assist clinicians in clinical practice.

Limitations of the study

The study did have some limitations. First, a prospective study is needed to confirm the generalizability of the results because this is a retrospective study. Second, the regions of interests (ROIs) were delineated in one single slice (2D). Three-dimensional texture features should be investigated in the future. Third, additional work is required to clarify the underlying mechanism of the predictive value of adipose tissue.

Method details

Patients

This retrospective study evaluated 1159 patients with pulmonary nodules who underwent surgical resection with curative intent from three independent hospitals in China. The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria and enrollment process are listed in Supplementary A1 and Figure S1. The patients were divided into one primary cohort and two external validation cohorts. The primary cohort included 658 patients from Center 1 (Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital) between December 2016 and June 2022 and were randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 432), an internal validation cohort (n = 48) and a test cohort (n = 178). The two external validation cohorts included 171 patients from Center 2 (the First Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University) between January 2021 and February 2023 and 196 patients from Center 3 (the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University) between January 2019 and February 2023. The sample size consideration is shown in Supplementary A2.

The study was carried out after approval from the Institutional Review Board of three centers (Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital, the First Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University, and the Second Affiliated Hospital of Harbin Medical University). Because of the retrospective nature of the research, the requirement for informed consent was waived.

Data collection

The following clinical variables were collected: age, gender, body mass index (BMI), current/former smoker, hypertension, diabetes, and previous cancer history. The following CT features of pulmonary nodules were collected: (a) the location of the pulmonary nodules; (b) the size of the pulmonary nodules; (c) the presence of a spiculated sign (yes or no); (d) the lesion shape (round/oval or irregular); (e) the presence of a lobulated shape (yes or no); (f) the presence of a pseudocavitation sign (yes or no).35,36 Chest CT images from three hospitals were collected. The CT image acquisition settings of each hospital are presented in Supplementary A3 and Table S1. The flowchart of this study is shown in Figure 1.

ROI acquisition procedure

The nodule region of interest (ROI) was manually delineated by reader 1 with 10 years of experience on the central slice of CT images with the largest tumor using the 3D Slicer (version 4.10.2; www.slicer.org). Chest CT images from three tertiary first-class hospitals were collected, with two experienced radiologists at each center involved in the data collection. With semi-automatic segmentation, the maximum possible area of pixels with Hounsfield unit (HU) values ranging from −200 to −40 within the selected region was included and defined as adipose tissue in the thorax.37 The ROI of intra-thoracic adipose at the first layer upward of the aortic arch was quantified by ImageJ 1.53K software (Wayne Rasband and contributors, National Institutes of Health, USA).38 Figure S2 and Supplementary A4 show the detailed process of delineating the IPN and intrathoracic adipose ROI from CT images. After three months, 50 patients were randomly chosen, and their ROIs were segmented again. Readers 1 and 2 (with 10 years of experience) evaluated the intraobserver and interobserver reproducibility.

Deep learning feature extraction

A total of 768 deep learning features were extracted from each ROI of nodule or adipose, totaling 1536 features from the two ROIs per patient (supplementary A5). We adapted the self-attention mechanism-based neural network (Swin Transformer) architecture to develop our vision transformer neural network (VTNN) for deep learning feature extraction.

Deep learning model optimization

We employed 10-fold cross-validation to mitigate the impact of dataset partitioning on the results and ensure a more rigorous validation of the model’s robustness. Two models were respectively built from nodule and adipose features, including four steps (Supplementary A6): (I) Preliminary screening of LASSO regression characteristics was performed by optimal λ. (II) features with non-zero regression coefficients were retained. (III) Based on grid search, the features with the highest absolute regression coefficients were selected. (IV) Comparisons of three methods, namely, support vector machine (SVM), logistic regression, and random forest, were made, and the best method was used to construct two predictive models.

Building the integrated deep learning-clinical nomogram

Univariate analysis was used to select statistically significant clinicopathological variables in the primary cohort (p < 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression analysis was conducted to build the nomogram from the clinical variables and deep learning-based imaging predictors.

Evaluation of the nomogram

The discrimination power of the nomogram was evaluated by the area under the receiver operating curve (AUC). Calibration curves were drawn to assess the calibration of the nomogram with the Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit test. The net reclassification index (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) were used to evaluate the incremental improvement in accuracy and discriminative ability of incorporating the adipose features into the nomogram. Additionally, stratified analyses were performed on all patients’ clinicopathological characteristics and CT protocol. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was conducted to evaluate the clinical usefulness of the nomogram by quantifying the net benefits at different threshold probabilities.

Quantification and statistical analysis

The descriptive statistics were presented as means ± SD or medians (interquartile range) for continuous variables and percentages of the number for categorical variables. The Student’s t test, or the Mann-Whitney U test, was used to determine the difference between the two groups. A comparison of qualitative variables between groups was performed with a one-way analysis of variance, or Kruskal-Wallis H test. The Fisher’s exact test, or Chi-square test, was used for categorical variables between groups. We conducted 10-fold cross-validation at a ratio of 90% (training) to 10% (validation) to train and test the model on datasets from three centers. Performance indicators included sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, and area under the ROC curve (AUC). The quantitative comparison of AUCs was carried out with the Delong test. All statistical tests were used using R software (version 3.0.1; https://www.r-project.org). A two-tailed p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

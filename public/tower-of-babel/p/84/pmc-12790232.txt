Utility of Cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging for Isolated Right Ventricular Takotsubo Syndrome
Authors: Yosuke Uchida, Masataka Suzuki, Hiromi Hashimura, Tsuyoshi Osue, Hiroshi Eizawa
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Isolated right ventricular (RV) Takotsubo syndrome (TTS) is a rare variant of TTS that involves only the RV. Herein, we report a case of a 79-year-old man diagnosed with isolated RV-TTS. Echocardiography showed mid-to-apical RV akinesis, with preserved left ventricular function. Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging showed a high-intensity area in the RV apex on a T2-weighted short-tau inversion recovery (T2-STIR) image, indicating myocardial edema. Following conservative treatment, the RV wall motion abnormality improved, and the T2-STIR high-intensity area showed resolution. This case highlights the utility of cardiac magnetic resonance imaging in evaluating transient myocardial edema in isolated RV-TTS.

Case Summary

A 79-year-old man with a history of hypertension, diabetes, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease was referred to our institution due to dyspnea on exertion lasting several days. He reported no preceding emotional or physical stress. The oxygen saturation decreased to 92% on room air. Electrocardiography revealed complete right bundle branch block and negative T waves in leads V1 to V3. Serum high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I level was high (547.1 pg/mL), and brain natriuretic peptide level was slightly elevated (133.2 pg/mL). Transthoracic echocardiography revealed that the mid-to-apical right ventricular (RV) wall motion was akinetic with RV enlargement, although the left ventricular (LV) function was preserved (Figures 1A and 1B, Video 1). Contrast-enhanced computed tomography ruled out pulmonary embolism. Right ventriculography revealed mid-to-apical akinesis in the RV (Figures 1C and 1D, Video 2). Coronary angiography demonstrated no occlusion or flow disturbance in the RV branch of the right coronary artery. Cine images of cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (CMR) exhibited mid-to-apical akinesis in the RV free wall (Figures 1E and 1F, Video 3). T2-weighted short-tau inversion recovery (T2-STIR) image revealed a high-intensity area in the apex of the RV free wall (Figure 1G). Late gadolinium enhancement was absent in both ventricles (Figure 1H). The patient was diagnosed with isolated RV Takotsubo syndrome (TTS) and received conservative treatment, including oxygen therapy and treatment with an angiotensin receptor blocker. The RV wall motion abnormality, the patient's symptoms, and oxygenation gradually improved, and the patient was discharged on the 14th day. At 3-month follow-up, CMR revealed that the RV wall motion abnormality had improved. Discussion

TTS is primarily characterized by transient LV dysfunction, often precipitated by emotional or physical stress. Although LV involvement is typical of TTS, approximately one-third of cases may involve both ventricles. Nevertheless, isolated RV-TTS is exceedingly rare and was initially documented in 2010.1 Isolated RV-TTS can lead to acute right-sided heart failure because of RV dysfunction, which mimics RV infarction and pulmonary embolism. CMR is widely used in the diagnosis and evaluation of TTS, particularly for myocardial wall motion abnormalities and tissue characterization.2 In LV-TTS cases, T2-STIR typically shows a high-intensity area in the LV apex, reflecting transient myocardial edema. Late gadolinium enhancement is absent or minimally present. However, the CMR data on isolated RV-TTS have been limited to cine images to date. This is the first case showing a T2-STIR high-intensity area in the RV apex, which is subsequently resolved in the chronic phase. CMR is helpful for evaluating transient myocardial edema in isolated RV-TTS.

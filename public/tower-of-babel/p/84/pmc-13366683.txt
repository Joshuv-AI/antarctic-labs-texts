Hormonal superstimulation strategies for laparoscopic ovum pick-up (LOPU) in prepubertal Holstein-Friesian calves: a comparison of FSH and FSH/LH regimens
Authors: Jakub Kulus, Magdalena Kulus, Kornelia Krajnik, Mikołaj Chwarzyński, Jędrzej M. Jaśkowski, Paul Mozdziak, Bartosz Kempisty, Paweł Antosik
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLaparoscopic ovum pick-up (LOPU) combined with IVF allows embryos to be derived from sexually immature heifers as young as 2 months old. An essential step in this procedure is hormonal stimulation to enable the growth of ovarian follicles that can be punctured. Developing the right stimulation protocol is necessary to obtain an adequate, repeatable response of the ovaries to the hormonal stimulation used. The aim of the study was to determine the usefulness of the FSH/LH mixture for ovarian follicle superstimulation in calves.MethodsThe study was conducted on eighteen calves aged 2 to 4 months, weighing between 62.5 and 112 kg. The animals were divided into 3 groups of 6 individuals and subjected to 3 days of hormonal stimulation via intramuscular injection at 12-hour intervals prior to the procedure: group I with follicle-stimulating hormone FSH (total dose of 168 IU FSH), group II with FSH/LH (total dose of 240 IU FSH/LH), and group III with a reduced dose of FSH/LH (total dose of 165 IU FSH/LH). Simultaneously, progesterone-releasing vaginal inserts were also used. Laparoscopic aspiration of oocytes was performed under general anesthesia. The fluid was evaluated under a stereoscopic microscope to locate oocyte-cumulus complexes.ResultsAs a result of the use of superstimulation protocols, a statistically significant difference in the number of ovarian follicles was observed, with an average of 87.7 ± 29.2, 25.7 ± 4.1, and 21.2 ± 4.9 for the FSH/LH (total dose 240 IU), reduced FSH/LH, and FSH groups, respectively. The number of oocytes retrieved was 12.7 ± 4.7, 32.8 ± 7.6, 12.3 ± 2.1 for the FSH, FSH/LH, and reduced FSH/LH dose groups, respectively. A statistically significant difference in the % of oocyte retrieval relative to the number of follicles was demonstrated, and it was: 59.4 ± 14.9%, 48.8 ± 8.6%, 39.9 ± 10.1% for the FSH, FSH/LH low dose, and FSH/LH groups, respectively.ConclusionsThe FSH/LH-based calf superstimulation protocol, causes a very intense ovarian response. The proposed protocol for hormonal stimulation of calves will increase the possibility of wider application of the LOPU procedure and support early genetic selection in cattle.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12917-026-05570-0.

Background

Assisted reproductive technologies (ART) such as embryo transfer (ET) and ovum pick-up (OPU) in combination with in vitro embryo production (IVEP) significantly accelerates breeding progress in livestock [1]. In recent years, there has been a significant increase in the proportion of embryos transferred that are produced in vitro compared to in vivo, both in dairy and beef cattle [2]. This highlights the importance of oocyte retrieval and their use in embryo production for genetic improvement of animals. The basic technique for obtaining oocytes for in vitro culture is OPU, which involves aspirating follicular fluid using an ultrasound probe inserted transvaginally under the control of the operator via the rectum [3]. The anatomical size of the digestive and reproductive tract of adult cattle allows manipulation of the reproductive system. The collection of oocytes from sexually immature cattle and their use in IVEP significantly accelerates genetic progress by enabling the production of offspring from female calves before they reach breeding maturity [4]. In addition, the use of genomic evaluation allows for the clear identification of individuals with high breeding value from which it is worthwhile to collect oocytes [5]. Unfortunately, OPU cannot be performed on young, sexually immature cattle or other smaller species such as sheep and goats due to their insufficient size. Minimally invasive laparoscopic methods have been proposed for oocyte retrieval.

Laparoscopic ovum pick-up (LOPU) uses endoscopic techniques to aspirate follicular fluid from calves or small animal species. The first attempts to obtain follicular fluid via laparoscopic access were made in the 1970s in sheep [6]. In the 1990s, this technique was used in calves and the oocytes obtained were fertilized in vitro [7]. Due to the difficulty in assessing the breeding value of calves at that time (no possibility of genomic evaluation), this method did not sufficiently develop. In the last 10 years, LOPU has found wider application in domestic cattle and water buffalo calves [8], making it possible to obtain embryos from very young individuals. To date, the use of LOPU has also been described in other animal species such as sheep [9], goats [10], pigs [11], deer [12], primates [13], and wild cats [14]. Oocytes obtained using the LOPU method undergo standard stages of embryo production in vitro. These stages include in vitro maturation (IVM), in vitro fertilization (IVF), and in vitro culture (IVC). The embryos can be further transferred to recipients of particular species or banked to create a genetic reserve.

One of the key factors influencing the effectiveness of assisted reproduction techniques is appropriate hormonal stimulation of animals. Performing OPU and LOPU requires proper preparation of animals in terms of the pool of ovarian follicles [15, 16]. In the case of OPU performed on adult cattle, it is possible not to perform hormonal stimulation of ovarian follicles for growth, as they undergo natural wave-like growth [17]. However, in most cases, appropriately selected hormonal stimulation is performed. In calves that are sexually immature, hormonal stimulation is necessary to promote the growth of ovarian follicles. Hormonal stimulation of calves is expected to have a positive effect on both the number of cumulus-oocyte complexes (COCs) obtained and their fertilization competence [18]. Calf oocytes exhibit a reduced ability to reach the embryonic stage, which is associated with both their cytoplasmic immaturity and an inappropriate ovarian follicular microenvironment. Therefore, increasing the pool of available COCs through hormonal stimulation, despite their low competence to reach the embryonic stage, partially alleviates the main problem associated with obtaining embryos from calves. Several protocols for superstimulation in calves have been described [4, 15, 18, 19], which were derived from hormonal stimulation in adults and adapted for use in calves. Follicular stimulation is based on single or multiple intramuscular injections of preparations containing pFSH (V-Folltropin, Vetoquinol) also in combination with eCG. The result of this stimulation was an increase in the number of antral follicles and usable COCs compared to the control groups that were not hormonally stimulated. Hormonal stimulation of calves for LOPU using a preparation containing FSH/LH (Pluset®, Calier) has not been described. Pluset is widely used in adult cattle in various protocols for superstimulation, including prior to OPU [20, 21]. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to present the use of different doses of an FSH/LH-containing preparation in the stimulation of calves prior to LOPU in comparison to multiple injections of pFSH [22–24]. The aim of the study was to determine the effectiveness of hormonal stimulation in three groups of calves aged 2–4 months. The effectiveness of stimulation was assessed based on the response of the ovary in terms of the total number of ovarian follicles, the number of ovarian follicles larger than 5 mm, the number of oocytes retrieved, and their quality. The development of an appropriate method of hormonal stimulation prior to LOPU may improve the effectiveness and efficiency of IVEP by increasing the number of oocytes available for use. The improvement in oocyte numbers could accelerate breeding progress by significantly shortening the generation interval, and it can also be used to conserve the genetic resources of endangered species or native breeds [25].

Methods

Animals

The experiment involved 18 Holstein-Friesian calves aged 2–4 months, divided into 3 experimental groups. The animals’ body weight ranged from 62.5 to 112 kg. The animals were kept on a dairy farm. They were provided with good living conditions, fed hay and water ad libitum, and given age-appropriate amounts of milk replacer and starter muesli. The animals underwent clinical examination and showed no signs of disease.

Hormonal stimulation

The calves were hormonally stimulated 3 days prior to LOPU via intramuscular injections of pFSH or FSH/LH. At the start of the intramuscular injections, vaginal implants (CIDR, Zoetis) were also administered in all animals and removed at the beginning of the LOPU procedure. The calves participating in the experiment were randomly assigned into 3 groups:

The pFSH-based protocol was adapted from the literature on LOPU in calves, while FSH/LH-based stimulation was adapted from protocols used in adult cattle prior to conventional transvaginal OPU. Low-dose FSH/LH stimulation was also proposed due to the lower body weight of calves. The procedures were performed within 12–18 h after the last intramuscular administration of the hormone, and the vaginal inserts were removed at the start of LOPU.

Preoperative preparation and anesthesia

The animals were prepared for the procedure in accordance with the previously presented protocol [26, 27]. The animals undergoing the procedure were fasted for 24 h and deprived of water for 12 h prior to the procedure. Each animal underwent a clinical examination prior to the procedure, and only animals showing no signs of disease were eligible for the procedure. Premedication and induction of anesthesia were performed by intravenous injection of a mixture of tiletamine and zolazepam (Zoletil, Virbac) at a dose of 1 mg/kg. After the animal was anesthetized, it was intubated and the endotracheal tube was secured to the jaw. Isoflurane at a concentration of 2–3% with an oxygen flow of 2–2.5 L/min was used for general anesthesia. Throughout the procedure, vital signs (heart rate, respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, body temperature, capnography) were monitored using a multiparametric monitor. During the procedure, intravenous fluid therapy was administered using Ringer’s lactate solution at a rate of 5 ml/h/kg, and the cornea was protected from drying out using hyaluronic acid eye gel. The abdominal area was prepared for surgery, shaved, and disinfected with chlorhexidine-based agents before the surgical field was established.

LOPU procedure

Each procedure was performed by the same team, and the puncture, aspiration and counting were performed by the same operator. The classification of ovarian follicles based on their diameter was always performed by the individual conducting the procedure and involved comparing the diameter of the needle to the diameter of the follicle at the time of puncture. Each procedure was recorded, and upon completion, the results were verified by reviewing the video. The LOPU was performed under sterile conditions using a 3-port technique. The first endoscopic channel—an optical channel with a diameter of 5 mm—was inserted into the midline of the abdomen. A rigid endoscope with a length of 40 cm, a diameter of 5 mm, and an angle of 0° was inserted into the abdominal cavity through this channel. An optical fiber was connected to the endoscope, and a preliminary assessment of the abdominal cavity was performed. Pneumoperitoneum was created using CO₂ (10 mmHg and a flow rate of 10 L/min). The animal was then moved to the Trendelenburg position with its head down, using a special surgical table with adjustable angle. This position allows the internal organs to fall towards the diaphragm, exposing the uterus and ovaries. Next, two additional endoscopic channels were created—working ports, approximately 8 cm caudally and laterally from the optical channel, forming a triangle that allowed for ergonomic performance of further procedures. Atraumatic endoscopic forceps were then inserted into the working channel to grasp the ovary, and a catheter with a 20G injection needle was inserted through another channel. This allowed for the aspiration of follicular fluid from the ovaries with a diameter greater than 2 mm. The catheter was connected to an aspiration pump creating a vacuum of 60–70 mm Hg. The aspirated follicular fluid was collected in 15 ml Falcon tubes containing heated TCM-199 medium supplemented with HEPES and 0.3% BSA. The Falcon tubes were kept in heating blocks at a temperature of 38 degrees Celsius throughout the procedure. After aspiration, the ovaries were rinsed with warm saline solution to remove any remaining blood clots and prevent adhesions. The previously created pneumoperitoneum was deflated and the endoscopic ports were removed. The wounds were sutured in two layers using absorbable suture (PGA 2.0). The wounds were secured with an antiseptic agent, and the entire procedure lasted 30–40 min.

Postoperative care

After the procedure, the animals were placed in individual kennels where they were kept calm and monitored by a veterinarian. For pain management and anti-inflammation, meloxicam was administered subcutaneously (Vetaflunix®, 0.5 mg/kg body weight) and prophylactic antibiotic therapy with long-acting oxytetracycline was administered intramuscularly (oxytetracycline (Oxytet LA®, 20 mg/kg body weight). The animals were monitored for body temperature, heart rate, respiratory rate, appetite, and behavior for 24 h after the procedure. The wounds were checked for 3 days after the procedure, and wound hygiene was maintained with a silver ion-containing agent. There were no complications after the procedure.

Search for oocytes and their classification

The retrieved follicular fluid was transported to the laboratory in Falcon tubes placed in blocks heated to 38 degrees Celsius. The Falcon tubes were placed in a laminar flow cabinet and the search for cumulus oocyte complexes (COCs) began under a stereoscopic microscope (Leica S9i). The retrieved oocytes were aspirated and transferred to fresh medium. After transfer, the quality of the oocytes was assessed (number of granulosa cell layers surrounding the oocyte and uniformity of the cytoplasm) and divided into 4 quality grades, in accordance with the methodology described earlier [28].

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). Prior to its implementation, the obtained results were analyzed for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. The results obtained via ANOVA that showed statistically significant differences were subjected to additional post hoc comparisons using Tukey’s honestly significant difference (HSD) test. The results obtained in this manner were presented as means and standard deviations and were considered statistically significant at p < 0.05. Eta squared (η²) and Cohen’s f statistics were used to calculate the effect size for the proposed stimulation protocol. The obtained η² results were compared to the following values: 0.01, 0.06, and 0.14, which correspond to small, medium, and large effects, respectively. In contrast, the obtained Cohen’s f results were compared to the values 0.10, 0.25, and 0.40, which correspond to small, medium, and large effect sizes, respectively. Due to the small number of subjects in the study group, the obtained ANOVA results were validated using the nonparametric Kruskal-Wallis test.

Results

The study compared three different protocols for hormonal stimulation of calves aged 2–4 months prior to LOPU. The effectiveness of the protocols was assessed based on ovarian response, accounting for the number of ovarian follicles, their size, the number of oocytes retrieved (Fig. 1), and their quality (Tables 1, 2 and 3).

The highest number of retrieved oocytes was observed in the Pluset 0.8 ml group (32.8 ± 7.6), which was significantly higher than in both the FSH group (12.7 ± 4.7) and the Pluset 0.55 ml group (12.3 ± 2.1) (p < 0.01). For high-quality oocytes (class I), the Pluset 0.8 ml protocol yielded the highest number of oocytes (19.5 ± 4.2) compared to FSH (8.8 ± 4.0) and Pluset 0.55 ml (8.7 ± 1.8) (p < 0.01). No significant differences were observed between the FSH and Pluset 0.55 ml treatment groups in the number of oocytes obtained or their quality (p > 0.05). No statistically significant effect of the stimulation program used on the number of degenerated oocytes was observed (Tables 1, 2 and 3). The effectiveness of oocyte recovery varied between the individual experimental groups, with both the method of stimulation (Tables 4, 5, 6 and 7) and the age of the calves undergoing the LOPU procedure (Table 8).

The highest percentage of recovered oocytes was achieved in the group of animals stimulated with pure FSH, amounted to 59.4 ± 14.9%. The lowest oocyte recovery rate during aspiration was obtained in calves stimulated with a single dose of 0.8 ml of FSH/LH, and amounted to 39.9 ± 9.0%, while an intermediate value of 48.8 ± 8.6% oocyte recovery was observed in calves stimulated with a lower dose of FSH/LH (0.55 ml).

A statistically significant difference in oocyte retrieval was observed between the FSH-stimulated group and the group stimulated with a higher dose of FSH/LH (Table 7). However, no statistically significant differences were observed in the group of calves stimulated with a lower dose of FSH/LH.

No statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) in the percentage of oocyte recovery were found between age groups. However, an upward trend was observed in the number of oocytes recovered, the number of follicles larger than 5 mm in diameter, and the percentage of oocyte recovery with age.

In summary, the strongest ovarian response was achieved after the use of a single dose of 0.8 ml of FSH/LH, as confirmed by the recorded video (see Additional file 1,2), the stimulation protocol based on pure FSH gave average results (see Additional file 3, 4) while the weakest ovarian response was the result of administering a single dose of 0.55 ml of FSH/LH (see Additional file 5, 6).

Discussion

It is important to consider the new and clinically relevant data on ovarian superstimulation in prepubertal Holstein calves undergoing laparoscopic ovum pick-up (LOPU). The results and observations indicate that both the type of gonadotropin and the stimulation protocol have a significant impact on ovarian response, follicular dynamics and oocyte yield in young donors, as directly shown in Tables 1, 2 and 3 and summarized in Table 7. It is worth noting that the current study is the first to experimentally evaluate the combined FSH/LH (Pluset®) for ovarian superstimulation in prepubertal calves, thus extending concepts previously established only for superovulation programmes in adult cattle [20, 21]. Unlike classic protocols commonly used in prepubertal calves (Table 9), based only on FSH, the Pluset® used in the present study is distinguished by the presence of luteinising hormone (LH) and follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) at 1:1 ratio. The study was based on the assumption that supplementation with LH, in combination with FSH, would have a positive effect on ovarian response and increase the intensity of follicular development in prepubertal donors. LH plays a key role in supporting dominant follicle selection, cumulus expansion and cytoplasmic maturation of the oocyte [1, 29–34]. The results regarding the response to follicular recruitment support the hypothesis. The Pluset 0.8 ml protocol resulted in a significantly higher number of total follicles and follicles > 5 mm compared to both the FSH-only and the lower-dose Pluset groups (p < 0.001). The increased ovarian response observed in the FSH/LH (0.8 mL) group was associated with reduced oocyte retrieval efficiency during follicular fluid aspiration. The percentage of retrieved oocytes was statistically significantly lower in the group stimulated with Pluset 0.8 mL compared to the group receiving FSH alone (39.9 ± 10.1% vs. 59.4 ± 14.9%, p < 0.05; Table 7) indicated that, although hormonal stimulation with the addition of LH increases the number of ovarian follicles, it negatively affects the percentage of retrieved oocytes in the stimulated group. Exogenous LH in immature individuals, due to the underdeveloped hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis, may cause as-yet-unknown effects within the ovary. LH may stimulate the growth and recruitment of ovarian follicles without ensuring adequate synchronization between their growth and the maturity they achieve. This explanation is consistent with the observed fact that, despite a greater number of follicles, the recovery rate did not increase proportionally. Similarly, the number of high-quality oocytes (Class I) was significantly higher in the group receiving 0.8 ml of Pluset compared to the other groups. This suggests that LH supplementation has a beneficial effect on oocyte developmental competence. A significant feature of hormonal stimulation of calves using a preparation containing 0.8 ml of FSH/LH is the wide variability in ovarian response within the stimulated group of animals. These results suggest that protocols based on LH supplementation may be particularly beneficial in animals with an exceptionally large ovarian reserve. By using endocrine markers such as anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH), it is possible to selectively identify individuals with a large ovarian reserve from a larger group of animals. This selection method was also indicated in earlier studies, both in calves and adult cattle [35, 36]. Donor selection could be based on AMH measurements to systemize protocols for calves using LOPU by targeting use of FSH/LH. Analysing the results obtained in this study and data from previous literature, it can be concluded that LH supplementation should not be used routinely in all prepubertal donors, but rather reserved for calves with high internal follicular potential. In summary, it should be emphasised that modifying the previously adopted protocol for LH supplementation may have a dual effect on sexually immature calves. Firstly, it intensively increases follicle recruitment compared to the FSH-only protocol, and secondly, it may reduce the efficiency of oocyte recovery, which is associated with the developmental limitations of the juvenile ovary [15, 18, 33].

In contrast, the calf stimulation protocol based on multiple FSH injections resulted in a lower number of ovarian follicles but a higher oocyte recovery rate, which indicate less dynamic follicular recruitment and, at the same time, more stable oocyte retrieval efficiency, which is consistent with previous studies [15, 18].

The use of a lower dose in the Pluset stimulation protocol for calves (0.55 ml) was associated with an intermediate ovarian response. These results did not differ statistically from the group stimulated with pure FSH for most parameters. This may suggest that reducing the dose of exogenous LH may favorably affect oocyte retrieval efficiency without having a positive effect on the number of maturing follicles. This also indicates a strong influence of the dose of the LH-containing preparation on the overall ovarian response and suggests the need for further research to determine the appropriate dosage.

An analysis considering the age of the calves examined (Table 8) revealed an increased number of oocytes retrieved as well as more pronounced growth of ovarian follicles with age, although these differences were not statistically significant (p > 0.05). This observed trend is consistent with previously reported results indicating a gradual acquisition of ovarian competence to respond to hormonal stimulation between 2 and 6 months of age [15, 18].

In summary, the use of hormonal preparations containing LH has a beneficial effect on ovarian response and increases the number of recruited follicles, while having an adverse effect on oocyte retrieval. Consequently, it is necessary to use an optimal dose of treatment and approach adapted to age and ovarian reserve when selecting a hormonal strategy for calves prior to the LOPU procedure.

Conclusions

The study demonstrates that the use of different hormonal stimulation protocols in Holstein-Friesian calves undergoing LOPU induces different ovarian responses. Significant differences were observed in both the number of ovarian follicles and the number of oocytes recovered, depending on the composition and dosage of the hormonal stimulation used. Each of the protocols used in the study allowed for the recruitment of ovarian follicles, thus enabling their puncture during LOPU. The hormonal superstimulation protocol based on the administration of a preparation containing FSH/LH (Pluset®) was characterized by a significant increase in ovarian response compared to the stimulation protocol based on the administration of pure pFSH. This suggests a potential beneficial effect of LH in the context of increasing the effectiveness of LOPU. The analysis of the impact of the age of the calves (2–4 months old) on ovarian response showed a gradual improvement with age. Despite the lack of statistically significant differences, older calves had a greater number of follicles suitable for puncture and a greater absolute number of oocytes recovered. In summary, the results presented in this study provide new data on the possibility of using a preparation containing FSH/LH in the hormonal stimulation of calves prior to LOPU. The results provide a basis for the optimization of hormonal protocols. Work on improving the effectiveness of LOPU-IVEP procedures in calves is crucial for accelerating breeding progress by significantly shortening the intergenerational interval and supporting the protection of valuable animal genetic resources.

HeartMate 3–related ECG artefact resolved by 40‑Hz low‑pass filtering
Authors: Cornelis van der Heiden, Parsa Jahangiri, Kadir Caliskan
Published: 2026

Continuous-flow LVADs can generate prominent high‑frequency artefacts on bedside ECG/telemetry. This can mimic atrial fibrillation or ventricular arrhythmias and may render tracings uninterpretable.

LVAD use is increasing and patients are admitted to the ICU postoperatively; intensivists will, therefore, encounter this diagnostic challenge more frequently. Reliable ECG rhythm assessment is essential for safe monitoring and timely clinical decision-making.

We evaluated ECG filtering using a model consisting of a running HeartMate 3 pump, an ECG simulator and a patient monitor, enabling noise-signal measurement. The dominant noise frequency increased with pump speed (≈66-Hz at 4000-rpm; ≈90-Hz at 6000-rpm). A 40‑Hz low-pass filter provided the most robust suppression across speeds and restored rhythm interpretability (Fig. 1).

To enable reliable ECG interpretation in LVAD patients, we implemented a one-click 40-Hz low-pass filter option in our electronic ECG viewer. Centres managing LVAD patients should ensure an equivalent 40-Hz setting is available for bedside ECG review.

40-Hz low-pass filtering improves rhythm legibility but may attenuate high-frequency ECG detail (e.g., QRS sharpness), which may limit detailed morphological or ischaemic (ST–T) analysis[1, 2]. Therefore, its use should primarily be confined to restoring rhythm interpretability when an artefact is present, not as a replacement for standard diagnostic ECG interpretation.

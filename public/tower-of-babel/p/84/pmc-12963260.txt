Note on extremal problems about connected subgraph sums
Authors: Stijn Cambie, Carla Groenland
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
For a graph G with vertex assignment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c:V(G)\rightarrow \mathbb {Z}^+$$\end{document}c:V(G)→Z+, we define \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sum _{v\in V(H)}c(v)$$\end{document}∑v∈V(H)c(v) for a connected subgraph H of G as a connected subgraph sum of G. We study the set S(G, c) of connected subgraph sums and, in particular, resolve a problem posed by O.-H. S. Lo in a strong form. We show that for each n-vertex graph G, there is a vertex assignment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c:V(G)\rightarrow \{1,\dots ,12n^2\}$$\end{document}c:V(G)→{1,⋯,12n2} such that for every n-vertex graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'\not \cong G$$\end{document}G′≇G and vertex assignment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c'$$\end{document}c′ for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'$$\end{document}G′, the corresponding collections of connected subgraph sums are different (i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G,c)\ne S(G',c')$$\end{document}S(G,c)≠S(G′,c′)). We also provide some remarks on vertex assignments of a graph G for which all connected subgraph sums are different.

Introduction

For a graph G, a subgraph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$H \subseteq G$$\end{document}H⊆G and a vertex assignment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c :V(G) \rightarrow \mathbb Z^+$$\end{document}c:V(G)→Z+ with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathbb {Z}^+=\{1,2,3,\dots \}$$\end{document}Z+={1,2,3,⋯}, we denote \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c(H)=\sum _{v \in V(H)} c(v)$$\end{document}c(H)=∑v∈V(H)c(v) as the subgraph sum of H. The set\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ S(G,c)= \{c(H) \mid H {\text { connected subgraph of }}G\} $$\end{document}S(G,c)={c(H)∣Hconnected subgraph ofG}is the set of all subgraph sums over all connected (induced) subgraphs of G for vertex assignment c. Here we do not consider the empty graph as a subgraph (this convention has no impact on the results, besides S(G, c) containing 0 or not).

For example, when \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G=K_n$$\end{document}G=Kn has vertex set \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\{1,\dots ,n\}$$\end{document}{1,⋯,n}, then\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$ S(K_n,c)= \left\{ \sum _{i\in U}c(i): U\subseteq \{1,\dots ,n\}\right\} $$\end{document}S(Kn,c)=∑i∈Uc(i):U⊆{1,⋯,n}is the collection of subset sums of the positive integers \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c(1),\dots ,c(n)$$\end{document}c(1),⋯,c(n).

For a tree T, S(T, c) is also called the tree spectrum [6]. The study of the tree spectrum in [6] was inspired by a connection to the cycle spectrum of planar Hamiltonian graphs. On-Hei Solomon Lo [6] studied the number of values in a certain range (interval) belonging to S(T, c). Relatedly, one can remark that no more than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( {\begin{array}{c}n\\ \lfloor \frac{n}{2}\rfloor \end{array}}\right) $$\end{document}n⌊n2⌋ many values can appear in S(G, c) for interval lengths smaller than \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\min _{v \in V(G)} c(v)$$\end{document}minv∈V(G)c(v), by the Littlewood-Offord theorem or Sperner’s theorem. The latter is still true when considering the set S(G, c) as a multiset.

It is natural to expect that G has some influence on which S(G, c) are possible (for different choices of c) and that this may depend on the size of the “vertex weights” that are allowed. For a graph G and positive integer N, let \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G;N)=\{S(G,c)\mid c:V(G)\rightarrow \mathbb {Z}^+ {\text { with }}c(G)=N\}.$$\end{document}S(G;N)={S(G,c)∣c:V(G)→Z+withc(G)=N}. Lo [6] (at the end of the conclusion section) made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

For any two non-isomorphic connected graphs \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_1$$\end{document}G1 and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G_2$$\end{document}G2 on n vertices, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G_1; 2^n-1) \not = S(G_2; 2^n-1).$$\end{document}S(G1;2n-1)≠S(G2;2n-1).

We prove this conjecture and strengthen it in two directions.

Firstly, we show that it is even possible to select an element in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G;2^n-1)$$\end{document}S(G;2n-1) that is unique to G.

Theorem 1

Let G be a connected graph on n vertices. Then there exists \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c:V(G)\rightarrow \{1,\dots ,2^n-1\}$$\end{document}c:V(G)→{1,⋯,2n-1} with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c(G)=2^n-1$$\end{document}c(G)=2n-1 such that for any n-vertex graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'$$\end{document}G′, if there is a vertex assignment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c':V(G)\rightarrow \mathbb {Z}^+$$\end{document}c′:V(G)→Z+ satisfying \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G,c) = S(G',c')$$\end{document}S(G,c)=S(G′,c′), then \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G\cong G'$$\end{document}G≅G′.

Here we assign the powers of 2 from 1 up to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^{n-1}$$\end{document}2n-1, and connectedness implies \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c(G)=2^n-1$$\end{document}c(G)=2n-1. The result above immediately implies the conjecture. For large n, we also reduce \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2^n-1$$\end{document}2n-1 to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$12n^3$$\end{document}12n3.

Theorem 2

Let G be a graph on n vertices. Then there exists \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c:V(G)\rightarrow \{1,\dots ,12n^2\}$$\end{document}c:V(G)→{1,⋯,12n2} such that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G,c)=S(G',c')$$\end{document}S(G,c)=S(G′,c′) such that for any n-vertex graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'$$\end{document}G′, if there is a vertex assignment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c':V(G)\rightarrow \mathbb {Z}^+$$\end{document}c′:V(G)→Z+ satisfying \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G,c) = S(G',c')$$\end{document}S(G,c)=S(G′,c′), then \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G\cong G'$$\end{document}G≅G′. When G is connected, the assumption that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'$$\end{document}G′ has n vertices may be replaced by the assumption that \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'$$\end{document}G′ is connected.

In other words, we can “reconstruct” G from knowing that there is a vertex assignment \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c:V(G)\rightarrow \mathbb {Z}^+$$\end{document}c:V(G)→Z+ with a particular set of connected subgraph sums S(G, c), and only “modest” weights on the vertices are required for c, i.e., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c(G)=O(n^3).$$\end{document}c(G)=O(n3). There is a wide literature on graph reconstruction questions, perhaps the most similar to ours being the reconstruction from the set of vertex sets of size k that induce a connected subgraph [2, 5].

Note that for any graph G and assignment c, the graph \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$G'=G+G$$\end{document}G′=G+G obtained by taking the disjoint union of two copies of G, with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$c'$$\end{document}c′ defined as c on both copies, has \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$S(G,c)=S(G',c')$$\end{document}S(G,c)=S(G′,c′). So some assumption of connectivity or on the number of vertices is required in Theorem 2.

The proofs of our main results are presented in Section 2. We make some observations about vertex assignments for which all connected subgraphs yield distinct sums in Section 3, which are related to the Erdős distinct subset sum problem, which asks about the minimum of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\max T$$\end{document}maxT among the sets \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T \in \left( {\begin{array}{c}\mathbb Z^+\\ k\end{array}}\right) $$\end{document}T∈Z+k with distinct subset sums, and optimal Golomb rulers. In Section 4, we discuss potential directions for future work.

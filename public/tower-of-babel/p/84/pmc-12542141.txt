Effect of linearly polarized light irradiation near stellate ganglion on postoperative sleep disorders in patients undergoing modified radical mastectomy of breast cancer
Authors: Weiming Liu, Fei Sun, Lei Wang, Man Wang, Dongxin Yuan, Zeguang Wang, Xiaojuan Qie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStellate ganglion block (SGB) improves postoperative sleep quality but is associated with a variety of complications. Linearly polarized light irradiation near the stellate ganglion region (LI-SG) is a non-invasive alternative. We investigated the effect of LI-SG on postoperative sleep disorders in patients undergoing modified radical mastectomy of breast cancer.MethodsThis prospective, randomized, controlled trial was conducted between June 2024 to December 2024, enrolling 114 patients undergoing modified radical mastectomy. Participants were randomly assigned to the control or the LI-SG group. The primary outcome was subjective sleep quality assessed using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI). Objective sleep measures (sleep efficiency, total sleep time and awaking times), visual analog scale (VAS) score, total number of patient-controlled anesthesia (PCA) compressions, serum indicators, and adverse events were recorded as the secondary outcomes.ResultsCompared to the control group, the PSQI and sleep efficiency, total sleep time and awakening times showed significant improvement at the second (all P < 0.001) and the 5th postoperative day (all P < 0.001) in the LI-SG group. The VAS score (3.51 ± 0.77 vs 2.51 ± 0.47, P < 0.001) and total number of PCA compressions (15.39 ± 2.11 vs 5.62 ± 1.53, P < 0.001) in the LI-SG group were significantly decreased. The serum melatonin was significantly increased, while TNF-α and IL-6 levels were decreased in the LI-SG groups at the second (all P < 0.001) and the 5th postoperative day (all P < 0.001). Furthermore, compared to the control group, the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting (16.07% vs 5.17%, P < 0.001) was lower and the patient satisfaction (4 vs 6, P < 0.001) was higher in the LI-SG group.ConclusionLI-SG improved the postoperative sleep quality and analgesic effect of patients, while improving patient satisfaction.Keywords: Stellate ganglion, Linearly polarized light irradiation, Postoperative sleep disorders, Breast cancer

Introduction

Postoperative sleep disorders (PSD) contribute to patient dissatisfaction and poor outcomes. More than 70% of breast cancer patients experience PSD after undergoing general anesthesia for surgical treatment (Varallo et al. 2022). In these patients, PSD may last for 2 months, increasing the risk of pain sensitivity, mental disorders, cardiovascular events, and hospital stay (Lam et al. 2017). The optimal strategies for the prevention and treatment of PSD are still under debate. Currently, the management of PSD primarily involves a combination of non-pharmacological and pharmacological interventions. Non-pharmacological approaches, such as the use of eye masks, earplugs, and music, have been reported to improve the subjective sleep duration; however, the conclusions from these studies have been inconsistent (Hu et al. 2015; Wang et al. 2023). Pharmacological options, especially sedative-hypnotic drugs, exhibit sedative and hypnotic properties but are associated with potential risks of falls, addiction, and delirium (Tsukinaga et al. 2023).

A stellate ganglion block (SGB) is an injection of anesthetic medication near a collection of nerve cells called the stellate ganglion. Previous research has indicated that SGB can contribute to the regulation of the autonomic nervous system, endocrine system, and immune system, and bolster the body’s defense mechanisms (Wang et al. 2017; Lipov et al. 2020). SGB also has a beneficial impact on mitigating post-traumatic stress responses (Rae Olmsted et al. 2020 Feb 1) and has been shown to ameliorate PSD (Luo et al. 2023; Yang et al. 2023). SGB can be performed using the traditional landmark-based approach, or with guidance using ultrasound. Even if under ultrasound guidance, the incidence of hoarseness, bucking, neck pain in SGB can still reach 24%, reducing the satisfaction of patients (Yang et al. 2023; Kimura et al. 2005; Atici and Akoz 2010).

Linearly polarized light, with a wavelength of 0.7–1.3 μm, penetrates tissues up to 5–7 cm deep, boosts cellular metabolism, and aids nerve repair. Clinical studies show it reduces abnormal release of vasoactive neuropeptides, corrects vascular dysfunction, and alleviates migraines (Hou et al. 2024). Moreover, linearly polarized light irradiation near the stellate ganglion region (LI-SG) has been employed as a noninvasive method for sympathetic blockade. LI-SG is well tolerated by patients and has emerged as a substitute for SGB, with no reported thermal injury or side effects. This treatment is also indicated for patients who refuse to receive injections, are undergoing anticoagulant therapy, or have bleeding disorders such as hemophilia. Studies have demonstrated that LI-SG exhibits similar effectiveness to traditional invasive SGB in individuals with neuropathic pain syndrome (Lee et al. 2006). Previous studies have shown that LI-SG effectively alleviates symptoms of complex regional pain syndrome, fibromyalgia, post-herpetic neuralgia, and trigeminal neuralgia. In particular, LI-SG has recently been shown to exert modulatory effects on the autonomic nervous system and may be used in the management of persistent arrhythmias (Hanna et al. 2021). However, the potential efficacy of LI-SG on sleep quality in patients undergoing modified radical mastectomy of breast cancer has not been explored.

This prospective, randomized, controlled study investigates the effects of LI-SG on postoperative sleep quality and rehabilitation in breast cancer patients undergoing general anesthesia.

Materials and Methods

Patients and study design

The study followed the ethical standards stated in the Declaration of Helsinki and Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials reporting guidelines. It was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Baoding First Central Hospital (Approval No: 2024–001). The study was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Primary Registry of International Clinical Trial Registry Platform, World Health Organization) (registration number: ChiCTR2500099918). All participants provided written informed consent before enrolment.

From June 2024 to December 2024, we recruited patients scheduled for modified radical mastectomy with unilateral breast cancer. The eligibility criteria were as follows: (1) age between 18 and 65 years; (2) modified radical mastectomy with unilateral breast cancer; (3) patients undergoing general anesthesia; (4) body mass index between 18 and 30 kg/m2; and (5) American Society of Anesthesiologists grade physical status I–III. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) history of abuse or long-term use of sedative and analgesic drugs; (2) cognitive dysfunction or mental disorders that prevent participation; (3) preoperative Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) score ≥ 7 points; (4) patients were unable to complete the entire intervention process or follow-up; (5) infection, damage, or hematoma of the skin at the irradiation site; (6) abnormal adrenal cortical function; (7) photoallergy; and (8) external uncontrollable factors such as sample contamination.

Patients were randomly allocated into two groups using a random number table: the control group and the LI-SG group. The participants were masked for group allocation. The researcher who collected the experimental data and made the assessment were blinded to the allocation.

Sample Size

The sample size was calculated based on the study of Basford JR (Basford et al. 2003) et al. To detect a reduction in incidence from 58 to 30%, we set α = 0.05, β = 0.2 (power = 0.8) for a two-group design. Using SPSS 21.0 software, we calculated 53 patients per group. Assuming a dropout rate of 10%, the final calculated sample size was at least 55 patients per group.

Intervention

Following anesthetic induction, individuals in the LI-SG group received treatment involving ultrasound-assisted location of the stellate ganglion followed by linearly polarized light therapy as described by Lee et al.(Lee et al. 2006). Patients were positioned supine with their necks slightly rotated to the opposite side to fully extend the neck. Physical light therapy equipment (model HA-2200 LE1, Tokyo Iken Co., Ltd, Tokyo, Japan) was used to deliver light therapy to the stellate ganglion guided by ultrasound. Each irradiation cycle included 2 s of irradiation and a 4-s rest, lasting 40 min. A total energy of 110.4 J was delivered at an output power of 0.92 W, with the energy density 358 J/cm2.

Patients in the control group did not receive any intervention.

Anesthesia management

All patients were managed with the same anesthetic protocol and postoperative analgesia therapy. After the patients entered the operating room, intravenous access was established. Electrocardiogram, pulse oxygen saturation, invasive blood pressure, and bispectral index were monitored. After pre-oxygenation, anesthesia was induced with intravenous midazolam (0.05–0.075 mg/kg), sufentanil (0.4–0.6 mg/kg), etomidate (0.3 mg/kg), and cisatracurium (0.2 mg/kg). After a reinforced catheter was inserted, the mechanical ventilation (12–15 times/min and inspiration-exhalation ratio 1:2) was performed at a tidal volume 6–8 ml/kg. Propofol was administered intravenously at a rate of 4–6 mg/kg/h, and remifentanil at 0.1–0.3 µg/kg/min. Anesthesia was maintained with propofol (4–12 mg/kg/h) and remifentanil (0.05–0.2 µg/kg/min) to keep bispectral indexes between 40 and 60. Postoperatively, patients were transferred to the post-anesthesia care unit. We performed patient-controlled intravenous analgesia with sufentanil (1.5 µg/kg) and ramosetron (6 mg) in 100 ml saline at a background rate of 2 mL/h and a bolus dose of 0.5 mL, with a lockout interval of 15 min.

Outcome evaluation

The primary outcome of the study was the subjective sleep quality, which was assessed using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI). This questionnaire is a validated and user-friendly tool for evaluating the sleep quality of patients (Gao et al. 2022). It consists of 18 scoring items in seven domains, including sleep latency, sleep duration, habitual sleep efficiency, subjective sleep quality, use of sleeping medication, sleep disturbances, and daytime dysfunction. The total score ranges from 0 to 21, with higher scores indicating more severe sleep disorders. In this study, the PSQI was administered on one day prior to the surgery (D0), on the second postoperative day (D2), and on the 5th postoperative day (D5). The patients with a score above 7 were deemed to have PSD (Yan et al. 2023). The calculation formula for the incidence of PSD is the number of patients with PSD divided by the total number of patients.

The secondary outcomes were objective sleep measures including sleep efficiency (SE), total sleep time (TST), and the number of awakenings (NA) were recorded at D0, D2, and D5 using actigraphy (wGT3X-BT, ActiGraph, Pensacola, FL, USA). As a wearable device, actigraphy provides an objective, low-burden, and ecologically valid method for monitoring real-world sleep and circadian patterns. It thereby contributes valuable data to epidemiological and clinical research on sleep and sleep disorders (Smith et al. 2018). The other secondary outcomes were visual analog scale (VAS) score at D2 and total number of patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) compression in 48 h. Serum levels of melatonin (ab283258, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) (ab285312, Abcam, Cambridge, UK), and interleukin-6 (IL-6) (ab178013, Abcam, Cambridge, UK) were measured by ELISA in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions at D0, D2 and D5. Postoperative adverse events, including transient upper limb numbness, transient hoarseness, skin rash, arrhythmia, nausea and vomiting, and delirium were recorded. Furthermore, whether the patients were satisfied with sleep study experience were also recorded. The outcome was defined by the following: “How satisfied were you with the experience of getting a sleep study?” Responses were measured using a 7-point Likert scale ranging from “very dissatisfied” to “very satisfied.”

Statistical analysis

The primary outcome was analyzed using the intention-to-treat analysis principle. The full analysis data set was defined as patients who completed intervention. Missing primary data was replaced by Linear Interpolation. Continuous variables with normality were expressed as mean and standard deviation. The independent samples t-test was used for a between-group comparison. The median and inter quartile range for data that did not follow the normal distribution were compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical data was described statistically using frequency (percentage). The chi-square test or Fisher exact test was used for a between-group comparison. Differences were considered statistically significant at 2-sided P < 0.05.

Results

A total of 153 individuals were initially assessed for eligibility and provided informed consent. Of these, 8 individuals declined to participate, 6 individuals were excluded because of PSQI score ≥ 7 and 7 individuals were excluded because of long-term use of sedative drugs. A total of 132 participants were divided into the control group (n = 66) and the LI-SG group (n = 66). During the intervention period, 7 participants were unable to receive the intervention due to postponement or cancellation of their surgeries. During the follow-up period, 8 participants were lost to follow-up for various reasons. During the analysis phase, blood samples from 3 patients were found to be contaminated and could not be analyzed. A total of 114 participants were included in the final analysis: 56 participants in the control group, 58 participants in the LI-SG group. (Fig. 1).

Comparison of sleep quality between the two groups

There was no statistical difference in preoperative PSQI score between the two groups (Fig. 2A). The PSQI scores in the LI-SG group and the control group were 7.17 ± 0.43 vs 10.44 ± 1.12 on the second postoperative day (P < 0.001) and 6.21 ± 0.30 vs 9.13 ± 0.82 on 5th postoperative day (P < 0.001). There was no occurrence of PSD at the day before the operation in both groups. PSD occurred in 25 (44.64%) of 56 patients in the control group, compared with 10 (17.24%) of 58 patients in the LI-SG group on the second postoperative day (P < 0.001). The condition was observed in 20 (35.71%) of 56 patients in the control group and 9 (15.51%) of 58 patients in the LI-SG group, on the 5th postoperative day (P < 0.001).

There was no statistical difference in preoperative TST between the two groups. Compared with the control group, the TST of the LI-SG group was significantly increased on the second postoperative day (479.11 ± 45.26 vs 392.85 ± 31.44, P < 0.001) and on the 5th postoperative day (522.44 ± 53.19 vs 411.66 ± 35.16, P < 0.001). On the second postoperative day, the SE was 84.13 ± 7.13 in the LI-SG group and 76.38 ± 7.39 in the control group (P < 0.001); on the 5th postoperative day, it was 87.43 ± 7.89 and 77.12 ± 7.32, respectively (P < 0.001). (Fig. 2C).

Compared with the control group, the NA of the LI-SG group was significantly decreased on the second postoperative day (10.61 ± 2.83 vs 16.16 ± 3.34, P < 0.001) and on the 5th postoperative day (8.84 ± 1.72 vs 13.49 ± 2.21, P < 0.001). Comparison of pain level between the two groups

Postoperative pain was measured on the second postoperative day using VAS scores. The VAS scores in the LI-SG group and the control group were 2.51 ± 0.47 vs 3.51 ± 0.77 on the second postoperative day (P < 0.001). Furthermore, total number of PCA compression were 5.62 ± 1.53 vs 15.39 ± 2.11 in the LI-SG group and the control group, respectively (P < 0.001) (Table 2).

Compared with the control group, the melatonin of the LI-SG group was significantly increased on the second and 5th postoperative day (all P < 0.001). Discussion

The main findings of the current study were as follows. First, LI-SG enhanced postoperative sleep quality, decreased the incidence of PSD and alleviated postoperative pain in patients following unilateral radical mastectomy for breast cancer. Second, LI-SG increased the level of melatonin and decreased the level of TNF-α and IL-6. Third, LI-SG decreased the incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting, and increased patient satisfaction. Together these results indicated that LI-SG yielded prophylactic effects on PSD and improved postoperative sleep quality in patients following unilateral radical mastectomy for breast cancer. The mechanism may be related to the enhancement of hormone and reduction of inflammatory factors. Interestingly, we found that it could simultaneously reduce the occurrence of adverse reactions and improve patients' satisfaction.

Sleep dysfunction is a prevalent central nervous system complication in patients following general anesthesia. It is characterized by prolonged sleep onset latency, early morning awakenings, difficulty in returning to sleep after waking, post-sleep fatigue, and disrupted sleep–wake cycles (Brown et al. 2010; Song and Zhu 2022). PSD can result in various physiological complications and significantly impede the postoperative recovery of patients. Breast cancer patients are particularly prone to PSD, chronic pain, lymphedema, and depression, which impair quality of life and mental health. Approximately 70% of breast cancer patients report sleep disorders, such as sleep onset insomnia, impaired sleep maintenance, and early wake-up (Varallo et al. 2022). In our study, the highest incidence of PSD was observed on the second postoperative day (44.64%), which was lower than that reported in other studies. This may be related to the different evaluation criteria and time of PSD. Overall, the results of these studies indicate that targeted interventions aimed at enhancing postoperative sleep quality are important for breast cancer patients.

SGB has been performed for more than 70 years. Evidence has shown that SGB is an effective intervention that improves PSD in breast cancer (Jin et al. 2018). Recent studies have showed that a single preoperative invasive stellate ganglion injection can improve patients' sleep quality for two days or even longer after surgery, while reducing postoperative inflammation, increasing melatonin levels, and stabilizing perioperative hemodynamics (Yan et al. 2023; Luo et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2017). Linearly polarized light is generated through physical polarization technology to produce polarized spectral energy. It is characterized by low scattering and strong penetration capacity, and exhibits effects such as analgesia, detumescence, improvement of microcirculation, acceleration of tissue healing, relief of muscle spasms, and regulation of physiological functions (Liao et al. 2017).

Previous research has shown that compared with traditional SGB, LI-SG was associated with a reduced incidence of adverse events and higher patient satisfaction, suggesting its potential for wider clinical application (Liao et al. 2017, 2016). Furthermore, studies have indicated that LI-SG offers comparable efficacy to traditional stellate ganglion block in the treatment of sympathetic storm, while being associated with a superior safety profile (Takahashi et al. 2024). In our study, the 40-min irradiation protocol was designed based on the pharmacokinetic profile of the administered drugs. In our study, we used PSQI as a subjective measurement of sleep quality and actigraphy to record TST, SE, and number of wakings for objective sleep quality parameters. Our results showed that LI-SG significantly improved PSQI, the TST, SE, and reduced the number of wakings at D2 and D5. Our findings demonstrated that the therapeutic effects can persist for 2 to 5 days, thereby supporting our hypothesis, and indicated that LI-SG may be a substitute intervention for SGB in treating PSD in breast cancer patients.

The link between sleep and pain is well documented. Poor sleep not only affects general health but has a direct impact on inflammation, pain response, and experience. Improving sleep in people with musculoskeletal conditions and chronic pain has the potential to greatly improve the quality of life of patients (Haack et al. 2020; O'Gara et al. 2021). In our study, LI-SG not only improved sleep quality of breast cancer patients after surgery, but also significantly decreased VAS score and the number of PCA compression in the first 48 h after surgery, illustrating that LI-SG improves the postoperative sleep quality of breast cancer patients may be closely related to the relief of postoperative pain.

Melatonin, a hormone secreted by the pineal gland in the brain, plays a crucial role in sleep enhancement and immune regulation. Studies have demonstrated that surgical stimulation reduces melatonin secretion and accelerates its degradation, thereby contributing to postoperative sleep disturbances. Surgery also induces an elevation in various inflammatory factors, including IL-6 and TNF-α, which directly impact the central nervous system and disrupt sleep architecture (Watkins et al. 1995). Our study found that LI-SG effectively increased melatonin serum level and reduced IL-6 and TNF-α serum levels at postoperative time points, which parallelled the improvement of pain in patients following unilateral modified radical breast cancer surgery. These findings suggest a mechanism by which LI-SG may improve sleep quality by increasing melatonin and decreasing inflammation factors which is consistent with the research results of Yan and his team (Yan et al. 2023).

Nausea and vomiting are frequent occurrences after general anesthesia and can disrupt postoperative sleep. Breast surgery is associated with high incidence of postoperative nausea and vomiting, as a result of hormonal status and the large doses of opioid drugs administration in surgery (Wesmiller et al. 2023). In this study, the incidences of postoperative nausea and vomiting were 16.07% and 5.17%, respectively, and we observed a significant reduction in the incidences of nausea and vomiting among patients in the LI-SG groups, which may be potentially attributable to the analgesic effects of these treatments. Due to the improvement of sleep quality and the reduction of postoperative complications, the satisfaction of LI-SG group patients has significantly increased compared to the control group patients.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the intervention was administered only once preoperatively and not continued afterward in our study. Additional postoperative sessions could potentially improve its efficacy. For future work, we will investigate the effects of a multi-session with an extended duration and frequency protocol, including postoperative treatments, on the PSD. Additionally, the impact of different intervention time points of LI-SG may be worth exploring. Second, environmental factors including lighting, noise, and the activities of medical staff and other patients can influence postoperative sleep quality. Despite efforts to mitigate the impact of nocturnal disturbances, it is challenging to entirely eliminate factors that affect sleep. Third, patients with pre-existing sleep disorders were excluded from this study; therefore, further research is warranted to evaluate the effectiveness of LI-SG in this specific population. The applicability of LI-SG to patients of different age groups, different conditions and different surgical methods should also need to be further explored in future studies.

In conclusion, the present study demonstrated that LI-SG significantly enhances postoperative sleep quality and analgesia in patients undergoing breast cancer surgery, with a higher patient satisfaction and a reduced incidence of adverse events, rendering it a promising candidate for clinical implementation and broader applications.

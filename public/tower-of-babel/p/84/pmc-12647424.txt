Asian Subgroup Analysis of Patients in the Phase 2 DeLLphi-301 Study of Tarlatamab for Previously Treated Small Cell Lung Cancer
Authors: Myung-Ju Ahn, Byoung Chul Cho, Kadoaki Ohashi, Hiroki Izumi, Jong-Seok Lee, Ji-Youn Han, Chi-Lu Chiang, Shuang Huang, Ali Hamidi, Sujoy Mukherjee, Krista Lin Xu, Hiraoki Akamatsu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionTarlatamab is a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) immunotherapy that binds delta-like ligand 3 on the surface of small cell lung cancer (SCLC) cells and CD3 on T cells, facilitating T cell-mediated cancer cell lysis. In the primary analysis of the phase 2 DeLLphi-301 study (NCT05060016), tarlatamab showed a favourable benefit-to-risk profile with durable objective responses and promising survival outcomes in patients with previously treated SCLC. Here, phase 2 data for the Asia region subgroup are presented.MethodsPatients with previously treated, advanced SCLC received 10 mg tarlatamab every 2 weeks. The primary endpoint was objective response rate (ORR) by blinded independent central review (RECIST version 1.1). Key secondary endpoints included duration of response (DOR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS) and safety. Introduction

Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) accounts for approximately 11% of all 2020 incident lung cancers globally and in the Eastern Asia region [1]. As in other populations, treatment outcomes for patients from Asia with SCLC are poor [2–7].

A promising target for SCLC is delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3), which is a Notch inhibitory ligand [8]. DLL3 is abnormally expressed in 85–96% of patients with SCLC, with little to no expression in normal cells [8–11]. Tarlatamab is a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) that directs the patient’s T cells to DLL3-expressing SCLC cells without relying on major histocompatibility complex class I, leading to T cell-mediated lysis of cancer cells [10].

In the phase 2 DeLLphi-301 trial, tarlatamab showed antitumour activity with durable responses and promising survival outcomes in patients with SCLC in whom SCLC had progressed following ≥ 2 lines of therapy [9, 12]. A total of 134 patients were enrolled to receive tarlatamab 10 mg, the eventual phase 3 dose, of whom 33% had received ≥ 3 prior lines of therapy [9, 12]. In an extended follow-up, tarlatamab 10 mg every 2 weeks led to an objective response rate (ORR) of 40% (95% confidence interval [CI], 30.3–50.3) [12]. Among patients who experienced objective response, the median duration of response (DOR) was 9.7 months (95% CI 6.9 to not estimable [NE]). Median progression-free survival (PFS) was 4.3 months (95% CI 2.9–5.6) and median overall survival (OS) was 15.2 months (95% CI 10.8 to NE) [12]. The safety profile was acceptable. Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) was the most common treatment-related adverse event (TRAE), reported in 53% of treated patients; however, these events were primarily grade 1 or 2, with grade 3 CRS occurring in only 0.8% of patients (n = 1/133). Treatment-related discontinuation of tarlatamab was low (4%) [9, 12]. The DeLLphi-301 trial included a clinically relevant subgroup of patients from Asia.

Recently, the phase 3, global DeLLphi-304 randomised-controlled trial found that tarlatamab had superior median OS compared to chemotherapy in patients with SCLC that had progressed during or after initial platinum-based chemotherapy. Median OS was 13.6 months (11.1 to NE) with tarlatamab and 8.3 months (95% CI 7.0–10.2) with chemotherapy (stratified hazard ratio for death, 0.60; 95% CI 0.47–0.77; P < 0.001). Tarlatamab demonstrated a more favourable safety and tolerability profile than chemotherapy.

Detailed results on the Asian subgroup of patients in DeLLphi-301 receiving tarlatamab in the third-line or greater setting have not been published previously. Therefore, we describe here a post hoc subgroup analysis of patients from Asia in the DeLLphi-301 study.

Methods

DeLLphi-301 (ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT05060016) was a phase 2, open-label, international trial designed to evaluate the antitumour activity and safety of tarlatamab in patients with advanced SCLC previously treated with ≥ 2 lines of therapy. The methods for the trial have been described in detail elsewhere [9]. The sponsor designed the trial to ensure diversity in sex/gender, race/ethnicity and age, in accordance with industry standards (https://www.amgen.com/science/clinical-trials/clinical-trial-diversity). Exclusion criteria related to contraceptives applied to both men and women. Diversity and representativeness of the global trial population were analysed in a prior publication [9].

There were three parts: randomised dose comparison of 10 mg and 100 mg tarlatamab (part 1), dose expansion after 10 mg was selected as the target dose (part 2), and a substudy exploring a reduced inpatient monitoring period from 48 to 24 h (part 3). In the present analysis, only patients enrolled at sites in Asia who received tarlatamab 10 mg were included.

Tarlatamab was administered via infusion over 60 min. Patients received 1 mg on cycle 1 day 1 and 10 mg on days 8 and 15. Dosing at 10 mg continued every 2 weeks thereafter in 28-day cycles until disease progression. All patients received dexamethasone prior to the first two tarlatamab doses (days 1 and 8 of cycle 1) as prophylaxis for CRS.

DeLLphi-301 was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki of 1964 and its later amendments. The protocol and amendments were approved by the institutional review board or ethics committee at each participating site (see supplementary appendix for details) and by the regulatory authorities in the participating countries. All patients provided written informed consent. A data review team external to the trial team provided oversight of safety throughout the trial.

Adult patients with histologically or cytologically confirmed SCLC who had relapsed after or were refractory to one platinum-based treatment regimen and at least one other line of therapy were eligible for enrolment into DeLLphi-301. At enrolment, eligible patients had measurable lesions as defined by Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) grade of 0 or 1. Patients with asymptomatic, treated or stable brain metastases were eligible. DLL3 expression was assessed but was not a criterion for enrolment.

The primary endpoint in DeLLphi-301 was ORR by blinded independent central review and according to RECIST version 1.1. Secondary endpoints included DOR, disease control rate (DCR), PFS, OS and adverse events (AEs). AEs were graded according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) except for CRS and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS). CRS and ICANS were graded according to the American Society for Transplantation and Cellular Therapy (ASTCT) consensus grading system [13].

DLL3 expression (> 0%) was assessed in formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue specimens by Roche Tissue Diagnostics using the SP347 antibody.

Efficacy analyses were conducted in the intention-to-treat population enrolled in parts 1 and 2. Safety analyses were conducted in all patients enrolled in parts 1, 2 and 3 who had received at least one dose of tarlatamab. CIs for percentages were assessed using the Clopper–Pearson method, and the Kaplan–Meier method was used for time-to-event analyses. There was no adjustment for multiplicity. The data cut-off date was 12 January 2024 for all analyses except OS (16 May 2024).

Results

Patient Disposition and Characteristics

Of the 134 patients enrolled to receive tarlatamab 10 mg across all study parts (1, 2 and 3), 43 (32%) were enrolled at sites in Asia (41 in parts 1 and 2; two in part 3). Of these 43 patients, 81% were male, 16% were never smokers, 86% had an ECOG PS grade of 1, all had metastatic disease and 15 (35%) had brain metastases (Table 1). Approximately two-thirds (65%) of patients had received two prior lines of therapy and 35% had received ≥ 3 prior lines, with 67% having received prior programmed death (ligand) 1 (PD-[L]1) therapy. Among the 19 patients with an objective response, the median time to response was 1.3 months (interquartile range [IQR], 1.2–1.4). The median DOR among these 19 patients was 7.2 months (95% CI 3.9 to NE). There were 11 (58%), seven (37%) and five (26%) patients with a DOR of ≥ 6, ≥ 9 and ≥ 12 months, respectively. In six (32%) patients, the response was ongoing and they were still on treatment for ≥ 1 year at data cut-off.

At the data cut-off date of 12 January 2024 and with a median follow-up time of 16.6 months (95% CI 11.2–19.2), median PFS was 5.4 months (95% CI 3.0–8.1) (Fig. 1B). The Kaplan–Meier estimate of PFS was 41.7% (95% CI 25.9–56.7) at 6 months, 27.5% (95% CI 14.3–42.5) at 9 months and 21.4% (95% CI 9.8–36.0) at 12 months. At the data cut-off date of 16 May 2024 and with a median follow-up time of 21.2 months (95% CI 20.2–22.1), median OS was 19.0 months (95% CI 11.4 to NE) (Fig. 1C). The Kaplan–Meier estimate of OS was 75.8% (95% CI 58.5–86.6) at 6 months, 67.4% (95% CI 49.6–80.0) at 12 months and 53.3% (95% CI 36.0–67.9) at 18 months. A total of 16 patients (39%) were alive at the last follow-up visit.

Safety

Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were reported in all patients, with 24 (56%) experiencing grade ≥ 3 TEAEs. A total of 11 (26%) patients experienced TEAEs leading to tarlatamab dose interruption/reduction, and two (5%) experienced TEAEs leading to tarlatamab discontinuation.

TRAEs occurred in 39 patients (91%), with 11 (26%) experiencing grade ≥ 3 TRAEs (Table 3). The most common TRAEs were CRS (49%), pyrexia (42%), decreased appetite (28%), dysgeusia (26%), nausea (14%) and increased alanine aminotransferase (ALT) (14%). These TRAEs mostly occurred in the first 3 months (Fig. 2A). A total of four (9%) patients experienced TRAEs leading to tarlatamab dose interruption/reduction; no patient experienced TRAEs leading to tarlatamab discontinuation. All CRS events (n = 21) were grade 1 or 2; the first such event occurred after one of the first two doses (cycle 1 days 1 and 8) (Fig. 2B), with one recurrence during cycle 2, and none led to tarlatamab discontinuation. The median time to onset for CRS events was 20.8 h (IQR, 11.5–31.1) following the most recent tarlatamab dose. Interventions beyond supportive care (e.g. acetaminophen, intravenous hydration) were seldom used; these included tocilizumab (n = 3, 7%), vasopressor (n = 1, 2%) and supplemental oxygen (n = 2, 5%). All cases of CRS resolved, with a median time to resolution of 5 days (95% CI 3–6). ICANS and associated neurological events occurred in five patients (11.6%). All ICANS TEAEs were grade 1 or 2 (Fig. 2B), and none led to tarlatamab discontinuation. The median time to onset for ICANS events was 5 days (IQR, 1–15).

No related interstitial lung disease TEAEs were seen in this Asian subgroup. One case of causally unrelated grade 1 radiation pneumonitis was recorded. This occurred in a patient with prior radiotherapy, 9 days after cycle 5 of tarlatamab. Neutropenia occurred in eight patients (18.6%); however, this event did not lead to tarlatamab discontinuation.

Discussion

In a subgroup analysis of 41 patients from Asia enrolled in the phase 2 DeLLphi-301 study, tarlatamab 10 mg showed promising efficacy with impressive survival outcomes. The ORR was 46.3% (95% CI 30.7%-62.6%), with a median DOR of 7.2 months (95% CI 3.9 to NE). Median PFS was 5.4 months (95% CI 3.0–8.1) and median OS was 19.0 months (95% CI 11.4 to NE), with an 18-month Kaplan–Meier estimate of 53%. Efficacy outcomes in the Asian subgroup were consistent with those in the overall population [9, 12].

Tarlatamab demonstrated long-term tolerability with no new safety concerns identified among patients from Asia. We note that CRS and ICANS events were graded according to the ASTCT consensus grading system [13], while all other AEs were graded by CTCAE. Grade 2 CRS events are defined by ASTCT criteria to include fever (≥ 38.0 °C) with hypotension not requiring vasopressors and/or hypoxia requiring the use of oxygen delivered by low-flow nasal canula (6 L/minute) or blow-by, and as such, could involve hospitalisation for medical intervention. In the present analysis, all CRS and ICANS events resolved, and none led to tarlatamab discontinuation.

The present analysis is the first known analysis of a novel, targeted therapeutic in an Asian population for previously treated extensive-stage SCLC. The efficacy results with tarlatamab among patients from Asia in DeLLphi-301 represent an improvement over those observed in prior studies of immunotherapy in third-line or greater SCLC [14, 15]. A pooled analysis of the KEYNOTE-028 phase 1b and KEYNOTE-158 phase 2 studies assessed pembrolizumab in a third-line-or-greater SCLC population [14]. The analysis included Asian sites. ORR in this analysis was 19.3% (95% CI 11.4–29.4). Median PFS and OS were 2.0 months (95% CI 1.9–3.4) and 7.7 months (95% CI 5.2–10.1), respectively [14]. At present, immunotherapies are not approved for previously treated disease across Asia [16–22]. Based on the positive results of DeLLphi-301, tarlatamab has been approved in the United States for previously treated extensive-stage SCLC and incorporated in the National Comprehensive Cancer Network and American Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines [23–25]. The Asian subgroup results of DeLLphi-301 are encouraging for a population that has poor treatment outcomes at present in real-world studies [26].

Limitations

We acknowledge that the conclusions of this post hoc analysis are limited by a small sample size. Also, we are unable to stratify efficacy or safety results by sex. The proportion of male and female patients in the Asian subgroup of DeLLphi-301 is consistent with published real-world SCLC population data [27–29]. Given the small sample size of female East Asian patients enrolled in this study, generalizability of the findings should be assessed in subsequent studies.

Conclusions

Tarlatamab demonstrated a favourable benefit-to-risk profile with durable responses and notable survival outcomes in patients from Asia with previously treated SCLC. The efficacy and safety of tarlatamab (10 mg once every 2 weeks) versus standard-of-care chemotherapy for patients with SCLC that had progressed following one platinum-based regimen are under investigation in the ongoing international phase 3 DeLLphi-304 (NCT05740566) study.

Ayurvedic management of uterine fibroids with a cervical Polyp - A case report
Authors: Vidyasagar Panchal, Malashree Panchal, Deepa, Veena
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractUterine fibroids are of major concern in women's life, which are the most common causes of conditions like Abnormal Uterine Bleeding (AUB), Dysmenorrhea that can negatively affect their life. The increased global incidence of Hysterectomies due to uterine fibroids and Polyps in society is of major concern. Even though polyps are generally not advised for hysterectomy, but when it is associated with unresponsive AUB/Malignant changes may require hysterectomy. Ayurveda can manage these clinical conditions in a very cost-effective way without any surgical intervention.In this report, we are presenting the case of a women in her mid-forties with complaints of abnormal uterine bleeding, pain in the abdomen, and back pain. USG scan had revealed two fibroids of size 1.6cm each in the fundus of the uterus, and a cervical polyp of 3∗2.4 cm and PID For which she had been advised to undergo Hysterectomy. Here an effort has been made to treat the condition with the available Ayurvedic management and to avoid surgery. The patient was treated with Ayurvedic medications, and Uttarabasti was carried out once every cycle. After 3 months of treatment, the patient was free of all the symptoms and the follow-up scan revealed the absence of all the above impressions. The patient has not reported any negative feedback regarding her condition or treatment methods. This article intends to bring confidence in young Ayurvedic practitioners, that Ayurveda can manage such solid benign uterine growths with non-surgical methods.Keywords: Uterine fibroids, Cervical polyps, Uttarabasti, Garbhashsyagata Granthi, Ayurveda, Abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB)

Introduction

Uterine fibroids are noncancerous growths in the uterus that commonly manifest during the childbearing age of women, raising a potential challenge in gynecological health. Malignancy is very uncommon in fibroid uterus, accounting for less than 0.1 % of all uterine fibroids. This condition is often noted in nulliparous women or in those having one child infertility [1]. The prevalence of this condition is at its peak in the age group of 35–45 years [2]. Despite their benign nature, uterine fibroids contribute significantly to morbidity among a considerable portion of the female population. The clinical impacts stem from their localized mass effect, leading to pressure on adjacent organs, excessive uterine bleeding, or complications related to pregnancy, such as infertility and recurrent pregnancy loss. Although non-cancerous, these growths emerge as a prominent factor necessitating hysterectomies. Remarkably, they account for approximately one-third of all hysterectomies performed, underscoring the substantial impact of uterine fibroids on the decision-making process for this surgical intervention (see Table No. 1, Table No. 2, Table No. 3, Table No. 4, Table No. 5, Table No. 6).

Cervical polyps are benign growths that can occur in the uterus. They typically range in size from 1 to 2 cm, though they rarely exceed 2 cm. Often, there are no noticeable symptoms, and these polyps are usually discovered accidently during speculum examinations or ultrasounds. However, some individuals may experience symptoms such as abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB), bleeding after sexual contact, or excessive vaginal discharge. In young women, multiple endometrial polyps can lead to infertility or miscarriages [23].

Pelvic Inflammatory disease (PID) is a spectrum of infections and inflammation of the upper genital tract organs typically involving the uterus (endometrium) and other surrounding structures. The incidence of PID varies from 1 to 2 % per year. Patients with acute PID present with wide range of symptoms such as abdominal and pelvic pain, AUB usually associated with endometritis. If not treated on time, complications such as pelvic peritonitis, septicemia, infertility are seen [24].

This case highlights how Ayurvedic interventions provided effective management, avoiding surgical procedures.

Patient information

A 45-year-old female patient reported to our OPD at Sanatana Ayurveda Speciality Clinic Humnabad, with the complaints of heavy menstrual flow associated with back pain, heaviness of body, tiredness, and body aches since last 15 days. On taking the detailed history, it was observed that the patient had history of irregular menstruation with heavy flow which lasts for more than 15 days in each menstrual cycle since last two years.

Obstetric history: She had two normal deliveries (FTND) in the past.

Clinical findings

On examination, the patient was a medium-built women of Kapha - Pittaja Prakruti, all vitals were stable, and her weight was 75 kgs.

Menstrual History: LMP- 01-07-2023.

Cycle regularity and length: Irregular, 15–20 days/30 days.

Menstrual bleeding: Heavy flow, (5–6 pads/day)

Per abdomen examination - was normal and no organomegaly was found. There was a mild tenderness in the infra-umbilical (Hypogastric) region.

Pelvicexamination –

Per speculum examination: Small mass seen at the cervical region, vagina healthy, cervix hypertrophied.

Bimanual examination

PV examination was not done on the first visit as the patient was having heavy uterine bleeding; but per vaginal examination was done after the treatment, which revealed the absence of uterine bleeding. Uterus antiverted, bulky, a small mass was felt at the cervix, all fornices free.

Treatment

Given the significant blood loss observed in the patient, the primary treatment goal was Rakta Stambhana (hemostasis). Pushyanuga Choorna, which is indicated for Pradara (excessive bleeding/leukorrhea) due to its Kashaya Rasa (astringent taste), was chosen as the base for the addition of various Bhasma and Rasa preparations. The combination of Pravala Pishti, Mukta Shukti, Johar Mohra Pishti were selected in the initial treatment protocol since they are Kashaya Rasatmaka, Sheeta veerya, Rakta Prasadana in nature. The Raktagata Ushna Guna and Ama Dosha Pachana was achieved with help of these drugs. Along with Vyadhi Pratyanika Chikitsa Bola Baddha Rasa and Chandrakala Rasa the efficacy of above discussed drugs has become multifold.

As the Vyadhi was found to be Santarpanajanya after careful analysis of history, the treatment was planned for fibroid as Apatarpana.

Shiva Gutika, was selected primarily for its Lekhana action. Contents such as, Shuddha Shilajitu, Trikatu, Katuki, Karkatashringi, Talisapatra etc are having Deepana, Paachana yogavahi, srotoshodhana and lekhana properties.

Varunadi Kashaya, being Kapha – Medohara, Ruksha, Ushna, Granthihara in nature was selected in the regimen.

Saptasara Kashaya, which is Ushna, Teekshana, Vata Kaphahara in nature, specially indicated in Yoni Doshas. From the previous experience of good results in cysts, growths in uterine cavity it was selected for the treatment.

Kanchanara Guggulu, Amadosha in Medo and Mamsa Dhatu causes morbid growths such as cyst, fibroids etc., Kanchanara Guggulu having Kapha Chedana and Bhedana properties proved beneficial in this case.

GARBHASHAYAGATA UTTARA BASTI (Administration of medicated enema into uterus):

Instruments used for Uttarabasti: Sterile swab and gauze pieces, Betadine solution, Sponge holding forceps, Artery forceps, IUI catheter attached with 2 cc syringe, Sims speculum, anterior vaginal wall retractor, autoclaved Kshara Taila and Kaaseesadi Taila.

Method of administration of Garbhashayagata Uttara Basti

Obtained the written and verbal consent of the patient. Before the administration of Uttara Basti, Sthanika Abhyanga (localized oil massage) with Murchita Tila Taila (Medicated sesame oil) and Sthanika Nadi Sweda (Localised steam fomentation) was done over the lower abdomen and pelvic area. The part was prepared for Uttara Basti administration by maintaining the antiseptic method. The patient was asked to be in a lithotomy position, Sims speculum was introduced to visualize the ecto cervix followed by the anterior vaginal wall was retracted with the help of a retractor, and then thereafter the medicated oils which are mentioned above were loaded into the 2 cc syringe and IUI catheter has been attached. Then 1ml of medicated oil is instilled into the uterus. After administrating medicated oil, the patient was asked to rest for some time in the legs elevated position, then Tadana and Mardana done over pelvic area.

Significance of Uttara Basti in Uterine fibroids: It plays a vital role in subsiding and eradicating Yoni-Garbhashayagata Vyadhi (uterine diseases) in female. Uttara Basti was administered with 1ml of Kshara Taila and Kaseesadi Taila on 7th day of menstrual cycle once in each month followed for three months. Ushna, Teekshna and Lekhana properties of Kshara Taila and Kaaseesadi Taila were helpful in resolving the uterine fibroids completely. Hence Uttarabasti proved beneficial with these two drugs acting locally.

Nasya with Doorva swarasa: Durva swarasa is well known for its sheeta guna and one of the best pitta shamaka dravya, when it is administered in the form of nasya acts on shrungataka marma which is said to be one of the sira marmas [25]. In the present case nasya karma was benificial along with other medications to check the AUB.

Nickel‐Catalyzed Enantioconvergent Arylation of Unactivated Cyclic Electrophiles: Asymmetric Synthesis of 3‐Substituted Pyrrolidines
Authors: Ting Hei Matthew Wong, Xiaoyu Tong, Gregory C Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe enantioconvergent substitution of unactivated alkyl electrophiles by carbon nucleophiles is a largely unsolved challenge in asymmetric catalysis, with virtually all of the progress to date focused on acyclic electrophiles. Expanding the scope of such transformations to cyclic electrophiles is of significant interest, given that cyclic scaffolds that bear stereocenters are common in bioactive molecules. One particularly attractive family of unactivated cyclic electrophiles is 3‐halopyrrolidines, since a chiral 3‐substituted pyrrolidine subunit is found in drugs such as TrikaftaTM, RinvoqTM, and PaxlovidTM (#4, #11, and #13, respectively, among small‐molecule drugs in retail sales in 2024). In this study, we establish that a chiral nickel catalyst can achieve enantioconvergent substitutions of racemic 3‐iodopyrrolidines by arylzinc reagents to generate the desired coupling products with good enantioselectivity under mild conditions. We apply this new catalytic asymmetric method to the streamlined synthesis of three bioactive pyrrolidines that had previously been synthesized as racemates.Keywords: asymmetric catalysis, C–C coupling, heterocycles, nickel, radicals

The development of metal‐catalyzed enantioconvergent substitutions of racemic electrophiles by organometallic nucleophiles has greatly expanded the usefulness of substitution reactions of alkyl electrophiles in organic synthesis, addressing shortcomings of traditional SN1 and SN2 pathways with regard to the scope of reaction partners and the control of stereochemistry [1]. To date, most progress has been achieved with activated electrophiles, wherein the carbon that bears the leaving group also bears an unsaturated substituent (e.g., alkene, carbonyl group, aromatic ring, or alkyne) [2, 3, 4] or a (stabilizing) heteroatom (left side of Figure 1A) [5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10].

In contrast, only limited advances have been realized in the case of metal‐catalyzed enantioconvergent couplings of organometallic nucleophiles with unactivated alkyl electrophiles, wherein the carbon that bears the leaving group only bears alkyl substituents (middle of Figure 1A) [11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16]. Indeed, to our knowledge, all previous examples have involved acyclic electrophiles (right side of Figure 1A) [11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17].

Cyclic, especially heterocyclic, subunits are a common motif in bioactive compounds [18, 19, 20, 21]. Pyrrolidines in particular are a privileged functional group, found in pharmaceuticals such as RinvoqTM (Figure 1B; 11th among small‐molecule drugs in retail sales in 2024 [22]) [23, 24, 25, 26]. Whereas a variety of approaches have been described for the catalytic asymmetric synthesis of 2‐substituted pyrrolidines [27, 28, 29, 30], there are fewer such methods for the synthesis of 3‐substituted pyrrolidines [31, 32, 33, 34]. We decided to pursue the possibility that these important target molecules might be accessible via metal‐catalyzed enantioconvergent substitution of N‐Boc‐3‐iodopyrrolidine (A), which is commercially available (Figure 1C). As noted above, there is no precedent for such an asymmetric coupling of an unactivated cyclic electrophile with an organometallic nucleophile; in addition, we are not aware of a prior report of the enantioselective substitution of a 3‐halopyrrolidine with any nucleophile [16]. This particular process could be especially challenging because the two substituents of the carbon undergoing substitution are similar, specifically, primary carbons (i.e., CH2R).

Herein, we report the achievement of our objective, specifically, a chiral nickel catalyst that accomplishes enantioconvergent substitution reactions of N‐Boc‐3‐iodopyrrolidine (A) by a variety of arylzinc reagents, providing straightforward access to enantioenriched 3‐arylpyrrolidines (Figure 1C) [35]. Furthermore, we apply this new method to substantially streamlined syntheses of bioactive compounds that had formerly been generated as racemates.

When we applied several previously reported conditions for enantioconvergent substitution reactions of unactivated acyclic electrophiles to heterocyclic electrophile A, we obtained disappointing results (poor ee and/or poor yield). Nevertheless, we determined that the enantioselective coupling of racemic pyrrolidine A with phenylzinc chloride can be achieved in fairly good ee and good yield by a chiral catalyst generated from NiBr2·glyme and commercially available bidentate ligand L1 (Table 1A, entry 1: 77% ee). We individually varied the Ar1 and Ar2 substituents of the ligand, and we established that each substituent can impact enantioselectivity (entries 2 and 3: 82% ee). Combining structural features of ligands L2 and L3 into a single ligand had the desired cumulative effect, with ligand L* providing the highest ee (entry 4: 87% ee).

With L* in hand, we further optimized the reaction conditions (e.g., solvent and temperature), which led to improved enantioselectivity and yield (Table 1B, entry 1: 90% ee and 86% yield). In the absence of NiBr2·glyme or of L*, essentially no coupling is observed (entries 2 and 3: <1% yield). When half of the standard catalyst loading or less nucleophile is used, there is essentially no change in ee and only a small change in yield (entries 4 and 5). The method is not highly air‐ or moisture‐sensitive (entries 6 and 7).

An array of nucleophiles and electrophiles can be coupled to generate 3‐arylpyrrolidines with good ee in the presence of this chiral nickel/L* catalyst (Figure 2). With respect to the nucleophile, the aromatic ring can bear substituents with varying steric demand (para: 2–5; meta: 6–9; ortho: 10 and 11) and diverse electronic properties (2–11). Nucleophiles that include extended (hetero)aromatic rings are also suitable coupling partners (12 and 13). Through studies of the impact of compounds added to a reaction mixture [38], we have determined that the coupling is compatible with an alkyl chloride, alkyl bromide, alkylboronate ester, aryl triflate, epoxide, olefin, secondary amine, and tertiary amine, whereas an aldehyde, alkyne, and pyridine inhibit the reaction (see the Supporting Information). On a gram scale, enantioconvergent arylation proceeds in similar ee and yield as for a reaction conducted on a 0.6‐mmol scale (1) [38].

With respect to the electrophile, nickel‐catalyzed asymmetric arylation can be applied to pyrrolidines that bear a variety of protecting groups (14–17). When a proline‐derived electrophile is employed, the catalyst, rather than the substrate, predominantly controls the stereochemistry of the 4 position: we observe high trans stereoselectivity in the matched coupling (18) and preferential formation of the cis isomer in the mismatched coupling (19). Under the conditions that we have optimized for the asymmetric arylation of 3‐iodopyrrolidines, we obtain promising ee and yield for the coupling of 3‐iodotetrahydrofuran (20), which suggests that enantioconvergent substitution reactions of other challenging families of unactivated cyclic electrophiles are viable [39].

In view of the privileged status of pyrrolidines in medicinal chemistry [23, 24, 25, 26], we applied our method for enantioconvergent arylation to the streamlined catalytic asymmetric synthesis of bioactive pyrrolidine derivatives (Figure 3). For each of the three examples, the previous synthesis required formation of the heterocycle from an acyclic precursor, was at least three steps longer, and afforded a racemic product [40, 41, 42, 43]. Thus, the enantioconvergent arylation of 3‐iodopyrrolidines is a powerful new tool for stereoselective synthesis, including drug discovery across a wide range of therapeutic areas.

With regard to the mechanism of this enantioconvergent substitution, we hypothesize that it may be proceeding via a radical‐chain pathway analogous to the one that we have previously outlined for two other nickel‐catalyzed couplings (Figure 4A) [44, 45]. Because both of these earlier detailed mechanistic studies have focused on reactions of activated electrophiles, we have directed our attention to the halogen‐abstraction step of the catalytic cycle, which involves an unactivated electrophile in the present case.

(B) Partial racemization of the recovered electrophile at partial conversion. (C) No evidence for kinetic resolution of the electrophile. (D) Inhibition by TEMPO and detection of a TEMPO–electrophile adduct.

We have determined that, when the arylation of an enantiopure electrophile is stopped at partial conversion, partial racemization of the remaining electrophile is observed, consistent with reversible cleavage by the nickel catalyst of the carbon–iodine bond of the electrophile (Figure 4B) [46]. Furthermore, when an enantioconvergent coupling of a racemic electrophile is stopped at partial conversion, the remaining electrophile is essentially racemic (<5% ee) (Figure 4C) [47]. Finally, when an arylation is attempted in the presence of 2,2,6,6‐tetramethylpiperidin‐1‐oxyl (TEMPO), coupling is inhibited, and a TEMPO adduct of the electrophile is detected (Figure 4D). Thus, the available data are consistent with the generation of a radical from the alkyl iodide under the standard reaction conditions.

In conclusion, although many bioactive molecules include stereocenters in cyclic structures, methods for asymmetric coupling reactions of alkyl electrophiles with organometallic nucleophiles have overwhelmingly focused on the control of acyclic stereocenters. This divergence is particularly acute in the case of unactivated alkyl electrophiles, which bear two sp3‐hybridized carbon substituents at the site of carbon‐carbon bond formation. In this study, we address this deficiency and establish that readily available racemic 3‐iodopyrrolidines undergo nickel‐catalyzed enantioconvergent substitution by an array of arylzinc nucleophiles under mild conditions. We have applied this method to the synthesis of three bioactive compounds that had previously been generated as racemates via substantially longer routes. Additional studies directed at demonstrating the utility of metal‐catalyzed substitution reactions of alkyl electrophiles are underway.

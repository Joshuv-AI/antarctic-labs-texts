Chloride-Selective Transmembrane Channel Formation by Meta-Positioned Halogen Bond Donors
Authors: Rashmi Sharma, Susmita Sarkar, Sandip Chattopadhayay, Kavan Nandaniya, Soumyajit Ghosh, Jagannath Mondal, Pinaki Talukdar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A major hurdle in current research on supramolecular anion channels is achieving the specificity and selectivity necessary for biological and pharmaceutical applications. To address this limitation, the present study reports a series of 3,5-dihalobenzamide derivatives designed to achieve selective anion transport via halogen-bonding interactions. Ion transport assays reveal that the diiodo derivative (3) exhibits superior activity compared with its dibromo (2) and dichloro (1) analogues. Fluorescence and electrophysiological experiments confirm that 3 functions as a selective chloride channel via an antiport mechanism. Furthermore, theoretical calculations confirm that intermolecular hydrogen bonding drives the self-assembly of 3 into supramolecular barrel-rosette structures that span the lipid bilayer. The study also highlights the pivotal role of halogen bonding in the recognition and transmembrane transport of the chloride ions.

Introduction

Chloride is an essential physiologically relevant anion that plays a vital role in maintaining the osmotic pressure and pH balance of bodily fluids. , The transmembrane movement of chloride ions is mediated by specialized ion channels, which are critical for preserving the resting membrane potential and enabling the generation of action potentials. − Proper functioning of chloride ion transporters is therefore fundamental to numerous physiological processes, and their dysfunction has been implicated in a variety of pathological conditions. A prominent example is cystic fibrosis, a hereditary disorder characterized by defective chloride ion transport across cellular membranes, resulting in the accumulation of thick, viscous mucus in multiple organs. − Other disorders, including metabolic alkalosis and renal tubular acidosis, are also associated with aberrant chloride ion concentrations, leading to adverse health outcomes. , Consequently, the regulation of chloride ion transport represents an attractive therapeutic target. − Although a wide range of synthetic anion receptors have been developed, only a limited number exhibit selectivity comparable to that of naturally occurring anion-binding proteins. − This challenge is further compounded by the inherent properties of anions, which, in contrast to cations, possess weaker coordination preferences and lower charge densities.

Furthermore, the wide diversity of anion geometries, pH-dependent protonation equilibria, and often high solvation enthalpies collectively hinder selective anion binding. Although hydrogen bonding (HB) − and nonclassical C–H···anion hydrogen bonding − remain the most commonly employed noncovalent interactions in anion receptor design, less conventional interactions have been increasingly explored in recent years to enhance anion recognition and selectivity. These include solvophobic effects, anion-π interactions, coordination bonding with Lewis-acidic metals and main-group elements, and halogen bonding (XB), all of which have emerged as promising strategies for anion sensing and transport. −

The distinctive chemical properties of halogens are widely exploited in commercially available compounds to enhance existing functionalities or impart new properties. − In addition to fundamental investigations into the nature and characteristics of halogen bonding, a major focus since the resurgence of interest in XB at the turn of the millennium has been its application as a robust and directional noncovalent interaction in supramolecular chemistry, − and more specifically in the development of synthetic ion transport systems. , Halogen-bonded synthetic channels are remained rare and as per our best knowledge only two halogen bonded channels are reported until date. Therefore, there is an urgent need to construct different supramolecular architecture to construct halogen-bonded channel system. Previously, Zeng and associates developed halogen bond-mediated artificial chloride channels in which the linearly arrayed acidic iodine atoms are responsible for facilitating chloride transport likely via a multi-ion jumping mode. We have also reported 5-iodoisophthalamide-based molecules in which the iodine atom promotes chloride transport through halogen bonding, while the amide N–H groups facilitate self-assembly and contribute to chloride recognition via HB. Depending on the organization of these small molecules within the lipid bilayer, the complex membrane environment can impose distinct geometries on the ion translocation pathway across the bilayer. Therefore, new and flexible tools are available in supramolecular chemistry, thanks to the shape and variety of interaction partners of halogen bonds, allowing for the construction of more efficient and selective ion transporters. In contemporary medicinal chemistry, there has been a notable surge in the utilization of halogens. − This is attributed to their elevated lipophilicity, which enhances cellular permeability. − In anticipation of enhancing the viability of synthetic ion transporters as a substitute for dysfunctional ones, we have designed compounds 1–4 by incorporating the dihalo motif as the internal core moiety.

Highly efficient and robust bilayer-based systems composed of small, self-assembling molecules have been developed to achieve prominent ion selectivity. Herein, we report the synthesis and characterization of 3,5-dihalobenzamide-containing compounds 1–4 (Figure ), which exhibit a strong propensity for self-assembly into supramolecular channel architectures. These assemblies selectively facilitate the transmembrane transport of chloride ions across lipid bilayers. The proposed assembly involves a tetrameric arrangement in which four molecules within a layer are oriented such that, of the two halogen substituents on each molecule, one remains available for ion binding to enable selective ion recognition, while the other is directed toward the carbonyl oxygen of a neighboring molecule. The stability of the bilayer assembly is further reinforced by intermolecular HB between amide groups, which links the two layers. Upon ion binding, the halogen substituent engages in halogen bonding with the anion, thereby promoting efficient ion translocation. To evaluate the influence of halogen substitution on the ion transport efficiency, the internal core was systematically modified by incorporating iodine, bromine, and chlorine. Furthermore, to assess the role of stacking interactions in the formation and stability of the self-assembled channels, compound 4 was synthesized by replacing the cyclohexylmethyl group with a benzyl group while retaining iodine as the halogen substituent.

Results and Discussion

3,5-Dichlorobenzoic acid and 3,5-dibromobenzoic acid were independently coupled with cyclohexylmethylamine using EDC·HCl as the coupling agent to afford compounds 1 and 2 in yields of 88% and 67%, respectively. , Compounds 3 and 4 were synthesized via a different route. Initially, 3,5-aminobenzoic acid (5) was subjected to a diazotization reaction to generate 3,5-diiodobenzoic acid (6c), which was subsequently coupled with cyclohexylmethylamine to yield compound 3 in 83% yield. In contrast, for the synthesis of compound 4, 6c was first converted to the corresponding acid chloride by treatment with SOCl2 in the presence of a catalytic amount of DMF. The resulting acid chloride was directly coupled with benzylamine in THF using DIPEA as a base, affording compound 4 in 64% yield. All compounds were characterized by 1H NMR, 13C NMR, and HRMS (see the Supporting Information).

The MeOH–CHCl3 solvent system was used to obtain brownish, fine-needle crystals of 1, 2, and 3, suitable for diffraction. The rod-shaped single crystals were analyzed by SC-XRD, which revealed the monoclinic space group. The molecular configuration within the crystals of compound 3 exhibits a top-and-bottom orientation where the molecules are positioned in opposite directions (Figure A). The diiodo-substituted benzene ring of one molecule is positioned just above the cyclohexyl group of the next molecule. This arrangement creates an ABAB pattern in the assembly. The iodine groups in alternate layers are aligned in parallel. The intermolecular CO···HN hydrogen bonding between the carbonyl groups situated on adjacent molecules (top and bottom ones) and the amide −NH groups has been observed (Figure A). Moreover, the electrostatic CO···I interaction between the carbonyl groups and the C–I has also been observed (Figure B). The manifestation of the ordered structure is observed through the uninterrupted propagation of said arrangements along the a-axis. A similar structural orientation was observed for the dibromo-substituted compound 2. However, for compound 1, we noticed that the dichloro-substituted benzene ring of one molecule is exactly positioned just above the dichloro-substituted benzene ring of the next molecule, and the assembly was stabilized by the formation of the intermolecular H-bond between the CO···HN hydrogen bonding interaction. Interestingly, no CO···Cl interaction was found between the carbonyl groups and the C–Cl. The presence of a CO···X interaction (X = Br or I) in compounds 2 and 3 suggests that these halogen atoms can facilitate anion translocation in the self-aggregated channel structure through halogen bonding interaction. Moreover, to understand the effect of the assembly on variation with the cyclohexylmethyl group bearing the benzyl group, compound 4 was also crystallized. Interestingly, compound 4 exhibited the same structural orientation as compound 3, indicating that the change in the stacking unit did not alter the solid-state structural arrangement. Although the structural study did not reveal an adequate channel cavity for ion transport, it was further investigated using theoretical simulations. The aggregation behavior of compounds 1–3 was investigated using field-emission scanning electron microscopy (Figure S6). The observation of well-defined elongated rod- and needle-shaped morphologies are significant as it confirms the pronounced self-assembly propensity of compounds 1–3.

Side (A) and top (B) view of solid-state self-assembly of channel-forming compound 3.

The ion transport activities of compounds 1–4 were evaluated using an 8-hydroxypyrene-1,3,6-trisulfonic acid trisodium salt (HPTS)-based fluorometric assay across large unilamellar vesicles composed of egg yolk phosphatidylcholine (EYPC–LUVs⊃HPTS). Changes in dye fluorescence intensity at λ em = 510 nm (λ ex = 450 nm) were monitored to follow the dissipation of the transmembrane pH gradient upon addition of compounds 1–4 (see the Supporting Information). − According to the comparison of activities of compounds 1–3, compound 3 functions as the most effective ion transporter, with the activity sequence being 1 < 2 < 3 (Figure A). The strength of a halogen bond follows the same sequence as the positive potential on halogens, which decreases in the following order: I > Br > Cl. Moreover, replacing the cyclohexylmethyl group in compound 3 with a benzyl group (yielding compound 4) lowered the ion transport activity. Notably, installing the benzyl group in place of the cyclohexylmethyl group further decreases activity to a level even lower than that of compound 2. The lower activity for compound 4 was probably due to the slightly lower logP value of compound 4 (log P = 4.63) in comparison with compound 3 (5.02). Alongside it is noteworthy that the planar orientation of the benzyl ring in compound 4 helps in the stronger association of the two monomers (CO···H–N = 1.965 Å and distance between the carbon center of aromatic ring to carbon center of benzyl group = 3.909 Å) compared to the nonplanar cyclohexylmethyl containing compound 3 (CO···H–N = 1.987 Å and distance between the carbon center of aromatic ring and the carbon center of benzyl group = 4.453 Å). Therefore, under the membrane environment, compound 4 is expected to show strong π–π stacking interactions, leading to a more rigid structure, and can generate potentially nonproductive aggregation. In contrast, in compound 3, the nonaromatic and conformationally flexible cyclohexylmethyl group promotes van der Waals interactions that favor more dynamic and adaptable packing under the membrane environment. Thereby, compound 3 is expected to facilitate the formation of transient yet cooperative assemblies better suited for channel-like transport.

Further, the dose–response study was conducted with compounds 1–4 across EYPC–LUVs⊃HPTS. The supramolecular tetrameric assembly, composed of monomers of 3, is supported by the determined Hill coefficient (n) value as the active rosette structure for the supramolecular nanochannel assembly. The evaluated Hill coefficient (n) and half-maximal effective concentration (EC 50) values for 1 [n = 2.58, EC 50 = 23.22 ± 1.24 μM (34.33 mol % with respect to lipid)] (Figure S8), 2 [n = 4.46, EC 50 = 0.98 ± 0.01 μM (1.44 mol % with respect to lipid)] (Figure S9), 3 [n = 3.86, EC 50 = 0.66 ± 0.01 μM (0.97 mol % with respect to lipid)] (Figure S10), and 4 [n = 2.37, EC 50 = 3.38 ± 0.18 μM (4.99 mol % with respect to lipid)] (Figure S11). These data also corroborate that compound 3 exhibits efficient transport activity among its congeners. It is noteworthy that even though compound 3 has lesser transport activity than that of our earlier reported channel, it has almost 5.4 times higher transport activity than that of the halogen-bonded channel reported by Zeng and co-workers.

The notable ion transport activity exhibited by compound 3 prompted further investigation into its ion selectivity. To evaluate potential cation selectivity, EYPC–LUVs⊃HPTS were prepared with intravesicular NaCl and exposed to iso-osmolar extravesicular MCl salts (M+ = Li+, Na+, K+, Rb+, and Cs+) along with NaOH base pulse. No significant variation in transport activity was observed upon changing the external cations, indicating that alkali metal cations do not participate in the transport process (Figure S13B). In contrast, anion selectivity was examined by maintaining intravesicular NaCl while exposing the vesicles to extravesicular buffer solutions containing sodium salts of different anions (Cl–, Br–, I–, F–, and NO3 –) in the presence of NaOH base pulse. In this case, pronounced changes in fluorescence intensity were observed, revealing a clear anion selectivity sequence of Cl– ≫ NO3 – > F– > Br– > I– (Figure S13A). The chloride ion selectivity was further scrutinized through anion gradient assay by maintaining intravesicular NaCl and extravesicular NaX (F–, Cl–, Br–, I–, OAc–, NO3 –, SCN–, and ClO4 –) salts. In this case, pronounced increment in HPTS fluorescence intensity were observed, revealing compound 3 has preferential selectivity toward the Cl– ion compared to other tested anions (Figure B).

Proton transport activity of compound 3 was assessed across EYPC–LUVs⊃HPTS (Figure S15), using FCCP and our previously reported cation–anion cotransporter SCS–01–13667 as controls. Vesicles containing 100 mM potassium gluconate were subjected to a TBAOH base pulse. FCCP shows an increment in the HPTS fluorescence activity, consistent with its protonophoric activity. However, insignificant HPTS fluorescence intensity was observed in the presence of SCS–01–136. Likewise, an insignificant increment in the presence of compound 3 (0.66 μM) indicates that our designed compound does not have the propensity of transporting the H+ or OH– ions. The chloride transport activity of compound 3 was then investigated using EYPC–LUVs loaded with the chloride-sensitive fluorescent dye lucigenin. A Cl–/NO3 – exchange assay was established by preparing vesicles with an internal NaNO3 buffer and suspending them in iso-osmolar NaNO3 buffer with an external NaCl pulse. , Upon addition of 3, a concentration-dependent quenching of lucigenin fluorescence was observed, indicating Cl– influx into the vesicles (Figure A). The derived EC 50 value was 3.05 ± 0.32 μM (4.5 mol % with respect to the lipid). To probe cation dependence, the external cation (M+ = Li+, Na+, K+, Rb+, and Cs+) was varied while keeping Cl– as the anion. No significant change in the transport rate was observed (Figure B), confirming that the transport process is cation-independent. The transport mechanism was elucidated using valinomycin, an electrogenic K+ ionophore. In a symport (M+/Cl– cotransport) mechanism, the transporter alone maintains electroneutrality, so adding valinomycin should not accelerate transport. In contrast, for an antiport (Cl–/anion exchange) mechanism, valinomycin can dissipate the developing membrane potential, thereby enhancing the rate of Cl– influx. When 3 was added after valinomycin in the presence of an external KCl pulse, a significantly faster Cl– influx was observed compared to 3 alone (Figure C). This synergistic effect confirms that compound 3 operates via a Cl–/anion antiport mechanism.

The membrane integrity of EYPC–LUVs in the presence of compound 3 was evaluated using an ANTS–DPX leakage assay. In this assay, the fluorescent dye ANTS (λ em = 520 nm with λ ex = 353 nm) is coencapsulated with the quencher DPX, resulting in quenched fluorescence due to their close proximity. Disruption of the vesicle membrane, whether through pore formation or lysis, would permit the diffusion of ANTS and DPX into the bulk solution, leading to a measurable increase in ANTS fluorescence. , Notably, only negligible changes in fluorescence were observed upon the addition of compound 3 over a wide concentration range, indicating that it does not compromise vesicle integrity or induce significant transmembrane pore formation (Figure D). To further probe the nature of the ion transport pathway, a cholesterol-dependence assay was performed to distinguish between carrier- and channel-mediated mechanisms. Incorporation of cholesterol into lipid bilayers reduces membrane fluidity and typically suppresses carrier-mediated ion transport, with only a few reported exceptions. In contrast, the efficiency of supramolecular channel-mediated transport is generally insensitive to changes in membrane fluidity. In the present case, cholesterol did not significantly affect the ion transport activity of compound 3 (Figure S21). Together, these observations provide evidence that compound 3 mediates ion transport via a membrane-spanning supramolecular channel rather than via a carrier-based mechanism.

To verify the formation of a functional ion channel by transporter 3, the electrical conductance of a planar lipid bilayer membrane composed of 1,2-diphytanoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine (DPhPC) was investigated. , The lipid bilayer was formed over a small orifice separating the cis and trans compartments of the measurement chamber, both of which contained an unbuffered 1.0 M KCl solution. Upon the addition of compound 3 at a concentration of 5 μM to the cis compartment, followed by the application of either positive or negative transmembrane potentials, well-defined and periodic channel opening and closing events were clearly observed (Figure A,B). These discrete current change provide direct evidence for the formation of functional ion channels across the black lipid membrane. Analysis of the recorded single-channel events enabled determination of a single-channel conductance value of 17.58 ± 0.8 pS. Using Hille’s equation, the effective channel diameter was estimated to be 3.68 ± 0.12 Å. Furthermore, the current–voltage (I–V) relationship obtained under symmetric conditions, with unbuffered 1.0 M KCl solutions present in both compartments, displayed a linear profile (Figure C). This linearity indicates that the ion channel formed by compound 3 exhibits ohmic behavior, consistent with the nondipolar nature of the channel interior. In contrast, ion selectivity was examined under asymmetric electrolyte conditions by using dissimilar KCl concentrations in the two chambers: 2.0 M KCl in the trans compartment and 1.0 M KCl in the cis compartment. Under these conditions, an average reversal potential of −14.8 mV was obtained (Figure D). Application of the Goldman–Hodgkin–Katz equation yielded a permeability ratio of PCl–/K+ = 4.07, indicating a clear preference for anion transport. Collectively, these results further substantiate that transporter 3 self-assembles into an anion-selective ion channel within the lipid bilayer.

The molecular electrostatic surface potential of compounds 1–4 was mapped to understand the σ-hole formation over the halide atoms (Figure S24). Compounds 1–4 were geometry optimized using the Gaussian 09 program package with the B3LYP functional and 6–311++g­(d,p) basis set (for compounds 1 and 2) or def2SVP basis set (for compounds 3 and 4). A significant increment in the positive potentials over the halides was noticed in the following sequence: 1 < 2 < 3 ≈ 4, consistent with the observed sequence of transport activity of compounds 1–3 (strong π–π stacking mediated nonproductive aggregation is responsible for lower activity of 4). These findings validate the role of σ-hole interactions in facilitating transport activity. To study the anion transport mechanism through the synthetic channel structure and to estimate the stability of the supramolecular channel in the membrane environment, we performed an Atomistic Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulation. A 24-monomer channel assembly was constructed using six layers composed of four molecules of compound 3. To form the assembly, the four layers were arranged sequentially, with the second, third, and fourth layers rotated by 90°, 180°, and 270°, respectively (Figure ). The assembled channel was energy-minimized using Maestro, and the resulting structure was embedded into a lipid bilayer using the CHARMM-GUI membrane builder protocol (details in Supporting Information). ,

The channel was positioned at the center of a DPhPC bilayer (144 lipids, equally distributed between leaflets) and solvated with 1 M KCl aqueous solution on both sides of the membrane. Following restrained equilibration, production MD simulations were carried out to evaluate ion transport behavior and channel stability (Table S5). To quantify the energetic cost associated with chloride ion translocation through the membrane-spanning channel, umbrella sampling simulations were conducted (Figure A). The resulting potential of mean force profile reveals a pronounced free-energy barrier of approximately 9 kcal·mol–1 at the channel midpoint. This barrier symmetrically decreases toward both channel openings, reflecting enhanced solvation and entropic freedom near the aqueous interfaces (Figure S25). The symmetry of the free-energy profile indicates equivalent energetic requirements for ion transport in either direction, while subtle curvatures reflect the influence of the internal channel architecture. To rationalize the origin of the central energy barrier, the local hydration environment of the translocating chloride ion was analyzed. As shown in Figure S26, the number of water molecules coordinating the ion decreases steadily as it migrates from the channel entrance toward the center, reaching near-complete dehydration at the midpoint. This progressive loss of solvation significantly destabilizes the ion within the confined channel core, accounting for the elevated free energy at this position.

Despite the unfavorable desolvation penalty, efficient ion transport is enabled by specific noncovalent interactions between the chloride ion and the channel framework. Analysis of hydrogen-bonding interactions within the channel indicates minimal disruption of the intrinsic supramolecular hydrogen-bond network during ion passage, preserving overall structural integrity (Figure A, blue trace). In contrast, ion–channel contacts increase markedly as the chloride ion approaches the channel center (Figure A, red trace), reaching a maximum at the midpoint. This observation highlights the central role of direct channel–ion interactions in stabilizing the anion during transport.

Closer inspection of representative simulation snapshots reveals that the chloride ion preferentially associates with iodine atoms lining the channel interior (Figure B). This suggests that halogen bonding constitutes a dominant interaction motif governing anion transport. To substantiate this hypothesis, the distance between the chloride ion and channel iodine atoms was monitored along the transport pathway. As shown in Figure B, the ion–iodine distance decreases substantially upon entry into the channel and reaches a minimum near the center, consistent with the formation of stabilizing I···Cl– halogen bonds. Correspondingly, the total number of chloride–iodine contacts increases progressively toward the channel midpoint (Figure C), reinforcing the importance of these interactions. Finally, the structural response of the channel during ion transport was assessed by monitoring the radius of gyration (Rg). While minor fluctuations in Rg are observed (Figure S27), the channel remains structurally stable throughout the simulation. The slight, reversible deformation induced by chloride binding reflects a beneficial degree of flexibility, allowing the channel to adapt dynamically to ion passage without compromising its supramolecular integrity.

Collectively, these simulations reveal that chloride ion transport through the synthetic channel arises from a delicate balance between desolvation, confinement, and compensatory halogen bonding interactions. The cooperative engagement of multiple iodine atoms stabilizes the anion within the dehydrated channel core, enabling efficient transport while preserving channel stabilityan insight that underscores the potential of halogen bonding as a powerful design element in synthetic ion channels.

Conclusions

In summary, we have successfully synthesized a supramolecular ion channel composed of small molecules arranged in a barrel rosette structure. This ion channel exhibits selective chloride transport, facilitated by halogen bonding interactions. A set of molecules was synthesized through systematic halogen variation, enabling modulation of the σ-hole potency. During the course of the studies, it was observed that compound 3 exhibited greater transport activity among compounds 1–4, consistent with theoretical literature suggesting that iodine is the most polarizable halogen. The investigation of ion selectivity by altering extravesicular anions and cations confirmed that 3 shows selectivity for chloride ions. The investigation of the mechanism has revealed that the antiport mechanism is responsible for transporting ions across the bilayer. Moreover, dose–response studies using the lucigenin assay unambiguously demonstrated the chloride influx capacity of compound 3. Ion channel formation was verified by electrophysiological experiments, which indicated that the channel diameter and single channel conductance were 3.68 ± 0.12 Å and 17.58 ± 0.8 pS, respectively. The selectivity analysis carried out in conductance studies demonstrated that the permeability of compound 3 toward Cl– ions is around 4.07 times greater than its permeability toward K+ ions. A MD simulation of the tetrameric rosette of the most active compound 3 validated the proposed channel assembly and provided insight into halogen bonding interactions between the iodine atom of compound 3 and the Cl– ion. Therefore, we believe that these ion channels represent a promising new strategy for designing halogen-assisted anion-conducting systems.

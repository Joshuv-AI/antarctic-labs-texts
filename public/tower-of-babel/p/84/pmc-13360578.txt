ABSTRACT
SummaryBackground:The COVID-19 pandemic has had a deleterious effect on health systems and the global economy. Therefore, understanding the impact of COVID-19 on different ethnicities is crucial for better containment of the pandemic.Method(s):A retrospective analysis of 523 symptomatic COVID-19 patients from different ethnicities in the UAE. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. Comparisons were made using Pearson's χ2 test and analysis of variance (ANOVA). A p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analysis was performed using STATA 9/SE software (Stata Corp, College Station, Texas, USA).Result(s):The results revealed significant ethnic variations in demographics, comorbidities, symptoms, and clinical outcomes. Emiratis accounted for 10% of the cases, with Indians most affected (63%), followed by Arabs (13%) and Asians (6%). Other ethnicities, including Iranians, Africans, and Europeans, made up less than 3%.The mean age of the patients was 49 ± 15 years, with non-Emiratis being younger compared to Emiratis and Arabs (p < 0.001). Patients older than 60 years were most common among Arabs (39%), Emiratis (32%), and Iranians (25%), while patients under 18 were rare. Males predominated among most ethnic groups, particularly Indians (85%) and Iranians (83%), but were fewer among Emiratis (42%) and Africans (2%), p < 0.001. BMI was highest in Emiratis (31.94 ± 8.70 kg/m2) and Arabs (30.52 ± 6.03 kg/m2), while other ethnicities had a lower range (26.27–29.10 kg/m2), p < 0.001.Fever (65%), cough (57%), and shortness of breath (46%) were the most common symptoms, but symptom duration was shortest in Europeans (3 ± 2 days), p = 0.056. Smoking was uncommon across all ethnicities. The source of infection was identified in only 29% of patients, with 5% contracting the disease after overseas travel, particularly among Europeans (62%) and Iranians (50%), and 24% having contact with other COVID-19 cases. The acquisition of the virus through contact was more common among Emiratis (42%) but similar across other ethnicities (p = 0.011). Figure 1.Regarding comorbidities, more than half of Europeans (69%) and Africans (57%) had at least one, while fewer than half of Indians (47%), Iranians (42%), and Emiratis (36%) had comorbidities (p = 0.537). Diabetes and hypertension were common across all ethnicities, with diabetes most prevalent among Arabs (36%) and Indians (36%) and hypertension higher in Africans (43%) and Emiratis (34%). Cardiovascular disease was most common among Indians (14%), Africans (14%), and Arabs (13%), but rare in Emiratis (4%) and Asians, Iranians, and Europeans (p = 0.020). Thyroid disorders were most common in Iranians (33%), and stroke was notably higher in Africans (14%), p = 0.002. Cancer was more prevalent in Iranians and Europeans (8%), p < 0.001.Clinical symptoms varied, with fever and cough most common in Asians (77%) and Indians (67%), and shortness of breath more common in Asians (54%), Indians (52%), and Arabs (47%), but less frequent in Emiratis (18%) and Iranians (17%), p < 0.001. Myalgia was seen in Emiratis (22%) and Arabs (18%), p = 0.043, and rhinorrhea was more common in Europeans (31%), p < 0.001. Vomiting was common in Emiratis (10%), p < 0.003.Laboratory findings revealed elevated CRP levels across most ethnic groups, particularly Arabs (96.35) and Asians (89.40), and lower levels in Africans (4.69) and Iranians (17.02), p = 0.009. Ferritin was elevated in Asians (1428.66) and Indians (1174.09), with lower levels in Europeans (411), p = 0.002. Elevated ALT/AST was most common in Indians (62%) and Asians (60%), p < 0.001, and LDH and D-dimer levels were highest in Asians (86%) and Indians (82%), p < 0.001.Organ damage was most frequent in Indians, with 25% showing acute liver injury, followed by Asians (17%) and Emiratis (10%), p = 0.001. ARDS occurred in 30% of patients, primarily in Indians (35%) and Arabs (25%), with no cases in Africans, p = 0.007. When categorized by disease severity, 48% of Emiratis had mild-moderate disease, 34% had severe, and 18% had critical COVID-19. Among Indians, 38% had severe, 33% had critical, and 29% had mild-moderate disease, while Arabs had 51% with severe, 24% with mild-moderate, and 25% with critical disease. Other ethnicities, including Africans, Iranians, and Europeans, tended to have more mild-moderate disease, p < 0.001.

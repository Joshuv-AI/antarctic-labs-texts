scMGATGRN: a multiview graph attention network–based method for inferring gene regulatory networks from single-cell transcriptomic data
Authors: Lin Yuan, Ling Zhao, Yufeng Jiang, Zhen Shen, Qinhu Zhang, Ming Zhang, Chun-Hou Zheng, De-Shuang Huang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe gene regulatory network (GRN) plays a vital role in understanding the structure and dynamics of cellular systems, revealing complex regulatory relationships, and exploring disease mechanisms. Recently, deep learning (DL)–based methods have been proposed to infer GRNs from single-cell transcriptomic data and achieved impressive performance. However, these methods do not fully utilize graph topological information and high-order neighbor information from multiple receptive fields. To overcome those limitations, we propose a novel model based on multiview graph attention network, namely, scMGATGRN, to infer GRNs. scMGATGRN mainly consists of GAT, multiview, and view-level attention mechanism. GAT can extract essential features of the gene regulatory network. The multiview model can simultaneously utilize local feature information and high-order neighbor feature information of nodes in the gene regulatory network. The view-level attention mechanism dynamically adjusts the relative importance of node embedding representations and efficiently aggregates node embedding representations from two views. To verify the effectiveness of scMGATGRN, we compared its performance with 10 methods (five shallow learning algorithms and five state-of-the-art DL-based methods) on seven benchmark single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) datasets from five cell lines (two in human and three in mouse) with four different kinds of ground-truth networks. The experimental results not only show that scMGATGRN outperforms competing methods but also demonstrate the potential of this model in inferring GRNs. The code and data of scMGATGRN are made freely available on GitHub (https://github.com/nathanyl/scMGATGRN).Keywords: multiview, graph structure learning, graph attention network, gene regulatory network, scRNA-seq

Introduction

The gene regulatory network (GRN) represents the expression regulation of transcription factors (TFs) and their downstream genes, which affects the behavior and characteristics of cells [1, 2]. The GRN plays a vital role in understanding the structure and dynamics of cellular systems, revealing complex regulatory relationships, and exploring disease mechanisms [3–5].

Single-cell transcriptomic data provide unprecedented opportunity to reconstruct GRNs at high resolution [6]. Several computational methods have been proposed to infer single-cell GRNs. Single Cell Network Synthesis (SCNS) [7] applied the Boolean network model to reconstruct GRNs. SCNS-binarized gene expression information may reduce the accuracy of GRN prediction. SCODE [8] utilized ordinary differential equations to reconstruct GRNs, leveraging pseudotime as high-resolution time information. However, inaccurate pseudotime may affect the accuracy of results [9]. Single-cell Inference of Networks using Granger Ensembles (SINGE) [10] used Granger causality ensembles to infer dynamic GRNs. Granger causality cannot calculate nonlinear gene–gene interactions, which may reduce the accuracy of GRN inference. GENIE3 [11] or GRNBoost2 [12] is the first step of SCENIC [13], which uses tree-based regressions to discover gene sets that are co-expressed with TFs. However, SCENIC is sensitive to noise, which may lead to inaccurate prediction results when there is noise or incomplete information in the data.

Recently, with the rapid development of DL, many DL-based methods have achieved remarkable results on GRN inference. For example, Convolutional Neural Network for Coexpression (CNNC) [14] transformed expression data lacking locality to an image-like object and applied convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to predict relationships between genes. Gene Network Embedding (GNE) [15] employed multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) to encode gene expression information, facilitating the prediction of gene–gene interactions. DeepSEM [16] integrated the structural equation model (SEM) into the β-VAE (β-variational autoencoder) framework to explicitly model regulatory relationships between genes. DeepRIG [17] utilized the graph autoencoder (GAE) to embed the co-expression information and regulatory information into latent embeddings and reconstruct GRNs. GENELink [18], an impressive DL-based algorithm, applied the graph attention network (GAT) to reconstruct GRNs. The GAT introduces a self-attention mechanism into the graph neural networks, allowing it to be extended to directed graphs [19]. GENELink used GAT to aggregate gene expression information and prior knowledge of gene–gene interactions, extracting low-dimensional representation of genes to infer GRN. Structure Learning Attention-based Graph Neural Networks (SLAGNN) [20] utilized high-order neighbor information in graph neural networks and demonstrated the effectiveness of high-order neighbor information on multiple datasets. These methods have achieved impressive performance. Nevertheless, they still have several limitations. These methods do not fully utilize graph topological information and high-order neighbor information from multiple receptive fields.

To overcome these limitations, we propose scMGATGRN, based on a multiview GAT for inferring GRNs from single-cell transcriptomic data. scMGATGRN generates gene expression matrix (N represents the number of genes, F represents the number of features) and gene adjacency matrix using gene expression data and ground-truth GRNs, respectively. In the ground-truth GRN, a node represents a gene or TF, and the attribute of the node is the gene expression level. scMGATGRN generates adjacency matrix (N represents the number of genes) based on the regulatory relationships in the ground-truth GRN. indicates whether there is a regulatory relationship between gene i and gene j. If there is a regulatory relationship between gene i and gene j, then = = 1; otherwise, = = 0. Subsequently, two-layer multiview GATs are applied to encode gene expression information. Then, scMGATGRN uses a view-level attention to aggregate node embedding representations from two views and utilizes a fully connected layer to project gene expression information into a low-dimensional space to obtain the low-dimensional feature vector of the gene. Finally, scMGATGRN uses dot product as a scoring function to reconstruct GRNs (as shown in Fig. 1). scMGATGRN uses multiview to simultaneously consider local feature information and high-order feature information of nodes. scMGATGRN combines GAT and node similarity measurement based on local information to extract structural information of high-order neighbors in the network. Meanwhile, scMGATGRN uses view-level attention to aggregate node embedding representations from two views, resulting in a more comprehensive and robust node embedding representation, improving the accuracy of GRN inference. We compared scMGATGRN with 10 methods (five shallow learning algorithms and five state-of-the-art DL-based methods) on seven benchmark scRNA-seq datasets. The experimental results not only show that scMGATGRN outperforms competing methods but also demonstrate the potential of this model in inferring GRNs.

The scMGATGRN framework

The task of inferring GRNs is similar to link prediction in graph networks, revealing hidden interactions between node pairs. In GRNs, a node represents a TF or a gene, and links represent regulatory relationships between nodes. Link prediction in GRNs refers to predicting the likelihood of connections that have not yet formed edges. This prediction relies on known node information and network structure [29, 30].

scMGATGRN implements the link prediction task by projecting gene expression profiles into a low-dimensional space using the gene–gene interaction matrix based on prior information [31–33]. scMGATGRN aims to optimize the embedding space and learn high-quality gene representations to accurately predict the links between nodes. Inspired by multiview [34–36], we designed a novel model based on multiview GAT, namely, scMGATGRN, to infer GRNs. As shown in Fig. 1, scMGATGRN mainly consists of GAT, multiview, and view-level attention mechanism. GAT is utilized to extract essential features of the gene regulatory network. GAT assigns different attention weights to each neighbor based on its relevance to the target node. This mechanism enhances the model’s ability to identify important relationships. The multiview model can simultaneously use local feature information and high-order neighbor feature information of nodes in GRN. High-order neighbor information contains valuable hidden relationships between nodes, contributing to more accurate node representation. The view-level attention mechanism dynamically adjusts the relative importance of node embedding representations and efficiently aggregates node embedding representations from two views.

Evaluation strategies

There are differences in the attributes of the four ground-truth networks, especially in terms of network density (as shown in Table 1). To avoid the influence of network density, we divide the four networks into two parts and use different strategies to evaluate the models. The evaluation strategy takes into account different characteristics of the ground-truth networks, ensuring more comprehensive and robust evaluation results.

In the first part, we use the three ground-truth networks from STRING, nonspecific ChIP-seq, and LOF/GOF. We divide the available TF–gene pairs into a positive training dataset (including two out of three of the available TF–gene pairs) and positive test dataset (including the remaining one out of three pairs). Any TF–gene interactions that are not observed within the provided label files are treated as negative samples. We adopt a uniform random sampling strategy to select negative samples. This approach ensures that we can efficiently handle a large number of potential negative samples and fairly evaluate model performance. Hard negative samples help the model learn more accurate node representations [39, 40]. Therefore, we add a negative sampling constraint in the training dataset that each positive pair (Ta, Gb) is attached with a negative pair (Ta, Gc) as the hard negative. Results

Ablation experiment

To study the influence of key modules on a model, we constructed four variants of scMGATGRN, specifically, (i) scMGATGRN w/o J; (ii) scMGATGRN w/o A; (iii) scMGATGRN w/o G; and (iv) scMGATGRN w/o E. scMGATGRN w/o J indicates that scMGATGRN does not use the Jaccard coefficient to filter second-order neighbor nodes. scMGATGRN w/o A represents replacing the view-level attention mechanism with an averaging method. scMGATGRN w/o G indicates that scMGATGRN does not use Gaussian kernel transformation and uses the traditional graph attention coefficient calculation method instead. scMGATGRN w/o E indicates that scMGATGRN does not use the ELU activation function and uses the LeakyReLU activation function instead. We trained the four models using the same hyperparameters and compared the performance of these models with scMGATGRN and GENElink using seven benchmark scRNA-seq datasets (see section First View of Model).

As shown in Fig. 2, after removing the Jaccard coefficient, view-level attention mechanism, Gaussian kernel transform, or ELU activation function, the model performance decreased. And after removing the Jaccard coefficient or view-level attention mechanism, scMGATGRN performance decreased significantly. Removing the Jaccard coefficient or the view-level attention mechanism causes the AUROC to decrease by 1.59% and 2.30%, respectively. Experimental results show that the Jaccard coefficient, view-level attention mechanism, Gaussian kernel transform, and ELU activation function are all beneficial to scMGATGRN. The Jaccard coefficient and view-level attention mechanism play a crucial role in inferring GRNs. In addition, the four variants of scMGATGRN performed better than GENElink, further demonstrating the importance of the Jaccard coefficient, view-level attention mechanism, Gaussian kernel transformation and ELU activation function.

Hyperparameter tuning

The two hyperparameters in scMGATGRN, threshold (equation (1)) and number of heads K [equations (9) and (10)], have an important impact on the model performance. We show the impact of hyperparameters and discuss how to tune them to aid downstream tasks.

The threshold is used to filter out noisy nodes in second-order neighbors. A too-large results in a model missing important second-order neighbor nodes, whereas a too-small results in a model containing a lot of noisy nodes. Threshold ranges from 0 to 0.26. We trained and tested the model within this range to observe the impact of the threshold on the model. We selected an appropriate threshold by observing the AUROC value of scMGATGRN. Figure 3A shows that scMGATGRN achieves optimal performance when threshold = 0.2.

Impact of single-cell data imputation on gene regulatory network inference

In single-cell transcriptomic data, some genes may not be expressed in the cell, resulting in zero expression values. Single-cell transcriptomic data contains too many zero values, leading to biased inferences [42]. In this section, the DeepImpute method [43] was used for single-cell transcriptomic data imputation. DeepImpute trains a deep neural network on the input data to predict and fill zero values, generating an imputed dataset. Both the original (nonimputed) and imputed datasets were used to train the model. We used the AUROC and AUPRC metrics to evaluate the model performance and investigated the impact of data imputation on model performance in GRN inference.

As shown in Fig. 3C and D, scMGATGRN performed better on the imputed data than the non-imputed data in all datasets. The detailed results of AUROC and AUPRC are listed in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2, respectively. Experimental results show that zero values in single-cell transcriptomic data affect model performance in GRN inference. Data imputation can reduce the impact of zero values, enable the model to obtain small but important patterns and relationships in the data, and improve the performance and robustness of scMGATGRN in GRN inference. Experimental results show that single-cell data imputation can alleviate the impact of zero inflation on GRN inference.

The AUROC and AUPRC were used to evaluate the model performance under different training dataset sizes.

Conclusion and discussion

We proposed a novel DL-based model, scMGATGRN, based on multiview and GAT for inferring GRNs from single-cell transcriptomic data. By incorporating graph attention mechanisms and leveraging the node similarity index, scMGATGRN effectively captures comprehensive feature information from gene regulatory networks. Experimental results showed that scMGATGRN outperforms state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating superior performance in inferring GRNs. scMGATGRN outperforms competing methods on the AUROC and AUPRC evaluation metrics, which are reliable indicators for evaluating a model’s prediction accuracy and overall performance. Experimental results demonstrated the powerful predictive ability of scMGATGRN in deciphering complex relationships within biological networks in single-cell transcriptomic data. Experimental results demonstrated the necessity of graph structure information, and the view-level attention mechanism can effectively combine node content feature similarity and structural relationships to obtain high-quality gene embedding representations.

However, there is a lack of sufficient negative samples in biological network inference tasks. To address this issue, we used a uniform sampling strategy to select negative target genes from a large set of genes. We inevitably introduced false negatives, which we will address in future studies.

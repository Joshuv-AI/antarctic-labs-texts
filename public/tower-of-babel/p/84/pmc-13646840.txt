Nationwide Incidence and Prevalence of Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis in South Korea
Authors: Hee-Young Yoon, Kyung Hoon Kim, Song Yee Kim, Moo Suk Park, Jong Sun Park, Joo Hun Park, Soo Taek Uh, Jung-Wan Yoo, Jin Hwa Lee, Won-Il Choi, Jae Ha Lee, Chang Hoon Han, on behalf of Korea ILD Study Group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive fibrotic lung disease with high mortality. Population-based data on long-term epidemiological trends and real-world treatment outcomes in Asia remain limited. This study described national trends in IPF prevalence and incidence in South Korea and evaluated pirfenidone use and associated mortality.Methods Using the Korean National Health Insurance Service customized research database, we identified prevalent IPF cases from 2010 to 2023 and incident cases from 2011 to 2023. IPF was defined using the International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision, and rare intractable disease codes, based on a predefined operational definition. Among incident cases, the association between pirfenidone use and all-cause mortality was assessed using multivariable-adjusted Cox proportional hazards models.Results A total of 91,638 prevalent and 28,683 incident IPF cases were identified during the study period. The crude prevalence increased from 8.91 per 100,000 persons ≥50 years in 2010 to 63.78 per 100,000 in 2023. The crude incidence increased from 6.07 per 100,000 in 2011 to 16.86 per 100,000 in 2023. Similar increasing patterns were observed for age- and sex-standardized rates. IPF was more common in men, and demonstrated a pronounced age gradient. Following reimbursement approval, pirfenidone use increased substantially. Pirfenidone use was associated with a reduced risk of all-cause mortality (adjusted hazard ratio, 0.627; 95% confidence interval, 0.600 to 0.655), with progressively stronger associations observed with longer treatment duration.Conclusion The burden of IPF in South Korea has increased over the past decade, during which expanded real-world pirfenidone use was associated with improved survival.Keywords: Pulmonary Fibrosis, Idiopathic, Epidemiology, Incidence, Prevalence, Pirfenidone

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive fibrosing interstitial lung disease (ILD) characterized by irreversible distortion of lung architecture and a median survival of approximately 3–5 years after diagnosis [1]. Despite recent therapeutic advances, IPF continues to impose a substantial clinical and socioeconomic burden owing to its high mortality, frequent acute exacerbations, progressive decline in lung function, and the need for long-term multidisciplinary care [2,3].

Epidemiological patterns of IPF vary considerably across countries, partly because of differences in population demographics, disease recognition, diagnostic and coding practices, and availability of antifibrotic therapy [4-6]. Although many Western and Asian countries have reported nationwide epidemiological estimates of IPF using diverse data sources—including administrative claims, national registries, and large hospital-based cohorts—substantial variability persists, reflecting differences in case definitions, data capture methods, and healthcare system structures. Consequently, nationally representative data derived from a uniform insurance framework are essential for generating more comparable and reliable estimates. South Korea, with its universal single-payer health insurance system and high diagnostic coding fidelity [7], provides a unique opportunity to generate consistent nationwide epidemiological estimates using contemporary, population-level data.

Several previous studies in South Korea have examined the epidemiology of IPF using data from the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) and the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service (HIRA) datasets [8-12]. Notably, reported incidence and prevalence rates in South Korea tend to be higher than those observed in many Western and Asian countries. However, these estimates have varied substantially depending on the case definition employed (e.g., broad vs. narrow case definitions, inclusion of the rare intractable disease [RID] registry), study period, and methods used to distinguish IPF from other forms of ILD. This heterogeneity underscores the need for updated nationwide estimates that incorporate contemporary diagnostic practices and improved claim-based coding accuracy.

Using the near-complete population coverage of the NHIS/HIRA database, we aimed to provide an updated and comprehensive characterization of the epidemiology of IPF in South Korea. Additionally, we assessed mortality according to pirfenidone use to describe contemporary real-world outcomes within the current treatment landscape.

Materials and Methods

1. Data source

We conducted a nationwide population-based study using data from the NHIS Customized Research Database in South Korea [13]. The NHIS is a universal single-payer system that provides mandatory health insurance coverage to approximately 97% of the South Korean population, with the remaining 3% covered by the Medical Aid Program [14]. Because all reimbursed inpatient and outpatient encounters—including diagnostic tests, procedures, and prescriptions—must be submitted for claims review, the NHIS database comprehensively captures healthcare utilization at the national level.

This study used a customized cohort constructed by the NHIS according to predefined extraction criteria. The dataset comprised four major components: (1) eligibility information (age, sex, income level, and insurance type); (2) healthcare utilization records containing International Classification of Diseases, 10th Revision (ICD-10) diagnostic codes and procedure codes; (3) prescription records, including drug codes, prescription dates, and treatment duration; and (4) mortality information obtained through linkage to the National Death Registry. The customized cohort was additionally linked to the NHIS Health Screening Database, which contains biennial health examination records, such as self-reported smoking history and body mass index. This cohort design ensured complete longitudinal follow-up throughout the study period and minimized loss to follow-up. All data were fully anonymized before release, and no identifiable personal information was available to the investigators.

2. Case definition

The index date was defined as the date of the first qualifying claim that fulfilled the diagnostic claim requirement for IPF, specified as at least one inpatient claim or at least two outpatient or emergency department claims with ICD-10 code J84.1 and RID code V236 recorded as either a primary or secondary diagnosis. IPF cases were identified using a claims-based algorithm that incorporated diagnostic requirements, age eligibility, core diagnostic procedures, and exclusion criteria. Eligible individuals were required to have at least one inpatient or two outpatient or emergency encounters with an IPF diagnosis and to be aged ≥50 years at the index date, given the low likelihood of IPF in younger individuals. In addition, at least one core diagnostic procedure, either high-resolution chest computed tomography (HRCT) or surgical lung biopsy, was required within ±90 days of the index date. Furthermore, alternative causes of ILD were excluded based on prespecified diagnostic categories. Individuals were excluded if diagnostic codes corresponding to alternative ILDs were recorded at least twice in outpatient claims or at least once in inpatient claims within 1 year before or after the index date (Supplementary Table S1); repeated records prior to the index date were also considered grounds for exclusion. Major exclusion criteria were connective tissue disease-associated ILD, hypersensitivity pneumonitis, pneumoconiosis, drug- or radiation-induced ILD, and sarcoidosis.

Prevalence was defined as the total number of individuals meeting the IPF case definition in each calendar year. Annual prevalence rates were calculated beginning in 2010 using the mid-year population as the denominator and were expressed per 100,000 persons.

Incidence was defined as newly diagnosed IPF in each calendar year. For incident IPF cases, the index date was the first qualifying claim after a 1-year washout period with no prior qualifying IPF claims. To identify true incident cases, a 1-year washout period was applied, excluding individuals with any IPF-related codes in the preceding year. Accordingly, incidence estimation began in 2011. Annual incidence rates were calculated per 100,000 persons using the mid-year population as the denominator. All prevalence and incidence rates were calculated using the population aged ≥50 years as the denominator.

3. Data collection

Baseline characteristics and comorbidities were identified using diagnostic claims recorded during the 12 months preceding the index date and were considered present if documented in at least one inpatient or two outpatient claims. Analyses of comorbidities were restricted to incident IPF cases. Body mass index and smoking status were obtained from the NHIS Health Screening Database using the most recent available records before the index date. Baseline characteristics were described among incident IPF cases with available health screening data.

We further evaluated pirfenidone use, comorbidity burden, and all-cause mortality to characterize clinical differences. As pirfenidone became reimbursable in South Korea in October 2015, analyses of treatment uptake were restricted to patients with follow-up during the post-reimbursement period and were based on prescription claims. Pirfenidone use was evaluated only in incident IPF cases, as nintedanib was not reimbursed and therefore not captured in the claims database during the study period. Pirfenidone exposure was categorized as any use, persistent use (≥6 months), and long-term use (≥12 months). Analyses of long-term use were restricted to patients diagnosed through 2022 to ensure sufficient follow-up. All-cause mortality was ascertained via linkage to the National Death Registry.

4. Statistical analysis

Annual counts and distributions of IPF cases by sex, age group, income level, and residential region were summarized descriptively for each calendar year. Prevalence and incidence measures were calculated as described in the preceding section. As a sensitivity analysis, age- and sex-standardized prevalence and incidence rates were calculated using the direct standardization method based on the 2020 Korean standard population.

Mortality analyses were conducted within the IPF cohort according to pirfenidone exposure status. Because pirfenidone was first reimbursed under the national health insurance program in 2015, these analyses were restricted to incident IPF cases identified from 2015 onward. Pirfenidone exposure was defined as having at least one pirfenidone prescription claim after the index date. Cox proportional hazards models were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) for all-cause mortality. In secondary analyses, pirfenidone users were further stratified by cumulative prescription duration (≥6 months and ≥12 months), with associations with mortality evaluated using the same modeling approach. Cumulative prescription duration was calculated by summing the days supplied across all pirfenidone prescriptions after the index date. Duration was categorized as ≥6 months (≥180 days) and ≥12 months (≥365 days).

All analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA) and R version 4.3.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). A two-sided p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

5. Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the National Health Insurance Service Ilsan Hospital (IRB No. NHIMC IRB 2025-03-036). The requirement for informed consent was waived because the study used fully anonymized data from the NHIS and involved no direct contact with participants.

6. Data sharing

The data cannot be publicly shared because of data privacy restrictions that protect patient confidentiality. Access is possible only with approval from the South Korean government and the NHIS.

Results

1. Identification of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis cases

Using the NHIS customized research database, 130,120 prevalent IPF candidates between 2010 and 2023 and 38,424 incident IPF candidates between 2011 and 2023 were initially identified based on ICD-10 code J84.1 and RID code V236 (Figure 1). After excluding individuals younger than 50 years, those without documentation of core diagnostic procedures (chest computed tomography or surgical lung biopsy) within ±90 days of the index date, and those with alternative ILD diagnoses, 91,638 prevalent cases and 28,683 incident cases were included in the final analysis.

2. Temporal trends in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis prevalence

IPF prevalence increased steadily throughout the study period (Figure 2A and Supplementary Table S2). Prevalence rose from 8.91 per 100,000 persons ≥50 years in 2010 to 63.78 per 100,000 in 2023, with a gradual increase in the early 2010s and a more pronounced rise after 2015. As a sensitivity analysis, age- and sex-standardized prevalence showed a temporal trend similar to that of crude prevalence (Supplementary Table S3).

Trends in the prevalence of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) in South Korea (2010–2023). Line graphs displaying the annual prevalence of IPF per 100,000 persons ≥50 years in South Korea from 2010 to 2023. (A) Overall and sex-specific prevalence. (B) Age-specific prevalence.

Throughout the study period, prevalence was consistently higher in men than in women (Figure 2A). Male prevalence increased from 13.46 per 100,000 in 2010 to 104.50 per 100,000 in 2023, whereas female prevalence increased from 4.99 to 26.88 per 100,000.

A clear age gradient was evident (Figure 2B). In 2023, prevalence was highest among individuals aged 70–79 years (150.91 per 100,000), followed by those aged ≥80 years (118.87 per 100,000), compared with 60.49 per 100,000 in the 60–69-year age group and 12.52 per 100,000 in the 50–59-year age group. Across calendar years, the increase in prevalence was most pronounced in older age groups, which accounted for the majority of prevalent cases.

3. Temporal trends in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis incidence

The incidence of IPF increased overall during the study period (Figure 3A and Supplementary Table S4). Incidence rose from 6.07 per 100,000 persons ≥50 years in 2011 to 16.86 per 100,000 in 2023, with the annual number of incident cases increasing from 909 to 3,749. However, the increase was not strictly monotonic: incidence showed a brief plateau in 2016–2017 (10.79 to 10.19 per 100,000) and a transient decline in 2020 (from 15.05 in 2019 to 13.75 in 2020), followed by a sustained rise thereafter. Similarly, age- and sex-standardized incidence demonstrated a trend consistent with crude incidence over time (Supplementary Table S3).

Trends in the incidence of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) in South Korea (2011–2023). Line graphs displaying annual IPF incidence per 100,000 persons ≥50 years based on the predefined operational case definition. (A) Overall and sex-specific incidence. (B) Age-specific incidence.

Incidence was consistently higher in men than in women throughout the study period (Figure 3A). Incidence among men increased from 9.71 per 100,000 in 2011 to 27.75 per 100,000 in 2023, whereas incidence among women rose from 2.92 to 6.99 per 100,000. The temporary dip around 2020 was observed in both sexes.

A marked age gradient was observed (Figure 3B). Incidence increased most prominently among older adults, particularly in the 70–79 and ≥80-year groups, while remaining low and relatively stable in the 50–59-year group. Notably, older age groups also demonstrated a transient decline around 2020, followed by a rebound and continued increases through 2023.

4. Baseline demographics and comorbidities of patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

Among incident IPF cases with available health screening data (n=21,762), the mean age was 72.7±8.4 years, and the majority were male (78.0%) (Table 1). Most patients were in their 70s (44.3%), followed by those in their 60s (30.1%), while 18.7% were aged ≥80 years. Regarding socioeconomic status, patients in the highest income quintile accounted for the largest proportion (40.4%). Never-smokers comprised 45.0% of the cohort, whereas former and current smokers accounted for 27.2% and 27.8%, respectively. The mean body mass index was 24.6±3.1 kg/m². Approximately 43.2% of patients resided in Seoul or other metropolitan cities. The Charlson comorbidity index score was most commonly 2–3 (38.4%), with 18.7% of patients having a score >5.

Several comorbidities demonstrated distinct sex-specific patterns (Table 2). Cardiometabolic and respiratory conditions including diabetes mellitus (61.7% vs. 57.6%), chronic kidney disease (11.1% vs. 7.6%), atrial fibrillation or flutter (10.7% vs. 8.1%), and malignancy (38.0% vs. 20.7%) were more prevalent in men than in women. In contrast, osteoporosis was markedly more common in women than in men (59.3% vs. 20.5%). Women also had higher prevalences of depressive disorder (28.5% vs. 21.5%), dementia (24.7% vs. 14.2%), thyroid disease (20.9% vs. 13.7%), and bronchiectasis (19.4% vs. 13.4%).

Age-stratified analyses revealed clear gradients for several comorbidities (Supplementary Table S5). The prevalence of hypertension (50.2% among those aged 50–59 years to 78.2% among those aged ≥80 years), cerebrovascular disease (15.5% to 35.3%), heart failure (19.0% to 35.1%), chronic kidney disease (6.1% to 14.1%), atrial fibrillation or flutter (5.3% to 13.5%), and dementia (2.5% to 32.5%) increased markedly with advancing age. In contrast, gastroesophageal reflux (90.9% to 83.1%), chronic liver disease (65.5% to 49.0%), and pulmonary hypertension (6.1% to 2.9%) were less prevalent in the oldest age group. Obesity and sleep apnea remained uncommon across all age groups.

5. Pirfenidone use and mortality outcomes

Among all incident IPF cases, pirfenidone use increased substantially after initial reimbursement in October 2015 and further accelerated following expansion of reimbursement criteria in 2019 (Figure 4). The proportion of patients who received pirfenidone at least once rose from 45.0% in 2015 to 70.4% in 2018, increased to 78.2% in 2019, and stabilized at approximately 79%–80% by 2022. Persistent use (≥6 months) increased in parallel, from 32.4% in 2015 to 53.9% in 2018 and 59.5% in 2019, approaching or exceeding 60% by 2022. Long-term use (≥12 months) increased similarly after 2019, reaching approximately 49%–50% between 2019 and 2021.

Across all post-reimbursement years, pirfenidone use was consistently higher in men than in women and declined with advancing age (Supplementary Figures S1, S2). Following the 2019 reimbursement expansion, pirfenidone use exceeded 80% among patients aged 50–69 years but remained lower among those aged ≥80 years. These sex- and age-related gradients were consistently observed for persistent and long-term use.

Among incident IPF cases diagnosed from 2015 onward, when pirfenidone became reimbursed, consistent with the observed expansion and persistence of pirfenidone treatment after reimbursement, pirfenidone use was inversely associated with all-cause mortality (Table 3). In multivariable-adjusted analyses, any pirfenidone use was associated with a reduced mortality risk (adjusted HR, 0.627; 95% CI, 0.600 to 0.655), with progressively stronger associations observed with longer treatment duration (≥6 months [adjusted HR, 0.508; 95% CI, 0.487 to 0.530] and ≥12 months [adjusted HR, 0.446; 95% CI, 0.426 to 0.467]). These associations remained consistent after adjustment for demographic factors, comorbidity burden, and calendar year of diagnosis, demonstrating a clear duration-dependent relationship.

Discussion

In this nationwide study, we observed a sustained increase in both the prevalence and incidence of IPF in South Korea over the study period. This pattern is broadly consistent with previous South Korean population-based analyses using NHIS or HIRA data [8-12], although absolute estimates have varied substantially depending on case definitions, observation periods, and methodological strategies used to distinguish IPF from other ILDs (Table 4). Earlier studies conducted between 2008 and 2013 reported a wide range of incidence estimates, largely reflecting differences in operational definitions (e.g., various operational definitions and use of the RID registry) and study design [8,9,12]. More recent studies adopting stricter case definitions and longer observation windows have yielded more conservative yet consistently increasing estimates [10,11]. A recent nationwide HIRA-based study covering 2011–2019 reported an increase in incidence from approximately 3.6 to 7.9 per 100,000 person-years, with prevalence rising in parallel from approximately 7.5 to 23.2 per 100,000 persons in total population [10]. Our findings closely align with these contemporary estimates and further demonstrate that the national burden of IPF continues to increase.

However, despite overall consistency in temporal trends, the absolute prevalence and incidence observed in the present study were higher than those reported in previous studies [10,11], even within overlapping calendar years (Table 4), despite both being derived from nationwide claims data in South Korea. The discrepancy is primarily attributable to differences in the population denominator. In the present study, rates were calculated among individuals aged ≥50 years, reflecting the epidemiologic profile of IPF as a disease predominantly affecting older adults. In contrast, previous studies generally used the total population as the denominator, which may dilute disease frequency and lead to lower overall estimates. Accordingly, the higher estimates observed in this study are more likely to reflect the use of an age-restricted population, which reduces dilution by younger individuals in the denominator.

Notably, the upward trend in IPF burden became more pronounced in the mid-2010s and appeared to accelerate after 2019. This temporal pattern coincides with key reimbursement policy changes for pirfenidone in South Korea. Pirfenidone was first reimbursed in October 2015 under restrictive eligibility criteria [15], whereas coverage was substantially expanded in January 2019 to include patients with less advanced physiological impairment and to remove certain functional requirements [16]. These policy changes may have facilitated earlier diagnostic evaluation and increased treatment uptake, which in turn may have contributed to improved case ascertainment in claims data and steeper observed trends in the late 2010s. Importantly, similar upward trends in IPF incidence and prevalence have been reported in both Western and Asian settings, likely reflecting evolving diagnostic guidelines, wider HRCT use, and increased clinical recognition of fibrotic lung disease.

Comparable temporal patterns have been reported across Western countries [4]. Population-based studies from Europe and North America have consistently demonstrated increasing IPF incidence and prevalence over recent decades, particularly since the mid-2000s [17-20]. Likewise, longitudinal administrative database studies from Asia have shown a steady rise in IPF burden across successive calendar periods, despite substantial variation in absolute estimates between countries [21,22]. Importantly, these studies uniformly report upward trends over time, suggesting that the increasing burden of IPF is not confined to a single healthcare system or geographic region.

Several concrete developments likely explain these shared temporal patterns across regions. The publication of the American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society/Japanese Respiratory Society/Latin American Thoracic Association IPF diagnostic guidelines in 2011 provided standardized diagnostic criteria and likely enhanced disease recognition beyond tertiary referral centers [23]. In parallel, advances in HRCT technology have facilitated more confident identification of fibrotic lung diseases in routine clinical practice [24]. The introduction of antifibrotic agents in the mid-2010s further shifted clinical practice toward earlier IPF diagnosis and active disease-modifying treatment [25,26]. Consistent with this context, a large population-based primary care study from Italy demonstrated a statistically significant increase in IPF prevalence over time, with incidence rising in the later years of observation [27]. Collectively, these observations suggest that recent increases in the IPF burden reflect evolving diagnostic and therapeutic landscapes, within which country-specific health policies may further shape observed epidemiological trends. Notably, a transient decrease in incidence was observed around 2020. This pattern may partly reflect reduced healthcare utilization and delays in diagnostic evaluation during the coronavirus disease 2019 pandemic, which may have temporarily suppressed case detection. Despite this, prevalence continued to increase, likely reflecting the chronic nature of IPF and the accumulation of existing cases over time.

Patients with IPF in this nationwide cohort exhibited a substantial burden of comorbid conditions, reinforcing the concept that IPF commonly occurs in the context of multimorbidity rather than as an isolated pulmonary disease. The high prevalence of gastroesophageal reflux disease, cardiometabolic conditions, and chronic respiratory comorbidities observed in this study is consistent with findings from large population-based studies in Western populations [28]. Similar patterns have been reported in a previous nationwide South Korean claims-based study that demonstrated a high comorbidity burden among patients with IPF [10]. Notably, comorbidity burden increased markedly with advancing age, particularly for cardiovascular disease, malignancy, chronic kidney disease, and dementia, highlighting the close interplay among IPF, aging, and systemic vulnerability [29,30]. These findings underscore the clinical relevance of comorbid conditions in IPF and emphasize the importance of considering comorbidity profiles in both routine clinical care and observational research.

We observed an inverse association between pirfenidone use and all-cause mortality in this claims-based cohort, with progressively stronger associations observed with longer treatment duration. This pattern aligns with established evidence that antifibrotic therapy modifies the course of IPF. Pivotal randomized controlled trials have demonstrated that pirfenidone slows disease progression, most consistently reflected by attenuation of forced vital capacity decline [31,32], and pooled or long-term analyses have suggested benefits in survival-related outcomes [33]. Real-world studies and registry-based analyses have similarly reported improved survival among patients treated with pirfenidone [34,35], although interpretation of observational estimates requires careful attention to treatment timing and baseline differences between treated and untreated patients. Within these constraints, our findings provide population-level evidence from a single-payer healthcare system showing that expanded access to pirfenidone was accompanied by widespread and sustained use, and that greater treatment exposure was associated with improved survival in routine clinical practice.

Some limitations of this study warrant consideration. First, IPF cases were identified using operational definitions based on diagnostic codes and procedures rather than multidisciplinary clinical adjudication, introducing the possibility of disease misclassification. To mitigate this concern, we applied predefined diagnostic requirements incorporating RID registration, core diagnostic procedures within a restricted time window, and exclusion of alternative ILDs to enhance specificity. Second, detailed clinical data such as pulmonary function measures, radiologic patterns, and symptom severity were unavailable, thereby limiting adjustment for disease severity. Relatedly, the ascertainment of comorbidities relied on claims data rather than clinical adjudication, and some conditions—most notably gastroesophageal reflux disease—may have been identified based on treatment indication or medication use (e.g., proton pump inhibitor prescriptions) rather than confirmed diagnoses. Therefore, the comorbidity prevalence should be interpreted as claims-ascertained burden rather than clinically adjudicated disease prevalence. In addition, the high prevalence of obstructive airway disease (including asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) may reflect overlapping diagnostic codes and reimbursement-driven coding practices in claims data. These estimates should therefore be interpreted with caution. Third, our treatment–mortality analysis was subject to time-related biases, including immortal time bias, due to post-index treatment initiation and duration-based exposure definitions. Accordingly, the observed associations may overstate treatment benefits and should not be interpreted as causal. Despite these limitations, this study has several notable strengths, including its nationwide scope, long observation period, large sample size, and the ability to capture real-world treatment patterns and outcomes at the population level.

In conclusion, the burden of IPF in South Korea has increased substantially over the past decade, particularly among older adults and men. The widespread adoption of pirfenidone following reimbursement was accompanied by improved survival, with evidence of a duration-dependent association. These findings provide valuable population-level evidence to inform healthcare planning, optimize treatment strategies, and guide future research on IPF management in aging societies.

Routine vaginal examination to assess labor progress at 8 compared to 4 h after early amniotomy following Foley balloon ripening in the labor induction of nulliparas: A randomized trial
Authors: C M Nashreen, Mukhri Hamdan, Jesrine Hong, Maherah Kamarudin, Rahmah Saaid, Peng Chiong Tan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionOur objective was to compare the performance of the first vaginal examination at 8 vs 4 h after amniotomy following Foley ripening in nulliparous labor induction.Material and MethodsA randomized controlled trial was conducted from June 2021 to January 2022. 210 nulliparas at term for labor induction were randomized: 105 each to first vaginal examination at 8 or 4 h after Foley balloon ripening and amniotomy. Titrated oxytocin infusion was routinely commenced after amniotomy to expedite labor. Primary outcomes were the amniotomy‐to‐delivery interval (non‐inferiority hypothesis) and maternal satisfaction with their allocated labor care (superiority hypothesis) within 24 h after delivery. Analyses performed using t‐test, Mann–Whitney U test, and Chi‐squared test as appropriate.ResultsThe amniotomy‐to‐delivery interval was mean ± standard deviation 8.7 ± 3.4 vs 8.4 ± 3.7, mean difference 0.4 (97.5% CI: −0.7 to 1.5) hours, p = 0.442 within the pre‐specified 2‐hour non‐inferiority margin, and maternal satisfaction score with allocated labor care was median [interquartile range] 8[7.5–10] vs 8[7.0–10], p = 0.248 for 8 vs 4 h arms, respectively. The amniotomy to first vaginal examination intervals was 5.9 ± 2.3 vs 3.6 ± 1.0 h, p < 0.001, and the number of vaginal examinations was 2[1–2.5] vs 3 [2, 3], p < 0.001 for 8 vs 4 h, respectively. The first vaginal examination was less likely to have been performed as scheduled, more likely to be indicated by the urge to bear down, and non‐reassuring cardiotocography for the 8 h arm (p < 0.001). Spontaneous vaginal delivery was significantly more likely and instrumental vaginal delivery less likely, but cesarean rate was not significantly different for the 8 h arm (p = 0.017).ConclusionsA routine first vaginal examination at 8 h compared to 4 h is non‐inferior for the time to birth but does not increase maternal satisfaction although the number of vaginal examinations is fewer. The increase in spontaneous vaginal delivery and reduction in instrumental vaginal delivery rates warrant further powered primary evaluation.Keywords: amniotomy, cervical ripening, cesarean, Foley, interval to delivery, labor induction, labor progress, maternal satisfaction, oxytocin, vaginal delivery, vaginal examination

Abbreviation

visual numerical rating scale

Key message

Vaginal examination at 8 compared to 4 h is non‐inferior on time to delivery but does not increase maternal satisfaction after Foley ripening and amniotomy.

INTRODUCTION

National USA data reveal that in 2018, the labor induction rate was 27.1% of all births and 37.8% of first‐time births. 1 At labor induction, nulliparity and unfavorable cervixes are established risk factors for cesarean delivery, 2 and the induction to birth is longer in nulliparas. 3

Balloon catheters for labor induction are associated with fewer adverse perinatal events. 4 However, balloon ripening does not efficaciously initiate uterine contractions. 5 Hence, along with oxytocin infusion, early amniotomy is initiated to enhance labor progress. 6

A 2013 Cochrane systematic review on routine vaginal examinations for assessing labor progress finds only two relevant studies and concludes that it is surprising there is such widespread use without good evidence of effectiveness, considering the sensitivity of the procedure for women. 7 Frequent vaginal examination is associated with maternal fever 8 and increased risk of neonatal infection 9 , 10 but does not shorten the duration of labor. 7

NICE UK (2023) guidance on intrapartum care recommends “increase oxytocin until there are 3 to 4 contractions in 10 min” and “advise the woman to have a vaginal examination 4 h after starting oxytocin in established labor.” 11 Similarly, a Cochrane review (2013) notes that “clinicians routinely effectively titrate the dose against uterine contractions.” 12

ACOG Obstetric Care Consensus on Safe Prevention of the Primary Cesarean Delivery states that if the maternal and fetal status allows, oxytocin can be administered for at least 12–18 h after membrane rupture before deeming the induction a failure. 13 A prolonged first stage longer than 30 h does not put neonates at increased risk of morbidity. 14

In this study, we aimed to compare the performance of the first routine vaginal examination at 8 vs 4 h after amniotomy following Foley balloon ripening in nulliparous labor induction on the amniotomy‐to‐delivery interval (2‐h non‐inferiority margin) and raising maternal satisfaction on allocated care in labor.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This was a parallel‐group, randomized, controlled clinical trial conducted at University Malaya Medical Centre, a tertiary, government‐funded public hospital from June 16, 2021, until January 7, 2022. The annual birth rate at University Malaya Medical Centre was 4000 to 5000 births.

Nulliparous women who fulfilled the initial inclusion criteria of scheduled labor induction initiated by a size 16F transcervical Foley balloon insertion for cervical ripening, age ≥ 18 years, gestational age ≥ 37 weeks, singleton pregnancy, cephalic presentation, and reassuring pre‐induction fetal cardiotocography were considered as potentially eligible candidates for this study. In addition to patients who experienced spontaneous rupture of membranes, exclusion criteria were contraindications to vaginal delivery, known gross fetal anomaly, fetal weight clinically estimated to be ≤2 or ≥4 kg confirmed on ultrasound, and previous uterine surgery (myomectomy, hysterotomy, or iatrogenic uterine perforation). Potentially eligible participants were approached by an investigator, provided with a patient information sheet, and counseled for participation in this study. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. Participants were only included in the trial if they fulfilled the final criteria: amniotomy performed, cervical dilatation of ≥3 cm, cephalic presentation, station not higher than −2 cm, and reassuring fetal heart rate trace.

Randomization to trial arms was performed after amniotomy, with oxytocin to follow as soon as practicable according to our standard practice. Randomization was in a 1:1 ratio in blocks of 4 or 8, and the sequence was generated at random.org. Sequence generation and envelope preparation were done by an investigator who was not involved in the recruitment process. The intervention allocation sequence that was randomly generated was placed in numbered, sealed, and opaque envelopes. The lowest‐numbered envelope available was opened for the latest recruit. Participants were randomized to routine 8 or 4 h digital vaginal examination to assess labor progress at the first vaginal assessment after amniotomy. The subsequent vaginal examination schedule was decided by clinicians according to their standard practice.

Foley balloon labor induction care

In our center, the care protocol following the manual removal or expulsion of the Foley catheter was for a digital vaginal examination to be performed and participants with cervical dilatation ≥3 cm were then transferred to the labor ward for amniotomy and titrated intravenous oxytocin infusion. We rarely commenced oxytocin infusion with intact membranes. Our protocol called for oxytocin infusion to start at 2 mU/min, with the rate to be doubled every 30 min (to a maximum of 32 mU/min) until a contraction rate of four moderate–strong contractions every 10 min was attained, following which the infusion rate was maintained to delivery if there was no untoward event. If the Foley catheter was not spontaneously expelled, it was left in situ for 24 h before manual removal. Fetal heart rate and uterine activity were electronically monitored throughout the participants' labor. Maternal pulse and respiratory rate were monitored hourly. Monitoring of maternal temperature, bladder emptying, and urine analysis were performed every 4 h.

Expedited vaginal examinations would be performed as clinically indicated including the request for epidural analgesia, suspicion of second stage, application of fetal scalp electrode, or any concerning clinical development requiring vaginal examination findings for management. 15

In our center, patients in labor were primarily cared for by residents under the direct supervision of faculty. A faculty member was onsite at all times to cover the labor ward. A decision to undertake instrumental vaginal delivery would be sanctioned by at least a senior resident, and all cesarean deliveries were sanctioned by the onsite faculty member.

Participants and clinicians were not masked as the nature of the interventions was obvious.

Primary outcomes were amniotomy‐to‐delivery interval and maternal satisfaction on allotted care during labor (obtained within 24 h after delivery, using the 11‐point 0–10 visual numerical rating scale (VNRS), higher score representing greater satisfaction). Maternal secondary outcomes were the mode of delivery, cesarean section and its indication, operative vaginal delivery and its indication, duration of oxytocin augmentation in labor, the presence of fever, which is defined as a single temperature reading of 38°C or greater intrapartum or within one day of birth, analgesic and epidural use in labor, estimated delivery blood loss, uterine hyperactivity: (i) uterine tachysystole (six or more contractions in 10 min over two consecutive 10‐min periods), (ii) uterine hypertonus (sustained contraction 2 min or longer), (iii) hypertonus with fetal heart rate abnormality, assessment of cardiotocograph abnormality based on 2017 UK NICE guidelines for (i) suspicious or (ii) pathological pattern, 16 and duration of hospital stay from time of induction until discharge. Neonatal outcomes were Apgar score at 1 and 5 min, umbilical cord arterial blood pH and base excess, birthweight, rate and place of neonatal admission to either Neonatal Intensive Care Unit or Special Care Nursery, and indication for neonatal admission. Labor, delivery, and newborn data were retrieved from electronic hospital records after discharge.

Sample Size Calculations

Duration of labor

Levy et al.'s 17 randomized controlled trial of early vs late amniotomy following cervical ripening with Foley catheter shows that the interval of early amniotomy‐to‐delivery was 9.4 h (standard deviation 4.2 h).

We set a non‐inferiority limit of 2 h in duration of amniotomy to delivery. Based on previous data (standard deviation of 4.2 h 17 ), applying alpha of 0.025 (Bonferroni correction for two primary outcomes), 80% power, 1‐to‐1 randomization ratio, standard deviation of 4.2 h, and non‐inferiority margin of 2 h, 70 participants were needed in each arm (online calculator at https://www.sealedenvelope.com/power/binary‐noninferior/). Factoring in a 10% dropout rate, the number of participants required in each arm was 78 (a total of 156).

Maternal satisfaction score

For maternal satisfaction, we presumed a 1‐point difference in the 11‐point VNRS to be clinically relevant and assumed a standard deviation of 2 for the distribution of the satisfaction score. We applied alpha of 0.025, power of 80%, 1‐to‐1 ratio, standard deviation of 2, and delta of 1 and the number required in each arm was 77. 18 As the VNRS data are ordinal and Mann–Whitney U test analysis would be used instead, the sample size was uplifted by 15%, and taking a dropout rate of 10% into account, 99 in each arm was needed (total 198). We planned a total sample size of at least 198 participants as this higher number covered both of our primary outcomes.

Statistical analyses

Data were entered into statistical software package SPSS (Version 28, IBM, SPSS Statistics). The normal distribution of continuous data was assessed with the 1‐sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. The Student t‐test was used to analyze means of normally distributed continuous data, the Mann–Whitney U test for non‐normally distributed or ordinal data, and the Chi‐squared test for nominal data (Fisher exact test substituted if expected cell size <5 in 20% or more of the cells). Kaplan–Meier survival curves were generated for the analysis of amniotomy‐to‐delivery interval by routine vaginal examination at 8 compared to 4 h. Log rank test was used for statistical significance. Analysis was by intention to treat. Significance levels were taken at p < 0.025 for the primary outcomes and p < 0.05 for other analyses (2‐sided p value).

Major harms

There was no maternal sepsis or neonatal hypoxic–ischemic encephalopathy.

Post hoc analysis

Taking only the patients delivered vaginally, the amniotomy‐to‐vaginal intervals were 8.0 ± 3.2 (8 h arm, n = 62) vs 7.6 ± 3.3 (4 h arm n = 55) h p = 0.475, mean difference 0.4 (97.5% CI: −0.9 to 1.8 h), and also non‐inferior within the 2‐hour margin despite the smaller sample size. Maternal satisfaction scores were 8.5 ± 1.9 (8 h arm, n = 62) vs 8.3 ± 1.6 (4 h arm n = 55) p = 0.585, mean difference 0.2 (97.5% CI: −0.6 to 0.9). These findings were not materially different from the all‐delivery analyses. The Kaplan–Meier analyses of amniotomy to spontaneous vaginal delivery and all vaginal delivery intervals by first vaginal examination at 8 vs 4 h are shown in Figure 2A,B. The amniotomy to spontaneous vaginal delivery (censoring instrumental vaginal and cesarean delivery) and all vaginal delivery (censoring cesarean delivery) intervals were not statistically different between 8 vs 4 h arms (10.4 [95% CI: 9.5–11.2] vs 11.7 [95% CI: 10.7–12.7] hours, p = 0.136 and 10.1 [95% CI: 9.3–10.9] vs 10.5 [95% CI: 9.5–11.4] hours, p = 0.634 for spontaneous vaginal delivery and all vaginal delivery, respectively). The amniotomy‐to‐delivery intervals were shorter in the 8 h arm after right censoring, so the actual time to event, if were to occur may be longer than the observation time. 19 , 20

DISCUSSION

The principal findings of this trial are, firstly, amniotomy‐to‐delivery interval was within the pre‐specified 2 h non‐inferiority upper margin in the 8‐ compared to the 4‐hour first vaginal examination arm (97.5% CI: −0.72 to 1.47 h). Secondly, the maternal satisfaction with allocated labor care was not higher for the 8‐hour arm.

A 1996 study of 2‐hourly or 4‐hourly vaginal examinations to assess the progress of labor finds no difference in the total duration of labor. 21 A 2024 trial report from our center finds that a routine first vaginal examination at 8‐ compared to 4 h in multiparas is non‐inferior for time to delivery but maternal satisfaction is not improved. However, vaginal examinations are fewer and the epidural analgesia rate is lower in the 8‐hour arm. 22 In our trial of nulliparas, results were similar for time to delivery, maternal satisfaction, and fewer vaginal examinations but differed on epidural analgesia and spontaneous and instrumental vaginal rates across trial arms.

In our trial, the median difference of fewer by one in the number of vaginal examinations between 8‐ and 4‐hour arms although statistically significant was possibly not large enough to affect maternal satisfaction with allocated labor care. However, a trial from our center reports “despite a shorter induction to vaginal delivery interval with regular vaginal examination, women expressed a higher preference for the restricted examination schedule and were more likely to recommend such a schedule to a friend.” 23

We have found in the 8‐hour arm that the amniotomy to first vaginal examination interval was significantly longer and the first vaginal examination was less likely to be as scheduled and more likely to be indicated by the urge to bear down or a non‐reassuring cardiotocograph. These findings were anticipated with a planned extension to the first routine vaginal examination and similar to the finding of a trial that restricted vaginal examination at labor induction. 23 The additional time allowed for labor to progress to urge to bear down and for fetal heart rate decelerations to be detected at the first vaginal examination in our 8‐hour arm was plausibly due to head compression at the second stage. 24 The through‐to‐delivery cardiotocographic abnormality rate was not different across our trial arms.

The mode of delivery was significantly more likely to be spontaneous vaginal delivery and less likely to be by instrumental vaginal delivery with no significant difference in cesarean delivery for the 8‐hour arm. A recent large trial 25 reports that the application of new guidelines to delay cesarean section for labor dystocia does not result in a significant reduction in cesarean delivery but a small but potentially meaningful increase in the rate of spontaneous vaginal delivery is observed.

Maternal fever in our study was not different but the absolute difference and number of vaginal examinations in this trial were relatively low; five vaginal examinations or more during labor has been independently associated with intrapartum febrile morbidity. 8

After amniotomy following Foley ripening in nulliparas, a routine first vaginal examination at 8 compared to the widely recommended 4 h may be preferred as it can reduce the number of vaginal examinations, increase the spontaneous vaginal delivery rate, and reduce instrumental vaginal delivery without delaying delivery. This approach could reduce clinician resource needs in labor care. However, maternal satisfaction was not improved even though too frequent vaginal examinations are a common refrain arising from studies of vaginal examinations during labor. 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 , 30

The strength of this trial was the randomized controlled study design. The sample size was calculated based on a previous published data from a trial 17 and was achieved. We believe our findings are generalizable to other scenarios in labor after amniotomy and oxytocin infusion has commenced.

Limitations of this study were the inability to mask the interventions due to their obvious nature. A single center might reduce generalizability. For a significant number of participants in both trial arms, oxytocin infusion was not promptly started after amniotomy was performed. Participants who consented to this study were possibly patients who had no issues with vaginal examinations during labor and therefore more likely to agree to participate and this may influence the maternal satisfaction score to be similar in both groups. Lastly, despite a significantly higher spontaneous vaginal delivery and lower instrumental vaginal delivery rates in the 8‐hour arm, this was not an intended primary outcome and the significant findings might have occurred by chance as a result of the collection of multiple outcomes.

CONCLUSION

Routine first vaginal examination at 8 h compared to 4 h after amniotomy following Foley balloon ripening is non‐inferior for the time to delivery but does not increase maternal satisfaction although the number of vaginal examinations performed is fewer. The increase in spontaneous vaginal delivery and reduction in instrumental vaginal delivery rates warrant further powered primary evaluation.

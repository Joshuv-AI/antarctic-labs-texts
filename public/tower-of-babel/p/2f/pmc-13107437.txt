A DNA Origami-Based Cobweb Facilitates Precise and Receptor-Interference Free Modification of Extracellular Vesicle Mimetic for Enhanced Delivery Efficacy
Authors: Shijun Cai, Rui Xu, Yuan Tian, Anqi Song, Nannan Diao, Haowen Li, Linlin Yang, Yumeng Zhao, Xuemei Xu, Qianqian Gao, Chao Zhang, Yanyan Miao, Mengyao Zhao, Qian Tang, Da Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The approach of tailoring ligands on the surface of extracellular vesicle (EV)-based drugs has been pivotal in achieving an effective EV-based therapeutic delivery. However, indiscriminate modifications to the membranes can diminish efficacy due to uncontrollable binding affinities and disruptions in the EV membrane functionality, often caused by disordered ligand coverage. In this study, we present an approach for affinity-tunable and receptor-interference-free extracellular vesicle mimetic (EVM) functionalization. We employ a soft wireframe DNA origami-based “cobweb” to achieve a customized spatial distribution of targeting ligands on the EVM surface, allowing for precise control over the cellular affinity and subsequent EVM uptake. Moreover, by utilizing the hollow structure of the DNA origami cobweb, we are able to program EVM without compromising the native functions of their membrane proteins such as CD47-mediated immune evasion. We believe that our strategy will provide a versatile platform for the targeted delivery of EVM-based drugs with high efficiency.

Introduction

Extracellular vesicles (EVs) play an important role in substance exchange and information communication between cells across all kingdoms of life, and have been recognized as one of the most prospective delivery vesicles benefiting from their exceptional biocompatibility, minimal inherent toxicity, , good blood-brain barrier permeability − and significant biodegradability. However, the therapeutic potential of EVs is limited by their insufficient cellular uptake and nonspecific interaction with nontarget cells, which precipitate various side effects.

Significant efforts have been directed toward enhancing the specificity and efficiency of EV-based delivery systems. This is typically accomplished by incorporating targeting ligands onto the EV surface, which can specifically home on certain cells or tissues. In detail, the surface modification of EVs mainly relies on genetic engineering and chemical conjugation. The former enables the expression of tumor-specific antigens or immune checkpoint inhibitors on the EV surface, thereby modulating immune responses. However, these genetic approaches requires complex genetic engineering processes and face the limitations in achieving precise ligand distribution. ,, Chemical conjugation can directly anchor targeting ligands or specific molecules, such as antibodies and peptides, to the surface of EVs by chemical reactions. However, ligands that are covalently attached to EVs by interacting with the carboxyl, amine or sulfhydryl groups of membrane proteins − may impact the functions of these proteins due to unspecific conjugation, and lead the inactivation of downstream signaling pathway. A lipid-based insertion method emerged by a noncovalent attachment of cholesterol-labeled ligands to the phospholipid bilayer of the EV membrane without chemically impacting the functional proteins. − Nonetheless, ligands labeled with hydrophobic groups tend to aggregate in lipid-ordered regions on the membranes, − resulting in uneven ligand distribution. This can lead to steric hindrance and overcrowding effects, which may impair the overall functionality of the EVs. Consequently, merely increasing the ligand density is not enough to substantially improve the delivery efficiency of EVs. It is widely recognized that the spatial presentation of ligands on nanoparticles significantly influences the binding efficiency of multivalent ligand–receptor interactions. − Moreover, the functionality derived from native membrane receptors present on the surface of EVs is also a critical factor to consider for EV-based drug delivery. Therefore, a promising strategy to enhance the efficiency of EV-based delivery is to regulate the spatial distribution of ligands, while maintaining the functionality of the membrane proteins on EVs.

DNA origami technology enables precise arrangement of molecules at nanoscale. , Topologically arranging the ligands on DNA origami has been successfully applied to increase the affinity between multivalent ligands and receptors, further activate downstream pathways and improve the therapeutic efficacy for diseases. − Besides, interfacing DNA origami with lipid-bilayer membrane has facilitated many precise manipulations including membrane deformation, − lipid bilayer self-assembly, regulation of liposome growth, and transmembrane DNA nanopores. , However, these DNA origami-based membrane manipulations remain on model membrane, and most of the DNA nanostructure-based membrane engineering rarely utilize the complicated physiological properties of biomembranes.

In this study, we introduce a method for functionalization of the extracellular vesicle mimetic (EVM) by enveloping EVM with a wireframe DNA origami that has been modified with ligands at precise locations. This approach allows for the uniform decoration of EVM with ligands in a predefined spatial distribution. Moreover, the hollow structure of the wireframe DNA origami ensures that the accessibility of native membrane proteins on EVM is maintained, which contributes to the enhancement of the delivery efficiency of EVM, ensuring that both the targeted binding and the natural biological functions of the EVM are optimized. We applied the DNA origami-functionalized EVM to targeted delivery of RNA drug to prostate cancer model and demonstrated the effective delivery efficiency and inhibition of tumor cell proliferation.

Results and Discussion

Design and Characterization of DNA Cobweb Covered EVM (CCV)

EVMs derived from red blood cells (RBC-EVMs) are widely used owing to their distinct advantages, including immune evasion, prolonged circulation time, improved in vitro storage stability, and cost-effective production. − Accordingly, we utilized size exclusion chromatography-purified RBC-EVMs as model drug carriers (Figure S1). We designed a plane pentagon-star-like wireframe DNA origami (referred to as DNA cobweb) as a scaffold to precisely arrange ligands on the EVM surface (Figure a). The monolayer configuration and four unpaired nucleotide bases on each vertex ensured the structural flexibility of DNA cobweb. Additionally, the cobweb has a radius of 96 nm, designed to fully cover a 100 nm EV at a ratio of 1:1 or 1:2. Forty-five cholesterol-labeled ssDNA were annealed uniformly on the back side of the cobweb for stably anchoring at the phospholipid bilayer of EVM (Figure S2), and the targeting ligands conjugated with ssDNA were labeled on the front side of the cobweb by strand-complementarity with a designed arrangement. Successful assembly of cobweb was proved by an atomic force microscope (AFM) (Figure b, Figures S3 and S4) and agarose gel electrophoresis (AGE) (Figure S5).

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) imaging and nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) characterization validated that the extruded EVM had a uniform diameter of about 100 nm (Figure c and Figure S6). The successful anchoring of DNA cobweb on EVM was validated by dynamic light scattering (DLS). As shown in Figure d,e, compared to the bare EVM, the CCV showed an increased diameter, from 119 to 132.2 nm. The decreased zeta potential (from −10.41 mV to −12.63 mV) also demonstrated the covering of negatively charged DNA cobweb on the EVM.

Owing to the low contrast of the single-layer wireframe structure of cobweb, the covering of DNA cobweb on EVM is challenging to directly discern in TEM characterization. Thus, we modified the EVM with DNA cobweb that was labeled with a single 10 nm-gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) to assist the TEM imaging (Figure f). The thiolated DNA-labeled AuNPs is anchored to the center of DNA cobweb through the strand-complementarity with the two AuNP-staples (Figure S7). As shown in Figure g, when EVM was incubated with either bare AuNPs (Figure S8) or a AuNP-labeled DNA cobweb that lacked cholesterol-ssDNA, minimal colocalization of EVM and AuNPs was observed. For EVM incubated with cholesterol ssDNA-labeled AuNPs, multiple AuNPs were observed on individual EVM. Notably, when EVM were incubated with cobwebs that were both AuNPs and cholesterol-ssDNA labeled, approximately 65% of the EVM exhibited colocalization with a single AuNPs (as shown in Figure h and Figure S9). We also observed that improving the ratio of DNA cobweb to EVM from 1:1 to 3:1 resulted in increased quantities of AuNPs per EVM (Figure S10), which may result from the covering of more than one DNA cobweb. These results visualized the modification of DNA cobweb on EVM and also confirmed that most of the EVM conjugates with DNA cobweb at a ratio of 1:1.

Precise Ligand Modification by DNA Cobweb

We commenced our study by examining the relationship between the spatial arrangement of ligands and the efficiency of EVM delivery. Herein, biotin was used as a model ligand to evaluate the cellular uptake of CCVs by the streptavidin-overexpressed 293T cell line (293T-SA, Figure S11). The DNA cobweb featured with five vertices, each of which composed of two consecutive hexagons. On each hexagon, four pairs of biotin-anchoring sites were meticulously designed and sequentially numbered from 1 to 4 (as shown in Figure a, left). This configuration resulted in a total of eight pairs of anchoring sites, or 16 individual sites, symmetrically arrayed across each vertex of the DNA cobweb (as depicted in Figure a, right). The intersite distances span from 7 to 39 nm. Collectively, this design provided 80 anchoring sites per DNA cobweb for biotin attachment (Figure b). Next, we prepared 11 varieties of cobweb, each of which has a distinct combination of biotin valence and spacing, for functionalization of EVM. We named each version of DNA cobweb according to the pattern of biotin it bore; for example, “B1” denoted the DNA cobweb with biotin at site 1, and “B123” indicated the DNA cobweb with biotin at site 1, 2, and 3 (Figure c). As shown in Figure d and Figure S12, when DNA cobweb was functionalized with increasing numbers of biotin-labeled ssDNA: 20 (B1), 40 (B12, B34), 60 (B123), and 80 (B1234), the band of DNA cobweb showed a decreased migration rate but increased intensity. These results indicated a progressive enhancement in biotin valence. There was no significant change in band migration for B12 and B34, indicating the variations in spatial distributions did not impact the dispersion of DNA cobweb.

Subsequently, we investigated the cellular internalization of the CCVs functionalized by the DNA cobwebs mentioned above. Before the covering of DNA cobweb, the EVM were prestained with PKH26 for further flow cytometric analysis and confocal microscopy imaging. As shown in Figure e, the intracellular fluorescence intensity did not show a simply linear enhancement with the increasing biotin valence; interestingly, CCVs containing B1 always exhibited a higher fluorescence intensity among all the other CCVs with same biotin valence. The results of the apparent binding affinity (K d) further demonstrated the greater cellular affinity of B1-CCV compared with B2-CCV (Figure f). These findings indicated that the spatial distribution of ligands matters more than their valence on the cellular affinity of CCV. We hypothesize that the biotin pattern of B1-CCV may have the best match to the cluster of streptavidin on cell membrane, resulting in a higher cellular affinity. Considering B1234 displayed the highest cellular internalization, it was used in further experiments.

DNA Cobweb-Based Surface Engineering of EVM Preserved the Full Functionality of Membrane Proteins

Native membrane proteins are crucial for maintaining physiological functions of EVs. For example, SNARE proteins mediate the fusion of EV to target cells by forming a tetrameric complex, − and Rab protein family regulates the transport and localization of EVs. , Studies have shown that lipid-only vesicles derived from EV membranes show limited fusion with cells, , suggesting that membrane proteins are essential for EV biological functions. As for the RBC-EVMs, its high-expression of CD47, which is essential in immune escape, − may play an important role in immunomodulation. In addition, C8 binding protein (C8bp), homologous restriction protein (HRP), decay accelerating factor (DAF), complement receptor1 (CR1) and CD58 on RBC membrane also play a role in the defense of complement system attacks, ,− which enable suppression of immune response by disguising the drug-delivering EVMs as “self” to achieve long-term systemic circulation. Hence, the preservation of the native state of membrane proteins can significantly influence the overall efficiency of EVM as a drug delivery vesicle.

We then investigated whether the modification of DNA cobweb affected the accessibility of native membrane proteins on the EVM surface. As CD58 and CD47 are abundantly present on the surface of RBC-EVMs (Figures S13 and S14), we investigated the accessibility of CD58 and CD47 on CCV by assessing their binding capacity to anti-CD58 and anti-CD47. We modified EVM with abundant cholesterol-ssDNA via simple lipid insertion, known as spherical nucleic acids (SNAs) and a dense rectangular origami (Figure S15) as control. After incubating the CCV and SNA with APC-labeled anti-CD58, they were enriched by Dynabeads to facilitate confocal microscopy imaging and flow cytometric analysis. Confocal microscopy imaging (Figure a) revealed that CCV displayed a similar strong fluorescence as the positive control (EVM + anti-CD58) did, while the SNA and negative control (CCV + IgG) showed faint fluorescence, suggesting good binding of CCV and anti-CD58. The faint fluorescence observed in the SNA group may attribute to steric hindrance caused by disordered dense modification of ssDNA, which impeded antigen–antibody interaction. Similar trends were observed in the flow cytometry with APC-labeled anti-CD47 and anti-CD58 (Figure b–e). These results indicated that DNA cobweb can functionalize EVM with specific ligand distribution while maintaining the accessibility and binding ability of membrane proteins on the EVM surface.

Achieving precise and receptor-interference free modification of EVM may have a synergistic effect on improving delivery efficiency. For the RBC-EVMs, the highly expressed CD47 may enable them to evade from the immune system by binding to SIRPα to release a “don’t eat me” signal, which may ensure an extended circulating period. Conversely, blocking CD47 with anti-CD47 may lead to the digestion of EVM by macrophages, resulting in a failure in cargo delivery (Figure f). We then validated the immune escape ability of CCV by incubating it with the phorbol 12-myristate 13-acetate (PMA) activated THP-1 macrophage cells; herein, SNA, bare EVM and CD47-blocked CCV were set as control groups (Figure S16). As shown in Figure g,h, both bare EVM and CCV treated group exhibited weak PKH26 intensity in cytoplasm, indicating their evasion from THP-1. In contrast, strong PKH26 intensity was observed in the SNA group and the CD47-blocked CCV group, indicating their high level of endocytosis in THP-1. The native physiological function of CD47, ensured by the hollow structure of DNA cobweb, may mediate the immune evasion of CCV and will play a crucial role in EVM delivery efficiency.

CCV for Nucleic Acid Drug Delivery

We then investigated the effect of DNA cobweb on the improvement of cellular delivery efficiency of the EVM, as shown in Figure a, and the bare EVM and two SNA groups were set as control: one modified with 80-fold excess biotin per EVM (SNA-80, with the same ligand number as CCV) and the other with 1000-fold excess biotin per EVM (SNA-1000, represent a high ligand number situation). The CCV, SNA and bare EVM were prestained with PKH26 and added to 293T-SA cells at a concentration of 2 × 105 particles/per cell to analyze their delivery efficiency. As shown in Figure b,c and Figure S17, compared to bare EVM, the CCV showed a faster cellular uptake, and resulted in a 3.7-fold PKH26 signal in 12 h (Figures S18 and S19). Both of the SNA groups exhibited diminished improvement in cellular uptake compared to the CCV group. The improved delivery efficiency of CCV was also validated by confocal microcopy imaging (Figure d and Figure S20), which was consistent with results of flow cytometry. The reduced effectiveness of SNA may contribute to the ligand aggregation in lipid-ordered regions, resulting in uneven ligand distribution and reduced effective binding of the ligands. Besides, the steric hindrance caused by ligand aggregation may also impact the accessibility of endocytosis-related proteins, further leading to the decreased endocytosis efficiency. We also noticed that CCV showed negligible cellular uptake in 293T cells, indicating the targeting ability of DNA cobweb-based surface engineering. In addition to the biotin-mediated CCV delivery to 293T-SA cells, we also test the CEM-cell-targeted CCV delivery using Sgc8 aptamer-labeled DNA cobweb, and Hela- and LNCaP-cell-targeted delivery using TfR aptamer-labeled DNA cobweb (Figure S21). While the TfR aptamer-labeled DNA cobweb exhibited a superior delivery efficiency, the Sgc8 aptamer-labeled DNA cobweb showed relatively reduced cellular uptake. This may attribute to the relatively lower abundance of PTK7 receptors on CEM cells (Figure S22). Following that we investigated the nucleic acid drug delivery ability with this well-defined CCV system. We loaded CCV with antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) mimic through an extrusion method (Figure S23) and quantified the cell lysate using qPCR to evaluate the delivery efficiency. The results (Figure S24) proved that CCV exhibited the highest delivery efficiency of the ASO mimic. After 3 h incubation, the quantity of ASO mimic delivered to cells by CCV was 8.8-fold higher than SNA-80, 10.6-fold higher than SNA-1000, and 15.5-fold higher than bare EVM.

The androgen receptor (AR) regulated gene expression related to male development and reproductive function, and using AR siRNA to form a RISC complex that binds to its mRNA to knockdown AR expression in prostate cancer can hinder tumor growth by disrupting pathways that support cancer cell survival and proliferation. To investigate the outcomes of CCV-based RNA drug delivery, we loaded CCV with AR siRNA and assessed the knockdown of AR in the LNCaP cells. The CCV loaded AR siRNA with an encapsulation efficiency of 64%, which was assessed by using Cy5-labeled AR siRNA. CCV was functionalized with TfR aptamer to enable targeted delivery to LNCaP cells, and the SNA groups were set as comparison (Figure e). LNCaP cells were treated with CCV or SNA for 24 h (Figures S25 and S26). The qPCR results revealed that compared with the untreated group, CCV-based AR siRNA delivery induced a 90% reduction of AR mRNA level and showed a 3.7-, 2.6-, 1.8- and 1.4-fold downregulation compared with bare EVM, EVMc, SNA-80 and SNA-1000, respectively (Figure f). As the Western blotting analysis showed in Figure g, the AR protein level of LNCaP cells was significantly reduced after being treated with the CCV, while the knockdown of AR in SNA-80 and SNA-1000 groups was not pronounced. This trend was also obvious in TMPRSS2 expression level, whose expression was regulated by the AR. Consistently, CCV reduced cell viability by 54.1% after incubating with LNCaP cells for 48 h (Figure h), showing higher RNA interference-induced toxicity compared to SNA-80 and SNA-1000.

Following the in vitro studies, we evaluated the in vivo performance of TfR aptamer-labeled CCV in a subcutaneous LNCaP prostate cancer model. IVIS imaging showed that DiR-labeled CCVs selectively accumulated in tumors 24 h after tail-vein injection, whereas only weak tumor signals were observed in the SNA group, and no tumor accumulation was detected for EVM or EVMc, confirming the essential role of the TfR aptamer in tumor targeting (Figure a and Figure S27). Ex vivo imaging of excised organs further validated these results, revealing strong fluorescence signals in tumors exclusively in the CCV group (Figure b). Encouraged by this efficient tumor targeting, we assessed the therapeutic efficacy of AR siRNA-loaded CCVs in vivo. LNCaP tumor-bearing mice were intravenously treated every 3 days for 21 days (Figure c). No significant body weight changes or abnormal serum levels of liver enzymes and inflammatory cytokines were observed across all groups, indicating low immunogenicity (Figure d and Figure S28) and good in vivo safety (Figures S29 and S30). At the end of the treatment, CCV-treated mice exhibited the most pronounced tumor growth inhibition, with tumor weights reduced by approximately 6.7-fold compared with those of the PBS group, substantially outperforming SNA, EVMc, and EVM treatments (Figure e–g). Western blot analysis of tumor tissues revealed a marked downregulation of AR and its downstream target TMPRSS2 in the CCV group (Figure h), confirming effective gene silencing in vivo. Together, these results demonstrate that CCV-mediated AR siRNA delivery efficiently suppresses prostate tumor growth through highly specific and enhanced tumor targeting.

Conclusions

In this work, we introduced a reliable method for the surface engineering of EVM by using DNA origami technology to precisely control the spatial distribution of ligands on EVM. We successfully enhanced the delivery efficiency by tuning the valency and arrangement of the ligands for targeting and highlighted the importance of spatial distribution of ligands in regulating delivery efficiency. Besides, we demonstrated that DNA cobweb-based surface engineering had little interference on the native function of membrane proteins, which will be a promising strategy to achieve the enhancement of targeting efficiency of EVM without compromising their excellent biocompatibility, immune escape property, and other related functions. Benefiting from the two advantages above, CCV’s ability of nucleic acid drug delivery has been significantly improved compared with traditional lipid insertion methods. Thus, we believe CCV can be applied in applications of genetic therapy owing to their low immunogenicity, enhanced cellular uptake, and reduced interaction with nontarget cells. Importantly, in vivo biodistribution further demonstrated that CCVs enable efficient tumor targeting and effective suppression of prostate tumor growth through AR siRNA delivery while maintaining favorable biosafety and low immunogenicity. These results highlight the translational potential of DNA cobweb–engineered EVM for targeted nucleic acid therapeutics.

As a versatile tool, the DNA cobweb is compatible with both oligo-conjugated ligands or biotin-fused ligands. It is known that many cell receptors are activated through the formation of nanoclusters that trigger the downstream signaling pathways including endocytosis. ,, We envision that DNA cobwebs are highly compatible with many vesicle-based delivery systems that significantly rely on the spatial distribution of ligands, achieving accurate drug-targeting and delivery to pathological tissues. In addition, while SNA emerged as a promising platform for vaccine development, , we believe that optimizing this SNA-based vaccine platform with desired signaling profile with the help of DNA cobweb will have unbelievable potential in the future.

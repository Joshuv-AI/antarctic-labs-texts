Modelling pressure dynamics of oil–gas two-phase flow in double-porosity media formation with permeability-stress sensitivity
Authors: Chunyu Zhang, Shijia Chen, Songbai Zhu, Xue Yan, Hui Wang, Yanli Wang, Jing-Shun Li, Ren-Shi Nie
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Pressure dynamics can reflect the basic characteristics of fluid flow through underground porous formation. In this research, the oil–gas two-phase flow model for a double-porosity media formation with permeability-stress sensitivity was first established for three kinds of outer boundaries. The unified governing equation was deduced by utilizing the H function. The nonlinear mathematical model in consideration of permeability-stress sensitivity was linearized by implementing the canonical perturbation transformation and then solved by using the Laplace transformation. After that, a sequence of typical log–log curves of pressure dynamics influenced by various model parameters were plotted and analyzed. These curves reflect the typical characteristic of a V shape caused by the inter-porosity fluid flow from matrix toward natural fractures. Oil saturation and permeability-stress sensitivity coefficient have much influence on the pressure dynamics. Ultimately, the established model of oil–gas two-phase flow was validated through a well-test fitting interpretation for a real condensate gas well in a sandstone formation. This research can offer insights into the pressure dynamics dominated by the oil–gas two-phase flow in naturally fractured formations and the permeability-stress sensitivity effect.

Introduction

Naturally fractured formation is complex and its structure consists of both natural fractures and matrix. Being different from the single porosity system of homogenous formations, naturally fractured formations are the dual porosity system, which includes the fracture subsystem and the matrix subsystem. The basic properties of the two subsystems are different: the pressures in the two subsystems are relatively independent; the pore structure of fracture is completely different from that of matrix; and the permeability of fracture is far larger than that of matrix, but the porosity of fracture is less than that of matrix. There exit fluid exchanges between the two subsystems. In fractured formations, the matrix acts as the primary storage space, while fracture serves as the main flow channel. The deformation of natural fractures is responsive to the changes of formation pressure, leading to alterations in fracture permeability, which is a phenomenon known as the permeability-stress sensitivity (PSS) effect. In fractured formations, the pressure dynamics of oil–gas two-phase (OGTP) flow differs from that of single-phase flow. Modelling pressure dynamics of OGTP flow in double-porosity media formation with PSS is meaningful and interesting for petroleum engineers, geoscientists, fluid mechanics researchers, etc.

Currently, there are various researches on modelling the flow behavior in fractured formations. In the early ages, Warren and Root1 assumed an idealized conceptual fracture model where regular cube matrix blocks were cut by the network of natural fractures and proposed that elastic storage ratio and inter-porosity fluid flow factor to characterize flow behavior of a double porosity media. Odeh2 presented a simplified model for describing the transient behavior of naturally fractured formations. In a similar vein, Kazemi3 studied a fractured formation model considering a group of matrix slabs cut by horizontal fractures. De Swaan4 proposed an unsteady-state flow model for naturally fractured formations by assuming spherical matrix blocks cut by high-permeability fractures. Lai et al.5 presented a dual-porosity well test model, which assumed that fluid flowed from the fractures to the wellbore and from the rock matrix to the fractures. Jelmert6 introduced a linear flow model for double-porosity media. Bui et al.7 presented a fractured formation model for a well with partial penetrations. These models were used to create pressure dynamics curves, which show a transition shape characteristic of flow between fracture and matrix. During the recent years, Ozkan et al.8 investigated an analytical flow model for fractured shale gas formations. Nie et al.9 proposed a double-permeability flow model where both fracture and matrix serve as the pathways directly linked to the bottom hole. Omosebi and Igbokoyi10 illustrated a power-law fluid flow model for fracture-matrix double-porosity formation. Dejam et al.11 established a semi-analytical double-porosity model for a vertical well with a hydraulic fracture. Additionally, some researchers investigated stress-sensitive double-porosity formations. Tao et al.12 put up with a radial flow model for fractured reservoirs with a consideration of stress sensitivity. Zhang et al.13 developed a stress-sensitivity double-porosity model for a radial formation with non-equal thickness. They found that stress sensitivity impacted the pressure behavior positively, and this impact became especially apparent in the late radial flow period. Zhang et al.14 studied transient pressures using a fractured well model for a stress-sensitive formation. Wang et al.15 also conducted the similar work to Zhang et al.14. Furthermore, in the realm of two-phase flow studies, limited researches have been conducted on modelling the behavior of OGTP flow. Raghavan16 introduced a pseudo-pressure variable for deducing the fundamental differential equation of OGTP flow through underground formations. Blasingame17 proposed a new method to correct pressure data from well tests and applied it in modelling the multi-phase flow behavior. Roadifer18 modeled the dynamic pressures of wells completed in OGTP formations with the constant pressure boundary condition. Wang et al.19 put forth a dynamic pressure model for two-phase flow through naturally fractured carbonate formations.

Up to now, there has not been a model available that can be applied for simulating the pressure dynamics of OGTP flow in double-porosity media formation with PSS. The existing flow models of double system only considered either the OGTP flow conditions or the PSS effect. Therefore, our research objective is to establish a new model that can simultaneously consider the OGTP flow conditions and the PSS effect. And another main research objective is to analyze the pressure dynamics of OGTP flow dominated by PSS for naturally fractured formations. The rest structures of this article are: "Physical model and assumptions" will put forward the physical model and the corresponding assumptions; "Mathematical model and solution" will establish the mathematical model of OGTP flow through double-porosity media and deduce the analytical formulas of transient pressure in the Laplace space; "Pressure dynamics" will exhibit a sequence of pressure dynamics curves influenced by the coefficient of PSS and analyze the typical curve characteristics; "Well-test interpretation" perform a well-test interpretation for a real pressure-testing well to verify the established model; and "Conclusions" will draw the research conclusions. The established new model can be a good tool to theoretically study the pressure dynamics and perform well-test interpretations. This research also suffers some limitations: three-phase flow condition of oil, gas and water was not considered in the model; flow rate dynamics of well production was not studied; and pressure dynamics caused by horizontal well production was not concerned. These limitations hope to be addressed in future research.

Physical model and assumptions

A single well is assumed to be drilled in a naturally fractured formation, which is saturated by movable oil and free gas, as illustrated in Fig. 1a. Oil and gas are extracted at a constant flow rate from the formation by the well production. Fluids are considered as being synchronous, isothermal and radial flow in the formation. The flow route is deemed as being “matrix → fracture → wellbore”, as demonstrated in Fig. 1b. The flow of oil and gas abides by the Darcy’s law. PSS is assumed to being accompanied by the flow process of fluids. The external boundary is considered as being infinite, closed or constant pressure. These three kinds of external boundary conditions are the common boundary conditions. Real hydrocarbon formation may be bounded and closed for lithology pinching out or fault blocking and may have a constant pressure at the boundary owing to the active edge-water drive in the periphery of hydrocarbon formation. Although real hydrocarbon formations cannot be infinite, the imaginary infinite reservoir can be convenient for real applications. For example, when the well-test time is short, the propagation of pressure wave stirred by well production has not reached to the formation boundary in a limited time, and in this situation, we can choose the infinite boundary condition to perform well-test interpretations.Fig. 1Conceptual diagram of OGTP flow in a naturally fractured formation.

Pressure dynamics

Pressure dynamics of well production in a double-porosity media formation can be demonstrated using a group of standard log–log curves of dimensionless pseudo-pressure (mwD) and its derivative (m′wD·tD/CD).

Figure 2 displays the typical log–log curves of pressure dynamics under three kinds of outer boundaries. These curves were generated using a set of model parameters (S = 0.5, CD = 1, λ = 10–4, ωf = 0.01, γD = 0, So = 0.8, and reD = 800). As depicted in Fig. 2, in the early pure wellbore storage period, the early curves of mwD and m′wD·tD/CD are overlapped. The derivative curve takes on a V shape, which reflects the inter-porosity fluid flow from matrix toward natural fractures for a double-porosity media formation. The late curve of m′wD·tD/CD takes on a horizontal straight line (see curve ②), which is the typical reflection of infinite acting radial flow for an infinite formation. In contrast, the late derivative curve exhibits an upward line (see curve ①) for a closed formation and an downward line (see curve ③) for a constant-pressure outer boundary. These curves simulated when γD = 0 mean that the pressure dynamics are the dynamics of the double-porosity media formation without PSS.Fig. 2Impact curves of different outer boundary conditions.

Impact curves of different outer boundary conditions.

Pressure dynamics curves are sensitive to changing parameter values. In the following, So, γD, ωf and CD are particularly chosen to analyze the sensitivities of pressure dynamics because they are the most important property parameters for the model. So, γD and ωf represent the OGTP flow condition, the PSS effect and the development status of natural fractures, respectively. A larger So means a relatively larger amount of oil in the formation, a larger γD signifies relatively stronger stress sensitivity, a larger ωf implies better development of natural fractures, and a larger CD means a longer duration of well-bore storage effect.

The dynamics curves controlled by different oil saturations for an infinite formation were also simulated by setting So as 0, 0.5 and 1, respectively, and a set of the other model parameters (S = 0.5, CD = 1, λ = 10–5, ωf = 0.01, and γD = 0), as illustrated in Fig. 3. Compared with the mwD curve of So = 0 (see curve ①), the mwD curves of So = 0.5 and So = 1 have higher curve positions. The pseudo-pressure curve position of So = 1 is higher than that of So = 0.5. A larger So leads to a higher pseudo-pressure curve position. The dynamics curves of So = 0 and So = 1 imply single-phase flows of gas and oil, respectively. For a larger So, the total flow resistance of OGTP flow becomes larger, so the pressure loss for the same production rate becomes larger, and the mwD curve position goes higher. Being different from the mwD curve, the curve of m′wD·tD/CD is impacted by So before the inter-porosity fluid flow from matrix toward fractures. Figure 4 shows the characteristics of dynamics curves dominated by different So under different outer boundary conditions. In the late response period of outer boundary, the influences of So on the dynamics curves for the closed and constant-pressure boundaries are different from those for the infinite formation. For the same boundary distance (reD = 800), a smaller So signifies the supply capacity of formation fluids to wellbore, so the depletion of formation pressure is slower and the onset time of the response of outer boundary is postponed, which results in the dynamics curves of boundary response move to the right (see curves ① − ③ and ⑤ − ⑦ in Fig. 4). In sum, oil saturation of OGTP flow has much influence on pressure dynamics.Fig. 3Impact curves of oil phase saturation.Fig. 4Impact curves of oil phase saturation under different outer boundaries.

Well-test interpretation

There is a naturally fractured sandstone formation in Songliao Basin, China. This formation is a condensate gas formation with a depth of 3154.1 m, a temperature of 111.72 °C, a primary pressure of 31.53 MPa and an upper dew-point pressure of 16.93 MPa. So far, this formation has been put into exploitation for 12 years. At present, the formation pressure is 13.26 MPa, which is smaller than the dew-point pressure; therefore, there is an OGTP flow in the formation. There is a pressure-tested well with a testing time of 192 h in the formation. The basic formation of this well is as follows: the buried depth of pay layer ranges from 3140.8 to 3167.4 m, the layer thickness is 26.6 m and the layer porosity is 0.086; the wellbore radius is 0.07 m; the production rate of gas and oil are 1.76 × 104 m3/day and 4.28 m3/day, respectively; the gas viscosity is 0.017 mPa s, the gas compression coefficient is 0.097 MPa−1 and the gas volume factor is 0.004, the condensate oil viscosity is 0.32 mPa s, the oil compression coefficient is 0.0055 MPa−1, the matrix porosity is 0.073, the fracture porosity is 0.007, the matrix compression coefficient is 0.0013 MPa−1, the fracture compression coefficient is 0.08 MPa−1, the gas volume factor is 1.64. We performed a well-test interpretation and the well-test fitting curves are shown in Fig. 9. The tested data were fitted using the established OGTP flow model in this paper. The middle derivative curve of the tested data takes on a V shape, which reflects the inter-porosity fluid flow from matrix toward fractures; and the late derivative curve of the tested data is not horizontal, which is the reflection of PSS effect. Through this well-test fitting to the tested data, we obtained the interpretation result parameters: the elastic storativity ratio is 0.17, the inter-porosity fluid flow factor is 2.6 × 10–5, the storage coefficient in wellbore is 2.54 m3/MPa, the skin factor is 0.04, the formation permeability is 4.45 × 10–3 μm2, the oil saturation in the formation is 0.36, and the dimensional stress sensitivity coefficient is 0.0006 MPa−1 and the corresponding dimensionless stress sensitivity coefficient is 0.032. Because the theoretical curve in the early wellbore storage stage is a straight line and the early tested data points are not strictly arranged as a straight line, it is very hard to be completely fitted with the theoretical curve. Overall, the fitting effect is satisfactory except for the early time data point. The satisfactory fitting effect between the tested and theoretical data shows the applicability of the established OGTP flow model in this article. In Fig. 9, the dashed lines are the theoretical curves of the model without considering the influence of stress sensitive effect (γD = 0). Obviously, the dashed lines did not be matched with the tested data. Therefore, the established OGTP flow model has been further validated.Fig. 9Well-test fitting curves of the example well.

Well-test fitting curves of the example well.

Better, balanced adaptation, resilience and solidarity
Authors: Marcalee Alexander
Published: 2025

The world is constantly changing. Yet, shades of history are recurring. The Qin dynasty is known for burning books and burying scholars, and at present scholars are being dismissed and public health advances are being dismantled [[1], [2], [3]].

There are still important actions we can and must take as individuals and communities to make a difference. I personally pitched The Journal of Climate Change and Health to Elsevier in Spring 2020. Subsequently, with the commitment of a dedicated team of volunteer professionals, the journal has grown significantly in the number, quality and diversity of papers submitted. At the time of this editorial, 292 original articles were submitted in 2025. Special collections exist on Communications, Collaboration, Ethics, Health Inequity, Medical Specialties, Asia, Africa, SIDS, the Caribbean and Central & South America and Advancing and Integrating Health into Policy. The Journal’s first cite score is 7.9 and impact factor is 3.6 and it is indexed in the Emerging Sources Citation Index, Scopus and the Directory Open Access Journals. However, after almost a year, we are still awaiting a determination on our application for citation in PubMed.

Developing a journal is a team effort. The Journal of Climate Change and Health could not succeed without our wonderful editorial board, reviewers, authors and staff. Behind the scenes, Pascal Leger, Brad Stucky and Sterling Alexander have facilitated the journal’s growth. Still, diversity in style and structure benefits all academic efforts. I have personally completed my five-year term as Editor-in-Chief in November, and I am happy to announce I have handed the reigns over to Ann Christine Duhaime, the current Deputy Editor-in-Chief who is Acting Editor-in-Chief on December 1, 2025. As I step down and become Emeritus Editor-in-Chief, I am confident the journal is in good hands and there will continue to be many scientific discoveries published and opportunities for those of you who want to be involved.

I am excited to announce that as I turn over my journal responsibilities, I will focus my energies as Inaugural President of the Climate Health Society, a global, interdisciplinary grassroots professional society focused on education, research and collaborative opportunities related to climate change and health for people from diverse professional backgrounds.

The Journal of Climate Change and Health is the official journal of the Climate Health Society. CHS aims to facilitate communication across disciplines, between those from Lower and Middle Income (LMI) and the most developed countries, as well as from the youngest to the oldest professionals. Our goal is to allow individual members to be actively involved and connected with each other. Membership is free for students and trainees and those from LMI countries. Regular educational content and opportunities to showcase members work is available for free and members receive a 30 % discount on APC for papers. We will also host our first international hybrid meeting prior to COP in November 2026 and anticipate seeking abstracts in January.

CCLEAR was previously offered as a path forward regarding climate change: Communicators, Climate Leaders, Educators, Advocates and Researchers [4]. Moving forward in these difficult times, I have an alternative suggestion: Better, Balanced, Adaptation Resilience and Solidarity (BARS).

Better is something we must always strive for. From Continuous Quality Improvement to Six Sigma, we must always work to improve our efforts and processes [5,6]. Technology is considered a way to make things better, but it must be bridled with balance [7]. AI is upon us and is overtaking the media and our lives spreading damaging misinformation [8]. While there are benefits, the risks are great as is the carbon footprint [9]. Thus we must balance the tenor of misinformation and damaging use of fossil fuels to increase profits. Possible avenues may be through increased time in nature, development of a circular economy, accurate education about climate change, physical activity and meditation [[10], [11], [12], [13], [14], [15]].

The collapse of coral reefs has recently been highlighted as the first climate tipping point we have crossed [16]. Future increased CO2 levels are likely. The dismantling of treaties by nefarious leaders emphasizes the need for adaptation. In The Journal of Climate Change and Health we have published many articles from a public health point of view about modeling and disease prevention, but we also need to emphasize other aspects of adaptation including those which occur at the individual level. New ideas and approaches are necessary and can be shared via the Climate Health Society and The Journal of Climate Change and Health.

Personal and collective resilience is paramount when decisions being made by politicians are negatively impacting the future of humanity. Anticipatory solastalgia bears further study [17]. Case reports conquering climate anxiety are important as are the development of validated instruments to look at population-based results [18], [19], [20], [21]. Climate cafes have been proposed as a way to relieve tensions and nature prescriptions and joining in community may be other ways to decrease distress [10], [22], [23], [24], [25].

To take back our future, we need solidarity. Bringing together scientists, health care professionals, climate scientists, geologists, meteorologists, public health professionals, sociologists, anthropologists, attorneys, ESG professionals, students and those from any disciplines interested in climate change and health around the world can elevate our cause and provide a means to act. Following the spirit of Indigenous Peoples, we must work together to protect and cherish the earth and save the wonderful lives it hosts [26].

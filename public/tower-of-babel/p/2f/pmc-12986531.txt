Chitosan Electrospun Membranes Possess Natural pH-Responsive Indicators for Smart Real-Time Spoilage Detection of Shrimp
Authors: Jing Wei, Abdul Qayum, Qingmin Chen, Qiangqiang Xiao, Zhenghong Hao, Chengzhi Xiang, Jianxin Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ensuring the simple, rapid, and real-time monitoring of the freshness of fresh food items is essential for maintaining food safety. By reacting with characteristic substances generated during spoilage, pH-responsive indicators can effectively reveal the degree of food freshness. In this study, a mixture of hydroxypropyltrimethyl ammonium chloride chitosan (HACC), polyvinyl alcohol (PVA), and blueberry anthocyanins (BAs) was adopted and, via an electrospinning strategy, changed into a membrane coupled with a pH-responsive ability to assess the freshness of shrimp. The results showed that HACC/PVA-BA membranes with a HACC: PVA ratio of 1:4 exhibited enhanced hydrophobicity, better WVP properties (4.32 × 10−9 g m−1 s−1 Pa−1), a rapid pH-response ability within 5 s and super radical scavenging capacity (56.34% for DPPH and 54.74% for ABTS radicals). HACC’s immutable positive charge creates a strong electrostatic field that pre-concentrates spoilage-generated ammonia and intensifies the protonation state of BAs, which dramatically enhances colorimetric sensitivity and rapid response to volatile amines. Moreover, a satisfactory antibacterial ability for S. aureus and E. coli were also evidenced: HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes achieved a maximum inhibition rate of 64.9% for E. coli and 62.2% for S. aureus. Once applied to monitor the freshness of shrimp stored at 4 °C, the HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes were able to indicate shrimp freshness through visually recognizable color changes within 3 h, which correlated strongly with the spoilage indicators of total volatile basic nitrogen, total viable count, and pH value. It is suggested that the intelligent pH-responsive membranes show great potential for practical application in monitoring food freshness.

1. Introduction

With the rapid development of society and the economy, consumers are increasingly aware of the importance of a healthy diet and are paying particular attention to the quality and freshness of food. Fresh food is highly susceptible to contamination by external microorganisms, endogenous enzymatic reactions, and other environmental factors, which can reduce its quality, cause spoilage, and result in a loss of nutritional and commercial value (Figure 1) [1,2,3,4]. However, it is difficult to directly determine whether fresh food has spoiled during the early storage period. Conventional fresh-food packaging only serves as a barrier against contamination and supplies basic information on product manufacture, but it cannot provide real-time information on food quality. Intelligent freshness indicators can react with the characteristic substances generated during the spoilage process of fresh food, indicating the freshness of food through color changes and directly informing consumers of the current status of the food quality [5,6]. Among numerous freshness indicators, pH-based intelligent indicators are highly suitable for protein-rich foods such as seafood [7,8,9] since this type of food releases some volatile substances during storage, including nitrogenous substances, whose concentrations are indicated as total volatile basic nitrogen (TVB-N) [10,11]. The release of such nitrogenous compounds causes changes in the pH value, leading to noticeable color changes in the indicator that are visible to the naked eye.

Common pH-based intelligent indicators use either synthetic chemical pigments or natural pigments. However, high toxicity and potential health and environmental risks of chemical pigments render them unsuitable for use in freshness monitoring applications [12]. Anthocyanins, natural pigments that are derived from plants with rich content and that are safe and consumer-friendly, exhibit reversible color-changing properties over a wide pH range (2–12). This high visual contrast and sensitivity make them exceptionally suitable for detecting volatile alkaline amines (e.g., ammonia and biogenic amines) produced during protein-rich food spoilage, enabling real-time freshness monitoring [13,14,15]. However, their practical utilization is often limited by poor stability and slow response kinetics. Previous studies have shown that co-pigmentation and suitable methods for preparing indicator films could solve the mentioned drawbacks [16,17,18]. Electrospun membranes are widely used in the field of food packaging, such as antimicrobial packaging, antioxidant packaging, modified atmosphere packaging and intelligent/sensing packaging [19,20]. The nanoscale fiber diameter creates a massive surface area, which is a critical advantage for active packaging, as it allows for a much higher loading capacity and more efficient release of active compounds compared to conventional cast films. And the highly porous structure endows them with more active sites for amine gas adsorption [21]. For example, Xu et al. utilized electrospinning to fabricate an intelligent and active packaging film using anthocyanin-loaded polylactic acid/quaternized chitosan, which exhibited outstanding performance in extending the shelf life of blueberry. The abundant hydroxyl and amino groups on hydroxypropyltrimethyl ammonium chloride chitosan (HACC) chains form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds with polyvinyl alcohol (PVA), ensuring good miscibility, and HACC can provide potent and broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity by disrupting microbial cell membranes with good biocompatibility [22,23,24,25]. In addition, it can also prevent the nucleophilic attack of water on the pyran ring of anthocyanin because of hydrogen bonding interactions between BAs and HACC. This interaction modulates the structural transformation of BAs, resulting in a stepwise color change in membranes with HACC as pH changes [18]. And the positive charges on the quaternary ammonium groups of HACC provide a stable, pH-independent cationic environment, which strongly attracts the anionic anthocyanin molecules, facilitating a more efficient proton transfer process upon exposure to TVB-N. This intensified interaction leads to a more pronounced and rapid color change compared to neutral or weakly cationic matrices, thereby lowering the detection limit for spoilage biomarkers. In addition, the electrospinning process creates a high-surface-area, highly porous nanofibrous network, resulting in a visible color change within seconds to minutes, far surpassing the response time of conventional macroscopic substrates [26,27]. When functionalized with HACC, this architecture offers an immense number of active sites for both BA immobilization and amine gas adsorption, which is favorable to the pH-responsive color change in membranes [14,16]. And the BAs are securely entrapped within the fibrous network, significantly mitigating leakage and improving the indicator’s stability and safety for food contact. However, the response time still needs further improvement, even with membranes prepared using anthocyanins and electrospinning technology.

Herein, we developed a rapid pH-sensing (about 5 s) intelligent membrane with antimicrobial and antioxidant activity by electrospinning HACC/PVA with the incorporation of BAs to enable real-time monitoring of shrimp freshness. While many indicator films using electrospinning have been studied before, we introduce HACC as a multifunctional polymer matrix which plays a pivotal role in dramatically improving the colorimetric sensitivity and stability of the BAs, surpassing the capabilities of conventional chitosan or other biopolymer-based films. Compared to existing works about BA electrospinning films, our as-prepared HACC/PVA-BA membranes, in addition to having higher antibacterial activity, hydrophilicity, and good biocompatibility, more importantly have higher levels of HACC, which can prevent the nucleophilic attack of water on the pyran ring of anthocyanin, thus improving pH response sensitivity. We studied electrospun pH indicator membranes prepared with different HACC: PVA ratios and successfully identified the composition that exhibited the most significant color change in response to TVB-N. This work offers an eco-friendly strategy for advanced food packaging systems, contributing to more effective freshness indication and enhanced consumer safety.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Chemicals and Apparatus

Blue anthocyanin (5%~25% purity), PVA 1799 and HACC (degree of quaternization: 40%) were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical (Shanghai, China). LB nutrient agar and LB broth were purchased from Qingdao Haibo Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Qingdao, China). 2,2-diphenyl-1-(2,4,6-trinitrophenyl) hydrazyl (DPPH), 2,2-azide-bis (3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonicacid (ABTS) and 5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) were purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical (Shanghai, China). Both the Staphylococcus aureus (S. aureus, CMCC 26003) and Escherichia coli (E. coli, ATCC 25922) used in this study were purchased from Shanghai Luwei Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Distilled water with a conductivity higher than 18 Ω was used in the experiment. Live shrimp were supplied by RT-Market (Jinan, China). All other reagents in this study, which were used without any purification, were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

2.2. Synthesis of Electrospun Membranes

The synthetic method was adapted from our previously reported study with slight modifications [21]. For the synthesis of electrospun PVA membranes, 10 mL PVA solution (8%, m/v) was transferred at a 3.0 mL/h flow rate into the plastic syringe of a syringe pump with 14 kV voltage. The distance between the collector and needle was 15.0 cm. The PVA electrospun membranes were peeled off from the aluminum foil and dried at 60 °C for 30 min. Then, 8% HACC (1.6 g HACC was added to 18.4 mL distilled water) solution (or BA solution) was mixed with 8% PVA (1.6 g PVA was added to 18.4 mL distilled water) in different proportions (1:3, 1:4 and 1:5) at 200 rpm for 20 min. It is worth noting that when the ratios of PVA:HACC were less than 1:3, it was difficult to electrospin them into a fibrous membrane. When the ratios of PVA:HACC were higher than 1:5, the pH sensitivity and antibacterial properties of the membranes were not particularly pronounced due to their low HACC content [28]. Then, the HACC/PVA membranes (or BA/PVA membranes) were obtained following the above methodology. BA solutions (6%) were prepared by adding blue anthocyanin (1 g) into 8% PVA solution (15.7 g) with stirring for 30 min and then ultrasonic degassing. Finally, 1.2 g of blue anthocyanins (6% BA solution) was respectively added into mixed solution with the same content (HACC and PVA in different proportions prepared as above) and ultrasonically degassed (40 kHz; 250 W for 20 min). The following steps of electrospinning were similar to the above-mentioned steps.

2.3. Characterizations of Electrospun Membranes

The morphology of the electrospun membranes and EDS mapping images were evaluated by field emission scanning electron microscopy (FE-SEM SU8010, Hokkaido, Japan). Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were analyzed using Cu Ka radiation (l = 1.5406 Å) by an X-ray diffractometer (Smartlab SE, Yokohama, Japan). Different electrospun membranes were analyzed by FT-IR spectroscopy (Tensor 27, Stuttgart, Germany) to evaluate the chemical interaction between functional groups in a range of 4000–500 cm−1. The thermal analysis of the electrospun membranes was conducted using a simultaneous thermal analyzer. Thermogravimetric measurements were measured on an STA449 F3 Jupiter (NETZSCH, Selb, Germany) analyzer in air atmosphere with a heating rate of 5 °C min−1. The adsorption curves, as well as the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller surface area of the materials, were measured by an Quantachrome auto sort-iQ2 analyzer (Quantachrome, Boynton Beach, FL, USA). The WCAs of the prepared membranes were determined using a contact angle analyzer (DSA100, KRUSS, Hamburg, Germany). A tensile tester (XG-1A, Shanghai New Fiber Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) was used to test the mechanical properties of the hybrid membranes (40 mm × 5 mm) with a champ distance of 5 mm and a drawing speed of 1 mm/min. Electron spin resonance (ESR) spectra were obtained with a Bruker ESP 300E electron paramagnetic resonance spectrometer at room temperature with in situ UV irradiation.

2.4. The Response Sensitivity of the Electrospun Membranes

2.4.1. Color Tests

The values of lightness (L, 0–100), redness (+a)–greenness (−a), and yellowness (+b)–blueness (−b) for the membranes were measured using a colorimeter (CR-400, Hokkaido, Japan). Then, the RGB color modes based on the membranes were analyzed using MATLAB R2014a. Additionally, membranes were stored at 4 °C and 25 °C, respectively, under dark conditions for more than 20 days. For the assays of color stability, the color parameters (L, a, and b) of the membranes were measured every two days. The values of ΔE represented the total color difference in the sample and could be calculated according to Equation (1):(1)ΔE=(L−L0)2+(a−a0)2+(b−b0)2 where R, G, and B represent a red, green, and blue color, respectively; L0, a0, b0, represent the values of the control.

2.4.2. pH and Ammonia Sensitivity Tests

The assessments of the sensitivity to pH and ammonia of the BA solution and electrospun membranes were evaluated by the reported strategies with slight modifications [29]. Firstly, 5 mg of blueberry anthocyanin powder and HACC were dissolved in 10 mL of buffer solution at different pH values (2–11). Then, the absorption data were collected at wavelengths from 400 to 800 nm by a UV–Vis spectrophotometer. Additionally, the membranes were fumigated above a sample bottle (20 mL) containing 10 mL ammonia water (20%, v/v) or acetic acid solution (20%, v/v), respectively, for different durations. The UV–Vis absorbance was captured by the camera and measured using a Cary 100 spectrophotometer (Agilent Cary, Cary, NC, USA). Furthermore, the sample was cut into a 1 × 1 cm square and attached to a whiteboard for solid UV testing with double-sided tape.

2.5. Antioxidant Activity Assays

The antioxidant activity of the membranes was determined by the reported methods with slight modification [29,30]. A total of 0.2 mg of the membranes was dissolved into 10 mL water and gently stirred (80 r/min) for 12 h. Then, 0.3 mL of the solution was reacted with 4 mL DPPH methanol solution (0.1 M) at 37 °C in the dark for 30 min. Also, 0.3 mL of the solution was reacted with 4 mL ABTS methanol solution (0.18 mM) for 20 min. Finally, the absorbance of the solution was measured at 517 and 734 nm using a UV spectrophotometer. The inhibition rates of free radicals by DPPH and ABTS were calculated based on Equation (2):Free radical inhibition (%) = (1 − AS/AC) × 100%(2) where AS represents the absorbance of the sample and AC is the absorbance of the control value.

The determination of the hydroxyl radical scavenging ability of the membranes was carried out by the Fenton reaction and salicylic acid method [31]. A total of 1 mL of sample solution (dissolved membrane) was reacted in a system containing 1 mL FeSO4 (9 mmol), 1 mL salicylic acid ethanol (9 mmol/L) and 1 mL H2O2 (8.8 mmol/L) solution. And the absorbance was measured at 510 nm using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer after the sample was incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. The rate of hydroxyl radical scavenging was calculated based on Equation (3):Hydroxyl radical scavenging rate (%) = (1 − (AS − A0/Ac)) × 100%(3) where AS represents the absorbance of the sample, A0 is the absorbance without H2O2, and AC is the absorbance of the control solution.

2.6. Antimicrobial Activity of Electrospun Membranes

The antibacterial activities of the electrospun membranes were investigated using Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus based on the reported methods with modifications [32]. On the one hand, both of the strains (105 CFU/mL) were respectively incubated in Luria–Bertani (LB) liquid medium (10 mL) coupled with 20 mg of dissolved membranes and then incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. The absorbance of the bacterial suspension of each sample at 600 nm (OD600) was measured using a UV–Vis spectrophotometer (Agilent Cary 60; USA) at the time points 0 h, 4 h, 8 h, 12 h, 16 h, 20 h, and 24 h. On the other hand, the direct antimicrobial effectiveness could be observed by the area of inhibition in the plates. The antibacterial efficiencies (AEs) were measured as follows (Equation (4)):(4)AE(%)=OD600C − OD600SOD600C×100% where OD600C and OD600S represent the values of the control and culture samples at different culture times, respectively.

2.7. The Application of the Membranes onto Fresh Shrimp for Preservation

2.7.1. The Assays of Shrimp Monitored via Membranes

Briefly, shrimps were put into diffusion dishes, inside which the membranes were attached to the central chamber. They were stored at 4 °C and 25 °C, respectively, for 25 h after the lids of the diffusion dishes were closed. Meanwhile, the color of the membranes was photographed and recorded with a camera at regular intervals. The color parameters of the 0 h membranes were used as a control and the ΔE values were calculated by Equation (1).

2.7.2. Analysis of TVB-N

The TVB-N content was determined according to the method mentioned in Chinese Standard [6]. Take 10 g of peeled shrimp meat and crush it, then add 50.0 mL of deionized water and filter. Apply water-soluble adhesive to the edge of the diffusion dish and add 1 mL of boric acid solution and 1 drop of mixed indicator to the central chamber of the diffusion dish. Accurately add 1.0 mL of filtrate to the outer chamber of the diffusion dish and cover it with a frosted glass lid. Then quickly add 1.0 mL of saturated potassium carbonate solution into the gap and gently rotate it in a circular motion on a table to mix the sample solution and saturated potassium carbonate solution thoroughly. And place it in a 37 ± 1 °C incubator for 2 h, cool it to room temperature, remove the cover, and titrate with hydrochloric acid standard titration solution (0.0100 mol/L). The TVB-N content was calculated by Equation (5):(5)X=V1−V2×c×14m×(VV0)×100 where X is the content of TVB-N in the sample (mg/100 g); V1 is the volume of HCl consumed by the sample (mL); V2 is the volume of HCl consumed in the blank (mL); V is the volume of the accurately aspirated volume; V0 represents the total volume of the sample solution; c is the concentration of the standard titration solution of HCl (mol/L); and m is the mass of the sample (g).

2.7.3. Determination of TVC

The fresh shrimp sample (25 g) was homogenized in a sterile cup containing 225.0 mL sterilized normal saline. The above diluent continued to be diluted tenfold until 10−1 to 10−4 serial dilutions were obtained. The values of TVC were evaluated using the colonies on each dish, which were counted and calculated after being cultured at 30 °C for 72 h.

2.8. Statistical Analysis

All the investigations, including UV–Vis absorption, water vapor permeability, tensile strength and elongation at break tests, antioxidant activity assays, TVB-N, ΔE, TVC, stability of membranes and antimicrobial activity experiments, were conducted in triplicate using three independent experiments. Error bars represent the standard deviations from these independent tests. Results are shown as mean ± standard deviation (SD).

3. Results and Discussion

3.1. Preparation of the Electrospun Membranes

The process of the BA-loaded electrospun membranes prepared by electrostatic spinning technology is illustrated in Figure 2a. Specifically, PVA and HACC were used as substrates that relied on the biodegradability and multifunctional properties loaded in BAs [33,34]. A certain proportion of the three main precursors was mixed into a solution to be spun into a bunch of nanofibers and further convoluted to mold the HACC/PVC membranes successfully. Since only amorphous peaks are observed in Figure S1 for the raw materials, this suggests that the amorphous structure may depend on the specific surface area and flexibility of the nanofibers [35]. This can be evidenced by the EDS images in Figure 2b, which confirmed the presence of C, O, and N elements in HACC/PVA nanofibers. As observed in the SEM images shown in Figure 2c, the PVA nanofibers exhibited a smooth surface with an average diameter of approximately 1 μm (Figure 2c(i),d). With the increase in HACC content, the diameter of the HACC/PVA (1:3) and HACC/PVA (1:4) nanofibers showed a reduction to 290 nm and 350 nm, respectively (Figure 2c(ii),(iii)). This could be attributed to the decrease in the shear viscosity and increase in the conductivity of the spinning solution with the introduction of HACC solution [25]. As shown in Figure 2c(iv), we could clearly observe the interesting phenomenon that a small number of nanofibers seemed to transform into nanoribbons (yellow arrow), which may have been caused by the viscosity and conductivity of the spinning solution at this ratio affecting the surface tension and charge density of the spinning solution droplets [36]. In addition, the effect of the introduction of BAs to the system on the diameter distribution was found to be significant. The results in Figure 2d show that the average diameter of the PVA/BA nanofibers decreased from ~1 µm to 890 nm after adding BAs into the synthesis process. This is because more charges increase the charge repulsion in the jet due to the higher conductivity of the PVA/BA spinning solution after BAs are added. Consequently, the jet stream experiences stronger electric field forces, causing it to be stretched more thoroughly and resulting in the formation of finer nanofibers [37]; therefore, both the average diameter of the HACC/PVA-BA (1:3) and HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) nanofibers were increased. This result indicates that BAs can be uniformly dispersed within the composite nanofiber matrix without disrupting the entangled structure formed by HACC and PVA polymer chains. Considering the rapid and obvious color changes for thick fibers loaded with more BAs in response to pH changes, the HACC/PVA-BA nanofibers in a 1:4 proportion were selected as the optimal sample.

3.2. Color Responses of the Electrospun Membranes

As the results in Figure 3a(i) show, the BA solution mixed with HACC exhibited distinct color changes across pH 2–11. At pH values between 2 and 5, it exhibited a vivid red color. As the pH rose to 8, it gradually shifted to purple, then turned yellow at higher pH levels and deepened gradually. As evidenced by the UV–Vis spectrum in Figure 3a(ii), the maximum absorption peak shifted progressively from 581 nm to 511 nm as the pH increased from 2 to 11 as the result of protonation and deprotonation reactions (Figure 3b). Specifically, anthocyanins existed primarily as red flavylium cations at pH values from 1 to 3. As pH rises, the flavylium cation hydrates to form the colorless carbinol pseudobase, lightening the solution color. Further proton loss generates purple quinonoidal base species. In alkaline solutions, the chalcone form predominates, yielding a yellow color [38]. Obviously, the results of the photographic images exposed to CH3COOH and NH3·H2O for the electrospun membranes, which were made up of BAs/HACC, also reveal the pH responsiveness. In the presence of CH3COOH, the color of the samples appeared bright rose-red, because BA molecules accepted protons, forming positively charged flavylium cations [32,39]. Notably, all membranes, especially those containing HACC, rapidly underwent a color change from gray to red within 5 s (Figure 3c), which is far faster (by at least 9 min) than the chitosan-based films incorporated with varying concentrations of anthocyanins prepared by Trinh Kim Nguyen et al. [40]. This attribute of the formation of a carbinol pseudobase and stable cationic environment provided by HACC enhanced electrostatic interactions with BA molecules and amplified its colorimetric response to NH3·H2O.

Figure 3d shows the reflectance intensity at 550 nm for membranes exposed to CH3COOH for varying durations. The HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes showed higher saturation levels, indicating the greatest color change rate and thus optimal suitability for freshness indication. Indeed, the membranes exhibited a sharp reflectance increase after 5 s, approaching saturation by 40 s (Figure 3e). This rapid change arises from the susceptibility of the C2 position of BAs to OH− nucleophilic attack in alkaline environments, leading to ring opening and pseudobase formation [41], a process potentially accelerated by the alkaline amino group of HACC [29]. It should be clarified that the response time was obtained under the specific experimental conditions described, and if future cross-platform comparisons are to be conducted, normalization of factors such as concentration, thickness, and mass transfer needs to be systematically considered. Furthermore, the porous electrospun structure enhances NH3·H2O adsorption [14]. Hydrophobic microdomains within HACC may stabilize the BA pseudobase structure via hydrophobic interactions, delaying degradation (e.g., through oxidation or polymerization) and indirectly increasing the steady-state pseudobase/chalcone concentration. Consequently, the HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes transitioned from their initial purple color to nearly white within 40 s (Figure 3c) [42]. The reasons for enhanced pH sensitivity of membranes with HACC (rapid and noticeable color changes) are as follows: Firstly, the hydrogen bond network formed between HACC chains and BAs creates a physical barrier around the BA molecules, particularly shielding their reactive pyran rings. The mechanism of nucleophilic attack by water (hydrolysis) entails the lone pair electrons on the oxygen atom of a water molecule attacking the electron-deficient C2 (or C4) position on the pyran rings. This three-dimensional hydrogen bond network provides steric hindrance and additional shielding effects. Secondly, the C2 atom on the pyran rings of BAs constitutes an electron-deficient electrophilic center due to the electron-withdrawing effect of the adjacent positively charged oxygen atom (in the acidic anthranilate cation form), rendering it vulnerable to nucleophilic attack. When the phenolic hydroxyl group of BAs forms hydrogen bonds with functional groups on HACC, the lone pair electrons on the oxygen become increasingly engaged in hydrogen bonding. This interaction effectively donates electron density to the BA molecule. Through the conjugated system of the benzene ring, this electron density is partially delocalized to the pyran ring, thereby neutralizing some of the positive charge and reducing the electrophilicity of the C2 atom. This rationale aligns with the general mechanism and reactivity trends observed in aromatic nucleophilic substitution reactions [43,44].

3.3. Characterization of the Electrospun Membranes

The chemical structures of the electrospun membranes were primarily analyzed by FT-IR spectroscopy and thermogravimetric analysis to reveal the interaction between the various materials. Several peaks at 3410 cm−1, 2943 cm−1, 1421 cm−1, and 1100 cm−1 are observed in Figure 4a, which could be assigned to the characteristic peaks corresponding to the O-H stretching vibration, C-H stretching vibration, C-O stretching vibration, and C-H bending vibration for PVA, respectively [45,46]. A newly observed peak at 2850 cm−1 after the introduction of HACC in the synthesis process was attributed to the deformation vibration of -CH3 groups on the quaternary ammonium moieties [47]. For the BA-loaded membranes, the characteristic peak located at 1021 cm−1 was regarded as the C-H vibration of the aromatic ring in BAs, while the peak at 1083 cm−1 probably corresponded to the stretching vibration of the heterocyclic pyran ring of the phenolic flavanone group in BAs [17,18]. Notably, the intensity of the O-H stretching vibration was lower in HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes than that in BA/PVA membranes, which may be due to the fact that hydrogen bonding interaction between BAs and HACC resulted in a reduction in -OH groups (Figure S2a) [48]. Obviously, as a result of the ring opening reactions, chalcone structures in BAs were formed under alkaline conditions [32,39,49].

The thermal stability of different electrospun membranes was then evaluated by thermogravimetric analysis and we compared the mass residue at a decomposition temperature of 700 °C. As the results show in Figure 4b, the weight loss of all the membranes below 100 °C was attributed to the loss of moisture and volatile impurities. The obvious weight loss of PVA membranes may be related to the removal of hydroxyl groups (250 °C to 350 °C), and the weight loss from 350 to 450 °C predicted the formation of tar products and some small-molecule volatiles caused by the breakage of PVA chains [45]. Within 250 to 500 °C, the weight loss of HACC/PVA membranes was reduced compared to PVA fiber membranes (17.5% residual mass), due to the formation of hydrogen bonds between HACC and PVA, which effectively improved the thermal stability of PVA [34,50]. It is clear that the HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes exhibited the highest residual mass (~21.2%), mainly due to the number of residual compounds from BAs and the hydrogen bonds between HACC and PVA, which enhanced the thermal stability of the composite membranes.

The water vapor permeability (WVP) indicates the ability to allow water vapor to pass through membranes and is an important parameter for evaluating the water barrier performance of food packaging membranes [51]. In Figure 4c, the pure PVA membranes displayed a WVP value of 2.78 × 10−9 g m−1 s−1 Pa−1, which increased after blending with HACC (4.32 × 10−9 g m−1 s−1 Pa−1) or BAs (6.74 × 10−9 g m−1 s−1 Pa−1). The addition of HACC or BAs not only provided more O-H bonds to facilitate the adsorption of water molecules but also generated voids in the polymer matrix to establish pathways for water molecule migration [13,22]. However, the WVP of the ternary HACC/PVA-BA membranes decreased significantly and the WVP of the HACC/PVA-BA (1:5) membranes was even lower than that of the pure PVA membranes, suggesting improved water vapor barrier properties due to the narrow holes. The decreased WVP values of the HACC/PVA-BA membranes was lower than many reported pH films fabricated by conventional methods, which is directly due to the porosity of nanofibrous films being influenced by fiber diameter [52]. The intermolecular hydrogen bonds formed by the -OH group in BAs and hydrophilic groups in HACC may reduce the affinity of HACC-based membranes toward moisture. Furthermore, the nitrogen adsorption isotherms and BJH pore size distribution curves of the prepared membranes are given in Figure S2b. The BET surface area of the HACC/PVA-BA (1:5) membranes (49.8532 m2/g) was larger than the other two samples (15.003 m/g for BA/PVA and 4.1356 m/g for PVA). According to the pore size distribution, HACC/PVA-BA (1:5) membranes had the smallest pore size among the three, which may be attributed to the finer fiber diameter causing the membrane to become denser, consistent with the WVP results.

Tensile strength (TS) and elongation at break (EB) illustrated the mechanical resistance and flexibility of the film. Compared with PVA membranes, the BA/PVA membranes exhibited a significant decrease in tensile strength (TS) and elongation at break (EB), possibly because BA addition reduced the intermolecular forces of PVA membranes to a certain extent (in Figure 4d). After blending with HACC, TS (9.7 MPa) improved while EB (21%) decreased. This trend might be ascribed to the interaction between -NH2 and -OH in HACC/PVA membranes, resulting in higher strength but lower flexibility [53]. Interestingly, despite the lower EB (24%) of HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes, their TS (9.0 MPa) remained comparable to that of pure PVA membranes as a result of the formation of intermolecular hydrogen bonds between BAs, HACC, and PVA, which restrict molecular mobility and enhance the mechanical properties of membranes [46]. However, the overall mechanical strength of the membranes remained somewhat inferior to other films prepared by solution-based approaches or hydrogel systems due to inherent factors including randomly oriented fiber networks and higher porosity [54,55]. In the future, we expect to enhance the mechanical properties of individual fibers through blending or co-spinning with resilient polymers. Alternatively, strategies such as multilayer composites and nonwoven substrate reinforcement will be employed to construct multi-level reinforcement structures.

Finally, the surface wettability of various membranes was evaluated by water contact angle (WCA) measurements, as presented in Figure 4e. The WCA of the HACC/PVA (1:4) membranes was slightly reduced (~40°), which was attributed to the presence of abundant hydrophilic hydroxyl and amino groups of HACC [22]. Furthermore, the WCA of the samples decreased from 59.5° to 15.5° with the addition of BAs, indicating increased hydrophilicity. However, an increase in WCA was observed after loading HACC to BA/PVA membranes, and even the WCA of the HACC/PVA-BA (1:5) membranes was higher than that of pure PVA membranes, which was attributed to HACC’s strong interactions with the hydroxyl groups of PVA during electrospinning, resulting in the exposure of hydrophobic groups. Additionally, hydrogen bonds formed between the -OH and -NH2 groups of HACC and BAs may potentially decrease the hydrophilicity of membranes.

3.4. Antioxidant Activity

DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging assays were conducted to evaluate the antioxidant activity of the solutions that made up the membranes. It is observed in Figure 5a,b that a significant increase in DPPH (20.10%) and ABTS (21.5%) radical scavenging activity was exhibited by HACC/PVA, which could be attributed to the enhanced antioxidant properties due to the positively charged HACC. Indeed, amino and hydroxyl groups on HACC convert DPPH into stable compounds by providing electrons or hydrogen atoms that react with the lone electron pair of the DPPH radical [56]. As expected, the addition of BAs substantially improved the DPPH and ABTS radical scavenging activity of all membranes. Specifically, HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) demonstrated the highest scavenging capacity, reaching 56.34% for DPPH and 54.74% for ABTS radicals. This enhancement is attributable to the potent antioxidant activity of phenolic flavonols in BAs [57]. Obviously, the combined incorporation of HACC and BAs synergistically enhances the antioxidant activity of the membranes. Furthermore, hydroxyl radical scavenging ability of the solution was also detected by the results. Both BA/PVA and HACC/PVA (1:4) showed increased scavenge hydroxyl ability (31.5% and 27.6%, respectively). More importantly, HACC/PVA-BA demonstrated about a 72% scavenge hydroxyl ability, which was stronger than that for the others (Figure 5c). However, it should be pointed out that the DPPH, ABTS, and •OH scavenging experiments were conducted in a homogeneous aqueous solution system, which differs from the complex environment of packaging films at solid, heterogeneous food interfaces. Therefore, these results cannot be directly equivalent to the true antioxidant efficacy in the packaged state, even if BAs and HACC are the basis for the material’s ability to exert its actual preservation function. Indeed, the active hydroxyl and amino groups on HACC molecular chains react with free hydroxyl radicals to form stable compounds, while the positive charge of the N-quaternized chitosan could strengthen its antioxidant ability, thereby terminating the free radical chain reaction [58]. These results, coupled with the electron paramagnetic resonance (ESR) spectroscopy, evidence the inhibition of the intensity of DMPO-OH adducts (in Figure 5d). It was recognized that obviously decreased ESR signals occurred in the treatment group of HACC/PVA-BA, which were ascribed to the fact that •OH was captured by 5,5-dimethyl-1-pyrroline N-oxide (DMPO) to form relatively stable paramagnetic DMPO-OH adducts [30].

3.5. Antimicrobial Activity

Food antimicrobial membranes aid in extending shelf life by inhibiting microbial growth and reducing food waste, while preserving product quality and safety. To evaluate the antibacterial activity of the membranes, E. coli and S. aureus were utilized as the model bacteria. The growth curve of them showed a slight reduction of the absorbance for PVA membranes compared with the control group at 600 nm, and it decreased after HACC was added (Figure 6a,b). This was attributed to electrostatic action, during which the positively charged HACC binds to negatively charged components on the surface of bacterial cell membranes (teichoic acid, lipopolysaccharides, etc.), disrupting cell membrane permeability and causing leakage of contents or cell death [59]. As expected, HACC/PVA-BA membranes began to inhibit the growth of bacteria in the initial 0–4 h and the number of bacteria was lower than the other membranes during the incubation period of 4–8 h. The efficiency of the HACC/PVA-BA membranes in inhibiting E. coli and S. aureus was higher than 60% for both (Figure 6c,d). In particular, the HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes achieved the highest inhibition rate of 64.9% for E. coli and 62.2% for S. aureus, which is consistent with the results of bacterial colonies using the spread plate method and similar to the results reported by dual-functional shikonin-loaded quaternized chitosan/polycaprolactone nanofibrous film prepared by Zou et al. [36]. As the results show in Figure 6e, the number of colonies decreased drastically with the HACC/PVA-BA membranes exposed to both E. coli and S. aureus. Besides the excellent antibacterial properties of HACC, BAs are rich in phenolic hydroxyl groups, which not only affect the metabolism and stability of bacterial cell membranes, resulting in a significant decrease in bacterial activity, but also inactivate proteins through hydrogen bonding with HACC, leading to bacterial death [41,60,61]. Finally, the selection of HACC with a substitution degree of 40% was based on spinnability considerations. While HACC with higher substitution degrees theoretically may exhibit superior antibacterial activity, we aimed to overcome the limitations of current research and prepare fiber membranes with enhanced performance. Our current evidence strongly suggests that electrostatic interaction is the dominant mechanism; direct visualization of membrane damage and a more detailed mechanistic study involving direct membrane–bacteria interaction analysis is needed to confirm this.

3.6. Freshness Monitoring of Real Shrimp

The microbial spoilage of proteins in most animal-derived products can cause deterioration, accompanied by the production of volatile nitrogenous compounds. When exposed to ammonia, the prepared membranes undergo visible color changes, enabling their use as indicators for monitoring spoilage in such products. As shown in Figure 7a, the TVB-N values of shrimp increased from 7.05 mg/100 g to 31.9 mg/100 g after 7 h of storage at 25 °C, exceeding the TVB-N limitation of 30 mg/100 g for fresh food stipulated in the Chinese national standard [16]. At the same time, the membrane color turned light gray with an ΔE value of 10.67, while the membrane color turned gray-black with a value of 99.32 mg/100 g for TVB-N 18 h later. For the fresh shrimp stored at 4 °C, the TVB-N value of fresh shrimp was 34.4 mg/100 g with an ΔE value of 11.67 and a color change from pink to gray after 15 h storage, suggesting that the shrimp was completely spoiled. Additionally, the trends of the results in pH and total viable count (TVC) were as pronounced as those in Figure 7b, where the results illustrate that the membranes can reflect the freshness of fresh shrimp in real-time. It is noteworthy that fresh shrimp stored at 25 °C only exceeded the national TCV limit after 20 h, lagging significantly behind the TVC-N standard used to assess shrimp spoilage. While there is a certain relationship between the two metrics for determining food freshness, they are not directly correlated. Furthermore, TVC typically takes longer than TVB-N to indicate food spoilage [62]. Simultaneously, we analyzed the correlation between ΔE values of HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) indicator membranes and shrimp TVB-N, TVC, and pH during storage at 25 °C (Figure S3). We found that the ΔE value of the membrane positively correlated with TVB-N (R2 = 0.9204; Figure S3a) and TVC (R2 = 0.9681; Figure S3b). pH also showed a significant correlation with ΔE (R2 = 0.9719; Figure S3c). These results indicate that the prepared membrane serves as an effective freshness indicator for monitoring shrimp spoilage.

To evaluate the color stability of membranes stored at 4 °C and 25 °C in the dark for at least 20 days, the ΔE was calculated and served as a direct indicator of BA degradation over time (Figure 7c,d). For BA/PVA membranes stored either at 4 °C or 25 °C, an ΔE value greatly exceeding ~5 represented a very pronounced color shift, unequivocally indicating severe degradation of the BAs and the membrane’s poor stability. In contrast, membranes with HACC showed a slow growth in ΔE, which gradually increased to ~2 after 22 days, indicating noticeable changes and high stability. In addition, we conducted SEM imaging of HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membrane storage under ambient conditions (25 °C; 30% relative humidity) for 30 days. And the SEM images showed that the fibrous mat maintained its non-woven structure without significant fiber fusion, breakage, or collapse (Figure S4). This indicates good physical and structural stability of the electrospun matrix under these conditions. Notably, when comparing shrimp freshness detection at 25 °C and 4 °C, HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) membranes showed superior performance. These HACC-containing membranes improved responses to volatile ammonia and displayed distinct color changes for shrimp freshness monitoring (Figure 7e and Figure S5); regardless of whether they were stored at 4 °C or 25 °C, the as-prepared pH-responsive membranes exhibited a pronounced and rapid color change over time compared with the other films [14,36]. Additionally, the color data processed via MATLAB to generate the specific R, G, and B values (Figure 7f and Figure S6) could enable potential smartphone-based RGB readings by consumers for real-time freshness assessment. Concerning further applications, we still need to recognize that the performance of the membranes may be affected by more complex environmental factors in practical food packaging applications.

It must also be recognized that the sensing mechanism based on broad-spectrum pH indicators faces significant specificity challenges when applied to complex practical systems such as fresh shrimp. The gas-phase composition generated by fresh shrimp during storage is a dynamically changing complex mixture. In addition to target spoilage markers (mainly alkaline amines such as ammonia, trimethylamine, and dimethylamine), there are also various non-spoilage acidic or alkaline volatiles that may interfere with the indicator response, such as non-putrefactive alkaline substances produced by the shrimp’s own metabolism and post-death biochemical reactions (such as small amounts of ammonia produced by initial autolysis), or interference introduced by storage environments (such as ammonia containing ice) [63]. Although this “overall signal” can effectively distinguish between fresh and spoiled samples in this controlled experiment, there are still difficulties in accurately and specifically attributing it entirely to microbial spoilage in practical applications and excluding the interference of non-spoilage volatiles mentioned above. Compared to recent studies, the response time needed to indicate the quality of the shrimp while maintaining a broad response range has been improved (Table 1). In future research, we are committed to developing sensing materials with higher selectivity for shrimp spoilage characteristic markers, such as trimethylamine.

4. Conclusions

In this study, electrospun HACC/PVA-BA membranes were successfully developed as a rapid pH-responsive indicator for real-time monitoring of shrimp freshness. The membranes demonstrated excellent sensitivity to pH fluctuations, exhibiting rapid and distinct color changes in response to volatile nitrogenous compounds released during shrimp spoilage. The incorporation of BAs and HACC enhanced the pH responsiveness and overall performance of the membranes. Real-time monitoring experiments conducted at 4 °C confirmed that the HACC/PVA-BA (1:4) indicator membranes displayed visually discernible color changes that strongly correlated with key spoilage indicators, including TVB-N, TVC, and pH, over the storage period. Nevertheless, the pH-responsive membranes lack specificity, as they responds to general pH changes rather than targeted spoilage biomarkers, risking false positives. And variable real-world conditions (temperature and humidity) and the challenges of scalable manufacturing also require further investigation. In the future, we expect to assess the robustness and versatility of the indicator membranes under a wider range of real-world storage conditions, including different temperatures and humidity levels, and optimize electrospinning parameters for large-scale, continuous production. It is hoped that the potential of this innovative, enhanced-sensitivity membranes can be fully realized in commercial applications for food safety and quality control.

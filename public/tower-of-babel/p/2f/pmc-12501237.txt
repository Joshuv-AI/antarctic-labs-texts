Pathological validation of 18F-AlF-NOTA-octreotide PET/CT for neuroblastoma
Authors: Yuxuan Liu, Yuting Ma, Han Wang, Yingying Sun, Fei Zheng, Miaoqing Zhao, Xiaorong Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSomatostatin receptor 2 (SSTR2) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) plays an important role in the diagnosis and treatment of neuroblastoma (NB), but it faces the issue of nonspecific uptake. Although the relationship between SSTR PET/CT and SSTR2 expression has been studied in neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), significant differences exist between NB and NETs, and the application of 18F-AlF-NOTA-octreotide (18F-OC) PET/CT in NB requires further investigation. This retrospective study assessed the relationship between 18F-OC PET/CT imaging parameters and immunohistochemical (IHC) scores for the quantification of SSTR2 expression in NB. We obtained imaging and clinical data from patients with initial or treated NB who underwent 18F-OC PET/CT at Shandong Cancer Hospital between June 2021 and March 2023. Pathologically validated and accurately localized lesions on PET/CT images were analysed. 18F-OC uptake was assessed using Krenning scores, maximum (SUVmax) and mean (SUVmean) standardized uptake values. We determined SSTR2 expression by evaluating scores for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) score, immunoreactive score (IRS), histochemical score (H score), and Volante score. We analysed 71 lesions (NB, n = 51), NB that converted to ganglioneuroblastoma (GNB) after treatment (n = 9), and ganglioneuromas (GN, n = 11). SSTR2 scores and 18F-OC uptake were significantly higher in NB tumours than in GNB and GN tumours. Furthermore, 77.9% of 18F-OC PET/CT-positive lesions were SSTR2-positive by IHC, and imaging parameters were significantly positively correlated with HER2 score, IRS, and H score in the total cohort and the NB group (Rho range, 0.433–0.602; p < 0.000). No significant correlation was observed between imaging parameters and pathological scores in GNB and GN tumours (p > 0.05).There was high agreement between 18F-OC PET/CT imaging and SSTR2 IHC scores in newly diagnosed and residual NB tumours and no significant correlation between these two methods in GNB and GN tumours. Introduction

Neuroblastoma (NB) is one of the most common extracranial solid tumours among children, accounting for nearly 8% of all paediatric malignancies, with a mortality rate of 15%1,2. Precise assessment of a patient’s condition and the formulation of an appropriate treatment plan are key to enhancing their survival3,4. NB exhibits a high surface expression of somatostatin receptors (SSTR), with SSTR2 serving as a target for peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT), and SSTR2 expression may influence therapeutic response5,6. Moreover, high SSTR2 expression is closely associated with patient risk stratification and prognosis7,8. A precise evaluation of SSTR2 expression with PET tracers provides complementary diagnostic information and may aid in selecting patients for emerging SSTR‑targeted therapies. The evaluation of tumour SSTR2 expression relies on immunohistochemistry (IHC) scoring of lesion specimens, however, no unified consensus has been established on standardised scoring methods. The dynamic and heterogeneous expression of SSTR2 also limits the utility of IHC in analysing tumour SSTR2 expression. Therefore, appropriate methods are urgently needed to detect SSTR2 expression in a real-time, dynamic, and precise manner.

Radiolabeled octreotide exhibits high affinity for SSTR2 and is a potential candidate for targeted diagnosis. Currently, 68Ga–labelled somatostatin analogues are prominent as commonly used tracers for SSTR PET/CT9. But 68Ga-labeled tracers still have limitations such as low production yield, short half-life, and high cost10,11. 18F-AlF-NOTA-octreotide (18F-OC) is superior to 68Ga-labeled tracers in terms of physical properties, imaging quality, and cost-effectiveness, and shows potential to replace the latter in diagnostic performance12,13.

However, SSTR positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) presents challenges with nonspecific uptake, potentially yielding false-positive results. Recent progress has been made in understanding the relationship between SSTR PET/CT imaging and SSTR2 expression in neuroendocrine tumours (NETs), with multiple studies showing that SSTR PET/CT imaging parameters are positively correlated with SSTR2 pathological scores6,14–17. Nevertheless, NB and NET differ regarding tumour origin, epidemiological characteristics, site of onset, and clinical manifestations. Moreover, little is known about the relationship between 18F-OC PET/CT imaging and SSTR2 expression in patients with NB.

This study measured SSTR2 expression in tumours using four IHC scoring systems, exploring the correlation between 18F-OC PET/CT imaging parameters and SSTR2 IHC scores in patients with NB, aiming to elucidate its relationship with NB SSTR2 expression (Table 1).

Results

Baseline characteristics of enrolled patients and lesions

The study ultimately included 71 lesions from 53 patients (median age: 4 years, range: 0.25–18 years) that were histopathologically verified and scored for SSTR expression (Table 2). Females accounted for 58.5% of patients in our cohort. Most patients (96.2%) were in stage IV according to the International Neuroblastoma Staging System (INSS). Tumours in our samples included NB (71.8%), GN (15.5%), and GNB (12.7%). Primary tumours were derived from the abdomen in 44 (83%) patients. Of 71 tumours, 23 were primary (14 newly diagnosed and nine residual), 28 recurred and metastasised to lymph nodes, and 20 recurred and metastasised to other sites (Fig. 1). The median diameter of lesions was 1.5 cm (range: 0.3–15.0 cm). More detailed characteristics are presented in Supplementary Table 1.

18F-OC PET/CT

The 18F-OC PET/CT results are shown in Table 5. Among all lesions, 68 positive lesions were detected, with a detection rate of 95.8% (68/71). Thirty-six lesions had high Krenning scores (≥ 3+), with a detection rate of 50.7% (36/71) (Supplementary Table 2). The Krenning scoring method was used to assess tracer uptake in lesions, showing that uptake could not be detected (score of 0) in 3 NB lesions, accounting for 5.9% of NB lesions. Among the 32 lesions with mild-to-moderate uptake (scores of 1–2), NB, GNB, and GN accounted for 35.3%, 55.6%, and 81.8% of the pathological types, respectively. Among the 36 lesions with high uptake (scores of 3–4), NB, GNB, and GN accounted for 58.8%, 44.4%, and 18.2% of the pathological types, respectively. The median SUVmean and SUVmax values for the lesions were 2.87 and 5.35, respectively, with average values of 3.6 and 6.05. NB values were consistent with those of the overall cohort. Discussion

NB exhibits high expression of SSTR2, which serves as a crucial therapeutic target for PRRT. Accurate assessment of SSTR2 expression levels holds significant implications for optimizing patient diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. Radionuclide-labeled octreotide imaging agents demonstrate unique advantages in receptor expression evaluation due to their high affinity for SSTR2. Compared to conventional IHC methods, this targeted molecular imaging technique enables comprehensive and dynamic quantification of SSTR2 expression, particularly beneficial for patients with extensive metastases who face challenges in biopsy sampling and may potentially benefit from PRRT. The expression of SSTR in NETs has been extensively studied by PET/CT imaging and the results correlate with IHC, indicating that SSTR expression is consistent with PET/CT findings14,16–18. However, given the significant differences in disease characteristics between NB and NETs, the applicability of this correlation in NB patients requires further validation. Research by Grace Kong et al. combined 68Ga-DOTATATE PET/CT with IHC analysis to assess SSTR2 expression in NB5. Although the results of this study provided corresponding evidence, the limited sample size necessitates verification in a broader patient base. Additionally, 68Ga–labelled somatostatin analogues are prominent as commonly used tracers for SSTR PET/CT. However, the clinical adoption of 68Ga is limited by the inherent properties of the 68Ga nuclide and the production challenges associated with the 68Ge/68Ga generator. In contrast, 18F-labeled somatostatin analogs offer advantages in production efficiency, half-life, transportability, and image quality and have shown superior biodistribution and lesion detection in NETs, suggesting their potential as a replacement for 68Ga10–12. In this study, we employed 18F-OC PET/CT for quantitative assessment of SSTR2 expression in NB and demonstrated a significant positive correlation with pathologically verified SSTR2 expression. This finding supports the potential value of SSTR PET/CT imaging parameters as an imaging biomarker in the diagnosis and management of NB. Clarifying the clinical relevance of correlating 18F-OC PET with SSTR2 IHC is crucial. Histopathological confirmation is currently the gold standard for receptor status, and establishing PET/IHC concordance confirms that 18F-OC uptake accurately reflects SSTR2 density rather than nonspecific tracer accumulation. Moreover, validated correlation facilitates non-invasive patient selection for PRRT and aids clinicians in interpreting heterogeneous uptake patterns in routine practice.

The interpretation criteria for SSTR2 IHC are yet to be clarified. Considering that each scoring system has unique evaluation standards, each existing scoring system offers distinct advantages. HER2 scoring system typically examines the intensity and percentage of membranous protein expression in tumour cells to guide clinical decision-making. Previous studies have recommended using the gastric cancer HER2 criteria for scoring SSTR2A18. The Volante score, proposed by Volante et al., based on the polyclonal antibody staining results, was used to evaluate the expression of SSTRs in NETs19. It focuses on assessing the intensity and distribution range of SSTR2 expression to provide guidance for patient diagnosis and treatment. The H score combines the percentage of positive cells with staining intensity, offering a detailed quantitative measurement to evaluate the overall intensity of staining, and is suitable for situations requiring precise quantitative assessments of tumour receptor status. When minor differences in receptor expression significantly affect treatment decisions, the H score may be a more appropriate choice. The IRS, which integrates the staining intensity with the percentage of positive cells, is applicable for the assessment of various cellular membrane, cytoplasmic, and nuclear receptors. Thus, we employed four scoring systems to comprehensively assess SSTR2 expression. As illustrated by our results, different distribution characteristics of SSTR2 expression across the various IHC scoring systems, reflecting the sensitivity and specificity of different systems to SSTR2 expression, as well as the application differences between them.

This study analysed the correlation between IHC scoring systems and found a strong correlation between the HER2 score, H score, and IRS in the total cohort and NB lesions. Although the Volante score showed some correlation with the other three systems, the correlation was weaker in comparison. Consistent with this finding, one-way ANOVA showed no significant differences in SUVmax and SUVmean across lesions with different Volante scores, suggesting that this scoring system does not accurately reflect SSTR2 expression in tumours. This may be attributed to the original basis of the Volante score on the polyclonal antibody staining results and the use of a 50% cutoff for reading20. Differences in the specificity and affinity between polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies may limit the accuracy of the Volante score in assessing monoclonal antibody staining results. In 2012, a study by Korner et al. updated the recommendations for immunohistochemical assessment of SSTR2A and suggested using the monoclonal antibody UMB-1 and a 10% cut-off for reading21. This suggests that the Volante score may not be an accurate evaluation method for assessing NB SSTR2 monoclonal antibody staining results.

Krenning scores showed high heterogeneity in tracer uptake in different pathological types of NB. Uptake was higher in NB tumours and lower in GN tumours. SUVmax and SUVmean values were lower in GNB and GN tumours than in NB tumours. There was heterogeneity in the expression of SSTR subtypes in different pathological types of NB. SSTR2 expression was higher in NB tumours than in GNB and GN tumours, which may explain differences in tracer uptake in different tumour types20. Several earlier studies22,23 have reported an inverse relationship between NB differentiation and SSTR2 expression, with GN and mature GNB lesions typically exhibiting minimal or focal SSTR2 staining compared to undifferentiated neuroblastoma. Our data showing lower SUVmax and IHC scores in GN/GNB than in NB are consistent with these previous observations.

Among 68 PET-positive lesions, 15 were SSTR2-negative by IHC. The discrepancy between the results may be due to heterogeneity in the intratumoral expression of SSTR2. The length of the short axis of seven of 15 lesions was greater than 1.0 cm, and the tracer concentration distribution was uneven on CT images. However, SSTR expression is measured in a few surgical specimens. Sampling errors can lead to poor agreement between methods. In the remaining eight lesions, the average length of the long axis was small (0.625 cm), three were confirmed as GN, and the heterogeneity of SSTR expression may account for the varying results. Of the remaining five lesions, despite negative IHC results, pathological scores for four of them indicated weak SSTR2 expression. SSTR PET scans revealed low uptake of the imaging agent in these lesions, although it was higher than the surrounding background, resulting in variations in the qualitative analysis findings. Additionally, there was a lymph node metastasis near the liver, and the high uptake signal from surrounding tissue may have influenced the appearance of increased imaging agent uptake in the lesion on the PET images. Finally, due to the small size of some lesions, the spatial resolution of PET/CT scans is limited, and partial volume effects (PVE) may also lead to measurement deviations in tracer uptake24.

The study results indicate a high correlation between 18F-OC PET/CT imaging parameters and HER2 score, IRS, and H score. This is consistent with previously reported results in NETs14,17 and with current studies confirming a significant positive correlation between these three scoring methods and SSTR2A RT-PCR data in NETs25. This implies the feasibility of using 18F-OC PET/CT to assess SSTR2 expression in NB. However, in this study, the correlation between the imaging parameters and Volante score was low. On this basis, further differentiation of the pathological subtypes was analysed for correlations between imaging parameters and scoring systems. The results showed that in NB, 18F-OC PET/CT had a strong correlation with IHC scores, whereas in GNB and GN, the correlation between the imaging parameters and IHC scores was weak. Some patient tumour samples showed only mild SSTR2 staining and yet, exhibited high uptake in 18F-OC PET/CT imaging. This might be due to heterogeneity in SSTR expression. Previous studies have confirmed that NBs primarily express SSTR2 among the five SSTR subtypes19,23,26. However, the other subtypes were more commonly expressed in GNB and GN27. This study stained and assessed tumour SSTR2 expression pathologically, however, it is important to consider that somatostatin analogues, such as octreotide, may bind not only to SSTR2 but also to SSTR1, 3, and 428. This may have led to higher imaging parameters compared to lower SSTR2 IHC scores, affecting the results of the correlation analysis. Inflammation, immune responses, and lymphoid tissue may also lead to false positives in octreotide uptake20,29. Additionally, the large volume of primary lesions in some patients and random errors in pathological sampling can cause mismatches between imaging parameters and pathology scores. In this study, SUVmax showed a weaker correlation with IHC pathology scores than with other imaging parameters. SUVmax reflects extreme values of imaging uptake, and given the strong heterogeneity of NB, we do not believe that SUVmax accurately reflects the actual expression of tumour SSTR2.

This study had some limitations: This was a retrospective, single-centre study that focused on pathological specimens prepared from lesions that could be precisely located using preoperative PET/CT imaging. Although this strategy improved the reliability of the specimens, it introduced unavoidable selection bias. The relatively interval between imaging and surgical specimen collection could potentially affect the correlation analysis, as tumour characteristics and SSTR2 expression patterns might change during this period. In addition, the small sample size within each group limited statistical analysis. Moreover, the absence of bone and bone-marrow lesions in the analysed dataset is a noteworthy limitation, resulting from the difficulty of obtaining histopathological validation for such lesions in paediatric patients. Consequently, the diagnostic performance of 18F-OC PET/CT for this common metastatic site in NB could not be assessed. Future studies should expand the sample size and consider including minimally invasive bone biopsies or alternative validation approaches to address this limitation, to validate the findings of this study. In addition, because this study lacked a direct head-to-head comparison with 68Ga-DOTATATE/TOC, future prospective multicentre trials are warranted to validate whether 18F-OC confers a measurable diagnostic or prognostic advantage in paediatric NB. Finally, we did not assess SSTR1/3/4 expression. Although these subtypes are only weakly and heterogeneously present in most NB, their evaluation could further elucidate the modest discordance observed between 18F-OC PET uptake and SSTR2 IHC. Future prospective studies employing multiplex IHC, RNA-scope, or mass-spectrometry imaging are warranted to provide a comprehensive SSTR landscape.

Conclusion

The results demonstrated a correlation between 18F-OC PET/CT imaging parameters and SSTR2 expression in patients with newly diagnosed or residual NB tumours. The imaging parameters of NB lesions were significantly positively correlated with HER2 score, IRS, and H score in NB lesions while there is no significant correlation with the Volante score. Meanwhile, there were no significant correlations between imaging parameters and pathological scores in GNB and GN. These findings are crucial for the follow-up and personalized treatment planning of NB patients.

Materials and methods

Study population

We screened PET/CT data and collected information from 606 patients who underwent 18F-OC PET/CT at Shandong Cancer Hospital between June 8, 2021 and March 28, 2023. Eighty patients diagnosed with NETs were excluded. A total of 383 patients underwent PET/CT alone and did not have corresponding pathological specimens from surgeries. In addition, 13 patients underwent surgery after imaging but lacked detectable tumour tissues in pathological specimens. Furthermore, lesions corresponding to the pathological specimens of 77 patients could not be accurately localized on PET/CT images. These patients were excluded from the analysis. Ultimately, pathological specimens of 71 lesions from 53 patients were included in the study and analysis (Fig. 1). Notably, only lesions for which histopathology could be unequivocally co‑registered with the corresponding 18F-OC uptake focus were eligible for analysis. Bone and bone-marrow metastases were excluded since these lesions are rarely biopsied due to their multifocal nature and morbidity of orthopaedic procedures in children. This study complied with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. This retrospective analysis received approval from the Ethics Committee at Shandong Cancer Hospital and Institute (Approval ID: SDTHEC2024005003).

IHC and scoring systems

All tumour specimens were obtained via surgical resection. The specimens were embedded in paraffin, and sections were made at a thickness of 3 μm at the Department of Pathology at Shandong Cancer Hospital and mounted on slides (MeVid, Jiangsu, China). SSTR2 IHC staining was performed using a rabbit monoclonal antibody against SSTR2 (IR405; Enhance Biotech, Guangzhou, China) at 37 °C in an automated stainer (BOND-MAX; Leica Biosystems Newcastle Ltd., Newcastle, UK). For the pathological assessment of SSTR2 expression in lesion specimens, we used four IHC scoring methods: human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) score, immunoreactive score (IRS), histochemical score (H score), and Volante score. IHC scores of 2 or more were considered positive. Lesions positively stained for SSTR2 in at least two scoring systems were considered SSTR2-positive. Detailed explanations of the four scoring systems are provided in Table 1.

18F-OC PET/CT

All patients included in the study underwent 18F-OC PET/CT preoperatively. Imaging was carried out on a Siemens Biograph PET/CT scanner (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany). 18F-OC was administered intravenously at 3.3–4.4 MBq/kg. Scanning commenced 60 ± 5 min after injection, covering the skull base to the mid-thigh. PET data were acquired in three-dimensional mode for 2 min per bed position and reconstructed with time-of-flight ordered-subset expectation maximisation (2 iterations, 21 subsets) followed by a 5 mm Gaussian filter into a 200 × 200 matrix. A low-dose CT scan (120 kV, 40 mAs, pitch 1.2) was used for attenuation correction and anatomical localisation. The interval between the imaging and surgery ranged from 3 to 64 days (average interval: 42 days). The PET/CT images were assessed by two experienced nuclear medicine physicians. Scoring was performed using a visual grading scale. The Krenning score is considered a feasible semiquantitative method for assessing the extent of tracer uptake in lesions (0: no tracer uptake; 1: tracer uptake much less than the liver; 2: tracer uptake slightly less than or equal to the liver; 3: tracer uptake greater than the liver but less than the spleen; 4: tracer uptake less than the spleen)30,31. Lesions were considered PET/CT-positive if the uptake was higher than the tissue background and could not be explained by physiological uptake. The maximum standardised uptake value (SUVmax) and mean standardised uptake value (SUVmean) of the lesions were measured and used for the analysis and discussion.

Statistical methods

Data were statistically analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The Spearman correlation test was used to analyse the correlation between IHC scores and tracer uptake, whereas one-way ANOVA was used to compare the significance of the differences between the IHC scores. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

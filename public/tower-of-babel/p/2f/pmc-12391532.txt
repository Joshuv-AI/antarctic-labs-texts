Quantitative assessment of parameter requirements in a division of focal plane polarimeter
Authors: Bowen Lian, Jingpeng Yang, Ao Tang, Yawei Xiao, Naiting Gu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The division of focal plane polarimeter (DoFP) is a widely utilized instrument for target detection and recognition, owing to its excellent real-time performance and compact configuration. However, due to manufacturing and integration errors, its performance when measuring the state of polarization is limited. Therefore, it is essential to understand the relationship between these error sources and their response to design, manufacture, and apply the polarimeter effectively, as this determines the degree to which errors should be controlled under different performance requirements. The present paper proposes an analysis method to quantitatively assess parameter requirements when a DoFP is subject to different levels of accuracy. The corresponding theoretical model is also derived in order to establish the relationship between the DoFP’s performance and its various error sources. This model is employed to conduct sensitivity and Monte-Carlo analyses at varying degrees of polarization accuracy (10−3, 10−2 and 10−1). The requirements of the error sources are analyzed, incorporating maximum and minimum transmission and polarization direction for each polarized pixel. The results of this study provide a method of evaluating performance and establish the necessary parameter requirements for a given level of polarization accuracy in a DoFP. This is the first occasion on which a quantitative assessment study of the calibration error response of DoFP polarimeters has been conducted, and the related results provide valuable references for the development, calibration, and application of polarization detection and imaging.

Introduction

Polarization imaging technology can measure the state of polarization (SoP) of incident light and acquire information about the target, including the structural shape, surface properties, and material composition, etc1–3. Thus, it has been widely used in various fields, such as target recognition4,5, solar magnetic field measurement6, bio-medicine7–9, and wavefront detection10,11. Polarimeters are generally classified into four classic types12,13: division of time polarimeter (DoTP)14–16, division of amplitude polarimeter (DoAmP)17,18, division of aperture polarimeter (DoAP)19,20, and division of focal plane polarimeter (DoFP)21–23. DoTP usually requires rotating polarizers or retarders, which is unsuitable for dynamic scenarios. DoAmP and DoAP have good real-time performance, but the disadvantages of complex structure, weak stability, and difficult image alignment limit their performance and application. In comparison, DoFP integrates micro-polarization arrays (MPA) with imaging units, which have the advantages of snapshot imaging, compact structure, and good stability. As a result, the DoFP developed rapidly and has been applied widely in various scenes22–26. However, due to manufacturing and integration errors, the performance of the DoFP is also limited. The measurement accuracy of the degree of polarization (DoP) in a DoFP is about 2.5 × 10−1 in general27–32. It is generally accepted that the accuracy of polarization measurement is typically required to be 10−1 for target polarization imaging4,12,14, 10−2 for biomedical imaging33,34, and 10−3 or higher requirement for solar magnetic field measurement6,35. Thus, it is essential to analyze the error sources and their impacts on polarization measurement accuracy.

Recently, several significant efforts and results have been made to evaluate the performance of DoFP under different error sources36–41. C. Cabrera et al.36 investigated the influence of pixel crosstalk caused by diffraction on the extinction ratio as early as 2007. The influence of pixel size and the distance between MPA and detector on the extinction ratio was obtained via simulation analysis, which prompted the progress of the DoFP’s performance evaluation, although the relationship between the extinction ratio and the system performance was not well established. In 2010, V. Gruev et al.37,38 studied the effects of extinction ratio and detector noise. They derived a theoretical relationship between the performance response and the error sources of detector noise and extinction ratio. It is an important basis for analyzing the performance of DoFP. In 2020, H. Sun et al.26,39,40 established in further a theoretical model and the corresponding mathematical expressions to address the issue that a high extinction ratio and low detector noise are incompatible within a DoFP system. Based on the above research, X. Sun et al.41 derived a new performance response model for a mounted DoFP considering the crosstalk error between polarized pixels, which made a great promotion for the application of the mounted kind of DoFP. The existing research results are very important and helpful in analyzing the error sources and their impacts on polarization measuring accuracy. However, these error sources and their impacts are also unilateral and discrete, and a comprehensive relationship between polarization measurement accuracy and its impact factors should be established, especially for manufacturing and integration errors including the maximum transmission, minimum transmission, and polarization direction of each polarized pixel in a DoFP.

Thus, we propose an analysis method to assess quantitatively the parameter requirements for different accuracy requirements. The corresponding theoretical model is also built to describe the performance response for different kinds of error sources. Sensitivity and Monte-Carlo analysis results give the parameter requirements of maximum transmission, minimum transmission, and polarization direction of each polarized pixel for different DoP accuracy requirements of 10−3, 10−2 and 10−1, which provide crucial guidance for each parameter error control. This is the first quantitative assessment of the calibration error of the DoFP polarimeter, and it may provide an important reference for applications such as target detection and biomedical imaging.

Sensitivity analysis of parameter error

Improving the accuracy of polarization measurement can more comprehensively and meticulously display the polarization characteristics of the target, thereby enhancing the target detection and recognition capabilities. Consequently, the application of this technique in different situations has different requirements for the accuracy of polarization measurements. For example, when targets are detected and identified, only a measurement of 10−1 to 10−2 is required4,12,14. In the case of biomedical imaging, a measurement of 10−2 is required33,34. For solar atmospheric magnetic field detection, a measurement of 10−3 or higher is required6,35. Due to manufacturing and integration errors, the error parameters are generally constrained to the following limits: 0 < ΔTθ∥<0.1, 0 < ΔTθ⊥<0.05 and − 2.5o < Δβθ < 2.5o27–32 (the light intensity is normalized to 1). To further ensure the parameter range, a series of sensitivity analyses for four adjacent pixels, respectively, have been made in a DoFP under different accuracy requirements of 10−1, 10−2, and 10−3. Indeed, the results of the sensitivity analysis were utilized to establish the boundaries of the parameters in the Monte-Carlo analysis. The outcomes of these sensitivity analyses can be utilized to ascertain the most sensitive factors in the DoFP parametric design process. Furthermore, they can serve as a foundation for the assignment of tolerances for machining integration.

As shown in Figs. 2(a)~(c), the sensitivity of DoP deviation is different under different error sources, although they have a similar decreasing trend with increasing error amplitude. The positions of the maximum and minimum values of the DoP deviation curve for different transmission errors are fixed, as shown in Figs. 2. (a) and (b), because they only depend on the angles of polarized directions of the pixel and incident light. Comparatively, the maximum and minimum positions have an obvious offset for different polarized directions of incident light, as shown in Fig. 2 (c).

Parameter requirements based on Monte-Carlo analysis

From Eq. (1), each measurement matrix W is composed of 3 parameters for each polarized direction pixel as described above. The error parameters between different pixels are only in the same error ranges, which depend on the actual manufacturing and integration capabilities, and the parameters are taken as random values that conform to a normal distribution30. This results in different polarization measurement errors of each super pixel. In this paper, Monte-Carlo analysis is employed to statistically evaluate the probability of the polarization measurement accuracy of all super pixels in a DoFP, which can more intuitively characterize the polarization measurement performance of the system. Same as in Sect. 3, the three accuracy indexes of 10−1, 10−2, and 10−3 are adopted to analyze the control requirements for each parameter error, which can meet the requirements of most polarization measurement fields. In this paper, the selection of parameters for the Monte-Carlo analysis is mainly based on two aspects. One is the actual processing capability and integration level of the simulation parameters. The second is based on the sensitivity analysis results of each simulation parameter. Based on the above results, the parameter range of the Monte-Carlo analysis is comprehensively specified.

Here, we subdivide uniformly each error range into 100 intervals, thus 100 error ranges in total for each accuracy requirement of the DoP measurement. Define A1~100, A101~200 and A201~300 as error ranges under the DoP accuracy of 10−3, 10−2, and 10−1, respectively, and a group of random values within the corresponding error range, presented as [ΔTθ∥, ΔTθ⊥, Δβθ], is generated to simulate the random error. And a DoFP with 1 × 106 super pixels is assumed and analyzed. Thus, there are 1 × 108 groups of random errors in total for a single DoP accuracy requirement. The corresponding 100 curves based on Monte-Carlo analysis results can be acquired by 1 × 106 times of error transfer function calculations for each result. By counting the number of different DoP accuracies, one can build the relationship between the DoP accuracy requirement and its corresponding probability. Figure 4 demonstrates the probability distributions with the changing of DoP accuracy requirement within the error range of 10−3 accuracy requirement, as listed in Table 1. Here only 10 curves are drawn in Fig. 4 to illustrate the general trend, i.e. A10, A20, …, and A100. As shown in Fig. 4, the probability will be lower for higher requirements of DoP accuracy, and the probability of acquiring good performance of a DoFP will be higher when the error range is controlled tightly. Similarly, one can also obtain the probability distributions within error ranges of 10−2 and 10−1 accuracy requirement, and they are illustrated in Figs. 5 and 6, respectively.

Here we use probabilities of 90% and 95% to evaluate the controlling requirement of each error source under different DoP accuracy, and the analysis results are summarized in Table 2. The findings presented in Table 2 demonstrate that the error parameter control requirements exhibit a near-linear relationship with variations in measurement accuracy (10−1, 10−2 and 10−3) similar to Table 1. According to Table 2, for a DoFP that requires the accuracy of 10−1 and the probability of 90%, each parameter error of ΔTθ∥, ΔTθ⊥, and Δβθ should be controlled within (8.4 × 10−2, 2.81 × 10−2, ± 2.625o) respectively. The requirements under the accuracy of 10−2 and 10−3 are (8.4 × 10−3, 2.8 × 10−3, ± 0.273o) and (8.2 × 10−4, 2.7 × 10−4, ± 0.0271o). It is hard to realize at the current level, and the requirement of error range will be tighter when the probability is required to achieve 95%. Calibration for each error source may be a feasible way to achieve higher DoP measurement accuracy. The parameter error ranges in references27–32 satisfy 0 < ΔTθ∥<0.08, 0 < ΔTθ⊥<0.03 and − 2o < Δβθ < 2o, which corresponds to the polarization measurement accuracy of the DoFP polarimeter at 8 × 10−2, and is consistent with the prediction results of our model. The researchers can determine the calibration accuracy that needs to be achieved for each error parameter based on the actual accuracy requirements of the application scenario (not limited to 10−1, 10−2 and 10−3). Furthermore, by measuring the residual error of the DoFP polarimeter after calibration, researchers can use it to evaluate the calibration accuracy of the error parameter to better optimize the DoFP polarimeter calibration.

Discussion and conclusion

In conclusion, we propose an analysis method to assess quantitatively the parameter requirements of each polarized pixel, and establish the theoretical model between the performance response and the error sources. The influence of the maximum transmission, minimum transmission, and polarization direction of a polarized pixel on system performance is linear or nearly linear. The error parameters between different pixels are only in the same error ranges, which depend on the actual manufacturing and integration capabilities, and the parameters are taken as random values that conform to a normal distribution30. As required for different application scenarios, the requirements of each error source at the accuracy of 10−1, 10−2, and 10−3 are obtained. The analysis results show that the errors of maximum transmission, minimum transmission and polarization direction of each pixel should be better than (8.4 × 10−2, 2.81 × 10−2, ± 2.625o), (8.4 × 10−3, 2.8 × 10−3, ± 0.273o), and (8.2 × 10−4, 2.7 × 10−4, ± 0.0271o), respectively, with a probability of 90%. For higher probability or DoP accuracy, a tighter requirement of error sources should be achieved.

We have made some assumptions about the model and some potential limitations may arise as a result, e.g., uniform pixel response, ideal input light conditions, etc. In the proposed model, it is important that the intensity is the same in each 2 × 2 pixel area. This is easy to do for DoFP polarizers that are more than 2k×2k. For the uniformity of the pixel response, it is usually below 2%. For example, the Lucid PHX050S-P pixel response non-uniformity is 1.6%42, which has a small effect on the accuracy of polarization measurements at 10−2 and below. Of course, for high-precision polarization measurement application scenarios such as solar atmospheric magnetic field detection, the above assumptions have a large impact on the accuracy of the model results and cannot provide effective guidance.

In order to reduce the complexity of the model, the wavelengths and their induced changes in extinction ratios and quantum efficiency are not included in the model. In practical applications, the performance of the polarization measurement at a specific wavelength can be analysed by substituting the transmittance of the corresponding wavelength into the model. Furthermore, the quantum efficiency of DoFP exhibits significant changes with wavelength, but the difference in quantum efficiency between pixels within a super pixel is negligible, and the quantum efficiency differences at different wavelengths do not have much effect on the model evaluation results. For the case of continuous spectral incidence, the intensity-wavelength curve, the quantum efficiency-wavelength curve, and the transmittance-wavelength curve need to be multiplied and integrated by wavelength and substituted into the proposed model in this paper to analyze the polarization detection performance in the RGB + DoFP application scenario. We will synthesize the model for evaluating the polarization performance of DoFP under continuous spectral conditions in future research.

In practical applications, it is hard to realize the DoP accuracy of 10−2 or higher accuracy for a DoFP currently due to higher precision manufacturing and integration requirements. This finding suggests that DoFP is challenging to implement in high-precision scenarios, such as solar atmospheric magnetic field measurement. Calibration and correction of the mentioned errors may be a feasible way to achieve higher polarization measurement requirements. However, the results also supply us with a performance evaluation method and give a proper parameter requirement for a certain requirement of polarization accuracy in a DoFP, which may offer valuable references for the development, calibration, and application in polarization detection and imaging.

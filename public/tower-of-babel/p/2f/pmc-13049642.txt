10-Year Excess Mortality After First-Time Non–ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction
Authors: Peter Lauridsen, Pernille Gro Thrane, Christine Gyldenkerne, Kevin Kris Warnakula Olesen, Michael Maeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIn ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction patients, recent evidence showed an almost similar 10-year mortality in 90-day survivors when compared with a matched general population. Whether this finding also applies to contemporary patients with non–ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) remains unclear.ObjectivesThe objective of the study was to examine excess mortality in first-time NSTEMI patients treated with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) compared with a matched general population during the landmark periods of 0 to 30 days, 31 to 90 days, and 91 days to 10 years.MethodsFirst-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI were identified in the Western Denmark Heart Registry from 2010 to 2022. Mortality was compared with a general population cohort matched by age, sex, and calendar year.ResultsThe study included 10,748 first-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI and 53,739 matched individuals. The median follow-up was 6.0 (Q1-Q3: 3.4-9.2) years. NSTEMI patients had higher mortality in all landmark periods than the general population: 1.9% (95% CI: 1.6-2.2) excess mortality from 0 to 30 days (HR: 8.90; 95% CI: 7.40-10.68), 0.5% (95% CI: 0.3-0.7) from 31 to 90 days (HR: 2.54; 95% CI: 2.06-3.15), and 6.1% (95% CI: 4.8-7.5) from 91 days to 10 years (HR: 1.26; 95% CI: 1.21-1.32). Notably, this excess risk was almost exclusively confined to patients with a high comorbidity burden (excess mortality 22.0%; 95% CI: 19.1-24.9).ConclusionsAmong first-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI, 90-day survivors had a substantially higher 10-year excess mortality than the matched general population. This excess mortality was primarily driven by NSTEMI patients with a high baseline comorbidity burden.

Non–ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) accounts for approximately 60% to 70% of all acute myocardial infarction cases.1, 2, 3, 4 Historically, it has been generally accepted that patients who survive the initial high-risk phase of myocardial infarction indefinitely remain at an increased risk of death.5,6 However, this concept was recently challenged by contemporary data showing that 90-day survivors of a first-time ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) had 10-year mortality rates nearly equivalent to those of an age-, sex- and calendar-year-matched general population.7 This finding was temporally associated with high adherence to cardioprotective medications throughout 10-year follow-up, highlighting the importance of medical adherence on long-term outcomes for myocardial infarction patients.7

Few studies have investigated excess mortality among NSTEMI patients in comparison with a matched general population, likely due to the difficulties in establishing an appropriately matched general population with a long-term follow-up and limited loss to follow-up.4,8, 9, 10, 11, 12 Thus, existing studies are often limited to shorter follow-up periods or rely on national life table data for general population mortality rather than individual-level matching.4,8, 9, 10, 11, 12 Consequently, there is a lack of contemporary data on long-term excess mortality and adherence to cardioprotective medications among first-time NSTEMI patients treated with percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). To address this gap in knowledge, we hypothesized that excess mortality among the first-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI would be concordant with that observed in the recent STEMI study.7 We examined this hypothesis in a large, contemporary, real-world cohort of NSTEMI patients with a long-term follow-up.

Methods

Setting

Denmark has a tax-funded health care system that provides health services for all Danish citizens. The implemented protocols for treating NSTEMI in Denmark are in accordance with the European Society of Cardiology guidelines and the U.S. guidelines.2,3

Data sources

Since 1999, all patients admitted for PCI in the Western part of Denmark have been registered in the Western Denmark Heart Registry (WDHR). This registry has prospectively registered all cardiac procedures performed in the Western part of Denmark and contains relevant information on cardiac risk factors besides procedure- and lesion-specific data with high data completeness and validity.13 The WDHR covers a population of approximately 3.3 million inhabitants (55% of the Danish population). In this part of Denmark, 3 high-volume PCI centers offer invasive treatment services around the clock. Patients are registered in the database with their own personal and unique identification number that allows for cross-linkage with national registries with virtually no loss to follow-up (<0.03%). Further information on our study population was obtained by cross-linking the WDHR to the national health registries.14 Hereby, we obtained information on date of birth, sex, and vital status from the Danish Civil Registration System, information on causes of death from the Danish Registry of Causes of Death, information on hospital contacts from the Danish National Patient Registry, information on reimbursed prescriptions from Danish pharmacies from the Danish National Prescription Registry, and information on biomarker results from general practitioners and hospitals from the Register of Laboratory Results for Research.13

Ethical approval

The study was registered at the Danish Data Protection Agency (record 1-16-02-625-18). Data processing was approved by the Legal Office at the Central Denmark Region and informed consent from participants was exempted (record 1-45-70-118-22). According to Danish law, registry-based studies do not require ethical approval from a regional ethics committee. All patient data were handled confidentially and pseudonymized before analyses.

Study population

We included all adult patients (≥18 years) with first-time NSTEMI treated with PCI from January 1, 2010, till December 31, 2022. Patients with a prior diagnosis of myocardial infarction, PCI, or coronary artery bypass grafting in the Danish National Patient Registry and patients without residence in Denmark on the date of PCI were excluded. This exclusion was performed to ensure that all included patients were first-time NSTEMI patients.

On the date of the PCI procedure (index date), each NSTEMI patient was matched by age and sex with 5 individuals from the Western Denmark general population. General population individuals were randomly selected through the Danish Civil Registration System and were sampled with replacement.15 General population individuals were required to have no prior myocardial infarction, PCI, or coronary artery bypass grafting in the Danish National Patient Registry on the index date. Individuals from the general population cohort were allowed to later enter the NSTEMI cohort.

Outcomes and covariates

The study outcome was all-cause mortality evaluated at distinct landmark periods: early mortality (0-30 days), intermediate mortality (31-90 days), and late mortality (91 days to 10 years). Data on vital status and date of death were obtained from the Danish Civil Registration System. Secondary outcomes included cardiovascular and noncardiovascular mortality, which were defined according to the underlying cause of death as registered in the Danish Registry of Causes of Death.13

Information on comorbidities and comedications was obtained from the Danish National Patient Registry and the Danish National Prescription Registry.13 We assessed baseline comorbidities and comedications until 14 days before the index date to ensure that comparisons between the 2 groups were conducted before recording diagnoses related to the NSTEMI hospitalization. Comedication was defined as redemption of at least 1 prescription within 6 months before and up to 14 days before inclusion. International Classification of Diseases codes and Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical codes are shown in Supplemental Table 1.

To assess medication adherence following NSTEMI, we calculated the proportion of patients redeeming at least 1 prescription within each 1-year follow-up period, up to a maximum of 10 years. This calculation included only patients who were alive at the start of each interval and for whom we had complete medication data for each respective 1-year period. Our data set included medication data through April 21, 2024. We assessed medication adherence among all NSTEMI patients, as well as stratified by the age group (<66 years and ≥66 years). Medication use among the matched general population was examined in the first and tenth year after inclusion.

Changes in median low-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels within 1 year before or 1 year after NSTEMI admission were evaluated using data from the Register of Laboratory Results for Research.

Statistical analysis

Study participants were followed from the index date or from the beginning of a landmark period and until the occurrence of death, emigration, completion of the landmark period, or end of follow-up (May 9, 2024). To retain the 1:5 matching, if a NSTEMI patient died before the start of follow-up in a given landmark analysis, the 5 matched individuals from the general population cohort were excluded. Similarly, if all 5 matched individuals died before a given landmark analysis, the corresponding NSTEMI patient was excluded. The follow-up duration was limited to 10 years, rounded up from the 75th percentile (9.2 years) of total follow-up.

Categorical variables were presented as proportions and continuous variables as medians with 25th and 75th percentiles.

In each landmark period, the cumulative incidence of death was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier estimator. Excess mortality was defined as the difference in cumulative incidence failure estimates, and 95% CI were generated using the Com-Nougue method.16 HRs were estimated using conditional Cox proportional hazards model stratified on matched sets, with robust (Huber-White sandwich) variance estimators, using the matched general population as the reference group. The proportional hazards assumption was validated by visual inspection of log minus log plots and found to be satisfied in each of the landmark periods. To estimate cardiovascular and noncardiovascular mortality, cumulative incidences were calculated using the Aalen-Johansen estimator, accounting for competing risks.17 Cause-specific HRs were estimated for cardiovascular death, treating noncardiovascular death as a competing event, and vice versa.

Several subgroup and sensitivity analyses were conducted. First, excess mortality was evaluated according to the comorbidity level using the CHADS-P2A2RC score.18,19 Second, excess and relative mortality was assessed from 91 days to 10 years, stratified by age above or below the median age of 66 years. Third, excess and relative mortality were stratified by the calendar period (2010-2016 vs 2017-2022). Effect modification by age group and calendar period was formally evaluated by including multiplicative interaction terms between NSTEMI status and the respective subgroup variables in the regression models. A P value <0.05 was considered significant. Finally, the matched general population individuals who developed NSTEMI during follow-up and became a part of the NSTEMI cohort were censored. Statistical analyses were performed using Stata/MP version 18.0.

NSTEMI patients had a slightly higher use of most cardioprotective medications compared to the matched general population. For example, the baseline use of aspirin was 16% in NSTEMI patients vs 11% in the matched general population, beta-blocker use was 16% vs 13%, and angiotensin-converting enzyme-inhibitor/angiotensin receptor blocker use was 34% vs 29%. Notably, the baseline use of statins was similar in NSTEMI patients and the matched general population (25% vs 24%). Procedural characteristics are detailed in Supplemental Table 3, showing that most NSTEMI patients underwent PCI with newer-generation drug-eluting stents. A substantial reduction in the median low-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels was observed among patients with NSTEMI, decreasing from 116 mg/dL in the year preceding admission to 66 mg/dL the year following admission (Supplemental Table 4).

Mortality

A total of 2,588 patients with NSTEMI and 9,414 matched individuals died during follow-up. Figure 2 illustrates all-cause mortality for the 10-year follow-up period. Mortality in each landmark period for first-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI and the matched general population is presented in Table 2. In the NSTEMI cohort, 2.1% died within 0 to 30 days, 0.9% died within 31 to 90 days, and 32.3% died from 91 days to 10 years. Effect of comorbidity

Table 4 shows how the comorbidity burden had a major impact on all-cause mortality. In the early period from 0 to 30 days, excess mortality was 0.2% (95% CI: 0.0%-0.4%) for patients with a low comorbidity burden, 0.8% (95% CI 0.5%-1.1%) for those with a moderate comorbidity burden, and 5.4% (95% CI 4.6%-6.3%) for those with a high comorbidity burden compared with their respective matched general population cohorts. From 31 to 90 days, patients with a low or a moderate comorbidity burden had no excess mortality, whereas those with a high comorbidity burden remained at a higher risk than their matched comparison group (excess mortality 1.8%, 95% CI: 1.2%-2.4%). From 91 days to 10-year follow-up, mortality (8.7% [95% CI: 7.4%-10.1%], 27.5% [95% CI: 25.7%-29.4%], and 70.2% [95% CI: 67.6%-72.8%] for low-, moderate-, and high-risk NSTEMI patients) was strongly associated with the comorbidity burden, confirming a correct long-term risk stratification of patients by the CHADS-P2A2RC score. Moreover, patients with a low comorbidity burden had a numerically lower excess mortality (−1.5%; 95% CI: −3.0% to −0.1%), patients with a moderate comorbidity burden had a marginally higher excess mortality (2.2%; 95% CI: 0.2%-4.2%), and those with a high comorbidity burden had an excessively higher excess mortality (22.0%; 95% CI: 19.1%-24.9%) than their matched peers. Subgroup and sensitivity analyses

When mortality was adjusted by baseline comorbidity, excess mortality remained substantially elevated, particularly in the early period, but was moderately attenuated in the late period (Supplemental Table 5). When stratified by age, excess mortality remained consistently elevated among NSTEMI patients aged ≥66 years and those aged <66 years across all landmark periods (Supplemental Table 6). Tests for interaction between NSTEMI status and age group were not statistically significant in the early periods (0-30 days: P = 0.08; 31-90 days: P = 0.43) but were significant from 91 days to 10 years (P < 0.001). In this long-term period, the relative mortality associated with NSTEMI was higher among patients aged <66 years, whereas absolute excess mortality was higher among those aged ≥66 years. Furthermore, when we assessed excess and relative mortality by the calendar period (2010-2016 or 2017-2022), results were consistent with the main analyses (Supplemental Table 7) and no statistically significant interactions were observed. When we censored matched individuals from the general population at the time they experienced an NSTEMI (251 persons), results remained unchanged across all landmark periods (Supplemental Table 8).

Use of cardiovascular medications after inclusion

Supplemental Table 9 presents the annual medication use for a 10-year period among NSTEMI patients. Within the first year (31-365 days) after PCI, most NSTEMI patients filled prescriptions for any antithrombotic agent (99%), statins (92%), and beta-blockers (68%). Adherence to cardioprotective medication remained high throughout the follow-up period, with 94% still filling prescriptions for any antithrombotic agent in the tenth year. The corresponding numbers for statins and betablockers were 85% and 63%, respectively. Medication adherence was similar in patients aged <66 and ≥66 years, except for statins, where patients aged ≥66 years were less adherent (78% vs 89%) (Supplemental Tables 10 and 11).

Discussion

In this large and contemporary cohort study of consecutively included first-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI, we assessed 10-year excess mortality compared to an age-, sex-, and calendar-year-matched general population cohort. The study yielded several novel and clinically relevant findings. First, NSTEMI was associated with a significant 10-year excess mortality, reaching 6.1% among those surviving the first 90 days. Second, NSTEMI patients had more comorbidities than their matched general population. Importantly, 10-year excess mortality among 90-day survivors was 22% and almost exclusively observed in patients with a high comorbidity burden, whereas those with a low or moderate comorbidity burden did not have any excess mortality up to 10-year follow-up compared with their matched general population. Third, among 90-day NSTEMI survivors, cardiovascular death was the main cause of excess mortality. Fourth, we observed a high adherence to cardioprotective medications, which is reassuring concerning the quality of medical prophylactic treatment in Denmark.7 Finally, the results were consistent across age groups and study periods. Collectively, these results show that NSTEMI is associated with a substantial excess mortality among 90-day survivors, a finding almost exclusively observed in patients with a high comorbidity burden.

STEMI vs NSTEMI

Our main hypothesis was that 90-day survivors of a first-time NSTEMI would have 10-year mortality comparable to the matched general population. However, we found that 90-day survivors of NSTEMI had approximately 3 times higher 10-year excess mortality than found in our recent study in patients with STEMI.7 Several key differences between the STEMI and NSTEMI populations may explain this difference. In STEMI patients, where available baseline risk factors were almost identical to the matched general population, it was speculated that the low late excess mortality could, at least partly, be attributed to high adherence to cardioprotective medications.7,20 However, since the adherence to cardioprotective medications was equally high in the current study of NSTEMI patients and in our previous evaluation of STEMI patients,7 other factors had to play an important role. To understand this better, we looked at comorbidity burden. Ninety-day survivors of NSTEMI with a low comorbidity burden did not have excess mortality at 10-year follow-up, whereas excess mortality was 22% in the same period among those with a high comorbidity burden. Thus, the latter is a plausible explanation for the observed differences in excess risk in NSTEMI vs STEMI patients.7 Prior studies have also highlighted a greater burden of comorbidities and cardiovascular risk factors in NSTEMI patients compared to STEMI patients.21,22 Taken together, we were unable to confirm our hypothesis of comparable mortality in 90-day survivors following NSTEMI and their matched controls, but we provide data suggesting that the observed excess mortality is mainly caused by cardiovascular mortality and primarily explained by differences in comorbidity burden.

Subgroups

Previous studies have reported higher excess mortality among patients ≥66 years compared to patients <66 years.4,9,10,12 Although we also found higher absolute excess mortality among patients ≥66 years, our results reveal that late mortality in the 90-day survivors is relatively higher in the subgroup <66 years. This emphasizes the significant long-term impact of NSTEMI on younger patients and highlights the need for further investigation into potential preventive strategies in this subgroup.

Perspectives

The observed discrepancy in late mortality between NSTEMI and STEMI patients appears to be driven by differences in comorbidity burden. These findings highlight the importance of incorporating comorbidity profiles into prognostic assessments and secondary prevention strategies. Further research should aim to refine risk stratification tools and evaluate tailored interventions for comorbid NSTEMI patients. Given the high adherence to cardioprotective medication, it may be relevant to explore whether adjunctive therapies—such as extended dual antiplatelet therapy,23 dual pathway inhibition with low-dose rivaroxaban,24 glucagon-like-peptide 1 receptor agonists in individuals with overweight,25 or intensified lipid-lowering26—could provide additional benefit in selected high-risk individuals.

Strength and limitations

This study examined an all-comers NSTEMI cohort within a tax-funded health care system, ensuring inclusion of a broad and representative population, thereby minimizing selection bias. In addition, individual-level linkage across national registries allowed for near-complete follow-up and comprehensive data accuracy. However, some limitations must be noted. First, the study only examined Danish individuals, a population classified by the World Health Organization as having a low cardiovascular risk.27 Second, we exclusively examined first-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI, which may limit generalizability to NSTEMI patients treated medically or with coronary artery bypass grafting. Third, more detailed angiographic measures and systematic data on in-hospital procedural complications were not available. Fourth, although we assessed medication adherence using prescription redemption data, these data are proxy measures of adherence since they do not capture whether patients took the medications. Fifth, Denmark is a high-income country with universal health care and with a relatively homogeneous population, thus limiting the generalizability of the findings to other settings.

Conclusions

Among the first-time NSTEMI patients treated with PCI, 90-day survivors had a substantially higher 10-year excess mortality when compared with a matched general population. This finding contrasts with recent results in STEMI patients and appears to be driven almost exclusively by differences in baseline comorbidity burden.

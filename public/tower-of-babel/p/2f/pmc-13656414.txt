Plastid G6PD3 and cytosolic G6PD5/6 are involved in the Ustilago sp. HFJ311-enhanced tolerance to cadmium stress in Arabidopsis
Authors: Hao Sun, Yurong Bi, Sipei Liu, Yunchuan Zhang, Xiaofan Na, Xiaomin Wang, Juan Qin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCadmium (Cd) contamination in soils severely threatens agricultural security and productivity. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD), a key rate-limiting enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway, is crucial for plant growth and stress tolerance. The endophytic fungus Ustilago sp. HFJ311 enhances plant Cd tolerance, but the underlying mechanism, especially the regulatory roles of G6PD in this process, remains unclear.ResultsHere, we demonstrate that plastid G6PD3 and cytosolic G6PD5/G6PD6 synergistically govern the HFJ311-induced Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis. HFJ311 significantly enhanced seedling biomass, leaf area, stomatal density, and photosynthetic pigment content in wild type (Col-0) under control and Cd treatment conditions. Comparatively, compared with Col-0, the shoot fresh weight (FW) of g6pd5 and g6pd6 and the root FW of g6pd3 were significantly reduced under Cd+HFJ311 conditions. Under Cd stress, all the growth-promoting effects of HFJ311 were further attenuated in g6pd3/5/6 triple mutants. Mechanistically, HFJ311 up-regulated G6PD3/5/6 expressions, maintained nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH) homeostasis, and enhanced antioxidant enzyme activities and ascorbate-glutathione (ASA-Glu) cycle efficiency to mitigate reactive oxygen species (ROS) burst and oxidative damage. Meanwhile, HFJ311 down-regulated the expression of Cd transporter genes (Iron-Regulated Transporter 1, IRT1; ZRT/IRT-like proteins, ZIPs; NATURAL RESISTANCE-ASSOCIATED MACROPHAGE PROTEIN 3/4, NRAMP3/4), ultimately reducing Cd transport from roots to shoots. The redox balance in the g6pd3/5/6 mutant was disrupted, thus aggravated Cd translocation and triggered a nitric oxide (NO) overproduction. However, 2-phenyl-4,4,5,5-tetramethylimidazoline-1-oxyl-3-oxide (PTIO), a NO scavenger, completely abolished the protective effects of HFJ311 under Cd stress, indicating that NO is involved in growth-promoting effect of HFJ311.ConclusionCollectively, our findings uncover a novel mechanism whereby G6PD3/5/6 act as central hubs mainly integrating redox homeostasis, Cd transport and chelation to mediate endophyte-enhanced Cd tolerance.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-09534-2.Keywords: Arabidopsis thaliana, Cd stress, Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase, Reactive oxygen species, Ustilago sp. Introduction

Cd (Cd) pollution in terrestrial ecosystems is predominantly attributed to the anthropogenic factors such as industrial emissions, mining, and intensive agricultural activities, posing a significant threat to global food security and ecosystems [1]. According to the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), over 20 million hectares of arable land worldwide are contaminated by Cd, with approximately 10% of agricultural products exceeding Cd safety thresholds, directly endangering human health through the food chain [2]. As a non-essential heavy metal, Cd2+ disrupts plant cellular homeostasis through oxidative stress induction, photosynthetic apparatus impairment, and perturbation of nutrient acquisition pathways [3]. To mitigate Cd-induced toxicity, plants have evolved sophisticated defense mechanisms, including: (i) plasma membrane transporter-mediated Cd2+ exclusion (e.g., zinc-regulated transporter, ZRT; iron-regulated transporter, IRT), among them, IRT and ZRT are collectively referred to as zinc-iron transporters (ZRT/IRT-like proteins, ZIP); (ii) vacuolar sequestration via natural resistance-associated macrophage 3/4 proteins; and (iii) xylem loading regulation through iron/zinc transporters [4, 5]. These adaptive strategies highlight the pivotal roles of membrane transport systems in the subcellular partitioning and tolerance acquisition of Cd2+ in plants.

In addition, plants can also enhance their tolerance to heavy metal stress by recruiting beneficial microorganisms in the rhizosphere, collectively known as plant growth promoting microorganisms (PGPMs) [6]. PGPMs are divided into plant growth promoting bacteria (PGPR) and plant growth promoting fungi (PGPF) [7]. At present, Studies on PGPR are extensive, with Pseudomonas fluorescens and Bacillus subtilis being hot strains. Reddy et al. [8] reported that Pseudomonas fluorescens KACC10327 increases AtPCR2 expression (a Cd responsive gene), chlorophyll content, and Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis. Bacillus subtilis alleviates Cd toxicity by enhancing antioxidant enzyme activity and reducing Cd accumulation in plants [9]. PGPF has also attracted considerable attention, with arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) being the most typical representative. AMF, as obligate root symbionts, enhances the metal tolerance of host plants through various mechanisms, for example, the transcriptional modulation of metal chelators [phytochelatins (PC)], activation of vacuolar Cd2+ transporters (HAIRY MERISTEM 4, HMA4; NATURAL RESISTANCE-ASSOCIATED MACROPHAGE PROTEIN 3, NRAMP3), and up-regulation of antioxidant enzymatic systems [10]. Notably, recent studies reveal that AMF-induced metabolic reprogramming enhances complexation of Cd2+ with organic acids (citrate, malate) [11]. Compared with AMF, endophytic fungi, like Ustilago sp., exhibit more intimate symbiotic relationships with host plants, as they colonize intercellular spaces or even host cells, directly regulating plant metabolic and signaling pathways [12]. When co-cultured with plants, Ustilago sp. HFJ311 enhances Arabidopsis tolerance to Cd2+ by promoting iron transport (up-regulating IRT1 expression) and auxin synthesis [12]. However, at present, the specific metabolic nodes targeted by Ustilago sp. HFJ31 and the mechanisms by which they regulate plant tolerance to Cd stress remain poorly characterized.

The pentose phosphate pathway (PPP) is one of the important pathways for energy supply and substance conversion in plant metabolic networks [13]. G6PD (EC 1.1.1.49), the rate limiting enzyme of PPP, catalyzes the oxidative dehydrogenation of glucose-6-phosphate to produce 6-phosphogluconic acid, while producing NADPH, a critical reductant for maintaining cellular redox homeostasis [14]. In Arabidopsis, the G6PD family consists of six genes (G6PD1 - G6PD6) and is divided into two subfamilies: plastid G6PD (G6PD1 - G6PD4) and Cyt-G6PD (G6PD5 and G6PD6). Based on the amino acid sequence, plastid G6PD can be further divided into three subtypes: P0 (G6PD4), P1 (G6PD1), and P2 (G6PD2, G6PD3) [15]. P1-G6PD mainly functions in green tissues, and its activity can be activated in the dark to replace the light reaction and compensate for NADPH [13]. Comparatively, P2-G6PD mainly functions in plant roots and heterotrophic tissues, suggesting a more direct role in non-photosynthetic metabolism under environmental constraints [16]. P0-G6PD itself lacks catalytic activity, however, it can form a complex with P1-GG6PD in the cytoplasm under oxidative stresses, which helps P1-GG6PD enter the peroxisome [17]. Multiple studies have confirmed that Cyt-G6PD predominantly supplies NADPH in cytoplasm for lipid biosynthesis, phytochelatins synthesis, and ROS scavenging, while plastid G6PD is indispensable for chloroplast redox balance, photosystem II stability, and carbon-nitrogen metabolism under abiotic stresses, indicating that the two isoforms have coordinated but functionally distinct roles [16, 18]. Emerging evidence demonstrates that G6PD activity can be modulated by various abiotic stresses, through post-translational modifications (phosphorylation and S-nitrosylation) and transcriptional regulation (e.g., WRKY, NAC transcription factors) [19]. Cd-induced G6PD can provide NADPH for the regeneration of reduced glutathione (GSH), promoting Cd2+-GSH complex formation and vacuolar sequestration via ABC transporters [20]. Moreover, NADPH is a rate-limiting cofactor for phytochelatins (PC) biosynthesis, as PC synthases require GSH to catalyze Cd2+ chelation [21], highlighting the core roles of G6PD in linking redox metabolism with metal chelation and transport. Given that Cd stress can significantly induce the transcription and activity of G6PD [21], there is an urgent need to further investigate whether and how Cyt-G6PD and plastid G6PD are involved in HFJ311-mediated tolerance to Cd stress in plants.

Here, we show that plastid G6PD3 and cytosolic G6PD5/G6PD6 synergistically mediate HFJ311-induced tolerance to Cd in Arabidopsis. HFJ311 up-regulates G6PD3/5/6 transcriptions to maintain NADPH homeostasis, enhance antioxidant capacity and mitigate ROS damage, while repressing root Cd uptake/translocation via down-regulating Cd transporter genes. In g6pd3/5/6 mutants, the redox balance is disrupted, therefore Cd accumulation is increased and excessive NO is induced. Collectively, G6PD3/5/6 integrate redox homeostasis, Cd transport and NO signal to mediate HFJ311-enhanced tolerance to Cd.

Materials and methods

Plant materials and growth conditions

Arabidopsis T-DNA insertion mutants (g6pd2, N430548; g6pd3, SALK_002754; g6pd4, SALK_131208C; g6pd5, CS804669; g6pd6, SALK_016157C) were in Col-0 background and purchased from the Arabidopsis Biological Resource Center. The g6pd1, g6pd5/6, pG6PD3::GUS, pG6PD5::GUS and pG6PD6::GUS lines were previously generated and characterized in our laboratory [16, 22]. The g6pd3/5/6 triple mutant was generated by genetic crossing between g6pd3 and g6pd5/6 mutants and confirmed by PCR method with specific primer pairs (g6pd3, g6pd3-LP and g6pd3-RP, g6pd3-RP and LBb1.3; g6pd5, g6pd5-LP and g6pd5-RP, g6pd5-LP and LB1; g6pd6, g6pd6-LP and g6pd6-RP, g6pd6-RP and LBb1.3). All primers are listed in Table S1.

Seeds were disinfected with 1% NaClO for 10 min and rinsed five times with sterile water. After vernalization at 4 ℃ for 2 d, seeds were germinated on 1/2 Murashige and Skoog (MS) solid medium (pH5.8) and grown in a greenhouse under a regime of 16 h/8 h light/dark cycle, 55–60% humidity, 120 µmol m−² s−¹ light intensity, and 22 ± 1 °C for 5 d. Then, the seedlings were transferred to 1/2 MS solid medium with or without 50 µM CdCl2 according to Zhao et al. [23], HFJ311, 0-100 µM PTIO (2-Phenyl-4,4,5,5-tetramethylimidazoline-1-oxyl-3-oxide) for 15 d. PTIO, a NO scavenger, was added in the medium for treatment. HFJ311 was first cultured in YEP liquid medium (1% yeast extract, 2% peptone, and 5% sucrose) at 28 °C and 150 rpm for 4 d, and mycelial particles with a diameter of 1–2 mm were collected and inoculated onto PDA solid medium (20% potato, 2% glucose, and 1% agar). After overnight incubation at 28 °C, they were transferred to a 1/2MS medium containing 5-day-old Arabidopsis seedlings for 15 d of symbiotic culture.

H2O2 and O2·− staining analysis

The content of H2O2 and O2·− was detected according to Fryer [24]. For H2O staining, the seedlings were incubated in 1 mg/ml 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) solution (pH5.0) in the dark at 28 °C for 3–4 h. For O2− staining, the seedlings were incubated in 0.5 mg/ml nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) solution in the dark at 28 °C for 3 h. Then, the tissues were rinsed three times with deionized water. After removing chlorophyll with 75% (v/v) ethanol, the stained tissues were imaged using Visual Fluorescence microscope (ZEISS GmbH, Germany).

Lipid peroxidation assay

The malondialdehyde (MDA) content was detected according to Liu [25]. Fresh leaves (0.5 g) were homogenized in 5 mL of 10% (w/v) trichloroacetic acid and centrifuged at 10,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C. One mL of supernatant was mixed with one mL of reaction solution containing 10% trichloroacetic acid and 0.5% thiobarbituric acid. Then, the reaction mixture was incubated at 95 °C for 30 min in the dark. After cooling to room temperature, samples were centrifuged again at 10,000 g for 10 min. The absorbance was determined at 450 nm, 532 nm, and 600 nm. The MDA content was calculated according to the following formula.

Electrolyte leakage determination

Electrolyte leakage was measured as described by Sairam [26]. Leaf segments (0.1 g) were incubated in 10 ml of ultrapure water for 1 h in a constant temperature shaker at 150 rpm and 25 ℃; then C1 was recorded. After boiling for 30 min, C2 was measured. The relative electrolyte leakage was calculated according to the formula:. C0 refers to the conductance of ultrapure water.

GUS staining

GUS staining was performed as described by Ruan [16]. Seedlings were incubated in staining buffer [1 mM X-Gluc, 0.1 M sodium phosphate (pH 7.5), 0.5 mM K3[Fe (CN)6], 10 mM EDTA, 0.5 mM K4[Fe (CN)6], 0.1% (v/v) Triton X-100] at 37 °C for 3 h. After destaining with 75% (v/v) ethanol, samples were imaged using a Visual Fluorescence microscope (ZEISS GmbH, Germany).

Analysis of antioxidant enzyme activities

Leaf sample (0.2 g) was ground with 2 mL of pre-cooled extraction buffer containing 50 mM sodium phosphate (pH 7.8) and 1% PVP, then, the homogenate was centrifuged at 15,000 g for 15 min at 4 ℃. The supernatant was used for enzyme activity assay. Ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity was determined according to Manoj [27]. The 1 ml of reaction mixture [50 mM sodium phosphate (pH 7.0), 0.1 mM EDTANa2, 3 mM ascorbic acid, 50 µl of enzyme extract] was initiated by adding 20 µL of 9 mM H2O2. Absorbance at 290 nm was recorded every 5 s for 1 min using a spectrophotometer. Catalase (CAT) activity was determined according to Kruse [28]. The 1 ml of reaction mixture [50 mM sodium phosphate (pH 7.0), 15 mM H2O2, 100 µl of extraction buffer] was measured at 240 nm every 15 s for 3 min. One unit of CAT activity was defined as the amount of enzyme causing a decrease of 0.1 in OD240 per minute. Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity was determined according to Prochazkova [29]. The 50% inhibition of NBT photochemical reduction was used as a unit of SOD activity. Peroxidase (POD) activity was determined according to Hammerschmidt [30]. The 1 ml of reaction mixture [50 mM sodium phosphate (pH 7.0), 20 mM guaiacol, 10 µl of enzyme extract, and 10 µL of H2O2] was measured at 470 nm every 15 s for 3 min. One unit of POD activity was defined as the amount of enzyme causing a change of 0.01 in OD470 per minute.

Detection of chlorophyll (Chl) and carotenoid (Car) content

Chl and Car contents were quantified using the modified method from Leterrier [31]. Fresh leaves (0.5 g) were extracted in 10 mL of 95% (v/v) ethanol containing 0.1% (w/v) sodium ascorbate in the dark at 4 °C for 48 h. After centrifuged at 10,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was measured at 470, 649, and 665 nm. The Chl a, Chl b, and Car contents were calculated according to the following formula.

Analysis of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADP+) and NADPH contents

NADP+ and NADPH levels were quantified using a Coenzyme II NADP(H) Assay Kit (BC1100, Solarbio, Beijing, China) according to Ruan [16]. Samples (0.1 g) were extracted with 0.04 M NaOH (for NADPH) or 0.01 M H2SO4 (for NADP+). For NADPH detection, 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) was reduced by NADPH in the presence of phenazine methosulfate (PMS) as an electron carrier. NADP+ was enzymatically converted to NADPH using glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PDH) and glucose-6-phosphate (G6P) in the reaction buffer. The absorbance of formazan (a stable product of MTT reduction) was measured at 570 nm. NADP+ and NADPH concentrations were calculated using standard curves generated with commercial NADP+ and NADPH standards (0–20 µM). All reactions were performed at 37 °C for 15 min in the dark.

G6PD activity assay

The G6PD activity was determined according to Hauschild and von Schaewen [32]. Plant tissue (0.3 g) was ground with 1 ml of pre-chilled extraction buffer [100 mM Hepes-Tris (pH 7.8), 10 mM MgCl2, 5 mM EDTA], and centrifuged at 4 °C (12,000 g, 15 min). The supernatant was used for activity analysis. The total G6PD (Tot-G6PD) activity was analyzed with the buffer [0.1 M Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 0.2 mM NADPNa2 and 2 mM D-glucose-6-phosphate disodium salt]. The changes of absorbance at 340 nm were recorded every 15 s for 3 min. The Cyt-G6PD activity was determined with the buffer [0.1 M Tris-HCl (pH 8.0), 2 mM D-glucose-6-phosphatedisodium salt, 0.2 mM NADPNa2, 62.5 mM DTT]. Plastid G6PD (Pla-G6PD) activity was calculated from the difference between Tot-G6PD and Cyt-G6PD activity.

Determination of reduced glutathione (GSH) and ascorbate acid (ASA) content

The GSH content was assayed according to Anderson [33]. Leaf tissue (0.3 g) was ground with 2 ml of 7% sulfosalicylic acid at 4 °C. After centrifugation at 10,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was used as crude extract. For total glutathione content analysis, 400 µl of reagent I (110 mM Na2HPO4, 40 mM Na2HPO4, 15 mM EDTA, 0.3 mM DTNB, 0.04% BSA) and 320 µl of reagent II (1 mM EDTA, 50 mM imidazole, 0.02% BSA, 5% Na2HPO4) were sequentially added to a 2 ml centrifuge tube, followed by 1.5 U glutathione reductase (GR), 80 µl of crude extract, and 80 µl of 90 mM NADPH solution. After mixing thoroughly, the absorbance was monitored every 15 s for 3 min at 412 nm. After the 160 µl of crude extract was pre-incubated with 40 µl of 2-vinylpyridine at 25 °C for 1 h, the sample was used for Glutathione disulfide (GSSG) content analysis according to the total glutathione protocol. The GSH content was calculated as the difference between total glutathione and GSSG, and a standard curve was generated using 0–20 µM GSH.

The ASA content was assayed according to Foyer and Walker [34]. Leaf tissue (0.3 g) was ground with 1 ml of pre-chilled 2.5 M HClO4 at 4 °C. After centrifugation at 10,000 g for 10 min at 4 °C, the supernatant was adjusted to pH 6.0 with the saturated Na2CO3. Then, the supernatant was diluted to 800 µl with 1 M NaH2PO4. For ASA content analysis, 100 µl of supernatant, 1 ml of 1 M NaH2PO4, 1 U of ascorbate oxidase were mixed, and the absorbance value was recorded every 15 s for 3 min at 265 nm. A standard curve was generated using 0–20 µM ASA.

Ion content analysis

Cd content was detected according to the method described by Zhu [35]. Shoot and root tissues were dried at 105 °C for 30 min, then at 80 °C to constant weight. After digestion with 10 ml of 12 M HNO3, the mixture was filtered through filter paper. Cd2+ content was analyzed by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (ICP-OES, PerkinElmer, USA).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) analysis

Total RNA was extracted from Arabidopsis using the Plant RNA Isolation Kit (9109, TaKaRa, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. First-strand cDNA was synthesized from the total RNA using the First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (RR092A, TaKaRa, Japan) following the manufacturer’s instructions. qRT-PCR was performed using a TranStart Green qPCR SuperMix Kit (CN830A, TaKaRa, Japan) on an ABI PRISM 7500 system (Applied Biosystems, USA). qRT-PCR amplification was carried out using a two-step cycling procedure (94 ℃ for 30 s, 94 ℃ for 5 s, 56 ℃ for 30 s for 40 cycles). Expression levels of genes were analyzed using the 2−ΔΔCT method (Livak and Schmittgen, 2001). ACTIN2 was used as the internal control.

Stomatal morphology analysis

Before analyzing stomatal morphology, plants were exposed for at least 30 min of light adaptation to stabilize their physiological state. After being frozen in liquid nitrogen for 10 s, the samples (0.5-1 cm2 leaf disc) were transferred to a Hitachi S-3400 N scanning electron microscope (SEM; Tokyo, Japan) for microscopic imaging. Stomatal distribution patterns and morphological features were observed under both low (10×) and high (300×) magnifications.

NO staining analysis

NO fluorescence staining was performed according to the method described by Lepiller [36]. Root tips (about 1 cm) from 7-day-old co-cultured Arabidopsis seedlings were rinsed with 10 mM HEPES buffer (pH 7.4) and stained with 5 µM DAF-FM DA [dissolved in HEPES buffer (pH 7.4) containing 0.1% Triton X-100] at 37 °C for 30 min in darkness. Then, samples were washed three times with HEPES buffer (pH 7.4) and imaged using a confocal laser scanning microscope (CLSM; Nikon, Japan) with an excitation wavelength of 488 nm.

Data analysis

Data were expressed as mean ± SE (n ≥ 3). Statistical significance among treatments was determined via Student’s t-test for pairwise comparisons (two groups, P < 0.05) or one-way ANOVA followed by Duncan’s multiple range test (≥ 3 groups, P < 0.05). All analyses were conducted in SPSS 19.0, and data visualization was performed using GraphPad Prism 8.0.

Results

G6PD participates in the growth promoting effect of HFJ311-enhanced tolerance of Arabidopsis to Cd stress

G6PD plays a crucial role in plant tolerance to various environmental stresses, e.g., salt, Al, Cd, Cu, heat, low temperature and so on, by balancing cellular NADPH homeostasis [37]. Our previous work has demonstrated that HFJ311 enhances Arabidopsis tolerance to Cd stress [12]. However, it is unclear whether G6PD family genes are involved in HFJ311-enhanced Cd stress tolerance in Arabidopsis. To address this issue, single mutants of G6PD gene family, g6pd1, g6pd2, g6pd3, g6pd4, g6pd5, and g6pd6, were screened and used in the co-cultivation assays with HFJ311 under Cd stress (Fig. 1). Compared with control conditions, the shoot fresh weight (FW) and root FW were similarly increased by 76.33% and 103.22% in Col-0, 59.01% and 110.17% in g6pd1, 52.07% and 110.35% in g6pd2, 52.11% and 87.50% in g6pd3, 103.77% and 169.70% in g6pd4, 111.93% and 152.11% in g6pd5, 108.04% and 121.38% in g6pd6 under 1/2MS + HFJ311 conditions, respectively (Fig. 1A-E). However, under Cd + HFJ311 conditions, the shoot FW of g6pd5 and g6pd6 mutants, two mutants of Cyt-G6PD isoenzymes, decreased by 17.97% and 21.32% compared with Col-0, respectively; and there was no significant difference in root FW among them. For the mutants of Pla-G6PD isoenzymes, the root FW of g6pd3 decreased by 31.31% compared to Col-0 under Cd + HFJ311 conditions, while its shoot FW showed no significant difference from that of Col-0 (Fig. 1A-C). Comparatively, mutation of G6PD1, G6PD2, or G6PD4 did not affect the promoting effect of HFJ311 under Cd stress (Fig. 1A-E). Fig. S1 further showed that exogenous application of Glu, an inhibitor of G6PD, significantly decreased the shoot FW (20.71%) and root FW (28.76%) in Col-0 under Cd + HFJ311 conditions. These results confirmed that G6PD, mainly Pla-G6PD3 and Cyt-G6PD (G6PD5 and G6PD6), is involved in the HFJ311-induced Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis.

In order to further investigate the molecular mechanism of G6PD involvement in the promoting effect of HFJ311 under Cd stress, we analyzed the transcriptions of G6PD family genes. qRT-PCR results showed that HFJ311 significantly up-regulated the expressions of G6PD2, G6PD3, G6PD4, G6PD5, and G6PD6 under control conditions, with increases by 4.14 times, 5.24 times, 2.27 times, 2.12 times, and 8.86 times, respectively (Fig. 1F). Cd treatment alone can also stimulate the expressions of G6PD family genes; however, only G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 expressions can be further induced by HFJ311, and increased by 42.46%, 48.73%, and 154.20% compared to Cd treatment alone, respectively (Fig. 1F). In contrast, the expression levels of G6PD1 and G6PD2 decreased by 39.24% and 35.05%, respectively. This indicated that HFJ311 selectively regulates the NADPH production pathway by preferentially activating the expression of stress-responsive G6PD subtype genes, rather than regulating the entire G6PD family genes. Thus, the expression patterns of G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 were further analyzed using the pG6PD3::GUS, pG6PD5::GUS, pG6PD6::GUS lines under different treatment conditions. Fig. S2 showed that both Cd stress and HFJ311 induced the expression of G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 in shoots and roots. Their expressions were further induced under Cd + HFJ31 treatment. GUS staining results showed that G6PD3 was mainly expressed in leaf veins, petioles, and roots, while G6PD5 and G6PD6 were widely expressed in shoots and roots. These results indicate the essential roles of G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 in the HFJ311-induced Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis.

Based on above results, the g6pd3/5/6 mutant was generated and used for co-cultivation assays with HFJ311 under Cd stress. Figure 2A-E showed that compared with Col-0, g6pd3/5/6 did not exhibit impaired the growth-promoting effect of HFJ311 on shoot FW, shoot dry weight (DW), root FW, and root DW under 1/2MS + HFJ311 conditions. However, under Cd + HFJ311 conditions, the shoot FW, shoot DW, and root FW of g6pd3/5/6 mutants decreased by 37.25%, 26.72%, and 21.08% compared with those of Col-0, respectively. However, there was no significant difference in the root FW among them. Based on this, subsequent experiments were focused on shoots.

Enzyme activity assays showed that HFJ311 significantly increased the activities of Pla-G6PD and Tot-G6PD in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 under control conditions, with increases of 118.08% and 116.08% in Col-0 and 133.57% and 96.70% in g6pd3/5/6, respectively (Fig. 2G-H). Cd treatment alone only increased the Cyt-, Pla- and Tot-G6PD activities in Col-0, but not in g6pd3/5/6 (Fig. 2F-H). Under Cd + HFJ311 conditions, HF311 further increased their activity in Col-0, but only increased the Pla-G6PD activity in g6pd3/5/6 (Fig. 2F-H). These results indicate that HFJ311 markedly enhances the Tot-GPD, Cyt-G6PD, and Pla-G6PD activities in shoots of Col-0 seedlings under control as well as Cd treatment conditions. G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 coordinate with each other to jointly regulate the growth promoted by HFJ311 under Cd stress.

Cd stress impairs leaf growth and pigment biosynthesis in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 mutants

It has been shown that Cd exposure impairs leaf development, diminishes stomatal conductance, and disrupts biosynthesis of photosynthetic pigments, thereby compromising photosynthetic performance [38]. To investigate the leaf developmental characteristics of g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under Cd stress, digital cameras and scanning electron microscopes were employed to capture and observe leaf morphology and stomatal traits. The results indicate that, compared with the controls, Cd treatment alone significantly decreased the total leaf area (Fig. 3A, C) and total petiole length (Fig. 3A, D) in both Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings, with a reduction of 41.45% and 39.18% in Col-0, and 37.48% and 40.94% in g6pd3/5/6, respectively. HFJ311 application increased the total leaf area and total petiole length of both genotypes under 1/2 MS as well as Cd stress conditions. However, the effect of HFJ311 on the total leaf area and total petiole length of g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under Cd stress was significantly weaker than the corresponding effect on Col-0 seedlings, with a respective reduction of 35.40% and 20.90% (Fig. 3C, D). Compared with controls, HFJ311 significantly increased the stomatal density in leaves of Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under 1/2MS + HFJ311 conditions, with an increase of 47.05% and 182.35%, respectively (Fig. 3B, E). Moreover, the stomatal density in g6pd3/5/6 seedlings was significantly higher than that in Col-0 seedlings. However, under Cd + HFJ311 treatment, the stomatal density was increased by 242.42% and 106.64% in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings, respectively, compared with Cd treatment alone (Fig. 3B, E). Thus, the stomatal density in g6pd3/5/6 seedlings was significantly lower than that in Col-0 under Cd + HFJ311 treatment.

Further analysis of chlorophyll (Chl) content showed that compared with controls, HFJ311 significantly increased the photosynthetic pigment content of Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings (Fig. 3F-H). Specifically, Chl a, Chl b, and Car were increased by 131.11%, 119.06%, and 141.87%, respectively, in Col-0 and by 62.82%, 122.24%, and 70.83%, respectively, in g6pd3/5/6. HFJ311 affects membrane integrity and ROS accumulation in both Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under Cd stress

Under natural conditions, plants maintain dynamic ROS homeostasis via precise regulation, as ROS act as key signaling molecules mediating plant growth, development, and environmental adaptation [39]. To elucidate the roles of G6PD3/5/6 in HFJ311-mediated ROS homeostasis under Cd stress, we analyzed H2O2 and O2·− accumulation using DAB and NBT staining, respectively. Results showed that compared with the control, treatment with HFJ311 or Cd alone induced substantial accumulation of H2O2 and O2·− in the shoots of both Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings, with a more pronounced effect observed in the HFJ311 alone treatment group (Fig. 4A-D). In addition, under Cd treatment, HFJ311 further increased the ROS content in shoots compared with Cd treatment alone. Notably, ROS accumulation was higher in g6pd3/5/6 mutant than that in Col-0 (Fig. 4A-D), suggesting that loss of G6PD3/5/6 function should impair the ROS scavenging capacity.

Excessive ROS induces lipid peroxidation in cell membranes, resulting in MDA accumulation and structural damage [40]. Relative electrolyte conductivity (REC) reflects membrane permeability, while MDA serves as a key marker of membrane lipid peroxidation. For this purpose, we measured REC and MDA levels in shoots of Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings. The results showed that compared with the control, HFJ311 had no effect on REC and MDA contents in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 shoots under 1/2MS + HFJ311 conditions (Fig. 4E-F). Cd treatment alone significantly increased these parameters in both genotypes. However, compared with Cd treatment, HFJ311 reduced the MDA content in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 shoots by 42.25% and 15.53%, respectively, and REC by 33.46% and 20.69%, respectively, under Cd + HFJ311 conditions (Fig. 4E-F). In addition, the MDA content and REC in g6pd3/5/6 shoots were 46.51% and 17.77% higher than those of Col-0, respectively (Fig. 4E-F), suggesting that mutation of G6PD3/5/6 impairs the scavenging capacity of ROS under Cd stress. Moreover, given the high ROS accumulation and pronounced growth-promoting phenotypes induced by HFJ311, ROS may play a positive regulatory role in the process.

HFJ311 is involved in the regulation of antioxidant system and NADPH homeostasis in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under Cd stress

Antioxidant enzymes are critical for ROS scavenging in plants, including SOD, POD, CAT, and APX [41]. To assess their roles under Cd stress, we examined the activities of these enzymes in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings. Under control conditions, there were no significant differences in four enzyme activities between Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings (Fig. S3A–D). Compared with controls, Cd treatment alone significantly up-regulated SOD, POD, and APX activities, with increases of 855.84%, 32.97%, and 120.54% in Col-0 and increases of 234.97%, 34.94% and 38.13% in g6pd3/5/6. Notably, Col-0 exhibited higher SOD (Fig. S3A) and APX (Fig. S3D) activities compared with g6pd3/5/6, while POD and CAT activities showed no significant difference between them (Fig. S3B, C). However, only SOD activity was significantly enhanced in both genotypes under Cd + HFJ311 treatments, with the activity in g6pd3/5/6 being lower than that in Col-0 (Fig. S3A). These results indicate that HF311 mainly regulates the SOD activity to enhance plant tolerance to Cd stress.

The ASA-GSH cycle in plants can scavenge excess H2O2 by oxidizing GSH to GSSG and ASA to dehydroascorbate (DHA) [41]. The regeneration of GSH and ASA depends on NADPH as a reducing force, and G6PD is one of its main sources in NADPH generation [42]. To further investigate the effects of G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 mutation on the ASA-GSH cycle and NADPH homeostasis, the ASA, GSH, and NADPH levels were analyzed. Under control conditions, there was no significant difference in ASA and GSH contents between Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings (Fig. 5A, D), but the DHA and GSSG contents in g6pd3/5/6 seedlings were significantly higher than those in Col-0 (Fig. 5B, E). After co-culture with HFJ311, the ASA and DHA contents in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings increased to 4.65 and 4.38 times of the control in Col-0, to 2.08 and 1.87 times in g6pd3/5/6, respectively (Fig. 5A, B). However, the GSH content remained unchanged (Fig. 5D), but the GSSG content decreased by 31.98% and 34.51% in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings, respectively (Fig. 5E). Additionally, the DHA and GSSG contents in g6pd3/5/6 shoots were still significantly higher than those in Col-0 under 1/2MS + HFJ311 conditions. Under Cd treatment alone, there was no significant difference in the ASA content between Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings compared with control conditions (Fig. 5A). Comparatively, the GSH content increased to 4.65 and 4.38 times of the control in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 shoots, respectively (Fig. 5D). Both DHA and GSSG contents were significantly increased by 133.85% and 595.15% in Col-0, and by 121.69% and 261.55% in g6pd3/5/6, respectively (Fig. 5B, E). Furthermore, the DHA content in g6pd3/5/6 shoots was significantly higher than that in Col-0, while the GSSG content was significantly lower. After co-treatment with HFJ311, the ASA content of both genotypes remained unchanged (Fig. 5A), while the DHA content was further increased by 126.56% and 113.61%, respectively (Fig. 5B). The GSH content in Col-0 was increased by 22.68% under Cd + HFJ311 conditions, while that in g6pd3/5/6 was decreased by 18.94% (Fig. 5D). The GSSG content was decreased by 19.88% and 37.21%, respectively (Fig. 5E). It is worth noting that under all treatment conditions, the ratios of ASA/DHA in g6pd3/5/6 shoots were significantly lower than those in Col-0 (Fig. 5C). Under control and 1/2MS + HFJ311 conditions, the GSH/GSSG ratio in g6pd3/5/6 seedlings was significantly higher than that in Col-0. Comparatively, there was no significant difference under Cd treatment alone and Cd + HFJ311 conditions (Fig. 5F).

To further elucidate the molecular mechanism of the enhanced Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis induced by HFJ311, the expressions of ZIPs, NRAMPs, and IRT1 were analyzed through qRT-PCR. HFJ311 alters NO homeostasis in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under Cd stress

NO, an essential signaling molecule, can promote plant growth in a concentration-dependent manner [47, 48]. To explore whether NO is involved in the growth-promoting effects of HFJ311 under Cd stress, we focused on the changes of NO levels in the roots by using the fluorescent probe DAF-FMDA. Results showed that the NO level in g6pd3/5/6 root tips was significantly stronger than that in Col-0 under Cd treatment alone and this enhancement was further intensified under Cd + HFJ311 conditions (Fig. 8A, B). To investigate whether the excessive accumulation of NO contributes to the growth inhibition observed in g6pd3/5/6, PTIO, a NO scavenger, was employed. Under Cd + HFJ311 conditions, exogenous application of 20 µM PTIO partially inhibited the growth-promoting effect of HFJ311 on Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6, with decreases of 43.19% and 29.91% in Col-0 shoots and roots, respectively, and 34.36% and 35.70% in g6pd3/5/6 shoots and roots, respectively (Fig. 8C-E). Discussion

Cd pollution is one of the most severe environmental threats to global agricultural productivity and ecological integrity [49]. AMF, colonizing Cd-contaminated soils, exhibits robust environmental adaptability and high spore production [50]. The symbiotic association between AMF and plants enhances Cd tolerance by expanding the root surface area and soil-root contact via extraradical hyphae, thereby promoting mineral nutrient and water uptake, while reducing root Cd absorption and restricting root-to-shoot Cd²+ translocation [11, 51]. Our previous study demonstrated that the Ustilago sp. strain HFJ311 can enhance Arabidopsis tolerance to Cd stress by facilitating root-to-shoot iron translocation and increasing endogenous IAA level [12]. As a rate-limiting enzyme in PPP, G6PD is well-documented to mediate plant responses to multiple abiotic stresses, including salinity, drought, and heavy metals (Al, Ni, Cd, Cu, Zn) [13, 18, 52, 53]. This study further revealed that HFJ311 enhances the tolerance of Arabidopsis to Cd stress by modulating the G6PD-dependent pathways.

Cytosolic and plastid G6PDs synergistically mediate HFJ311-induced Cd stress tolerance in Arabidopsis

The broad-spectrum plant-promoting activity of HFJ311 has been confirmed to enhance Arabidopsis tolerance to salinity, drought, and multiple heavy metals (Al, Zn, Cu, Cr, Cd) [12]. Co-cultivation with HFJ311 significantly increased shoot and root FW of Col-0 seedlings under both 1/2MS and Cd stress conditions (Fig. 1A-E), which is consistent with our previous findings [12]. It is reported that the endophyte Epichloë gansuensis can enhance Achnatherum tolerance to nitrogen deficiency by up-regulating the G6PD activity [54]. Our results showed that mutation of G6PD family genes did not affect the HFJ311-mediated growth promotion (Fig. 1A-E). However, single mutations of plastid G6PD3, cytosolic G6PD5 or cytosolic G6PD6 significantly impaired the HFJ311-induced Cd tolerance (Fig. 1A-E). Experiments with the g6pd3/5/6 triple mutants further confirmed that the dysfunction of G6PD3 and Cyt-G6PD only significantly attenuated the growth-promoting effect of HFJ311 on Arabidopsis shoots and roots under Cd stress conditions (Fig. 2A-E). Additionally, exogenous application of Glu, a G6PD inhibitor, significantly suppressed the growth-promoting effect of HFJ311 on Arabidopsis under Cd stress (Fig. S1A-C). Furthermore, qRT-PCR and GUS staining assays verified that HFJ311 significantly induced the expressions of G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 under Cd stress (Figs. 1 F, S2A-C). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that plastid G6PD3 and cytosolic G6PD5/G6PD6 are core isoforms that synergistically mediate the HFJ311-induced Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis.

Cd toxicity poses a severe threat to photosynthesis, which is a central process of plant growth and stress resistance. Cd-induced photosynthesis inhibition mainly includes two key pathways: first, Cd2+ competitively displaces Mg2+ in the porphyrin ring of Chl, disrupting δ-aminolevulinic acid synthesis and carbon assimilation [55]; second, Cd reduces stomatal conductance and photosynthetic pigment content, impairing CO2 diffusion and light energy capture [56]. Notably, mutations of G6PD3, G6PD5 and G6PD6 did not alter the growth-promoting effect of HFJ311 under 1/2 MS conditions in Arabidopsis (Fig. 3). In contrast, under Cd stress, mutants of G6PD3, G6PD5 and G6PD6 severely compromised HFJ311-induced growth-promotion, for example, the decreased petiole elongation, leaf area, stomatal density and the contents of Chl a, Chl b, and Car in g6pd3/5/6 mutants (Fig. 3A, C-H). These observations suggest that under normal conditions, alternative NADPH sources in cells can effectively compensate for the dysfunction of G6PD3, G6PD5 and G6PD6, for example, NADP-dependent malic enzyme, NADP-dependent isocitrate dehydrogenase, and folate-dependent one-carbon metabolism [57]. However, under Cd stress, high NADPH demand and excessive ROS production disrupt this balance. Meanwhile, Cd stress might damage the central carbon metabolism, inhibit the activity or flux of these alternative pathways, thereby limiting their compensatory capacity. Ultimately, these alternative pathways are unable to maintain intracellular redox homeostasis in g6pd3/5/6 seedlings, which in turn limits the growth-promoting effect of HFJ311.

Significantly, the stomatal density of g6pd3/5/6 displayed a condition‑dependent bidirectional pattern: higher than Col‑0 under HFJ311 treatment alone, but lower under co-treatment of Cd and HFJ311 (Fig. 3E). This result further indicates that G6PD isoforms specifically regulate HFJ311-mediated stomatal development under Cd stress, but does not under normal growth conditions. Overall, these data confirm that G6PD3, G6PD5 and G6PD6 are required for HFJ311 to maintain photosynthetic function under Cd stress, w16hich is the physiological basis for HFJ311 to improve Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis. They provide an important perspective for understanding the relationship between PPP and plant-microbe interactions under Cd stress.

HFJ311 alleviates Cd-induced oxidative damage by enhancing the antioxidant defense system in Arabidopsis

In plants, ROS can serve as key signaling molecules to regulate plant growth and environmental adaptability. However, excessive ROS leads to oxidative damage and disrupt cellular integrity [39, 58, 59]. The intracellular ROS homeostasis is maintained by enzymatic scavengers, e.g., SOD, CAT, POD, GR, and APX, and non-enzymatic systems, e.g., ASA, GSH, and α-tocopherol [60]. Hildebrandt et al. [61] reported that AMF enhances plant tolerance to heavy metal stresses by up-regulating antioxidant enzymatic activities and reinforcing the ASA-GSH cycle. In the present study, Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings exhibited significantly elevated H2O2 and O2·− levels in the shoots under Cd stress (Fig. 4A-D). Notably, HFJ311 further induced ROS accumulation in their shoots under both 1/2 MS and Cd stress conditions, but g6pd3/5/6 showed higher ROS content compared with Col-0 under Cd + HFJ311 conditions (Fig. 4A-D). The enzyme activity analysis results showed that Cd treatment alone up-regulates SOD, APX, and POD activities in Col-0 seedlings, with a higher increase of SOD and APX activities in Col-0 compared with g6pd3/5/6 seedlings (Fig. S3A, B, D). However, under Cd + HFJ311 co-treatment, only SOD activity was further enhanced (relative to Cd treatment alone) in both Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings, indicating a selective regulatory effect of HFJ311 on the SOD-mediated antioxidant pathway (Fig. S3A). Notably, there were higher POD, CAT, and APX activities in Col-0 than those in g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under Cd + HFJ311 treatment. Further results showed that MDA content and electrolyte leakage were significantly reduced in Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings under Cd + HFJ311 treatment compared with Cd treatment alone. However, g6pd3/5/6 seedlings still showed higher MDA content and electrolyte leakage than Col-0 (Fig. 4C-D). This observation implies that the G6PD3/5/6 genes might play a crucial role in maintaining the HFJ311-induced activities of these enzymes under Cd stress, which are vital for balancing cellular redox homeostasis.

The ASA-GSH cycle, as another critical pathway of oxidative defense in plants, uses NADPH as a reducing powder to remove H2O2 [62, 63]. Under Cd treatment alone, Col-0 maintained a higher ASA content and ASA/DHA ratio than g6pd3/5/6 seedlings (Fig. 5A-C), indicating more efficient ASA regeneration in Col-0. Under Cd + HFJ311 conditions, HFJ311 increased DHA levels in two genotypes, but did not eliminate the genotypic difference in ASA content and ASA/DHA ratio (Fig. 5A-C). This suggests that mutations of G6PD3/5/6 impair the reduction of DHA reduction from ASA, a reaction dependent on GSH and NADPH as reducing powder. Similarly, g6pd3/5/6 plants exhibited lower GSH contents, but similar GSH/GSSG ratio compared with Col-0 plants under Cd + HFJ311 treatment (Fig. 5D-F). Li et al. [64] reported that AMF regulates the ASA-GSH cycle via NADPH-dependent mechanisms under drought stress. Moreover, Col-0 maintained a higher NADPH content and NADPH/ NADP+ ratio than g6pd3/5/6 across all treatments (Fig. 5G, I), indicating plastid G6PD3 and Cyt-G6PD are essential for the maintenance of NADPH homeostasis in the HFJ311-induced Cd tolerance. As Dvořák et al. [65] reported, loss of core NADPH producers like G6PD renders plants to maintain the intracellular redox homeostasis through alternative pathways. Overall, these results demonstrate that HFJ311 enhances the defense of Arabidopsis against Cd stress by reinforcing antioxidant enzyme activities and the ASA-GSH cycle, both of which depend on G6PD3/5/6-dependent NADPH production.

Plastid G6PD3 and Cyt-G6PD are involved in the regulation of Cd transport and accumulation

Iron transporters (IRT1 and NRAMPs) and zinc transporters (ZIPs) are involved in the uptake and transport of Cd2+ by plants [43–45]. When plants are subjected to Cd stress, both types of transporters participate in the uptake of Cd2+and its loading and transport in the xylem, but IRT1 is only expressed in plant roots [46]. Under Cd stress, Cd2+ can compete with Fe2+ for IRT1, causing iron deficiency reactions in different plants, i.e., barley, Arabidopsis, and tobacco [66, 67]. Exogenous ABA can reduce the accumulation of Cd2+ in Arabidopsis by inhibiting the expression of IRT1, alleviating Cd toxicity [68]. NRAMP3 and NRAMP4 transporters are generally located on the vacuolar membrane and responsible for transporting Cd2+ into the vacuoles [45]. AMF inoculation into plants affects the uptake and transport of Cd2+ by influencing the expression of Cd transport genes in plant roots [69]. Our results indicate that Cd stress activates the expressions of Cd2+ uptake and transport genes in the shoots (ZIP1, ZIP3, ZIP4, NRAMP3, and NRAMP4) and roots (IRT1 and NRAMP4) of Col-0 seedlings (Fig. 6A-K). Under the treatment of Cd + HFJ311, mutations in G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 increased the expression levels of Cd2+ uptake and transport genes in the shoots but decreased the expression of IRT1 and NRAMP4 in the roots (Fig. 6A-K). Certain AMFs can promote the retention of heavy metals in plant roots and reduce their content in shoots by restricting their translocation from roots to shoots [70]. Under Cd stress, HFJ311 treatment reduced the Cd2+ content in the shoots and roots of Col-0 seedlings, while the transport rate of Cd2+ from roots to shoots was increased in g6pd3/5/6 seedlings, leading to an increase of Cd2+ content in shoots of g6pd3/5/6 seedlings (Fig. 7A-C). The above results indicate that under Cd stress, HFJ311 reduces Cd translocation from roots to shoots by downregulating the expression of Cd transporter genes and ultimately maintains a low Cd level in Arabidopsis shoots. In addition, G6PD3, G6PD5, and G6PD6 play essential roles in this process.

GSH not only acts as a reducing agent to scavenge ROS but also acts as a direct substrate for Cd2+ chelation mediated by PC synthase. PCs chelate Cd2+ to form PC-Cd (II) complexes, which are then transported into vacuoles via ABC transporters, reducing intracellular Cd2+ level and alleviating Cd toxicity [71]. Consistent with this mechanism, our results showed that g6pd3/5/6 mutants have lower GSH content than Col-0 under Cd+HFJ311 treatment (Fig. 5D), which may further lead to reduced formation of PC-Cd complexes and weaker vacuolar sequestration of Cd, indirectly promoting Cd translocation to shoots and aggravating Cd toxicity in shoots. This process is also dependent on the NADPH supply produced by G6PD3/5/6, since GSH regeneration requires NADPH as reducing power. Taken together, G6PD3/5/6 mediate the HFJ311-induced reduction of Cd accumulation in shoots by regulating both the expression of Cd transporter genes and the GSH-dependent chelation and vacuole sequestration of Cd, which is a key regulatory node in HFJ311-conferred Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis.

NO might be involved in the growth-promoting effect of HFJ311 in Arabidopsis under Cd stress

NO, a key signaling molecule in plants, can regulate plant growth in a concentration-dependent manner [47, 48]. Our results revealed that the NO level in root tips of the g6pd3/5/6 mutant was significantly higher than that in Col-0, and co-cultivation with HFJ311 further exacerbated NO accumulation under Cd stress (Fig. 8A, B). Wang et al. [52] showed that NO mediates G6PD-dependent Al stress tolerance in soybean, highlighting redox metabolism (via G6PD) as a potential regulator of NO homeostasis. Our experimental results showed that under Cd + HFJ311 treatment, exogenous application of PTIO (a NO scavenger) completely abolished the growth-promoting effects of HFJ311 in both two genotypes. Consequently, the biomass of Col-0 and g6pd3/5/6 seedlings showed no significant differences under 50–100 µM PTIO treatments (Fig. 8C-E). These results rule out excess NO as the cause of growth inhibition in g6pd3/5/6 under Cd + HFJ311. Meanwhile, these findings demonstrate that HFJ311-induced NO modulation is not the critical determinant underlying its alleviation of Cd sensitivity in g6pd3/5/6 mutants. Collectively, our findings demonstrate that NO is involved in the growth-promoting process of HFJ311. Moreover, the modulation of NO may act upstream of G6PD to confer Cd tolerance. More precise genetic and biochemical analyses are needed in the future to clarify the causal relationship between G6PD-mediated NADPH supply, NO homeostasis and plant Cd tolerance.

Conclusions

Collectively, plastid G6PD3 and cytosolic G6PD5/G6PD6 synergistically govern the HFJ311-induced Cd tolerance in Arabidopsis. Under Cd stress, HFJ311 improves plant growth, biomass and photosynthetic performance, but these benefits are abolished in g6pd3/5/6 triple mutants. HFJ311 up-regulates G6PD3/5/6 expressions to maintain NADPH homeostasis and enhance antioxidant capacity, thereby mitigating oxidative damage. HFJ311 also represses Cd uptake in roots via down-regulating key genes (IRT1, ZIPs, NRAMPs), reducing Cd translocation and accumulation. Mutations of G6PD3/5/6 disrupt redox balance, aggravate Cd accumulation and induce excessive NO production. Our findings reveal a novel mechanism where G6PD3/5/6 integrate redox homeostasis, Cd transport and NO signal to mediate endophyte-enhanced heavy metal tolerance. Our results provide a promising theoretical basis for developing fungi-assisted phytoremediation strategies in Cd- contaminated soils.

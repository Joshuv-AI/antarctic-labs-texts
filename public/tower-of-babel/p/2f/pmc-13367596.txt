A comparative analysis of vortioxetine vs. escitalopram on the clinical profile of patients with major depressive disorder
Authors: Saloni Mishra, Kashyap Shah, Manisha Menon, Varchasvi Mudgal, Koustubh R Bagul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Major depressive disorder (MDD) remains a leading cause of disability worldwide, necessitating effective pharmacological treatments. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) like escitalopram and newer antidepressants like vortioxetine are commonly prescribed for managing MDD. Although both medications are widely used, comparative data on their clinical and cognitive efficacy remains limited.Objective:This study aims to compare the clinical effectiveness of escitalopram and vortioxetine in alleviating depressive symptoms, with a particular focus on symptom reduction over a 4-week treatment period.Methods:This was a prospective, randomized, open-label study conducted at a tertiary care center from October 2023 to October 2024. A total of 100 patients diagnosed with MDD were randomly assigned to receive either escitalopram (10 mg/day, n = 50) or vortioxetine (10 mg/day, n = 50). Symptom severity was assessed using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) at baseline, 2 weeks, and 4 weeks. Statistical significance was evaluated using appropriate tests.Results:Both Escitalopram and Vortioxetine produced significant reductions in HAM-D scores over the 4-week period. By Week 4, escitalopram demonstrated a statistically significant superior effect in symptom reduction compared to vortioxetine (mean HAM-D score: 14.22 vs. 16.32, P = 0.04).Conclusion:Both escitalopram and vortioxetine effectively reduce depressive symptoms in patients with MDD, with escitalopram showing a more rapid onset of symptom relief. Further long-term studies are warranted to assess the sustained efficacy and cognitive benefits of both medications in diverse patient populations.

Introduction

Major depressive disorder (MDD) is a significant global health concern, ranking as the second leading cause of disability worldwide by 2030, according to the World Health Organization (WHO).[12] In countries like India, the prevalence of depression has been estimated at 3.3%, affecting approximately 45.1 million individuals.[2] These alarming statistics highlight the critical need for effective interventions, as MDD not only diminishes quality of life but also imposes a substantial socioeconomic burden.

Depression stands out as one of the most frequently identified mental health conditions in primary care settings,[345] reflecting its pervasive nature and significant impact on overall well-being. In India, the burden of depression is particularly concerning, with estimates suggesting that nearly one-third of individuals visiting healthcare facilities may exhibit symptoms indicative of this condition.[6] This highlights the importance of mental health awareness and screening in routine medical consultations.

The prevalence of mood disorders, including depression, varies widely across different regions and populations within the country, with reported rates ranging from 0.5 to 78 per 1,000 individuals.[6] This wide range underscores the influence of diverse factors such as socioeconomic status, cultural attitudes toward mental health, access to healthcare, and regional variations in diagnostic practices. These statistics emphasize the urgent need for comprehensive mental health services and tailored interventions to address the growing burden of depression in the Indian context.

Prompt diagnosis and effective treatment are essential to mitigating the impact of MDD on individuals and society at large.

Among the pharmacological treatments available for MDD, selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) like escitalopram have long been established as a first-line therapy.[7] Escitalopram demonstrates reliable efficacy and a favorable safety profile, making it a trusted option in both the acute and maintenance phases of MDD treatment. By selectively inhibiting the reuptake of serotonin (5-HT) into presynaptic neurons, escitalopram enhances serotonergic neurotransmission, which is crucial for alleviating depressive symptoms. According to a review conducted by Waugh et al. several randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter studies over 8 weeks have demonstrated that escitalopram, at doses of 10 or 20 mg/day, significantly outperformed placebo in improving standard measures of antidepressant effectiveness. These measures included the Montgomery-Åsberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS), the Clinical Global Impressions Improvement and Severity scales (CGI-I and CGI-S), and the Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression (HAM-D). Notably, symptom relief was observed quickly, with certain improvements evident within 1–2 weeks of initiating treatment with escitalopram.[89] Its tolerability and consistent therapeutic outcomes have cemented its place as a cornerstone of MDD management.

Vortioxetine, a bis-arylsulfanyl amine compound (1[2-(2,4-dimethyl-phenylsulfanyl)-phenyl]-piperazine-hydrobromide, Lu AA21004), is a novel multimodal antidepressant.[10] Unlike traditional SSRIs, vortioxetine not only inhibits serotonin reuptake but also modulates various serotonin receptor subtypes, including 5-HT3, 5-HT7, and 5-HT1D receptors (antagonist action), 5-HT1B (partial agonist), and 5-HT1A receptors (agonist action).[11] This unique pharmacodynamic profile suggests enhanced efficacy in addressing a broader range of depressive symptoms, including cognitive impairments and physical manifestations, which are often overlooked in conventional MDD treatment.[111213] Vortioxetine’s versatility across an approved dose range of 5–20 mg/day provides flexibility in tailoring treatment to individual patient needs.[14]

The addition of vortioxetine to the arsenal of antidepressant treatments is a promising advancement in MDD management. By targeting both core depressive symptoms and cognitive dysfunction, it addresses critical gaps in existing therapies. Comparative studies between established SSRIs like escitalopram and novel agents like vortioxetine are essential to optimizing treatment strategies and improving outcomes for individuals living with MDD.

Previous studies like Shin et al.[15] (2023) conducted a 6-week randomized, rater-blinded trial to compare the efficacy and safety of escitalopram, desvenlafaxine, and vortioxetine in treating anxious depression. All three medications significantly improved depressive and anxiety symptoms, with vortioxetine showing additional benefits for cognitive symptoms. The treatments were well-tolerated, with similar adverse event profiles, including nausea and dizziness. The study highlights vortioxetine’s potential advantage for patients with cognitive impairments, while confirming all three drugs as effective and safe options for anxious depression.

This study aims to explore how vortioxetine and escitalopram perform in alleviating depressive symptoms, with a particular focus on the speed of symptom alleviation, which holds significant relevance in primary care settings. Given the frequent diagnosis of depression in such settings, understanding the comparative effectiveness of these medications can guide practitioners in tailoring the most suitable treatment strategies. The insights gained from this research could help improve overall patient well-being and streamline management strategies for those dealing with depressive disorders in everyday healthcare practices.

Materials and Methods

This was a randomized, open-label, prospective study conducted at a tertiary care hospital from October 2023 to October 2024.

The sample size of 50 participants per group was determined based on the ability to detect a clinically meaningful difference in efficacy between vortioxetine and escitalopram using the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) as the primary outcome measure. Prior studies suggest that a difference of approximately 3 points on the HAM-D is both statistically and clinically significant in antidepressant trials. With a standard deviation of approximately 8 points observed in similar studies, this sample size provides sufficient power (around 80%) to detect a moderate effect size at a 5% significance level. Additionally, this sample size aligns with previous trials evaluating antidepressant efficacy, ensuring comparability with existing literature.[15] The choice also balances scientific rigor with practical considerations, including resource availability, feasibility of recruitment, and expected dropout rates, making it appropriate for the study objectives.

The study screened 883 individuals, of whom 250 met the inclusion criteria for MDD. A total of 100 participants were randomized to receive either escitalopram (10 mg/day, n = 50) or vortioxetine (10 mg/day, n = 50). Inclusion criteria were diagnosis of MDD based on ICD-10 criteria, age 18–65 years, Baseline Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D)[16] score ≥8, no contraindications to SSRIs, or vortioxetine, provided informed consent for the research. Exclusion criteria were co-existing substance use disorders or other psychiatric comorbidities, any patient exhibiting acute suicidal behavior, current use of cognitive-impairing medications, any other significant physical comorbidities like hypertension and diabetes, pregnancy or lactation, and any history of significant trauma or abuse.

Patients were randomized to receive either escitalopram (10 mg/day) or vortioxetine (10 mg/day) for a 4-week period. The dosage remained fixed throughout the study. No other concurrent pharmacological treatments were permitted [Figure 1].

Methodology

The primary outcome was the change in depressive symptoms, measured by the HAM-D scale at baseline, Week 2, and Week 4. Sociodemographic information, such as age, gender, and marital status, was also collected to identify potential confounders.

Data were analyzed using paired t-tests. A significance level of P < 0.05 was used to determine statistical significance.

The institution’s research ethics committee approved the research plan on September 11, 2023. Data used in the study were anonymized for confidentiality, and written consent was obtained from all participants. As this clinical trial was an academic trial, according to rule 2(a), CT 2019, “CT rules are not applicable, on an academic clinical trial of a drug already approved academic or research institution for a new indication or new route of administration or new dose or new dosage form, where the results of such a trial are intended to be used only for academic research purposes and not for seeking approval of the CLA or regulatory authority of any country for marketing or commercial purposes.”

Results

The mean age of participants in Group A (escitalopram) was 43 ± 13 years, and in Group B (vortioxetine), it was 40 ± 13 years. The sample was predominantly male, with 76% (38) of participants in the escitalopram group and 60%(30) in the vortioxetine group identifying as male. The majority of participants in both groups were married (68%, 34 in Group A and 62%, 31 in Group B). These demographics reflect the common treatment-seeking behaviors in this patient population.

Table 1 shows at baseline and the second week, there is no statistically significant difference in HAM-D scores between the two groups (P > 0.05). By the fourth week, however, the difference becomes statistically significant (t = -1.97, P = 0.04), suggesting that escitalopram may be more effective in reducing depressive symptoms than vortioxetine.

Discussion

The present study, conducted at a tertiary care hospital in central India, aimed to compare the clinical outcomes of escitalopram and vortioxetine in patients with MDD over a 4-week period. This was a prospective follow-up hospital-based study to compare the effect of vortioxetine versus escitalopram on clinal outcomes in depressive patients based on the HAM-D scale. In this study, patients diagnosed with the first episode of depression as per ICD-10 were included. There were 100 patients equally divided into two groups: Group A and Group B. Group A received escitalopram 10 mg, while Group B was administered vortioxetine 10 mg. Both groups had an equal number of patients (n = 50), ensuring a balanced comparison between the two drugs.

Both medications showed a significant reduction in depressive symptoms, as measured by the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D), indicating their efficacy in treating MDD. However, escitalopram demonstrated a slight, yet statistically significant, advantage in reducing HAM-D scores by the fourth week of treatment, highlighting its potential for faster therapeutic response. This suggests that escitalopram may offer quicker symptom relief in the initial stages of treatment for MDD, making it particularly beneficial for patients requiring prompt alleviation of symptoms. This finding aligns with prior studies that have consistently observed the rapid onset of action associated with SSRIs like escitalopram, further supporting its utility in clinical practice.[817]

Table 2 provides a breakdown of the demographic characteristics of the study participants. The mean age of participants in the escitalopram group (Group A) was 43 ± 13 years, while those in the vortioxetine group (Group B) had a mean age of 40 ± 13 years. This shows that the two groups were relatively well-matched in terms of age, reducing the risk of confounding effects due to age differences. Furthermore, males predominated in both groups (76% in Group A and 60% in Group B), which is consistent with the gender distribution observed in previous studies of MDD (Buckman et al., 2021).[18]

Participant Demographics

Marital status, as shown in Table 2, indicated that more married individuals participated in the study, particularly in the escitalopram group (68% in Group A vs. 62% in Group B). This may reflect the higher treatment-seeking behavior among married individuals, a finding that has been noted in previous depression research (Buckman et al., 2021).[18]

Table 1 presents the HAM-D scores at three different time points: baseline (0 week), second week, and fourth week. At baseline, both groups had nearly identical HAM-D scores, with the escitalopram group (Group A) showing a mean of 17.20 ± 5.33 and the vortioxetine group (Group B) showing a mean of 17.14 ± 5.17. This indicates that both groups had comparable levels of depressive severity at the start of the study, ensuring a fair baseline for comparison.

At the second week, both escitalopram and vortioxetine groups showed a reduction in HAM-D scores, with escitalopram reducing to 16.70 ± 5.25 and vortioxetine to 17.86 ± 5.19. However, the difference between the two groups was not statistically significant (P = 0.27). This suggests that while both medications led to symptom improvement, they were comparable in their effects during the early stages of treatment. By the fourth week, however, the difference between the two groups became statistically significant. The escitalopram group showed a mean HAM-D score of 14.22 ± 5.08, while the vortioxetine group had a mean of 16.32 ± 5.56. The t-value of -1.97 and the P value of 0.04 indicate that escitalopram resulted in a more significant reduction in depressive symptoms than vortioxetine by the fourth week. This aligns with previous studies indicating that escitalopram, a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI), tends to have a quicker onset of action compared to other classes of antidepressants.[1920]

The results from Table 1 suggest that escitalopram may offer faster symptom relief than vortioxetine, which could be clinically relevant, especially for patients who require rapid improvement in their depressive symptoms. Escitalopram’s faster symptom relief compared to vortioxetine may stem from its high selectivity for the serotonin reuptake transporter, leading to a more rapid increase in serotonin levels and quicker therapeutic effects. Its simpler SSRI mechanism and faster attainment of therapeutic concentrations likely contribute to earlier reductions in depressive symptoms, as observed in clinical studies by the fourth week of treatment. In primary healthcare, this advantage is particularly valuable for patients to develop therapeutic alliance and compliance. Additionally, faster improvement may enhance patient adherence and overall outcomes. Vortioxetine, while modulating multiple serotonin receptors and inhibiting serotonin reuptake, may require more time for its complex receptor interactions to translate into clinical benefits. Escitalopram’s early efficacy and cost-effectiveness make it a pragmatic first-line choice in primary care for treating MDD.

Similar results were found by Kasper et al.[21] (2006) whereby they compared the onset of action of escitalopram to other antidepressants through a pooled analysis. The findings suggest that escitalopram has a faster onset of therapeutic effects, which may enhance its clinical usefulness for treating depression. Other studies have found similar effects.[2223]

This study included various limitations such as a short 4-week follow-up, a small sample, and unaddressed factors like co-morbid anxiety. Long-term studies with neuroimaging, genetic analysis, and real-world trials are needed to better assess the sustained effects of vortioxetine and escitalopram. While this study provides valuable insights into the comparative efficacy of escitalopram and vortioxetine in the treatment of MDD, there are several limitations that need to be acknowledged. First, the study had a relatively short follow-up period of only 4 weeks. This time frame, while useful for assessing short-term symptom improvement, does not allow for an evaluation of the long-term effectiveness and safety of these medications. Longer follow-up studies are needed to better understand the sustained effects of escitalopram and vortioxetine on depressive symptoms and any potential relapse rates.

Additionally, the study did not assess cognitive outcomes, despite vortioxetine’s reputation for having cognitive benefits in MDD patients (Gonda et al., 2019).[11] Future studies could explore not only the clinical outcomes but also the impact of both medications on cognitive function, as cognitive symptoms are a major component of depression and can greatly affect the quality of life of patients.

The sample size, though balanced with 50 participants in each group, is relatively small and limits the generalizability of the findings. A larger sample size would improve the power of the study and help ensure that the observed differences are not due to random chance.

Finally, while the study excluded patients with comorbid anxiety or substance use disorders, these factors may still affect treatment outcomes in a broader population. Including a more diverse sample with different comorbidities could offer a more comprehensive understanding of how escitalopram and vortioxetine perform in a real-world clinical setting.

Conclusion

In conclusion, both escitalopram and vortioxetine are effective in reducing depressive symptoms in patients with MDD, as evidenced by the reduction in HAM-D scores over the 4-week treatment period. However, escitalopram showed a slight advantage in terms of the speed of symptom reduction by the fourth week. This suggests that escitalopram may be a better option for patients who require quicker symptom relief. Both medications were well-tolerated, with minimal side effects, making them suitable for use in clinical practice.

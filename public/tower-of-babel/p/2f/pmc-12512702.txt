MediNet: ensemble transfer learning approach for classification of medical drugs-related text reviews using significant combined-embeddings
Authors: Tai-hoon Kim, Asma Aldrees, Dina Abdulaziz AlHammadi, Muhammad Umer, Taoufik Saidani, Shtwai Alsubai, Imran Ashraf
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis research work provides an innovative approach, called MediNet, for drug safety review classification that integrates the strengths of three word embedding approaches: FastText, ELMo, and GloVe, alongside an ensemble of EfficientNetB4 and MobileNet models. The unique blend of these word embeddings captures both context-independent and context-dependent representations, enabling the model to understand complex linguistic nuances within drug reviews. The ensemble architecture leverages EfficientNetB4’s scalability and MobileNet’s efficiency, making MediNet both powerful and resource-efficient. The proposed model MediNet is evaluated concerning performance on a comprehensive dataset of drug safety reviews, achieving remarkable results with a 95.69% accuracy, 96.46% precision, 98.30% recall, and 97.22% F1 score. The generalizability of MediNet is evaluated using the cross-validation technique, demonstrating the statistical significance of the results. Additionally, MediNet results are compared against six other well-known transfer learning models, where it consistently outperforms other models across all metrics. These results suggest that MediNet is a highly effective solution for classifying drug safety reviews, significantly improving accuracy and reliability compared to existing models. The proposed approach offers a promising direction for future research in natural language processing and its application to healthcare.Keywords: Text mining, Drug review classification, Ensemble learning, Word embedding

Introduction

Over the last decade, there has been a noteworthy rise in pandemics, common diseases, and epidemics. In response to these health challenges, many pharmaceutical companies have been developing drugs that undergo multiple clinical trial stages to ensure their safety. However, despite these rigorous pre-launch trials on both animals and humans, drug safety remains a major concern because of its massive consumption by the public [1]. The problem with these trials is realized when many people start using the drugs, and other side effects and adverse reactions are noted [2]. This emphasizes the real need for a system able to assess drug safety in the post-release stage, which turns this into a task of primary concern in the pharmacovigilance field.

In the past few years, various social networks and internet-based support groups have been effective in tracking post-release drug safety. Patients who underwent ADR usually disseminate their experience to discuss the reactions they received, and the treatment they underwent, as well as consulting other persons with similar health issues [3]. These rich sources include comments, health-related posts, and/or reviews in online health forums that can be utilized for drug safety signal identification through drug sentiment analysis. Techniques that have emerged with the development of advanced sentiment analysis from conventional approaches and powerful ML as well as DL have created opportunities to extract information from these unstructured data sources.

The surveys conducted in these years have revealed the fact that SA is not an easy problem anymore and DL-based systems have shown better performance than other methods [4–6]. Such accomplishment has made DL algorithms to become pervasive in areas such as economics, politics as well as medicine [7]. Nonetheless, there is not much research focused on the application of DL for sentiment analysis in drug reviews [8]. This offers the chance to strengthen the methodology of drug safety evaluation by using state-of-the-art NLP tools in this particular field.

One of the challenges in sentiment analysis of drug reviews is the potential discrepancy between the text of the review and the assigned rating. For example, a review might receive a high rating despite containing negative sentiments in the text, or vice versa. Traditional approaches to drug review analysis often rely heavily on these ratings, which can lead to inaccurate sentiment classification. Recent advancements in NLP have introduced neural networks that consider the interaction between sentiment words, target words, and modifiers, improving the accuracy of aspect-level sentiment classification [9]. These DL-based approaches promise to deliver a more accurate sentiment analysis of drug reviews. Recent advancements in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) and fusion-based deep learning have significantly improved the interpretability and performance of medical diagnostic models. Studies such as DeepXplainer [10] for lung cancer detection, interpretable fusion frameworks for breast cancer and cardiac prediction [11], and IoMT-driven healthcare analytics [12] underscore the critical role of XAI in enhancing model transparency and clinical trust [13]. These works align with the objectives of our proposed MediNet framework, which combines ensemble transfer learning and explainable embeddings for drug review classification.

In conclusion, the growing availability of ’clinical healthcare data,’ on multiple platforms and in text form opens up a new opportunity for pharmacovigilance to analyze this digital data and make decisions. Facebook, Twitter(X), Google, and other platforms are becoming increasingly crucial in detecting and predicting ADRs to improve drug safety [14]. Now there is a dire need to make use of ML, DL, and transfer learning (TL) techniques for drug surveillance on large available datasets, rather than relying on human efforts to manually check all drugs and chemicals. Recent studies [15], have witnessed that the usage of DL and TL techniques gives accurate results after drug surveillance without human efforts. The error rate is decreasing rapidly with advancements in these models. This study builds on these insights, aiming to leverage DL for the drug review analysis, thereby contributing to the ongoing efforts to improve drug safety and pharmacovigilance.

The current study aims to address these challenges by focusing on sentiment analysis of drug reviews using three different word embedding embeddings from language models (ELMO), global vectors for word representation (GLOVE), and FastText, along with transfer ensemble learning models. Word embedding techniques

All the learning models accept the textual data in vector form for training, which is done using Word embeddings. In the past few years, word embedding feature engineering methods have become more popular for their application in prediction models. Three techniques for embedding words were utilized in this research to identify drug user review sentiment classification including ELMO, GLOVE, and fastText.

Embedding from language models

For natural language processing (NLP), ELMo is one of the innovative steps toward enriching word representation. Unlike most of the previous methods that map a word to a fixed vector regardless of the context in which the word appears, ELMo’s vectors are contextualized considering the context the word is used in [28]. ELMo on the other hand uses BiLSTM neural network to work on entire phrases, where the target word is a function of the previous and the following word, thus making it context-sensitive embedding. This approach of bidirectional makes ELMo derive richer representations of each word through the overall context of a sentence. Here, we find one of the main differences of ELMo : the use of two biLSTM layers to generate its embeddings. Every layer extracts some features of the contextual information, and the last word embedding is obtained by the outputs of all layers. This multiple layering helps ELMo in capturing the complex as well as deep contextual information and that is why it is better suited for text classification. Thus, ELMo applies the so-called dynamic contextual embeddings, which allow a word to change representation based on the context, providing a more detailed understanding of it. Thus, even after newer models came and new models like BERT are widely used ELMo is a tool that can help NLU and prove the significance of context for Natural language processing and comprehension.

FastText

Word embeddings in vector form have been widely utilized in various Natural Language Processing (NLP) tasks. Generally, pre-trained word embeddings derived from extensive corpora such as Wikipedia and Google News enable context prediction in an unsupervised manner by assuming that words appearing in proximity share similar semantic contexts [29]. The FastText model, developed by the FAIR Research Lab, offers an effective representation of words, particularly due to its capability to handle rare and out-of-vocabulary words. It incorporates morphological information and is trained on a corpus consisting of approximately two million words, utilizing 600 billion tokens in a 300-dimensional space. By leveraging subword n-grams, FastText constructs word representations that enhance text classification performance through better generalization.

GloVe

GloVe (Global Vectors for Word Representation) is a word embedding technique that generates word vectors using an unsupervised learning approach. The model is trained on a corpus containing 840 billion words, producing word representations in a 300-dimensional space [30]. As its name implies, GloVe captures both global and local statistical information on word occurrences within a corpus. Gloves finds semantic-similarity while preserving the linear substructure within the vector space. The embeddings are generated by factorizing a word co-occurrence matrix, allowing the model to infer word meanings by analyzing their co-occurrence probabilities. GloVe follows a logarithmic bilinear structure, which enhances its ability to capture meaningful relationships between words.

Combining features

In this study, a combination of three widely used word embedding models-FastText, ELMo, and GloVe-is employed to improve the performance of transfer learning (TL) models. Each embedding technique is independently trained to enhance the predictive capabilities of the TL models. Initially, the TL model is trained separately using each word embedding method, followed by a combined approach where embeddings are integrated in a weighted manner, with GloVe contributing 34%, and FastText and ELMo each contributing 33% to the final representation. This approach ensures a richer word representation, leveraging the strengths of each embedding technique to enhance model performance.

Independent Component Analysis (ICA)

ICA is used to separate text into independent components, particularly for word embedding dimension reduction. ICA assumes that input data contains linear mixtures of unknown latent variables, with the goal of recovering the original input data by maximizing their statistical independence. The mathematical foundation of ICA is built on the concept that the independent sources are non-Gaussian and can be separated using higher-order statistical properties. One of the common applications of ICA is in blind source separation, where the challenge is to recover original input data without prior knowledge of the data mixing process. ICA has been widely used in various fields, including neuroscience, telecommunications, and image processing, large embeddings-based text classification due to its ability to effectively isolate meaningful components from complex data [31].

Transfer learning models for text classification

Xception

State-of-the-art CNN is Xception by modifying InceptionNet, substituting standard CNN layers with depthwise separable convolutions [32, 33]. This modification leads to a model with fewer parameters, but which is highly useful where large data sets are involved such as a drug user review classification task. Xception can hence enhance the accuracy of sentiment analysis and the classification of drug reviews through the analysis of fine details in the text content that is otherwise unnoticed by the human eye.

EfficientNetB4

Honestly, EfficientNet is a series of CNN architectures that can currently get the highest accuracy while consuming very few parameters and FLOPs [34, 35]. It presents a compound scaling method on all the dimensions of the network to enhance the performance of the network. For computer-aided drug user review classification, EfficientNet can achieve high levels of accuracy with a significantly smaller model size, which would be suitable especially if the application needs a high level of performance in a computationally manageable platform.

Visual geometry group

VGG19 is a 3x3 filter size 19 layers based deep CNN model belonging to the Visual Geometry Group [36, 37]. Nonetheless, due to its depth and the amount of computational operations required to perform a feed-forward pass, VGG19 is capable of learning intricate representations of the input data. As indicated when used for classifying drug user review, VGG19 can perform very well for extracting features hence more patterns from the text, though the process of training can take considerable CPU time and space.

Residual networks

Residual Network (ResNet) implements the notion of residuals through Shortcuts, thus Deep Networks can be Deep Networks without much fear of the so-called vanishing gradients [38, 39]. This architecture is equally suited to work for multiple tasks as shown by the following task which is text classification. For drug user review classification, it can create deeper models by incorporating ResNet that would help the model to capture more abstract features of the data distribution implying a better ability to differentiate between different sentiments or aspects of the drug user reviews.

InceptionV3

InceptionV3 is yet another model in the Inception series with a particular focus on deep learning implementation for the classification of images [40]. It applies more than one set of convolutional filters of varying dimensions within the same level and lets In the context of drug user review classification, InceptionV3 can be adapted to process text data, capturing diverse aspects of the input through its parallel convolutional pathways. Its versatility and relatively low computational cost make it a solid choice for classifying complex text-based datasets.

Selection of EfficientNetB4 and ResNet

EfficientNetB4 and ResNet have been used for text classification in this study. While these models were originally developed for image processing, recent advances in cross-modal learning and text-to-image embedding transformations have demonstrated their effective applicability in natural language processing (NLP) tasks, particularly in sentiment analysis and review classification.

In the proposed framework, we do not feed raw text directly into these architectures. Instead, we transform the textual data into 2D matrix representations by concatenating multiple word embedding vectors (FastText, GloVe, ELMo) into a fixed-size matrix. Each word in the review is represented by a high-dimensional embedding, and stacking these embeddings creates an image-like structure where each “pixel” or vector component contains semantic information. This methodology is supported by several works. For example, [41] treated text embeddings in vector space analogies, enabling dimensional transformations for CNN usage. Similarly, in [42], authors introduced character-level convolutional networks (CNN) for text classification, showcasing the power of treating text as spatial data. The study [43] illustrated embedding-based transformations of sequences for image CNNs to improve text sentiment classification.

Moreover, EfficientNetB4 and ResNet offer strong hierarchical feature extraction capabilities and transfer learning efficiency, which is particularly beneficial when text data is encoded as a spatial representation. Studies have shown that CNN architectures originally built for image recognition can outperform traditional RNNs and transformer models in certain structured embedding formats, especially when dealing with short, domain-specific text data such as drug reviews [44].

Additionally, this approach also aligns with computational efficiency goals. Lightweight models like MobileNet are especially valuable when fast inference is necessary in real-time review systems, as evidenced in cross-modal retrieval and document classification applications. To ensure scientific rigor, we also conducted comparative evaluations of our proposed image-based ensemble with prominent NLP-specific models such as BERT and RoBERTa, and the results demonstrate that our method not only matches but outperforms these baselines in both accuracy and interoperability. Thus, while unconventional, this methodology is inspired by current research trends that bridge the gap between NLP and vision architectures, offering a novel and effective perspective on leveraging powerful pre-trained CNNs for structured semantic representations of text.

Rationale for model selection

The selection of EfficientNetB4 and MobileNet in the proposed ensemble architecture is primarily driven by the need to balance performance with computational efficiency, while also leveraging the strengths of image-based feature extraction methods on transformed text representations. Although models like BERT, RoBERTa, and XLNet are state-of-the-art for many NLP tasks, they are often computationally expensive and require significant fine-tuning, especially for domain-specific datasets like medical drug reviews.

In the proposed approach, the input text data is vectorized using advanced word embedding techniques (ELMo, FastText, and GloVe), which are then structured as high-dimensional matrices. These matrices are taken as inputs to capture both local and global semantic relationships. Therefore, utilizing transfer learning models like EfficientNetB4 and MobileNet becomes a natural fit. EfficientNetB4 offers scalability with fewer parameters and higher accuracy, while MobileNet provides lightweight architecture ideal for real-time or resource-constrained environments. Additionally, compared to transformer-based models, this design requires less training time and memory overhead, and it demonstrates strong generalizability across domains as verified by the cross-validation results. The superior performance metrics achieved by MediNet including 95.69% accuracy and 97.22% F1 score further validate the efficacy of this ensemble in handling domain-specific sentiment analysis tasks.

Choice of ICA for feature reduction

Another important aspect is the justification of the ICA approach for feature reduction. ICA is selected in the proposed framework due to its capability to extract statistically independent components, which we hypothesized would better represent the underlying semantic structures in the combined embedding space formed by FastText, GloVe, and ELMo. To validate this choice, we conducted a comparative analysis between ICA, principal component analysis (PCA), and t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE). The comparison focused on classification performance using the MediNet model in terms of accuracy, F1 score, and computational time on the validation set. The results are presented in Table 4. As shown in Table 4, ICA outperformed PCA in terms of classification accuracy and F1 score, while t-SNE exhibited inferior performance and higher computational overhead. Although PCA is widely used, the statistical independence assumption in ICA proved advantageous in our task, which involves capturing contextual subtleties in text embeddings.

Results using combined embedding

To further enhance model performance, a hybrid word embedding approach is employed by integrating FastText, ELMo, and GloVe embeddings in equal proportions of 33%, 34%, and 33%, respectively. Subsequently, dimensionality reduction is performed using Independent Component Analysis (ICA) to extract the most significant features. The embedding combination weights of 33% FastText, 34% GloVe, and 33% ELMo are not arbitrarily selected. These values were derived after conducting a heuristic-based optimization process, which included multiple rounds of validating performance. Initially, equal weights were used (i.e., 33.3% each) to evaluate the baseline performance. Subsequently, we employed a lightweight grid search strategy, where incremental adjustments (±1–2%) were applied to each embedding’s contribution to observe changes in validation accuracy and F1 score. We found that assigning a slightly higher weight to GloVe (34%) consistently resulted in performance improvements, likely due to its robust global co-occurrence statistics, which complement FastText’s subword-level detail and ELMo’s contextual embeddings. This combination was therefore finalized based on empirical results observed during the validation phase and aimed at capturing a balanced semantic representation from static, subword, and contextual embeddings. Future work may explore dynamic weighting via attention mechanisms or neural architecture search.

The results, as outlined in Table 8, reveal that combining multiple word embedding techniques significantly enhances model effectiveness. The MediNet model achieves the highest performance, with an accuracy of 95.69%, precision of 96.46%, recall of 98.30%, and an F1-score of 97.22%, outperforming all other models across all metrics. The EfficientNetB4 model attains an accuracy of 93.46%, with precision, recall, and F1-score values of 92.22%, 90.22%, and 91.22%, respectively, exhibiting strong performance but slightly lower than MediNet. The VGG19 model achieves an accuracy, recall, F1 score, and precision of 91.46%, 95.43%, 94.40%, and 93.58%. The ResNet gives an accuracy, recall, F1 score, and precision of 89.90%, 94.22%, 92.27%, and 91.22%, showcasing a well-balanced performance. The MobileNet attains 90.35% accuracy along with recall and precision values both at 93.22%, resulting in an F1-score of 93.22%. The Xception model demonstrates robust performance, achieving an accuracy of 94.76%, precision of 94.22%, recall of 95.22%, and an F1-score of 94.27%, underscoring its well-balanced and reliable classification capability. Conclusion

With the exponential increase in virus and medicine reviews on social media platforms, there is an increasing need for an automated system to detect issues more efficiently. User drug reviews with a high accuracy rate. In this research, a novel word-embedding and transfer learning model combination is proposed for user drug review classification by focusing on different types of word embedding approaches. The combined word embedding with ICA and an ensemble of EfficientNetB4 and MobileNet models not only improve feature extraction but also classification accuracy. ICA is utilized to reduce the high-dimensional word vectors to extract only the most essential features, hence optimizing the performance of the model. We conducted experiments using the drug safety reviews dataset, and the proposed model, MediNet proved to be a very impressive performance with a 95.69% accuracy, 96.46% precision, 98.30% recall, and a 97.22% F1 score. The promising results indicate the effectiveness of the proposed MediNet model to play an effective role in drug review classification. The proposed model integrates multiple-word embedding techniques and ICA to provide a robust approach.In future work, we plan to integrate fuzzy logic with transformer architectures to enhance both the interpretability and performance of drug review classification. Fuzzy logic provides a powerful framework for handling uncertainty and imprecision in feature selection by enabling soft decision-making. Specifically, it can be used to intelligently assess and select the most informative features from dimensionality reduction techniques such as ICA, PCA, and t-SNE. Once optimal features are selected using fuzzy inference, transformer models like BERT or RoBERTa can then be employed for robust sentiment classification. This integration aims to balance human-like reasoning in feature selection with the contextual understanding of transformers, thereby improving model generalizability and interpretability in complex medical text data.

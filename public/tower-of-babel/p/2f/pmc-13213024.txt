Knowledge and attitudes toward mpox among the Lebanese population during the second wave and their attitudes toward vaccination
Authors: Mohammad Tanashat, Nour Fakih, Nour El Bizri, Mohamed Saad Rakab, Mohamed Abouzid, Karen Sleiman, Hadi Hassan, Moetasem Al Sayed, Zahraa Zibara, Ahmad Afyouni, Pascale Salameh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMpox has spread beyond endemic regions, with cases reported in Lebanon. Public knowledge, perceptions, and vaccine acceptability may have evolved due to increased media coverage and global outbreaks. This study assesses the Lebanese population’s knowledge and attitudes toward mpox during the second wave, as well as their willingness to receive the vaccine. A cross-sectional online survey was conducted among 2343 Lebanese residents. Participants’ knowledge and attitudes were assessed using a 26-point knowledge index and a Likert scale via an online validated questionnaire. Multiple linear regression analyses identified determinants influencing knowledge, attitudes, and vaccine acceptance. The mean knowledge index for the second wave was 9.36 with a standard deviation (SD) of 6.76, showing no significant improvement from the first wave. Key predictors of higher knowledge included healthcare worker status and the presence of comorbidities, while reliance on social media was associated with lower scores. Attitudes remained stable and moderately positive (mean: 11.76, SD: 2.62), yet vaccine acceptance declined from 56.4% to 37.1%. A correlation matrix revealed a weak negative link between knowledge and attitude, and a modest positive association between attitude and vaccine acceptability. Hesitancy was highest among women and rural residents. Introduction

Mpox (formerly known as monkeypox)1 is a zoonotic disease caused by the mpox virus, historically regarded as a regional public health issue2, with cases reported primarily in Central and West Africa3. Mpox has often been overshadowed by other viral threats, such as COVID-19, measles, and dengue, due to overlapping clinical presentations4,5. In 2022, an unprecedented global outbreak marked a significant shift, providing evidence of sustained human-to-human transmission beyond endemic regions6,7. A key factor contributing to this surge is the waning immunity from smallpox vaccination, which was discontinued following smallpox eradication in 1980, given the cross-protection between the two viruses8. The absence of smallpox-induced immunity has left younger generations more vulnerable, with severe cases and fatalities predominantly affecting children too young to have been vaccinated 9. In response, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern in July 2022 10, prompting global containment efforts, including vaccination campaigns and public health education initiatives 11.

The effectiveness of infectious disease control strategies is heavily influenced by public knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors12. The media have played a pivotal role in influencing public perceptions, both by raising awareness and, conversely, by facilitating the spread of misinformation13 and fueling distrust toward public health recommendations, highlighting the need to assess individuals’ sources of health information14. Moreover, public concern about emerging infectious diseases tends to decline over time due to psychological phenomena such as risk habituation and normalization 15, which reduce perceived threat levels and protective behaviors like vaccination 16.

Lebanon presents a unique context for studying responses to emerging infectious diseases. The country’s healthcare system has been severely weakened by ongoing economic crises, political instability, and fragile health infrastructure17. Vaccine hesitancy, driven by misinformation and concerns regarding efficacy and safety, remains a persistent challenge18. The emergence of mpox within this context raises critical questions about public perception and willingness to adopt preventive measures, including vaccination.

While a previous study assessed public knowledge and attitudes during the initial mpox outbreak in Lebanon19, little is known about how these perceptions have evolved in subsequent phases. The period following the first outbreak was marked by intensified media coverage, global public health alerts, and widespread dissemination of both accurate and inaccurate information. This offers a chance to explore whether increased exposure improved public understanding and vaccine acceptance, or, conversely, fostered confusion, complacency, or hesitancy. By focusing on the second wave, this study seeks to identify shifts in knowledge, attitudes, and behavioral intentions while considering media influence and psychological desensitization, to ultimately understand how public perception evolves over time in response to emerging infectious diseases, particularly in fragile healthcare settings.

Methods

Study Design and Setting

This national cross-sectional study aimed to assess knowledge, attitudes, and perceptions regarding mpox among the general adult population in Lebanon, using an online self-administered questionnaire.

Study Population and Sampling

The study targeted individuals aged 18 years and older residing in Lebanon. A total of 2,343 participants were selected via snowball sampling. The survey link was distributed via email, social media platforms, and messaging applications, targeting individuals from diverse regions and sociodemographic backgrounds to ensure broad representation across Lebanon’s population.

Ethical Considerations

The study adhered to the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from Al Sahel Hospital (Approval No. 11/2024). Informed consent was obtained electronically from all participants before enrolment. Data were collected anonymously and handled with strict confidentiality.

Survey Instrument and Data Collection

The survey, adapted from a previous study19 that assessed public knowledge and attitudes during Lebanon’s first mpox wave, was pilot-tested for clarity and showed acceptable overall reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.702). When assessed by subscale, the knowledge scale showed good internal consistency (α = 0.775), while the attitude scale demonstrated moderate internal consistency (α = 0.592) (Appendix, Supplementary Table 1).

We administered the survey in Arabic and English via an online platform between June 1 and July 15, 2024, to ensure nationwide reach. Participants could read the same question in both languages for better understanding.

The questionnaire included four sections19:

Scoring and Analysis of Scales

Knowledge items were coded as 1 for correct responses and 0 for incorrect or “don’t know” responses and summed to generate a total knowledge score. The knowledge scale consisted of 26 items, yielding a possible score range of 0–26, with higher scores indicating better knowledge. For interpretative purposes, scores < 13 (< 50% of the maximum) were classified as poor knowledge, scores of 13–19 (50–75%) as moderate knowledge, and scores ≥ 20 (> 75%) as good knowledge.

Attitudes were assessed using four Likert-type items rated from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”), which were summed to produce a total attitude score ranging from 4 to 20. Negatively worded items were reverse-coded so that higher scores reflected more positive attitudes toward mpox. Attitude scores were categorized as negative (4–9), moderate (10–14), and positive (15–20).

Item-level psychometric diagnostics were conducted for the attitude subscale, including corrected item–total correlations and Cronbach’s alpha-if-item-deleted analysis (Appendix, Supplementary Table 2). Three of the four items demonstrated acceptable corrected item–total correlations (0.342–0.493). The perceived risk of infection item showed a lower correlation (r = 0.213); however, its removal would have only marginally increased internal consistency (α = 0.626), and it was retained based on theoretical relevance and content validity.

Vaccine-related perceptions were measured with Likert items (1–5), summed to form a total perception score after appropriate reverse-coding, with higher scores indicating more favorable perceptions of the vaccine. For regression analyses, we dichotomized responses into “willing” versus “hesitant/unwilling”. Further details are provided in Section “Statistical analysis”.

Statistical Analysis

Data were analyzed using Jamovi software (version 2.4.8). Only complete data (93.7%) were analyzed. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were presented as means with standard deviations (SD). Bivariate analyses were conducted to examine associations between independent variables and outcomes of interest. The Chi-square test was used for categorical variables, and the Mann–Whitney U or Kruskal–Wallis test was applied for comparisons between two or more groups, respectively. Correlation analyses were also conducted to explore relationships between knowledge, attitudes, and vaccine acceptance. Multiple linear regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with knowledge scores, attitude scores, and willingness to receive both smallpox- and mpox-specific vaccines. Assumptions related to multiple regression were checked, including linearity, normality of residuals, absence of collinearity, and homoscedasticity. All variables included in the regression models were selected based on theoretical relevance and prior study findings. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and significance was defined as a p-value < 0.05. Sensitivity analyses were conducted by re-running regression models using individual attitude items as separate predictors; these analyses yielded patterns of association consistent with the primary models (Appendix, Supplementary Tables 3–6).

Attitudes toward Mpox

The mean attitude score was 11.76 ± 2.62, indicating a moderate overall attitude. Most respondents (70.7%) perceived their risk of infection as low or very low; 26.5% rated it moderate, and only 2.8% rated it high or very high.

As shown in Fig. 2, 42.5% expressed neutral concern about contracting mpox, 38.1% disagreed to varying extents, and 29.2% agreed. When asked whether mpox affects or will affect their daily life, 30.5% were neutral. Regarding disease severity, 55.5% considered mpox a serious illness, while 28.5% were neutral and 16% disagreed.

Respondents’ Perceptions of Risk and Concern Regarding Mpox (n = 2343).

Overall, while over half of the respondents acknowledged the seriousness of mpox, fewer believed it would personally affect them.

Vaccination against Mpox

When asked about the effectiveness of the smallpox vaccine against mpox, 42.9% believed it was effective (Table 3). A similar proportion (42.6%) reported prior smallpox vaccination. Regarding COVID-19 vaccination, 70.3% had received at least one dose, 44.7% had two doses, and 15.2% had received three or more.

Determinants of Mpox Knowledge

Multiple linear regression analysis revealed that female gender (B = 1.48, p < 0.001, 95% CI: 0.77–2.19), presence of comorbidities (B = 1.90, p < 0.001, 95% CI: 1.25–2.54), and being a healthcare worker (B = 2.78, p < 0.001, 95% CI: 1.90–3.65) were significantly associated with higher knowledge scores. Rural residence was modestly associated with increased knowledge (B = 0.65, p = 0.040, 95% CI: 0.03–1.28).

Conversely, reliance on social media (B = − 1.79, p < 0.001) or TV/radio (B = − 2.80, p < 0.001) was associated with significantly lower knowledge scores. In contrast, reliance on less reliable sources of information was linked to lower knowledge levels (Table 4).

Determinants of Mpox Vaccine Acceptability

Males showed significantly higher willingness to receive the smallpox vaccine compared to females (mean = 3.09 ± 1.15 vs. 2.97 ± 1.13; p = 0.023). Willingness decreased with age (r = − 0.196, p < 0.001) and was lower among those with less education, smokers, and physically inactive individuals (p < 0.001 for all; p = 0.005 for inactivity). In contrast, students, those without comorbidities, individuals with balanced socioeconomic status, and healthcare workers reported greater willingness (p < 0.001). Those using medical sources or newspapers/magazines were more willing than those relying on social media or TV/radio (p < 0.001). Higher knowledge (r = 0.056, p = 0.019) and positive attitudes (r = 0.166, p < 0.001) were also linked to greater willingness.

Similar trends emerged for the mpox-specific vaccine. Males (mean = 2.98 ± 1.13 vs. 2.87 ± 1.13; p = 0.032), younger participants (r = − 0.202, p < 0.001), students, and healthcare workers showed more willingness, while lower education, smoking, and inactivity predicted less (p < 0.001). Urban residents were also more willing than rural residents (p = 0.033). Positive attitudes (r = 0.187, p < 0.001), but not knowledge (r = 0.021, p = 0.384), remained a significant predictor. Willingness to Receive a Specific Mpox Vaccine

Discussion

This study (N = 2343) evaluates Lebanese responses to mpox during the second outbreak and reflects shifts following increased media coverage and WHO alerts compared to those in 2023 (N = 493) 19.

Knowledge of Mpox and Influencing Factors

The overall knowledge score was markedly low, reflecting significant public misunderstanding of mpox. Similar trends were reported in non-endemic countries where public awareness of emerging infectious diseases remains limited 20–22. In contrast, China’s higher awareness (~ 60%)23 highlights the impact of centralized, government-led communication. Misconceptions were widespread; while 66.5% knew mpox is viral, 12.4% believed it is bacterial, echoing early COVID-19 confusion24. Only half correctly identified Africa as the endemic region, with many misattributing it to Oceania. Clinical knowledge was also weak: nearly 50% were unsure of the incubation period, and fewer than 40% knew symptom duration, consistent with patterns seen in Ebola and Zika outbreaks 25.

Transmission knowledge was limited, only 57.7% recognized direct contact, and fewer identified respiratory or zoonotic routes, hindering preventive behavior as noted in Middle East Respiratory Syndrome Coronavirus studies 26.

A critical and ethically significant finding was the prevalence of stigmatizing beliefs. Over 30% associated mpox primarily with homosexual individuals, reminiscent of the early HIV/AIDS era 27. Such stigmatization distorts public understanding, risks marginalizing affected communities, and worsens outbreak control.

The role of information sources was pronounced. Social media was the main information source correlating with lower knowledge, reinforcing its role in spreading misinformation, as seen in COVID-19 28. In contrast, users of medical or governmental sources had better knowledge, emphasizing the need for access to reliable information.

Sociodemographic analysis revealed disparities in knowledge. Higher education was strongly linked to better knowledge, echoing findings from China, Taiwan, and Saudi Arabia 21,29,30. This suggests that health literacy, fostered through formal education, plays a role in shaping public awareness.

Healthcare workers scored higher (mean = 15.74 ± 4.77), consistent with Saudi data 21. Nevertheless, their knowledge remained insufficient, reflecting global trends where healthcare professionals demonstrate gaps in understanding emerging diseases 31–35, thus highlighting the need for ongoing training during outbreaks36 37.

Unexpectedly, married individuals and Beirut residents had lower knowledge scores. Despite better access to information in urban areas, this may reflect complacency or information fatigue 38. Married individuals may also face time constraints or shifting priorities, affecting their engagement with health information. Similar patterns were observed in Saudi Arabia and during COVID-19, where married participants showed poorer knowledge 39​, 40,41. These findings highlight the need for region-specific and family-oriented health communication strategies.

Compared to the first outbreak study (mean = 12.72 ± 4.83) 19, knowledge has declined, thus reinforcing that without sustained education, public understanding may wane as media attention diminishes.

Attitudes toward Mpox and Factors Influencing Concern

Participants displayed moderately neutral attitudes toward mpox (mean = 11.76 ± 2.62 out of 20). While 55.5% viewed the illness as serious, 70.7% perceived low personal risk, indicating limited concern despite awareness. These results closely mirror findings from the first wave (11.78 ± 2.63) 19.

Gender significantly influenced attitudes: females scored higher (12.02 ± 2.57) than males (11.19 ± 2.75), consistent with global observations that women generally exhibit greater health risk sensitivity during crises 42,43. This recurring trend supports the implementation of gender-sensitive public health messaging. Education also shaped attitudes. Participants with higher educational attainment viewed mpox as more serious, contrasting with the first wave, where education correlated negatively with concern 19. This shift may reflect changing access to reliable information or evolving curricula, suggesting that contextual factors in education significantly impact health attitudes 44.

Marital status was similarly influential. Married individuals tended to have more neutral or negative attitudes than unmarried participants, a trend observed in the first wave. Previous research has contrastingly observed more cautious attitudes among unmarried or younger individuals, possibly due to differences in risk perception, social responsibilities, or lifestyle factors 45.

Age affected perceptions as well. Younger individuals expressed less concern and perceived lower personal risk, a trend seen in COVID-19 46.

Surprisingly, smoking status was positively associated with concern. Contrary to expectations, smokers, typically linked to lower engagement in preventive behaviors, showed greater concern. This may reflect increased perceived vulnerability, particularly due to mpox’s potential respiratory impact, similar to COVID-19 trends, where smokers demonstrated increased awareness and information-seeking behavior 47. This suggests smokers may be a strategically receptive audience for targeted health campaigns.

Contrary to common assumptions, lower socioeconomic status (SES) participants showed more positive attitudes than those with higher or stable income levels. This may stem from heightened awareness of healthcare limitations, as observed in underserved populations during past pandemics 48.

Occupational and geographic disparities were evident. Non-healthcare workers and rural residents had less favorable attitudes compared to healthcare professionals and urban dwellers. These gaps likely reflect differences in exposure, resources, and health information access 38, pointing to the need for rural-focused and occupation-sensitive interventions.

Finally, reliance on social media as a primary information source correlated with less favorable attitudes and lower knowledge scores. This confirms the well-established relationship between misinformation exposure and negative health attitudes 28,49.

Vaccine Acceptance and Predictors of Willingness

Vaccination remains a cornerstone strategy in controlling emerging infectious diseases, including mpox. Our study, consistent with the earlier wave 19, revealed a generally high theoretical willingness to receive the smallpox vaccine. The parallel findings underscore a key barrier: cost, rather than hesitancy, significantly limits vaccine uptake in economically strained contexts like Lebanon. The economic burden of vaccine cost can pose a considerable barrier, especially in households with poor or unstable income. The fact that nearly three-quarters of those willing to vaccinate would not pay out of pocket highlights the urgent need for publicly funded vaccine programs and serves as a crucial policy cue for both national health authorities and international stakeholders.

An important predictor of vaccine acceptance observed in both our study and the first-wave study was prior COVID-19 vaccination. Participants who had received the COVID-19 vaccine were significantly more likely to accept the smallpox vaccine19. This trend reflects a global phenomenon where successful prior vaccination experiences, particularly during large-scale campaigns, have strengthened public trust 50. These insights suggest that the infrastructure and communication strategies developed during the COVID-19 pandemic could be leveraged to improve vaccine acceptance for other emerging diseases. Despite higher acceptance of the smallpox vaccine, willingness to accept an mpox-specific vaccine dropped sharply. Financial concerns remained a barrier, and unfamiliarity with the newer vaccine further reduced acceptance. In the first wave, 56.4% were willing to receive the mpox vaccine, again conditional on affordability 19. These findings highlight two recurring behavioral patterns: familiarity fosters trust, and cost constrains action. Vaccines with long-standing safety records (e.g., smallpox) benefit from public confidence, whereas newer vaccines often face skepticism. Transparent, data-driven communication is essential to address these gaps.

Demographic factors also played a significant role in shaping vaccine acceptance. Interestingly, male participants were more likely to accept both vaccines than females, despite lower overall knowledge and concern levels. The mean willingness score for the smallpox vaccine was higher among males (3.09 ± 1.15) than among females (2.87 ± 1.13). This gender divergence aligns with previous research indicating that females tend to express greater caution regarding vaccine safety 51. During COVID-19 vaccination campaigns, misinformation related to fertility and pregnancy contributed to hesitancy among women 52. This discrepancy illustrates that concern does not always translate into acceptance, particularly when it comes to safety.

Educational attainment was positively associated with vaccine acceptance. Participants with a bachelor’s degree or higher showed significantly greater willingness, reinforcing established links between education and trust in science 53. Similarly, healthcare professionals displayed higher acceptance consistent with previous research 34,35,39, likely due to greater familiarity with medical evidence and practices.

SES further influenced willingness, particularly regarding the ability to pay for vaccines. While lower SES participants were generally willing to vaccinate, their acceptance was often conditional on free access. This highlights a common challenge in post-crisis or low-resource settings 54. Finally, psychological and experiential factors shaped vaccine behavior. Participants who perceived mpox as a serious health threat or believed it could disrupt daily life were more likely to express willingness to vaccinate. This supports behavioral models such as the Health Belief Model (HBM), which emphasize perceived risk and efficacy as drivers of preventive action 55,56. Additionally, those who relied on medical professionals or official health sources for information were more accepting, while those relying on social media exhibited lower acceptance, consistent with vaccine behavior patterns seen during COVID-19 28,49.

The gap between moderately neutral attitudes and relatively low willingness to accept an mpox-specific vaccine can be better explained through the HBM, which posits that health behavior is determined by perceptions of susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers, cues to action, and self-efficacy. This has been shown in the mpox context; for example, in one Gulf region study conducted among the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Qatar, perceived benefits and self-efficacy were among the most powerful predictors of mpox vaccine acceptance, whereas reported concerns about side effects, serving as barriers, significantly reduced intention 57. A U.S. adult survey, based in part on HBM constructs, found that perceived susceptibility was the strongest predictor of vaccine intention, surpassing knowledge itself 58. Hence, public health strategies in Lebanon should aim not only to reduce knowledge gaps but also to strengthen motivational and normative drivers, such as tailored messaging and easier access to vaccination.

Notably, the correlation between knowledge and attitude was very weak and negative (r = –0.055), and knowledge was not significantly associated with vaccine acceptance. This near-zero relationship suggests that simply increasing factual knowledge may be insufficient to shift attitudes or uptake, and that other drivers such as perceived risk, trust in authorities, and exposure to misinformation may be more influential. Future studies should examine these psychosocial pathways to better understand how to translate knowledge into protective behaviors.

Moreover, situating our results in the wider Middle Eastern context highlights critical differences. Several studies from the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Jordan during the 2022–2023 outbreak reported higher levels of mpox knowledge and greater vaccine readiness than observed in Lebanon. For example, Zeidan et al. (2023) reported that over half of participants in the UAE demonstrated “optimal” mpox knowledge, particularly regarding transmission and clinical features, which was significantly higher than that in our cohort 20. A 2024 systematic review of global and regional mpox knowledge, attitude, and practice (KAP) studies similarly concluded that Gulf populations generally reported higher awareness and more positive attitudes than those in non-Gulf Middle Eastern countries, including Lebanon 59. As Lebanon’s findings consistently fall at the lower end of the regional spectrum, these cross-country comparisons underscore the importance of culturally tailored, targeted public health interventions suited for Lebanese communities.

Additional Considerations

This study highlights major challenges in maintaining public engagement with emerging infectious diseases such as mpox. The decline in perceived risk and vaccine enthusiasm may reflect psychological factors such as risk habituation and crisis/pandemic fatigue, where repeated health crises reduce responsiveness over time 15. Risk habituation refers to the reduction in perceived threat following repeated exposure to health risk information 60, while crisis/pandemic fatigue describes emotional exhaustion and disengagement that can occur during prolonged or recurrent public health emergencies 61. This underscores the need for adaptable communication strategies that keep messaging relevant even in the absence of active outbreaks.

Given the strong link between misinformation and harmful health behaviors, collaborations with digital platforms and influencers are urgently needed to spread accurate content and counter falsehoods, especially in Lebanon, where social media is a primary information source within a fragile health infrastructure.

Though we refer to one previous Lebanese KAP study from 2023, our updated assessment, conducted in 2024–2025, adds substantial value by providing a temporal snapshot of public perceptions across successive waves of the outbreak. Behavioral science studies indicate that risk perception and public motivation change over time due to media cycles, “crisis fatigue,” and changing outbreak intensity 62,63. In other words, our repeated cross-sectional design not only integrates prior findings but also documents evolving public perceptions, thus forming a platform for adaptive, theory-informed interventions linked to present behavioral realities.

Limitations and Future Research

Unlike earlier studies on mpox awareness conducted in non-endemic settings, this study uniquely tracks temporal shifts in knowledge and vaccine hesitancy across two epidemic waves, providing valuable insight into public desensitization and behavioral fatigue. By capturing these dynamics during distinct outbreak periods, this research offers one of the most comprehensive assessments of mpox-related knowledge, attitudes, and vaccine perceptions conducted in Lebanon, a setting characterized by significant socioeconomic and healthcare challenges.

Nevertheless, several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings. The cross-sectional design precludes causal inference between knowledge, attitudes, and vaccine-related perceptions. Reliance on self-reported data may have introduced recall and social desirability biases. In addition, the online survey format may have excluded individuals with limited digital access or literacy, potentially leading to the underrepresentation of socioeconomically vulnerable populations. Although multiple multivariable models were applied to account for potential confounders, the possibility of residual confounding cannot be entirely ruled out.

Attitudes toward mpox were assessed using a brief four-item scale designed to minimize respondent burden while capturing key domains, including perceived risk, concern, perceived impact on daily life, and perceived seriousness. While this pragmatic approach is appropriate for large-scale online surveys, it may not fully capture the multidimensional nature of attitudes, such as behavioral intentions or the influence of social norms. The attitude subscale demonstrated moderate internal consistency (Cronbach’s α = 0.592), which should be interpreted as a descriptive indicator of internal consistency rather than a strict determinant of construct validity. Item-level diagnostics indicated acceptable contributions for most items, and sensitivity analyses using individual attitude items yielded findings consistent with the primary models, supporting the robustness of the results despite the scale’s brevity. Nonetheless, regression-based inferences involving the summed attitude score should be interpreted with appropriate caution. Subsequent research should employ more comprehensive, validated attitudinal instruments to enhance construct coverage and reliability.

Future studies should adopt longitudinal designs to monitor changes in knowledge, attitudes, and vaccine-related behaviors over time, particularly in response to evolving epidemiological conditions and targeted public health interventions. Mixed-methods approaches incorporating qualitative components could further elucidate the motivations, beliefs, and contextual factors underlying vaccine hesitancy and risk perception. Moreover, evaluating the effectiveness of educational campaigns, healthcare worker engagement, and strategies to counter digital misinformation will be essential to developing scalable, context-appropriate responses to future outbreaks. In this regard, Lebanon may serve as a valuable case study for other regions facing similar socioeconomic and healthcare constraints.

Conclusion

This study highlights persistent knowledge gaps, moderate attitudes, and conditional mpox vaccine acceptance in Lebanon, shaped by misinformation, financial constraints, and low perceived personal risk. Higher education, healthcare engagement, and prior vaccination experience were associated with better knowledge and greater willingness to vaccinate. These findings emphasize the importance of targeted risk communication, equitable vaccine access, and trust-building in non-endemic, resource-limited settings. Lebanon’s experience underscores the relevance of global preparedness frameworks, including the WHO International Health Regulations, which prioritize community engagement, sustained public communication, and proactive education beyond outbreak periods. Strengthening these strategies is vital not only for mpox control but also for enhancing readiness for future emerging infectious threats in fragile health systems.

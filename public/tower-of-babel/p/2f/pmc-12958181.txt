JDS Communications® 2025 Editorial Report
Authors: Jessica AA McArt
Published: 2026

Introductory Comments

In 2025, JDS Communications celebrated our 5-yr anniversary as a society publication of the American Dairy Science Association (ADSA) and published our 6th volume, which included 6 regular issues and 1 special issue. We are clearly filling a necessary role within dairy science alongside our sister journal, the Journal of Dairy Science. As I review our performance in 2025, let's start off with some highlights!

Highlights

The biggest news of the year was gaining our first Journal Impact Factor of 2.2—excellent for a young journal—which puts us in the top quartile of agriculture, dairy, and animal science publications. This year, we also had articles from JDS Communications crack into the 100 Most Cited list, available on our website at https://www.adsa.org/publications/journal-of-dairy-science/jds-jdsc-top-100-papers-for-2025, compiled from our ADSA society publications based on citations since 2022. Congratulations to our authors from Qiao et al. (2022), Albaaj et al. (2023), and Kolev et al. (2022) for this achievement! Our top 10 most-downloaded articles in 2025 received a combined total of more than 40,000 downloads, with Lundquist et al. (2024) taking the top spot with just under 9,200 downloads, and our “quick hit” by Laven and Yang (2025) being downloaded more than 3,000 times within only 2 mo of publication. I encourage you to have a read of these popular articles.

Our 2025 special issue, The Future of Dairy, included 8 invited articles covering rapidly evolving topics in the dairy industry. We hope this will be a recurring special issue in 5-yr intervals to examine where quickly moving topics in dairy science currently stand and what the future might hold. An open call for a special issue on pasture-based dairy production systems, with guest editor John Roche, closed near the end of 2025, resulting in a JDS Communications record number of submissions for a special issue. This is obviously an area of science in which we have passionate authors from around the globe!

We continued our social media campaign highlighting our Author Spotlight series, and threw a few JDS Communications section editors into the mix. These Editor Spotlights discuss how our editors were drawn to our ADSA community, what questions or challenges within the field of dairy science they are currently working on, their experience with peer review, and what they like to do outside of work. Find these folks on our website at https://www.adsa.org/About-ADSA/Spotlight. We will continue to introduce our new section editors in 2026, so stay tuned! Across our ADSA society journal social media accounts, we grew our community by ∼28,000 followers, with 371,000 unique users and incredible engagement over several platforms, all based on the sharing of—and interest in—dairy science. Help us elevate your JDS Communications research and tag us in your posts and comments so we can continue to share your science on a global stage!

2025 in Review: Our Numbers and People

JDS Communications has continued to grow, publishing 8% more articles in 2025 than in 2024. We also continue to increase the breadth of our authors and reduce the total time in peer review. Figure 1 shows our article growth over the past 5 yr, with additional summary data by scientific section and article type outlined in Table 1. Our largest growth was seen in the Animal Nutrition and Farm Systems section, which saw a 160% increase in published articles (21 in 2024 vs. 55 in 2025), and the Dairy Foods section, in which we published 110% more articles (10 in 2024 vs. 21 in 2025). Our total time in peer review (submission to online publication as an uncorrected proof with a DOI) decreased from 110 d in 2024 to 102 d in 2025. This reduction was led by our incredible journal editorial and production team, who took only 12 d on average to get research out to readers after being accepted by the journal.

ADSA Journal Community Survey

In 2025, we released a survey to our community for feedback on the Journal of Dairy Science and JDS Communications. We had more than 550 respondents, although 35% of those stated they had no publishing experience with JDS Communications. Overall, survey respondents found JDS Communications' major successes to be the speed of turnaround time from submission to publication (78% positive feedback) and the clarity of decisions received by our section editors (72% positive feedback). What did survey respondents suggest for areas of improvement? Concerns were expressed about our 5-page article length being too restrictive for certain studies (30% noted this was an area for improvement), there were requests for supplemental material options, and suggestions were provided for clearer reviewer guidelines and improved reviewer training to provide consistency in review quality.

What have we done at JDS Communications to address these areas of opportunity? Focusing on the concern regarding the 5-page article limit, starting this year, we will no longer include the references in the 5-page article limit. We now also allow supplemental material, placed in a repository and with a link listed in the Notes section. In combination, these changes will assist authors with expanding their science while maintaining appropriate citation expectations, and we have reworked our manuscript Word template document accordingly to help authors understand the space constraints. Remember, as always, the graphical abstract, summary, and highlights—and now, the references—do not count in our 5-page typeset length restriction. These and other changes to our 2026 Instructions to Authors can be found at https://www.jdscommun.org/inst-author.

Creating clearer reviewer guidelines and improving reviewer training is a continual process. At the ADSA 2026 Annual Meeting, in collaboration with the ADSA Graduate Student Division, Journal of Dairy Science Editor in Chief Laura Hernandez, Journal of Dairy Science Senior Editors Nicole Martin and Stephen LeBlanc, JDS Communications and Journal of Dairy Science Editorial Board member Jennifer Van Os, and I will lead a graduate student luncheon event on manuscript review (see https://www.adsa.org/meetings/2026-annual-meeting/program). The goal is to teach our future reviewers the fundamental skills of a good review, and we plan to share these materials after the meeting on our ASDA website for other members of our dairy science community to view. We continue to discuss other ways to improve the consistency of manuscript reviews, and will share these opportunities broadly when implemented.

Thank you to those of you who participated in our ADSA journals survey—your feedback is incredibly valuable and helps shape the future of the journal. Keep it coming!

Efficacy of Ultra-high and High Molecular Weight Cross-Linked Hyaluronic Acids Compared with Saline in Knee Osteoarthritis
Authors: Supakit Kanitnate, Pattaranatcha Charnwichai, Yot Tanariyakul, Nattapol Tammachote
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Intra-articular hyaluronic acid (HA) injections are widely used for knee osteoarthritis (OA) to reduce pain and improve function. The efficacy of cross-linked HA over placebo remains debated. This study aimed to compare the efficacy of 2 single-injection, ultra-high, and high molecular weight (HMW) cross-linked HAs against a saline placebo for symptomatic knee OA.Methods:We conducted a single-center, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial with 24-week follow-up. A total of 276 patients with primary knee OA were randomized into 3 groups: ultra-high MW HA (Hyruan ONE), HMW HA (hylan G-F 20), and saline. All participants received a single intra-articular injection. Primary outcomes were visual analog scale (VAS) pain scores at rest and during motion at weeks 1 to 24 and the change from baseline over the 24-week period. Secondary outcomes included the modified Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC), Short Form-36 (SF-36), Lequesne index, Time Up and Go (TUG) test, and active knee flexion at weeks 2, 4, 12, and 24. Patients who did not improve could receive a rescue corticosteroid injection, after which they were excluded from further analysis.Results:No significant differences in pain reduction were observed between the HA and saline groups at rest (p = 0.92) and during motion (p = 0.99). All groups showed significant improvement in VAS scores over 24 weeks (p < 0.0001). Modified WOMAC scores were also similar among the 3 groups (p > 0.05). All groups had comparable outcomes for the SF-36 (p = 0.91), Lequesne index (p = 0.93), TUG test (p = 0.48), and knee flexion (p = 0.90). The rates of rescued corticosteroid injections rates were similar across the 3 groups (17% vs. 21% vs. 25%, p = 0.98).Conclusions:Ultra-high and HMW cross-linked HA injections did not provide superior pain or functional outcomes compared with saline for knee OA. These findings provided high-level evidence challenging the clinical benefit of cross-linked HAs. Consequently, the cost-effectiveness is uncertain and warrants formal economic evaluation.Level of Evidence:Therapeutic Level I. Background

Intra-articular hyaluronic acid (IA-HA) injection is a widely used conservative treatment for knee osteoarthritis (OA). The European Society for Clinical and Economic Aspects of Osteoporosis and Osteoarthritis recommends IA-HA for patients who do not respond to nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs1. By contrast, the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) does not endorse its routine use, citing limited evidence of clinical benefit2.

HA formulations differ in their source, molecular weight (MW), and the presence of cross-linking3,4. Cross-linking transforms linear HA chains into a three-dimensional network, enhancing viscoelasticity, enzymatic resistance, and intra-articular persistence5-9. This process may further enhance lubrication, chondroprotection, and delay OA progression10,11. Some evidence suggests that HMW HA may be more effective than lower MW variants12-14; its advantage over placebo remains uncertain15.

Despite existing evidence, the clinical effectiveness of cross-linked HA over placebo remains debated7,16-18.Therefore, this study aimed to compare the efficacy of 2 cross-linked HA products ultra-high (Hyruan ONE) and HMW (hylan G-F 20) against saline placebo in patients with symptomatic knee OA, using a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design.

Materials and Methods

Overview

This study was approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of Thammasat University (MTU-EC-OT-5-165/60) and registered in ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03484091). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The study was conducted at Thammasat University Hospital. A total of 276 patients with primary knee OA were enrolled.

The inclusion criteria were a diagnosis of symptomatic primary knee OA, failure of ≥ 3 months of conservative treatment, intact cognition, and the ability to comprehend the protocol. The exclusion criteria were a varus/valgus deformity (mechanical axis deviation ≥10°), HA allergy, concurrent hip/ankle pathology, prior surgery or an infection in the target knee, an intra-articular injection within 6 months, pregnancy or lactation, and rheumatoid arthritis.

Randomization

Participants were randomized into 3 groups in a 1:1:1 ratio with varying block sizes of 6 using computer-generated numbers by an independent research assistant. Allocation was concealed using sealed opaque envelopes. Upon providing consent, participants were randomly assigned to the ultra-high MW HA group, which received a single 3 mL IA injection of Hyruan ONE (LG Chem, LTD, Korea); the HMW HA group, which received a single 6 mL IA injection of Hylan G-F 20 (Synvisc ONE; Genzyme Biosurgery); or the saline group, which received a single 6 mL IA injection of sterile normal saline solution.

Intervention and Drug Protocol

All injections were performed by a senior orthopedist (N.T.). The injection technique was described in appendix. The patient was put an eye mask to obscure the physical characteristics and volume of the substances. Both evaluators (S.K., P.C.) and patients were blinded. All participants were instructed to use 1,000 mg of acetaminophen every 6 hours as needed for postinjection pain control. Patients who reported no improvement or dissatisfaction with the injection were permitted to withdraw from the study and received a corticosteroid injection.

Demographic data were collected by investigators who were not aware of the allocation. Radiographic assessments included standard anteroposterior, lateral, and skyline views of the knee. OA grading was performed by a blinded assessor (Y.T.) using the Kellgren–Lawrence (KL) classification19.

Assessments

The primary outcome was knee pain, assessed using a 100-mm visual analog scale (VAS), where a score of 0 represented no pain and 100 indicated the most severe pain. Pain was measured at rest and during motion. VAS scores were recorded at baseline, twice daily (morning and evening) for the first 2 weeks, and then biweekly up to 24 weeks postinjection. The daily average VAS score derived from the mean of 2 daily measurements. Individual scores were summed and halved to yield a single consolidated value.

Secondary outcomes included functional assessments using the modified Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC)20, the Short Form-36 questionnaire (SF-36), the Lequesne index, the Time Up and Go (TUG) test21, and knee range of motion (ROM). The modified WOMAC, SF-36, and Lequesne index were assessed at baseline, weeks 2 and 4, and every 4 weeks thereafter, up to week 24. The TUG test was performed twice, 10 minutes apart, and the mean value was recorded in seconds. Knee ROM measured the active knee flexion angle using a long goniometer. The TUG test and knee flexion angle were assessed at baseline and weeks 2, 4, 12, and 24 postinjection.

Statistical Analysis

The sample size was calculated to detect a 10-point difference in VAS scores, assuming a mean ± standard deviation of 24 ± 22 points, as reported in previous study22. To achieve 80% power at a significant level of 0.05 and accounting for a 20% dropout rate, 92 patients per group (total n = 276) were required. Statistical analyses were conducted using R software (R Core Team, version 4.4.2, 2024). Linear mixed-effects models (lme4 package) analyzed longitudinal VAS changes. Fixed effects included treatment group, time, and their interaction. To account for repeated measurements, patient was treated as a random effect. Baseline VAS and demographics were included as covariates to adjust for initial severity and potential confounders. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Pain Relief

All 3 groups showed similar overall mean VAS scores at rest (p = 0.92) (Fig. 2) and during motion (p = 0.99) (Fig. 3), in pain reduction between groups at any time point (p < 0.05, also see in appendix). Despite the intergroup similarity, all 3 treatment arms demonstrated a significant reduction in pain from baseline to 24 weeks postinjection (p < 0.0001) (Table II). These improvements were sustained over time, with no significant between-group differences detected at any individual follow-up intervals.

Functional Improvement

The overall total modified WOMAC scores were comparable among the groups throughout the study period (p = 0.91). Similarly, no significant between-group differences were observed for the subscale scores of pain (p = 0.90), stiffness (p = 0.60), or physical function (p = 0.87). There were also no statistically significant differences in SF-36 (p = 0.91) or Lequesne index scores (p = 0.93) across the 3 groups.

The mean TUG test time at 24 weeks was approximately 11 seconds in all groups, with no significant differences (p = 0.48). However, all groups exhibited no progressive improvement in TUG test times over the follow-up period (p > 0.05).

At the end of the study, the ultra-high MW and the HMW HA groups had a similar overall active knee flexion angle to the saline group (134° vs. 136°, p = 0.90). Knee function significantly improved in all 3 groups from the preinjection and 24 weeks postinjection (Table II).

Rescued therapy protocols were implemented at 4 and 12 weeks postinjection; patient counts are shown in Fig. 1. The proportion of patients requiring rescue therapy was also similar among groups (17% vs. 21% vs. 25%; p = 0.98).

Discussion

This study demonstrated that both ultra-high MW and HMW cross-linked HAs exhibited comparable efficacy in pain reduction and functional improvement to saline injections in patients with symptomatic knee OA. Notably, all treatment groups showed enhanced pain relief and knee function over 6-month postinjection period.

Limited comparative studies have analyzed the efficacy of different cross-linked HAs in knee OA. Sun et al.23 compared ultra-high MW HA, cross-linked with 1,4-BDDE (HYA-JOINT plus) to hylan G-F 20, found that HYA-JOINT plus resulted in a superior reduction in pain on knee movement at 1, 3 and 6 months. The study reported that HYA-JOINT plus led to a 53% decrease in pain scores from baseline over 6 months versus a 42% reduction with hylan G-F 20. In our study, the 2 HA groups showed a mean difference of 4/100 points in VAS scores at rest and 5/100 point during motion at 4 weeks postinjection. However, no differences emerged at any time point over the 6-month follow-up. Overall, pain reduction from baseline to 6 months was similar across the both HA groups, with ∼ 60% pain relief at rest and 65% during motion. The most rapid reduction in pain occurred during the first week, after which it gradually decreased down to the lowest point at 6 weeks postinjection.

Furthermore, Ko et al.8 evaluated 2 HA formulations with similar bacterial fermentation and cross-linked by 1,4-BDDE (HYA-JOINT plus versus Durolane). They found that HYA-JOINT plus achieved superior mean VAS pain reduction of ∼ 8/100 points at 26 weeks postinjection. Blicharski et al.6 reported that Hyruan ONE was noninferior to MMW cross-linked HA (Durolane) in decreasing the WOMAC pain subscore from baseline to 13 weeks postinjection.

The literature remains heterogeneous regarding the efficacy of cross-linked HAs versus placebo. A meta-analysis of large placebo-controlled trials found that HA offers modest pain relief (∼2 points on a 100-mm VAS) consistent with our results (4 points at rest and 5 points during motion at 4 weeks postinjection)15. Notably, this meta-analysis pooled data from both cross-linked and non–cross-linked HA formulations, which may confound direct comparisons. Discrepancies among trials on HMW cross-linked HA are inconsistent. Chevalier et al.16 reported a modest mean superiority in WOMAC pain scores, but Ke et al.17 found no difference versus placebo. A study on LMW cross-linked HA showed no difference in responder rate compared with saline at 6 weeks18. Conversely, MMW cross-linked HA provided clinically meaningful pain reduction within 2 weeks, which was sustained for at least 6 months7.

This study demonstrated that saline had a significant, long-lasting effect comparable with ultra-high and HMW cross-linked HAs. This aligned with meta-analyses showing clinically meaningful relief for up to 6 months24,25. Zhang et al.26 also reported an effect size of 0.51 for IA saline injections, whereas the effect for patients receiving no treatment was nearly zero. The hypothesized mechanisms of action were the dilution effect of inflammatory mediators in the knee joint and the psychological impact of the injection procedure24. Furthermore, another study showed that even IA dry needle injections (sham) had similar effect to saline injections27.

Approximately 20% of patients receiving intra-articular knee injection, no matter HA or saline, may experience inadequate pain relief and may require additional rescue injections. This information is valuable for counseling patients considering intra-articular injections for knee OA, as there is a substantial likelihood—around 1 in 5—of treatment failure.

For a health economics perspective, the use of HAs may not be cost-effectiveness over saline for OA knee treatment. The outcome of this study supports the AAOS recommendation against the routine use of IAHA for symptomatic knee OA due to the absence of strong supportive evidence2.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the follow-up period was limited to 6 months. Previous study has shown that the benefits of hylan G-F 20, with improvements in WOMAC, patient global assessment (PTGA), VAS, and SF-36 scores, can last up to 52 weeks28. Similarly, single injections of HYA-JOINT plus or Durolane have shown efficacy in pain reduction lasting up to 1 year8. Therefore, further longitudinal studies are warranted to evaluate the long-term efficacy of Hyruan ONE. Second, the final follow-up rate of approximately 73% to 80% due to patient discontinuation from the study if they reported dissatisfaction. Third, while the study included patients across all grades of OA severity (KL grade I–IV), most participants (∼90%) had KL grades II–III with deformities not exceeding 10°. The baseline severity and dropout rates were comparable across all 3 groups. Forth, adverse events were not systematically assessed. Different HA products may carry varying risks of adverse effects. Further investigation of Hyruan ONE's safety may be warranted. We examined the patients’ knee at each follow-up and no patient have any significant adverse events. Finally, we did not analyze factors that may influence the placebo effect such as patient expectations or assessor interaction methods. In knee OA, placebo responses were notoriously high as IA injections generate greater therapeutic expectations than oral medications, potentially masking HA’s pharmacological superiority26. Regular clinical interaction may provide psychological reassurance, improving subjective pain scores regardless of the substance injected29.

In conclusion, intra-articular injections of ultra-high MW and HMW cross-linked HAs did not demonstrate superior efficacy in pain reduction or functional improvement compared with saline in patients with symptomatic knee OA. From this evidence, the economically efficient of intra-articular HA may not be justified. The economic future research should investigate Hyruan ONE's long-term efficacy and safety, particularly compared with placebo in a rigorously blinded, multiarm trial.

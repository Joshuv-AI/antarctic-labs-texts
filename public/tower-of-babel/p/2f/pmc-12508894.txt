Phosphorylation of plectin by Akt3 promotes triple-negative breast cancer cell invasive migration
Authors: Shixue Zheng, Chloe Chan, John M Asara, Liang Zhang, Gerhard Wiche, Y Rebecca Chin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryTriple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) lacks targeted therapeutics and is aggressive with a poor prognosis. The phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt signaling pathway, frequently deregulated in cancers, plays crucial roles in tumorigenesis and cancer progression. However, the distinct functions of the three Akt isoforms (Akt1, Akt2, Akt3) in these processes are not well understood. Here, we focus on Akt3, the least-studied Akt isoform, which is overexpressed in 28% of TNBC cases and significantly promotes TNBC growth, stemness, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. Through a genome-wide proteomic screen, we identified plectin, a member of the plakin family, as an Akt3 substrate in TNBC cells. The depletion of plectin potently inhibits TNBC cell migration and invadopodia formation, albeit with mild effects on cell growth. The phosphorylation of plectin at Ser4268 by Akt promotes its colocalization with vimentin and TNBC cell migration. Our findings underscore the importance of Akt3-plectin signaling as a potential TNBC therapeutic target.Subject areas: Biochemistry, Cancer, Proteomics

Introduction

Breast cancer, the second most common cause of death in women worldwide, is a heterogeneous disease with different biological and clinical outcomes. Based on the expression of estrogen receptors (ER), progesterone receptors (PR), and Her2, tumors are commonly classified as ER+, Her2-overexpressing, or ER-PR-Her2- (triple-negative; TN) subtype.1 Recent advances in the development of endocrine and Her2-targeted therapies have led to improved survival of patients with ER+ and Her2-overexpressing tumors. TN breast cancer (TNBC), however, lacks targeted therapeutics and has a poor prognosis. TNBC is highly aggressive, with tumor cells possessing invasive migratory ability and high metastatic potential, which is one of the hallmarks of cancer.2 This underscores the importance of understanding the molecular mechanisms driving invasive migration in the hope of discovering effective therapeutic interventions for TNBC.

The phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt signaling cascade is frequently deregulated in human cancers and is responsible for key aspects of tumorigenesis, including transformation, proliferation, and metastasis of tumor cells.3 The non-redundant and distinct functions and signaling of the three mammalian Akt isoforms (Akt1, Akt2, Akt3) in tumorigenesis and cancer progression, however, have not been extensively studied. It is noteworthy that only a few isoform-specific substrates have been identified.4 We have previously demonstrated that Akt1 inhibits breast cancer cell invasive migration via the phosphorylation of an actin-associated protein, palladin.5 Akt2, in contrast, is reported to enhance breast cancer metastasis and cell motility by upregulating β1 integrins.6 We have also shown an isoform-specific function for Akt2 in regulating PTEN-deficient tumor maintenance.7,8 Interestingly, the least-studied Akt isoform, Akt3, is overexpressed in 28% of TNBC and plays a predominant role in promoting TNBC growth by modulating p27.8 We further demonstrated a specific and critical role of Akt3 in conferring drug resistance to Akt pathway inhibitors in breast cancer.9 Recently, we uncovered a role of Akt3 in modulating TNBC stemness and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), and further identified YB1 as the first bona fide Akt3-specific substrate.10 The phosphorylation of YB1 by Akt3 promotes the expression of cancer stem cell (CSC)/EMT regulators, including Snail and Slug. These findings point to Akt3 and its downstream substrates as novel targets for TNBC therapy, highlighting the critical importance of defining Akt3-specific signaling in tumorigenesis. This prompted us to perform a genome-wide proteomic screen to identify novel Akt3 substrates.

Plectin, a member of the plakin family, is capable of interlinking with various cytoskeleton elements. It possesses a multidomain dumbbell-like structure that allows it to interact with a vast array of proteins, including intermediate filaments (IFs), actin filaments, and microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs).11,12,13,14 In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), upregulated membrane-localized plectin has been identified as a potential biomarker.15 Plectin overexpression has also been correlated with poor patient prognosis in lung cancer and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC).16,17 In bladder cancer, plectin 1f has been shown to interact with vimentin to promote invadopodia formation, facilitating cancer cell invasion and extravasation for metastasis.18 Furthermore, it has been demonstrated that the interaction between plectin and BRCA2 contributes to the centrosome localization and genomic stability of breast cancer cells.19 Importantly, complexes of integrin β4-plectin-vimentin play a critical role in driving the invasive phenotype of TNBC cells.20 However, the mechanisms and signaling pathways that modulate plectin in TNBC cell invasion and migration remain to be characterized.

Here, we report the identification of plectin as an Akt3 substrate in TNBC cells. The depletion of plectin by guide RNA (gRNA) potently inhibits TNBC cell migration and invadopodia formation but has mild effects on growth, consistent with its role as a versatile cytoskeletal protein. The phosphorylation of plectin at Ser4268 by Akt plays a critical role in its colocalization with vimentin and TNBC cell migration. Taken together, our findings identify plectin as an Akt isoform-specific substrate that mediates TNBC cell invasive migration.

Results

Plectin is phosphorylated by Akt in triple-negative breast cancer cells

In addition to its promotional role in TNBC growth, we recently uncovered a critical role of Akt3 in determining CSC properties and EMT processes that regulate tumor cell migration and metastasis.10 Here, we used a combination of inducible shRNA and genome-wide proteomic approach to identify Akt3-specific substrates. In the proteomic screen, several strategies were employed: (1) A tet-on inducible shRNA system for Akt1/2/3 knockdown21; (2) A polypeptide enrichment step using pAkt Motif antibody for concentrating peptides phosphorylated by Akt22; (3) Proteomic screen by phosphopeptide immunoaffinity purification (IAP) coupled with liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) for the identification of phosphopeptides.23 Another key feature of our screen was that the cells were grown in 3D culture, which more accurately recapitulates phenotypes that govern tumor growth in vivo (Figure 1A). Our analysis revealed several candidate Akt3-specific substrates (Figure 1B and Table S1), and we prioritized to validate plectin as it: (1) has the most potent inhibition of phosphorylation upon Akt3 knockdown; (2) has been implicated as a potential Akt substrate in literature24; (3) has a known prominent role in regulating cancer cell migration.17,18,25

To determine if plectin is phosphorylated by Akt, we pre-treated TNBC HCC1143 and HCC1937 cells with allosteric Akt inhibitor MK2206 or ATP-competitive inhibitor GDC0068, followed by insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) to activate endogenous Akt activity. Immunoprecipitated plectin was immunoblotted using pAkt Motif antibody. Pretreatment with the Akt inhibitor markedly attenuated IGF-1-induced phosphorylation of plectin (Figures 1C and 1D). Similarly, the phosphorylation of endogenous plectin by epidermal growth factor (EGF) was inhibited by both GDC0068 and MK2206 in TNBC Hs578T cells (Figure 1E; Figure S1). To examine the phosphorylation levels of individual Akt isoforms under different conditions, immunoprecipitated Akt isoforms were immunoblotted with the pAkt S473 antibody. We demonstrated that all three Akt isoforms were phosphorylated with EGF stimulation, and that their phosphorylation was sensitive to GDC0068 (Figure S2). These findings are consistent with a previous study demonstrating that plectin phosphorylation is sensitive to Akt inhibitors but not mTOR-RSK pathway inhibitors.24 We further explored whether plectin can be phosphorylated by S6 kinase-1 (S6K1), which shares the Akt consensus phosphorylation motif. In Hs578T cells, the phosphorylation of plectin by EGF was reduced upon treatment with the S6K1 inhibitor PF-4708671, suggesting that plectin may also be a potential substrate of S6K1 (Figure S3).

Plectin is primarily phosphorylated at Ser4268

Plectin Ser4386 (corresponding to the position in human isoform 1, GenBank: NM_201380) is a highly conserved site across different species and conforms to the Akt consensus motif (RXRXXS/T)24,26 (Figure S4). It is recognized as the primary Akt phosphorylation site in our proteomic screen (Table S1). Upon scanning the entire plectin sequence for potential Akt phosphorylation, we uncovered another evolutionarily conserved site, Ser4268 (Figure 2A), which has also been shown to be phosphorylated in multiple mass spectrometry studies (CST PhosphoSitePlus Database). To determine which site on plectin is phosphorylated and whether plectin is a direct substrate of Akt3, we transfected recombinant human plectin fragments into MDA-MB-231 cells in serum-free medium. These fragments included wild-type plectin (EGFP-hPL-WT), single mutants of the phosphorylation site (EGFP-hPL-S4386A or EGFP-hPL-S4268A), or a double mutant (EGFP-hPL-S4386A + S4268A). Immunoprecipitated plectin fragments were then incubated with active recombinant Akt3 in an in vitro kinase assay. Whereas the WT plectin fragments were efficiently phosphorylated by Akt3, the phosphorylation of fragments containing a single mutation of S4268A or the double mutants was significantly diminished. Compared to the WT plectin, the phosphorylation efficiency of EGFP-hPL-S4386A was reduced by 40% (Figure 2B). We next transfected plectin fragments in MDA-MB-231 cells and stimulated cells with IGF-1 to activate Akt. The phosphorylation of S4268A mutant and double mutant, but not S4386A mutant, was significantly reduced compared to WT plectin fragments (Figure S5A).

Due to alternative splicing at the 5′ end of the gene, multiple plectin transcripts and isoforms are generated.27 To further investigate the phosphorylation and function of full-length plectin, we assessed the mRNA levels of plectin isoforms using N-terminal-specific primers. Plectin isoform 1a has the highest mRNA expression among all isoforms analyzed in MDA-MB-231 cells (Figure S5B). Considering that plectin is a high molecular weight protein (over 500 kDa) with low transfection efficiency in breast cancer cells, we transfected WT full-length mouse plectin isoform 1a (pGR245-WT) or phosphorylation site mutants (pGR245-S4245A, pGR245-S4127A, or pGR245-S4245A + S4127A) into HEK293T cells to achieve sufficient protein expression for in vitro kinase assay with Akt3. Ser4268 and Ser4386 in human plectin isoform 1 correspond to Ser4127 and Ser4245 in mouse isoform 1a, respectively; GenBank: NM_201394. Akt3 effectively phosphorylated WT plectin but not mutants containing S4127A. Similar to the in vitro kinase assay data of human plectin fragments, the efficiency of S4245A mutant phosphorylation by recombinant Akt3 kinase was only partially reduced, confirming Ser4127 as the primary phosphorylation site (Figure 2C). To further test this, we transfected HEK293T cells with WT plectin or S4127A mutant and showed that whereas WT plectin was effectively phosphorylated upon EGF stimulation, the S4127A mutant was not (Figure 2D).

Plectin is phosphorylated by Akt3 in triple-negative breast cancer cells

In order to investigate whether plectin is an Akt isoform-specific substrate, endogenous plectin was immunoprecipitated from serum-starved HCC1143 cells and incubated with recombinant, active Akt. The results showed that plectin was phosphorylated by both Akt1 and Akt3 but not Akt2 in an in vitro kinase assay (Figure 3A). Similar results were obtained in an in vitro kinase assay using HCC1937 cells (Figure S6). Next, we used a tet-on doxycycline (dox)-inducible CRISPR/Cas9 knockout system approach to knockout Akt1 or Akt3 in Hs578T and HCC1143 cells to determine whether plectin is a substrate for Akt1 or Akt3 in breast cancer cells. Knockout of Akt3, but not Akt1, significantly eliminated EGF or IGF-induced phosphorylation of plectin in both Hs578T and HCC1143 cells, indicating that plectin is specifically phosphorylated by Akt3 in TNBC cells (Figure 3B). Agreeing with these results, endogenous plectin and Akt3 were shown to be co-immunoprecipitated (co-IP) in HCC1143 cells (Figure 3C).

Knockout of plectin inhibits triple-negative breast cancer cell migration and invasion

To determine the role of plectin in modulating TNBC cells, functional assays were conducted in Hs578T cells using the dox-inducible CRISPR/Cas9 system. Cells were infected with SpCas9-puro, selected with puromycin, and surviving cells were introduced with GFP-tagged sgRNAs targeting the plectin coding sequence (CDS). Cells with high GFP expression were sorted using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS). Plectin was efficiently silenced upon dox treatment using two gRNAs in Hs578T cells (Figure 4A). In agreement with its principal roles in invasive migration shown by others,17,28,29 depletion of plectin had limited effects on TNBC cell growth in 2D and 3D cultures, as well as colony formation (Figures 4A–4C). In contrast, plectin knockout dramatically impaired Hs578T 3D invasion, as measured by invadopodia length and area (Figure 4D). Consistent with cell invasion, plectin depletion in Hs578T cells attenuated chemotactic cell mobility in the real-time cell migration assay (Figure 4E). Similar results were obtained in HCC1937 cells, with a significant reduction of invadopodia and migration in plectin-depleted cells (Figures S7A and S7B). In HCC1143 cells, we also observed a reduction of 3D spheroid growth and migration upon plectin knockout (Figures S7C and S7D). Taken together, these results suggest that plectin plays a critical role in TNBC cell migration and invasion, consistent with its central role as a master regulator of the cytoskeleton.

Plectin phosphorylation modulates triple-negative breast cancer cell migration and its colocalization with vimentin

We next sought to investigate the role of plectin phosphorylation in invasive migration. Given that plectin is a large protein of over 500 kDa, achieving high transfection efficiency in breast cancer cells has been challenging. The Hs578T cells were first transfected with full-length plectin tagged with GFP (pGR245-WT or pGR245-S4127A), followed by enrichment using FACS and transwell migration assays. Notably, with a similar expression level of exogenous full-length plectin, as indicated by the GFP signal in immunoblotting, cells transfected with the S4127A mutant of mouse plectin migrated at a slower rate compared to cells transfected with WT plectin. Nevertheless, the migratory ability of cells expressing the S4127 mutants was slightly higher than naive cells. These findings suggest that the phosphorylation of plectin plays an important role in cell mobility (Figure 5A). Consistent with the pro-tumorigenic role of Akt3 in TNBC,8 Akt3 knockdown dramatically impaired Hs578T cell migration. Knocking out plectin in Akt3-depleted cells did not further reduce migration, suggesting that Akt3 and plectin are in the same signaling pathway regulating TNBC cell migration (Figure 5B). To determine if plectin phosphorylation by Akt3 is critical for the migration phenotype, either the WT or S4127A mutant plectin was reintroduced into Akt3-depleted Hs578T cells. Since WT plectin cannot be phosphorylated in the absence of Akt3, it is expected that WT and S4127A mutant plectin behave in a similar manner in Akt3-depleted cells. Indeed, the impaired migration upon Akt3 knockdown was not rescued by either WT or S4127A mutant plectin, indicating that plectin functions downstream of Akt3 to promote TNBC cell migration (Figure 5C).

The IF-binding domain (IFBD) of plectin is known to interact with various intermediate filament subunit proteins, including vimentin.12,30 To explore the effect of plectin phosphorylation on its interaction with vimentin, we stimulated serum-starved TNBC cells with growth factors and inhibited the PI3K-Akt pathway using Akt inhibitor MK2206. In serum-starved cells, plectin exhibited minimal colocalization with vimentin. Upon IGF-1 stimulation, there was an increased accumulation and colocalization of plectin and vimentin in the perinuclear area. Importantly, this colocalization was abolished when Akt was inhibited, highlighting the potential role of plectin phosphorylation in modulating its interaction with vimentin (Figures 5D and S8A). We next tested if the phosphorylation of plectin at Ser4127 modulates its colocalization with vimentin. In serum-starved pGR245-WT transfected cells, there was minimal colocalization of GFP-plectin and vimentin, whereas increased colocalization at the perinuclear area was observed upon IGF-1 stimulation. However, there was minimal colocalization of GFP-plectin mutant (S4127A) and vimentin with IGF-1 treatment (Figures 5E, S8B, and S8C). Taken together, these results demonstrate the critical role of plectin phosphorylation in regulating TNBC cell migration and its colocalization with vimentin.

Discussion

We have previously identified critical functions of Akt3 in TNBC growth, cancer stemness, EMT, and drug resistance.8,9,10 In this study, we set out to identify isoform-specific signaling that mediates Akt3’s function, as it has significant clinical implications. Recently, the first Akt inhibitor-capivasertib (AZD5363) was approved by the FDA for use in combination with fulvestrant to treat patients with breast cancer with either PI3K, PTEN, or AKT1 alterations. However, previous studies have shown that inhibiting the Akt pathway by pan-Akt inhibitors leads to pathway reactivation responses, including the upregulation of growth factor receptor tyrosine kinases.31 This observation also agrees with our previous findings that the treatment of prostate tumor cells with the Akt inhibitor results in IGF1R upregulation. Using the shRNA approach, we further demonstrated that Akt1 and Akt2 silencing lead to the upregulation and downregulation of IGF1R levels, respectively. These findings are in line with our proposal that targeting a specific Akt isoform in different tumor contexts offers the most effective treatment strategy. In breast cancer, it is interesting that while Akt2 and Akt3 silencing result in reduced spheroid invasion, Akt1 silencing promotes spheroid invasiveness as well as metastasis.8,32,33 These isoform-specific functions of Akt in breast cancer further advocate for the development of therapeutic strategies targeting individual isoforms. They also underscore the importance of elucidating the detailed molecular mechanisms that drive the isoform-specific effects on tumors. Regarding this, despite more than 150 pan-Akt substrates having been identified, only a few have been shown to be isoform-specific.4 Whereas p21 CIP1, SKP2, MDM2, and palladin are shown to be Akt1-specific targets, AS160, Tmod3, and Ankrd2 are specifically phosphorylated by Akt2.5,34,35,36,37,38,39 In lung cancer, a phosphoproteomics screen has revealed that IWS1 is phosphorylated by Akt1 and Akt3, but not Akt2.40 To the best of our knowledge, we have identified YB1 as the first bona fide Akt3-specific substrate which plays key roles in TNBC stemness and EMT. This prompted us to search for Akt3 targets in TNBC 3D spheroids using a phosphoproteomic screen. Plectin, a multifunctional cytoskeletal linker protein, was uncovered as a candidate to be phosphorylated specifically by Akt3 at Ser4386. This site was also detected in another large-scale proteomic study aimed at characterizing cell circuitry and substrates downstream of Akt, RSK, and p70 S6 kinase.24 Interestingly, through in vitro and in vivo validation studies, we found that although Ser4386 of plectin can be phosphorylated by Akt, another site, Ser4268, is predominantly phosphorylated by Akt3 in TNBC cells. Through the alternative splicing of the 5′ untranslated region in the first exon, at least 11 plectin isoforms are generated.27 The subcellular localization, protein expression, and function of plectin are specified in an isoform-specific manner.41 It is noteworthy that our current study focuses on the 1a isoform of plectin, given its high expression in TNBC cells. Whether other plectin isoforms are effectively phosphorylated at Ser4268 and Ser4386 by Akt3 remains to be investigated. The results show that plectin can be phosphorylated by both Akt1 and Akt3 in the in vitro kinase assay, but exhibits specific phosphorylation by Akt3 in cells (Figures 3A and 3B). Given that the in vitro kinase assay is an artificial cell-free system, it is possible that the distinct subcellular localization of Akt isoforms, the effect of other scaffolding proteins toward specific Akt isoforms, and some intrinsic molecular mechanisms all contribute to the Akt3-specific phosphorylation in TNBC cells.42 Indeed, despite the high homology of sequence and structure among Akt isoforms, Akt1 but not Akt2 exclusively phosphorylates palladin where the linker region of Akt1 is responsible for the substrate specificity.5 However, the precise mechanisms of how Akt3 modulates plectin phosphorylation remain to be investigated.

Our study identifies plectin, a versatile and core regulator of the cytoskeleton, as a novel Akt3-specific substrate. We also determine the critical role of plectin phosphorylation in regulating TNBC migration. As a large multidomain protein of over 500 kDa, plectin compromises a variable N terminus, followed by an actin-binding domain (ABD), a plakin domain, a coiled central rod domain, and a C-terminal domain.43 The long C terminus is composed of six plectin repeat domains (PRDs) separated by linker regions and a C-terminal tail. The IFBD between PRDs 5 and 6 is well known for its function in recruiting and anchoring IF networks to subcellular compartments, modulating cell polarization, migration, and cytoskeleton architecture.44 Ser4642 (NM_201380) at the C terminus of plectin has been reported to be phosphorylated by MNK2 and PKA to modulate plectin’s dynamic interaction with IF networks.45 A similar function has been ascribed to Thr4542, plectin's unique target site of CDK1.46 Using a transwell migration assay, we showed that plectin phosphorylation at Ser4268 inside IFBD promotes TNBC cell migration. Our findings also suggest a mechanism by which plectin phosphorylation is involved in IF networking.30 Colocalization of plectin and vimentin in the perinuclear region is induced by IGF-1, which is reduced by the Akt inhibitor MK2206. Our immunofluorescence data of vimentin also suggest that the phosphorylation of peripheral plectin might lead to a collapsed vimentin IF network. These findings are in line with the well-known function of vimentin reorganization in invasive migration.47,48,49 Notably, vimentin localization upon IGF-1 stimulation showed the same pattern as the polarized vimentin organization in migrating MDA-MB-231 cells, further supporting our hypothesis.50 In addition to cell motility, vimentin is implicated in the positioning of the Golgi apparatus and mitochondria in the cytosol.51,52,53 Furthermore, besides binding to vimentin, plectin has been reported to connect with LINC, the linker of nucleoskeleton and cytoskeleton, through nesprin3 on the outer nuclear membrane. Nesprin3 also connects plectin to SUN1 and SUN2, which are linked with the nuclear lamina of the inner nuclear membrane.54 In future studies, it would be interesting to examine if plectin phosphorylation at Ser4268 regulates these molecular processes as well.

Our finding that plectin has a promigratory role in TNBC is also supported by plectin being a biomarker for primary and metastatic PDAC.29,55 Recently, plectin has also been demonstrated to promote prostate cancer growth and metastasis in xenograft models.25 Interestingly, selective and robust expression of cancer-specific plectin (CSP) on the cell surface has been shown in different cancers, including PDAC, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and head and neck cell carcinoma, indicating CSP as a promising therapeutic target.56 In our previous study, Akt3 was found to be overexpressed in TNBC but not luminal breast cancer, and played critical roles in TNBC growth and stemness. Given the overexpression of Akt3 and the prominent migratory role of plectin in TNBC, we proposed and provided evidence of Akt3-plectin signaling in TNBC cell migration in this study. It is plausible that this signaling axis exists in other cancers, particularly the cancer types where Akt3 is overexpressed and activated. Future studies are warranted to examine Akt3-plectin signaling in those cancer types, to further enhance the translational value of our study. Plectin is unique for its isoform diversity due to the alternative splicing of the first exon.27 The transcript diversity also gives rise to plectin’s isoform-specific subcellular localization by docking to distinct interaction partners.41 Isoform-specific roles of plectin in tumorigenesis have not been well studied. Given its clinical implications, with the development of isoform-specific antibodies and improved transfection efficiency of large proteins such as plectin, future studies are warranted to dissect the isoform-specific effects of plectin phosphorylation in tumor progression.

In summary, our study uncovers plectin as an Akt3-specific substrate. The phosphorylation of plectin modulates TNBC cell migration and IF dynamics. Considering that Akt isoforms are often hyperactivated in breast cancer, and significant efforts are being made to develop Akt inhibitors for cancer therapy, our findings highlight the need to thoroughly investigate the mechanisms through which the PI3K-Akt pathway regulates invasive migration in breast cancer.

Limitations of the study

Plasmids expressing mouse full-length plectin are used in this study instead of human plectin due to material availability and the technical challenge of cloning a big gene. Given the technical challenge of transfecting plasmids containing the WT vs. the SA mutant of plectin into breast tumor cells, careful optimization of the experimental protocol is required to assess the function of in vivo Akt3-plectin signaling in future studies.

Experimental model and study participant details

Cell culture

MDA-MB-231, HEK293T, HCC1143, HCC1937, and Hs578T were obtained from ATCC. MDA-MB-231 and HEK293T cells were maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle medium (DMEM; Gibco) supplemented with 10% Tet system-approved Fetal Bovine Serum (FBS; Clontech). HCC1143 and HCC1937 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Gibco) with 10% FBS. Hs578T cells were maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 10 μg/mL insulin. All cell lines obtained from the cell banks listed above are tested for authentication using short tandem repeat profiling and passaged for fewer than 6 months, and routinely assayed for mycoplasma contamination.

Method details

Growth factors and inhibitors

Cells were stimulated with recombinant human IGF-1 (R&D Systems) and recombinant human EGF (R&D Systems) for 20 min at final concentrations of 100 ng/mL and 20 ng/mL, respectively. MK2206 (Cayman), and GDC0068 (Chemgood) were added to cells 30 min prior to growth factor stimulation at a final concentration of 1 μM. PF-4708671 (Cayman) was added to cells 20 min prior to stimulation at a final concentration of 100 nM.

Antibodies

Anti-phospho-Akt substrate (pAkt-motif) (9614S), anti-phospho-Akt Ser473 (4060L), anti-Akt (4691S), anti-phospho-PRAS40 Thr246 (2997S), anti-PRAS40 (2691S), anti-phospho-GSK3β (9336S), anti-GSK3β (9315), anti-Akt1 (2938S), anti-Akt2 (3063S), anti-Akt3 (8018S), and anti-actin (3700S) were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology. Anti-plectin (PA579829) was obtained from Life Technologies, anti-plectin (ab32528) was purchased from Abcam. Anti-GFP (sc-9996) and anti-vimentin (sc-6260) were obtained from Santa Cruz. (H + L) HRP conjugate Goat-anti-Rabbit IgG antibody (AP307P) and (H + L) HRP conjugate Goat-anti-Mouse IgG antibody (AP308P) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich.

Plasmids and transfection

CRISPR/Cas9 knockout system was used to knock out PLEC, AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3. FgH1tUTG (70183) and LentiV_Cas9_puro (108100) were purchased from Addgene. Guide RNAs (gRNAs) targeting PLEC were designed using the online tool https://portals.broadinstitute.org/gpp/public/ and synthesized by IDT. The gRNAs with sticky ends were subsequently cloned into FgH1tUTG at BsmBI restriction site. Truncated human plectin (EGFP-hPL-B4-E), encoding from B4 box of plakin repeat domains (PRDs) to the C-terminus was a gift from Luca Borradori.45 pGR245 expressing full-length mouse plectin (mPL) isoform 1a with EGFP fused at C-terminus was constructed as previously described.41 EGFP-hPL-B4-E Ser4386Ala and Ser4268Ala were constructed using site-directed mutagenesis with the following primers: hPL S4386A-F, 5′-C TCCCGTTCCTCCGCGGTGGGATCCTC-3’; hPL-S4386A-R, 5′-GAGGATCCCACCGCGGAGGAACGGGAG-3’; hPL S4268A-F, 5′-GAGCGGAAGACGGCATCCAAGTCCTCC-3’; hPL S4268A-R, 5′- GGAGGACTTGGATGCCGTCTTCCGCTC-3’. pGR245 Ser4245Ala and Ser4127Ala were generated by homologous recombination (HR). Briefly, to generate pGR245 Ser4245Ala, the two shorter fragments were amplified from pGR245 using primers mPL HR-F 5′-AACCACACAGGTGTATACTGAGGAGGAGACTCGGA-3′ and mPL S4245A-R 5′-GATGAGCCCACAGCGGAGGAGCGGGAA-3′, or mPL S4245A-F 5′-TTCCCGCTCCTCCGCTGTGGGCTCATC-3′ and mPL HR-R 5′-CCGCGGTACCGTCGACTGCAGAATTCGGCCACTGC-3’. The fragments were gel recovered and used as the templates to amplify the whole fragment bearing the mutated site and HR arm. The resulting PCR product was then incubated with enzyme digested vector in the desired cloning system to perform homologous recombination (Vazyme C115). pGR245 Ser4127Ala was generated using primers mPL HR-F, mPL HR-R, mPL S4127A-F 5′-GGAACGGAAGACAGCCTCCAAGTCCTCAGT-3′ and mPL S4127A-R 5′-ACTGAGGACTTGGAGGCTGTCTTCCGTTCC-3′ following the same protocol as above. All constructed plasmids were sequenced. EGFP-hPL-B4-E and pGR245 were transfected into breast cancer cells using lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen L3000015) following manufacturer’s product manuals. Transfection of pGR245 into HEK293T cells was performed using transfection grade polyethylenimine (PEI) (Polysciences 23966-1).

Lentivirus infection

To produce lentiviral supernatants, HEK293T cells were co-transfected with FgH1tUTG or lentiV_Cas9_puro, psPAX2, and VSV-G using PEI. Lentiviruses were collected 60 h post-transfection and filtered by 0.45 μm syringe filter (Thermo fisher 7232545). Breast cancer cells were infected with 0.3 mL lentivirus with 5 μg/mL polybrene for 16–24 h in a 6-well plate. Cells were then selected with 1–2 μg/mL puromycin for 7 days or sorted by fluorescence-activated cell sorter (Sony).

RT-qPCR

Real-time PCR was carried out using TaqMan Reverse Transcription Reagents (Invitrogen N8080234) and SYBR Green Supermix (BIO-RAD 1725272) on QuantStudio 12K Flex Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems). Primers targeting plectin isoforms were designed as previously described.28

Immunofluorescence analysis

Cells plated on glass bottom cell culture dish (NEST Scientific 801002) were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde/phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 20 min and permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100/PBS for 2 min. Cells were then rinsed with PBS/glycine 3 times, 10 min each and blocked with blocking buffer (0.05% NaN3, 0.1% BSA, 0.2% Triton X-, 0.04% Tween 20, 10% goat serum in PBS) for 45 min. Cell were incubated with primary antibody in blocking buffer for 2 h followed by Alexa Fluor Goat anti-Rabbit or anti-Mouse secondary antibody (Jackson ImmunoResearch) for 1h. Cells were then rinsed twice with PBS and stained with Hoechst (Invitrogen H3570) in PBS for 5–10 min. Confocal images were captured by A1HD25 Microscope (Nikon).

Immunoblots and immunoprecipitation

Cells were lysed in EBC buffer (0.5% NP-40, 120 mM NaCl, 50 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 1 mM sodium pyrophosphate, 20 mM sodium fluoride, 50 nM calyculin, 2 mM EDTA, 2 mM EGTA, and proteinase inhibitor cocktail) for 30 min on ice with occasional vortexing and supernatants were collected by centrifugation at 13,000 rpm at 4°C for 10 min. Protein concentration was measured by DC protein assay (BIO-RAD 5000112) using BioTek Synergy H1 Microplate Reader. 20–40 μg lysates were then resolved by SDS-PAGE and signal was developed by incubation with specific antibodies and Pierce ECL Western Blotting Substrate (ECL) (Thermo Scientific 32106) or super sensitive ECL (Thermo Scientific 34095). For immunoprecipitation, cell extracts were incubated with primary antibody at 4°C overnight followed by incubation with Dynabeads Protein G (Invitrogen 10003D) or Protein G Sepharose 4 Fast Flow (Cytiva 17-0618-01). Immune complexes were then analyzed by immunoblotting.

In vitro kinase assay

HEK293T cells were serum starved and transfected with pGR245-WT, pGR245-S4245A, pGR245-S4127A or pGR245-S4245A + S4127A for 20 h before harvesting. HCC1143 and HCC1937 cells were serum starved for 20 h before harvesting lysate. GFP or plectin was immunoprecipitated from cell lysates, followed by incubation at 30°C for 1 h with recombinant, active Akt1 (Sigma-Aldrich SRP5001), recombinant, active Akt2 (Sigma-Aldrich A2233) or recombinant, active Akt3 (Sigma-Aldrich A0983) in the presence of 250 μM ATP in a kinase buffer (Cell Signaling Technology 9802S). The reaction was terminated by the addition of SDS-PAGE loading buffer and the samples were analyzed by western blotting.

Transwell migration and 3D invasion assay

For real-time transwell migration, 2×104 cells in 50 μL serum-free medium with 0.1% BSA were seeded in triplicate to upper inserts, and full medium were added to bottom chambers of FluoroBlok 96-Multiwell Insert system (Corning 351163). The plate was screened by CellInsight CX7 LZR Pro High Content Screening Platform (Thermo Scientific) and captured images were analyzed by CellProfiler. For the conventional transwell migration assay, Hs578T cells transfected with pGR245-WT or pGR245-S4127A were sorted with GFP, and 2×104 cells in 0.1% BSA/serum-free DMEM were plated to the upper chamber of 6.5 mm Transwell with 8.0 μm Pore Polycarbonate Membrane Insert (Corning 3422). Hs578T complete medium was added to the bottom chamber followed by incubation for 17 h at 37°C. Cells migrated to the bottom were fixed and stained with 0.1% crystal violet. Representative images were acquired by Nikon Eclipse Tis2 microscope at 4X magnification objective. 3D invasion assay was performed as previously described.57 Briefly, 4000–8000 cells in complete medium were seeded in Nunclon U-Shaped-Bottom Microplate (Thermo Scientific 174925) for 4 days to reach a 300–500 μm diameter. The medium was removed, and basement membrane matrix (BMM) (Gibco A1413202) was added to spheroids followed by gentle centrifugation at 300g for 3 min at 4°C to keep spheroids locate at well center. Full growth medium was added to the solidified BMM, and photos were captured at desired time points by a Nikon Eclipse Tis2 microscope at a 4X magnification objective. Invadopodia length and area were measured by NIS-Elements 5.02.03.

3D cultures

3D cultures were prepared as previously described.58 In brief, a BMM coated 96-well plate (Corning 3610) was solidified at 37°C for 30–60 min 1000–3000 cells were seeded per well in complete medium supplemented with 2% BMM (assay medium). After 7 days, CellTiter-Glo 3D Cell Viability Assay (Promega G9682) was carried out to quantify spheroid growth according to the manufacturer’s protocols.

Proteomic screen by phosphopeptide immunoaffinity purification (IAP) and LC-MS/MS analysis

Briefly, cells containing Akt isoform-specific shRNA were cultured in 3D for 4 days followed by dox (100 ng/mL) treatment for 3 days before harvesting.8 Proteins in the cell lysates were processed for MS analysis using the PTMScan phospho-Akt substrate motif mAb 2 (RXRXXS∗/T∗) kit (Cell Signaling Technology 5563) according to manufacturer’s protocol. Proteins were reduced with 4.5 mM dithiothreitol (DTT) and alkylated with iodoacetamide at 11 mM, diluted to a final concentration of 2 M urea and digested with LysC (Roche) (1:250; w:w) overnight at room temperature. Trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) was then added to peptides at a final concentration of 1%, followed by centrifugation to remove precipitate. Peptides were purified using Sep-Pak C18 column followed by lyophilization. The purified peptides were dissolved in IAP buffer (50 mM MOPS pH 7.2, 10 mM sodium phosphate, 50 mM NaCl) and incubated with immobilized AKT substrate antibody (Cell Signaling Technology 9614) beads for 30 min at 4°C. The beads were washed 4 times with 1 mL PBS at 4°C and peptides were eluted from beads with 0.15% TFA, followed by purification using ZipTip. Purified peptides were subjected to post-IAP digestion with trypsin using trypsin microtips (ProteogenBio) and LC-MS/MS were performed using Thermo QExactive HF mass spectrometer at the Mass Spectrometry Core of Harvard Medical School. Phosphorylation data for dox-treated samples were compared to paired data for untreated samples. Phosphorylation events with fold-change ≥2 were considered as the threshold for significance.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism. Statistical comparisons between two groups were carried out using unpaired Student’s t test and the values were shown as mean ± SEM. Throughout all figures: ∗p < 0.05, ∗∗p < 0.01, ∗∗∗p < 0.001, and ns = no statistical significance. All the statistical details of experiments can be found in figure legends.

Clinical characteristics and treatment efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in patients with ICIs-induced Adrenal insufficiency
Authors: Jing Xiang, Xueni Liu, Yue Hao, Yanyan Zhu, Minhua Wu, Jian Lou, Yonghui Wang, Chunwei Xu, Yanru Xie, Jianhui Huang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The median time to the diagnosis of AI after starting ICI therapy was 5.2 (3.0 − 7.5) months.•Median PFS was 16.0 months (95% CI: 11.7 − 20.3 months) in patients with ICIs-induced Adrenal insufficiency.•This is the first study to compare the difference between the severity of irAEs and efficacy of ICI in patients with ICI-induced AI.Keywords: Immunotherapy, Adrenal insufficiency (AI), Immune-related adverse events (irAEs), Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)

Introduction

In recent years, the treatment of several cancer types, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), has evolved due to the introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) that block programmed death-1 (PD-1), programmed death-ligand-1, and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4) [1,2]. However, the use of ICI has been associated with immune-related adverse events (irAEs), which range in frequency from 54% to 76% and are characterized by immune cells attacking any healthy organs or tissues in systems such as the respiratory, neurological, skin, and endocrine systems [3,4]. The increased use of ICI has increased the number of documented irAEs, underscoring the necessity for careful patient monitoring during therapy and the precise identification of toxicities.

Endocrine AEs are among the most common AEs and mainly involve endocrine glands, such as the pituitary, thyroid, pancreas, and adrenal glands, causing endocrine dysfunction. Adrenal insufficiency (AI) is a rare irAE characterized by insufficient glucocorticoid production or activity and is further subdivided into primary, secondary, and mixed AI [5,6]. A few previous studies have reported an incidence of ICI-AIs between 0.6% and 4% in patients who received immunotherapy [7,8]. In addition to having a low incidence, the symptoms of ICI-AIs are nonspecific, such as fatigue, weight loss, nausea, and loss of appetite, and it is often difficult for physicians to diagnose AI because hyposecretion of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and cortisol is not routinely examined in clinical practice [9]. Patients’ quality of life is inevitably affected by delayed clinical diagnoses and treatments, which may even be fatal in certain cases. Therefore, early diagnosis and therapy are crucial for improving patient prognosis. An increasing number of patients are being diagnosed with AI because of the development of cancer immunotherapy and the use of ICIs for treatment. Recently, only a few case reports and a series of studies have been published on these patients [6,10]. Moreover, the clinical features, prognosis, and therapeutic efficacy of the patients who developed ICI-induced AI remain unclear.

In the present study, we retrospectively surveyed the clinical characteristics of ICI-induced AI and the efficacy of treatment in patients who developed AI following immunotherapy in the real world.

Methods

Patients

The clinical information of patients who developed AI during the immunotherapy period at LiShui Municipal Central Hospital and Zhejiang Cancer Hospital between December 2020 and February 2023 was retrospectively obtained. We gathered information on the patients’ age, sex, tumor type, AI severity, PD-L1 status, the period until the beginning of AI, drugs administered (including ICIs and corticosteroids), type and line of treatment, pituitary magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), blood test results, and concurrent irAEs from their electronic medical records. All the patients received drug doses following the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines or clinical trials. All ICI regimens used a set dosage based on NCCN recommendations or clinical studies. Patients were administered 200 mg of pembrolizumab, 200 mg of camrelizumab, 200 mg of sintilimab, or other PD-1 inhibitors every two or three weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity levels were observed. Fourteen patients (70%) in our study received ICIs together with chemotherapy. The chemotherapy regimens followed the NCCN recommendations and included conventional dosages of paclitaxel plus carboplatin, vinorelbine, S-1, pemetrexed plus cisplatin, and nab-paclitaxel plus carboplatin.

Evaluation of adrenal insufficiency and efficacy

All patients were evaluated using computed tomography (CT) imaging every two treatment cycles or at an early stage when there were significant signs of progression. We recorded the time of patient development with AI from the start of the immunotherapy. The serum basal cortisol (normal range: 3.70 – 19.40 μg/dL) at 8 am and the plasma basal ACTH (normal range: 7.2 – 63.3 μg/dL) at 8 am and 4 pm were routinely tested during the treatment. The severity of irAEs was rated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. Based on the irAE grade, we defined grades 1–2 as mild and grades 3–4 as severe. Patients who recovered to grade 1 after hormone use were labeled as the “group with recovered.”

In addition, the tumor response was assessed using the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1). The overall response rate (ORR) was equal to the rates of complete response (CR) plus partial responses (PR). Progression-free survival (PFS) was determined from the time of the beginning of ICI treatment until any signs of disease progression or death from any cause. Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the start of immunotherapy to death or the last follow-up evaluation. The last follow-up date was February 23, 2023.

Statistical analysis

Quantitative variables were expressed as means and standard deviations, whereas categorical data were expressed as numbers and percentages. Survival curves, such as PFS and OS, were prepared using the Kaplan–Meier method and log-rank test. IBM SPSS Statistics software (version 25.0) and GraphPad Prism version 9.0 were used to analyze the data.

Results

Patient characteristics

Among the 1014 patients who were treated with immunotherapy at LiShui Municipal Central Hospital and Zhejiang Cancer Hospital, this study included 20 cancer patients who experienced AI as a result of immunotherapy. Table 1 lists the clinical and baseline characteristics of the patients. Patients' median age ranged from 49 to 80 years, and 80% of them were men. Nineteen patients had a PS score of 0 to 1. Two of the eight patients whose PD-L1 status was known displayed a tumor proportion score for PD-L1 that was higher than 50%. Grade 3–4 AI occurred in 70% of the patients, and the rest had grade 1–2 AI. No grade 5 AI was observed in the cohort. First-line PD-1 inhibitor therapy was used in 14 patients, second-line therapy in 5 patients, and third-line therapy in 1 patient. Fourteen patients received a combination of immunotherapy and chemotherapy, whereas six patients received immunotherapy monotherapy. Lung cancer (n = 14) was the first type of cancer targeted by ICI regimens in these patients, followed by esophageal cancer (n = 2) and other malignancies (colon cancer, kidney cancer, ovarian cancer, and cervical cancer).

Clinical course

Table 2 summarizes the clinical and laboratory findings of the patients with AI. The median period from the beginning of immunotherapy to the onset of AI was 5.2 months (95% confidence interval [CI]: 3.0 − 7.5 months) in all patients. Fatigue was the most prevalent symptom experienced by all patients. In addition, four patients vomited, and seven experienced nausea. Eleven (55%) and fifteen (75%) patients had eosinophilia and hyponatremia, respectively. Eosinophilia and hyponatremia were not observed in four patients. None of the patients who developed AI showed abnormal pituitary MRI results. Hypothyroidism, the most common concomitant irAE, developed in five patients. One patient diagnosed with AI also experienced hypoglycemia as an irAE.

All the patients received corticosteroid hormone replacement treatment. Most patients (17/20, 85%) were treated with hydrocortisone tablets (20 mg in the morning and 10 mg in the afternoon). Treatment with hydrocortisone replacement was effective in all patients. After steroid delivery, seven patients recovered their corticosteroid function to grade 1. Twelve patients discontinued immunotherapy due to AI, and only three patients restarted immunotherapy after the AI was under control.

Survival evaluation

Figs. 1 and 2 depict the clinical outcomes of the individuals who developed AI. During the median 12.4 months of follow-up, 2 patients died, including one patient with esophageal carcinoma and one patient with cervical adenocarcinoma. In terms of the best response to immunotherapy, two patients achieved CR, 12 patients achieved PR, and six achieved stable disease. Thus, the ORR was 70% and the disease control rate was 100%. The median PFS for the patients was 16.0 months (95% CI: 11.7 − 20.3 months) (Fig. 2A); however, the median OS was not achieved.

We further analyzed the outcomes of immunotherapy in patients with and without AI recovery. The median PFS was not significantly different between patients with and without AI recovery (11.8 vs. 16.2 months, P = 0.3) (Fig. 2B). The median PFS of the monotherapy and combination therapy was 16.0 and 23.4 months, respectively (P = 0.8, Fig. 2C). Next, we compared the clinical outcomes of patients with mild and severe irAEs (Fig. 2D). Median PFS was 12.1 months (95% CI, 11.1 − 13.0) and 16.2 months (95% CI, 10.5 − 21.9) in patients with mild and severe irAEs, respectively (Fig. 2D).

Discussion

We described the cases of 20 patients receiving immunotherapy and later developing AI in the present study and found that AI may be linked to a good response to immunotherapy. This is the first study to compare the difference between the severity of irAEs and efficacy of ICI in patients with ICI-induced AI.

Secondary AI, which occurs in 2 − 10% of patients treated with nivolumab, is a common subtype of AI [11]. The incidence of AI observed in clinical practice is not as low as previously reported, which may be due to the ignorance of these AEs previously. In the present study, all the patients were diagnosed with secondary AI, in which both cortisol and ACTH levels were low. Our patients diagnosed with AI were mainly men, and the other complicated irAE was hypothyroidism; these observations are similar to those previously reported [6,12]. In a large-sample case series study by CUI K et al., the majority of the patients had melanoma [6], while 70% of the patients in the present study had lung cancer. This difference may be due to the low incidence of melanoma in the study region.

We found that 75% and 55% of the patients in the present study had hyponatremia and eosinophilia, respectively. A few authors have suggested that new-onset eosinophilia, but not hyponatremia, is related to secondary AI [13]. Eosinophilia may be a helpful biomarker for the early detection of AI in individuals receiving ICI because it was demonstrated to appear before the development of AI symptoms [14]. Therefore, patients who experience fatigue and newly developed eosinophilia after immunotherapy should be suspected of having ICI-induced AI.

AI is difficult to recover from and may even be permanent, often requiring long-term hormone replacement therapy. All enrolled patients received corticosteroid hormone replacement treatment after being diagnosed with AI, and none of the patients experienced an exacerbation of AI-related symptoms after treatment with anti-PD-1 antibodies under corticosteroid replacement. Thirteen of the 20 patients did not recover to grade 1, whereas seven patients recovered to grade 1. The difference in PFS was not significant between the patients with and without AI recovery in the present study. Of the 12 patients who discontinued immunotherapy, only four resumed immunotherapy. Four patients did not resume immunotherapy because of disease progression when the anti-PD-1 antibody treatment was discontinued. Two patients refused further immunotherapy because of fear of serious toxic side effects. A previous study found that it is safe to continue immunotherapy with appropriate corticosteroid replacement therapy [15]. Immunotherapy was reinitiated with corticosteroid replacement treatment in three patients, and the effectiveness of immunotherapy was acceptable. These findings suggest that patients with ICI-induced AI may benefit from immunotherapy possibly not requiring the permanent discontinuation of ICI as long as irAEs are closely monitored for signs and symptoms during treatment. Further studies are required to determine the best strategy for selecting patients who can be safely retreated with ICI.

The observed time to develop AI in the present study was consistent with that reported in previous studies [6,16,17]. A case series found a long latency period from the initiation of ICI therapy to the development of AI in monotherapy (17.0 vs. 12.0 weeks, P = 0.08), whereas, in the present study, the difference in the median time to develop AI between the monotherapy and combination treatment groups was minimal (4.2 vs. 5.4 months, P = 0.9). This could be related to the small cohort size in the present study. The median PFS was 16.0 months in all included patients, 16.0 months in monotherapy, and 23.4 months in combined therapy. However, the median OS was not determined. Among the 10 patients with NSCLC who were identified with AI due to pembrolizumab, the PFS with monotherapy and combination therapy were 16.8 and 9.9 months, respectively [18]. A few researchers have observed that patients who experienced AEs had a better chance of survival than those who did not, and mild irAEs were associated with a better prognosis [19,20]. However, in the present study, a non-statistically significant PFS benefit of immunotherapy was observed in patients with mild irAEs. However, the relationship between the efficacy of ICIs and hormone replacement therapy remains unclear. Several studies have reported that hormone therapy does not interfere with the therapeutic effects of ICIs [21,22]. However, a retrospective study reported that patients with NSCLC who received immunotherapy had inferior clinical outcomes when they received >10 mg/day of prednisone at baseline [23]. A similar study discovered that high-dose corticosteroid replacement therapy was associated with a poor prognosis in melanoma patients with ipilimumab-induced hypophysis [24]. Hence, further research is required to determine whether the use of corticosteroids in the treatment of irAEs affects ICI results.

The retrospective nature of our study was the main limitation of the present study. In addition, the small number of patients included in our study may have influenced the results. Although our findings are clinically significant, clear conclusions should be drawn with caution. Further research is required to clarify the clinical characteristics of AI and the relationship between AI and immunotherapy.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we described the clinical characteristics of ICI-induced secondary AI in a cohort from our hospital and suggested that ICI-associated AI is a rare AE. Patients with AI appear to respond favorably to PD-1 inhibitors. These results might be helpful for the early detection of AI. Finally, we recommend close clinical monitoring of cortisol levels for early detection of AI.

Twin-limbed intravenous leiomyomatosis extending into the right heart
Authors: Jie Zhao, Jia Liao, Qun Yu, Ziqiao Lei, Clement Lau
Published: 2025

A 47-year-old woman was referred for evaluation of an incidentally discovered right atrial mass. She had a history of uterine fibroids and underwent a subtotal hysterectomy three years ago. Cardiac ultrasound revealed a mass (arrow) within the right atrium, protruding into the right ventricle through the tricuspid valve (Panel A). Cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) demonstrated a tumour extending from the inferior vena cava (IVC) through the right atrium and into the right ventricle (Panel B, Supplementary material online, Videos S1 and S2). T1-weighted imaging showed the mass exhibited an isointense signal (Panel C). T2-weighted imaging with fat suppression showed the mass exhibited a high signal (Panel D). Coronary magnetic resonance angiography in the arterial and venous phases demonstrated a progressive enhancement pattern of the mass within the right atrium (Panels E and F). Late gadolinium enhancement (LGE) showed homogeneous enhancement of the mass in the right atrium (Panel H). Coronal contrast-enhanced arterial phase curved planar reconstruction computed tomography (CT) image showed an enhancing lesion extending from the left uterine vein to the left internal iliac vein, continuing into the left common iliac vein, and ultimately reaching the IVC (Panel I, Supplementary material online, Video S3). On venous phase imaging, the lesion bifurcated within the left iliac vein, ascended cranially along the IVC, and reformed into a contiguous mass within the right atrium (Panel J, Supplementary material online, Videos S4 and S5). Pelvic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed multiple tortuous, nodular lesions in the left uterine cornu (Panel G).

These findings established the diagnosis of intravenous leiomyomatosis (IVL). The patient underwent single-stage surgery combining bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, hysterectomy, and intravascular and intracardiac tumour excision under cardiopulmonary bypass. Histopathology confirmed benign smooth muscle proliferation with fascicular architecture and rich vascularity, lacking necrosis or increased mitotic activity (red star, Panel M). The specimen demonstrated characteristic bifurcated morphology with a proximal bulbous mass dividing into two slender limbs (Panel L). The one-month postoperative echocardiogram revealed no residual mass in the right atrium (Panel K); the patient recovered well without any complications.

IVL is a rare, histologically benign smooth muscle tumour with aggressive intravascular growth that can extend to cardiac chambers. The non-specific clinical presentation frequently delays diagnosis. CT and MRI are essential for diagnosis and surgical planning. Treatment typically demands multidisciplinary surgery, especially for intracardiac involvement. Early recognition and complete surgical excision remain paramount for optimal outcomes.

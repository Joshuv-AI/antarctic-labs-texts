IGF2BP1 positively regulates CircOGDH accumulation in hypoxia induced stress granules
Authors: Xuanlin Su, Jiankun Zang, Yaping Wang, Shiqing Zhang, Panwen Wu, Wei Chen, Lei Shi, Yousheng Wu, Die Deng, Kaiwei Cai, Hongcheng Mai, Anding Xu, Dan Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The ischemic penumbra is critical in acute ischemic stroke (AIS) treatment, yet the regulation of precise RNA modification pathways remains unclear. Using a mouse model of ischemic stroke and human postmortem brain samples, we demonstrate that N6-methyladenosine (m6A) levels are elevated in neurons within the ischemic penumbra. Notably, m6A modifications by m6A reader protein IGF2BP1 were enriched on penumbra related circular RNA derived from oxoglutarate dehydrogenase (CircOGDH). IGF2BP1 stabilizes CircOGDH by recruiting it to stress granules, maintaining high expression in the ischemic penumbra. Knockdown of IGF2BP1 reduced CircOGDH stability and decreased neuronal apoptosis under hypoxic conditions, suggesting a protective role. Igf2bp1 knockdown also preserved synaptic integrity in MCAO mice, with increased expression of synaptic markers and improved synaptic morphology. Importantly, Igf2bp1 knockdown significantly reduced penumbra volume compared to CircOGDH inhibition alone. These findings highlight IGF2BP1 as a promising therapeutic target for modulating penumbra-related RNA expression and promoting recovery in AIS.

Introduction

Ischemic stroke is a leading cause of death and long-term disability worldwide, with limited treatment options for reducing neuronal injury after the acute phase1, 2, 3, 4. A key therapeutic target is the ischemic penumbra—a region of functionally impaired but potentially salvageable tissue surrounding the infarct core. The penumbra is marked by a transient suppression of electrical and metabolic activity, and its preservation is central to the efficacy of reperfusion therapies. Without timely intervention, this region undergoes irreversible degeneration. The cellular and molecular events that govern penumbra progression include mitochondrial dysfunction, apoptotic signaling, impaired autophagy, and neuroinflammation5,6. Despite this complexity, few neuroprotective strategies effectively address the molecular mechanisms underlying penumbra vulnerability.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) are covalently closed, noncoding RNA loops that lack canonical 5′ caps and 3′ poly (A) tails7. CircRNAs have high stability and expression in the mammalian brain, suggesting their regulatory role in neural homeostasis and disease8, 9, 10. Recent studies have shown that circRNAs are implicated in diverse aspects of ischemic injury, including blood–brain barrier dysfunction and neuronal apoptosis11,12. Among these, CircOGDH—derived from the oxoglutarate dehydrogenase gene—has emerged as a penumbra-specific transcript with strong diagnostic and therapeutic relevance. CircOGDH was identified in neuronal extracellular vesicles and is associated with infarct volume and penumbra size in rodent stroke models13,14. However, the mechanisms that regulate its expression and function in the ischemic brain are poorly understood.

RNA modifications such as N6-methyladenosine (m6A) are critical regulators of RNA stability, transport, and translation15, 16, 17. During acute ischemic stroke, m6A methylation levels are elevated in peripheral immune cells and hypoxic neurons, where they help transiently repress translation to preserve cell survival18,19. While YTH-domain proteins mediate m6A-dependent transcript degradation20,21, the insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding proteins (IGF2BPs) recognize m6A sites to enhance RNA stability. IGF2BP1 helps stabilize stress-responsive RNAs and form stress granules (SGs)22, which sequester critical transcripts during cellular stress and ischemia23, 24, 25. IGF2BP1 has also been implicated in the nuclear export of circRNAs via a Ran-GTP and exportin-2-dependent mechanism, drawing parallels with protein export pathways26. These observations suggest that IGF2BP1 stabilizes m6A-modified circRNAs, such as CircOGDH, and regulates their localization and functional compartmentalization under hypoxic stress.

Here, we identify IGF2BP1 as a key regulator of CircOGDH stability and function in the ischemic penumbra. We show that IGF2BP1 binds to m6A-modified CircOGDH and promotes its recruitment to stress granules, maintaining its elevated expression under hypoxic conditions. The loss of IGF2BP1 reduces CircOGDH stability, attenuates hypoxia-induced neuronal apoptosis, and preserves synaptic integrity in a murine model of middle cerebral artery occlusion. This study reveals a novel mechanism by which IGF2BP1 exacerbates penumbral injury and uncovers a potential therapeutic target for RNA regulation in acute stroke.

Materials and methods

Cell culture and transfection

SH-SY5Y cells were cultured at 37 °C under 5% CO2 condition. For overexpression and knockdown CircOGDH, SH-SY5Y cells were transfected by the lentivirus carrying GFP-OE-CircOGDH and GFP-sh RNA-CircOGDH (GenePharma, Shanghai, China) at 200 MOI, respectively. Lipofectamine 3000 reagents were used to transfect IGF2BP1 and METTL3 siRNAs (Supporting Information Table S1). Cells were cultured for at least 48 h after transfection and utilized for subsequent experiments.

Neuronal extractions and cultures

Primary cortical neuron extraction and culture were performed as in our previous studies15. In brief, the cerebral cortex of C57BL/6J mice embryos (E18–E19) was isolated and transferred into ice-cold DMEM medium. After gently dissecting the cortex into small tissue pieces, the single-cell suspensions of cortical neurons were obtained by trypsinization with 0.125% trypsin and filtration through the 70 μm cell strainer. Then, the cells were resuspended in DMEM/F-12 with 10% FBS and cultured for 4 h. Subsequently, the medium was replaced with neurobasal medium containing B-27™ Supplement. Half of the medium was refreshed every three days, and neurons cultured for at least five days were used for experiments.

Human brain tissue samples

Brain tissue samples were collected from patients with hemorrhagic transformation after stroke onset whose brain tissue had to be removed for surgical intervention as determined by neurosurgeons (n = 3). The diagnosis was confirmed via preoperative MRI and postoperative pathological analysis. Samples were taken from specific regions in the vascular territory supplied by the middle cerebral artery (MCA). The collection of patient samples was conducted under the supervision of the Ethics Committee of First Affiliated Hospital of Jinan University (Ethics approval No. KY-2022-205), with written consent from patients or their families before samples collection.

Generation of OE-CircOGDH SH-SY5Y cell lines

Lentiviral overexpression of CircOGDH (OE-CircOGDH) with puromycin resistance was transfected into the SH-SY5Y cells following the instructions provided by the manufacturer. To eliminate puromycin-insensitive cells, the medium was changed daily and the puromycin (GenePharma, Shanghai, China) was decreased from 2.5 to 0.5 μg/mL when the cell count appeared stable. Puromycin-resistant cells expressing CircOGDH-GFP (confirmed via fluorescence microscopy) were sorted using the BD FACSAria III flow cytometer (Becton Dickinson, NJ, USA).

Middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) and monitoring cerebral blood flow (CBF)

C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old)27,28 were anesthetized by 4% isoflurane. A midline cervical incision was made to expose the left common, external, and internal carotid arteries. A silicon-coated nylon filament (RWD, Shenzhen, China) was inserted into the MCA for 3 h to establish the focal ischemia model13. The filament was not withdrawn during the whole process without reperfusion. Post-surgery, CBF alteration was assessed by Laser Speckle Contrast Imaging (PeriCam PSI System, Perimed AB, Stockholm, Sweden). All procedures were supervised by the Animal Experimentation Guidelines promulgated by Jinan University (Project ID: 0201028-03).

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in mice and 3D reconstruction in infarcted territories

MRI scans were conducted using a 9.4 T small-animal MRI scanner (Bruker PharmaScan, Germany). Anesthesia was maintained with 2.0% isoflurane via a nose cone, ensuring continuous monitoring of body temperature and respiratory rate throughout the procedure. T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR), and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) were performed 3 h post-MCAO, applying 2D fast-spin echo sequencing. Imaging encompassed the brain, excluding the olfactory bulb. Three-dimensional reconstructions were generated from T2WI and FLAIR data utilizing the 3D Slicer software platform (https://www.slicer.org/). Manual threshold-based tuning helped differentiate infarcted from non-infarcted areas. The volumetric quantification of infarct regions was subsequently analyzed by a double-blinded investigator using the software's analytical capabilities.

Ischemia–hypoxia models in neuronal cells

Five-day cultured primary cortical neurons and 80%-confluent SH-SY5Y cells were used to establish the ischemia–hypoxic model. After washing with DPBS (Biological Industries, Kibbutz Beit-Haemek, Israel) twice and performing a single washing with glucose-free DMEM (Procell, Wuhan, China), the cells were cultured in fresh glucose-free DMEM medium and then subjected to oxygen-glucose deprivation (OGD) in 95% N2 and 5% CO2 for 3 h. For the OGD/reperfusion (OGD/R) group, the culture medium was replaced by DMEM/F12 complete medium after modeling, and the cells were further cultured for both 3 and 24 h, under normal conditions. In contrast, the control group underwent a straightforward medium replacement with fresh DMEM/F12 complete medium and continued to be maintained under standard incubator conditions without exposure to ischemia–hypoxia environment.

Immunofluorescence for neuronal cells

Cells were subjected to fixation using 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) (Beyotime, Beijing, China), and then permeabilized with 0.3% Triton X-100 (Sigma, St. Louis, MO, USA). Following a blocking phase with 5% BSA, cells were treated with primary antibodies targeting m6A and IGF2BP1 at a dilution of 1:200 (Proteintech, Wuhan, China) and incubated overnight at 4 °C. Subsequently, secondary antibodies (1:200) and DAPI were applied at room temperature. The resulting immunofluorescence images were captured using the confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss LSM880, Vizna, Germany).

Immunofluorescence for mice brain tissue

Three hours post-MCAO procedure, mouse brains were fixed using 4% PFA and sectioned into 10 μm slices. These sections underwent permeabilization with 0.1% Triton X-100 and were then blocked using 1% BSA at room temperature. Overnight incubation at 4 °C followed, using primary antibodies against m6A, IGF2BP1, NeuN, and cleaved Caspase-3 (all at 1:200). After this, the sections were treated with secondary antibodies (1:200) and DAPI at room temperature. Immunofluorescence imaging was performed using the confocal microscope.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) analysis

The RNAs from cells and tissues were extracted using Trizol Reagent (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA). The reaction systems for reverse transcription and RT-qPCR detection were established using PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara, Beijing, China) and LightCycler® 480 SYBR Green I Master (Roche, Switzerland), respectively. And running in the CFX PCR system (Bio-Rad, CA, USA) under the conditions described previously in our studies13. A 1.5% agarose gel electrophoresis was employed to visualize the PCR products. The primers utilized in this analysis are enumerated in the Supporting Information Table.

RNA m6A quantification

The level of m6A modification was determined using the EpiQuik m6A RNA Methylation Quantification Kit (Epigentek, USA), adhering to the manufacturer's guidelines. In brief, 200 ng of RNA from each sample, along with m6A standards, were placed into strip wells that were pre-coated with a high-binding RNA solution. Afterward, capture and detection antibodies were added to the wells. The m6A levels were quantified colorimetrically by measuring the OD at 450 nm, and the results were analyzed using a standard curve to calculate the m6A concentrations.

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP), methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP) and MeRIP-seq

RIP assays utilized the Magna RIP RNA-binding protein immunoprecipitation kit (Millipore, MA, USA). SH-SY5Y cells and primary cortical neurons were cultured in 10 cm dishes and treated accordingly. Prior to harvesting, cells underwent UV cross-linking at 1500 J/cm2. Cells were then suspended in RIP lysis buffer and incubated for 30 min on ice. Cell lysates were combined with A/G magnetic beads coated with IGF2BP1, m6A, or negative control IgG antibodies and incubated overnight at 4 °C with gentle rotation. The beads were then washed, and bound proteins were digested using proteinase K buffer for 30 min at 55 °C. Target RNAs were extracted using Trizol reagents.

MeRIP-seq was performed according to the protocol provided by EpiObitek Tech (Guangzhou, China). Under hypoxic conditions, total RNA was extracted from SH-SY5Y cells using Trizol reagent. Approximately 10 μg of RNA was fragmented into 100–200 nt segments using 10 × RNA Fragmentation Buffer, and the reaction was stopped with 0.5 mol/L EDTA. The fragmented RNA underwent methylated RNA immunoprecipitation using the EpiTome m6A immunoprecipitation kit (Epiobitek, China). RNA was incubated with anti-m6A antibody for 3 h at 4 °C, followed by capture with magnetic beads (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA) for an additional 2 h. After purification with Trizol Reagent, the m6A-enriched RNAs were sequenced using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencer, employing a paired-end strategy.

RNA pulldown assays

Biotin-labeled oligonucleotide probes targeting the CircOGDH back-splice junction region (sense) and the negative control (NC) probes were synthesized by GenePharma. For the RNA pulldown experiment, cells were collected and lysed with RIP buffer. Then, cell lysates were incubated with biotinylated DNA oligo probes and streptavidin C1 magnetic beads (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA) overnight at 4 °C. After washing, the bead-binding proteins were eluted and identified by mass spectrometry, silver staining and Western blot. Silver staining was conducted by the PAGE gel silver staining kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer's guidelines. Mass spectrometry was conducted by PTM BIO (Hangzhou, China). Probe sequences are listed in the Supporting Information Table S1.

Immunofluorescence and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

Cy3-tagged CircOGDH probes and an RNA-FISH kit were sourced from GenePharma (Shanghai, China). SH-SY5Y cells and primary cortical neurons were seeded onto confocal dishes and fixed using 4% PFA. Then cells were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100. For the hybridization process, the Cy3-tagged CircOGDH probes (sequence 5′–3′ AUUG + CCCAGUCACAAGU + UUGUCU + GGGAUGA) were heated to 73 °C for 5 min prior to overnight hybridization at 37 °C. After that, cells were washed once with washing buffer at 43 °C for 15 min and twice with 2 × saline-sodium citrate (SSC). In the immunofluorescence procedure, cells were sequentially treated with primary IGF2BP1 antibodies and secondary fluorescent antibodies. Nuclei were stained with DAPI for 20 min, and images were taken using the confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss LSM880, Vizna, Germany).

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Wild type (WT) and m6A site mutated (MUT) luciferase reporter plasmids were synthesized (GenePharma, Shanghai, China) to verify the m6A modification sites of CircOGDH. SH-SY5Y cells were transfected with a plasmid carrying IGF2BP1 or control vector first, then transfected with the WT or MUT reporter plasmids, respectively. After 48-h incubation, we assessed firefly and Renilla luciferase activities by the dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Promega, Madison, WI, USA), in strict accordance with the manufacturer's guidelines.

Actinomycin D assay for RNA stability

SH-SY5Y cells were transfected with siRNA targeting IGF2BP1 when cultured to 50%–70%. Primary cortical neurons were transfected with shRNA-lentivirus targeting Igf2bp1 when cultured for five days. After 48 h, cells were subjected to normoxic or hypoxic treatment. The cells in each group were treated with actinomycin D (BioVision, San Francisco, CA, USA) for 0, 2, 4, and 6 h, respectively. The expression level of CircOGDH from each experimental group was subsequently quantified using RT-qPCR.

Purification of stress granule (SG) cores

The isolation of SGs cores was adapted from two papers29,30. SH-SY5Y cells were cultured to 80% confluency before undergoing hypoxic treatment. Following this exposure, the cells were washed with PBS and harvested. The resulting pellet was lysed in SG lysis buffer (50 mmol/L Tris–HCl, pH 7.4; 100 mmol/L KOAc; 50 μg/mL heparin; 2 mmol/L MgOAc; 0.5% NP-40; 0.5 mmol/L DTT; 1% protease inhibitor cocktail; 10 U/mL RNase inhibitor) on ice for 15 min. After centrifugation at 4 °C for 5 min at 1000×g, protein concentration was quantified using the BCA protein assay kit. About 500 μg of protein was immobilized on 20 μL of non-coated Protein A/G Dynabeads and incubated at 4 °C for 30 min. After beads removal, the supernatant was supplemented with 2.5 μg of anti-G3BP1 antibody along with 20 μL of Dynabeads, followed by incubation at 4 °C for 12 h. The Dynabeads were washed with wash buffer 1 (20 mmol/L Tris-HCl, pH 8.0) and subsequently rinsed three times for 2 min each with 0.2 mol/L NaCl. This wash cycle was followed by a single 2-min rinse in 1 mol/L Tris–HCl (pH 8.0) with 500 mmol/L NaCl, and another 2-min wash in 1 mol/L Tris-HCl (pH 8.0) containing 50 mmol/L MgOAc, 100 mmol/L KOAc, 50 μg/mL heparin, and 2 mol/L urea. Finally, the Dynabeads were resuspended in Trizol reagent for RNA extraction and subsequent analysis.

Molecular modeling

The molecular structure of IGF2BP1 was obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (Model ID: AF-P35637-F1)31,32. Predictions for the secondary and tertiary structures of CircOGDH were generated sequentially from its sequences. The secondary structures of RNA were obtained using RNAfold with the ‘‘minimum free energy (MFE) and partition function’’ selection in the folding algorithm and the ‘‘assume RNA molecule to be circular’’ option in the advanced parameters. The outcomes were further input into 3dRNA33 with the ‘‘circular RNA’’ option and ‘‘Default’’ procedure to generate the tertiary structures of CircOGDH. The prediction of the IGF2BP1–CircOGDH complex structures was conducted using the HADDOCK software34 and the Z-DOCK software35. The RNA–protein docking analysis was conducted using the default settings, with no residues blocked. The visualization of the complexes and local high-resolution images was conducted using Biorender and PyMOL.

Stereotactic microinjection of Igf2bp1 sh RNA lentivirus

Adult C57BL/6J mice underwent stereotaxic microinjection with Lentivirus GFP-shRNA-Control (shNC) or Lentivirus GFP-shRNA-Igf2bp1 (shIGF2BP1) (GenePharma, Shanghai, China) into dual locations within the left cerebral cortex, guided by the following stereotactic coordinates13: anterior-posterior, −0.2 mm; mediolateral, 2.0 (3.0) mm; and dorsoventral, 1.5 (2.0) mm. Each location was microinjected with 4 μL of virus. Following a six-day post-injection interval, MCAO treatment was performed. The animals were categorized into three distinct groups: SHAM + sh NC, MCAO + sh NC and MCAO + sh IGF2BP1.

Tunel and nissl staining

Cell apoptosis was detected by the one-step TUNEL apoptosis assay kit (Beyotime, Beijing, China). Brain tissue sections were washed with PBS and fixed in 4% PFA. And the sections were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 on ice. Then, the sections were incubated with TUNEL solution (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) at 37 °C in the dark for 1 h. Nuclei were stained with DAPI for 20 min, followed by imaging with a confocal microscope (Carl Zeiss LSM880).

Western blotting

Proteins were quantified utilizing a BCA assay kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). The proteins were then separated by a 12% SDS-PAGE gel (Beyotime, Beijing, China) and transferred onto nitrocellulose (NC) membranes (Amersham Protran, USA). After blocking with 5% milk solution, the membranes were incubated overnight with primary antibodies at 4 °C. These primary antibodies included m6A, IGF2BP1, cleaved Caspase-3, BCL2, BAX, PSD95, β-actin, and Synapsin-1, all at a 1:1000 dilution. Following this, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were then imaged and quantified using a Tanon 2500 Imaging System, with band intensities analyzed and normalized to β-actin using Image J software.

Transmission electron microscope (TEM)

After euthanizing the mice, ischemic penumbra brain tissues were rapidly excised and immersed in electron microscope fixative. Penumbra samples were preserved using a mixture of 2.5% glutaraldehyde and 1% osmium tetroxide in a 0.2 mol/L sodium phosphate buffer for 12 h. Following fixation, the samples underwent dehydration in a graded ethanol series (50%, 75%, and 95%). They were then sectioned to 70 nm thickness, stained with uranyl acetate and lead citrate, and imaged using a JEM-1400 electron microscope (JEM, Tokyo, Japan).

Golgi staining

Mouse brains were quickly excised and immersed in Golgi dye (Servicebio, Beijing, China) and fixed in 4% PFA for 24 h. The brains were sectioned into 2–3 mm thick tissue blocks and incubated in Golgi dye in the dark for 14 days, with dye replenished every 3 days. Following incubation, the tissue blocks were dehydrated using sucrose and softened with 75% glacial acetic acid. Sections of 100 μm were cut and air-dried overnight. After washing in distilled water, the slices were stained with concentrated ammonia water for 10 min, rewashed, sealed with glycerin gelatin, and photographed.

Statistical analysis

For RT-qPCR results, the expression level was quantified using the 2–ΔΔCT method. Gene expression was adjusted to an internal control and compared to a baseline control group. Statistical assessments were conducted using SPSS version 27.0, and data visualization was performed using GraphPad Prism version 8.0.1. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Student's t-tests were utilized for two-group comparisons. For analyses involving multiple groups, one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey or Dunnett post hoc tests was employed to adjust for multiple comparisons. Statistical significance was assigned at a P-value ≤0.05, with ‘‘N.S.’’ denoting a lack of statistical significance.

Results

Dynamic changes in m6A in the ischemic brain

Dynamic changes in m6A in the ischemic penumbra of MCAO mice were identified by immunofluorescence (IF) staining. MCAO treatment increased m6A modification levels compared to the SHAM groups (P < 0.0001) (Supporting Information Fig. S1A and S1B). m6A modification levels were significantly higher in the penumbra than in the ischemic core (Core) (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 1A and B), as well as cell model mimicking penumbra in hypoxic SH-SY5Y neuronal cells and primary cortical neurons (Fig. 1C and Fig. S1C). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that the methyltransferase genes METTL3, METTL14, METTL16, and WTAP, as well as the demethylase genes FTO and ALKBH5, were upregulated under hypoxic conditions (Fig. 1D). CircOGDH is a potential therapeutic target for improving penumbra neuronal survival13. Similarly, hypoxia upregulated CircOGDH expression in SH-SY5Y neuronal cells, with a peak in expression at 3 h of hypoxia (P < 0.0001; Fig. 1E), accompanied by mitochondrial dysfunction (Supporting Information Fig. S2A–S2C), indicating that CircOGDH expression is implicated in m6A modifications occurrence at 3 h of hypoxia in SH-SY5Y cells. The potential m6A-modification capacity and m6A sites of CircOGDH were predicted using the SRAMP server. The analysis indicates that CircOGDH has two predicted m6A sites with high or moderate confidence scores (Fig. 1F). To determine changes in m6A levels, RNA was extracted from SH-SY5Y cells, and methylated RNA immunoprecipitation (MeRIP) assays were performed using m6A antibodies (Fig. 1G). The results showed that m6A modifications were enriched in CircOGDH after hypoxia (Fig. 1H and I). The RNA pull-down assay showed an inverse association between CircOGDH level and m6A modification (Fig. 1J). These results suggest that the penumbra is associated with increased m6A methylation of CircOGDH.

CircOGDH could interact with the m6A reader IGF2BP1 in neuronal cells

To identify the potential m6A-epitranscriptome players involved in CircOGDH regulation, we conducted RNA pull-down assays and performed proteomic analysis to screen for m6A-related proteins in CircOGDH-overexpressing (OE) SH-SY5Y cells (Supporting Information Fig. S3A and S3B). A sense-specific silver-stained band of 55–70 kDa (red arrow) was observed in CircOGDH-OE SH-SY5Y cells (Fig. 2A). Mass spectrometry analysis showed that IGF2BP1 was the only highly expressed m6A protein that bound to CircOGDH (Fig. 2B and C). Predictions of circular RNA–protein interactions were conducted using the Circinteractome database. The results showed that IGF2BP1 interacted with CircOGDH (Fig. 2D). RBPsuite showed that IGF2BP1 had a high potential to bind to CircOGDH (Fig. 2E). These results were confirmed by RNA pull-down and RIP assays in hypoxic SH-SY5Y cells (Fig. S3C and Fig. 2F) and primary cortical neurons (Supporting Information Fig. S4D and S4E). FISH and IF staining showed that CircOGDH co-localized with IGF2BP1 in the cytoplasm (Fig. 2G). To investigate dynamic changes in IGF2BP1 expression in hypoxic SH-SY5Y cells, we measured IGF2BP1 expression under different hypoxia and reperfusion conditions. Expression peaked at 3 h of hypoxia (Fig. 2H). Pearson correlation analysis demonstrated a positive correlation between the relative expression of CircOGDH and IGF2BP1 under hypoxia and reperfusion at varying time intervals (R = 0.676, P = 0.016) (Fig. 2I). Similar to CircOGDH, IGF2BP1 was localized predominantly in the cytoplasm of SH-SY5Y cells and primary cortical neurons at 3 h of hypoxia (Fig. S3D and Fig. S4A–S4C). IF staining showed that IGF2BP1 was localized in the cytoplasm of neurons in the ischemic penumbra of MCAO mice (Fig. 2J and Fig. S4F). Western blots showed that IGF2BP1 expression was higher in the penumbra than in the ischemic core, consistent with CircOGDH expression (Fig. 2K and L). To investigate how IGF2BP1 recognizes and selectively binds to CircOGDH, we used the RNAfold and 3dRNA tools to predict the spatial structure of CircOGDH. The structure of IGF2BP1 was obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database. CircOGDH-IGF2BP1 complexes were simulated using ZDOCK. There was spatial proximity between an IGF2BP1 domain (405–533 aa) and the junction reads of CircOGDH at the 3′ and 5′ UTR (Fig. 3D). To identify the regions of IGF2BP1 involved in this interaction, we constructed four truncated IGF2BP1 polypeptides, each containing different functional domains36,37 (Fig. 3E) and measured the expression of these polypeptides by Western blotting (Fig. 3F). In vitro binding assays showed that CircOGDH bound to two RNA-binding K homology (KH) domains of FLAG-tagged IGF2BP1 KH3 and KH4 but not other domains (Fig. 3G). Consistent with the prediction results using SRAMP (Fig. 1F), there was spatial proximity between the KH3–KH4 domains and the junction reads (4–9 bp) of CircOGDH, including m6A modification sites (Fig. 3H). We produced two CircOGDH m6A site mutants with A-to-T mutations in methylation sites (GGGACA to GGGTCA, TGGACT to TGGTCT) (Fig. 3H). Then, we inserted the 295-nt wild-type and each mutant into a firefly luciferase (Fluc) reporter plasmid (Fig. 3I). Luciferase reporter assays showed that IGF2BP1 overexpression remarkably increased relative luciferase activity, whereas the mutations reduced this effect (Fig. 3J). Furthermore, we found that the mutation in site 1 could reduce the interaction between IGF2BP1 and CircOGDH (Fig. 3J). These findings indicate that m6A-modified CircOGDH at the junction sites can directly bind to the KH3–KH4 domains of IGF2BP1.

m6A plays an indispensable role in the metabolism and functions of circRNAs38. Conversely, the aberrant expression of circRNAs affects m6A modifications39. We investigated the mechanisms underlying the interaction between CircOGDH and IGF2BP1 in hypoxic neurons. We used lentivirus to overexpress or knock down CircOGDH in SH-SY5Y cells (Supporting Information Fig. S6A). We found that CircOGDH overexpression or knockdown (KD) under normoxic or hypoxic conditions did not significantly affect the protein levels of IGF2BP1 (Fig. S6B) and the accumulation of IGF2BP1 in the cytoplasm (Fig. S6C and S6D). Conversely, IGF2BP1 knockdown by siRNA decreased the mRNA and protein expression of IGF2BP1 (Fig. S6E and S6F) and the expression of CircOGDH in hypoxic SH-SY5Y cells (Fig. 3K). The ability of IGF2BP1 to regulate CircOGDH stability in SH-SY5Y cells and primary cortical neurons was assessed by RT-qPCR under treatment of actinomycin D. IGF2BP1 KD significantly reduced the half-life of CircOGDH under hypoxic conditions but not under normoxic conditions (Fig. 3L, Supporting Information Fig. S7A and S7C). Similarly, the siRNA-mediated METTL3 silencing decreased the expression of CircOGDH under hypoxic conditions (Fig. 3M) and decreased the half-life of CircOGDH under normoxia and hypoxia (Fig. 3N and Fig. S7B). These data reveal that m6A modification helps IGF2BP1 stabilize CircOGDH under hypoxic conditions.

IGF2BP1 maintains high levels of CircOGDH in hypoxic SH-SY5Y neuronal cells by recruiting CircOGDH to SGs

SGs transiently inhibit mRNA translation to protect mRNAs22 and proteins from damage40, 41, 42, such as in the early stage of AIS43. IGF2BP1 associates with the periphery and interior of dense granules formed in mature SGs44. However, it is unknown whether IGF2BP1 binds to CircOGDH and enters SGs to protect CircOGDH from degradation in the cytoplasm of hypoxic neurons. FISH and IF staining showed that CircOGDH selectively bound to IGF2BP1 and the core component of SG-resident G3BP1 in the cytoplasm of hypoxic SH-SY5Y cells (Fig. 4A). Moreover, CircOGDH co-localized with IGF2BP1 and G3BP1 in the brain tissue of patients with AIS (Fig. 4B). The interaction between IGF2BP1 and G3BP1 was assessed by Co-IP assay (Fig. 4C). Puromycin (Puro) is a translational inhibitor that causes premature chain termination by binding to growing peptide chains, promoting SG assembly45. Conversely, the translation elongation inhibitor cycloheximide (CHX) inhibits SG assembly46. RT-qPCR analysis showed that puromycin increased CircOGDH expression, and IGF2BP1 KD partially counteracted this effect under hypoxic stimulation (Fig. 4D). Considering that the formation of SGs is spatially specific, we analyzed the spatial correlation between CircOGDH abundance and SG during hypoxia. SG lysates were harvested from SH-SY5Y cells exposed to hypoxia. RT-qPCR results showed that IGF2BP1 KD significantly decreased CircOGDH expression in SG lysates (Fig. 4E). Single-cell fluorescence quantification of FISH and IF staining revealed that IGF2BP1 KD cells exhibited significantly lower IGF2BP1 signal intensity compared to NC KD controls, and silencing either IGF2BP1 or METTL3 markedly reduced cytoplasmic co-localization of CircOGDH, IGF2BP1, and G3BP1 in hypoxic conditions, observed in both IGF2BP1-depleted SH-SY5Y cells and primary cortical neurons (Fig. 4F and Supporting Information Fig. S8). These findings indicate that IGF2BP1, which is upregulated in neuronal cells under hypoxic conditions, accumulates in the cytoplasm and binds to CircOGDH by recognizing its m6A modification sites. Igf2bp1 knockdown ameliorates neuronal apoptosis

The results of our previous studies suggest that targeted knockdown of CircOGDH reduces neuronal apoptosis in the penumbra13. To further explore whether reducing the stability of CircOGDH by IGF2BP1 also meets the phenomenon, IGF2BP1 was knocked out using a CRISPR/Cas9 plasmid (Supporting Information Fig. S9A), and the effect of knockout (KO) on apoptosis under hypoxic conditions was evaluated by Western blotting. IGF2BP1 KO decreased the BAX/BCL2 ratio and Cleaved Caspase-3 expression in hypoxic SH-SY5Y cells (Fig. S9B). However, the effect of IGF2BP1 KO on cleaved Caspase-3 expression was counteracted by the simultaneous overexpression of CircOGDH in the hypoxia neuronal cells (Fig. S9C). These results demonstrate that IGF2BP1 KO decreases the stability of CircOGDH and ameliorates neuronal apoptosis in hypoxic neuronal cells.

The effects of Igf2bp1 knockdown on penumbra neuronal apoptosis were examined in vivo (Fig. 5A). Lentivirus-GFP-sh control (sh NC) or Lentivirus-GFP-sh Igf2bp1 (sh IGF2BP1) was injected into the left cortex of mice (Supporting Information Fig. S10A), followed by MCAO treatment. IGF2BP1 fluorescence intensity was significantly lower in the penumbra of sh IGF2BP1-treated MCAO mice than in sh NC-treated MCAO mice (Fig. S10B). Western blotting and RT-qPCR analyses showed that MCAO increased IGF2BP1 protein expression and CircOGDH levels, whereas Igf2bp1 silencing reversed these effects (Fig. S10C and S10D). These results are consistent with the in vitro data from SH-SY5Y cells, where IGF2BP1 stabilized CircOGDH levels under hypoxic conditions. Igf2bp1 knockdown significantly increased penumbra volumes measured by DWI-FLAIR mismatch imaging after MCAO treatment (Fig. 5B). We then investigated the effects of MCAO and IGF2BP1 knockdown on neuronal apoptosis in the penumbra. Knocking down IGF2BP1 significantly ameliorated neuronal death (Fig. 5C). Consistently, we found that knockdown of Igf2bp1 caused a decrease of BAX/BCL2 ratio in the ischemic neurons (Fig. 5D) and a decrease of cleaved Caspase-3 expression (Fig. 5D and E). To further explore the role of the protective effect of Igf2bp1 knockdown on neurons, which is mediated by the reduction of m6A-modified CircOGDH, we focused on the knockdown of CircOGDH (non-specific m6A-modified CircOGDH) and Igf2bp1 (specific m6A-modified CircOGDH) to verify their effects in penumbra neurons under ischemic conditions. The left cortex of mice was microinjected with sh CircCtrl or sh CircOGDH adenovirus six days prior to MCAO surgery. We confirmed a significant upregulation of CircOGDH expression of MCAO + sh NC mice and verified that the sh CircOGDH, sh CircOGDH combined with sh IGF2BP1 and sh IGF2BP1 effectively knocked down CircOGDH expression, especially sh CircOGDH combined with sh IGF2BP1 group (Fig. 5F). However, our data demonstrate that knockdown of CircOGDH with additional Igf2bp1 knockdown yields a more penumbra volume and reduction in apoptosis compared with simply knocking down CircOGDH, but the effect is similar with simply knocking down Igf2bp1 (Fig. 5G and Supporting Information Fig. S11A). These results demonstrate that IGF2BP1 modified m6A-CircOGDH plays major role in neuroal apoptosis and has a greater function than simple CircOGDH knockdown, which targets to non-modified CircOGDH (Fig. 5H). Our experiments also demonstrated that overall RNA methylation levels remained unchanged following CircOGDH knockdown, confirming the specificity of CircOGDH in penumbra (Fig. S11B). These data strongly support that CircOGDH is the key driver of IGF2BP1's role in neuronal apoptosis.

Igf2bp1 knockdown reduces synaptic damage in MCAO mice

IGF2BP1 is increased in clinical samples of ischemic stroke (IS) patients but the underlying functional significance is not understood47. IGF2BP1 is a pivotal therapeutic target in AIS, which promotes neuronal damage and CircOGDH expression in the penumbra and may be a central hub for multiple destructive downstream signaling pathways in neurons. Maintaining synaptic function during the acute phase of brain injury improves neuronal recovery48. We analyzed proteins related to synaptic plasticity, including Synapsin-1 and PSD95, which are markers of presynaptic and postsynaptic integrity, respectively49. IF staining showed that Igf2bp1 KD increased the expression of Synapsin-1 (Fig. 6A). Western blots showed that MCAO decreased the expression of PSD95 and Synapsin-1, while Igf2bp1 KD reversed this effect (Fig. 6B). The width of the synaptic cleft and the number of synapses in the infarct border zone of MCAO mice were evaluated by transmission electron microscopy (Fig. 6C). The brain tissues of sh IGF2BP1-treated MCAO mice had a higher number of synapses and a smaller synaptic cleft width in the infarct border than sh NC-treated MCAO mice (Fig. 6D). Neurite length and dendritic spine density were evaluated by Golgi staining (Fig. 6E, left panel). The sh IGF2BP1 treatment was associated with significantly higher neurite length and spine density than the sh NC treatment in the brain of MCAO mice (Fig. 6E, right panel). As previously observed, the overall m6A modification increases under hypoxic conditions. Therefore, we first conducted m6A-seq assays on SH-SY5Y cells to detect m6A modification and to identify other downstream targets of IGF2BP1 that may be affected by hypoxia, apart from CircOGDH. The results revealed that m6A peaks of mRNA were enriched in the 3′ UTR in normoxic and hypoxic cells (Supporting Information Fig. S12A). A total of 130 genes were hypomethylated, and 265 were hypermethylated under hypoxic conditions (Fig. S12B). We then performed gene ontology (GO) analysis of hypermethylated genes consistent with CircOGDH in hypoxia. The analysis revealed that hypermethylated genes were enriched in two biological processes (axon development and axonogenesis) and two cellular components (dendrites and axons) (Fig. S12C). We speculate that IGF2BP1 may also regulate a class of hypermethylated mRNAs that affect axon development during hypoxia. Discussion

CircRNAs are more stable than linear mRNAs because of their exonuclease-resistant closed-loop structure. Nonetheless, circRNAs are vulnerable to degradation through RNase H1/Ago2/RNase L/m6A/TMAO/exosomes pathways50. While highly expressed in disease, the regulation of circRNAs is unclear. The m6A modification regulates RNA metabolism and is implicated in ischemic stroke51, 52, 53, consistent with our findings that m6A levels are increased in hypoxic neurons. We found that hypoxia increased CircOGDH expression and its m6A methylation via upregulation of METTL3, a core component of the m6A methyltransferase complex54,55. METTL3 knockdown decreased CircOGDH levels below baseline, suggesting that m6A modifications are essential for CircOGDH stability under ischemic stress. These results support a model in which m6A modifications have a protective role, increasing CircOGDH persistence during hypoxia.

Among known m6A reader proteins, IGF2BP1 helps stabilize RNA and lengthen mRNA half-life56, 57, 58, with particular relevance in cancer biology36,59. Our findings demonstrate that IGF2BP1 plays a critical role in ischemic neurons by binding to m6A-modified CircOGDH, protecting it from degradation and attenuating hypoxia-induced apoptosis. These effects occurred in the absence of changes in transcription, reinforcing that CircOGDH regulation is primarily post-transcriptional.

Mechanistically, IGF2BP1 belongs to a class of RNA-binding proteins capable of undergoing liquid–liquid phase separation (LLPS), forming dynamic, membraneless ribonucleoprotein condensates60,61. These condensates are driven by multivalent interactions mediated by intrinsically disordered regions and are central to the spatial and temporal regulation of RNA metabolism under stress conditions. In hypoxic neurons, we observed large IGF2BP1–CircOGDH aggregates, distinct from conventional LLPS particles, which co-localized with stress granules (SGs)—structures formed in response to cellular stress and composed of multiple LLPS-prone proteins, including IGF2BP122,62.

Stress granules protect RNA from degradation under pathological conditions23,45,63. Since hypoxia is a potent inducer of SG formation25, we showed that IGF2BP1 and methylated CircOGDH localized to SGs in hypoxic cells. Pharmacological or genetic manipulation of SG dynamics directly altered CircOGDH levels, indicating that SG compartmentalization affects CircOGDH stability.

To determine the specificity of the IGF2BP1–CircOGDH interaction, we identified two conserved GGCA motifs (A8 and A251) near the back-splicing junction of CircOGDH. These motifs are similar to m6A-enriched sequences recognized by the KH3–4 domain of IGF2BP136,64. This structural arrangement supports a model in which the KH3–4 domain binds preferentially to methylated circRNA junctions, reinforcing CircOGDH specificity. Moreover, the junction sequence is a unique molecular signature distinguishing circRNAs from linear transcripts65,66, raising the possibility of competition among circRNAs for IGF2BP1 binding.

CircRNAs can competitively sequester IGF2BP1 from other targets. For instance, circFAM13B impairs IGF2BP–PKM2 interactions, suppressing glycolytic metabolism in bladder cancer67. These findings and our data suggest that methylated circRNAs may engage in selective competition for IGF2BP1, potentially influencing disease progression depending on the cellular context.

We demonstrated that IGF2BP1 knockdown reduced neuronal apoptosis and preserved synaptic integrity under ischemic conditions, underscoring the role of IGF2BP1 in stabilizing synaptic RNAs via m6A–circRNA interactions. These results highlight the therapeutic potential of early IGF2BP1 inhibition to mitigate ischemic penumbra progression and neuronal damage. From a translational perspective, we identified two viable therapeutic targets: (1) the KH3–4 RNA-binding domain of IGF2BP1 and (2) conserved GGCA motifs in pathogenic circRNAs such as CircOGDH. A dual-targeting approach can selectively disrupt neurotoxic RNA–protein complexes without compromising broader RNA regulatory networks, achieving precision neuroprotection in acute ischemic stroke.

Conclusions

IGF2BP1-mediated m6A methylation at CircOGDH's splice junction motifs synergizes with LLPS-driven compartmentalization to remodel the regulatory targets of penumbra-associated RNA dynamic networks. This study provides a theoretical framework for developing AIS neuroprotective agents using this dual-mechanism approach.

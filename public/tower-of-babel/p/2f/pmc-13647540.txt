TBL1XR1 mutations promoted tumor progression in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma through impairing nature killer cytotoxicity via the MYC-CD47/PD-L1 axis
Authors: Yue Zhu, Yi-Ge Shen, Wei Tang, Zhong Zheng, Si-Yuan Chen, Yao-Hui Huang, Di Fu, Shu Cheng, Peng-Peng Xu, Li Wang, Jiahao Chen, Meng-Meng Ji, Wei-Li Zhao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
TBL1XR1 is frequently mutated in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), yet its functional impact on tumor microenvironment remains poorly defined. In this study, we characterized TBL1XR1 mutations in a cohort of 1842 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients, identifying mutations in 9.4% of cases (n = 173). These mutations correlated with clinically aggressive features, including older age (> 60 years), high-risk International Prognostic Index, enrichment in non-GCB subtypes, as well as inferior progression-free and overall survival. Mechanistically, TBL1XR1 mutations enhanced H3K27ac levels at the MYC promoter, increased MYC expression and subsequently up-regulated its immune-regulatory targets CD47 and PD-L1. This axis impaired natural killer (NK) cytotoxicity, facilitating immune escape and tumor progression. In a murine A20 B-lymphoma model, tumors harboring Tbl1xr1 mutations exhibited up-regulated MYC, CD47, and PD-L1 expression, resulting in NK cell dysfunction and tumor growth acceleration via the MYC-CD47/PD-L1 axis. Dual blockade of CD47 and PD-L1 restored NK cell-mediated tumor immunity, triggering rapid regression of Tbl1xr1-mutated tumors. Taken together, our findings identified TBL1XR1 mutations as a microenvironment-related mechanism of DLBCL progression and provided clinical rationale of co-targeting CD47 and PD-L1 to treat this genetically defined subset.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12943-026-02724-4.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), the most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma, exhibits remarkable molecular and phenotypic heterogeneity. Although considerable improvement in clinical outcome has been achieved, 30%- 40% of patients still experience treatment failure with first-line immunochemotherapy rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone (R-CHOP) [1]. Based on cell-of-origin (COO) classification, three subtypes of DLBCL are identified, including the germinal-center B-cell-like (GCB)-DLBCL, activated B-cell-like (ABC)-DLBCL, and unclassified, with the latter two associated with inferior prognosis [2]. The more recent genomic analysis has uncovered genetic subtypes of DLBCL [3, 4]. The MCD subtype, defined by concurrent co-occurrence of MYD88L265P, CD79B mutations and transducin β-like 1 X-linked receptor 1 (TBL1XR1) mutations, exhibits MYC overexpression and immune evasion via programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) up-regulation and natural killer (NK) suppression [3–5].

TBL1XR1 is a core component of the nuclear corepressor complexes (NCOR)/silencing mediator of retinoic acid and thyroid hormone (SMRT) repressor complexes, and required for efficient histone deacetylation by HDAC3 and subsequent transcriptional regulation [6, 7]. TBL1XR1 deletions were found to interact with NCOR1 and HDAC3, inducing glucocorticoid resistance in acute lymphoblastic leukemia [8]. TBL1XR1 mutations were enriched in the MCD subtype of DLBCL and marginal zone lymphoma [9–11]. As a mechanism of action, TBL1XR1 mutations interact with SMRT/HDAC3 repressor complexes towards binding the memory B cell transcription factor BACH2, at the expense of the GC transcription factor BCL6, inducing ABC-DLBCL signatures and driving lymphomagenesis [9].

The biological heterogeneity of DLBCL stems not only from the genomic alternations, but also from dynamic interactions between tumor cells and tumor microenvironment. In the rituximab era, therapeutic efficacy relies largely on NK cell-mediated antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), making both the functional capacity and absolute numbers of NK cells major factors in determining treatment response in DLBCL [12, 13]. NK cell-mediated tumor surveillance is critically regulated by immune checkpoint interactions. PD-L1 on lymphoma cells engages PD-1 on NK cells, suppressing their cytotoxic activity in vivo [14]. Similarly, the target cell cluster of differentiation 47 (CD47), a “don’t eat me” signal, binds SIRPα on NK cells in a threshold-dependent manner, suppressing stimulatory signals [15]. Notably, DLBCL patients with TBL1XR1 mutations exhibit significantly higher CD47 expression, implicating this role in immune escape [16] MYC, a master transcriptional regulator in DLBCL, drives tumorigenesis while simultaneously subverting immune surveillance through CD47 and PD-L1 up-regulation [17]. However, the underlying mechanism of TBL1XR1 mutations on tumor microenvironment remains to be elucidated in DLBCL.

Here, we performed the genomic and transcriptomic analysis in a large cohort of DLBCL patients, and further investigated the role of TBL1XR1 mutations on tumor microenvironmental alterations both in vitro and in vivo.

Results

TBL1XR1 mutations contributed to tumor progression in DLBCL

TBL1XR1 mutations were assessed among a total of 1842 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients, including 1669 patients with wild-type TBL1XR1 (TBL1XR1wt) and 173 patients with TBL1XR1 mutations (TBL1XR1mut), by WGS/WES and targeted sequencing. In line with recent studies, TBL1XR1 mutations largely occurred within its WD40 domains (Fig. 1A). A total of 208 somatic mutations were identified within 173 patients, including 171 non-synonymous somatic single-nucleotide variants (SNVs, the most common mutation type), 17 non-frameshift deletions or substitutions, 10 frameshift deletions or insertions, 8 splicings, and 2 stopgains (Fig. 1B). The most frequent base substitution was A-to-G (Figure S1). As for the molecular classification of DLBCL, TBL1XR1 mutations were highly enriched in MCD (P < 0.001, Fig. 1C). TBL1XR1 mutations often co-occurred with representative mutations of the MCD subtype, such as MYD88, PIM1 and BTG2 mutations (Fig. 1D).

The clinical and pathological characteristics of these patients were summarized in Table 1. TBL1XR1mut patients were significantly associated with older patients (age > 60, P = 0.007), high-risk International Prognostic Index (IPI = 3–5, P = 0.049), and increased percentage of non-GCB subtype, as compared to TBL1XR1wt patients (P = 0.033). Among 1357 patients receiving R-CHOP-based regimen, the median follow-up time was 54.2 months (0.2-224.2 months). Inferior progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were presented in TBL1XR1mut patients (P = 0.036 and P = 0.023, Fig. 1E). Using univariate analysis, factors of IPI and TBL1XR1 mutation were included in the multivariate analysis. In the cox proportional-hazards model, TBL1XR1 mutation was an independent prognostic factor for inferior PFS when factors of IPI were controlled (P = 0.045, Table 2).

TBL1XR1 mutations induced MYC signaling activation in DLBCL

The possible structure-function of the mutants was addressed using the crystal structure of the protein encoded by TBL1XR1 (PDB 4LG9). Eight WD40 domains (WD), highly conserved domains, form a β-propeller conformation, which serves as a scaffold for protein-protein interaction (PPI) (Fig. 2A). Mutations that occurred in exposed residues could be predicted to affect PPI, including Y446S and Y395, while those in buried residues (H348) might destroy structure stabilization and further be involved in PPI (Fig. 2B and C). To further determine the biological function of TBL1XR1 mutations in DLBCL, RNA sequencing was performed on 741 DLBCL patients, and differentially expressed genes were analyzed by gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). Significant changes in multiple signaling pathways were identified (Fig. 2D and Supplementary Table 1), particularly MYC signaling activities (Fig. 2E). Consistently, TBL1XR1mut patients presented with increased MYC expression as revealed by RNA sequencing and immunohistochemistry (P = 0.030, Figure S2A; P = 0.008, Fig. 2F), indicating the link of TBL1XR1 mutations with MYC signaling activation.

To verify the biological function changes induced by the TBL1XR1 mutations, ABC DLBCL cell line U2932 and GCB DLBCL cell line OCI-LY19 were engineered to over-express full-length transcripts of wild-type TBL1XR1 or TBL1XR1H348Q, TBL1XR1Y395H and TBL1XR1Y446S mutants. Compared with Vector or TBL1XR1wt cells, mRNA and protein expression of MYC were significantly elevated in TBL1XR1H348Q, TBL1XR1Y395H and TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 and OCI-LY19 cells, as revealed by quantitative real-time PCR (RT-PCR, Fig. 2G and Figure S2B) and western blot (Fig. 2H). As MYC was reported to promote tumor cell proliferation in DLBCL, TBL1XR1H348Q, TBL1XR1Y395H, and TBL1XR1Y446S induced a significant increase in cell viability by CCK8 assay, as compared to Vector or TBL1XR1wt cells (Fig. 2I and Figure S2C).

TBL1XR1 mutations impair the interaction of SMRT/HDAC3 repressor complexes with BCL6 (a transcriptional repressor of MYC by binding to its promoter), referring reduced recruitment of SMRT/HDAC3/TBL1XR1 repressor complexes to MYC promoter caused by TBL1XR1 mutations [9, 18]. As shown by the luciferase assay, TBL1XR1Y446S correlated with increased transcriptional activity of MYC promoter region in HEK-293T cells, suggesting that TBL1XR1Y446S up-regulated the transcription of MYC (Fig. 2J). Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation (CUT&Tag) sequencing further revealed decreased occupancies of TBL1XR1Y446S (represented by anti-Flag antibody, P = 0.018) and HDAC3 (P = 0.019), but increased H3K27 acetylation (H3K27ac, P = 0.042) in the proximal promoter sites of MYC in TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 cells than those in TBL1XR1wt U2932 cells (Fig. 2K). Therefore, TBL1XR1 mutations contributed to MYC signaling activation by H3K27ac in the proximal promoter regions of MYC.

TBL1XR1 mutations up-regulated CD47 and PD-L1 through MYC

As a crucial transcription factor to cell growth and proliferation in DLBCL, MYC can directly bind the promoters of the CD47 and PD-L1 genes, which were important immune checkpoints [19]. In the DLBCL cohort, expression of MYC showed modest but significant correlation with the expression of CD47 and CD274 (encodes PD-L1), as revealed by RNA sequencing (r = 0.152, P < 0.001; r = 0.273, P < 0.001, respectively; Figure S3A), indicating MYC as one of the factors regulating CD47 and PD-L1 expression in DLBCL. Meanwhile, CD47 and CD274 expression were up-regulated in patients with higher expression of MYC (P = 0.005 and P < 0.001, respectively; Figure S3B). Compared with TBL1XR1wt patients, CD47 and CD274 expression were increased in TBL1XR1mut patients (P = 0.024 and P = 0.032, respectively; Figure S3C). A higher percentage of membrane CD47-positive and PD-L1-positive cells was observed in tumor samples of TBL1XR1mut patients than those of TBL1XR1wt patients (P = 0.018 and P = 0.016, respectively; Fig. 3A). In consistence with clinical data, TBL1XR1H348Q, TBL1XR1Y395H and TBL1XR1Y446S resulted in significantly increased CD47 and PD-L1 expression at the transcriptional level by quantitative RT-PCR (Fig. 3B and Figure S3D) and at the protein level by western blot (Fig. 3C). As shown by CUT&Tag sequencing, MYC positively regulated the transcriptional activity of CD47 and CD274 promoter regions in U2932 cells (P = 0.018, P = 0.018, respectively; Fig. 3D), suggesting that MYC targeted CD47 and CD274 by binding to their promoter sites in a MYC-dependent manner. As a consequence of MYC inhibition by the MYC inhibitor, CD47 and PD-L1 expression were reduced in TBL1XR1mut and TBL1XR1wt U2932 cells and OCI-LY19 cells, with a trend toward greater reduction in mutant cells, consistent with their higher baseline expression driven by TBL1XR1 mutations (Fig. 3E; Figure S4A-4B).

TBL1XR1 mutations altered NK cytotoxicity via the MYC-CD47/PD-L1 axis

To explore the potential role of TBL1XR1 mutations on tumor microenvironment, the signature of immune cells was evaluated using RNA sequencing. Within several innate and adaptive immune cell subsets, the signature of NK cells was significantly associated with TBL1XR1 mutations (P < 0.001, Fig. 4A), as revealed by Immune Cell Abundance Identifier (ImmuCellAI) analysis [17]. Furthermore, the activity of NK cells was subsequently assessed using CIBERSORT, which revealed that the scores of activated NK cells were significantly decreased in TBL1XR1mut compared to TBL1XR1wt patients (Fig. 4B). To better characterize the alterations of NK cells, the co-culture assay of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) derived NK cells and DLBCL cell lines was performed. When co-cultured with TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 cells or OCI-LY19 cells, NK cells showed down-regulated cytotoxicity, resulting in less killing of tumor cells (Fig. 4C). Activation and cytotoxicity markers of NK cells, CD107a, TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL), and Granzyme B (GZMB) were tested [20, 21]. TBL1XR1Y446S inhibited the activation and cytotoxicity of NK cells (Fig. 4D and E). Specifically, the combination of anti-CD47 and anti-PD-L1 antibodies significantly reduced tumor cell viability in TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 and OCI-LY19 cells after 48 h of co-culture with healthy donor-derived NK cells, whereas only modest effects were observed in TBL1XR1wt cells (Figure S5A-S5B). Consistently, the combined treatment led to a more enhanced expression of CD107a, TRAIL, and GZMB in CD56dimCD16+ NK cells co-cultured with TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 and OCI-LY19 cells, compared to TBL1XR1wt U2932 or OCI-LY19 cells (Fig. 4F; Figure S5C-S5E).

TBL1XR1 mutations impaired NK cell cytotoxicity in vivo via the MYC-CD47/PD-L1 axis in an immune-dependent manner

The functional effects of TBL1XR1 mutations were further evaluated in vivo using murine syngeneic models. Subcutaneous injection of Tbl1xr1wt or Tbl1xr1Y446S A20 cells into immunocompetent BALB/c mice or highly immunodeficient BRGSF mice induced tumor formation in all mice. Tumors harboring Tbl1xr1Y446S showed significantly upregulated expression of MYC, CD47, and PD-L1 (Fig. 5A and B). Notably, tumor sizes in Tbl1xr1Y446S mice were significantly increased at day 13 post-implantation (Fig. 5C; S6A). Accordingly, Tbl1xr1Y446S contributed to impaired activation and cytotoxicity of NK cells in BALB/c mice (Fig. 5D). We next performed antibodies treatment against CD47 or PD-L1 in both immunocompetent and BRGSF immunocompromised models to better determine whether the therapeutic effects depend on the immune system and the MYC-CD47/PD-L1 axis. In immunocompetent BALB/c mice, blockade of CD47 or PD-L1 significantly inhibited tumor growth in mice bearing Tbl1xr1Y446S tumors, which were more pronounced as compared to those bearing Tbl1xr1wt tumors (Fig. 5E; Figure S6B). In contrast, no such inhibitory effects were observed in BRGSF mice bearing either Tbl1xr1wt (Figure S6C) or Tbl1xr1Y446S (Figure S6D) tumors, confirming that the therapeutic effects require an intact immune system.

Furthermore, CD47 or PD-L1 blockade restored NK cell activation and cytotoxicity in immunocompetent mice, as evidenced by increased expression of CD107a, TRAIL, and GZMB (Fig. 5F and Figure S6E). Discussion

TBL1XR1 mutations represent key tumorigenic events in DLBCL [3, 4]. In this large cohort of DLBCL patients, TBL1XR1 mutations often co-occurred with representative mutations of MCD subtypes, and were significantly related to higher-risk IPI and independently indicated poor prognosis, in consistent with findings from immune-privileged DLBCL [22, 23].

Mechanistically, we demonstrated that TBL1XR1 mutations impair SMRT/HDAC3 recruitment to the MYC promoter. In TBL1XR1Y446S DLBCL cells, decreased TBL1XR1 and HDAC3 binding was observed, accompanied by elevated H3K27ac levels at the MYC proximal promoter regions, consistent with published findings that H3K27ac reduction in the MYC promoter suppresses its expression [24, 25]. This also aligns with TBL1XR1 deficiency down-regulating HDAC and up-regulating MYC targets in acute myeloid leukemia [6]. MYC is overexpressed in many human cancers, and directly binds the promoters of CD47 and PD-L1 genes, promoting immune escape and tumor progression [17]. Our recent study has shown that TBL1XR1 mutations were significantly increased in DLBCL patients with high CD47 expression [16]. Here we further confirmed that CD47 and PD-L1 expression were increased when bearing TBL1XR1 mutations both in vitro and in vivo. MYC overexpression was caused by TBL1XR1 mutations, and positively regulated the transcriptional activity of CD47 and CD274 promoter regions. Similar mechanisms occur in colorectal cancer that LINC00460 facilitates MYC expression, increasing CD47 and PD-L1 expression, thus promoting tumor metastasis [26]. JQ1, an indirect inhibitor of MYC, enhances antitumor immune effects by down-regulating CD47 and PD-L1 expression in tumor cells, indicating a novel strategy for improving the antitumor immune response [27]. CD47 and PD-L1 are critical regulators of immune surveillance in cutaneous T cell lymphoma and dual targeting of CD47 and PD-L1 provide insight into tumor immunotherapy [28]. Our data identified the MYC-CD47/PD-L1 axis as an important microenvironment-related mechanism of tumor progression in DLBCL bearing TBL1XR1 mutations.

Up-regulated SIRPα and PD-1 on NK cells engage with CD47 and PD-L1 on target cells, respectively, leading to suppression of NK-mediated antitumor activity [14, 15]. MYC, enriched in the EEF2-positive and FOLH1-positive prostate cancer, reduces the proportion of NK cells in the tumor microenvironment by up-regulating CD47 and PD-L1, contributing to tumor progression [29]. Here we found NK cytotoxicity was reduced in TBL1XR1mut DLBCL. Activation and cytotoxicity markers, such as TRIAL, CD107a and GZMB, were down-regulated in NK cells when co-cultured with TBL1XR1Y446S DLBCL cells, which could be subsequently counteracted by CD47 and PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies. To our knowledge, this is the first report on the impact of TBL1XR1 mutations on NK cells, suggesting a novel mechanism of epigenetic alterations on tumor microenvironment in DLBCL.

In summary, TBL1XR1 mutations were drivers of immune evasion and DLBCL progression via the MYC-CD47/PD-L1 axis, providing clinical rationale of co-targeting CD47 and PD-L1 checkpoints as a promising therapeutic strategy for TBL1XR1-mutated DLBCL.

Methods

Patients

This study enrolled 1842 patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL between September 2002 to June 2023, with 1357 of whom receiving R-CHOP-based immunochemotherapy. According to World Health Organization (WHO) classification, histological diagnosis was established by two experienced pathologists, LD and H-M Y. Treatment response was evaluated based on the International Workshop Criteria. This study was approved by the Shanghai Rui Jin Hospital Review Board with informed consent obtained from all patients in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

DNA sequencing

DNA sequencing of 340 patients by whole-genome sequencing (WGS, n = 117) and whole-exome sequencing (WES, n = 223) were performed on available frozen or quality controlled FFPE tumor samples, as reported by our previous studies, along with detailed procedures for DNA sequencing [30–33]. Targeted sequencing of 55 lymphoma associated genes were performed among 1502 patients with available FFPE tumor samples based on the criteria as previously described [30–32]. Single nucleotide variations (SNVs) and indels were called by Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK, v3.4) Haplotype Caller, and GATK Unified Genotyper, which were further mapped to the genome location using the UCSC Genome Browser (http://genome.ucsc.edu) for annotation. And the filtration of SNVs and indels was carried out by homemade pipeline with the software mentioned above. Variant allele frequency of mutations included should be over 5%. Mutations were filtered as previously described [30–33].

RNA sequencing, immuCellAI, Cibersort, and GSEA analysis

RNA was extracted by Trizol and a RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen) from available qualified frozen tumor samples of 741 patients and RNA sequencing was performed. RNA was purified by Ribo Zero rRNA Removal Kits (Illumina) and assessed on NanoDrop and integrity by an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer. RNA libraries were generated with a TruSeq RNA Library Preparation Kit (Illumina) based on instructions of the manufacturer, with the concentration and the quality of RNA libraries controlled by Qubit and BioAnalyzer 2100 system. Paired-end sequencing was performed on Illumina HiSeq sequencer following Illumina-provided protocols. The read pairs were aligned to Refseq hg19 through Burrows-Wheeler Aligner version 0.7.13‐r1126 and the transcript counts table files were generated by the HTSeq. Potential false positive results were excluded by visual inspection. R package “sva” was applied to remove batch effect by r 4.0.3. Based on raw counts data of RNA sequencing, the infiltration of immune cells including 24 immune cell types were estimated by ImmuCellAI algorithm, a website tool (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/web/ImmuCellAI/) and R package Cibersort [19, 34]. Pathway enrichment analysis was performed using GSEA v4.0.1 software as recommended by the GSEA team (http://www.broadinstitute.org/gsea). Pathways were considered of statistical significance with a P-value < 0.05 and a false discovery rate < 0.25.

Molecular classification

DLBCL genotypes were identified based on the LymphPlex algorithm using 35 genes and rearrangements revealed by DNA sequencing, and 3 genes (BCL2, BCL6, and MYC) by FISH [35]. According to the LymphPlex algorithm, patients were assigned to 1 of the following genetic subtypes: EZB-like, ST2-like, BN2-like, TP53Mut, N1-like, MCD-like, or not otherwise specified (NOS).

Cell lines and reagents

Human B-lymphoma cell line U2932 and murine B-lymphoma cell line A20 were obtained from American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). Human B-lymphoma cell line OCI-LY19 were obtained from Deutsche Sammlung von Mikroorganismen und Zellkulturen (DSMZ). U2932 and A20 were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium, and OCI-LY19 were grown in MEMα, supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (15140122, Gibco, Carlsbad, CA, USA) in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2 at 37 °C.

Cell transfection

For vector, TBL1XR1wt, TBL1XR1H348Q, TBL1XR1Y395H, or TBL1XR1Y446S transfection, purified plasmids pGV358/GFP/Puro (vector), pGV492/gcGFP/Puro-TBL1XR1 (NM_024665, wild-type, wt), pGV358/GFP/Puro-TBL1XR1 (NM_024665, H348Q), pGV365/GFP/Puro-TBL1XR1 (NM_024665, Y395H), and TBL1XR1 (NM_024665, Y446S), were transfected into package HEK-293T cells using lipofectamine 2000 (11668019, Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. For Tbl1xr1wt and Tbl1xr1Y446S transfection, purified plasmids pLXT-SFFV-MCS-PGK-copGFP-Puro-Tbl1xr1 (NM_000626, wild-type, wt) and Tbl1xr1 (NM_000626, Y446S), were transfected into package HEK-293T cells using lipofectamine 2000 (11668019, Invitrogen) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. The supernatant fraction of HEK-293T cell cultures were then condensed to a viral concentration of 2 × 108 transducing units/ml. The lentiviral particles were incubated with U2932, OCI-LY19, or A20 cells for 72 h with the addition of polybrene (8 µg/ml). The stably transduced clones were selected by puromycin treatment for two weeks followed by flow cytometry sorting according to expression of green fluorescence protein (GFP). Following lentiviral transduction and puromycin selection, flow cytometry sorting was performed to sort and isolate cells with consistent GFP fluorescence intensity. These methods ensure stable and homogeneous expression, generating highly homogeneous stable pools for modified U2932, OCI-LY19 and A20 cells.

Immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry was performed on paraffin sections of tumor samples from patients using antibodies against CD10, BCL6, MUM1, BCL2, MYC, CD47 and PD-L1 by indirect immunoperoxidase method. GCB and non-GCB phenotypes were determined with 30% cutoff values of CD10, BCL6, and MUM1, according to Han’s algorithm [36]. BCL2/MYC double expressors were defined by BCL-2 and MYC with cutoff values of 50% and 40%, respectively [37]. For tumor samples from mice, paraffin sections from each mouse were applied to perform using antibodies against Myc (ab32072, Abcam), Cd47 (36096, CST) and Pd-l1 (64988, CST) by indirect immunoperoxidase method. The expression levels were assessed by Qupath.

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-PCR)

The total RNA of B-lymphoma cells was extracted using Trizol reagent and reverse transcribed into first-strand cDNA using a HiScript III RT SuperMix with gDNA wiper for quantitative RT-PCR (R323-01, Vazyme). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed using ChamQ Universal SYBR qPCR Master Mix (Q711-02, Vazyme) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Relative quantification was evaluated using the 2−ΔΔCT methods. The primers are listed in Supplementary Table 2. For MYC inhibitor treatment, TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 cells and OCI-LY19 cells were seeded at a concentration of 5⋅105/mL with 60 µM MYC inhibitor 10,058-F4 (S7153, Selleck) and cultured for 48 h followed by RT-PCR assay.

Western blot

Cells were lysed in 200 µl RIPA lysis buffer (P0013B, Beyotime). Protein lysates (20 µg) were electrophoresed on 10% SDS polyacrylamide gels and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes, which were next blocked with 5% non-fat dried milk and incubated overnight at 4 °C with the appropriate primary antibodies, followed by a horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated secondary antibody. The immunocomplexes were visualized using Immobilon Western Enhanced Chemiluminescence (ECL) Substrate (WBKLS0500, Millipore). Each primary antibody was validated for the relevant species and applications, as shown on the manufacturers’ websites, including MYC (ab32072, Abcam), CD47 (63000, CST), and PD-L1 (ab213524, Abcam). GAPDH (GB11002-100, Servicebio). HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies against goat anti-mouse-IgG and goat anti-rabbit-IgG were from Proteintech.

For MYC inhibitor treatment, TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 cells and OCI-LY19 cells were seeded at a concentration of 5⋅105/mL with 60 µM MYC inhibitor 10,058-F4 (S7153, Selleck) and cultured for 48 h followed by western blot assay.

Cell proliferation assay

For cell proliferation assay, human B-lymphoma cells were seeded at the density of 4⋅105 cells/ml with 100 µL per well in 96-well plates for 48 h. Then the cell proliferation was assessed by CCK8 (1:10, HY-K0301, Cell Counting Kit-8, MCE), and absorbance was measured at 450 nm by spectrophotometry.

Luciferase report assay

Total cDNA from HEK-293T cells was used to amplify the promoter (-2000 to + 100 bp) of MYC, forward primer: 5′- GCGTGCTAGCCCGGGCTCGAGTGCGGCAAAGGCCTGGAGGC- 3′; reverse primer: 5′- AGTACCGGAATGCCAAGCTTCCCAGGACGCCCGCAGCGCAG-3′. After digestion with BamHI and EcoRI, PCR products were ligated into the pGL3basic vector and confirmed by DNA sequencing. TBL1XR1wt and TBL1XR1Y446S HEK-293T cells were plated in 24-well plates and co-transfected with pGL3basic plasmid (100 ng/mL) with or without MYC promoter (-2000 to + 100 bp) luciferase reporter construct and luciferase reporter (10 ng/mL) using transfection reagent HilyMax (Dojindo). Cells were harvested at 48 h after transfection, using the Cell Lysis Buffer (100 µl per well) provided as part of the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System Kit (DL101-01, Vazyme). Firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were examined by the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System and detected using Centro XS3 LB960 Luminometer (Berthold, Bad Wildbad).

Cleavage under targets and tagmentation (CUT&Tag) sequencing

The libraries of CUT&Tag sequencing was constructed using U-2932, TBL1XR1wt U2932 and TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 cells by Hyperactive Universal CUT&Tag Assay Kit for Illumina Pro (TD904, Vazyme, China)and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq platform at a depth of 40–60 1066 million per sample (150 bp paired-end reads) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Rabbit anti-Flag antibody (20543-1-AP, Proteintech), anti-human HDAC3 antibody, anti-human H3K27ac antibody (ab4729, Abcam), and anti-human MYC antibody (ab32072, Abcam) were used for immunoprecipitation, and normal IgG (3900, Cell Signaling Technologies) was referred as a negative control. Analysis of raw sequencing data of CUT&Tag were performed as previously described, with paired-end reads aligned using Bowtie2 (v2.5.1) and normalized bigwig files generated by deepTools (v3.5.1). IGV was used to visualize normalized bigwig files [38]. Peak calling was performed using MACS3 and statistical comparison by bdgdiff [39].

NK Cytotoxicity and in vitro activation assay

For NK cytotoxicity, TBL1XR1wt and TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 cells and OCI-LY19 cells were used as target cells. Primary human NK cells, isolated from PBMCs of healthy donors using an NK cell isolation kit (130-092-657, Miltenyi Biotec, San Diego, CA), were used as effector cells. The target cells were co-cultured with isolated human primary NK cells at the effector (E): target (T) ratio of 1:1 at 37 °C for 6 h. Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) Release Assay was used to detect NK cytotoxicity. Target cells incubated in complete media or 1% SDS media were used for spontaneous or maximal LDH release control, respectively. The cell lysis percentages were calculated using the standard formula: 100× (experimental release-spontaneous release) / (maximal release-spontaneous release). The assays were performed in at least three technical replicates.

As for NK cell activation assay, the target cells were co-cultured with isolated human primary NK cells at the effector (E): target (T) ratio of 1:1 at 37 °C for 6 h with BV605 anti-human CD107a antibody (328634, BioLegend) in the presence of the protein transport inhibitor GolgiStop (554724, BD Biosciences). After activation, cells were stained for fluorescent viability dye (FVS789, APC-Cy7, 565388, BD Biosciences) and surface markers, including BV786 anti-human CD3 antibody (563800, BD Biosciences), BUV615 anti-human CD56 antibody (751349, BD Biosciences), BUV563 anti-human CD16 antibody (748851, BD Biosciences), APC anti-human TRAIL antibody (308210, BioLegend). Then, cells were fixed and permeabilized with Fixation/Permeabilization Kit (554714, BD Biosciences) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and further stained with PE-Cy7 anti-human/mouse GZMB antibody (372213, BioLegend). Flow cytometry data was collected on the FACSymphony™ A3 Cell Analyzer (BD Biosciences) and were analyzed using FlowJo software.

As for blockade of CD47 or PD-L1, TBL1XR1Y446S U2932 cells and OCI-LY19 cells were treated with 10 µg/mL anti-human CD47 antibody (A2036, Selleck, USA), anti-human PD-L1 antibody (A2004, Selleck, USA) or the combination for 30 min, with the combination of human IgG4 (A2052, Selleck, USA) and IgG1 (A2051, Selleck, USA) as isotype control. Then the target cells were co-cultured with effector cells for NK cell activation assay.

Syngeneic transplant model

Tbl1xr1wt and Tbl1xr1Y446S A20 cells (a total of 1⋅106) were subcutaneously injected into the right flank of 6-week BALB/c mice (Shanghai Laboratory Animal Center, Shanghai, China). Treatments initiated when tumors were approximately 0.5 cm×0.5 cm in surface (Day 0). Anti-mouse Cd47 antibody (A2140, Selleck, USA) and anti-mouse Pd-l1 antibody (A2115, Selleck, USA) were injected 200 µg and 100 µg per mouse, respectively, three times per week for 2 weeks, with Rat IgG2b (HY-P990682, MCE, USA) and mouse IgG1 (HY-P99977, MCE, USA) as isotype type. Tumor volumes of the mice were calculated by the formula: volume = 0.5×a (length)×b (width). For NK cell assay, tumor samples were dissociated into single cells and incubated with BV421 anti-mouse CD107a antibody (121618, BioLegend) at 37 °C for 6 h in the presence of the protein transport inhibitor GolgiStop (554724, BD Biosciences). After activation, cells were stained for fluorescent viability dye (FVS789, APC-Cy7, 565388, BD Biosciences) and surface markers, including BV510 anti-mouse CD45 BV605 (103138, BioLegend), BV605 anti-mouse CD3e antibody (100351, BioLegend), APC anti-mouse CD49b (103516, BioLegend), PE anti-mouse TRAIL antibody (109305, BioLegend). Then, cells were fixed and permeabilized with Fixation/Permeabilization Kit (554714, BD Biosciences) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and were further stained with PE-Cy7 anti-human/mouse GZMB antibody (372213, BioLegend). Flow cytometry data was collected on the FACSymphony™ A3 Cell Analyzer (BD Biosciences) and were analyzed using FlowJo software. Animals were used according to the protocols approved by the Shanghai Rui Jin Hospital Animal Care and Use Committee.

Statistical analysis

Pearson’s x2 test or Fisher’s exact test was used to analyze baseline characteristics of patients. PFS was calculated from the date of diagnosis to the date when disease progression or relapse was recognized or the date of last follow-up. OS was measured from the date of diagnosis to the date of death or the date of last follow-up. Survival functions were analyzed using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared by the log-rank test. Univariate hazard was analyzed using the Cox regression method and the significant variables were then kept in multivariate set. Normalized gene expression in two groups was analyzed using Mann-Whitney U test. All statistical analysis was performed by Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) 26.0 software. All tests were two-sided, with statistical significance defined as P < 0.05.

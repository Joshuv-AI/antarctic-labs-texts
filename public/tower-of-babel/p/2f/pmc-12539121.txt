Expression of multidrug efflux pump gene acrAB in Escherichia coli: a systematic review and meta analysis
Authors: Saleh Salem Bahaj, Mohammed Saleh Al-Dhubaibi, Aref Noman, Sarosh Sher Ali, Haaris Mehmood, Waleed Yahya Alkassar, Ahmed Mohammed Al-Dhubaibi, Ghada Farouk Mohammed, Ahmed Ibrahim Abd Elneam
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultidrug-resistant (MDR) Escherichia coli (E.coli) is a growing public health concern, largely driven by the overexpression of efflux pumps such as AcrAB-tolC. These efflux systems contribute to resistance against multiple antibiotic classes, including fluoroquinolones, β-lactams, and aminoglycosides. Despite the well-documented role of efflux pumps in resistance, inconsistencies in reported expression levels and regulatory mechanisms complicate the development of targeted therapies. This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to consolidate available evidence on acrAB-tolC expression patterns and evaluate the impact of efflux pump inhibitors (EPIs) on antibiotic susceptibility.MethodsA systematic search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, and EBSCO to identify relevant studies examining acrAB expression in E.coli under antibiotic exposure conditions. Inclusion criteria encompassed experimental studies utilizing qPCR, RNA-seq, or microarray techniques to quantify acrAB expression, as well as research assessing the efficacy of EPIs in restoring antibiotic susceptibility. Data synthesis was performed using a random-effects meta-analysis model, and heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic.ResultsA total of 10 studies were included in the final meta-analysis. Pooled analysis demonstrated a significant increase in acrAB expression (SMD: 3.5, 95% CI: 2.1–4.9) in MDR E.coli isolates compared to susceptible strains. Efflux inhibition resulted in a ≥ 4-fold reduction in minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) for fluoroquinolones and β-lactams across multiple studies. Risk ratio analysis showed that EPIs significantly restored antibiotic susceptibility (RR: 4.2, 95% CI: 3.0–5.8). However, substantial heterogeneity was noted among studies due to methodological variations.ConclusionThese findings confirm that acrAB-tolC overexpression is a major contributor to antibiotic resistance in E.coli and that efflux inhibition is a viable strategy for restoring antibiotic susceptibility. However, clinical translation remains a challenge due to toxicity concerns and pharmacokinetic limitations of current EPIs. Future research should focus on developing safer efflux inhibitors, optimizing combination therapies, and standardizing efflux pump expression assays to facilitate their integration into antimicrobial treatment strategies.Keywords: Escherichia coli, Multidrug resistance, Efflux pumps, AcrAB-tolC, Efflux pump inhibitors, Antibiotic resistance, Systematic review, Meta-analysis

Background

Multidrug-resistant (MDR) Escherichia coli (E. coli) is a growing global health crisis, posing a significant threat to public health and clinical treatment strategies. The World Health Organization (WHO) has classified MDR E.coli as a priority pathogen due to its increasing resistance to frontline antibiotics and its role in hospital-acquired infections [1]. E.coli is a leading cause of urinary tract infections (UTIs), bloodstream infections (BSIs), pneumonia, and intra-abdominal infections, all of which contribute to rising morbidity, prolonged hospitalizations, and increased healthcare costs [2]. Among the various mechanisms contributing to antibiotic resistance, efflux pumps play a pivotal role in actively removing antibiotics from bacterial cells, reducing intracellular drug concentrations, and decreasing treatment efficacy [3].

One of the most clinically significant efflux systems in E.coli is the AcrAB-TolC multidrug efflux pump, a tripartite system consisting of AcrA (a periplasmic adaptor protein), AcrB (an inner membrane transporter), and TolC (an outer membrane channel) [4]. This efflux pump confers resistance to multiple antibiotic classes, including fluoroquinolones, β-lactams, aminoglycosides, tetracyclines, and macrolides, making it a key determinant of MDR in E.coli [5]. Studies have consistently demonstrated that acrAB overexpression is associated with high-level antibiotic resistance in clinical E.coli isolates, particularly those from hospital-acquired infections, making it a significant target for understanding resistance mechanisms [6]. The expression of the acrAB-tolC operon is tightly regulated by multiple global transcriptional regulators, notably MarA, SoxS, and Rob [7]. These regulators are activated in response to various environmental stressors, including exposure to antibiotics, oxidative stress, and toxic compounds [4]. Once activated, they upregulate acrAB expression, enhancing the bacterial cell’s ability to expel harmful agents and survive under hostile conditions [8]. Understanding these regulatory networks is crucial for contextualizing the role of AcrAB-TolC within broader stress response systems and antibiotic resistance mechanisms. However, despite its well-characterized function, acrAB expression remains highly variable, influenced by bacterial strain type, antibiotic exposure, and regulatory proteins such as MarA, SoxS, and Rob [9]. MarA, SoxS, and Rob are known transcriptional regulators that modulate the expression of the acrAB-TolC efflux pump system in response to environmental stressors such as exposure to antibiotics. MarA is a key regulator of the mar operon, which controls the expression of several efflux pumps, including AcrAB. SoxS, which is activated by oxidative stress, also enhances the expression of acrAB, while Rob, a regulator of the RpoS regulon, contributes to the regulation of the efflux pump during stationary phase. This variability complicates efforts to develop standardized diagnostic markers and therapeutic interventions targeting efflux pump-mediated resistance [10].

Efflux pumps such as AcrAB-TolC play a crucial role in bacterial survival and adaptation by actively extruding antimicrobial agents, allowing E.coli to evade antibiotic stress and develop MDR [11]. However, the extent of acrAB overexpression varies significantly due to environmental factors, mutations in regulatory networks, and bacterial adaptation mechanisms [12]. While some studies establish a strong link between acrAB overexpression and antibiotic resistance [13, 14], others suggest that its expression is influenced by factors such as exposure duration [15] and bacterial lineage [16, 17]. Given this complexity, efflux pump inhibitors (EPIs) have been investigated as potential adjuvant therapies to counteract resistance by blocking efflux activity, thereby increasing intracellular antibiotic concentrations and restoring bacterial susceptibility [18]. Despite promising results with synthetic and naturally occurring EPIs, such as Phenylalanine-Arginine Beta-Naphthylamide (PAβN), Carbonyl Cyanide m-Chlorophenylhydrazone (CCCP), and plant-derived polyphenols, their clinical application remains hindered by toxicity, poor pharmacokinetics, and bacterial adaptation [19]. Advances in structure-based drug design have led to the development of AcrB-targeted inhibitors, offering new therapeutic possibilities [20]; however, further clinical trials and pharmacokinetic optimizations are necessary before EPIs can be effectively integrated into routine clinical practice.

While molecular techniques such as quantitative PCR (qPCR), RNA sequencing (RNA-seq), and microarrays have been used to analyze acrAB gene expression, inconsistencies in methodology, bacterial isolates, and antibiotic exposure conditions have led to conflicting findings, making it difficult to draw definitive conclusions about the clinical significance of acrAB overexpression [21]. Given these discrepancies, a systematic review and meta-analysis are necessary to consolidate the available evidence and provide a clearer understanding of acrAB expression patterns across different antibiotic exposures [22].

This systematic review and meta-analysis aim to evaluate acrAB efflux pump gene expression in E.coli under various antibiotic exposure conditions. By synthesizing data from multiple studies, we will analyze acrAB expression patterns, assess their association with antibiotic resistance phenotypes, and examine the potential role of EPIs in restoring antibiotic susceptibility. Furthermore, we aim to identify key regulatory mechanisms influencing acrAB activity, addressing discrepancies in reported findings across different bacterial strains and methodologies. Through this comprehensive analysis, we seek to provide evidence-based insights that will enhance antibiotic stewardship strategies, inform the development of novel therapeutic approaches, and support global efforts to combat MDR E.coli infections.

Methods

Criteria for selecting studies

This systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) guidelines [23], ensuring methodological rigor and transparency. The study aimed to assess acrAB efflux pump gene expression in E.coli following antibiotic exposure and evaluate the impact of EPIs on bacterial antibiotic susceptibility.

Studies were included if they examined acrAB gene expression in E.coli isolates using molecular techniques such as qPCR, RNA-seq, or microarrays. Eligible studies are needed to compare acrAB expression levels in antibiotic-exposed E.coli isolates with non-exposed controls and report quantifiable gene expression data. Research investigating the effect of EPIs on acrAB expression and antibiotic susceptibility was also considered. Only peer-reviewed articles published in English, Portuguese, or Spanish were included, with non-English studies translated for analysis.

Exclusion criteria were applied to studies that did not quantify acrAB gene expression, focused solely on efflux pump function without molecular analysis, or examined non-E.coli species. Review articles, case reports, and editorials were omitted due to the lack of extractable data for meta-analysis. Additionally, studies investigating multiple resistance mechanisms without isolating acrAB expression were excluded. Research using genetically modified E.coli strains with artificially induced acrAB overexpression, rather than antibiotic-induced expression, was not considered. Studies without a clearly defined control group (antibiotic-exposed vs. non-exposed E.coli), or those that failed to specify antibiotic concentration and exposure duration, were also excluded.

Despite the abundance of studies investigating the AcrAB-TolC efflux pump, strict inclusion criteria were applied in this review to focus specifically on studies that (1) quantitatively measured acrAB gene expression levels (2), did so under defined antibiotic exposure conditions, and (3) provided sufficient data for comparative or meta-analytical analysis. Studies that explored efflux mechanisms without direct expression quantification, those focusing solely on efflux pump structure or non-antibiotic stressors, and reviews or non-primary research articles were excluded. A total of 63 studies were initially identified and screened for eligibility. After applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 33 studies were excluded based on title and abstract review. Following full-text assessment, an additional 20 studies were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria, resulting in 10 high-quality studies that were included in the final meta-analysis. Workflow timeline and author roles

A comprehensive literature search was conducted across PubMed, Scopus, Google Scholar, and EBSCO, covering studies published from 1999 to March 2025. The search strategy incorporated Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) terms and free-text keywords, ensuring a broad yet precise identification of relevant literature. The initial search yielded 854 studies, of which 783 were excluded due to irrelevance, lack of gene expression data, or other predefined criteria. The remaining 63 studies were assessed for eligibility.

The study selection process involved two independent reviewers who screened titles and abstracts, followed by a full-text assessment to confirm eligibility. Disagreements were resolved through discussion or consultation with a third reviewer. Data extraction was performed using a standardized data collection form, including information on the studies included in the meta-analysis (Table 1). Extracted data were independently verified before statistical analysis. The PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. 1) illustrates the study selection process.

PRISMA flow diagram illustrating study selection for systematic review and meta-analysis. Initial database searches identified 854 articles, of which 10 were included based on predefined inclusion criteria focusing on acrAB expression under antibiotic exposure

Types of outcome measures

The primary outcome measure was the quantification of acrAB gene expression in E. coli isolates following antibiotic exposure. Gene expression levels were reported as fold-change values, determined through qRT-PCR or RNA-seq, and analyzed across different antibiotic classes. The summary of acrAB expression patterns details variations in gene expression between antibiotic-resistant and susceptible strains.

The secondary outcome measure assessed the impact of EPIs on bacterial antibiotic susceptibility, evaluated through changes in minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values. The effectiveness of EPIs was defined by a ≥ 50% reduction in MIC values, indicating a significant restoration of bacterial susceptibility.

Comparison of different study designs

The included studies varied in bacterial strain origins, resistance profiles, and experimental methodologies. To account for methodological diversity, studies were categorized based on strain type (clinical isolates, laboratory strains, MDR strains), as detailed in Table 2. Additionally, the studies employed different approaches to genetic analysis of acrAB expression, including qRT-PCR, RNA-seq, and microarray-based methods. The studies included mutational analysis, which primarily focused on point mutations in acrAB, acrA, and tolC genes. Some studies also employed genetic deletion studies to determine the contribution of specific efflux pump genes to antibiotic resistance. A summary of the methodologies utilized across studies is provided in Table 3. A subset of studies also investigated the role of regulatory genes, such as MarA, SoxS, and Rob, in acrAB overexpression. Some studies incorporated mutational analyses to examine potential genetic alterations affecting efflux pump activity.​.

Data analysis and quality assessment

Data synthesis was conducted using Review Manager (RevMan) 5.4 [29], applying a random-effects model to account for anticipated heterogeneity across included studies. Sensitivity and specificity values were extracted where applicable to evaluate acrAB expression as a potential biomarker for antibiotic resistance. Forest plots were generated to visualize the variation in acrAB expression across studies.

Heterogeneity was assessed using the I² statistic, which quantifies variability not due to chance [30]. A low I² value (0–25%) indicated minimal heterogeneity, while moderate heterogeneity (26–50%) suggested that methodological or biological differences influenced acrAB expression levels. High heterogeneity (>50%) suggested substantial variability, prompting further exploration through subgroup analyses based on bacterial strain type (clinical vs. laboratory isolates), antibiotic class (fluoroquinolones vs. β-lactams vs. aminoglycosides), and the presence or absence of EPIs.

If substantial heterogeneity was detected, sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the robustness of the results and determine whether specific studies disproportionately influenced the overall findings. The presence of publication bias was evaluated using Egger’s test and funnel plots, where asymmetry suggested the possibility of reporting bias.

The quality of the included studies was assessed using QUADAS-2 (Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies 2) for experimental studies and the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for observational studies [31]. Each study was systematically evaluated for selection bias, performance bias, detection bias, and reporting bias, ensuring methodological rigor and reliability.

Results

A systematic search identified a set of eligible studies examining the expression of the AcrAB-TolC efflux pump system in E. coli and its role in MDR. Following screening and the application of inclusion criteria, a total of 10 studies were selected for the final meta-analysis. These studies encompassed diverse geographical regions, including India, the USA, Egypt, China, Germany, and Turkey, and featured various study designs. The included research ranged from in vitro laboratory experiments using clinical isolates to investigations involving genetically modified bacterial strains (Table 1).

The bacterial strains analyzed comprised both clinical isolates and laboratory strains, with a primary emphasis on MDR strains resistant to fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, and β-lactams (Table 2). Antibiotic susceptibility testing revealed a notable decrease in MIC values upon the introduction of EPIs, reinforcing the role of the AcrAB-TolC efflux system in resistance mechanisms (Table 3). However, not all 10 studies were included in the MIC analysis. Studies were excluded if they did not report MIC values or if data was unavailable for specific antibiotic classes. The studies excluded from this analysis were [15–17]. Although Rahmati et al., did not explicitly report resistance profiles for their laboratory strains, the study was included because it provided quantifiable acrAB expression data under defined experimental conditions involving antibiotic exposure, fulfilling our primary inclusion criteria [15].

Genetic analysis of acrAB-tolC and regulatory genes

qRT-PCR gene deletion studies, and DNA sequencing consistently demonstrated overexpression of the acrA, acrB, and tolC genes in drug-resistant E.coli strains. Mutation analysis identified point mutations in these genes within a subset of resistant isolates. Additionally, transcriptional regulators such as AcrR, MarA, SoxS, and MarR were found to contribute to the upregulation of these efflux pump genes, further reinforcing their role in MDR (Table 3).

Antibiotic susceptibility and efflux Inhibition

MIC values were analyzed before and after efflux pump inhibition across multiple studies. Efflux inhibitors such as CCCP, MBX2319, CPZ, and NMP significantly lowered MICs, with reductions ranging from 2-fold to 8-fold (Table 4). Among these, MBX2319 exhibited the most substantial effect, reducing ciprofloxacin MIC from 2 µg/mL to 0.5 µg/mL (Table 5). These findings underscore the potential of efflux pump inhibition as a strategy to restore antibiotic susceptibility, reinforcing their viability as therapeutic targets.

Quality assessment of included studies

The methodological quality of the included studies was evaluated using the QUADAS-2 tool for experimental studies and the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) for observational studies. QUADAS-2 assessed potential biases related to patient selection, index testing, reference standards, and study flow, while NOS focused on selection criteria, comparability, and outcome assessment in clinical research.

Overall, the studies exhibited moderate methodological quality, with key limitations including heterogeneity in experimental conditions and limited clinical validation. Differences in bacterial strain selection, antibiotic exposure conditions, and efflux pump expression quantification methods contributed to variability in results. Addressing these issues through standardized efflux pump expression assays, improved study designs, and in vivo validation will be essential for enhancing the reliability and clinical applicability of future research (Table 10).

Statistical analysis outcomes

Statistical analyses confirmed significant differences between EEP and non-EEP strains. Principal component analysis (PCA) identified distinct clustering based on MIC changes post-EPI treatment. ANOVA testing, corrected for false discovery rate (FDR), demonstrated significant overexpression of acrB (P < 0.001) and other efflux-related genes among EEP strains. Multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) further supported these findings (P < 0.001). Additional nonparametric analyses validated the robustness of these observations.

Discussion

The findings from this systematic review and meta-analysis confirm that AcrAB-TolC efflux pump overexpression plays a central role in MDR in E.coli. Across the included studies, elevated acrAB expression was consistently observed in antibiotic-resistant isolates, particularly those exposed to fluoroquinolones, β-lactams, and aminoglycosides. The pooled analysis further demonstrated that EPIs significantly reduced MICs, effectively restoring bacterial susceptibility to antibiotics. These results align with previous research suggesting that efflux inhibition can mitigate resistance mechanisms and enhance antimicrobial efficacy [10, 11].

The notable overexpression of acrAB in clinical isolates, particularly those causing bloodstream infections and urinary tract infections, highlights its potential as a biomarker for MDR E.coli. Interestingly, consistent with Camp et al. (2021) [26], our findings suggest that efflux-mediated resistance was less pronounced in ST131 clones compared to other Multidrug-resistant E. coli strains, as confirmed by our principal component and gene expression analyses [6, 32]. These findings suggest that targeting efflux mechanisms could be particularly effective in combating high-risk MDR strains.

Comparison with previous literature

Previous studies have established the AcrAB-tolC efflux system as a key contributor to both intrinsic and acquired resistance mechanisms [3, 4]. The findings from this meta-analysis reinforce these conclusions, providing quantitative evidence that acrAB overexpression is directly associated with increased MICs. To better illustrate the relationship between acrAB expression and antibiotic resistance, we plotted the studies based on reported changes in acrAB expression and corresponding MIC reductions following EPI treatment (Fig. 5). Studies that reported acrAB upregulation generally also observed significant MIC reductions, supporting the role of efflux in resistance mechanisms. Although exact expression levels and MIC fold-changes were not uniformly reported, the trend suggests a positive association between efflux pump expression and antibiotic resistance. Furthermore, the observed MIC reductions following efflux pump inhibition are consistent with earlier reports indicating that EPIs such as PAβN and CCCP can enhance the efficacy of fluoroquinolones and β-lactams [12, 24].

However, this study also highlights significant heterogeneity in acrAB expression levels across research findings, suggesting that environmental factors, bacterial genetic background, and antibiotic exposure duration may influence expression patterns. This variability underscores the necessity for standardized methodologies in efflux pump expression studies, as differences in experimental techniques (e.g., qPCR vs. RNA-seq) could contribute to inconsistencies in reported gene expression levels [21].

Clinical and therapeutic implications

The identification of acrAB overexpression as a major contributor to MDR underscores the need for efflux pump-targeting therapeutic strategies. This study reinforces that efflux inhibitors (EPIs), whether used as standalone agents or adjunctive therapies, can significantly restore antibiotic susceptibility. However, despite promising in vitro findings, the clinical application of EPIs remains limited due to concerns regarding toxicity, poor pharmacokinetics, and bacterial adaptation [18, 20]. Further research is essential to develop clinically viable EPIs with improved safety profiles and efficacy in vivo models.

Beyond efflux inhibition, targeting regulatory pathways that modulate acrAB expression may offer alternative therapeutic approaches. Several studies have identified transcriptional regulators such as MarA, SoxS, and Rob as key modulators of efflux pump activity [9, 10]. This association between acrAB overexpression and efflux-mediated resistance modulation has been previously reported. Studies by Opperman et al. (2014) [24], Chetri et al. (2019) [13], and Mohamed et al. (2016) [25] demonstrated that strains exhibiting higher acrAB expression were more responsive to EPI-mediated reductions in MIC, underscoring the pivotal role of this efflux system in multidrug resistance.

Limitations and future recommendations

Despite the robustness of this meta-analysis, several limitations must be acknowledged. Methodological heterogeneity across studies-including differences in bacterial strains, antibiotic exposure conditions, and gene expression quantification methods-may have influenced pooled effect sizes. Standardizing efflux pump expression assays and MIC testing protocols would enhance comparability across studies. Another limitation of this review is the relatively small number of studies included. This reflects the strict inclusion criteria emphasizing quantitative expression measurements under specific antibiotic exposures. While numerous studies describe AcrAB-TolC’s role in resistance, relatively few provide direct gene expression quantification suitable for systematic meta-analysis. Future research expanding standardized expression reporting across diverse clinical settings would enhance the ability to synthesize broader conclusions.

Additionally, the predominance of in vitro experiments limits the direct clinical applicability of these findings. Future research should prioritize in vivo studies and clinical trials to evaluate the efficacy of efflux inhibitors and regulatory gene modulators in patient-derived isolates. This finding aligns with previous observations reported by Camp et al. (2021) [26], which highlighted variability in efflux expression profiles among different E. coli lineages.

Finally, the emergence of novel EPIs and alternative resistance mitigation strategies warrants further investigation. Advances in structure-based drug design have facilitated the development of AcrB-specific inhibitors, which may provide greater efficacy than broad-spectrum EPIs [20, 24]. Moreover, recent studies suggest that combination therapies incorporating efflux inhibitors and membrane-permeabilizing agents could enhance drug penetration and more effectively counteract efflux-mediated resistance [22].

Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis provide compelling evidence that acrAB-tolC overexpression is a key driver of antibiotic resistance in E.coli. The findings confirm that efflux inhibition significantly reduces MICs, reinforcing the potential of targeting efflux pumps as a therapeutic approach. However, clinical translation remains challenging, highlighting the need for further research into safer, more effective efflux inhibitors and alternative regulatory targets. Future investigations should focus on standardizing efflux pump expression assays, developing novel inhibitors with enhanced pharmacokinetics, and conducting clinical trials to validate their therapeutic efficacy. Addressing these challenges will be essential to optimizing treatment strategies against MDR E.coli infections.

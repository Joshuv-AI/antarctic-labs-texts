Public Preference for Off-Label Use of Drugs for Cancer Treatment and Relative Importance of Associated Adverse Events: A Discrete Choice Experiment and Best-Worst Scaling
Authors: Kailu Wang, Ho-Man Shum, Carrie Ho-Kwan Yam, Yushan Wu, Eliza Lai-Yi Wong, Eng-Kiong Yeoh
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivePatients may get more treatment options with off-label use of drugs while exposed to unknown risks of adverse events. Little is known about the public or demand-side perspective on off-label drug use, which is important to understand how to use off-label treatment and devise financial assistance. This study aimed to quantify public preference for off-label cancer treatment outcomes, process, and costs, and perceived importance of associated adverse events.MethodsA discrete choice experiment and a best-worst scaling were conducted in Hong Kong in December 2022. Quota sampling was used to randomly select the study sample from a territory-wide panel of working-age adults. Preferences and willingness to pay (WTP) for treatment effectiveness, risk of adverse events, mode of drug administration, and availability of off-label treatment guidelines were estimated using a random parameter logit model and latent class model. The relative importance of different adverse events was elicited using Case 1 best-worst scaling.ResultsA total of 435 respondents provided valid responses. In the discrete choice experiment, the respondents indicated that extra overall survival as treatment effectiveness (WTP: HK$448,000/US$57,400 for 12-month vs 3-month extra survival) was the most important attribute for off-label drugs, followed by the risk of adverse events (WTP: HK$318,000/US$40,800 for 10% chance to have adverse event vs 55%), mode of drug administration (WTP: HK$42,000/US$5300 for oral intake vs injection), and availability of guidelines (WTP: HK$31,000/US$4000 for available versus not available). Four groups with distinct preferences were identified, including effectiveness oriented, off-label use refusal, oral intake oriented, and adverse event risk aversion. In the best-worse scaling, hypothyroidism, nausea/vomiting, and arthralgia/joint pain were the three most important adverse events based on the perceptions of respondents. Risk-averse respondents, who were identified from the discrete choice experiment, had different perceived importance of the adverse events compared with those with other preferences.ConclusionsKnowing the preference and WTP for cancer treatment-related characteristics from a societal perspective facilitates doctors’ communications with patients on decision making and treatment goal-setting for off-label treatment, and enables devising financial assistance for related treatments. This study also provides important insight to inform evaluations of public acceptance and information dissemination in drug development as well as future economic evaluations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40258-024-00912-1.

Introduction

Off-label drug use often refers to the prescription of drugs available in the market for indications that have never been approved by regulatory authorities, such as the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), which does not control how the drugs on the market are prescribed and allows physicians to apply new therapeutic options to patients that they deem suitable based on updated evidence [1, 2]. Doctors may make use of the drugs approved for one condition for other conditions with similar pathological features, or apply a drug to a condition where the other drugs of the same class have been approved for that condition, especially when the patients encounter a life-threatening or terminal condition [3, ]. In the context of cancer treatment, numerous new treatment options, including targeted therapy and immunotherapy, have been developed in recent years. Consequently, off-label use of these drugs may occur for patients with types of cancers expressing the same target, even if the drugs have not been officially approved for that specific treatment [4].

Off-label drug use is commonly adopted by clinicians worldwide. A study based on electronic health records in Canada revealed that 11% of the overall prescriptions and 12% of antineoplastic treatment, were off-label use [5]. Moreover, 79% of overall off-label prescriptions and 100% of off-label prescriptions for antineoplastic treatment were issued without strong scientific evidence [5]. A systematic review summarized that 18–41% of inpatient cancer treatments involved off-label use, and 13–71% of patients with cancer received at least one off-label treatment [6]. Specifically, in a more recent study in the USA, 18% of patients received pembrolizumab and nivolumab, two FDA-approved immunotherapy agents targeted to block programmed cell death protein 1, for off-label indications [7]. The substantial proportion of patients receiving off-label treatment, and limited evidence associated with off-label drug use, could expose more patients to unknown risks. It has been found that off-label use of different types of drugs contributes to 44% additional risks of adverse events (AEs) on average [8]. Among them, off-label use with strong evidence contributes to 10% additional risks, while those without strong evidence contribute to 54% additional risks [8]. All these have suggested off-label drug use as a double-edged sword, where the patients may gain access to new options for the treatment of indications not resolved by standard or on-label treatment, but also face greater risks that may potentially harm their health status and well-being.

To understand what and how the drugs should be used for off-label treatment, views from different parties need to be examined. However, most of the existing studies on off-label use primarily focused on clinicians rather than considering the patient (demand-side) perspective. Furthermore, these studies often lacked a preference-based method to quantify the factors influencing their decisions [9–11]. The perspective of drug users has become increasingly important in current healthcare services owing to the emphasis on person-centered care, and the need for off-label drug use arising from patient or family member’s demand for new treatment when the standard treatments failed [1]. Understanding people’s perspectives and preferences regarding off-label drug use can enhance doctor-patient communication, especially when disparities of opinions arise in critical decisions that carry risks for patients [11]. On the other hand, the public’s WTP on cancer treatment outcomes (effectiveness/AEs) and process (delivery of treatment) provides important information for understanding how financial subsidies should be provided to cover the expensive costs of cancer treatment [12]. Therefore, this study aims to quantify people’s preference for off-label cancer treatment outcomes and process, and estimate their WTP. Given that the users of off-label treatment are more likely to experience various AEs, this study also aimed to elicit the perceived relative importance of different type of treatment-associated AEs in terms of perceived impact on their quality of life, following elicitation of preferences for off-label drug use. In this study, a public perspective will be adopted in order to inform a health technology assessment and guide the allocation of public resources related to off-label drug use in future practice.

Methods

A discrete choice experiment (DCE) and a Case 1 best-worst scaling (BWS) were conducted in an online survey to understand people’s preference for off-label use of drugs for cancer and the perceived relative importance of associated AEs. The survey was conducted among working-age adults aged 18–64 years in Hong Kong during December 2022.

DCE Attributes and Design

The DCE includes choice tasks for respondents to choose between alternatives in each of them, which are described by attributes of cancer drugs. The attributes are shown in Table 1, including extra overall survival in addition to standard treatment (3/6/9/12 months), risk of AEs (10%, 25%, 40%, 55%), mode of drug administration (oral, injection, cannula), out-of-pocket payment (HK$50K [10% of total price], HK$200K [40%], HK$350K [70%], HK$500K [100%], equal to US$6.4K, US$25.6K, US$44.9K, and US$64.1K, respectively; US$1 = HK$7.8), and availability of guidelines for off-label use of the drug (available, not available). These attributes were determined based on previous studies for factors influencing preference for oncology treatment and the use of experimental drugs in clinical trials [13, 14]. Levels of the attributes were derived from existing systematic reviews on related topics (for overall survival, risk of AEs, and guidelines for off-label drug use) and both local and global practice of novel cancer treatments (for mode of administration and costs). For the attribute of extra overall survival in addition to the standard treatments, studies have shown that new cancer drugs in 2013 improved 2–9 months of overall survival for different cancers compared with drugs in 2003, and the overall survival improvement increased around 5.7 months in every 10 years during 1986–2016 [15, 16]. The grade 3+ AEs (i.e., AEs that are severe or medically significant, requiring hospitalization but not immediately life-threatening) of immune checkpoint inhibitors would be observed in or reported by 14–55% of people in different populations in clinical trials [17, 18]. For the mode of administration, the drugs can be given through oral intake, injection, and cannula (inserted for 6 months) [19]. The cost of programmed cell death protein 1 inhibitors per dose in Hong Kong is reported to be approximately HK$58,600 (one dose every 3 weeks) and the entire treatment duration on average is 183 days; therefore, the total cost per course of treatment is around HK$470,000–530,000 (assuming eight to nine doses administered) [20]. We also assume the costs can be covered by insurance, which resulted in different levels of copayments in DCE tasks as stated. As for guidelines for off-label use, it was reported that 7–31% of off-label use of the drugs for cancer was not supported by relevant guidelines [6], thus its importance is worthy to be examined in the DCE as well. To design the DCE choice sets, a D-efficient algorithm with zero prior means of attribute main effects was used, as it is not practically feasible to include all combinations of the attribute levels in the choice sets. A total of 36 choice sets with two alternatives in each of them were selected. To further reduce cognitive burdens posed to the respondents, these choice sets were assigned to six blocks with six choice sets in each block, and the respondents were randomly assigned to one of the blocks stratified by age and sex. The choice sets were pilot tested within three public health professionals and three members of the general public, who were shown a complete set of DCE questionnaires and asked to provide their responses on the online platform. Their feedbacks were collected immediately after completing the DCE tasks for refinement of clarity of the hypothetical scenarios, introduction to the attributes, and DCE tasks. In this DCE, a hypothetical scenario was described as the context for decision making with explanations of the attributes. Off-label use of drugs and definitions of the attributes were explained in detail, followed by an exercise choice task for respondents to get familiar with the attributes and DCE tasks before the formal tasks. In each choice set, the respondents were first asked which of the two drug alternatives is the preferred drug and then asked whether they would choose to use the drugs for the hypothetical family member if it were available. If the respondent chose “No” in the latter question, indicating that neither of the off-label drug alternatives would be chosen, this respondent was considered as “opt-out” in this choice set. The inclusion of an opt-out option and the use of this dual response design were found to result in data of higher quality [21]. The order of the choice sets and attributes within each choice set was randomized across individuals. A sample choice set is shown in Table S1 of the Electronic Supplementary Materia (ESM).

BWS Design

As it was not feasible to incorporate various types of AEs into a DCE either as extra attributes or as different levels of an extra attribute because the number of AEs is expected to be large, an object-case (Case 1) BWS was designed to determine the level of relative importance of different AEs in affecting quality of life. The other DCE attributes were not further investigated in the BWS as they did not encounter the same difficulties as analyzing various types of AEs. To identify and generate factors for the BWS study, a literature review was conducted by the researchers to search for AEs that are relevant and important in cancer treatments. Studies and reviews are included if they reported the incidence of AEs incurred by patients using novel cancer treatments such as immunotherapy (including monoclonal antibodies, checkpoint inhibitors, cell therapy). To include the most common AEs among the patients, incidence rates of the AEs summarized in the systematic reviews and meta-analysis were extracted and ranked [17, 22, 23]. The list of AEs was shortened to a manageable level in BWS to further reduce the cognitive burdens of the respondents based on the criteria that the AE occurred in over 5% of the patients. The AEs were non-specific to cancer treatments as they can also be experienced in other health conditions, which enabled the respondents to understand the health implications of the AE attributes. Finally, a list of nine AEs was selected for the BWS study to elicit their relative importance as shown in Table S2 of the ESM, which are also frequently used in health preference research on treatment side effects [12].

A balanced incomplete block design was adopted to generate the BWS tasks, where each attribute (BWS factor) is replicated and appears an equal number of times within the blocks (choice tasks), ensuring the probabilities of selecting each attribute are equal. In this study, there were 12 choice tasks with three attributes in each of them. Before the choice tasks, a brief introduction was presented to the respondents for them to understand the context of such AEs. In each choice task, the respondents were asked to select one AE with the greatest adverse impact and one AE with the smallest adverse impact on the patient’s quality of life out of the three AEs (attributes) presented in the choice task. All the respondents were asked to complete all 12 choice tasks; therefore, 24 choices were made by each of them. The order of the 12 tasks and the order of the AEs within each task was randomized across individual respondents. The BWS tasks were pilot tested among the same group of public health professionals and members of the general public and followed the same procedure as the pilot test for the DCE tasks. A sample BWS task is shown in Table S2 of the ESM.

Study Sample and Data Collection

The survey incorporating the DCE and BWS was conducted among adults aged 18–64 years in Hong Kong. The preferences were elicited from a taxpayer perspective to understand how the public resources should be allocated in enabling the development and use of off-label drugs to optimize their societal benefits. Eliciting the public perspective also helps us understand the preferences of potential patients and potential caregivers of the patients, which could be used to inform future usage of the off-label drug. A quota sampling approach was used to recruit survey respondents from a panel of Hong Kong residents established by our research team that includes adults with different occupations of all age groups. The quota was assigned to different age and sex groups based on the age and sex distributions of the Hong Kong population as reported in the 2021 local census to enhance sample representativeness. After initial screening for eligible adults in the panel based on their age, an invitation for the survey was sent to each of the randomly selected candidates through instant messaging applications and/or e-mail. This method was feasible because over 99% of people aged 15–54 years and around 95% of those aged 55–64 years in Hong Kong possessed a web-accessible mobile device [24]. People who accepted the invitation were directed to an online survey link for signing the informed consent and completing the questionnaire. The questionnaire was in Chinese and self-administered with a research staff available via the Internet or telephone to address their questions on the survey. In addition to DCE and BWS, their sociodemographic characteristics, health status, and medical service-seeking behaviors were recorded in the survey.

As the utility cannot be measured directly, the binary choice of respondents on an alternative (1-Yes/0-No), including the “opt-out” option, in the choice set was considered as the dependent variable. The attribute levels were included as independent variables of the regression models. An alternative-specific constant was also included as an independent variable to indicate the opt-out option for that choice set. A random parameter logit (RPL) model and a latent class logit (LCL) model were used for analyzing DCE results. The RPL model assumes a preference for the attribute levels to be varied across individuals, so it takes into account the correlation of preferences within the same respondent and preference heterogeneity across different respondents. In the RPL model, 500 Halton draws were used to simulate random parameters. The cost was specified as a variable following log-normal distribution while the other attributes followed normal distribution. The LCL model was used to find out the respondent segments with different preferences and identify the socioeconomic characteristics associated with different classes of preferences. The number of classes used in the LCL model was determined based on the Bayesian Information Criterion of the models [26, 27]. The relative importance of the DCE attributes was estimated based on the difference between the highest coefficient and the lowest coefficient of different levels of that attribute in the RPL regression model. To take into account the utility associated with the opt-out option (i.e., probability of not choosing the presented off-label treatment alternatives), a Hicksian compensating variation method was used to estimate the willingness to pay (WTP) for the attributes [25, 28]. For the BWS data, count analysis and regression analysis were applied to find out the relative importance of AEs. In the count analysis, three types of scores were calculated to describe the frequency of the BWS attributes, including (1) BWS score, referring to the difference between the number of times that an attribute was chosen as most influential and least influential AEs; (2) mean BWS score, referring to average BWS scores per respondent for the attribute; and (3) scaled BWS score, referring to the square root of the ratio of total best score to total worst score for that attribute, which indicates choice probability relative to the most influential attribute. In the regression analysis, a multinomial logit (MNL) model and an RPL model were adopted, where the dichotomous choice to the specific combinations of choice outcomes to the task is the dependent variable (an example of choice outcome combination: attribute A selected as best, B selected as worst, C not selected; if this combination appeared in response, its dependent variable is coded as 1; otherwise it is coded as 0). The independent variables are the nine BWS attributes (AEs; coded as 1 if selected as best, coded as −1 if worst, 0 if not selected). The coefficients of both models show the relative importance of the BWS attributes, and the RPL model also examines the preference heterogeneity for the AEs. The RPL model was performed for respondents under the different latent classes of preference for off-label use of drugs as estimated in the DCE LCL model to explore the relationship between preferences for drug attributes and the type of AEs. Stata 15.0 and R 4.0.5 were used for the analysis of both DCE and BWS data. Statistical significance was claimed when the P<0.05.

Results

Sample Characteristics

A total of 700 were invited to the survey, and 698 formally agreed to participate by signing the consent form. Among them, 263 people did not complete the questionnaire and were excluded from the analysis. The remaining 435 respondents completed the questionnaire, including DCE and BWS, therefore the response rate was 62.1%. Their characteristics are shown in Table 2. There were 56.3% female, 30.3% aged 18–34 years, 32.2% aged 35–49 years, and 37.5% aged 50–64 years, which is matched to age and sex distribution in the Hong Kong population (55.9% female; 28.4% aged 18–34 years, 35.2% aged 35–49 years, and 36.4% aged 50–64 years) [31]. There were 53.1% married respondents, 51.8% with a bachelor’s degree or above, 71.5% with full-time jobs, and 63.4% with personal income under HK$30,000 per month. For health information, 40.2% reported their health to be fair or poor, and 28.3% reported having a family history of cancer. Characteristics of the study sample

The respondents took 13.7 minutes (822 seconds [median]; interquartile range: 506–1327 seconds) on average to complete the questionnaire, including DCE and BWS. There were 22 of them (5.1%) who finished within 5 minutes. In DCE tasks, there were only 9.7% who constantly refused to use any of the 12 drug alternatives presented to them.

Preference for Off-Label Use of Cancer Drugs in a DCE

Table 3 shows the RPL model outcome of a DCE on the preference for the drug attributes and levels used in this study as well as the WTPs. For the non-monetary attributes, the extra overall survival associated with off-label use of the drugs was deemed as the most important attribute, followed by risk of grade 3+ AEs, mode of drug administration, and availability of guide for off-label use of the drug. The respondents were also sensitive to changes in out-of-pocket payments. Compared with an off-label drug option at the base-case scenario (i.e., 3-month extra overall survival, 55% adverse event, administered by injection, no off-label use guidelines), the respondents were willing to pay an extra HK$214,000 (US$27,400), HK$259,000 (US$33,200), and HK$448,000 (US$57,400) for 6-month, 9-month, and 12-month extra survival, respectively. They were willing to pay an extra HK$191,000 (US$24,500) and HK$318,000 (US$40,800) for 25% and 10% chances of AEs versus the base-case scenario, while the WTP was not significantly different from zero for a 40% chance. The WTP was HK$42,000 (US$5300) for oral-intake drugs compared with the base-case scenario that is administered by injection. The WTP for guidelines of off-label use was estimated as HK$31,000 (US$4000), while it was not significantly different from zero. Table 4 also shows the socioeconomic characteristics of individuals associated with different classes of preferences. Compared with Class 4 (oral intake/cost insensitive/higher acceptance), Class 1 (effective oriented) tended to have a lower income and a family history of cancer. Class 2 (refusal of off-label use of drugs) tended to have a bachelor’s degree or above and have a relatively lower income level. Class 3 (AE risk aversion) tended to be people who were aged 35–49 years, had a bachelor’s degree or above, had a lower income, and had a family history of cancer. Compared with the other classes, respondents in Class 4 tended to be male, have a higher income, and did not have a family history of cancer.

Relative Importance for Different Types of AEs in BWS

The relative importance of the AEs in affecting patients’ quality of life elicited in BWS tasks is reported in Table S4 of the ESM. Based on a count analysis, it found that nausea/vomiting was found to be the most important AE that the respondents believed would have the greatest impact on the quality of life of patients using cancer treatments (mean score: 1.83 [95% confidence interval 1.66, 1.99]). In contrast, decreased appetite was the one selected as the least important AE (mean score: − 2.09 [95% confidence interval − 2.27, − 1.92)). Similar results can be observed in the MNL and RPL model outcomes. Decreased appetite was selected as the reference level because it was the least important factor in the count analysis. In the MNL model, nausea/vomiting was still found to be the most important AE among the nine candidates, followed by hypothyroidism, arthralgia/joint pain, rash, diarrhea, pruritus/itchy, asthenia/weakness, fatigue, and decreased appetite. Both fatigue and decreased appetite were the least important AE in the MNL model as there was no significant difference between these two factors. In the RPL model, the relative importance ranking was the same as the MNL model outcome, except that hypothyroidism was slightly more important than nausea/vomiting. Based on the reported standard deviations of RPL model coefficients, it was found that there was substantial heterogeneity of relative importance of the AEs across individuals for all the AEs except diarrhea.

AE Relative Importance in BWS According to Heterogeneous Preference in a DCE

To further explore whether the perceived relative importance of AEs was different across individual segments with different preferences for the drug attributes, subgroup analyses were performed (Table S5 of the ESM). The respondents were assigned to the class with the highest posterior probabilities of class membership of the four classes. The RPL model for BWS responses was performed for the four classes separately. For Class 1 respondents who primarily focused on the overall survival of the drugs, the AE relative importance ranking was the same as the overall sample as it takes the largest share among all the classes. The Class 2 respondents, who were likely to refuse off-label use of the drugs, presented a similar perceived relative importance ranking as Class 1 and overall sample. Class 3 respondents who primarily focused on the risk of AEs, however, had a rather different ranking, where nausea/vomiting, rash, and arthralgia/joint pain were the top three important AEs that were perceived to affect patients’ quality of life. Class 4 were the respondents who preferred an oral intake of drugs and were insensitive to out-of-pocket payments, and they perceived arthralgia/joint pain as the most important AE, followed by nausea/vomiting and hypothyroidism.

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is one of the first studies quantifying the preference for off-label use of cancer drugs among members of the general population. The general public’s response enables the measurement of preference for cancer treatment-related outcomes and characteristics from a societal perspective that can be used to inform devising public or private insurance-based assistance, future drug development as well as economic evaluations for related treatments [22, 32]. By adopting a DCE and BWS, this study has elicited preference for overall survival, risk of AEs, mode of drug administration, availability of off-label drug use guidelines, and relative importance of the type of AEs that are perceived to affect patients’ quality of life, and its association with preferences for attributes.

From the DCE for off-label use of drugs, it was found that extra overall survival associated with off-label use of the drugs was considered as the most important attribute. This outcome is consistent with the perceived importance of cancer treatment characteristics in previous studies that clinical benefit is more important than risks or other attributes, and overall survival is more important than the other benefits including progression-free survival and quality of life, although overall survival was used less frequently as an attribute in a DCE for cancer treatment [14]. Based on the marginal WTP of 12-month overall survival versus 3-month overall survival (HK$448,000), the WTP for 1 extra year of survival is approximately HK$597,000 (US$76,600). This aligns with the World Health Organization recommendation that WTP for one disability-adjusted life-year is approximately one to three times of local gross domestic product per capita (Hong Kong 2021 GDP per capita: US$49,661/HK$387,356) [33], although the estimation did not take into account quality of life. The risk of AEs is the second most important attribute, where a lower risk of AEs is apparently preferable. Based on the RPL model outcome, the WTP is around HK$70,700 (US$9100) for every 10% point reduction in AE risk. For the mode of administration, oral intake is a preferable option to injection or use of a cannula as suggested by previous studies [34]. The availability of off-label drug use guidelines, however, did not significantly affect the preference, which suggests that members of the general public in general trust the doctors’ expertise and judgments [35].

Individual segments with heterogeneous preferences and their characteristics were identified. The majority of the respondents who would take most of the attributes into account in making the choice while primarily effectiveness oriented were in general older and had a family history of cancer, which makes sense as they probably had a greater awareness of, and paid more attention to, cancer treatment outcomes that are presumed critical to their health [36]. On the contrary, people without a family cancer history are inclined to either refuse off-label drug use even if standard treatment is not working (Class 2) or prefer oral intake and the lowest AE risk (Class 4) that is related to the level of comfort in treatment. Furthermore, there is a small group (around 10%) of individuals (Class 3) who focus on AE risks, who are highly averse to higher risks of AEs associated with the use of cancer drugs. They may easily refuse to use drugs that can potentially induce more frequent or severe AEs, and could benefit from introducing new treatment options with lower side effects or AEs even if the overall survival and other characteristics are not improved.

In addition to the DCE, BWS was used for determining the relative importance of different types of common AEs associated with cancer drugs. Hypothyroidism, nausea/vomiting, and arthralgia/joint pain are the three most important AEs that affect quality of life. In previous studies, nausea and vomiting were reported as one of the top three or even the most concerning AEs in chemotherapy [37], and bone pain was also perceived to be much more important than extreme tiredness and fogginess (i.e., memory and concentration) by patients with prostate cancer [38], while there were fewer studies investigating the relative importance of hypothyroidism or another thyroid hormone-related AE. In contrast, fatigue and decreased appetite were deemed as the least important AEs, where the former was usually relatively less severe than the other cancer treatment AEs as elicited from the patients [38, 39].

Heterogeneity of the AE relative importance was identified according to respondent segments with heterogeneous preferences for off-label drug use in order to determine their associations. Noticeably, respondents in Class 3 (AE risk aversion) presented a rather different AE relative importance from the sample average, where they considered nausea/vomiting, rash, arthralgia/joint pain, and diarrhea more important than hypothyroidism. One of the possible reasons could be that the most common symptoms of hypothyroidism are tiredness, cold intolerance, and slow movement and thoughts [40], which are usually not perceived to be as damaging to daily well-being as nausea/vomiting or pain; therefore, people who are desperate to avoid AEs would perceive nausea/vomiting as a more critical effect on quality of life, implying that drugs that can easily induce severe nausea/vomiting, rash, and arthralgia/joint pain are expected to be refused by patients with primary concerns on AE risks. Moreover, Class 4 respondents (oral intake) perceived arthralgia/joint pain as more important than the other AEs, which is probably because their preference for oral intake implies they are inclined to avoid pain or discomfort induced by medical interventions, thus joint pain becomes one of the primary AEs that they want to avoid.

These findings provided insight into how to enhance doctor-patient communication relating to cancer treatment by facilitating the discussions and decisions to use cancer drugs and setting treatment goals, which enables better responsiveness of healthcare services. The preference elicited in the study could help drug developers to evaluate anticipated acceptance of their potential products, and provide sufficient evidence on related characteristics of products to better meet the expectations of the public. The WTP values estimated in the study can be used to inform policy on the provision of financial assistance to address the affordability of cancer treatment. Willingness-to-pay results could also inform the intangible costs/benefits in future economic evaluations including cost-benefit analyses. Methodically, different methods have their own advantages in eliciting health preferences, and their findings can be integrated as shown in this study. In some occasions, there are attributes not suitable to be incorporated in a DCE, such as the various types of AEs that would induce a cognitive burden for DCE respondents, which may be investigated using less complicated methods such as BWS [41].

There are a few limitations in this study. First, the DCE part of the study can only include a limited number of attributes, while the preference for other characteristics was not reflected in this study. Nevertheless, the key characteristics regarding outcome, process, and cost were included as suggested by other studies [42], which has formed a relatively comprehensive set of attributes for analysis. Second, the general public usually has difficulties in understanding the probabilities in the DCE tasks (e.g., risks of AEs), which may hinder them from reflecting preferences to the probabilities in their choices. Third, there was a higher percentage of respondents with higher education experience than the general working population in Hong Kong. This may limit the generalization of the findings to people with lower education levels. Last, BWS did not include more serious AEs such as aspartate transaminase/alanine aminotransferase increases, lymphopenia, and hyponatremia because they were less frequent than the nine AEs included, and it was more difficult to explain the meanings, and more importantly, the clinical implications and consequences that affect their daily-life well-being of these events to members of the general public. Only the nine AEs that are more common and familiar to respondents were incorporated.

Conclusions

This study showed that people tend to focus more on treatment outcomes in terms of overall survival of the cancer treatment than the risk of AEs and mode of drug administration. The availability of off-label drug use guidelines was not found to influence people’s preference for the drugs. Based on their preferences, people can be classified as effectiveness oriented, off-label use refusal, oral intake oriented, or AE risk aversion in off-label drug use, who presented different rankings for AE relative importance in the impact on quality of life. The preference and its heterogeneity identified in this study could enhance doctor-patient communication on discussion and decisions regarding the off-label use of cancer drugs, which can be used to inform information provision in drug development and policies to increase affordability through financial assistance, and future economic evaluations. The approach used to examine relationships between health preferences identified in a DCE and BWS can be adopted and refined in future studies.

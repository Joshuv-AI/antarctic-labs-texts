Factors Related to Abnormal Endoscopic and Histologic Findings on Upper Gastrointestinal Endoscopy in Children
Authors: Hyun Jin Kim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeUpper gastrointestinal endoscopy is important in evaluating gastrointestinal symptoms and providing pathological descriptions. In this study, abnormal endoscopic and histological findings were identified in children, and the factors related to these findings were evaluated.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed the medical records of patients aged <18 years who underwent upper endoscopies between January 2017 and June 2022. While performing endoscopy, biopsies were taken, and the rapid urease test was performed simultaneously.ResultsThe endoscopic and histological findings were abnormal for 86 of (71.7%) and 111 (92.5%) of the 120 patients, respectively. Only four patients had normal endoscopic and histological findings. The most common endoscopic and histological findings were erythema (35%) and chronic gastritis (53.5%), respectively. Age ≥12 years (odds ratio [OR], 2.55; 95% confidence interval [CI], 1.21–6.68; p=0.033), poor eating habits (OR, 0.30; 95% CI, 0.10–0.88; p=0.029), melena (OR, 3.45; 95% CI, 1.22–6.42 p=0.043), and nausea (OR, 0.31; 95% CI, 0.13–0.74 p=0.009) were significant factors related to endoscopic abnormalities in the multivariate analysis. More frequent Helicobacter pylori infection was observed in patients with atrophy and intestinal metaplasia than in those with other histological abnormalities.ConclusionMost children with dyspepsia gave endoscopic abnormalities, and routine biopsies are important when performing endoscopy. We identified predictors of endoscopic findings.Keywords: Pediatrics, Endoscopy, Histology

INTRODUCTION

Upper endoscopy is important in evaluating gastrointestinal (GI) symptoms and pathological descriptions [1,2,3]. Endoscopic yields were positive in 65.6% of the upper endoscopy procedures evaluating the main endoscopic indications of abdominal pain [4]. Gastritis and reflux esophagitis were the most common diagnoses in upper endoscopy in a Korean study [5].

Dyspeptic symptoms such as epigastric pain and nausea are common indications for upper endoscopy, and endoscopic findings weakly correlated with GI symptoms [6,7]. However, alarm signs such as weight loss, dysphagia, bleeding, anemia, and early satiety are higher risk factors for serious endoscopic lesions [8].

Adult gastroenterologists generally perform tissue biopsies only when endoscopic abnormalities are identified [9]. However, biopsies are performed with pediatric endoscopies, even in the absence of gross abnormalities, because of the risks associated with repeated procedures and anesthetics [10]. A total of 40–50% of patients with normal endoscopic findings have histological abnormalities [11,12], supporting the need for routine biopsies due to the weak correlation between endoscopic and histologic findings. Histopathology is key in precisely diagnosing conditions and providing proper treatment in normal endoscopy, such as eosinophilic GI disease and Helicobacter pylori infection. However, studies are limited on the upper endoscopic and histological findings in children, and knowledge is lacking of the factors related to abnormal endoscopic and histological findings. Therefore, we aimed to identify endoscopic and histological findings in children with upper GI symptoms and the factors related to these findings.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

We retrospectively analyzed the medical records of patients aged <18 years who underwent upper endoscopy for upper GI symptoms between January 2017 and June 2022. Demographic data and endoscopic and histological findings were recorded. The body mass index (BMI) was calculated by dividing weight in kilograms by the square of height in meters. Obesity was defined as a BMI ≥the 95th percentile for sex and age, and overweight was defined as 85th≤BMI<95th percentile for sex and age [13].

A family history of H. pylori infection was considered positive when any first-degree relative had a H. pylori infection. Poor eating habits included eating too fast, irregular mealtimes, spicy and salty foods, and irregular meal sizes.

All endoscopies were performed by one experienced pediatric gastroenterologist. Biopsy samples were obtained from the corpus and antrum while performing endoscopy, and the rapid urease test was simultaneously performed. H. pylori is observed on normal endoscopy; therefore, we performed a rapid urease test even evidence was lacking of nodularity or ulceration suggesting H. pylori. Endoscopic and histologic findings were categorized according to the Sydney classification [14]. Endoscopic findings included erythema gastritis, erosive gastritis, reflux esophagitis, peptic ulcer disease (PUD), and duodenitis. The recorded histological findings included chronic gastritis, chronic atrophic gastritis, chronic intestinal metaplasia, and eosinophilic gastritis, Eosinophilic gastritis was diagnosed as the presence of ≥30 eosinophils/high-power fields in at least 5 high-power fields [15]. Atrophic gastritis and intestinal metaplasia are risk factors for gastric cancer; therefore, we divided the patients into two groups according to histologic findings [16].

Ethics

This retrospective analysis was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Chungnam National University Hospital and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (IRB number: 2022-11-076). The requirement for informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of this study.

Statics

Continuous data are reported as the median or mean (±standard deviation) and interquartile range. These data were compared using the Mann–Whitney U-test or Student’s t-test. Discrete data are reported as numbers and percentages and were compared using Fisher’s exact or chi-square tests. We used odds ratios (ORs) for logistic regression models to evaluate the factors associated with endoscopic abnormalities. Statistical significance was set at p<0.05. Statistics were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics ver. 24.0 software (IBM Co.).

RESULTS

The endoscopic and histological findings were abnormal in 86 (71.7%) and 111 (92.5%) of the 120 patients, respectively. The median age was 12.8 years (range 6–18 years), and the male-to-female ratio was 0.79:1. The main symptoms according to endoscopy were epigastric pain (39.9%) and nausea (35.5%). H. pylori was detected in 15 (23.5%) patients, and the 11/15 (73.3%) of the H. pylori infections were eradicated with first-line treatment. Older age, anemia, and melena were substantially more frequent in patients with abnormal endoscopic findings than in those with normal endoscopic findings. Poor eating habits and nausea were more frequent in patients with normal endoscopic findings than those with abnormal endoscopic findings. The histological abnormalities and rates of H. pylori infection did not differ between the groups according to endoscopic findings. DISCUSSION

The endoscopic and histological findings were abnormal in 71.7% and 92.5% of the 120 patients, respectively. The rate of abnormal endoscopic findings was higher in this study (>70%) than in previous studies (50–60%) [1,17]. We also frequently observed histological abnormalities in patients with normal endoscopic findings, indicating the importance of conducting routine biopsies [18,19,20].

Epigastric pain was the most common reason for performing upper endoscopy, with a frequency of 39.9%. The most frequent lesions on endoscopy and histology were erythema and chronic gastritis, respectively. These results are similar to those reported in previous studies [21,22].

The OR for histological and endoscopic abnormalities was relatively high, the difference been histological and endoscopic abnormalities was not significant (p>0.05). Some endoscopic findings strongly correlated with abnormal histologic finding in a prior study [19]. Therefore, biopsies must be obtained multiple sites. Biopsies should be randomly obtained from various areas even if the endoscopic appearance of the mucosa is normal.

Some symptoms were related to endoscopic abnormalities in our study. The median hemoglobin level did not differ between the normal and abnormal endoscopic groups; however, the proportion of patients with anemia and melena was higher in the abnormal endoscopy group than in the normal endoscopy group. PUD was more commonly observed in patients with melena. However, the incidence of nausea was higher in patients who underwent normal endoscopy. The relationship between GI symptoms and endoscopic findings has been debated. The prevalence of peptic ulcers was significantly higher in individuals with dyspepsia, with an OR of 2.07, than in healthy controls [23]. Gyedu et al. [24] reported that those with alarm symptoms (GI bleeding, dysphagia, or weight loss associated with dyspepsia) had a positive endoscopic finding with an OR of 2.34 (95% CI, 1.74–3.13). Conversely, Wallace et al. [25] reported that age and the presence of alarm symptoms were not effective predictors of endoscopic findings in patients with dyspepsia. Further systematic research is required to clarify the reasons for the discrepancies in these results.

Age and BMI were significantly related with overall pathological findings in a healthy population [26]. Patients aged ≥12 years were more likely to have endoscopic abnormalities; however, we found no correlation between BMI and the endoscopic findings.

We did not identify any factors significantly associated with histological abnormalities. However, atrophy and intestinal metaplasia were more often found in patients with H. pylori infection than in those without infection, similar to the results in other studies [27,28]. In addition, the prevalences of atrophy and intestinal metaplasia were relatively high (39.2%) and similar to those in children with H. pylori infection (34.7%) [29]. More attention must be paid to these histologic findings, and the necessity of eradicating H. pylori in children when detected in the rapid urease test, as in adults, must be studied. Atrophy and intestinal metaplasia are the major precursor lesions of gastric cancer in adults, and eradicating H. pylori is crucial for preventing cancer via regressing atrophic gastritis and intestinal metaplasia [30].

Our study has several limitations. First, the retrospective study design may have limited our control over potential confounding variables and prevents the inference of causal relationships. Second, the sample size was too small to draw definitive conclusions. Additional well-designed studies are required to address these limitations. Despite these limitations, our study contributes to the literature by describing endoscopic and histological findings and their related factors in children.

In conclusion, we found that most children with dyspepsia had abnormalities on endoscopy. Our results show that routine biopsies are important when performing endoscopy. Furthermore, we identified predictors of endoscopic findings.

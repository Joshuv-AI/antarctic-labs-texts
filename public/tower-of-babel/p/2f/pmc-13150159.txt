Effectiveness of Single Versus Double Burr Hole for Chronic Subdural Hematoma: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Deepak Kumar, Abhishek Kumar, Majid Anwer, Abdul Vakil Khan, Harendra Kumar, Rachith Sridhar, Abdul Hakeem, Anurag Kumar, Anil Kumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveBurr-hole craniostomy is a common surgical intervention for patients with chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH). Single burr hole craniostomy (SBHC) and double burr hole craniostomy (DBHC) are widely practiced techniques; however, debate continues regarding the superior approach owing to limited comparative literature. This study aimed to compare the clinical outcomes, complications, and recurrence rates between SBHC and DBHC in patients with cSDH.MethodsThis prospective, open-label, non-inferiority, randomized controlled trial was conducted at the AIIMS Patna. Sixty-six patients with cSDH were enrolled in this study and randomized to either SBHC or DBHC. Assessments were performed before surgery and at 1 week and one month after surgery. Preoperatively, no differences were observed in the non-contrast computed tomography scans, Glasgow Coma Scale scores, ventilator/intensive care unit days, recurrence, or complications. Statistical analysis was done using SPSS v22.0.ResultsPatient characteristics were comparable between groups. One week after surgery, mortality rate was higher in the SBHC group (11.43%) than in the DBHC group (3.12%), but the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.34). The duration of ventilator use was significantly longer in the SBHC group (p=0.001). Subsequent analysis showed that recurrence and reoperation rates were higher with SBHC (20.69% each) than those with DBHC (9.68% each), although the differences were not statistically significant (p=0.13 and 0.07, respectively). Neurological improvements and radiological outcomes showed similar trends. The difference in the non-recurrence rate (11.01%) remained within the non-inferiority margin (11.5%).ConclusionBoth SBHC and DBHC were effective in evacuating cSDH. SBHC was shown to be non-inferior to DBHC, but had a slight increase in the recurrence rate and ventilator time. Based on patient characteristics, SBHC may be a simple and less invasive treatment option.Trial RegistrationInternational Standard Randomised Controlled Trial Number Identifier: ISRCTN53688663Keywords: Chronic subdural hematoma, Single burr hole, Double burr hole, Randomized controlled trial, Neurosurgery, Recurrence, Complications

INTRODUCTION

Chronic subdural haematoma (cSDH) is the most severe brain injury in the elderly, resulting from the slow accumulation of blood between the dura and arachnoid mater, which causes elevated intracranial pressure and neurological manifestations.35) It frequently happens after a little or unrecognised injury and shows non-specific symptoms like headaches, cognitive impairment, motor problems, and trouble walking, which makes it hard to diagnose early on.17) Major risk factors are being older, taking anticoagulants or antiplatelet medicines, drinking too much alcohol for a long time, and brain atrophy that happens with age. These things make bridging veins more likely to break and bleed.17)

The estimated incidence of cSDH globally varies from 1.72 to 13.1 per million individuals annually, with a significant increase observed in individuals over 70 years of age (2 to 58 per 100,000 person-years).4) As the number of older people continues to rise, the burden of cSDH is projected to rise as well.7) Non-contrast computed tomography (NCCT) is still the best way to quickly and accurately find subdural fluid accumulation.1. For patients who are really weak, bed rest, steroids, or mannitol may be an option, although these treatments don't usually fix the condition and can make hospitalisation longer.31) Surgical treatment is the sole main type of treatment. The most common method is burr hole craniostomy (either single or double), followed by craniotomy and twist-drill craniostomy.1,3) The best way to do it is still up for debate. Some studies indicate markedly reduced recurrence and reoperation rates using double burr hole craniostomy (DBHC),22) whereas others observe no distinction between single and double burr-hole methodologies.11,36) Additionally, prudent meta-analyses presently indicate that both procedures demonstrate comparability in terms of efficacy and safety, while variations in hospital stay duration and surgical sequelae, such as seizures, are observed between research.13,21,33)

Due to the current trend towards minimally invasive techniques like burr hole drainage and the absence of comparative data from the east, this study seeks to evaluate the clinical outcomes, complications, and mortality related to single versus double burr hole craniostomies in patients with cSDH. This study was the first of its type in the region, aiming to ascertain whether single burr hole drainage is as effective as double drainage, while potentially reducing problems, and positioning it as a simpler, quicker, and less invasive treatment option.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

This was a single-centre, open-label, non-inferiority randomized controlled trial conducted at the Department of Trauma Surgery and Emergency, AIIMS Patna, aimed at comparing clinical outcomes of single versus DBHC in cSDH patients. Participants were followed up at 1 week and 1 month postoperatively to assess recovery and detect complications.

Sample size

Sample size was calculated using the non-inferiority trial formula8) with α=0.05, power=80%, and a non-inferiority margin (Δ) of 11.5%. Expected success rates were 93% for double burr hole (group A)25) and 84% for single burr hole (group B).15) A sample size of 30 per group was determined, increased to 66 (33 per group) to account for 10% attrition. This was verified using an online calculator.34)

Sampling method

Simple random sampling was used to select 66 patients with cSDH, assigning 33 to each surgical group.

Inclusion criteria

• Diagnosed cases of chronic subdural hematoma.

Exclusion criteria

• Recurrent or asymptomatic cSDH.

• Acute-on-cSDH.

Randomization & allocation

Participants were randomized via sealed opaque envelopes using a simple randomization sequence. Blinding was not feasible; hence, the study was open-label.

Surgical procedure

• Group A (double burr hole): burr holes in frontal and parietal regions; dura opened cruciately; hematoma irrigated with saline; subgaleal drains placed for 24–48 hours.

• Group A (double burr hole): burr holes in frontal and parietal regions; dura opened cruciately; hematoma irrigated with saline; subgaleal drains placed for 24–48 hours.

• Group B (single burr hole): burr hole in parietal region; otherwise, same technique as above.

• Group B (single burr hole): burr hole in parietal region; otherwise, same technique as above.

All patients underwent pre- and postoperative NCCT scans at 6 hours and at discharge. Follow-up included clinical assessment and structured questionnaire at one week and one-month post-op.

Data collection

Data were collected using structured patient forms and compiled into an Excel master chart. Demographics, comorbidities, clinical presentation, and imaging findings were recorded. Postoperative assessments focused on neurological recovery and complications.

Data analysis

Statistical analysis was done using SPSS v22.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Normality was tested using the χ2 test. Proportions were calculated for qualitative variables; means and standard deviations were used for quantitative data. χ2/Fisher’s exact test, t test, Spearman’s rank test, and logistic regression were used as appropriate. The p<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical approval & registration

Ethical clearance was obtained from the Institutional Ethics Committee (Ref: AIMS/Pat/IEC/PGTh/July22/16). The trial was registered at International Standard Randomised Controlled Trial Number (ID: ISRCTN53688663). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

RESULTS

Most of the patients had a history of head injury (87.88%) and headache (90.90%), while only 7.58% experienced seizures. Hypertension (59.09%) and diabetes mellitus (22.72%) were the most prevalent comorbidities. DISCUSSION

The baseline characteristics were statistically similar including age, sex, comorbidities, trauma history, GCS, hemorrhagic volume, midline shift, and density (all p>0.05), thus suggesting good randomization was done.

The sample size counted bilateral hematomas as two cases, and this meant that there were 31 and 32 final SBHC and DBHC patients, respectively, thereby ensuring that intergroup comparison was valid. The mortality was higher in SBHC (11.43%) than in DBHC (3.12%), but the difference was not statistically significant (p=0.34). Results almost similar were obtained by Narendra and Chihnara19) and Wan et al.33) Wan et al.33) conducted a meta-analysis and showed that there was no change in mortality (OR, 1.38; p=0.58).

DBHC was associated with a markedly shorter duration of ventilator support (p<0.001), which corroborated the findings by Shamim et al.27) Lengths of stay in the ICU and hospital did not show significant differences, supporting the results by Khan et al.14) and Wan et al.,33) but other studies, like those of Salem et al.,26) supported SBHC for shorter hospital stays. Surgical site infection rates were similar (p=0.97), thus aligning with the results of KC et al.12)

There were no significant differences in complications (p=0.67) and in accordance with the results of Shrish et al.,29) Lee et al.,16) and Regan et al.23) Non-surgical complications were similar, in contrast to elevated rates reported in studies by Lee et al.,16) Zumofen et al.,38) and Rohde et al.24) SBHC has been associated with increased recurrence and morbidity by Taussky et al.32) GCS-assessed neurological improvement showed no significant intergroup difference (p=0.336), in agreement with those found by Smith et al.,30) Omerhodžić et al.,21) and Shrish et al.29)

One-month mortality did not differ considerably (p=0.49), validating Chiappini et al.,5) Zolfaghari et al.,37) Miranda et al.,18) and Gupta and Jhanwar.9)

Hospital stays were longer in the DBHC group (p<0.001), consistent with Shamim et al.27) and with Shankar et al.,28) but inconsistent with Khan et al.14) and Wan et al.,33) who found no change. SBHC had a higher recurrence rate (20.69%) compared with DBHC (9.68%), although this difference was not statistically significant (p=0.13). This concurs with the findings of Nayil et al.,20) although Taussky et al.32) observed a higher recurrence in SBHC. On the other hand, Ducruet et al.6) preferred SBHC since it had a lower rate of recurrence.

The reoperation rate was also higher in SBHC (20.69%); however, this was not statistically significant (p=0.07), as shown by Han et al.10) and Camel and Grubb.2)

This study is novel due to its prospective randomised design comparing the non-inferiority of SBHC with DBHC in an Indian tertiary care setup. In contrast to the prior retrospective or single-arm trial, this trial offers controlled data for comparison of uniformly perioperative management, outcome assessment, and short-term follow-up.

These results contribute to the global knowledge by endorsing simpler surgical techniques from the perspective of resource-constrained neurosurgery. Broadly speaking, single and double burr-hole surgery produces comparable clinical and radiological outcomes among patients with cSDH.

SBHC has not shown short-term results that are worse than DBHC, with the difference in non-recurrence rates (11.01%) falling within the set non-inferiority margin of 11.5%. The SBHC group had larger numbers of recurrences and longer ventilator durations, although not reaching statistical significance.

As a consequence, SBHC might be regarded as an attractive and minimally invasive option for selected cases, on condition of careful postoperative monitoring to facilitate prompt identification of any likely recurrence.

Limitations

The primary objective of this study was to compare the efficacy of single versus DBHC in patients with chronic subdural hematoma. This single-center study from AIIMS Patna focused on short-term outcomes with a one-month follow-up, which may not fully capture long-term recurrence or functional recovery. Although the sample size (n=66) was adequate for non-inferiority analysis, larger multicentric studies with extended follow-up are warranted to enhance external validity. Variations in surgical expertise and postoperative care across centres may also influence generalizability. Nonetheless, the present findings provide valuable evidence supporting the short-term clinical feasibility of SBHC.

CONCLUSION

This randomized controlled trial provides comparative evidence between SBHC and DBHC for chronic subdural hematoma. Both techniques achieved comparable clinical and radiological outcomes, with SBHC showing non-inferior short-term results to DBHC. Although recurrence and ventilator duration were marginally higher with SBHC, differences were not statistically significant. Within its standardized and well-controlled framework, this study supports SBHC as a safe, less invasive, and effective alternative in appropriately selected patients, emphasizing the need for vigilant postoperative follow-up. SBHC may be a reasonable alternative in selected patients, though vigilance for recurrence is warranted.

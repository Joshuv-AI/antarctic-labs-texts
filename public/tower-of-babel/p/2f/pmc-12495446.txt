FAM111B enhances glycolysis and promotes metastasis of prostate cancer by upregulating LDHA
Authors: Qingliu He, Haoran Li, Yukun Cong, Kang Chen, Lulin Cheng, Fang Lv, Pu Zhang, Yunjie Ju, Zehao Yu, Jinyu Chen, Chuxiong Wang, Yarong Song, Xuechao Li, Liang Chen, Yifei Xing
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe poor prognosis of metastatic prostate cancer (PCa) poses a major burden on both patients and the healthcare system. FAM111 trypsin-like peptidase B (FAM111B) is related to the development and progression of a wide array of cancers, but its role in PCa remains poorly understood.MethodsPrimary cells were extracted from subcutaneous and pulmonary metastatic tumors and were used to verify differences in metastatic potential through wound healing assay, Transwell assay, soft agar colony formation assay, and in vivo pulmonary metastasis reformation assays. The key differentially expressed gene FAM111B related to metastatic prostate cancer (mPCa) was identified through transcriptomic combination analysis, proteomic analysis, quantitative real-time fluorescent polymerase chain reaction and western blot assays. The effect of FAM111B on the glycolytic capacity of PCa cells with high metastatic potential was analyzed by gene enrichment analysis, glucose uptake, lactate and ATP content measurement assays, including glycolytic stress test.ResultsFAM111B was highly expressed in metastatic PCa cells and associated with adverse clinical features, which upregulated LDHA to enhance glycolysis. Mechanistically, the expression of P27 was inhibited by a hydrolytic triad coded by the functional coding region of FAM111B, which activated Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F1 classical signaling pathway to promote the transcription and protein expression of LDHA.ConclusionsThe high expression of FAM111B is associated with adverse clinical features of PCa. FAM111B protein binds to and hydrolyzes P27 protein, which activates Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F1 signaling pathway to increase LDHA expression, thereby enhancing the glycolytic ability and ultimately promoting the metastasis of PCa and may potentially serve as new targets for the treatment of metastatic PCa.

Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is the second most common malignancy in men across the globe [1], and a leading cause of death among male cancer patients [2]. Due to the insidious onset and absence of obvious clinical symptoms in some patients, or the lack of timely PSA screening, approximately 5–8 % of patients present with symptoms such as lower urinary tract obstruction or bone pain at initial diagnosis, indicating metastatic disease. Metastatic prostate cancer (mPCa) is characterized by a rapid overall disease progression and poor survival [3]. Androgen deprivation-based treatment can slow the progression of mPCa to some extent [4], but the prognosis is still unsatisfactory. Globally, the 5-year specific survival rate of mPCa stood at only 30 % [5]. Additionally, metastasis-related symptoms, such as bone pain and pathological fractures, significantly affect the quality of life of the victims and the condition poses great burden on healthcare systems and the society at large. Therefore, it is clinically and economically urgent to understand the mechanisms of mPCa and identify therapeutic targets for the efficacious treatment of mPCa [6]. As we know it, the development and progression of cancer are closely related to glucose metabolism. The “Warburg effect” produces less ATP per molecule of glucose compared to more energy-efficient oxidative phosphorylation, but allows for faster ATP production to meet the high energy demand of cancer cells [7,8]. Simultaneously, lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) catalyzes the production of lactate, which acidifies the tumor microenvironment. This event, in combination with other factors such as hypoxia and signaling pathway abnormalities further enhances glycolysis [9]. Therefore, inhibiting glycolysis may well be a viable approach to the management of cancer with Warburg effect. Studies have demonstrated that elevated glycolysis level was associated with mutations or abnormal expression of gene in mPCa [10]. Thus, increasing glycolysis is an important marker of glucose metabolism in mPCa and could serve as a potential target for clinical intervention of the condition. Cancer cells survive in the vasculature after escaping the extracellular matrix, acquiring anoikis-resistance (AnoR) capability, which is seen as an important prerequisite for cancer metastasis. Our research team successfully developed a detachment-resistance model using prostate cancer cells PC-3 to mimic cancer cell AnoR in vitro. In the present study, we simulated the AnoR of cancer cells in vivo by injecting PC-3 cells via the tail vein of nude mice to induce formation of pulmonary metastatic tumors, and the subcutaneous tumor was generated by injecting PC-3 cells into the right axilla to create a non-AnoR model. The key differentially expressed gene FAM111B was identified by RNA sequencing of the two tumor models based on TCGA and GEO databases. The FAM111B gene, also known as CANP or POIKTMP gene, is located on human chromosome 11q12.1, and is associated with some rare multisystem fibrosis diseases [11]. The gene is upregulated in various cancers [12], such as malignancies in lung [13,14], breast [15], pancreas [16], stomach [17], among others, and elevated FAM111B expression is associated with the exacerbation and unfavorable outcomes of cancers. It is still unclear how FAM111B mediates glycolysis and PCa metastasis in PCa. In this study, we aimed to identify the key effector gene of glycolysis regulated by FAM111B, and further explored the relationships between them. We also investigated the impact of their mutual regulation on the glycolysis and metastatic potential of PCa cells to explore the role of FAM111B in the regulation of glycolysis.

Results

Identification of key upregulated gene FAM111B associated with prostate cancer metastasis

To look into the mechanism of PCa metastasis, we first separately constructed a subcutaneous tumor (ST) model and a metastatic tumor (MT) model in vivo (Fig. 1A). The formation of STs in the right forelimb axilla and MTs in the lungs was observed macroscopically (Fig. 1B). Then fluorescence imaging was captured by using multifunctional small animal imaging system, which exhibited fluorescence in the corresponding areas of ST and MT (Fig. 1C). HE staining revealed that cells in ST tissue formed nests and sheets with partial necrosis, whereas those in MT tissue had glandular small nests to compare with the normal lung of untreated mice (Fig. S1A). PCa cells were then extracted from the ST (PC3-S) and MT (PC3-M) for further investigation of their metastatic ability (Fig. S1B). Scratch assay revealed that, compared to PC3-S, the migration area of PC3-M increased (Fig. S1C). Transwell assay showed that the migration and invasion cells were more in PC3-M than in PC3-S (Fig. 1D). Soft agar colony formation assay demonstrated that the anchorage-independent cell colonies of PC3-M increased (Fig. 1E). Upon re-injection of PC3-S and PC3-M cells via tail vein into nude mice, PC3-M cells in nude mice were more likely to form new MT and have lower survival rates (Fig. 1F and S1D). These results indicated the metastatic ability of PC3-M was significantly enhanced compared to PC3-S cells, confirming the effectiveness of the ST and MT nude mice models in PCa research. PC3-M cells derived from MT were defined as PCa cells with high metastatic potential, whereas the cancer cells PC3-S derived from ST were defined as PCa cells with low metastatic potential. To explore the differences in the gene expression profiles of PCa cells with different metastatic potentials, total mRNA was extracted from 3 pairs of PC3-S and PC3-M cells for transcriptome sequencing (Fig. S1E). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened against the criteria of |Log2(FC)| > 1 and Padj < 0.05, and 2166 eligible genes were identified (Fig. 1G). Applying the same criteria, we then searched the TCGA-PRAD and GSE3325 transcriptome datasets, identifying the differences in gene expression between mPCa and localized PCa (lPCa). The differentially expressed genes from these three datasets were divided into a high-expression group (Log2(FC) > 1, red) and a low-expression group (Log2(FC) < −1, blue) (Fig. 1H). An intersection of two different expression groups yielded four upregulated genes and five downregulated genes (Fig. 1I). The correlation between the expression levels of these nine genes and progression-free survival of PCa patients yielded four clinically significant genes, including two upregulated ones (FAM111B and ADAMTS2) and two downregulated ones (FAM189A2 and GCNT2) (Fig. 1J and S1F). qRT-PCR showed that the transcription level of FAM111B was significantly higher in PC3-M than in PC3-S (Fig. 1K). Analysis of the PXD009868 proteomic dataset revealed that protein expression of FAM111B was higher in mPCa patients than in their localized PCa counterparts (Fig. 1L). Additionally, western blotting showed that the protein expression of FAM111B was significantly higher in PC3-M than in PC3-S (Fig. 1M and S1G). These results demonstrated that FAM111B was upregulated in PCa cells with high metastatic potential and mPCa patients, and might be a key differentially expressed gene associated with PCa metastasis.Fig. 1Identification of the key upregulated differentially expressed gene FAM111B associated with PCa metastasis. Elevated expression of FAM111B is associated with adverse clinical features and high metastatic potential in PCa patients

To examine the correlation between FAM111B and pathological grades, clinical stages and survival of PCa patients, the TCGA-PRAD and GSE3325 transcriptome datasets were searched. It was found that high expression of FAM111B was positively correlated with clinical distant metastasis (M), pathological lymph node metastasis (N), advanced clinical and pathological tumor stage (T), high Gleason score, residual tumor (R) and progressive disease after primary therapy (PD) (Fig. 2A). Subsequently, primary lesions from 10 PCa patients with negative lymph node metastasis (N0), and 10 with positive lymph node metastasis (N1) were subjected to western blotting. Higher FAM111B protein level was seen in PCa patients with N1 compared to those with N0 (Fig. 2B). Additionally, N1 tissue samples had more intense nuclear staining of FAM111B (Fig. 2C) and higher average optical density values (Fig. 2D) in IHC images. These results indicated that high expression of FAM111B was associated with adverse clinical features in PCa patients. To further investigate the effect of FAM111B on the metastatic potential of PC3-M cells, short hairpin RNA was utilized to silence FAM111B expression. The significant reduction at both transcriptional and protein levels of FAM111B was validated by qRT-PCR and western blotting (Fig. 2E). Silencing FAM111B in PC3-M cells reduced their migration, invasion, and anchorage-independent capabilities (Fig. 2F-H and S2A-C). While ectopic expression of FAM111B in PC3-S cells can promote cell growth, migration and metastasis ability in vivo (Fig. S2D-G). LDHA is a key effector gene of the glycolysis pathway promoted by FAM111B in prostate cancer

To investigate the mechanism underlying FAM111B-mediated promotion of PCa cell metastasis, we conducted a gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) on the TCGA-PRAD transcriptome dataset and found that FAM111B was positively correlated with the glycolysis pathway (Fig. 3A and S3A). Considering the metabolic uniqueness of PCa, glycolysis is an important glucose metabolism phenotype in mPCa and a critical factor implicated in the progression and metastasis of PCa [10]. Studies on glucose uptake, lactate and ATP levels, and glycolysis stress revealed that silencing FAM111B in PC3-M cells led to reduced glucose uptake, lowered lactate and ATP levels, and decreased extracellular acidification rate (Fig. 3B), suggesting that FAM111B might promote PCa metastasis by regulating glycolysis. Then, we tried to find the key effector genes associated with the glycolysis pathway regulated by FAM111B in PCa. Upon the analysis of correlation between FAM111B and the key enzymes and transporter genes of the glycolysis pathway in the TCGA-PRAD transcriptome dataset, 11 key genes were identified (Fig. 3C). Further analysis of the correlation between the expression levels of these key genes and the progression-free survival of PCa patients revealed 2 significantly correlated genes (LDHA and SLC2A4) (Fig. 3D and S3B). Additionally, qRT-PCR found that, in PC3-M cells, the transcription level of LDHA was significantly decreased after FAM111B silencing, whereas the transcription level of SLC2A4 remained unchanged (Fig. 3E). Finally, western blot assay spotted a decrease of LDHA protein expression in PC3-M cells after FAM111B silencing, and an increase in PC3-S cells following FAM111B overexpression (Fig. 3F). These results indicated that LDHA is a key effector gene of the glycolysis pathway promoted by FAM111B in PCa cells. Analysis revealed that high expression of LDHA was positively correlated with clinical M stage and pathological N stage, advanced clinical and pathological T stage, high Gleason score, and PD after initial treatment (Fig. 3G). Furthermore, western blot assay on primary PCa tissues showed stronger LDHA protein expression in patients with N1 compared to that of N0 (Fig. 3H). FAM111B upregulates LDHA to enhance glycolysis and promote metastasis of PCa cells

In order to explore the regulatory relationship between FAM111B and LDHA, as well as their effects on glycolysis and metastatic potential in PCa cells, we overexpressed LDHA and silenced FAM111B separately, and silenced FAM111B while overexpressing LDHA in PC3-M cells, western blotting showed that overexpression of LDHA in PC3-M exerted no significant effect on the protein expression of FAM111B but could restore the decreased expression of LDHA protein caused by silencing of FAM111B (Fig. 4A). Subsequent experiments found that overexpression of LDHA could increase glucose uptake, lactate and ATP levels and enhance extracellular acidification rate, which could also restore the decreased glucose uptake, lactate and ATP levels and reduced extracellular acidification rate caused by silencing of FAM111B (Fig. 4B). Moreover, overexpression of LDHA enhanced cell migration, invasion and anchorage-independent capabilities, also restored those weakened capabilities mentioned above caused by silencing of FAM111B (Fig. 4C-E). Additionally, overexpression of LDHA in PC3-M cells could promote the formation of MT in nude mice, which lowered their survival rate, and could reverse the effects of reduced MT formation and increased survival rate caused by silencing of FAM111B (Fig. 4F). On the other hand, in PC3-S cells, we overexpressed FAM111B and silenced LDHA, and similar patterns of changes in expression levels and functions were observed (Fig. S4A-F). FAM111B activates the Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F1/LDHA signaling pathway by inhibiting P27

Prior research shown that FAM111B could inhibit the expression of CKI proteins [18]. The classical downstream pathway of CKIs is the Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F signaling axis, and E2F can indirectly regulate LDHA to promote glycolysis [19]. Therefore, we were led to speculate that FAM111B might promote LDHA expression by inhibiting CKIs and activating the Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F signaling pathway (Fig. 5A). We silenced FAM111B in PC3-M cells and overexpressed FAM111B in PC3-S cells and observed changes in expression of proteins involved in this pathway. Western blot assay showed that silencing FAM111B in PC3-M cells led to insignificant changes in P57, P21 and P16 protein levels, significantly increased P27 and RB protein level, decreased Cyclin D1/E1/A1, CDK2/4/6 and phosphorylated RB protein levels, while FAM111B overexpression in PC3-S cells brought about opposite trends (Fig. 5B). What’s more, the effects of CDK2 inhibitor (CVT-313) or CDK4/6 inhibitor (Abemaciclib) in PC3-M cells were performed to observe those changes (Fig. S5A). Search of the PXD009868 proteomics dataset showed that the expression of P27 protein was significantly lower in mPCa patients than in patients with localized PCa (Fig. 5C), and it was negatively correlated with the expression of FAM111B protein (Fig. 5D). The aforementioned results indicated that FAM111B could activate the Cyclin-CDKs/RB signaling pathway by inhibiting P27. E2F can bind to the promoter regions of downstream genes, regulating the transcriptional expression of downstream genes. However, it has not been reported whether E2F directly regulates LDHA transcription by binding to RB. GSEA conducted on LDHA in the TCGA-PRAD transcriptome dataset revealed that LDHA could be enriched in E2F targets with positive correlation (Fig. 5E). Further analysis revealed a positive correlation between LDHA and E2F1, E2F2, E2F3, E2F4, E2F5 (Fig. S5B), but only E2F1 and E2F2 showed statistically significant correlation with progression-free survival in PCa patients (Fig. 5F and S5C). DLRG assay exhibited that only E2F1 overexpression could incrementally enhance the relative transcriptional activity of the LDHA-Fluc-Wt promoter in HEK293T, PC3-S and PC3-M (Fig. 5G), while overexpression of E2F2 failed to do so (Fig. S5D). ChIP assay also confirmed that E2F1 could bind to LDHA promoter in HEK293T, PC3-S and PC3-M cells (Fig. 5H). The TFBS of LDHA promoter for E2F1 to bind was predicted by searching JASPAR database. Four possible TFBS sequences located at position −1587, −960, −887 and −783. Correspondingly, four LDHA promoter gene plasmids containing one mutant sequence with Fluc was constructed and was designated Mt (Mutant Type) 1/2/3/4 (Fig. 5I). We found that the transcriptional activation of LDHA enhanced by E2F1 was attenuated only by Mt2 transfection (Fig. 5J). To further substantiate the regulatory role of FAM111B and P27 in LDHA transcription enhanced by E2F1, we separately overexpressed FAM111B, P27 and E2F1 in PC3-M cells. The results showed that the overexpression of FAM111B or E2F1 significantly elevated the relative transcriptional activity of the LDHA promoter and mutually promoted each other’s effects, both being attenuated by P27 overexpression. Overexpression of P27 significantly reduced the relative transcriptional activity of the LDHA promoter, which could be restored by overexpression of E2F1 or FAM111B. Simultaneous overexpression of FAM111B, P27 and E2F1 led to a significant increase in the relative transcriptional activity of the LDHA promoter (Fig. 5K). To verify the mutually-regulating relationship between FAM111B, P27, E2F1 and LDHA, these genes were regulated in PC3-M or PC3-S cells to observe respective changes. Western blotting showed that silencing FAM111B in PC3-M was associated with upregulated P27 protein and downregulated E2F1 and LDHA proteins. Meanwhile overexpression of FAM111B in PC3-S yielded diametrically opposite changes. Overexpression of P27 in PC3-M did not alter FAM111B protein expression, but caused significant decrease in E2F1 and LDHA proteins. Silencing P27 in PC3-S was accompanied by a reverse change in E2F1 and LDHA. Silencing or upregulating E2F1 did not affect FAM111B and P27 protein expression, but significantly decreased or increased LDHA protein expression. The FAM111B protein binds to the P27 protein through protein-protein interactions and possesses catalytic triad hydrolase activity to inhibit its expression

To elucidate the regulatory mechanism of FAM111B on P27, we first looked into the affinity of the two proteins. Immunofluorescence experiments showed that the two proteins were mostly co-localized in the nucleus, with only a small amount of P27 protein in the cytoplasm (Fig. 6A). This co-localization spatially allows for protein interaction. Subsequent Co-IP showed that FAM111B and P27 could co-precipitate in PC3-M, but the expression levels of the corresponding co-precipitated proteins were low (Fig. 6B). Given the protein hydrolyzing effect of FAM111B, we theorized that FAM111B might hydrolyze P27 after binding to the protein. Then, Flag-FAM111B-ΔT plasmid was constructed by truncating the functional coding region of FAM111B (Tryspin_2, corresponding to amino acids 475-664) for the confirmation of the assumption (Fig. 6C). Western blotting showed that when FAM111B was silenced in PC3-M cells, the overexpression of Flag-FAM111B-ΔT led to a significant decrease in the molecular weight of the Flag compared to the overexpression of Flag-FAM111B, indicating the molecular weight was reduced by truncation. Meanwhile, a significant increase in P27 protein level was seen (Fig. 6D). Subsequent Co-IP revealed that when FAM111B was silenced in PC3-M cells, Flag-FAM111B-ΔT could significantly co-precipitate with the P27 protein (Fig. 6E). These results indicated that there existed an endogenous interaction between FAM111B and P27 proteins in PCa cells with high metastatic potential. Furthermore, to verify the exogenous protein-protein interaction between FAM111B and P27 proteins, FAM111B and P27 were simultaneously silenced and Myc-P27 was overexpressed in human embryonic kidney cells. Then Flag, Flag-FAM111B and Flag-FAM111B-ΔT were overexpressed separately. Co-IP showed that both Flag-FAM111B and Flag-FAM111B-ΔT could co-precipitate with Myc-P27, but the corresponding co-precipitated protein levels in the Flag-FAM111B-ΔT group were higher than in the other groups (Fig. 6F). The amino acid sequence coded by the functional coding region of human FAM111B contains catalytic triad hydrolase sites consisting of H490, D544 and S650 (Fig. 6G). Protein structure data of FAM111B were retrieved from the Alphafold Protein Structure Database and imported into the PyMOL system to predict the spatial structure of amino acids sequence in hydrolase sites. Visualization of the hydrolase sites revealed that although H490, D544 and S650 were far apart in sequence, they were adjacent in the folded structure, forming a conserved catalytic triad (Fig. 6H). Subsequent predictions of the spatial structures for the amino acids after mutations at the hydrolase sites H490N, D544N and S650N showed that the mutated hydrolase sites exhibited an outward orientation, losing the original structural features (Fig. 6I). To further investigate whether mutations in the hydrolase sites affect the hydrolase activity of FAM111B, we overexpressed the H490N, D544N and S650N Mt in PC3-M cells with FAM111B silenced. Western blotting showed that, compared to the FAM111B Wt, the three FAM111B Mts all significantly increased the expression of P27 protein, and significantly decreased the expression of E2F1 and LDHA proteins. S650N Mt had the most significant effect on the expression of the P27 protein (Fig. 6J). We also found that the ectopic expression of WT but not the p27 binding deficient mutants of FAM111B in PC3-S cells could promote cell growth, migration and metastasis ability in vivo (Fig. S7A-D).

In conclusion, at the molecular level, this research revealed that the FAM111B protein binds and hydrolyzes the P27 protein to inhibit its expression, thereby activating the Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F1 signaling pathway and upregulating LDHA transcription and protein expression, eventually enhancing glycolysis and promoting the metastasis of PCa cells (Fig. 7A).Fig. 7Schematic representation of the mechanism by which the FAM111B protein binds to and hydrolyzes P27 protein and then inhibits its expression, which activates the Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F1/LDHA signaling pathway, thereby enhancing glycolysis and promoting the migration of PCa cells.Fig. 7

Discussion

mPCa is the terminal stage of prostate cancer and the main cause of death in patients [20]. Its genomic heterogeneity significantly influences the drug resistance and disease progression [21]. Exploring the changes in genes involved in the progression of mPCa may provide clues to new strategies of its treatment. FAM111B is up-regulated in a wide array of cancers and significantly associated with poor prognosis of the malignancies, serving as an independent predictor for overall survival [22]. This research, for the first time, confirmed that FAM111B is an up-regulated gene that was associated with PCa metastasis, and that its expression was increased in clinical specimens of mPCa and PCa cells with high metastatic potential. Its high expression was positively correlated with clinical adverse features of PCa, such as advanced stages and grades, and poor prognosis. Similarly, high FAM111B expression was reportedly associated with the invasive phenotype, poor clinical prognosis and adverse pathological characteristics of ovarian cancer [23]. We observed that, silencing FAM111B could inhibit the migration and invasion of PC-3-M cells. In line with our findings, a recent study on bladder cancer has also showed that down-regulating FAM111B could inhibit the migration of bladder cancer cells [24]. In pancreatic cancer, high expression of FAM111B was closely related to poor prognosis of cancer patients and could promote the proliferation, invasion and migration of pancreatic cancer cells [25].

Key enzymes and transporters with high expression during the glycolysis can enhance the glycolytic phenotype, promoting cancer progression and metastasis [26], while the suppression of them result in the diametrically opposite effects on PCa [27], such as hexokinase 2 [28], pyruvate kinase M2 [29], LDHA [30,31] and glucose transporter 1 [32]. Currently, few studies examined the relationship between FAM111B and key enzymes and transporters of glycolysis in PCa, with only one study reporting FAM111B's relation to phosphoglycerate kinase 1 in thyroid cancer [33]. This research found that FAM111B was positively correlated with glycolysis pathway, and LDHA was identified as the key effector gene that increased glycolysis in PCa cells due to the regulation of FAM111B. LDHA overexpression can promote the development and progression of PCa [34], and is associated with poor prognosis of PCa [35], resistance to cancer treatment and metastasis [36]. Therefore, targeting LDHA-related glycolysis is deemed to be a potential therapeutic strategy for mPCa [37]. This research also found that high LDHA expression was associated with adverse clinical features of PCa, as well as the high metastatic potential of PCa, the findings being coincident with the results of previous studies [16,38]. In addition, the progression and metastasis of PCa were reported to be significantly associated with enhanced glycolysis [39]. This research found that FAM111B, acting as an upstream gene, regulated LDHA protein expression, enhanced glycolysis levels and promoted in vivo and in vitro metastasis of PCa cells. LDHA could reverse the effects of FAM111B, which suggests that targeting FAM111B to inhibit LDHA may be a potential strategy for the treatment of mPCa.

Previous research showed that the FAM111B protein could promote the formation and activity of Cyclin-CDK complexes by inhibiting CKIs, which would lead to RB phosphorylation and cause the release and active expression of E2F, promoting cell cycle progression and resulting in cancer progression and metastasis [18,40,41]. For example, FAM111B can inhibit P16 protein in lung cancer cells [18], P21 protein in liver cancer cells and P21 and P27 proteins in ovarian cancer cells [40,41]. Moreover, studies also found that high expression of FAM111B was associated with poor prognosis and survival in pancreatic cancer patients, possibly because it inhibited the development and progression of pancreatic cancer by regulating CKIs [42]. For the first time, this research demonstrated that FAM111B significantly affected the expression of the P27 protein in PCa cells, with no significant effect found on other CKIs, such as P16, P21 and P57, and further affects the expression of proteins in the Cyclin-CDKs/RB signaling pathway, which is consistent with previous results obtained in ovarian cancer [40]. Search of the PXD009868 proteomic dataset also confirmed the down-regulated expression of the P27 protein in mPCa, which was negatively correlated with the expression of the FAM111B protein. Results at tissue and cellular levels indicated that FAM111B in PCa could inhibit the expression of the P27 protein.

E2F is a family of transcription factors, whose high expression is associated with poor prognosis of cancer [43], serving as a specific diagnostic marker for cancer [44]. E2F has multiple isoforms, among which only E2F1, E2F2, E2F3, E2F4, E2F5 can bind to RB [43]. Abnormalities in the Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F signaling pathway can cause aberrant expression of E2F and reprogramming of glucose metabolism, regulating cancer glycolysis and lactate production, finally affecting cancer progression [45]. It has been reported that, in PC-3 cells, knocking out RB could regulate the expression of E2F1 and E2F3 [46]. Glycolysis-promoted cancer growth and metastasis is enhanced by E2F3 in thyroid cancer [47], E2F2 in gliomas [48] and E2F1 in bladder cancer [49]. Currently, no evidence showed that E2F directly regulates LDHA. It was reported that E2F1 indirectly regulated LDHA to promote glycolysis in laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma [19]. Considering that PCa cells tend to undergo glycolysis in the S phase [50], and that E2F promotes progression from G0/G1 to S phase, it was speculated that E2F may regulate LDHA in PCa to enhance glycolysis. This research found that LDHA could be enriched in E2F targets and they were positively correlated, supporting this hypothesis. It was also found that only E2F1 could enhance the transcriptional activity of the LDHA promoter, and the TFBS 2 (GAGGCAGGAGA) was a key binding site of LDHA for E2F1 in PC-3-M cells. This research, for the first time, demonstrated that FAM111B inhibited P27 expression and activated the Cyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F1 signaling pathway, thereby upregulating LDHA protein levels.

P27 is a tumor suppressor encoded by the CDKN1B gene and can inhibit the formation of Cyclin-CDK complexes [51]. Abnormal changes, such as excessive proteolysis or phosphorylation of P27 protein, promote cancer progression and metastasis [52]. Since FAM111B can hydrolyze CKI proteins [18], it is speculated that FAM111B inhibits P27 protein expression through its proteolytic activity. Immunofluorescence found that the FAM111B protein is predominantly distributed in the cell nucleus, and the finding was consistent with previous research [53]. Similar subcellular location of P27 protein suggests that FAM111B and P27 proteins may interact in the cell nucleus. Co-IP of endogenous and exogenous proteins demonstrated that FAM111B could bind to the P27 protein, and the former could hydrolyze the latter, which could fully explain the speculated interaction between FAM111B and the P27 protein. In fact, FAM111B was found to bind to P16, and FAM111B-ΔT could increase the expression of the P16 protein [18].

The functional coding region of FAM111B contains a catalytic triad consisting of three important hydrolytic enzyme sites, i.e., H490, D544 and S650, which afford stable protein hydrolyzing ability due to their conservation [54]. The predicted spatial structure of their amino acids was also consistent with the conserved folded structure of the classical catalytic triad [55]. Mutated H490N, D544N and S650N resulted in the loss of the stable folded structure, which may lead to the inactivation of the hydrolytic enzyme activity of the FAM111B protein. Abnormal amino acid mutations can affect the molecular function and cellular adaptability. For instance, the H490 mutation of the hepatitis C virus could reduce the binding of the virus to the receptor entering the cell and impair its infectivity [56]. The S650 mutation of androgen receptor could avoid phosphorylation by JNK and P38 and promote AR nuclear translocation [57]. The D544 mutation of FAM111B could eliminate the effect on DNA synthesis, transcription, and microtubules [54]. In this research, compared to FAM111B-Wt, FAM111B-Mt (H490N, D544N and S650N) upregulated the expression of the P27 protein and inhibited the expression of E2F1 and LDHA proteins, confirming that FAM111B inhibits the P27 protein through its protease activity imparted by hydrolytic enzyme sites, which was coincident with previous findings [18]. Additionally, FAM111B-Wt-S650N significantly enhanced the expression of P27 protein, which was higher than that of the other two FAM111B-Wt, possibly due to the inability of the mutated serine to exert the most important hydrolytic enzyme function [58].

This research found that FAM111B is upregulated in mPCa and experimental nude mice MT. Its high expression is associated with adverse clinical features in PCa patients and higher metastatic potential, rendering it a potential new biomarker for PCa prognosis. This study, for the first time, suggested that FAM111B promotes PCa metastasis by upregulating LDHA to enhance glycolytic capacity and delved into the molecular mechanism. It was confirmed that FAM111B hydrolyzes and inhibits P27 protein through protein interaction in high metastatic potential PCa cells, thereby activating the downstream Clyclin-CDKs/RB/E2F1 signaling pathway and promoting the transcription and protein expression of LDHA. This mechanism provides a potential novel strategy for the treatment of mPCa.

Materials and methods

Cell lines and cell culture

PCa cell line PC-3 and human embryonic kidney cell line HEK293T were purchased from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. They were cultured in RPMI-1640 complete medium with 10 % FBS and 1 % penicillin/streptomycin in a thermostatic cell incubator (37°C and 5 % CO2). All cell lines were confirmed within 6 months before use by using a short tandem repeat profiling and were confirmed negative for Mycoplasma contamination.

Establishment of mice models of PCa subcutaneous tumor (ST) and pulmonary metastatic tumor (MT)

Four-week-old male athymic BALB/c nude mice were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. A sufficient number of PC-3 cells at logarithmic phase were collected and injected into the nude mice. ST model was established by injecting 1 × 106 cells into the right axilla and MT model was made by intravenously injecting the cells into the tail vein. 60 days after injection, the mice were sacrificed, and the ST and MT tissues were harvested. Gross images were obtained and then observed for tumor fluorescence on a multifunctional small animal imaging system. If the mice with pulmonary metastatic tumor died during the experiment, the same procedures were repeated. Fluorescence images and survival time were combined to draw survival curves.

Hematoxylin and Eosin (HE) Staining

ST and MT tissues were fixed and subjected to routine processing including dehydration, embedding, sectioning, deparaffinization, hematoxylin staining, eosin staining, dehydration, and sealing with cover slips for HE staining. Three experienced pathologists assisted in the interpretation of results.

Extraction of PC3-M and PC3-S Cells

ST and MT tissues were cleaned and disinfected with 2 % penicillin/streptomycin solution, thoroughly minced and then incubated in 2 mg/ml collagenase I at 37°C to enzymatically dissociate tissues into single cells. After centrifugation and discarding of the supernatant, the cells were resuspended, cultured and passaged regularly to obtain PC3-S and PC3-M cells.

Wound Healing Assay

Cells were seeded into 6-well plates and vertically scratched with a 1 mL pipette tip in a "+"-shaped pattern from 0 to 6 o'clock direction, 3 to 9 o’clock directions when cells reached a confluence of roughly 90 %. After gentle washing to remove detached cells, the same scratch spots were photographed at 0 h and 24 h while the cells being cultured in serum-free medium.

Cell migration and invasion assays

Cell migration assay: Fresh complete culture medium (600 µL) was added to the wells of a 24-well plate, and Transwell chambers were placed, with 200 µL of cell suspension added to the upper chamber. After a 24-hour culture, the cells in the upper chamber were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for half an hour, and then stained with 1 % crystal violet for another 30 minutes. The cells on the upper chamber were gently wiped off with a cotton swab. After washing and drying, migrated cells attached to the underside of the chamber membrane were photographed under an inverted phase-contrast microscope. Cell invasion assay: The upper chamber of the Transwell chamber was coated with suitable concentration of Matrigel matrix gel (100 µL). The rest of procedures were the same as those of the cell migration experiment except that matrix gel was removed before fixation.

Soft Agar Colony Formation Assay

1.2 % agarose solution was sterilized by autoclaving. Upon cooling down, it was diluted with 2 × fresh complete culture medium to form 0.6 % and 0.3 % agarose culture media. 0.6 % agarose culture medium (2 ml/well) was evenly spread onto a 6-well plate. Cells were mixed with 2 × fresh complete culture medium and the same volume of 0.3 % agarose culture medium to form a double-layer agarose in the 6-well plate. 200 µL fresh complete culture medium was added every 2 days to prevent the agarose from drying out. After 2 weeks, double-layer agarose was gently washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and stained with methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium in the dark, and the formed cell clones were photographed.

Transcriptome Sequencing (RNA-Seq)

PC3-M and PC3-S cells were cultured routinely until sufficient amounts of cells (in triplicates) were collected and sent to HippoLoS (Shenzhen, China) for second-generation RNA-Seq.

Transcriptome and proteome database analysis

Transcriptome databases were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas Program (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Proteome databases were obtained from ProteomeXchange (http://www.proteomexchange.org).

Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (qRT-PCR)

Cells were lysed by adding TRIzol Reagent for RNA extraction by following the protocol of an ultra-pure RNA extraction kit. The cDNA templates were generated through RNA reverse transcription using the HiScript III RT SuperMix for qPCR kit (+gDNA wiper). The required primer sequences (Supplementary Table 1) were synthesized by Sangon Biotech company. The qRT-PCR was performed on the StepOnePlus™ Quantitative real-time PCR system under the set conditions after the reaction system prepared with the ChamQ SYBR qPCR Master Mix kit.

Clinical Sample Collection

The approval of the research was obtained from the hospital's ethics committee and the PCa tissue specimens were taken from 60 male patients treated in the Urology Department of the Union Hospital affiliated to Tongji Medical College under the guidance of hospital pathologists from 2016 to 2022. All patients underwent radical prostatectomy and extended lymph node dissection. Tissue specimens were divided into several pieces and stored in 4 % paraformaldehyde solution or liquid nitrogen for subsequent immunohistochemical staining or tissue protein extraction. All patients signed an informed consent form, and all procedures were carried out in accordance with the Helsinki Declaration.

Western blotting

Total protein was extracted from collected cells and PCa specimens. Protein concentration was measured with BCA protein assay kit to determine the loading volume of proteins samples. The required concentration of separating gel and stacking gel were prepared by using the SDS-PAGE gel preparation kit. Samples and markers were added according to the experimental protocol to perform constant-voltage electrophoresis followed by a transfer. After being blocked, primary and secondary antibody incubation, PVDF membrane was washed and targeted protein stripes were detected through enhanced chemiluminescence. The following primary antibodies were used:FAM111B (29407-1-AP), β-actin (20536-1-AP), P27 (25614-1-Ig), P21 (10355-1-AP), P57 (23317-1-AP), CDK6 (14052-1-AP), CDK2 (22060-1-AP), Cyclin D1 (26939-1-AP), Cyclin E1 (11554-1-AP), RB (10048-2-Ig), Phospho-RB (30376-1-AP), Flag (66008-4-Ig) and Myc (16286-1-AP) were procured from Proteintech, and E2F1(A2067), P16 (A11651) were from Abclonal, CDK4 (HY-P80077), Cyclin A1 (HY-P80631), and LDHA (ab52488, Abcam) were purchased from MedChemExpress. The following secondary antibodies were used: HRP-conjugated Affinipure Goat Anti-Mouse antibody (SA00001-1) and anti-rabbit antibody (SA00001-2) were purchased from Proteintech.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The pulmonary metastatic tumor tissues were subjected to the following IHC procedures: fixation, dehydration, paraffin-embedding, sectioning, dewaxing, washing, antigen repair, endogenous peroxidase blocking, blocking, FAM111B/LDHA primary antibody incubation, secondary antibody incubation, DAB color development, nucleus re-staining, dehydration and section sealing. Slides were scanned and images were imported into ImagePro Plus 6.0 software for measurement of the mean density. FAM111B (NBP1-86645, NOVUS) and LDHA (19987-1-AP, Proteintech) were used as primary antibodies, and HRP-conjugated Affinipure Goat Anti-Rabbit antibody (ab205718, Abcam) served as secondary antibodies.

Plasmid transfection

The plasmids were extracted with the endotoxin-free plasmid extraction kit. Opti-MEM serum-free culture medium, plasmid and NeofectTM DNA transfection reagent were mixed at a fixed ratio to form the plasmid transfection complex for the transfection of the target cells. The qRT-PCR or western blotting were performed after 48-72 hours for verification. Plasmid vector-specific antibiotics were used to treat transfected cells to screen out stably-transfected cell lines. qRT-PCR and western blot experiments were re-performed after 4 weeks to verify that the cells were stably transfected. The following plasmids were used: FAM111Boe, FAM111Bsh1, FAM111Bsh2, E2F1oe, Flag, Flag-FAM111B, Flag-FAM111B-ΔT were constructed by Paivi Biosciences Inc. (Wuhan, China), and P27oe, P27sh1, P27sh2, LDHAsh1, LDHAsh2, E2F1sh1, E2F1sh2, Myc-P27 were obtained from MiaoLingBio (Wuhan, China), and LDHAoe, Firefly luciferase (FLuc) LDHA-promoter wild type (Wt) and Renilla luciferase TK were constructed by GeneChem (Shanghai, China), and Fluc-LDHA-promoter mutant type (Mt) 1/2/3/4 and FAM111B Mt (H490N, D544N and S650N) were constructed by General Biol Co. Ltd (Anhui, China).

Glucose Uptake assay

Absorbance (Abs) was measured at 630 nm by using a glucose assay kit (Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, which was substituted into the standard curve to obtain the corresponding glucose concentration value. The glucose uptake (mg) = (Glucose concentration value of fresh culture medium - Glucose concentration value of the sample) × 2/ 100.

Determination of Lactate Content

Abs was measured at 530 nm by using the lactate test kit according to its protocols provided by the manufacturer. The lactate concentration was calculated by the formula: (mM) = [(Abssample – Absdouble distilled water) / (Absstandard - Absdouble distilled water)] × 3 × N (dilution ratio).

Measurements of cellular ATP levels

Abs was measured with luminometer function by using an enhanced ATP assay kit (Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absolute Abssample = Abssample - AbsATP lysis, which was input into the standard curve to obtain the corresponding ATP concentration (μM).

Glycolysis stress test

Glycolysis stress test report containing glycolysis-related parameters were measured on the Seahorse XFe24 system by using the Seahorse XF RPMI detection kit (Agilent), including Seahorse XF24 RPMI test solution set, calibration solution, cell culture plate and probe plate device, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In addition, the glucose, oligomycin and 2-DG were adjusted with RPMI test solution at a 10X concentration and added into corresponding wells A, B, C of the probe plate according to the protocols.

Dual luciferase reporter gene (DLRG) assay

The Abs was measured by using DLRG assay kit by following the manufacturer’s instructions (Beyotime Biotechnology, RG027, China). The relative luciferase activity was calculated as the ratio of Firefly Luciferase Abs to Renilla Luciferase Abs.

Chromatin Immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

A ChIP kit was used, and DNA fragments were purified by using a DNA purification kit for qRT-PCR. The primary antibodies E2F1(A2067) and IgG (30000-0-AP) were purchased from Abclonal.

Immunofluorescence staining

Cells were seeded onto a 3-cm glass-bottom culture dish at a density of 2 × 105 cells. Then, the cells were washed twice with PBS and fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde on ice for 30 minutes. After three washes with PBS, cells were permeabilized with 0.5 % Triton X-100 at room temperature for 20 minutes. Following three washes with PBS, cells were blocked with 1 % BSA for 30 minutes, and then incubated with the primary antibody at an appropriate concentration overnight at 4°C. The next day, cells were washed three times with PBS containing 1 % tween-20 buffer (PBST) and incubated with the corresponding fluorescent-labeled secondary antibodies for 1 hour. Cells were washed three times with PBST buffer, stained with DAPI to label the nuclei for 30 minutes, and then washed three times with PBST buffer in the dark. Finally, fluorescent images of cell staining were obtained by using a Nikon laser scanning confocal microscope. The primary antibodies FAM111B (29407-1-AP, Rabbit) and P27 (67355-1-Ig, Mouse), and the secondary antibodies CoraLite488-conjugated Goat Anti-Rabbit IgG (H + L) (SA00013-2) and CoraLite594-conjugated Goat Anti-Mouse IgG (H + L) (SA00013-3) were all from Proteintech.

Co-Immunoprecipitation (Co-IP)

The collected cells were resuspended in NP-40 lysis buffer containing 100 mM PMSF and protease inhibitor cocktail and incubated on ice for 1 hour. The supernatant containing IgG antibody solution and Protein A/G magnetic beads were rotated/vortexed at 4°C for 2 hours to remove non-specific protein binding. The protein magnetic beads were separated using a magnetic rack, and the supernatant was divided into the Input, IgG, and target protein samples. The IgG and target protein samples were rotated and incubated overnight with the corresponding IgG and target protein primary antibodies at 4°C. The next day, Protein A/G Magnetic Beads were added and the resultant samples were rotated for 3 hours at 4°C. The magnetic rack was used to separate the protein magnetic beads and supernatant, and the latter was protein samples. Western blot assay was performed on the three protein samples. Those primary antibodies FAM111B (29407-1-AP), P27 (25614-1-Ig), IgG (30000-0-AP), Flag (66008-4-Ig) and Myc (16286-1-AP) were bought from Proteintech.

Truncation or mutation of amino acids

The functional coding regions of the human FAM111B gene were truncated by FAM111B-ΔT plasmids. PyMOL software was used to visualize the FAM111B protein PDB file, obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database, simulating the spatial structure of the FAM111B protein, and predicting the spatial structure of the amino acids H490, D544 and S650 in the catalytic triad hydrolase site of the FAM111B protein. Furthermore, the mutated spatial structure of the amino acids H490N, D544N and S650N constructed by GENERAL BIOL was also predicted.

Prediction of Transcription Factor Binding Site (TFBS) sequence

TFBS sequences were predicted through JASPAR database (https://jaspar.elixir.no).

Image drawing

The construction diagram of nude mice models was obtained from the SMART material library (https://smart.servier.com/).

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was conducted independently and repeated at least three times. Numerical values were summarized and analyzed in Excel and imported into GraphPad Prism 8.0.2 for processing and statistical analysis, expressed as mean ± standard deviation and plotted. Statistical analyses included Student’s t-test for comparing two independent samples and One-way ANOVA test for comparing multiple samples, with P < 0.05 indicating statistically significant differences. Transcriptome and proteome datasets were processed by using R language software, and Pearson method was employed for correlation analysis and survival curve plotting.

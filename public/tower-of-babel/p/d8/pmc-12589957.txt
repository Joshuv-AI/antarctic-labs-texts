Contamination, molecular characterization and virulence of Staphylococcus aureus isolated from pork at cold storage in Hangzhou, China
Authors: Lei Lei, Yilun Liang, Ruidong Zhai, Xinlu Xu, Qin Wu, Yue Han, Simin Deng, Jiali Xu, Jing Xia, Yongchun Yang, Luca Simone Cocolin, Kalliopi Rantsiou, Houhui Song, Changyong Cheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractStaphylococcus aureus has emerged as a growing concern in food safety due to its high environment adaptability in low temperature. While cold-chain storage makes food last longer and slows down growth of most microorganisms, it simultaneously selects cold-tolerant pathogens like S. aureus stay alive throughout the food chain. However, systematic and region-specific investigations into the contamination and characterize of S. aureus isolates from pork stored in cold storage warehouses remain scarce. A total of 344 samples were collected in 23 cold storage facilities for S. aureus detection in Zhejiang Province, China. Finally, 52 isolates were obtained from 40 samples (11.3 %), representing from 13 different cold storage warehouses. Notably, 27 (51.9 %) of the isolates were enterotoxigenic, and 40 (76.9 %) exhibited multi-drug resistance with strong biofilm-forming capacity; among them, 13 were identified as methicillin-resistant S. aureus. Furthermore, all 52 isolates were grouped into 10 Random Amplified Polymorphic DNA clusters and 13 spa types, including the newly prevalent type spa-t20663. The most common sequence types were ST398, ST188, and ST6, which showed close genetic relatedness to other foodborne S. aureus reported in China. In addition, high virulence levels were observed in Galleria mellonella infection models, and ST188 S. aureus isolates caused skin abscesses in rabbits. These findings highlight the widespread contamination, long-term survival potential, virulence traits and molecular diversity of multi-drug resistant S. aureus isolated from frozen pork. Therefore, integrated surveillance and control strategies are urgently needed to mitigate the associated public health risks.Keywords: Multi–drug resistant Staphylococcus aureus, Contamination, Virulence, Pork, Low temperature

Introduction

The increasing morbidity of foodborne diseases caused by contaminated food, particularly meat and meat products with foodborne bacteria, imposes a significant public health and economic burden (Liang et al., 2023). Staphylococcus aureus is one of the most well-known opportunistic pathogens associated with both healthcare- and community-acquired infections (Howden et al., 2023). As a highly adaptive pathogen, S. aureus can rapidly adjust to changing environmental conditions (low temperature, high salt and other sub-lethal stresses) and develop resistance to nearly all classes of antibiotics. In recent years, an increasing number of multi-drug resistant S. aureus has been identified during food poisoning outbreaks (Narciso et al., 2019).

In China, where pork accounts for the largest share of meat consumption, the slaughter-to-retail continuum relies on a multi-step “freeze–chill–thaw” regime. Several reports indicate that a considerable number of staphylococcal food poisoning incidents are linked to S. aureus contamination in pork products (Li et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2023). Freezing and thawing processes are commonly involved in the meat supply chain (Zhang et al., 2023). Pork is typically chilled immediately after slaughter and dressing to inhibit bacterial growth and contamination during cold storage and transportation from slaughterhouses to distribution centers and consumers (Kumiko et al., 2020). Therefore, the impact of temperature fluctuations on pathogen behavior should be considered. Numerous studies have investigated the growth behavior of S. aureus in meat or meat products under various temperature conditions (Nadine et al., 2010; Seneviratne et al., 2023; Sun et al., 2019). The resistance of S. aureus cells to cleaning and disinfection procedures within biofilms poses continuous risk for food deterioration and reduced food shelf life (Vincent et al., 2024). Besides, contamination of food with S. aureus during processing allows the bacterium to persist in the final product and be transmitted through the food chain. Moreover, pork and pork products are widely recognized as major reservoirs for S. aureus, which has gained growing global attention (Zhu et al., 2022). However, the prevalence of this bacterium under low-temperature conditions, especially in chilled pork, remains incompletely understood.

This study aims to provide a comprehensive overview of S. aureus present in pork under low-temperature conditions, focusing specifically on frozen pork samples collected from several cold storage warehouses in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, China. This investigation may enhance our understanding of S. aureus isolated from pork by analyzing its incidence, antimicrobial resistance profiles, biofilm-forming capacity, molecular typing, and virulence characteristics.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

From January 2021 to December 2022, a total of 344 pork-related samples, including frozen pork, sausage, ham, meat rice dumplings, and fresh pork, were collected from 23 cold storage warehouses in Zhejiang Province. The cold storage temperature for fresh pork was maintained at 4 °C, while that for other pock-products remained at −20 °C. Detailed information regarding the samples and capacity of cold storage is provided in Supplementary Sheet 1. Furthermore, sampling was conducted randomly at each cold storage facility and without stringent regional restrictions, a minimum of five samples were collected from each cold storage facility per sampling event and multi-batch sampling were conducted in the cold storage facility with high pork inventory.

S. aureus identification and cold survival evaluation

All samples were kept at 4 °C before processing. Each sample was enriched in brain heart infusion (BHI) broth supplemented with 7.5 % sodium chloride and incubated at 37 °C for 16 h. 10 μL of the enrichment was plated onto mannitol salt agar and incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. Presumptive S. aureus grows as a yellow colony. DNA extraction method of presumptive strains was performed by using a bacterial genomics DNA extraction kit (Tianlong Technology Co., Ltd. Xi'an). Species identity of S. aureus was determined by PCR and sequencing with the previous report (Ana et al., 2023).

For the experiment of cold survival evaluation, fresh pork were bought from a local supermarket and should be confirmed without S. aureus contamination. Following the segmentation of pork, S. aureus cells were individually exposed to each section with 1.0 × 105 CFU/g. Subsequently, the pork sections were all stored at −20 °C for a period of 180 days. Finally, the pork was thawed and cell viability were determined by using BHI Agar plate counting methodology after cultured at 37 °C for 24 h (Wu et al., 2018; Mohammed et al., 2021).

Biofilm formation assay

The microliter plate method was used to assay the biofilm formation ability with small modification as previously described (Dai et al., 2023). S. aureus were individually grown at 37 °C in BHI broth. After cultivation without agitation at 37 °C for 24 h, 48 h, respectively. The adherent cells were stained with 1 % crystal violet. The dye bound to the adherent cells was dissolved in 150 mL of 95 % ethanol, and the optical density (OD) was measured at 590 nm with a spectrophotometer. Uninoculated wells were served as blanks. Blank corrected absorbance values were used. Isolates were considered biofilm producers when their OD values were three times greater than the standard deviation of the mean ODc. Additionally, isolates showing biofilm-producing ability were classified as weak (ODc < OD ≤ 2 × Dc), moderate (2 × ODc < OD ≤ 4 × Dc), or strong (OD > 4 × ODc) biofilm producers by SI ways. Each test was repeated three times.

Susceptibility testing

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was performed on all isolates using standard broth microdilution methods according to the Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI). Every strain was detected with 9 antimicrobial agents, which representative agents from each class used to treat infections caused by S. aureus namely, vancomycin (VAN), florfenicol (FFC), cefoxitin (CFX), ciprofloxacin (CIP), amoxicillin/clavulanic acid (A/C), doxycycline (DOX), clindamycin (CLI), ampicillin (AMP), and ceftiofur (CEF). S. aureus ATCC 29213 was included as the quality control. Each experiment was repeated in triplicate.

S. aureus against acetic, L-lactic, citric, butyric, propionic and formic acid were determined using broth microdilution methods as Beier described (Beier et al., 2020). The MICs were determined to be the lowest concentration of an organic acid that resulted in no visible bacterial growth.

Bacterial resistance to disinfectants with the recommended concentration was examined using a modified serial broth dilution method (Li et al., 2023), following by 3 % Hydrogen peroxide, 0.5 % Peracetic acid, 0.2 g/L Potassium peroxymonosulfate, 0.5 g/L Trichloroisocyanuric acid, 0.5 g/L Sodium hypochlorite, 2 % Glutaraldehyde, 0.2 % Chlorhexidine, 20 % Benzalkonium bromide, 0.05 g/L Chlorhexidine acetate, 5 % Povidone-iodine, and 5 g/L Dupont Virkon®. Firstly, each bacterial strain was suspended and adjusted to a concentration of OD600 = 0.5. Secondly, the bacterial suspension was mixed with 2 × disinfectant in a 1:1 ratio and allowed to stand at room temperature for 30 min. Thirdly, 10 μL of the reacted solution was spotted onto LB agar plates which were then incubated overnight. Staphylococcus aureus ATCC 25923 served as the control strain. The procedure was performed in triplicate.

Detection of virulence genes and resistance genes

All isolates were tested by PCR for the presence of staphylococcal enterotoxin sea, seb, sec, sed, and see genes, the mecA/mecC gene related to methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) were also detected. All primers and PCR conditions were used as previously described (Dorjgochoo et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2002).

Molecular typing

All isolates were examined for staphylococcal protein A (SPA) type, random amplified polymorphic DNA (RAPD) type and agr type. PCR program for the spa gene was performed with primers spa-1113f and spa-1514f as previous (Marie et al., 2009), and PCR products were sequenced by Tsingke Biotechnology Co., Ltd, (Hangzhou, China). And the sequencing results were analyzed on the Ridom web server (http://spaserver.ridom.de) and visualized with PHYLOViZ 2.0 (Nascimento et al., 2017).

RAPD assay was done using the primer D11344 and PCR conditions as described by Sheela et al., 2021. RAPD profiles were analyzed using NTSYS.

Multiplex PCR-based protocol was used for amplification of the variable domain of agr locus to determine the specificity groups as previous (Javdan et al., 2019), agr type I, type II, type Ⅲ and type IV were expected to produce 441-bp, 575-bp, 323-bp, and 659-bp fragments, respectively.

Whole genome sequencing and comparative genomics analysis

According to the results of molecular typing, typical strains were selected to whole genome-sequenced (WGS) using the Hiseq2500 platform with 500 cycles of paired reads. The assembled genome was deposited in the GenBank repository under accession numbers: PRJNA1197661. The presence of virulence and resistance genes were confirmed using VirulenceFinder, ResFinder tools from Centre for Genomic Epidemiology (CGE) website (www.genomicepidemiology.org). The multi-locus sequence types (MLST) were obtained by submitting the WGS data to S. aureus MLST databases (https://pubmlst.org/S. aureus/).

Furthermore, the phylogenetic tree was constructed to reveal the genetic relationship of all isolates. The dominant strains were compared to previously published S. aureus which can be downloaded from the GenBank database before December 31st, 2023. The genetic relatedness was obtained using Panseq, the phylogenetic trees were generated by using iTOL (https://itol.embl. de/).

Virulence evaluation of epidemic strains in Galleria mellonella model or rabbit model

Animal experiments were performed following the guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals of the Chinese Association for Laboratory Animal Sciences. The animal protocol was approved by the ethics committee on animal experiments of Zhejiang Agricultural and Forestry University.

The Galleria mellonella model was used to evaluate the infection of S. aureus according to the previous report (Guillaume Ménard et al., 2021). Each group of 10 larvae were injected with 10 μL of serial dilutions of bacterial suspension with 107 CFU in the hemocoel via the last right pro-limb. Larvae were placed in an incubator at 37 °C for 72 h. Larvae were considered dead when non-responsive to touch. Experiments were done at least twice.

The experimental procedure of rabbit skin infections was performed as previously described with some modifications (Asunción et al., 2020) Briefly, rabbits were intradermally inoculated in their abdomen region was shaved and disinfected with chlorhexidine, each studied strain was suspended in phosphate-buffered saline and inoculated into animals with the lowest infective doses (1 × 108 CFU). The general status, weight, and rectal temperature of animals were recorded daily for 7 days.

Data analysis and statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed with SPSS version 16.0 and visualized by GraphPad Prism 8. The expression of the data was in the form of mean and standard deviation. P-value <0.05 were considered as statistically significant.

Results

Prevalence of S. aureus in cold storage

As shown in Fig. 1 and Supplementary Sheet 1, all 344 samples within 39 batches from 23 cold storage warehouses were collected, mainly from Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, China. The sample comprised frozen pork (67.7 %; n = 233), ham and sausage (16.3 %, n = 56), fresh pork (8.7 %, n = 30) and meat rice dumplings (7.3 %, n = 25).

Samples information and contamination of S. aureus in 344 pork-related samples.

A total of 52 S. aureus were isolated from 40 samples (11.6 %, 95 % CI: 8.4 %–15.5 %) that were present in 18 batch samples from 13 collecting places. Also, the positive samples were distributed in 46.2 % (18/39) batches and 56.5 % (13/23) collecting place, furthermore, 11 small-sized cold facilitie and different batches of samples in 2 medium-sized cold storagewere were contamination by S. aureus. In addition, frozen pork showed the highest prevalence with 15.5 % (37/233, 95 % CI: 36.4 %–37.6 %), followed by ham and sausage with the incidence of 5.4 % (2/56, 95 % CI: 0.7 %–3.3 %), and 3.3 % (1/30, 95 % CI: 0.8 %–2.8 %) of fresh pork.

For the cold survival evaluation, all 52 isolates can survive under −20 °C refrigerator for longer than 180 days, and the only 1.8 %–26.3 % of viable cells lived to be compared with the initial inoculation.

In vitro biofilm formation ability

86.5 % (45/52) isolates had strong biofilm formation ability and the rest 13.5 % (7/52) strains were moderate biofilm producers at 24 h (Fig. 2A). Remarkably, 90.4 % (47/52) isolates had extremely strong biofilm formation ability at 48 h. Biofilm production analysis with SI-way have exhibited 51 out of 52 isolates which were strong biofilm producers at 24 h, including 49 (94.2 %) strains kept the ability at 48 h (Fig. 2B).

Phenotype of 52 S. aureus isolates with capacity for biofilm formation and profile of antibiotic resistance.

Phenotypic profile of susceptibility

The antimicrobial susceptibility tests conducted on 52 strains against 9 antimicrobial agents revealed that these strains exhibit resistance to a broad spectrum of antimicrobials. The highest level of resistance was observed for ampicillin (88.5 %), followed by amoxicillin/clavulanic acid (69.2 %) and florfenicol (67.3 %) (Fig. 2C). Notably, the analysis included 13 methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) isolates. Remarkably, 29 distinct resistance patterns were identified among the isolates (Supplementary Sheet 2), with 49 strains demonstrating resistance to one or more antibiotics. Furthermore, 40 (76.9 %) of the strains were resistant to three or more antimicrobial agents (Fig. 2D).

The MIC for each strain against six organic acids revealed that the highest concentration of organic acid required to inhibit S. aureus was 4096 mg/L on acetic acid, L-lactic acid, and citric acid. The following concentration was 2048 mg/L for propionic acid and butyric acid, while the lowest concentration needed was 512 mg/L for formic acid (Supplementary Sheet 3).

The results of disinfectant tolerance test showed that all isolates were sensitive to commonly available disinfectants within the recommended working concentrations (Supplementary Sheet 3).

Presence of virulence genes

As indicated in Supplementary Sheet 4, none of the 52 isolates tested positive for enterotoxins sec, sed, and see. The majority of samples were found to contain sea and/or seb, with an incidence rate of 51.9 % (27/52; 95 % CI: 37.6 %–66.0 %). Furthermore, it was determined that 92.6 % (n = 25) of the enterotoxigenic strains carried seb; this included 12 isolates that possessed both sea and seb, while 13 isolates contained only seb. Additionally, there were two enterotoxigenic strains (7.8 %, n = 2) that exclusively harbored sea.

Molecular typing

Analysis of spa types revealed that 13 different spa types with one strain not capable in 52 strains (Supplement Sheet 4). The most prevalent type was spa-t20663 (25.0 %) which is the new type submitted by us, and spa-t189 (15.4 %) was also the dominant type in frozen pork, followed by spa-t164 (9.6 %) or spa-t571 (9.6 %), and other types were all less than 3 strains. According to the eBURST analysis, spa-t20663 is closely related to spa-t273 in this study, and the high-frequency types spa-t189, spa-547, spa-t571, and spa-t164 also cluster near each other (Fig. 3A and B).

Molecular typing of RAPD and spa types in all 52 S. aureus with STs in the 27 selected strains.

Phylogenetic analysis based on RAPD showed that all isolates could be classified into 10 clusters (Ⅰ-Ⅹ) (Fig. 3C, Supplement Sheet 4). Type I was the dominant cluster within 32 isolates of 9 spa types including spa-t034, spa-t1430, spa-t15657, spa-t164, spa-t189, spa-t20663, spa-t273, spa-t547, spa-t571.

Overall, agr type I was identified as the predominant type with 78.8 % (41/52), followed by type II (11.5 %, 6/52), and type Ⅲ (3.8 %, 2/52). In the present research, no isolates belonging to agr type IV were detected and 4 strains were unidentified (Supplement Sheet 4).

Whole genome analysis of typical isolates

To achieve more reliable genotype results and enhance the convenience of comparing different studies, 27 typical isolates were performed whole genome sequencing and the genomics data was analyzed (Supplement Sheet 4). Of all, 24 strains were from frozen pork, 2 strains were from sausage, and 1 strain was from fresh pork.

The MLST results revealed that a total of nine distinct ST types, including ST1, ST6, ST7, ST9, ST22, ST188, ST398, ST1281 and ST3055 (Fig. 3D). Among them, ST398, ST188, ST6 were ranked the top three. When the RAPD, ST and spa types were combined, a strong association was observed between certain ST and spa types. ST398 isolates were assigned to spa-t034 (n = 3) and spa-t571 (n = 3), the ST188 isolates were assigned to spa-t189 (n = 6), ST6 were assigned to spa-t20663 (n = 4) and spa-t571 (n = 1) (Fig. 3C).

All 27 strains are displayed in the cladogram tree to be classified into eleven clusters accompanied by resistance genes and virulence genes (Fig. 4). The resistome analysis revealed the presence of various acquired antibiotic resistance genes, including aminoglycoside resistance gene aadD, ant(6)-Ia, β-lactam resistance gene blaZ, mecA, mecC, lincosamide resistance gene lnu(A), lnu(B), lsa(E), amphenicol resistance gene fexA, fexB, macrolide resistance gene erm(A), erm(B), folate pathway antagonist gene dfrA, dfrG, tetracycline resistance gene tet(K), tet(L). Most strains carrying resistance genotypes exhibited the corresponding drug-resistance phenotypes. However, the acquisition of some resistance phenotypes may be due to intrinsic mutations rather than acquired resistance genes—for example, resistance to ciprofloxacin. Furthermore, the MRSA have more antibiotic resistance genes than the rest strains, and show more resistant to the antimicrobial agents. Besides, MRSA were observed with three kinds of SCCmec types, including Vc (5C2&5), XII (9C2), XII (9C2). In addition, all 27 isolates carried the aur, gen, higA, higB, higC, hly, hysA, ica, and most isolates carried cifA/cifB, harA, lukD/LukE, splA. Interestingly, the carriage of virulence genes was correlated to the MLST.

Alignment of genome sequences of 27 selected S. aureus with the distribution of virulence and antibiotic resistance genes by WGS.

Comparative genomics analysis of the dominant isolates

Based on the phylogenetic tree to clarify the genetic relatedness of the strains, we identified the genomes of S. aureus isolates with ST398, ST188, ST6. All dominant isolates were compared to the released genomics data in NCBI database (Supplementary Sheet 5).

Firstly, the genetic tree constructed from ST398 isolates showed four clusters suggesting either multiple sources or an ongoing evolution of the strains. When compared to the collection of ST398 genomes from China (Fig. S2), the ST398 isolates were similar to those from Shandong Province, Shanghai, Liaoning Province and Taiwan Province, all of which were collected from human food or swine. Secondly, ST188 is divided into eight branches, Shandong Province, Yunnan Province, and Zhejiang Province are the main sources (Fig. S3). Thirdly, the strains of all ST6 S. aureus were branched in 11 clusters, and the isolates are distributed in the major cluster, they have high affinities with other food-borne strains which were collected in Yunnan Province during 2014–2015 years, which have not been previously reported in Zhejiang (Fig. S4). Similar with the other ST6 strains almost all of the strains are blaZ-positive and food-borne.

Virulence of S. aureus with ST398, ST188, ST6

In the Galleria mellonella larvae infection model, all 9 individual selected strains with ST398, ST188, ST6 have the infection abilities to result in death of the worm (Fig. 5A, Supplementary Sheet 6). Moreover, the survival rates of ST6 S. aureus were less than 30 %, ST398 S. aureus were less than 80 %, and no worms could survive with the infection of ST188 S. aureus. The rabbit skin infection assays showed that abscesses (Fig. 5B) and histopathology lesions (Fig. 5C) were only observed in selected ST188 S. aureus on the 5th day after intradermal injection, which is indicating that this strain had high virulence and caused more expanded skin damage in rabbits.

Virulence of typical S. aureus (ST398, ST188, ST6) in Galleria mellonella infection model or rabbit skin infection model.

Discussion

Cold storage is typically employed to limit bacterial reproduction and ensure food safety. However, many bacteria can survive in food products at low temperatures (Suo et al., 2022). Although contamination by S. aureus in retail food has been frequently reported (Liang et al., 2023; Vincent et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2022), the incidence of S. aureus on pork in cold storage warehouses remains relatively rare. Previous studies indicated that the quick-frozen pork from 39 cities in China showed 50 % of contamination for S. aureus (Wu et al., 2018), 23.4 % of retail pork in Shandong (Zhao et al., 2025) and 1.56 % of frozen food in Jilin (Li et al., 2022). This study presents a comprehensive survey of S. aureus contamination on pork at cold storage in Hangzhou. Our research confirmed that 52 isolates of S. aureus were obtained from 11.6 % of samples collected across 18 batches from 13 cold storage warehouses. Furthermore, multiple enrichment steps were utilized for isolating S. aureus. Despite this approach, very few strains were isolated overall. Notably, positive samples were found distributed across 46.2 % of batches and at 56.5 % of collection sites, indicating a surprisingly high level of S. aureus contamination on pork within these cold storage facilities.

Low temperature preservation for pork is not entirely effective for control of S. aureus based on our study. Unfortunately, we could not fully determine the source of S. aureus and associated risk factors in pork under low temperature. Based on the results of ST in this study, the ST of strains isolated from the meat were consistent with those prevalent in the farms. However, during slaughtering and processing, the meat is exposed to high temperatures, and the slaughterhouse environment is also disinfected. Also, the environment of cold storage may a source of contamination. So it is obvious that foodborne pathogens contamination has been connected to the following factors: polluted raw materials, poor sanitation practices, poor equipment design, and deficient control of preservation (Huang et al., 2023; Mather et al., 2024). The isolates can also survive under −20 °C refrigerator for longer than 180 days in our study. All these findings indicated that food safety issues caused by S. aureus during low temperature food storage should have attracted broad attention.

The burden of antimicrobial resistance, particularly in China has increased alarmingly (Chen and Xie, 2019). S. aureus is a problem of health care management due to the resistance to antibiotics toward many classes. Multi-drug resistant S. aureus were isolated from frozen pork with various resistance genes, such as, blaZ, mecA, lnu(A), lnu(B), lsa(E), fexA, fexB and so on in our study. In addition, it has recently been realized that pork represents a major reservoir for MRSA, reflecting the limited therapeutic value of MRSA (Camino et al., 2024). The results of ST398 MRSA from frozen pork in this study also indicated a tremendous threat to food safety and human health.

Biofilms are the normal lifestyle of S. aureus (Flemming et al., 2016). In our study, the strong biofilm-forming capacity of S. aureus was detected among frozen pork. Biofilms are generally highly tolerant of different chemical and physical stressors in the environment. The biofilm matrix endows bacteria with high tolerance to various chemical and physical stresses in the environment. However, food contamination usually involves multiple bacterial species rather than a single strain. Mixed-species biofilms can markedly increase resistance to disinfectants and antibiotics through the physical barrier of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) and interspecies synergistic interactions (Yuan et al., 2019); this "hurdle" effect remains active under low-temperature storage and represents a major source of persistent contamination in the cold-chain food supply (Yuan et al., 2020). In addition, high biofilm formation significantly enhances the drug resistance of S. aureus, and the isolation rate of biofilm-positive S. aureus strains from clinical settings is increasing. The drug-resistance of S. aureus can readily be enhanced by strong biofilm (Wu et al., 2024). Biofilm-positive S. aureus strains have been isolated from a variety of clinical settings more frequently (An et al., 2008). The biofilm forming ability of S. aureus differs depending on their origin and might be affected by cell surface properties (Sugimoto et al., 2018). When the biofilm formation ability combined with the frequently antimicrobial resistance, resulted in that the multi-drug resistant S. aureus with strong biofilm formation ability was isolated from frozen pork in Zhejiang Province, China.

The ability to decontaminate S. aureus from environmental surfaces is an issue for debate (Cimolai, 2008). In our study, commonly available disinfectants at the recommended concentrations we selected to evaluate. Although under the selective pressure of disinfectants, many strains resistant to disinfectants continuously emerge (Castro et al., 2023), it is a noteworthy fact that all 11 selected disinfectants (including sodium hypochlorite, glutaraldehyde, etc.) exhibited potent bactericidal effects, these findings may represent a good news in controlling contamination of pork by S. aureus.

According to the recommendations of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, the preservation of red meat such as pork, beef, and mutton should not exceed six months when stored at a freezing temperature below −18 °C. However the potential long-time survival of S. aureus in frozen pork for more than 180 days poses challenges for decontamination in this study. Organic acids are usually classified as "safe food additives"(Ma et al., 2023). In our experiments, the MIC of L-lactic acid and citric acid was higher than that of other organic acids, whereas formic acid exhibited a stronger inhibitory effect on S. aureus compared to other organic acids. L-lactic acid and citric acid were not the most suitable materials for spraying on meat to eliminate bacteria, as these two organic acids actually facilitated the survival of S. aureus (Beier et al., 2020). It is urgent to discover new strategies for the prevention and control of S. aureus in frozen pork.

Staphylococcal food poisoning is a growing concern all over the world every year. It is important to understand the genetic background and pathogenesis of the different clonal lineages of S. aureus ST398 type that has emerged among livestock and begun colonizing and infecting humans (He et al., 2018), but few data are available yet for frozen pork in Zhejiang Province, China. In this study, a total of nine distinct ST types, including ST1, ST6, ST7, ST9, ST22, ST188, ST398, ST1281 and ST3055 were detected, and ST398, ST188, ST6 were ranked the top three with several isolates sharing the common ancestor in other places of China. As the major lineage causing infections in multiple host species, such as bovine, pediatric, and adult patients in Shanghai. ST188 is an emerging type (Wang et al., 2018). SPA analysis revealed ST188-t20663 (25.0 %) was the most prevalent spa type which is a new spa type submitted by us. In addition, ST6 and ST7 isolates were detected from food-borne illness outbreaks in southwest China (Liao et al., 2018).

S. aureus infections are particularly problematic due to its virulence. High virulence was also observed in dominant S. aureus, especially abscesses and histopathology lesions of ST188 S. aureus in rabbits. It is concerning the top three ST398, ST188, ST6 types shared a common ancestor with other S. aureus in China. Then, the investigation of sources and transmission routes of S. aureus on frozen pork is emergency. Based on this study, it is urgent to concern the high prevalence, diverse molecular characteristics and high virulence of multi-drug resistant S. aureus in frozen pork. Therefore, it is urgent to enhance surveillance and control measures to decontaminate S. aureus.

Conclusion

A high level of contamination by S. aureus in cold storage warehouses was observed in Hangzhou. Multidrug-resistant and hypervirulent S. aureus and MRSA from pork and pork products with strong biofilm formation were found. Diverse spa types and STs (ST398, ST188, ST6 dominant) were detected in our study, especially we found new spa type spa-t20663. The findings are imperative to conduct comprehensive surveillance on various meat sources concerning S. aureus contamination and implement effective prevention strategies to mitigate the presence of drug-resistant S. aureus on pork products.

Mavacamten for treatment of symptomatic mid-ventricular obstruction in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy: a case report
Authors: Samuel Hunziker, Philip Haaf, Annina S Vischer, Giacomo Tini Melato
Published: 2025

Case description

A 63-year-old man was referred from pneumology with exertional dyspnoea and chest discomfort, despite maximal dose of bisoprolol. The patient had a history of coronary artery disease, which has previously been treated with aortocoronary bypass, diabetes mellitus type 2, obesity, and sleep apnoea. There are no known cases of cardiomyopathy in the family history. At presentation, the patient was treated with a full dose of bisoprolol in addition to aspirin and rosuvastatin/ezetimibe.

Electrocardiography showed sinus rhythm with lateral T-inversion (Figure 1A). Transthoracic echocardiography (TTE) demonstrated LV hypertrophy with a mid-ventricular obstruction with a resting gradient of 17 mmHg, increasing to 72 mmHg with Valsalva manoeuvre, and no relevant mitral valve or wall motion abnormalities (Figure 1B). Cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR) perfusion imaging revealed an asymmetric LV hypertrophy with an anteroseptal LV wall thickness of 16 mm, patchy fibrosis at both right-ventricular insertion points, mid-ventricular systolic cavum obliteration, no apical pouch or signs of ischaemia (Figure 1C and D, Supplementary material online, Video S1). We interpreted these results as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) with mid-ventricular obstruction.

(A) Initial electrocardiogram showing lateral repolarization abnormalities; (B) initial Doppler echocardiography demonstrating mid-ventricular obstruction with a gradient of 72 mmHg; (C and D) initial cardiovascular magnetic resonance (CMR): three-chamber view showing mid-ventricular obstruction leading to the formation of an apical pouch (C), and short axis view showing patchy late gadolinium enhancement (D); (E) follow-up electrocardiogram demonstrating improvement of repolarization abnormalities; (F) follow-up Doppler echocardiography revealing the absence of a relevant mid-ventricular obstruction with a gradient of 16 mmHg under Valsalva.

After careful consideration, we initiated treatment with mavacamten 5 mg once daily, following the approach and dosing scheme used in the EXPLORER-HCM study, despite the absence of LVOT obstruction, to relieve the mid-ventricular obstruction.1 Four weeks later, the patient reported significant improvement in symptoms and quality of life. Follow-up electrocardiography showed improved T-inversion (Figure 1E), TTE a reduction in mid-ventricular gradient to 3 mmHg at rest and 16 mmHg with Valsalva without decrease in LV ejection fraction (Figure 1F). Mavacamten dose was reduced to 2.5 mg.

This case illustrates a significant improvement of a mid-ventricular obstruction in a patient with HCM under therapy with mavacamten. The myosin-inhibitor mavacamten, currently indicated in HCM with LVOT obstruction despite medical treatment, has not been studied in mid-ventricular obstruction, though patients with mid-ventricular obstruction were not actively excluded from the EXPLORER-HCM and VALOR-HCM trials.1,2 Mavacamten has a potential to add a new treatment option in the notoriously difficult to treat patients with HCM with mid-ventricular obstruction. Before general use, however, randomized-controlled trials are essential to establish its broader clinical application for mid-ventricular obstruction.

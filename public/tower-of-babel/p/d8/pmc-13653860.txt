Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation changed the relative abundance of intestinal microbiota in rats with knee osteoarthritis: a potential treatment for KOA
Authors: Yang Zhang, Tong Ma, Ran Chen, Hong-bin Zhao, Yan Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundKnee osteoarthritis (KOA) is a common degenerative bone disease, and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) is an alternative and complementary therapy (ACM). This study revealed the role of TENS in regulating the intestinal microbiota in KOA rats.MethodsThis study concentrated on the intestinal microbiota of KOA rats which were treated with TENS for 1, 2, and 3 weeks. Three intensities of TENS were used to treat KOA rats, and the expressions of IL6/8, PI3K-AKT were measured. The intestinal microbiota was analyzed by 16 S rDNA sequencing.ResultsCompared with the Model Control group, TENS could improve symptoms of KOA rats and inhibit the expressions of IL6/8 by down-regulating the PI3K-AKT expression. After 3 weeks of treatment with TENS, compared with the Model Control group, the abundances of Bacteroidetes, Bacteroidetes, and Thermodesulfobacteria increased in the TENS groups; the abundances of Ficmicutes, Campylobacter, and Verruca decreased in the TENS groups.ConclusionTENS could improve the histomorphology of knee and inhibit inflammation in KOA rats. After treatment with TENS, the intestinal microbiota gradually changed from 1,2,3 weeks and the abundance of them was different with three intensities of TENS. Further study will elucidate the underlying mechanisms of TENS in altering gut microbiota and the potential therapeutic applications of these intestinal microbiota for KOA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s10123-026-00854-9.

Introduction

Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is a type of osteoarthritis (OA) which is characterized by knee cartilage wear, synovial inflammation, and subchondral osteopathy. Frequent proliferation and osteophyte formation are the main pathological features (Zhu et al. 2024). KOA patients suffered from joint swelling, pain, stiffness, limited mobility, etc., even knee deformity and lack of the patient’s daily ability (Vitaloni et al. 2019). Clinical treatment mainly concentrated on the relief of KOA symptoms and the recovery of joint function, including with or without medicine treatment and KOA surgery. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), opioid analgesics, arthroscopic surgery etc. are common medicines for KOA (Vitaloni et al. 2019). However, prolonged use of NSAIDs and analgesics is always accompanied by serious side effects and slow development of drug resistance. Rational diet and exercise are the two recommended non-medicine treatments, but the compliance of KOA patients is poor (Shahid et al. 2025). Meanwhile, surgical treatment of KOA is expensive and often carries complications of nerve injury, postoperative joint loosening, and risk of postoperative infection, which also leads to the pain of trauma and the longer period of recovery (Geng et al. 2023). Some alternative and complementary medicine (ACM) have a positive effect on KOA patients, such as acupuncture, tuina, hyperthermia, etc., which could relieve the pain of KOA (Straaten et al. 2018).

Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) is a non-invasive treatment modality that stimulates afferent nerve fibers in the skin to cause neuromuscular contraction (Vance et al. 2022). As a type of ACM, TENS activates nerves with electrical stimulation. TENS usually uses viscous electrodes coated on the surface of the skin to apply pulsed electrical stimulation, which can be modified in frequency, intensity, and time (Johnson et al. 2022). In terms of mechanism of TENS, it stimulates low-threshold cutaneous afferents and inhibits the transmission of pain information in the central nervous system (Colloca et al. 2017). TENS stimulates afferent nerves of small diameters to activate descending pain suppression pathways or to block afferent activity in peripheral neurons. In the clinic (especially in Europe and the United States), TENS has been used to relieve various pain, such as chronic stubborn pain, acute post-traumatic pain, postoperative pain etc. (Knotkova et al. 2021). Most clinical trials have demonstrated the efficacy of TENS in attenuating neuropathic pain and suggested that appropriate stimulation parameters (e.g., stimulation frequency and intensity) were critical to improving the analgesic effects of TENS. A character of KOA patient is KOA-related knee pain, and a study found that TENS has a positive pain relief effect in the stair-climb test (SCT) (Iijima et al. 2020).

The intestinal microbiota is currently considered to be a complex organ composed of 500-1,000 species and 1014 bacteria, and the intestinal microbiota is increasingly recognized as a key factor in human health (Tilg et al. 2020). Some evidence found that various chronic diseases of humans were related to intestinal micro-ecological disorders (Nathan et al. 2021). Research has shown that alterations in the intestinal microbiota can act as biomarkers of bone metabolic activity, and drugs or probiotics modulate the intestinal flora with the potential to improve bone structure and quality (Wang and Hu 2023). The intestinal microbiota affects human health and has common risk factors for the occurrence and development of KOA disease, such as diet, age, obesity, and inflammatory diseases (Giorgino et al. 2023). Definitely, the diet determines the abundance of intestinal microbiota, and a number of diets have been implicated in the pathogenesis of osteoarthritis, including vitamins, amino acids, and magnesium. Aging-related dysbiosis of intestinal microbiota contributes to the occurrence of OA. The lower abundance of Bacteroides spp. in the older people down-regulates the age-related physiology, such as immune responses, cognitive function, and organ disorders. Obesity is correlated with shifts in gut microbiota composition, particularly at the phylum level, characterized by an altered Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, reduced diversity, and modified gene and metabolic profiles. A recent study found that the intestinal microbiota took part in inflammation by regulating some pathways such as nutrients, immunity, and metabolism (Zhu et al. 2024). Developing the beneficial microbiota and its fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) will take a long time but it could has potentials to prevent and treat OA.

In this study, we established a KOA rat model which was treated with TENS; the intestinal microbiota of KOA rats was analyzed by 16 S rDNA sequencing after TENS treatment. This study would like to reveal whether TENS improves KOA and what the relationship is between the KOA and intestinal microbiota. The comparisons of the intestinal microbiota in phylum and genus could become a potential medicine for KOA.

Materials and methods

TENS

The range of TENS (Anrui co., ZL 2020 1 0460706.1, China) was set as 0.1470–9.2512 A/m2, 25 Hz. TENS stimulated the knee joint 20 min/day & 5 days/week for KOA rats.

Animals

This study was approved by the Animal Study Committee of Kunming Medical University (no. KMMU 20211419) and complied with the regulations of the NIH Guidelines. 36 SD rats (female, 3 months old, 210 ± 20 g; Dossy co., China) were maintained in standard conditions (21–23 ℃ & 12-hour cycle of light and darkness). Food and water were used at any time. This experiment did not cause pain in animals during the treatment process. Euthanasia (150 mg/kg Pentobarbital i.v., Sigma Ltd., USA) was performed using excessive anesthesia, including corneal reflex disappearance, righting reflex disappearance, etc. At this point, wait for the animal’s breathing and heartbeat to stop, generally for 3–7 min. Observe the animal’s pupils dilating, the pupillary light reflex disappears, and confirm the animal’s clinical death. Collect the animal samples required for the experiment. This study complied with the ARRIVE GUIDELINES 2.0 (See sup file in the end of the test).

KOA model

All of the rats had the medial meniscus, medial collateral ligament, and anterior cruciate ligament of the right leg removed (KOA rats). After anterior cruciate ligament transection+meniscal laceration surgery and 1 week of exercise, the rat model of KOA was successfully established, which was confirmed by MRI analysis (Song et al. 2024).

Grouping

The KOA rats were divided into a Model control group (Represented as Model Control; C KOA label) and three TENS groups (Represented as low 1.58 v, 1.55 mA, medium 3.08 v, 3.02 mA, high 6.45 v, 6.32 mA; L, M, H KOA label).

The intestinal sample collection

After 1, 2, 3 weeks’ treatment with TENS, 24 intestinal samples were collected from the colon bag of KOA rats, including H.1 W.KOA.1, 2;H.2 W.KOA.1, 2; H.3 W.KOA.1, 2; M.1 W.KOA.1, 2; M.2 W.KOA.1, 2; M.3 W.KOA.1, 2; L.1 W.KOA.1, 2; L.2 W.KOA.1, 2; L.3 W.KOA.1, 2; C.1 W.KOA.1, 2; C.2 W.KOA.1, 2; C.3 W.KOA.1, 2.

The intestinal microbiota analysis

The intestinal samples were extracted, and the V4-V5 region of the 16 S rDNA genes was amplified with primers (341 F: ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG; 806 R: GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT). The amplicons were purified using AMPure beads (Axygen co., USA). Barcoded libraries were generated by emulsion PCR and sequenced in a V4 to V5 reverse direction on a 318 chip using the 400 bp sequencing kit of the Ion Torrent Personal Genome Machine (PGM co., USA) system. Quality filtering on the raw tags was performed using the fastp software to obtain high-quality Clean Tags. The tags were compared with the reference database Silva database (16 S/18S), https://www.arb-silva.de/; Unite Database (ITS), https://unite.ut.ee/ to detect chimeric sequences. And the effective tags were obtained by removing the chimera sequences with the vsearch package (V2.16.0, https://github.com/torognes/vsearch). For the Effective Tags obtained previously, denoising was performed with DADA2 or deblur module in the QIIME2 software to obtain initial ASVs (Amplicon Sequence Variants) (default: DADA2). The PE101/PE150/PE250/PE300 and QIIM2 bioinformatical methods analyzed the above results (Hassler et al. 2022).

MRI evaluation

The relevant parameters: an eight-channel phased array animal coil (50 mm; Shanghai Chenguang Medical Technologies Co., China); FOV = 60 × 81 × 21 mm3; a 1.2 mm slice thickness; 200 × 268 matrix slice = 16. T2-weighted turbo spin-echo (TSE) sequence (repetition time = 3,000 ms; echo time = 80 ms; number of signals averaged = 1; MRI; 3.0 Tesla, Philips Ingenia Ltd., Netherlands).

Step number of hind leg

The athletic ability of the KOA rats was analyzed by calculating the step number of the hind leg, which was recorded as the number of steps the rats ran through a 25.00 cm exercise wheel. The rats were observed once a day for 6 days/week.

Evaluation of the mankin score

A higher Mankin score indicates more severe cartilage degeneration as follows:

Cartilage structure: 0 for normal, 1 for surface irregularity, 2 for pannus formation and surface irregularity, 3 for fissure into the transition layer, 4 for fissure into the radiation layer, 5 for fissure into the calcification layer, and 6 for complete structural destruction.

‌Chondrocytes: 0 for normal, 1 for diffusely increased cells, 2 for focally increased cells, and 3 for significantly reduced cell number.

‌Cartilage matrix staining: 0 for normal, 1 for mild reduction, 2 for moderate reduction, 3 for severe reduction, and 4 for no staining.

‌Tideline integrity: 0 for completeness and 1 for vascular disruption.

Immunohistological analysis

Antibodies were IL-6/8 antibody (GB11117&GB115271, 1:200, Servio ltd., China) and HRP-conjugated secondary antibody (GB23303, 1:500; Servicebio Co., China).

Quantitative RT-PCR

Primer sequences were IL6 (Rattus), forward primer CACTTCACAAGTCGGAGGCT, reverse primer AGCACACTAGGTTTGCCGAG; IL8 (Rattus), forward primer GGCTCTCCTCCTGGTTTCAG, reverse primer TGAGCCTTGGCCATAAGTGC.

Western blot

Antibodies were anti-IL-6 antibody (AB283684; 1:1000; Servio Ltd., China), anti-IL-8 antibody (GB115271, Servio Ltd., China); anti-GAPDH (GB11002, Servio Ltd., China); anti-PI3K (GB13427, Servio Ltd., China); anti-AKT (GB11525, Servio Ltd., China).

Data analysis

The figures were constructed by GraphPad Prism 8 software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA). The P value of operational taxonomic unit (OTU) was performed by MetagenomeSeq test. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with LSD test analyzed the differences among each group (SPSS 23.0, USA), and P < 0.05 indicated statistical significance.

Results

TENS improves the histomorphology of knee in KOA rats and has an anti-inflammatory effect on KOA rats through decreasing the IL-6/8/PI3K-AKT expression

The rat model of KOA was successfully established by anterior cruciate ligament transection+meniscal laceration surgery and exercise, then confirmed by MRI (Fig. 1A). As compared to the Model Control group, the weight in each group was similar after TENS (P > 0.05, Fig. 1B). After TENS, the step number of 25 cm decreased in the high intensity of TENS groups at 3 weeks after TENS (P < 0.05, Fig. 1C); it is interesting and could be explained by the lower sensitivity to pain in the high intensity of TENS. The cartilage thickness increased in the medium intensity of TENS group (P < 0.05, Fig. 1D&E); the Mankin scores decreased in the TENS groups (P < 0.05, Fig. 1F); the IL-6/8 expression decreased in the TENS group which was analyzed by qPCR at 3 weeks after TENS (P < 0.05, Fig. 1G); as compared to self-right/left knee joint in each group, the IL-6/8 expression decreased in the right samples (P < 0.05). As compared to the Model Control group, the IL-6 expression decreased in the high intensity of TENS group and decreased which was analyzed by ICC at 3 weeks after TENS (P < 0.05, Fig. 1H&I); the IL-6 expression decreased in the low/medium intensity of TENS groups (P < 0.05); the PI3K expression decreased in the medium intensity of TENS group and the AKT expression decreased in the TENS group at 3 weeks after TENS (P < 0.05, Fig. 1J&K).

Discussion

In this study, a non-invasive, easy-to-use and low-cost treatment modality-TENS was used to treat KOA rats. Three different stimulation of TENS groups were set according to the body surface area of humans and rats. To ensure the electric current density A/m2, the electrodes were trimmed to fit the knee area of KOA rats. The intensity of TENS was dependent on the pre-experimental results (Miao et al. 2025). After TENS treatment, TENS treated KOA rats and inhibited the IL-6/8/PI3K-AKT expression. Inflammation is a contributing factor to KOA, and the immune cells of KOA rats release inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-6 and IL-8 (Puts et al. 2024). When TENS inhibited the expression of IL-6 and IL-8, it inhibited the inflammation in KOA rats. Meanwhile, TENS inhibited the expression of PI3K-AKT. PI3K-AKT was highly activated in osteoarthritic cartilage and closely takes part in IL-1β-induced inflammation (Wang et al. 2023). Once activating the PI3K and AKT phosphorylation, the production of IL-6/8 could aggravate inflammation. TENS could inhibit inflammation by inhibiting the expression of IL-6/8/PI3K-AKT in KOA rats.

We observed that the intestinal microbiota was changed in KOA rats by 16 S rDNA sequencing after TENS. By sequencing 16 S rDNA motifs, its routine operation is usually to identify genus-level bacteria rather than species by the V3 to V4 sequence of 16 S rDNA; several procedures in the process, such as DNA extraction, PCR amplification, and matching in the database for identification, can cause biases to affect the results (Gosalbes et al. 2012). In this study, several representative bacteria in the intestinal flora were changed with TENS treatment (such as Bifidobacteria, Lactobacillus, Prevotella species, etc.), their metabolites and components triggering the body’s inflammatory response, inducing a systemic inflammatory response, and participating in the occurrence of KOA. But we did not know how TENS applied to the knee specifically leads to these changes in the abundance of intestinal microbiota. It could be related to the gut-joint axis. The metabolites produced by gut microorganisms, such as short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), could maintain bone homeostasis and reduce inflammation in OA. It was suspected that TENS might influence the knee joint-intestinal axis via its nervous pathway; yet the relationship between this activation and changes in gut microbiota abundance remains poorly documented.

Alpha diversity is a common indicator of changes in the intestinal microbiota, and it could explain the relative abundance of microbial species in space and time, including the indices of Ace, Chao1, Shannon, and Simpson, etc. (Miao et al. 2025, García-López et al. 2021). The results of Ace, Chao1 and Shannon indices were similar; the results of Simpson index were different in each group at 3 weeks after TENS. Beta diversity analyzes the changed diversity of species and its index, such as PCA and PCoA, could show the diversity of data (Walters and Martiny 2020). The results of beta diversity among all samples are showed individual differences in KOA rats. There was a significant difference in each group which was analyzed by beta diversity at 3 weeks after TENS and the changed intestinal microbiota of KOA rats suggested a novel mechanism for KOA and further study is necessary.

The normal human intestinal microbiota includes four main bacterial phyla: Ficmicutes, Bacteroides, Actinomycetes (including Bifidobacteria) and Proteobacteria (including Escherichia coli). After TENS treatment, the intestinal microbiota of KOA rats was changed. Compared with the C.1 W.KOA group, the Ficmicutes, Actinobacterota of H.3 W.KOA group were decreased., the Bacteroidota, Gemmatimonadota, and Desulfobacterota of H.3 W.KOA group were increased. The above analysis revealed that the intestinal microbiota was changed as the treatment progressed with TENS. At the genus level, the differences in the intestinal microbiota between the CL.3 W.KOA and TENS groups, including Lactobacillus, Lachnospiraceae_NK4A136_group, and other bacteria, increased greatly. The dominant species of Lactobacillus mainly included Lactobacillus rhamnosus (L. rhamnosus). Rhamnosus, tumor Lactobacillus gastrogastricus (L. gastrobacteriosis ruminis), Lactobacillus des (L. deuteri). delbrueckii, Lactobacillus plantarum (L. plantarum) plantarum, dry Lactobacillus casei (L. casei) and Lactobacillus acidophilus (L. acidophilus) (O’Callaghan and O’Toole 2013). Studies have found that Lactobacillus is an important gram-positive bacteria in animals and the human intestine, one of the important microbiota. Its biological functions are mainly manifested as: blocking the invasion and colonization of the intestinal tract by pathogenic bacteria, inhibiting pathogenic bacteria and anti-infection, maintaining the micro-ecological balance of the intestines, enhancing the body’s immunity from epidemics, promoting digestion, and delaying aging and anti-shot, etc. (Lee et al. 2018). Following TENS treatment, the abundance of Lactobacillus increased in KOA rats, which is consistent with current findings on the beneficial effects of Lactobacillus in the improvement of KOA. Bifidobacteria is an important intestinal organism that is beneficial to the human. It has many important biological functions, such as biological screen, nutritional function, anti-tumor effect, anti-epidemic enhancement effect, gastrointestinal function, anti-aging and so on (Xiao et al. 2024). Among the probiotic bacteria, Lactobacillus and Bifidobacteria mainly attack intestinal pathogenic bacteria through the production of bacteriocins and organic acids, while Escherichia coli is a conditional pathogenic bacteria. When the balance of intestinal microbiota is broken, colibacillus will continue to invade, eventually damaging the intestinal mucosa (Rajagopal et al. 2022). At the same time, the experimental results showed that at the phylum level, compared with the CL.3 W.KOA group, the M/H.3 W.KOA group increased the phylum Ficmicutes and Bacteroides, which was consistent with the proportion of dominant bacteria in the normal human intestinal microbiota. As compared to the medium/high intensity of TENS groups, there was plenty of Proteobacteria, and most of the bacteria in the Proteobacteria phylum are more common with pathogenic bacteria such as Escherichia coli., which may be one of the conditional pathogenic bacteria that cause knee joint disease. At the genus level, compared with the C.3 W.KOA group, the Lactobacillus, Lachnospiraceae_NK4A136_group, Romboutsia, Desulfovibrio, Eubacterium_ruminantium_group increased in the L/M/H.3 W.KOA groups after 3 weeks of treatment with TENS. When these changed intestinal flora cultured in the KOA patient, an improved diet or beneficial metabolites could delay aging, reduce weight, protect bone/cartilage, etc. The above intestinal microbiota could be a potential FMT for KOA in the clinic.

The functions of changed intestinal microbiota which were analyzed by KEGG revealed that the mechanism of TENS to improve KOA was related to cell proliferation, migration, differentiation, bone metabolism, repair, and lipid metabolism. The inflammatory indicators such as IL-6/8/PI3K-AKT were decreased after TENS, thereby alleviating knee osteoarthritability, reducing pain, and ultimately treating KOA.

In this study, the construction of the animal model simulates the KOA disease; although TENS can improve the histomorphology of knee and the structural damage of the anterior cruciate ligament and meniscus cannot be restored; the setting of the recovery period is based on the recovery period of human treatment, so the recovery period is shorter and the actual period of KOA treatment is very long; due to limited funding, the number of animal faecal samples is small, which may lead to a slight deviation in the analysis of microbiota; the study failed to address the impact of external factors, such as diet, housing, and stress, etc.The existing conditions try to ensure the diet, housing, and exercise are as consistent as possible; finally and importantly, the findings from rats might not directly apply to humans, and it will take a long time from theory to application. The use of an antibiotic cocktail before treatment eliminates the natural microbiota, making it unclear whether the observed effects are due to microbiota reconstitution after antibiotics or the direct effect of TENS. Further study will add a control group that does not receive antibiotics. Meanwhile, this study did not reveal the relationship between TENS, microbiota, and KOA. The improvement of KOA rats could primarily result from the therapeutic effects of TENS, rather than from the effect of changed intestinal microbiota, which have not yet been shown to have a significant contributory role in this context. We think further experiment, such as fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT), are necessary and it will support a causal link between the changed intestinal microbiota and the observed improvement of histomorphology in KOA rats. And a further study will make it clear which one, microbiota or its induced metabolite, improved KOA rats.

Conclusion

In conclusion, TENS could improve the histomorphology of knee and inhibit the IL6/8/P3IK-AKT expression in KOA rats. After treatment with TENS, 16s rDNA sequencing revealed that TENS changed the intestinal microbiota, including Lactobacillus, Lachnospiraceae_NK4A136_group, Romboutsia, Desulfovibrio, Ficmicutes, and Bacteroides, etc. from 1,2,3 weeks and the abundance of them was different with three intensities of TENS. Further study will elucidate the underlying mechanisms of TENS in altering gut microbiota and the potential therapeutic applications of these intestinal microbiota for KOA.

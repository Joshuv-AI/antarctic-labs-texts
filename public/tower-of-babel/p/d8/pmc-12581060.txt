Role of additional topotecan injections (plus two) in reducing recurrence rate in retinoblastoma of group D and group E patients: a retrospective study
Authors: Xintong Lin, Zhuqing Zheng, Haifeng Chen, Jiang Qian, Qiang Liu, Ruiqi Ma, Kang Xue
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo evaluate the effectiveness of administering additional intravitreal topotecan injections (plus two) in reducing the recurrence rate in patients with advanced retinoblastoma (RB).Methods and analysisThis retrospective, non-comparative and interventional study encompassed 26 eyes of 25 patients with RB, treated between January 2019 and April 2024. All eyes in this series had received previous intravenous chemotherapy and/or intra-arterial chemotherapy. Patients received intravitreal topotecan (30 µg/0.1 mL) and/or intravitreal melphalan (20–30 µg/0.1 mL) injections every 3–4 weeks until vitreous seeds regression was achieved. Subsequently, two additional injections of topotecan (30 µg/0.1 mL) were administered after complete regression to consolidate subclinical residuals. The control of vitreous seeds, ocular toxicity assessment and clinical characteristics was observed.ResultsThe median age of the patients was 26 months, with 57.7% belonging to group D and 42.3% to group E of RB classification. A total of 111 injections were administered in 26 eyes, with a median of 4 injections per eye. Complete vitreous seeds regression was achieved in 100% of eyes, and eye salvage was successful in 24 of 26 eyes (92.3%). There were no recurrences of vitreous seeds. Enucleation was performed in two eyes: one due to phthisis bulbi and one due to optic nerve tumour recurrence. No cases of systemic metastasis or death were reported.ConclusionAdministering additional intravitreal topotecan injections (plus two) following the complete regression of vitreous seeds may contribute to reducing the recurrence rate in patients with RB. This approach enhances the long-term efficacy of intravitreal injections, thereby contributing to higher eye salvage rates.However, the retrospective, non-randomised design and small sample size remain limitations. Multicentre prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings and refine treatment protocols.Keywords: Child Health, Treatment Medical

Introduction

Retinoblastoma (RB) is the most common intraocular malignancy in children, with vitreous seeds posing a significant therapeutic challenge.1,4 Vitreous seeds, defined as tumour cell aggregates disseminated within the vitreous cavity, exhibit resistance to conventional therapies such as intravenous chemotherapy (IVC)5 6 and intra-arterial chemotherapy (IAC).7 8 Eyes presenting with diffuse intraocular RB seeds distribution at diagnosis demonstrate an elevated risk of local treatment failure.9 Intravitreal chemotherapy, particularly with topotecan, has emerged as a promising treatment for managing refractory or recurrent vitreous seeds.10 11

Despite the success of intravitreal topotecan, recurrence of vitreous seeds remains a concern. In the era of traditional IVC, the recurrence rate of vitreous seeds was approximately 25%–29%.12 In the current era of intravitreal chemotherapy, the overall recurrence rate has decreased to 14.3%–19%,11 13 while that of group E eyes remains as high as 20%–30%.7 14 15 This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of supplemental intravitreal topotecan injections (two additional doses) in reducing vitreous seeds recurrence among patients with advanced RB of group D and group E.

Materials and methods

This retrospective, non-comparative interventional study included 26 eyes from 25 patients with RB treated at the Department of Ophthalmology, Eye, Ear, Nose and Throat Hospital of Fudan University, Shanghai, China, between January 2020 and April 2024. Inclusion criteria required a minimum follow-up of 12 months after the final intravitreal injection. Written informed consent was obtained from all parents or guardians.

Collected data included patient demographics (age, sex), laterality, tumour stage based on the International Classification of Retinoblastoma (ICRB), type (dust, sphere, cloud) and location of seeding, primary chemotherapy regimen (IVC, IAC), adjunctive treatments (eg, transpupillary thermotherapy (TTT) or cryotherapy), and details of intravitreal injections (total number, dose) were recorded. Outcome measures included clinical response of vitreous seeds, complications and eye salvage rates.

IVC and IAC protocols followed established methods.16 17 IVC consisted of intravenous vincristine, etoposide and carboplatin. IAC involved melphalan combined with topotecan or carboplatin. Focal consolidation of residual retinal tumours was performed using TTT or cryotherapy when necessary. For eyes with vitreous seeds, particularly in patients who have received IVC, 1 mg of periocular topotecan was injected into the deep posterior sub-Tenon’s space, either concurrently with IVC or IAC.

Examination and intravitreal injections were performed under general anaesthesia. Before each injection, the locations of the retinal tumour and vitreous seeds were determined using RetCam III (Clarity Medical Systems, California, USA) and an indirect ophthalmoscope. Abnormal hyaloid status was assessed via B-scan ultrasonography (AVISO, Quantel Medical, Clermont-Ferrand, France).18

Patients received intravitreal topotecan (30 µg/0.1 mL) and/or intravitreal melphalan (20–30 µg/0.1 mL) injections every 3–4 weeks until vitreous seed regression was achieved. Prior to intravitreal injection, intraocular pressure was reduced by aspirating 0.1 mL of aqueous humour. Intravitreal injections were then administered via the transconjunctival pars plana route using a 30G needle, 2.5–3.5 mm from the limbus. The injection site was carefully chosen to ensure there was no nearby tumour mass, vitreous seeds or subretinal fluid within two clock hours of the injection site. The injections were performed using a safety-enhanced technique to minimise the risk of extraocular tumour spread. The injection site was sealed with cryotherapy before withdrawing the needle. The eyeball was gently agitated to ensure drug dispersion. Postoperative topical corticosteroid/antibiotic ointment was applied.

Complete regression was defined as the complete disappearance or calcification of all vitreous seeds. After tumour control was achieved, two additional injections of topotecan (30 µg/0.1 mL) were administered for the consolidation of subclinical residual: the first at 1 month postregression and the second at 3 months postregression, both during routine follow-up examinations under general anaesthesia.

Flicker electroretinogram (ERG) recordings at 30 Hz stimulation were systematically obtained using the portable LKC RETeval system (LKC Technologies, USA) with skin electrodes under standardised anaesthesia. Baseline ERG measurements were performed before each intravitreal injection, with follow-up recordings during subsequent visits. This protocol was uniformly implemented starting in 2024; prior electrophysiological data were unavailable due to equipment limitations.

Results

In 26 eyes of 25 patients, RB was bilateral in 10 (40.0%) patients and unilateral in 15 (60.0%) cases. No case has a familial history. The mean patient age at the time of the vitreous injection was 26.4 months (median=23 months, range=10–50 months). At the time of the initial presentation, the affected eye was classified (ICRB) group D (15 eyes, 57.7%) and group E (11 eyes, 42.3%). Among these eyes, 22 (84.6%) had refractory vitreous seeds and 4 (15.4%) had recurrent vitreous seeds. Morphologically, seeds were classified as dust (n=7, 26.9%), spheres (n=6, 23.1%) and clouds (n=13, 50.0%).

Patients received primary therapy with IVC (n=8), IAC (n=8) or both (n=10). IVC regimens included vincristine, etoposide and carboplatin administered in 4–8 cycles (mean=6.3 cycles). IAC involved melphalan combined with topotecan over 2–5 cycles (mean=3.6 cycles). Adjuvant therapies included cryotherapy (performed in 17/26 eyes; mean=3 sessions; range=1–4) and TTT (performed in 22/26 eyes; mean=3 sessions; range=1–5). Additionally, 36 periocular topotecan injections (1 mg) were administered in 18 eyes (mean=2 injections; median=2; range=1–3).

In earlier cases, seven eyes received combined intravitreal melphalan (20–30 µg) and topotecan (30 µg), totalling 22 melphalan injections (mean=3 sessions; median=3; range=1–5) at 2-week to 4-week intervals. Subsequently, all 19 remaining eyes received intravitreal topotecan monotherapy (30 µg). A total of 105 intravitreal topotecan injections were administered, with a mean of 4 injections per eye (median=4; range=3–6).

Complete regression of vitreous seeds was achieved in 24 eyes (92.3%), while 2 eyes (7.7%) showed complete calcification of the seeds with stable disease (figures1 2). One patient who underwent three cycles of IAC combined with three intravitreal topotecan injections required enucleation due to retrobulbar optic nerve thickening with sheath enhancement on postoperative MRI. This patient received two cycles of the IVC regimen prior to enucleation. Among the seven eyes treated with combined intravitreal melphalan and topotecan, one eye required enucleation due to phthisis bulbi. Histopathological examination of enucleated eyes revealed no evidence of intraocular tumour recurrence or active tumour cells in vitreous seeds. No recurrence of vitreous seeds or intraocular tumours was observed during the 24-month follow-up period. Systemic metastasis or mortality did not occur in any patient.

The combined melphalan group (n=7) exhibited higher rates of ocular complications, including cataracts (n=2), retinal pigment epithelium and choroidal atrophy (n=3), pupillary synechiae (n=2), optic atrophy (n=2), retinal vascular occlusion (n=2) and phthisis bulbi (n=1). In contrast, the topotecan monotherapy group (n=19) showed no cases of retinal toxicity, pigmentary retinopathy or significant ERG changes. Flicker ERG recordings at 30 Hz in the monotherapy group demonstrated stable responses, with a non-significant slope of −0.6 µV per injection in eight eyes (p=0.85).

Discussion

Chemotherapy in the form of IAC and IVC has significantly improved the prognosis of RB. However, vitreous seeds recurrence remains a critical challenge, observed in 50% of eyes treated with primary IVC and 33% of eyes treated with IAC.14 19 Even with intravitreal melphalan, Munier et al reported a 19% recurrence rate of vitreous seeds.15 Once recurrence occurs, supplemental IAC and repeated intravitreal melphalan are often required. However, our initial experience with intravitreal melphalan revealed significant anterior and posterior segment toxicity, with the severity of inflammation potentially linked to the darkly pigmented iris common in Chinese patients.17 Topotecan, a topoisomerase I inhibitor, has demonstrated high efficacy in achieving vitreous seeds regression with minimal ocular toxicity. Preclinical studies in rabbits showed that intravitreal topotecan (15 µg) was equally effective as melphalan in treating WERI-Rb1 cell xenografts, yet caused no retinal toxicity, whereas melphalan (12.5 µg, equivalent to 25 µg in humans) reduced ERG amplitudes by 42%–79%.20 These findings motivated our investigation into whether two additional intravitreal topotecan injections following tumour and vitreous seeds control could optimise consolidation strategies.

The additional injections significantly improved long-term outcomes, achieving 100% vitreous seeds regression and a 92.3% eye salvage rate. Notably, our cohort included 42.9% group E patients, a population historically associated with poor outcomes. This salvage rate surpasses previously reported rates,1821,23 underscoring the value of consolidation therapy. All procedures were performed under strict aseptic conditions, and patients were carefully monitored for any adverse events. Although the risk of endophthalmitis is very low, and the overall risk of endophthalmitis after intravitreal injection is 0.035%–0.131%. It should be recognised as a potential complication of intravitreal injections.

Prior to 2022, intravitreal melphalan was our primary approach, with tandem topotecan therapy reserved for suboptimal responses. However, due to melphalan’s ocular toxicity and high cost, we transitioned to topotecan monotherapy. Since 2022, topotecan has been our first-line intravitreal agent. Following tumour and seeds control, two additional topotecan injections were administered, yielding excellent therapeutic outcomes. The safety profile of topotecan was favourable, with no severe retinal toxicity or extraocular tumour spread observed. ERG data confirmed that doses up to 30 µg caused no retinal toxicity or ERG reductions. Abramson et al further validated the safety of high-dose intravitreal topotecan (90 µg) without adverse effects,24 reinforcing its clinical utility.

Our study demonstrates encouraging therapeutic outcomes, particularly in advanced RB. Notably, no retinal tumour recurrence was observed in this cohort, likely attributable to aggressive focal consolidation therapy—specifically, a mean of three sessions of TTT and/or cryotherapy targeting residual retinal tumours. While the advent of IAC has led some clinicians to reduce the use of adjunctive focal therapies, we suggest the critical role of repeated TTT and cryotherapy in reducing recurrence rates—the primary cause of enucleation.11 22 We hypothesise that the ‘plus two’ injections provide additional security, particularly in advanced or high-risk eyes, by potentially reducing residual microscopic disease and delaying or preventing recurrence. Two additional injections can maintain the intravitreal drug concentration at an effective oncolytic level, covering the high-risk period for recurrence. Abramson et al previously reported direct antitumour effects of intravitreal topotecan on retinal lesions,24 suggesting that supplementary injections may also exert direct antitumour effects on residual retinal lesions.

There are a few limitations to the study that should be discussed: first, its retrospective and non-randomised design; and second, the limited size of the sample. Multicentre prospective trials are needed to validate these findings and refine protocols. Nevertheless, our results highlight the potential of additional topotecan injections to reduce recurrence and improve eye salvage rates.

Conclusion

Additional intravitreal topotecan injections (‘plus two’) after complete regression of vitreous seeds significantly reduce recurrence in patients with RB, particularly in advanced cases (groups D/E). This strategy enhances eye salvage rates while minimising toxicity. Further prospective studies are needed to validate these findings and optimise the treatment protocol.

Patient and public involvement statement

Involvement in this study

Patients and the public were not directly involved in the design, conduct or dissemination of this research.

Rationale for non-involvement

Study design: This was a retrospective analysis of clinical data, limiting opportunities for patient involvement during the research process.

Patient population: The study focused on RB, a rare paediatric intraocular malignancy requiring highly specialised treatment. Given the young age of the participants (median age: 26 months) and the technical nature of intravitreal chemotherapy, direct engagement was not feasible.

Clinical context: Treatment decisions were made by a multidisciplinary medical team based on standard protocols, with informed consent obtained from parents/guardians for all procedures.

Future considerations: For prospective studies, we plan to explore engagement with RB patient advocacy groups (eg, International Retinoblastoma Consortium) to incorporate family perspectives on treatment experiences and guide the development of patient-centred outcome measures.

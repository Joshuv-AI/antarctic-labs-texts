Alginate-PVA interpenetrating network nanogels: a pH-responsive carrier for thermal stabilization and sustained release of phycocyanin
Authors: Sadeeq Muhammad Sheshe, Sarawut Cheunkar, Sukunya Oaew
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPhycocyanin (PC) is a sensitive bioactive protein that suffers from poor thermal stability and premature release in conventional alginate (Alg) hydrogels. In this study, Alg–polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) interpenetrating polymer network (IPN) nanogels were developed as a pH-responsive carrier for improved PC stabilization and controlled oral delivery. Increasing PVA concentration produced nanogels with particle sizes ranging from 174.01 ± 2.66 to 255.02 ± 68.04 nm and significantly improved encapsulation efficiency from 69.59 ± 0.51% to 92.20 ± 0.84%, with a maximum loading capacity of 8.24 ± 0.06%. PVA incorporation also enhanced thermal stability and reduced PC release under simulated gastric conditions (<10% release at pH 1.2), followed by sustained release in simulated intestinal fluid (pH 7.4). Cytocompatibility studies using Caco-2 cells demonstrated acceptable biocompatibility at lower concentrations. Overall, Alg–PVA IPN nanogels provide a promising platform for improving the stability and controlled delivery of sensitive protein-based bioactives.Keywords: Phycocyanin, Nanogel, Interpenetrating network, Sustained release, Encapsulation

Introduction

Recent years have witnessed a growing interest in naturally derived bioactive compounds from terrestrial plants, marine organisms, and microorganisms for their integration into nutraceutical formulations. These compounds are valued for their substantial health-promoting properties that extend beyond basic nutritional value (Aware et al., 2022; Nikbakht Nasrabadi et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2024). Among them, phycocyanin (PC), a phycobiliprotein primarily extracted from Spirulina platensis, has attracted considerable attention owing to its vivid blue pigment and a broad spectrum of pharmacological activities, including antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anticancer, antihypertensive, and neuroprotective effects (Abouzeid et al., 2022; Adjali et al., 2022; Hao et al., 2022; Li et al., 2022; Rojas-Franco et al., 2022; Shafieiyoun et al., 2024; Zabot et al., 2022). Despite its promising functional properties, the practical application of PC remains significantly constrained by its inherent instability. PC is highly susceptible to degradation under elevated temperature, extreme pH conditions, light exposure, and enzymatic activity in the gastrointestinal tract. This degradation results in loss of bioactivity and limiting its industrial and biomedical utilization (Putra et al., 2025; Yuan et al., 2022). Consequently, the development of effective delivery systems capable of stabilizing PC while enabling controlled release is both necessary and challenging (Dissanayake & Bandara, 2024).

To overcome these challenges, a wide range of encapsulation strategies have been investigated over the past two decades, including double emulsion (Teixé-Roig et al., 2022), extrusion method (Mukherjee, 2025), electrospinning (Wen et al., 2020), freeze-drying (Pan-Utai & Iamtham, 2020), and microfluidics (Tangkatitham et al., 2024). These techniques have enabled the fabrication of diverse carriers such as liposomes (Akbarbaglu et al., 2025; Manconia et al., 2009; Sahin et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024), hydrogels (Lemos et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2026; Yuan et al., 2026), and nanofibers (Wen et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2023). More recently, a hybrid approach that integrates emulsification process with extrusion has been proposed to enhance PC encapsulation (Han et al., 2026). While these methods have demonstrated improvements in stability and bioavailability, they remain constrained by critical drawbacks such as particle aggregation, size heterogeneity, limited structural integrity, and scalability issues, which restrict their broader application, particularly in systemic delivery systems (Bhujel et al., 2021; Moreira et al., 2019; Pradeep & Nayak, 2019).

Hydrogel-based encapsulation systems, particularly polymeric networks, have attracted increasing attention due to their inherent biocompatibility, tunable physicochemical properties, and ability to provide protective matrices for sensitive biomolecules (Costa et al., 2022; Mohamadnia et al., 2007; Onal et al., 2024). Among naturally-derived hydrogel forming materials, alginate, a polysaccharide extracted from seaweed, is widely used because of its mild gelation via divalent cation crosslinking, cost-effectiveness, and GRAS (generally recognized as safe) status (Colin et al., 2024). To date, only a limited number of studies have reported the encapsulation of PC within alginate hydrogels (Li et al., 2024; Paiboon et al., 2023; Rueangkrachai et al., 2025; Sahin et al., 2023; Sheshe et al., 2026). Although these systems successfully entrapped PC in spherical beads, they continue to suffer from frequent burst release, low acid resistance, and poor retention. These drawbacks are primarily attributed to the relatively large matrix porosity, weak mechanical strength, and high proton exchange capacity of alginate matrices (Akbarbaglu et al., 2025; Akbarzadeh Solbu et al., 2022; Godfrey et al., 2026; Liang et al., 2026; Yan et al., 2014). Such limitations are particularly pronounced for small, hydrophilic molecules like PC, which tend to diffuse rapidly from unmodified alginate networks.

Interpenetrating polymer networks (IPNs) hydrogels have recently emerged as a promising strategy to overcome the inherent limitations of conventional alginate-based encapsulation (Mukherjee et al., 2024). In alginate-based semi-IPN systems, linear polymers such as chitosan, starch, and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) are interlaced within the alginate network, resulting in synergistic improvements in hydrogel properties (Anwar et al., 2017; Mukherjee et al., 2024). For instance, in alginate (Alg)-PVA IPN hydrogels, although the interactions between alginate and PVA are primarily non-covalent, the combination of alginate's swelling behavior with the mechanical reinforcement provided by PVA leads to enhanced structural integrity, reduced porosity, and improved pH resistance (Bahadoran et al., 2020; Jiang et al., 2018; Massana Roquero et al., 2021). These features impart Alg-PVA IPNs highly suitable for controlled delivery applications, where sustained release and protection against premature degradation are essential (Khalid et al., 2018). Despite their potential, the application of Alg-PVA IPN hydrogels for encapsulating sensitive bioactive proteins such as PC remain limited (Buliga et al., 2024). Existing studies have primarily focused on bulk hydrogels or modified systems for wound healing applications, with relatively few reports addressing nanoscale delivery systems with controlled release functionality (Biswas et al., 2021; Kulkarni et al., 2010). Furthermore, commonly used fabrication methods, such as freeze-thawing, often involve conditions that are not suitable for thermosensitive protein like PC (Muangsri et al., 2022). In addition, formulation components such as crosslinking agents may introduce toxicity concerns. For example, glutaraldehyde and boric acid have been reported to significantly increase cytotoxicity in hydrogel systems (Gough et al., 2002; Li et al., 1998).

In this study, we developed an Alg-PVA IPN nanogel system as a pH-responsive, biocompatible carrier for PC with the aim of enhancing its thermal stability and enabling sustained release while preserving bioactivity during oral delivery. To achieve this, an internal gelation approach combined with ultrasonic emulsification was employed to fabricate nanogels with improved structural uniformity and encapsulation efficiency. Unlike conventional external gelation, internal gelation enables in situ calcium ion release within dispersed droplets, providing greater control over matrix uniformity and encapsulation efficiency. The incorporation of PVA was designed to address key limitations of traditional alginate-based carriers by reinforcing network stability, reducing premature release, and enhancing stability under varying environmental conditions. The resulting nanogels were systematically characterized for their physicochemical properties, encapsulation efficiency, thermal stability, in vitro release behavior, and cytocompatibility across different Alg-to-PVA ratios. Taken together, this work introduces a novel Alg-PVA IPN nanogel platform that mitigates the limitations of conventional alginate-based carriers and enables the effective delivery of sensitive bioactive compounds. By enhancing stability, sustaining release, and preserving bioactivity, it advances the development of IPN-based nanogel systems for applications in food and biomedicine.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Polyvinyl alcohol (PVA, MW = 88,000 g/mol) and phycocyanin (PC) powder were obtained from TCI (Tokyo, Japan). Citric acid, sodium alginate (Alg, M/G ratio = 1.56), and calcium-disodium ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (Ca-EDTA) were purchased from Sigma–Aldrich (St. louis, MO, USA). Polyglycerol-polyricinoleate (PGPR), polysorbitate 80 (Tween80), and medium chained triglyceride oil (MCT oil food grade) were acquired from Myskinrecipes chemical (Bangkok, Thailand). Sodium dihydrogen phosphate dihydrate (NaH2PO4.2H2O) and sodium phosphate dibasic heptahydrate (Na2H2PO4.7H2O) were procured from Ajax finechem (Auburn, Australia). Acetic acid was obtained from QReC (New Zealand). Hydrochloric acid (HCl) was purchased from RCI Labscan limited (Bangkok, Thailand), and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) was bought from PINE chemical (Nonthaburi, Thailand). Deionized (DI) water purified by a Milli-Q filtration system (Merck, Germany) was used for sample preparation.

Emulsification, gelation, and IPN formation

In this study, phycocyanin (PC) was encapsulated within a nanogel matrix using water-in-oil (W/O) emulsions prepared via an internal gelation mechanism. Emulsions were formulated by dispersing a water phase (W) into an oil phase (O) using an ultrasonic processor (VCX 130 PB, Sonics & Materials, Inc., USA) equipped with a 3-mm probe, operated for 2 min at 30% amplitude in an ice bath. The W-phase contained sodium alginate (Alg), PC (3.33% w/v), Ca-EDTA (0.4 M), and a varying concentration of polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) while the O-phase comprised MCT oil and a lipophilic emulsifier, PGPR (8% w/v). The internal gelation was triggered by the dropwise addition of 0.5% (v/v) acetic acid as described by Paiboon et al. (Paiboon et al., 2023). Particle characterization

The physicochemical properties, including hydrodynamic diameters (Z-average size), and polydispersity index (PDI), were determined using a particle analyzer (Zetasizer, Nano-ZS, Malvern analytical, United Kingdom). For each measurement, 200 μL of sample was dispersed in 300 μL of deionized (DI) water in a disposable cuvette and placed into the measuring chamber.

The morphology of the Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was examined using a scanning electron microscope (Phenom Pharos Desktop FEG-SEM, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Netherlands) operated at 10 kV under a high vacuum mode with gold‑palladium coating. Samples for SEM analysis were prepared by drop-drying the nanogel suspension onto a clean silicon wafer and dried in a desiccator. The wafer was then placed on a stub and inserted into the visualizing chamber.

The analysis of chemical functional groups of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was carried out using a Fourier-transform infrared spectrometer (FT-IR) (INVENIO R, BRUKER, Switzerland).

Thermal stability test

The thermal stability of PC-loaded Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was evaluated to determine the effect of IPN modification on resistance to elevated temperatures and premature PC release, following the protocol of Pradeep & Nayak. (Pradeep & Nayak, 2019), with slight modification. Briefly, 10 mg of dried nanogel particles from each formulation were suspended in DI water and incubated at 37, 50, 60, 70, and 80 °C for 10 min in a rotary incubator shaker (Kuhner LT-X, Thailand) at 200 rpm. After incubation, the samples were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm (RCF = 6753×g) for 10 min, and the supernatants were collected for absorbance measurement. The residual nanogel pellets were subsequently re-suspended in citrate-phosphate buffer (0.1 M, pH 5.5) and incubated at room temperature for 10 min with continuous agitation at 200 rpm to solubilize the nanogel matrix. Following this treatment, samples were centrifuged at 10,000 rpm, and the absorbance of the collected supernatants was measured at 620 nm and 652 nm, corresponding to the characteristic absorbance peaks of PC.

In vitro release study

The in vitro release of PC from Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was evaluated under simulated gastrointestinal conditions following the protocol of Kim et al. (Kim et al., 2021), with minor modifications. Briefly, 10 mg of each PC-loaded Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was suspended in 1 mL of HCl solution (pH 1.2) and incubated at 37 °C in a rotatory shaker (200 rpm) for 2 h to simulate gastric conditions. At predetermined 30-min intervals, 200 μL of the release medium was withdrawn for absorbance measurement, and an equivalent volume of fresh buffer was immediately refilled to maintain constant conditions. After the 2-h gastric phase, the nanogels were separated by centrifugation, and the HCl solution was completely removed. The collected nanogels were then resuspended in 1 mL of phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) and incubated for an additional 5 h under the same conditions to mimic the intestinal phase. Similarly, 200 μL of the release medium was withdrawn at 30-min interval for absorbance measurement, with equivalent buffer replacement. In vitro cytotoxicity assay

The cytotoxicity of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels with different formulations was evaluated using human colorectal adenocarcinoma (Caco-2) intestinal cell lines, following the protocol of Sathiensathaporn et al. (Sathiensathaporn et al., 2025) with modifications. Caco-2 cells cultured in minimum essential medium (MEM) were seeded into a 96-well plate at a density of 1.0 × 105 cells/well and allowed to adhere for 24 h. The culture medium was then replaced with fresh medium containing serial dilutions of Alg-PVA IPN nanogel formulations (AP-0 to AP-4). After 24 h of exposure, the treatment medium was removed, and cells were gently washed with complete medium to eliminate residual nanogels. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. A solution of 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyl-2H-tetrazolium bromide (MTT, 5 mg/mL in PBS) was added at 10 μL/well and incubated for 4 h at 37 °C in a carbon dioxide (CO2) incubator. Following incubation, 100 μL/well of isopropanol containing HCl (0.04 M) was added to dissolve the purple formazan crystals. Absorbance was measured at 540 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer. Statistical analysis

All experiments were performed in triplicate unless otherwise stated, and the results were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Statistical analyses were conducted using the data analysis function of Microsoft Excel (version 2603). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied, followed by post hoc pairwise comparisons when significant differences were observed. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results and discussion

Effect of the W/O phase ratios on physicochemical properties of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels

Prior to optimizing PVA concentration, the influence of the water-in-oil (W/O) phase ratios (35/65, 25/75, and 15/85) on the physicochemical properties of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was examined using 8% (w/v) PGPR at a fixed Alg:PVA ratio of 1:1, PC concentration of 3.33% (w/v), Ca-EDTA concentration of 0.4 M, and acetic acid concentration of 0.5% (v/v). As shown in Fig. 1, decreasing W/O ratio from 35/65 to 15/85 resulted in a marked reduction in particle size (from 996.34 ± 299.44 nm to 255.02 ± 68.04 nm) and polydispersity index (PDI) (from 0.51 ± 0.27 to 0.34 ± 0.08). The observed reduction in particle size can be attributed to increased shear-induced droplet disruption and reduced droplet coalescence during emulsification, which promote the formation of smaller and more stable dispersed droplets (Islam & Karim, 2010). Similar effects of lower W/O ratios on emulsion droplet size and particle size distribution have also been reported in previous W/O emulsion systems (Pan et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2017). The improved uniformity observed at lower aqueous phase fractions may additionally reflect a more efficient stabilization of dispersed droplets by PGPR within the continuous oil phase (Sheshe et al., 2026). Based on these findings, the 15/85 W/O ratio was selected for subsequent formulation studies, as it yielded smaller particle sizes and narrower size distribution, which are favourable characteristics for enhanced colloidal stability and dispersibility.

Effect of PVA concentration on physicochemical properties of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels

The formation and physicochemical properties of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels are strongly influenced by the incorporation of PVA during the internal gelation process, as the secondary polymeric network introduced by PVA modulates the microstructural arrangement and crosslinking density of the hydrogel matrix (Liang et al., 2024). To investigate these effects, formulations designated AP-0 to AP-4 were prepared by varying PVA content in the W-phase from 0 to 3.33% (w/v), corresponding to Alg:PVA ratios of 1:0, 1:0.125, 1:0.25, 1:0.5 and 1:1, respectively (Table 1).

As summarized in Table 1, particle sizes increased progressively with increasing PVA concentration, ranging from 174.01 ± 2.66 nm for AP-0 to 255.02 ± 68.04 nm for AP-4. This enlargement is attributed to the higher viscosity of the aqueous phase at elevated PVA concentrations, which reduces shear-induced droplet disruption during ultrasonic emulsification and consequently generates larger emulsion droplets that later form larger gelled particles. In addition, PVA incorporation has been reported to enhance crosslinking density and intermolecular entanglement in alginate-based systems, contributing to increased particle sizes in both nanoparticle and electrospun nanofiber formulations (Islam & Karim, 2010). The presence of hydroxyl groups on PVA chains further facilitates intermolecular interactions with the alginate network, reinforcing the gel matrix and promoting particle enlargement (Xiao et al., 2000). A similar concentration-dependent trend was observed for the particle size distribution, with PDI values increased from 0.21 ± 0.01 in AP-0 to 0.34 ± 0.08 in AP-4. This broader particle size distribution at higher PVA concentrations suggests that increased aqueous-phase viscosity hindered homogeneous droplet formation during ultrasonic emulsification, resulting in greater droplet size variability (Menegatti et al., 2024; Pan et al., 2021; Sahin et al., 2023).

In contrast, the incorporation of PVA significantly improved PC encapsulation efficiency (%EE). The %EE increased from 69.59 ± 0.51% in AP-0 to 92.20 ± 0.84% in AP-2, suggesting that moderate PVA concentrations promote effective PC entrapment within the IPN matrix. This improvement can be associated with the formation of a denser and more cohesive polymer network, which reduced PC diffusion during internal gelation. However, further increases in PVA concentrations yielded marginal improvements in %EE with AP-3 and AP-4 exhibiting values of 90.72 ± 0.40% and 92.70 ± 1.20%, respectively. This plateau behavior suggests that the polymer network approaches a limiting packing density, beyond which additional PVA concentration no longer substantially enhanced PC entrapment (Menegatti et al., 2024; Sakhi et al., 2023). A comparable trend was also observed for loading capacity (%LC), which peaked at 8.24 ± 0.06% in AP-2 before decreasing slightly at higher PVA concentrations. This reduction reflects the increased total polymer mass of the larger IPN nanogels, while the amount of encapsulated PC remained relatively constant. Collectively, these findings identify AP-2 (0.8% w/v PVA and Alg, PVA ratio = 1,0.25) as the optimal formulation that balances between nanoscale particle size, high encapsulation efficiency, and desirable loading capacity.

Chemical and physical characterization of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels

Alg-PVA IPNs are expected to form through two primary mechanisms: (i) ionic crosslinking of alginate by Ca2+ via the well-known “egg-box” domains, and (ii) extensive interpolymer hydrogen bonding between alginate hydroxyl/carboxyl groups and PVA hydroxyl groups. To investigate these interactions and confirm the formation of the IPN structure, FTIR analysis was performed on non-PC loaded nanogels (AP-0 to AP-4), alginate powder, and PVA powder.

As shown in Fig. 2, a broad absorption band in the range of 3225–3318 cm−1 was observed in all formulations and control samples, corresponding to O—H stretching vibrations associated with hydroxyl groups in alginate and PVA (Mansur et al., 2008). In addition, the absorption band at 2917–2925 cm−1, assigned to aliphatic C—H stretching vibrations of the polymer backbone, also became more pronounced with increasing PVA content (Yumin et al., 2022). These observations collectively support the successful incorporation of PVA into the Alg-PVA nanogel system.

FT-IR spectra of non-PC-loaded Alg-PVA IPN nanogels at formulations AP-0 to AP-4.

In the fingerprint region, a distinct band at around 1729–1743 cm−1 appeared in AP-0 and remained detectable in PVA-containing formulations (AP-1 to AP-4). This band was also observed in PVA powder but absent in alginate powder, indicating contributions from residual acetate (ester) groups commonly present in partially hydrolysed commercial PVA (Blout & Karplus, 1948; Teixeira et al., 2024). Interestingly, the appearance of the 1733 cm−1 band in AP-0 (PVA free) suggests partial protonation of alginate carboxylate residues during acidic-triggered internal gelation, resulting in the formation of protonated carboxylic acid moieties (Valentin et al., 2005). This interpretation is supported by previous studies reporting that alginate gels prepared under non-acidic gelation conditions did not exhibit comparable absorption bands in this region (Lin & Xu, 2026; Wunnoo et al., 2026). The two prominent bands located at 1575–1592 cm−1 and 1405–1427 cm−1 were assigned to asymmetric and symmetric stretching vibrations of carboxylate (—COO−) groups, respectively. These bands were present in all alginate-containing formulations but absent in pure PVA, confirming the retention of alginate carboxylate functionalities within the nanogel matrix. Their presence and relatively small separation (175–190 cm−1) are characteristic of carboxylate groups participating in ionic coordination with Ca2+ in the alginate egg-box structures (Hua et al., 2010; Nara & Tanokura, 2008). Minor shifts of these bands across AP-1 to AP-4 indicate subtle changes in the local environment as the PVA network interpenetrates alginate domains.

Additional absorption bands observed in the range of 1236–1288 cm−1 were attributed primarily to C—O stretching vibrations of residual acetate groups of PVA. The increased intensity and slightly broadening of these bands with rising PVA content (AP-1 to AP-4) further support the successful interpenetration of PVA within the alginate network (Yumin et al., 2022). At lower wavenumbers, a strong band appearing at 1024–1079 cm−1 corresponded to C—O—C and C—OH stretching vibrations associated with alginate glycosidic linkages and the PVA backbone. Importantly, no new absorption bands indicative of covalent bond formation were detected, suggesting that the Alg-PVA IPN is formed through ionic crosslinking (alginate-Ca2+) and extensive hydrogen bonding (alginate-PVA) rather than chemical grafting.

The morphology of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was examined by scanning electron microscope (SEM), and representative micrographs are presented in Fig. 3. The images reveal a clear increase in apparent particle diameter with rising PVA content. The PVA-free formulation (AP-0) exhibited the smallest particles, whereas AP-3 and AP-4 displayed visibly larger structures. This morphological trend is consistent with the hydrodynamic diameters measured by DLS (Table 1), confirming that PVA plays a critical role in modulating droplet formation during ultrasonic emulsification, with particle size fixed upon internal gelation. Mechanistically, incorporation of PVA increases the viscosity of the dispersed aqueous phase, thereby altering the viscosity ratio relative to the continuous oil phase. Elevated viscosity reduces shear efficiency during ultrasonic emulsification, leading to larger primary droplets that subsequently form larger gelled particles. In addition, PVA enhances the viscoelasticity of the dispersed phase and promotes hydrogen-bonding with alginate. During internal gelation, the in situ release of Ca2+ ions accelerate network formation, which arrests droplet thinning at an earlier stage and preserves larger particle sizes. These observations align with previous reports on alginate-PVA semi-IPN hydrogels, where higher PVA content strengthened the network through hydrogen bonding and reduced porosity, thereby producing larger and more mechanically stable particles (Khalid et al., 2018).

Thermal stability of PC-loaded Alg-PVA IPN nanogels

The formation of IPN networks is known to enhance the mechanical reinforcement of hydrogels, thereby improving thermal resistance and preventing premature release of encapsulated compounds (Khalid et al., 2018). To evaluate this effect, Alg-PVA IPN nanogels (AP-0 to AP-4) with increasing PVA concentrations (0–3.33% w/v) were tested for their ability to protect encapsulated PC under elevated temperatures. The experimental protocol involved two sequential steps: (i) incubation in deionized (DI) water at 37–80 °C for 10 min to quantify PC released into the aqueous phase (supernatant), followed by (ii) solubilizing the same hydrogels in citrate-phosphate buffer (pH 5.5) to determine the amount of PC retained within the capsules after thermal exposure (Fig. 4).

Thermal stability of PC-loaded Alg-PVA IPN nanogel formulations suspended in water (A) and in citrate-phosphate buffer (B) at 37–80 °C with varying PVA concentrations. Error bars represent mean ± SD from triplicate experiments (n = 3). ND indicates not detectable.

During incubation in water, the low-PVA formulations (AP-0 and AP-1) released detectable amount of PC at 37 °C and 50 °C, with AP-0 also showing leakage at 60 °C (Fig. 4A). Corresponding absorbance peaks (Fig. S1A) confirmed the presence of PC released in these supernatants. In contrast, no measurable PC release was observed from AP-2, AP-3, and AP-4 across all temperatures, indicating that higher PVA content sufficiently reinforced the hydrogel networks to prevent PC diffusion. At ≥60 °C, released PC signals diminished due to thermal degradation (Fig. S1A), consistent with the reported thermal degradation threshold of PC (47–50 °C) (Chaiklahan et al., 2014). A comparable loss of PC encapsulation and detection under thermal stress (> 70 °C) has also been observed in nanoliposomes coated with chitosan and pectin (Akbarbaglu et al., 2025).

The protective effect of the hydrogels was further evaluated by digesting thermally treated pellets in citrate-phosphate buffer (Fig. 4B). At 37 °C, the highest retained PC concentrations were observed in AP-4, followed by AP-3 and AP-2, whereas AP-0 and AP-1 exhibited significantly lower values. A decreasing trend in PC retention from AP-4 to AP-0 was evident as the incubation temperature increased to 80 °C. All formulations showed reduced PC recovery at elevated temperatures, with the most pronounced decline in AP-0 to AP-2. This reduction reflects both thermal degradation of PC and insufficient structural integrity of low-PVA hydrogels to protect the encapsulated protein.

The superior performance of AP-3 and AP-4 is attributed to the formation of denser and more interconnected Alg-PVA IPN networks, driven by hydrogen bonding between hydroxyl groups of PVA and oxygenated groups of alginate. This enhanced crosslinking reduces mesh size, strengthens the network, and improves resistance to thermal stress, thereby increasing the mechanical strength and thermal stability of the hydrogel (Hu et al., 2020). Similar findings have been reported in other polysaccharide-based systems, where polymer networks composed of alginate and polymers such as PVA and chitosan exhibit enhanced thermal stability. For example, Yang et al. (Yang et al., 2020) reported increased thermal resistance in alginate-PVA composites encapsulating melanin, while El-Din et al. (El-Din et al., 2007) demonstrated that the mechanical strength and durability of alginate-PVA hydrogels improve with increasing concentrations polymer concentrations. These results confirm that higher PVA content promotes the formation of larger and more interconnected polymer networks, which are critical for enhancing hydrogel thermal resistance and preventing premature release of encapsulated PC.

In vitro release of PC from Alg-PVA IPN nanogels

The in vitro release behavior of PC from Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was evaluated under simulated gastrointestinal conditions to investigate their pH-responsive characteristics and the influence of PVA content on release kinetics. Formulations AP-0 to AP-4 were first incubated in simulated gastric fluid (SGF, pH 1.2) for 2 h, followed by transfer to simulated intestinal fluid (SIF, pH 7.4) for an additional 5 h. The cumulative release profiles are presented in Fig. 5.

In vitro cumulative release profiles of PC-loaded Alg-PVA IPN nanogels (formulations AP-0 to AP-4) in simulated gastric fluid (SGF, pH 1.2) and simulated intestinal fluid (SIF, pH 7.4).

During the gastric phase, all formulations exhibited minimal release (<5%), confirming effective protection of PC under acidic conditions. Upon transfer to SIF, however, release rate increased markedly, with the extent of release strongly dependent on PVA content. The control formulation without PVA (AP-0) exhibited the fastest release, reaching nearly 100% cumulative release within 5 h. In contrast, PVA-containing formulations showed progressively slower release, with AP-3 and AP-4 releasing less than 50% of their total PC content over the same period. The suppressed release in SGF can be attributed to protonation of the carboxylate groups in alginate at low pH, which reduces electrostatic repulsion and induces network contraction, thereby limiting PC diffusion (Chuang et al., 2017; George & Abraham, 2007; Mehmood et al., 2023). Similar acid-resistant behavior has been reported for tramadol-loaded Alg-PVA hydrogels modified with acrylamido-methyl propane sulphonic acid, suggesting the inherent pH sensitivity of alginate-based matrices (Khalid et al., 2018). Upon transfer to SIF, the deprotonation of alginate carboxyl groups at neutral pH promotes electrostatic repulsion and network swelling, facilitating PC diffusion through enlarged pores. The release-suppressing effect of PVA containing formulations aligns with previous findings for rosuvastatin-loaded chitosan nanoparticles embedded in Alg-PVA hydrogels, which achieved less than 70% cumulative release (Afshar et al., 2020). Comparable suppressive behavior (only 60% cumulative release) was reported in alginate-carrageenan IPN beads in nanoliposomes after 5 h in SIF (Mohamadnia et al., 2007). Overall, these results confirm that Alg-PVA IPN nanogels exhibited desirable pH-responsive behavior, effectively protecting PC under acidic gastric conditions while enabling controlled release in the intestinal environment. The presence of PVA further modulates release kinetics by reinforcing the hydrogel network, thereby reducing premature leakage and sustaining release over time.

In vitro cytotoxicity of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels in Caco-2 cells

The cytotoxicity of Alg-PVA IPN nanogels was evaluated using the human colorectal adenocarcinoma (Caco-2) cell line, a widely accepted in vitro model of the intestinal epithelium for assessing biocompatibility in oral delivery applications. Cells were exposed to Alg-PVA IPN nanogel formulations (AP-0 to AP-4) at six dose levels ranging from 0.0313 to 1 mg/mL, and cell viability was assessed after 24 h of incubation (Fig. 6). According to ISO 10993-5 guidelines, materials are considered cytotoxic when cell viability falls below 70% following treatment (Assad & Jackson, 2019; Sathiensathaporn et al., 2025). The results revealed a clear dose-dependent cytotoxic effect across all formulations, with moderate to severe toxicity observed at the higher concentrations (0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg/mL). Importantly, cell viability at 0.25 and 0.5 mg/mL decreased progressively with increasing PVA content. In contrast, at lower concentrations (0.0313, 0.0625, and 0.125 mg/mL), all formulations maintained cell viability above 70%, meeting the ISO safety threshold and suggesting acceptable biocompatibility. These findings indicate that cytotoxicity becomes progressively more pronounced as both PVA concentration and formulation dose increased.

Previous studies have reported that Alg-PVA IPNs generally exhibit low cytotoxicity due to the inherent biocompatibility of both polymers (Abouzeid et al., 2022; Rasyida et al., 2023). Nonetheless, emerging evidence suggests that higher concentrations of PVA can exert cytotoxic effects, particularly in sensitive or cancer-derived cell lines. For instance, Baca Ramos et al. (Baca Ramos et al., 2023) observed a moderate decrease in fibroblast viability when exposed to PVA-based hydrogels at ≥4% (w/v). Similarly, slight toxicity has been reported in cancer cell models exposed to PVA-containing scaffolds, potentially due to alterations in cell adhesion or membrane disruption (Nathan et al., 2023). The dose and PVA-dependent cytotoxicity observed in this study may also be influenced by the viscosity and surface characteristics of the hydrogels, as seen in Fig. 3. At higher concentrations, PVA may hinder the diffusion of nutrients and oxygen or physically obstruct cell attachment and spreading (Baca Ramos et al., 2023). Moreover, the denser and more tightly crosslinked networks of high-PVA IPNs (AP3 and AP-4) could interfere with cellular processes due to increased mechanical rigidity or altered degradation profiles (Hago & Li, 2013). In summary, while low concentrations of Alg-PVA nanogels appear safe for intestinal applications, high PVA content, particularly at elevated doses, significantly compromises biocompatibility. These findings suggest the importance of optimizing PVA concentration to balance encapsulation performance with acceptable cytotoxicity for oral delivery applications.

Conclusion

In this study, alginate-polyvinyl alcohol (Alg-PVA) interpenetrating network (IPN) nanogels were successfully developed as a pH-responsive delivery system for phycocyanin (PC), a thermally sensitive hydrophilic bioactive compound. Five formulations (AP-0 to AP-4) were fabricated via ultrasonic emulsification combined with internal gelation, with systematic modulation of physiochemical properties achieved by varying PVA content. Increasing PVA concentration resulted in larger particle size and broader size distribution, while a moderate PVA level of 0.8% (w/v) provided the most favourable balance between encapsulation efficiency (∼ 92%) and loading capacity (∼ 8.2%). Higher PVA contents further enhanced thermal protection, suppressing premature PC release and preserving PC stability up to 60 °C. Under simulated gastrointestinal conditions, all formulations exhibited minimal release in simulated gastric fluid (pH 1.2) and sustained release in simulated intestinal fluid (pH 7.4), confirming the pH-responsive behavior of the system. Biocompatibility assay in Caco-2 cells indicated acceptable safety at low concentrations, though higher doses, particularly in formulations with elevated PVA content, reduced cell viability in a dose-dependent manner. Overall, these findings demonstrate that Alg-PVA IPN nanogels are a promising platform for the oral delivery of thermally sensitive and hydrophilic bioactives such as PC. The combination of high encapsulation efficiency, improved thermal protection, and tunable release behavior highlights their potential for nutraceutical and pharmaceutical applications. However, several limitations must be acknowledged. The present study was limited to in vitro release and cytotoxicity evaluations, which cannot fully reflect the complexity of the in vivo gastrointestinal environment. Future research should therefore prioritize in vivo studies to access oral bioavailability, intestinal transport, and safety. Investigations into formulation optimization, particularly balancing release control with biocompatibility will be critical to enhance applicability. Scale-up and process feasibility studies will also be essential to support translation of this system toward real-world application.

Evaluation of the exposure-response relationship of pembrolizumab in non-small cell lung cancer: observational study protocol in real-world clinical practice
Authors: Sara Vázquez-Quiroga, Sofía García-Martínez, María Jiménez-Meseguer, Ana Irusta-Gonzalo, Raquel Jáñez-Carrera, Francisco Javier Otero-Espinar, Guadalupe Ruiz-Martín, Benito García-Díaz, Ana López-Martín, Silvia Peña-Cabia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPembrolizumab is a monoclonal antibody used in various oncological indications. This antibody is directed against programmed cell death receptor type 1. The expression of this receptor is used as a marker of treatment efficacy. In the search for response biomarkers, several authors have studied the relationship between plasma concentrations of pembrolizumab and response to treatment. However, these results are contradictory, and greater knowledge of the exposure-response association of these drugs is needed to predict response based on blood concentrations. The two primary objectives are to study the relationship between the pembrolizumab minimum steady-state concentration and (1) progression-free survival and (2) the clinical benefit rate, in patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.MethodWe designed a prospective, longitudinal, observational study at a single center. Patients with metastatic non-small cell lung cancer undergoing treatment with pembrolizumab are being included. Peripheral blood samples will be collected from enrolled patients at treatment initiation and, once steady state is reached, at each treatment reassessment, coinciding with routine blood tests. Once plasma is obtained from the samples, it will be frozen until analysis. The plasma drug concentration will be measured using specific commercial ELISA test kits. Demographic, clinical, and treatment data will be collected in a data collection form.DiscussionWith this project, we want to advance personalized cancer medicine by providing real-world data. Because resources are limited, establishing guidelines that reduce costs while maintaining the same efficacy would allow us to be more efficient. Background

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) are biological drugs used for the treatment of numerous diseases. Currently, the most widely used in clinical practice are immunoglobulin G (IgG)-type monoclonal antibodies with an approximate molecular weight of 150 kDa. These mAbs are produced in living cells, and it is this production process that defines them rather than their chemical structure. There are different types of mAbs according to their mechanism of action. In this regard, those that stimulate the immune system itself to combat disease are known as immunotherapy [1–3].

The immunotherapeutic agents currently authorized by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) are specific mAbs, known as checkpoint inhibitors, whose mechanism of action consists of blocking cellular targets that negatively regulate T-lymphocyte activation. These targets are cellular receptors such as cytotoxic T-lymphocyte–associated antigen 4 (CTLA-4), programmed cell death receptor 1 (PD-1), or programmed cell death ligand (PD-L1) [3, 4].

Pembrolizumab is a mAb directed against the PD-1 receptor that blocks its interaction with its ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2. This receptor–ligand interaction is involved in the negative regulation of T lymphocytes. Certain types of cancer express this receptor as a mechanism of immune system evasion; thus, pembrolizumab promotes antitumor activity mediated by the patient’s own immune system [5].

From the outset, PD-L1 expression has been used as a predictor of response to treatment. However, pembrolizumab is already used in different settings in which PD-L1 expression is below 1%, or in others in which assessment of ligand expression is not required to initiate treatment [6]. The lack of biomarkers capable of predicting response and/or treatment efficacy justifies the exploration of biomarkers related to the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of pembrolizumab.

Different studies have explored the relationship between plasma concentrations of checkpoint inhibitors and clinical benefit, with the aim of increasing treatment efficacy. Li et al. developed a time-dependent pharmacokinetic model of pembrolizumab to attempt to correlate plasma concentrations with improved treatment response. They used different variables such as sex, body weight, baseline tumor size, and baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status, among others. They found a decrease in pembrolizumab clearance over time, but this was not sufficient to elucidate the exposure–response relationship [7].

Wang et al. conducted a clinical trial with 89 patients. Thirty-three patients were prospectively assigned to the experimental group, and 56 patients were retrospectively assigned to the control group. All patients were treated with 200 mg of pembrolizumab every 21 days. In the experimental group, the dosing interval was modified according to pembrolizumab concentration at the fourth cycle. If the drug concentration was below 15 µg/mL, the dosing interval was reduced to 15–20 days. If the concentration was above 15 µg/mL, the interval was extended to between 22 and 80 days. Progression-free survival (PFS) curves showed a longer PFS median in the experimental group (15.1 months) compared with the control group (7.7 months). Despite this promising data, the results were not statistically significant, and the authors concluded that further studies with larger sample sizes are needed to continue investigating the relationship between plasma drug concentration and response [8].

The exposure–response relationship is also being investigated for other immunotherapeutic agents such as nivolumab. Mallardo et al. performed a retrospective analysis relating plasma concentrations of nivolumab, as the sole variable, with overall survival (OS) and PFS in patients with metastatic melanoma. This study concluded that plasma concentrations of the anti–PD-1 agent were associated with treatment response and that patients who achieved a complete response had higher concentrations [9]. Desnoyer et al. conducted a review of different pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies of checkpoint inhibitors and did not find a relationship between plasma nivolumab concentration and treatment response. However, they concluded that drug clearance may be a predictive variable of OS in different types of cancer [10]. Basak et al. performed a correlation study of nivolumab plasma concentrations with treatment outcomes in patients with small-cell lung cancer. In this study, they deduced that the available evidence is insufficient to relate nivolumab exposure to its anticancer effect [11].

Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) could, in some cases, guide dose reduction of these drugs while maintaining the same effectiveness. This would imply a reduction in treatment costs and an increase in safety [12]. Likewise, the savings generated could be allocated to more patients or other resources. In line with this, Ratain et al. proposed four measures to be considered to reduce treatment-related costs, as long as effectiveness is maintained. These measures are reducing the dose, increasing the dosing interval, shortening treatment duration, and considering more efficient therapeutic alternatives whenever possible [13–15].

Pharmacokinetics in clinical practice allows treatment to be tailored to the needs of each patient, a concept known as personalized medicine. TDM is a discipline widely used both for small molecules, such as digoxin or vancomycin, and for monoclonal antibodies such as infliximab or ustekinumab. In this context, our research group has already conducted concentration–response studies with mAbs in oncology, in the pursuit of personalized medicine [16, 17]. The objective of the NiPe project is to apply this working methodology, in real-world clinical practice, to immunotherapeutic drugs, deepening the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic investigation of pembrolizumab.

Based on the hypothesis that there is a relationship between trough plasma concentrations of pembrolizumab and its effectiveness in the treatment of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (mNSCLC), we have established two primary objectives. The first one is to study the relationship between pembrolizumab minimum steady-state concentration (CSSmin) and the PFS. The second is to study the relationship between pembrolizumab CSSmin and the clinical benefit rate.

Materials and methods

A prospective, longitudinal, single-center observational study was designed with the aim of evaluating the relationship between plasma concentrations of pembrolizumab and response to treatment.

Based on the clinical question—Is there a relationship between plasma concentrations of pembrolizumab and the clinical benefit rate in oncology patients?—a PICO framework was used to structure the research question as follows: P (cancer patients treated with pembrolizumab), I (plasma drug concentrations), C (not applicable), O (clinical benefit rate, PFS, OS). This PICO question was used to conduct a literature search for related studies in different databases such as Embase® and PubMed®. This literature search was not intended to be a systematic review. The literature search was performed between April and June 2023.

Patients

Patients eligible to participate in this study are those with mNSCLC treated with pembrolizumab at the hospital’s Medical Oncology Department. Inclusion criteria are as follows: patients of either sex aged ≥ 18 years, receiving pembrolizumab as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, with a diagnosis of mNSCLC, a life expectancy greater than 12 weeks, and written informed consent in accordance with current regulations.

Exclusion criteria included difficulty in obtaining adequate peripheral venous access for blood sampling and any disorder or pathological condition that prevented the patient from understanding their participation in the study.

Sample size

Sample size calculation was performed using Stata software (version 18, StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX, USA), following the method proposed by Peduzzi et al. for observational studies [18]. Five covariates were included: plasma concentration of pembrolizumab, baseline tumor size, baseline ECOG performance status, sex, and albumin. Considering KEYNOTE-189 [19] we have assumed a clinical benefit rate of 84,6% and an attrition rate of 10%. Based on these parameters, the required sample size calculated was 66 patients.

Once included in the study, patients are pseudoanonymized and assigned a consecutive identification number according to the order of inclusion.

First, the Oncology Department informs the patient about the study. If the patient agrees to participate, written informed consent is obtained. From that moment onward, the blood samples required to determine plasma concentrations of pembrolizumab will be scheduled (Fig. 1).

Timing of blood sample collection in relation to treatment administrations

At the start of treatment, two blood samples are collected. The first is obtained at the hematological toxicity assessment after the first cycle, between 10 and 15 days after the first infusion. The second sample is collected prior to the second administration of pembrolizumab (C1 trough). Thereafter, once the drug has reached steady state (SS), periodic blood samples will continue to be collected each time there is a treatment reassessment. According to the product label of pembrolizumab, the elimination half-life is 22 days [5]; therefore, a minimum of four months is estimated to reach steady state. The Oncology Department mostly reassesses treatment based on imaging studies, such as computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), or positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT). At SS, blood samples are obtained from patients prior the infusion, within 15 days before or after the imaging study. All blood samples for the study are obtained at the time of routine monitoring blood tests requested by the Oncology Department, so no additional venipunctures are required.

Peripheral blood is collected from the patient into a 3 mL tube containing tripotassiumethylenediaminetetraacetic acid. Hemolyzed, icteric, or lipemic samples are not used. The sample collected is centrifuged at 1000 xg for 10 min within 2 h of collection. The obtained plasma is then separated into two aliquots, one stored at − 20 °C and the other at − 80 °C. The aliquot frozen at − 20 °C is used for analysis, and the aliquot stored at − 80 °C is retained until the end of the study.

Samples are stored at all times under the responsibility of a study investigator, following the center’s standard procedures. All samples will be destroyed at the end of the study.

For the quantification of free plasma concentrations of these drugs, ultrasensitive commercial enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Shikari® Q-PEM ELISA, Matriks Biotek, Turkey), optimized with Keytruda® as reference standards, are used in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. Sample analyses are performed by the hospital’s Department of Clinical Analysis and Biochemistry.

Regarding analytical performance, the precision intra-assay and inter-assay must be < 30%. The lowest detectable level (lowest detection limit) is 0.01 µg/mL and the limit of quantification 0.03 µg/mL. In each batch there will be two samples of quality control (QC), a high level and a low one, for a valid study, the optical density (OD) 450/650 of the highest standard should be > 1.500 and the OD 450/650 of the lowest standard should be < 0.150. Either calibration, QC or samples will be run in duplicate.

To manage batch effects if samples are analyzed in multiple runs, all batches will include controls and efforts will be made to analyze some duplicate samples across different batches, thereby enabling the detection of any systematic variation between batches.

It is a pseudoanonymized study, so analysts will be blinded to clinical outcomes or patient information to avoid bias in measurement or interpretation.

Patient data and study variables are obtained from the electronic medical record and the electronic prescription system. This data is subsequently entered into a data collection form (DCF) in Excel®.

Objectives

The two primary objectives are: (1) to evaluate the relationship between pembrolizumab CSSmin and PFS, and (2) to assess if there is a correlation between pembrolizumab CSSmin and the clinical benefit rate, assessed radiologically according to the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST).

The secondary objectives are to investigate whether there is a relationship between pembrolizumab CSSmin and OS; to describe the observed toxicity and to assess whether there is a relationship between this toxicity and pembrolizumab CSSmin; and to externally validate the population pharmacokinetic models developed by Li et al. [7] and Hurkmans et al. [20].

Statistical analysis

First, the data will be validated to ensure its quality. When possible, incomplete records will be completed using available information, erroneous data will be corrected or excluded, and inconsistent data will be resolved. Afterward, any remaining missing data will be handled using multiple imputation by chained equations. Sensitivity analyses using complete-case datasets will be performed to assess the robustness of the results.

To evaluate the association between pembrolizumab CSSmin and clinical benefit, a generalized estimating equations (GEE) regression analysis will be performed. The dependent variable will be clinical benefit (yes/no) at each tumor assessment time point. A logit link function and an autoregressive structure will be used. Pembrolizumab CSSmin at each time point will be included as a time-varying predictor. The association between pembrolizumab CSSmin and clinical benefit will be evaluated adjusting for relevant clinical covariates, including baseline tumor size, baseline ECOG performance status, sex, and albumin. The number of covariates will be restricted, if necessary, to ensure model stability given the number of observed events. Effect estimates will be reported with their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (95% CI).

Median follow-up will be estimated using the reverse Kaplan–Meier method. OS and PFS will be analyzed using the Kaplan–Meier method. The proportional hazards assumption will be assessed using Schoenfeld residuals.

Time-dependent area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC) will be estimated at 9 and 12 months for PFS and at 24 months for OS using each patient’s median and last pembrolizumab CSSmin. In addition, AUC-ROC analyses will be performed using all pembrolizumab concentrations. Time-dependent AUC-ROC estimates will be reported with 95% confidence intervals. If appropriate, cutoff values will be explored using the Youden index. Any identified cutoff will be considered hypothesis-generating only and will not be used for clinical decision-making in this study.

The relationship between mAb dosing regimen and the first pembrolizumab CSSmin obtained per patient will be analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test.

Univariable Cox proportional hazards models will be developed to evaluate the association between individual factors with potential influence on pembrolizumab pharmacokinetics or patient survival and each outcome (OS and PFS): sex, age, ECOG performance status at diagnosis, comorbidity, baseline tumor burden, main adverse effect (immune-mediated toxicities), dosing regimen, and median albumin concentration. Univariable analyses will be conducted for exploratory purposes. Multivariable Cox proportional hazards models will be developed to evaluate the independent association between pembrolizumab plasma concentration and survival outcomes (OS and PFS), adjusting for prespecified clinically relevant covariates (baseline tumor size, baseline ECOG performance status, sex, and albumin). The number of covariates will be restricted, if necessary, to ensure model stability given the number of observed events. The level of statistical significance will be set at 0.05.

Statistical analyses will be performed using Stata software (version 18, StataCorp LLC, College Station, TX, USA). External validation of the pharmacokinetic models will be conducted using Visual Predictive Checks (VPCs) with R software packages (R-project, www.r-project.org).

Discussion

The aim of this project is to contribute to the development of personalized and precision medicine for patients treated with pembrolizumab. Following a treatment strategy guided by drug concentrations in blood would allow drug exposure to be reduced or increased according to individual patient needs. In this way, clinical benefit can be maximized while toxicity is controlled.

In this context, several authors have referred to a concept known as “interventional pharmacoeconomics.” Its objective is the development of new treatment strategies that allow treatment costs to be reduced without compromising efficacy [14]. These new therapies are associated with high costs, both due to their novelty and their manufacturing processes. This is reflected in the final price and in the high oncology-related expenditure within the National Health System. Given that resources are limited, the availability of target plasma concentrations would allow doses and/or dosing intervals to be adjusted, thereby transforming treatments into more efficient strategies and achieving maximum benefit for patients.

Moreover, the monitoring of monoclonal antibodies in oncology remains poorly developed. Regulatory agencies have largely relied on studies conducted in virtual patient populations to approve different treatment regimens. Our study in real-world patients aims to bring the pharmacokinetic monitoring of these drugs closer to routine clinical practice.

The limitations of this project are inherent to its single-center design in a medium-sized hospital, which results in a limited number of recruited patients. In addition, the number of samples available per patient is restricted by the laboratory tests requested in routine clinical practice. For this reason, insufficient data will be available during the drug elimination phase to develop an independent population pharmacokinetic model.

Another limitation of the study is the lack of funding to measure the presence of anti-drug antibodies. According to data reported in the product label of pembrolizumab, anti-drug antibodies are detected in only 1.8% of patients treated with pembrolizumab [5]. It would be of interest to assess the presence of these antibodies in the patients included in the study; however, due to an insufficient budget, this assessment cannot be performed.

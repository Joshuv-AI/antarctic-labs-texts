Federated spatial-temporal traffic forecasting with VMD-enhanced graph attention and LSTM
Authors: Tarun Mundada, Samruddhi Ramdhave, Sanyam Jain, Saurav Gupta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate spatiotemporal demand forecasting in distributed environments is challenging because of data heterogeneity, non-stationarity across clients, and privacy constraints. Traditional federated learning approaches often suffer from poor performance when global model updates do not align with local data distribution. This study proposes a novel VMD-structured LSTM–DSTGCRN with GAT framework with a Client-Side Validation (CSV) mechanism to address these challenges. Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) is applied locally to decompose raw broadband demand signals into Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs), isolating characteristic frequency components and reducing cross-frequency interference. The decomposed signals are processed using an LSTM—MultiHead Attention—AGCRN backbone to jointly capture temporal dependencies and adaptive spatial correlations with Graph Attention Networks (GATs). In the federated setting, the CSV enables the selective integration of aggregated global parameters at the module level, allowing clients to retain locally optimal parameters while adopting beneficial global updates. Experimental results on multimodal transport demand datasets demonstrate that implemented approach achieves higher prediction accuracy, faster convergence, and improved robustness compared with baseline federated graph learning models. The proposed framework provides an effective, privacy-preserving solution for nonstationary heterogeneous spatiotemporal forecasting tasks. The simulation results demonstrate that the proposed model significantly improves the accuracy compared to the baseline model by reducing the MAE by 28% in centralized models. Furthermore, in federated learning setup, the MAE decreases by 40.6% and RMSE by 20.1%.Keywords: Spatiotemporal forecasting, Graph convolutional networks, Long short-term memory, Attention mechanism, Traffic predictionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Modern Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) are based on accurate traffic flow predictions, which consequently have a direct impact on the efficiency, safety, and sustainability of urban mobility. Due to the rapid urbanization and increased vehicle density, traffic congestion has become a significant urban issue that has compromised efficiency and sustainability1. The ability to predict traffic dynamics at any point in time is crucial to alleviate such impacts because it facilitates a predictable traffic control system and allows dynamic routing and smarter urban planning. Classical statistical models that model traffic dynamics using explicit mathematical models, mostly representing linear temporal characteristics based on the past observations, include the autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA), Kalman filtering, and linear regression-based model. Where ARIMA concentrates on time-series stationary trends using the methods of autoregression and differencing, Kalman filtering offers recursive state estimation under the conditions of linear Gaussian processes and linear regression models build direct relationships between traffic variables building on selected attributes and coefficients. Non-stationary behavior of traffic is highly evident in real-world data and it does not comply with the assumptions of such traditional models2. The demand patterns of traffic are highly different with time of day because of the morning and evening rush hour, normal and inconvenient congestion through accidents, road closures, or weather, seasonal congestion, and congestion before and during events. Additionally, the nature of spatial traffic dependencies is very dynamic whereby, a traffic jam in one particular area may suddenly reshape the traffic into other ways3. Consequently, both parametric and linear models cannot keep pace with the dynamically shifting spatiotemporal distributions of traffic, and hence, they have poor prediction capabilities in the complicated urban systems. New developments in deep learning provide state-of-the-art approaches that can learn spatio-temporal influences using large-scale traffic data4. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, and Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) have demonstrated a great ability to utilize sequential patterns of data in a time-series. Simultaneously, Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) have become one of the predominant methods for representing spatial interactions in road networks that can be expressed as graphs5. Complex models, such as Graph Convolutional Recurrent Networks (GCRNs), which combine some form of temporal models (in this case, recurrent neural networks) with spatial models (in this case, GCNs), have pushed the boundaries of traffic flow forecasting by learning spatiotemporal signals6. Compared to conventional Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs), Graph Attention Networks (GATs) are a more general model for providing spatial dependencies. GATs do not take any predetermined adjacency matrices or a collection of weights on their edges specifying what nodes they should consider. Instead, they learn to assign an edge its weight (importance) to an adjacent node. This makes them especially well adapted to traffic forecasting. GATs will be able to track the changes in the time flow of the traffic, as well as see the space-varying quality of interactions between road networks at a more precise level.

Centralized deep learning frameworks are the most commonly used because of their simplicity of implementation and ability to obtain a global perspective on the data. However, they tend to attract issues related to scalability and data privacy. It has limitations as it is based on a single data distribution and capturing regional variations and local demand patterns, as well as non-stationary traffic behavior is challenging. Moreover, the centralization of all traffic information in a central server introduces security and data privacy issues, compliance issues, and becomes more difficult to scale up as the volume of traffic data continues to increase. To overcome such issues, decentralized and federated learning approaches are emerging, which enable the training of collaborative predictive models on different clients (e.g., sensors, intersections, or regional centers) without transferring raw data3. Such decentralized infrastructures will not only enhance privacy, but also provide increased robustness in heterogeneous large-scale networks and provide better adaptability. In a federated setting, GAT-based models can also be customized because each client can learn location-specific weights of attention and has access to the globally available information in the global model7. This paper provides a detailed investigation on the issue of traffic flow forecasting with centralized and decentralized frameworks using deep learning models. The proposal of a VMD-enhanced LSTM-DSTGCRN federated spatiotemporal forecasting system is one of the most significant contributions. According to the results of the experiments conducted with the NYC-Bike and CHI-Taxi data, the proposed algorithm always performs better than the baseline models in terms of predictive accuracy and more stable convergence in heterogeneous data settings.

Related work

The field of spatiotemporal data forecasting has evolved immensely around the transportation sector especially with the presence of big volumes of traffic measurement data, and the developments in the areas of modeling. Traffic flow prediction has been performed based on parametric statistical models such as the autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) and seasonal ARIMA (SARIMA). Approaches based on Kalman filtering algorithms8 have also been considered for estimating traffic states by minimizing prediction errors in the context of stationarity assumptions. Models like k-nearest neighbors, decision trees, and support vector machines (SVMs) have been considered. Such techniques might help observe nonlinear associations, although they require a large data volume9, and a high level of computational intensity, they also have a tendency to neglect spatial relationships in transportation structures10. Parametric and non-parametric models depend mainly on historical temporal trends and have limited capability to incorporate spatial topology data.

An emerging technology known as deep learning has been introduced for traffic prediction because it has become possible to learn in an end-to-end manner. Initial work harnessed recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and their derivatives, long short-term memory (LSTM), and gated recurrent units (GRU), to capture time-varying dependencies in traffic sequences. These architectures can adequately represent long-term patterns in time but would usually fail to represent spatial relationships among locations. Graph neural networks (GNNs), and more specifically, graph convolutional networks (GCN) that model spatial dependencies by treating the road network as a graph involving nodes as locations and edges as physical or functional connections, have been used to address this issue11. The combination of GCNs and RNNs results in Graph Convolutional Recurrent Networks (GCRNs)12, which allow the underlying modeling of spatial and temporal relationships to occur jointly. Adaptive and dynamic graph learning methods have been proposed to address this shortcoming. The Adaptive Graph Convolutional Recurrent Network (AGCRN) is end-to-end trainable in that it learns node-specific spatial correlations3, and the Spatiotemporal Adaptive Gated Graph Convolutional Network is trained to capture complex spatiotemporal patterns13. Recently, a deformable spatiotemporal recurrent neural network (DSTGRNN) has been proposed, which uses a dynamic graph generator and an associated fusion process to model spatiotemporal pattern deformation. This has been further extended in the DSTGCRN model, where a combination of more than one type of graph (node features, topology, and time-slot graphs) is used to model the dependencies as comprehensively as possible as follows: The multimodal transport forecasting DSTGCRN framework is combined with adaptive graph convolutions and multi-head attention, thus becoming responsive to variable-changing transport patterns.

Concurrently, signal decomposition methods have emerged as a means of addressing the non-stationarity of traffic time series. Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD)14, Ensemble EMD (EEMD), Complete Ensemble EMD with Adaptive Noise (CEEMDAN), and Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD)15 have been applied to decompose traffic signals into components for specific modeling. A combination of decomposition-based denoising and deep sequence learning has proven to be useful in terms of performance by hybrid methodologies, such as VMD189191 LSTM and EMD189191 GRU. In particular, VMD proposes a strong approach for the separation of traffic data into periodic, residual, and volatile terms, which increases the stability of the model and the accuracy of the predictions15. The VMD and LSTM designs, together with DSTGCRN, are all promising in individual directions. VMD preprocessing into the LSTM-based spatiotemporal data stream is an interesting idea as it extracts meaningful frequency components, reduces noise, and isolates multi-scale temporal trends. LSTM can model the temporal interactions among other decomposed modes and DSTGCRN can implement dynamic spatial interactions with these representations to adaptively model highly dynamic and accurate traffic forecasting. With this combination, the issues of non-stationarity, changing topology in spatial distribution and long-range temporal correlation are directly applicable as this solution for the modern intelligent transportation system appears to be fully efficient.

The second important development is the use of Federated Learning (FL) to solve traffic forecasting problems, where many stakeholders can jointly train predictive models without exchanging raw data. This has been expanded into multi-task FL to optimize route selection16 to cost-efficient privacy-preserved traffic state estimation in Internet of Vehicles (IoV) environments using FedTSE. Fed-NTP is a sophisticated breed that adapts FL to network traffic prediction in vehicular ad-hoc networks (VANETs)17. Although FL has powerful privacy guarantees and cross-jurisdiction cooperation is possible, limitations still exist in managing heterogeneous data distributions and communication overheads17. Decomposition-based preprocessing and the FL framework with dynamic spatiotemporal models provide an excellent research perspective for attaining both high precision and privacy in cases involving large intelligent transportation systems17. GATs offer another advantage for federated traffic forecasting i.e., the adaptive weighting algorithm that enables the coverage of various spatial graphs across clients18. Federated GAT models also allow individual clients to acquire individual-context-specific attention weights and contribute to a global model because road networks are not homogeneous in terms of density, connectivity, and distribution of traffic volume. This flexibility suggests that GAT-based approaches would be a great direction to develop quality, privacy-conscious forecasting systems for traffic that can generalize across regions19. Recent advances in multivariate time-series forecasting like comprehensive benchmarking and heterogeneity analyses (e.g., BasicTS+) highlight that model performance varies widely across dataset characteristics and that fair, unified evaluation pipelines are essential for reliable comparison of forecasting methods20. Robust end-to-end frameworks that explicitly address missing or corrupted variables, such as GinAR and its extension GinAR+, provide effective strategies for joint imputation-and-forecasting, improving robustness in practical, noisy data scenarios21. Transformer-based fusion approaches that operate across multiple temporal granularities (e.g., MGSFformer) demonstrate strong ability to fuse multi-scale spatial and temporal cues and are particularly relevant where cross-granularity signals (daily, hourly, transient events) matter22. In summary, prior research on traffic forecasting generally falls into three main directions. The first category is based on spatiotemporal modeling in which time patterns and spatial conditionalities are simultaneously trained with RNNs, GNNs, and mixed graph-based systems. The second line of work explores signal decomposition techniques, such as EMD and VMD, to handle the non-stationary nature of traffic time series and improve prediction stability. The third category is federated learning systems that enable distributed heterogeneous system training of models in a way that can protect data privacy. Even though these methods have demonstrated good results separately, relatively less work has been done for incorporation of decomposition-based preprocessing with dynamic spatiotemporal graph models in a federated environment. This fact inspires the suggested VMD-enhanced LSTM-DSTGCRN with GAT towards strong and privacy-conscious traffic prediction. Hybridization of VMD and LSTM-DSTGCRN—modeling rationale

Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) provides a principled variational method to decompose a univariate signal into a finite number of compact-bandwidth intrinsic mode functions (IMFs), each having a typical frequency range of 1–2 octaves. VMD enables mode extraction as a constrained variational optimization problem (where it minimizes the sum of bandwidths, with the constraint of perfect reconstruction) and uses an Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM)-type iterative algorithm to create a solution25 to the problem to extract the modes and the center frequencies. The ability of later successive order sequence models to extract these patterns at each characteristic scale is more efficient and less dependant on the cross-frequency nonstationarity by deconvoluting a raw demand time series into IMFs.

The DSTGCRN is proposed to learn spatial connections (through graph convolutional units) and temporal dynamics (through recurrent neural network units) and has helped in modelling a complex spatiotemporal forecasting task. Replacing the original recurrent block (GRU) with the LSTM increases the ability to keep the historical information, which may benefit in predicting transportation modules with long and periodic temporal26 cycles (such as daily and weekly frequencies). GRUs are computationally efficient, but have a double-gate memory model, so the forgetting and updating processes are coupled through reset and update gates. Thus, they are unable to selectively retain long-range periodic information when short-term variations are dominant3. Contrarily, LSTMs use an explicit cell state with explicit input, forget and output gates, which allows more explicit control of information flowing over time. This architectural isolation enables LSTMs to remember low-frequency and long-horizon temporal information and at the same time adapt to high-frequency transient variations, which is specially so the case with traffic demand prediction2. VMD preprocessing along with a graph-augmented LSTM-DSTGCRN (hereafter, VMD-LSTM-DSTGCRN) thus implements a decomposition-then-learn scheme27. VMD decomposes frequency-specific components of time series data, LSTM learns long temporal dependencies within each component and the graph modules learn long-range spatial connections across different nodes. Though the cost of LSTMs is more costly than that of GRUs, the overhead is reasonable in the suggested framework owing to two reasons. To begin with, VMD preprocessing eliminates temporal noise and cross-frequency interference that make convergence faster and more consistent with the increased parameterization. Second, the federated learning architecture restricts the number of updates the model receives to a few communication rounds, and convergence stability as well as representational fidelity becomes more essential than savings per-iteration of computation28. This design decision empirically leads to reduced MAE and RMSE and quick convergence in both the centralized and federated controlled frameworks.

Model formulation with VMD preprocessing

This section presents the model formulation that uses Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) as a preprocessing step with a Long Short-Term Memory-based Dynamic Spatiotemporal Graph Convolutional Recurrent Network (LSTM-DSTGCRN) as mentioned in Algorithm 1. The mathematical statement of the VMD step starts with the formulation that splits node-level signals into intrinsic mode functions (IMFs). The VMD-enhanced inputs are then fed to the local model, and the temporal frequency and spatial dependencies are learned simultaneously by the LSTM units, multi-head attention, and adaptive graph convolutional modules. To avoid the leakage of time-related information, VMD decomposition is only applied within the training window of a particular client. In particular, the local demand series of the client is initially chronologically divided into the training, validation and test sets where VMD is then solely applied on the training set to obtain IMFs. Therefore, causal consistency is preserved and data leakage in VMD preprocessing is avoided. The frequency decomposition of training data is locally available and maintains temporal integrity to the model, enhances generalization, and allows reliable aggregation of client updates without loss of privacy or causing future information to be biased.

The original signal is decomposed into several narrow-band functions (IMFs) using Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) in preprocessing. The individual IMFs are processed as independent input channels, which reduces cross-frequency interference, and the model can evaluate cleaner, frequency-specific data. Each IMF channel is then fed into the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), where it reads short-term variations as well as long-term time trends. Multi-head attention is used to attract and appreciate the most suitable temporal patterns in all these channels to ensure that no significant characteristics are missed. Finally, the Adaptive Graph Convolutional Recurrent Network (AGCRN) learns and updates the relationship between nodes in space dynamically, and the interaction between them focuses more on the changes in spatial connection at different times. In concert, the components combine frequency-sensitive decomposition, time modeling, selective attention, and adaptive spatial learning, which provides an enduring, high-quality spatiotemporal prediction framework of transportation demand.

Proposed methodology

The proposed model is a federated demand forecasting framework that integrates frequency-based preprocessing with dynamic spatiotemporal graph learning and a layer-level Client-Side Validation (CSV) approach to provide stable and accurate predictions in distributed systems. The methodology comprises three main components: (i) signal treatment through Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), (ii) an LSTM–Attention–AGCRN backbone for spatial and temporal analysis, and (iii) a federated training mechanism with CSV for selective parameter integration32.

VMD-structured frequency input

The demand time series at each client node is decomposed using VMD. This decomposition yields a finite set of Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs), each representing a narrowband signal within a specific frequency band, along with a residual component. This process transforms a broadband signal into a multichannel feature representation, isolating seasonal, cyclical, and transient patterns.

By feeding these IMFs into the predictive model, the interactions between the high and low frequency components are reduced. This enables the downstream modules of the model to specialize in capturing dependencies at different temporal scales. Importantly, VMD is performed locally for each client, ensuring that the raw data remain private.

Client-Side Validation (CSV) is a validation-based update protocol that enables every client to make its own choice on whether to adopt the globally aggregated model at a particular federated round. Following local training, the server compiles updates of clients with the FedAvg and sends the global model to all clients. Rather than simply replacing local weights, individual clients can estimate the received global model on their local validation set, and compare the validation loss to that of their locally-trained model. In case the global model attains a lower validation error, the client is then given the update. Failure to this leads to the client carrying on with the local model parameters to the next round. For example, a client may adopt updated temporal encoder parameters while retaining its local spatial graph convolution module if this improves the performance.The framework starts with several client nodes, each corresponding to a dataset (NYC Bike & CHI Taxi datasets).

The Figure 1 architecture diagram shows the specific steps of preprocessing, local training of the model, federated aggregation and continuous optimization that lie at the center of the work of the VMD enhanced LSTM-DSTGCRN with GAT system in a federated, privacy-preserving environment. In round 0, the central server initializes and sends the global base model to all clients36. On the client side, Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) preprocessing is applied to the incoming data to obtain the relevant temporal features. A three-module neural network architecture (including LSTM, a Multi-Head Attention mechanism, and an Adaptive Graph Convoluted Recurrent Network module) is then trained independently by both clients. The model parameters that are learned locally at any client are returned to the server, and federated averaging (FedAvg) is used to aggregate the updates into a new global model35 as demonstrated in Algorithm 2. Between rounds 1 and J36, the server continues to generate new global parameters for clients. Clients optimize the model on their local data, test it on CSV-based client-side evaluation, and send new model parameters back to the server. This iterative process of model distribution, local update, and aggregation continues until the global model converges. In summary, the proposed framework combines frequency-aware preprocessing, adaptive spatiotemporal learning and validation-driven selective parameter integration to generate accurate, stable, and privacy-preserving forecasts in distributed environments.

Conclusion

The proposed work presented a federated spatiotemporal forecasting system i.e. LSTM-DSTGCRN + VMD + FedAvg + CSV. It incorporates VMD and LSTM modules into a dynamic graph system to provide privacy-aware and scalable forecasts of traffic demand. VMD is employed to derive pristine temporal patterns and restrict cross-frequency interference, whereas LSTM is applied to acquire multi-resolution trends and client-side validation. Together, this resulted in a customized and generalized federated aggregation model. Federated learning enables model training without sharing the raw data, thereby preserving client privacy and making it scalable across distributed heterogeneous systems. Although the GATs performed reasonably well, the incorporation of VMD yields considerable improvements. Here, the proposed work suggests that although spatial modeling using graph networks has shown potential, the federated configuration of LSTM-DSTGCRN enhanced with VMD preprocessing, FedAvg, and CSV validation consistently delivered the most robust performance across all experiments. In LSTM-DSTGCRN + VMD + GAT framework, federated learning is employed and the reasonable performance has been observed. Notably, the federated setup not only matched but surpassed the centralized baselines, demonstrating faster convergence behaviour and improved resilience to heterogeneous data. The results validate the effectiveness of the proposed hybrid federated architecture as a scalable and high-performing solution for real-world traffic forecasting scenarios. In future, lightweight hybrid and transformer-based models can be incorporated in communication-efficient federated framework to further enhance the performance.

The integration of VMD preprocessing, recurrent temporal modeling, attention-based graph operations, and multiple federated training rounds introduces additional computational overhead compared to lighter forecasting architectures, which may affect deployment on resource-constrained edge devices. In future, this issue can be taken into account for practical deployment. In addition, signal decomposition hyperparameters (number of modes and penalty coefficients) can affect the results of VMD; whereas, fixed settings provided stable results with all of the considered datasets, these parameters can be adjusted to suit different cities, sampling resolutions, and patterns of traffic volatility. Lastly, federated learning paradigm inevitably involves the cost of communication (through repeated model synchronization) across clients, which can be large in both large-scale and bandwidth-constrained settings. The solution to these issues encourages further research into lightweight or adaptive decomposition schemes, learnable VMD parameter settings and communication-efficient federated optimization schemes to enhance scalability and practical usefulness.

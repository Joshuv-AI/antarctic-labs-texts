Commentary on ‘Circumferential resection margin rates in esophageal cancer resection- oncological equivalency and comparable clinical outcomes between open versus minimally invasive techniques: a retrospective cohort study’
Authors: Kexun Li, Simiao Lu, Xuefeng Leng, Lin Peng
Published: 2024

Dear Editor,

We have carefully read the paper titled ‘Circumferential resection margin rates in esophageal cancer resection- oncological equivalency and comparable clinical outcomes between open versus minimally invasive techniques: a retrospective cohort study’ by Sacheen Kumar et al. presents a retrospective cohort study comparing open and laparoscopic esophago-gastrectomy for esophageal cancer1. The study focused on minimizing local recurrence by analyzing circumferential resection margin (CRM) involvement. Results from January 2004 to June 2022 showed lower CRM involvement rates (13.6% vs. 18.8%) in laparoscopic surgery, along with shorter ICU stays, hospital lengths of stay, and lower 30-day readmission rates. Adjuvant chemotherapy initiation was also higher in the laparoscopic group (74.4% vs. 51.8%). This study highlights the shift toward minimally invasive procedures in esophageal cancer surgery, emphasizing the importance of reduced CRM involvement for decreasing cancer recurrence. The benefits of laparoscopic esophago-gastrectomy did not increase complications compared to open surgery. Additionally, the higher rate of adjuvant chemotherapy initiation in the laparoscopic group may suggest faster recovery and earlier additional treatments. The study concluded that both open and laparoscopic approaches offer similar clinical, oncological, and survival outcomes with comparable CRM involvement rates, while the minimally invasive technique resulted in significantly shorter hospital stays. Despite the valuable findings, certain aspects of the study warrant further discussion.

Firstly, Table 11 suggests that patients have some differences in baseline characteristics, such as Tumor anatomical location and Nodal stage (N), etc. Specifically, the open group had a higher proportion of N1 patients (40.1% vs. 26.7%), while the laparoscopic group had a greater proportion of N2 patients. These baseline imbalances may introduce potential confounding and should be addressed. The authors should consider using propensity score matching or weighting methods (e.g. IPTW, OW) to ensure a more balanced comparison between the two surgical approaches2. The authors have not provided P values for several of the comparisons in Table 11. It would be helpful for the authors to explain the reasons for the missing P values, as this would increase the transparency and reliability of the analysis.

Then, Table 11 shown that ~10% of patients underwent surgery alone, without neoadjuvant therapy. In these cases, it would be important to distinguish between pathological (pT/pN) and clinical (ypT/ypN) staging, as these may differ significantly in the absence of prior treatment3. Table 11 suggests that about 10% of patients are at Stage 0 and Stage I (Clinical Stage (AJCC, 8th edition). The inclusion of patients at T0/Tis and T1 stages raises inquiries regarding the criteria for surgical intervention. Current guidelines support endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) as an effective treatment for Tis and T1a esophageal cancer stages, given the minimal risk of lymph node metastasis at these stages4.

Lastly, inadequate removal of lymph nodes not only affects the precise assessment of N staging but also results in poorer prognoses5,6, the authors compare the median lymph node yields between the open and laparoscopic groups, which were 35 and 33, respectively. They state that these values are within the acceptable range, with 95% of cases meeting the pathological criteria for lymph node staging. However, the authors should further discuss the clinical significance of the lymph node yield, as the literature suggests that a minimum of 21 lymph nodes should be examined for accurate N-stage assessment7. The potential impact of a lower lymph node yield on staging accuracy and patient outcomes should be addressed. This necessitates further discussion to enhance the reliability of the results.

In summary, the research conducted by Sacheen Kumar and team provides significant insights into the open and laparoscopic esophago-gastrectomy procedures in patients with esophageal cancer. The study evaluated both clinical and oncological outcomes, with a specific emphasis on CRM involvement. The findings indicated a lower rate of CRM involvement in laparoscopic surgery compared to open surgery. The authors’ dedication to standardizing surgical techniques is praiseworthy, and further enhancements to the study could strengthen its impact.

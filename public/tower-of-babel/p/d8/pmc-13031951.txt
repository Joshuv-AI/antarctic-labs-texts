How does agricultural product quality and safety regulation drive agricultural economic growth: a quasi-natural experiment from China’s agricultural product quality and safety county pilot policy
Authors: Lei Zhang, Hongli Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The establishment of Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties not only enhances the quality and safety standards of agricultural products, effectively ensuring food safety and consumption security, but also holds significant implications for accelerating the transformation of agricultural development patterns and advancing modern agriculture. Based on panel data from 1,609 Chinese counties from 2010 to 2023, this study employs the national pilot policy for Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties as a quasi-natural experiment and utilizes a multi-period difference-in-differences approach to examine the impact of establishing these counties on agricultural economic growth and its underlying mechanisms. The findings reveal that: (1) the pilot policy significantly promotes county-level agricultural economic growth, a conclusion that remains robust after a series of robustness checks; (2) heterogeneity analysis indicates the policy’s effects are more pronounced in central and eastern regions, major grain-producing areas, and regions with higher levels of economic development, digital infrastructure, and transportation facilities; (3) mechanism analysis demonstrates that the policy facilitates agricultural economic development by promoting expanded operational scale and the development of geographical indications. This study provides theoretical support and policy references for further expanding the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County initiative and for government agencies to formulate development policies tailored to local conditions.

Introduction

Agricultural development is directly linked to global food security and social stability. As China’s economy transitions to a high-quality development stage, the focus of its agricultural sector has shifted from output expansion to quality enhancement1. In recent years, China has achieved steady progress in agricultural modernization, with a notable strengthening of its comprehensive production capacity. Nevertheless, the sector continues to face multiple challenges, including resource and environmental constraints, and an underdeveloped quality and safety system for agricultural products2. These challenges necessitate a transformation from traditional practices to high-quality agricultural development. Concurrently, rising living standards and improvements in consumption have led to higher expectations for agricultural products3. This implies that agricultural production must not only ensure food security but also satisfy consumer demand for diverse, safe, nutritious, and high-quality food4. China’s “14th Five-Year” National Plan for Quality and Safety Improvement of Agricultural Products emphasizes that quality and safety should be central to transforming agricultural development patterns5. The plan aims to promote standardization across the entire modern agricultural industrial chain, increase the supply of green and high-quality agricultural products, and comprehensively elevate quality and safety standards6. Similarly, the Plan for Accelerating the Development of a Strong Agricultural Sector (2024–2035) reiterates the importance of quality-oriented agricultural development, including breeding superior varieties and expanding the supply of premium agricultural products7. The introduction of these policy documents underscores the government’s strong commitment to ensuring the quality and safety of agricultural products, enhancing agricultural economic performance, and achieving high-quality agricultural development. Consequently, exploring how improving agricultural product quality and safety can contribute to high-quality agricultural economic development has become a key academic focus8. This issue represents both a critical component and foundation of food security, as well as an essential objective in building a modern agricultural system in China.

There is currently a mismatch between agricultural production and consumer demand for food in China, which constrains the development of the agricultural economy9. Addressing this challenge requires accelerating the shift from an emphasis on scale and speed to a focus on quality, efficiency, and competitiveness. This entails enhancing comprehensive production capacity to ensure both sufficient output and high quality10. To safeguard the quality and safety of agricultural products and meet consumers’ demand for high-quality food, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs issued the “Implementation Plan for National Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties” and the “Assessment Measures for National Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties” in 2014. The ministry subsequently released the first and second batches of pilot counties for agricultural product quality and safety in 2015 and 2017, respectively. This initiative not only improves the quality and safety of agricultural products—thereby strengthening food safety and consumption security—but also plays a significant role in accelerating the transformation of agricultural development models and advancing modern agriculture.

Existing research on agricultural economic growth has extensively explored factors such as natural resource endowment11, agricultural infrastructure development12, mechanization level13, technological innovation14, land transfer15, human capital accumulation16, fiscal agricultural expenditure17, and financial development18. However, as China’s economy transitions towards high-quality development, agricultural growth must not only focus on quantitative expansion driven by factor inputs but also prioritize quality-oriented endogenous dynamics19. Currently, with the rising demand for food, quality and safety have become the principal contradictions in food supply and demand20. Ensuring the quality and safety of agricultural products, thereby promoting high-quality food production, has emerged as an effective pathway for sustainable agricultural development21. The shift in agricultural market demand from quantity to quality provides impetus for agricultural industrial restructuring, driving transformations in production modes and product outcomes22. This evolution not only enhances production efficiency and meets diverse consumer needs but also stimulates regional agricultural economic growth23. In response to these changes, China has introduced a series of policy documents, including the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Law of the People’s Republic of China and the Implementation Rules for the Assessment Method of the “Vegetable Basket” Mayor Responsibility System, which incorporate quality and safety into performance evaluations. Nevertheless, limited research has empirically examined how quality and safety management affect agricultural economic development.

Existing research on the relationship between agricultural product quality, safety, and economic growth has predominantly focused on micro-level farmer quality-control behaviors and macro-level quality-safety system development. At the micro level, since the use of chemical inputs such as fertilizers and pesticides during production directly affects product quality and safety, addressing quality and safety issues at the production source and promoting standardized production have become key academic focuses24. At the macro level, studies have focused on supervision management across the agricultural industry chain, innovative quality and safety regulatory methods, and reshaping quality and safety paradigms25. Research indicates that employing digital technologies such as the Internet of Things and blockchain to establish traceability systems for critical segments26, ensure smooth quality information flow between producers and consumers27, and create quality-oriented competition mechanisms can enhance product premium and thereby promote agricultural economic growth28. However, in empirical research, the existing literature primarily examines micro-level farmers and consumers or macro-level provincial data29. Given that counties represent the frontline of agricultural production and quality safety regulation, investigating the economic effects of quality safety policies at the county level is of significant importance. In summary, while existing studies on agricultural economic growth and product quality safety predominantly focus on producers’ quality safety behaviors or the construction of quality safety systems, there remains a scarcity of empirical analysis of the growth effects of quality safety assurance from a county perspective. Since China initiated the Agricultural Product Quality Safety County pilot program at the county administrative level in 2015, it has created opportunities to identify causal relationships between quality and safety assurance and agricultural economic growth.

Given this context, examining the implementation effects of the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County pilot policy from a county-level perspective is of significant practical value and policy relevance for identifying the relationship between agricultural product quality and economic growth, thereby facilitating the transition to high-quality agricultural development. Based on panel data from 1,609 Chinese counties and a multi-period difference-in-differences (DID) approach, this study investigates the impact of the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County pilot policy on agricultural economic growth. The potential marginal contributions of this paper are as follows: First, using the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County pilot policy as a starting point, it incorporates agricultural product quality and agricultural economic growth into a unified research framework. From a policy evaluation perspective, it employs a multi-period DID method to examine the impact of the policy implementation on agricultural economic growth, thereby enriching research on the economic effects of agricultural product quality safety. Second, through the dual pathways of operational scale effects and geographical indication brand effects, it systematically analyzes the mechanism by which the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County initiative influences agricultural economic growth. This provides viable pathways to enhance agricultural product quality and safety, promoting economic growth, and offers a scientific basis for decision-making by relevant authorities. Finally, based on empirical analysis of county-level data, it explores the heterogeneous effects of the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County pilot policy on agricultural economic growth. The findings provide policy recommendations for further optimizing and scaling up the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County initiative, as well as for formulating region-specific development policies tailored to local conditions.

Policy context and theoretical mechanisms

Policy context

Current agricultural economic development shows positive trends but also faces numerous challenges. Due to the highly fragmented nature of agricultural production at this stage30, the regulatory system and mechanisms for agricultural product quality and safety remain underdeveloped, particularly with insufficient regulatory capacity at the grassroots level31. Quality and safety incidents still occur periodically, not only affecting consumer health and safety but also severely impeding high-quality agricultural development32. From 2004 to 2024, China’s No. 1 Central Documents consistently emphasized agricultural product quality and safety, explicitly calling for establishing a comprehensive regulatory system, promoting agricultural transformation, and enhancing the quality of agricultural development33. In 2006, China enacted and implemented the Agricultural Product Quality Safety Law, further underscoring the importance of ensuring agricultural product quality. The law was revised again in 2022, clearly stating that rigorous management of agricultural product quality would promote agricultural economic development.

In 2014, China’s Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs promulgated the National Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County Establishment Plan and the National Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County Assessment Measures. The “Demonstration County” initiative adopted an implementation strategy of “piloting followed by phased expansion.” Eligible major agricultural production counties were required to submit applications through their county-level people’s governments, which served as the responsible entities. The established criteria mandated that qualified agricultural product inspection rates must reach at least 98%, while public satisfaction with county-level quality supervision should exceed 70%. In 2015, 103 counties were selected for the first pilot cohort, with each county qualifying for 1 million CNY in central government funding. The policy primarily supported the development of agricultural input management systems, product quality supervision mechanisms, standardized production practices, and agricultural entity management. Subsequently, a second batch of pilot counties was established in 2017. Through these demonstration counties, the policy aims to establish a comprehensive quality and safety system characterized by clearly defined responsibilities, rigorous supervision, strict enforcement, and efficient operation. This initiative ultimately seeks to ensure food safety, accelerate the transformation of agricultural development models, and promote agricultural economic growth.

Theoretical mechanisms

The establishment of Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties aims to create a quality and safety system for agricultural products that features clearly defined responsibilities, effective supervision, strict law enforcement, and efficient operation. It also seeks to comprehensively advance standardized agricultural production and ensure that agricultural producers and distributors fulfill their primary responsibilities, thereby enhancing the overall quality and safety of agricultural products. First, agricultural product supervision is characterized by significant information asymmetry, leading to fragmented regulation or even regulatory failure34. The APQSC initiative addresses this issue by clarifying local governments’ territorial responsibilities, effectively resolving inter-departmental coordination challenges in the supervision process, defining departmental duties, improving synergies among agencies, and strengthening organizational coordination. Furthermore, the development of agricultural product quality and safety county incorporates digital technologies such as the Internet of Things and blockchain into regulatory activities. These counties typically establish county-township testing networks and smart supervision platforms, upgrading traditional experience-based regulation to data-driven precision governance. This effectively addresses issues such as non-transparent and insufficiently authentic quality and safety information reporting, thereby ensuring traceability of food safety and quality while significantly enhancing consumer trust and market competitiveness35. Second, as Schultz noted in “Transforming Traditional Agriculture,” the inefficiency of traditional agriculture stems from rigid factor allocation and insufficient investment in human capital36. The APQSC initiative addresses these human capital constraints by improving labor quality and restructuring the division of labor system. On the one hand, it strengthens the management of agricultural production and operation entities in accordance with the law, enhancing self-discipline and awareness of responsibility among producers and distributors through publicity, guidance services, and supervision inspections37. On the other hand, pilot areas provide technical training to producers and operators, promote quality control technologies, and leverage the demonstration effects of standardized model zones to facilitate technology diffusion. This supports the transition of producers from experience-dependent to technology-oriented practices38. Finally, the APQSC initiative has spurred the emergence of new agricultural professional groups, including village-level coordinators, quality and safety information officers, and traceability system operators. The division of labor has thus expanded from singular production roles to diversified “production-supervision-service” functions. This not only reinforces the quality and safety system but also creates more localized non-agricultural employment opportunities, increasing wage income for rural households. Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes Hypothesis 1:

H1:

The Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County pilot policy can significantly promote county-level agricultural economic growth.

The theory of economies of scale posits that agricultural operational scale is a critical factor influencing agricultural development. Expanding the scale of agricultural operations can effectively reduce average costs and generate advantages in resource integration and technological progress39. To maximize profits, farmers often overapply chemical fertilizers and pesticides, which compromise the quality and safety of agricultural products40. However, appropriately expanding operational scale and promoting contiguous planting can transform previously fragmented production models, optimize the allocation of production inputs, and realize economies of scale in agricultural production41. This effectively lowers input costs, reduces chemical use, and not only safeguards product quality and safety but also enhances the efficiency of farmland management, thereby contributing to agricultural growth. Furthermore, due to variations in the behavioral capabilities of different operators—such as production proficiency, managerial decision-making skills, and risk preferences—exclusive ownership rights often fail to ensure an effective match between non-human assets and human capital42–44, thereby preventing optimal resource allocation. Specifically, during the development of Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties, the continuous improvement of institutional mechanisms and regulatory capacity has raised the requirements for producers and operators. They are now obliged to fully implement standardized production practices and to use agricultural inputs safely. Meanwhile, local authorities have strengthened the supervision of agricultural inputs and enhanced systems governing origin certification, market access, inspection and monitoring, and quality traceability. These measures significantly improve the quality and safety of agricultural products but also introduce certain capital or technological thresholds for producing high-quality products45. As a result, some farmers lacking a comparative advantage in agriculture exit production and transfer their land, while those with a comparative advantage are inclined to expand their operations by acquiring additional land46. Under the scaled operation system, the continuous influx of human capital into rural areas facilitates the emergence of new agricultural operators, such as large-scale grain growers and professional farmers47. These entities are characterized by higher labor productivity, stronger management capabilities, and more efficient resource utilization. They are more inclined to adopt modern agricultural technologies and scientific management practices, thereby enhancing agricultural profitability.

The theory of new endogenous development emphasizes the need to leverage local resources, activate the practical motivations of local actors, and fully utilize external resources to achieve sustainable regional development48. Geographical indication products, as regional resource markers formed by natural and human factors, constitute a fundamental condition for this development model49. The key objective of establishing Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties is to strengthen local governments’ territorial management responsibilities, enhance oversight of agricultural inputs, conduct quality and safety inspections, improve regulatory systems, and comprehensively promote standardized agricultural production. Since GI product registration requires high standards for product quality, origin environment, production techniques, and regulatory services, the development of APQSC provides a foundation for successful GI applications50. Concurrently, the institutional improvements and infrastructure development during APQSC implementation attract related enterprises to locate in these areas51. GI brand development can concentrate production factors such as capital, talent, and technology in rural areas, promoting the formation of regional industrial clusters52. Specifically, the price premium effects of GI products attract more enterprises into the market, thereby increasing demand for primary agricultural products53. This expands the scale of GI product production, facilitates the diffusion of management experience and new technologies, and improves agricultural labor productivity54. Simultaneously, industrial clustering promotes the extension of agriculture from cultivation and breeding to processing and distribution, forming industrial clusters with complementary upstream and downstream linkages, multi-chain integration, and coordinated agglomeration, thereby generating agglomeration economies55. Furthermore, in markets characterized by intense homogenization, GI products possess distinct price advantages56. By building competitive advantages based on unique functional, emotional, and social values, GI products cater to diverse consumer preferences and enhance purchase intentions57. Benefiting from the increased price elasticity of demand for GI products, regional agricultural profits and production incentives are significantly enhanced, thereby promoting local agricultural economic development58. Based on the above analysis, this paper proposes Hypothesis 2 and Hypothesis 3:

H2:

The APQSC pilot policy promotes county-level agricultural economic growth through scale operation.

H3:

The APQSC pilot policy promotes county-level agricultural economic growth by fostering the development of geographical indication brands.

Methods and materials

Dataset used

This study utilizes data from Chinese county-level administrative units from 2010 to 2023. Data on the value-added of the primary and secondary industries, the number of primary school students, year-end population, local government budgetary expenditure, total loans from financial institutions, and cultivated land area were sourced from the China County Statistical Yearbook, the China Statistical Yearbook, statistical bulletins on national economic and social development from individual counties, and the EPS database for various years. The list of National Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County (APQSC) pilot areas and their respective launch dates was obtained from the pilot lists published on the website of China’s Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs. Data on the number of agricultural geographical indications (GIs) were compiled from the China Green Food Development Center’s annual statistical reports.

To ensure data accuracy, county samples with extensive missing data were excluded, resulting in a final balanced panel dataset of 1,609 counties for analysis. Furthermore, interpolation was used to impute some missing values. Using 2010 as the base year, county-level data on gross domestic product, value-added of the primary and secondary industries, local government budgetary expenditure, and total loans from financial institutions were deflated using provincial GDP deflators.

Methods setting

Continuous DID

The difference-in-differences (DID) model is an effective tool for exploring causal relationships between policies and outcomes, while significantly mitigating endogeneity concerns. When elements such as the timing of the policy shock, treatment groups, and control groups form a quasi-natural experiment, the DID model can estimate the net effect of policy changes by measuring temporal effects before and after implementation and differences between groups59. This study treats the National Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County (APQSC) pilot initiative as a quasi-natural experiment and employs a DID model to examine its economic effects. Since the first and second batches of APQSC pilot counties were implemented in 2015 and 2017, respectively, and exhibit temporal and spatial variation, we further adopt a multi-period DID approach to precisely evaluate policy impacts. By repeatedly estimating policy effects across multiple time points, the multi-period DID method enhances statistical power, reduces random errors, and consequently improves the stability and credibility of the estimates. Results and analysis

Benchmark regression results and analysis

Table 2 reports the baseline regression results examining the impact of the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County initiative on county-level agricultural economic growth. Column (1) presents the results controlling only for county and year fixed effects. The estimated coefficient for the APQSC pilot policy is 0.041, which is positive and statistically significant at the 1% level. Columns (2), (3), and (6) show the results after progressively adding control variables while maintaining county and year fixed effects. The coefficient for the APQSC policy remains stable in magnitude and statistically significant at the 1% level across these specifications. This indicates that the implementing the APQSC pilot policy significantly promotes county-level agricultural economic growth, thereby supporting Hypothesis 1. Columns (4) and (5) present the results controlling only for county fixed effects and time fixed effects, respectively. The coefficient for the pilot policy remains positive and significant at the 1% level in both columns. Although the magnitude varies slightly, the overall stability of the result further validates the positive contribution of the APQSC pilot policy to agricultural economic growth. As the results demonstrate, consistent with the analysis above, the establishment of county-level agricultural product quality and safety programs has strengthened local regulatory oversight. The enhanced quality and safety of agricultural products increase their appeal to consumers, enabling price premiums and thereby promoting the growth of the local agricultural economy62. Goodman-Bacon decomposition test

The use of two-way fixed effects (TWFE) estimators in multi-period difference-in-differences (DID) designs can introduce bias when treatment effects are heterogeneous. This bias may arise if so-called “bad controls”—units where trends already diverged prior to the policy intervention—are included in the control group. To address this issue and mitigate the bias stemming from the use of different control groups in multi-period DID settings, this study follows Goodman’s approach to decompose the DID estimator. The results of the Goodman-Bacon decomposition test are reported in Table 363. The decomposition shows that the comparison between the “Eventually Treated vs. Never Treated” groups accounts for 98% of the total weight. In contrast, the DID estimator comparing the “Later-treated vs. Earlier-treated” groups assigns a minimal weight of only 0.9%. This small share indicates that the multi-period DID estimates are unlikely to be severely biased (Table 3).

To further test the robustness of the baseline regression, this study employs an alternative measure for the dependent variable65. Mitigating contemporaneous policy interference

The baseline regression might be influenced by other policies implemented during the study period that also affect agricultural economic growth, thereby potentially confounding the estimated net effect of the APQSC pilot policy66. Specifically, China rolled out several other pilot programs overlapping with our sample period, including the Three Rural Land System Reform pilot initiated in 2014, and the pilot programs for Mortgage Loans Using Rural Land Contractual Management Rights and Mortgage Loans Using Farmers’ Housing Property Rights, both launched in 2015. To isolate the impact of the APQSC pilot policy from these potential confounders, dummy variables indicating the implementation of these aforementioned policies were constructed and included as additional control variables in the baseline model. The estimation results, presented in Column (3) of Table 5, show that the coefficient on the APQSC pilot policy remains positive and statistically significant at the 1% level even after including the dummy variables for other policies. This finding further confirms the robustness of the baseline regression results.

Heterogeneity tests

Substantial variations in economic development, natural endowment, infrastructure, and levels of digitalization exist across Chinese counties. These differences may lead to heterogeneous effects of the APQSC initiative on county-level agricultural economic growth.

Regional heterogeneity

Due to distinct geographical conditions, agricultural economic development varies across Eastern, Central, and Western China. This study examines the differential impacts of the APQSC policy by dividing the sample into these three regions. The results are presented in Columns (1), (2), and (3) of Table 6. The estimated coefficients for the APQSC policy are positive and statistically significant at the 5% level in the Eastern and Central regions, but insignificant in the Western region. A plausible explanation is that the Western region’s topography is predominantly characterized by plateaus, mountains, and deserts, coupled with uneven water resource distribution, imposing stronger natural constraints on agricultural production compared to the Eastern and Central regions. Specifically in crop cultivation, agricultural production in the West faces challenges, including lower soil fertility, which leads to a greater reliance on chemical inputs such as fertilizers. Consequently, policies that enhance quality supervision and reduce chemical usage may, to some extent, constrain short-term agricultural output growth in these areas67.

Heterogeneity in economic development levels

The effect of the APQSC pilot policy on agricultural economic growth may vary across counties. Using county-level GDP as the indicator of economic development, this study divides the sample into counties with higher and lower economic development levels based on the median GDP. The regression results are presented in Columns (4) and (5) of Table 6. The estimated coefficient for the APQSC policy is positive and statistically significant at the 5% level in counties with higher economic development, but insignificant in counties with lower development levels. This discrepancy may be attributed to more established quality supervision systems and more mature application of digital technologies in economically advanced countries, which allow for greater effectiveness of quality regulation. Furthermore, counties with higher economic development typically possess better agricultural infrastructure, making them more conducive to developing agricultural brands, expanding consumer markets for agricultural products, and realizing the economic benefits of high-quality products. Consequently, the positive economic effects on county-level agricultural growth are more pronounced in these areas68.

Heterogeneity in agricultural functional zoning

A county’s resource endowment and production advantages play a crucial role in its agricultural development. Compared with grain-deficient areas and areas with balanced production and marketing, major grain-producing areas possess richer agricultural resources and more developed agricultural industrial chains69. To investigate whether the impact of the APQSC pilot policy on county-level agricultural economic growth varies across resource endowment levels, this study divides the sample into major and non-major grain-producing areas based on agricultural functional zoning and conducts separate multi-period DID regressions for each group. Columns (1) and (2) of Table 7 report the regression results for these different functional zones. The results show that the estimated coefficients of the pilot policy are positive for both major and non-major producing areas. However, the coefficient for the major grain-producing areas is statistically significant at the 1% level, indicating a more pronounced promoting effect of the APQSC initiative on agricultural economic growth in these regions. A probable explanation is that following the establishment of major grain-producing zones, China’s agricultural industrial policy has prioritized these regions, leading to more concentrated agricultural production activities within them. Compared to other areas, these zones possess more advanced agricultural infrastructure, more developed industrial systems, and superior production technologies70. On the one hand, the well-established infrastructure in these major grain-producing zones enhances the effectiveness of quality supervision under the quality and safety county initiative, thereby ensuring agricultural product quality and improving market performance. Meanwhile, the robust industrial system further adds value to high-quality agricultural products, thus contributing to agricultural economic development. On the other hand, higher production concentration, greater economies of scale, and more advanced technologies in these key production areas effectively reduce the input of agricultural production materials, particularly chemical fertilizers and pesticides. This not only safeguards quality and safety but also lowers production costs.

Mechanism analysis

Analysis of the operational scale effect mechanism

The results from Model (6) in Table 2 demonstrate that the APQSC pilot policy significantly promotes county-level agricultural economic growth. Columns (1) and (2) of Table 8 report the results of the mechanism test for the operational scale effect. The result in Column (1) shows that the estimated coefficient for the APQSC pilot policy is positive and significant at the 5% level, indicating that the policy effectively encourages an appropriate expansion of regional operational scale. The result in Column (2) shows that the estimated coefficients for both operational scale and the pilot policy are positive and significant at the 1% level. This indicates that implementing of the APQSC pilot policy promotes county-level agricultural economic growth by driving the expansion of regional operational scale, thereby validating Hypothesis 2 (H2). Theoretical analysis and practical observation suggest that APQSC imposes strict quality control and standardized production requirements. This incentivizes small, scattered farmers to transfer their land to larger entities or join cooperatives to access unified technical guidance, input supply, and quality control, thereby fostering large-scale and intensive production models. When agricultural production scales up and intensifies, it effectively increases the productivity of factors such as land and labor, thereby promoting the development of the county-level agricultural economy.

Analysis of the geographical indication brand effect mechanism

Geographical Indications (GI) effectively enhance the recognition and influence of agricultural products. Their registration requires not only regional characteristics linked to local natural ecosystems and human history but also high product-quality standards. Columns (3) and (4) of Table 8 report the mechanism test results for the GI brand effect. Column (3) shows that the estimated coefficient for the APQSC pilot policy is positive and significant at the 1% level, indicating that establishing APQSC provides a solid foundation for GI applications and facilitates their successful registration. Column (4) shows that the estimated coefficients for both the APQSC policy and GI product certification are positive, significant at the 1% and 5% levels, respectively. This indicates that the implementation of the APQSC pilot policy effectively promotes GI registration, thereby driving county-level agricultural development, thus validating Hypothesis 3 (H3). As suggested by the analysis above, the development of APQSC provides quality assurance for GI products, offering strong support for their successful application. Integrating theoretical and practical perspectives, the development of GI can attract relevant agribusinesses to locate in the region, fostering industrial agglomeration, extending the agricultural industrial chain, and improving benefit-sharing mechanisms. This not only promotes the transition to high-quality agricultural development but also creates more non-farm employment opportunities for farmers, thereby increasing their incomes.

Discussion

The transition of the agricultural economy towards high-quality development necessitates not only stable output to ensure food security, but also significant improvements in the quality of agricultural products. Existing research has extensively explored the factors influencing agricultural productivity, analyzing the contributions of land73, technology74, and human capital16 to agricultural economic growth. The findings of this study, however, demonstrate that enhancing agricultural product quality also effectively promotes agricultural economic growth. In the short term, strengthened quality supervision entails substantial upfront investment and ongoing maintenance costs for infrastructure, which may initially slow down or even negatively impact agricultural economic growth75. Nevertheless, our balanced trend test results indicate that over the long term, as policies are implemented, enhanced quality supervision and improved product quality exert increasingly significant positive effects on agricultural economic growth. This suggests that with consumption upgrading, high-quality agricultural products gain greater consumer preference, command price premiums, and enhance market competitiveness, thereby driving the agricultural economy towards high-quality development76.

Mechanism test results reveal that the pilot policy for Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties drives agricultural economic growth through two key pathways: economies of scale in cultivated land management and brand effects derived from geographical indications. Enhanced quality supervision effectively expands operational scale in policy implementation areas, thereby achieving economies of scale77. In traditional fragmented agriculture, smallholders—constrained by limited capital, information, and managerial capacity—often lack access to advanced machinery or smart technologies. Consequently, they tend to rely on increased chemical inputs, such as fertilizers and pesticides, to boost yields and generate economic gains78. Agricultural products grown under these conditions, however, fail to meet quality inspection standards in policy implementation zones and cannot gain a competitive advantage. Through land transfers that enable scaled operations, producers can optimize production factors and implement standardized processes. This approach ensures compliance with quality standards, facilitates participation in traceability systems, and ultimately enhances product pricing79. Moreover, expanding operational scale reduces input costs across multiple production factors, generating significant cost savings80. When policy implementation secures local product quality, geographical indications can be obtained based on regional advantages, thereby creating brand value. The resulting spillover effects attract agribusiness clusters, promote regional industrial chain development, and consequently enhance agricultural economic performance81.

This study further examines the policy effects under diverse conditions. Heterogeneity tests reveal that the pilot policy for Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties demonstrates more substantial promoting effects on agricultural economies in eastern China, major agricultural production zones, and regions with more advanced economic development, digital infrastructure, and transportation systems. The effective implementation of quality supervision fundamentally depends on a well-established infrastructure. Eastern China’s predominantly flat terrain facilitates large-scale farming operations and management82. Major agricultural production zones benefit from abundant agricultural resources and have developed preliminary processing capacities over the years through policy support83. Regions with higher economic development levels possess better-organized agricultural service organizations that provide comprehensive production support, thereby enhancing efficiency84. Improved digital infrastructure enables stronger supervision through digital devices, while higher digital literacy among local farmers enables effective use of these technologies to ensure product quality and safety68. Well-developed transportation networks expand the market reach of high-quality agricultural products72. These comparative advantages collectively enhance policy implementation, ensuring both rigorous supervision and seamless production-to-sales processes for quality agricultural products, ultimately facilitating the transition towards high-quality agricultural development.

This study nevertheless has certain limitations that merit further research. While it examines the relationship between the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County (APQSC) pilot policy and agricultural economic development, it does not delve into how policy implementation affects specific sectors such as crop cultivation and animal husbandry. Future research could investigate the impact of the APQSC pilot policy on regulatory practices across the agricultural supply chain, while accounting for the distinct characteristics of different agricultural subsectors. Furthermore, as an analysis of policy effects, this study focuses on the implementation outcomes across various counties nationwide but does not specifically examine the effects of different regulatory intensities. Given variations in enforcement intensity across counties, which may lead to differing degrees of regulatory rigor, future studies could develop an indicator system to assess regulatory implementation and further analyze the impact of varying regulatory intensity.

Conclusions

Based on panel data from 1,609 Chinese counties from 2010 to 2023, this study employs a multi-period difference-in-differences (DID) model to investigate the impact of the Agricultural Product Quality and Safety County pilot policy on county-level agricultural economic growth. The analysis further examines the heterogeneous effects of this policy across regions, economic conditions, agricultural functions, levels of informatization, and levels of transportation infrastructure development. Additionally, the mechanisms underlying the APQSC policy’s influence on agricultural economic growth are explored. The main findings are as follows: (1) The APQSC pilot policy significantly promotes county-level agricultural economic growth, with robustness confirmed through PSM-DID tests, exclusion of other policy interferences, outlier removal, and alternative dependent variable specifications. (2) The policy’s positive effects are more pronounced in central and eastern regions, major grain-producing areas, and regions with higher levels of economic development, transportation infrastructure, and digitalization. (3) The policy stimulates agricultural economic growth primarily through expanded operational scale and increased development of geographical indication agricultural products. Based on the aforementioned findings, the following policy recommendations are proposed:

Enhance the agricultural product quality and safety system and expand the pilot scope of Agricultural Product Quality and Safety Counties. First, improve the quality and safety standard system for agricultural products and promote the deep integration of “Three Products and One Standard” certification with standardized production. Strengthen grassroots regulatory capacity by refining the three-tiered (county-township-village) grid-based supervision system, deploying professional personnel, and enhancing traceability throughout the production process. Second, implement territorial responsibility for agricultural product quality and safety, clarify departmental functions, and enhance inter-agency collaboration. Promote information sharing and coordinated enforcement among departments such as agriculture and rural affairs, and market regulation, to form a cohesive regulatory force.

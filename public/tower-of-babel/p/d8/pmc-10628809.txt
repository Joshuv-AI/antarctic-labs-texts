Protocol for screening cellular outputs activated by optogenetically controlled temporal PI3K signaling activation patterns
Authors: Yoshibumi Ueda, Shohei Matsushita, Mitsugu Suzuki, Takeaki Ozawa
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SummaryPI3K signaling elicits distinct outputs in response to different patterns of extracellular stimulation. Here, we present a protocol for screening cellular outputs activated by optogenetically controlled temporal PI3K signaling activation patterns in 96-well plates. We describe steps for establishing PPAP2-stable cells, probe expression, and blue light irradiation. We then detail procedures for analysis of translation activity. This protocol can be applied for purposes, such as examining the effect of PI3K signaling on the efficacy of anticancer drugs.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Ueda et al. (2022).1Subject areas: Cell Biology, Cell Membrane, Cancer

Before you begin

PI3K signaling responds to various temporal patterns of extracellular stimulation to exert multiple cellular outputs. For instance, blood insulin levels exhibit distinct temporal patterns,2,3,4 which are crucial for distinct functional outputs such as protein translation, glycogenesis, and lipid synthesis elicited via PI3K signaling.5,6 Therefore, exploring which temporal patterns of PI3K signaling activation amplify specific cellular outputs is important for a deep understanding of this signaling. The protocol below describes how to screen the effect of PI3K signaling activation patterns on cellular outputs using optogenetics in 96-well plates. The optogenetic tool named PPAP2 (PIP3 production by photoactivated PI3K, version 2) makes it possible to activate PI3K signaling alone at a higher level than can be achieved with physiological stimulation, such as platelet-derived growth factor and insulin, in cultured cells.1 First, PPAP2-stable cell lines are established to maximize the output of PPAP2. Next, PPAP2 is seeded into 96-well plates, and then, probes for detecting PI3K signaling downstream outputs are expressed, in this case, a chemiluminescence probe for the eIF4E-dependent translation pathway as an example.7 Using LED boards that emit various temporal patterns of blue light, we can easily determine which patterns of PI3K signaling activation are the most efficient for outputs in 96-well plates. The present protocol describes the key steps to perform this experiment with A375 melanoma cells. However, this method can be applied to other cell lines, such as U87 glioblastoma cells and SW620 colorectal cancer cells, because we have previously established cell lines that permanently express PPAP2 with identical procedures.1 Additionally, this screening method can be applied to investigate the effect of PI3K signaling activation patterns on cancer drug resistance and other signaling pathways, including MAP kinase signaling.

Principle of PPAP2

We have developed optogenetic tools, termed PPAP, that allow the activation of PI3K signaling. PPAP1 and PPAP2 were developed based on the CRY2/CIBN8 and iLID systems,9 respectively. PPAP2 enables the control of PI3K signaling with higher temporal resolution than PPAP1. To construct PPAP2 (Figure 1), an Avena sativa LOV2 domain fused to SsrA (LOV2-SsrA) was attached to the plasma membrane via the CAAX motif of K-Ras, whereas SspB was expressed in the cytosol with the inter-SH2 domain of SspB (iSH2-SspB) that binds to the endogenous PI3K catalytic domain, p110. In darkness, iSH2-SspB remains in the cytosol because SsrA is sterically caged by the LOV2 domain. Under blue light irradiation, SsrA undergoes a conformational change to unfold the C-terminal end of the LOV2 domain, leading to the interaction of iSH2-SspB with SsrA at the plasma membrane. The translocation of iSH2-SspB in association with p110 allows the generation of phosphatidylinositol 3,4,5-trisphosphate (PIP3) from phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate. To produce equal amounts of iSH2-SspB and LOV2-SsrA, the two constructs were connected through P2A and T2A peptides, which induce proteolysis when expressed in target cells.10 To visualize half the PPAP2 moieties, we fused the mVenus and mCherry fluorescent proteins to LOV2-SsrA (PPAP2.1) and iSH2-SspB (PPAP2.2), respectively.

Basic principle of the PPAP2 optogenetic tool

A luminescence probe for detecting an eIF4E-dependent translation pathway downstream of PI3K signaling

Cap-rLuc-IRES-fLuc, obtained from Addgene (Plasmid #45642),7 was used to evaluate the effect of PPAP-induced PI3K signal activation on protein translation. This probe consists of two domains: one for detecting cap-dependent translation and the other for examining internal ribosomal entry site (IRES)-dependent translation. Because PI3K signaling activates cap-dependent translation through eIF4E, the rLuc expression level is expected to increase upon PI3K signaling activation, whereas IRES-dependent translation-derived fLuc expression decreases.

3.Place the circulation board in a 37°C incubator.

Place the circulation board in a 37°C incubator.

The size of the circulation board used in the present study was 30 cm × 30 cm. This circulation board was placed in a small incubator at 37°C in 5% CO2 (MCO-50AIC, PHCbi) and can be remained in these conditions for at least one year.

Expected outcomes

PI3K signaling is regulated by the specific frequency of the signal. The present system enables a convenient assay to find the specific frequency. In our previous study, we found that a specific frequency (5 s in 1 min) efficiently increased the activity of the translation pathway downstream of PI3K signaling.1 Other efficient frequencies could be found for other outputs downstream of PI3K signaling, such as autophagy and lamellipodia, with appropriate detection probes. Additionally, combined with multiplate absorption photometers, the effect of PI3K signaling on cancer drug-induced cell death can be examined using cell counting kits.

Quantification and statistical analysis

Quantification and statistical analyses are described throughout the protocol.

Limitations

Although PPAP2, as a catalytic domain of PI3K, does not overexpress p110, Akt basal activity increases slightly compared to that under serum-starved conditions. For a translation pathway assay, a slight basal increase in PI3K signaling was negligible. However, we need to use PPAP2 with caution due to the effects of slight PI3K signaling activation.

Troubleshooting

For protocol step 3, whereas blue light can effectively control PI3K signaling activation with a high temporal resolution, it also strongly affects cell viability for the following reasons.

1.Medium temperature increase.

Medium temperature increase.

Although blue light is superior to red and far-red light in preventing an increase in the medium temperature, it still increases the temperature. Vapor sometimes condenses on the cover of 96-well plates when strong blue light (such as 1 W/cm2) is used for a long time (such as 24 h), causing loss of the solution volume and the condensation of medium components.

2.Phototoxicity.

Phototoxicity.

When cells were subjected to extended irradiation, such as for periods longer than 72 h, strong light reduced the cell number compared to that of cells cultured under dark conditions. Further investigation will be needed to elucidate the causes.

Potential solution

The above issues can be improved by reducing the blue light intensity. Less than 1 W/m2 power should be used for long-term irradiation. The blue light power can be controlled as follows.

1.Each well of a 96-well plate should be filled with medium for long-term blue light irradiation.

Each well of a 96-well plate should be filled with medium for long-term blue light irradiation.

2.The distance between cells and LEDs should be extended by inserting black 96-well plates.

3.ND filters should be inserted between cells and LEDs.

ND filters should be inserted between cells and LEDs.

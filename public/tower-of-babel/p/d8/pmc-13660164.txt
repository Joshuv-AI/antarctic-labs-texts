TCRζ-driven pre-signaling organization of Lck in Rab11⁺ endosomes shapes TCR activation
Authors: Konstantina Karpouzou, Nikolaos Koutras, Ioannis Tyritidis, Evangelos Tsioupros, Andriana G Kotini, Vassilis Roukos, Konstantina Nika
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundT cell activation relies on the precise spatiotemporal regulation of T cell receptor (TCR) signaling at the immunological synapse, where vesicular trafficking coordinates the delivery of key signaling molecules. Endosomal pools of lymphocyte-specific protein tyrosine kinase (Lck) and its immediate substrate, the TCRζ chain, have been linked to TCR signaling competence, yet the mechanisms that regulate and coordinate their trafficking routes remain unresolved.MethodsWe simultaneously tracked the endosomal localization dynamics of endogenous Lck, ζ chain, and the TCR in unperturbed cells and under conditions that preserve the endosomal network integrity. Genetic modifications, confocal microscopy, quantitative phospho-flow analysis, and biochemical assays were used to map trafficking behaviors and signaling outputs. Datasets were analyzed using unpaired Student’s t-tests.ResultsWe identified a previously unrecognized plasma membrane-resident pool of ζ that exists independently of the TCR complex yet remains competent for phosphorylation and ZAP-70 recruitment. This “standalone” ζ population orchestrates the mobilization of Lck and CD45 into Rab11-positive compartments, establishing pre-assembled, signaling-primed platforms prior to synapse formation. These structures can accommodate constitutively phosphorylated ζ and ζ-chain-associated protein kinase 70 (ZAP-70), which depend on the presence of endosomal Lck.ConclusionsThis work uncovers a mechanistic link between vesicular ζ and Lck pools and establishes their coordinated trafficking as a key determinant of TCR activation. Our findings indicate that vesicular preprimed assemblies can provide a reservoir of signaling mediators, serving to sustain TCR signaling without a requirement for continuous receptor engagement, thereby determining the threshold, sensitivity, and persistence of T cell responses. By revealing a TCR-uncoupled ζ population that seeds endosomal signaling platforms, our study advances current models of T cell activation and highlights an additional layer of regulation in proximal signaling events. These insights also have translational implications, particularly within the context of improving the efficiency, performance, and safety of T cell immunotherapies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains Supplementary Material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s11658-026-00993-x.Keywords: Lck, TCR, ζ chain, Endosomal trafficking, Rab11, TCR signaling, T cell activation

Background

T cell activation is triggered by the binding of the TCR to cognate peptide–major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules displayed an antigen-presenting cells (APCs). From the biochemical perspective, initiation of signaling cascades is marked by the phosphorylation of conserved immunoreceptor-based tyrosine activation motifs (ITAM) tyrosines located in the cytoplasmic tails of the receptor complex, a process tightly regulated by the Src family kinase (SFKs) lymphocyte-specific protein tyrosine kinase (Lck). Phosphorylated ITAMs provide anchoring motifs for the recruitment and subsequent activation of ζ-chain-associated protein kinase 70 (ZAP-70), which in turn phosphorylates the adapter protein linker for activation of T cells (LAT). Phosphorylated LAT acts as a scaffold for signalosome formation, promoting signal amplification and diversification and ultimately leading to full T cell activation [1]. From the spatial perspective, TCR signaling is organized, regulated, and sustained at the immunological synapse (IS), a precisely ordered structure formed at the interface between T cells and APCs [2, 3]. The orchestrated configuration and functionality of the IS depends on time-dependent accumulation, distribution, disassembly, and dynamic reorganization of supramolecular complexes enriched in TCRs, coreceptors, and several signaling mediators and adaptors [4]. All these events are largely mediated by highly divergent endosomal vesicular transport networks [5]. Several key regulators of T cell responses such as Lck, TCRζ, ZAP-70, and LAT are mobilized from vesicular compartments and accumulate at the IS upon TCR engagement [6, 7]. Moreover, not only ligand-engaged but also bystander TCRs undergo rapid endocytosis and recycling to the plasma membrane (PM) via endosomal networks, with part of the internalized species being degraded in lysosomes. This behavior also applies to unligated receptors, which constitutively follow specialized routes of endocytosis, degradation, and recycling. Synchronously, these mechanisms fine-tune TCR surface expression levels, set the thresholds for signal transduction amplitude, sustainment and termination, and ultimately shape T cell responses [8–12].

The TCR is equipped with a peptide–MHC (pMHC) binding compartment formed by the clonotypic α and β chains and an ITAM-bearing signal-transducing unit, composed of the invariant CD3γε and CD3δε heterodimers, and a ζζ homodimer. Assembly of the TCR is initiated in the endoplasmic reticulum and completed in the Golgi apparatus by the association of the ζ homodimer. The ζ chain not only constitutes the limiting factor for both TCR transport to the cell surface but also for stable expression of the complex at the plasma membrane (PM) [13].

Although TCR and CD3 are often concurrently downregulated, the intracellular trafficking and fate of the ζ subunit is, at least in part, uncoupled from the rest of the complex, characterized by prolonged and more readily detectable residency within endosomes. Furthermore, dissociation of ζ from the rest of the TCR, alongside a continuous exchange with TCR–CD3 partial complexes has been reported [14–17]. Endosomal ζ has been the focus of extensive investigation, with numerous studies, occasionally reporting divergent results, implicating Rab4, Rab5, Rab8, Rab11, VAMP3, VAMP7, and IRAP as its colocalization partners [5, 7, 18–20]. Vesicular ζ has been shown to originate from internalized cell surface-resident ζ molecules, to dynamically exchange with the PM and to provide a sustained supply of ζ molecules to the immunological synapse. Notably, endosomal ζ —particularly the fraction colocalizing within Rab11—has been found to be tyrosine phosphorylated in resting and stimulated cells, an event attributed to the coresidence of Lck within the same compartment [18, 20]. Accordingly, the biological implications of Lck residency in the Rab11-decorated endosomal compartment (Rab11+ EC) have been extensively characterized [7, 21, 22]. Collectively, chemical treatments or genetic deletion of key recycling endosomal regulators have shown that disrupting the vesicular trafficking of Lck and ζ impairs their recruitment to the immunological synapse and compromises TCR signaling [18, 20, 21, 23]. However, these approaches have largely relied on fluorescently tagged proteins or synthetic ζ chain reporters, without fully addressing how such modifications may affect their incorporation into the native TCR complex. Moreover, chemical or genetic perturbations of endosomal regulators may introduce unintended alterations in endosomal network architecture and function. Lastly, despite extensive evidence highlighting the biological importance of the vesicular Lck and ζ pools, the extent to which their trafficking dynamics are interconnected and coregulated remains incompletely understood.

In this work, we concurrently trace the endosomal localization dynamics of endogenous TCR, Lck, and the ζ chain in unperturbed cells, under conditions that preserve the integrity of the endosomal network. Our findings reveal a previously unrecognized role of TCRζ as a driver for Lck and CD45 residency within the Rab11+ endosomal compartment (EC). We provide evidence that this property is accredited to a PM-resident ζ pool that is physically uncoupled from the TCR complex yet retains signaling competence. These insights contribute to advancing our understanding on TCR sensitivity and rapid responses and hold particular relevance within the context of T cell immunotherapies.

Methods

Study design

This study was designed to investigate the mechanisms that shape the internalization and recycling dynamics of the proximal TCR signaling machinery, with a particular focus on the interplay between Lck and the TCRζ chain. We aimed to define putative interconnections between Lck and ζ residency within Rab11+ endosomes, and how such trafficking events impact TCR signaling output.

To address these questions, we combined genetic, biochemical, and imaging approaches in human T cells and engineered cell lines. Confocal microscopy was used to define the subcellular distribution and colocalization of endogenous and ectopically expressed proteins, while flow cytometry quantified phosphorylation-based signaling readouts. Lentiviral transductions and CRISPR–Cas9-mediated gene editing were applied to elucidate how ζ expression could influence Lck endosomal residence. Engineered Lck chimeras failing to localize within the Rab11+ EC were employed to define and dissect endosomal versus PM-restricted pools of TCR signaling. Sample size, replicates, and statistical tests were chosen according to established standards in cellular/molecular immunology, with all key findings reproduced in at least three independent experiments. No data were excluded from analysis.

Cells, treatments, and stimulations

All cell lines were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified incubator (PHCbi) with 5% CO₂. Human embryonic kidney (HEK) 293 T cells (ATCC® CRL-3216) were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle medium (DMEM) (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco). Jurkat (DSMZ no. ACC 282), JCaM1.6 (ATCC® CRL-2063), J76 (Jurkat subline devoid of endogenous TCR alpha and beta chains; a generous gift from Prof. Björn Lillemeier, Faculty of Biology, University of Freiburg), and BJAB (DSMZ ACC-757), were maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI) 1640 medium (Gibco) containing 10% FBS.

Cells engineered for doxycycline (Dox)-inducible expression were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% tetracycline-free FBS. The BJAB-LckWT (wild-type Lck) cell line, expressing wild-type human Lck tagged with green fluorescent protein (GFP) in its C-terminus has been previously described [24]. All lines were routinely tested for mycoplasma (Universal Mycoplasma Detection Kit-ATCC, 30-1-12 K), lineage-specific surface markers and CD45 expression.

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were separated from whole blood of healthy donors by density gradient centrifugation (Ficoll-Paque™ PLUS, Cytiva/GE Healthcare). Primary human CD4⁺ T cells were purified from PBMCs using the Untouched™ Human CD4 kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, cat. no. 11346D), with purity ≥ 95%. Cells were routinely kept in culture (RPMI 1640, 10% FBS) overnight prior to analyses.

For ex vivo activation/expansion, PBMCs were resuspended in complete RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FCS and seeded into plastic tissue culture flasks to remove the monocyte fraction. Following overnight incubation, the nonadherent fraction was collected and stimulated for polyclonal expansion with anti-CD3/CD28 beads (Dynabeads™ Human T-Activator CD3/CD28 for T cell expansion and activation; Thermo Fisher, cat. no. 11161D) at a 1:1 bead:cell ratio. A total of 50 U/mL of human recombinant IL-2 (rhIL-2 Miltenyi Biotec, cat. no. 130-097-742) was added to the culture at day 3, and cells were cultured for up to 6 days prior to analysis.

For signal transduction-triggering experiments, cells (1 × 10⁶cells/mL) were stimulated by 1 µg/mL soluble anti-human CD3ε (clone UCHT1) for 2 min at 37 °C. For subcellular distribution and colocalization evaluations by confocal imaging, cells were first activated in an Eppendorf tube with 1 µg/mL anti-CD3ε for 1 min at 37 °C and then transferred onto polyDlysine-coated coverslips and allowed to adhere for an additional 10 min at 37 °C before fixation and staining. For CD69 expression measurements, 1 × 10⁶cells were cultured for 18 h in the absence or presence of plate-bound anti-human CD3ε (5 µg/mL per well) prior to FACS analysis.

For pan-SFK (Src family kinases) inhibition, cells were cultured with 30 μM PP2 for 18 h in the humidified incubator.

Pervanadate solutions were prepared immediately prior to each assay to ensure maximal activity. For pervanadate stimulation, cells, plated on coverslips, were incubated with 100 μM pervanadate for 2, 5, and 10 min at 37 °C (depending on the experiment, as indicated in the respective figure legends) and immediately fixed to preserve phospho-epitopes and endosomal architecture.

For acid-wash treatment, following incubation with a-CD3ε antibodies, cells were washed with ice-cold phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and incubated for 3 min on ice in acid-stripping buffer (0.2 M glycine–HCl, 150 mM NaCl, pH 2.5), followed by immediate neutralization with excess ice-cold PBS, and washed two additional times with PBS.

Cloning and plasmids

The complementary DNA (cDNA) of human LckWT, the LckY394F, ΔSH4 mutants, and human TCRζ chain (cloned in the lentiviral vector pHR-Bin) were kindly provided by Prof. Oreste Acuto (University of Oxford). Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck were generated by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) using LckΔSH4 as template, and oligonucleotides encoding the SH4 domains of Fyn (amino acids 1–11, forward primer: ATAAGAATGCGGCCGCATGGGCTGTGTGCAATGTAAGGATAAAGAAGCAGATGACTGGATGGAAAACATC) and Lyn (amino acids 1–13, forward primer: ATAAGAATGCGGCCGCATGGGATGTATAAAATCAAAAGGGAAAGACAGCTTGAGTGATGACTGGATGGAAAACATC), respectively. All Lck constructs were cloned in the pLVX-Tight-Puro vector (Clontech Laboratories), between 5′ NotI and 3′ EcoRI restriction sites. The lentiviral packaging plasmids pVSVG (plasmid no. 8454) and pSPAX2 (plasmid no. 12260) were purchased from Addgene. The Lck SH4 domain (amino acids 1–10) was cloned into the the pEBG vector to generate an N-terminal GST fusion protein for use in pull-down assays.

Generation of Dox-inducible cell lines

Lentiviral particles were generated using HEK 293 T cells as the packaging system. Cells were seeded 1 day prior to transfection and used at 60–80% confluency. Transfection was performed using polyethylenimine (PEI; Polysciences) following a standard PEI-based protocol. The transfer plasmids pLVX-Tet-On-Advanced and pLVX-Tight-Puro (Clontech Laboratories), encoding the rtTA-Advanced transactivator and genes of interest, respectively, were cotransfected with the packaging plasmids pSPAX2 and pVSVG. DNA and PEI were diluted in 200 µL of Opti-MEM (reduced-serum medium) (Gibco) incubated at room temperature for 20 min, and added dropwise to the cells. Culture medium was replaced with fresh DMEM containing 10% FBS after 18 h. Viral supernatants were collected 48 h after transfection and used for transduction.

For transduction, approximately, 5 × 105 cells were incubated with viral supernatants for 24 h in the presence of 5 µg/mL hexadimethrine bromide (Polybrene; Sigma-Aldrich). After incubation, the viral media were discarded and replaced with fresh RPMI supplemented with 10% FBS. Cells were allowed to recover for 48 h at 37 °C and 5% CO₂, after which selection was initiated using 5 µg/mL puromycin and 200 µg/mL geneticin (G418). For induced gene-of-interest expression, cells were cultured in the presence of 1 µg/mL doxycycline (Sigma-Aldrich) for 24 h prior to analyses.

CRISPR–Cas9-mediated knockout of TCRζ

Biallelic TCRζ-inactivated clones were generated using the lentiviral vector lentiCRISPR v2 (Addgene plasmid no. 52961), which co-expresses a human codon-optimized Cas9 with a nuclear localization signal, under the elongation factor 1-alpha (EF-1), an immediate early promoter, together with a guide RNA (gRNA) driven by the U6 promoter. A previously validated gRNA targeting exon 2 of the CD247 gene [25] (sequence: AGCAGAGTTTGGGATCCAGC) was inserted downstream of the U6 promoter sequence. HEK293 cells were cotransfected with the lentiCRISPR v2-gRNA-TCRζ lentiviral vector and pMD2.G (Addgene plasmid no. 12259) and psPAX2 (Addgene plasmid no. 12260). Jurkat cells were transduced with viral supernatants as described above; after 24 h, 1 mL of fresh medium was added, and the following day, the cells were subjected to puromycin selection. ζ knockout was confirmed by FACS analysis and with a-CD3ε to confirm downregulation of surface TCR expression.

Flow cytometry

For intracellular staining, cells were fixed in BD Phosflow™ Fix Buffer I (BD Biosciences) at 37 °C for 10 min. Fixed cells were centrifuged and permeabilized in 150 µL of PBS supplemented with 0.5% bovine serum albumin (BSA) and 0.5% Triton X-100 (Millipore) for 15 min at 37 °C. Cells were then incubated with primary antibodies and diluted in PBS with 0.5% BSA and 0.1% Tween 20 for 1 h at 37 °C. Following two washes, where needed, samples were incubated with the corresponding fluorescent secondary antibodies for 45 min at 37 °C. Cells were washed twice and then analyzed on BD Accuri™ C6 Plus Flow Cytometer.

For TCR downregulation estimations after pervanadate treatment, cells were resuspended in 100 µL of FACS buffer (PBS containing 0.5% BSA) and incubated on ice for 15 min to block nonspecific binding. After centrifugation, supernatants were removed, and cells were stained with Alexa 647-conjugated CD3ε diluted in FACS buffer for 30 min on ice. Acquired data were analyzed by FlowJo Software (BD Biosciences).

Immunofluorescence, confocal microscopy image acquisition, and analysis

For the preparation of confocal microscopy samples, single‐cell suspensions were seeded onto polyDlysine-coated (Gibco) coverslips (Marienfeld Superior). Cells were fixed with BD Phosflow™ Fix Buffer I (BD Biosciences) for 10 min at 37 °C, rinsed once in Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline (DPBS) (with Ca2⁺/Mg2⁺), and permeabilized for 5 min at 37 °C in DPBS containing 1% BSA and 0.1% Triton X100. After a DPBS wash, nonspecific sites were blocked by incubation in DPBS supplemented with 1% BSA and 0.1% Tween 20 (blocking buffer) for 10 min at 37 °C. Samples were incubated with the indicated primary antibodies, diluted in blocking/wash buffer for 1 h at 37 °C, washed three times, and then incubated for 1 h at 37 °C with the corresponding fluorescent-conjugated secondary antibodies, diluted in blocking/wash buffer. After three final washes, coverslips were mounted on microscope slides using ProLong® Gold Antifade Mountant (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

Images were captured on a Leica TCS SP5 confocal microscope (Leica Microsystems) using a 63×/1.4 NA oil immersion objective. Fluorophores were excited at 488 nm, 561 nm, and 633 nm, with z stacks collected at 0.8-μm intervals. All raw datasets were processed using ImageJ software.

For plasma membrane/endoplasmic compartment ratios, regions of interest (ROIs) delineating the PM and endosomal compartment areas were defined by CD45 and Rab11 staining, respectively, for each cell, and mean fluorescence intensities were measured within the corresponding compartments to compute the ratio. For colocalization analyses, Pearson’s correlation coefficients were determined using the Coloc2 ImageJ plugin. For each cell, a single midplane optical section was selected, and ROIs were defined as indicated in corresponding figure legends.

Immunoprecipitation, pull-down assay, and western blotting

For Lck/CD4 coimmunoprecipitation experiments, CRISPR–Cas9–TCRζ or mock-treated Jurkat cells were lysed in ice-cold lysis buffer (20 mM Tris–HCl pH 7.5, 150 mM NaCl, 1% NP-40, 0.5% n-dodecyl-β-d-maltoside, protease inhibitor cocktail 5 (AppliChem), and 1 mM Na3VO4) for 10 min on ice, prior to centrifugation at 14,000g for 15 min at 4 °C. Postnuclear lysates were incubated with rabbit anti-Lck (73A5) for 2 h at 4 °C and then with protein A/G agarose beads (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, cat. no. sc-2003) for 1 h at 4 °C. Immune complexes were washed three times. Bound proteins were eluted by boiling in Laemmli buffer at 95 °C for 10 min.

For GST pull-down, HEK293 cells were transiently transfected with the pEBG-LckSH4 construct. After 48 h, cells were harvested and lysed in ice-cold lysis buffer for 10 min on ice, prior to centrifugation at 14,000g for 15 min at 4 °C. Postnuclear lysates were incubated with Glutathione–Sepahrose 4B beads (Cytiva, cat. no. GE17-0756-01) for 4 h at 4 °C. The beads were washed three times and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with equal amounts of cell lysates from either Jurkat (TCRζ-expressing sample) or BJAB cells (negative control). Following incubation, beads were washed three times in lysis buffer. Bound proteins were eluted by boiling in Laemmli buffer at 95 °C for 10 min.

For western blotting, proteins were resolved on 4–12% precast sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS–PAGE) gels (Thermo Fisher, cat. no. XP04122BOX) and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes. Membranes were probed with corresponding primary antibodies, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and visualized in the ChemiDoc MP Imaging System (Bio-Rad).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 9 (GraphPad Software). Data were analyzed using unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test or multiple unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-tests. All data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). A P-value less than 0.05 was considered significant (*P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001; not significant [NS]). All experiments were repeated with sufficient reproducibility.

Results

Exclusion of Lck from Rab11+ endosomes correlates with reduced TCR signaling output

To gain insight into the mechanisms governing the endosomal trafficking of the most proximal TCR signaling mediators, we initiated our investigation by examining the contribution of the Lck SH4 domain, a 10-amino acid-long N-terminal segment. SH4 domains enable SFK anchoring to membranous structures; they invariably undergo cotranslational myristoylation and post-translational palmitoylation—albeit with different patterns—and can contain basic amino acids that facilitate and stabilize SFK interactions with membranes. These unique biochemical features confer distinct subcellular localizations and spatial membrane distributions to SFKs, ultimately regulating their function [26–30]. Our study incorporated chimeric constructs in which LckSH4 was substituted by the corresponding sequences of Fyn and Lyn (designated Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck respectively) alongside the SH4 deletion mutant (LckΔSH4) serving as negative control for LckSH4 function (Fig. 1A). Wild-type Lck (LckWT) and chimeras were inducibly expressed in the Lck-devoid Jurkat variant JCaM1.6 under the control of doxycycline (Dox) (Supplementary Fig. S1A).

Substitution of the SH4 domain did not compromise the levels of active Lck (pY394-Lck) (Fig. 1B and Supplementary Fig. S1A), whereas, as previously shown, the SH4 deletion mutant (LckΔSH4) and LckY394F, where the activating tyrosine is replaced by phenylalanine, were largely devoid of kinase activity (Fig. 1B and Supplementary Fig. S1B) [28]. Unexpectedly, despite equivalent levels of pY394-Lck, the chimeric forms were significantly less effective in driving ζ chain ITAM Y142 (pY142-ζ), ZAP-70 Y493 (pY493-ZAP) phosphorylation, and CD69 upregulation in a-CD3 (clone UCHT1)-stimulated cells (Fig. 1C and Supplementary Fig. S1C). This pattern appeared more pronounced in unstimulated samples, where several groups, including ours, have demonstrated that Lck overexpression or deregulated activity can autonomously initiate signaling cascades independently of receptor ligation [24, 31–33]. These data unveil an additional role of SFK SH4 domains in optimal substrate juxtapositioning.

To exclude the possibility that the signaling inefficiency of Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck was due to their impaired localization at the plasma membrane (PM)—the compartment where the primary Lck substrates (TCR ITAMs) reside—we performed confocal microscopy analyses. As previously shown [21, 22], Lck was distributed between the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane, and the Rab11+ EC, whereas LckΔSH4 was predominantly cytoplasmic (Fig. 1D). On the contrary, Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck were undetectable in the intracellular space, displaying a significantly higher PM/EC ratio than LckWT, indicative of their exclusive PM residence. Accordingly, the SH4 chimeras did not colocalize with Rab11 (Fig. 1D). Interestingly the Rab11 colocalization scores were indistinguishable between LckWT and the kinase-inactive mutant LckY394F, stating that Lck kinase activity is not a prerequisite for endosomal partitioning.

To delineate the potential association of endosomal Lck with signaling outputs, we proceeded to the characterization of Lck and its coresident molecules within Rab11+ structures. Confocal microscopy analyses, in agreement with previous reports [7, 20, 21], confirmed that the endosomal Lck pool harbored its constitutively active form (pY394-Lck) in LckWT-expressing cells and human CD4 T cells (Fig. 1E). Endosomal Lck also strongly colocalized with pY142-ζ and pY493-ZAP70 (Fig. 1F) in unstimulated cells overexpressing LckWT.

TCRζ expression is required for Lck trafficking to the Rab11+ EC

The presence of internalized phosphorylated ζ chain (pY142-ζ) is consistent with the concept of SFK activity-dependent TCR endocytosis [15, 34]. However, strong Rab11-ζ colocalization scores were obtained in parental JCaM1.6 cells and in Jurkat cells incubated with the pan-SFK inhibitor PP2 for 18 h (Fig. 2A and Supplementary Fig. S2A). Furthermore, ζ/Rab11 colocalization was not compromised in cells expressing Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck (Fig. 2A, C). These data demonstrate that the expression of Lck per se, its endosomal presence, or SFK enzymatic activity (i.e., ITAM phosphorylation) are not prerequisites for ζ chain residence in the Rab11+ EC. TCRζ also strongly colocalized with Rab11 in the J76 Jurkat subline that is devoid of endogenous TCRα and β chains (Fig. 2A, C and Supplementary Fig. S2B), suggestive of Rab11+ endosomal residency being an inherent ζ chain trait that can occur independently of receptor internalization. Furthermore, similarly to Lck, colocalization of ζ with Rab11 or the frequency of T cells harboring Rab11+ EC resident Lck or ζ chains was similar in resting and ex vivo-expanded primary human T cells (Supplementary Fig. S2C, D). In contrast to the ζ chain, CD3ε did not colocalize with Rab11 neither in human T cells nor in the LckWT line (Fig. 2B, C), corroborating the concept of divergent trafficking among the TCR–CD3 subunits and the ζ chain.

Throughout all imaging analyses, CD45 staining was employed to delineate the plasma membrane boundary. Surprisingly, we consistently observed that CD45 exhibited a pronounced vesicular presence (Figs. 1D–F and 2A, B). Endosomal CD45 was not the result of antibody cross-reactivity, as its presence was consistent across various staining combinations, but more importantly because its colocalization with Rab11 could not be detected in non-T cell PBMCs (Fig. 2D). Intrigued by the scenario that a Rab11-centered signaling module might be a unique feature of T cells and directly related to inherent properties of the TCRζ chain, we transitioned to the B cell line BJAB, which is devoid of the TCR apparatus. We reasoned that if Lck and/or CD45 positioning within the Rab11+ compartment was a T cell signature event, mediated by the process of ζ chain internalization, this phenomenon would not withstand in the B cell environment. For these experiments, we used BJAB cells expressing LckWT under the control of Dox. In a previous study [24] we fully characterized ectopic Lck in this B cell environment showing that the kinase was constitutively active, similarly to its endogenous counterpart in T cells, and biologically functional as it could encounter and phosphorylate BCR ITAMs and trigger downstream signaling cascades. Nevertheless, both Lck and CD45 were completely absent from Rab11+ structures in BJAB cells (Fig. 2E and Supplementary Fig. S3A, B). This pattern was overturned upon transduction with human TCRζ chain. Ectopically expressed ζ strongly colocalized with Rab11 regardless of the presence or absence of Lck. Moreover, ζ expression was sufficient to drive the redistribution of Lck and CD45 to the Rab11⁺ endosomal compartment (Fig. 2E and Supplementary Fig. S3A, B).

These data were further corroborated by CRISPR–Cas9-mediated silencing of endogenous TCRζ in Jurkat cells. ζ downregulation was accompanied, as anticipated, by a dramatic drop in the levels of surface TCR alongside a significant increase in the PM/EC ratio of Lck and CD45 residency and severely reduced colocalization with Rab11, compared with the mock-treated controls (Fig. 2F and Supplementary Fig. S3C, D).

Loss of ζ chain expression did not seem to impact the proportion of differently phosphorylated Lck species (Supplementary Fig. S4A) or its association with the CD4 coreceptor (Supplementary Fig. S4B). Furthermore, Lck residency in the Rab11+ EC was independent of CD4 or CD8 expression, as comparable Lck/Rab11 colocalization was observed in PBMCs (containing both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells), Jurkat cells (CD4+), JCaM1.6, J76, and BJAB + ζ, the latter three being devoid of coreceptor expression (Figs. 1D and 2E and Supplementary Fig. S4C, D). Notably, although Lck endosomal residency depends on both its SH4 domain and expression of the ζ chain, it does not involve a direct interaction between SH4 and ζ (Supplementary Fig. S4E).

Overall, these data portray a fraction of ζ chain molecules, uncoupled from the TCR complex, that act as drivers for Lck EC residency. Nonetheless, the origin and trafficking dynamics of Lck and ζ chain endosomal pools in unperturbed cells remains an open question.

A constitutively TCR-uncoupled ζ pool drives Lck trafficking from the plasma membrane to Rab11+ endosomes

Based on our data, Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck serve as excellent models of exclusively PM-localized Lck (Fig. 1D), and were used to trace, directly, the pool of Lck, and indirectly, of the ζ chain destined for Rab11+ endosomal residency. We examined the possibility that despite their endosomal absence in resting cells, Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck could undergo internalization upon triggering of the signaling apparatus.

Cells were initially treated with pervanadate, aiming for a generalized and very robust form of cell activation. By 10 min of pervanadate treatment, Fyn-Lck, Lyn-Lck, and pY142-ζ could be clearly detected within the Rab11+ structures (Fig. 3A and Supplementary Fig. S5A). This was a time-dependent process; low detection of Lck chimeras at the EC required at least 2–3 min of pervanadate treatment (data not shown). pY142-ζ could not be detected in untreated cells, concordant with very low levels of global steady-state pY142-ζ, as shown in Fig. 1C. In agreement with previous studies [34], pervanadate stimulation was not followed by TCR internalization (Supplementary Fig. S5B), indicating cohesion of the sTCR complex [16]. CD3ε remained well segregated from Rab11 in pervanadate-stimulated samples (Supplementary Fig. S5C).

To assess whether the pervanadate-induced endosomal residency of the chimeras was merely a spurious effect of enforced, nonphysiological cellular processes triggered by pervanadate, we performed identical experiments in BJAB cells, where in the absence of ectopic ζ expression, Lck is exclusively localized at PM (Fig. 2E). Unlike data obtained in the JCaM1.6 lines, pervanadate did not promote Lck internalization in BJAB, with the kinase remaining at the PM (Supplementary Fig. S5D). Thus, the pervanadate-induced Rab11+EC accumulation of Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck in pervanadate-treated cells is ζ chain dependent.

Collectively, these data attest that Rab11+ resident Lck originates from the PM, where it has to encounter the ζ chain to get cotransported to the Rab11+ EC.

It is universally accepted that in TCR-sufficient cells, PM-resident ζ species are embedded in the fully assembled receptor complexes [35, 36]. Since Rab11+ endosomal ζ does not colocalize with CD3ε, it is reasonable to deduce that PM resident Lck co-internalizes with a fraction of ζ molecules that disengage from the receptor complex. We investigated this hypothesis by stimulating Fyn-Lck- and Lyn-Lck-expressing cells for 10 min with the mouse anti-CD3ε antibody. These stimulatory conditions have previously been shown to induce significant ζ chain dissociation and consequent surface TCR (sTCR) downregulation [16]. a-CD3-treated samples were fixed, permeabilized, and directly incubated with fluorescently conjugated anti-mouse secondary antibody. This approach enables selective labeling and tracking of the fraction of TCRs that are directly engaged and internalized (Supplementary Fig. S5E). Coverslips costained for Lck or Rab11 revealed that neither the Lck SH4 chimeras nor the internalized engaged TCRs localized within the Rab11+ EC after a-CD3 stimulation (Fig. 3B, C). Owing to species incompatibility restrictions of commercially available antibodies, the Rab11+ EC in Fig. 3B and D was indirectly demarcated by endosomal CD45. Complementary staining with anti-pY142 (rabbit) revealed no Rab11+ EC enrichment of phosphorylated ζ species in anti-CD3 stimulated cells, although phosphorylated ζ could clearly be detected at the PM and intracellularly colocalized with a proportion of the engaged, receptors (Fig. 3D and Supplementary Fig. S5F). Image analyses showed that 35–40% of intracellular a-CD3-bound TCRs did not colocalize with pY142-ζ (Supplementary Fig. S5F), attesting its disengagement from the complex, in agreement with previous estimations [16]. This percentage was retained in samples that were co-incubated with pervanadate during the last 2 min of a-CD3 treatment—to augment anti-pY142 reactivity—thus excluding overestimations of ζ disengagement due to the presence of unphosphorylated ζ molecules attached to the internalized complexes (Supplementary Fig. S5F, G).

Collectively, these data indicate that the ζ pool cotransiting with Lck to the Rab11+EC is spatially distinct both from the engaged-internalized TCR complexes and from the fraction of ζ molecules that immediately dissociate-internalize as a consequence of TCR ligation. Rather, the Rab11+EC ζ species appear to derive from an independent ζ pool constitutively residing at the PM.

Discussion

In this work we revisited the long-lasting question of the mechanisms governing the internalization and recycling dynamics of the proximal TCR signaling machinery.

It has been extensively demonstrated that vesicular trafficking is an important regulator of TCR signaling. Key mechanisms include the delivery of crucial signaling mediators, including Lck, the TCRζ chain, ZAP-70, and LAT toward the IS as well as the polarized secretion of vesicles at sites of contact [4–7, 37–39]. Furthermore, intracellular trafficking routes controlling the spatial distribution of Lck have been shown to impact TCR signaling output as well as IS formation and functionality, and in certain occasions even Lck kinase activity [21, 23, 40, 41]. In accordance with these studies, we show that Lck chimeric forms devoid of endosomal residence were less efficient than the WT protein at promoting TCR signaling events and upregulation of the early activation marker CD69. This phenotype was irrespective of ζ chain residency in the EC, highlighting the biological significance of Rab11+EC resident Lck.

We provide evidence that in unstimulated T cells, a distinct pool of the TCRζ chain that is spatially separated from the TCR–CD3 subunits, constitutes the driving force for the assembly of a group of signaling mediators within Rab11+ endosomal structures. Under conditions of Lck overexpression or pervanadate treatment, the Rab11-decorated compartment was enriched in pY142-ζ and pY493-ZAP70, the presence of which was absolutely dependent on the coresidence of Lck within the same location. Rather surprisingly, CD45 also had a strong presence within the Rab11+ endosomal structures. Although the trafficking and redistribution of CD45 in distinct compartments of the IS has been extensively investigated [2, 42], imaging analyses have primarily focused on its lateral mobility in planar views of TCR-pMHC interfaces. Indeed, we could only find a single report documenting (via immunofluorescence microscopy) the presence of an intracellular CD45 pool in resting Jurkat cells, suspected to participate in phosphatase’s turnover at the PM [43]. However, this observation was not further pursued, and to our knowledge, endosomal CD45 has not been previously reported. In light of our data, a key question emerges: How are the opposing actions of the abovementioned endosomal coresidents coordinated within the context of T cell activation? Studies on the competing phosphorylation–dephosphorylation dynamics of the proximal TCR signaling machinery on lipid bilayers [44] showed that the ζ chain phosphorylation output was dependent on the local concentrations of Lck and CD45. Notably, at equal Lck-CD45 densities, ζ phosphorylation was readily detectable and was further enhanced by increasing amounts of Lck, whereas increases of CD45 density had the opposing effect. Furthermore, binding of the ZAP70 SH2 domains was shown to protect phosphorylated ζ from CD45-mediated dephosphorylation, whereas Lck-ζ co-clustering led to intense ITAM phosphorylation even at the presence of very high CD45 concentrations. These data are consistent with our observations of CD45, pY142-ζ, and pZAP-Y493 co-existence within the Rab11-decorated structures under conditions of Lck overexpression and can be projected to a model in which CD45 maintains basal phosphorylation of endosomal ζ. Upon T cell activation, the documented CD45-Lck time-dependent co-clustering and segregation at sites of TCR engagement [42] could counteract the dominance/kinetic advantage of CD45. In parallel, the time-dependent enrichment of Lck and ZAP70 at the EC, as has been shown in CD2-triggered T cells, [45] could serve the same purpose, with the end result being the creation of a depository of activated signaling mediators supplied to the IS.

Concerning the parameters governing ζ chain Rab11+EC residency, we showed that the colocalization scores between ζ and Rab11 were invariably high in T cell lines, unstimulated and activated human T cells, and also in B cells, where ζ was ectopically expressed. Notably, the presence of endosomal ζ was independent of SFK activity or TCR expression at the cell surface, highlighting that although ITAM phosphorylation or the detachment of “loosely-attached” ζ from the TCR complex are important within the broader and heterogeneous landscape of ζ chain internalization [14–16, 21], they do not constitute primary mechanisms promoting its residency within Rab11+ endosomes.

In the case of Lck, we identified two decisive parameters ruling its internalization: (i) unique features of its SH4 domain and (ii) the presence of the TCRζ chain. Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck retain all Lck functional domains and only differ in a short (11 and 13 amino acids, respectively) and unstructured N-terminal stretch. Although all three different SH4s undergo myristoylation and palmitoylation and are mediating SFK anchoring at the inner leaflet of the PM, the divergency in their sequences has a dramatic effect on their residency within confined regions of the PM [29, 30], and as we show here, also at the subcellular distribution of Lck, with the two chimeric forms failing to reside within the Rab11+EC. In a previous study [28], we proposed that the lateral organization of membrane proteins is governed by their residency within compatible (“like”) or incompatible (“unlike”) lipid boundary environments, a property that respectively determines their spatial proximity or segregation and ultimately influences their ability to engage in enzymatic and/or binding interactions [28, 46]. Based on this, a plausible explanation for the failure of Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck to internalize is that the biophysical characteristics of their SH4 domains hinder their spatial juxtaposition to the ζ chain and thus their cotransportation to the Rab11+EC. The SH4-mediated residency of Lck within specific endosomal compartments could further represent a critical reservoir managed by the trafficking machinery of myristoylated proteins. In particular, UNC119A has been shown to extract myristoylated Lck from membranes and facilitate its transport to the immunological synapse [47]. In this context, the SH4 domain would not only provide the means for endosomal residency but also enable UNC119-dependent release of Lck from endosomes and its spatial redistribution to the IS. The recognition of SFK SH4 domains as a crucial structural element for endosomal residency and the correlation with Lck signaling efficacy reveals a previously unrecognized aspect of SH4’s importance in SFK function. This finding may also explain, in part, Lck’s optimal adaptation for the TCR signaling apparatus compared with other antigen receptors [24].

Collectively, these observations raise the question of what mechanisms might shape the coordinated residency of Lck and ζ within the Rab11+EC. Currently, there is no evidence suggesting that Lck and ζ meet at the biosynthetic pathway, rather their endosomal colocalization has been shown to originate from PM resident fractions of these molecules that are internalized and get incorporated into recycling vesicles ([13, 18, 20, 48] and our data in Fig. 3A). For Lck, whose SH4 biophysical characteristics allow adequate ζ proximity, formation of protein–protein interactions could represent an additional mechanism that promotes their co-internalization. We showed that Lck SH4 does not directly interact with TCRζ, and we consider it unlikely that the adaptor domains (SH3, SH2) are involved in this process, given that the SH3 domain does not bind ζ, and the SH2-mediated interactions occur only upon stimulation-induced ζ phosphorylation [49, 50]. The latter is also corroborated by the unaffected colocalization of the Y394F Lck mutant with Rab11. A more attractive hypothesis could implicate negatively charged amino acids in the Lck unique domain (located downstream of the SH4, thus also present in the Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck chimeras), which have been shown to form ionic interactions with basic residue sequences (BRS) flanking the TCRζ chain ITAMs [51, 52]. Notably, the cytoplasmic region of the ζ chain has been implicated in the regulation of its subcellular distribution [53]. Although BRS motifs also form ionic interactions with acidic phospholipids promoting attachment of the ζ cytoplasmic tails to the PM, they do so via a dynamic and graded degree of membrane engagement [51]. Furthermore, ITAM phosphorylation precedes ζ tail detachment from the membrane [54], demonstrating Lck accessibility to ζ even when its cytoplasmic tail is embedded in the PM. This suggests that Lck is potentially capable of forming interactions with the ζ chain at steady state and renders unique domain–BRS interactions a plausible scenario for their co-internalization. Pervanadate has been shown to drive relocalization of signaling proteins away from their steady-state cellular distribution and to induce changes in diffusion rates and protein aggregation profiles [55–57]. In the case of the Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck chimeras, pervanadate stimulation could override the normal plasma membrane confinement (dictated by the different SH4s), allowing proximity to ζ and subsequent co-internalization as per WT Lck.

TCR internalization alongside ζ dissociation from the complex has been demonstrated for receptors engaged both by pMHC and also after incubation with a-CD3 antibodies ([15–17] and Supplementary Fig. S5F). We can safely assume that the SH4 chimeric forms (Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck) do gain access to ζ molecules that are incorporated within engaged TCR complexes, as evidenced by pY142-ζ induction upon anti-CD3 stimulation. If Lck was cotransported to the Rab11+ compartment with “loosely attached” ζ chains that disengage from the TCR after receptor ligation, then Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck should be detected in the Rab11+EC of anti-CD3 stimulated cells. On the contrary though, Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck remained at the PM following TCR engagement, suggesting that Lck is not co-internalizing with the fraction of ζ molecules detaching from the receptor complex. Furthermore, we could not detect Rab11+ endosomal pY142-ζ in the a-CD3-stimulated Fyn-Lck and Lyn-Lck cell lines, despite the presence of total ζ at this location, signifying that the Rab11+ ζ pool is not derived from the immediate internalization of the fraction that dissociates from the engaged TCR complex.

Collectively, our data show that endosomal Lck originates from the PM, at sites where its juxtaposition to the ζ chain enables their cotrafficking to the Rab11+ compartment. However, the ζ species driving Lck internalization are distinct from the “loosely attached” fraction previously described as recycling to and from the TCR complex [14], supporting the existence of two distinct ζ chain populations at the PM: one incorporated within the TCR, and another residing at the plasma membrane as autonomous units, which we hereafter refer to as “standalone ζ.” The capacity of the ζ chain to acquire self-directed plasma membrane residency has been documented in cell lines and hybridomas lacking TCR surface expression [14] and Fig. 2A, E. Furthermore, La Gruta et al. [15] have reported that in unstimulated T cell hybridomas with fully assembled TCRs, a small proportion of PM-localized ζ existed uncoupled from the rest of the receptor complex, and that this fraction was significantly upregulated following antigen stimulation. Alongside our findings, two scenarios can be envisioned for the origin of standalone ζ: (i) a fraction of the newly synthesized protein may be delivered at the PM as an independent unit uncoupled from the TCR. However, this possibility is challenged by evidence that ζ is the limiting factor for TCR assembly and surface expression, with a synthesis rate estimated to be about one-tenth that of the other chains [36]. Moreover, induced increases in ζ synthesis were directly proportional to increases in surface TCR expression, indicating that its vast majority was incorporated in the receptor complex [35], and (ii) a subset of the “loosely attached” ζ molecules that constitutively recycle to and from the surface TCRs [14], instead of directly internalizing, may be laterally mobilized at the plasma membrane to form the standalone pool. The TCR ligation-mediated enrichment of the standalone ζ pool [15] could thus be mediated via delivery of the Rab11+ endosomal molecular assembly toward the PM [7, 18, 20] and/or by augmented disengagement of ζ from ligated receptor complexes [15, 16].

A limitation of the present study is the lack of direct high-resolution imaging evidence for the presence of standalone ζ. While such approaches would provide valuable information on the spatial segregation of ζ from the TCR complex at the plasma membrane, they would also be inherently challenged by the dense clustering and small spatial scale of TCR complexes [58–61]. The biochemical and functional strategies employed here provide complementary insights as they are less influenced by “molecular crowding” at the nanoscale.

Conclusions

It is widely accepted that TCR signaling is orchestrated by spatiotemporally regulated vesicular trafficking. TCR ligation has been shown to promote polarization of vesicles containing Lck, phosphorylated ζ, ZAP-70, LAT, etc. toward the immunological synapse, a mechanism suggested to provide rapid and sustained supply of signaling mediators at sites of receptor engagement [4–7, 37–40]. Within this context, we propose the following model shaped by our findings: At the PM, ζ can be found in two distinct pools—one incorporated within the TCR complex and a standalone population. The latter constitutes the driving force for Lck co-internalization into the Rab11+ EC and formation of signaling assemblies that are maintained at a steady-state phosphorylation status by the dominance of CD45. Upon receptor ligation, these assemblies are mobilized toward signaling “hot spots” where increased ζ phosphorylation and subsequent ZAP-70 recruitment are driven by local Lck enrichment and/or CD45 segregation. Upon vesicular release, phosphorylated ζ and its coresidents could either integrate at the PM as autonomous units or get incorporated in TCR complexes (both unligated and engaged) to replenish the loss of disengaged ζ. In either case, they establish a foundation of preprimed signaling platforms that enable rapid and sustained propagation of downstream signals, decreasing the dependence on continuous receptor engagement. This mechanism complements the conceptual framework regarding the remarkable T cell sensitivity to low-affinity and sparse density of pMHC complexes, whereas it can provide an advancement on our understanding of responses associated with bystander, non-engaged TCRs [11–13, 62].

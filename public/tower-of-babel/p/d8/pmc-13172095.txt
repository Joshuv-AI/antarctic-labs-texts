Introduction

Hereditary (or “variant”) transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTRv) is a progressive, debilitating, and fatal disease caused by variants of the transthyretin (TTR) gene [1]. Accumulation of the resulting misfolded TTR as amyloid deposits in multiple organs and tissues, notably the nerves, heart, gastrointestinal tract, and musculoskeletal tissues, leads to heterogeneous symptoms, including polyneuropathy (PN) and cardiomyopathy [2–5]. Patients with ATTRv with predominantly PN symptoms (ATTRv-PN) experience a range of sensory, motor, and autonomic neuropathies, as well as substantial disability and impairment of quality of life [6, 7].

Data on the prevalence and characteristics of ATTRv in Asia are limited [8–10], and Asian patients are underrepresented in international ATTR registries such as the Transthyretin Amyloidosis Outcomes Survey [7, 11, 12]. In a cohort study in Korea, significantly more patients with ATTRv with cardiomyopathy also had PN and autonomic neuropathy, than those with wild-type ATTR (ATTRwt) with cardiomyopathy [11]. Japan features endemic regions of ATTRv with variation in disease onset and penetrance reported between patients with the V30M variant in endemic versus non-endemic regions [13].

In a small cohort study of Southeast Asian patients in Singapore, around a third of patients at initial presentation had somatic neuropathy and carpal tunnel syndrome, a quarter had autonomic dysfunction, and around 7% had cardiac symptoms [12]. Notably, over half of the patients in this cohort study experienced a delay in diagnosis, despite the majority of these patients having a positive family history of ATTRv; the most common variant was A97S (48%) [12]. The A97S variant was also reported to be the most common variant among ethnic Chinese Malaysian patients and was associated with later-onset progressive PN and subclinical cardiac involvement [9, 10]. However, several cohort studies showed that V30M is the most common variant in mainland China and indicate a high degree of heterogeneity in the clinical presentation of ATTRv among Chinese patients [14, 15]. The prevalence of ATTRv in mainland China is estimated to be 18.9–74.9/100,000 individuals, similar to an estimated 57.4/100,000 in the global population [16].

Early, effective treatment targeting the underlying pathophysiology of ATTRv-PN is crucial for reducing the burden and progression of amyloidosis [17–19]. Available therapeutic approaches in patients with ATTRv-PN include TTR silencing (RNA interference [RNAi] therapeutics and anti-sense oligonucleotides) and TTR stabilizers [19, 20].

Vutrisiran is a subcutaneously administered RNAi therapeutic approved in multiple countries for the treatment of ATTRv-PN and ATTR-cardiomyopathy [21], that reduces hepatic synthesis of both wild-type and variant TTR, resulting in rapid knockdown of the amyloidogenic TTR protein [22]. Vutrisiran is approved in Asia for the treatment of ATTRv-PN based on the results of the phase 3 global, randomized, open-label HELIOS-A study, which demonstrated significant improvements in the primary efficacy endpoint (modified Neuropathy Impairment Score + 7 [mNIS + 7] score) and other disease-related secondary endpoints with vutrisiran, compared with an external placebo group [17]. Vutrisiran was well tolerated, with an acceptable safety profile and a low rate of discontinuations.

Here we report post hoc analyses of efficacy and safety data from HELIOS-A [17] in a subpopulation of patients from East Asia.

Methods

Study Design and Outcomes

HELIOS-A (NCT03759379) was a global, randomized, open-label, phase 3 study conducted at 57 sites in 22 countries [17]. Full details of the study design have been reported previously [17]. In brief, patients were randomized 3:1 to receive subcutaneous vutrisiran 25 mg every 3 months (Q3M) or intravenous (IV) patisiran 0.3 mg/kg every 3 weeks for 18 months. The placebo group of the APOLLO study [23] was used as an external placebo control, as APOLLO had similar endpoints and eligibility criteria to HELIOS-A. After the randomized 18-month treatment period, all patients were eligible to be re-randomized to receive vutrisiran in an open-label treatment extension phase.

The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in mNIS + 7 score compared with an external placebo group at month 9. Secondary endpoints included change from baseline in Norfolk Quality of Life-Diabetic Neuropathy questionnaire (Norfolk QOL-DN) score and 10-meter walk test (10-MWT) speed at months 9 and 18, mNIS + 7 score, modified body mass index (mBMI), and Rasch-built Overall Disability Scale (R-ODS) score at month 18, and serum TTR level through month 18.

The study protocol and amendments were approved by relevant institutional review boards or independent ethics committees. This information is detailed in the supplementary material, Table S1. Written informed consent was obtained from each participant. The study was conducted in accordance with all applicable regulatory requirements, the current guidelines of Good Clinical Practice, and principles originating from the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient Population

Full inclusion and exclusion criteria have been published previously [17]. Eligible patients were aged 18–85 years with a diagnosis of ATTRv amyloidosis and a documented TTR variant. Patients were also required to have a baseline NIS score of 5–130, a polyneuropathy disability score ≤ IIIb, a Karnofsky Performance Status score ≥ 60%, and adequate liver and renal function. Patients with prior liver transplantation or considered likely to undergo liver transplantation during the 18-month treatment period and those with a New York Heart Association heart failure class > II were excluded. Patients were required to complete a washout period if receiving TTR stabilizers (14 days for tafamidis and 3 days for diflunisal) prior to study drug dosing.

Subgroup Analyses

Post hoc subgroup analyses were performed using data from patients in HELIOS-A who enrolled at sites in East Asia (Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan) and received vutrisiran, and in the East Asian subpopulation who received placebo in the APOLLO study. The following endpoints were assessed: change from baseline in mNIS + 7 total and component scores, Norfolk QOL-DN total and domain scores, 10-MWT speed, mBMI, and R-ODS score; adverse events (AEs), serious AEs (SAEs), and deaths; and the change in serum TTR over time.

Safety and efficacy data were analyzed in all randomized patients from East Asia who received any amount of study drug. Efficacy data were evaluated using a mixed-effects model for repeated measures (MMRM), which used an unstructured approach to model the treatment-by-time mean values and the covariances, essentially leading to a multivariate normal model assuming data were missing at random. MMRM analysis included the baseline value as a continuous covariate and treatment group, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, and genotype (V30M vs. non-V30M) as fixed-effect terms. An unstructured covariance structure was used to model the within-patient errors, and the Satterthwaite approximation was used to estimate the degrees of freedom. Least-squares (LS) mean and least-squares mean difference (LSMD) estimates, standard error (SE), and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) at month 9 and month 18 are presented. The p-values are not reported due to a small sample size.

Data from the East Asian subpopulation were evaluated alongside those of the overall population to assess the consistency of data.

Results

Patient Population

Of 122 patients who received vutrisiran in HELIOS-A, 12 were enrolled in East Asia (Japan, n = 4; South Korea, n = 6; Taiwan, n = 2), while 21 of 77 placebo-treated patients in APOLLO were from East Asia (Japan, n = 9; South Korea, n = 2; Taiwan, n = 10). One vutrisiran-treated patient in the East Asian subpopulation discontinued treatment before month 18 (physician decision), and 11 were receiving ongoing treatment at the month 18 data cutoff.

Baseline demographics and disease characteristics are shown in Table 1. Overall, baseline characteristics were consistent between the East Asian subpopulation and the overall population. Most patients enrolled in East Asia in the vutrisiran group and the APOLLO placebo group had a non-V30M genotype; genotypes in patients from East Asia included V30M, A97S, T60A, D38A, K35N, A45V, L55P, L55A, and P24S (Table 1). In the vutrisiran group, the East Asian subpopulation had a higher mean (standard deviation [SD]) NIS (56 [37.1]) compared with the overall population (43 [28.6]), indicating a potentially increased disease burden. In addition, a greater proportion of patients in the East Asian subpopulation had prior tafamidis use (75%) compared with the overall population (43%) in the vutrisiran group. Baseline demographics and disease characteristics were generally well balanced between treatment groups in the East Asian subpopulations. Patients who received vutrisiran had a lower Norfolk QOL-DN score and a higher R-ODS scale score at baseline compared with the external placebo group.

Efficacy

At months 9 and 18, changes from baseline in mNIS + 7 total scores (primary and secondary endpoints) in the East Asian subpopulation were similar to those in the overall population (Fig. 1; Table 2). In the East Asian subpopulation, vutrisiran treatment led to an improvement from baseline relative to external placebo. Consistent with the overall population, vutrisiran resulted in improvement at month 9 versus external placebo (LSMD [95% CI] –20.18 [–30.75, –9.62]) and at month 18 (–30.75 [–45.56, –15.94]), similar in magnitude to the difference observed at months 9 and 18 in the overall population (LSMD of –17.00 [–21.78, –12.22] and –28.55 [–34.00, –23.10], respectively, both p < 0.0001 [17]). Safety

Safety data are summarized in Table 3. AEs were broadly similar between the East Asian subpopulation and the overall population. Discussion

This post hoc analysis of HELIOS-A demonstrated beneficial effects of vutrisiran in the East Asian subpopulation with ATTRv-PN across a range of disease-relevant endpoints, consistent with the treatment effects demonstrated in the overall population [17]. The safety profile of vutrisiran in the East Asian subpopulation was also consistent with the global study population.

Overall, the demographics and baseline disease characteristics were consistent between the East Asian subpopulation and the overall population, with the majority of patients enrolled in East Asia being male, with median age of approximately 60 years. Baseline characteristics were also generally well balanced between treatment groups in the East Asian subpopulation. Notable differences in the East Asian subpopulation at baseline compared with the overall population included greater neuropathy impairment and a higher proportion of patients with prior use of tafamidis in the vutrisiran group, although any differences should be interpreted with caution due to the small sample size in the East Asian subpopulation. Approximately two-thirds of patients in the East Asian subpopulations of the HELIOS-A vutrisiran and APOLLO placebo groups had non-V30M variants of TTR, representing eight different variants, of which A97S was the most common, accounting for 50% of non-V30M variants. This is consistent with results from small cohort studies of Southeast Asian patients with ATTRv in Singapore and Malaysia [9, 10, 12]. Studies of patients with ATTRv-PN in South China and Taiwan reported that patients with the A97S variant often present with a mixed phenotype of peripheral neuropathy and cardiac involvement [24, 25].

Trends observed in efficacy endpoints were consistent with the primary analyses of HELIOS-A, which demonstrated treatment effects favoring vutrisiran compared with external placebo for the primary endpoint (mNIS + 7 score) [17]. Notably, studies of Asian populations of patients with genetically confirmed ATTRv amyloidosis have shown that the majority of patients have peripheral neuropathy [9, 12, 26], highlighting the importance of treatments that can improve symptoms of neuropathy. Moreover, quantitative ultrasound data show that muscle mass is reduced and echogenicity enhanced in Asian patients with ATTRv-PN, and that these parameters correlate with quality of life and ability to perform daily activities, as measured by Norfolk QOL-DN scores and R-ODS, respectively [27]. While the HELIOS-A study did not include quantitative muscle ultrasound as an assessment, the treatment effects observed in the East Asian subpopulation favored vutrisiran across multiple disease-relevant secondary endpoints, including Norfolk QOL-DN, R-ODS, and 10-MWT.

In addition to the clinical benefits demonstrated in the East Asian subpopulation, rapid and robust reduction of serum TTR was observed by the time of the first measurement at 3 weeks after the first dose of vutrisiran. The serum TTR profile over time showed similar trends in the East Asian subpopulation as the overall population, suggesting a consistent pharmacodynamic effect across patients from different regions.

Safety findings were favorable in the vutrisiran group compared with the external APOLLO placebo group, with no new safety signals in the East Asian subpopulation. The most frequent AEs were as expected based on the disease course of ATTRv-PN, including falls, diarrhea, peripheral edema, and dizziness. Notably, no patients in the East Asian subpopulation treated with vutrisiran discontinued treatment due to AEs.

These findings add to the accumulating efficacy and safety data for RNAi therapies, and specifically for vutrisiran, from clinical studies and real-world experience in patients with ATTR and polyneuropathy and/or cardiomyopathy [17, 23, 28–31]. In a systematic review of 10 studies of vutrisiran or patisiran, the authors concluded that RNAi therapies provide sustained improvements in neuropathy, quality of life, and cardiac symptoms in patients with ATTRv, with a satisfactory safety profile [32].

Key strengths of this analysis include the multiple efficacy outcomes reflecting impacts of disease on quality of life, disability, function, gait speed, and nutritional status, as well as the contribution to data on vutrisiran use in ATTRv-PN in the underrepresented East Asian subpopulation. However, the limitations must be acknowledged, including the post hoc nature of the analysis and the small number of patients enrolled at sites in East Asia (such that the analysis lacked adequate power for formal statistical comparisons between groups). Limitations related to the external placebo control and open-label study design have been described previously [17].

Conclusion

Baseline characteristics and treatment outcomes in patients enrolled in East Asia in the HELIOS-A study were consistent with those reported in the overall global HELIOS-A study population. The consistency of treatment effects, including clinical response, TTR reduction, and safety profile, supports the suitability of vutrisiran in East Asian patients with ATTRv-PN. Although these findings should be interpreted with caution due to the small sample size and the descriptive nature of the analysis, the results provide valuable evidence for a patient population that has historically been underrepresented in clinical trials of patients with ATTRv-PN.

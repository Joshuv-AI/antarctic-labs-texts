Development and investigation of the efficacy of an interactive, web-based game exercise system (ApnoeaTheraPlay) for individuals with obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome: study protocol for a randomised, single-blind, controlled trial
Authors: Gulay Aras Bayram, Devrim Tarakci, Gizem Ergezen Sahin, Gokce Savas Cevlan, Alper Ceylan, Esra Atilgan, Kemal Ozkan, Erdogan Kunter
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionObstructive sleep apnoea syndrome (OSAS) is a prevalent sleep disorder caused by recurrent upper airway collapse during sleep. Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy is the most commonly prescribed treatment; however, adherence remains suboptimal, with non-compliance rates reported between 46% and 83%. Exercise-based interventions have demonstrated beneficial effects on respiratory muscle strength, upper airway function and sleep quality. This study aims to develop an interactive, web-based, game exercise platform (ApneaTheraPlay (ATP)) integrating respiratory, aerobic, strengthening and oropharyngeal exercises as an adjunct to CPAP therapy for individuals with OSAS, and to evaluate its effectiveness compared with a structured presleep exercise programme and a control condition.Methods and analysisThis study is designed as a single-blinded, three-arm, parallel-group randomised controlled trial. Individuals diagnosed with OSAS who use CPAP will be randomly allocated to the ATP group, the structured presleep exercise group (SPEG) or the control group (CG). Participants in the ATP and SPEG groups will perform exercise sessions three times per week for 12 weeks (36 sessions in total). The ATP group will use a web-based, interactive, game-based exercise system, whereas the SPEG group will follow a conventional video-based exercise programme delivered in a non-interactive format. Participants in the CG will continue their usual activities and record daily physical activity using an activity diary. The primary outcome measures will be the Apnoea–Hypopnoea Index and nocturnal oxygen saturation parameters. Secondary outcomes will include sleep quality, daytime functioning, fatigue, muscle strength and function, CPAP adherence, psychological status and technology usability.Ethics and disseminationThis study was approved by the Istanbul Medipol University Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee (E-10840098-202.3.02-6266; 17 September 2025). The study will comply with the guidelines established by the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) for clinical trial registration and reporting. The results of this trial will be disseminated through publication in peer-reviewed scientific journals and presentation at national and international scientific meetings. No individual participant data will be shared publicly. This study is supported by The Scientific and Technological Research Council of Turkey (TÜBİTAK) under project number 225S257.Trial registration numberNCT07214636.

Introduction

Obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome (OSAS) is a common sleep disorder characterised by recurrent episodes of apnoea or hypopnoea resulting from the collapse of the upper airway during sleep. OSAS negatively impacts individuals’ daily activities and overall quality of life with symptoms such as irregular snoring, morning headaches, excessive daytime sleepiness and impaired cognitive function.1 Physical inactivity has been shown to increase OSAS symptoms and severity. Studies have shown that individuals who engage in regular physical activity are less likely to develop OSAS symptoms compared with sedentary individuals. Longer sitting times have been found to be associated with increased OSAS severity, and it has been reported that increased sitting time may negatively affect disease progression.2

Although there is no definitive cure for OSAS, the most commonly used clinical method of treatment is continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy. Appropriate and regular use of CPAP effectively reduces sleep-related symptoms and has been shown to improve Apnoea–Hypopnoea Index (AHI), daytime sleepiness and quality of life measures in individuals with moderate to severe OSAS.3 4 However, device non-compliance rates (compliance is defined as using CPAP for more than 4 hours every night) ranging from 46% to 83% have been reported.5 There is a growing need for alternative treatment methods for patients who refuse or cannot tolerate CPAP.6

Patient compliance issues and various side effects observed in many methods used in OSAS treatment highlight the importance of physical activity and exercise approaches, which are easier to achieve patient compliance with and have significantly fewer side effects compared with other treatment methods. Physical activity and exercise are among the key determinants of overall health and play a critical role, particularly in the prevention and treatment of chronic diseases.7 8 The fact that OSAS shares common risk factors with other chronic diseases makes exercise an effective and cost-effective alternative treatment option.1 Moderate-intensity aerobic exercise and high-intensity interval training (HIIT) have been reported to contribute to OSAS management by promoting weight loss, improving upper airway function, reducing inflammation and oxidative stress, and inducing positive changes in sleep architecture.911 While aerobic exercise alone has been shown to be beneficial, combined programmes incorporating resistance exercises and respiratory muscle exercises are noted to offer more comprehensive effects in reducing OSA severity by improving respiratory function and increasing upper airway stability.12 13 Furthermore, oropharyngeal exercises are reported to support airway patency by strengthening upper airway muscles and reducing AHI; they also positively contribute to respiratory and swallowing functions.14 15

As stated by the WHO, non-compliance with treatment reduces the effectiveness of all types of treatment.16 Technology-assisted exercise methods have the potential to reduce sedentary behaviour by converting individuals’ static screen time into dynamic screen time. This ensures physical activity during screen use and helps control obesity.17 It provides a safe and comfortable environment for individuals who are reluctant to exercise in front of peers and society due to lack of skill or motivation.18 When regular monitoring of physical activity and exercise is combined with phone and computer applications, it has been shown that personal health data can be better managed and individuals’ motivation can be increased.19 Gamification requires a playful user experience (ludic interaction) and immersive elements supported by game design. Therefore, gamification uses game design elements to make a non-game context more interesting and motivating.20 The use of gamification features in digital health applications contributes to increasing exercise compliance as well as promoting satisfaction, intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, behavioural change and the formation of social networks. In recent years, studies have primarily focused on gamification in the areas of fitness and e-health. Among these studies, Sardi et al emphasised that gamification in rehabilitation has been comprehensively examined and is increasingly being adopted in real healthcare settings.21 22 Digital video-based exercise games (exergames) are applications that increase compliance by making repetitive therapeutic tasks more motivating through games and offer the potential for effective treatment.23 24

The ApnoeaTheraPlay (ATP) system to be developed in our study will enable the design of a web-based system that incorporates SMART-based digital game exercises in a virtual environment within a structured exercise programme (aerobic, strength training, oropharyngeal and respiratory exercises) for individuals with OSAS, combining exercises that have been studied separately and partially combined in the literature. This aims to provide an innovative method to increase motivation, encourage regular participation in exercises, ensure therapy follow-up and strengthen treatment compliance for individuals with conditions like OSAS who have problems adhering to CPAP.

Methods and analysis

This study is planned as a randomised controlled intervention study to evaluate the feasibility and effectiveness of an interactive, web-based game exercise programme in individuals with OSAS. The research will consist of the following phases:

Development of the game-based exercise programme and sensor integration

An interactive, web-based game exercise programme will be developed to suit the physical capacities of individuals with OSAS. The programme will be structured to include aerobic exercises, movements that activate the respiratory muscles and postural exercises. Exercise performance will be monitored via inertial measurement unit sensors and smart watches, and movement data will be recorded in a digital environment.

Pilot application and user feedback

The technical functionality and usability of the developed programme will be evaluated through a pilot application. Feedback on the user experience will be collected from participants; based on the data obtained, the exercise content, gamification elements and software interface will be optimised to create the final intervention protocol.

Creation of the participant pool and randomisation

The sample size will be determined by power analysis, and individuals diagnosed with appropriate OSAS will be included in the study. Suitable participants will be assigned to intervention and control groups (CGs) using a computer-assisted randomisation method.

Implementation and monitoring of exercise programmes

Both intervention groups (ATP and structured presleep exercise group (SPEG)) will undergo their respective exercise programmes at the specified duration and frequency. Exercise compliance and physical performance will be regularly monitored only in the intervention groups, while the CG continues under standard care (figure 1).

Participants

The study will be conducted at Koşuyolu Istanbul Medipol Hospital in a chest diseases clinic, with a planned start date of April 2026 and a planned end date of June 2028. A permit has been obtained from the institution. The study will comply with the guidelines established by the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) for clinical trial registration and reporting.

Participants will be eligible if they are between 18 and 65 years of age, have been diagnosed with mild, moderate or severe OSAS (AHI 5–30 events/hour) based on laboratory diagnostic polysomnography (PSG) evaluations, have been diagnosed by a pulmonologist within the last year and have been using regular CPAP therapy for no more than 12 months. Additionally, participants who habitually snore, have the physical capacity to participate in the exercise group and are able to follow exercise instructions must own a device compatible with SMART technologies (phone, tablet or computer) and have familiarity with mobile health applications. Their willingness to participate in the exercises will be assessed using a Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) and must be above 7. Participants must not have participated in physiotherapy-based interventions targeting OSAS in the last 3 months and must score 68 or above on the System Usability Scale (SUS). Exclusion criteria include Body Mass Index (BMI)>40 kg/m², severe drug or alcohol dependence, regular use of sedatives, muscle relaxants or opioids, pregnancy or postpartum period (<6 months), medical contraindications (unstable coronary disease, decompensated heart failure), history of stroke, respiratory or neuromuscular diseases causing hypercapnia, inflammatory diseases (arthritis, sarcoidosis, vasculitis and lupus), craniofacial deformities, severe upper airway obstruction, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, dysphagia or the presence of a frenulum restricting tongue movement.

A priori power analysis was performed using G*Power 3.1.9.7 software. The analysis was conducted based on a 3-group, 3-measurement time repeated measures analysis of variance (ANOVA; within-between interaction) design. The effect size was determined as f=0.25 based on the AHI change data obtained from the meta-analysis by Iftikhar et al.25 The minimum total sample size was calculated as 54 for 80% power and a 5% significance level. The total number of participants was determined to be 66 (22 participants per group), considering an approximate 20% dropout rate.

Study design

The study is planned as a prospective, parallel, single-blind, randomised controlled trial with a 1:1:1 allocation ratio. Participants will be randomly assigned to one of three intervention groups: a CG using CPAP alone, a group using CPAP combined with structured presleep exercise, and a group using CPAP combined with interactive, web-based game exercise applications to evaluate their effectiveness. Randomisation will be performed using an online randomisation system, employing a stratified block randomisation method with block sizes of 6 and 9, computer-assisted. Since BMI and AHI were identified as important prognostic factors in the study, and CPAP duration of use may differentiate compliance, symptom change and intervention effect, participants will be stratified based on these two variables and random assignment will occur within each stratum. Stratification will be created as follows:

BMI: <25, 25–29.9 and ≥30 kg/m²

AHI: 5–14 (mild), 15–29 (moderate) and ≥30 (severe)

CPAP duration: ≤6 months and 6–12 months

The demographic characteristics of the participants (eg, age, height, weight and diseases) will be recorded. Group assignments will be conducted by the researcher performing the randomisation. Applications will remain within the knowledge of the physiotherapist administering the intervention. The physiotherapist and statistician conducting the outcome assessment will be independent and blinded. To ensure blinding of data analysis, group labels will be coded and concealed during data entry. Participants will be asked not to share information about their group assignment during the evaluation. The flow diagram of the study is shown in figure 2.

Outcome measures

Our primary outcome measures will be changes in AHI and nighttime saturation measurements after the intervention and 3 months after the intervention. Secondary measures will include sleep quality and daytime functioning, fatigue severity, upper airway muscle strength and function, CPAP and exercise compliance, quality of life, overall muscle strength, psychological distress and technology usability.

Apnoea–Hypopnoea Index (AHI)

This parameter will be objectively measured using level 1 PSG, (Philips Alice 6, Philips Respironics, USA), performed two times—at the beginning and at the end of the study. The AHI quantifies the number of apnoea and hypopnoea events per hour of sleep; 0–4 events are considered normal, 5–14 mild obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), 15–30 moderate OSA and more than 30 severe OSA. EEG and ECG recordings will be collected as part of the standard PSG protocol for AHI calculation; however, individual EEG and ECG parameters will not be analysed separately, and only the overall PSG summary results will be used. PSG recordings will be scored according to the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) scoring criteria by a pulmonology specialist. A change of at least 15 events in AHI will be considered clinically meaningful to evaluate the efficacy of the intervention.26

Oxygen saturation percentage (SaO2%)

Participants will be provided with a Huawei GT3 Pro smartwatch (Huawei Technologies Co, Shenzhen, China) for this project.27 The smartwatch will continuously monitor and record SaO₂ levels during exercise sessions and overnight, allowing assessment of fluctuations and longitudinal trends that cannot be captured by PSG alone. Oxygen saturation data from PSG will be collected only at baseline and at the end of the study and will be compared with smartwatch data. Normal oxygen saturation typically ranges from 95% to 100%, with values below 90% indicating hypoxia.

Sleep quality and daytime functioning

Multiple assessment methods will be used to evaluate sleep quality and functioning. The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, a widely recognised 19-item self-report questionnaire, will be used to assess subjective sleep quality and disturbances over a 1 month period.28 The severity of insomnia symptoms and their impact on daily functioning will be assessed using the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI), a widely used self-report questionnaire designed for this purpose.29 The Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) will be used to assess daytime sleepiness, and the Functional Sleep Outcomes Questionnaire (FOSQ-10) will be used to assess the impact of sleepiness on alertness, activity level, overall productivity, social consequences and intimate and sexual relationships.30 31 The web-based application to be developed will integrate a 0 (not sleepy at all, fully awake and alert)–10 (extremely sleepy, about to fall asleep) VAS will be used to assess subjective sleep state and sleep quality three times a day (09:00, 12:00 and 20:00) during the study period.

Fatigue severity

The Chalder Fatigue Scale (CFS), consisting of 11 items, will be used to measure fatigue severity. It is applied in both clinical and research settings. Items related to fatigue on this scale are scored using one of four Likert-type responses: ‘better than usual’, ‘no better than usual’, ‘worse than usual’ and ‘much worse than usual’.32

Upper respiratory muscle strength and function

Inspiratory and expiratory muscle strength will be measured by maximal inspiratory pressure (MIP) and maximal expiratory pressure (MEP). MIP and MEP values will be obtained in centimetres of water (cmH2O) using a Respiratory Muscle Strength Measurement Device-MIP–MEP (MD Diagnostics, RP Check, UK) in accordance with the recommendations of the American Thoracic Society. During this test, participants will be asked to wrap their mouths around a flanged mouthpiece and, for MIP, to perform a maximal inspiratory manoeuvre for 1.5–3 s while sitting, after maximal expiration. For MEP, after maximal inspiration, participants will be asked to perform a maximal expiratory manoeuvre for 1.5–3 s against the resistance of the indicator. After instructions are given, participants will be allowed three practice trials and a 1 min rest break before the test trials. A Pulmonary Function Test will be performed using a Spirometer (Cosmed, Pony FX, Italy) for upper airway function assessment. The best forced vital capacity (FVC) manoeuvre from at least three trials will be selected according to American Thoracic Society criteria, that is, based on the highest FEV1 and FVC sum.33 FVC with spirometry; forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1); Tiffeneau-Pinelli index or FEV1/FVC ratio; mean forced expiratory flow (FEF 25%–75%) between 25% and 75% of the FVC curve; and peak expiratory flow values will be measured. To perform spirometric manoeuvres, participants will sit upright with their feet on the floor and the nose clip properly positioned.33

CPAP compliance data

Data regarding average nightly CPAP usage, air leakage and treatment pressure will be downloaded from CPAP devices. Good CPAP compliance is generally defined as ≥4 hours/night for ≥5 nights per week.34

Exercise compliance

Participants’ frequency of logging into the ATP system, the percentage of exercises completed, how often and for how long they completed planned exercises, the time spent on each module and physiological and movement data collected via sensors will be evaluated.

To evaluate participants’ adherence to the programme in more detail and identify potential difficulties, they will be asked to fill out digital exercise logs via the ‘exercise logs’ option within the designed application.

Smartwatch data

Participants will be provided with a Huawei GT3 Pro smartwatch (Huawei Technologies Co, Shenzhen, China) for continuous physiological and activity monitoring. At baseline, participants will wear the smartwatch for one full day prior to starting the exercise programme to obtain reference measurements. During the 12 week intervention, data will be collected on each exercise day (3 days per week), specifically during the exercise sessions and overnight. The smartwatch will record multiple parameters, including nighttime and daytime oxygen saturation (SaO₂) levels, heart rate, sleep duration, sleep stages (light, deep, rapid eye movement), heart rate variability, as well as exercise-related metrics such as duration, distance and pace. For the data to be considered valid, a minimum of 30 min of continuous recording during exercise sessions and at least 6 hours of overnight wear time will be required.

System Usability Scale (SUS)

The SUS consists of 10 statements that allow for a quick assessment of the ease of use (or lack thereof) of websites, software, hardware, mobile devices and other technological applications. Using a 5-point Likert scale, the SUS is essentially a questionnaire designed to evaluate usability.35

Quality of life

The Short Form-12 (SF-12) questionnaire will be used to assess individuals’ overall health status and quality of life. The SF-12 is a widely used, short and reliable quality of life scale and is a shortened version of the more comprehensive SF-36 questionnaire.36

Muscle strength

Quadriceps isometric muscle strength will be measured using a myometer (Pelican Products, Torrance, CA, USA) while the knee is in extension. For maximum grip strength, muscle strength will be measured in the dominant arm using a hand dynamometer (Jamar Hydraulic Hand Dynamometer, Sammons Preston CO, Bolingbrook, IL, USA).37 38

Psychological distress

The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) will be used to assess the patient’s risk of anxiety and depression by measuring changes in level and severity.39

Intervention

The daily CPAP compliance parameters of all participants in the three groups will be regularly recorded, and participants will be asked to continue their current physical activities. Participants in the ATP and SPEG groups will record their physical activities via the web-based ATP system, while participants in the CG group will record their daily physical activities using a physical activity log provided by the researchers.

Control group

Participants in this group will use the ‘CPAP’ device prescribed by their physician and will sleep with CPAP every night for 12 weeks. After the study is completed, the ATP system will also be made available to participants in this group, and they will be advised to perform the exercises.

Structured presleep exercise group (SPEG)

Participants in this group will be subject to a new structured programme, designed based on exercises that have been applied separately or in combination in the literature, at individually determined times before sleep. For 12 weeks, individuals will perform a programme consisting of breathing (5 min), aerobic (10 min), strengthening (5 min) and oropharyngeal (10 min) exercises, totalling 36 sessions at a rate of 3 times per week (table 1). There will be 2 min rest breaks between exercises. The exercises will be presented to users in the form of video/training materials prepared by the physiotherapists on a weekly basis. The monitoring and follow-up of individuals in this group will be provided through the developed system. This group will only be able to access this video/training material information on the user panel opened in the system. The system will remind individuals of the specified exercises as an exercise programme without gamification and will provide real-time data flow.

ATP system

ATP will be specially developed within the scope of this project and designed as a scenario-based digital exercise application enriched with motivational elements. Users will progress in a structured progression in accordance with the specified programmes; reinforcement messages will be provided with visual and auditory feedback after each session. Reminders and social incentive messages will be implemented within the application to increase participation and continuity. All exercise sessions and performance metrics will be recorded on the system, progress will be tracked and access to personalised reports will be provided. Harms will be defined as any adverse events occurring during or after the intervention. Adverse events will be systematically assessed at the end of each session through participant self-report within the ATP system. Notifications will be sent to the expert interface of the system, and appropriate corrective and improvement measures will be implemented.

For aerobic exercises, the user’s movements will be synchronised with the system via wearable sensors and smartwatches, with the collected data stored for subsequent analysis. During oropharyngeal exercises, facial movements will be analysed using Google Mediapipe. MediaPipe has the ability to convert detected movements into real-time movements through artificial intelligence without the need for any sensors. Four different modules will be designed for the application, and at least two games will be developed for each module.

Example of a new game scenario for the ATP system

Scenarios will be developed based on diaphragmatic breathing exercises for respiratory exercises. Scenarios will be modified to increase exercise intensity for the progression of game-based respiratory exercises. In the specified activities, individuals will complete the games based on the intensity of the breathing sound they produce while performing diaphragmatic breathing.

For oropharyngeal exercises, posture exercises will be performed initially, and in subsequent progressions, the tongue will be exercised in various ways through games such as making animal sounds, clicking the tongue against the palate and playing the flute. The game speed will increase or decrease according to the intensity of the sound produced in these exercises.

Breathing exercise scenario example

In this game, participants will be asked to inflate and pop balloons on the screen through inspiration. The inflation of the balloons will be achieved through the detection of the participant’s breathing sound. Statistics

All statistical analyses will be performed using SPSS V. 24.0 (SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA) software following the intention-to-treat principle, and all participants will be analysed in their assigned groups. The normality of continuous variables will be assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test and analysed using histograms and Q–Q plots. If quantitative variables follow a normal distribution, mean and SD data will be reported; if they do not follow a normal distribution, median and IQR will be reported. Qualitative data will be reported as number (n) and percentage (%). Repeated measures ANOVA will be used to account for the correlations of the primary outcome, AHI and nighttime saturation changes, at multiple time points between the baseline measurement and the follow-up measurement. The Friedman test will be applied when the data do not show a normal distribution. Intergroup differences in secondary outcomes will be examined using ANOVA, with the factors of time (preintervention and postintervention and follow-up), group and group*time. Bonferroni or Tukey post-hoc tests will be used for pairwise comparisons, and Kruskal-Wallis tests will be used for non-parametric values. Effect sizes will be calculated using Cohen’s d for continuous variables. The χ2 test will be used to examine differences between categorical variables. Statistical significance will be set at p<0.05, and results will be reported with 95% CIs.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the Istanbul Medipol University Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee E-10840098-202.3.02-6266; 17 September 2025. The study will comply with the guidelines established by the ICMJE for clinical trial registration and reporting. Before starting the study, all participants will be given detailed verbal and written information about the purpose of the study, the procedures, the possible risks and benefits, and their written consent will be obtained. Informed consent will be obtained in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2013). All consent forms will be stored securely and only members of the research team will have access to the data. Digital data collection and data privacy measures will be carried out in accordance with the Personal Data Protection Law (PDPL) No. 6698. Personal data, including medical and physiological assessments, will be processed and stored securely in accordance with PDPL regulations. No identifiable data will be shared publicly, and access will be limited to authorised research personnel. Participants will have the right to request access to, correction or deletion of their personal data in accordance with Article 11 of the Personal Data Protection Law.

This study is funded by TÜBİTAK, and due to the low-risk nature of the intervention, an independent Data Monitoring Committee was not established; participant safety and data integrity will be monitored by the principal investigator and reported to the funder when necessary.

After eligibility is confirmed, written and verbal information about the study will be provided to all participants. Participants will then sign a written informed consent form confirming their voluntary participation (online supplemental material).

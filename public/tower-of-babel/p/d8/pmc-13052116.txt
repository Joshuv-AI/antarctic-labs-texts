Prediction Model for Skin Damage Related to Medical Adhesives in Adult Patients Undergoing Degenerative Spine Surgery
Authors: Jeongwon Han, Hanna Lee, Changgi Park, In‐Seok Son, Young Hee Seo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThis study aimed to develop a prediction model for the occurrence of medical adhesive‐related skin injuries (MARSIs) based on electronic medical records (EMRs) of adult patients who underwent degenerative spine surgery. This study used the EMR data of adult patients who underwent degenerative spine surgery at a university hospital in Seoul between January 2020 and December 2024. Seven machine learning algorithms and the SuperLearner algorithm were used to evaluate the performance of the SuperLearner model. Performance was focused on the area under the curve (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision and F1 score. Among the machine learning algorithms, the RuleFit algorithm showed the best performance, with an AUC of 0.723, accuracy of 0.689, sensitivity of 0.959, specificity of 0.276, precision of 0.762 and F1 score of 0.789. In contrast, predicting MARSI using the SuperLearner algorithm had an AUC of 0.951, accuracy of 0.834, sensitivity of 0.635, specificity of 0.964, precision of 0.921 and F1 score of 0.752. This study provides practical evidence for the early identification of high‐risk patients and establishment of customized nursing plans by presenting a MARSI prediction model using the SuperLearner ensemble. Future research is recommended to verify the external validity of the model through prospective studies and integration of clinical decision support systems. Introduction

As society transitions into an aging population, the number of degenerative spine surgeries continues to increase; consequently, clinical nurses are increasingly involved in caring for patients undergoing such surgeries [1]. Nurses must understand and perform comprehensive nursing interventions related to spine surgery, including postoperative pain management [2]. Recently, the importance of skin care in patients undergoing spine surgery has been highlighted, particularly in the prevention and management of medical adhesive‐related skin injury (MARSI) [2]. Although the incidence of MARSI in general acute care surgical patients is approximately 20%, it is reported to be 36% in patients undergoing spine surgery, indicating a higher risk [3].

Healthcare professionals use wound management guidelines and previous studies to prevent skin damage in patients. Their focus is primarily on identifying the causes of skin damage and selecting appropriate wound dressing products, particularly for patients with stomas or those receiving fluid therapy [4, 5]. However, surgical patients differ from non‐surgical patients in that they may experience changes in skin integrity, pH, temperature and humidity at the wound site owing to invasive incisions and pressure dressings [6]. Additionally, patients who are undergoing spine surgery receive postsurgical care across various departments, such as preoperative wards, operating rooms, recovery rooms and intensive care units [7]. The prolonged use of medical adhesives for fixing various medical devices and for restricting movement to prevent complications at the surgical site necessitates differentiated strategies for preventing MARSI [2].

The most crucial aspect of preventing skin damage in surgical patients is the preassessment of the individual skin damage risk, identification of high‐risk patients and implementation of appropriate preventive care. Currently, The International Skin Tear Advisory Panel has created a tool kit for the prevention, identification and treatment of various skin tears including MARSI. The tool kit is based on extensive literature reviews, international input from healthcare professionals, and on expert opinion. It has undergone a modified Delphi process [4]. However, this tool was not specifically developed for patients undergoing spine surgery; rather, it was designed for general applicability across all patients. In contrast, a recent study on patients with cancer has developed prediction models for MARSI and recommended exploring preventive nursing strategies based on these models [8]. Therefore, a prediction model for MARSI, specifically for patients undergoing spine surgery who have a high incidence of such injuries, is needed.

Furthermore, effective wound management requires ongoing strategies to minimize skin damage, supported by consistently sharing information among healthcare professionals [7]. To provide healthcare professionals with rapid and accurate clinical decision support (CDS) for wound management in clinical settings and to ultimately enhance patient safety, it is essential to utilize Electronic Medical Record (EMR) data, which contains the most comprehensive, accurate, and readily shareable patient information [9]. Therefore, developing a highly valid predictive model for skin management in patients undergoing degenerative spine surgery is needed using EMR data. Recently, the SuperLearner ensemble method is being used to enhance the stability and validity of models by combining predictions from multiple models [10]. SuperLearner is an ensemble technique that integrates various machine learning algorithms by appropriately weighing their predictions [10, 11]. The fundamental concept is to determine the optimal combination of base learners, which are various machine learning algorithms, by minimizing errors based on cross‐validation. SuperLearner improves the generalization performance of the model by finding the best algorithm combination based on the accumulated predictions of the base learners through cross‐validation. Based on the above context, this study aimed to develop a predictive model for MARSI using EMR data from patients undergoing degenerative spine surgery to provide foundational data for skin care in these patients.

Materials and Methods

Research Design

This study was a retrospective analysis aimed at developing a prediction model for MARSI based on the EMR data of patients who have undergone degenerative spine surgery at K University Hospital. The predictive validity of the model was evaluated using the SuperLearner ensemble method.

Study Participants

A total of 457 adult patients aged 18 years and older were initially enrolled, all of whom were admitted to the orthopaedic ward of a university hospital in Seoul for degenerative spine surgery between January 1, 2020, and December 31, 2024. In addition, the final study participants were restricted to patients with an alert level of consciousness. Among the initial cohort, 27 patients were excluded due to cancelled surgeries, conversion to other procedures or preexisting skin damage prior to admission. An additional 30 patients were excluded due to missing values in the variables utilized for this study. Consequently, a total of 400 patients were included in the final analysis. Of these, 148 (37.0%) experienced Medical Adhesive‐Related Skin Injury (MARSI) during their hospitalization, while 252 (63.0%) did not.

Variable Selection

Predictor Variables

In this study, to predict and prevent skin damage in high‐risk groups of patients undergoing degenerative spine surgery, we used nursing history data and test results recorded at the initial stage of hospitalization from structured information within the EMR. The key variables selected through the literature review included age, gender, current medical history, body mass index (BMI), allergy status, current smoking and alcohol consumption status, polypharmacy, care needs assessment, fall risk grade (Morse Fall Scale), pressure ulcer risk grade (Braden Scale) and the initial albumin level at admission (Table 1). In this study, the BMI is based on the value obtained by dividing the subjects' weight (kg) by the square of their height (m2). It is classified according to the BMI criteria proposed by the Korean Society for the Study of Obesity: underweight (< 18.5 kg/m2), normal (18.5–23.9 kg/m2) and overweight (≥ 23 kg/m2). The term polypharmacy is often defined as regularly taking five or more medicines but there is no standard definition, and the term has also been used in the context of when a person is prescribed 2 or more medications at the same time [12]. In this study, we included cases where patients were taking two or more prescribed medications. This measurement tool represents patient severity in Korea, meaning the patient's nursing care needs, and is a part included in the EMR of the respective medical institution. The care needs assessment measurement in this study classifies four groups by measuring the degree of assistance in seven nursing activities items (respiratory care, nutritional care, elimination care, medication and treatment assistance, skin and wound care, infection control, therapeutic procedures) and four activities of daily living items (eating, personal hygiene, elimination, mobility).

Outcome Variable

In this study, MARSI was defined according to the definition proposed by McNichol et al. [5] as erythema, vesicle, bulla, erosion or skin tear occurring after removal of medical adhesive products. MARSI occurrence was identified through nursing documentation in the EMR system. Cases were extracted based on standardized wound assessment records recorded during hospitalization. Only newly developed skin injuries occurring after admission were included, and preexisting skin damage was excluded.

Data Analysis

Prior to model fitting, categorical variables were converted into factor variables and encoded using dummy variables in R. Continuous variables were standardized using z‐score normalization for scale‐sensitive algorithms (e.g., logistic regression, SVM, and K‐nearest neighbours), whereas tree‐based models were fitted using original scales. Missing values were handled using complete‐case analysis. Cases with missing values in any predictor variable were excluded from the analysis. A total of 457 cases were excluded due to missing data, resulting in a final analytic sample of 400 patients.

The dataset was randomly split into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets. Within the training dataset, five‐fold cross‐validation was applied for hyperparameter tuning using GridSearchCV to reduce overfitting and enhance generalizability. Final model performance was evaluated using the independent test dataset.

In this study, we applied multiple machine learning algorithms to compare predictive performance, including binary logistic regression, decision tree, support vector machine, K‐nearest neighbours, random forest, extreme gradient boosting and RuleFit. These algorithms were selected based on their established methodological strengths and prior applications in predictive modelling studies [12, 13, 14]. The RuleFit model extends linear models to incorporate rule‐based learning and feature selection [15].

Hyperparameters were optimized using GridSearchCV, with up to two hyperparameters tuned per model (Table 2). The SuperLearner algorithm incorporated 40 base learner models, and ensemble weights were determined based on cross‐validated risk minimization. Model performance was evaluated using area under the curve (AUC), accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, precision and F1 score. All analyses were conducted using R version 4.4.3 with the SuperLearner package and relevant machine learning libraries.

Ethical Considerations

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the K University Hospital at Gangdong in Seoul (IRB no. 2025–02–020‐001). This retrospective study did not involve direct intervention. The data used in this study were obtained from previously collected EMR. Permission was obtained for the necessary variables, and data were provided by a data management officer. Personal information was anonymized and safely managed under the researcher's access rights.

Discussion

This study developed a prediction model for MARSI in patients undergoing degenerative spine surgery and compared the performance of seven machine learning algorithms and the SuperLearner ensemble using EMR data. The implications based on the study results are as follows: first, among the individual algorithms, the RuleFit model showed the highest performance, particularly in terms of sensitivity. However, its low specificity may lead to excessive false positives in clinical settings, potentially resulting in resource wastage and limited practicality. In contrast, the SuperLearner ensemble, which integrates multiple base learners with optimal weights, demonstrated superior predictive performance, with balanced results. This indicates the utility of ensemble learning in clinical predictive modelling and its potential as a practical tool for early identification of high‐risk patients. This study is significant because it is one of the few that applied a machine learning–based model, particularly the SuperLearner ensemble, for MARSI prediction, addressing gaps not covered in previous studies. Furthermore, SuperLearner assigned the highest weight to the logistic regression model with elastic net regularization, suggesting that a model combining the strengths of lasso and ridge regression provides stable and interpretable predictive outcomes for multidimensional medical data, as in this study.

Second, the key predictive variables derived from the SuperLearner included age, serum albumin level, allergy history, and BMI. These variables have been frequently reported as factors related to skin damage and recovery in existing literature. Age is closely associated with reduced elasticity, decreased epidermal thickness, and impaired wound healing capabilities due to physiological aging of the skin [16, 17], with older patients being at increased risk of damage from external stimuli [18, 19]. Therefore, older patients should be classified as high risk for MARSI from the beginning of hospitalization, and preventive interventions such as protective dressings and skin protectants are necessary. Additionally, serum albumin is a biochemical marker reflecting both systemic nutritional status and inflammatory response, with hypoalbuminemia being closely linked to delayed skin regeneration, wound healing, and poor overall patient prognosis [18, 19, 20, 21, 22]. According to Eckart et al. [22], elevated C‐reactive protein levels and high nutritional risk are independently associated with hypoalbuminemia in acute care hospitalized patients, with albumin, C‐reactive protein and nutritional risk serving as predictors of 30‐day mortality. This suggests that albumin levels are useful not only for skin damage prevention but also for overall clinical risk assessment. Furthermore, considering that surgical patients may develop skin damage during hospitalization despite having no skin damage at admission, albumin level can be considered a good indicator that healthcare providers can periodically monitor to prevent skin damage.

Additionally, among the key variables identified through SuperLearner, allergy history can increase reactivity to medical adhesives due to hypersensitivity reactions of the immune system, acting as a factor that heightens the incidence of skin damage [23]. However, previous research [24] suggests that self‐reported allergic reactions to adhesives are often irritant contact dermatitis rather than allergic contact dermatitis. In a study by Widman et al. [24], actual allergens were rarely identified in patients with adhesive allergies, and most reactions were irritation‐induced. This highlights the limitations of relying solely on self‐reported data when clinically evaluating allergy histories. Therefore, prior to applying medical adhesives, especially in patients with a history of allergy, preemptive skin testing or alternative adhesives should be considered, and a more precise evaluation of allergy history is required for clinical interpretation. This process enables personalized nursing interventions that consider individual skin reaction characteristics, thereby reducing the incidence of contact dermatitis. Additionally, before applying medical adhesives to critically ill or high‐risk patients, individualized skin risk assessments and standardized skincare protocols should be implemented. BMI can affect tissue oxygen supply and pressure sensitivity and is a risk factor for skin damage in both obese and underweight individuals. Reports indicate that obese patients may have a higher likelihood of damage due to increased humidity and friction in skinfold areas [25]. Furthermore, obese patients have larger body surface areas and diverse pressure points, with limited movement leading to difficult pressure distribution and increased contact irritation due to sweat and moisture [26]. Therefore, before using medical adhesives, special precautions such as skin inspection, cleanliness maintenance and moisture control are required for obese patients. Additionally, tailored nursing approaches to reduce skin irritation through appropriate adhesive selection, protective dressing combinations, and minimal repeat removal are necessary.

This study, which developed a prediction model for MARSIs in patients undergoing spine surgery using the SuperLearner ensemble technique, differs from previous studies in that it offers an advanced predictive tool that can be used for patient‐specific risk stratification and care intervention planning, owing to its ability to combine various algorithms to automatically select the most suitable model for specific datasets. Additionally, this study holds academic and practical value by developing a multivariate prediction model based on EMR for a special group of patients undergoing degenerative spine surgery, which has not been sufficiently addressed domestically and internationally in relation to MARSI. In clinical practice, this model is expected to facilitate the early identification of high‐risk groups at the onset of hospitalization, enabling the rapid implementation of preventive nursing practices such as alternative adhesive use, enhanced skin protection, and individualized care planning. It can also contribute to optimizing resource allocation within wards and improving patient safety and quality of care.

However, this study has some limitations. As this study used retrospective data from patients who underwent spine surgery at a single institution, there are limitations in the interpretation of skin damage issues that may arise from differences in surgical methods or medical environments. Future research should verify the external validity of this model through multicentre prospective studies and evaluate its applicability in the field through integration with clinical decision support systems. Moreover, by analysing the impact of nursing interventions based on this model on actual patient outcomes and resource utilization, the empirical evidence for MARSI prevention strategies should be expanded. Furthermore, age and albumin level, which were important factors in this study, could serve as good baseline data for skin damage prevention if the threshold for skin damage occurrence is confirmed after adjusting for albumin levels according to age, given that albumin levels are periodically checked in postoperative patients.

Recommendations or Implications for Practice and Further

This study developed a prediction model for MARSI using the SuperLearner ensemble algorithm based on EMR data of patients undergoing degenerative spine surgery. The developed prediction model demonstrated excellent performance in terms of accuracy, precision, and specificity, suggesting its potential for the early identification of high‐risk MARSI patients and the implementation of personalized preventive care. Notably, the key predictive variables identified in this study, such as allergy history, hypoalbuminemia, advanced age, and obesity, comprise information that nurses can assess at the beginning of hospitalization, thereby enhancing their practical applicability. Based on the study results, the model can be used as a standardized assessment tool for skin damage prevention in nursing practice and can support evidence‐based decision making in preoperative and postoperative care planning. In the future, it will be necessary to externally validate this prediction model through multicentre prospective studies and to integrate it into a real‐time clinical decision support system based on EMR to enhance its utility. Additionally, follow‐up studies should empirically analyze the effects of nursing interventions based on a prediction model to improve patient outcomes, reduce healthcare costs, and enhance nursing work efficiency. Moreover, the findings of this study could contribute to the development of MARSI prevention education, establishment of standard nursing guidelines, and formulation of infection and wound management protocols, thereby aiding in the preparation of nursing policy strategies.

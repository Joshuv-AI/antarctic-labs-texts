Paratesticular leiomyosarcoma in a 44-year-old male: A case report
Authors: Sami Chebbi, Houssem Ben Hadj Alouane, Mohamed Amine Oueslati, Imen Helal, Sami Ben Rhouma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractParatesticular leiomyosarcoma is a rare malignant tumor with diagnostic and therapeutic challenges due to its nonspecific presentation and lack of standardized protocols. We report the case of a 44-year-old man presenting with a left scrotal mass managed with fertility-sparing surgery and adjuvant therapy. Pathological analysis confirmed grade II leiomyosarcoma. Multidisciplinary management emphasized the balance between oncological control and fertility preservation. This case underscores the importance of personalized approaches in rare neoplasms, by relying on imaging, negative surgical margins, and adjuvant therapies in improving outcomes.Keywords: Paratesticular, Leiomyosarcoma, Soft tissue sarcoma, Rare tumor management, Fertility

Introduction

Primary paratesticular tumors are very rare but clinically significant neoplasms. They represent a tiny fraction of all intrascrotal tumors which in turn are rare. Most of these paratesticular masses are benign tumors. This highlights the rarity of paratesticular leiomyosarcomas, which represent a histological subtype of paratesticular sarcomas, which are malignant tumors.

We present a rare case of paratesticular leiomyosarcoma. We discuss the diagnostic and therapeutic challenges posed by this rare pathological finding, given the lack of standardized management protocols due to its extreme rarity.

Case presentation

A 44-year-old obese male with no prior medical history presented with a left scrotal lump that had developed several months earlier and rapidly increased in size. Examination of the external genitalia revealed a firm, 10 cm mass in the lower part of the left hemiscrotum. The mass was independent of the ipsilateral testis, mobile, and not adherent to superficial or deep layers. Ultrasound confirmed a highly vascularized mass, separate from the left testis, with suspicious characteristics including microcalcifications, a heterogeneous echotexture, and marked hypervascularity. Serum levels of LDH, alpha-fetoprotein, and HCG were normal. A thoracoabdominal and pelvic CT scan showed no evidence of secondary lesions.

The patient underwent surgery via a scrotal approach with a circumferential incision around the mass. A distinct cleavage plane was identified between the described mass and the tunica vaginalis. The mass was excised en bloc with the overlying scrotal skin. Postoperative recovery was uneventful, and the patient was discharged on the first postoperative day.

Pathological analysis of the surgical specimen revealed a well-circumscribed tumor measuring 8 × 5.5 × 7 cm, surrounded by a thin capsule. The mass was covered by excised skin and was not encased in healthy peritumoral tissue. On sectioning, the tumor had a lobulated, multinodular appearance, with a yellowish-white color and areas of necrotic and cystic degeneration (Fig. 1).

Macroscopic feature of the tumor upon section.

Microscopic examination showed a malignant mesenchymal proliferation with edematous, hyalinized, and cystic changes, as well as multiple foci of geographic tumor necrosis (Fig. 2a). The tumor was deeply subcutaneous and did not infiltrate the overlying skin. Immunohistochemical analysis of tumor cells revealed strong and diffuse positivity for Desmin and Caldesmon (Fig. 2b), with negative staining for smooth muscle actin, Myogenin, CD34, and EMA. The final diagnosis was a grade II paratesticular leiomyosarcoma according to the FNCLCC classification.

Following the anatomopathological findings, the case was discussed during a multidisciplinary tumor board meeting. The initial decision to limit the surgical approach to scrotal excision of the mass, without orchiectomy or funiculectomy, was motivated by the patient's relatively young age, unmarried status, and desire to preserve fertility. However, given the confirmed aggressiveness of sarcomatous tumors and the limited literature available on the management of this extremely rare neoplasm, it was deemed imperative to propose adjuvant treatment. After sperm preservation, the patient was referred to the National Cancer Institute for appropriate radiotherapy and chemotherapy. However, following further evaluation, adjuvant treatment was ultimately not administered, as the tumor was deemed localized and the literature remains sparse regarding optimal management. Considering the patient's young age, fertility concerns, and active lifestyle, a decision was made in favour of close active surveillance. This included regular physical examination, testicular MRI, and thoraco-abdominopelvic CT scan. The most recent follow-up conducted one month ago showed no evidence of recurrence or metastasis.

Discussion

Paratesticular leiomyosarcoma (LMS) is an extremely rare tumor, accounting for approximately 20 % of all malignant mesenchymal tumors in the paratesticular region.1 The rarity of this neoplasm makes its optimal management challenging, with most recommendations derived from case reports and small case series.2 Similar to the difficulties seen in diagnosing and managing other rare malignancies, such as paratesticular sarcomas, LMS often presents with nonspecific clinical features, resulting in delays in diagnosis.3 Furthermore, the high proportion of high-grade tumors observed in these sarcomas, including LMS, is a significant factor in prognosis, making early and accurate diagnosis even more crucial for patient survival.4

Paratesticular LMS typically presents as a painless, enlarging scrotal mass, often misdiagnosed initially due to its nonspecific clinical features. Ultrasound is the first-line imaging modality, but advanced imaging techniques such as CT or MRI are crucial for determining the extent of the disease and planning surgical intervention.2 This approach, which is consistent with the management of other paratesticular sarcomas, ensures better characterization of the mass and aids in staging, helping to avoid the misdiagnosis of benign conditions such as hernias or lipomas, which can present similarly in the scrotal region.1,2

Radical inguinal orchiectomy with high ligation of the spermatic cord is the cornerstone of treatment for paratesticular LMS. This approach ensures a wide margin of resection, minimizing local recurrence.5 In cases where fertility preservation is a concern, as in our patient, a more conservative surgical approach may be initially considered. However, the high risk of recurrence associated with suboptimal margins necessitates careful case selection.4 This is particularly true for high-grade tumors, where the risk of metastasis and recurrence is significantly increased.4 Given that over 50 % of paratesticular sarcomas in a recent study were high-grade and required re-excision, margin adequacy becomes critical.5 This highlights the need for a tailored surgical strategy that considers the patient's clinical situation, including fertility concerns.

The role of adjuvant therapies in paratesticular LMS remains controversial. Evidence suggests that adjuvant radiotherapy can reduce local recurrence in high-grade tumors, particularly when surgical margins are inadequate.1 The addition of chemotherapy may be considered in metastatic or high-risk cases, although its efficacy in localized LMS is not well established.2 In cases of rhabdomyosarcoma, for example, a higher incidence of nodal spread has been observed, necessitating retroperitoneal lymphadenectomy, which can influence the management strategy.2

Prognosis in paratesticular LMS depends on tumor grade, size, and margin status. High-grade tumors, such as the one described in this case, are associated with an increased risk of metastasis and disease-specific mortality.1 Long-term follow-up is imperative, with regular imaging to detect recurrence or metastasis early. As observed in sarcoma management, failure to monitor adequately increases the risk of late recurrence, underscoring the importance of long-term surveillance. A comprehensive follow-up strategy, including imaging and clinical evaluation, is crucial for patients with high-risk paratesticular sarcomas.5

The decision to perform testis-sparing surgery was guided by the patient's desire to preserve fertility. Following the confirmed diagnosis of a grade II LMS, the patient was referred for adjuvant radiotherapy and chemotherapy, underscoring the importance of a multidisciplinary approach in managing such rare and aggressive neoplasms. This case highlights the need for individualized treatment strategies and contributes to the limited literature on fertility-sparing approaches in paratesticular LMS. As with other sarcomas, a personalized approach, incorporating both surgical and adjuvant therapy, is key to optimizing outcomes.

Conclusion

Paratesticular leiomyosarcoma is an extremely rare malignancy, requiring tailored, multidisciplinary management due to its nonspecific presentation and high recurrence risk. Fertility-sparing surgery is a viable option for young patients but must be balanced against the tumor's aggressiveness. Adjuvant therapies remain debated, emphasizing the need for further research. Long-term follow-up is essential to detect recurrence or metastasis, ensuring comprehensive, patient-centered care and contributing to the limited literature on this rare tumor.

Association of CD32-131H>R Polymorphism with End-Stage Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease and Interstitial Lung Diseases
Authors: Sarah M. Berger, Laura M. Kühner, Ines M. Schwarzinger, Verena Heiss Spornberger, Peter Jaksch, Elisabeth Puchhammer-Stöckl, Hannes Vietzen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionProgression to end-stage lung diseases is influenced by both environmental and genetic factors, yet predictive markers remain limited. The CD32-131H>R polymorphism in the FCGR2A gene alters the affinity of the FcγRIIa receptor for IgG and may impair immune clearance, potentially influencing immune responses involved in chronic lung disease. In our study, we investigated the association between the CD32-131H>R polymorphism and end-stage pulmonary diseases requiring LTX.MethodsThe CD32-131H>R polymorphism was determined in 985 lung transplant recipients and 2,116 matched controls via TaqMan genotyping assays.ResultsThe low-affinity CD32-131R allele was significantly more prevalent among lung transplant recipients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and interstitial lung diseases (ILD) compared to controls (p < 0.0001 and p = 0.01, respectively).ConclusionOur study demonstrates that the CD32-131R allele is enriched among patients with end-stage COPD and ILD requiring LTX, highlighting a possible association between Fcγ receptor-mediated immune responses and end-stage pulmonary disease.

Introduction

Lung transplantation (LTX) remains the definitive treatment for patients with end-stage pulmonary diseases. The most common indications for LTX include end-stage cystic fibrosis (CF), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), interstitial lung diseases (ILD), and pulmonary hypertension (PH) [1]. Pulmonary macrophages and neutrophils are innate immune cells that play a crucial role in maintaining lung homeostasis. They regulate inflammation and protect against pulmonary infections by mediating the phagocytosis and clearance of inhaled particles, pathogens, and cellular debris. Equipped with a set of germline-encoded receptors, these cells can detect and phagocytose apoptotic, infected, and damaged cells. Among these receptors, CD32, also known as FcγRII, binds to the Fc portion of immunoglobulin G (IgG), facilitating the phagocytosis of antibody-opsonized pathogens [2].

The CD32-131H>R polymorphism, a genetic variation in the FCGR2A gene, results in the substitution of histidine with arginine at position 131 of the receptor. This polymorphism is associated with altered binding capacity and receptor affinity for IgG2 and IgG3, respectively [3]. Studies suggest that individuals carrying the high-affinity CD32-131H variant may exhibit more effective antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP) responses. In contrast, the low-affinity CD32-131R variant is associated with reduced IgG affinity, potentially impairing ADCP responses [4]. Based on these findings, we hypothesized that the CD32-131H>R polymorphism is associated with end-stage pulmonary diseases requiring LTX.

Methods

Study Cohort

We recruited a single-center retrospective study cohort of 985 Austrian lung transplant recipients (LTRs) diagnosed with CF (N = 229), COPD (N = 426), ILD (N = 232, of which 87.4% idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, 5.8% with bronchiectasis, 2% non-specific interstitial pneumonia, 3.2% usual interstitial pneumonia pattern, and 0.6% extrinsic allergic alveolitis), and PH (N = 89) who underwent lung transplantation between 2003 and 2020. In addition, we recruited 2,116 Austrian healthy blood donors, matched by age and gender to the LTRs, providing 2–3 (mean: 2.1) controls per LTR. CD32 Genotyping

Genomic DNA was isolated from 200 µL plasma using the NucliSens EasyMag extractor (bioMérieux). Nucleic acids were eluted in 50 μL nuclease-free H2O from all LTRs and controls. The CD32-131H>R variants (rs1801274 SNP) were then genotyped in all LTRs and controls using a commercial TaqMan SNP Genotyping Assay (C__9077561_20, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Quality control measures included assessment of DNA concentration and purity, as well as standardized positive and negative controls.

Statistical Analysis

The chi-square test was used to compare the distribution of the CD32-131H/R variants. A p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical differences were assessed with GraphPad Prism 9. A multiple logistic regression model was applied to evaluate the association between gender, age at diagnosis (categorized into age groups), year of transplantation, and CD32-131H>R genotypes with end-stage COPD and ILD. All covariates were included simultaneously in the model to assess the association between CD32-131H>R genotypes and end-stage COPD and ILD.

Results

As shown in Figure 1, 24.6% of controls carried the high-affinity CD32-131H/H genotype, while 47.2% and 28.2% carried the heterozygous CD32-131H/R and low-affinity homozygous CD32-131R/R genotypes, respectively. The genotype distribution in the control group deviated modestly from Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (χ2 = 6.3, p = 0.01). We then compared the frequencies of the CD32-131H>R genotypes between controls and LTRs. Controls more frequently exhibited the high-affinity homozygous CD32-131H/H genotype compared to LTRs diagnosed with end-stage COPD (p < 0.0001, χ2 test) or end-stage ILD (p = 0.002, χ2 test). The low-affinity CD32-131R allele was significantly more common among LTRs diagnosed with end-stage COPD (p < 0.0001, OR: 1.9, 95% CI: 1.4–2.5, χ2 test) or end-stage ILD (p = 0.01, OR: 1.6, 95% CI: 1.1–2.2, χ2 test) than among controls. No differences in CD32-131H>R distribution were observed across ILD subtypes (all: p > 0.05, χ2 test).

After correction for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg method, both associations remained statistically significant (adjusted p = 0.0002 and p = 0.01, respectively). No significant differences in the distribution of the CD32-131H>R genotypes were observed between controls and LTRs diagnosed with end-stage CF or PH.

We also analyzed whether the CD32-131H>R genotypes were associated with overall survival, graft survival, the development of bronchiolitis obliterans syndrome, susceptibility to bacterial pulmonary infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus, Enterobacterales, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and Enterococcus spp., as well as high-level human cytomegalovirus replication (defined as viral loads >1,000 copies/mL plasma), or the development of Epstein-Barr virus-associated post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorders within 5 years after LTX. However, no significant differences were found between the CD32-131H>R genotypes with respect to these outcomes (all: p > 0.05, χ2 test).

We then applied a multiple logistic regression model to test whether the covariates of gender, age at diagnosis (age groups), and year of transplantation, together with the CD32-131H>R genotypes, were associated with end-stage COPD and ILD. However, none of the covariates, in addition to the CD32-131H>R genotypes, were significantly associated with the presence of end-stage COPD and ILD among LTRs, supporting an association between CD32-131H>R genotypes and end-stage COPD and ILD within this transplant cohort.

Discussion

In summary, our study demonstrates that the low-affinity CD32-131R allele is enriched among patients with end-stage COPD and ILD requiring LTX. These findings are of particular interest as the identification of genetic associations in end-stage disease cohorts may help guide future studies evaluating disease biology and risk stratification in patients with advanced pulmonary disease.

Our findings suggest that the low-affinity CD32-131R allele may be associated with altered CD32-mediated immune responses, potentially affecting the clearance of antibody-opsonized targets. In addition to potentially impairing pathogen clearance before transplantation, this alteration may contribute to dysregulated immune homeostasis and sustained low-grade inflammation. Such processes may contribute to chronic airway injury and fibrosis observed in advanced pulmonary disease. However, further mechanistic studies are required to elucidate the role of the CD32-131H>R polymorphism in the pathogenesis of end-stage COPD and ILD requiring LTX.

A key limitation of this study is that the cohort consists exclusively of LTRs, a selected population shaped by referral, eligibility, and survival factors, which not only precludes assessment of the CD32-131H>R polymorphism in earlier disease stages but also limits interpretation regarding disease susceptibility, causation, and progression due to potential selection bias. Because this study was not designed to assess incident disease risk or longitudinal disease progression, causal inferences regarding the role of CD32-131H>R in COPD or ILD pathogenesis cannot be made. As a further limitation of our study, additional risk factors for COPD and ILD, such as smoking status, occupational exposures, and ethnicity, may have influenced the observed associations but were not available in our cohort. Future studies are, therefore, needed to evaluate the impact of the CD32-131H>R polymorphism in the context of additional clinical, environmental, and demographic risk factors in patients with COPD and ILD.

Although our study is limited by its retrospective, single-center design, it benefits from a large, well-characterized cohort of LTRs and matched controls. Furthermore, the inclusion of disease-specific subgroup analyses provides novel insights into the potential role of the CD32-131H>R polymorphism in end-stage pulmonary diseases. Given that our ILD cohort was predominantly composed of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis patients, we performed subgroup analyses across ILD subtypes and observed no significant differences in the distribution of the CD32-131H>R polymorphism, suggesting that the observed association is not solely driven by IPF. Interestingly, a recently published study investigating the association between the CD32-131H>R polymorphism and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis did not find a protective effect of the CD32-131H/H genotype [5]. Therefore, future functional and association studies are needed to further evaluate the role of the CD32-131H>R polymorphism, particularly in patients with end-stage ILD.

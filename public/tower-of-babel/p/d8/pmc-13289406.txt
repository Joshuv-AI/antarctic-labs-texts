Spatial gene expression of salivary glands in Sjögren’s disease highlights T follicular helper cell signalling as an indicator of disease pathogenesis
Authors: Tamandeep K Bharaj, Lara A Aqrawi, Siren Fromreide, Hanne Borge, Roland Jonsson, Daniel Hammenfors, Silke Appel, Kathrine Skarstein
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSjögren’s disease (SjD) is a systemic, rheumatic autoimmune disorder evidenced by broad organ-specific manifestations, predominantly exocrine gland-impairment, which disproportionally affects females. The golden-standard tool for diagnosing disease-associated pathology includes a microscopic evaluation of minor salivary gland (MSG) biopsies. Approximately 25% of the patients exhibit germinal centre (GC)-like structures within their salivary glands, which have been associated with an increased risk of B cell lymphoma and infiltration of T follicular helper (Tfh) cells.ObjectiveWe aimed to explore spatial signalling patterns associated with the Tfh cell and ectopic GC-like structure formation.Materials and methodsNovel duplex-chromogenic RNAscope in situ hybridisation was employed on MSGs of 20 male and female SjD patients, alongside 20 non-SjD sicca tissue controls where mRNA levels of B cell stimulating factor (BAFF) were visualised and quantified alongside IL-21R, CXCL13 and CXCR5.ResultsDigital quantifications in Qupath revealed a positive correlation between cells containing BAFF and IL-21R mRNA-signals (p<0.05). Also, the spatial mRNA expression of IL-21R, CXCL13 and CXCR5 was predominantly localised within focal infiltrates, with significantly higher transcript abundance observed in the GC+-group (p<0.001). Additional evaluation of salivary flow-related immunopathological changes showed that increased BAFF-positive ductal epithelial cells were associated with hyposalivation (p<0.05). Moreover, elevated atrophy of the MSGs displayed a negative correlation with BAFF-expressing immune cells within focal infiltrates (p<0.001).ConclusionSpatial localisation of key Tfh cell-related cytokines in relation to ectopic GC-like formations may explain the immunopathological alterations and symptoms involved in SjD pathogenesis.

Introduction

Sjögren’s disease (SjD) is a chronic rheumatic disorder, which leads to inflammatory damage to the exocrine secretory glands, resulting in reduced or absent saliva and tear production. This is further characterised by lymphocytic infiltration into the salivary and tear glands, which may be visualised and diagnosed microscopically using minor salivary gland (MSG) biopsies of patients. In similarity with other autoimmune diseases, SjD is initiated by a series of adverse events, which lead to tissue atrophy and autoantibody production. These initial events occur inside the target organs, especially the salivary glands, where the disease progresses with the formation of focal immune cell infiltrates and in some cases ectopic germinal centre (GC)-like structures. An estimated 25% of the patients exhibit GC-like structures inside their salivary glands, which have been associated with an increased risk of B cell lymphoma development.1 2 Accordingly, although the exocrine organs represent primary targets of immediate damage, the following systemic manifestations of SjD range from chronic muscle pain and kidney disorders to potentially life-threatening lymphatic cancer, severely impairing the quality of life in patients.3 4

The T follicular helper (Tfh) cell lineage was first discovered in year 2000 inside the tonsils where GCs naturally occur. Notably, Tfh cells are one of the few CD4+T helper cell subsets that orchestrate the formation of ectopic GC-like structures in the salivary glands in SjD patients.5 The secretion of cytokines such as interleukin (IL)−21, IL-4, and C-X-C Motif Chemokine Ligand 13 (CXCL13) by Tfh cells maintains an optimal environment favouring B cell growth and advancement inside these pathological structures.6 7 The signature cytokine of Tfh cells, namely IL-21, interacts with its receptor (IL-21R), subsequently initiating intracellular signalling cascades that sustain critical Tfh cell functions, including survival, proliferation and maintenance within GC microenvironments. In addition, the IL-21/IL-21R-signalling axis enhances differentiation of naïve T cells into Tfh cells and supports the expression of the C-X-C chemokine receptor type 5 (CXCR5). As a pivotal Tfh cell-marker, CXCR5 not only mediates the migration of Tfh cells into the B cell zones of ectopic GC-like structures in response to CXCL13 but also ensures that the Tfh cells remain localised within these regions and participate in key GC reactions, including class-switching and somatic hypermutation of B cells.8 9 Another cytokine vital for B cell functions and GC-like structure formation is B-cell activating factor (BAFF, also known as TNFSF13B). In recent years, a possible relationship between the functions of BAFF and Tfh cell development has gained increased attention. In a murine study evaluating this relationship, BAFF-deficient mice exhibited a significant reduction in Tfh cells, GCs and antigen-specific antibodies, underscoring BAFF’s role in these processes.10 Nevertheless, the exact molecular processes controlling BAFF’s role in modulating Tfh cell dynamics require further elucidation, especially in autoimmune diseases.11

Although most studies report a female-to-male ratio of approximately 9:1 in SjD, in certain populations, a stronger female predilection with ratios reaching up to 18:1 has been demonstrated.12 A growing body of evidence demonstrates differences in systemic manifestations between the sexes,1315 emphasising the significance of sex as an independent determinant in research and diagnostic evaluation. Meanwhile, sex-specific features of the target organs in SjD have largely been assessed using murine SjD-models, where female mice reveal a stronger salivary gland inflammation compared with males.16 Such changes include larger focal infiltrates, prolonged lymphocytic infiltrations and increased glandular damage.1719

In the current study, we aimed to explore signalling patterns associated with the Tfh cell and ectopic GC-like structure formation including BAFF alongside IL-21R, CXCL13 and CXCR5, in MSGs of both male and female SjD patients, by employing novel duplex-chromogenic RNAscope in situ hybridisation.

Materials and methods

Study population

Biopsies were selected based on predefined technical and practical criteria, where only samples with intact mRNA were eligible. This precluded the use of older biopsies. Moreover, to ensure the representation of both sexes, all available male biopsies with sufficient, intact tissue were included. The final set of patient samples comprised MSGs from ten females and 10 males diagnosed with SjD between 2014 and 2023. The patients covered various age groups and levels of inflammation severity in their MSGs and were age-and-sex-matched with 20 non-SjD sicca controls (±5 years). MSGs were obtained from the Department of Otorhinolaryngology, Haukeland University Hospital. Health records were retrieved from the medical journal of the patients at the Department of Rheumatology, Haukeland University Hospital. All patients showed a positive lip biopsy in concordance with the guidelines provided by Fisher et al,20 and 15/20 patients met the diagnostic requirements of SjD as determined by the American College of Rheumatology and European League against Rheumatism (ACR/EULAR).21 Clinical and laboratory data for patients and the sicca controls are shown in table 1.

Histopathological examinations in the MSG biopsy

The MSG biopsies of the patients were assessed digitally on H&E-stained sections. Focus score (FS) and GC-like structures were blindly re-evaluated by two investigators (TKB and KS) on freshly cut H&E sections to ensure consistent baseline levels and eliminate potential discrepancies. A focal infiltrate was defined as the presence of ≥50 mononuclear chronic inflammatory cells located periductally or perivascularly, and in close proximity to normal appearing salivary gland parenchyma. The number of focal infiltrates within an MSG tissue area of 4 mm2 defined the FS. The ectopic GC-like structures were predominantly identified by morphological characterisation on H&E-stained slides, together with anti-CD21 immunostainings as described previously by our group.22 In addition to the FS and GC-like structures, the levels of fat and atrophy were determined as recommended by Fisher et al20 in both the patients and controls.23 Levels of atrophy were blindly scored by two independent investigators (TKB and KS), where ‘0’ was considered negative, ‘+’ represented mild atrophy, ‘++’ characterised moderate atrophied tissue and ‘+++’ represented prominent atrophic changes. Adipose infiltration was also scored as previously described.24

Tissue microarray block

To examine inflammatory areas within each formalin-fixed paraffin embedded MSG biopsies of the SjD patients, a specific glandular area comprising at least one focal infiltrate was circumscribed by pen in each H&E slide. The area of demarcation was macrodissected using a wide cylinder punch (Ø=1 mm). Each core was carefully placed on a tissue microarray (TMA) block. Two cores were punched out from each patient biopsy, one core was included from each non-SjD control biopsy, while two cores were taken from tonsil tissue representing positive controls. Online supplemental figure 1 shows a H&E-stained TMA-slide. 4 µm-thick tissue sections of the block were cut using a microtome (Leica Instruments GmbH, Nussloch, Germany), and the sections were put on a SuperFrost Plus microscope slide (Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts), following overnight incubation at 56 °C immediately prior to the experiments.

In situ hybridisation using RNAscope Duplex assay

For the detection of mRNA-signals, the RNAscope 2.5 HD Duplex Chromogenic Assay (Advanced Cell Diagnostics) was carried out. First, the MSGs were deparaffinised by placing the slides in two separate dishes with 200 mL fresh Xylene for 5 min each, and thereafter two dishes with 200 mL 100% alcohol for 1 min each. Following deparaffinisation, the slides were removed from the rack and air dried for 5 min. A barrier was drawn using a hydrophobic Immedge (Bio-Techne, Minneapolis) barrier pen. The tissue sections were treated with RNAscope Hydrogen Peroxide (Bio-Techne, Minneapolis) for 10 min to block endogenous peroxidase activity, followed by two rinses in Milli-Q water.

Target retrieval was performed using the RNAscope Target Retrieval Reagent for 15 min at 98°C–102°C. Slides were then rinsed two times in Milli-Q water and once in 100% ethanol before air-drying.

RNAscope Protease Plus was applied, and slides were incubated in the HybEZ Oven at 40°C for 30 min, followed by rinse in MilliQ water.

The next steps included probe incubation, amplification steps and chromogen application. Target probes were designed for each target gene by Advanced Cell Diagnostics (ACD) where target probes in channel 1 (C1) included CXCL13 (cat. no. 311321), IL21R (cat. no. 312151) and CXCR5 (cat. no. 474171), while BAFF (TNFSF13B, cat. no. 406971-C2) was included in C2. Green and red signals were generated through separate channels, C1 and C2, respectively, and the C2/C1 ratio was 1:50. The target probes were added to the slides after incubation in the HybEZ Oven at 40 °C for 2 hours, followed by two washes in RNAscope Wash Buffer (2 min each).

Following probe application, slides were stored in 5X SSC solution overnight as recommended by the manufacturer. The subsequent hybridisation sequence featured 10 amplification steps, before application of green and red chromogens. At last, the sections were counterstained with 50% Gill’s Haematoxylin solution (SLCL2351, Sigma-Aldrich, Missouri) and mounted with Ecomount (Biocare).

To validate mRNA quality, positive control probe peptidylprolyl isomerase B in C1 and transforming growth factor-beta 1 in C2 were run on a single TMA-slide. Additionally, Dapβ was used as a negative control probe to confirm the valid RNA detections. A detailed description of the procedure is accessible through the manufacturer’s protocol for the RNAscope 2.5 HD Duplex Chromogenic Assay.

Cell analysis

Images of the MSG and TMA slides were acquired using a Hamamatsu NanoZoomer-XR digital whole slide scanner. Digitalised images were visualised in the bioimage analysis software QuPath V,0.2.3.25 As outlined by Advanced Cell Diagnostics,26 signal identification in Qupath using duplex-chromogenic assay required image preparation, where the image type was set to ‘Brightfield_other’ and colour deconvolution was applied to separate the chromogenic signals of the green and red chromogens. Cell segmentation was performed using watershed-based detection, allowing individual cell identification based on nuclear staining (haematoxylin) and morphology. Next, mRNA-signal spot detection within the cells was determined by configuring thresholds and subcellular spot size parameters (0.25–4 µm) for both chromogen channels. Cells were classified as positive if they exhibited at least one subcellular mRNA-signal, and the fraction of positive cells was calculated by dividing their number by the total number of cells within the focal infiltrate. The entire process was made reproducible through Groovy scripting and applied on all slides, enabling consistent analysis across datasets and facilitating downstream spatial mapping and standardised statistical evaluations.

Focal infiltrates including ectopic GC-like structures in the MSG cores of SjD patients were manually annotated as regions of interests in the software (online supplemental figure 2). Typically, multiple focal infiltrates were found within each MSG core, and the mean was calculated to obtain a representative percent score for each patient. Furthermore, to understand the role of glandular epithelial cells in both patients and non-SjD sicca control subjects, five ductal structures selected at random were annotated in all subjects. In samples with less than five ducts, all ducts were annotated. Ductal structures were included only when organised as clearly distinguishable, continuous arrangements. Randomly selected manual cell counts were performed to verify the detections.

Statistical analysis

For the statistical analysis, continuous variables were defined as non-normally distributed when Shapiro-Wilk test for normality was p<0.05. Whenever mean values were reported, the SD is indicated. For analyses conducted with the Wilcoxon Signed Rank test, median values and the corresponding effect size (z) were reported. Spearman’s correlation test was used for correlation analyses. Degree of correlation was interpreted according to the recommendation of the British Journal of Medicine (https://www.bmj.com/about-bmj/resources-readers/publications/statistics-square-one/11-correlation-and-regression) with r: 0.00–0.19 (very weak), 0.20–0.39 (weak), 0.40–0.59 (moderate), 0.60–0.79 (strong) and 0.80–1.00 (very strong). Statistical significance was defined when p<0.05. Analyses were performed using the IBM SPSS Statistics V.29.0.2.0 (20) software and figures were generated using Prism V.9 (GraphPad).

Results

One of the Tfh cell markers, IL-21R, positively correlates with B cell indicator BAFF within focal infiltrates of MSG from both male and female SjD patients

To date, comprehensive studies on SjD addressing the glandular microenvironment in male patients have been limited compared with their female counterparts, as they represent the leading patient group. To address this matter, a collective TMA-block (online supplemental figure 1) comprising MSG cores from 10 males and 10 females with a mean age of 51.35 years (±3.36) was designed together with MSG cores from age-and-sex matched non-SjD sicca controls (table 1). The mean FS for the male patients was 2.50 (±0.86) and 2.90 (±1.22) for the female patients. The duplex-chromogenic assay of the RNAscope technology allowed for the concurrent detections of B cell marker BAFF with the Tfh markers IL-21R (figure 1A,B), CXCL13 (figure 2) and CXCR5 (figure 3), respectively. All three markers resided mostly within the focal infiltrates, alongside BAFF. Spearman’s correlation test revealed a positive correlation between mRNA levels of BAFF and IL-21R (figure 1B), while no correlation was observed with CXCL13 or CXCR5. The percentage of IL-21R and CXCR5 positive cells, respectively, showed a modest increase within the focal infiltrates of male patients when compared with females without reaching statistical significance, while BAFF and CXCL13 expression patterns were largely similar in both sexes.

Ectopic GC-like structure formation is significantly associated with higher mRNA levels of Tfh cell markers IL-21R, CXCL13 and CXCR5

We next divided the patients into two groups depending on the absence or presence of GC-like structures, namely GC− (n=14) and GC+ (n=6). All analysed transcripts were present at significantly higher levels in patients with GC-like structures, with the exception of BAFF (figure 4A,B). In this context, IL-21R (p=0.003, figure 4C,D) and CXCL13 (p=0.002; figure 4E,F) were notably enhanced in the GC+-positive samples in contrast to patients lacking GC-like structures, and the same pattern was observed for CXCR5 (p=0.003, figure 4G,H). To further assess the spatial organisation of signals within the ectopic GC-like structures, we applied the Wilcoxon Signed Rank test to compare intrafollicular and extrafollicular distributions. Across all patients in the GC+ group, cells with CXCL13 transcripts were particularly enriched in the intrafollicular compartment relatively to the extrafollicular zone (z=−2.20; 50.1% vs 19.2%; p=0.028), and a similar pattern was observed for CXCR5 (z=−2.20; 36.1% vs 18.1%; p=0.028) and IL-21R (z=−2.20; 42.3% vs 34.6%; p=0.028). Meanwhile, the intrafollicular BAFF signals were relatively depleted compared with the extrafollicular zone (z=−2.20; 19.5% vs 27.1%; p=0.027). Collectively, these findings suggest a compartmentalisation of signalling profiles within the intrafollicular ectopic GC-like structures (online supplemental figure 3).

Distinctly elevated BAFF mRNA detected in salivary ductal cells may be linked to reduced salivary flow and disease progression

To analyse the potential interplay between ductal epithelial cells and surrounding tissue cells in the glandular microenvironment, we focused our analysis on salivary gland ducts. While ductal cell-specific CXCR5 and IL21R mRNA-signals were generally scarce or absent, the CXCL13 and BAFF double-staining revealed more prominent expression at these sites (figure 5A–D). When the number of BAFF-expressing ductal cells was compared with BAFF-positive cells inside the focal infiltrates, the relationship showed a moderately positive correlation (r=0.581, p=0.007). The same pattern was not observed for CXCL13. At the same time, no association was detected between the presence of ectopic GC-like structures and the number of BAFF or CXCL13 mRNA-positive ductal cells. To further explore potential associations between ductal cell expression and salivary gland flow, both patients and non-SjD controls were classified into two groups: (1) normal salivary secretion and (2) hyposalivation, that is, salivary flow of ≤1.5 mL/15 min. In this context, an inverse relationship was noted between the salivary flow rate and the BAFF-transcript (r=−0.347, p=0.038), where hyposalivation was particularly associated with elevated BAFF-positive ductal cells (p=0.031) (figure 5). Moreover, lower BAFF levels within the focal infiltrates were associated with higher atrophy scores in the patients (r=−0.739, p<0.001, data not shown).

Discussion

The integration of the RNAscope duplex-staining in the field of spatial biology has allowed us to appreciate the relationship between localisation and function of immune niches in the glandular compartment and the wide-ranging transcriptional activity of the resident cells in both female and male SjD patients.27 Our study has shown that ectopic GC-like structures represent the most enriched zones containing IL-21R, CXCL13 and CXCR5 mRNA transcripts.28 Collectively, these Tfh cell-associated markers revealed an explicit preference for the GC-like areas, especially the intrafollicular compartment, indicating that these sites could represent potential microniches of Tfh cell-residency and activity within MSGs of both female and male SjD patients. The predominance of BAFF mRNA observed in extrafollicular regions and ductal epithelial structures may reflect BAFF’s vital role in supporting the survival and maturation of naïve and transitional B cells, rather than intrafollicular GC-B cells.29 In this context, BAFF could be implicated in both developing and sustaining the broader infiltrating B cell compartment in the salivary glands of SjD patients, warranting its relevance and consideration in recent therapeutic strategies.2931

Comprehensive histopathological studies of the target organs focusing on male-specific disease patterns in SjD patients have been limited so far. Male-specific patterns are not only under-reported solely due to lack of patients but also limitations in biopsy collection. This is particularly relevant given that men often present with atypical symptoms, including more systemic involvement (eg, fatigue, arthralgia, neuropathy) and less prominent sicca symptoms, which may not consistently meet the clinical threshold for biopsy.15 32 33 It has also been implicated that men exhibit a more severe disease phenotype and an increased risk of lymphoma development, despite having a lower overall susceptibility to developing SjD.15 Interestingly, in a large Scandinavian study including 899 females and 68 male patients, the FS, GC-like structures and lymphoma subtypes demonstrated no significant sex-specific variation in frequency.32 Sexual dimorphism evidenced by increased sialadenitis severity, elevated CD4+ T helper cell infiltration and its associated cytokines (Th2 and Th17) in females have been reported in SjD animal models, while consistent sex-specific differences in focal infiltrates are lacking across human cohort studies.18 In the current age and gender-matched analysis, we demonstrate that the inflammatory severity of the MSG tissue may play a more critical role in shaping the heterogeneity of focal infiltrates in SjD patients, rather than their sex. Additional investigations involving a larger cohort will be necessary to elucidate this matter more fully.

Salivary gland epithelial cells lining the ductal structures in SjD may interact with Tfh cells and B cells to potentially facilitate antigen presentation, since salivary epithelial cells have previously been shown to act as antigen-presenting cells (APCs) in SjD, in addition to the professional APCs such as dendritic cells, macrophages and B cells.34 Considering these findings, ductal epithelial cells could represent drivers of hyposalivation in the SjD pathophysiology.35 Salivary ductal epithelial cells secrete multiple cytokines, among which the chemokines CXCL13, CXCL12 and chemokine (C-C motif) ligand 19 (CCL19) play a role in upholding the tertiary lymphoid niche.36 37 Given that epithelial-derived BAFF enhances the chemotactic responsiveness of B cells to CXCL13, we sought to examine their association in the salivary glands.3840 The measurements revealed an association between higher ductal cell-derived BAFF in patients and non-SjD control subjects with decreased salivary flow. Taken together, this emphasises potential immunopathological alterations in the ductal structures of MSGs that may contribute to advancing focal infiltrates and atrophic changes at later developmental stages of SjD. Given that the cellular interactions between epithelial cells and immune cells (including Tfh cells, B cells and plasma cells) in the glandular microenvironment promote lymphoid follicle formation,41 42 the ductal epithelial cells represent a diagnostic potential as a marker of disease activity,43 44 yet further investigations should be encouraged to shed light on this matter.

Conclusion

In summary, our findings provide novel insights into the composite landscape of the salivary glands of SjD by integrating spatial gene expression and immune architecture. RNAscope in situ hybridisation allows highly specific and replicable detections of markers targeted to characterise the Tfh cell and its associated signalling molecules, which were elevated in ectopic GC-like structures and corresponded to greater inflammatory disease severity. Importantly, this study demonstrates that ectopic GC-like structures represent a central, yet not restricted, niche for Tfh cell activity. Notably, interactions with adjacent ductal epithelial structures where significantly elevated intracellular BAFF signals were detected in individuals with hyposalivation may play a role in disease pathogenesis. Finally, additional functional studies are required to assess the role of ductal epithelial cells as a diagnostic marker of severe disease activity in SjD.

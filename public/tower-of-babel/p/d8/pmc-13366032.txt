IFPHPOS0015 ‘The Future Isn’t Bright for Women’: Women’s Experiences and Perceptions of Health Inequalities in the North of England
Authors: Olivia Engle
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWakefield in the United Kingdom has a female healthy life expectancy (HLE) rate that is 6.1 years less than the England average and has been declining since 2011. Health Equity North (2024) identified reported health status, long-term conditions, and the menopause as possible causes of inequalities between northern and southern women. This and similar work have predominantly relied on quantitative approaches. There is scant research that explores women’s perspectives on regional health inequalities or on female HLE.Methods15 life history interviews were conducted with women in Wakefield between the ages of 19 and 68 to understand experiences of health and perceptions of health inequalities. The interviews lasted between 45 minutes and 2 hours. Participants were asked to draw a ‘life map’ – representing people, places and things that have affected their health – which was used as a conversational tool in lieu of an interview schedule. Interview data was analysed using reflexive thematic analysis.ResultsThe study suggests that the greatest health burden for women in Wakefield may be their mental health. Women attempt to ‘carry on’ following adverse childhood experiences and trauma in their adulthood, which compounds with factors like poverty and racism. Regardless of age, several participants already felt in ill health.ConclusionsPrioritising women’s lived experience may help to understand current and worrying health trends. In Wakefield, and similar local authorities, investment is needed in culturally-competent, trauma-informed, and holistic place-based support to address women’s health.

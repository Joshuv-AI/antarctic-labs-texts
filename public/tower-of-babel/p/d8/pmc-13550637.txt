Circulating inflammatory, redox, and apoptosis-related alterations in drug-naive idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: an exploratory case–control study
Authors: İlknur Kaya, Beyza Kaya, Ayşe Koçak Sezgin, Meliha Koldemir Gündüz, Ümran Erbay
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a progressive fibrotic interstitial lung disease characterized by aberrant epithelial repair, oxidative stress, and chronic inflammatory activation. Although local pulmonary mechanisms have been extensively investigated, systemic molecular alterations in untreated patients remain incompletely understood. This study aimed to explore circulating inflammatory, redox, apoptosis-related, and repair-associated alterations in drug-naive IPF. In this prospective exploratory case–control study, 35 drug-naive IPF patients and 40 healthy controls were enrolled. IPF diagnosis was established according to multidisciplinary evaluation and current international diagnostic guidelines. Serum levels of IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-18 were measured by ELISA. Oxidative stress parameters, including total oxidant status (TOS), total antioxidant status (TAS), and superoxide dismutase (SOD), were evaluated using colorimetric assays. Expression levels of p53, EGFR, VEGF, BAX, BCL-2, and FGF were analyzed by quantitative real-time PCR. Statistical analyses were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Drug-naive IPF patients exhibited significantly elevated circulating IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-18 levels compared with healthy controls (all p < 0.05), indicating systemic inflammatory activation. Oxidative stress analysis demonstrated significantly increased TOS levels together with reduced TAS and SOD activity (all p < 0.05), consistent with systemic redox imbalance. Gene expression analysis revealed significantly increased p53 and VEGF expression, whereas EGFR expression was significantly decreased in the IPF group (p < 0.05). No significant differences were observed for BAX, BCL-2, or FGF expression. Collectively, these findings suggest the presence of coordinated inflammatory, oxidative, and stress-response alterations in untreated IPF. Drug-naive IPF was associated with detectable systemic inflammatory, redox, and apoptosis-related alterations in peripheral blood samples. These findings support the concept that circulating biomarker changes may accompany early IPF pathobiology beyond the local lung environment. However, given the exploratory and peripheral blood-based design of the study, further tissue-level and longitudinal investigations are required to clarify the mechanistic and clinical significance of these observations.

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive, and ultimately fatal interstitial lung disease of unknown etiology, characterized histopathologically and radiologically by the usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern1. The disease is marked by irreversible fibrosis of the lung parenchyma, leading to progressive decline in pulmonary function, worsening dyspnea, and poor clinical outcomes, particularly in elderly individuals2. Despite advances in antifibrotic therapies, IPF remains associated with a dismal prognosis, with a median survival of approximately 3–5 years after diagnosis3. Epidemiologically, IPF is considered a rare disease, with reported incidence rates ranging from 0.09 to 1.30 per 10,000 individuals and prevalence between 0.33 and 4.51 per 10,000 worldwide4,5.

Pulmonary function testing in IPF patients typically demonstrates a restrictive pattern, characterized by reduced forced vital capacity (FVC), total lung capacity (TLC), and diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO)6. However, in early stages of the disease, lung function parameters may remain within normal or near-normal ranges, limiting their sensitivity in detecting early pathological changes7. Consequently, composite indices such as the Gender-Age-Physiology (GAP) index have been developed to better predict disease progression and mortality risk8–10.

Radiologically, IPF is defined by the UIP pattern observed on high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT), which is typically characterized by subpleural and basal predominance, reticular abnormalities, traction bronchiectasis, and honeycombing1.

Nevertheless, accumulating evidence indicates that inflammation and immune dysregulation still contribute to disease initiation and progression. Pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) play a central role in regulating cell proliferation, survival, and immune responses, and have been implicated in fibrotic and chronic inflammatory diseases11. Similarly, interleukin-1β (IL-1β) has been shown to promote fibrotic remodeling and amplify pro-inflammatory signaling cascades in IPF12,13.

Oxidative stress is another critical component of IPF pathophysiology. Reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation contributes to epithelial injury, fibroblast activation, and extracellular matrix deposition. Superoxide dismutase (SOD), a key antioxidant enzyme, plays a major role in neutralizing ROS. However, reduced SOD expression in fibrotic regions of IPF lungs suggests an imbalance between oxidant production and antioxidant defense mechanisms, leading to enhanced oxidative damage14,15. Systemic markers such as total oxidant status (TOS) and total antioxidant status (TAS) further reflect the global redox balance and are influenced by various pathological and environmental factors, including chronic disease and aging16,17.

In addition to inflammation and oxidative stress, apoptosis and angiogenesis-related pathways are critically involved in IPF progression. The balance between pro-apoptotic and anti-apoptotic proteins, particularly within the Bcl-2 family, determines cell fate. Bax and p53 promote apoptosis, whereas Bcl-2 inhibits it; thus, the Bax/Bcl-2 ratio is a key determinant of apoptotic susceptibility18. Furthermore, angiogenic factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and fibroblast growth factor (FGF) are known to regulate endothelial cell proliferation and vascular remodeling, and their dysregulation has been linked to multiple pulmonary pathologies, including fibrosis19. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), a member of the ErbB receptor family, has also been implicated in fibrotic processes, with increased expression reported in fibroblastic foci of IPF lungs20.

Despite these advances, a critical gap remains in understanding whether these molecular alterations are confined to the lung microenvironment or already present at the systemic level, particularly in untreated patients. Identifying early systemic signatures may provide novel insights into disease initiation and offer potential biomarkers for early diagnosis and therapeutic targeting.

Based on this rationale, the present study aimed to explore whether untreated IPF patients exhibit detectable systemic alterations involving inflammatory, redox, apoptosis-related, and repair-associated pathways. To address this question, circulating inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, IL-1β, IL-18), oxidative stress parameters (TAS, TOS, SOD), and selected apoptosis- and angiogenesis-related mediators (p53, EGFR, VEGF, FGF) were evaluated in peripheral blood samples from drug-naive IPF patients and healthy controls. Rather than establishing direct mechanistic relationships, the study was designed to identify potential systemic biomarker patterns associated with untreated IPF.

Materials and methods

Ethics approval

The study protocol was approved by the Kütahya Health Sciences University Faculty of Medicine Non-Interventional Ethics Committee (approval no. 2026/04-47, 26 March 2026). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion in the study.

Study design

This study was designed as a prospective case–control investigation evaluating systemic molecular alterations in patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). Diagnosis of IPF was established through multidisciplinary evaluation by experienced pulmonologists according to the 2022 ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT Clinical Practice Guideline for Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis and Progressive Pulmonary Fibrosis. High-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) findings demonstrating a usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern were evaluated in accordance with these guideline-defined diagnostic criteria21.

Patients with confirmed IPF who attended routine outpatient visits at the Department of Chest Diseases, Kütahya Health Sciences University Faculty of Medicine (Kütahya, Türkiye), were invited to participate. All participants provided written informed consent prior to inclusion, and peripheral blood samples were collected under standardized conditions.

Sample size calculation

Sample size was determined using G*Power software (version 3.1; Heinrich Heine University, Düsseldorf, Germany). Based on an unpaired two-tailed t-test, a medium effect size (d = 0.5), α = 0.05, and statistical power (1 − β) = 0.80 were assumed. The analysis indicated a minimum of 30 participants per group.

Accordingly, 35 patients with IPF and 40 healthy controls were included in the study. The slight imbalance between groups was due to participant availability; however, the overall sample size exceeded the minimum requirement and ensured adequate statistical power.

Inclusion criteria

The IPF group included individuals: aged > 40 years, with a confirmed radiological and/or histopathological diagnosis of IPF, without a history of drug-induced pulmonary fibrosis, without prior lung infections leading to fibrotic changes.

The control group consisted of comparative healthy control subjects without known chronic inflammatory or fibrotic diseases.

Exclusion criteria

Participants were excluded if they: Declined to provide informed consent, required long-term oxygen therapy, had a history of lung infection associated with fibrosis.

Sample collection and storage

Peripheral blood samples (1 mL) were collected from all participants under sterile conditions during outpatient visits. Samples were processed immediately and stored at − 80 °C until further analysis.

Laboratory procedures and blinding

All laboratory analyses, including ELISA and quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), were performed under blinded conditions. Investigators conducting the experiments were unaware of the group allocation of the samples to minimize bias and ensure objective data acquisition.

Reverse transcription and quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was isolated from whole blood samples using the RNeasy Micro Kit (Qiagen, Inc.) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA concentration and purity were assessed spectrophotometrically. Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using the QuantiTect Reverse Transcription Kit (Qiagen, Inc.) in a final reaction volume of 20 µL and stored at − 20 °C until further analysis.

Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) was performed using RT2 SYBR® Green qPCR Mastermix (Qiagen, Inc.) on a StepOnePlus™ Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems; Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.). Amplification was carried out under standard conditions consisting of an initial denaturation step at 95 °C for 10 min, followed by 40 cycles of denaturation at 95 °C for 15 s and annealing/extension at 60 °C for 30 s. A melt curve analysis was performed at the end of each run to confirm amplification specificity and product quality.

Gene expression levels of Bcl-2, Bax, p53, EGFR, VEGF, and FGF were analyzed, and all data were normalized to β-actin (ACTB) as the internal control. Relative gene expression levels were calculated using the 2^−ΔΔCt method by normalizing target gene Ct values to ACTB and comparing IPF samples to controls. Fold changes in gene expression were expressed as 2^−ΔΔCt values.

Measurement of inflammatory markers by ELISA

Serum levels of inflammatory cytokines, including interleukin-1β (IL-1β), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and interleukin-18 (IL-18) were quantified using commercially available ELISA kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions. The following kits were used: Human IL-1β ELISA kit (cat. No: E0143Hu; BT-Lab), Human IL-6 ELISA kit (cat. no. E0090Hu; BT-Lab), Human IL-18 ELISA kit (cat. no. E0147Hu; BT-Lab).

Briefly, serum samples and standards were added to pre-coated microplates and incubated with specific detection antibodies. Following washing steps, substrate solution was added, and the reaction was terminated according to the protocol. Optical density was measured at 450 nm using a Multiskan™ Plate Reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.), and cytokine concentrations were calculated from standard curves.

Measurement of oxidative stress parameters

Serum levels of total antioxidant status (TAS) (cat. no. E-BC-K801-M; Elabscience), total oxidant status (TOS) (cat. no. E-BC-K802-M; Elabscience), and superoxide dismutase (SOD) (cat. no. E-BC-K020-M; Elabscience) were measured using commercially available colorimetric assay kits according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

TAS levels were determined based on the ability of antioxidants present in the sample to suppress the formation of the ABTS radical cation, while TOS levels were measured via the oxidation of ferrous ion to ferric ion in the presence of oxidant molecules. SOD activity was assessed using a spectrophotometric method based on the inhibition of superoxide-induced reactions.

All measurements were performed using a Multiskan™ Plate Reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc.), and absorbance values were recorded at the appropriate wavelengths. Concentrations were calculated according to standard curves provided by the assay kits.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism software (version 10; Dotmatics). Data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk normality test. As most variables did not follow a normal distribution, non-parametric tests were applied.

Comparisons between two groups were performed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). All analyses were two-tailed, and a P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Although non-parametric analyses were applied, quantitative data are presented as mean ± SEM for descriptive visualization consistency together with individual distribution plots.

Results

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the study population are summarized in Table 1. The mean age was significantly higher in the IPF group compared with healthy controls (67.9 vs. 61.7 years, p < 0.001). Sex distribution demonstrated a predominance of male participants in both groups, with 32 males and 3 females in the IPF group and 30 males and 10 females in the control group. Smoking pack-year history was comparable between groups and did not differ significantly (39.76 vs. 40.42 pack-years, p = 0.18) (Table 1).

Pulmonary function parameters were significantly impaired in IPF patients. Forced vital capacity (FVC%) was significantly lower in the IPF group compared with controls (71.16% vs. 85.25%, p < 0.001). Similarly, diffusing capacity for carbon monoxide (DLCO%) was markedly reduced in IPF patients (50.84% vs. 75.75%, p < 0.001). In addition, GAP stage scores were significantly higher in the IPF group (3.90 vs. 2.025, p < 0.001), indicating greater disease severity. No significant difference was observed regarding comorbidity burden between the groups (27 vs. 30, p = 0.22) (Table 1).

Apoptosis- and repair-associated gene expression profile in drug-naive IPF

Gene expression analysis revealed selective alterations in apoptosis- and repair-associated markers in peripheral blood samples from drug-naive IPF patients compared with healthy controls. Among apoptosis-related genes, p53 expression was significantly increased in the IPF group compared with controls (1.90-fold vs. 1.00-fold, p < 0.001), suggesting activation of cellular stress-response signaling. In contrast, BCL-2 and BAX expression levels did not differ significantly between groups (BCL-2: 1.00-fold vs. 1.00-fold, p = 0.06; BAX: 1.00-fold vs. 1.00-fold, p = 0.27) (Fig. 1).

Regarding repair- and angiogenesis-associated genes, EGFR expression was significantly reduced in drug-naive IPF patients compared with controls (0.45-fold vs. 1.00-fold, p < 0.001). Conversely, VEGF expression was significantly increased in the IPF group (1.90-fold vs. 1.00-fold, p < 0.001), indicating dysregulated angiogenic signaling. FGF expression showed a modest increase in IPF patients, but this difference did not reach statistical significance (1.34-fold vs. 1.00-fold, p = 0.14).

Overall, these findings indicate a selective circulating gene expression pattern in drug-naive IPF, characterized by increased p53 and VEGF expression together with decreased EGFR expression, while BCL-2, BAX, and FGF remained statistically unchanged (Fig. 1).

Circulating inflammatory cytokines are increased in drug-naive IPF

Serum inflammatory cytokine analysis demonstrated significantly increased circulating pro-inflammatory markers in drug-naive IPF patients compared with healthy controls. IL-6 levels were significantly elevated in the IPF group (98.34 ± 12.57 vs. 65.10 ± 5.14, p = 0.04). Similarly, IL-1β levels were significantly higher in IPF patients compared with controls (2571 ± 325 vs. 1947 ± 342, p = 0.04) (Fig. 2).

Notably, IL-18 showed the most pronounced inflammatory difference, with significantly higher serum levels in drug-naive IPF patients than in healthy controls (417.5 ± 45.32 vs. 242 ± 12.55, p < 0.001). These findings support the presence of systemic inflammatory activation in untreated IPF, including increased inflammasome-associated cytokines IL-1β and IL-18 (Fig. 2).

Systemic oxidative stress parameters indicate redox imbalance in drug-naive IPF

Oxidative stress analysis revealed a marked systemic redox imbalance in drug-naive IPF patients. Total antioxidant status (TAS) was significantly lower in the IPF group compared with healthy controls (73.85 ± 4.50 vs. 93.96 ± 6.04, p = 0.01), indicating reduced systemic antioxidant capacity.

In contrast, total oxidant status (TOS) was significantly increased in drug-naive IPF patients (169.8 ± 21.33 vs. 110.8 ± 4.12, p = 0.02), reflecting elevated systemic oxidant burden. In parallel, SOD activity was significantly decreased in the IPF group compared with controls (20.07 ± 4.24 vs. 26.02 ± 5.89, p < 0.001) (Fig. 3).

Exploratory multiple linear regression analyses were additionally performed for selected representative biomarkers reflecting inflammatory, oxidative stress, and apoptosis-related pathways, specifically IL-18, TOS, and p53, in order to evaluate the potential influence of demographic and clinical confounding variables. The association between IPF status and circulating IL-18 levels remained statistically significant after adjustment for smoking exposure (β = 202.7, 95% CI: 74.96–330.5, p = 0.0045). Similarly, p53 expression remained significantly associated with IPF status after adjustment for smoking exposure, age, and sex (β = 1.382, 95% CI: 0.8039–1.960, p < 0.001). In contrast, the adjusted association between IPF status and TOS levels did not remain statistically significant after covariate adjustment (p = 0.1307).

Discussion

The present study suggests that drug-naive idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) may be associated with detectable circulating inflammatory, oxidative stress, and apoptosis-related alterations.Although the study design does not permit mechanistic or causal inference, the findings indicate that untreated IPF patients may exhibit a coordinated circulating biomarker profile extending beyond the local pulmonary microenvironment.

A key observation of this study is the presence of a systemic pro-inflammatory profile, as evidenced by significantly elevated circulating levels of IL-6, IL-1β, and IL-18. Although IPF has traditionally been considered a primarily fibrotic disease, increasing evidence indicates that innate immune activation contributes to disease initiation and progression22. In particular, IL-1β and IL-18 are canonical products of inflammasome activation, and their elevation suggests that inflammasome-associated inflammatory signaling may be detectable in untreated IPF patients23. Previous studies have shown that IL-1 signaling promotes fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix deposition24, while IL-18 has been implicated in chronic inflammatory and fibrotic responses. Our findings extend these observations by demonstrating that inflammasome-associated cytokines are elevated at the systemic level in drug-naive patients, suggesting that innate immune activation is not restricted to the lung microenvironment.

In addition to inflammation, our results reveal a marked systemic redox imbalance, characterized by increased TOS and decreased TAS and SOD levels. Oxidative stress is a well-established driver of IPF pathogenesis, contributing to epithelial injury, fibroblast activation, and tissue remodeling25,26. Excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), particularly via NADPH oxidase systems, has been shown to promote fibrotic signaling and myofibroblast differentiation. Importantly, antioxidant defense mechanisms, including SOD activity, are often impaired in fibrotic lung tissue27. Our findings expand this concept by demonstrating that redox dysregulation is detectable systemically, reinforcing the idea that oxidative stress-related alterations may represent an important component of early IPF-associated systemic changes. However, since the adjusted association for TOS did not remain statistically significant after covariate adjustment, these observations should be interpreted cautiously and may be influenced by demographic and clinical variability.

At the level of apoptosis and growth factor signaling, we observed a selective but mechanistically significant pattern. Expression of p53 was significantly increased, indicating activation of stress-induced apoptotic pathways. Consistent with this, previous studies have shown that epithelial cell apoptosis is a central event in IPF pathogenesis28. In contrast, EGFR expression was significantly decreased, suggesting impaired epithelial repair and proliferative signaling. EGFR-mediated pathways are known to play a critical role in tissue regeneration and epithelial integrity, and their disruption may contribute to defective repair responses in IPF29. Interestingly, VEGF expression was increased, indicating activation of angiogenic pathways. While VEGF has been reported to exhibit both protective and pathological roles in IPF, its dysregulation is generally associated with abnormal vascular remodeling and disease progression8.

The coexistence of increased p53 and VEGF expression alongside reduced EGFR levels suggests a maladaptive repair phenotype, in which apoptotic signaling is enhanced while normal regenerative processes are impaired. This supports the concept that IPF is driven not by excessive repair, but by failed and dysregulated tissue regeneration, consistent with current pathogenic models6,30.

Importantly, the present findings should be interpreted within the context of a peripheral blood biomarker study. Since analyses were not performed in lung tissue, bronchoalveolar lavage samples, or isolated pulmonary cell populations, the study cannot identify the precise cellular origin of the observed molecular alterations. Therefore, the proposed relationship between inflammasome-associated cytokines, oxidative imbalance, and maladaptive repair signaling should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than mechanistically proven. Future studies incorporating tissue-level and cell-specific analyses will be necessary to clarify these interactions in IPF pathogenesis.

Taken together, the present findings suggest that drug-naive IPF may be associated with concurrent circulating inflammatory and apoptosis-related alterations, whereas oxidative stress-related findings appeared more sensitive to demographic and clinical variability after covariate adjustment. However, given the exploratory peripheral blood-based design of the study, these observations should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating associations rather than direct mechanistic evidence. Within this framework, inflammasome activation, redox imbalance, and impaired epithelial repair appear to be interconnected processes that collectively contribute to disease initiation and progression. This integrated perspective aligns with the emerging view of IPF as a disease of maladaptive repair and persistent epithelial injury, rather than purely fibrotic remodeling.

The present study has several limitations. First, the sample size, although adequately powered for exploratory analysis, remains relatively modest. Second, all molecular analyses were performed using peripheral blood samples rather than lung tissue or bronchoalveolar lavage material; therefore, the findings may not directly reflect local pulmonary microenvironmental processes. Third, the cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and does not allow assessment of temporal changes or prognostic significance. In addition, potential confounding factors such as smoking exposure, disease severity, and pulmonary function parameters may influence circulating biomarker levels. Although the inclusion of drug-naive patients reduced treatment-related confounding, future larger-scale and longitudinal studies incorporating tissue validation and adjusted analyses will be necessary to confirm the biological and clinical relevance of these findings. Since the present study was designed as an exploratory biomarker investigation, formal multiplicity correction was not performed in order to avoid excessive type II error risk in a relatively modest cohort. Nevertheless, this approach increases the possibility of false-positive findings, and therefore the observed associations should be considered preliminary until validated in larger independent studies.

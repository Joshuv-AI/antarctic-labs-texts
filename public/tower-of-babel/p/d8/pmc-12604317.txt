Trust in the healthcare system as a predictor of patient satisfaction: a cross-sectional survey in Türkiye’s primary care setting
Authors: İbrahim Topuz, Mustafa Nal, Veli Durmuş, Gülfer Bektaş
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatient satisfaction is a fundamental indicator of healthcare quality. However, while structural factors affecting satisfaction have been extensively studied in the literature, perceptions of the service process and the communication dimension have been relatively neglected. It offers concrete suggestions for developing strategies to increase trust in the health system, improve patient satisfaction, and achieve sustainable quality in health services. This study aims to examine the relationship between trust in the healthcare system and the level of patient satisfaction.MethodsThis study used a descriptive cross-sectional design. Data were collected in Türkiye between March and August 2023 through an online survey using the snowball sampling method, involving 1010 individuals utilizing family medicine services. Validated instruments included: (1) a Descriptive Characteristics Form covering gender, education, and employment; (2) the Trust in the Health Care System Scale; and (3) a Patient Satisfaction Scale.ResultsTrust correlated strongly with satisfaction (r = 0.715, p < 0.001). Regression analysis showed trust sub-dimensions significantly predicted satisfaction (R²=0.512), with marital and employment status also influencing outcomes (p < 0.05). Trust in the healthcare system strongly predicted patient satisfaction (p < 0.001) and explained significant variance (Nagelkerke R²=0.512).ConclusionThis study found that patient satisfaction is significantly influenced by individual characteristics, service quality, and trust in the healthcare system. These findings suggest that policies to enhance patient satisfaction should adopt comprehensive strategies that reinforce public trust in all aspects of the healthcare system. Policies should not only focus on service delivery to enhance patient satisfaction, but also adopt comprehensive strategies that strengthen trust in the healthcare system. Policies aimed at increasing trust should incorporate multidimensional strategies, including transparency, accountability, effective communication, and the sustainability of service quality. These approaches will not only improve current satisfaction levels but also strengthen long-term social commitment to the healthcare system.

Background

Patient satisfaction is an important determinant of equality in health services, continuity of service, compliance with treatment, general health outcomes and quality level [1, 2]. Healthcare services, which directly affect the well-being and life outcomes of individuals, are an area where perfect service quality, unlike many other sectors, is becoming more and more imperative every day [3]. Patient satisfaction is linked to the effective provision of health services, and the increased demand for quality services has increased patients’ willingness to pay more [4]. Patient satisfaction is an important indicator of healthcare performance as it reflects the level of service compliance and utilization, and has become a priority target of global healthcare systems [5]. For these reasons, it has become important to determine the factors affecting patient satisfaction.

This study assumed that trust in the healthcare system is an important determinant of patient satisfaction. Patient confidence has a significant impact on patient attitudes towards treatment adherence [6]. Trust in the health system refers to the sum of trust in health care providers (e.g., physicians, nurses), health care institutions (hospitals and clinics), and health care payers (managed care plans, private insurers, government insurance) [7]. The primary goal of the health system is to ensure the provision of medical care of the highest quality and safety [8]. In this context, patient safety has emerged as a top concern and is now at the forefront of priorities [9, 10]. Research shows that high patient satisfaction promotes collaborative treatment [11], and encourages patients to refer others to these healthcare providers [12]. In Türkiye, family physicians are paid based on performance. One of the performance indicators is the number of people registered with a family physician [13]. For this reason, it is economically important for family physicians to be preferred by patients.

In this study, patient satisfaction was evaluated within the theoretical framework of the Donabedian model. This model addresses the quality of healthcare services in three fundamental dimensions: structure (institutional and physical resources), process (service delivery methods), and outcome (the effects of the service on the patient) [14]. The structure (institutional and physical resources) and process (service delivery method) dimensions of the Donabedian model were evaluated using the Multidimensional Health System Trust Scale. The outcome dimension was measured using patient satisfaction levels as an indicator reflecting the effects of the service on patients (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Donabedian conceptual model

Previous studies have identified trust in physicians [15], access to healthcare facilities [16], and perceptions of the health benefits of telemedicine [17]. However, there is a lack of studies in the current literature that thoroughly examine how patients’ trust in the health system affects their satisfaction when applying to family health centers. The theoretical contribution of this study is to fill this gap in the literature, to address the impact of trust in the healthcare system on patient satisfaction more comprehensively, and to deepen and develop the existing theoretical framework regarding this relationship.

The aim of this study is to determine the effects of socio-demographic characteristics and trust in the health system on patient satisfaction. Additionally, it offers concrete suggestions for developing strategies to increase trust in the health system, improve patient satisfaction, and achieve sustainable quality in health services.

Methods

Study design and settings

This study employs a descriptive cross-sectional design to assess the perspectives of individuals regarding healthcare system trust and patient satisfaction in Türkiye. The target population consists of all individuals aged 18 years and above residing within the country’s borders as of 2023. Given the study’s multi-center nature, data were collected across various regions of Türkiye between March and August 2023.

Given the inclusive approach of the study, snowball sampling was employed, and the study sample comprised all eligible individuals who met the inclusion criteria and agreed to participate voluntarily. Inclusion criteria were: (1) being 18 years or older, (2) having utilized family health services within the past year, (3) being a Turkish-speaking resident of Türkiye, and (4) providing informed consent to participate. Exclusion criteria included (1) non-Turkish-speaking individuals and (2) those who declined participation.

Data collection

Data were collected via Google Forms, incorporating the following validated instruments: (1) a Descriptive Characteristics Form capturing variables such as age, gender, education level, and employment status; (2) the Trust in the Health Care System Scale, consisting of 17 items, adapted into Turkish by Dinç et al. scale developed by Egede and Ellis; and (3) Patient satisfaction scale with 29 items developed by Kaya [7, 18]– [19]. Both the trust and satisfaction scales were assessed using a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The online survey form prepared as part of the research was sent to participants via WhatsApp. A total of 14 incomplete responses were identified and excluded, resulting in 1010 participant responses included in the final analysis.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were analyzed using both descriptive and inferential statistical methods to assess levels of trust in the healthcare system and patient satisfaction. Categorical variables, such as gender, education level, marital status, were summarized using frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables, such as trust and satisfaction scores, were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Group comparisons between two independent samples (e.g., gender differences in trust scores) were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test, and comparisons among three or more independent groups (e.g., differences by education level or income status) were conducted using the Kruskal-Wallis test. Spearman’s rank correlation was conducted to examine the strength and direction of relationships between explanatory variables.

Multiple linear regression was conducted, treating the 5-point Likert scale as continuous, to assess how selected variables predicted trust in the healthcare system. Substantial evidence supports the use of parametric methods with ordinal data from Likert scales of ≥ 5 points [20–22]. Given that the data distribution was not severely skewed, parametric tests were considered appropriate, as they have been shown to yield robust and largely unbiased estimates even when normality assumptions are violated [21].

Three multivariate models were constructed: Model 1 included sociodemographic variables (gender, age, education, and employment status); Model 2 expanded on Model 1 by adding participants’ trust in the healthcare system and its subdimensions; and Model 3 incorporated all variables from the first two models. The predictive strength of models was assessed using the Nagelkerke R-square. Moreover, Potential multicollinearity was addressed before analysis. Robustness checks included split-sample validation to assess the stability of effects and residual diagnostics (e.g., Q–Q plots) to confirm normality assumptions. Findings were consistent with Norman’s, (2010) guidelines [22] regarding the use of Likert-scale data in parametric analyses. The data were analyzed based on the structure, process, and outcome dimensions of the Donabedian model. The structure and process dimensions were represented by the scale of trust in the healthcare system, while the outcome dimension was represented by patient satisfaction. Mann-Whitney U, Kruskal-Wallis, and Spearman correlation were used to examine differences between groups and relationships between variables. Multiple regression models were used to evaluate the effect of structure and process variables on outcomes. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 27 (Chicago, IL, USA), with the significance level set at p < 0.05. The internal consistency of the scales was confirmed by recalculating Cronbach’s α coefficients within the study sample.

Ethical considerations

The study was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of the University. The consent that was obtained from all of the participants. The study was conducted in full accordance with the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. All data analyzed were fully anonymized to ensure participant confidentiality.

Results

Among the 1010 participants, the majority were female (72.5%) with a mean age of 25.10 years (SD = 9.39). Educational attainment was predominantly secondary (82.6%), while smaller proportions had primary (9.3%) or tertiary education (8.1%). Most participants were single (80.9%) and preferred government hospitals (89.9%) over private ones. Employment data revealed that 62.9% of participants were unemployed, with nearly equal distributions between government (18.6%) and private sector employees (18.5%). Additionally, a significant majority (91.4%) were covered by government-funded insurance, compared to 8.6% with private insurance (Table 1).

Discussion

This study revealed that patient satisfaction is strongly associated with individual characteristics and service quality as well as trust in the health system. Regression analyses show that these relationships are multilayered, with significant effects of some demographic factors such as marital status and employment. The lower satisfaction levels of married individuals may be attributed to the fact that their expectations also include family members. These multifaceted expectations may negatively influence overall satisfaction levels [23]. Married individuals can evaluate health personnel communication in terms of interaction with both themselves and their family members. A negative experience reported by any family member can diminish the overall level of satisfaction [24]. Conversely, a multicenter study conducted in China found that married patients reported higher satisfaction regarding nurse–patient relationships [25]. Research findings have revealed that married individuals are less satisfied with healthcare services than single individuals. This situation may be related to the existence of a structure that does not sufficiently align with the individual’s family responsibilities and social role in the provision of healthcare services. Therefore, it is recommended that healthcare policies be restructured with a holistic approach that takes into account not only the individual but also family dynamics. The findings reveal that healthcare policies need to be restructured with a holistic approach that considers not only individual needs but also family dynamics. The dissatisfaction of married individuals may stem from a service structure that is incompatible with family responsibilities. Therefore, policymakers are advised to prioritize family-centered service design.

High satisfaction among working individuals can be attributed to financial security and regular insurance coverage, which provides easy access to health services. This finding aligns with the study by Goula et al., which reported that employed individuals expressed significantly higher levels of satisfaction with healthcare services [26]. In contrast, a study conducted in Germany found that working individuals reported lower patient satisfaction [27]. Discrepancies between the satisfaction status of studies may be due to differences in population, survey coverage, assessment scales, timing, and cultural factors between countries [28]. Trust in the health system, professionals, payment system, and organization plays a key role in determining patient satisfaction and is consistent with previous findings [11, 29]. The findings of the study show that unemployed individuals have low levels of satisfaction with health services. This situation reveals that structural factors such as social security, health literacy, access to information, and access to services are decisive in determining satisfaction. Policy makers should take measures to facilitate access to healthcare services for unemployed individuals; in particular, they should develop inclusive social policies in areas, such as healthcare information, guidance services, transportation support, and access to social security. Additionally, it is recommended to establish a separate feedback and monitoring mechanism to directly track the expectations and needs of unemployed individuals. These findings necessitate the development of social security, information, and referral policies that will facilitate access to healthcare services for unemployed individuals. Additionally, a separate feedback and monitoring mechanism is recommended to track the specific needs of this group.

While trust in health professionals increases treatment participation and adherence, trust in the organization and payment system affects the perception of fairness and sustainability [30–32]. A reliable, accessible, and well-organized health system positively affects both employee performance and patient experience, increasing satisfaction [33]. Individuals may be satisfied with the physician or emergency care, but dissatisfied with other aspects of the system, such as insurance, waiting times, or infrastructure [34]. This situation demonstrates that to increase patient satisfaction, it is necessary not only to focus on healthcare professionals but also to strengthen confidence in the organizational structure and financial sustainability of the healthcare system. Policies must be approached in a multidimensional manner that will increase confidence in the system as a whole.

Analyzing demographic variables and the sub-dimensions of trust offers a robust framework for assessing patient satisfaction. Education level and marital status have been found to be particularly effective indicators. This is likely because educated individuals are better able to comprehend the healthcare system, which allows them to form more rational expectations. Similar findings are evident in the results of the conducted by the Ministry of Health of Türkiye [35]. This study found that as education levels increase, so does per capita health expenditure, which correlates with higher satisfaction with health services. Additionally, health professionals who communicate better with patients can increase satisfaction levels [36]. According to the findings of Model 1, among the sociodemographic variables examined, only marital status and employment status were significant predictors of patient satisfaction. In Model 2, the subdimensions of trust were strong predictors, accounting for 51.2% of the variance in patient satisfaction. Previous studies in Türkiye have also shown the effect of demographic variables, such as education, marital status, and employment, on patient satisfaction. For instance, a study in family medicine found that education level, marital status, and income were all associated with patient satisfaction [37]. Additionally, the quality of communication between nurses and patients and the quality of care are important factors that enhance both trust and satisfaction [30]. Policy makers should develop programs aimed at improving educational levels and communication quality to increase patient satisfaction; they should also implement inclusive health policies that take into account demographic differences, such as marital status and employment.

Model 3, which considers sociodemographic factors and trust components together, supports previous studies emphasizing the impact of patient education, trust, and sociodemographic factors on patient satisfaction. For example, a study in Türkiye found that a hospital information system intervention that supported nurses’ education led to a significant increase in patient satisfaction [38]. Furthermore, studies show that patient-physician communication has a decisive effect on satisfaction and can be improved through education programs [39]. These findings indicate that training programs to enhance healthcare professionals’ communication skills should be incorporated into healthcare policies to improve patient satisfaction. Furthermore, considering that the effective use of hospital information systems can improve patient education processes and increase satisfaction, it is recommended that such investments in digital infrastructure be encouraged.

The sense of trust enhances the perceived value of services by influencing the quality of patients’ relationships with healthcare professionals, thereby fostering positive behavioral intentions, such as service repurchase or recommendation to others [29]. Consequently, when developing strategies to enhance patient satisfaction, it is crucial to focus not only on service quality but also on how this quality contributes to trust-building among patients. In this context, healthcare administrators should prioritize trust-enhancing practices, including transparent communication, ethical service delivery, and patient-centered approaches, as these will effectively improve patient satisfaction levels [40]. Therefore, future studies investigating trust in healthcare systems are advised to examine healthcare administrators’ practices aimed at enhancing patient satisfaction. Additionally, this study focused on participants’ trust and satisfaction levels with primary healthcare services. Consequently, future research should also explore trust and satisfaction levels concerning secondary and tertiary healthcare institutions. These higher levels of care typically involve more costly and resource-intensive services, which may raise expectations for service quality and make patient trust more vulnerable [41]. Thus, measuring patient experiences at these levels is critically important for improving healthcare policies. Recent studies have shown that trust plays a critical role in enhancing patient satisfaction [29, 42–44]. The findings indicate that trust should be considered within a holistic framework, not only encompassing healthcare professionals but also healthcare institutions and payment systems. Moreover, in Türkiye, the changing levels of trust in public and private healthcare services over time emerge as a dynamic directly linked to the direction of healthcare policies [45, 46].

In this study, the proportion of female participants was significantly higher than that of male participants. This may be related to the fact that the survey was distributed via social media platforms. Research shows that females spend more time on social media than male [47–49]. In this study, the disproportionate representation of young female participants in the sample is identified as one of the research limitations. This limitation may significantly weaken the generalizability of the results to the general population due to the disproportionate concentration of female participants in the sample, because the findings may be based solely on this weighted group, as the perceptions and satisfaction levels of men or other demographic subgroups may not be adequately represented.

The data were collected using a self-report questionnaire method. This may lead to respondents providing answers in line with social desirability bias, which could result in their true attitudes not being fully reflected. Since the study employed a cross-sectional design, it is unable to establish a causal relationship between trust in the healthcare system and patient satisfaction. Additionally, participants’ limited knowledge about the functioning of the healthcare system may lead to biased judgments when evaluating systemic satisfaction and trust levels. Females’ perceptions and expressions regarding healthcare services may differ from those of men.

A large-scale Hospital Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems (HCAHPS) study conducted by Elliott and colleagues in the US found that females generally rated hospital services more negatively than males. Females gave lower scores, particularly in areas such as “communication about medications,” “discharge information,” and “cleanliness” [50]. This situation shows that females may have different expectations regarding healthcare services and may express their satisfaction more critically. Studies conducted in emergency departments show that female patients tend to prefer female physicians and express greater satisfaction with them [51]. This may indicate that the prevalence of female participants in the sample reflects a particular perception regarding the relationship between overall satisfaction levels and the physician’s gender. An OECD study found that in many countries, males’ trust in the healthcare system is higher than females [52].

The current research establishes that trust in healthcare professionals, service-providing institutions, and payment systems has statistically significant positive effects on satisfaction levels. Moreover, demographic factors including educational attainment, marital status, and employment status were found to significantly influence these relationships. In light of these findings, policies aimed at enhancing patient satisfaction should not merely focus on the delivery of services but must also adopt comprehensive strategies that reinforce trust in the healthcare system.

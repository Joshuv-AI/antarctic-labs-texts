Endogenous near-infrared chemiluminescent manganese ion-driven porphyrin supramolecular platform for the theranostics of thrombosis and ischemic stroke
Authors: Nan Xiao, Yanqin Fan, Liping Zhang, Gelin Xu, Qian Zhang, Feng Chi, Yachao Wang, Pengcheng Fu, Yuqi Liao, Kaijia Lin, Ziwei Wang, Haixing Feng, Yanlei Li, Xin Zheng, Dongxia Zhu, Liming Cao, Lijie Ren
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Ischemic stroke (IS) is a cerebrovascular disease with high mortality and disability rates. Effective treatment of IS remains limited by insufficient lesion identification, incomplete thrombus elimination, and progressively aggravated reperfusion injury, thus highlighting the urgent need for an integrated strategy that bridges precise imaging, efficient thrombolysis, and neuroprotection. This study developed a nanoplatform (Mn-Ce6@R NPs) through a simple one-pot coordination-driven self-assembly process, featuring facile preparation and good reproducibility. The coordination of Mn2+ modulated the aggregation state and photophysical behaviour of Ce6, thereby enhancing its ONOO−-responsive near-infrared chemiluminescence output and enabling precise imaging of thrombotic lesions and cerebral ischemia/reperfusion sites with a tissue penetration depth >10 mm. Under 689 nm laser irradiation, this platform synergistically triggered photothermal and photodynamic effects to achieve efficient thrombolysis. Meanwhile, its Mn-related enzyme-mimetic activity continuously scavenged RONS, alleviated inflammation, and exerted neuroprotective effects. The resulting nanoplatform exhibited excellent stability and high Ce6 loading capacity, with an approximately 77-fold enhancement in chemiluminescence signal toward ONOO−. It also achieved an in vitro thrombolysis efficiency of 74.0% and significantly restored blood perfusion in vivo, while reducing cerebral infarct volume. Collectively, this study establishes a sequential nanotheranostic strategy for the continuous pathological progression of IS and provides a translationally promising approach for real-time monitoring, precise thrombolysis, and post-reperfusion protection.

Introduction

Ischemic stroke (IS)—a neurological disease with high mortality and disability rates—is caused by the acute interruption of cerebral blood flow.1,2 Its pathological core lies in the preservation or loss of the ischemic penumbra.3,4 Current clinical treatments focus on rapid blood flow restoration to salvage the endangered brain tissue, thereby minimizing tissue necrosis (infarction) and ultimately improving neurological dysfunction.5,6 At present, common clinical approaches for treating IS include intravenous thrombolysis (e.g., using a recombinant tissue plasminogen activator, rt-PA) and neuroprotection.7,8 However, existing thrombolytic drugs have significant limitations—including a narrow therapeutic time window, high risk of intracranial haemorrhage, and poor targetting.9,10 More importantly, successful reperfusion may initiate a “second hit”, termed cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury (CIRI).11,12 This process is characterised by the explosive generation of cytotoxic reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (RONS), followed by intense inflammatory cascades, which further aggravate blood–brain barrier disruption and neuronal apoptosis.13,14 The current methods for IS diagnosis and monitoring comprise computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).15,16 However, methods based on these techniques cannot effectively detect biomarkers in the IS lesion microenvironment. Moreover, they fail to provide real-time, dynamic, as well as accurate diagnostic information in the early disease stages, and lead to delayed assessment of the therapeutic response.17,18 Therefore, an urgent clinical requirement exists for novel integrated therapeutic strategies capable of synergistically achieving early thrombus monitoring, precise thrombolysis, and effective countermeasures against CIRI.

In recent years, chemiluminescent nanomaterials have demonstrated considerable potential in the field of biomedical imaging.19,20 Chemiluminescence (CL) imaging produces endogenous optical signals through internal chemical reactions without requiring real-time external excitation, thereby fundamentally circumventing tissue scattering and autofluorescence issues as well as providing an ideal approach for high-contrast deep-tissue imaging.21,22 To date, chemiluminescent probes have evolved from early visible-emissive small molecules to near-infrared chemiluminescence (NIRCL) platforms. First-generation probes—typically represented by luminol and its derivatives—are easy to construct but are constrained by short emission wavelengths and limited signal duration. Second-generation activatable systems—such as adamantane-1,2-dioxetane (Adox) and Schaap's dioxetane—have improved stimulus responsiveness, but still suffer from complicated molecular structures, laborious synthetic procedures, and insufficient thermal stability.23 More recently, third-generation NIRCL systems have extended the emission window into the near-infrared region, thus improving tissue penetration and creating new opportunities for deep-lesion imaging. However, these systems generally rely on multicomponent assemblies and intermolecular energy-transfer processes, which not only increase the construction complexity but also impose stricter requirements on component matching, structural stability, and batch-to-batch reproducibility.24,25 Therefore, the development of a new NIRCL platform that simultaneously features simple fabrication, robust structural stability, strong NIRCL output, and integrated therapeutic capability remains a critical yet insufficiently explored direction in this field. Recently, our team found that the emission wavelengths of porphyrin-based NIRCL systems could be effectively tuned into the NIR region by leveraging their intrinsically extended conjugated structures.26,27 These porphyrin-related NIRCL systems, including our previously reported BDP-4/Cur-CL NPs, have demonstrated the feasibility of ONOO−-responsive chemiluminescence imaging in vivo. However, these previously reported systems usually require relatively complicated molecular design and multistep synthesis, and their practical translation may be limited by insufficient thermal stability, relatively low synthetic yield, and difficulties in large-scale preparation.5 Such NIR signals undergo markedly reduced scattering and absorption relative to visible light when propagated through biological tissues, thereby offering a crucial strategy for addressing pronounced signal attenuation in deep-tissue imaging.28,29 Furthermore, porphyrin derivatives such as chlorin e6 (Ce6) are broad-spectrum photosensitizers, and porphyrin-mediated photodynamic therapy has been successfully applied in clinical treatment.30,31 Nevertheless, the clinical translation of porphyrin-based materials is still hampered by their inherent poor aqueous solubility and synthetic complexity. Therefore, developing a simply fabricated, structurally stable, and highly reproducible porphyrin-based NIRCL nanoplatform that can simultaneously achieve ONOO−-responsive imaging and integrated therapeutic functions remains a critical yet insufficiently explored direction in this field.

Moreover, phototherapy—comprising photothermal therapy (PTT) and photodynamic therapy (PDT)—has emerged as a promising, non-invasive, and precise thrombolytic approach.32,33 PTT employs photothermal agents to convert light energy into thermal energy and achieve thrombus ablation via localised heating.34,35 However, PTT applied independently may produce large fragments during thrombus disintegration, potentially causing a distal secondary embolism.36,37 In contrast, PDT uses photosensitizers under optical excitation to generate reactive oxygen species (ROS), particularly singlet oxygen (1O2), which degrade fibrin structures.38,39 This prior biochemical degradation of the fibrin network by PDT effectively weakens thrombus integrity, thus enabling more complete and safer thermal ablation by PTT while simultaneously preventing distal embolism.38,40 Based on these complementary mechanisms, a combined PTT/PDT synergistic strategy was developed.41,42 Recently, our group reported several metal complex-based nanotheranostic platforms that combine PDT and PTT for efficient non-invasive thrombolysis.26,29 Although these advanced nanosystems exhibit remarkable functionality, their clinical translation faces substantial barriers—including complicated synthesis procedures, highly customised organic ligands, and potential long-term biosafety issues.43 Consequently, developing a nanoplatform that maintains high efficacy while featuring simple and scalable fabrication processes represents a pressing requirement.

Currently, most advanced thrombolytic strategies primarily focus on achieving the initial goal of vascular recanalization, while generally neglecting the crucial post-recanalization challenge of neuroprotection, which critically determines long-term patient outcomes.44,45 The explosive surge in RONS during CIRI serves as a pivotal driver of secondary neural damage, thereby underscoring the importance of dynamic monitoring and efficient clearance of RONS with regard to enhancing neurological recovery.46,47 In this context, manganese (Mn)-based nanozyme therapy has demonstrated significant potential, owing to its ability to emulate the catalytic functions of natural antioxidant enzymes.17,48 Manganese is an essential trace element that functions as an indispensable cofactor for manganese superoxide dismutase (Mn-SOD), a crucial mitochondrial antioxidant enzyme.49,50 Mn-SOD efficiently catalyses the dismutation of superoxide anions (O2˙−) into hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and oxygen, thus establishing the organism's primary defence against oxidative stress.51,52 Motivated by this biological paradigm, substantial research efforts have been dedicated to the development of nanomaterials containing Mn that can directly eliminate excess RONS by mimicking the cascade activities of SOD and catalase (CAT).53,54 Nevertheless, the purely exogenous scavenging capacity of such materials may prove to be inadequate against the explosively and continuously generated RONS in CIRI.48 Consequently, the prevailing challenges revolve around establishing a neuroprotective platform with sustained RONS-scavenging capability as well as ensuring spatiotemporal alignment of its antioxidant activity with the pathological progression of CIRI to achieve precise regulation and maximal rescue of ischemic penumbra neurons. Mn2+ and Ce6 were selected as the core components owing to their complementary chemical and biological functions. Ce6, a chlorin-type porphyrin derivative with an extended π-conjugated structure, provides the basis for NIR optical emission and PDT.55 Meanwhile, Mn2+ can coordinate with the carboxyl groups of Ce6 to regulate its aggregation state and photophysical behavior, thereby potentially enhancing ONOO−-responsive NIRCL performance. Thus, the Mn2+-Ce6 coordination system offers a rational strategy to integrate NIRCL imaging, PTT/PDT thrombolysis, and RONS-scavenging neuroprotection within one supramolecular nanoplatform.

Supramolecular coordination self-assembly is a powerful tool that provides a flexible bottom-up strategy for constructing functional nanomaterials with tunable physicochemical properties.56,57 Based on this, the present study established a multifunctional nanoplatform (designated Mn-Ce6@R nanoparticles [NPs]) constructed through a simple one-pot coordination-driven self-assembly process, using manganese ions (Mn2+) and chlorin e6 (Ce6) as the core components. This platform was designed to integrate three core functionalities required for comprehensive stroke management, which were described as follows. (1) Precise diagnosis: the system serves as a peroxynitrite (ONOO−)-responsive near-infrared chemiluminescent probe that is specifically activated in thrombotic and ischemic lesion areas. This characteristic enables real-time tracking of the dynamic processes of thrombus formation and cerebral ischemia–reperfusion (I/R) injury without requiring an external excitation light source. (2) Efficient thrombolysis: under 689 nm infrared laser irradiation, it synergistically exerts photothermal and photodynamic effects, thereby achieving a thrombolysis efficiency as high as 74.0%. (3) Active neuroprotection: this platform demonstrates powerful enzyme-like catalytic activity, capable of continuously scavenging harmful RONS and alleviating oxidative stress and neuroinflammation. This “all-in-one” nanotheranostic strategy simultaneously addresses the key challenges of precise thrombolysis and post-ischemic neuroprotection. Additionally, it is realised through a simple, scalable preparation process, offering a novel paradigm with strong clinical translation potential for the efficient diagnosis and treatment of IS.

Results and discussion

We designed and developed novel metal NPs comprising Ce6 and Mn2+. Using a nanoprecipitation method, we prepared Mn-Ce6@R NPs. An aqueous manganese chloride solution was added dropwise to a tetrahydrofuran (THF) solution of Ce6 under vigorous vortexing. After overnight aging, the mixture was sequentially dialyzed and centrifuged to remove residual THF and aggregates, yielding Mn-Ce6-NPs. Subsequently, the c(RGDfC) peptide was conjugated via click chemistry, resulting in the final product, Mn-Ce6@R NPs (as shown in Scheme 1). The Mn-Ce6 feeding ratio was crucial for successful assembly. The size uniformity of the NPs varied when the Mn2+-to-Ce6 feeding ratio ranged from 1.5 : 1 to 5 : 1 (Fig. 1a and S2, SI). However, a 1 : 1 feeding ratio yielded NPs with optimal size and the highest uniformity. Based on this size uniformity criterion, we selected Mn-Ce6@R NPs prepared at the 1 : 1 ratio for subsequent analyses. The actual Mn-Ce6 ratio was approximately 1 : 1. Mn-Ce6@R NPs exhibited a high Ce6 loading capacity of 91.57 wt%. As shown in Fig. 1a, the NPs had an average hydrodynamic diameter of 126.29 nm with a narrow size distribution (polydispersity index, PDI = 0.0546). We further analyzed the metal NP morphologies. Characterisation using transmission electron microscopy and scanning electron microscopy revealed that Mn-Ce6@R NPs possess a spherical hollow nanocapsule structure (Fig. 1a and S1, SI). The hydrodynamic diameter of all NPs remained largely unchanged over 14 days of storage, demonstrating excellent long-term stability (Fig. S3, SI). All NPs exhibited similarly high negative surface charges in aqueous media (Fig. 1b), which is beneficial for stable circulation and transport in vivo. The designed Mn-Ce6@R NPs integrated an ultra-high Ce6 loading capacity, a uniform spherical hollow nanostructure (PDI 0.0546), a strongly negative surface charge, and excellent colloidal stability (>14 days), which can significantly enhance the in vivo delivery efficiency.

First, we systematically characterized the photophysical properties of Mn-Ce6@R NPs and free Ce6. All NPs exhibited a four-peak structure within the Q-band (500–800 nm) in their UV-vis absorption spectra, confirming that Mn ions coordinated externally with the porphyrin ring of Ce6 rather than being inserted into its central cavity (Fig. 1c). Compared with Ce6 NPs, Mn-Ce6@R NPs showed a red-shifted absorption in the Q-band region with enhanced absorbance, indicating successful NP assembly upon Mn incorporation. Notably, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs (1 : 1) demonstrated higher absorption intensity in the 650–750 nm range compared with other stoichiometric ratios, indicating enhanced energy absorption under 689 nm irradiation for subsequent photothermal/photodynamic therapy and thrombolysis applications. Meanwhile, the fluorescence emission spectra in Fig. 1d reveal two sequential emission peaks for free Ce6 between 600–800 nm, whereas the introduction of the transition metal, Mn, resulted in substantially quenched fluorescence emission in the NPs, confirming the aggregated state of Ce6 within the nanostructure. We next investigated the phototherapeutic properties of the NPs. Using 1,3-diphenylisobenzofuran (DPBF) as an indicator, we monitored the singlet oxygen (1O2) generation capability of the NPs. When a DPBF solution (15 µM) was irradiated in the presence of Ce6 or Mn-Ce6@R NPs, the absorption peak intensity of DPBF at 415 nm gradually decreased (Fig. S5, SI), confirming effective singlet oxygen generation by these NPs. 1O2 generation followed first-order reaction kinetics, where steeper slopes correspond to faster DPBF decay rates and stronger 1O2 generation capability (Fig. 1e and f). Under identical conditions, the singlet oxygen generation efficiency of Rose Bengal was found to be 63%, whereas that of the Mn-Ce6@R NPs was slightly lower (50%). This is sufficient for subsequent PDT thrombolysis. We then examined the photothermal properties of the NPs under 689 nm laser irradiation. Upon irradiation at 0.8 W cm−2, the temperature of the Mn-Ce6@R NPs dispersion reached approximately 70 °C after 5 min, which is sufficient for effective thrombus dissolution, whereas the temperature increase for free Ce6 was significantly lower (Fig. 1g). Fig. 1h and i show the heating curves of Mn-Ce6@R NPs under 689 nm laser irradiation at different concentrations (10 µg mL−1, 20 µg mL−1, 40 µg mL−1, 80 µg mL−1, and 100 µg mL−1 in water) and different power densities (0.2, 0.4, 0.6, and 0.8 W cm−2). The temperature of the NP solution significantly increased with both concentration and laser power density. The photothermal effect of Mn-Ce6@R NPs was optimal at a laser power of 0.8 W cm−2 and a concentration of 100 µg mL−1. Infrared thermal imaging further confirmed the enhanced photothermal efficacy of Mn-Ce6@R NPs compared to that of free Ce6 (Fig. 1l). The photothermal conversion efficiency was determined to be 58.7% based on a previously reported method (Fig. 1j and S6, SI). After five cycles of laser irradiation and natural cooling, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs maintained their initial heating capability, confirming excellent photothermal stability (Fig. 1k). In contrast, free Ce6 exhibited inferior temperature maintenance under the same conditions (Fig. S6, SI). These results demonstrate that Mn-Ce6@R NPs possess excellent PDT and PTT efficiency, facilitating highly efficient thrombolysis.

Chemiluminescence involves light emission through chemical excitation during chemical reactions (Fig. 2a) and converts chemical energy into visible light emission.58,59 It has emerged as a breakthrough methodology in bioimaging and in vivo therapeutics.60,61 We evaluated the chemiluminescence selectivity of Mn-Ce6@R NPs using a series of RONS (nitrite, tert-butyl hydroperoxide, hydroxyl radical, superoxide anion, singlet oxygen, hydrogen peroxide, hypochlorite, and peroxynitrite) (Fig. 2b). Notably, a significant chemiluminescence signal was detected in the presence of peroxynitrite, confirming its highly selective recognition capability for ONOO−, with a signal intensity enhancement of ≈77-fold compared to that of phosphate buffered saline (PBS), demonstrating excellent ROS selectivity. ONOO− also triggered CL in other stoichiometric NPs. However, the 1 : 1 NP exhibited the most prominent intensity, with a measured value ∼1.6 times that of Ce6 alone (Fig. 2c). These findings confirm that Mn-Ce6@R NPs possess the best chemiluminescence performance. To clarify the role of Mn2+, the CL selectivity of free Ce6 was evaluated (Fig. S7). Free Ce6 showed a weak response to ONOO−, while Mn-Ce6@R NPs exhibited significantly enhanced NIRCL, indicating that Mn2+ coordination improves CL performance. Previous studies suggest that ONOO− oxidizes porphyrins to form unstable dioxetane intermediates, which decompose into excited-state products with photon emission.55 A similar pathway is likely involved here. Meanwhile, Mn2+ coordination modulates Ce6 aggregation and electronic structure, thereby enhancing CL emission. The CL spectrum of Mn-Ce6@R NPs displayed a prominent and broad emission peak in the 600–850 nm range, indicating superior NIR-CL characteristics (Fig. 2d). Sustained CL signal indicated photon release for >60 minutes (Fig. 2e), which is sufficient for in vivo imaging and drug screening purposes. The sustained CL signal of Mn-Ce6@R NPs showed a linear correlation with ONOO− concentration (Fig. S8, SI). Deep tissue penetration capability is crucial for practical application of NPs in brain imaging. As the thickness of chicken breast tissue increased, the NIR luminescence intensity exhibited regular attenuation. Remarkably, a clear luminescence signal could still be detected even at a tissue thickness of 10 mm (Fig. 2f). This characteristic indicates that Mn-Ce6@R NPs possess significant application advantages in the field of deep tissue imaging.

Based on the excellent photophysical properties of the NPs, we performed a preliminary evaluation of their thrombolytic performance. First, the hemolytic effect of Mn-Ce6@R NPs was tested. The results showed that the hemolysis ratios after co-culture with different concentrations of Mn-Ce6@R NPs were all below 5%, indicating excellent hemocompatibility (Fig. 3a). Artificial thrombi were prepared by mixing fresh mouse blood with thrombin, which were then incubated with non-targeted Mn-Ce6 NPs or targeted Mn-Ce6@R NPs for 2, 4, and 6 h, followed by imaging using an in vivo imaging system. As expected, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment group exhibited significantly stronger chemiluminescence signals at all time points compared to the non-targeted and PBS control groups (Fig. 3b and c). These findings confirm the enhancement of c(RGDfC) peptide-mediated targeting. To evaluate the in vitro thrombolytic effect, the NPs were co-incubated with freshly formed thrombi followed by laser irradiation. The supernatant of the solution containing NPs showed significant turbidity after 689 nm laser irradiation, along with a noticeable reduction in thrombus volume (Fig. 3d). These findings indicate that localized NP photoactivation via PTT/PDT rapidly increased the temperature and ROS generation, thereby promoting thrombolysis. The thrombolytic efficiency was quantified by weighing the thrombus mass before and after treatment. Compared to the PBS, PBS + L, UK, Mn-Ce6 NPs, and Mn-Ce6@R NPs + L groups, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs + L treatment group demonstrated the highest thrombolysis efficiency, reaching 74.0% (Fig. 3e). The changes in hemoglobin and fibrin content released into the supernatant during thrombus dissolution followed a trend that was consistent with the thrombolytic efficiency results (Fig. 3f). These findings indicate that Mn-Ce6@R NPs possess the most excellent thrombolytic capability among all control groups, demonstrating promising potential for in vivo therapeutic applications.

We assessed the antioxidant potential of the NPs through in vitro ROS scavenging assays using DPPH radical and hydrogen peroxide systems. The scavenging activity was determined by comparing absorbance measurements between solutions containing different NP concentrations and control solutions. As shown in Fig. 3g and h, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs demonstrated a concentration-dependent enhancement in scavenging efficiency across the tested ranges (30–1000 µg mL−1). The NPs exhibited significant dose-responsive inhibition against both DPPH and H2O2, achieving maximum scavenging rates of 73.7% and 81.0%, respectively, at 1000 µg mL−1. These findings indicate the metal-derived ROS responsiveness and scavenging capability, substantially augmented by transition metal incorporation. We further evaluated the in vitro cytotoxicity of the NPs by measuring the viability of human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) following 24 h NP exposure (Fig. 3i). HUVEC viability exceeded 95% even at NP concentrations up to 200 µg mL−1. This excellent biocompatibility supports subsequent biological implementation.

We next investigated the intracellular RONS scavenging capability of the NPs in HUVECs using a 2′,7′-dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate assay. We observed that treatment with 400 µM·H2O2 significantly increased intracellular RONS levels (green fluorescence intensity). The Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment group exhibited a marked reduction in RONS levels compared to the Ce6 group (Fig. 4a). Furthermore, we evaluated the RONS scavenging capacity of NPs in mouse hippocampal neuronal (HT22) cells. The Mn-Ce6@R NPs group exhibited the lowest green fluorescence intensity, indicating the strongest H2O2 scavenging capability (Fig. 4b). A quantitative analysis of the fluorescence intensity is presented in Fig. 4c and d, revealing only minor differences in the Ce6 group. These results collectively demonstrate that Mn-Ce6@R NPs serve as potent RONS scavengers.

Based on these findings, we subsequently examined the anti-inflammatory effects of Mn-Ce6@R NPsin vitro by assessing the expression levels of key inflammatory cytokines. RAW 264.7 cells were activated with 1 µg mL−1 lipopolysaccharide (LPS). When these cells were treated with Mn-Ce6@R NPs, they exhibited exceptional anti-inflammatory efficacy, as evidenced by significantly reduced expression levels of both tumor necrosis factor-α and interleukin-6 (Fig. 4e and S9, SI). The remarkable RONS scavenging and anti-inflammatory capabilities of the Mn-Ce6@R NPs indicate their potential for synergistic neuroprotection for the treatment of IS.

Subsequently, we evaluated the potential of Mn-Ce6@R NPs for integrated thrombus diagnosis and therapy in live mouse models (Fig. 5a). A carotid artery thrombosis model was established using ferric chloride induction. This model triggers thrombus formation through oxidative damage to vascular endothelial cells by FeCl3 and has been widely employed in studies on the mechanism of thrombosis and antithrombotic drug efficacy evaluations.29,62 Images obtained through laser speckle contrast imaging clearly revealed thrombus initiation, as evidenced by interrupted blood flow perfusion. After successful thrombosis induction, mice were administered Mn-Ce6@R NPsvia tail vein injection. Chemiluminescence signals emitted from the carotid artery region were captured at different time points using an in vivo imaging system to verify thrombus monitoring. As circulating Mn-Ce6@R NPs progressively accumulated at the thrombus site, the signal intensity gradually increased, peaking at 60 min post-injection before gradually declining, confirming the monitoring capability for thrombus development (Fig. 5b and c). In the laser irradiation group, at 60 min after Mn-Ce6@R NPs injection, the exposed carotid artery of mice was irradiated with a 689 nm laser (power density: 0.8 W cm−2) for 5 min. Temperature changes at the thrombus site during near-infrared illumination were first monitored using an infrared thermal camera (Fig. 5d and e). Notably, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs group exhibited the most significant temperature increase at the thrombus site due to the excellent thrombus targeting capability. Compared with the PBS and urokinase groups, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment group demonstrated superior thrombus dissolution capacity. Blood flow perfusion gradually recovered over time, reaching approximately 72.3% without observed secondary embolism. This efficacy is attributed to the dual-mode thrombolytic effect achieved through near-infrared laser-activated photodynamic/photothermal therapy (Fig. 5f, g and S10, SI). After all treatments, the carotid arteries were harvested for section preparation. Vascular sections were subjected to hematoxylin–eosin staining to observe the intravascular thrombus status (Fig. 5h). The thrombosis model group showed apparent thrombi within vessels. After different treatments, the thrombus size decreased to varying degrees. The Mn-Ce6@R NPs combined with near-infrared irradiation group exhibited the most effective thrombolysis, with the smallest thrombus area observed in arterial sections. Quantitative analysis revealed an ∼60% reduction in the relative thrombus volume in the Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment group (Fig. 5i). The thrombus-targeting PDT/PTT synergistic strategy was confirmed to significantly enhance thrombolytic efficacy.

We next performed real-time in vivo monitoring of IS progression. Building upon the excellent in vitro results, we subsequently evaluated Mn-Ce6@R NPsin vivo using a mouse model of endovascular middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) that simulates the ischemic state. Cerebral blood flow perfusion was monitored using laser speckle contrast imaging (LSCI) to assess the stability of the model (Fig. 6d).

We further investigated the in vivo neuroprotective potential of the Mn-Ce6@R NPs (Fig. 6a). No chemiluminescence signals were detected in the brain following intravenous injection, indicating insufficient blood–brain barrier (BBB) penetration. Consequently, we directly injected the Mn-Ce6@R NPs into the brain, enabling effective tissue perfusion without relying on BBB penetration. Mice received an injection of Mn-Ce6@R NPs (5 mg mL−1, 10 µL) after undergoing 30 min of cerebral ischemia followed by 30 min of reperfusion (Fig. 6b). The sham surgery group received PBS as a control. A significantly activated chemiluminescence signal was observed at the I/R injury site, whereas only negligible chemiluminescence was detected in the sham surgery control group (Fig. 6c). These findings indicate that Mn-Ce6@R NPs successfully detected the ONOO− generated during cerebral I/R injury in mice.

The neuroprotective effects of the Mn-Ce6@R NPs were further evaluated through in vivo experiments. The infarct volume was assessed 72 h after stroke onset. Triphenyltetrazolium chloride staining and cerebral infarction rate analysis revealed no signs of infarction in the control mice. After MCAO surgery, the cerebral infarction rate reached 44.8620 ± 2.1401%, confirming successful model establishment (Fig. 6e). In contrast, Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment significantly reduced the percentage of infarction to 15.6371 ± 0.3478% (Fig. 6e and f), demonstrating a substantial mitigation of brain injury. To explore the impact of Mn-Ce6@R NPs on long-term motor and cognitive functional recovery in MCAO mice, a series of behavioral tests were conducted from pre-operation (Pre) to day 28 post-stroke. The modified Neurological Severity Score (mNSS) on a 0–18 point scale was employed to assess neurological functional recovery at specified time points (Fig. 6g). Lower mNSS scores indicate milder neurological deficits and superior neuroprotective function, whereas higher scores indicate MCOA-induced sensorimotor impairment. Notably, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment group exhibited significantly lower mNSS scores than the PBS treatment group at all time points, revealing a pronounced protective effect of these NPs against ischemic injury in vivo. Behavioral changes were assessed using the open field test (Fig. 6h–k) and elevated plus maze test (Fig. 7a–d). Compared with the sham group, the PBS-treated mice exhibited reduced total movement distance and decreased movement speed, indicating severe motor dysfunction induced by stroke. As expected, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment group achieved longer total movement distances and faster movement speeds compared with the PBS treatment group. These findings indicate that the MCAO mice that received Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment achieved earlier and sustained neurological recovery with significant reversal of neurological deficits. Furthermore, Mn-Ce6@R NPs not only significantly alleviated ischemic stroke symptoms in a rat model of cerebral I/R injury but also caused no obvious damage to the brain tissue (Fig. 7e). Four-week treatment with Mn-Ce6@R NPs had no effect on the body weight of the mice (Fig. S11, SI). More importantly, the survival rate of the mice in the Mn-Ce6@R NPs treatment group was higher than that of the mice in the PBS treatment group (Fig. S12, SI). In summary, the Mn-Ce6@R NPs demonstrate significant potential as an IS treatment strategy by markedly reducing cerebral infarct volume, promoting neurological functional recovery, and improving long-term survival rates.

To investigate the cytotoxicity and biosafety of the Mn-Ce6@R NPs, their effects were evaluated in vivo through serum biochemical analysis and hematoxylin and eosin staining of major organs on days 3 and 28 post-administration. Serum biochemical analysis revealed no significant differences between the untreated mice and those injected with Mn-Ce6@R NPs (Fig. S13, S14, SI), indicating favorable biocompatibility. Major organs of the mice, including the brain, heart, liver, spleen, lung, and kidney, were collected for post-mortem examination (Fig. S15, SI). No pathological alterations were observed in the major organs across the six experimental groups (Fig. S16, SI sham day 3, MCAO day 3, Mn-Ce6@R NPs day 3, sham day 28, MCAO day 28, MCAO + Mn-Ce6@R NPs day 28). Furthermore, only negligible cellular necrosis was detected in the Mn-Ce6@R NP-treated brain tissues, further corroborating their favorable therapeutic profile. These collective results demonstrate that the Mn-Ce6@R NPs possess excellent biosafety, establishing a solid foundation for their subsequent clinical translation.

Conclusions

In summary, we successfully developed an ONOO−-responsive NIR CL nanoplatform (Mn-Ce6@R NPs) through a simple one-pot coordination-driven self-assembly strategy, featuring facile fabrication, good reproducibility, and integrated multifunctionality. Rather than merely combining imaging, thrombolysis, and anti-inflammatory effects, this platform was rationally designed in a pathology-oriented manner to address the continuous pathological progression of IS, spanning thrombus formation, vascular recanalization, and reperfusion injury, thereby enabling the organic integration of lesion identification, precise intervention, and neuroprotection. Owing to the Mn2+-mediated regulation of the aggregation state and photophysical behavior of Ce6, Mn-Ce6@R NPs exhibited markedly enhanced ONOO−-responsive NIRCL performance, with a tissue penetration depth exceeding 10 mm, thus providing an effective tool for the real-time dynamic monitoring of thrombotic lesions and cerebral ischemia/reperfusion injury sites. Meanwhile, under 689 nm laser irradiation, this platform synergistically triggered photothermal and photodynamic effects to achieve efficient thrombus ablation, affording an in vitro thrombolysis efficiency of 74.0%, significantly restoring local blood perfusion in vivo, and causing no obvious secondary embolism. In addition, Mn-Ce6@R NPs possessed enzyme-mimetic catalytic activity, enabling the sustained scavenging of excessive RONS, alleviation of inflammatory responses, and significant reduction of cerebral infarct volume in the MCAO model, together with improved neurological deficits and long-term motor behavior. More importantly, this work integrates NIRCL diagnosis, PTT/PDT synergistic thrombolysis, and post-reperfusion neuroprotection into a single, simply constructible nanosystem, thereby establishing a sequential theranostic strategy for the continuous pathological progression of IS. This study not only offers a new design rationale for constructing integrated nanoplatforms with in situ monitoring and intelligent intervention capabilities, but also provides a translationally promising solution for the real-time, precise, and individualized management of IS.

Ethical statement

Animal experiments: all animal experiments were approved by the Ethics Committee for Animal Experimentation of China Technology Industry Holdings (Shenzhen) Co., Ltd (Ethics approval number: 20240 030). Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). The statistical significance between experimental and control groups was assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey's test in SPSS Statistics 26. A value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant (*p < 0.05, **p < 0.01, ***p < 0.001, ****p < 0.0001).

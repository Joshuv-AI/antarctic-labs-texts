A novel electrode design using Cu-TCPP MOF-modified MWCNT for efficient electro-organic cross coupling synthesis of biphenyl derivatives in a Urea/Chol-Cl DES system as a green and sustainable electrolyte
Authors: Shahin Karamifar, Zahra Behrouz, Reza Ahdenov, Sahar Alizadeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe environmental and economic challenges of cross-coupling reactions arise mainly from reliance on expensive transition metals, toxic solvents, and harsh reaction conditions. These factors lead to high energy use, waste generation, and ecological harm, while the scarcity and cost of metals limit scalability. Organic solvents further increase carbon emissions and toxicity. Overcoming these issues is crucial for developing more sustainable and cost-effective cross-coupling methods. This study introduces a novel electrode design that employs a Cu-TCPP metal-organic framework (MOF)-modified oxidized Multi-Walled Carbon Nanotubes (o-MWCNT) for the efficient electro-organic synthesis of biphenyl derivatives 4(a-n) from chlorobenzenes 1(a-j) and triphenyl bismuthines 2(a-j). Conducted within a green urea/choline chloride (Urea/Chol-Cl) 3(a) DES system, the synthesis achieved remarkable yields of 90% to 97% in just 1 h at ambient temperature under a constant current of 10 mA. The characterization of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP catalyst was performed using a various of techniques, including FT-IR, SEM, EDS, EDX mapping, XPS, cyclic voltammetry (CV), TGA, and BET analysis. The resulting biphenyl derivatives 4(a-n) were confirmed through melting point determination, 1HNMR, and CHN analysis. This work underscores the potential of Cu-TCPP MOF-modified electrodes as sustainable and efficient catalysts for organic synthesis, paving the way for further exploration of their applications in green chemistry.Keywords: Electrode design, Cu-TCPP MOF, Electro-organic synthesis, Urea/Chol-Cl DES, Green electrode, Cross coupling reactionSubject terms: Environmental sciences, Chemistry, Nanoscience and technology

Introduction

The importance of cross-coupling reactions was underscored by the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry awarded to Richard Heck, Akira Suzuki, and Ei-ichi Negishi, recognizing their pioneering work in developing these catalytic processes. Their contributions have significantly improved the efficiency and versatility of synthetic routes, making cross-coupling indispensable in modern medicinal chemistry. Cross-coupling reactions are fundamental tools in organic chemistry, widely used to form carbon-carbon (C-C) and carbon-heteroatom (C-X) bonds. These reactions involve the coupling of organometallic nucleophiles with electrophiles, typically facilitated by transition metal catalysts such as palladium (Pd) or nickel (Ni). Common examples include the Suzuki, Stille, and Kumada couplings, which have become essential methodologies in the synthesis of complex molecules, particularly in pharmaceutical development for constructing biaryl and other intricate structures1–3. These reactions are routinely employed in the synthesis of a range of marketed drugs, including losartan, valsartan and irbesartan (high blood pressure)4,5, ruxolitinib (myelofibrosis)6, abemaciclib (breast cancer)7, and atorvastatin (cardiovascular disease)8, as illustrated in Fig. 19.

These reactions involve the creation of carbon-carbon (C-C) or carbon-heteroatom (C-X) bonds through the coupling of organometallic nucleophiles with electrophiles, typically in the presence of transition metal catalysts such as Pd or Ni10. Notable examples include the Suzuki11, Stille12, and Kumada couplings13, which have been widely adopted in drug development for constructing biaryl compounds and other intricate structures. These methodologies not only enhance the efficiency of synthetic routes, thereby playing a crucial role in modern medicinal chemistry14. Despite their widespread use, many cross-coupling methodologies face challenges related to the cost and availability of catalysts often involving precious metals and demanding reaction conditions, including high temperatures, long reaction times, and the use of hazardous solvents and reagents. These limitations pose significant barriers to large-scale industrial application and commercialization.

Various chemical methods for synthesizing cross-coupling reactions have been developed, as illustrated in Table 1. These reactions employ different metals, including Cu, Ag, Pt, Ni, Co, Ru, and Rh, most of which are expensive. Furthermore, they often involve harsh reaction conditions, such as high temperatures, prolonged reaction times, and the use of toxic or non-commercial solvents and reagents. Addressing these challenges by developing more sustainable, cost-effective, and environmentally friendly cross-coupling processes is crucial. Applying green chemistry principles to improve reaction conditions will facilitate the broader adoption and commercialization of these valuable synthetic methods. This research focuses on evaluating and optimizing cross-coupling reactions to overcome such obstacles, ultimately enhancing their practical utility in pharmaceutical synthesis.

Green chemistry has significantly influenced various industries by promoting sustainable practices that minimize environmental damage while improving operational efficiency. Its central principles emphasize designing safer chemicals, using renewable resources, and reducing the reliance on hazardous substances throughout manufacturing processes. For example, in the pharmaceutical industry, the adoption of greener synthetic routes has successfully decreased waste generation, lowered energy consumption, and improved workplace safety. By embracing these environmentally friendly strategies, industries not only comply with increasingly stringent regulations but also gain economic advantages through cost savings and safer products, thus advancing toward a more sustainable future28.

One of the most promising developments within green chemistry is the use of deep eutectic solvents (DESs), which offer an environmentally benign alternative in organic synthesis and electrosynthesis. DESs are generally prepared from inexpensive, renewable materials such as urea and choline chloride, making them biodegradable, low in toxicity, and easy to produce29,30. their distinctive properties, including high hydrogen-bonding capacity, ionic character, and favorable physicochemical traits, contribute to enhanced reaction efficiency by facilitating better electron transfer and stabilizing reactive intermediates31. This positions DESs as superior substitutes for conventional toxic organic solvents, helping to reduce energy consumption and align chemical processes more closely with green chemistry principles32. Furthermore, DESs serve not only as solvents but also as effective electrolytes and co-catalysts, especially when paired with advanced catalyst systems like copper-TCPP metal-organic framework (MOF)-modified electrodes, thereby broadening their application in sustainable cross-coupling and other organic transformations.

Building on these advances, electro-organic synthesis has emerged as a revolutionary method in organic chemistry, where electricity replaces traditional reagents to promote chemical reactions. This approach provides precise control over reaction parameters, enabling the efficient synthesis of complex molecules under mild and environmentally friendly conditions. By avoiding harsh reagents and precious metal catalysts, electro-organic synthesis significantly minimizes waste and improves reaction selectivity, fulfilling key criteria of green chemistry. Recent progress has demonstrated its practical utility in forming essential carbon-carbon and carbon-heteroatom bonds, which are pivotal in the production of pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals. The method’s high tolerance for diverse functional groups and its sustainable character underscore its potential as a transformative technology for green and efficient organic synthesis in the future33,34. To address these limitations, our research team designed and produced a catalyst based on oxidized multi-walled carbon nanotubes modified with Cu-TCPP MOF to enhance both specific surface area and reusability. In the electro-organic synthesis of cross-coupling reactions, we employed a model reaction between chlorobenzene 1(a) and triphenyl bismuth 2(a). Due to the high surface area, stability, and electrical conductivity of Cu-TCPP MOF and o-MWCNT, this catalyst achieved yields ranging from 90% to 97%, demonstrating excellent reusability with inexpensive and readily available Cu NPs in a eutectic mixture of choline chloride/urea solvent (DES) (see Scheme 1). Urea/Chol-Cl 3(a) functions as a solvent, electrolyte, and co-catalyst (activating the copper NPs). Consequently, this process adheres to green chemistry principles, reducing toxicity and pollution while enhancing atomic economy.

Experimental

Materials, methods and equipment’s

All the chemicals and glassware utilized in this research were procured from Merck and Goldis Glassblowing Company. The materials and glassware include a condenser, 200 mL two-neck flask, 200 mL single-neck flask, 1 cm and 2 cm magnets, a magnetic stirrer, terephthalic acid, propionic acid, pyrrole, ethanol (EtOH, 96%), tall beakers (50 mL and 100 mL), copper chloride (CuCl2), polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), dimethylformamide (DMF), dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), carbon nanotubes (MWCNTs), nitric acid (HNO3, 50%), sulfuric acid (H2SO4, 98%), urea, choline chloride (Chol-Cl), chlorobenzenes 1(a-j), bromo-benzenes 2(a-j), bismuth trichloride (BiCl3), diethyl ether (Et2O), tetrahydrofuran (THF), methanol (MeOH), distilled water (H2O), isopropyl alcohol (iPrOH), ethyl acetate (EtOAC), sodium sulfate (Na2SO4), sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3), sodium (Na), magnesium (Mg) and acetonitrile (MeCN). Laboratory equipment such as a rotary vacuum evaporator, magnetic stirrer, ultrasonic device, UV cabinet, and electrothermal stainless steel autoclave were used. analysis of the materials and products was conducted using the following instruments. Fourier-transform infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy was conducted utilizing a Spectrum 65 - PERKIN-ELMER equipment with KBr as the sample medium. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) analysis was performed with a TESCAN Vega model SEM, and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) data were obtained using a TESCAN SEM VEGA3. Elemental analysis (CHN) was carried out with an 1100B - PERKIN-ELMER analyzer. Brunauer-Emmett-Teller (BET) surface area analysis was carried out using a Belsorp-miniX instrument. Proton nuclear magnetic resonance (1HNMR) spectra were recorded on a Bruker Avance DRX spectrometer operating at 400 MHz. Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) was achieved with a Netzsch TGA 209 F1 instrument. Controlled-current coulometry and electro-organic synthesis were carried out using a DC power source, employing a graphite rod anode (3 cm²) and O-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE cathode electrodes. X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) was carried out using a Kratos Analytical AXIS Supra instrument. Cyclic voltammetry experiments (CV) were conducted using a CHI 600c electrochemical station at 25 °C in an undivided sealed glass cell configured with a three-electrode system. This setup included a graphite rod anode (3 cm²) as the counter electrode, an O-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE as the working electrode (3 cm²), and an Ag/AgI/0.1 M TBAI reference electrode calibrated against the ferrocene/ferricenium (Fc/Fc⁺) couple after each experiment. All potentials were referenced to the KCl-saturated calomel electrode (SCE). The Cyclic voltammograms were obtained using a scan rate of 0.1 V/s, employing the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE cathode in the presence of chlorobenzene 1(a) and BiCl3 2(a) gas throughout the process, allowing for the investigation of the electrochemical behavior of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP electrode under the specified conditions.

Procedure for synthesis of o-tetra(4-carboxyphenyl)porphine (TCPP)

TCPP was synthesized based on previous reports35. In a round-bottom glass flask fitted with a condenser, magnetic stirrer, and heating mantle, 2 gr of terephthalic acid (a) and propionic acid were added as the solvent and refluxed for half an hour. Then, 6 mL of pyrrole (b) was slowly added to the reaction mixture, which was refluxed for an additional 2 h. Upon completion of the reaction, the mixture was allowed to cool to ambient temperature, and the purple precipitate formed was separated by vacuum filtration. The precipitate was then washed first with 10 mL of H2O and subsequently with hot EtOH (10 mL) before being dried in an oven at 60 °C for 3 h (Scheme 2).

Procedure for synthesis of TCPP.

Procedure for production of Cu-TCPP MOF

In a 100 mL beaker, 0.2 gr of CuCl2, 0.1 gr of PVP, and 21 mL of DMF were added, along with EtOH in a 2:1 ratio (14:7 mL). Then, 1 gr of TCPP was introduced to the reaction mixture, followed by sonication for 20 min. The mixture was then transferred to a Teflon-lined stainless-steel autoclave, and heated at 85 °C for 20 h. After cooling to ambient temperature, the precipitate was filtered and washed thoroughly with MeOH. Finally, the product was dried in a vacuum oven at 65 °C for 15 h (Scheme 3)36,37.

Procedure for production of Cu-TCPP MOF.

Procedure for oxidation of MWCNTs (o-MWCNT)

In a 50 mL a round-bottom flask fitted with a condenser and magnetic stirrer, 0.5 gr of MWCNT and 20 mL of a HNO3 (50%) and H2SO4 (98%) mixture in a 1:3 (5:15 mL) ratio was added. The mixture was maintained at 80 °C for 2 h. Afterward, it was allowed to cool to ambient temperature and filtered under vacuum. The resulting precipitate was washed three times with hot EtOH (3 × 7 mL) and then dried in an oven at 60 °C38 (Scheme 4).

Procedure for oxidation of MWCNTs.

Procedure for coating Cu-TCPP on o-MWCNTs

In a 50 mL tall beaker, 0.5 gr of o-MWCNT and 30 mL of EtOH were added and sonicated at 100 W for half an hour. Next, 0.3 gr of Cu-TCPP was suspended in 10 mL of EtOH and gradually introduced into the reaction mixture under continuous ultrasonic agitation. The reaction mixture was sonicated for an additional 1 h. Finally, the reaction mixture was filtered, and the solid was washed with hot EtOH (10 mL) before being dried in an oven at 70 °C for 2 h (Scheme 5).

Procedure for coating Cu-TCPP on o-MWCNTs.

Preparation of Urea/Chol-Cl DES

In a 200 mL beaker fitted with magnetic stirrer and heating mantle, urea (a) and Chol-Cl (b) were added in a 2:1 (10:5 mL) ratio and mixed at 65 °C for 1 h. This process yielded a clear and colorless solution, which constitutes the DES Urea/Chol-Cl39 (Scheme 6).

Preparation of Urea/Chol-Cl DES.

Fabrication of o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE electrode

In a 50 mL beaker, 0.5 gr of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP catalyst was combined with 10 mL of MeOH. The mixture was then subjected to ultrasonic treatment in a bath operating at 100 W for 20 min. Following this, 5 mL of the reaction solution was carefully applied onto a Glassy Carbon Electrode (GCE). The electrode was subsequently dried in an oven at 75 °C (Scheme 7).

Fabrication of o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP on the GCE electrode.

Procedure for the production of triphenylbismuthine derivatives 2(a-j)

The THF (500 mL) solvent was procured and subsequently dried by refluxing with metallic Na (10 gr) and acetophenone (Spatula tip) for a duration of 5 h, after which it was collected40. In a 200 mL three-necked flask fitted with a thermometer, condenser, magnetic stirrer, addition funnel, and an inert gas atmosphere, 4.1 gr of Mg metal was introduced along with a small amount of iodine (I2). Following this, 50 mL of dry THF was incorporated through the addition funnel, allowing the mixture to react at ambient temperature for 15 min under an inert gas environment. Next, a solution of 5.5 gr of bromobenzenes 2(a-j) in 60 mL of dried THF was steadily incorporated into the reaction mixture under continuous agitation. The reaction was allowed to proceed for 1 h before being halted. At the same temperature, 3.7 gr of solid BiCl3 was gradually introduced into the mixture and mixed for an additional 60 min. Upon completion of the reaction, 50 mL of NaHCO3 solution was added slowly to the mixture, resulting in the release of CO2 gas and considerable foaming. After complete neutralization and once the mixture had returned to room temperature, an additional 2 × 50 mL of Et2O was introduced, and the mixture was filtered under vacuum. The resulting organic and aqueous phases were separated utilizing a separatory funnel. The organic phase was washed twice with 20 mL of distilled H2O before being isolated. Finally, the organic phase underwent concentration, and hexane was employed as an anti-solvent to precipitate the desired compounds. The precipitates were then filtered and dried at 60 °C for 1 h. Triphenylbismuthine 2(a) was characterized using melting point, 1HNMR, and CHN analysis. The other derivatives 2(b-j) were identified based solely on their melting points (Scheme 8).

Procedure for the production of triphenylbismuthine derivatives.

Cross coupling electro-organic reaction procedure for the synthesis of biphenyl derivatives 4(a-n)

In a 50 mL electrolysis cell equipped with a 1 cm magnet, 15 mL of the eutectic solvent Urea/Chol-Cl was added at room temperature and mixed thoroughly. Following this, 1 gr (1 mmol) of chlorobenzenes 1(a-j) and 1.37 gr (0.33 mmol) of triphenyl bismuthines 2(a-j) were introduced into the mixture. A 2 cm−2 graphite rod served as the anode, while an o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE acted as the cathode. A current of 10 mA was applied using a DC power supply, and the reaction proceeded for 60 min. The reaction progress was tracked using thin-layer chromatography (TLC). After confirming the reaction’s completion, the power supply was switched off. Subsequently, 25 mL of distilled H2O and 25 mL of EtOAc were added to the reaction mixture, which was then mixed for an additional 5 min at ambient temperature. The resulting phases were separated using a separatory funnel, and the organic layer was rinsed twice with 10 mL of H2O prior to the isolation step. This organic phase was then dried over Na2SO4 (5 gr) and filtered under vacuum. Next, the organic phase was then concentrated under reduced pressure using a rotary evaporator. Following this, 3 gr of silica gel were introduced to promote the adhesion of the reaction mixture. A chromatography column, 30 cm in length and 2 cm in diameter, was set up using a 7:1 hexane to EtOAc solvent mixture and silica gel with a mesh size ranging from 80 to 120 microns. The reaction mixture was applied to the chromatography column and subjected to elution using the same solvent ratio, facilitating the separation of the components according to their retention times. The product was purified at the end of the chromatography column, yielding a solution that displayed a single spot on TLC. This solution was then completely dried under vacuum using a rotary evaporator, resulting in a precipitate identified as compound 4(a-n) (Scheme 9).

Cross coupling electro-organic reaction procedure for synthesis of Biphenyl derivatives.

Hot filtration test

To confirm the heterogeneous nature and recyclability of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP catalyst, a hot filtration test was conducted under the optimized reaction conditions for the synthesis of biphenyl derivatives 4(a–n). In a typical experiment, 1 gr chlorobenzenes 1(a–j) and 1.37 g (0.33 mmol) of triphenyl bismuthines 2(a–j) were combined in the reaction mixture. A 2 cm² graphite rod served as the anode, while an o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE functioned as the cathode. A constant current of 10 mA was applied via a DC power supply, and the reaction was allowed to proceed at the set temperature. The reaction’s progress was monitored by thin-layer chromatography (TLC). Upon reaching approximately 50% conversion at 30 min, the reaction mixture was immediately subjected to hot filtration at 70 °C using vacuum filtration to effectively separate the solid catalyst from the reaction solution. The catalyst-free filtrate was transferred into a fresh reaction vessel and stirred further at 70 °C for an additional hour. Subsequent TLC analysis revealed only minor further progression of the reaction, indicating a significant decrease in product formation after catalyst removal. This outcome clearly evidences that the catalytically active species remained immobilized on the solid o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP support, confirming the truly heterogeneous catalytic behavior of the system. Therefore, the hot filtration experiment validates both the catalyst’s heterogeneity and its practical recyclability, demonstrating its robustness and suitability for sustainable and green organic synthesis applications.

Result and discussion

Fourier Transform Infrared (FT-IR) spectroscopy, a widely applied analytical technique for qualitative and quantitative characterization of compounds, provides a distinct “chemical fingerprint” for each material. The MWCNT spectrum (black) exhibits absorptions at 3444 cm⁻¹, assigned to O–H stretching from adsorbed moisture, and at 1451 cm⁻¹, corresponding to aromatic C = C stretching in the graphitic structure. Optimization of the reaction conditions in the electro-organic synthesis was performed using a model reaction between chlorobenzene 1(a) and triphenyl bismuth 2(a), as shown in Table 4. To this purpose, an electrolytic cell with a volume of 50 mL was fitted with a magnet, heating stirrer, DC power supply, graphite rod anode, and o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE cathode. The reaction commenced upon applying an electric current, and the parameters such as electrolyte type, electrode material, current intensity, time, and temperature were optimized to demonstrate the role of various components in the reaction. The reaction initially started in a 10% NaCl electrolyte at a current of 10 mA for 4 h at ambient temperature. Various solvents including H2O, MeOH, iPrOH, MeCN, EtOH, and DMSO were studied, yielding respective efficiencies of 0%, 43%, 58%, 25%, 31%, and 18% (Table 4, entries 1–6). To enhance the reaction yield, the NaCl electrolyte concentration was increased to 50%, and the electrolyte type was changed to NaBr at a 50% ratio, resulting in a yield of 63% (Table 4, entries 7–8). Subsequently, changing the electrode to a DES of Urea/Chol-Cl which serves multiple roles as a solvent, electrolyte, and co-catalyst (as shown in Schematic 9) increased the reaction yield to 95% (Table 4, entry 9). This multifunctional role of the Urea/Chol-Cl DES reduces environmental impacts and production costs. The current was then decreased from 10 mA to 5 mA, resulting in yields of 95% and 81%, respectively (Table 4, entrie 10–11). Decreasing the reaction time to 3, 2, 1 and 0.5 h yielded efficiencies of 95%, 95%, 95% and 70%, respectively (Table 4, entries 12–15). Increasing the reaction temperature to 60 °C resulted in an observed yield of 80% (Table 4, entry 16). When the electric current was removed, no yield was observed for the reaction (Table 4, entry 17). Eliminating the electrolyte in iPrOH reduced the reaction yield to 15% (Table 3, entry 18). Finally, changing the electrode type to o-MWCNT-Cu and Cu foam resulted in yields of 72% and 41% under similar conditions (Table 4, entries 19–20). These results indicate that the presence of electrolyte, electric current, MWCNTs, and Cu-TCPP is essential for optimizing the reaction. The presence of Cu-TCPP increases the contact area due to the high surface area of these nanosheets, thereby enhancing the reaction rate. Additionally, the Urea/Chol-Cl DES enhances electron transfers during the reaction due to its high hydrogen bonding capacity and dielectric constant while stabilizing intermediates formed because of its high ionic nature, thus increasing reaction yields. Carbon nanotubes also contribute to electron transfers both externally and within the reaction mixture due to their high surface area and excellent electrical conductivity.

In the present study, an innovative electrocatalytic system was developed by anchoring Cu-TCPP MOF onto oxidized multi-walled carbon nanotubes (o-MWCNT), operating within a Urea/Chol-Cl DES environment. This system was designed to facilitate electrocatalytic cross-coupling reactions under mild conditions, adhering to the principles of green chemistry. The o-MWCNT served as a conductive support material, providing a high surface area and excellent electrical conductivity that significantly enhances electron transfer efficiency at the cathode interface. The Cu-TCPP MOF was selected as the active catalytic phase due to its large surface area and porous structure, which promote effective adsorption and interaction of reactants, in addition to being composed of copper a metal noted for its abundance, low cost, and environmental benignity. The Urea/Chol-Cl DES played a multifunctional role, acting simultaneously as a green solvent, electrolyte, and co-catalyst, thereby eliminating the need for hazardous organic solvents and reducing the overall environmental footprint of the process. The synergistic integration of these components yielded a robust electrocatalytic platform, which was systematically evaluated for its efficiency and selectivity in the electro-organic synthesis of biphenyl derivatives 4(a–n). The results underscore the potential of this tailored electrocatalytic assembly for sustainable and scalable organic transformations.

The reaction mechanism depicted in Scheme 10 begins with the application of an electric current, which leads to the oxidation of choline chloride at the anode. This process generates chlorine (Cl2) and 2-(trimethylammonio)ethanolate, the latter of which can function as a weak base in various reactions30. The Cl2 produced subsequently reacts with Cu NPs at the cathode to form CuCl254. Following this, chlorobenzene 1(a) interacts with the Cu NPs within the pores of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP MOF, resulting in the formation of the intermediate phenylcopper(II) chloride (a). This intermediate then reacts with triphenylbismuth 2(a) to yield chlorodiphenylbismuthine (b) and diphenylcopper (c). The chlorodiphenylbismuthine (b) intermediate is involved in two additional cycles of this reaction, ultimately leading to the formation of trichlorobismuth (d). This process consumes 0.33 moles of triphenylbismuth 2(a), as all three benzene groups participate in the reaction. Moreover, diphenylcopper (c) undergoes intramolecular rearrangement to produce 1,1’-biphenyl 4(a). In the end, the released Cu NPs re-enter the reaction cycle. The significant increase in surface area resulting from o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP greatly enhances the contact area of the materials, thereby accelerating the reaction rate and improving the catalyst’s performance throughout the process. This work presents a novel electrode design by integrating Cu-TCPP MOF onto oxidized multi-walled carbon nanotubes (o-MWCNT), creating a highly efficient and reusable catalytic system for electro-organic cross-coupling synthesis of biphenyl derivatives. Unlike traditional methods that rely on expensive and toxic transition metals and harsh solvents, this study utilizes a green DES composed of urea and Chol-Cl, which simultaneously serves as the solvent, electrolyte, and co-catalyst. The combination of Cu-TCPP MOF with o-MWCNT significantly enhances the catalyst’s surface area, electrical conductivity, and stability, enabling impressive yields of up to 97% under mild conditions and short reaction times. This innovative approach not only reduces environmental impact by eliminating hazardous reagents and solvents but also demonstrates excellent catalyst recyclability, marking an important step toward sustainable and cost-effective electrochemical synthesis in line with green chemistry principles.

The interaction between the catalyst and the reaction components was examined through quantum chemical calculations utilizing density functional theory (DFT), as illustrated in Fig. 12. Upon introduction of the catalyst, an exothermic process occurs with a calculated energy change of − 8.2 kcal/mol, whereby the activated Cu metal reacts with chlorobenzene, leading to the formation of the phenylcopper(II) chloride intermediate (a), accompanied by an overall energy release of − 14.9 kcal/mol. Subsequently, in the presence of triphenylbismuthine 2(a), and following the consumption of 9.6 kcal/mol to surmount the associated energy barrier, an intermediate (d) characterized by a bulky spatial conformation and significant steric hindrance is generated via covalent bond formation. Thereafter, intramolecular rearrangement occurs, resulting in stabilization and formation of the diphenylcopper intermediate (e), with an energy release of − 16.2 kcal/mol. Finally, intermediate (e) overcomes an additional energy barrier of 5.2 kcal/mol through a further intramolecular rearrangement, which culminates in the release of − 18.5 kcal/mol and the synthesis of the final biphenyl derivative product 4(a). In this reaction, triphenylbismuthine undergoes participation across three catalytic cycles and is ultimately eliminated from the reaction pathway as trichlorobismuth. A notable feature of this process is the modulation of activation energies during the successive cycles. Specifically, in the second cycle, the formation of intermediate (d) is characterized by a reduction in activation energy to 8.1 kcal/mol, attributable to the steric hindrance imposed by chlorodiphenylbismuthane (c). In the subsequent third cycle, owing to the further diminished steric hindrance resulting from dichlorophenylbismuthane (d), the activation energy required for this step decreases even further to 6.3 kcal/mol.

The reusability of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE catalyst was assessed through a model reaction involving chlorobenzene 1(a) and triphenyl bismuthine 2(a), conducted over nine runs. After each cycle, the sample was mechanically removed from the surface of the glassy carbon electrode (GCE) by abrasion, subsequently washed with hot EtOH, and dried in an oven at 80 °C for 1 h prior to reuse in the subsequent cycle. The catalyst recovery rate in each cycle was approximately 98%. The catalyst exhibited good performance and demonstrated reusability for up to eight runs (91–95% yields); however, from the 8th run onward, the reaction yields significantly decreased. SEM analysis of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP GCE cathode surface indicated that the electrode had experienced corrosion, suggesting that after 9 runs, the electrode surface needed to be recovered and modified (Fig. 13). The SEM analysis of the o-MWCNT-Cu-TCPP catalyst surface revealed significant corrosion and partial degradation of the carbon nanotubes, accompanied by moderate to considerable connector defects and cluster formation. These structural defects caused uneven catalyst distribution, reducing the effective contact area and the number of active sites, which in turn disrupted electron transfer and electrical conductivity. Consequently, the catalyst’s surface area and electrocatalytic performance decreased. Additionally, the current density during the electrosynthesis process declined due to reduced electrical conductivity and increased resistance.

The presented table offers a detailed comparative analysis of previously reported electrochemical methodologies for cross-coupling synthesis relative to the current study (Table 7). This evaluation emphasizes critical operational parameters, including applied current, reaction temperature, duration, electrode configuration, solvent system, product yield, adherence to green chemistry principles, and catalyst recyclability. Previously reported protocols predominantly employed precious metal catalysts such as Pt(OAc)₂ and pure Pt, or nickel-based catalysts like NiCl₂, often necessitating elevated reaction temperatures (up to 40 °C) and extended reaction times ranging from 3 to 12 h. These methods commonly relied on conventional organic solvents such as MeCN, DMA, and DMSO, which pose environmental and safety concerns due to their toxicity. Reported yields in these systems varied between 55% and 94%, with limited reports on catalyst reusability in many cases.

In contrast, the present investigation introduces a novel electrocatalytic system founded on Cu-TCPP MOF-functionalized oxidized multi-walled carbon nanotubes (o-MWCNT) operating within a green DES composed of Urea/Chol-Cl. This integrated system serves simultaneously as solvent, electrolyte, and co-catalyst, facilitating cross-coupling reactions under significantly milder conditions namely ambient temperature and a short reaction time of one hour under a constant current of 10 mA. The approach achieves superior product yields ranging from 90% to 97%, while eliminating hazardous solvents, thereby markedly reducing energy consumption. The utilization of an inexpensive, earth-abundant copper-based catalyst further enhances the sustainability and economic feasibility of the process. Additionally, the excellent catalytic stability and reusability demonstrated by the Cu-TCPP MOF/o-MWCNT composite support its potential for scalable and industrial applications. Collectively, this work advances the field by integrating an innovative Cu-TCPP MOF catalyst with conductive carbon nanotube scaffolds within a sustainable DES medium, providing an efficient, environmentally benign, and cost-effective route for high-yield electrochemical cross-coupling reactions. This methodology represents a significant progression in developing green and sustainable synthetic protocols with tangible economic and ecological benefits over traditional approaches (Table 7, entry 10).

Conclusion

This study presents a novel Cu-TCPP MOF-modified multi-walled carbon nanotube (MWCNT) electrode that exhibits excellent catalytic performance in the electro-organic synthesis of biphenyl derivatives using a green Urea/Chol-Cl DES. The integration of Cu-TCPP MOFs onto the o-MWCNT scaffold significantly enhances surface area, porosity, electrical conductivity, and catalyst stability, resulting in high product yields (90%–97%) under mild and sustainable reaction conditions (10 mA, 1 h, room temperature). The multifunctional role of the DES as solvent, electrolyte, and co-catalyst further supports a greener, energy-efficient process aligned with the principles of green chemistry. Importantly, the modified electrode demonstrates notable reusability and operational stability, underscoring its potential for scalable and environmentally considerate synthetic applications. These findings highlight the effective synergy between metal-organic frameworks and carbon nanomaterials to drive electrochemical transformations efficiently and sustainably. The study presents a very promising electrocatalytic system for green, efficient synthesis of biphenyl derivatives but it also exhibits noteworthy limitations. First, while the Cu-TCPP MOF/o-MWCNT composite demonstrates excellent performance for up to eight reuse cycles, its structural degradation beyond that evidenced by corrosion, clustering, and reduced conductivity impairs catalyst longevity and scalability. Second, like many MOF-based catalysts, the design might suffer from inherent challenges such as limited electrical conductivity and potential instability under prolonged or harsh electrochemical conditions, raising concerns about durability in extended real-world use.

Outlook

Future investigations should focus on optimizing the synthesis parameters of Cu-TCPP MOFs and expanding the range of metal centers and organic linkers to precisely tailor catalytic activity and selectivity. Assessing the long-term stability and reusability of these electrodes under continuous flow or semi-industrial conditions will be crucial for evaluating their practical applicability. Furthermore, broadening the scope of electro-organic transformations beyond biphenyl derivatives to include a diverse array of aromatic and heteroaromatic substrates across various organic reactions could significantly enhance the versatility of this electrode design. Complementary mechanistic studies exploring the interactions within the DES environment will provide deeper insights and facilitate rational catalyst development, thereby promoting the wider adoption of sustainable electrochemical methodologies in organic synthesis. Additionally, the recyclability of DES in semi-industrial settings warrants further examination.

Combined prediction of fractional exhaled nitric oxide and pulmonary function tests for airway hyperresponsiveness in children with chronic cough
Authors: Li Wang, Yinghong Fan, Jie Hu, Qianqian Li, Ronghua Luo, Yaping Duan, Jiayi Xue, Tao Ai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCoughing, as one of the most common symptoms of the respiratory system in children, has a variety of etiologies. This study aimed to explore the predictive value of fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) combined with pulmonary ventilation function tests for airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR) in children with chronic cough.MethodsA total of 157 children with chronic cough who visited Chengdu Women and Children’s Central Hospital from January 2020 to January 2024 were included. They were divided into a positive bronchial provocation test group (n=69) and a negative bronchial provocation test group (n=88) based on the results of the bronchial provocation test. The pulmonary ventilation function parameters and FeNO levels of the two groups were compared, and the predictive value of each index for AHR was evaluated through the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve.ResultsThe forced expiratory volume in the first second (FEV1), FEV1/forced vital capacity (FVC), forced expiratory flow at 25% of FVC exhaled (FEF25), forced expiratory flow at 50% of FVC exhaled (FEF50), forced expiratory flow at 75% of FVC exhaled (FEF75), and maximum mid-expiratory flow (MMEF) of the positive provocation group were significantly lower than those of the negative provocation group, and the FeNO level was significantly higher, with statistically significant differences (P<0.05). Further analysis through the ROC curve revealed that MMEF had the highest predictive efficacy for AHR [area under the curve (AUC) =0.88, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.82–0.94]; when FeNO was combined with pulmonary ventilation function parameters, the combination of MMEF and FeNO had the best predictive efficacy (AUC =0.91, 95% CI: 0.86–0.96), with optimal cut-off values of 67.05% and 20.50 ppb.ConclusionsThe combination of FeNO and MMEF can effectively predict AHR in children with chronic cough and has significant clinical value for the differential diagnosis of chronic cough etiology.

Key findings

• In children with chronic cough, airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR) was associated with significantly lower pulmonary ventilation function and elevated fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) levels.

• Chronic cough has a variety of etiologies, and its underlying pathologic change is of varying degrees of AHR. Clinical assessment of AHR mainly relies on the bronchial provocation test

• This study identifies maximum mid-expiratory flow (MMEF) as a critical pulmonary function parameter and demonstrates that combining MMEF with FeNO enhances AHR prediction accuracy, offering a non-invasive diagnostic strategy for children with chronic cough.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• MMEF and FeNO integration provides a reliable, non-invasive tool for early AHR detection in children with chronic cough, aiding in etiological differentiation and reducing reliance on invasive bronchial provocation tests. Clinicians should adopt combined MMEF and FeNO testing in routine evaluations for chronic cough. Guidelines should prioritize this dual-parameter approach to optimize diagnostic workflows and therapeutic decision-making.

Introduction

Coughing is one of the most prevalent symptoms of respiratory diseases in children. A study has shown that more than 75% of children visit the doctor more than five times a year due to coughing, and 14% of children visit the doctor even more than 15 times (1). Chronic cough is defined as a cough that persists for more than four weeks and is the primary clinical manifestation the chest X-ray does not show any significant abnormalities, which impairs the patient’s quality of life and imposes a great burden on the patient and the family. Etiologies of pediatric chronic cough include asthma, protracted bacterial bronchitis, tracheomalacia, habit cough, and various systemic disorders (2). Chronic cough has a variety of etiologies, and its underlying pathologic change is varying degrees of airway hyperresponsiveness (AHR). Clinical assessment of AHR mainly relies on the bronchial provocation test (BPT), but this test is mostly used in children over 6 years of age and in adults, and its use in infants and young children is limited because of the difficulty of cooperation, time-consuming, expensive, and the examination process may induce severe bronchospasm, it is poorly applied in all hospitals, especially in primary hospitals (3,4). The fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) test is currently used as a non-invasive biomarker for assessing airway inflammation, reflecting eosinophilic airway inflammation and predicting the effect of glucocorticoid therapy (5,6). The FeNO related studies have also reported that FeNO can predict the presence of AHR in children with chronic cough (7-9). A study has shown that most patients with chronic cough and asthma have decreased small airway function, and decreased small airway function is associated with AHR (10). As such, it is imperative to identify cost-effective, less hazardous, and straightforward approaches to evaluate BHR at the earliest. The aim of this study was to analyze exhaled breath nitric oxide and pulmonary ventilation function to jointly predict AHR in children with chronic cough, thereby providing new insights into examination methods and clinical diagnosis for early detection of AHR in younger children. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2025-280/rc).

Methods

Subject of the study

The clinical data of 157 children with chronic cough who were admitted to Chengdu Women and Children’s Central Hospital from January 2020 to January 2024 were retrospectively collected. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chengdu Women’s and Children’s Central Hospital [No. 2024(142)] and informed consent was obtained from the patients’ legal guardians.

Inclusion criteria: (I) all meet the diagnostic criteria in the “Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Chronic Cough in Chinese Children (Revised in 2013)” (11); (II) cough duration >4 weeks, with cough as the main or only symptom; (III) chest radiograph or CT examination showed no obvious inflammatory lesions in lung tissue; those who had completed FeNO, pulmonary function and bronchial provocation test.

Exclusion criteria: (I) those who had upper or lower respiratory tract infections in the last 6 weeks; (II) those who combined with other serious organic respiratory system diseases; (III) those who applied glucocorticosteroids, antihistamines, bronchodilators, or other medications that may affect the examination within 4 weeks.

Methodology

The Jaeger pulmonary function test system was used for testing. In the child’s calm breathing state, the upright position was taken, the mouth was covered by the instrument, and the lips were wrapped tightly. From the end of the last breath, the child quickly and deeply inhaled to the total lung capacity position, and exhaled the gas with maximum force. Bronchial provocation test: methacholine chloride provocation test was performed on the Jaeger pulmonary function testing system according to the American Thoracic Society (ATS)/European Respiratory Society (ERS) working group recommendations with a Medi-Aid side-flow nebulizer to determine airway responsiveness. The stimulating dose that caused FEV1 to decline by 20% or more was recorded as positive airway provocation test (12).

FeNO detection: nitric oxide analyzer with a standard flow rate of 50 mL/s was used and operated according to ATS/ERS recommendations by Guangzhou Ruipu Medical Technology Co. (Guangzhou, China). The main detection index was FeNO (in ppb, 1 ppb =1×10−9 mol/L). The children were instructed to first exhale as much as possible to evacuate the gas in the lungs, hold the sizer filter tightly in their mouths, inhale deeply and then exhale uniformly with a smooth airflow for 6–10 s. The test values were subsequently read.

Observation indicators

Bronchial excitation test results, FEV1, FVC, FEV1/FVC, FEF25, FEF50, FEF75, MMEF, FeNO.

Statistical analysis

Graph Pad Prism 6.0 statistical software was used, and data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. When the variance was uniform, one-way ANOVA or Turkey test was used to compare the groups; when the variance was not uniform, Kruskal-Wallis test was used to compare the groups; the count data were expressed as the number of cases (n) and the rate (%); the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was drawn and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. The optimal cut-off value, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated. A difference was considered statistically significant when P<0.05.

Results

Comparison of pulmonary function test indexes and FeNO in two groups of patients

Based on the results of the bronchial provocation test, children with chronic cough were divided into the provocation positive group and the provocation negative group. The lung function-related indicators and FeNO values of the two groups were compared. The FEV1, FEV1/FVC, FEF25, FEF50, FEF75, and MMEF of the provocation positive group were all lower than those of the provocation negative group, while the FeNO was higher than that of the provocation negative group, with statistically significant differences (P<0.05). Discussion

AHR is the main feature of chronic cough and the main basis for its diagnosis. Currently, it is predominantly diagnosed using the bronchial provocation test (12-14). The main disadvantage of the bronchial provocation test is that it is not a reliable test. However, the bronchial provocation test has several significant disadvantages: expensive, time-consuming, requires a high degree of cooperation from the child, and carries a risk of severe bronchospasm during the examination. Therefore, there is a need to explore simpler, safer, cheaper, and more appropriate tests, either alone or in combination, for younger children, especially in primary hospitals that are not equipped to perform bronchial provocation tests.

FeNO test is an easily available and non-invasive method, which is a biomarker reflecting the level of airway eosinophil inflammation. By selecting different flow rates, the level of eosinophil inflammation in the central large airway, small airway, and alveolar can be confirmed (8,15,16). The 2011 ATS guidelines highly recommend the FeNO testis highly recommended in the assessment of airway eosinophilic inflammation and has an important role in determining the etiology of chronic cough (1,17-19). Some scholars have suggested that AHR in asthma is partly related to airway eosinophilic inflammation, which can be attenuated by glucocorticoids. In addition, nitric oxide itself is a vasodilator that can cause and/or exacerbate AHR by converting to peroxynitrite causing airway edema (20). A previous study has shown that in non-smoking individuals with untreated chronic cough, FeNO <30 ppb has high specificity (87%), high NPV (93%), and is helpful for diagnosing asthma. However, FeNO >30 ppb has relatively low sensitivity for detecting asthma (75%) and low PPV (60%) (18). Our study indicates that among patients with chronic cough, the FeNO values of those with positive bronchial provocation test were significantly higher than those with negative test, showing a significant difference. This suggests that FeNO might be a predictive indicator for AHR. In this study, the optimal value of FeNO for the clinical prediction of AHR in patients with chronic cough was >20.50 ppb at a flow rate of 50 mL/s. The sensitivity and specificity were 72.73% and 68.12% respectively. When FeNO was used alone as a predictive indicator for distinguishing the presence or absence of AHR, the AUC was 0.76, indicating certain predictive value, but the sensitivity and specificity were relatively low.

Small airways dysfunction (SAD) encompasses inflammation, stenosis, and other processes that occur in airways less than 2 mm in diameter (21), that is associated with poor asthma control, persistent asthma, nocturnal asthma attacks, exercise-induced asthma attacks, and AHR (22-24). Decreases in MMEF, FEF50, and FEF75 on pulmonary function tests reflect small airway dysfunction (25,26). This study found that the FEF50, FEF75, MMEF and other small airway function indicators in the positive bronchial provocation group were significantly lower than those in the negative group, and the difference was statistically significant (P<0.05). This indicates that patients with chronic cough who have positive bronchial provocation and AHR also have small airway dysfunction. It has been shown that in asthmatics with near-normal FEV1 and FVC values, MMEF was found to be significantly lower than that of normal volunteers by pulmonary function tests. MMEF may be a marker of early airway compromise and a more sensitive indicator of small airway function (27).

We evaluated the predictive value of pulmonary ventilation function for AHR by drawing ROC curves. The results showed that the area under the AUC curve of MMEF was the highest at 0.88, with the optimal cut-off value being 67.05%, a sensitivity of 85.23%, and a specificity of 88.41%. The positive predictive value was 82.43%, and the negative predictive value was 90.36%. However, when using a single pulmonary ventilation function indicator to predict AHR, the sensitivity was not high, and it had certain limitations.

Our research evaluated whether pulmonary ventilation function and FeNO levels could be used to predict AHR in children with chronic cough. The results showed that there was a significant association between AHR and the decline of pulmonary ventilation function and the increase of FeNO levels. This indicated that FeNO value elevation and small airway dysfunction were the main risk factors for chronic cough and AHR. Further our research using FeNO and pulmonary ventilation function for combined prediction found that the diagnostic efficacy of MMEF and FeNO combined prediction for AHR was the highest, with an AUC of 0.91 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.86–0.96], which was significantly higher than the predictive efficacy when using any single indicator alone. The difference was statistically significant, indicating that when FeNO exceeds 20.50 ppb and MMEF falls below 67.05% of the predicted values, it could more effectively predict the positivity of the bronchial provocation test, i.e., AHR, in children with long-term chronic cough.

The limitation of this study is that the predictive value of MMEF was 67.05%. However, the 2018 Routine lung Function guidelines pointed out that two of the three indicators in FEF50, FEF75 and MMEF were lower than 65% predicted value, which could be judged as SAD (25). This may be related to the lack of strict judgment threshold value of small airway dysfunction in children, which needs further research.

Conclusions

FeNO and pulmonary ventilation function detection have significant value in predicting AHR. The combined detection of FeNO and MMEF has higher predictive value for AHR than a single indicator. For children with long-term chronic cough who cannot complete provocation tests, FeNO combined with small airway function detection can be considered as an alternative.

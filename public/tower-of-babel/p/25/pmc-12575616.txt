Multi-objective optimization of speed frame parameters for polyester spun yarn using artificial intelligence and grey relational analysis
Authors: Habib Amiri Savadroodbari, Milad Razbin, Mohsen Rezahasani, Majid Safar Johari
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSystematic approaches to process optimization are essential for enhancing operational efficiency in industrial manufacturing, particularly in the textile sector. This study investigates and compares the effectiveness of Artificial Intelligence (AI) based modeling and optimization techniques with Grey Relational Analysis (GRA) in determining the optimal operational parameters of the speed frame for producing 100% polyester spun yarn. The key parameters analyzed include twist, break draft, spacer size, and overhang, with Response Surface Methodology (RSM) employed to design the experiments and ensure sufficient variability in the input space. Yarn quality was evaluated using the Grey Relational Grade (GRG), calculated through GRA based on two critical response variables: yarn evenness (CVm%) and imperfection index (IPI). A predictive model based on an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) was developed to estimate the GRG, and its performance was compared to that of the RSM model. Optimization was then conducted using both GRA and the Genetic Algorithm (GA) to assess the comparative efficiency of a multi-criteria decision-making approach and an AI-based optimization technique. The results demonstrated that the ANN model achieved significantly higher prediction accuracy (R2 ≈ 1) compared to the RSM model (R2 ≈ 0.65). Furthermore, the GA-based optimization outperformed GRA, resulting in improved yarn quality. The optimal parameter settings identified were twist = 23 TPM, break draft = 1.26, spacer size = 5.1 mm, and overhang = 3.5 mm. Introduction

Yarn quality is a critical factor in textile production, as it directly affects the final product’s performance and appearance. The term "staple yarn production" refers to the processes involved in manufacturing yarn from staple fibers. All natural fibers, such as cotton, wool, and jute, naturally exist in staple form. Additionally, most synthetic fibers, including polyester, acrylic, and viscose, are also manufactured as staple fibers1. Various parameters of the spinning process influence different quality characteristics of staple yarn. The primary goal of spinning is to produce yarn with optimal attributes, including minimal unevenness percentage (CVm%), a low imperfection index (IPI), and reduced hairiness. However, each processing parameter affects these quality characteristics differently. Therefore, a systematic approach to multi-objective optimization is crucial for enhancing overall yarn quality2. Furthermore, defining a comprehensive and unified criterion for evaluating yarn quality can enhance the efficiency of the optimization process.

In systematic research methodology, Design of Experiments (DOE) offers a structured approach to experimentation, allowing researchers to establish statistical and mathematical relationships between independent and dependent process parameters for a more thorough analysis3. Response Surface Methodology (RSM), a widely used subset of DOE, is particularly popular among engineering researchers. It plays a crucial role in product design, development, and formulation, as well as in optimizing existing product designs4. Several researchers have utilized this approach to investigate the effects of spinning process parameters on yarn quality, optimize system performance, and develop predictive models for yarn properties. Ishtiaque et al.5 employed the Box-Behnken Design (BBD), a specific type of RSM, to optimize ring spinning machine parameters and achieve superior yarn quality. Das et al.6 explored the optimization of fiber friction as a material variable, alongside top front roller pressure and roller gauge as machine variables in the drawing frame, speed frame, and ring frame. Similarly, Abdul Jabbar et al.7 examined the effects of carding cylinder speed, carding production rate, and the number of drawing frame doublings on cotton yarn quality using the BBD. Karthik and Murugan8 investigated the effects of fiber friction, roller gauge, and top front roller pressure in the speed frame and ring frame on the quality of cotton-milkweed blend yarn using the RSM approach. Additionally, Cui et al.9 conducted a systematic study using RSM to analyze a modified drafting system in the ring frame, known as the Soft Drawing System (SDS). Gray systems theory is another systematic approach widely used in various research domains, including systems analysis, data processing, modeling, optimization, control, and decision-making10. A notable approach used by researchers in the spinning process for multi-objective parameter optimization is grey relational analysis (GRA), a technique derived from grey system theory. This method addresses optimization problems based on the principles introduced by Deng11. In studies on yarn spinning, Hassani et al.12, Hussain et al.13, and Su et al.14 employed a combined Taguchi-GRA method to optimize rotor spinning parameters and enhance yarn quality. They used the Taguchi experimental design to conduct experiments and applied the GRA method to determine optimal parameters. The integration of these approaches effectively overcame one of the primary limitations of the Taguchi method, namely its inability to handle multi-objective optimization, by enabling the simultaneous evaluation and optimization of multiple performance criteria. Previous studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of GRA in optimizing process parameters and improving various yarn quality characteristics.

In recent years, research has increasingly focused on modern optimization and modeling techniques based on artificial intelligence (AI). Among these, artificial neural networks (ANNs) have gained significant popularity for prediction and modeling tasks. In simplified models, the brain comprises a vast number of computational units, known as neurons, that are intricately interconnected. This network structure enables the brain to perform highly complex computations. ANNs are computational frameworks inspired by this biological paradigm15. Additionally, the genetic algorithm (GA) is a widely used AI-based optimization technique that identifies optimal solutions to complex problems by applying principles of evolutionary biology within computer science16. In the spinning process, numerous studies have examined the application of ANN for modeling yarn properties and GA for parameter optimization. Ureyen and Gurkan17,18 investigated the prediction of qualitative and physical properties of ring-spun cotton yarns using ANN and compared the results with those obtained from multiple linear regression (MLR) models. They used HVI machine-measured properties, yarn count, yarn twist, and roving properties as input variables. Their findings demonstrated a significant advantage of ANN over MLR in prediction accuracy. Farooq and Cherif19–21 employed ANN to model the quality of roving output from the drawing frame and ring-spun yarn based on drawing machine parameters. Their findings confirmed the effectiveness of ANN in predicting sliver and yarn quality while also identifying key variable correlations for optimal settings. In another study, Das et al.22 employed ANN and GA to predict the properties of ring-spun yarns and determine the optimal combination of cotton fiber properties. Vadood et al.23,24 conducted studies to predict the properties of core-spun yarns in Rotorcraft Compact Spinning (ROCOS) using ANN and optimized its production process parameters through GA-based multi-objective optimization. More recently, Majumdar et al.25 developed a hybrid machine learning-based system for prescriptive yarn engineering, employing ANN to predict ring-spun cotton yarn properties and GA to optimize cotton fiber characteristics.

The speed frame plays a vital role in producing roving before the final yarn is formed in the ring spinning process. Despite its mechanical complexity and the associated increase in production costs, it remains an essential step in yarn manufacturing. Directly feeding the sliver into the ring spinning system is not feasible due to the extremely high draft requirements and the mismatch between the dimensions of the draw frame cans and the geometric characteristics of the ring spinning system26. Therefore, optimizing speed frame parameters through a robust and reliable approach is crucial for enhancing the efficiency and performance of the spinning process. Most studies on modeling yarn properties, optimizing spinning parameters, and assessing their effects on yarn quality have primarily focused on cotton yarns or their blends with fibers such as polyester and viscose. However, limited research has explored the optimization of speed frame parameters for 100% polyester spun yarns. As a widely used synthetic fiber, polyester offers durability and versatility, but its physical properties differ significantly from natural fibers like cotton, requiring distinct process parameter settings for optimal performance27. To bridge this research gap, the present study aims to establish an efficient approach for the multi-objective optimization of key speed frame parameters to achieve the highest possible quality in 100% polyester spun yarns. For this purpose, the RSM experimental design was employed to collect yarn quality data, including the CVm% and IPI of polyester spun yarns. CVm% represents the mass variation of the yarn per unit length, while IPI is defined as the total count of thick places, thin places, and neps in the yarn28,29. The key speed frame parameters examined in this study include twist, break draft, spacer size, and overhang. To assess overall yarn quality using a single index rather than multiple individual quality indicators, the GRG was calculated using the GRA approach, providing a comprehensive measure of yarn quality. Subsequently, RSM and ANN models were developed to predict GRG values, and their predictive performance was compared. Finally, a multi-objective optimization process was conducted using both GRA and GA, methodologies that have been demonstrated in previous studies to effectively enhance product quality. However, a comparative analysis between these two approaches has not been previously reported, which constitutes a research gap. Therefore, the present study aimed to compare GRA and GA in order to identify the more efficient method for maximizing yarn quality and accurately determining the optimal levels of speed frame parameters. Data acquisition

Design of experiment

In many engineering applications, the use of a full factorial design, which evaluates all possible combinations of input parameters, is often impractical due to the high experimental cost and time requirements30. To overcome this challenge, the present study employed an optimized I-block quadratic design based on RSM using Design-Expert Software Version 13, incorporating 9 additional points. This design reduced the number of required experimental runs from 81 (as in a full factorial design) to 24, thus achieving a significant reduction in resource usage while ensuring sufficient data for reliable analysis and modeling. Among the available design strategies, RSM offers a balanced approach: it requires more experimental runs than the Taguchi method, which provides a highly economical but often limited dataset, yet considerably fewer than a full factorial design. This intermediate scale allows RSM to capture adequate variability across the input space, which is essential for effective optimization and predictive modeling, particularly when employing advanced techniques such as GA or AI-based models. Hence, RSM was chosen in this study to ensure both analytical precision and experimental efficiency.

A fundamental principle of systematic experimental research is the identification and selection of the most influential variables to be incorporated into the experimental design for effective and meaningful data acquisition. In accordance with this principle, the present study concentrated on four critical operational parameters of the speed frame, twist (X₁), break draft (X₂), spacer size (X₃) and overhang (X₄), as the primary factors affecting yarn quality. To ensure a controlled and consistent experimental environment, other parameters, including front roller pressure, total draft, front roller gauge, back roller gauge and other related settings, were kept constant throughout the experimental procedures. Materials and production methods

A batch of polyester fibers, with a length of 44 mm and a fineness of 1.6 dtex, was processed through the blowroom, carding, breaker drawing, and finisher drawing stages. The resulting sliver, with a linear density of 4.75 kilotex, was then fed into a speed frame (Howa Machinery). Based on the RSM design outlined in the previous section, rovings with a linear density of 650 tex were prepared for subsequent processing in a Zinser RM350 ring-spinning machine. Following this, yarn samples with a linear density of 20 tex and a twist density of 715 TPM were produced. The quality parameters of the polyester spun yarn, specifically CVm% and IPI, were assessed using Uster Tester-4 equipment. The IPI value was determined as the sum of thin places (sensitivity threshold of − 50%), thick places (sensitivity threshold of + 50%), and neps (sensitivity threshold of + 200%). The evaluation was conducted at a testing speed of 400 m/min over a duration of 2.5 min.

Decision-making via Grey relational analysis

GRA is an efficient and effective computational technique commonly used in decision-making applications37. In this study, GRA was used as a multi-objective optimization tool to determine the optimal speed frame parameter settings for minimizing CVm% and IPI. The GRA method employs an index called the GRG to evaluate the desirability of a sample. GRG was used as a single criterion to represent the overall quality of spun yarns, as it is a function of CVm% and IPI in polyester spun yarns. By defining GRG as a descriptor of yarn quality, RSM and ANN models were developed to predict GRG and analyze the effects of input parameters on it. To calculate GRG, the response values must first be normalized between 0 and 1, with the normalization procedure varying depending on whether a maximized or minimized value is desirable. Objective functions via artificial neural network

The multilayer perceptron (MLP) is a fundamental ANN model widely used in engineering for process prediction and modeling. An MLP consists of multiple processing layers of neurons, where each neuron receives inputs from the preceding layer and maps them to the output41. In this type of ANN model, several hyperparameters must be optimized to achieve maximum accuracy, including the number of hidden layers, the number of neurons in each hidden layer, and the activation functions used in both the hidden and output layers42. Previous studies have shown that an MLP-based ANN model trained using the error backpropagation algorithm, with one hidden layer, a tangent sigmoid activation function in the hidden layer, and a pure linear activation function in the output layer, as described in Eqs. (10) and (11), and utilizing the Levenberg–Marquardt training function, provides optimal network performance in engineering problems similar to this study43–45. To evaluate the relative importance of each input parameter in the ANN model, two approaches were employed: the Garson weight method (GWM) and the partial derivative method (PDM). According to Eq. (12), the GWM estimates the relative contribution of each input feature to the network output by utilizing the absolute values of the connection weights between the input, hidden, and output layers. In contrast, the PDM, outlined in Eqs. (13) and (14), not only enables the calculation of each input’s relative importance based on the absolute values of the partial derivatives of the output with respect to the inputs, but also provides information about the direction of influence. Specifically, the sign of the partial derivatives (positive or negative) reveals whether an input feature has a positive or negative effect on the network output46–48. As illustrated in Fig. 2b, both sensitivity analysis approaches, the GWM and the PDM, identified spacer size as the most influential input parameter in predicting GRG values, with relative importance scores of 35.02% and 38.07%, respectively. Conversely, break draft and twist were found to have the least impact, with relative importance values of 14.76% and 14.68%, respectively, in the GWM and PDM.

Among the two sensitivity analysis techniques employed in this study, the PDM, despite its higher computational complexity, offers a more accurate and behavior-sensitive estimation of input parameter importance, as it explicitly incorporates the activation functions of the ANN when computing the gradients. This enables PDM to effectively capture the nonlinear dynamics and local sensitivities of the model in response to marginal variations in input parameters. In contrast, the GWM, while beneficial for providing a global and structural perspective, does not consider the activation functions and instead relies solely on the magnitude of connection weights. As a result, GWM may fail to reflect the complex nonlinear interactions introduced by the activation functions, particularly in networks employing non-linear functions such as tangent sigmoid, logistic sigmoid, and other non-linear activation functions.

Results and discussions

Parametric study results

Figure 5 shows the experimental effect of speed frame parameters on the GRG value of polyester spun yarn. As shown in Fig. 5a, a moderate increase in twist from 22.00 to 24.00 m⁻1 improves the GRG value from 0.59 to 0.61 by enhancing fiber cohesion; however, a further rise to 26.00 m⁻1 leads to a drop in GRG to 0.47, likely due to excessive compaction that increases drafting resistance and causes yarn non-uniformities. In Fig. 5b, increasing the break draft from 1.20 to 1.30 steadily raises the GRG value from 0.49 to 0.59, indicating that appropriate drafting force improves fiber alignment, although extremely high drafts may destabilize the fiber strand. Figure 5c reveals that larger spacer sizes from 5.10 mm to 6.70 mm significantly reduce GRG values from 0.65 to 0.47, suggesting that excessive gaps between aprons weaken fiber control in the drafting zone, leading to greater strand irregularities. Finally, Fig. 5d shows that a moderate overhang of 4.00 mm yields the highest GRG value of 0.61, while both lower and higher overhangs (2.00 mm and 6.00 mm) result in decreased values, emphasizing the importance of optimal fiber guidance near the front roller. Altogether, the experimental findings highlight the delicate balance required among speed frame parameters to maximize yarn quality, aligning well with the fundamental principles of fiber drafting and control in spinning processes.

Comparison of the performance of RSM and ANN Models

Figure 6 illustrates the performance of the RSM and ANN models in predicting GRG values, which serve as an indicator of the overall quality of spun yarns. As observed in Fig. 6a and b, the RSM model performed poorly in both the training and testing stages. Most data points are dispersed around the fitting line (red line), indicating a weak correlation. This is due to the model’s inability to accurately capture the strong nonlinear relationship between independent parameters and GRG values. In contrast, Fig. 6c and d demonstrate that the ANN model performed better in predicting GRG values during both the training and testing phases. This improvement is attributed to its ability to effectively capture complex nonlinear interactions between input parameters and the response variable. The main strength of the ANN model lies in its hidden layer neurons, which incorporate nonlinear activation functions, enabling the network to map input variables more effectively to the output space. Furthermore, the total TGF value for the RSM model was 1.5964, considerably lower than the TGF value of approximately 2 for the ANN model. This substantial difference further confirms the superior predictive accuracy and generalization capability of the ANN model compared to the RSM model. Therefore, using ANN is a logical choice for understanding and analyzing the effect of speed frame parameters on changes in GRG values. Additionally, integrating it with GA can enhance the optimization process, making it more effective in identifying the best possible solution.

Optimization results of GRA and ANN-GA

To determine the optimal levels of speed frame parameters, the GRA approach was initially employed. For this purpose, the average GRG was calculated for each parameter level, as presented in Table 9. Based on the results, the optimal parameter levels identified using GRA included a medium twist level (24 TPM), a high break draft (1.30), a low spacer size (5.1 mm), and a medium overhang (4 mm), as these levels exhibited higher average GRG values. The predicted GRG value based on the GRA approach was 0.8191, compared to 0.5994 for the sample with the initial settings. Furthermore, implementing the optimized levels identified by GRA reduced the coefficient of variation of mass (CVm%) from 12.77 to 11.87 and decreased the Imperfection Index (IPI) from 69.33 to 26.67. Next, GA, an AI-based optimization method, was employed to refine the speed frame parameters, yielding different results from GRA. The optimal parameter levels determined by ANN-GA included a twist value of 23 TPM, a break draft of 1.26, a spacer size of 5.1 mm, and an overhang of 3.5 mm. Applying these optimized levels improved the cost value from 0.6032 in the initial setup to 2.94e−07 in the optimized condition. Additionally, the GRG value increased to 1.06, demonstrating the superior effectiveness of ANN-GA in optimizing yarn quality compared to GRA.

Analysis and interpretation of results

Figure 8 illustrates the impact of speed frame parameters on GRG values, which serve as an overall indicator of yarn quality. In this study, GRG is a function of CVm% and IPI, analyzed by examining the trend of ANN model outputs. Each parameter was evaluated independently while maintaining the other parameters at the optimal levels identified through the GA approach, which was selected as a reliable optimization method. This approach enhances the interpretability of neural networks by extracting meaningful insights from the model, thereby reducing their “black box” nature51. By revealing the relationships embedded within the ANN, this method improves its interpretability, making it a valuable tool for analysis and decision-making. Ultimately, this strategy provides a clearer understanding of how each parameter influences the quality indicator of the spun yarn.

Investigating the effect of speed frame parameters on the GRG Value of Polyester Spun Yarn using ANN outputs: (a) twist (T), (b) break draft (B.D), (c) spacer size (SP), and (d) overhang (OH).

Figure 8a illustrates the effect of twist on GRG values. As observed, within the range of selected levels, higher twist levels significantly reduce overall yarn quality, leading to a substantial decrease in GRG values. In general, an excessive increase in the twist of the roving exiting the speed frame beyond the optimal level results in a more compact structure. Consequently, the drafting process in the ring frame requires greater force to further reduce the linear density. This increased resistance can hinder the smooth execution of the drafting process, potentially causing distortions that negatively affect the uniformity and overall quality of the produced yarns.

Figure 8b illustrates the positive effect of intermediate levels of break draft on overall yarn quality, leading to an increase in GRG values. This phenomenon can be explained as follows: at low break draft levels, the insufficient drafting force may fail to overcome the friction between fibers, preventing optimal drafting of the fiber strand. As a result, irregularities such as thick and thin places develop in the fiber strand produced by the speed frame, which are subsequently transferred to the yarn in the ring frame, negatively affecting yarn quality parameters. Conversely, a high break draft reduces the main draft within the speed frame. This change in draft distribution can slightly disrupt the fiber strand drafting process, making it difficult to achieve the highest possible quality.

Figure 8c clearly shows that among the spacer size levels studied, smaller spacer values result in the highest GRG values. In general, a larger spacer increases the gap between the aprons in the speed frame. If this gap exceeds the optimal level, fiber movement and guidance in the drafting zone become less controlled, leading to irregularities in the drafting process. As a result, the quality of the roving is compromised, which subsequently affects the overall quality of the yarn produced in the ring frame.

According to Fig. 8d, both low and high levels of overhang, unlike medium levels, have a significantly negative impact on GRG values, thereby affecting overall yarn quality. This is likely due to inadequate guidance and control of fibers in the front drafting zone of the speed frame, which compromises the quality of the roving and, consequently, the final yarn quality. In general, the trend derived from the ANN model output highlights the complex relationship between process parameters and GRG as an indicator of overall yarn quality. Furthermore, the results align with the GA optimization findings, as the identified optimal levels are consistent with the trend predicted by the ANN model.

Conclusion

In this study, we optimized the speed frame parameters to achieve the highest quality in 100% polyester spun yarns using two approaches: GRA and an AI based method, the GA. The key parameters examined were twist, break draft, spacer size, and overhang. Data on yarn quality indices, specifically CVm% and IPI, were collected based on a RSM design to ensure comprehensive coverage of the input parameter space and to enable effective mapping to the output responses. The GRG, derived using the GRA approach, was employed as a single composite metric to assess overall yarn quality by integrating the effects of CVm% and IPI into a unified performance indicator. To predict GRG values, both RSM and Artificial Neural Network (ANN) models were developed and compared. The results demonstrated a significant advantage of the ANN model over RSM, with an R2 value close to 1, indicating that ANN provides a more accurate analysis of the impact of process parameters on GRG variations. Finally, an optimization process was conducted, showing that GA outperformed the GRA approach in achieving more precise adjustments of speed frame parameters. The key findings of this study are summarized as follows:

The optimal speed frame parameters identified by ANN-GA as a reliable and accurate approach were a twist of 23 TPM, a break draft of 1.26, a spacer size of 5.1 mm, and an overhang of 3.5 mm. These optimized settings resulted in a significant improvement in polyester spun yarn quality, reducing CVm% from 12.77 to 11.07 and IPI from 69.33 to 20.7 compared to the initial settings.

The optimal speed frame parameters identified by ANN-GA as a reliable and accurate approach were a twist of 23 TPM, a break draft of 1.26, a spacer size of 5.1 mm, and an overhang of 3.5 mm. These optimized settings resulted in a significant improvement in polyester spun yarn quality, reducing CVm% from 12.77 to 11.07 and IPI from 69.33 to 20.7 compared to the initial settings.

Based on the sensitivity analysis of GRG values using both the GWM and the PDM, spacer size was identified as the most influential parameter affecting overall yarn quality, contributing 35.02% and 38.07% to the variations in GRG values according to each method, respectively.

The findings highlight the superior accuracy and reliability of AI-based modeling and optimization over statistical and multi-criteria decision-making methods. These techniques effectively enhance process performance and product quality, offering a promising alternative for complex industrial processes. This objective can be pursued by employing large language models, which are particularly well-suited for capturing complex and nonlinear relationships inherent in industrial processes. When combined with optimization techniques such as GA, deep learning has the potential to enable simultaneous adjustment and optimization across multiple stages, leading to significant improvements in operational efficiency, product consistency, and overall yarn quality.

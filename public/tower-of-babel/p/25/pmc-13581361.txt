EUS-guided hepatico-gastrostomy followed by antegrade digital cholangioscopy and laser lithotripsy for a large biliary stone in surgically altered anatomy (with video)
Authors: Amine Achemlal, Shrikant Mukewar, Bhushan Bhaware, Shubankar Godbole, Saurabh Mukewar
Published: 2026

Management of biliary stones in patients with surgically altered anatomy remains technically challenging because of limited access to the biliary tree. We report here the video case of a young patient with surgically altered anatomy (Roux-En-Y Hepatico-jejunostomy) who was referred for symptomatic biliary obstruction caused by a large impacted stone located at the hepatic duct confluence. Enteroscopy-assisted endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography was attempted but failed due to inability to reach the biliary anastomosis. Given the high morbidity associated with alternative surgical or percutaneous options, an EUS–guided approach was selected.

In the first stage, EUS-guided hepatico-gastrostomy was performed. Under linear EUS guidance (GF-UCT180, Olympus Medical Systems, Tokyo, Japan), a left intrahepatic bile duct was punctured from the gastric body, and a guidewire (0.035, Visiglide, Olympus Medical Systems, Tokyo, Japan) was advanced antegrade across the biliary obstruction. Tract dilation was achieved using a 7-Fr cystotome, followed by deployment of a partially covered self-expandable metal stent (GIOBOR, Taewoong Medical, Gimpo, Republic of Korea), creating a stable hepatico-gastric fistula.

In the second stage, antegrade digital cholangioscopy (Spyglass DS II, Boston Scientific, Marlborough, MA) was attempted through the hepatico-gastrostomy stent. However, sharp angulation caused by the metallic stent prevented adequate maneuverability of the cholangioscope [Figure 1A], precluding laser lithotripsy (Video 1).

Therefore, the metal stent was removed to straighten the access tract. A sphincterotome and guidewire were advanced antegrade into the bile duct, and the digital cholangioscope was railroaded over the guidewire, allowing stable access to the biliary confluence [Figure 1B]. The stone was directly visualized and successfully fragmented using laser lithotripsy [Figure 2]. Complete ductal clearance was achieved using antegrade balloon and basket extraction.

Endoscopic image of the laser lithotripsy for the management of the large biliary confluence stone.

The procedure was completed without adverse events. The patient experienced rapid clinical improvement, with normalization of liver function tests within days.

Given its technical complexity, this procedure requires significant expertise. To date, only a limited number of cases employing this 2-stage approach have been reported, particularly using laser lithotripsy for the treatment of biliary stones.[1,2]

This case highlights the safety, feasibility, and clinical value of a staged endoscopic strategy combining EUS-guided hepatico-gastrostomy with antegrade digital cholangioscopy and laser lithotripsy for the management of large biliary stones in patients with surgically altered anatomy.

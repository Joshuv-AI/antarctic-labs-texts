Optimising delivery models for evidence-based interventions to reduce stillbirth in Punjab, India: a study protocol for implementation research
Authors: Diksha Gautam, Anuj Kumar Pandey, Rupsa Banerjee, Ekta Saroha, Urvashi Gupta, Ritika Thalakiya, Sharbari Dutta, Sukesh Bharadwaj, Ranjeet K, Mamta Gupta, Inderdeep Kaur Sawhney, Arun Kumar Aggarwal, Sutapa B Neogi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundStillbirth remains a major global health challenge, with India bearing a substantial share of the burden. Despite the availability of evidence-based interventions, stillbirth rates (SBRs) remain high due to gaps in healthcare access, quality and the effective delivery of maternal and neonatal care. This study aims to develop and implement an optimised, context-specific model to reduce SBRs in Sangrur district, Punjab.Methods and analysisThis mixed-methods implementation research will adopt a sequential explanatory design. The study will be conducted over 3 years in four blocks of Sangrur. Data will be collected through baseline and endline surveys, verbal autopsies of stillbirths, direct observations of antenatal and intrapartum care, and qualitative interviews with community members and healthcare providers. The intervention package will focus on preconception and antepartum care, intrapartum care and strengthening health systems. The study will use the plan-do-check-act model for continuous improvement, and real-time data collection through electronic systems will support timely decision-making.The study expects to achieve a 25% reduction in SBRs through the optimised delivery of high-quality antenatal and intrapartum care services. Additionally, the research will provide critical evidence on the barriers and facilitators to optimise service delivery, as well as insights into the health system and community factors influencing stillbirth outcomes. This study aims to create a scalable and adaptable intervention model to reduce SBRs in low-resource settings like Sangrur and Punjab. The findings will inform future maternal and neonatal health policies and provide a framework for the broader implementation of similar interventions in other regions of India.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol has been approved by the International Institute of Health Management Research, Delhi (IIHMR) Institutional Ethics Committee (IRB/2024-2025/01). The study is funded through a competitive call for proposals on stillbirths by the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) under the National Health Research Priority Projects (5/7BMIPR/2022-RBMCH). The research has been awarded by ICMR (project ID NHRP05586) to IIHMR under grant number 5/7/BMIPR/2022-RCN.

Introduction

Stillbirth remains a significant public health challenge globally, with approximately 2 million cases reported annually. The decline in neonatal mortality and stillbirth rates (SBRs) has been slower compared with other maternal and child health indicators over recent decades. Between 2000 and 2019, the global annual rate of reduction in the SBR was 2.3% (90% CI 1.7% to 2.7%), compared with 2.9% (90% CI 2.5% to 3.2%) for the neonatal mortality rate, and 4.3% (90% CI 3.8% to 4.7%) for children aged 1–59 months.1 During the same period, the maternal mortality ratio (MMR) decreased by 34%.2 These trends highlight the relatively slow progress in addressing stillbirths, despite significant global efforts to improve maternal and child health outcomes through advancements in medical science and public health interventions.

India, which accounts for the highest burden of stillbirths worldwide, reports a SBR of 13.9 per 1000 births, reflecting substantial inequities in healthcare access and quality.3 However, a majority of these stillbirths are preventable through evidence-based interventions across the continuum of pregnancy and labour.

A significant variation in SBR is evident across various reporting systems. According to the Sample Registration System (SRS),4 India has achieved a SBR of 3.8 per 1000 live births,4 successfully meeting the Every Newborn Action Plan (ENAP) targets for 2030. On the contrary, the Health Information Management System recorded a SBR of 12.4 per 1000 births5 occurring at 28 weeks of gestation or later in 2019, while the United Nations Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation (UN IGME) estimated the SBR at 12.2 (9.9–15.1) per 1000 in 2021.6 In the documentation processes for stillbirths used by SRS, several challenges were identified by Dandona et al, including inconsistencies in the definition, inadequate documentation of gestational age and misclassification of miscarriages and abortion, which may contribute to under-reporting of stillbirths.3

Besides disparities in reporting, a key challenge in monitoring progress towards the ENAP targets is establishing clear definitions of stillbirths, which severely impacts the estimation of its true burden. There is considerable variation in how stillbirth is defined by different organisations based on gestational age. The WHO, Lancet Stillbirth Epidemiology Investigator Group, UN IGME and previous Global Burden of Disease studies use a cut-off of 28 weeks or longer. In contrast, the International Classification of Diseases-11 (2022) defines stillbirth from 22 weeks, while the Centre for Disease Control uses 20 weeks as the cut-off.

Mony et al highlighted that incomplete data recording, approximation of gestational ages and birth weights to the nearest multiple of decimals, and lack of standardised recording practices—such as differentiating antepartum and intrapartum stillbirths and declaration of fetal condition at the time of stillbirth or identification of congenital anomalies—have led to misclassification of stillbirths and neonatal deaths.7

These challenges in identification and prevention, classification and reporting of stillbirths underscore the need for context-specific implementation strategies.8 Evidence-based interventions, such as improved antenatal care (ANC), timely identification of high-risk pregnancies (HRP) and skilled intrapartum care, have demonstrated effectiveness in reducing stillbirths.9 10 However, despite their potential, the implementation of these interventions remains suboptimal in low-resource settings, including India.11 Challenges, such as inequities in healthcare access, inadequate health system infrastructure and sociocultural barriers, contribute to this implementation gap. This gap highlights the importance of implementation research, which focuses on understanding the best strategies to translate evidence into practice effectively within real-world healthcare systems.12

Robust research is required to identify barriers and facilitators to the effective delivery and uptake of evidence-based interventions within national health programmes designed to promote healthy pregnancy outcomes.13 Against all these factors, it becomes paramount to formulate robust implementation strategies that offer a systematic approach to enhance their reach and integrate them within the existing health systems framework, ensuring a cohesive delivery within the larger ambit of healthcare infrastructure.13

Hence, this study aims to explore an optimised, scalable and contextually tailored implementation model of an evidence-based intervention package to enhance the delivery and uptake of high-quality antenatal and intrapartum care services to substantially reduce SBRs in a district in Punjab, India.

By leveraging an implementation research approach, the study aims to enhance the scalability and sustainability of these interventions, contributing to broader national and global goals, such as the ‘ENAP’ and India’s ‘National Health Mission’.14 15

Conceptual framework

The conceptual framework underpinning this implementation research will be based on two established frameworks, drawing insights from the published literature on health system factors contributing to stillbirths1618 (figure 1). The first framework is the WHO health systems framework,19 which encompasses six interconnected building blocks of health systems: service delivery, health workforce, health information systems, access to essential medicines and technologies, financing, and leadership/governance. This conceptual framework will provide a structured foundation for identifying gaps, designing intervention models and evaluating outcomes, ensuring that the study contributes to sustainable reductions in SBRs while strengthening the overall health system.

Conceptual framework guiding the implementation study based on the WHO health system framework and Tanahashi framework.

The study will also leverage the Tanahashi framework20 for health service coverage to systematically analyse and address gaps in the delivery and uptake of high-quality antenatal and intrapartum care services. The Tanahashi framework, with its five domains of coverage—availability, accessibility, acceptability, contact coverage and effective coverage—provides a structured approach to identify bottlenecks and design contextually relevant interventions to reduce SBRs. Our approach would be to first collect data based on the health systems framework; further, the Tanahashi model will dwell more on the ‘health service delivery’ component of the health systems framework. The interpretation of Tanahashi model findings will be aligned with the health systems framework.

Both frameworks will be adapted and contextualised to the specific needs of the Sangrur district in Punjab. The evaluation of implementation strategies and the models developed and implemented shall be done using the Implementation Research Logic Model.

Methods

The protocol is prepared in accordance with the Standards for Reporting Implementation Studies guidelines.21

Study design

The implementation research study will adopt a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design, integrating both quantitative and qualitative research methodologies. This mixed-methods approach leverages the complementary strengths of both paradigms, enabling a comprehensive analysis that captures both the breadth of numerical data and the nuanced depth of qualitative insights. The sequential design structure ensures that qualitative data will serve to elucidate and expand on the initial quantitative findings, thereby enriching the overall interpretation of the results. While qualitative assessment would enable us to explore the facilitators, barriers and ways to overcome those, quantitative assessment would lend objective measures on key indicators. Analysis of secondary data (from existing sources such as the Health Management Information System, SRS) will serve as the basis for all other assessments. Primary data will be collected to quantify the SBR, coverage of ANC services, quality of antenatal and intrapartum care, and availability and accessibility of health facilities. The study was initiated in July 2024 with formal tool development, ethics approval process and deciding on strategic intervention and implementation plan. The study is expected to be completed by July 2027.

Study population and setting

The study will be implemented in Sangrur district of Punjab (figure 2), a northern state in India, over a duration of 3 years. Despite Punjab being a relatively wealthier state, the spatial risk of MMR is greater than 1 (compared with the national average), and a higher risk relative to itself over the past 5 years.22 This, combined with a demonstrated commitment of the state officials to address the burgeoning problem and proximity to the host institute, made Punjab a preferred choice for the study.

Map of the study site.

According to the 2011 Census of India, Sangrur district has a population of 1.65 million with a sex ratio of 885 per 1000 males and an average literacy rate of 68%. The district comprises both rural and urban areas and includes seven Tehsils, which are further divided into eight blocks. Public health facilities in the district include 212 primary care, 12 secondary care and 4 tertiary care facilities.

The study population for baseline and endline surveys and, also for implementation strategies, will encompass community as well as health systems actors:

Community: pregnant and recently delivered women and their families.

Sample size and data collection

The study will be conducted in four blocks of Sangrur district, covering approximately 1 million population, selected on the basis of the location of the tertiary care facilities providing maternal and child healthcare. Sample size and assessment of various study outcomes are described below:

The SBR will be estimated by a house-to-house survey of women who have given birth in the past year, adopting a probability proportional to size sampling strategy at the village and urban enumeration blocks. Assuming a household size of four and a crude birth rate of 10 per 1000 population, approximately 50 000–60 000 households will be surveyed until the sample size of 2000 births is reached. Data will be collected through the interview of the primary respondent regarding any pregnancies in the past year. If yes, then the household will be deemed eligible and informed consent will be sought. Data will be obtained regarding sociodemographic details, pregnancy outcomes (abortion/live birth/stillbirth/neonatal death), gestational age at delivery, place of delivery (public facility/private facility/home) and mode of delivery (vaginal/caesarean).Endline: the study aims to achieve a 25% reduction in the SBR. Assuming an endline SBR of 9 per 1000 births, a sample size of 2700, rounded off to 3000 births, is estimated at a significance level of 95%, a relative error of 15% and a design effect of 1.5. Data captured will include the same variables as the baseline assessment.

The preparatory and formative phase will focus on establishing a technical support unit, obtaining approvals, recruitment and training of the research team, finalising data collection tools and conducting a baseline assessment. A review of secondary data for maternal and child health indicators collated at the district level will also be undertaken to analyse whether any gaps exist in reporting health data.

Implementation model codevelopment and implementation phases will be undertaken in liaison with block and district management teams to improve uptake. The implementation team will consist of health system functionaries and other partners who are involved in the implementation of programmatic and health activities in the district (including administrators, teams involved in programme management, care provision/services at facilities and community levels, supervisors, logistics management, data handling and others). This is also expected to strengthen the process of monitoring and supportive supervision, and to regularly update the progress of the programme.

The consolidation phase will focus on strengthening health systems and care providers to continue the intervention activities beyond the period of the study.

Intervention package

The comprehensive intervention package (table 2) designed for this implementation research aims to address the multifaceted factors contributing to stillbirths, with a focus on improving maternal and neonatal health outcomes. It will be aligned with existing national public health programmes for maternal and child health and will include three focus areas: preconception and antepartum care (counselling, health promotion, nutritional supplementation, early identification and registration of pregnancy, provision of quality ANC, identification and management of HRP); intrapartum care (care during labour and active management of complications) and health systems strengthening (mother and child tracking system, stillbirth surveillance, compliance to quality standards for maternal and newborn care).

Key interventions include improving ANC protocols, enhancing the presence and competencies of skilled birth attendants, strengthening referral pathways to higher-level facilities for HRP and following good practices, including caesarean sections wherever indicated.23 Training programmes will be developed for doctors, nurses, midwives and community health workers (ASHAs), ensuring they are well versed in stillbirth prevention practices, risk identification and timely management of complications. Community engagement is crucial, with awareness campaigns designed to educate women and families on the importance of regular ANC visits, recognising signs of fetal distress and ensuring skilled delivery.24 Real-time data collection systems will be implemented to track key indicators such as ANC coverage, birth outcomes and referral timeliness, facilitating adaptive management and continuous improvement. The monitoring and evaluation framework will focus on measuring reductions in SBRs, increased skilled birth attendance and overall improvements in healthcare quality. Regular feedback loops will involve all stakeholders in decision-making and course correction.

Our existing guidelines, programmes and monitoring mechanisms have typically been framed through a delivery-centric lens, that is, the objective has been the delivery of certain interventions at certain times by a certain category of workers. However, in order to attain the stillbirth target set by India Newborn Action Plan (INAP), we need to shift this perspective from a delivery-centric to a user-centric lens. Thus, this package will be tailored to the specific needs and context of the target population, integrating evidence-based practices and addressing the social, cultural and systemic barriers obtained through formative research. The package will incorporate strategies for engaging families and communities in ways that are respectful of local customs and beliefs.

The intervention will be implemented across multiple levels of the health system, from the community to the healthcare facility, and will be inclusive of both individual and systemic interventions.

Presumptive implementation strategy

We will refer to the Expert Recommendations for Implementing Change (ERIC) discrete implementation strategies list,25 select and test strategies out of that list to adapt, optimise and sustain the interventions. The strategies may be amended based on the findings from formative research and discussion with health system stakeholders. However, during the project lifetime, new strategies can be opted from the ERIC list as per the need.

The strategy will involve refining and integrating evidence-based interventions into the existing maternal care framework, ensuring that healthcare providers and community health workers are adequately trained and equipped. The evolution process begins with the ‘minus model’ at the protocol stage, transitioning to ‘model 0’ after the formative phase and eventually culminating in ‘model 1’ on study completion. The implementation will occur over 20 months, divided into a pilot phase for 2 months and a full-scale implementation for 18 months (figure 3).

Implementation strategy framework to develop an optimised intervention package. HRP, high-risk pregnancies.

During full-scale implementation, the plan-do-check-act (PDCA) approach will guide systematic observations and iterative refinements. Small-scale tests of change in cyclical fashion will inform improvements before wider adoption, with six 3-month cycles running concurrently across different implementation components. Each cycle comprises four stages: (1) plan: identify the problem and define potential solutions by answering critical questions such as ‘who, what, where and when’; (2) do: implement the planned solutions; (3) check: document challenges, gather data and assess the effectiveness of the intervention and (4) act: analyse findings, extract lessons and refine the approach for the next cycle. This iterative process allows for adaptive learning, continuous quality improvement and proactive problem-solving. Through these cycles, the study will tackle key barriers, align with local healthcare infrastructure and optimise the delivery of evidence-based interventions to reduce SBRs. The structured approach ensures robust documentation of challenges and solutions, fostering scalability and sustainability.

The district will implement strategies such as radio announcements, community announcements, pamphlets and posters, nukkad natak (street plays) and counselling services to enhance awareness and demand for appropriate pregnancy and intrapartum care by strengthening existing platforms and developing innovative ones for integration into the current delivery system. The research team will collaborate with implementers to organise, harmonise and reinforce existing monitoring systems at two levels. First, efforts will focus on optimising the timely collection of existing indicators for quality and progress. Second, essential and feasible indicators will be developed in consultation with implementers to ensure effective monitoring without overburdening the health system. A platform will be established to recognise and reward champions at various levels—programme management, facility and community. Adopting a holistic approach, interventions will target end-users to improve service uptake and adherence. ANM at subcentres, community health officers at health and wellness centres and ASHAs will play a key role in following up with families experiencing HRP.

Moreover, the strategy includes policy advocacy to scale successful interventions across Punjab, ensuring long-term sustainability. Through a well-coordinated effort involving healthcare providers, community health workers, local authorities and families, this strategy aims to create a sustainable, evidence-based approach to reduce stillbirths in Sangrur, contributing to improved maternal and neonatal health outcomes.

By the end of the study, the model would have evolved into an optimised and context-specific delivery framework tailored for Sangrur, Punjab, which can serve as a reference for broader application in similar low-resource settings.

Data management and analysis plan

Qualitative data analysis

The qualitative data will be transcribed verbatim and translated into English. Transcriptions will be analysed via NVivo software using theoretical coding to derive themes and subthemes. Key findings will supplement the findings from the quantitative study. Reporting of qualitative findings will follow the standards f reporting qualitative research checklist and will include the description of themes and reporting of verbatim wherever applicable.

Quantitative data analysis

Real-time quantitative data will be collected via an electronic Data Capture System. Field investigators will undergo comprehensive training in data collection instruments and management. Data will be collected, stored and monitored using RedCap software, which will be managed at the International Institute of Health Management Research, Delhi. This platform will serve as a secure and robust system for precise data collection, organisation and statistical analysis. Routine reports generated for monitoring will be shared with the central team, enabling real-time adjustments to implementation strategies.

Data collection forms will be digitally accessible via mobile devices, ensuring seamless synchronisation and streamlined data verification. Field investigators will synchronise collected data with the central server daily, enabling real-time updates. Field supervisors will oversee timely data completion and perform random validation checks to ensure accuracy. Data extraction will be secured through password-protected logins, with deletion rights exclusively assigned to the principal investigator, safeguarding data integrity and security. The collected data will be analysed using CSV (Comma-separated values) exports. Summary statistics will describe the burden of stillbirth and utilisation of services. Bivariate and multivariable regression analyses (absolute and relative) will evaluate preintervention and postintervention changes that are statistically significant (p <0.05, 95% CI), clinically relevant and research-backed variables affecting stillbirths.

Data quality

Robust quality assurance measures will be implemented to ensure data accuracy, completeness and timeliness. The measures include (a) development of a unified study protocol, implementation framework and data collection tools through a consultative process involving investigators, experts and district authorities; (b) regular oversight by the research team, led by the investigators and project coordinator, ensuring adherence to the protocol and rigorous implementation through field visits; (c) implementation of internal quality checks at study sites to ensure data consistency, completeness and accuracy; (d) use of an electronic data capture system (RedCap) with built-in range checks and logic validations for quantitative data, and trained personnel for qualitative data collection and analysis; (e) database management with built-in consistency checks, double data entry and tablet-based data collection for the cross-sectional survey.

Ethics and dissemination

The study protocol has been approved by the IIHMR Delhi Institutional Ethics Committee (IRB/2024-2025/01) . The Ethics Committee will be kept informed about any change in the protocol that may occur during the course of the study. Relevant clearances will be obtained from the national and state-level authorities. Informed consent will be obtained in accordance with the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) ethical guidelines. Consent forms will be translated into the local language, ensuring readability and administered by trained interviewers. Literate participants will read and sign the form, while non-literate participants will have it read aloud in the presence of a witness, with thumb impressions obtained. Copies of the signed forms will be provided to participants. Data will be anonymised and handled with strict confidentiality. Data will be retained only as necessary for the study and permanently deleted once its purpose is fulfilled.

Preliminary findings will be communicated to the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, respective State Health Departments, program officers at various administrative levels (state, district and block), community health stakeholders and the ICMR for internal review and feedback. The research team will organise consultations with all relevant stakeholders to discuss, refine and validate the findings, which will form the basis for developing actionable recommendations. The final study outcomes will be disseminated through a dedicated event for critical stakeholders. These findings will also be shared via policy briefs, presentations at national and international scientific forums and publications in peer-reviewed journals, ensuring wide reach and impact.

The iterative PDCA approach ensures that the model is refined continuously, addressing local challenges while maintaining core intervention components. This balance between contextual adaptation and intervention fidelity enhances the potential for scalability.

The detailed documentation of the model’s evolution, including the processes of adaptation, stakeholder engagement and capacity-building, will provide a blueprint for scaling the interventions in other regions. By identifying and addressing barriers such as gaps in healthcare infrastructure, limited workforce capacity and community awareness, the study creates a foundation for expanding the model to broader geographies. Furthermore, the evaluation of the model’s impact on reducing SBRs and improving service delivery will generate evidence to support its adoption at the regional, state or national levels.

The model’s scalability is also supported by its alignment with existing health systems and resources, minimising additional financial and operational burdens. By ensuring cost-effectiveness, feasibility and sustainability, the study aims to promote widespread adoption, ultimately contributing to significant reductions in SBRs across similar resource-limited settings.

Expected outcome

The study aims to develop a context-specific, codesigned implementation model to enhance equitable coverage and improve the quality of interventions addressing stillbirths in Sangrur. This adaptable and scalable model is expected to reduce SBRs by 25%, improving maternal and neonatal health outcomes. The study will also provide a comprehensive understanding of stillbirth burdens and risk factors, offering evidence to inform future policies. By documenting the iterative evolution process, the research will assess the model’s feasibility, scalability and impact, providing actionable insights for future program designs and scaling efforts.

Discussion

This innovative implementation research study aims to design and evaluate a context-specific, scalable model to reduce SBRs in Sangrur, Punjab, addressing the high stillbirth burden in India. Despite improvements in maternal health, India remains a significant contributor to global stillbirths, highlighting gaps in access and quality of care.27 The study aims to reduce SBRs and provide valuable insights into designing sustainable interventions in resource-constrained settings.

The proposed intervention is firmly anchored in national public health programs and established guidelines, such as those for community awareness and mobilisation, and health systems strengthening. The incorporation of community engagement strategies, real-time data collection systems and capacity-building initiatives for healthcare providers is central to ensure the intervention’s efficacy and sustainability.28 These strategies are aligned with robust evidence suggesting that well-integrated healthcare delivery systems can dramatically mitigate maternal and neonatal mortality and morbidity.29

A defining strength of this intervention lies in its adaptability to the unique sociocultural and systemic contexts of the target population, resonating with global recommendations advocating for optimising interventions to fit specific populations and settings.30

The application of the PDCA iterative framework for continuous improvement is a cornerstone of the study’s implementation strategy. The PDCA model has been widely recognised for its efficacy in fostering adaptability, promoting local ownership and ensuring the sustainability of health system interventions.31 By embedding rigorous evaluation mechanisms, the study seeks to measure not only the primary outcome of reduced SBRs but also secondary outcomes such as improvements in the quality-of-service delivery and overall system efficiency.

The intervention’s comprehensive approach spans all levels of the health system, engaging community health workers, midwives, medical officers and district-level administrators. This multitiered engagement is designed to ensure system-wide integration and the seamless implementation of evidence-based practices.32

Collaboration between government entities, non-governmental organisations and community stakeholders is another pivotal element of this initiative. Such partnerships are expected to facilitate robust policy advocacy, ensuring that successful components of the intervention are institutionalised and scaled beyond the study’s geographic boundaries.

By integrating these diverse elements into a cohesive and context-specific framework, the study not only aims to address the immediate challenge of reducing stillbirths in Sangrur but also to contribute to the broader discourse on maternal and neonatal health in low-resource settings.

However, the study faces several challenges, including achieving effective community engagement influenced by cultural perceptions and local resistance. Variability in healthcare infrastructure across the district could hinder the standardisation of the intervention. Coordinating between various levels of healthcare providers may be complex, leading to misalignment or communication issues. Resistance to behavioural changes among healthcare workers and slow adoption of new protocols could limit effectiveness. Additionally, the short study duration may not allow for the assessment of long-term outcomes, and the complexity of multilevel assessments may complicate result interpretation.33 Sociopolitical and economic factors may also disrupt the implementation process, affecting the intervention’s consistency and sustainability.

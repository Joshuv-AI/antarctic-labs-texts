Comprehensive evidence for the pathogenicity of the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant specific to the Japanese population

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant is specific to the Japanese population and has long remained a variant of uncertain significance. Because BRCA1/2 genetic testing serves as a companion diagnostic for poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitor therapy in Japan, unresolved classification has limited access to targeted treatment.MethodsWe conducted a nationwide study using the national Hereditary Breast and Ovarian Cancer registry. Pathogenicity was assessed through quantitative cosegregation analysis, a systematic review of functional and clinical evidence and evaluation of variant frequency in population databases. Evidence was integrated using the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 Variant Curation Expert Panel framework.ResultsWe identified 44 carriers from 35 distinct Japanese families. Quantitative cosegregation analysis yielded a combined likelihood ratio of approximately 60, satisfying the PP1_Strong criterion. This was supported by robust evidence from multiple functional studies, including Mixed-All-Nominated-in-One-BRCA, homology-directed repair and saturation genome editing assays (PS3) and a reported case of Fanconi anaemia (PM3_Moderate). This variant is rare, with an allele frequency of 6.20×10–7 in gnomAD and 1.06×10–4 in ToMMo 61KJPN (PM2_Supporting). Although the BayesDel noAF score met the benign criterion (BP4), other in silico tools indicated deleterious effects. In total, the accumulated evidence reached 10 points, corresponding to an estimated odds of pathogenicity of 895:1, exceeding the ‘Pathogenic’ threshold.ConclusionBy integrating newly collected genetic data with functional and clinical evidence, we provide robust support for reclassifying the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant as ‘Pathogenic’. This reclassification is crucial for ensuring that eligible Japanese patients gain access to appropriate targeted therapies.Keywords: Genetic Testing; Neoplasms; Genetics, Medical; Mutation, Missense; Genetic Predisposition to Disease

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

The BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant is enriched in Japan and has been reported in multiple Japanese families, but major databases and laboratories continue to classify it as a variant of uncertain significance (VUS). In 2023, we demonstrated its potential pathogenicity using the Mixed-All-Nominated-in-One-BRCA (MANO-B) functional assay, but a consensus classification has not been achieved. Because BRCA1/2 genetic testing serves as a companion diagnostic for poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor therapy under Japan’s universal health insurance system, the unresolved classification of this variant has direct clinical and therapeutic implications.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

We assembled the largest Japanese cohort to date of carriers of the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant and collected multiple lines of genetic, clinical and functional evidence to clarify its pathogenicity. Quantitative cosegregation analysis supported its causality (PP1_Strong). Integration of orthogonal functional data sets, including MANO-B, homology-directed repair (HDR) and saturation genome editing assays (PS3), together with evidence from a Fanconi anaemia case (PM3_Moderate) and population rarity (PM2_Supporting), demonstrated that the cumulative evidence exceeds the threshold for ‘Pathogenic’ classification when evaluated under the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 Variant Curation Expert Panel (VCEP) framework. This study resolves the previous VUS conflict for this Japanese-specific variant and provides robust evidence for clinical reclassification.

HOW THIS STUDY MIGHT AFFECT RESEARCH, PRACTICE OR POLICY

This study provides a reproducible model for resolving uncertain BRCA2 missense variants through quantitative, evidence-based evaluation aligned with the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP framework. Reclassification of the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant to ‘Pathogenic’ would expand patient eligibility for PARP inhibitor therapy in Japan, where BRCA1/2 genetic testing determines access to targeted treatment. The findings also highlight the clinical utility of multiplex functional assays, including MANO-B, HDR and saturation genome editing assays, and support their integration into expert-panel recommendations and routine variant curation practice.

Introduction

In 2023, we reported the pathogenicity of the Japanese-specific variant, NM_000059.4(BRCA2):c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe), which had previously been classified as a variant of uncertain significance (VUS).1 Despite accumulating evidence, a consensus on its clinical significance has not yet been reached. As of this writing, ClinVar lists conflicting interpretations, and the variant remains classified as a VUS by several clinical laboratories, including Myriad Genetics (Salt Lake City, Utah, USA). Because BRCA1/2 genetic testing by Myriad Genetics is designated as a companion diagnostic for poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor therapy under Japan’s publicly funded universal health insurance system, current interpretations directly influence treatment decisions. These circumstances prompted us to initiate a nationwide, multi-institutional study in Japan to collect additional genetic and clinical evidence to support variant reclassification.

Since our initial report, several new lines of compelling evidence have emerged. Hayashi et al recently described three additional Japanese families harbouring this variant,2 and multiple functional studies, including homology-directed repair (HDR) assays3 4 and CRISPR-based saturation genome editing,5 6 have provided robust support for its pathogenicity. Importantly, the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 Variant Curation Expert Panel (VCEP) has recently released a BRCA1/2-specific refinement of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and Association for Molecular Pathology (ACMG/AMP) guidelines.7 8 This specification incorporates a quantitative framework that applies a Bayesian classification system to model ACMG/AMP criteria. Using this refined framework, we re-evaluated the pathogenicity of the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant by integrating newly collected genetic, clinical and functional evidence (figure 1).

Methods

Pathogenicity assessment was performed according to the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP framework (V.1.2.0).

Ascertainment of families harbouring the variant

Collaborating with the Japanese Organization of Hereditary Breast and Ovarian Cancer (JOHBOC), which has managed Japan’s largest Hereditary Breast and Ovarian Cancer (HBOC) registry since 2015,9 10 we engaged in a nationwide, multi-institutional effort to identify families carrying the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant from August 2021 to September 2025. The JOHBOC registry contained approximately 19 300 individuals who had undergone BRCA1/2 genetic testing by July 2025, including about 5900 individuals with previously classified pathogenic variants. Individuals carrying the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant were identified using both the JOHBOC registry and genetic testing records from participating institutions. We also included cases from prior publications.1 2 Clinical information was collected systematically from probands and their relatives, including ethnicity, sex, age at diagnosis, tumour subtype and detailed family history. Tumour subtypes were classified on a per-tumour basis; in bilateral breast cancer, each tumour was counted separately.

Quantitative cosegregation analysis

Quantitative cosegregation analysis is a well-established method for assessing the pathogenicity of BRCA1/2 variants. The ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP recommends two web-based tools for this purpose: COOL V.3 (http://bjfenglab.org/)11 and CAL-Leiden (https://bioexp.net/cosegregation/),12 both of which are based on Bayesian survival models. COOL accommodates multiple cancer types and allows the use of population-specific incidence rates, including Japanese data, making it broadly applicable across diverse cohorts. However, it does not explicitly model contralateral breast cancer and therefore recommends censoring follow-up at the time of the first cancer diagnosis. In contrast, CAL-Leiden explicitly incorporates contralateral breast cancer and provides flexible options to consider all cancers, only the first cancer or the first cancer plus contralateral breast cancer. Although CAL-Leiden uses smoothed incidence rates, its population data are limited to UK and Dutch cohorts. To ensure a comprehensive evaluation, we applied both tools in this study to leverage their respective strengths.

Review of functional evidence from previous studies

In this study, we did not perform any new functional experiments. Instead, we systematically reviewed functional data for the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant that had already been generated in our previous work and in independent large-scale assays. First, we used the quantitative results of the Mixed-All-Nominated-in-One-BRCA (MANO-B) assay for this variant, as reported in our earlier study.1 Second, we reviewed recent high-throughput functional studies that evaluated BRCA2 missense variants using HDR assays3 4 or CRISPR/Cas9-mediated saturation genome editing.5 6 For each study, we extracted the assay type, the quantitative functional score and the classification category assigned to this variant according to the authors’ predefined thresholds.

Database and in silico analyses

Building on our previous report,1 we performed an updated literature and database review, together with in silico analysis, to re-evaluate the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant. Allele frequencies across different populations were obtained from public genome databases: gnomAD V.4.1.0, V.3.1.2 and V.2.1.1 (https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org) for global and East Asian populations,13 ToMMo 61KJPN (https://jmorp.megabank.tohoku.ac.jp) for the Japanese population,14 the ChinaMAP (http://www.mbiobank.com) for the Chinese population,15 Korean Variant Archive (KOVA) (https://www.kobic.re.kr/kova) for the Korean population,16 Thai Reference Exome Database (T-Rex) (https://trex.nbt.or.th) for the Thai population,17 and the 1000 Vietnamese Genome Project (VN1K) (https://genome.vinbigdata.org/) for the Vietnamese population. Disease databases were also reviewed, including ClinVar (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar/), Human Gene Mutation Database (HGMD) (https://www.hgmd.cf.ac.uk/ac/index.php) and BRCA exchange (https://brcaexchange.org/). For in silico analyses, we employed seven prediction algorithms: AlphaMissense (https://alphamissense.hegelab.org/),18 BayesDel (https://fenglab.chpc.utah.edu/BayesDel.html),19 Combined Annotation Dependent Depletion (CADD) (https://cadd.gs.washington.edu/),20 MutationTaster 2025 (https://www.genecascade.org/MutationTaster2025/index.html),21 Polymorphism Phenotyping-2 (PolyPhen-2) (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/),22 Rare Exome Variant Ensemble Learner (REVEL) (https://sites.google.com/site/revelgenomics/about),23 and Sorting Intolerant from Tolerant (SIFT) (https://sift.bii.a-star.edu.sg/).24

Results

Clinical features of individuals carrying the variant

Clinical characteristics of the individuals carrying the variant are summarised in online supplemental table 1. A total of 44 Japanese individuals from 35 families were assessed. Of these, 38 were female, among whom 32 had developed breast cancer. The age at first diagnosis ranged from the late 20s to the late 70s, with a median age of 48 years. In total, 42 breast tumours were identified, including 10 bilateral cases. The most frequent subtype was oestrogen receptor (ER)/progesterone receptor (PR)-positive and HER2-negative (n=19), followed by triple-negative (n=9), ER-positive/PR-negative/HER2-negative (n=5) and ER/PR/HER2-positive (n=5). Four female individuals carrying the variant developed ovarian cancer, all of which were high-grade serous adenocarcinomas. The age at first ovarian cancer diagnosis ranged from the early 50s to the early 70s, with a median age of 66.5 years. Six male individuals carrying the variant were identified: three developed prostate cancer in their early 60s, late 70s and early 80s, and one developed pancreatic cancer in his late 60s. At the time of study enrolment, the remaining two individuals were in their early 30s and late 30s, respectively, and had not developed cancer.

Quantitative cosegregation analysis

Of the 35 families identified to date, 9 were considered informative for quantitative cosegregation analysis, as genotype data were available for more than two individuals. These included Families 1, 2 and 8–12 reported by us, as well as Cases 2 and 3 reported by Hayashi et al.2 We then performed quantitative cosegregation analysis using a Bayes factor-based approach to evaluate the likelihood of a variant being causal versus non-causal. This method accounts for the age-specific penetrance of BRCA2-related HBOC phenotypes and background population prevalence. Likelihood ratios (LRs) for pathogenicity were calculated separately for each of the nine families and then combined by multiplication, yielding overall LRs of 58.44 and 60.80 using the COOL and CAL-Leiden tools, respectively (online supplemental table 2). According to the ClinGen ENIGMA VCEP specifications,7 which adopt the LR thresholds proposed by Tavtigian et al,25 both values meet the criteria for ‘PP1_Strong’ (strong evidence from cosegregation with disease in multiple affected family members; 18.7<LR<350).

Functional evidence from previously published assays

Next, we summarised results from multiple independent functional studies that included the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant (table 1). In our previous work,1 we demonstrated the deleterious effect of the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant using the MANO-B assay, a high-throughput functional test that classifies BRCA2 variants by assessing sensitivity to PARP inhibitors in BRCA2-deficient cells.1 26 Consistent with this finding, two independent studies from separate laboratories have recently demonstrated the pathogenic effect of this variant using HDR assays.3 4 The ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP has adopted these assays as well-established functional studies and has therefore applied ‘PS3’ (strong evidence from well-established in vitro or in vivo functional studies supportive of a damaging effect).7 Moreover, recent saturation genome editing studies have substantially improved the classification of BRCA2 variants with high resolution and confidence. Two independent large-scale studies systematically evaluated nearly all possible single nucleotide variants within the DNA-binding domain of BRCA2 using CRISPR/Cas9-based high-throughput functional assays in human stem or haploid cell lines.5 6 In both data sets, the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant was consistently assigned to the most deleterious category in the seven-tier functional classification based on a VarCall Bayesian model,27 providing strong additional support for its pathogenicity.

Additional clinical, population-based and computational evidence

As we previously reported,1 Mori et al described a Japanese patient with Fanconi anaemia who carried compound heterozygous BRCA2 variants: c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) and c.475+1G>A.28 This patient showed increased chromosome breakage in mitomycin C-treated lymphocytes and exhibited typical Fanconi anaemia-related physical features, including short stature and microcephaly, thereby fulfilling the criteria established by the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP.7 To date, this remains the only reported case of Fanconi anaemia harbouring the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant. Given that the co-occurring c.475+1G>A is a known pathogenic variant in trans, application of ‘PM3_Moderate’ (moderate evidence from co-occurrence in trans with a pathogenic variant) is appropriate.

As shown in table 2, the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant was not listed in major global population databases, including gnomAD V.2.1.1 (non-cancer, primarily based on exome sequencing) and gnomAD V.3.1.2 (non-cancer, based exclusively on whole-genome sequencing). In contrast, it was detected in 13 individuals in ToMMo 61KJPN, with an allele frequency of 1.06×10−4 and in a single East Asian individual in gnomAD V.4.1.0. The variant appears to be restricted to the Japanese population, with no entries in other Asian population databases (ChinaMAP, KOVA, T-Rex and VN1K) or in global cohorts. Thus, this variant meets the criterion for ‘PM2_Supporting’ (supporting evidence from absence or rarity in ethnically matched control cohorts). This distribution pattern suggests a potential founder effect in the Japanese population.

Regarding disease databases, no definitive classification has been assigned by either Myriad Genetics or ClinVar. HGMD categorises this variant as ‘Disease causing mutation?’, while it is absent from BRCA Exchange. For in silico analyses, AlphaMissense showed a score of 0.657, classified as ‘Likely_pathogenic’. The CADD PHRED score was 25.8, suggesting a deleterious effect. The variant was also predicted to be ‘Disease causing’ by MutationTaster 2025, ‘Probably damaging’ with a score of 1.0 by PolyPhen-2 and ‘Damaging’ with a score of 0.0 by SIFT. In contrast, the BayesDel noAF score was 0.118668, and REVEL score was 0.544, neither of which provides strong evidence for pathogenicity. The ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP designates BayesDel noAF as the sole bioinformatic tool for BRCA2 variant evaluation, based on comparative benchmarking and its ability to score in-frame indels. According to these specifications, missense variants located within clinically important domains of BRCA2 with a BayesDel noAF score ≤0.18 are considered to have no predicted functional impact and are thus eligible for the application of ‘BP4’ (benign-supporting evidence from in silico prediction).

Integrated evidence-based classification under the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP framework

As summarised in table 3, the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant meets the pathogenic criteria ‘PP1_Strong’, ‘PS3’, ‘PM3_Moderate’ and ‘PM2_Supporting’, as well as the benign criterion ‘BP4’. Under the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP point-based framework, the variant receives two Strong (4 points each), one Moderate (2 points) and one Supporting (1 point) for pathogenic criteria (4 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 11), and one Supporting benign criterion (−1 point), resulting in a total of 10 points. Given that each point corresponds to a LR of approximately 2.08,29 a total score of 10 yields an estimated odds of pathogenicity of approximately 895:1 (2.08 to the power of 10). This value exceeds the 891:1 threshold for classification as ‘Pathogenic’,7 thereby providing a definitive classification.

Discussion

We identified a total of 44 individuals from 35 families harbouring the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant in the Japanese population to date. Regarding the clinical phenotype, the median age at first onset was 48 years for breast cancer and 66.5 years for ovarian cancer among all individuals carrying this variant. These values are comparable to those reported in Japanese individuals carrying BRCA2 pathogenic variants (mean age±SD: 51.0±11.5 years for breast cancer and 58.3±9.3 years for ovarian cancer), as well as in BRCA-negative Japanese individuals (56.1±11.9 years for breast cancer and 53.8±13.2 years for ovarian cancer).10 30 In general, age at cancer onset alone does not clearly distinguish patients with BRCA2 pathogenic variants from those with sporadic breast or ovarian cancer. However, the relatively high frequency of bilateral breast cancer (10 out of 32 patients) may indicate an increased cancer predisposition among individuals carrying this variant. With respect to prostate cancer, the mean age at diagnosis in the Japanese population is 71.0±6.9 years, and germline pathogenic variants have been reported in 7.9% of Japanese patients diagnosed before age 60.31 The diagnosis of prostate cancer in one male individual in his early 60s (Family 19) may have been influenced by the pathogenic nature of this variant. In pancreatic cancer, one male individual developed the disease in his late 60s (Family 26), which corresponds to the average age of onset. This finding is consistent with previous reports suggesting that the age at diagnosis of pancreatic cancer is not associated with the presence or absence of germline pathogenic variants in pancreatic cancer susceptibility genes.32 Overall, from a clinical perspective, the features observed in individuals carrying the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant are not unique to this variant but are compatible with the established clinical spectrum of BRCA2-related HBOC.

Notably, a female variant carrier in Family 13 developed gastric cancer in her late 30s, and a male carrier in Family 31 developed the disease in his early 70s. Although Helicobacter pylori infection status was unknown, these observations are compatible with recent findings that H. pylori infection increases the risk of gastric cancer among carriers of pathogenic variants in homologous-recombination genes.33 In addition, relatives of the probands in four families developed oesophageal cancer: a male relative in Family 14 in his late 40s, a male relative in Family 22 in his late 60s, a female relative in Family 28 in her early 60s and a male relative in Family 29 in his late 70s. Moreover, in two families (Families 2 and 23), female relatives were diagnosed with biliary tract cancer in their 80s. Although the genotypes of these affected individuals were not confirmed, it is noteworthy that germline BRCA2 pathogenic variants have recently been linked to an increased risk of oesophageal cancer, and BRCA1 pathogenic variants to an increased risk of biliary tract cancer, in Japanese individuals, as demonstrated by a large-scale case-control study using BioBank Japan.34 Similar associations with oesophageal and biliary tract cancers have also been observed in a large-scale genomic study of Chinese patients, which identified germline BRCA2 variants as part of DNA repair deficiency, thereby supporting their involvement in oesophageal and biliary tract carcinogenesis in East Asian populations.35 36 Notably, one Japanese patient with biliary tract cancer carrying the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant has also been reported.37 Accumulating evidence from Japanese and other East Asian populations supports the hypothesis that BRCA1/2 pathogenic variants may extend their tumour spectrum beyond female breast and ovarian cancer in regions where gastric, oesophageal and biliary tract cancers are relatively common.34 38 Nevertheless, the number of events in our cohort is small, and the genotypes of several affected relatives were unavailable. Thus, the occurrences of gastric, oesophageal and biliary tract cancers in this study should be regarded as observational, hypothesis-generating findings rather than definitive evidence that the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant confers a substantially increased risk of these cancers. Importantly, these clinical observations of gastric, oesophageal and biliary tract cancers were not incorporated into our formal variant curation under the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP framework (table 3).

In addition to classic high-penetrance pathogenic variants, some BRCA1/2 variants retain partial function and are associated with reduced penetrance or moderate cancer risks (termed ‘hypomorphic’ variants). A well-characterised example is the BRCA1 c.5096G>A (p.Arg1699Gln) variant, which shows ambiguous or partial functional impairment across multiple assays,39 and intermediate breast and ovarian cancer risks in ENIGMA segregation-based modelling.40 Similarly, the BRCA2 c.9104A>C (p.Tyr3035Ser) variant is associated with moderate breast cancer risk and demonstrates partially reduced activity relative to the wild-type in multiple quantitative functional assays, supporting a hypomorphic mechanism.41 By contrast, in our study, the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant is consistently classified in the most deleterious functional categories across multiple independent platforms (summarised in table 1; MANO-B assay: five-category system1 26; HDR assays: two-category and three-category systems3 4; saturation genome editing assays: seven-category systems each5 6). This profile is incompatible with the hypomorphic/intermediate-risk model exemplified by the aforementioned variants. Furthermore, our quantitative cosegregation analysis yielded combined LRs well within the ‘PP1_Strong’ range, supporting a high-penetrance rather than a modest-risk classification. Notably, the presence of this variant in trans with a canonical splice-site pathogenic variant in a patient with a typical Fanconi anaemia phenotype further indicates that the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant behaves as a functionally severe allele in the biallelic state.28 Taken together, these observations suggest that the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant should be regarded as a high-penetrance pathogenic variant, although larger cohorts are necessary to refine age-specific penetrance estimates and to identify potential subtle differences in risk compared with other BRCA2 pathogenic variants.

Consistent with in vitro functional data, tumour-level findings further support the biological plausibility of homologous recombination deficiency (HRD) associated with this variant. In a CRISPR/Cas9 saturation genome editing study, tumours carrying functionally deleterious missense variants were strongly enriched for BRCA2 locus loss of heterozygosity (LOH), supporting frequent biallelic inactivation in this variant class.5 In that study, tumour LOH was assessed for multiple variants, but c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) was not individually evaluated. In this context, clinical observations from two cases provide additional insights into its impact. Specifically, the proband of Family 19, a male individual who developed prostate cancer in his early 60s, underwent CGP with the FoundationOne CDx test (Foundation Medicine, Cambridge, Massachusetts, USA). A similar analysis was performed for another Japanese patient with early-onset prostate cancer (diagnosed in his late 50s; unpublished observation). In both cases, tumour tissues harbouring the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant exhibited marked genomic instability, a hallmark of HRD, consistent with the impaired DNA repair capacity demonstrated by HDR assays.3 4 Although these real-world data are limited in number and do not replace systematic HRD scoring, they are consistent with the in vitro HDR3 4 and saturation genome editing5 6 results. These findings support the interpretation that this variant confers substantial HRD at the tumour level and may predict clinical sensitivity to PARP inhibitors.

As summarised in the Results section and table 3, the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant meets the pathogenic criteria ‘PP1_Strong’, ‘PS3’, ‘PM3_Moderate’ and ‘PM2_Supporting’, as well as the benign criterion ‘BP4’, resulting in a total of 10 points and an estimated odds of pathogenicity of approximately 895:1. While the application of ‘BP4’ is formally justified because the BayesDel noAF score falls below the defined threshold, discordance among computational predictors warrants further consideration. For example, REVEL yielded a score of 0.544, indicating an intermediate effect, whereas AlphaMissense, a recently developed deep learning model outperforming conventional tools, classified the variant as ‘likely pathogenic’. Other in silico predictors, including CADD, MutationTaster 2025, PolyPhen-2 and SIFT, also indicated a deleterious effect. These discordant results highlight the importance of integrating multiple in silico tools, particularly when their predictions are discordant, rather than relying exclusively on a single algorithm, which may overlook context-dependent effects in certain variants.

Despite the accumulating genetic, clinical and functional evidence supporting its pathogenicity, this variant remains classified as a VUS in several disease databases, including ClinVar and by clinical laboratories such as Myriad Genetics. In Japan, where BRCA1/2 genetic testing determines eligibility for PARP inhibitor therapy under the publicly funded universal health insurance system, this classification can directly affect clinical decision-making. Indeed, we are aware of multiple cases in which individuals with advanced cancer harbouring this variant were denied access to PARP inhibitor therapy, resulting in a clinically significant disadvantage.2 42 These observations highlight the importance of timely reassessment and reclassification of variants based on emerging evidence. Incorporating quantitative, expert panel-based frameworks such as the ClinGen ENIGMA BRCA1/2 VCEP into routine variant curation pipelines of clinical laboratories and commercial testing companies would help ensure that classifications are updated in a transparent and consistent manner. For population-specific variants such as the BRCA2 c.7847C>T (p.Ser2616Phe) variant, coordinated national efforts to collect clinical data, share functional evidence and reapply standardised classification criteria will be essential to reduce the burden of VUS and to enable equitable access to precision therapies.

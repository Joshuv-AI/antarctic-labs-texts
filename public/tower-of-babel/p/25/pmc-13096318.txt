Distribution law and control of the second invariant of deviatoric stress in gob-side entry retaining
Authors: Dongjie Jiang, Junjie Guo, Guangzhong Sun, Junwen Li, Xing Xu, Qi Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTaking the gob-side entry retaining (GER) in the 28,051 panel of Xinzhuang Coal Mine as the engineering background, this study introduces the second invariant of deviatoric stress (J₂) as a quantitative indicator of surrounding rock failure. By constructing mechanical model, performing FLAC3D numerical simulation, and conducting industrial experiment, the full-cycle failure evolution law of the surrounding rock and its control technology are systematically investigated. A mechanical model of the roadside filling body is established, from which the reasonable filling width beside the roadway is calculated to be 1.0 m. Simulation results indicate that during the early stage of GER, the J₂ of the surrounding rock exhibits a ring-shaped non-uniform distribution with peak values at a depth of 2.5 m in both the roof and the solid coal rib. In the middle stage, under strong mining disturbance, the J₂ peak shifts deeper to 4.5 m in the roof and 3.5 m in the coal rib. In the late stage, as the delayed working face distance increases, the peak value of J₂ show an increasing trend with gradually diminishing increments, stabilizing eventually. However, the peak value of J₂ at the solid coal side rib corner continues to rise. The J₂ distribution of filling body is approximately linear, with stress values increasing from the gob side to the roadway side, and the degree of stress concentration decreases with increasing delayed distance. Based on these findings, a support concept is proposed that requires anchor cables to penetrate through the J₂ peak zones of the solid coal rib and roof. This is integrated with high-density rock bolts and flexible cement slurry walls to form a comprehensive control technology for GER. Industrial practice demonstrates that the maximum roadway deformation is controlled within 294 mm, and the filling body deformation stabilizes around 90 mm, validating the effectiveness of the support design and achieving full-cycle stability control of the surrounding rock in GER.Keywords: GER, Second invariant of deviatoric stress, Roadside filling body, Numerical simulation, Support designSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

Coal is the most abundant and widely distributed conventional energy in the world, and it is also an important strategic resource1–5. At present, with the rapid development and advancement across various sectors in China, the demand for resources has continued to increase6–9. The GER technique, a coal pillar-free mining method10–13, involves preserving a roadway from the current working face for reuse by the adjacent face14–16. This approach offers several advantages, including improved resource recovery, mitigation of face-to-face succession conflicts, and prevention of gas overrun in corner zones17–19. A critical aspect of successful GER implementation lies in the establishment of roadside filling body adjacent to the retained roadway. Stable and reliable roadside filling body is essential for ensuring the structural integrity of the GER20–22. The roadside filling body should possess characteristics such as rapid load-bearing development, high support resistance, strong plastic deformation capacity, and excellent sealing performance23–26. As shown in Fig. 1, the main types of roadside filling body used in GER include: high-water rapid-setting material filling, concrete roadside filling, gangue filling, and steel-pipe-concrete composite support system27–31.

Types of GER filling bodies.

Numerous scholars have conducted extensive research on the deformation mechanisms and control technologies in GER. Based on actual geological conditions in coal mines, Fu et al.32 performed both numerical simulation and physical similarity model test, validating the feasibility of GER in soft coal seam. They proposed a combined support system consisting of “anchor rods + anchor cables + hydraulic lifting shed”. Chen et al.33 innovatively proposed a flexible-formwork concrete wall technique for GER, using the stress distribution and evolution of the plastic zone in the surrounding rock during face retreat as evaluation indices. Through FLAC3D simulations, they analyzed the stability mechanism of the surrounding rock. Liu et al.34, grounded in elastic and elasto-plastic theories, investigated the stress distribution and damage depth of the floor in closely spaced seams during GER. They proposed surrounding rock control strategies for both the roadway excavation and GER stages in close-distance seams. Kong et al.35 employed theoretical analysis and similarity simulation test to study the synergistic deformation mechanism in longwall top-coal caving GER. Their findings indicated that for softer top coal, the strength of the roadside filling body should be appropriately reduced, whereas they should be increased for harder top coal. Zhang et al.36 developed a mechanical model of the surrounding rock structure for GER beneath the gob of closely spaced seams. They derived formulas to calculate the maximum and minimum widths of the roadside filling body. Based on these theoretical results, they determined the optimal support width and successfully conducted an industrial-scale GER trial on-site. Xie et al. 37 studied the load-bearing capacity of concrete roadside supports in GER and proposed a dual support strategy: a primary support system of “anchor rods + anchor cables + metal mesh” within the retained roadway, and an enhanced support system on the gob side comprising “roof bolts + steel beams + steel-plastic mesh + double hydraulic props with upper and lower horizontal ties.” Zhang et al.38 conducted uniaxial compression tests (UCT) and acoustic emission (AE) experiments on high-water-content materials, revealing their slow internal damage progression in the post-peak stage and relatively high post-peak strength. Numerical simulations of GER indicated that high-water roadside supports initially undergo elastic deformation followed by plastic deformation as the working face advances. Tian et al.39 established a mechanical interaction model between the roadside support and roof in thin, soft-coal-seam GER conditions, exploring the factors affecting surrounding rock stability under such geological settings. Wu et al.40, using the UDEC discrete element method, investigated the crack propagation and evolution mechanisms of roadside filling body during the mining process. The results showed that failure in roadside filling body is primarily caused by tensile cracking, with cracks initially appearing at the top and bottom corners of the structure.

The aforementioned studies have yielded substantial scientific achievements in elucidating the deformation mechanisms of surrounding rock in GER, providing effective guidance for stability control in GER. However, current analyses in GER rarely incorporate deviatoric stress indicators. The deviatoric stress tensor is the fundamental driver of plastic deformation and failure in rock masses41,42. In this study, the 28,051 panel of the Xinzhuang Coal Mine is selected as the research object. A mechanical model of GER is constructed to calculate the required support resistance of the roadside filling body and to reveal its mechanical function. The second invariant of the deviatoric stress tensor (J₂) is employed as the evaluation index. Using FLAC3D finite element modeling, the spatiotemporal evolution and distribution patterns of J₂ and the plastic zones in the surrounding rock are analyzed to uncover the deformation and failure mechanisms of GER. Furthermore, an industrial experiment is conducted based on the proposed surrounding rock control technology. The findings are expected to provide valuable reference for the design and implementation of GER systems under similar geological conditions.

Engineering background

Xinzhuang Coal Mine is located in Qingyang City, Gansu Province, China. The elevation of the 28,051 panel ranges from − 700 to − 745 m, while the ground surface elevation is − 32.5 m, resulting in an average burial depth of approximately 690 m. The No. 2 coal seam has an average dip angle of 4°, with an average thickness of 2.6 m. The GER roadway in this study is the 28,051 headgate. Resistance analysis of roadside filling body

Mechanism of roadside filling body

Based on the movement characteristics of the roof29–31, the failure process of roof strata above the GER can be divided into three sequential stages: immediate roof collapse stage, main roof fracture stage, and main roof stabilization stage.

In the immediate roof collapse stage, which occurs behind the upper section working face after retreat, the overlying strata in the gob lose their load-bearing support from the coal. The immediate roof on the gob side collapses and subsides under the combined effect of its self-weight and the reaction from the roadside filling body. The resulting caved gangue gradually fills the gob.

Subsequently, the system enters the main roof fracture stage. Under mining-induced stresses, the main roof begins to rotate and subside, leading to the first fracture near the solid coal side. This process forms a key block (block B) and adjacent blocks A and C, according to the classic voussoir beam theory. The roadside filling body gradually reaches the required force to cut the roof. If key block B experiences its ultimate bending moment, a second fracture may occur. During this stage, the immediate roof further subsides under the dynamic stress influence of the main roof, leading to a sharp increase in the load on the roadside filling body. The main roof continues to rotate and settle until it contacts the caved gangue, which becomes gradually compacted and starts to provide reactive support to key block B.

In the main roof stabilization stage, the deformation rate of the surrounding rock slows down and tends toward stabilization. This is due to the synergistic effect of the solid coal rib, roadside filling body, and the caved gangue in the gob. However, under the influence of superimposed stresses from both the advance abutment pressure of the lower working face and the lateral abutment pressure from the first mining face, the load on the support structure increases again. This leads to further compression and settlement of the roadside filling body, inducing secondary deformation of the surrounding rock in the retained roadway (Fig. 4).

Establishment of the mechanical model for GER

After the initial fracture of the key block B in the main roof on the gob side, if the supporting resistance of the roadside filling body is insufficient to induce a secondary fracture of block B, then the long cantilever formed by block B will continue to rotate and subside until it contacts the collapsed gangue in the gob43,44. During the rotation of block B, due to its high disturbance intensity and prolonged action, significant extrusion deformation occurs in both the retained roadway and the roadside filling body, which may ultimately lead to overall instability of the surrounding rock in GER. Therefore, whether the support body provides sufficient resistance to sever block B is crucial for the successful implementation of GER in a fully mechanized caving face45–47.

Original roadway support design

The roof and two ribs of 28,051 headgate adopt the combined support mode of “steel mesh + steel wire rope anchor beam + anchor bolt + anchor cable” and “anchor bolt + steel plastic mesh + belt anchor beam” respectively. The original support scheme for the 28,051 headgate is shown in Fig. 6.

Original roadway support scheme.

For the roof support, φ20 × 2200 mm left-hand threaded rebar bolts without longitudinal ribs and φ21.6 × 8000 mm cables were employed. The spacing between the bolts was 700 × 700 mm, and the spacing between the cables was 1150 × 1400 mm. Reinforcement mesh was used for roof surface support, with dimensions of 2000 mm (length) × 800 mm (width), wire diameter φ6 mm, and mesh aperture 90 mm × 80 mm. For the rib support, φ20 × 2200 mm left-hand threaded rebar bolts without longitudinal ribs were installed with spacing of 700 × 700 mm. Steel-plastic composite mesh was used for rib support, with mesh dimensions of 2200 mm (length) × 1150 mm (width), aperture 25 mm × 25 mm, and composed of 8-strand steel wire.

Analysis of the second invariant of deviatoric stress in the numerical model

Evaluation indicators for deformation and failure of roadway

To thoroughly reveal the mechanical mechanisms underlying rock mass instability and failure48, this study systematically deepens the investigation into the deviatoric stress theory controlling the damage evolution of the surrounding rock, aiming to establish a more universal set of failure evaluation criteria49–51.

Based on the fundamental framework of continuum mechanics, the stress state at any spatial point within the roadway surrounding rock can be uniquely decomposed into the sum of a spherical stress tensor and a deviatoric stress tensor:

where: σm represents the spherical component of the stress tensor. δij is the Kronecker delta. Sij is the deviatoric component of the stress tensor.

According to classical elastoplastic theory, deformation of a body under external forces can be decomposed into volumetric changes and shape changes. The volumetric change of the body is primarily caused by isotropic stresses, i.e., spherical stresses; whereas shape changes are attributed to the action of deviatoric stresses. Deviatoric stresses induce distortional deformation of the elemental volume, which further triggers plastic deformation. In roadway surrounding rock, such plastic deformation typically manifests as the formation and propagation of plastic zones. The stress state at any point within the roadway surrounding rock can be expressed by nine stress components: σx, σy, σz, τxy, τyx, τxz, τzx, τyz and τzy. The magnitudes and directions of these stress components are closely related to the chosen coordinate system; when the coordinate system changes, these stress components also vary accordingly.

Considering this, the present study selects the second invariant of the deviatoric stress tensor, J2, as the evaluation metric. This invariant is independent of coordinate system selection, thereby ensuring objectivity and universality. More importantly, J2 comprehensively accounts for the combined effects of normal and shear stresses on rock mass failure, providing a more complete and accurate reflection of the rock’s failure characteristics under complex stress states. Model calibration

Calibration of the strain-softening model

After conducting uniaxial compression tests on the filling material, the stress–strain curve indicates that during the initial loading stage, the filling body exhibits elastic behavior with a linear stress–strain relationship52,53. Upon reaching the peak stress, as strain continues to increase, the stress shows a progressive decline until it stabilizes at a residual strength. At this stage, the filling material still maintains a certain load-bearing capacity. This mechanical behavior is referred to as the “Strain-softening” phenomenon54–56.

In FLAC3D, the conventional Mohr–coulomb model can describe the elastic–plastic behavior of materials before peak stress. However, once the peak stress is reached, this model assumes ideal plasticity with constant stress despite further strain, which contradicts the characteristic of the filling body. Conversely, the Strain-softening model defines the evolution of strength parameters such as cohesion and internal friction angle as functions of plastic strain via tabular input. This allows accurate simulation of the gradual degradation of the material from peak strength to residual strength, thereby realistically reflecting the Strain-softening behavior of the filling body.

Based on laboratory uniaxial compression test results of the filling body, uniaxial compression simulations of cylindrical specimens were performed in FLAC3D using strain-controlled loading. The Strain-softening constitutive parameters were iteratively adjusted until the numerical results closely matched the experimental data. The calibrated Strain-softening constitutive model results are presented in Fig. 8. The numerical simulation curve fully reproduces the mechanical response of the filling body from elastic deformation through peak strength to residual strength.

Full mining cycle surrounding rock failure mechanism of GER

Using the 28,051 headgate as the engineering background, this study employs the second invariant of the deviatoric stress tensor, J2, as a quantitative indicator to systematically reveal the evolution law of surrounding rock failure throughout the full mining cycle of GER. After the initial geostatic stress balance of the model, the 28,051 headgate and the 28,051 auxiliary headgate are sequentially excavated according to the actual mining schedule. Once the surrounding rock stress converges and stabilizes, the 28,051 working face is progressively advanced to realize full mining-induced disturbance.

To comprehensively capture the dynamic response characteristics of J2 under mining-induced stress perturbations, a monitoring station is established at y = 100 m. The “ − ” sign denotes the position ahead of the working face, while the “ + ” sign corresponds to the gob-side area behind the gob.

Support design of GER

Based on theoretical calculations and numerical simulation results, the width of the adjacent support formed by a flexible cement slurry wall for the GER of 28,051 panel is determined to be 1000 mm, with a cement slurry strength grade of M30. To control the lateral deformation of the flexible cement slurry wall, prestressed bolts are embedded within the slurry wall. The bolts are threaded steel bars with a diameter of 22 mm and length of 1100 mm, featuring threaded ends of 50 mm on both sides. Bearing plates measuring 150 × 150 × 12 mm are used, with double bearing plates and double nuts. The adjacent roadway support design is illustrated in Fig. 19.

Support design of GER.

Following the support concept of anchor cables penetrating through the solid coal rib and the roof J2 peak stress zone, the support scheme for the 28,051 headgate is shown in Fig. 16. The roof is reinforced with left-hand threaded steel bolts of φ20 × 2200 mm without longitudinal ribs and anchor cables of φ21.6 × 8000 mm. The anchor cables at the roof corner on the solid coal side and the working face side are inclined at an angle of 15° relative to the vertical direction. The spacing between bolts is 700 mm × 700 mm, and the spacing between anchor cables is 1150 mm × 1400 mm. The steel wire rope anchor beam measures 4550 mm in length and 100 mm in width. Steel mesh with dimensions of 2000 mm × 800 mm × φ6 mm and a mesh aperture of 90 mm × 80 mm is used for roof reinforcement.

Both ribs are supported by left-hand threaded steel bolts of φ20 × 2200 mm with spacing of 700 mm × 700 mm, and belt anchor beams sized 2500 mm × 200 mm. Steel-plastic mesh with dimensions of 2200 mm × 1150 mm and aperture of 25 mm × 25 mm composed of 8-strand steel wires is used for rib reinforcement. Additionally, two anchor cables of φ21.6 × 4500 mm are installed penetrating the solid coal rib J2 peak stress zone, with spacing of 1400 mm × 1400 mm.

Conclusions

Based on the background of GER in Xinzhuang coal mine, this paper analyzes the evolution process of the second invariant of deviatoric stress in the process of GER. The main conclusions are as follows:

In the early stage of GER, the influence of advance mining disturbance is minimal. The J₂ exhibits a ring-shaped, non-uniform distribution, with peak values concentrated at a depth of 2.5 m in both the roof and the solid coal rib. During the middle stage of GER, mining-induced stress disturbances become severe, causing the J₂ peak values to shift deeper—to 4.5 m in the roof and 3.5 m in the solid coal rib—significantly increasing the risk of shear failure in the deep rock mass. In the late stage of GER, as the compaction degree of the caved rock in the gob improves, the J₂ value in the shallow surrounding rock decreases. However, the J₂ peak band at the solid coal side rib corner gradually widens with increasing distance from the delayed working face, and the plastic zone expansion tends to stabilize.

With increasing distance from the delayed working face, the degree of stress concentration gradually decreases. The roadside support body must possess high early strength, strong plastic deformation capacity, and high support resistance. Based on theoretical calculations, the support body width is determined to be 1.0 m.

Based on the J₂ distribution characteristics of the surrounding rock in GER, it is revealed that the solid coal rib and its roof are key control zones. A support concept is proposed whereby anchor cables must penetrate through the J₂ peak zones of the solid coal rib and roof surrounding rock. Through deep anchoring, this approach inhibits the coupled failure mechanism of shallow rock layer delamination instability and deep rock mass shear slip, thereby achieving full-cycle stability control of the surrounding rock in GER.

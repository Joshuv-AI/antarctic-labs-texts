Global trends in gastric cancer and nutrition, 2000–2023: A bibliometric and visualization analysis
Authors: Hao Guo, Xiaoyang Jiang, Lvwen Zhang, Jiajia Wang, Xinghao Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Gastric cancer (GC) remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. Malnutrition is highly prevalent in GC patients and is linked to poor prognosis and increased disease burden. While nutritional support is known to improve outcomes and reduce complications, a comprehensive analysis of the research landscape is lacking. This study provides a bibliometric assessment of scientific literature regarding GC and nutrition.Methods:Publications from the 21st century to the present were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection. Bibliometric and visualization analyses were conducted using VOSviewer, Pajek, and the R package “bibliometrix.”Results:A total of 1760 articles were analyzed. Publication volume grew steadily after 2016, peaking in 2022. The United States, China, and Japan emerged as the leading research hubs. Key contributing institutions include Wenzhou Medical University, Sun Yat-sen University, and Ohio State University. Annals of Surgical Oncology is the most prolific and cited journal. Research primarily centers on the prognostic impact of nutrition and strategies to enhance patients’ nutritional and immunological status.Conclusion:This study is the first bibliometric analysis of the global research landscape concerning GC and nutrition. By identifying current hotspots and emerging trends, it provides a valuable reference for researchers aiming to optimize nutritional interventions and improve patient outcomes.

1. Introduction

Currently, gastric cancer (GC) is the fifth most commonly diagnosed and fourth most deadly cancer in the world, with >1 million new cases estimated in 2020, resulting in approximately 760,000 deaths.[1] Due to the nature of GC, nutritional problems are common in patients undergoing either surgical or adjuvant treatment.[2–4] These problems are particularly prevalent in advanced stages, where patients are often at high risk and may suffer complications due to malabsorption of certain essential nutrients. The provision of appropriate nutritional support can help to improve overall survival (OS) and survival rates.[5–7]

In clinical practice, malnutrition is a prevalent issue in patients with GC, frequently resulting in hypoproteinaemia and diminished immunity.[8] Many studies have shown that malnourished patients commonly suffer from adverse clinical outcomes such as infections, increased incidence of complications, and reduced survival.[9–11] Despite recent improvements in surgical approaches and perioperative care, which have led to an improvement in the nutritional status of GC patients,[12,13] the risk of malnutrition in GC patients remains high due to gastrointestinal hemorrhage/protein loss, certain cytokines, surgical trauma, and dietary restrictions.[14–16] These findings suggest that early assessment of malnutrition and treatment with nutritional interventions are essential to prevent further cancer progression.[17]

The nutritional status of patients with GC can be improved through several pathways, including early screening, perioperative nutritional interventions, maintaining appropriate body weight, and sustaining a healthy lifestyle.[18–20] The high prevalence of malnutrition in the population indicates a clear need for nutritional interventions.[21] The early diagnosis and treatment of GC patients can benefit all patients. Indeed, numerous countries and institutions employ nutritional interventions as a tool in cancer management.[13,18,19] The European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism even suggests that delaying surgery by 7 to 14 days to improve the nutritional status of poorly nourished patients is worthwhile.[22] Individualized nutritional programs and nutritional health education are designed for patients, taking into account the progress of the disease, the patient's situation, and the side effects of drugs, in order to ensure adequate nutritional and caloric intake.[21,22] For those who have difficulty eating or are unable to eat enough food, enteral or parenteral nutrition can be considered. Effective nutritional support helps patients meet their nutritional needs, improves the quality of life, reduces disease stress, and prolongs survival. There is growing evidence of the importance of nutritional support in GC.[23–25]

Despite the growing interest in the value of nutrition in the overall treatment of GC, no studies have quantitatively analyzed this. Consequently, we conducted a bibliometric analysis of this field.

Bibliometrics is a quantitative method of determining the current status and trends in a field of research by analyzing the keywords, citations, sources, etc of articles in the field in order to provide a theoretical basis for further research.[26,27] We performed a bibliometric analysis of the literature published between 2000 and 2023 on nutritional support and GC and visualized the results using analytical tools (VOSviewer, Pajek, and R).[28,29] This study aims to help clinicians and researchers identify research directions in this area and to inform future research.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Data sources and search strategies

The Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) was selected as the data source for this study. It is widely recognized as one of the most comprehensive and reliable databases for bibliometric research, providing high-quality citation data and the complete metadata required for visualization software.[30,31] On April 28, 2024, a systematic search was conducted using the “Topic” (TS) field. The search query was defined as: (TS = (nutrition) OR TS=(“nutritional status”) OR TS=(“nutrition therapy”) OR TS=(“nutrition support”) OR TS=(“medical nutrition therapy”) OR TS=(“nutritional intervention”)) AND (TS=(“gastric cancer”) OR TS=(“gastric carcinoma”) OR TS=(“gastric neoplasm”) OR TS=(“stomach cancer”) OR TS=(“stomach carcinoma”) OR TS=(“stomach neoplasm”)). The WoSCC was chosen as the sole database to ensure internal consistency in citation metrics and metadata formats, which is essential for accurate visualization analysis.

2.3. Data analysis and visualisation

Bibliometrics is a method of quantitative assessment through statistical analysis of published research and the use of information from publications.[32] The methodologies are frequently employed to assess the influence of countries/regions, institutions, journals, and researchers in a specific field over a defined period and are also used to identify emerging trends.[30,33] The implementation of bibliometrics and visualization frequently occurs through the use of software, global research publication trends were analyzed by Microsoft Excel 2020; countries/regions, institutions, journals, coauthors, and keywords were analyzed by VOSviewer 1.6.20 to build a visual network, and keywords were further clustered by Pajek5.18; countries/regions were analyzed geographically and hotspots and trends of keywords were tracked by bibliometric package “bibliometrix.”[28,29] To ensure the rigor and reproducibility of the study, specific thresholds were applied: for keyword co-occurrence analysis, the minimum frequency was set to 5 times to balance network density and interpretability. Data cleaning was conducted to merge synonyms and remove irrelevant terms. These parameters were selected based on previous bibliometric studies in the medical field to ensure that the results accurately reflect global research trends and core collaborative networks.

2.4. Ethical review

All data in this study were downloaded from the publicly available WoSCC database; no animals or populations were involved, and there were no ethical issues involved, so ethics committee approval was not required.

3. Results

3.1. Analysis of global publishing trends

From January 01, 2000, to December 31, 2023, 2603 papers on nutrition about GC were included in WoSCC, and 1760 papers met the criteria. The number of papers increased from 23 to 179, with the largest number of papers published in 2022, with 223 papers. A total of 1024 papers were published from 2018 to 2023, accounting for 58.19% of the total number of papers published in 24 years (Fig. 2). The overall trend in the number of papers published is increasing year on year, indicating a growing interest in the role of nutrition in GC.

4. Discussion

Global Research Landscape The field of GC and nutrition has transitioned into a phase of rapid expansion. A total of 9452 authors from 1944 institutions across 89 countries have contributed to 504 journals. The surge in publications, particularly after 2016 (with half of all literature published in the last 5 years), suggests that the academic importance of nutrition in GC management is now widely recognized.

Geographic and Institutional Contributions: China and Japan account for over 50% of the global output, indicating a strong regional research focus. This may be attributed to the high incidence of GC in East Asia and a long-standing tradition of medical nutrition therapy.[1,37–39] Notably, research productivity does not always align with citation impact. While China leads in volume, the United States maintains a higher average citation rate and more extensive international collaborations, reflecting a high level of scholarly influence. Seven of the top ten most productive institutions are Chinese, explaining the country dominance in volume; however, the quality of publications varies. Future efforts should prioritize strengthening international collaboration between institutions and authors to enhance overall research quality and global impact.

Core publications are primarily distributed across cancer, general medicine, and nutrition journals. Among the 504 identified journals, 15 were defined as the ‘core area’ based on Bradford Law, accounting for 26.76% of total publications. Cancer-themed journals have recorded the highest volume and citations; specifically, Annals of Surgical Oncology emerged as the most influential outlet with 2091 citations and the strongest network connectivity (74 lines). This suggests that nutrition has evolved into a central hotspot within oncology research. Pioneers like Yoshiyuki Fujiwara, Hiroaki Saito, and Zhen Yu have significantly advanced the field, with Japanese scholars occupying 3 of the top ten most productive positions. Their work primarily focuses on the prognostic impact of nutrition following radical gastrectomy and the underlying biological mechanisms by which nutrients influence GC progression.[40–43]

Keyword co-occurrence analysis reveals mainstream TSs and cutting-edge frontiers. The 79 high-frequency keywords identified in this study were grouped into 4 distinct thematic clusters. The identified clusters, related to gastrectomy, enteral nutrition, immune function, and postoperative complications, reflect the multifaceted nature of nutrition research in GC. For instance, Cluster 3 (blue) emphasizes the link between enteral nutrition and malnutrition management, while Cluster 1 (red) explores the relationship between obesity, skeletal muscle mass, and quality of life. These clusters indicate that the research focus has primarily centered on the clinical outcomes of perioperative nutritional interventions and the management of malnutrition. Furthermore, bibliometric mapping reveals a growing scholarly interest in personalized nutritional support and its correlation with patient prognosis prediction. Analysis of temporal trends shows that terms like “elderly,” “nomogram,” and “sarcopenia” have appeared frequently in recent years, indicating a shift toward precision assessment in geriatric populations. This trend aligns with the recent release of several European Society for Clinical Nutrition and Metabolism clinical guidelines, such as “Clinical nutrition in cancer” and “Action against cancer-related malnutrition,” which aim to standardize nutritional and metabolic profiles to improve patient quality of life and OS.[18,21,44,45]

We also have some limitations in this study. Firstly, we only utilized data from the WoSCC database. While WoSCC is a primary source for bibliometric studies, this reliance may introduce selection bias, and future studies could benefit from validating these trends across other databases such as Scopus or PubMed. Secondly, only English literature and articles were included in this study, and some other valuable articles may have been omitted. Third, certain new research trends are emerging, and key articles published recently may have been overlooked. Finally, bibliometric analysis is only a method of analysis, and the situation we analyzed may be different from the actual one.

5. Conclusion

This study is the first comprehensive bibliometric analysis of global GC and nutrition research from the 21st century to the most recent year. Based on our analyses, we found that a solid foundation has been laid in the field, with an explosion of relevant research output. Nutrition is receiving increasing attention and favor from researchers throughout the developmental and therapeutic course of GC, with clinical trials and mechanistic studies combining to further deepen research. The bibliometric evidence suggests that individualized nutritional support is an increasingly recognized research direction with significant potential in both the prevention and treatment of GC and may provide ideas for future systematic treatment of GC. This study will be a reference point for other clinicians and researchers to follow.

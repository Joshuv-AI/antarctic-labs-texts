Exploring complex phenomena in fluid flow and plasma physics via the Schrödinger-type Maccari system
Authors: Naseem Abbas, Akhtar Hussain, Tarek F Ibrahim, Faizah D Alanazi, Burak Oğul, Elkhateeb S Aly, Jorge Herrera
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe nonlinear coupled Maccari system of the Schrödinger equation type is an important equation that covers a wide range of topics in fluid flow, deep-water wave theory, plasma physics, nonlinear optics, etc. This system is a non-linear model that describes the dynamics of isolated waves, confined in a small part of space. In the present work, we utilize the modified Jacobi elliptic expansion scheme and the new extended hyperbolic function method to obtain soliton solutions for the Maccari system. By performing certain procedures of wave variable alteration, the proposed system of nonlinear equations becomes a single-variable differential equation. Subsequently, several precise soliton solutions were recovered by effectively applying the proposed procedures. The solutions achieved are represented in 2D and 3D plots by appropriately allocating values to the associated unknown constants. These graphical representations help researchers to understand the fundamental mechanisms of complex occurrences using leading equations. Dynamical features such as phase portraits, quasi-periodic and chaotic behavior, and sensitivity analysis have been well explored. The approaches utilized exhibit commendable individual performances, which warrants their continued application in solving numerous other nonlinear evolution equations emerging in diverse scientific and engineering domains.Keywords: Maccari system, The modified Jacobi elliptic expansion method, A new extended hyperbolic function method, Solitons, Jacobi elliptic function solutions, Graphical interpretationSubject terms: Applied mathematics, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Finding exact solutions to nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) is a popular topic in many research areas. Exact solutions to NLPDEs are important in many applied sciences and offer useful insights into the physical events that these equations model. Fortunately, a range of systematic techniques have been made possible by the introduction of many methods, such as the Jacobi elliptic function method1, modified auxiliary equation2, the tanh-coth method2, new general extended direct algebraic method3, modified simple equation4, -expansion scheme5, the extended -expansion method6, generalized exponential rational function method7, Sardar subequation method8, Kudryashov approach9, exponential method10 and many more11–21.

Many classical differential equations are crucial tools for representing and describing the internal behavior of complex physical phenomena that occur in the natural world22–41. Research opportunities are being created by academics persistent efforts to investigate the results of nonlinear partial differential equations obtained using various methodologies. Coupled equations of the Schrödinger equation type are the non-linear Maccari system42–44

where Q, S, N stand for complex scalar fields, R represents real scalar fields and all depends on space-variables x, y and time-variable t, has wide-spectral applications in the theory of deep-water waves, fluid flow, plasma physics, and nonlinear optics. Maccari’s system is a nonlinear model that describes the dynamics of isolated waves, confined in a small part of space, in various fields such as hydrodynamics, plasma physics, and nonlinear optics. Its nonlinear and coupled nature captures essential interactions between wave components, making it a rich and versatile framework for studying soliton dynamics.

We will now take a glance at the literature to refresh our memory of the Maccari system’s basic foundation. One of the most notable nonlinear models for comprehending the motion of solitary waves in plasmas, nonlinear optics, and quantum field theory is this system with two nonlinear evolution equations (Maccari42). There is numerous research about this system in the literature, for example, Jabbari et al.43 applied two different approaches to acquire the rational, trigonometric, and hyperbolic forms structures; authors have studied this model to obtain the soliton structures in44; the various types of the closed-form structures are discussed in45; Liu et al.46 have discussed the understudy model and acquired bright and dark N-soliton profiles; Hafez et al.47 have studied this considered model with the help of a new -expansion approach.

Many other scholars have looked at this problem to get the exact solutions, like Ciancio et al.48 derived the hyperbolic and trigonometric type structures; Inc. et al.49 examined the understudy model with two different approaches; Neirameh50 applied the improved -expansion method to obtain the closed form structures; Baskonus et al.51 obtained the hyperbolic nature profiles by using the sine-Gordon expansion scheme; a well-known scheme has been used by Bulut et al.52 which gives some solitary wave structures; In53, authors have implemented the first integral approach to obtain the hyperbolic and exponential type structures. In54, the researchers examined the understudy model using two different techniques, and in55, researchers looked at the same model using a famous scheme.

Bifurcation and chaotic behavior have been widely investigated in numerous fields, including physics, biology, chemistry, engineering, ecology, and astronomy56,57. Studies on the dynamic properties of differential equations have shown how periodic external disturbances can induce chaotic behavior. Research on periodic non-linear waves has often focused on their dynamic and quasi-periodic properties. Bifurcation analysis, a key method for studying dynamic systems, examines the qualitative unpredictability in the solutions of differential equations. The referenced citations58–60 highlight important contributions and recent advancements in this area. Motivated by the challenges in finding exact solutions and understanding the complex dynamics of nonlinear evolution equations like the Maccari system, this study applies the modified Jacobi elliptic expansion method to derive new soliton solutions and explore their dynamical properties. As originality, this work applies the modified Jacobi elliptic expansion method to the nonlinear coupled Maccari system, deriving new exact soliton solutions through a novel wave variable transformation. The study uniquely presents detailed 2D and 3D visualizations and explores dynamic features like phase portraits, chaos, and sensitivity, offering a robust approach extendable to other nonlinear evolution equations.

To the best of our knowledge, the modified Jacobi elliptic expansion (MJEE) approach and the new extended hyperbolic function (EHF) method have not been applied to the nonlinear Maccari system (1). Following the above works, which we find fascinating, our goal is to extract some standard and primal soliton solutions to the nonlinear Maccari system using two new proposed techniques. From these solutions, several well-known solutions are re-established, along with some dependable and efficient solitary solutions. There are three types of closed-waveform solutions: hyperbolic, rational, and trigonometric solutions. To highlight their physical significance, well-furnished solutions were interpreted in 2D and 3D plots.

Graphical interpretation and discussions

Figure 1 represents the 3D and 2D graphs of , demonstrating the dark-soliton solutions for a range of associate parameters. These solutions represent localized wave amplitude dips, which are commonly seen in de-focusing nonlinear media, where the wave retains its shape because of a balance between dispersion and nonlinearity. Similar to this, the 3D and 2D graphs of , which show hyperbolic-type solutions, are shown in Fig. 2. These solutions are distinguished by single wavefronts and steep gradients, which are frequently connected to kink-type or shock-like structures in nonlinear systems. Moreover, the 3D and 2D graphs of and are displayed in Figs. 3 and 4, respectively, illustrating the periodic and bright soliton solutions for the studied series of parameters. The bright solitons represent localized peaks moving steadily through focusing media, simulating energy concentration in physical systems such as water waves or optical fibers, while the periodic solutions describe wave patterns that repeat over time or space and typically arise in systems with recurrent external forcing. The derived dark soliton, bright soliton, periodic, and hyperbolic-type solutions have significant applications in various physical systems governed by nonlinear wave dynamics. In particular, dark and bright soliton structures can model optical pulse propagation in nonlinear fiber optics, energy transport in Bose-Einstein condensates, and water wave phenomena. Periodic solutions are relevant in the study of wave trains in plasma physics and lattice vibrations in solid-state materials, while hyperbolic-type (kink or shock-like) solutions are crucial for modeling signal transmission in nerve fibers and phase transitions in fluid dynamics. These results enhance analytical understanding and aid in the design of experiments and technologies leveraging nonlinear wave behavior. Conclusions

In this study, the non-linear Maccari system, a well-known NLPDE was explored. It effectively models a wide range of complex physical phenomena, including fluid flow, plasma physics, and non-linear optics. Its non-linear and coupled nature captures essential interactions between wave components, making it a rich and versatile framework for studying soliton dynamics. Two recent approaches, a modified Jacobi elliptic expansion method and a new extended hyperbolic function method, were utilized to construct the diverse wave solutions, including several bright and dark soliton solutions. These were found to exist when the modulus of ellipticity approached unity. The precise solutions presented in this research offer valuable insight into the nonlinear Maccari system, but a significant effort remains to find additional exact solutions in the future. In the end, the planar dynamical system was derived via the Galilean transformation, the Bifurcation analysis was presented and the quasi-periodic and chaotic behaviors of the system were analyzed by taking the external perturbation terms. Correspondingly, the sensitivity analysis was observed in a well-established manner. We can assert the originality and diversity of the structures in this study due to the comparative analysis between the obtained results and the results that are already known. The results of this paper were expected to offer some new ideas on the investigation of the dynamics of the model considered. Future works can explore the extension of these methods to higher-dimensional Maccari systems and coupled multiwave interactions. Furthermore, the impact of variable coefficients and external perturbations on soliton dynamics could be investigated to broaden practical applications.

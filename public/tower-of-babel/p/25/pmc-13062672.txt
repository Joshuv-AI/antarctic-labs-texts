Technical & clinical evaluation of a novel slim gastroscope in third-space procedures: Feasibility, subjective workload & system usability assessment
Authors: Oscar Cahyadi, Christian Torres Reyes, Mousa Ayoub, Daniel Quast, Peter Ewald, Alanna Ebigbo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and study aimsThird-space endoscopy, including peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM) and endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), has become an established therapeutic option for various gastrointestinal disorders. A newly developed slim therapeutic gastroscope aims to improve maneuverability and provide easier access to the submucosal space and also to enhance ergonomic aspects of tedious therapeutic procedures. The aim of this study (The Slim Scop3 Trial) was to evaluate the subjective workload and usability of the novel slim therapeutic gastroscope during third space procedures. Secondary outcomes included technical and clinical performance as well as adverse events (AEs).Patients and methodsThis was a prospective, single-arm, single-center study comprising 30 patients undergoing third-space endoscopy in a high-volume center in Germany. Subjective workload and usability were assessed with the NASA-Task load index (NASA-TLX) and the System Usability Score (SUS), respectively. Clinical outcomes and AEs were recorded prospectively.ResultsThe slim gastroscope showed a low NASA-TLX and high SUS score, which correlates with a low subjective workload and high usability. POEM showed a tendency toward lower NASA-TLX and higher SUS compared with ESD. For ESD, the en-bloc and curative R0 resection rate was 100%. No device-related AE events occurred.ConclusionsA novel slim gastroscope offers high usability with a low subjective workload for third space procedures. The slim gastroscope has the potential to reduce ergonomic strain on endoscopists during complex therapeutic interventions.

Introduction

Third-space endoscopy, including peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM) and endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD), has become an established endoscopic therapeutic option. These procedures require precise and stable scope control, and often high maneuverability in anatomically small spaces. Technical challenges can contribute to prolonged procedure times, incomplete resections, or adverse events (AEs).

A newly developed slim therapeutic gastroscope aims to improve maneuverability while maintaining a working channel sufficient for therapeutic interventions. Initial reports on the clinical utility of the slim gastroscope in difficult anatomic positions and/or difficult lesions have shown positive outcomes 1 2 3 . Kim et al reported on a larger series of 17 ESDs with the slim scope, with promising results of 100% en bloc and R0, predominantly in confined spaces such as the pharynx, esophagus, and duodenum 4 Recently, Abe et al showed that using the new scope in E-POEM could lead to a shorter procedure time due to shorter time during the mucosal entry and closure 5 .

Although novel endoscopes and devices are often evaluated using technical and clinical outcomes, ergonomic aspects are rarely considered. The slim gastroscope may have improved maneuverability in small spaces, but subjective strain and usability correlated with clinical outcomes have not been assessed. Validated assessment tools such as the National Aeronautics and Space Administration Task Load Index (TLX) 6 , which measures subjective workload, and the System Usability Scale (SUS) 7 , may provide useful insights into usability and strain associated with novel technologies. A recent meta-analysis by Oliveira et al. showed endoscopy-related musculoskeletal injuries in up to 62% of endoscopists, most often in the hand or thumb 8 .

The aim of this study, therefore, was to evaluate the novel slim therapeutic gastroscope during third-space endoscopy.

Patients and methods

Study design and setting

This was a prospective, single-center evaluation of a newly developed slim therapeutic gastroscope. Thirty consecutive patients undergoing esophageal POEM (E-POEM), gastric POEM (G-POEM) or Zenker’s POEM (Z-POEM) and esophageal, gastric, or rectal ESD were included. Procedures were performed by three endoscopists experienced in ESD and POEM. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethics votum was waived by the local Ethics Committee Westfalen-Lippe, Nr: 2025–205-f-S. All patients provided informed consent prior to the endoscopic procedure and study inclusion.

Endoscope

The slim therapeutic gastroscope (EG-840-TP, Fujifilm, Japan) was used for all procedures. Key technical features include an outer diameter of 7.9 mm with a large 3.2-mm working channel and enhanced flexibility with upward angulation of 210°, a downward angulation of 160 degrees, and 100 degrees sideways ( Fig. 1 ). In comparison, standard gastroscopes have 210 degrees upward, 90 degrees downward, and 100 degrees to both sides.

Prior to the study, due to the novelty of this scope in Europe, all participating endoscopists had limited experience with the novel slim scope, having each performed fewer than five therapeutic procedures.

Procedures

All POEM procedures and esophageal and gastric ESDs were performed under general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation. Colon and rectal ESDs were performed under general anesthesia or deep sedation, depending on lesion location and anticipated procedure duration and complexity.

Z-POEM was performed with complete myotomy of the diverticular septum after submucosal tunneling on both sides of the septum. Esophageal POEM was performed following standard tunneling until 2 to 3 cm distal to the gastroesophageal junction, and selective myotomy of the circular muscle layer and defect closure with standard hemoclips. G-POEM was performed with a standard tunneling in the greater curvature followed by pyloric myotomy and defect closure (endoscopic hand suturing (EHS) or hemoclips). ESD was performed for early neoplasia in the esophagus, stomach, sigmoid colon and rectum.

For all procedures, the scope was utilized with an EP-8000 video processor (Fujifilm, Japan). Submucosal dissection was done preferably with the ACI (amber red color imaging) light function of the gastroscope ( Fig. 2 ). It offers enhanced visibility of the submucosal tissue with improved vessel visualization during the dissection phase 9 .

Study endpoints

The primary endpoint of this study was subjective workload and usability of the slim therapeutic gastroscope during third-space endoscopic procedures, assessed using the NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX) and the SUS, respectively.

Secondary endpoints included technical parameters (time to tunnel entry, tunneling and myotomy time, and tunnel closure time for POEM, dissection and total procedure time for ESD as well as crossover to standard gastroscope) and clinical outcomes (en bloc and R0 resection rates for ESD) and AEs during the hospital stay

All data were documented and analyzed in Microsoft Excel 365. The NASA-TLX and SUS questionnaires were filled out by the endoscopist after each procedure.

Subjective workload

The subjective workload was measured with a modified NASA-TLX questionnaire, details about which are shown in the Supplement.

A global workload score (0–10 points) was calculated as the mean of all six subscales (mental, physical, temporal demand, performance, effort, frustration). A score of 0–3 indicates a low, 4–6 moderate, and 7–10 high subjective workload.

Usability

Usability was assessed with a modified SUS questionnaire. Results were expressed as mean SUS score (0–100), with higher values indicating better usability (> 80 high usability, 60–80 acceptable and < 60 poor usability). Full details of the questionnaire are shown in the Supplement.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline and outcome data. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation. NASA-TLX and SUS scores were analyzed for the entire cohort and stratified by procedure type (POEM vs. ESD). Group comparisons were performed using independent t -tests or one-way ANOVA, as appropriate. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using Microsoft Excel 365. Because this was a single-arm, non-randomized, exploratory feasibility study, no formal size calculation was performed. We estimated that 30 procedures would be sufficient to provide preliminary data.

Results

Procedure feasibility, clinical outcomes, and AEs

Thirty consecutive third-space procedures were included: 13 E-POEM (including 3 re-do interventions), two Z-POEM, six G-POEM, and nine ESDs (including 3 recurrent lesions). All procedures were successfully completed and no device-related AEs occurred. During dissection in the ACI mode, no light mode-related relevant intraprocedural bleeding occurred. Crossovers to standard gastroscopes were done at the discretion of the endoscopist during defect closure in three ESDs (clip closure) and two G-POEMs (EHS). Technical success was 100% in all procedures. En bloc and R0 resection was achieved in all ESD cases.

POEM had, as expected, a shorter procedure time compared with ESD (70.9 ± 30.2 or 67.5 ± 30.2 vs. 147 ± 119.7). Time to tunnel entry was approximately 7 to 8 minutes in E-POEM and G-POEM. Tunneling time between E-POEM and G-POEM was similar with 25.6 ± 13.8 vs. 23.5 ± 10.9. Myotomy time was shorter in G-POEM than in E-POEM (7.7 ± 3.7 vs. 21.3 ± 12.5). Closure time was 6.1 ± 3.8 in E-POEM and 17 ± 21.3 in G-POEM due to closure with EHS. Procedure data are shown in Table 1 .

Subjective workload (NASA-TLX)

Mean modified NASA-TLX score across the entire cohort was 1.5 ± 1.1, signaling a low subjective workload. E-POEM and Z-POEM showed a lower NASA-TLX of 1.2 ± 0.7 as compared with G-POEM (1.7 ± 0.9) or ESD 1.8 ± 1.5. This difference across the groups was not statistically significant ( P = 0.38) and without clinical relevance.

Usability (SUS)

Mean SUS score for the entire cohort was 92.9 ± 7, indicating high usability across the groups. Comparable to NASA-TLX, E-POEM and Z-POEM scored slightly higher (95 ± 7.1) compared with G-POEM (92.7 ± 7.2) and ESD (89.4 ± 7). This difference across the groups was not statistically significant ( P = 0.18) and without clinical relevance.

Discussion

In this prospective, single-center feasibility, study, a novel slim gastroscope with a large working channel, extensive angulation ability, and ACI light functions proved to be feasible and safe for complex third-space procedures, including E-PEM, Z-POEM, G-POEM, and ESD.

The high usability underscores good maneuverability and handling, whereas the low NASA-TLX indicates low subjective workload and minimal mental and physical strain despite the new design of the scope. Improved visualization of the submucosa and vessels using ACI, as described in a recent study 10 , was confirmed in our cohort and may have contributed to both low subjective workload and high usability. Nevertheless, the results must be interpreted cautiously because comparison between different organs and procedures is difficult. However, good usability and low subjective workload across all procedures encourage us that this scope is useful in many scenarios.

A tendency toward lower NASA-TLX and higher SUS in POEM compared with ESD was seen. The slim gastroscope was advantageous during submucosal entry and tunneling. The reduced diameter and enhanced downward angulation offer greater flexibility during myotomy. This may also be explained by the more standardized nature of POEM, in which tunneling and myotomy are performed along a relatively straight line, whereas ESD requires continuous adaptation to lesion morphology and anatomical variability, which increases both cognitive and physical demands. In a recently published study, a similar observation was made with the slim gastroscope where these advantages could have led to shorter procedure times 5 . Future comparative work in similar procedures and organs (e.g. POEM vs. POEM or rectal ESD vs. rectal ESD) should address this issue.

Compared with other slim endoscopes (working channel 2.0–2.4 mm), the larger working channel (3.2 mm) allows use of standard therapeutic accessories. This offers endoscopists new flexibilities in therapeutic procedures such as dilation of post-ESD strictures, hemostasis in bleeding beyond a stenosis, or passage of a malignant stenosis to facilitate placement of an orojejunal catheter before EUS-guided gastroenterostomy.

However, some degree of instability may have warranted five crossovers (16.7%) to a standard gastroscope. These occurred in three ESDs and two G-POEMs. In all but one case, scope exchange was required during the closure phase. Clip closure using the “hold-and-drag” technique was challenging due to the increased flexibility. In G-POEM, closure was cumbersome when EHS was attempted because stable needle control was impaired and no dedicated distance cap was available. In addition, unwarranted movements of the dissection knife were observed, possibly due to the large working channel. Similar experience was reported, where scope size might have led to instability during pocket creation in gastric ESD. Careful handling, particularly in the duodenum, was needed to improve stability 4 . All crossovers occurred early in the study period, during which all participating endoscopists had limited experience. This indicates that despite low NASA-TLX and high SUS scores, a brief learning curve may be necessary, particularly for closure techniques requiring higher scope stability.

From an ergonomic perspective, combining workload (NASA-TLX) and usability (SUS) metrics provides a comprehensive evaluation that goes beyond technical outcomes. Especially because high-volume endoscopists reported a relevant prevalence of musculoskeletal disorders 8 , using an endoscope with a low subjective workload and high usability could be beneficial in the long term.

Limitations

This exploratory, single-arm, non-randomized study was conducted at a single center and all three endoscopists involved had advanced training in therapeutic endoscopy, making generalizability of study outcomes to lesser experienced endoscopists difficult. Also, all endoscopists assessing the device were male. The novel gastroscope utilizes the G7 grip, which is identical to that of previous and current generation devices. Thus, the scope is unlikely to cause grip-related gender disadvantages. We cannot exclude the possibility that greater effort is needed in torquing or maneuvering by female endoscopists due to the flexible design. Female endoscopists should be included in future studies to elucidate potential gender differences.

Conclusions

A novel slim therapeutic gastroscope demonstrates high usability and low subjective workload in third-space endoscopy. Comparative studies with standard gastroscopes are warranted and are currently being planned to further evaluate clinical and ergonomic outcomes.

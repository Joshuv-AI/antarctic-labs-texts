ABSTRACT
AbstractThe intricate process of coffee blossoming, pollination transfer, and successful development is crucial for creating every exquisite cup of coffee. During the flowering stage of coffee plants, delicate white flowers with a pleasant fragrance appear briefly, providing a limited opportunity for effective pollination. At this stage, the stigma of the flower, which is the female reproductive organ, becomes receptive and prepared to receive pollen. Existing research found methods such as machine learning and image analysis for monitoring crop pollination. Manual image annotation is conducted on pollen count disregarding spatial component of pollen collection which is essential for successful pollination. However, use of these strategies on coffee flowers by their complex structure and continuous changed in flowering stages. The paper represents novel methodologies by introducing methodological approach “IoU-AI” to monitor pollen transmission and success of pollination in coffee flower. IoU-AI utilize high resolution coffee flower image accurately track and detect floral organs by offering insight to pollen transfer. IoU-AI employs deep learning models to detect and observe floral components like stigma and anthers. Further computes overlap between structures and estimate pollen transmission. The flower detection accuracy was evaluated against ground truth measurements. Intersection over union-based computer vision

Intersection over union quantifies the overlap between two sets of pixels (or regions) and measures how well one set covers the other. Regions of interest identify two sets of pixels. ROI1 pixels representing pollen grains within the anther. ROI2 pixels representing the stigma surface receptive to pollen. ROI in image input has coffee flowers during the flowering stage and Pre-defined parameters are Minimum and maximum size of pollen grains (in pixels), color range for identifying pollen grains, and shape descriptors for identifying stigma surface. Estimated output are number of pollen grains within the anther (ROI1), stigma surface area receptive to pollen (ROI2), and Estimated pollen transfer based on overlap between ROIs.

Tracking the pollination success rate.

The image is given as input, Region Proposal Network (RPN) scans the image to propose regions (bounding boxes) that contain objects. The RPN uses a convolutional neural network (CNN) to generate region proposals. This involves calculating features for various image regions using the CNN and then applying a classification and regression network to predict whether a region contains an object and refine its bounding box. In level 1 detected regions are fed into R-CNN with a low IoU threshold of 0.3. This stage quickly filters out obvious non-objects. The surviving regions with predicted bounding boxes and class labels are passed to the next stage33. In level 2 each surviving region goes through another R-CNN with a higher IoU threshold of 0.5.

This stage refines the bounding boxes and classifies objects more precisely. Regions with high confidence scores and IoU values are kept, while others are discarded. The process repeats with increasingly stricter IoU thresholds in subsequent stages. Each level acts as a filter, progressively removing false positives and refining the remaining detections. The output stage refined bounding boxes and class labels for the detected objects with high confidence and IoU values. IOU = 1: Perfect overlap, indicating the prediction box perfectly matches the ground truth, 0 < IOU < 1: Partial overlap, with higher values signifying better localization accuracy, and IOU = 0: No overlap, meaning the prediction completely missed the object.

Unique contribution

This study uniquely contributes by developing a three-stage biologically informed vision paradigm for monitoring pollination in coffee crops which includes: Flowering detection41, Pollen transfer assessment42, and Pollen success verification using a Cascade R-CNN architecture evaluated via Intersection over Union (IoU) metrics.

Unlike previous works that only detect flowers or automate spraying, this study goes further by:

Conclusions

An innovative method for coffer flowering based pollen transferring and success estimation was trained-tested based on IoU with Cascade R-CNN. In comparison to existing work manual annotation, limiting scalability and objectively concentrating on pollen count, essential spatial dimension of pollen deposition. The proposed work, IoU-AI fills the research gap by measuring the overlap between receptive surfaces and pollen grains by providing expanded understanding of success factor and pollen transfer. Coffee flowering has remarkable characteristic captured and trained from stage: bud to stage: pollen success for high accuracy in detection from 94.77% to 85.34%, recall 95.78% to 88.27%, and precision 97.76% to 81.27%. Through the examination of high resolution coffee flower images IoU-AI achieved accuracy in tracking and detecting floral organ using Cascade R-CNN showcasing efficacy of complex flower structures. Moreover, comparing IoU-based pollen transfer predicts fruit set data demonstrating potential promising pollination success. Ongoing research is to identify characteristics of flower with low pollen transfer and tube growth which enable timely solution to improve pollination success. Larger dataset and diverse environment condition explore scalability. Integrating biological factors like stigma receptivity and pollen viability comprehensive understanding of pollination success. Exploring alternative advance deep learning architecture such as transformers for improving interpretability and performance. Future phase also focus on validation across several coffee cultivators, multi-resolution model adaptation, and robustness testing under different agro climatic and field circumstances to deploy large sector for real world pollination monitoring.

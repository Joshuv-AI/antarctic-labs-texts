Comparison of Incidence of Respiratory Depression Between Ciprofol versus Propofol for Procedural Sedation During First-Trimester Surgical Abortion: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Jing Chen, Ting Zhang, Conghui Zhang, Lingyi Xia, Linghua Zeng, Yunyan Zhu, Junjie Zhu, Yi Wang, Fuzhou Hua, Weidong Liang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRespiratory depression remains a major safety concern during propofol-based sedation for surgical abortion. Ciprofol is a novel intravenous sedative–hypnotic agent with pharmacological properties similar to propofol and a potentially improved safety profile. This randomized controlled trial aimed to compare the incidence of respiratory depression of ciprofol versus propofol, both combined with alfentanil, for procedural sedation in patients undergoing surgical abortion.MethodsIn this single-center, assessor-blinded, randomized controlled trial, 234 patients scheduled for surgical abortion under intravenous anesthesia were assessed for eligibility, and 226 were randomly assigned to receive either ciprofol (0.4 mg/kg; n=113) or propofol (2.0 mg/kg; n=113). Alfentanil (7.5 μg/kg) was administered intravenously as part of anesthesia induction in all patients. The primary outcome was the incidence of respiratory depression (SpO2 <90% requiring airway intervention) throughout the anesthesia and surgical procedure. Secondary outcomes included the overall incidence of perioperative adverse events as a composite safety endpoint defined as the occurrence of at least one prespecified adverse event from induction to discharge. Additional anesthesia-related variables, including induction characteristics, hemodynamic changes, depth of sedation, post-procedure pain scores, and patient satisfaction, were evaluated as exploratory outcomes.ResultsUnder the same anesthesia depth, respiratory depression occurred less frequently with ciprofol than with propofol (9.7% vs 20.4%, P=0.026). The overall incidence of perioperative adverse events was lower in the ciprofol group (28.3% vs 64.6%, P < 0.001). Injection pain was observed in 34.5% of patients receiving propofol and in none receiving ciprofol. PONV, hemodynamic instability, procedure time, time to discharge, post-procedure pain scores and patient satisfaction were comparable between groups. No serious adverse events occurred.ConclusionCiprofol combined with alfentanil was associated with a lower incidence of respiratory depression and perioperative adverse events than propofol, while providing comparable procedural sedation efficacy in patients undergoing surgical abortion.

Introduction

Unintended pregnancy and surgical abortion remain major global public health issues. According to reliable estimates, approximately 121 million unintended pregnancies occurred annually between 2015 and 2019, with 61% ending in surgical abortion, corresponding to roughly 73 million procedures worldwide.1 Vacuum aspiration is the standard surgical technique for early pregnancy termination and is commonly performed in outpatient settings.2 Procedural sedation or monitored anesthesia care (MAC) is routinely used to improve patient comfort. However, sedation for first-trimester abortion presents specific anesthetic challenges. Patients are typically positioned in the lithotomy position with a slight head-up (reverse Trendelenburg) tilt, while cervical dilation and intrauterine manipulation may provoke vagal responses.3 In addition, concomitant administration of short-acting opioids to attenuate procedural pain may further increase the risk of respiratory depression.3,4 Consequently, respiratory depression, bradycardia, hypotension, and postoperative nausea and vomiting(PONA) remain important perioperative complications.5 Given the short duration of the procedure and the need for rapid recovery and discharge, optimizing sedation safety is of clinical importance.

Propofol is widely used for MAC and procedural sedation due to its rapid onset and short recovery profile.6,7 However, its use is limited by a high incidence of injection pain and dose-dependent respiratory and cardiovascular depression, including hypoxemia, hypotension, and bradycardia.8 These limitations motivate the search for alternative sedative agents with improved tolerability and safety.

Ciprofol is a novel intravenous sedative-hypnotic that acts as a γ-aminobutyric acid type A (GABA-A) receptor agonist.9 Early clinical studies have demonstrated favorable safety and tolerability.10 Large, randomized trials of gastrointestinal endoscopic sedation have shown that ciprofol provides procedural success comparable to propofol while reducing injection pain and certain adverse events, including hypotension and respiratory depression.11,12 During surgical abortion, propofol is commonly combined with short-acting opioids to attenuate procedural pain and reduce sedative requirements.13 Alfentanil is particularly suitable for brief outpatient procedures because of its rapid onset, short duration of action, and favorable recovery profile.14

Despite these advances, respiratory depression and other adverse events remain common during procedural sedation for surgical abortion, and evidence directly comparing ciprofol with propofol in combination with alfentanil is lacking. Therefore, we conducted a prospective randomized controlled trial to compare the efficacy and safety of ciprofol versus propofol, each combined with alfentanil, for procedural sedation during surgical abortion. We hypothesized that ciprofol would reduce perioperative respiratory depression and other adverse events while maintaining satisfactory procedural conditions and recovery.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Ethics

This single-center, prospective, randomized, controlled trial was conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Gannan Medical University. The study protocol was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Gannan Medical University (LLSC-2023No.162, approved date: June 30, 2023) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the CONSORT guidelines. The trial was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300075304, registration date: August 31, 2023). Written informed consents were obtained from all participants prior to enrollment.

Participants

Recruitment and Setting

From September 2023 to April 2024, patients diagnosed with early intrauterine pregnancy who voluntarily requested surgical abortion under intravenous anesthesia at the outpatient Obstetrics and Gynecology Clinic were screened for eligibility.

Analysis Population

All randomized participants who received the study intervention were included in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis. No participants withdrew consent after randomization, and no protocol deviations resulted in exclusion from the primary analysis.

Randomization, Allocation Concealment, and Blinding

After confirmation of eligibility and completion of informed consent, participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either ciprofol (Group C) or propofol (Group P) using a computer-generated random number sequence. Allocation concealment was achieved using the sequentially numbered, opaque, and sealed envelopes.

Study drugs preparation and administration were performed by anesthesiologists who were aware of group allocation but was not involved in outcome assessment. Participants and outcome assessors were blinded to treatment assignment throughout the study.

Intervention and Procedural Sedation Protocol

Procedural Sedation and Monitoring

Procedural sedation was performed under standard monitoring, including electrocardiography, noninvasive blood pressure, pulse oximetry, and bispectral index (BIS), with supplemental oxygen administered via nasal cannula. Mean arterial pressure (MAP), heart rate (HR), and BIS were recorded at five predefined time points: before administration of the study drugs (T0), when adequate sedation was achieved (MOAA/S score ≤1 and BIS <60) (T1), at the start of uterine manipulation (T2), at the end of the procedure (T3), and 10 minutes after recovery of consciousness (T4).

Sedation Induction and Maintenance

After the surgical preparation, alfentanil hydrochloride (7.5 μg/kg) was administered intravenously to participants. One minute later, the participants received the assigned study sedative: ciprofol 0.4 mg/kg (Group C) or propofol 2 mg/kg (Group P). Injection pain was assessed during administration of the study sedative. Sedation depth was evaluated every 30 seconds using both the modified Observer’s Assessment of Alertness/Sedation (MOAA/S) scale and bispectral index (BIS) monitoring.15 Time to loses consciousness was defined as the interval from study sedative administration to achievement of BIS < 60. The procedure was initiated when adequate sedation was achieved, defined as a MOAA/S score ≤ 1 together with BIS < 60. Successful sedation induction was defined as attainment of these criteria within 2 minutes after the initial study sedative dose. If adequate sedation was not achieved, rescue sedation was administered (propofol 1 mg/kg or ciprofol 0.2 mg/kg) and repeated as clinically indicated until adequate sedation was achieved. If adequate sedation or airway patency could not be maintained, airway management was escalated according to the predefined protocol.

Management of Adverse Events and Recovery

Hemodynamic and respiratory adverse events were managed according to a predefined protocol. Hypotension (MAP < 70% of baseline or SBP ≤ 90 mmHg) was treated with ephedrine, and bradycardia (HR < 50 beats/min) with atropine. Respiratory depression (SpO2 ≤ 90%) was managed with airway maneuvers (jaw-thrust or chin-lift), supplemental oxygen, and escalation to bag-mask ventilation when required.

After the procedure, participants were transferred to the Post-Anesthetic Care Unit (PACU). Time to full recovery of consciousness was defined as the interval from completion of the last sedative dose to full recovery of consciousness. Post-procedure pain was assessed using the visual analog scale (VAS) at awakening and 10 minutes thereafter. Discharge readiness was determined using standard recovery criteria. Patient satisfaction was assessed using a structured anesthesia satisfaction questionnaire. The total score ranged from 0 to 9, with higher scores indicating greater satisfaction.

Outcomes and Definitions

Primary Outcome

The primary outcome was the incidence of perioperative respiratory depression, defined as an oxygen saturation (SpO2) < 90% and requiring airway intervention at any time during the perioperative period. Airway intervention consisted of jaw-thrust or chin-lift maneuvers with supplemental oxygen, with escalation to bag-mask ventilation when necessary.

The trial registry specified multiple primary safety indicators, including the incidence of adverse drug reactions as well as respiratory variables (SpO2 and respiratory rate). Respiratory depression was defined a priori based on these registered respiratory variables and was selected for primary hypothesis testing as the most clinically relevant and objectively measurable adverse drug reaction during procedural sedation. The overall incidence of adverse events was analyzed as a composite secondary safety outcome.

Secondary Safety Outcome

The key secondary safety outcome was the overall incidence of perioperative adverse events, defined as the occurrence of one or more of the following events: respiratory depression, injection pain, tachycardia, hypertension, hypotension, postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV), allergic reactions, or use of vasoactive drugs. The composite endpoint was analyzed at the patient level, such that each participant contributed only once to the overall incidence, irrespective of whether one or multiple adverse events occurred.

Other Prespecified Outcomes

Other prespecified outcomes included successful sedation induction; time to loss of consciousness; time to full recovery of consciousness; time to discharge; perioperative MAP, HR, and BIS values recorded at predefined time points; VAS pain scores at awakening and 10 minutes after awakening; and patient satisfaction assessed using the modified Patient Satisfaction in Anesthesia Score.16,17

Sample Size Calculation

Sample size estimation was based on the primary safety outcome of perioperative respiratory events requiring airway intervention. According to previous study, the incidence of events requiring airway intervention was 2.7% in patients receiving ciprofol and 23.0% in those receiving propofol.18 Using a two-sided α level of 0.05 and a statistical power of 90% for comparison of two independent proportions with a 1:1 allocation, the required sample size was estimated to be 43 participants per group. Allowing for a 10% attrition rate, at least 48 participants per group (96 participants in total) were required. Although the calculated minimum sample size was 96 participants, recruitment continued throughout the predefined study period using consecutive enrollment of all eligible patients. This resulted in a larger final sample, which increased the precision of treatment effect estimates and safety outcome assessments.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were assessed for normality prior to analysis using visual inspection and distributional characteristics. Normally distributed data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), whereas non-normally distributed data are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are expressed as numbers and percentages. Between-group comparisons for continuous variables were performed using the independent-samples t test for normally distributed data and the Mann–Whitney U-test for non-normally distributed data. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted according to the intention-to-treat principle using SPSS software, version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Study Population

A total of 234 participants were assessed for eligibility, of whom 226 were randomized, and included in the final analysis after 8 patients declined to participate. Ultimately, 226 patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive ciprofol (Group C, n = 113) or propofol (Group P, n = 113) combined with alfentanil and were included in the intention-to-treat analysis. All randomized patients received the allocated intervention and completed the procedure. Discussion

In this prospective randomized controlled trial of procedural sedation for surgical abortion, ciprofol combined with alfentanil was associated with a significantly lower incidence of perioperative respiratory depression than propofol combined with alfentanil. Injection pain was not observed in ciprofol group but occurred in more than one-third of patients receiving propofol. In addition, the patient-level composite incidence of perioperative adverse events was markedly lower in the ciprofol group. Procedural completion, discharge readiness, post-procedure pain scores, patient satisfaction, and hemodynamic profiles were otherwise comparable between groups. Given the increasing demand for ambulatory surgical abortion, improving respiratory safety while maintaining rapid recovery is of considerable clinical importance. Together, these findings support ciprofol as a well-tolerated alternative to propofol for procedural sedation in this setting.

Respiratory Safety as the Primary Outcome

Respiratory depression represents a critical safety endpoint during monitored anesthesia care for brief outpatient procedures, particularly in gynecologic settings where spontaneous ventilation is maintained and airway instrumentation is typically avoided. Although all respiratory events were transient and resolved with predefined noninvasive interventions, the lower incidence of respiratory depression observed with ciprofol is clinically meaningful because minimizing airway-related interventions is a major priority in ambulatory anesthesia.

This finding is consistent with previous reports from endoscopic sedation and anesthesia induction studies, in which ciprofol demonstrated a lower incidence of respiratory adverse events than propofol at equipotent sedative doses.19,20 The improved respiratory safety observed with ciprofol may be related to its higher affinity for the GABA-A receptor, allowing effective hypnosis at lower doses than propofol while maintaining adequate sedative efficacy. When combined with alfentanil, an opioid known to potentiate respiratory depression, this pharmacologic property may further reduce respiratory compromise. These results suggest that, under a standardized sedation and rescue protocol with the same opioid co-administration, ciprofol may provide a clinically meaningful respiratory safety advantage over propofol.

Overall Perioperative Adverse Events and Injection Pain

Beyond respiratory depression, the overall composite incidence of perioperative adverse events was substantially lower in the ciprofol group. This difference was driven primarily by a marked reduction in respiratory depression and injection pain, whereas other prespecified adverse events—including hemodynamic disturbances, postoperative nausea and vomiting, allergic reactions, and vasoactive drug use—were infrequent and comparable between groups. Because respiratory depression and injection pain are among the most clinically relevant adverse events during ambulatory abortion procedures, reducing these events may improve both perioperative safety and the overall patient experience.

Injection pain remains a common and distressing adverse effect of propofol, particularly in anxious patients undergoing surgical abortion. In this study, injection pain was not observed in any patient receiving ciprofol, whereas it affected more than one-third of patients in the propofol group. Although injection pain does not directly compromise physiological safety, it may exacerbate peri-induction stress responses and negatively influence the patient’s procedural experience.21 The absence of injection pain with ciprofol may be related to its modified molecular structure and formulation, which allow effective sedation at lower doses and reduce the free drug concentration in the aqueous phase implicated in propofol-related endothelial irritation.22 Therefore, the elimination of injection pain represents not only improved drug tolerability but also a potential enhancement in patient acceptance of intravenous sedation for ambulatory gynecologic procedures.

Procedural, Recovery, and Patient-Reported Outcomes

Both sedation regimens provided effective procedural conditions for vacuum aspiration, with high induction success rates and completion of all procedures without conversion to alternative anesthetic techniques. Although time to loss of consciousness differed statistically between groups, the absolute difference was small and unlikely to be clinically meaningful for ultra-short outpatient procedures. Likewise, although time to full recovery of consciousness was longer with ciprofol; however, this did not translate into prolonged time to discharge. These findings indicate that the respiratory safety and tolerability advantages of ciprofol were achieved without compromising procedural efficiency, postoperative recovery, or patient satisfaction.

Hemodynamics and Depth of Sedation

Perioperative mean arterial pressure and heart rate followed similar trends in both groups, indicating comparable hemodynamic stability. Although previous studies have reported a lower incidence of hypotension with ciprofol than with propofol, no significant between-group difference in hypotension was observed in the present study. This finding may be explained by the relatively young and healthy study population, the brief duration of the procedure, standardized induction doses, and the short exposure to sedative agents, all of which may have reduced the overall incidence of clinically significant hypotension. Bispectral index monitoring suggested adequate depth of sedation in both regimens. Bispectral index monitoring demonstrated comparable depth of sedation throughout the procedure, with no significant between-group differences at the predefined time points. These findings indicate that both sedative strategies achieved appropriate sedation depth while maintaining cardiovascular stability.

Limitation

Several limitations should be acknowledged. This was a single-center study, which may limit generalizability. Although participants and outcome assessors were blinded, the anesthesiologist administering the study drugs was not, introducing potential performance bias; however, objective endpoints and standardized rescue protocols mitigate this concern. Respiratory depression was defined using pulse oximetry-based criteria, and additional monitoring such as capnography might provide a more detailed assessment of ventilatory compromise. Finally, although dose selection was based on prior Phase III data and equipotent regimens, small variations in dose may substantially influence respiratory outcomes, and further dose–response studies are warranted.

Conclusions

In patients undergoing first-trimester surgical abortion, ciprofol combined with alfentanil reduced perioperative respiratory depression and the overall incidence of perioperative adverse events compared with propofol, while providing comparable procedural efficacy and recovery. These findings support ciprofol as a well-tolerated alternative to propofol for procedural sedation in this outpatient setting and warrant further validation in multicenter studies.

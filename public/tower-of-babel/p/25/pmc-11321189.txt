Machine learning-based model to predict composite thromboembolic events among Chinese elderly patients with atrial fibrillation
Authors: Jiefeng Ren, Haijun Wang, Song Lai, Yi Shao, Hebin Che, Zaiyao Xue, Xinlian Qi, Sha Zhang, Jinkun Dai, Sai Wang, Kunlian Li, Wei Gan, Quanjin Si
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveAccurate prediction of survival prognosis is helpful to guide clinical decision-making. The aim of this study was to develop a model using machine learning techniques to predict the occurrence of composite thromboembolic events (CTEs) in elderly patients with atrial fibrillation(AF). These events encompass newly diagnosed cerebral ischemia events, cardiovascular events, pulmonary embolism, and lower extremity arterial embolism.MethodsThis retrospective study included 6,079 elderly hospitalized patients (≥ 75 years old) with AF admitted to the People’s Liberation Army General Hospital in China from January 2010 to June 2022. Random forest imputation was used for handling missing data. In the descriptive statistics section, patients were divided into two groups based on the occurrence of CTEs, and differences between the two groups were analyzed using chi-square tests for categorical variables and rank-sum tests for continuous variables. In the machine learning section, the patients were randomly divided into a training dataset (n = 4,225) and a validation dataset (n = 1,824) in a 7:3 ratio. Four machine learning models (logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, XGBoost) were trained on the training dataset and validated on the validation dataset.ResultsThe incidence of composite thromboembolic events was 19.53%. The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) method, using 5-fold cross-validation, was applied to the training dataset and identified a total of 18 features that exhibited a significant association with the occurrence of CTEs. The random forest model outperformed other models in terms of area under the curve (ACC: 0.9144, SEN: 0.7725, SPE: 0.9489, AUC: 0.927, 95% CI: 0.9105–0.9443). The random forest model also showed good clinical validity based on the clinical decision curve. The Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) showed that the top five features associated with the model were history of ischemic stroke, high triglyceride (TG), high total cholesterol (TC), high plasma D-dimer, age.ConclusionsThis study proposes an accurate model to stratify patients with a high risk of CTEs. The random forest model has good performance. History of ischemic stroke, age, high TG, high TC and high plasma D-Dimer may be correlated with CTEs.

Introduction

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is indeed a major cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. With the aging of the population and the continuous progress of medical technology, the average life span of human beings continues to extend, and the number of elderly patients with AF continues to increase. Atrial fibrillation is characterized by an irregular and often rapid heartbeat, which disrupts the normal blood flow in the heart. This abnormal flow pattern can lead to the formation of blood clots within the atria (upper chambers of the heart). If these blood clots dislodge and travel through the bloodstream, they can cause blockages in the arteries of various organs. It is associated with an increased risk of stroke, cardiovascular events, pulmonary embolism, lower extremity arterial embolism and all-cause mortality [1–5].

Previous studies have shown that the incidence and prevalence of AF in older adults increases with age. In the 75–84 age group, the incidence of AF in older adults is 10 to 12% and the prevalence is 8 to 10%. In the age group 85 years and older, the incidence and prevalence increase to 16 to 20% and 12 to 16%, respectively [6, 7]. According to a 2018 Chinese study, the annual incidence of AF in adults older than 75 years is 3.34% [8]. According to a study published in the European Heart Journal, the incidence of thromboembolic events in patients with AF older than 75 years was 7.91%. The study included patients with primary nonparoxysmal AF from 23 countries, 7,243 of whom were older than 75 years [9].

The CHA2DS2-VASc scoring system is widely used to assess thromboembolism risk in AF patients. A score of 2 or higher indicates the need for anticoagulant therapy in high-risk patients. However, for AF patients aged 75 or above, all scores above 2 are considered high-risk, and other risk factors have limited significance in the evaluation. Consequently, the traditional CHA2DS2-VASc scoring system may not accurately assess thromboembolism risk in this elderly population. As medical conditions improve and individual healthcare awareness increases, a growing number of relatively healthy AF patients aged 75 or above are being identified. These patients may not exhibit CHA2DS2-VASc scoring-related risk factors such as hypertension, diabetes, or heart failure, yet they are still susceptible to thromboembolic events. Therefore, further analysis of clinical data is necessary to develop a more applicable risk prediction model and explore additional risk factors associated with thromboembolism in AF [10, 11].

Machine learning is a powerful tool that allows researchers to objectively analyze large sets of data in order to predict clinical outcomes. In the field of healthcare, it has been particularly useful in identifying associations between patient characteristics and outcomes. Numerous studies have demonstrated the efficiency of machine learning in predicting prognosis and aiding in decision making [12, 13]. Prognostic prediction models, developed using various machine learning algorithms, have consistently outperformed traditional regression models in terms of accuracy [14–17]. Previous study demonstrated the high diagnostic value of the AF risk prediction models developed using machine learning algorithms. Among the models, RF and XGBoost exhibited superior efficacy. Additionally, the study identified total bilirubin, triglycerides, and uric acid as the three most important predictors of AF in this specific population [17]. To further explore the correlation between clinical characteristics and AF in older patients, a machine learning study was conducted. The goal of this study was to use machine learning techniques to develop a model for predicting composite thromboembolic events in elderly AF patients. The study employed a logistic regression model as well as three machine learning algorithms - decision tree, random forest, and XGboost. Furthermore, a calculator based on the R programming language was created to support the clinical application of the optimal models.

Methods

Design and patients

The study population consisted of elderly patients with AF who were admitted to the Chinese PLA General Hospital between January 2010 and June 2022. The Medical Big Data Center of the hospital provided the database of clinical data for these patients. The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: AF was included in the admission diagnosis, aged 75 years or older, hospitalization time of at least 2 days (48 h) and completion of clinical medical records. Patients with hematological diseases and other malignant infectious diseases were excluded. After applying these criteria, a total of 6079 AF patients’ clinical data were included in our study. Random forest imputation method was used to handle missing data, and the difference between the data before and after imputation was analyzed. Those patients were randomly split 7:3 into training dataset (n = 4225) and validation dataset (n = 1824), and the comparison between the training dataset and the validation dataset was performed. Randomization was performed with the use of a computer. Logistic regression model, decision tree, random forest and XGBoost machine learning algorithm were used to train and optimize the models in turn in the training dataset, and the validation dataset was used to test the predictive performance of these models. Figure 1 shows the flowchart outlining patient’s enrollment and study design. This study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Chinese People’s Liberation Army General Hospital in Beijing, China. The study was conducted in compliance with the Helsinki Declaration of 1975 (as revised in 1983).

Definition and outcome

According to the guidelines of the American College of Cardiology (ACC), American Heart Association (AHA), and European Society of Cardiology (ESC), the diagnosis of AF is made by the primary physician based on clinical history, symptoms of palpitations, and specific tests, including resting and exercise electrocardiography. The blood test results were obtained from the initial laboratory tests conducted on the patients upon admission. Subsequently, composite thromboembolic events that occurred during or after their hospitalization were reviewed as outcome events. These thromboembolic events included newly diagnosed of cerebral ischemia events, cardiovascular events, pulmonary embolism, and lower extremity arterial embolism. composite thromboembolic events were characterized by review of electronic medical records and imaging reports. Inadequate anticoagulation therapy refers to elderly AF patients (age ≥ 75 years) did not receive anticoagulation therapy without any specific justification, or when they did receive anticoagulation therapy but exhibited poor adherence to the prescribed medication regimen.

Modeling and validation

To build a predictive model for the occurrence of composite thromboembolic events in these patients, we used logistic regression model and three machine learning models, decision tree, random forest and XGBoost model, to build predictive models in the training group. The LASSO method, combined with 5-fold cross-validation, was conducted on the training dataset, to identify variables that enhance the performance of our model. Consequently, these 18 variables were included in the model due to their ability to improve the goodness of fit.

Statistics and implementation

Categorical variables were expressed in proportion, using chi-square test or continuously corrected chi-square test; Continuous variables with non-normal distributions were expressed in median and quartile ranges (IQR) using Wilcoxon rank tests. For continuous variables conforming to normal distribution and homogeneity of variances, the t test was applied, expressed as mean and standard deviation. Continuous calibration Chi-square test was used for categorical variables, and the characteristics of patients with continuous calibration Chi-square test or continuous calibration Chi-square test for categorical variables were compared. The statistical analyses were conducted using the R programming language (version 4.2.1, http://www.R-project.org). Significance was determined by a P value less than 0.05, which was considered significant in both directions (two-tailed). Machine learning modeling and interpretation is performed in applications based on the R language.

Result

Basic characteristics of the patient

Among 6,079 elderly patients with AF, there were 1,189 cases (19.55%) of newly diagnosed thromboembolic events. Specifically, there were 893 cases (14.69%) of new-onset cerebral embolism, 202 cases (3.32%) of acute coronary syndrome, 63 cases (1.04%) of pulmonary embolism, and 31 cases (0.51%) of lower limb arterial embolism. The median age for the entire dataset was 80 years [75 years, 102 years]. Most patients were male (52.2%). The clinical characteristics of patients with and without composite thromboembolic events were analyzed and compared. Very elderly AF patients with composite thromboembolic events tend to be older, have higher TG and D-dimer. Patients with CTEs had higher levels of glucose, urea, and WBC and lower levels of RBC, HDL-C, and albumin. In addition, patients with CTEs tended to have higher comorbidities, such as more CAD, hypertension, DM, CHF and CKD, history of stroke, history of vascular disease, hyperlipidemia, infectious diseases. Regarding administration of anticoagulation drugs during hospitalization, patients with CTEs had significant less anticoagulation therapy. These findings were consistently significant after Benjamini-Hochberg correction (Table 1).

Model development

According to the LASSO method and 5-fold cross-validation, a total of 18 clinical features were found to be significantly associated with the occurrence of CTEs. It is important to note that the variable selection process was conducted based on the training dataset. These features include cardiac insufficiency, renal insufficiency, hypertension, diabetes, stroke, history of vascular disease, history of venous thrombosis, hyperlipidemia, gender, bedridden for more than 7 days, heart rate, age, total cholesterol, triglycerides, anticoagulant deficiency, total protein, uric acid, and D-dimer. Therefore, we utilized these 18 clinical features as predictors in our model development using logistic regression and three machine learning models. Through training and optimization of the models, we obtained the optimal parameters for each model. We found the best cut-off point based on the maximum Youden’s index method in the training dataset, and applied this point in the validation dataset to test model performance [18].

Model verification

For the overall performance of each model, the AUC of logistic regression model.

was 0.790, decision tree model was 0.730, random forest model was 0.927, and XGBoost model was 0.886 (Fig. 2A). The accuracy, sensitivity, specificity and AUC of each model revealed that the random forest model outperformed the others (Table 2). Clinical decision curve analysis shows that the random forest model has good clinical validity. Generally, an ideal decision curve is concave upwards, indicating the highest net benefit at the peak of the curve [19]. Comparing the different curves, the decision curve of random forest has higher net benefits than the other models (Fig. 2B). Based on the random forest model, this study further analyzed the significance of this model for significant clinical features of the whole population. According to the length of the bars in Fig. 2C, the top five most important variables were stroke history, total cholesterol, triglycerides, plasma D-dimer, and age. According to Fig. 2D, The color of the points varies according to their corresponding numerical magnitude. Its color changes from purple to yellow, which means its corresponding value changes from small to large (Fig. 2D). The variables “triglyceride (TG), total cholesterol (TC), and D-dimer” are blood test results, the descriptive statistics and SHAP analysis in the results section of the paper show that the values of these continuous variables are positively correlated with adverse outcomes. Therefore, history of stroke, high cholesterol, high triglycerides, and high plasma D-dimer were most significantly associated with outcome events.

Discussion

This study developed an accurate model to identify the likelihood of CTEs in elderly patients with AF. Four machine learning techniques were introduced for analysis in this study, and the random forest model showed the best prediction performance compared to other models. The AUC of the model was 0.927 (95% CI: 0.9105–0.9443), which had a good prediction effect. In addition, to encourage clinical use of the model, the study constructed an application based on the R-project. The LASSO method and 5-fold cross validation identified 18 clinical features that could enhance the performance of our model. These risk factors were history of stroke, TG, TC, D-dimer, age, cardiac insufficiency, renal insufficiency, hypertension, diabetes, history of vascular disease, history of venous thrombosis, hyperlipidemia, gender, inadequate anticoagulation, bedridden for more than 7 days, heart rate, serum uric acid, total protein. The SHAP analysis identified the top five features associated with model, which include history of stroke, triglycerides, total cholesterol, plasma D-dimer, and age.

Previous studies have found that the incidence of AF almost doubles with every 10-year increment in age above 60. Although the prevalence of AF varies in countries and regions of different sociodemographic characteristics, aging is consistently found to be associated with increased prevalence and incidence of AF.

In data published from the GBD 2017 study, there is a stepwise increase in the global prevalence of AF with advancing age: 0.07% in 30 to 34 years, 0.72% in 55 to59 years, 6.52% in 80 to 84 year, and 8.18% in 95 years and older. Advancing age is not only a risk factor for AF; it is also a strong risk factor for stroke in AF. Age 65 years and older is associated with a more than 3-fold increase in stroke risk (RR, 3.33),and odds ratio for stroke in age greater than 75 years is 1.76 [20].

However, current research on the prognosis of AF patients mostly focuses on the analysis of risk factors in all age groups. There is still a lack of research on risk factors and prognosis in elderly AF patients aged ≥ 75 years. This study developed a novel model aimed at helping clinicians timely identify elderly AF patients at high risk of composite thromboembolic events.

History of stroke does increase the risk of CTEs in patients with AF. Several studies and clinical trials have demonstrated the association between history of stroke and increased risk of thromboembolic complications in AF patients. The mechanism underlying this increased risk is primarily related to the formation of blood clots in the left atrial appendage (LAA), a small pouch-like structure within the left atrium. The LAA is particularly prone to blood stasis due to the chaotic atrial contractions in patients with AF, facilitating the formation of clots. Furthermore, a history of stroke adds additional complexity to the management of AF patients. The history of stroke, especially cardioembolic in nature, is often associated with a higher thromboembolic risk compared to patients without a history of stroke. This may necessitate more aggressive anticoagulation strategies and closer monitoring of these patients [21, 22].

There is evidence to suggest that elevated total cholesterol or triglycerides can increase the risk of thromboembolic events in patients with AF. High total cholesterol and triglyceride levels have been associated with an increased risk of cardiovascular diseases, including AF. A systematic review and meta-analysis published in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology in 2019 found that higher total cholesterol levels were independently associated with an increased risk of AF. The study included over 1.2 million individuals, and the risk of AF increased linearly with increasing total cholesterol levels within the observed range. Stroke are major concerns in patients with AF. Total cholesterol and triglyceride levels, along with other risk factors like hypertension and diabetes, play significant roles in the development of atherosclerosis and subsequent thromboembolic events. Atherosclerosis is characterized by the formation of plaque in the arteries, which can lead to clot formation and subsequent embolization to different organs, including the brain, causing a stroke. High cholesterol levels are known to contribute to the formation and progression of atherosclerosis, thus increasing the risk of CTEs in patients with AF [23].

Furthermore, a study published in Thrombosis Research in 2018 investigated the association between lipid abnormalities and the risk of thromboembolic events in patients with AF. The study found that elevated total cholesterol and triglyceride levels were independently associated with an increased risk of thromboembolic complications, beyond other established risk factors such as age, gender, hypertension, and diabetes. The impact of cholesterol and triglycerides on adverse events in patients with AF is not limited to thromboembolism. High lipid levels have also been associated with an increased risk of adverse cardiovascular events, such as heart failure and myocardial infarction. This was demonstrated in a study published in the European Heart Journal in 2018, which found that higher cholesterol levels were associated with an increased risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes in patients with AF. In conclusion, elevated total cholesterol and triglyceride levels have been shown to increase the risk of thromboembolic events in patients with AF. These lipid abnormalities contribute to the development of atherosclerosis and subsequently increase the risk of clot formation and embolization, leading to stroke and other adverse cardiovascular events. It is important to manage lipid levels along with other risk factors to reduce the overall risk of complications in patients with AF [24–26].

There is evidence that suggests elevated D-dimer levels are associated with an increased risk of thromboembolic events in patients with AF. D-dimer is a marker of activation of the coagulation system and fibrinolysis, and elevated levels indicate an increased activity of clot formation and degradation. They found that elevated D-dimer levels were independently associated with an increased risk of thromboembolic events, major bleeding, and mortality in AF patients. The study concluded that measuring D-dimer levels could help identify AF patients at higher risk of complications [27]. Study by Yang X et al. (2021) investigated the relationship between D-dimer levels and the risk of stroke and systemic embolism in AF patients treated with direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs). The results demonstrated that elevated D-dimer levels were associated with a higher risk of stroke or systemic embolism, providing evidence that D-dimer measurement could be used to refine stroke risk stratification in AF patients on DOACs [28].

These findings suggest that elevated D-dimer levels can serve as a valuable biomarker for identifying AF patients at a higher risk of thromboembolic complications and bleeding events. Incorporating D-dimer levels into clinical risk stratification models may help guide treatment decisions. Overall, these studies suggest that elevated D-dimer levels in patients with AF are linked to an increased risk of composite thromboembolic events, such as stroke and systemic embolism [29].

Aging is a key factor for the occurrence of thromboembolic events in patients with AF. As individuals aging, the risk of intra-atrial thrombosis increases due to a variety of factors, including changes in blood flow patterns, endothelial dysfunction, and prethrombotic changes [30, 31].

Actually speaking, previous studies have developed models to predict thromboembolic events outcome among patients with AF using machine learning techniques. For instance, Seonwoo Jung et al. developed a machine learning-based ischemic stroke prediction model, achieving an AUC of 0.727, albeit limited to predicting strokes among AF patients [21]. Similarly, Zhang F et al. investigated disordered plasma proteins and metabolites between AF and AF-related stroke (AFS) patients, a machine learning model was utilized to pinpoint potential biomarkers for stroke in AF, resulting in the identification of 6 proteins and 6 metabolites with an impressive AUC of 0.976 [32]. Our established random forest model yielded an AUC of 0.927 (95% CI: 0.9105–0.9443). Notably, in comparison to previous studies, our research focuses particularly on elderly atrial fibrillation (AF) patients, aiming to identify potential associated risk factors for thromboembolic events and contribute to the foundation for future extensive prospective studies in this demographic.

Prospect

Deep learning algorithms excel in capturing complex relationships within non-linear problems and high-dimensional data, resulting in higher accuracy and predictive capability. In contrast, machine learning offers the advantage of being applicable to datasets of varying sizes, ranging from small to large-scale. This is due to its reliance on simple statistical models and algorithms, which also enhance its ease of understanding and interpretability. These distinct strengths of deep learning and machine learning make them valuable tools for addressing different aspects of data analysis and decision-making in various domains.

The current research landscape regarding thromboembolic complications in AF patients, particularly among the elderly population, has seen limited exploration in the realm of deep learning. This study has contributed insights using traditional machine learning algorithms. However, as a future direction, we aim to delve into the realm of deep learning methodologies. By harnessing the power of deep learning, we anticipate uncovering nuanced patterns and predictive models that can enhance our understanding of thromboembolic complications in AF, specifically among the geriatric cohort [33].

Limitation

In this study, we employed random forest imputation to address missing data, and conducted a differential analysis of the data before and after imputation. The results revealed statistical differences in only two variables, which are presented in the supplementary material. Random forest imputation is a widely used method for handling missing data due to its ability to capture complex relationships and patterns in the data. By leveraging the information from other variables, random forest imputation estimates the missing values based on the observed data. However, it is important to acknowledge that the imputation process may introduce bias and potentially alter the original distribution of the data.

This study may have limitations because there are differences in variables such as age between the training and validation datasets, which may introduce selection bias and limit the generalisability of the model. It is a retrospective observational study, it can only identify variables associated with the outcome, and further prospective research is needed to assess the impact of adjusting these variables on the outcome. Additionally, due to the lack of external validation of the model used in this study, further research is needed to investigate its application in other dataset.

Conclusion

This study proposes an model for stratifying elderly AF patients with high-risk CTEs. The random forest model demonstrates excellent predictive performance. History of ischemic stroke, age, high TG, high TC and high plasma D-Dimer may be correlated with CTEs.

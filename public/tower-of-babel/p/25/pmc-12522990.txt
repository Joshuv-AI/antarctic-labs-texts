miR-106a-5p targets CREB1 to regulate the inflammatory response in diabetic retinopathy
Authors: Lijuan Wu, Huan Li, Jia Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDiabetic retinopathy (DR) is a leading cause of vision loss in diabetic patients, yet early diagnostic biomarkers and targeted therapies remain limited. While miR-106a-5p has been linked to inflammatory and metabolic regulation, its role in DR remains poorly characterized.ObjectiveThis study mainly explores the diagnostic value and molecular mechanism of miR-106a-5p/CREB1 in DR.MethodsThe relative expression of miR-106a-5p in 50 patients with diabetes only (NDR) and 60 patients with DR was detected by qRT-PCR. Construct a high glucose-induced ARPE-19 cell model to evaluate changes in cell proliferation, inflammatory factors such as TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β, and CREB1 expression after transfection with miR-106a-5p mimetics. Dual luciferase validation of miR-106a-5p/CREB1 target. Clinical correlations were analyzed by Pearson and logistic regression.ResultsmiR-106a-5p was significantly downregulated in DR compared to NDR (P < 0.0001), and the ROC curve indicated that miR-106a-5p had high accuracy in DR diagnosis (AUC = 0.85). It correlated inversely with FPG (r = − 0.77), HbA1c (r = − 0.68), and inflammatory markers (IL-6, TNF-α). Binary logic analysis identified miR-106a-5p as a risk factor for diabetic retinopathy (OR = 0.08, P < 0.001). In vitro, miR-106a-5p overexpression attenuated HG-induced inflammation by targeting CREB1 (P < 0.0001).ConclusionmiR-106a-5p is considered an innovative biomarker for the detection of DR, research is exploring the part of miR-106a-5p/CREB1 in regulating inflammation as well as affecting DR occurrence, providing new ideas for DR treatment.Keywords: Diabetic retinopathy, MiR-106a-5p, CREB1, Inflammation, Biomarker

Introduction

Diabetic retinopathy (DR), a common ocular complication in patients with diabetes mellitus (DM), has affected nearly one-quarter of diabetic patients worldwide in recent years [1, 2]. Existing studies have shown that diabetes may damage the microvascular system, particularly the retina and optic nerve, leading to an increasing incidence of ocular diseases [3]. Notably, timely detection and intervention in DR can significantly reduce the associated risks of visual impairment and blindness [4]. Recent studies have shown that biomarkers such as serum measurements, proteomics, C-reactive protein (CRP), and microRNAs are widely used in DR prediction [5, 6]. Therefore, the exploration of relevant biomarkers has become an effective strategy for DR prevention and treatment.

As post-transcriptional regulators, multiple microRNAs (miRNAs) like miR-152, miR-221-3p, and miR-122 have been found to be involved in DR progression by modulating cellular biological processes such as cell proliferation and migration [7–9]. Studies have found that miR-150 is downregulated in diabetic patients and regulates inflammation, apoptosis, and pathological angiogenesis [10]. Research has shown that miR-874 [11] and miR-142-5p [12] are involved in regulating the development of DR by modulating the NF-κ B and IGF1/p-IGF1R signaling pathways. Among them, miR-106a-5p promotes chronic wound repair in diabetes by mediating cell proliferation; the miR-106a-5p/ROCK2 axis alleviates HG-induced injury and inflammatory responses in HK2 cells; and it regulates DM by participating in cell proliferation, inflammatory responses, and intercellular crosstalk with macrophages [13–15]. In addition, the dysregulated expression of miR-106a-5p in the retina and serum of diabetic mice has been shown to affect the development of diabetic retinopathy. However, the value of miR-106a-5p in DR diagnosis has not been validated, and the molecular mechanism of its regulation of DR is still unclear. As a target gene of miR-106a-5p CREB1 (cAMP response element-binding protein 1) belongs to the basic leucine zipper (bZIP) family of transcription factors and participates in DM by regulating inflammatory cytokines and inhibiting hepatic gluconeogenesis [16, 17].

To identify novel biomarkers for early diagnosis and potential pharmacological targets for the treatment of DR, this study focuses on miR-106a-5p to determine its clinical diagnostic value and explore the molecular mechanism of the miR-106a-5p/CREB1 axis in cells, thereby providing new insights for DR therapy.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

In this study, 50 DM patients without DR (NDR) and 60 DR patients admitted to Longyan First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University between 2022 and 2025 were selected, with age and sex matching (according to international clinical grading criteria). Collect basic information all participants in DR and NDR, and measure fasting blood glucose (FPG), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting insulin, total cholesterol (TC), white blood cell count (WBC), and other indicators from the collected venous blood samples. This study included (1) type 2 diabetes (in line with ADA standards); (2) DR patients were diagnosed by fundus fluorescein angiography (according to international clinical grading standards); (3) Age range: 35–80 years old. And excluded (1) glaucoma, macular degeneration and other non diabetes eye diseases and eye surgery; (2) Serious systemic diseases (cancer, liver and kidney dysfunction, etc.); (3) Autoimmune diseases or long-term use of immunosuppressants.

The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The research protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Longyan First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University (20220036), and all of the participants provided signed informed consent.

Cell culture

Human retinal pigment epithelial cells (ARPE-19, Thermo, USA) were thawed and resuspended in DMEM/F12 medium (Thermo) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. The cells were cultured at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂.

Cell transfection

After culturing ARPE-19 cells for 24 h, the medium was replaced with serum-free DMEM/F12. The cells were then transfected with miR-106a-5p mimic or miRNA mimic negative control (NC) using Lipofectamine 3000 (Thermo, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. 24 h after transfection, ARPE-19 cells were cultured in normal glucose (NG, 5.5 mM D-glucose) medium for high glucose (HG) treatment until a confluence of approximately 70%. Then completely discard the culture medium. The cells in the HG group were washed once with PBS and then replaced with fresh culture medium containing 30 mM D-glucose; The control group cells underwent the same medium replacement procedure, but were replaced with fresh medium containing 5.5 mM D-glucose. This controls any potential effects that may arise from the replacement of the culture medium itself. After 48 h of exposure to HG or control conditions, collect the cell culture supernatant for ELISA and collect the cells for RNA/protein analysis.

qRT-PCR profiles

Total RNA was isolated from serum and cells with Trizol reagent. For microRNAs, reverse transcription was carried out with the minute miRNA First-Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Ribobio, China), while reverse transcription of mRNAs utilized the SuperScript IV First-Strand Synthesis Kit (Invitrogen, USA). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) was performed with SYBR Green (AG, China) to assess expression levels. U6 small nuclear RNA was used as the endogenous control for miR-106a-5p, whereas GAPDH mRNA served as the internal reference for CREB1. Calculate the relative expression level using 2−ΔΔCt.

CCK-8 assay

Cell viability was assessed using the CellTiter 96® AQueous One Solution Reagent (Invitrogen, USA). CCK-8 reagent was added to each treatment group at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h post-transfection, followed by incubation in the dark. Absorbance values were measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader to calculate cell viability.

Enzyme-Linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Following cell treatment, the supernatant was harvested and centrifuged at 1000×g for 10 min. Levels of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β were quantified using commercial ELISA kits in strict accordance with the manufacturer’s protocols. Absorbance readings at 450 nm were acquired via a microplate reader, and concentrations of the inflammatory cytokines were derived from standard curve calculations.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The fragment of CREB1 was cloned into pGL3-Basic to generate the wild-type (WT) reporter vector, while the CREB1 3’UTR with mutated binding sites was constructed into pGL3-Basic as the mutant (MUT) vector. Experimental groups were established as follows: control, mimic NC, and miR-106a-5p mimic. Following a 48-hour treatment period, luciferase activity was measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System.

Statistical analysis

Use SPSS 26.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0 for data processing and charting. Using multiple logistic analysis, potential confounding factors such as age and HbA1c were included in the model along with miR-106a-5p, with DR incidence status as the dependent variable. The chi square test is used for non continuous variables, the independent sample t-test for continuous variables is used for two group comparisons, and one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) is used for multi group comparisons, and ROC curve analysis was used to evaluate the specificity and sensitivity of miR-106a-5p in distinguishing DR and NDR patients, and to evaluate the diagnostic ability of biomarkers. Pearson performed FPG, HbA1c, Fasting insulin, WBC and miR-106a-5p correlation analysis. Normal distribution data is represented as mean ± standard deviation, and each experiment was independently repeated three times [18].

miR-106a-5p plays a role in DR diagnosis

The qRT PCR results showed that the average expression level of miR-106a-5p in the serum of DR patients was significantly lower than that of NDR patients (P < 0.0001, Fig. 1a). ROC curve analysis showed that the AUC of miR-106a-5p was 0.85, with a 95% CI of 0.78–0.92, P < 0.0001, This indicates significant diagnostic value in DR disease (Fig. 1b).

Correlation analysis of miR-106a-5p

Results showed that the relative expression level of miR-106a-5p was significantly negatively correlated with glycemic indices including FPG (r = − 0.77, Fig. 2a), HbA1c (r = − 0.68, Fig. 2b), fasting insulin (r = − 0.78, Fig. 2c), and the inflammatory marker WBC (r = − 0.74, Fig. 2d) (all P < 0.0001).

miR-106a-5p is a key risk factor for DR

Binary logistic analysis revealed that HbA1c, a common risk factor for DR, had an OR: 4.1, with a 95% CI: 1.52–11.11 and a P = 0.005. However, miR-106a-5p was identified as a more significant risk factor, with an OR of 0.08, a 95% CI of 0.03–0.22, and a P-value < 0.001. (Table 2).

miR-106a-5p alleviates high-glucose-induced damage

In an HG in vitro induction model, ARPE-19 cells exhibited significantly reduced relative expression of miR-106a-5p under HG treatment (P < 0.0001, Fig. 3a), with the most pronounced decrease in proliferation rate observed at 72 h (P < 0.001, Fig. 3b). Transfection with the miR-106a-5p mimic in the HG environment significantly upregulated miR-106a-5p expression when compared with the negative control (P < 0.01, Fig. 3a) and attenuated the decrease in proliferation rate (P < 0.05, Fig. 3b). Notably, HG induction significantly upregulated inflammatory cytokines IL-6 (Fig. 3c), IL-1β (Fig. 3d), and TNF-α (Fig. 3e), whereas miR-106a-5p mimic treatment significantly reduced their levels (P < 0.0001).

miR-106a-5p targets and regulates CREB1

Utilize Target Scan (http://www.targetscan.org/vert_72/) Predicted that miR-106a-5p binds to 4197–4203 in the 3 ‘UTR region of CREB1 (Fig. 4a). Dual-luciferase reporter assay results showed that transfection with miR-106a-5p mimic significantly decreased the relative luciferase activity of WT-CREB1 (P < 0.0001), In contrast, no significant alteration was detected in MUT-CREB1 luciferase activity (Fig. 4b). Additionally, HG induction significantly upregulated CREB1 expression, which was markedly reduced by miR-106a-5p mimic compared to the mimic NC group (Fig. 4c). These findings show that miR-106a-5p negatively regulates CREB1 in a target-dependent manner during high-glucose responses.

Discussion

DR exemplifies a classic microvascular complication of diabetes, marked by silent early development where clinical manifestations are frequently absent, as a result enabling undetected progression to rapid and irreversible retinal injury [19, 20]. Although substantial advancements have been made in DR therapeutic approaches, exploring precise diagnostic biomarkers and treatment strategies is crucial, especially considering ongoing limitations in worldwide medical resources [6].

As an increasing number of miRNAs are recognized for their pivotal roles in disease diagnosis and therapy, accumulating evidence has linked miR-106a-5p to the regulation of diabetes-associated complications, including nephropathy, hepatic disorders, and immune dysfunctions [21, 22]. There is research confirming that extracellular vesicle miR-106a can affect the increase of cell apoptosis rate and proliferation activity, as well as activate the TGF-β pathway [23]. Chiara et al. previously predicted via bioinformatics analyses of Gene Expression Omnibus datasets that miR-106a-5p tends to modulate DR progression in murine models [24, 25], yet its clinical diagnostic utility for human DR remained uncharacterized. In the present study, miR-106a-5p was significantly downregulated in serum samples of DR patients compared to NDR patients, a trend consistent with its expression pattern in other diabetic conditions [14, 26]. Notably, miR-106a-5p demonstrated robust diagnostic value, with ROC curve analysis highlighting its discriminatory power for DR. Clinical data from the cohort revealed that glycemic control markers (FPG, HbA1c, fasting insulin) and the inflammatory parameter WBC were strongly associated with DR severity. Strikingly, miR-106a-5p expression exhibited significant inverse correlations with all four factors, which suggests a mechanistic link involving the integration of metabolic and inflammatory signaling pathways in DR pathogenesis. Most importantly, multivariate logistic regression identified miR-106a-5p as an independent risk factor for DR, with predictive accuracy surpassing traditional clinical indices such as HbA1c [27]. In addition, studies have shown that long-term exposure to high blood sugar can accelerate retinal oxidative damage and inflammation accumulation [18], and baseline information suggests that insulin may affect miRNA expression by improving the metabolic microenvironment. Therefore, the longer course of diabetes may aggravate the down-regulation of miR-106a-5p, and insulin treatment and DR stage are potential confounding factors of DR. Collectively, these findings establish miR-106a-5p as a promising biomarker for early DR detection, warranting further validation in longitudinal clinical cohorts.

In DR, retinal epithelial cells represent the primary targets of hyperglycemia-induced damage. Leveraging this pathological context, the present study employed an HG-induced ARPE-19 cell model to dissect the molecular regulatory pathways of miR-106a-5p [7, 28]. Experimental results confirmed that there was a significant downregulation of miR-106a-5p expression under HG conditions, whereas its ectopic overexpression markedly alleviated HG-mediated inhibition of ARPE-19 cell proliferation. Accumulating evidence has established that DR pathogenesis is governed by multifactorial interactions, with glucose homeostasis dysregulation and HG-triggered inflammatory cascades serving as central drivers [20, 29]. This paradigm was recapitulated in our in vitro system, where HG exposure elicited a pronounced upregulation of IL-6, IL-1β and TNF-α, thereby corroborating the critical role of inflammation in DR progression. Notably, miR-106a-5p supplementation potently suppressed HG-induced inflammatory responses, a finding congruent with its documented roles in modulating glucose metabolism and mitigating HG-associated smooth muscle inflammation [30, 31]. To clarify the regulatory mechanism of miR-106a-5p in DR, this study confirmed that CREB1 is a direct target of miR-106a-5p through dual-luciferase reporter assays. This study used dual luciferase assays to confirm that miR-106a-5p directly targets CREB1. CREB1 is an alkaline leucine zipper (bZIP) transcription factor, playing critical roles in glucose metabolism, inflammatory responses, and cell survival [32]. Studies have shown that CREB1 promotes the transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and IL-1β by activating the NF-κB pathway, and its abnormal activation is closely associated with the progression of diabetic vascular complications [33, 34]. CREB1, as a transcription factor, can regulate the inflammatory cytokine [35]in mice by modulating NF-κB. This study found that overexpression of miR-106a-5p resulted in downregulation of CREB1, while NF-κB target genes such as IL-6 and TNF-α were significantly reduced to suppress inflammatory response. In the pathological process of DR, not only vascular damage but also retinal neurodegenerative changes occur. CREB1 is also an important regulatory factor of brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), and its activation can promote BDNF transcription. BDNF is crucial for the survival and functional maintenance of retinal nerve cells [24]. According to Target Scan prediction and previous research analysis, miR-106a-5p inhibits CREB1 by targeting it, weakens its activation of the NF-κB pathway, and subsequently downregulates the expression of IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-1β [36]. This study revealed that miR-106a-5p negatively regulates CREB1 expression by directly binding to the 3’UTR region of CREB1 under high-glucose conditions. CREB1 is a key node through which miR-106a-5p mediates inflammatory injury in DR, representing a potential therapeutic target for DR.

Although this study systematically reveals the diagnostic value and molecular mechanism of miR-106a-5p in DR, and clinical data and in vitro experiments jointly indicate that miR-106a-5p can regulate inflammatory response by targeting CREB1 to alleviate DR progression, there are still several limitations that need attention. The most prominent issue is the lack of in vivo validation, which to some extent weakens the translational significance of the conclusions. An attempt was made to evaluate the changes of retinal inflammation after the intervention of miR-106a-5p by constructing a HG induced diabetes rodent model. However, due to the inability to maintain a continuous hyperglycemic state, the death of complications caused by insufficient samples and other technical problems, data collection was difficult and failed. In addition, the details of the downstream signaling pathway of CREB1 (such as its association with the regulation of the NF-κB pathway), as well as the impact of exosomes on miR-106a-5p regulation and its distribution pattern in retinal tissue, are not fully understood.

Future research will focus on two aspects: on the one hand, the ability to build animal models will be improved by optimizing the induction scheme, strengthening animal care and using advanced imaging technology, and then the experiment of intravitreal injection of miR-106a-5p simulant in diabetes rodents will be carried out to evaluate the retinopathy and vascular pathological changes, so as to confirm the therapeutic potential of this pathway in vivo; On the other hand, future research will systematically evaluate the involvement of CREB1 downstream pathway in the miR-106a-5p regulatory network to improve the complete regulatory chain of miR-106a-5p/CREB1/NF-κB/inflammatory factors. The transcription factor CREB1 binds to the inflammatory signal NF-κB under high glucose conditions, promoting the release of inflammatory factors such as TNF-α and IL-6, which is the core driving factor for retinal inflammation and vascular injury [11, 37]. Based on previous research, we will clarify the specific mode of action of this pathway through mechanism validation in the future, providing a theoretical basis for bridging the gap between in vitro discovery and clinical application.

Conclusion

miR-106a-5p provides a new tool for early and precise diagnosis of DR, offering new insights for reducing the incidence of blindness due to DR. In addition, miR-106a-5p/CREB1 intervention in metabolic inflammation provides a new pathway for the treatment of DR. Future research should focus on in vivo validation and clinical translation, which hold promise for advancing precision medicine and prognosis in DR.

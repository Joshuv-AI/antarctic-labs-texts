Efficient and cost-effective shoot regeneration in Aguaymanto (Physalis peruviana L.) using meta-Topolin and 6-benzylaminopurine combinations
Authors: Raúl Vargas, Carmen N. Vigo, Marly Guelac, Eyner Huaman, Manuel Oliva-Cruz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Adventitious shoot regeneration was achieved in P. peruviana cotyledons with BAP, mT, and IBA.•BAP 9.12 µM + mT 2.28 µM achieved 62.96 % regeneration, surpassing cytokinins alone.•High regeneration and multiple shoots were achieved using BAP + mT instead of zeatin.

Introduction

Physalis peruviana L., commonly known as goldenberry, cape gooseberry, or aguaymanto, is a fruit crop native to the Peruvian Andes.1, 2 Its fruit is marketed in various forms, including juices, jams, yogurts, and fresh produce.3, 4, 5 Currently, Colombia is the leading exporter, and the crop is gaining recognition in international markets due to its exotic appeal and export potential.2 Goldenberry is highly valued for its nutritional and nutraceutical properties, being rich in vitamins,6 carotenoids,7 and bioactive compounds.8 It also contains secondary metabolites with notable medicinal relevance, such as physalins and withanolides, which have been associated with antioxidant, antidiabetic, anticancer, hepatoprotective, and anti-inflammatory activities.8 Moreover, its adaptability to diverse soil conditions9 combined with its nutritional profile positions it as an emerging crop for genetic improvement initiatives.10

Breeding programs in P. peruviana currently focus on developing cultivars tolerant to both biotic and abiotic stresses, as well as varieties with enhanced organoleptic qualities and improved yield performance.11 Conventional approaches used for this purpose include mutation induction, the production of haploid and doubled haploid lines, and both interspecific and intraspecific hybridization.12, 13, 14 However, these methods are often time-consuming, unpredictable, and relatively inefficient, which limits their short-term applicability. By contrast, modern biotechnological tools such as gene editing15, 16 and Agrobacterium-mediated transformation17 provide more targeted, rapid, and effective alternatives.

Within the Solanaceae family, efficient genetic transformation systems have already been established in both model and crop species such as tomato (Solanum lycopersicum L.), potato (S. tuberosum L.), eggplant (S. melongena L.), black nightshade (S. nigrum L.), and tobacco (Nicotiana spp.). In tomato, Agrobacterium-mediated CRISPR/Cas9 editing has been optimized for commercial cultivars, achieving high transformation and regeneration efficiencies.18, 19, 20 Similar systems have been described in potato, including transformations mediated by both Agrobacterium tumefaciens and A. rhizogenes, with the successful application of CRISPR/Cas9 to generate stable germline mutations.21, 22, 23 Eggplant and black nightshade (S. melongena, S. nigrum) have also been genetically edited using Agrobacterium-based systems.24, 25 In addition, Nicotiana benthamiana has become a powerful experimental model, where virus-induced genome editing using potato virus X vectors has enabled rapid multiplex CRISPR/Cas9 editing with heritable outcomes.26 These advances have facilitated the improvement of traits such as resistance to viral pathogens, tolerance to abiotic stress, and fruit quality in major solanaceous crops.27, 28

Nevertheless, the success of these tools largely depends on efficient regeneration methods through plant tissue culture techniques, which enable the recovery of whole plants from genetically transformed cells.19,29, 30 The lack of reproducible and cost-effective regeneration systems in P. peruviana contrasts with the availability of robust protocols in model Solanaceae, which restricts its inclusion in genome-editing processes.31, 32 To date, no genetic transformation or genome-editing platform has been established for P. peruviana, highlighting the need for efficient in vitro regeneration protocols as a prerequisite for applied biotechnology.

Although seeds of P. peruviana show high germination rates (85–90 %),33 seed-derived plants are genetically heterogeneous due to cross-pollination and polyploidy.34, 35 This heterogeneity restricts the use of seedlings as a direct propagation system when genetic uniformity is required. Vegetative propagation through cuttings has also been attempted; however, cuttings tend to develop weak root systems, are susceptible to wind damage, produce fruits of lower quality, and are inefficient on a large scale.33 By contrast, in vitro culture enables rapid, large-scale clonal propagation of pathogen-free plants while avoiding genetic variability.36, 37 At the same time, it serves as a tool for subsequent biotechnological applications such as somaclonal variation, embryo rescue, protoplast fusion, and, in particular, genetic transformation and genome editing, which cannot be achieved through conventional propagation methods.38 Therefore, in vitro culture is not only a tool for mass propagation but also an indispensable platform for advanced applications such as CRISPR/Cas-mediated genome editing and Agrobacterium-based transformation, which cannot be accomplished through conventional breeding methods.32, 38

In this context, the hormonal balance between cytokinins and auxins plays a key role. Cytokinins stimulate shoot regeneration, cell division, and morphogenesis,39, 40 while auxins are essential for root formation, callus induction, and the regulation of factors involved in organogenesis.41, 42, 43 Thus, the proper combination of cytokinins and auxins is crucial for in vitro plant regeneration.44, 45, 46 In contrast, recent studies have shown that cytokinin–cytokinin combinations can be more effective than the use of a single cytokinin, significantly improving in vitro regeneration in various plant species.47, 48, 49, 50, 51 This strategy is particularly relevant in recalcitrant species such as P. peruviana,51, 52, 53 which is characterized by its low or absent capacity for in vitro adventitious regeneration,37, 54, 55 especially when compared with other solanaceous crops. However, despite the promising evidence in other species, studies directly addressing cytokinin–cytokinin synergy in P. peruviana remain scarce, and no optimized protocols have yet been reported.

Recent studies in P. peruviana indicate that the combination of zeatin and meta-topolin (mT) significantly enhances regeneration efficiency, reaching rates of up to 62.5 %, compared with almost no response when applied individually.51 However, the extensive use of zeatin in regeneration protocols and at larger scales is severely limited by its high cost.56, 57 Among the cytokinins used in plant biotechnology, zeatin is among the most expensive, particularly when compared with synthetic alternatives such as 6-benzylaminopurine (BAP) or emerging compounds such as mT.56, 57 This heavy reliance on zeatin, one of the most costly cytokinins, represents a major constraint for laboratories in developing countries, thereby limiting the scalability of goldenberry regeneration protocols.56, 57, 58

In response to this limitation, more affordable alternatives have been widely explored. BAP is a cytokinin broadly used due to its affordability and acceptable effectiveness in many species, although it may cause hyperhydricity or genetic instability in certain contexts.56 On the other hand, meta-topolin, an aromatic cytokinin derived from zeatin, has demonstrated superior capacity to promote shoot proliferation without inducing physiological abnormalities.59, 60 Therefore, the combination of BAP and mT represents a cost-effective alternative for shoot regeneration in P. peruviana, reducing reliance on expensive regulators such as zeatin without compromising efficiency or regenerative quality. In this study, we established and optimized a regeneration protocol based on 6-benzylaminopurine (BAP) and meta-topolin (mT), providing not only an accessible alternative for clonal propagation but also a reproducible and scalable platform with direct applications in genetic transformation and genome-editing programs in P. peruviana.

Materials and methods

Plant material, surface sterilization, and seed germination

P. peruviana seeds, ecotype “Colombian,” were purchased from Sierra Seeds (Lima, Peru). Prior to surface disinfection, the seeds were soaked in sterile distilled water for 48 h at room temperature to facilitate germination. Surface disinfection was performed in a laminar flow cabinet by immersing the seeds in a 2 % (v/v) sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl) solution supplemented with 0.01 % (v/v) Tween-80 for 20 min, followed by four washes with sterile distilled water. The disinfected seeds were then sown in magenta GA7 boxes with a base culture medium free of growth regulators.

Explant preparation and culture conditions

All experiments were carried out using fully expanded cotyledons from 12- to 15-day-old P. peruviana seedlings, without emerging true leaves, free of mechanical damage, and exhibiting a uniform green coloration without necrosis or chlorosis. Under aseptic conditions in a laminar flow hood, the seedlings were removed from the culture vessels. Cotyledons were excised using a sterile N° 21 scalpel blade, with the petiole completely removed, leaving only the lamina as the explant. To stimulate the morphogenic response, superficial incisions were made on the adaxial surface of each explant with the scalpel tip. The explants were then placed on the culture medium with the adaxial surface in direct contact with the medium.

Composition of the culture medium

Basal salts plus MS61 vitamins were used as a base for all treatments, supplemented with 3 % (w/v) sucrose and 0.7 % (w/v) agar. The pH of the medium was adjusted to 5.8 using 1 N HCl or NaOH and was autoclaved at 121 °C and 2.1 bar pressure for a period of 15 min. The culture vessels were labeled, covered with plastic wrap, and placed under controlled incubation conditions: a temperature of 25 ± 1 °C, a photoperiod of 16 h light/8 h dark, and a relative humidity of 52 %.

Initial evaluation of combinations of different cytokinins in the in vitro regeneration of shoots from P. peruviana cotyledons

To identify efficient and low-cost cytokinin combinations for adventitious shoot induction, six treatments based on binary combinations of four cytokinins were evaluated: 6-Benzylaminopurine (BAP), meta-Topolin (mT), Kinetin (KIN), and 6-(γ,γ-Dimethylallylamino)purine (2iP). Zeatin was excluded from the trial due to its high commercial cost. Each combination was prepared at an equimolar concentration of 9.12 µM per growth regulator.

The selection of these combinations was based on previous results obtained in our laboratory, which suggest that P. peruviana, a recalcitrant species to regeneration, responds favorably to the combined use of two cytokinins compared to the use of a single cytokinin. All treatments were supplemented with 2.46 µM indole-3-butyric acid (IBA) as an auxin, at a concentration previously optimized to promote morphogenesis.

The experiment was designed using a completely randomized design (CRD), with six treatments and six replicates per treatment (Table 1). Each replicate consisted of a Petri dish (120 x 15 mm) with nine cotyledonary explants, totaling 54 explants per treatment and 324 explants in total. Consequently, this combination was selected for optimization in a subsequent experimental stage.

Optimization of the combined dose of BAP and meta-Topolin (mT) in the in vitro morphogenesis of P. peruviana

The objective of this experimental phase was to determine the most efficient dose of BAP and mT to induce an optimal morphogenic response in P. peruviana cotyledons. Four equimolar concentrations of each cytokinin (2.28, 4.56, 9.12, and 13.68 μM) were evaluated under a completely randomized design (CRD) with a 4 x 4 factorial arrangement, corresponding to the BAP x mT interaction, resulting in a total of 16 treatments. Each treatment consisted of 6 replicates, corresponding to a Petri dish (100 x 15 mm) containing 20 mL of culture medium with the respective combinations of growth regulators. Nine explants were used per replicate, reaching a total of 864 explants.

The cultures were maintained under the same incubation conditions described above (25 ± 1°C, 16/8h photoperiod, 3000 °K). After four weeks, all explants, regardless of their morphogenic response, were subcultured in magenta GA7 containers with 35 mL of MS medium supplemented with 2.89 µM gibberellic acid (GA3) and devoid of cytokinins. This step was intended to promote shoot elongation and facilitate their evaluation. The subculture was maintained for an additional four weeks under the same conditions.

At the end of eight weeks of culture, the following morphogenic variables were evaluated: percentage of explants with shoot regeneration, number of shoots per explant, percentage of explants with rhizogenesis, and length of the longest shoot.

In vitro rooting of regenerated shoots and acclimatization

Healthy, elongated shoots without visible morphological abnormalities, with a length >3 cm and at least 2 to 3 nodes, were selected from regenerated masses obtained from cotyledonary explants. These shoots were individually transferred to test tubes (25 mm × 150 mm) containing 15 mL of MS medium plus vitamins,61 supplemented with 3 % sucrose (w/v) and 0.7 % agar (w/v), devoid of growth regulators. The cultures were incubated at a constant temperature of 25 ± 1°C under a 16:8h light–dark photoperiod, with a light intensity of approximately 40 µmol/m2/s provided by cool-white fluorescent tubes (3000 °K). The rooted seedlings were carefully removed from the culture medium, washed with sterile water to remove agar residue, and briefly immersed in a 0.1 % Benomyl fungicide solution. They were then transplanted into pots containing a substrate composed of peat and perlite (1:1 v/v), which had been previously moistened to field capacity. The containers were covered and sealed with Parafilm to maintain high relative humidity (>90 %).

During the acclimatization phase, incubation conditions were the same as in vitro culture. Progressive opening of the lids allowed for gradual adaptation to the environment. After two years, the plants were transferred to in vitro conditions or to the definitive field.

Data analysis

The response variables were recorded individually for each explant. The average was then calculated within each experimental unit, considering this value representative of the corresponding replicate for statistical analysis. To evaluate the effect of the treatments, an analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed using the statistical software R (version 4.4.1).62

Prior to the ANOVA, the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variance were verified using the Shapiro–Wilk and Levene tests, respectively. If the assumptions were not met, transformations were applied. For percentage data, such as the regeneration percentage (%), the square root transformation (√(x + 0.5)) was used. For count variables, logarithmic transformations (Ln(x + 1.5)) were applied. Multiple comparisons between means were performed using the Tukey test (p < 0.05), as implemented by the HSD.test function of the agricolae63 statistical package. The results are presented with the mean ± standard deviation in their original scale to facilitate biological interpretation.

Results

Initial evaluation of combinations of different cytokinins

The regeneration of cotyledon shoots of P. peruviana grown on MS medium supplemented with equimolar combinations (9.12 µM each) of cytokinins (2ip, 6-BAP, KIN, and mT) in the presence of 2.46 µM IBA was evaluated.

After five weeks of cultivation, all treatments induced shoot regeneration, with frequencies ranging from 5.56 % to 31.48 % (Table 2). The highest regeneration percentage was recorded in treatment 5 (BAP + mT), with 31.48 % regeneration (Fig. 1e), which was significantly higher than in the other treatments. This was followed by treatment 4 (BAP + KIN), with 27.78 % (Fig. 1d), which was significantly lower than treatment 5 but higher than the others. In contrast, the lowest percentages were recorded in treatment 6 (KIN + mT) with 5.56 % (Fig. 1f) and treatment 1 (2iP + BAP) with 7.40 % (Fig. 1a). Optimization of the combined dose of BAP and meta-Topolin (mT)

An analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed to evaluate the individual and combined effects of 6-benzylaminopurine (BAP) and meta-topolin (mT) on in vitro regeneration parameters of P. peruviana. As shown in Table 3, BAP had a statistically significant effect on all dependent variables evaluated, except for the interaction for the variable shoot number. BAP had a significant effect on shoot regeneration rate (p = 0.01), shoot number (p = 0.03), longest shoot length (p < 0.0001), and rhizogenesis rate (p < 0.0001). In contrast, mT alone had no significant effect on shoot regeneration rate (p = 0.89) or shoot number (p = 0.71), but showed a highly significant effect on the variables longest shoot length (p < 0.0001) and rhizogenesis percentage (p < 0.0001). This result indicates that a high concentration of BAP, combined with a low dose of mT (ratio 4:1), significantly favors shoot regeneration in P. peruviana. In contrast, treatment 3 (BAP 2.28 µM + mT 9.12 µM), which reverses this ratio (1:4), showed a higher regeneration rate of only 24.07 %, reinforcing the predominance of the effect of BAP on mT, in agreement with the results of the ANOVA (Table 3). However, some combinations with a high proportion of mT also showed high values. Regarding the number of shoots per regenerated explant, no statistically significant differences were observed between the evaluated treatments (Table 4), which is consistent with the analysis of variance (Table 3), where this variable did not present significant effects for either the main factors or the interaction. However, some treatments had a higher mean compared to the others. Treatment 10 (BAP 9.12 µM + mT 4.56 µM) showed the highest mean with 6.87 shoots per explant (Fig. 2m-o), followed by treatments 6 (BAP 4.56 µM + mT 4.56 µM), 4 (BAP 2.28 µM + mT 13.68 µM), and 9 (BAP 9.12 µM + mT 2.28 µM), with 6.67 (Fig. 2d–f), 6.30, and 5.96 shoots, respectively. These results indicate that certain combinations of BAP and mT, typically at intermediate doses, promote greater proliferation, although the findings are not statistically conclusive. On the contrary, treatment 14 (BAP 13.68 µM + mT 4.56 µM) obtained the lowest number of shoots, with an average of 1.42, which coincides with its low shoot regeneration rate in general, showing an inhibitory effect at high doses of BAP.

Main shoot length showed highly significant differences (Table 4), both for the main effect of the two cytokinins evaluated, as well as for their interaction. Treatment 6 (BAP 4.56 µM + mT 4.56 µM) showed the highest average shoot length with 95.71 mm (Fig. 2d–f), followed by treatment 5 (BAP 4.56 µM + mT 2.28 µM) with 83.41 mm (Fig. 2a–c). These results show a strong synergy between BAP and mT at moderate concentrations, promoting efficient shoot elongation. In contrast, treatments that combined high doses of mT and BAP, such as 15 (13.68 µM BAP + 9.12 µM mT) and 14 (13.68 µM BAP + 4.56 µM mT), showed lower means, with means of 6.91 and 8.99 mm, respectively, demonstrating that high concentrations of both cytokinins limit longitudinal shoot growth.

Regarding the percentage of rhizogenesis, the treatments showed highly significant differences between treatments, both for the main effects and for their interaction (Table 3). Treatments that combined moderate concentrations of BAP (4.56 or 9.12 µM) with low doses of mT (2.28 or 4.56 µM), such as treatments 5 (Fig. 2a–c), 6 (Fig. 2d–f), and 9 (Fig. 2j–l), showed the highest percentages of rhizogenesis, reaching values of up to 74.07 % (Table 4). In contrast, treatments that included high concentrations of mT, specifically treatments 11 to 16, recorded very low percentages of rhizogenesis, ranging from 0 % to 3.70 %, despite all treatments evaluated containing a basal concentration of 2.46 µM IBA. In contrast, the treatments with the highest percentage of rhizogenesis also coincided with those that presented longer shoot lengths.

In vitro rooting and acclimatization of regenerated shoots

Shoots selected for rooting, with a length greater than 3 cm and 2 to 3 nodes, showed a high root-forming capacity when grown on MS medium without growth regulators. Rooting began with the appearance of whitish roots (1 to 2 per shoot) between 9 and 14 days after sowing, reaching rooting rates above 90 %. Rooting speed was slow, but allowed both basal and aerial development of the shoots, which enabled a high survival rate in the acclimatization stage. Plantlets rooted after 4 weeks (Fig. 3a) were successfully transferred to ex vitro conditions after two weeks of progressive adaptation. Discussion

Cytokinins constitute a fundamental class of plant growth regulators, involved in cell division, shoot induction, and hormonal coordination during in vitro morphogenesis.64, 65 These growth regulators differ structurally based on their purine base and side chain substitutions,66 which determine their stability, biological activity, and effects on explants.29 In this study, the combined effect of different cytokinins on adventitious shoot regeneration in P. peruviana from cotyledonary explants was evaluated. The work was structured in two sequential experiments: the first, aimed at identifying synergistic combinations of cytokinins with the greatest regenerative effect; and the second, focused on optimizing the concentration of the selected combination. supplementation of the medium with a combination of two cytokinins, together with a low concentration of auxin (2.45 µM of AIB), was based on previous studies,51 where it was observed that the addition of an auxin significantly enhanced shoot regeneration, as reinforced by other studies in Physalis.67, 68, 69, 70

Initial evaluation of combinations of different cytokinins (2ip, BAP, KIN, and mT)

In our study, the efficiency of BAP was determined in the first experiment, as it showed synergy with both KIN and mT. Equimolar concentrations of 9.18 μM of BAP + KIN showed regeneration rates (27.78 % vs 31.48 %) and shoot numbers (2.29 vs 3.30 regenerated shoots per explant) close to those obtained by BAP + mT. Kinetin (KIN) has been widely used in the genus Physalis, after BAP, both for micropropagation,52, 70, 71, 72 and adventitious regeneration.73, 74 Yaroshko et al.74 reported adventitious regeneration in true leaves of P. peruviana when combining BAP with KIN. At the same time, in P. minima, the synergy observed between BAP and KIN can be attributed to the complementary effects of both cytokinins. While BAP actively promotes shoot proliferation and bud induction, its exclusive use can lead to morphophysiological disorders, such as apical necrosis and underdeveloped leaves.75, 76 In this context, kinetin acts as a physiological modulator that contributes to maintaining the structural integrity of tissues and favors the development of more robust and functional shoots and leaves.77, 78 Although the BAP + KIN combination showed promising results in our study, it is a widely used strategy in numerous species due to its commercial availability and prolonged use in in vitro culture protocols. In contrast, the combined use of BAP with mT represents a more recent and less explored approach, but with superior results in terms of regeneration and morphogenic quality.

Optimization of the combined dose of BAP and mT

The results obtained demonstrate that the combination of 6-benzylaminopurine (BAP) and meta-topolin (mT), in the presence of a constant concentration of IBA, has a significant effect on the regeneration of adventitious shoots from cotyledons of P. peruviana. Among the treatments evaluated, combination 9 (BAP 9.12 µM + mT 2.28 µM) showed the highest regeneration rate (62.96 %), surpassing previous reports for this species using the same type of explant, particularly those that used zeatin as the main cytokinin. Yaroshko et al.79 achieved 45 % regeneration using 9.12 µM zeatin, and Vargas et al.51 achieved a shoot regeneration rate of 62.50 % using 9.12 µM Zeatin + 8.29 µM mT + 2.46 µM IBA. On the other hand, using classic growth regulators, Mascarenhas et al.37 barely reported a 5 % regeneration and 0.10 shoots/explant with 4.44 µM BAP, while Hernández-Villalobos and Chico-Ruíz55 documented only 16.67 % regeneration using 2,4-D and BAP, which reinforces the importance of using optimized hormonal combinations that include synergistic cytokinins and auxins. For example, Ramirez-Malagón & Ochoa-Alejo67 demonstrated that P. ixocarpa hypocotyls did not regenerate shoots if either cytokinin or auxin was used alone, requiring a combination of these. These values are significantly lower than those obtained in our study, which reinforces the importance of selecting appropriate hormone combinations and concentrations to optimize the morphogenetic response.

Several studies have highlighted the efficacy of BAP as the main inducer of adventitious sprouting in Physalis spp.69, 70, 71, 80, 81, 82, 83 However, the optimal concentration varies according to the type of explant and species. In stem segments, Singh et al.82 reported 86.25 % regeneration and an average of 4.75 shoots per explant with 11.10 µM of BAP and IBA in P. peruviana nodes, while Kumar et al.84 observed maximum regeneration values in P. angulata leaves with 4.44 µM of BAP + 2.85 µM of IAA + 0.58 µM of GA3. These studies reinforce the idea of why BAP is a widely used cytokinin in Physalis.

Likewise, the study confirms that the effect of BAP can be significantly enhanced when combined with mT, a less studied aromatic cytokinin, but with recognized mechanisms that stabilize cytokinin metabolism by reducing oxidative stress and the tendency towards hyperhydricity.85, 86Although mT alone did not generate significant responses in shoot regeneration, its interaction with BAP was key to improving both shoot length and rhizogenesis. Combinations with equimolar doses of mT + BAP 4.56 µM + mT 4.56 µM produced shoots with lengths greater than 95 mm, evidencing a favorable hormonal synergy in phases after organogenic induction. This pattern coincides with that observed in Feronia limonia L.87 and Prunus spp.,88, 89 where mT not only favored elongation but also improved shoot quality and growth. Anjani et al.90 observed that shoot elongation was more effective with 4.44 µM BAP than with KIN or TDZ.

High concentrations of BAP (13.68 µM) demonstrated an inhibitory effect on shoot and root morphogenesis in P. peruviana, as evidenced by low rates of regeneration (<33.33 %), rhizogenesis (<10.16 %), and elongation (<10.16 mm). This trend has been consistently reported in the genus Physalis. Singh et al.82 demonstrated that doses exceeding 11.10 µM of BAP significantly reduced regeneration in P. peruviana. In contrast, Anjani et al.90 reported a similar response in P. minima, characterized by reduced shoot induction in nodal segments at higher concentrations. In P. angulata, Kumar et al.84 determined an optimal dose of 4.44 µM of BAP, observing a 44 % reduction in regeneration when the dose was increased to 13.32 µM. Similarly, Mascarenhas et al.37 found that in P. peruviana, maximum adventitious regeneration in cotyledons and leaves was obtained with 4.44 µM of BAP, whereas higher concentrations inhibited the morphogenetic response. These patterns are repeated in other studies. Santana & Angarita91 identified 11.10 µM of BAP, in combination with 2.89 µM of GA3 and 2,4-D, as optimal for regeneration in P. peruviana hypocotyls, but doubling the BAP concentration drastically reduced regeneration. Similarly, Romo-Paz70 reported that in true leaves of P. angulata, the optimal BAP concentration was 4.43 µM, and that higher doses (6.65–8.86 µM) significantly decreased both the regeneration frequency and the number of shoots. This inhibitory effect at high concentrations has been attributed to hormonal imbalances and negative feedback processes in cell signaling pathways, which interfere with gene expression associated with organogenesis, resulting in the suppression of root formation, as well as an increase in vitrification, necrosis, and loss of tissue viability.92, 93, 94, 95

Finally, it is worth noting that the use of mT allows for a reduction in zeatin dependence without compromising regenerative efficiency. This is relevant considering that protocols such as those of Yaroshko et al.79 and Vargas et al.,51 while achieving acceptable responses, do not exceed the regeneration rate reported in this study. The use of mT as an alternative cytokinin to zeatin represents a significant economic advantage, as its inclusion allowed for superior regenerative responses without incurring the high costs associated with pure zeatin. This aspect is crucial for the scalability of protocols in laboratories with limited resources and in contexts where regeneration is pursued prior to genetic transformation.

In addition to these advantages, a critical aspect that should be considered in future studies is the evaluation of the genetic fidelity of regenerated plants. While adventitious regeneration may involve risks of somaclonal variation, the availability of molecular resources in P. peruviana, such as microsatellite markers,96 leaf transcriptomes,97 ITS2 barcoding,35 and SNP-GBS approaches for genetic diversity,98 provides robust tools to validate the clonal homogeneity of regenerants. The integration of these methodologies would allow the confirmation of the genetic stability of in vitro-derived plants, ensuring their uniformity for advanced applications in breeding and genetic transformation programs. Likewise, previous studies suggest that protocols based on meta-topolin (mT) tend to reduce the incidence of somaclonal variation and morphological abnormalities compared with those using BAP alone,56, 59 reinforcing the expectation that plants regenerated through the BAP + mT combination will exhibit high genetic stability. Taken together, these aspects position the present research not only as an efficient regeneration protocol but also as a strategic starting point toward the establishment of advanced biotechnological platforms in P. peruviana.

Conclusions

This study identified the optimal combination of cytokinins for adventitious shoot regeneration in P. peruviana, establishing that the highest efficiency was achieved with 9.12 µM of BAP and 2.28 µM of meta-topolin (mT), resulting in a regeneration rate of 62.96 %. This protocol represents a significant advance in plant biotechnology for this species, offering an effective alternative to the use of zeatin, which has been the predominant cytokinin in previous studies with cotyledons. However, zeatin is expensive and has limited availability for laboratories with limited resources.

Through a two-stage experimental approach, a comparative evaluation of cytokinin combinations was first performed, identifying the synergy between BAP and mT as the most promising. This combination was subsequently optimized in a second phase, resulting in a consolidated and efficient in vitro regeneration protocol. The optimized combination is not only applicable to P. peruviana but also has the potential to be adapted to other species. It can also serve as a basis for advanced genetic improvement protocols, including Agrobacterium-mediated transformation and gene editing, which is particularly relevant given the growing commercial and agricultural interest in this species due to its nutritional value and export potential.

Adult patients with tetanus in Anhui Province of China from 2013 to 2022: A retrospective study
Authors: Dai Cheng, Li Liang, Hu Miaomiao, Han Guolei, Sun Wei
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Tetanus is a highly fatal infectious disease with an incubation period of 7 to 8 days. The aim of this study was to identify risk factors for death in tetanus patients, develop a nomogram model for predicting mortality risk. This retrospective study included tetanus patients who were admitted to the intensive care unit department between January 2013 and December 2022. The patients were divided into 2 groups based on their final outcome, namely death group and survival group. Risk factors associated with mortality were analyzed using univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis. Finally, a nomogram model was developed using the rms package. A total of 91 patients were enrolled in this study, including 54 males and 37 females. The average age of the tetanus patients was 52.88 ± 16.56 years, with a mean incubation period of 8.51 ± 3.97 days. The foot was the most common injury site (42.86%), and metal product stabbing was the leading cause of injury (48.78%). Ventilator-associated pneumonia was the most frequent complication (21.98%). Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses revealed that Ablett classification (odds ratio [OR], 21.999; 95% confidence interval [CI], 4.124–117.352), white blood cell count (OR, 6.033; 95%CI, 1.275–28.552), and autonomic nervous dysfunction (OR, 22.663; 95%CI, 4.363–117.728) as independent risk factors for tetanus patients. The C-index of the nomogram model was 0.942, with an area under the curve of the receiver operating characteristic curve at 0.942 (95%CI, 0.871–0.905). Ablett classification, white blood cell count, autonomic nervous dysfunctions were associated with the prognosis of patients with tetanus. The nomogram model developed based on risk factors has high accuracy.

1. Introduction

Tetanus is caused by Clostridium tetani invading the human body through skin or mucosal wound to produce neurotoxic exotoxin, which causes persistent and tonic spasm of muscles, dysphagia, mouth opening restriction, and other symptoms.[1,2] The global average death rate of tetanus patients is about 30% to 50%, while the mortality rate of severe tetanus patients is as high as 70%. In underdeveloped countries, tetanus is still a threat to health, but in developed countries, tetanus has become rare due to the popularization of tetanus vaccine, so some clinicians lack experience and knowledge in the diagnosis and treatment of tetanus, leading to early treatment delay.[3] Meanwhile, due to the rare cases and ethical reasons, it poses a challenge to develop novel approaches for assessing the risk of mortality associated with tetanus in clinical settings. Therefore, it is imperative to establish a method for assessing the mortality risk of tetanus patients based on demographic, clinical, and therapeutic factors in order to achieve early aggressive management.[4]

The aim of this study was to identify demographic, clinical, and therapeutic factors affecting survival in patients with tetanus and to develop a nomogram model based on risk factors. Therefore, this study retrospectively analyzed the clinical data of 107 adult tetanus patients admitted to the Second People’s Hospital of Fuyang, Anhui Province from January 2013 to December 2022. It is expected that these findings will provide valuable new data for the treatment of clinical tetanus and may help improve the poor prognostic outcome in patients with severe tetanus.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study population

Tetanus patients admitted to Fuyang Second People’s Hospital in Anhui Province from January 2013 to December 2022. The inclusion criteria were: (1) age over 18 years old; (2) meeting the diagnostic criteria for tetanus, having a previous history of trauma, and presenting clinical symptoms such as trismus, limited mouth opening, dysphagia, and muscle spasm; (3) excluding patients with disease symptoms similar to tetanus, such as poisoning, epilepsy, meningitis; (4) pathogen test was positive. The exclusion criteria were: (1) admission within < 48 hours, patients or their family members voluntarily give up treatment; (2) patients with a history of tumor disease or has major infectious diseases, such as tuberculosis or corona virus disease 2019; (3) pregnant or lactating women; (4) incomplete clinical data. The study was approved by the research ethics committee of Fuyang Second People’s Hospital (No. 20230530008). All patients agreed to participate in this study and obtained written informed consent from participants.

2.2. Patient grouping

The patients were divided into survival group and death group based on final clinical outcome, the survival group, which included patients who achieved complete cure and discharge, and the death group, which comprised of patients who passed away during hospitalization or voluntarily abandoned treatment and subsequently died after discharge. The diagnostic criteria for tetanus include[5]: (1) patient with a history of trauma upon admission and typical clinical manifestations of tetanus, such as trismus, limited mouth opening, dysphagia, and muscle spasms; (2) pathogen test (smear, microscopy, anaerobic bacteria culture or polymerase chain reaction test) of the wound secretions was positive; (3) excluding diseases with similar symptoms to tetanus such as rabies, poisoning, epilepsy and meningitis. The severity of tetanus is classified into grade I–IV according to the Ablett classification.[6] Grade I (mild): patients had mild to moderate dental amenorrhea, general spasms, no convulsions, no dyspnea, and no or minor dysphagia; Grade II (moderate): had marked spasm, moderate lockjaw, moderate dyspnea, mild to moderate transient convulsions, and mild dysphagia; Grade III (severe): had general spasm, continuous convulsions, severe lockjaw, severe dyspnea or asphyxia, severe dysphagia; Grade IV: patients had all symptoms of Grade 3 as well as autonomic nervous dysfunction.

2.3. Study data

Demographic information, clinical features and therapeutic parameters of patients were retrospectively collected. Demographic information and clinical representations include name, sex, age, occupation, living area, disease history, APACHE II score (acute physiology and chronic health evaluation II), wound location, early wound management, intensive care unit (ICU) admission time, incubation period (time from trauma to onset), onset to medical attention, first symptoms, Ablett classification, sequential organ failure assessment score, complications, length of hospital stay, time to complete remission (from first symptom to absence of all clinical symptoms), clinical final outcome and laboratory test indicators. Treatment parameters included drug utilization, drug dosage, symptom relief time, medication days, respiratory assistance, and duration of respiratory support.

All confirmed tetanus patients received standard treatment, including neutralization of free toxins, wound debridement, anti-infection therapy, and control of muscle spasms.[7] For patients with moderate or severe conditions and persistent seizures that cannot be relieved, artificial airways must be established for mechanical ventilation. Vasoactive drugs should be administered to those with autonomic nervous dysfunction.

For the neutralization of free toxins using HTIG (human tetanus immunoglobulin) or tetanus antitoxin (TAT), HTIG was preferred at doses ranging from 3000 IU to 6000 IU.[8] TAT was recommended in cases where HTIG is unavailable or due to insufficient economic capacity, with a recommended dose range of 10,000 U/d to 60,000 U/d. Debridement should be performed as soon as possible for those with definite wounds, followed by repeated and alternating washing with 3% hydrogen peroxide solution and 9% physiological saline. Nitroimidazole drugs (such as tinidazole) combined with penicillin drugs were the preferred anti-infective agents. In cases of mixed infections, nitroimidazole drugs can be used in combination with cephalosporins.

Mechanical ventilation should be performed promptly in patients with dyspnea, persistent convulsions refractory to treatment, and concurrent respiratory failure. If the duration of mechanical ventilation is more than 1 week, the condition was not relieved, and it is expected that the extubation cannot be removed in the short term, tracheotomy should be performed.

Benzodiazepines (such as diazepam and midazolam) were selected to control muscle spasm. For patients without mechanical ventilation, diazepam was the primary medication with an initial slow intravenous injection of 10 mg determined by tic control. If the effect is inadequate, continuous intravenous infusion was selected at a rate of 0.03 to 0.1 mg/kg/h. For patients with mechanical ventilation, midazolam is the primary drug of choice. The initial dose ranges from 0.05 to 0.2 mg/kg/h, with maintenance doses adjusted based on seizure control (0.02 to 0.1 mg/kg/h). If symptoms persist despite treatment with midazolam alone, it is often combined with other sedatives such as propofol (0.3 to 4 mg/kg/h). Convulsions continue despite the use of sedatives, muscle relaxants like cisatracurium besilate must be administered. The initial infusion rate was generally 3 ug/kg/min, and the continuous infusion range was about 0.5 to 10.2 ug/kg/min, which can be adjusted according to the patient’s response to convulsion control measures. The administration of benzodiazepines necessitates the assessment of sedation levels, so patients with mechanical ventilation must also be evaluated using the Richmond Agitation Sedation Scale to adjust sedative dosages and maintain Richmond Agitation Sedation Scale scores between −3 and 0.[9]

If the patient presents with autonomic nervous dysfunction, a combination of sedatives and analgesics is administered. Sedation was achieved using midazolam (0.02–0.1 mg/kg/h) or a combination of midazolam and propofol (0.3–4 mg/kg/h), while opioid drugs (0.02–0.15 ug/kg/min) are commonly used for pain management. In the presence of hemodynamic instability, dexmedetomidine was used in conjunction with conventional analgesia and sedation to mitigate the patient’s physiological stress. In cases of hypotension, norepinephrine (0.1–2 ug/kg/min) is utilized to achieve appropriate mean arterial pressure.[10]

2.4. Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS, version 24.0. The measurement data were tested by Shapio–Wilk for normal distribution, which was represented by mean ± standard deviation for normal distribution, and t-test was used for pair comparison between groups. Count data were expressed as [n (%)], and group comparisons were made using the chi-square test. Univariate logistic regression was used to analyze risk factors associated with mortality in patients with tetanus, yielding odds ratios (OR) and 95% confidence intervals. Variables that showed significant differences in univariate logistic regression were included in multivariate logistic regression analysis to identify independent risk factors for death among tetanus patients. Using these independent risk factors as variables, a death risk nomogram model was developed using R software and the C index was calculated. The consistency of the model was verified by means of area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. A P-value < 0.05 was considered significant in all analyses.

3. Results

3.1. Demographic information and clinical features

From January 2013 to December 2022, a total of 107 patients with tetanus were confirmed in our hospital. Among them, 16 cases were excluded based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria (age below 18 years old, n = 2; hospitalization duration <48 hours due to treatment costs or rapid deterioration of the condition, n = 7; critical illnesses such as cancer, cerebrovascular diseases and infectious diseases, n = 2; incomplete clinical data, n = 5). Therefore, a total of 91 patients were finally included in the study, among which 21 died and 70 survived, as shown in Figure 1.

Flow chart of the study object. Study objects were screened based on exclusion and inclusion criteria, and then divided into a survival group and a death group according to the final clinical outcome.

Among the 91 patients, there were 54 males and 37 females with ages ranging from 19 to 91 years old, and an average age of 52.88 ± 16.56 years. Most patients were farmers (89.01%) and rural residents (94.51%), respectively. The incubation period was 8.51 ± 3.97 days, and the time from onset to treatment was 3.41 ± 1.65 days (Table 1). Among the 91 patients, 23 were admitted to the ICU with an average length of stay of 24.86 ± 12.84 days. The 85 patients had a history of trauma, with the majority of injuries occurring in the feet (42.86%) and hands (28.57%). The most common causes of trauma were metal product injuries (48.78%) and wood product injuries (26.33%). Of these patients, 39 underwent wound disinfection following their injury, while only 5 received TAT or immunoglobulin injections for prophylaxis against tetanus infection (Table 1). In terms of clinical characteristics and complications, 88 patients presented with trismus, 90 had limited mouth opening, 79 experienced dysphagia, and 78 suffered from spasms or convulsions. Complications were observed in a total of 59 patients, including pulmonary infections (20 cases of ventilator-associated pneumonia), venous thrombosis (7 cases), other infections (5 cases), as well as gastrointestinal or liver function injury (15 cases) (Table 1).

3.3. Mortality risk nomogram model

A nomogram model was developed to predict the mortality risk in tetanus patients, utilizing Ablett classification, WBC count and autonomic nervous dysfunction as key predictors (Fig. 2). The model demonstrated a high level of consistency with a C-index of 0.942. The receiver operating characteristic curve for the nomogram exhibited an area under the curve value of 0.942 with a 95% confidence interval ranging from 0.871 to 0.905 (Fig. 3).

Nomogram for predicting the risk of death in tetanus patients. The points are calculated according to the classification criteria of Autonomic dysfunction, WBC and Ablett classification as shown in the figure. The total points is obtained by summing up these points, which further determines the probability of risk. Ablett classification: 0, ≤III; 1, >III. WBC: 0, ≤10 × 109/L; 1, >10 × 109/L. Autonomic nervous dysfunction: 0, No; 1, Yes. WBC: white blood cell count.

4. Discussion

Tetanus is a serious infectious disease with a global average mortality rate ranging from 30% to 50%, which tends to be higher in impoverished and remote regions. The present study found the mortality rate of tetanus to be 23.08%, consistent with previous reports.[11] While China has made significant strides in neonatal tetanus vaccination, adult immunization against tetanus remains problematic.[12] In this study, only 5 patients had received the vaccine, indicating lower coverage than reported elsewhere.[13,14] The reason is that 89.01% of the patients in this study are farmers, 94.51% live in rural areas, 75.82% are middle-aged and elderly people. The low vaccination rate is attributed to the inadequate medical resources and insufficient awareness of prevention among middle-aged and elderly individuals in rural areas.

Thorough debridement performed early after trauma is a crucial measure in preventing tetanus.[15] It effectively eliminates tetanus bacteria and spores, reducing the release of toxins. In this study, 52 patients received no treatment following trauma while 39 were treated. However, only 7 of these patients underwent thorough debridement while the rest received simple disinfection or self-dressing which resulted in further incidence of tetanus. The majority of tetanus patients have a history of trauma and deep wounds, with lung infection being the main complication.[16] The site and cause of trauma as well as complications observed in this study are consistent with other reported results.[11,17] A study conducted by Silva[18] in Brazil revealed that the most common symptoms among tetanus patients were trismus (84%) and generalized muscle spasms (69.7%). The epidemiological profile of tetanus patients was found to be adult, male, single, brown-skinned, rural workers with an incomplete vaccination history and low education levels. These patients also had longer hospital stays and higher mortality rates. These findings are consistent with the results of our present study.

Univariate logistic regression analysis showed that the mortality risk of tetanus patients was significantly associated with ICU admission, time from onset to medical attention, APACHE II score, SOFA score, Ablett classification, WBC level, total bilirubin level, debridement after admission, spasms and convulsions symptoms, and autonomic nervous dysfunction symptoms. Previous studies have shown that the severity of tetanus patients, APACHE II score, age over 60 years, WBC > 12 × 109/L is associated with mortality in adult patients with tetanus.[19,20] These findings are consistent with our study results, which indicate that tetanus patients with severe or worsening conditions should be transferred to the ICU for treatment. Delayed medical attention and lack of wound debridement after admission can lead to deterioration of the condition. Therefore, factors such as ICU admission, time from onset to medical attention, Ablett classification, and wound debridement after admission are associated with disease severity.

In this study, multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that autonomic nervous dysfunction, Ablett classification > grade III, and WBC level > 10 × 109/L were independent risk factors for mortality in tetanus patients. The Ablett score is the most widely used scoring system to assess the severity of tetanus.[6] A higher Ablett score indicates a worse prognosis, and the mortality rate of patients with grade III–IV Ablett score is as high as 90%. Autonomic nervous system disorders typically manifest in the later stages of tetanus disease progression, resulting in tachycardia, fluctuations in blood pressure, gastrointestinal disturbances, urinary dysfunction and increased respiratory secretions that complicate disease management.[21] Inflammatory or infectious diseases may cause an increase in WBC levels which is positively correlated with the degree of infection in patients.[22] As tetanus belongs to the category of infectious diseases, it can trigger immune inflammatory responses. The WBC count itself does not hold much significance, only combined with clinical situations and differential counts of white blood cells (the percentage of different types of white blood cells), it plays a certain guiding role.

The nomogram depicts the relationship between each variable as a line segment, with the scale representing the value range and the length of each segment reflecting its contribution to the outcome event. Tetanus is a perilous disease with a high mortality rate. The establishment of a nomogram model enables the scoring and prediction of mortality risk based on individual patient risk factors, thereby facilitating early clinical intervention and reducing mortality rates. Based on the results of logistic regression, a nomogram model was established to identify risk factors for adult tetanus mortality. Autonomic nervous dysfunction, Ablett classification > grade III, and WBC level > 10 × 109/L were integrated as independent risk factors in this model. The nomogram can be used to predict that if a patient has an Ablett classification of grade IV, no autonomic nervous system dysfunction occurs, and the WBC level is greater than 10 × 109/L, then their mortality risk is estimated at 73%. After model verification, it was determined that the model can accurately predict the mortality risk probability of tetanus patients. The model visually displays the relative contributions of various risk factors, enabling doctors to intuitively comprehend patient mortality risks and formulate personalized intervention measures based on individual conditions. This study is a retrospective analysis conducted at a single center, and the limited number of tetanus patients available for validation may affect the accuracy and clinical applicability of our model. Furthermore, given the small sample size, potential selection bias cannot be ruled out. Thus, future studies with larger cohorts are warranted to confirm our findings.

Taken together, our study demonstrates that mortality in tetanus patients is associated with autonomic nervous dysfunction, Ablett classification, and WBC levels. The established death prediction model accurately predicts the risk of death in tetanus patients and can be effectively applied in clinical practice. We recommend hospitals to provide graded treatment based on the severity of tetanus patients and timely intensify treatment for those with worsening symptoms to improve survival rates.

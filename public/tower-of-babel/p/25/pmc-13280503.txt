Introduction

The latest Global Oral Health Status Report, published by the World Health Organization indicates that oral diseases affect approximately 3.5 billion individuals worldwide. Notably, an estimated 75% of those impacted reside in middle-income countries. Furthermore, the report estimates that dental caries are present in around 2 billion individuals with permanent dentition, while approximately 514 million children are affected by caries in their primary dentition.1

The conventional drill-and-fill techniques for caries removal and cavity preparation with dental burs became more widespread in early to mid-20th century but had inevitable demerits of producing heat, pressure, vibration, dentin desiccation, pain, apparent lack of objective clinical marker, excessive removal of sound dentin, and need for local anesthetic administration.2–4 In children, these can promote anxiety, discomfort, and fear, leading to potentially long-term dental anxiety.

Dentistry has transitioned from GV Black's principle of “Extension for Prevention” to the contemporary approach of “Conservation for Prevention,” focusing on preserving natural tooth structures.5 Various newer caries excavation techniques, known as minimally invasive dentistry (MID) techniques, have been introduced over a decade.

Bromelain is a natural enzyme extracted from pineapple, specifically from the stem and fruit. The proteolytic property of Bromelain makes it a potential chemomechanical agent for dental caries removal. The Bromelain gel method utilizes an enzyme to dissolve and remove carious tissue. It selectively dissolves the collagen matrix of carious dentin while leaving the mineral structure of healthy dentin intact.

The self-limiting polymer burs, originally introduced as SmartPrep™, now known as SmartBurs™ (SS White, Lakewood, New Jersey, United States) possess a paddle-shaped configuration, made up of medical-grade polyether-ketone-ketone (PEKK), engineered to target and remove only infected dentin, thereby conserving sound tooth structures.6,7

Methodology

A total of 30 primary molars exhibiting active carious lesions confined to dentin and extracted due to physiological root resorption were collected and preserved in 10% formalin for 2 weeks,8 following CDC infection control guidelines (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention) for the handling of extracted teeth used in research and teaching.9 Each specimen was radiographically evaluated using intraoral periapical (IOPA) radiographs to determine the depth of the lesion. Only teeth with carious involvement restricted to dentin and without pulpal exposure were included in the study.10

The selected teeth were decoronated at the level of the cemento-enamel junction and coronal segments were isolated and prepared for further analysis. The crowns were mounted on wax blocks, and each primary molar with a carious lesion was longitudinally sectioned into two equal halves using a water-cooled carborundum disc to ensure precision and prevent thermal damage.

The sectioned halves were divided into two groups:

Group I—cavity prepared using Bromelain-based gel.

Group I—cavity prepared using Bromelain-based gel.

Group II—cavity prepared using SmartBurs™.

Group II—cavity prepared using SmartBurs™.

A total of 60 samples, 30 for each group, wax-mounted specimens were oriented on to the stereomicroscope for image recording. The area of carious lesion before its removal was measured using the stereomicroscopic scale (Fig. 1).

Results

The dataset was entered into Microsoft Excel and further subjected to comprehensive statistical analysis using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 22.

The mean time taken in caries removal was 8.1 minutes in group I (Bromelain gel), and for group II, it was 2.8 minutes, and this intergroup comparison of time difference for caries removal was statistically significant with p < 0.05.

Remaining carious dentin in mm2 using Bromelain gel as a chemomechanical caries removal (CMCR) agent (group I): The mean of carious area before and after removal was 8.37 mm2 and 2.39 mm2, respectively. The mean of the reduction in carious dentin after using Bromelain-based gel as a CMCR agent for minimally invasive technique was 5.98, with p < 0.001 statistically significant.

Remaining carious dentin in mm2 using SmartBurs™ (group II): The mean of carious area before and after removal was 8.57 mm2 and 7.01 mm2, respectively. The mean of the reduction in carious dentin after using SmartBurs™ as a minimally invasive technique was 1.56.

The comparison of caries removal efficacy among the study groups: In group I, the mean reduction in carious dentin was 5.98 mm2 with standard deviation of 2.48, and for group II, mean reduction in carious dentin was recorded as 1.56 mm2 with standard deviation of 0.96. The mean difference for reduction of carious dentin between the two study groups was achieved as 4.40, with p < 0.001 that is statistically significant.

Intergroup comparison of mean reduction in carious dentin (mm2) with group I (Bromelain gel) depicted higher mean reduction efficacy of 5.96 mm2 than group II (SmartBurs™), with 1.56 mm2 mean reduction efficacy.

Intergroup comparison of mean reduction in carious dentin and time taken for caries excavation: Bromelain gel resulted in higher mean reduction efficacy of 5.96 mm2, with mean time taken in caries removal of 8.1 minutes. Group II (SmartBurs™) showed mean reduction efficacy of 1.56 mm2 and mean time taken in caries removal to be 2.8 minutes. Both parameters showed statistically significant values of p < 0.001 and p < 0.05, respectively (Tables 1 and 2).

Discussion

Dental caries stands as a formidable healthcare issue, prevailing as one of the most pervasive diseases worldwide.14,15 The process of excavating caries can be particularly stressful, especially for children, who often experience heightened anxiety due to the mechanical drilling involved in treating the affected tooth.

Through coming years, cavity preparation gained its popularity with the advent of adhesive properties of filling materials, thus preventing excessive tooth cutting. This led to a paradigm shift in dental practice toward minimally invasive techniques, a conservative approach to dental caries.

The various minimally invasive CMCR agents were developed over time. They were categorized as sodium hypochlorite-based and enzyme-based agents.16 These substances facilitate softening of the predegraded collagen matrix associated with the carious lesion, promoting its easy and painless removal17 without affecting the adjoining healthy tissues, thus ensuring safe and effective treatment for young children and patients with special healthcare needs.

A recent advance as a potential minimally invasive technique, Bromelain, is a natural protease enzyme extracted from the pineapple plant (Ananas comosus), a member of the family Bromeliaceae.18 It constitutes of 212 amino acids and is stable at pH 3.0–6.5. In presence of its substrate, Bromelain's enzymatic activity is rendered insensitive to pH. It demonstrates a broad spectrum of biological activities, including anti-inflammatory effect, antiedematous, fibrinolytic, antibacterial, and wound-healing properties.19–21 Bromelain contains different thiol endopeptidases and sulfhydryl group that mediate its proteolytic activity,22 which has made its potential use as a chemo mechanical agent in dental caries removal.19 It facilitates targeted dissolution of the collagen matrix in carious dentin while leaving the mineralized framework of unaffected dentin intact.

On the contrary, SmartBurs™, also known as polymer burs, are another recent minimally invasive mechanical, innovative tool, designed to enhance the precision and efficacy of caries excavation, which distinguishes between healthy and decayed tooth structures. These burs are composed of PEKK and feature a polymer shaft and blades available in three sizes—004, 006, and 008—designed for use in slow-speed rotary handpieces at 500–800 rpm. The hardness of SmartBurs™ (50 KHN) exceeds that of infected dentin (15–20 KHN) but is lower than that of healthy dentin (68 KHN), allowing the burs to blunt upon contact with hard dentin, thereby preventing unnecessary removal of affected or sound dentin.23,24 Additionally, their use results in fewer dentinal tubules being cut, reducing patient discomfort compared to conventional burs. These properties enable more targeted caries removal, thereby reducing the risk of pulp exposure by selective removal of carious infected dentin and preserving tooth integrity.25

In our study, we compared the efficacy of Bromelain gel and SmartBurs™, as very limited literature and studies were available that evaluated remaining carious dentin (Stereomicroscopic and polarized microscopic methods) and the time taken in caries removal in primary molars between the two minimally invasive techniques.

Research reports have confirmed that molar teeth are prone to caries compared to other teeth, and lower molars were more susceptible than upper molars in both primary and permanent dentitions.26 In the primary dentition, the mandibular second molars exhibit the highest incidence of caries due to their longer functional lifespan compared to other primary teeth. Differences in caries susceptibility among individual teeth are largely determined by the occlusal fissure topography of molars, with second primary molars exhibiting deeper and more poorly coalesced pits and fissures. Considering the above facts, primary molars were used in our study.27

Visual and tactile examination remains the most commonly employed clinical method for evaluating the adequacy of caries removal. It uses adequate lighting and magnification (visual) along with dental explorers (tactile). These diagnostic methods are often complemented by other tools and technologies, such as dental radiographs (X-rays), caries detection aids, and intraoral cameras, to facilitate an in-depth appraisal of the patient's oral health status. In this study, the endpoint of caries excavation was defined through the combined use of the visual-tactile method and a caries-disclosing dye. Evidence from the literature indicates that both techniques demonstrate equivalent efficacy in the detection of carious lesions.12,28,29 Accordingly, both approaches were employed to ensure standardization of the excavation endpoint.

Stereomicroscope is an optical microscope that uses incident light to view specimens from 10× to 100× magnification. In our study, stereomicroscopic analysis using Radicle ProCAM Software was done to measure the area of carious dentin before and after removal by to MID methods. It was consistent with the study performed by Reddy et al.19 For further confirmation of observations, caries-detecting dye (Waldent Caries Detector) and polarized microscope was used.

A paramount objective in pediatric dentistry is to ensure the delivery of care that is effective, minimally distressing, and simultaneously minimize chair time for pediatric patients. Thus, time and effectiveness of caries removal by two minimally invasive approaches (Bromelain gel and SmartBurs™) were considered for the study. According to the result obtained in our study, Bromelain gel resulted in higher mean reduction efficacy of 5.96 mm2, with mean time taken in caries removal of 8.1 minutes. SmartBurs™ showed mean reduction efficacy of 1.56 mm2 and mean time taken in caries removal of 2.8 minutes. Both parameters showed statistically significant values of p < 0.001 and p < 0.05, respectively.

These above findings corroborate the study conducted by Abinaya et al. on caries removal and microhardness, wherein Bromelain gel exhibited superior efficacy compared to SmartBurs™.30 Similar results with regard to Bromelain with other CMCR agents were conducted has been reported by Reddy et al. that concluded that the Bromelain study group exhibited a lower amount of residual carious dentin.19

Khoury et al. concluded in their study with SmartBurs™ that depicted significantly lower caries removal efficiency (45% cases showed incomplete removal of caries) when compared to conventional carbide bur (95% complete removal).31

Shakya et al. reported similar findings, observing that SmartPrep™ burs were more conservative in selectively removing dentin caries compared to conventional burs; however, they required more time, as demonstrated in their stereomicroscopic study.5

Our results were also similar to those reported by Wahba et al. and Prabhakar and Kiran.23,32 They have attributed the prolonged excavation time of SmartBurs™ to their lower hardness relative to tungsten carbide burs. The results of the present study align with those of Kumar et al.,33 who compared CMCR, SmartBurs™, and conventional methods in primary molars with respect to caries removal time, reporting that SmartBurs™ facilitated faster excavation than CMCR. According to our findings, the Bromelain-based gel required the longest duration for complete caries removal, likely owing to the enzymatic mechanism of CMCR agents, which act by degrading collagen to soften carious tissue rather than mechanically excising it, thus, requiring more time than SmartBurs™ techniques.

Evidence suggests that polymer burs exhibit self-limiting properties, minimized thermal effects, improved patient comfort during caries excavation, and a reduced risk of pulpal exposure in deep lesions.34,35 Whereas, in our study, though increased time was taken by Bromelain gel, it proved to have a higher caries removal efficacy than SmartBurs™. Limitations of SmartBurs™ is that it becomes nonfunctional after coming in contact with calcified tooth structures, needed replacement; and thus, complete carious excavation may necessitate the utilization of multiple burs.

Conclusion

Minimally invasive approaches offer a viable alternative in the management of cases, particularly in pediatric populations (who required multiple restorations, very young children, having difficult behavior, and specially-abled).

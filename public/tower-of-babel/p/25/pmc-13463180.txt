Endoscopic Ultrasound-Guided Pancreatic Duct Drainage Using a Cystotome in a Patient with Post-Whipple Anatomy
Authors: Jeong Hoon Kim, Jade Wang, Anam Rizvi, Patrick Magahis, Reem Z. Sharaiha, David Carr-Locke, Kartik Sampath
Published: 2026

Video 1 EUS-guided pancreatic duct drainage using a cystotome in post-Whipple anatomy.

Pancreatitis treatment in post-Whipple patients can be challenging due to a severe pancreaticojejunostomy (PJ) stricture and the risk of stent migration during endoscopic procedures. We present a case utilizing a cautery-enhanced cystotome for endoscopic ultrasound (EUS)-guided pancreatic duct (PD) drainage in this setting.

An 81-year-old woman with pancreatic adenocarcinoma status post-Whipple presented with acute gallstone pancreatitis ( Video 1 ). Initial evaluation revealed a strictured PJ with a dilated PD ( Fig. 1 ). PD cannulation via endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) failed due to severe PJ stenosis. An anterograde EUS transgastric rendezvous procedure successfully created a pancreaticogastrostomy (PG) with wire cannulation and PJ stent placement. However, the wire was unremovable in a retrograde fashion through the PJ due to tension. Attempting to remove the wire per os through the PG tract caused the PJ stent to migrate proximally into the PG ( Fig. 2 ).

Initial endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) demonstrating a dilated pancreatic duct (PD) and severe pancreaticojejunostomy (PJ) stenosis.

X-ray of the abdomen showing a pancreaticogastrostomy (PG) stent due to inadvertent PJ stent migration during rendezvous ERCP.

Repeat EUS/ERCP 2 weeks later showed a 10 mm dilated PD. The stent had migrated out of the PG tract. Another PG tract was created via an antegrade EUS approach. A dilator balloon could not advance into the PD. A 6-French cautery-enhanced cystotome traversed the PG tract, and fluoroscopy-guided, thermal-based dilation allowed successful traversal of the PJ stricture. Two fully covered metal stents and a plastic stent were placed, bridging the PJ stenosis and PG tract, creating a jejunopancreaticogastrostomy (JPG) tract. The patient experienced resolution of pancreatitis. Two months later, all stents were removed. The upstream PD demonstrated patency on interrogation.

This case demonstrates that in post-Whipple anatomy, wire friction and tension with small-caliber PD stents within a severe PJ anastomotic stricture easily lead to stent malpositioning. 1 2 Cautery-enhanced dilation using a 6-Fr cystotome can traverse fibrotic tissue. Furthermore, cautery-enhanced cystotome dilation can be safely performed under purely fluoroscopic guidance without EUS, allowing for tandem stenting across anastomotic stenosis to create durable tract drainage.

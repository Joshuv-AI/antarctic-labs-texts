RT-3DE TEE combined with 2D-STI to evaluate the correlation between left atrial and left auricular function and stroke in patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation: a clinical investigation
Authors: Xinyi Li, Shiyi Huang, Chang Zhou, Xingyu Liu, Runyu Zhu, Wenshu Hu, Haotian Hu, Dian Shen, Aoyi Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AimNon-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) serves as the primary cause of cardiogenic embolic stroke (CES), and the recurrence rate and mortality of CES are on an annual upward trend. The objective is to explore the structural and functional changes of the left atrial (LA) and left atrial appendage (LAA) in patients with NVAF by means of real-time three-dimensional transesophageal echocardiography (RT-3D TEE) in combination with two-dimensional speckle tracking imaging (2D-STI), and to identify the risk factors for CES.MethodsA total of 125 patients with NVAF were recruited from our hospital, among whom 55 had CES and 70 did not. Additionally, 96 controls with sinus rhythm were included. General clinical data, as well as transthoracic and transesophageal echocardiographic data, were collected. RT-3D TEE and 2D-STI were employed to assess the structural and functional characteristics of the LA and LAA. Multivariate logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses were conducted to evaluate the independent risk factors and their predictive value for CES.ResultIn comparison with the control group, both NVAF groups exhibited elevated LA and LAA volumes as well as impaired functional parameters, with more pronounced alterations observed in the CES subgroup. Multivariate logistic regression analysis determined that CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥ 2, impaired LA reservoir strain (LASr), and an increased LAA maximum volume index (LAAVImax) were independent risk factors for CES. ROC curve analysis indicated that LAAVImax (AUC = 0.803) and LASr (AUC = 0.720) had greater predictive value than CHA₂DS₂-VASc ≥ 2 alone (AUC = 0.685). The combination of all three factors resulted in the highest predictive accuracy (AUC = 0.854).ConclusionNVAF patients with CES showed greater impairment of LA and LAA structure and function. LASr and LAAVImax were independently associated with CES and may improve risk stratification when combined with clinical risk scores.

Introduction

Stroke is an acute cerebrovascular disorder caused by the rupture or occlusion of cerebral vessels, leading to impaired cerebral perfusion and neuronal injury [1]. It is currently the second leading cause of death and the third leading cause of disability worldwide [2]. Ischemic stroke accounts for more than 80% of all cases [3], among which cardiogenic embolic stroke (CES) represents approximately 20–30% and is associated with greater severity, higher recurrence rates, and poorer prognosis [4–7].Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most important risk factor for CES, accounting for nearly 80% of cases [8]. Non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF), the predominant subtype, increases the risk of ischemic stroke by approximately five-fold compared with sinus rhythm [9, 10]. The CHA₂DS₂-VASc score is widely used for stroke risk stratification and anticoagulation guidance in AF patients [11–13]. However, this scoring system relies solely on clinical variables and does not incorporate imaging-based assessment of left atrial (LA) or left atrial appendage (LAA) structure and function, which play a critical role in thromboembolism. Consequently, its predictive accuracy may be limited, particularly in patients with low scores but significant atrial remodeling [14–16].In AF, pathological remodeling of the LA and LAA caused by hemodynamic disturbances promotes myocardial injury and a hypercoagulable state. Due to its unique anatomical structure, approximately 90% of AF-related thrombi originate from the LAA [17]. In addition, structural and functional abnormalities of the LA also contribute to thrombus formation and stroke risk [18, 19]. Therefore, quantitative assessment of LA and LAA structure and function may provide incremental value for stroke risk prediction beyond traditional clinical scores.Two-dimensional speckle tracking imaging (2D-STI) enables non-invasive evaluation of atrial myocardial deformation, providing sensitive parameters such as strain and strain rate, while real-time three-dimensional echocardiography (RT-3DE) allows accurate assessment of atrial volumes and dynamic structural changes [20]. Although each modality has demonstrated clinical utility, studies integrating RT-3DE and 2D-STI for comprehensive evaluation of LA and LAA remodeling remain limited.Accordingly, this study aimed to combine RT-3DE and 2D-STI to assess structural and functional alterations of the LA and LAA in NVAF patients with CES, explore potential mechanisms and risk factors for CES, and improve early risk stratification beyond the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score to support individualized clinical management.

Methods

Study population

This prospective observational cohort study consecutively enrolled 125 patients with NVAF without LAA thrombosis from the Department of Cardiology of our hospital between September 2022 and October 2024. All participants underwent baseline echocardiographic evaluation of the LA and LAA at enrollment and were prospectively followed, with CES defined as the study endpoint. The mean follow-up duration was 9.1 months.CES was diagnosed based on clinical assessment and neuroimaging findings. Ischemic stroke was confirmed by brain CT or MRI. Clinical data, including medical history, electrocardiographic findings, and cardiac imaging results, were reviewed to identify potential cardioembolic sources. The final diagnosis of CES was determined by cardiologists in consultation with neurologists. Alternative stroke etiologies, including large-artery atherosclerosis and small-vessel disease, were excluded according to established clinical criteria.Based on the occurrence of CES during follow-up, patients were classified into the NVAF group (n = 70) and the NVAF + CES group (n = 55). In addition, 96 individuals with sinus rhythm were recruited as the control group. Controls were not matched but were enrolled during the same period for comparative analysis.The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Clinical Medical College of China Three Gorges University (Approval No.: 2024-183-01), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients fulfilled the diagnostic criteria for NVAF and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation according to the 2024 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) Guidelines on the Management of Atrial Fibrillation [21]. The diagnosis of cardioembolic stroke (CES) followed the Chinese Expert Consensus on the Diagnosis of Cardiogenic Stroke (2020) [22]. All participants provided written informed consent.Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) congenital heart disease, cardiomyopathy, organic mitral valve disease, or severe heart failure; (2) pulmonary embolism, deep venous thrombosis, or other conditions that could lead to stroke; (3) carotid artery stenosis ≥ 75% or vulnerable carotid plaques confirmed by ultrasound; (4) severe hepatic or renal insufficiency, or malignant tumors; (5) incomplete clinical or imaging data; and (6) atrial fibrillation occurring during the ultrasound examination or image quality insufficient for post-processing analysis.

Echocardiography examination method

This study was performed using a Philips EPIQ CVx color Doppler ultrasound system equipped with an X5-1 three-dimensional matrix-array transducer (frequency range: 1–5 MHz). Subjects were examined in the left lateral decubitus position under quiet respiration, with continuous electrocardiographic (ECG) monitoring for synchronization.After clear visualization of the left atrial endocardium in the apical four-chamber view, two-dimensional dynamic images were acquired over at least four consecutive cardiac cycles. Subsequently, X-Plane imaging was applied to optimize left atrial visualization. The system was then switched to three-dimensional full-volume acquisition mode to obtain left atrial three-dimensional whole-volume datasets over no fewer than four cardiac cycles.All images were digitally stored in DICOM format and analyzed offline using QLAB software (version 13.0, Philips Healthcare).

Routine echocardiographic parameter acquisition

The patient was placed in the left lateral position, and the left atrium anterposterior diameter(LAD)was measured in the long-axis plane adjacent to the sternum at rest.The left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) was assessed in the same view using M-mode ultrasound. The left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) was determined via the biplane Simpson’s method. Pulsed Doppler measurements were obtained in the apical 4-chamber view to quantify the early peak flow rate of mitral valve filling (E) during early diastole. The pulsed Doppler sampling volume was positioned at the mitral annulus, both on the septal side and the lateral wall side of the left ventricle, to capture the mean peak early diastolic mitral annular velocity (e’), with the E/e’ ratio subsequently calculated. Four consecutive cardiac cycle images were recorded and stored, and the aforementioned parameters were averaged by repeating the measurements three times.

2D-STI parameter acquisition

The apical four-chamber heart image displayed on the left atrial wall was selected and imported into the QLAB 13.0 post-processing workstation. The AutoStrain LA mode was then chosen to manually adjust the left atrial endocardium, identifying three key points on both sides of the mitral valve and at the atrial apex to ensure the endocardial envelope was fully delineated. The software subsequently computed the left atrial reservoir strain (LASr), left atrial duct strain (LAScd), and left atrial auxiliary pump strain (LASct), as illustrated in Fig. 1. These parameters were measured three times to obtain an average value.

RT-3DE parameter acquisition

LA three-dimensional full-volume datasets covering at least four consecutive cardiac cycles were imported into the QLAB 13 workstation for offline analysis. Using the 3DQ Advanced mode, the LA endocardial border was manually traced and adjusted to generate LA time–volume curves. Left atrial maximal volume (LAVmax), Left atrial pre-systolic volume (LAVpre), and Left atrial minimal volume (LAVmin) were obtained as illustrated in Fig. 2 and indexed to body surface area to derive LAVImax, LAVIpre, and LAVImin .LA functional parameters were calculated as follows: Left atrial ejection fraction (LAEF) = (LAVmax − LAVmin)/LAVmax × 100%; Left atrial expansion index (LAEI) = (LAVmax − LAVmin)/LAVmin × 100%; Left atrial passive ejection fraction (pasEF) = (LAVmax − LAVpre)/LAVmax × 100%; and Left atrial active ejection fraction (actEF) = (LAVpre − LAVmin)/LAVpre × 100%. All measurements were performed three times and averaged.

Transesophageal echocardiographic parameter acquisition

TEE was performed using a Philips EPIQ CVx system equipped with an X8-2t matrix-array probe (2–7 MHz). All patients fasted for at least 8 h prior to examination and were studied in the left lateral decubitus position under stable hemodynamic conditions.LAA imaging is performed in sinus rhythm. Standard multiplane views at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° were obtained, and measurements from these views were averaged for analysis.Left atrial appendage diameter (LAAD) and left atrial appendage depth (LAAH) were measured at each angle. Pulsed-wave Doppler at the LAA orifice (sample volume placed ~ 1 cm inside the orifice at 90°) was used to obtain left atrial appendage emptying velocity (LAA-EV) and left atrial appendage filling velocity (LAA-FV). Left atrial appendage strain (LAAS) was analyzed using the aCMQ module after manual endocardial tracing, as illustrated in Fig. 3. Three-dimensional LAA volumes were measured using the 3DQ Advanced mode to obtain left atrial appendage maximal volume (LAAVmax) and left atrial appendage minimal volume (LAAVmin), from which left atrial appendage ejection fraction (LAAEF) was calculated, as shown in Fig. 4. Volumes were indexed to body surface area to derive LAAVImax and LAAVImin.Left atrial appendage maximal orifice area (LAA-OAmax) was measured at end-diastole using the GI-3DQ plugin by adjusting orthogonal planes on 3D datasets rather than automated cropping, as depicted in Fig. 5. All parameters were measured three times and averaged.

Clinical data acquisition

Medical records were reviewed to obtain the following data for each subject: sex, age, height, weight, creatinine levels, triglyceride levels, D-dimer levels, LDL cholesterol levels, history of hypertension, history of diabetes mellitus, history of previous stroke, body mass index (BMI), body surface area (BSA), and CHA2DS2-VASc score.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27.0 and GraphPad Prism version 9.4.1. Continuous variables with a normal distribution were expressed as mean±standard deviation (SD) and compared using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by the least significant difference (LSD) test for pairwise comparisons. Non-normally distributed data were presented as median (P25, P75) and compared using the rank-sum test.Categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages and compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with CES in patients with NVAF. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors (VIF), with VIF > 5 indicating significant collinearity.Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to evaluate predictive performance, and the area under the curve (AUC) was calculated. A two-tailed P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

For repeatability analysis, 20 image sets (10 from the study group and 10 from the control group) were randomly selected. The same ultrasonographer performed repeated measurements to assess intra-observer agreement, while a second experienced ultrasonographer independently analyzed the same images to evaluate inter-observer agreement. Repeatability was assessed using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), with ICC > 0.75 indicating good reliability.

Comparison of general clinical information

There were no statistically significant differences among the three groups regarding gender, age, BMI, BSA, TG, Cr, D-dimer, LDL, systolic pressure, and diastolic pressure (P > 0.05). However, compared with the control group, the NVAF + CES group exhibited higher CK-MB levels and a greater proportion of individuals with a CHA2DS2-VASc ≥ 2, which were statistically significant (P < 0.05). Additionally, when compared to the NVAF group, the NVAF + CES group had a larger proportion of individuals with a CHA2DS2-VASc ≥ 2, and this difference was also statistically significant (P < 0.05). Comparison of structural and functional parameters of the LA

Compared to the control group, both the NVAF and NVAF + CES groups exhibited elevated LAVImax, LAVIpre, and LAVImin values (P < 0.05), whereas LASr, LAScd, LASst, LAEI, LAEF, and pasEF levels significantly decreased (P < 0.05). Furthermore, the NVAF + CES group showed a statistically significant reduction in actEF relative to the control group (P < 0.05). When contrasted with the NVAF group, the NVAF + CES group demonstrated higher LAVImax, LAVIpre, and LAVImin values (P < 0.05) and lower LASr, LAScd, LASst, and LAEI levels, all with statistically significant differences (P < 0.05). Discussion

The LAA, an embryologic remnant of the primitive LA, possesses distinct anatomical and functional characteristics. It exhibits independent systolic and diastolic activity, contributes to atrial pressure regulation, adapts to changes in atrial volume, and serves as an important source of natriuretic peptide secretion [23]. In patients with AF, impaired contractile function of the LA and LAA, together with the complex and narrow tubular anatomy of the LAA, promotes blood stasis and hypercoagulability, thereby increasing the risk of thrombus formation. Consequently, the LAA is considered the primary source of CES [24, 25].The morphology of the LAA and its clinical significance remain subjects of ongoing debate. LAA morphology has commonly been classified into several types, including chicken wing, windsock, cactus, and cauliflower [26]. Qi Shuyuan et al. [27] reported that non–chicken-wing LAA morphology was an independent predictor of thromboembolic risk in AF patients with CHA2DS2-VASc ≤ 1. In contrast, Wu et al. [28] found no significant association between LAA morphology and stroke risk in patients with non-valvular AF. These inconsistent findings may be related to the subjective nature of morphological classification and the limited predictive value of anatomical features alone. Moreover, morphological enlargement of the LAA does not necessarily indicate functional impairment in all patients; structural dilation may coexist with relatively preserved contractile function. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation integrating both structural and functional parameters of the LAA may provide a more reliable assessment of thromboembolic risk.2D-STI enables quantitative assessment of myocardial deformation by tracking the motion of speckles within the myocardium, thereby providing strain and strain rate parameters that reflect myocardial mechanics [29, 30]. RT-3DE allows direct visualization of the three-dimensional structure of the LA and LAA and enables accurate quantitative assessment of atrial volumes and function. In particular, transesophageal RT-3DE provides high-resolution images and allows detailed evaluation of the LAA from multiple angles. Based on these advantages, the present study combined RT-3D TEE with 2D-STI to comprehensively evaluate LA and LAA structure and function in patients with NVAF and to explore potential mechanisms underlying CES.

In the present study, LASr, LAScd, and LASct were significantly lower, whereas LAVImax, LAVIpre, and LAVImin were significantly higher in both the NVAF and NVAF + CES groups compared with the control group. Furthermore, patients in the NVAF + CES group exhibited more pronounced reductions in atrial strain and greater increases in atrial volume than those in the NVAF group. These findings suggest that AF is associated with progressive impairment of LA function and structural remodeling, which are more severe in patients who develop CES. Previous studies have shown that AF-related atrial remodeling leads to myocardial fibrosis, increased atrial stiffness, reduced compliance, and impaired contractile function [31, 32]. Under normal physiological conditions, the LA contributes to left ventricular filling through reservoir, conduit, and booster pump phases, thereby facilitating effective ventricular filling and circulation.

Consistent with these findings, the present study demonstrated that patients with NVAF exhibited significant structural and functional alterations of the LAA. Compared with controls, both NVAF groups showed larger LAA dimensions and volumes, including increased LAAD, LAAH, LAA-OAmax, LAAVImax, and LAAVImin, accompanied by reduced LAA-EV, LAA-FV, LAAS, and LAAEF. Moreover, patients with NVAF + CES showed further increases in LAA dimensions and volumes and a significant reduction in LAA strain. These findings indicate that AF-related atrial remodeling affects both the LA and LAA, resulting in structural enlargement and impaired mechanical function. Fibrosis and altered myocardial compliance caused by chronic atrial remodeling may contribute to progressive dilation of the LAA and reduced contractility [33, 34]. Although the LAA normally exhibits high compliance and serves as an important reservoir for atrial blood storage [35], prolonged atrial and ventricular dysfunction may lead to reduced compliance, increased stiffness, and progressive structural and functional remodeling.

By comparing NVAF patients with and without CES, this study further explored potential mechanisms of AF-related embolic stroke. Patients who developed CES exhibited more severe atrial remodeling, characterized by reduced atrial strain, increased atrial volumes, and impaired LA and LAA function. The pathogenesis of AF-related CES is complex and involves multiple mechanisms, including blood stasis, endothelial dysfunction, and structural remodeling of the atrium, all of which promote thrombus formation. Approximately 90% of AF-related thrombi originate from the LAA [36]. However, the absence of stroke reduction with rhythm control therapy observed in previous studies suggests that a prothrombotic atrial substrate may precede the onset of AF [37]. Atrial cardiomyopathy associated with aging, diabetes, and other risk factors may contribute to thrombogenesis even before clinically apparent AF occurs and may interact synergistically with AF to increase stroke risk [38–40]. Furthermore, delayed detection of AF in patients with cryptogenic stroke highlights the complex and potentially bidirectional relationship between AF and stroke [41].

Multivariable logistic regression analysis in the present study identified CHA2DS2-VASc ≥ 2, LASr, and LAAVImax as independent predictors of CES in patients with NVAF. Maheshwari et al. [42] similarly reported that the combination of LASr and the CHA2DS2-VASc score improved stroke prediction. LASr reflects LA reservoir function and may detect early functional impairment before overt reductions in atrial ejection fraction. In addition, Cao Xuesong et al. [43] demonstrated that maximal LAA volume measured by TEE was significantly associated with CES, consistent with the findings of the present study. In AF, progressive dilation and increased stiffness of the LAA may promote blood stasis and thereby increase the risk of thromboembolism.

ROC curve analysis further demonstrated that LAAVImax showed better discriminatory ability than CHA2DS2-VASc ≥ 2 or LASr alone. Moreover, the combined model incorporating CHA2DS2-VASc ≥ 2, LASr, and LAAVImax achieved the highest diagnostic performance among all evaluated parameters, highlighting the incremental value of imaging-derived atrial indices beyond clinical risk scores alone. Previous studies have also suggested that LAA structural parameters may improve stroke risk prediction [44]. While LAA dysfunction is directly related to thrombus formation, LA functional impairment reflects more global atrial remodeling. Therefore, the combined evaluation of LA and LAA structure and function together with established clinical risk scores may provide a more comprehensive approach for stroke risk stratification in patients with NVAF.

This study has several limitations. First, it was a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, although only patients with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation were included, detailed information regarding AF burden and AF duration was not systematically collected. In addition, factors such as anticoagulation adherence and left ventricular diastolic function were not comprehensively assessed, and residual confounding related to these variables cannot be excluded. Third, stroke risk stratification according to CHA₂DS₂-VASc categories was not performed, and the relatively short follow-up period limited the evaluation of long-term stroke risk. Finally, internal validation of the predictive model was not conducted, and ROC-derived sensitivity and specificity were reported without confidence intervals. Larger multicenter prospective studies are warranted to confirm these findings.

Conclusion

In patients with NVAF, structural and functional impairment of the LA and LAA was more pronounced in those with CES. CHA2DS2-VASc ≥ 2, LASr, and LAAVImax were independently associated with CES. Compared with the CHA2DS2-VASc score alone, LASr and LAAVImax demonstrated improved discriminatory ability for identifying CES. Moreover, the combined model incorporating these parameters showed the best diagnostic performance. These findings suggest that integrating imaging-derived atrial functional indices with clinical risk scores may improve stroke risk stratification in patients with NVAF.

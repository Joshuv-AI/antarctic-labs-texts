Mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membrane (MAM)-associated calpains system in preeclampsia
Authors: Cui Zhang, Jingjing Jiang, Hongfang Kong, Xuyuan Ma, Hong Xin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPreeclampsia (PE) is a multisystem disorder characterized primarily by hypertension and proteinuria after 20 weeks of gestation, significantly impacting maternal and fetal health. The etiology remains unclear, but it is primarily attributed to placental dysfunction leading to systemic vascular endothelial damage and inflammatory responses. Mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membranes (MAMs) influence mitochondrial and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) function by regulating calcium homeostasis, autophagy, and apoptosis. In PE, mitochondrial dysfunction and ER stress have been widely observed. However, detailed investigations into the specific mechanisms and roles of MAMs in PE remain limited. Thus, this study aimed to investigate how MAM-associated biomarkers affect PE.MethodsThe study involved 31 women with early-onset PE, 30 with late-onset PE, and 54 healthy controls matched for gestational age. Placental morphology and MAM structure in both PE and control tissues were assessed using Hematoxylin-Eosin (HE) staining and electron microscopy (EM). Bioinformatics analysis of Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets (GSE190971, GSE24129) identified MAMs-related genes (MAMs-RGs) and candidate biomarkers through weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), machine learning algorithms (Least absolute shrinkage and selection operator, Support Vector Machine Recursive Feature Elimination), and functional enrichment. Real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) was used to validate biomarker expression in PE placentas. Calpain-1, calpain-2, and calpastatin expression levels were evaluated using Immunofluorescence and Western blotting techniques.ResultsThe results showed early-onset PE (EOPE) had higher abnormal pregnancy history, fetal growth restriction, systolic/diastolic ratio, and lower placental growth factor than late-onset PE (LOPE) and controls. Both PE types had fewer placental villi, but EOPE showed more severe ultrastructural changes in mitochondria-associated membranes. Bioinformatics identified CAST as potential MAM biomarkers, and its expression was significantly downregulated in PE samples (P < 0.01). Immunofluorescence and Western blotting showed calpain-1 and calpastatin were decreased in EOPE, while calpain-2 was reduced in both PE types, with calpain-1 increased in LOPE.ConclusionsEOPE is characterized by placental dysfunction, with increased MAM contact observed in PE placentas.The calpain-calpastatin system may be involved in placental dysfunction in PE, with calpain-2 downregulation observed in both PE subtypes and calpain-1 dysregulation differing between EOPE and LOPE. These changes are accompanied by increased MAM contacts in PE placentas, suggesting a potential link between MAM structural abnormalities and calpain system dysregulation, with calpain-2 levels potentially associated with disease severity.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-025-08244-1.Keywords: Preeclampsia, Calpains, Calpastatin, Mitochondria-associated membranes, Biomarkers, Electron microscopy

Introduction

Preeclampsia (PE) is a multifactorial pregnancy disorder that affects 2–8% of pregnancies, with primary clinical manifestations including hypertension, proteinuria, and, in severe cases, organ failure such as hemolysis, pulmonary edema, liver and kidney dysfunction, heart failure, blurred vision, fetal growth restriction (FGR), and life-threatening eclampsia, typically occurring after 20 weeks of gestation [1]. PE has long-term consequences, significantly increasing the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, especially in cases of early or severe PE [2]. The pathophysiology of PE is characterized by placental dysfunction resulting from abnormal vascular remodeling and a systemic inflammatory response [3]. Mitochondrial dysfunction and endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) are common features in the placentas of patients with PE, contributing to placental dysfunction and trophoblast cell abnormalities [4–7]. Mitochondria-associated endoplasmic reticulum membranes (MAMs), dynamic interfaces between the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and mitochondria, are pivotal in the pathogenesis of insulin resistance, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and neurological disorders, primarily through the regulation of calcium signaling, lipid metabolism, and mitochondrial dynamics [8–12]. The calpain/calpastatin system, a family of calcium-dependent proteolytic enzymes, is closely linked to cellular calcium signaling, apoptosis, and oxidative stress, thus contributing to the progression of these conditions [13–16]. MAMs may influence the pathophysiology of PE by modulating calcium homeostasis and mitochondrial dysfunction, while the calpain/calpastatin system may play a key role in regulating placental cell apoptosis and oxidative stress under PE conditions. Despite this, the role of the MAM-associated calpain/calpastatin system in PE remains underexplored. This study aims to investigate the expression and functional roles of calpain/calpastatin in the context of PE.

Methods

Subjects and tissue samples

Sample sizes were determined through a priori power analysis using preliminary data (mean difference: 0.65 ± 0.67; d = 0.966). The final cohorts (early-onset PE (EOPE) = 31, late-onset PE (LOPE) = 30, controls = 26–28) exceed minimum requirements to detect group differences at α = 0.05 and power >0.80, accounting for potential attrition.These These cases were obtained from Hebei General Hospital between June 2023 and April 2024. The diagnostic criteria for PE comply with the standards of the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists [17]: (1) Gestational hypertension is defined as systolic blood pressure (SBP) ≥ 140 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) ≥ 90 mmHg after 20 weeks’ gestation on at least two separate occasions four hours apart; (2) Urinary protein ≥ 300 mg/24 h or protein/creatinine ratio ≥ 0.3 mg/dL; (3) In the absence of proteinuria, the onset of any of the following symptoms: thrombocytopenia, renal insufficiency, impaired liver function, pulmonary edema, new-onset headache, or visual disturbances. Exclusion criteria included: (1) multiple pregnancies; (2) chronic hypertension, diabetes, chronic nephritis, cardiovascular diseases, autoimmune diseases, infections, and other complications. Controls were matched for gestational age and excluded individuals with chronic hypertension, immune system disorders, diabetes, or other diseases pathologically associated with PE. The majority of these controls were diagnosed with preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM). The study was approved by the hospital’s ethics committee (2024-LW-133). The placenta was obtained following elective cesarean sections excluding emergency cesareans and with active labor, a 1 cm3 tissue sample from the maternal side was obtained, washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and part was fixed in 4% formalin for immunofluorescence and hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining analysis. The remaining samples were stored at − 80 °C for Western blotting. Additionally, a 1 mm × 1 mm tissue sample was immediately collected post-delivery, avoiding mechanical damage, and immersed in electron microscopy fixative for subsequent analysis. Informed consent was obtained from each participant prior to delivery. The baseline demographic information of these participants is presented in Table 1.

PLGF levels via dry fluorescence immunoassay

After centrifuging the maternal whole blood of pregnant women at 3,000 rpm for 10 min to obtain plasma, 70 µL of the plasma is dropped into the sample loading slot. The reagent card is then inserted, allowing placental growth factor (PLGF) to bind with fluorescent-labeled anti-PLGF antibodiesbinds on the conjugate pad. The mixture then migrates along the nitrocellulose membrane via capillary action. Upon reaching the test line (T line), the complex is captured by the immobilized anti-PIGF antibodies. After 12 min, The amount of fluorescent-labeled antibodies bound to the T line is directly proportional to the concentration of PLGF in the sample.

Hematoxylin-eosin (H&E) staining

Placental tissues fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde were dehydrated, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at 4 μm thickness. Sections were flattened on a 45 °C water bath, baked at 60 °C for 2 h, and sequentially dewaxed in Eco-friendly Dewaxing Solution I and II (Servicebio, China), followed by hydration through graded ethanol series (100% ethanol I and II: 5 min each; 75% ethanol: 5 min; Sinopharm, China). H&E staining was performed using the HD Constant Staining Kit (Servicebio, China): sections were pretreated (1 min), stained in Hematoxylin (3–5 min), differentiated (10–15 s), blued (30s), dehydrated in 95% ethanol (1 min; Sinopharm, China), and counterstained with Eosin (15s). Final dehydration employed graded ethanol (100% I/II/III: 2 min each; Sinopharm), clearing in *n*-Butanol* (Sinopharm, China) and Xylene (Sinopharm, China), then mounted with Neutral Balsam (Sinopharm, China). Histological analysis was conducted under a Nikon Eclipse E100 microscope (Japan), with nuclei staining blue and cytoplasm pink-red.

Electron microscopy

Placental samples (1 mm³) were collected immediately post-delivery with minimal mechanical trauma and primary-fixed in TEM Fixative (Servicebio, China) at 4 °C for 24 h; rinsed in 0.1 M PB buffer (pH 7.4, 3 × 15 min); post-fixed with 1% osmium tetroxide (Ted Pella Inc, USA) in PB buffer (2 h, RT, dark); dehydrated through a graded ethanol series (30%→100% ethanol, 20 min/step; Sinopharm, China;) and acetone (2 × 15 min; Sinopharm, China); infiltrated with EMBed 812 resin (SPI, USA) in acetone ratios (1:1, 37 °C/2–4 h→1:2, 37 °C/overnight) followed by pure resin (37 °C/5–8 h); polymerized at 60 °C for 48 h; oriented via 1.5 μm toluidine blue-stained sections; ultrathin-sectioned (70–90 nm) with a diamond knife (Diatome, Switzerland; Ultra 45°); mounted on 150-mesh Formvar-coated copper grids; dual-stained with 2% uranyl acetate (SPI, USA) and 2.6% lead citrate (Sigma, USA); then imaged using a Hitachi HT7800 TEM (Japan) at 120 kV.

In this study, the “MAMs coupling distance” was defined as the shortest linear distance (perpendicular to membrane contact surfaces) between mitochondrial and endoplasmic reticulum membranes under electron microscopy, typically ranging from 10 to 50 nm. This parameter quantifies MAMs structural tightness, with distances ≤ 50 nm serving as the morphological criterion for functional membrane contact sites. Using Image J software (National Institutes of Health, USA), measurements were performed as follows: (1) Coupling distance was determined at the tightest membrane contact regions using the Straight Line Tool drawn perpendicular to both membrane planes, with software-calculated lengths (nm) recorded as individual values; (2) Total coupling length of all MAMs contact sites was measured, and the cumulative coupled area was quantified using ImageJ’s irregular polygon measurement function. All measurements were performed in ≥ 3 replicates at identical positions per EM field.

Data mining and biomarker identification

PE-related datasets (GSE190971 and GSE24129) were retrieved from the GEO database and analyzed alongside 59 mitochondria-associated membrane-related genes (MAMs-RGs). The “DESeq2” package (v 1.38.0) [18] and “WGCNA” package (v 1.71) [19] were used to perform differential expression analysis (with the criteria of |Log2fold-change (FC)| > 0.5 and adj.p < 0.05) and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), aiming to identify candidate genes associated with PE and MAMs. To identify potential biomarkers, the “glmnet” (v 4.1-4) [20] and “caret” (v 6.0–93) [21] packages were used to perform least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) and Support Vector Machine Recursive Feature Elimination (SVM-RFE) analyses. Candidate biomarkers were obtained by taking the intersection of genes derived from the two machine learning algorithms. Subsequently, the expression levels of candidate biomarkers were analyzed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was conducted via the “pROC” package (v 1.18.0) [22] to screen out the biomarkers. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was employed to explore the biological functions of the identified biomarkers. The expression of hub genes was validated through real-time reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). Total RNA was extracted from placental tissue by RNAiso Easy(Takapa code: TCH020, Japan). PrimeScriptTM RT reagent Kit with gDNA Eraser(Code NO. RR047ATaKaRa, Japan) was used to reverse transcribe the mRNA into cDNA. Using the TB Green® Premix Ex TaqTM II PCR (code NO.RR820A, Japan) and appropriate primers listed in Supplementary Materials. RT-PCR was conducted on an LightCycler® 480II(Roche, Switzerland). Based on a reference of GAPDH, it was calculated the relative expression levels of each characteristic gene. Each sample was tested in triplicate.

Immunofluorescence staining

Placental sections were deparaffinized in Eco-friendly Dewaxing Solution I/II/III (Servicebio, China), rehydrated through graded ethanol series (100% I/II/III: 5 min each; ethanol: Sinopharm, China), and subjected to antigen retrieval using EDTA Antigen Retrieval Buffer (pH 8.0; Servicebio, China) via microwave irradiation (medium-high power 10 min→medium-low power 5 min→low power 5 min, avoid drying of tissue sections). After cooling and PBS washes (Servicebio, China,3 × 5 min), sections were encircled with a hydrophobic barrier pen (Servicebio, China) and blocked with 3% BSA (Servicebio, China, 30 min, RT). Primary antibody incubation used *rabbit anti-calpain1* (Zenbio, China,1:200) overnight at 4 °C. Following PBS washes, sections were incubated with *Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG* (Servicebio, China,1:400; 50 min, RT, dark), then counterstained with DAPI (Servicebio, China,10 min, RT). Slides were mounted in Anti-fade Mounting Medium (Servicebio, China) and imaged on a Nikon Eclipse C1 microscope (Japan).

Western blotting

Fifty milligrams of placental tissue were homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer (Servicebio, China) supplemented with PMSF (Sinopharm, China) and NaF (Sinopharm, China) (100:1:1 ratio) on ice, sonicated (5 s/pulse ×4 cycles; Scientz, China; JY92-IIN), and centrifuged (12,000 ×g, 4 °C, 15 min). Protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay kit (Abbkine, USA) with BSA standards (0.5 mg/mL; 10-fold sample dilution in PBS). Forty micrograms of denatured protein (97 °C, 4 min in 5× loading buffer; Epizyme, China) were separated on 10% SDS-PAGE gels (Biotides, China) at 80 V (stacking) → 120 V (separation, 90 min), then transferred to methanol-activated PVDF membranes (Thermo Fisher, USA) at 400 mA (40 min, 4 °C) using a semi-dry transfer system (Bio-Rad, USA). Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk (Yili Group, China) in TBST (Servicebio, China), incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies: anti-calpastatin (Bioss, China; 1: 1000, anti-calpain-1 (Zenbio, China; 1:800), anti-calpain-2 (Zenbios, China; 1:800), and anti-GAPDH (Proteintech, China; 1:5000); washed in TBST (4 × 5 min); incubated with DyLight 800-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (Abbkine;1:5000, 1 h, RT, dark); rewashed; and imaged using an Odyssey CLX infrared imaging system (LI-COR, USA).

Densitometric analysis of Western blotting was performed on specific bands corresponding to the target proteins Calpastatin, Calpain-1, and Calpain-2 normalized to the internal reference protein GAPDH to correct for technical variations in total protein loading, transfer efficiency, and development time. Using ImageJ software (National Institutes of Health, USA),.tif-format images were analyzed via the integrated gel analysis module (Analyze > Gels > Select First Lane > Plot Lanes > Analyze). For each band, a tightly fitted rectangular selection (slightly wider than the band width and covering its full height) was defined to measure the mean gray value. Measurements were performed in triplicate per band. Target protein expression levels were normalized to GAPDH within the same lane using the formula: Normalized Target Protein Expression = (Mean gray value of the target protein band)/(Mean gray value of the internal reference protein ([GAPDH]) band in the same lane ensuring quantification reflected biological differences rather than technical artifacts.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 24.0 (IBM SPSS Statistics), and charts and graphs were generated with GraphPad Prism 8.1.2 (GraphPad Software, www.graphpad.com). Continuous variables are presented as means ± standard deviations (SD) or median values with interquartile ranges (IQRs). Categorical variables are expressed as counts and proportions. Comparisons between two groups were conducted using the parametric Student’s t-test for continuous variables or the nonparametric Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data. Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test was used for categorical variable comparisons. For comparisons among multiple groups, one-way ANOVA followed by Sidak’s multiple comparisons test was performed. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Clinical characteristics of the patients

Clinical and obstetric characteristics of the study population are shown in Table 1. A statistically significant difference was observed in the history of abnormal pregnancies among patients with EOPE (P = 0.006), whereas no significant difference was found in the LOPE group (P = 0.08). Regarding maternal complications, no significant differences were observed between the two groups in terms of placental abruption, renal insufficiency, impaired liver function, proteinuria, and postpartum hemorrhage (all P > 0.05). Both early-onset and LOPE newborns had lower birth weights compared to the control group (P = 0.04 and P = 0.001, respectively). The proportion of FGR was significantly higher in the EOPE group compared to the late-onset group (P = 0.03). The systolic/diastolic ratio (S/D) was higher in the EOPE group than in the control group, while no significant difference was observed in the LOPE group (P = 0.03 for EOPE, P = 0.51 for LOPE). PLGF levels were significantly lower in EOPE compared to the control group (P = 0.000), while no significant difference was observed in LOPE (P = 0.60).

Histopathological and ultrastructural features of PE

HE staining of placental tissues from normal pregnancies, EOPE, and LOPE revealed disorganized placental villi with fragmentation, nuclear hyperchromasia, and thickened vessels in EOPE and LOPE(Fig. 1A-D). Electron microscopy showed increased MAM contacts in PE, with mitochondrial swelling and ER dilation. The average MAM coupling length was 372.35 ± 369.97 nm in controls compared to 1869.57 ± 1088.23 nm in PE (P = 0.01), and the MAM contact area was 16,723.35 ± 7,902.12 nm2 in controls versus 133,365.57 ± 28,343.94 nm2 in PE (P = 0.006), with both differences being statistically significant.

CAST as MAM-associated biomarker

Building upon the clinical and obstetric characteristics observed in the study population, we further investigated the molecular mechanisms underlying PE by analyzing gene expression profiles from relevant datasets. Analyzing PE-related datasets (GSE190971 and GSE24129) from the GEO database alongside 59 MAMs-RGs, PE patients exhibited significantly elevated MAMs-RGs scores compared to controls (P = 0.0235). Analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) identified 694 dysregulated genes (Fig. 2A-B), which were subsequently integrated with WGCNA. This integration revealed three key MAMs-correlated modules (MEyellow, MEblue, MEturquoise), which ultimately included 6,099 critical module genes (Fig. 2C). Intersecting DEGs with these modules led to the prioritization of 43 candidate genes (Fig. 2D), functionally enriched in transcriptional regulation, energy metabolism, and cytoskeletal dynamics. Using LASSO and SVM-RFE machine learning algorithms, five robust biomarkers were identified: ABCD3, CAST, IFRD1, TXNRD1, and PAWR (Fig. 2E). Among these, ABCD3, CAST, and PAWR exhibited consistent downregulation in both the GSE190971 and GSE24129 datasets (P < 0.01). Specifically, in the PE samples of the GSE190971 dataset, the expression levels of ABCD3, CAST, and PAWR decreased by 23.48%, 14.39%, 17.72% respectively, while in the GSE24129 dataset, their expression levels decreased by 6.34%, 4.10%, and 2.74% respectively. The diagnostic ROC analysis achieving AUC values ranging from 0.884 to 0.973 (Fig. 2F-H). RT-qPCR confirmed the downregulation of CAST in PE placentas, consistent with the computational analysis (P = 0.02), there was no statistically significant difference in PAWR between the two groups(P = 0.06). while ABCD3 showed paradoxical upregulation (P = 0.007) (Fig. 2I).

Discussion

PE remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality for both mothers and newborns globally, with pregnancy termination being the only effective treatment [23]. Considerable efforts have focused on understanding the pathophysiology of PE to develop strategies for prevention, early detection, treatment, and prognosis [24]. Previous research has indicated that impaired mitochondrial function and persistent ERS in the syncytiotrophoblast contribute to the onset and persistence of PE [25]. However, the role of MAMs, which serve as crucial links between these cellular components, has been rarely explored in PE.

In this study, histopathological examination and electron microscopy of placental tissues revealed structural abnormalities and damage in EOPE. The observed increase in MAM contacts, along with ultrastructural changes such as mitochondrial swelling and ER dilation, suggests that these alterations may exacerbate placental dysfunction in PE by aberrantly activating calcium signaling, enhancing oxidative stress, and disrupting autophagy processes. These findings align with clinical observations. EOPE is associated with a higher incidence of FGR and abnormal umbilical blood flow compared to LOPE. Both early- and LOPE groups exhibited lower birth weights than controls, highlighting the significant impact of PE on fetal development. FGR results from impaired placental function and shallow trophoblast invasion, which reduces PLGF and increases umbilical blood flow resistance. Hassan et al. reported that EOPE is more likely to result in adverse neonatal outcomes, such as respiratory distress syndrome and neonatal death. However, no significant differences were found between early- and LOPE concerning fetal development and maternal outcomes [26]. Hemodynamic characteristics of PE were similar in both early- and late-onset cases, independent of gestational age at onset, but strongly correlated with the presence or absence of FGR. No significant differences were observed in maternal complications between the two groups. Additionally, women with a history of abnormal pregnancies were more likely to experience EOPE.

This study comprehensively explored the role of MAMs in PE by integrating bioinformatics analysis with experimental validation. It established the diagnostic potential of CAST and PAWR as MAMs-related biomarkers for PE, thereby offering a theoretical framework for targeted interventions in MAM pathways to mitigate the condition. Previous studies have shown that, the CAST gene is potentially implicated in the pathological mechanism of PE through the regulation of calpain activity. The gene-encoded calpastatin serves as a specific inhibitor of calpain, suppressing calpain-mediated cytoskeletal degradation and signaling pathway activation [27]. Dysregulated expression or functional defects of CAST lead to calpain activity imbalance, which impairs trophoblast invasion capacity, disrupts remodeling of placental spiral arteries, and consequently induces shallow placental implantation and ischemic-hypoxic injury [28, 29]. Additionally, excessive calpain activation triggers the release of proinflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6), exacerbating vascular endothelial damage, oxidative stress, and disruption of immune tolerance at the fetomaternal interface [30, 31]. Genetic polymorphisms in CAST may further contribute to PE-related placental dysfunction by modulating calpain-mediated regulation of trophoblast apoptosis. Collectively, these findings suggest that CAST influences trophoblast invasion, placental vascular remodeling, and fetomaternal inflammatory responses via calpain activity, thereby playing a critical role in PE pathogenesis.Takano J et al. reported that the calpains/calpastatin protease system is essential for embryo survival, with calpain-2 playing a key role in trophoblast survival by inhibiting caspase activation [32]. Goto S et al. also observed the presence of calpains and calpastatin in the cytoplasm of trophoblastic cells in FGR placentas, with calpain-1 expression suppressed in these tissues [33]. EOPE is frequently accompanied by FGR, and this study further demonstrates the involvement of calpain-1 and calpain-2 in the human placenta of PE.

In this study, immunofluorescence analysis revealed that both calpain-1 and calpain-2 were localized in the cytoplasm of villous trophoblast cells. Based on fluorescence intensity, calpain-1 and calpain-2 were primarily found in cytotrophoblast cells, whereas calpastatin was predominantly expressed in syncytiotrophoblast cells. Other researchers have found that syncytiotrophoblast cells differentiate into extravillous trophoblast (EVT) cells, which migrate to the spiral arteries to replace vascular endothelial cells, completing vascular remodeling [34]. This provides additional evidence that calpastatin may influence trophoblast cell function, leading to vascular remodeling disorders and abnormal placentation. Semiquantitative Western blotting revealed that calpain-2 expression was reduced in both early-onset and LOPE, indicating its critical role in uterine receptivity for implantation. Furthermore, calpain inhibition significantly reduced the number of implantation sites [35]. Calpain2 is also involved in placental formation during early pregnancy. Reduced levels of calpastatin were observed exclusively in EOPE. The increase in calpain-1 levels in LOPE may be associated with different pathophysiological processes in PE, such as myocardial injury, where calpain-1 is elevated in myocardial damage-associated diseases [36]. Kumagai K et al. demonstrated that calpain-1 levels were also significantly higher in human decidua from recurrent miscarriage cases, though the processes of decidualization and trophoblast invasion in the decidual tissue remain unclear [37]. Therefore, the role of calpain-1 in PE warrants further investigation. The calpain-2/calpastatin system likely shares a common regulatory mechanism that affects placental function, with calpain-2 appearing to be more closely associated with disease severity. In PE placentas, PINK1-mediated mitochondrial autophagy is disrupted, leading to increased levels of pyroptosis [38]. Nicola C et al. concluded that calpastatin modulates mitochondrial morphology, promoting an elongated shape to maintain hepatocyte function after ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury [39]. Additionally, calpastatin prevents Angiotensin II-mediated podocyte injury by maintaining autophagy [40]. Calpains influence PGE2-mediated EVT migration, and calpastatin inhibitors can block this process in PE [41]. These findings suggest that calpains and calpastatin may contribute to the pathogenesis of PE by affecting aberrant placentation. Therefore, it is proposed that the calpain-2/calpastatin system, rather than calpain-1, is more directly involved in the development of PE.

In conclusion, PE is associated with low birth weight in fetuses, with EOPE showing a higher prevalence of FGR and abnormal umbilical blood flow. EOPE is characterized by more severe placental structural abnormalities and increased MAM contacts. This study indicates the calpain-1, calpain-2, and calpastatin proteins are localized in the cytoplasm of villous trophoblast cells in human placentas, exhibiting distinct expression patterns. The calpain-calpastatin system may be involved in placental dysfunction in PE, with calpain-2 downregulation observed in both PE subtypes and calpain-1 dysregulation differing between EOPE and LOPE. These changes are accompanied by increased MAM contacts in PE placentas, suggesting a potential link between MAM structural abnormalities and calpain system dysregulation, with calpain-2 levels potentially associated with disease severity. However, this study has several limitations. First, we assessed only PLGF levels without concurrently analyzing sFLT-1 or the PLGF/sFLT-1 ratio. Furthermore, the control group predominantly comprised cases of PPROM, which may have impacted result accuracy. Additionally, variability in clinical and demographic characteristics among samples could affect the findings, and the precise mechanisms by which CAST, PAWR, calpain-1, calpain-2, and calpastatin influence PE require further elucidation. To address these limitations, future work will include: supplementing sFLT-1 measurements to calculate the PLGF/sFLT-1 ratio; optimizing the control group by incorporating healthy term pregnancies with careful matching of gestational age; ensuring greater homogeneity in clinical/demographic characteristics while controlling for confounders; and investigating the underlying mechanisms through integrated cellular models, animal studies, and multi-omics analysis of clinical samples to elucidate molecular pathways and protein interactions, thereby enhancing the scientific rigor and translational value of this research.

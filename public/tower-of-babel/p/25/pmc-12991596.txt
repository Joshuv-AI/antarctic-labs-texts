Assessing the Bone Condition in Osteolytic Lesions of the Jaw via Higher Diagnostic Imaging Techniques: A Cross-Sectional Study
Authors: Manoj Kumar Reddy Gade, Kajol Pattanaik, Happy Riba, Tanveer Ahmed, Sheetal Mujoo, Preetham Ravuri, Ajay K. Kubavat
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Osteolytic lesions of the jaw pose significant diagnostic challenges due to their varied etiology. Precise evaluation of bone conditions is essential for effective management.Objective:This study aims to assess the bone condition in osteolytic lesions of the jaw using advanced diagnostic imaging techniques and analyze their diagnostic value.Materials and Methods:A cross-sectional study was conducted with 50 patients presenting osteolytic jaw lesions, assessed using Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT), Digital Volume Tomography (DVT), and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI). Imaging outcomes were correlated with histopathological findings.Results:CBCT demonstrated superior cortical bone delineation, while MRI was more effective for soft tissue invasion. DVT provided comparable sensitivity but required extended processing time. A combination of modalities offered the highest diagnostic accuracy.Conclusion:Higher diagnostic imaging techniques significantly enhance the detection and characterization of osteolytic lesions, underscoring their role in clinical decision-making.

INTRODUCTION

Osteolytic lesions of the jaw represent a diverse group of pathological conditions characterized by localized bone resorption. These lesions can stem from benign, infectious, or malignant etiologies, with presentations ranging from asymptomatic to severely debilitating conditions. Accurate diagnosis is paramount to guide appropriate therapeutic interventions and improve patient outcomes.[1]

Traditional two-dimensional imaging techniques like panoramic radiography, although useful for initial assessments, often fail to provide detailed information regarding bone quality and lesion extent. Advanced imaging techniques such as Cone Beam Computed Tomography (CBCT), Digital Volume Tomography (DVT), and Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) have revolutionized diagnostic capabilities, offering high-resolution images and multi-planar visualization.[234] However, evidence comparing their diagnostic efficacy remains limited.

This study evaluates the bone condition in osteolytic jaw lesions using CBCT, DVT, and MRI, aiming to identify the most effective modality for clinical application.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design

A cross-sectional study was conducted over 12 months in a tertiary care center. The study included 50 patients aged 18–60 years, presenting with clinically and radiographically suspected osteolytic jaw lesions.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients with histopathological confirmed osteolytic lesions were included. Exclusion criteria were incomplete imaging data, prior surgical intervention in the lesion area, or systemic conditions affecting bone metabolism.

Data collection

All patients underwent imaging using standardized protocols. Two radiologists independently analyzed the images, and findings were corroborated with histopathological results. Imaging parameters included lesion size, cortical bone involvement, and soft tissue invasion.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used for patient demographics and lesion characteristics. Sensitivity, specificity, and diagnostic accuracy of imaging modalities were calculated using histopathology as the gold standard.

DISCUSSION

Osteolytic lesions of the jaw often present diagnostic dilemmas due to their diverse etiologies and overlapping clinical features. This study highlights the comparative strengths of CBCT, DVT, and MRI in evaluating these lesions.

CBCT: A game changer in bony pathology

CBCT has emerged as a cornerstone for evaluating osseous structures. Its high-resolution imaging capabilities enable detailed visualization of cortical bone and lesion boundaries, critical for preoperative planning in benign lesions like odontogenic cysts or fibrous dysplasia.[34] However, its inability to assess soft tissue limits its utility in malignant conditions.

MRI: Soft tissue excellence

MRI’s unparalleled soft tissue contrast makes it invaluable in assessing malignancies and inflammatory lesions with soft tissue involvement. Its ability to delineate lesion vascularity aids in distinguishing between benign and malignant processes. However, limitations such as cost, availability, and lower resolution for bone assessment necessitate its adjunctive use.[56]

DVT: A balanced alternative

DVT offers a balance between CBCT and MRI, providing moderate bone and soft tissue visualization. Its potential as a standalone modality is promising, especially in resource-limited settings. However, longer processing times and lower resolution compared to CBCT may hinder widespread adoption.[7]

Correlation with histopathology

The study reinforces the importance of correlating imaging findings with histopathology for definitive diagnosis. CBCT’s sensitivity in detecting cortical bone defects matched well with histopathological findings, while MRI demonstrated superior concordance in identifying malignant features.[8]

Combined imaging approach

A combination of modalities often provides the best diagnostic outcomes. For instance, using CBCT for initial bone assessment followed by MRI for soft tissue evaluation ensures comprehensive lesion characterization. Such integrated approaches align with current best practices in diagnostic imaging.[9]

Future perspectives

Advancements in imaging technology, including artificial intelligence integration and three-dimensional reconstruction, hold the potential to further enhance diagnostic accuracy. Future research should focus on comparative cost-effectiveness and patient-centered outcomes to guide clinical decision-making.[101112]

CONCLUSION

The findings of this study underscore the significance of utilizing advanced diagnostic imaging techniques for the evaluation of osteolytic lesions in the jaw. CBCT emerged as the most effective modality for assessing cortical bone integrity and lesion margins, making it invaluable for preoperative planning in benign conditions. MRI excelled in identifying soft tissue invasion and vascular features, proving critical in the diagnosis and management of malignancies. DVT, while demonstrating balanced sensitivity and specificity, served as a reliable alternative, particularly in settings with limited resources.

A combined approach, leveraging the strengths of each modality, offers the highest diagnostic accuracy and comprehensive lesion characterization. This integrated strategy aligns with current best practices and enhances clinical decision-making, ultimately improving patient outcomes. Future research should explore cost-effectiveness and further advancements in imaging technologies to optimize their clinical application.

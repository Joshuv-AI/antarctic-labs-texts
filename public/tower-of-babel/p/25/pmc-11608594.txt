Diagnostic Power and Reproducibility of Objective Perimetry in Glaucoma
Authors: Ted Maddess, Corinne F Carle, Maria Kolic, Özge Saraç, Rohan W Essex, Emilie MF Rohan, Faran Sabeti, Josh P van Kleef
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPrécis:An objective perimetry method provides four 30-2 style reports in 8 minutes. These comprise sensitivity and delay reports for both eyes. A combined report format shows comparable diagnostic power to 2 forms of automated perimetry.Purpose:To compare objective perimetry with 2 forms of standard automated perimetry (SAP) in glaucoma.Methods:The study cohort contained 40 persons with glaucoma (PwG) and 94 normal control subjects. The PwG had both perimetric and preperimetric eyes. Multifocal pupillographic objective perimetry was performed with the objectiveField Analyser (OFA), which independently assesses the visual fields of both eyes concurrently. Its OFA30 test assessed the central ±30 degrees, and the OFA15 test assessed the central ±15 degrees, both providing 30-2 style reports. The OFA tests were repeated 2 weeks apart to assess test-retest variability (TRV). OFA was compared with Matrix and HFA-SITA fast 24-2 threshold testing. Diagnostic power was quantified as the area under the receiver operating characteristic curves (AUROC). Test durations, mean defects, and pattern standard deviations of the 4 tests were compared.Results:At a median of 4.09±0.02 minutes/eye the OFA tests were quicker than SAP (all P≤0.0001), 2 minutes/eye if OFA per-region sensitivities and delays are considered separately. The %AUROCs for OFA, Matrix, and HFA were not significantly different, averaging 93±3% (mean±SD) in perimetric eyes, and 73±6% in preperimetric eyes. For moderate to severe fields, OFA TRV was less than the published results for SAP. OFA30 mean defects were significantly correlated between repeats (r=0.91) and with OFA15 (r=0.93, both P<0.0001).Conclusions:OFA provides extra functional measures in the form of per-region delays and between-eye asymmetries. Both the OFA wide-field and macular tests provided comparable diagnostic power to SAP and better TRV in damaged eyes.Key Words: glaucoma, objective perimetry, automated perimetry, per-region delays, 30-2 report, test-retest variability

Test durations in standard automated perimetry (SAP) have improved with the advent of methods like SITA and SITA Faster.1 SITA Faster has been reported by some researchers to have lower reliability than SITA,2,3 and higher test-retest variability (TRV).1 High TRV is a well-recognized problem for all SAP devices,4–7 because TRV seriously impairs our ability to track progression.8 Long test durations limit our ability to test the wider visual field, as exemplified by the 30-2 test pattern, and this is exacerbated by people’s dislike of threshold testing.9 A test that could assess 30-2 fields in a reasonable time with no need for subject input would, therefore, constitute an important advance. Such tests are examined here.

For some years, we have been developing a noncontact form of objective perimetry. Both the stimulus and analysis methods have evolved over time with new fourth-generation (4G) methods showing substantial increases in the quality of the measured objective responses.10 The objective method operates like multifocal visual evoked potential (mfVEP) but replaces electrical responses with relative changes in pupil diameter, and so has been referred to as multifocal pupillographic objective perimetry (mfPOP). When appropriate dichoptic multifocal luminance stimuli are used, the 2 pupils provide independent, direct, and consensual responses to every point in the fields of both eyes. The transient multifocal stimuli used have been shown to be cortically driven,11 in agreement with the known neuroanatomy of the pupillary system.12 Responses to these transient stimuli appear to be related to cortically driven stereopsis, which mediates the accommodative triad and are not derived from the extremely slow and spatially low-resolution responses of the melanopsin containing ganglion cells.13 Recent improvements in mfPOP methods have come from a better understanding of the dynamic gain control of the pupillary system.14 Even before those improvements, older mfPOP variants showed good diagnostic power for glaucoma13,15 and agreement with SAP testing.16 Since mfPOP tests both eyes concurrently, there is a good statistical basis for measuring asymmetries between eyes at each test location. These asymmetries have been shown to be diagnostically useful in early-stage diabetic eye damage and age-related macular degeneration (AMD).17,18 In testing one eye at a time, SAP confounds test-time and eye, thus the source of between-eye differences cannot be determined. Aside from measuring sensitivity at each field location, mfPOP also provides per-region response delays and asymmetry measures for sensitivities and delays. To date, 35 mfPOP studies have been published. The 4G OFA tests have been shown to be safe in persons with migraine and epilepsy.19,20

Here, we examine 2 mfPOP methods, 1 widefield and 1 macular. The widefield method presents sensitivity and delay data in a 30-2 format. The macular method provides a scaled report, much like the 10-2 pattern of SAP. Each test takes 4 minutes per eye. We present data on diagnostic power and TRV for the wide-field and macular mfPOP methods. They are, for the first time, compared head-to-head with SITA Fast 24-2 and Matrix 24-2 testing. The mfPOP tests were performed with an FDA-cleared prototype of the objectiveFIELD Analyser (OFA, Konan Medical USA).

METHODS

Subjects

Forty persons living with glaucoma (PwG) were recruited from Canberra-region ophthalmic practices. The 80 eyes of the PwG included 18 eyes which could not be diagnosed as having glaucoma by standard criteria. Nevertheless, these clinically normal-appearing eyes belonged to PwG, and so we refer to them as preperimetric eyes, and the remainder as perimetric eyes. The preperimetric eyes did not have any standard features like elevated IOP, suspect discs, or fields, so they are not suspect eyes in the usual sense but are fellow eyes of those with a diagnosis of glaucoma. The 94 normal control participants were recruited from Canberra-region optometry practices, the Australian National University campus, and the Australian College of Optometry. We excluded persons who had ophthalmological or neurological diseases other than glaucoma that would potentially affect visual acuity, visual fields, or pupillary function, lens or media opacities, trauma to eyes or head, and systemic diseases such as diabetes. The participants did not drink caffeinated beverages within an hour of the testing. Ethics approval was provided by the ACT Health Human Research Committee (eth.7.07.667) and the ANU Human Ethics Experimentation Committee (2010/061). All subjects provided informed consent in writing before experimentation, and the study adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Ophthalmic Examinations

We performed 24-2 threshold visual field testing with the matrix perimeter and the Humphrey Field Analyser 2 (both Carl Zeiss Meditec Inc., Dublin, CA). Also measured were best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) using log-MAR charts; slit-lamp examination to rule out pupillary abnormality and media opacity; intraocular pressure (IOP) by Goldmann applanation tonometry; and corneal curvature with an auto-refractometer (ARK-1s NIDEK Co. Ltd, AICHI Japan). After OFA and SAP testing the pupils were dilated with 1% tropicamide drops. OCT scans included retinal nerve fiber layer (RNFL), optic disc, and macular posterior imaging and were performed using a Spectralis OCT (Heidelberg Engineering GmbH, Germany). Color fundus images were taken by the same device. Central corneal thickness was entered into the OCT to calibrate axial length and ocular thickness measurements. Diagnoses were confirmed by 2 ophthalmologists (O.S. and R.W.E.).

Multifocal Pupillographic Objective Perimetry (mfPOP)

An FDA-cleared prototype of the mfPOP, the objectiveFIELD Analyser (OFA, Konan Medical USA, Irvine, CA) was used to present stimuli and monitor pupillary diameters (Fig. 1). One issue with the Goldmann Size III stimuli of SAP is that they only stimulate <0.41% of the standard perimetry visual field, contributing to their poor reproducibility.7,21,22 Even for the macular 10-2 test pattern, the Goldmann Size III stimuli cover <3.6% of the test grid area. According to sampling theory SAP could be substantially improved with larger stimuli that substantially test the whole field.21,23 The OFA stimuli used here meet those criteria (Fig. 2). OFA concurrently presents independent multifocal stimuli to both eyes while measuring direct and consensual responses from every visual field location stimulated.24 The relative pupil diameter is used to reduce age-effects25 and also the effect of drugs that change mean pupil diameter.24

Figure 2A shows the 44 regions of the OFA30 stimulus arranged in 5 partially overlapping rings, as suggested by sampling theory.21,23 Although some stimuli could potentially overlap, in practice, their pseudorandom presentation ensured that no stimuli overlapped in space or time. The brightness of each stimulus region was adjusted to achieve so-called luminance balancing, whereby stimulus regions with intrinsically higher sensitivity have dimmer stimuli. This causes the responses of a normal person to be more equal across the field and improves diagnostic power in glaucoma.15 OFA15 stimuli were similarly balanced (Figs. 2B, D). OFA outputs were subsequently mapped to a format resembling the Matrix 30-2 test with 4 extra regions centrally (Fig. 2C).26 The 3 Supplemental Figures (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A930, Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A931, Supplemental Digital Content 3, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A932, Supplemental Digital Content 4, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A933) show examples of the 30-2 reports of OFA30 for both sensitivity and delay data. The maximal luminance of the yellow OFA stimuli was 288 cd/m2 and they were presented on a 10 cd/m2 yellow background.

The individual OFA30 and OFA15 stimuli were presented for durations of 33 ms. The mean time between presentations at any single region was 4 seconds. The stimuli were presented in 9 segments with a duration of of 40 seconds, interleaved with rests of about 7 seconds, longer if subjects requested more time. This meant that each visual field region was tested 96 times. Before testing began, vergence was adjusted to a comfortable point. Trial lenses were used, but as the stimuli had no spatial frequencies higher than 2 cpd, the system is highly tolerant of defocus,27,28 so the trial lenses only refracted eyes to within 1.5 D. The stimuli were presented at optical infinity and the trial lenses were situated at the spectacle distance to minimize magnification effects.

All analyses were performed with MATLAB (2020b, The MathWorks, Natick, MA). The response waveforms for each of the 44 test OFA regions per eye were extracted from raw pupillary responses using a multiple regression method.29 The average response waveforms of each retinal region were modelled with a log-normal function,16,30 allowing per-region constriction amplitude (sensitivity) and time-to-peak (delay) to be measured. The regressive method also generates SE for each of the estimated per-region sensitivities and delays.24,25 Using independent dichoptic stimuli and measuring the responses of both pupils provides 4 sets of visual field data: one from each eye as recorded from the left and right pupils. This provides 4 sets of response sensitivity and delay data, 176 of each. The SE values at each field location are used as weights to pool the measures derived from each pupil, with the more reliable pupil for a given region having more weight. This results in the sensitivity and delay data for each tested region of each eye. The process is described in detail elsewhere.25 Transforming the relative changes in pupil diameter to dB removed much of the effect of age.30 Nevertheless, the OFA normative model includes age effects.

Even if the eyes of a particular normal subject differ considerably from the population, their left and right eyes will most often be similar to each other. The OFA asymmetry deviations are, therefore, sensitive measures of visual field defects. To calculate between-eye asymmetry information, the data for right eyes are first reflected left-right so that anatomically matching regions of the 2 eyes correspond. Differences between these paired regions produce a cyclopean data set, which is difficult to interpret. To provide 2-eyed asymmetry data the differences are assigned to eyes using the total deviation values from each eye. This assumes that the asymmetry between eyes is due to the worst-performing eye. For some subjects, the asymmetry data provide extra diagnostic information.

Perimetry Testing Sequences

Subjects each attended 2 test sessions about two weeks apart (13.7±2.4 d). In each session both the OFA15 and OFA30 were tested, in randomized order. OFA was performed before any other eye tests. In each session, either a Matrix 800 24-2 FDT Threshold test or an HFA2 SITA Fast 24-2 test was performed (both Carle Zeiss Meditech, Dublin, CA). The order of those 2 different tests was randomized across the 2 sessions, to reduce learning effects. To reduce the time burden on the subjects, those tests were not repeated. OFA is designed to follow the normal clinical practice of testing both eyes, so, to make results comparable, we also performed HFA and Matrix tests on both eyes. Due to significant visual field overlap between them, we have directly compared Matrix 10-2 and OFA15 in type 2 diabetes,31 including in progression/resolution of diabetic macular edema.32 However, to reduce the burden on participants, in this study we did not perform 10-2 testing.

Receiver Operator Characteristic Plot Analysis

For the receiver operating characteristic curve (ROC) analysis, we report the area under ROC plots (AUROC). Given that glaucoma severity was often different between eyes, the ROC analysis was performed by eye rather than by subject. To arrive at a single perimetry score per eye, we simply compared a mean value per field for each per-region perimeter output. The values employed were logits of the P values from each field from whichever per-region measure was being examined: total deviations, etc. The resulting mean values for control and PwG eyes were used to generate ROC plots using MATLAB’s perfcurve function, which employs a bootstrap method to estimate the mean and the 25th and 75th percentiles of the %AUROC values for a given discrimination task. To ensure good convergence of the final %AUROC values and accurate SE, 1000 bootstrap cycles were used.

The OFA reports on 8 basic types of per-region data for each eye. These data are the total and pattern deviations (TDs and PDs) for the sensitivity and delay data, and the same again for their between-eye asymmetries. To aid interpretation, the OFA also produces simplified reports that combine these data using the so-called p-fusion method. Here, we refer to 2 variants of these as the TP and TPA reports. T refers to total deviation P values, P to pattern deviation P values, and A to per-region between-eye asymmetry deviations P values. P-fusion simply reports the most deviating P-value from any of the 8 basic reports at each location: T, P, or A. This method was used because sensitivity and delay deviations frequently appear at independent field locations. That is, they often seem to be reporting on independent processes.33 The TP and TPA report thus direct the clinician’s attention to possible damage in the field, whatever its source. This simplifies the data for clinical use, although delay, sensitivity, and asymmetry reports can be investigated if desired. We examined the diagnostic power of TP and TPA and compared that with outcomes for HFA and Matrix TDs and PDs. We also compared summary statistics like the mean defects (MD) and pattern standard deviations (PSD) of the OFA, HFA, and Matrix devices.

Test-Retest Variability

Test-retest variability (TRV) of the OFA test sensitivities was examined by plotting the second visit data against the first repeat data as has been commonly performed in the literature.4–6,34 We also estimated coefficients of reproducibility (CoRs) from Bland-Altman plots35 for per-region sensitivities and delays.

We next examined the test-retest variability of the 2 OFA tests. Not wishing to excessively burden the participants we did not repeat the HFA and Matrix data. TRV data is commonly presented as boxplots of the second on the first visit for SAP methods.4–6,34 This is usually performed for the raw dB sensitivity, so we present the same plots here for comparison (Fig. 4), using the data from the PwG and all control subjects combined. For initial visit sensitivities of 4–20 dB the mean (±SD) of the interquartile range of SITA Fast 24-2 perimetry is 14.2±4.2 dB,5 where for OFA30 here it is 8.48±0.83 dB. Plotting second-on-first visit data can be affected by regression to the mean. A more robust method is to use Bland-Altman plots of the between-visit differences on the means across visits (Supplemental Fig. S4, Supplemental Digital Content 4, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A933).35 The coefficients of repeatability from Bland-Altman plots for sensitivities of OFA30 and OFA15 were 12.6 and 12.9 dB and for delays 55.7 and 62.1 ms (from a mean time-to-peak of ∼470 ms).

DISCUSSION

The diagnostic power of the OFA TP and TPA field measures was not significantly different from the HFA and Matrix tests (Fig. 3). The p-fusion method behind the TP and TPA methods might have been expected to cause false positives in control subjects, but the comparable diagnostic performance across all 3 devices suggests this did not occur. That makes these measures a clinically useful way of summarizing the 8 types of OFA per-region data. It is worth noting that the OFA per-region data provided to the ROC analysis included the outer locations of its 30-2 grids, whereas the HFA and Matrix only used data from 24-2 locations. In terms of test durations, the OFA was quicker than either Matrix or HFA SITA. In comparable PwG SITA Faster is quicker for 24-2 fields,1,2 but SITA 30-2 fields still take about 6 minutes/eye.36,37 In addition, OFA30 fields sometimes show damage only beyond the 24-2 border (eg, Supplemental Fig. S2, Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A931).

Figure 5A indicated no correlation between Matrix and HFA MDs. This may be caused by the nonlinear relationship between Matrix and HFA decibels.38 Figues 5B and C suggest a curvilinear relationship between OFA30 and HFA in which HFA data initially declines slowly and then declines more rapidly relative to OFA30. This mirrors the relation between HFA data and ganglion cell density in glaucoma (although that curve is usually plotted with HFA data on the abscissa and cell density on the ordinate).39 Taken together the data suggest that OFA sensitivity tracks ganglion cell loss more linearly than HFA. Structure-function data suggesting a more linear relation with ganglion cell loss has been reported for older OFA tests compared with HFA.40

The TRV results for the 2 OFA tests were similar (Fig. 4) and appeared to be better for damaged fields than published data for SAP devices.4–6,34 Part of the issue with SAP is that the Goldmann Size III stimulus covers <0.41% of the area of the typical 6×6 degrees grid of SAP test patterns. Coupled with normal eye movements and jagged scotomas, this can produce sampling errors that increase TRV.21,23,41 Noisy responses of unhealthy ganglion cells also contribute to TRV,42 although when tested head-to-head, the effect of poor sampling seems to contribute more.22 Threshold methods also contribute to poor reliability given that near-threshold behavioral responses are inherently unreliable. They are also unpopular with PwG.9 Experienced SAP users consistently rate OFA as a more pleasant experience.

OFA hypersensitivity was indicated by Figures 5B–D, F (OFA MDs >>0), possibly reflecting excitotoxicity, which is a feature of glaucoma43 and could contribute to early-stage disease. When employing threshold perimetry, hypersensitivity is difficult to measure because quantifying it via threshold measures requires test subjects to perform super-human discrimination of tiny contrasts, whereas OFA simply measures a larger neural response. The OFA provides probabilities that flag abnormally hypersensitive TDs, PDs, and MDs. Hypersensitivity is observed using OFA in early-stage retinal disease.11 It is also predictive of recovery of retinal thickness following initial anti-VEGF therapy for exudative age-related macular degeneration (eAMD),44 and the need for repeat therapy on pro re nata management of eAMD with anti-VEGFs.45 Importantly, this hypersensitivity is observed by both OFA and mfVEPs when tested in the same AMD and diabetic subjects on the same days.11 For mfVEPs, hypersensitivity is observed on EEG electrodes recording from the extra-striate cortex,11 which projects to the pupillary system.12 Of course, reduced sensitivity also occurs, just as in SAP (Figs. 6, and S1–S3, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A930, Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A931, Supplemental Digital Content 3, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A932), but the ability to quantify this hypersensitivity may have potential in glaucoma management.

In addition to measures of sensitivity, per-region OFA response delays have demonstrated high diagnostic power in early-stage retinal disease.46 A review comparing the diagnostic power of 44 measures from 23 studies for discriminating persons with diabetes without clinical retinopathy from normal controls showed that OFA has the highest diagnostic power, largely due to its ability to quantify regional changes in response delay.47 A recent study of persons with type 2 diabetes comparing OFA15 and OFA30 with Short Wavelength Automated Perimetry and Matrix perimetry showed OFA has superior diagnostic power.48

OFA response delays also appear to have a role to play in early-stage glaucoma.15,16 Anecdotally, Supplemental Figure S1 (Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/IJG/A930) shows a field in which neither HFA nor Matrix shows any damage but the OFA delay deviations do. Further studies are required to determine whether OFA delays are useful for quantifying glaucoma progression. OFA provides P values for TDs, PDs, and MDs that flag regions that are either faster or slower than normal. The OFA has a tool for tracking progression that is like the Visual Field Index, the OFI. A preliminary study of the OFI in 19 PwG over 5 years using OFA15 and OFA30 showed that OFI data for sensitivity and delay can have independent time courses.33 Glaucomatous fields mapped by saccadic response delays in response to Goldmann size 3 stimuli presented in a 24-2 array can have very large delays in parts of the field that SAP reports as normal.49 The saccadic delays are very similar to what OFA reports. By contrast, a systematic review of multifocal VEPs indicates that they produce relatively small changes in delay in glaucoma.50 We have also shown that in eyes affected by optic neuritis due to multiple sclerosis, the changes in OFA sensitivities and delays across the field over a period of 10 years were very different.51 Overall, it will take some time to decide the clinical value of the 8 types of data OFA reports, and also of OFA30 versus OFA15.

The basis of evidence-based medicine is to perform multiple tests that characterize different aspects of a disease, including different stages.52 That is to say, statistically independent data types are required to improve diagnostic certainty. A key statistic is the ratio of a test’s sensitivity on some meaningful false positive rate (FPR): the likelihood ratio (LR). If a test has an FPR of 5% at 95% sensitivity the LR is 0.95/0.06=19, we have 1 chance in 19 of an incorrect diagnosis. If 2 test types have highly correlated data, then the LR for doing both tests is the same as doing either. In the limit, if 2 tests are uncorrelated with each other (statically independent), but correlated with glaucoma, then the combined LR is the product for each, for example, 1 chance in 192=361.53

Simple linear combinations of OFA sensitivity and delay per-region measures have been shown to improve diagnostic performance in diabetes25 and glaucoma.15 This suggests they are statistically independent. As mentioned, previously published data on glaucoma progression shows that OFA sensitivities and delays may progress independently.33 New measures derived from nonlinear interactions between somewhat independent variables, like OFA’s per-region sensitivity and delay data, can be more effective than linear combinations. Such derived variables are the basis of machine and deep learning.54,55 The resulting models are complex, however, and typically require data from >100 subjects and stringent cross-validation methods to avoid overly optimistic results.56,57

We have recently introduced fifth-generation OFA tests that assess both eyes independently in <90 seconds. To do that, they sacrifice spatial resolution, only testing 18–20 regions/eye. Thus, they will not replace the higher-resolution fourth-generation tests described here but may be useful for diseases other than glaucoma or for children and infirm subjects. A macular version, M18, has stimuli that are the size and shape of bifurcated regions of the ETDRS OCT grid, making macular structure and function comparisons simple. M18 has shown excellent sensitivity and specificity in a cross-sectional study of different stages of AMD.46 A 20-region widefield fifth-generation stimulus, W20, has shown good diagnostic performance in multiple sclerosis.58

This study suffers from having only tested 40 PwG, each twice. Retesting HFA and Matrix would have been desirable. Larger and longitudinal studies are required to understand the possible benefits of OFA, especially some of its unique measurement types.

OFA can be complex in terms of the amount of new data it provides, but a simplified form of visual field reporting, p-fusion, appeared to have comparable diagnostic power to HFA and Matrix perimetry. OFA test duration was competitive, especially given 30-2 reports were produced for both per-region sensitivities and delays. The TRV of both OFA15 and OFA30 appeared to be excellent for damaged fields. OFA indices like MD and PSD appeared to behave similarly to SAP. The dichoptic stimuli of the OFA provide additional, data in the form of between-eye visual field asymmetries, both for per-region sensitivities and for delays, without significant differences in accuracy.

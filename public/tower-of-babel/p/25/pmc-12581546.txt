Association of combined folic acid and progesterone use with congenital malformations in women with vaginal bleeding during early pregnancy: a multicenter cohort study
Authors: Jiang-Nan Wu, Lin-Liang Yin, Xue-Dong Deng, Chun-Ya Ji, Jing Dong, Xi-Rong Xiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionProgesterone supplementation is used for miscarriage prevention. Animal studies have showed that folic acid supplementation (FAS) improves progesterone profiles. However, little is known about the interaction between progesterone supplementation and FAS in women with early pregnancy bleeding.MethodsWe conducted a multicenter prospective study of 17,713 women recruited at 11–13 weeks’ gestation in 23 tertiary hospitals from 12 provinces in China. The interaction between the treatment (e.g., injection or oral administration of progesterone) and FAS was investigated.ResultsWomen with bleeding in early pregnancy were more likely to have a baby with malformations than those without bleeding (hazard ratio [HR] 1.37; 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.05–1.78; P = 0.0208). Treatment for bleeding was not associated with malformations compared to no treatment (HR, 1.05; 95% CI 0.65–1.72). An insignificant interaction between FAS and progesterone injection was observed in total sample (HR, 1.55, 95% CI 0.19–12.61) and in women with bleeding (HR, 3.22, 95% CI 0.30–34.70). Among women without a history of adverse pregnancy outcomes, progesterone injection was not associated with malformations compared with untreated women (HR, 1.18, 95% CI 0.63–2.22). However, the association approached borderline statistical significance when compared with no bleeding women (HR, 1.61, 95% CI 1.00–2.60, P = 0.051).ConclusionsBleeding in early pregnancy was associated with a higher risk of malformations. The use of progesterone injection should be caution in women without a history of adverse pregnancy outcomes. Further studies are warranted to study the potential interactive effect of FAS and progesterone injection on malformation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03370-0.Keywords: Progesterone, Folic acid supplementation, Vaginal bleeding, Congenital malformations, Prospective cohort study

Introduction

About 25% of pregnant women experience bleeding in early pregnancy [1]. This symptom is associated with an increased risk of adverse antenatal complications and fetal outcomes, including preterm birth and low birth weight [2, 3]. Among the various causes of this symptom, miscarriage is the most common [4]. Progesterone is an essential hormone for a successful pregnancy. Despite its effectiveness is debated about its efficacy, progesterone supplementation is offered to women with early pregnancy bleeding in an attempt to improve pregnancy outcomes, particularly for women with a threatened miscarriage [5, 6].

Periconceptional folic acid supplementation (FAS) has a primary preventive effect on neural tube defects [7]. In addition, folic acid would attenuate the increased risk of heart defects induced by folic acid antagonists and the teratogenic damage of gestational influenza or high fever [8–10]. In our previous study, FAS also showed its protective effect on congenital malformations in women with early pregnancy symptoms [11]. These findings suggest that FAS might help to reduce the effects of complications or exposures during pregnancy. In a recent trial of heat-stressed Holstein cows, oral FAS (10 mcg kg−1) in the first month of gestation improved the progesterone profile [12]. Progesterone plays a key role in the treatment of abnormal uterine bleeding and may also cause side effects [13]. However, it remains unclear whether the preventive effect of FAS on malformations is influenced by progesterone when used to maintain pregnancy in women with bleeding in early pregnancy.

In this multicenter prospective study in China, we sought to investigate the association of the combinations of folic acid and progesterone supplementation with the risk of congenital malformations among women with bleeding in early pregnancy.

Methods

Study design

This multicenter, prospective cohort study initially focused on the value of pregnancy ultrasound screening for fetal malformations (www.chictr.org.cn, No. ChiCTRSOC-17010976). We performed a secondary analysis of the cohort to examine the association of bleeding, progesterone and FAS with the risk of malformations. The investigation was conducted between August 2017 and August 2020 across 23 tertiary hospitals in 12 provinces of China. Pregnant women with singleton pregnancies at 12–14 weeks of gestation were enrolled [11, 14].

During the recruitment, trained clinicians collected maternal data through face-to-face interviews. Participants were followed until 6 weeks after delivery. Women lost to follow-up were excluded from analysis. No significant difference were observed in baseline characteristics [11] or bleeding rates between included and excluded women (24.2% vs. 22.0%, P = 0.13).

Progesterone supplementation

The history of bleeding up to 13+6 weeks of gestation was recorded. For women reporting bleeding, details information, including the date, duration, and treatment, was extracted from medical records. Women who experienced bleeding were categorized into treatment and no-treatment groups. The treatment group was subdivided into progesterone supplementation (e.g., oral or injection) and other treatments. Progesterone was the most common treatment, administered either intramuscularly (20 mg/day for 5–7 days) or orally (20–40 mg/day for 3–5 days).

Folic acid supplementation

Based on FAS status, pregnant women were classified into groups with and without supplementation. Sixteen women with missing supplementation information were classified as the FAS-unknown group. In China, despite the guideline recommending pre-conceptional folic acid initiation [15], some women only begin obstetric care and supplementation in early pregnancy, and a small number does not supplement folic acid [11, 16]. As our objective was to investigate the interaction between FAS and treatment, women without FAS were set as the reference. The recommended daily dosages of FAS were 0.4 or 0.8 mg [15].

Congenital malformations

Congenital malformations were diagnosed through a standardized follow-up protocol involving five routine and additional essential examinations [11]. Routine examinations included serum Down screening in early pregnancy, first and second trimester ultrasound inspection, postnatal physical examination, and birth defect surveillance [14, 17]. Cases with suspected congenital anomalies, further investigations such as detailed echocardiography, magnetic resonance imaging, genetic testing of serum or amniotic fluid, or pathological anatomy [11, 14]. All malformations were adjudicated by the quality control committee through review of medical records.

Potential confounders

Potential confounders were adjusted for, including ethnicity (Han or other), maternal age (< 25, 25–34, or ≥ 35 years) and education (≤ 9, 10–12, 13–15, or ≥ 16 years), history of adverse pregnancy outcomes (yes or no), conception mode (natural or assisted reproductive technology), periconceptional non-bleeding-related medication use (yes or no), smoking (yes or no), history of diabetes (yes or no), and primiparous (yes or no). History of adverse outcomes included miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, infertility, induced labor, congenital malformation, or preterm birth.

Data analysis

We compared baseline characteristics and malformation prevalence between women with and without early pregnancy bleeding using chi-squared or Fisher’s exact tests. Cox regression was used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) for congenital malformations. The adjusted model was controlled for ethnicity, maternal age and education, history of adverse pregnancy outcomes, mode of conception, parity, smoking, history of diabetes, and use of non-bleeding-related medications.

We analyzed the interaction between FAS and treatment for vaginal bleeding on fetal malformations by introducing an interaction term between FAS and treatment into the model. The treatments included progesterone administration (e.g., oral or injection) and other treatment. Interaction analysis was also assessed in women with bleeding. We conducted sensitivity analyses by excluding women aged ≥ 35 years, who used non-bleeding related medication or conceived by assisted reproductive technology, as these factors were associated with malformations or bleeding [11, 18, 19]. Stratification analyses were performed by primiparous (yes or no) and history of adverse pregnancy outcomes (yes or no), as well as according to the duration (≤ vs. > 3 days) and gestational age (the first, second, or third month of pregnancy) of bleeding.

All analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.3 and Microsoft Office Excel. A two-sided test of P < 0.05 was deemed statistical significance.

Results

Basic characteristics of the participants

A total of 16,751 pregnant women were included in the analysis, of whom 3978 (23.7%) of them experienced bleeding in early pregnancy (Fig. 1). Most of the subjects were of Han ethnicity (95.4%), had a natural pregnancy (92.6%), and supplemented folic acid (94.6%). Eighteen percent of the women had an adverse pregnancy outcome and 9.8% of the participants were exposed to non-bleeding-related medication (Table 1).

The prevalence of congenital malformations was higher in women with vaginal bleeding than in those without bleeding (2.1% vs. 1.5%, P = 0.02). Most characteristics, except parity, smoking, and FAS, were unbalanced between women with and without bleeding (Table 1). Among women with bleeding, 1828 (46.0%) had progesterone treatment, 999 (25.1%) received other treatments, and the remaining 1151 (28.9%) received no treatments. Similar results were found for difference in general characteristics between the groups of women with various treatments (Table S1).

Association of bleeding and treatments with risk of malformations

Women with bleeding in early pregnancy had a higher risk of malformations compared with those without the symptom. The adjusted HR was 1.37 (95% CI 1.05–1.78) after controlling for maternal age and education level, parity, conception mode, ethnicity, FAS, history of diabetes and adverse pregnancy outcome, smoking, and medicine exposure. Compared with women with bleeding who did not receive treatment, those who received treatment were not associated with malformations (Table 2).

Interaction between treatment and FAS

Compared to women without bleeding and without FAS, no interaction effect between FAS and treatment was observed. However, the interaction term between FAS and progesterone injection differed from the other combinations (adjusted HR, 1.55 vs. 0.46 to 0.66). In women with bleeding, although the interaction remained statistically non-significant, the HR for the FAS and progesterone injection interaction increased to 3.22 (95% CI 0.30–34.70) comparing to those who received no treatment and had no FAS (Table 3).

This association disappeared when confined to women with bleeding, compared with the untreated women (adjusted HR, 1.18, 95% CI 0.63–2.22; Table 6).

Discussion

Principal findings of the study

Women with vaginal bleeding in early pregnancy were associated with an increased risk of malformations. Although the interaction did not reach statistical significance, the interaction between FAS and progesterone injection differed from that between other treatments and FAS. In addition, among women without a history of adverse pregnancy outcomes, progesterone injection showed a borderline association with malformations compared to those without bleeding.

Context within previous literature

Bleeding during early pregnancy is associated with pregnancy loss and several common adverse fetal outcomes, such as preterm birth and low birth weight [2, 3]. However, the association between this symptom and the risk of congenital malformations remains largely unclear, with limited studies reporting inconsistent findings [16, 20, 21]. Consistent with previous studies [18, 20], we observed a significant association between bleeding and congenital malformations. The null association between first-trimester bleeding and malformations might be attributed to the possible underestimation of the prevalence of this symptom (6.9%) [21].

In the present study, the heterogeneity observed in gestational age-stratified analysis, where the association between treatment and malformations varied with the timing of bleeding, may partly explain the inconsistent findings across different studies. In addition, the null associations in previous studies might be partly attributed to the protection of FAS. Although the mechanism remains unknown, the underlying causes of bleeding and the timing of treatment, particularly during the sensitive first month, may also play a role.

Periconceptional FAS is associated with a reduction risk of congenital malformations and interacts with gestational influenza or high fever to reduce the increased higher risk of malformations caused by these conditions [9–11, 22]. In the present study, although the results were not statistically significant, the effects of FAS combined with progesterone injection on malformations differed from those of FAS with other treatments. Progesterone is crucial for both the establishment and the maintenance of pregnancy through its endocrine and immunological effects [23]. FAS has shown to improve progesterone profile, pregnancy-associated glycoprotein, growth hormone, and Insulin-like growth factor-1 level in the first month of gestation in cows [12]. Injection has a better absorption than oral administration [24]. In addition, the route of administration depends on the severity of the symptom, and progesterone injections are usually associated with more severe bleeding. Heavy bleeding is associated with an excess risk of perinatal complications regardless of hormone usage [20]. The observed variation in effects between FAS with progesterone injection and other treatment combinations, while not statistically significant in our analysis, may suggest that bleeding severity could contributed to the potential differential effects observed across treatment approaches. Further studies are warranted to validate our findings.

Clinical implications of the findings

Vaginal bleeding is common symptom in early pregnancy, and FAS has been routinely recommended to prevent malformations (e.g., neural tube defects) [1, 3, 15, 25]. The novel findings of the current study are of broad clinical importance. The use of progesterone in women to prevent miscarriage remains controversial [5, 6, 26, 27] and warrants a balance of benefits and harms. According to the Chinese expert consensus, unexplained vaginal bleeding is a contraindication for progesterone use. Our findings suggest that even when indications are present, it is advisable to avoid the injection of progesterone for pregnant women without a history of adverse pregnancy outcomes. Although no significant association between progesterone injection and risk of malformations was observed compared with the untreated group, the risk approached borderline statistical significance when compared with the non-bleeding women. Oral or vaginal administration may be a preferable alternative. Furthermore, during critical periods of fetal growth and development, such as the first month of pregnancy, caution should be exercised regarding progesterone treatment, even in cases where indications exist, if vaginal bleeding occurs.

Strengths and limitations

Drug safety in pregnancy is a priority. In contrast to assess the efficacy of progesterone therapy, we focused on the association of vaginal bleeding, treatment, and FAS with malformations. This study design is the preferred choice for evaluating factors associated with congenital malformations as trials are unethical in the context of the evidence of benefit from FAS [28]. Several strengths, including accurate measurement of exposure (bleeding, treatment, and FAS), prospective and comprehensive collection of malformations, and good control for potential confounders, make a causal association precise and authentic [11].

There were also some limitations. First, the FAS in this study included folic acid alone and a multivitamin containing folic acid. Although folic acid is considered to be an effective component in preventing malformations, we cannot rule out the role of other micronutrients in reducing congenital malformations. In a recent study, folic acid and iron supplementation played a coordinated role in preventing CHD [29]. Therefore, other micronutrients might be partly responsible for the synergistic effect between folic acid and progesterone injection. Second, although bleeding, progesterone treatment, and FAS were confirmed and checked against medical records, the concentration of serum progesterone profiles and folate levels were not available for further investigation in this study.

Third, this study was confined to bleeding prior to 13+6 weeks of gestation, while the roles of treatment and FAS in later gestational period require further research. Finally, although the total sample included in the study was large, the sample sizes were small to compare the effectiveness of different treatments in reducing the risk of malformations and resulted in a wide confidence interval. In addition, factors, such as maternal health, lifestyle, and socioeconomic status that could influence the outcome, were not included in the analysis. Caution should be exercised in generalizing and large observational studies are needed to test the robustness of our findings.

Conclusions

In this secondary data analysis of a prospective cohort study, we observed that women with vaginal bleeding in early pregnancy were associated with an increased risk of malformations. Based on the expert consensus, the use of progesterone injections should be approached with greater caution in pregnant women with suspected threatened abortion, particularly those without a history of adverse pregnancy outcomes or those experiencing bleeding during the first gestational month. The potential interactive effect of FAS and progesterone injection on malformation warrants further investigation.

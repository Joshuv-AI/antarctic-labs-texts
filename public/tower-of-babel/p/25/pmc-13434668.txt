Hybrid topic classification approach integrating TF-IDF, BiGRU, and modified builder optimization algorithm (MBOA)
Authors: Xia Gao, Ruijun Li, Arsam Mashhadi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAutomatic categorization of textual documents into predefined topics is an important natural language processing (NLP) task. This work proposed a strong framework for topic classification, with many independent methods with the goal of potentially achieving the best results for topic classification. The first step in the system is data pre-processing. In this phase, pre-processing takes place, which includes converting file formats, removing stop words to reduce noise, and text normalization. Then, it was possible to vectorize the text for feature extraction using the Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) method, changing from unstructured documents to structured numerical documents, as well as capturing the importance of terms with respect to all documents. The core classification model is a Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU) neural network. This architecture is selected not only because it processes text data in forward and backward directions, but also because it extracts richer contextual information than unidirectional approaches. To enhance the BiGRU architecture, a new approach called the Modified Builder Optimization Algorithm (MBOA) is proposed. The MBOA was a new metaheuristic algorithm developed to optimize the model hyperparameters. The performance of the proposed framework has been tested on two benchmark datasets, including the BBC News dataset and the larger AG News dataset. The proposed MBOA-optimized BiGRU model demonstrated exceptional performance, establishing a new state-of-the-art on both datasets. On the BBC News dataset, the model achieved an accuracy of 98.62%, a precision of 98.71%, a recall of 98.59%, and an F1-Score of 98.65%. Similarly, on the more diverse AG News dataset, it attained an accuracy of 98.23%, a precision of 98.41%, a remarkable recall of 98.82%, and an F1-Score of 98.61%.Keywords: Topic classification, Bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU), Modified builder optimization algorithm (MBOA), Term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF), PreprocessingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Background

News has existed for a considerable time, making it nearly impossible to pinpoint its origin. News consists of data regarding recent happenings, which can encompass a wide range of events1. Online news platforms offer vast amounts of information, making it easy for anyone to access news reports with just a click. The coverage of local and general news information has been increasingly supported by online networks2. Furthermore, a significant volume of data has been shared across various platforms. Common sources include the websites of news agencies, personal blogs, and social media sites. While these technologies can offer diverse perspectives3, they may also restrict individuals from encountering more challenging viewpoints4, which is linked to the gradual adoption of more exhilarating attitudes5 and the potential misunderstanding of facts surrounding current events6.

Due to the abundance of online information and news sources, text mining has become the most prevalent method for text categorization. Text mining encompasses the area that equips machines with sophisticated optimizers capable of analyzing text, identifying patterns, and making informed decisions7. There are a variety of techniques employed in text mining, including information extraction, natural language processing, information retrieval, and deep learning or machine learning, which can be improved by using different optimization algorithms8–11. Examples of text mining applications are authorship attribution12, emotion detection13, sentiment analysis14, gender classification15, identification of fake information16, recommendation systems17, text summarization18, medical diagnosis19, and part-of-speech tagging20.

Topic classification represents a core task in natural language processing (NLP) that aims to automatically sort textual documents into specific thematic classes on the basis of their content21. It operates similarly to a digital filing approach, examining the semantic nuances, phrases, and words in a text, like a review, email, or news report, to allocate it the most appropriate label, such as sports, politics, or technology. This methodology facilitates the effective filtering, organization, and retrieval of extensive unstructured text information, making it essential for uses like organization of news feeds, managing customer support inquiries, and indexing academic papers. The approach learns to identify relationships and patterns between topics and language by training on a large collection of pre-labeled samples, eventually equipping it to accurately classify novel, unseen documents. A key element of any topic classification approach is the establishment of its taxonomy, the predetermined set of classes into which texts are classified. The array of possible classification topics is broad and encompasses various aspects of human activity and knowledge, including Business, History, Sports, Psychology, Politics, Arts, Technology, and Religion. The model in this research evaluates the textual content of a document and categorizes it into the appropriate class from the established list. This method converts unstructured text into arranged, actionable information, facilitating effective content management and information retrieval. The definition and choice of these topics are critical, since they serve as the basic labels upon which the machine learning approach has been assessed and trained. Typically, the categorization of news topics is a task in NLP that entails sorting news into various classes, including entertainment, business, politics, and sports. This area has significantly influenced the progression of numerous machine learning approaches throughout the years, encompassing both topic modeling22 and deep learning frameworks23. In addition, classifying news topics is a common downstream activity used to assess the effectiveness of LLMs (Large Language Models) in both fine-tuning and prompt tuning approaches24–26.

Feature extraction is recognized for converting features from audio, video, or text into data with reduced dimensions. There is a necessity to convert data into a mathematical representation27. The selection of these approaches must be done with care, since some methods yield better results on certain data types while others may not. The methods utilized for feature extraction encompass Bag-Of-Words (BOW)28, Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF)29, N-Grams30, Doc2Vec31, and Word2vec32.

Motivation and contribution

This study provides several key contributions to the field of automated text classification. First, a novel Modified Builder Optimization Algorithm (MBOA) has been introduced, specifically tailored for hyperparameter tuning in deep learning models, incorporating adaptive step control and elitist selection to improve convergence and outcome quality. Second, an optimized Bidirectional GRU (BiGRU) model has been presented whose architecture and parameters are automatically refined using the MBOA, resulting in a network meticulously calibrated for topic classification. Third, an integrated classification pipeline has been constructed that effectively combines TF-IDF feature extraction, the optimized BiGRU classifier, and the innovative MBOA, demonstrating powerful synergy between classical feature engineering and modern deep learning. Fourth, extensive empirical results on the BBC News and AG News datasets demonstrate that the approach achieves new state‑of‑the‑art results against classical machine learning models, recent transformers33,34, and standard deep learning architectures across all evaluation metrics. Finally, a thorough ablation analysis has been conducted that supports the individual and cumulative value of each component in the framework, demonstrating that the observed performance is due to the entire approach.

While the individual components of the pipeline, TF‑IDF feature extraction, BiGRU networks, and metaheuristic optimization, have each been explored separately in the NLP literature, the primary novelty of this work lies in the design of the Modified Builder Optimization Algorithm (MBOA) itself and its novel integration into a complete, high‑performance classification framework. Unlike standard metaheuristics such as HHO, BOA, WSO, and SO, the MBOA introduces three specific, non‑trivial algorithmic innovations: adaptive step control based on solution diversity, elitist selection to preserve high‑quality configurations across generations, and hybrid gradient feedback to accelerate convergence on continuous hyperparameters. These mechanisms are not present in general hyperparameter optimization frameworks such as Optuna, Ray Tune, or Bayesian Optimization, and they are not merely a recombination of existing techniques. As demonstrated in Table 1, the MBOA achieves a 20‑40% reduction in computational cost while maintaining marginally higher accuracy, representing a practically meaningful advance for real‑world applications where tuning budgets are limited. Furthermore, beyond the clean news datasets typically reported in similar works, we validate the robustness of the entire framework on the challenging Yahoo! Answers dataset (1.46 million samples, informal user‑generated content), confirming that the proposed pipeline generalizes to noisier, domain‑shifted text. Thus, while the integration itself is incremental, the algorithmic innovations within the MBOA, combined with rigorous validation across diverse data, constitute a substantive and credible contribution to the field of automated text classification.

Literature review

Koufakou35 gathered and processed a substantial quantity of publicly available course reviews from the internet. Machine learning methods were employed to gain insights into themes and sentiments. Some Natural Language Processing (NLP) methods were utilized, including deep neural networks and word embeddings, alongside advanced models like BERT, RoBERTa, and XLNet. Thorough experiments have been conducted to benchmark these techniques against traditional ones. This comparative analysis illustrated the application of machine learning methods for extracting polarity of sentiment and classifying topics based on course feedback. For analysis of sentiment polarity, RoBERTa emerged as the leading model with an accuracy of 95.5% and an F1-macro score of 84.7%, whereas an SVM was the most effective classifier for of topic, achieving 79.8% accuracy and 80.6% F1-macro. Additionally, a comprehensive examination of how specific hyperparameters influenced model performance was provided, and the findings were shared.

Zhang36 proposed a deep learning model, DCLSTM-MLP, for classification of news. Unlike previous methods that used only word vectors, this model incorporated both word-category relationships and word relationships. It processed these through parallel MLP and CNN modules to learn categorical, sequential, and spatial features. Experiments demonstrated that the model effectively addressed issues like text length and feature extraction, achieving higher accuracy and recall than other models. However, it had several issues in terms of achieving reliable performance.

De Clercq et al.37 presented the analysis of the EventDNA corpus, which consists of a collection of Dutch-language news articles labeled with event information based on a specific typology. The investigation revealed that the features assigned to events do not support semantic classification, prompting to explore the IPTC News Media Topics standard as a potential alternative. By aligning event kinds with the IPTC topics manually assigned, a more varied representation was discovered, where the IPTC classification served as a valuable proxy. Utilizing a historical dataset of Dutch news articles from 2018, a set of machine learning experiments were conducted aimed at predicting the top 2 degrees of the IPTC taxonomy in an automatic manner. Several multi-label categorization approaches have been created, employing BERTje with both top-down and bottom-up methods. The outcomes indicated that the top-down method produced the highest performance, achieving a Jaccard accuracy of 89.2% and an overall macro F1-score of 86.4% for level-one topics, along with scores of 87.5% and 83.7% for level-two forecasts.

Sunagar et al.38 examined news topic categorization using the AG Dataset with various machine learning classifiers, including multinomial Naive Bayes, linear support vector machines, Rocchio, K‑nearest neighbor, boosting, and bagging. It outlines three key stages in the categorization process. First, the pre-processing of text, followed by the application of feature extraction methods and then the implementation of machine learning algorithms.

Daud et al.39 employed machine learning (ML) methods to classify news articles due to their lower computational demands and simpler complexity. The study introduced hyperparameter-optimized SVM for the purpose of categorizing news articles based on their specific categories. Moreover, five additional ML methods, Logistic Regression (LR), Random Forest (RF), Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD), Naïve Bayes (NB), and K-Nearest Neighbor (KNN), were optimized to provide a comparative analysis for the news classification task. The findings indicated that the optimized SVM outperformed the other approaches, while its performance was inferior to that of the other ML approaches when not optimized.

Modified builder optimization algorithm

The theoretical principles underlying the newly introduced BOA (Builder Optimization Algorithm) have been extensively detailed in this section. The BOA is inspired by the systematic approaches that builders use throughout the construction process. The primary concept of the BOA is based on two fundamental construction methods.

One of them is implementing significant modifications in structure in accordance with the approach specifications, while the other is meticulously refining appearance and tackling intricate details. In fact, BOA’s foundational concepts are elucidated through various phases of mathematical modeling, including global and local search.

Inspiration

The construction of buildings is a systematic procedure that necessitates thorough implementation and planning at multiple phases. Builders follow a structured approach to convert raw materials into a completed architecture. The current method begins with the phase of shaping the building and concludes with an advancement stage focused on aesthetics, enhancements, and details. Drawing inspiration from this existing process, BOA replicates these two essential phases within an optimization framework.

Each solution within the BOA signifies a developing structural approach. The continuous enhancement of these approaches propels the optimization procedure, resulting in each structure becoming increasingly advanced through changes that align with an optimal architecture. The mathematical model of the current process seeks to enable BOA to find a balance between local search (minor advancements) and global search (significant structural changes).

The update rules in Eqs. (17) through (19) may appear superficially similar to the velocity and inertia update of particle swarm optimization (PSO) or to adaptive parameter schedules used in differential evolution (DE) and Harris hawks optimization (HHO). However, the proposed MBOA differs from these existing optimizers in three concrete and non‑trivial ways. First, MBOA does not maintain any velocity vector. Standard PSO updates positions through an explicit velocity term that introduces momentum and often leads to oscillatory convergence. MBOA updates the position directly using only a weighted combination of the global best and a local best (Eq. (17)), which completely removes velocity‑induced instability and simplifies the parameter space. Second, the adaptive step control in Eq. (18) is driven by the current population diversity. In PSO, inertia weight decays monotonically with iteration; in DE, the scaling factor F and crossover rate CR are adapted based on success history or random variation; in HHO, the escaping energy decreases linearly. None of these methods adjust the step size according to real‑time diversity of the candidate solutions. The diversity‑driven term in MBOA expands the step when the population is too similar (premature convergence) and reduces it when the population is already diverse, a mechanism that is absent in PSO, DE, and HHO. Third, Eq. (19) provides an optional gradient feedback from a validation batch. This hybridizes metaheuristic search with first‑order optimization and is not present in any of the three baseline optimizers. Additionally, MBOA applies an elitist selection step that preserves the best 10% of solutions unchanged across generations, whereas canonical PSO, DE, and HHO update every individual in every iteration. None of the existing optimizers combine all of the following features simultaneously: velocity‑free update, diversity‑driven step control, optional gradient feedback, and elitist preservation. Therefore, MBOA is not a simple recombination of existing rules but a purpose‑built algorithm for hyperparameter tuning in deep neural networks.

Regarding convergence, a rigorous global convergence proof for any metaheuristic on arbitrary non‑convex loss surfaces is exceptionally challenging and often requires strong assumptions, such as the ability to sample the entire search space infinitely often. However, the proposed MBOA possesses several properties that are known to promote convergence in practice and can be analyzed within the framework of Markov chains. The elitist selection mechanism guarantees that the fitness of the best solution in the population never increases over iterations. Formally, let . Because the top 10% of individuals are copied unchanged to the next generation, . Thus the sequence of best-fitness values is non-increasing and bounded below by the global minimum, ensuring that it converges to a limit. Moreover, the update rules in Eqs. (13) and (17) include stochastic components (the random numbers \:r, , ) that, combined with the diversity-driven step control in Eq. (18), allow the algorithm to escape local optima. The probability of generating a candidate in any subset of the search space is positive as long as the step sizes are non‑zero. Under the mild condition that the step size does not vanish prematurely (which is prevented by the diversity‑driven term, as remains positive when the population is not completely identical), the Markov chain induced by MBOA is irreducible and aperiodic. Consequently, the algorithm converges in probability to the global optimum given sufficiently many iterations, a property shared with many well‑established metaheuristics. The optional gradient feedback in Eq. (19) further accelerates convergence in the continuous subspace without compromising the global search capability, because the gradient term is added to the metaheuristic update rather than replacing it. Empirically, as demonstrated in Table 2 on the CEC‑BC‑2020 benchmark functions and in Table 3‑9 on real text classification tasks, MBOA consistently performs better than HHO, BOA, WSO, and SO, with a lower standard deviation across independent runs, indicating reliable convergence behavior. While a complete theoretical proof of global convergence under the most general conditions remains an open problem for any algorithm of this class, the combination of elitism, diversity‑driven step adaptation, and stochastic search provides strong practical and theoretical grounds for the convergent behavior of MBOA.

Comparison with standard hyperparameter optimization frameworks

To further validate the effectiveness of the proposed Modified Builder Optimization Algorithm (MBOA), a comparative experiment was conducted against three widely adopted hyperparameter optimization (HPO) frameworks: Optuna, Ray Tune, and standard Bayesian Optimization. Optuna implements the Tree‑structured Parzen Estimator (TPE) with pruning mechanisms for early stopping. Ray Tune provides distributed hyperparameter tuning using HyperBand scheduling. Bayesian Optimization, implemented via the scikit‑optimize library, builds a Gaussian process surrogate model to guide the search. All three frameworks are considered state‑of‑the‑art in automated machine learning and serve as strong baselines.

Table 1 presents the comparative results. The proposed MBOA achieved the highest best accuracy of 98.62%, marginally outperforming Optuna (98.53%), Ray Tune (98.47%), and Bayesian Optimization (98.41%). While the absolute difference in accuracy is small (approximately 0.1‑0.2%), this consistency confirms that all methods are capable of finding near‑optimal hyperparameters for this dataset. More notably, MBOA reached 95% of its best accuracy in only 18 trials, whereas Optuna required 24 trials, Ray Tune 29 trials, and Bayesian Optimization 34 trials. This faster convergence translates directly into lower computational cost: MBOA completed the optimization in 3.8 h, compared to 4.7 h for Optuna, 5.5 h for Ray Tune, and 6.3 h for Bayesian Optimization. The improved efficiency of MBOA can be attributed to its adaptive step control mechanism and elitist selection strategy, which balance exploration and exploitation more effectively than the fixed sampling strategies employed by the baseline frameworks. Although the final accuracy gains are modest, the reduction in convergence time and computational resources makes MBOA a practical and attractive choice for hyperparameter tuning, particularly when training large models or working with limited computational budgets.

The results in Table 1 demonstrate that the proposed MBOA achieves a best accuracy of 98.62%, which is marginally higher than Optuna (98.53%), Ray Tune (98.47%), and Bayesian Optimization (98.41%). While the accuracy differences are small, they are consistent across repeated runs and indicate that MBOA is at least competitive with state‑of‑the‑art HPO frameworks. More importantly, MBOA exhibits substantially faster convergence, reaching 95% of its optimal performance in only 18 trials compared to 24–34 trials for the other methods. This translates into a time saving of approximately one to two and a half hours, representing a 20‑40% reduction in computational cost. Such efficiency is particularly valuable when tuning large‑scale deep learning models where each evaluation can take hours or days. The adaptive step control mechanism (Eq. (18)) and elitist selection strategy embedded in MBOA enable it to quickly identify promising regions of the search space and refine them efficiently, whereas standard Bayesian Optimization and TPE rely on more conservative acquisition functions that require more samples to achieve similar accuracy. Thus, while all methods converge to comparable final results, MBOA offers a practical advantage in terms of speed and resource utilization.

Moreover, we briefly discuss the Secant Optimization Algorithm (SOA)50 and the Schrödinger Optimizer (SRA)51, to further contextualize the proposed MBOA. The SOA is a mathematics‑inspired method derived from the Secant Method, which enhances search efficiency by repeating vector updates using local information and derivative approximations across two phases: secant‑based updates for guided convergence and stochastic sampling with an expansion factor for global exploration. The SRA is a quantum‑mechanics‑inspired optimizer motivated by Schrödinger’s equation and wave, particle duality, employing a twin update mechanism that balances probabilistic exploration and deterministic exploitation. While both algorithms represent notable recent advances, the present work differs from them in two important respects. First, MBOA is specifically tailored for the mixed continuous‑discrete hyperparameter space of bidirectional recurrent neural networks (BiGRU), incorporating a diversity‑driven step control (Eq. 18) that adjusts the search step based on real‑time population diversity, a mechanism not present in SOA or SRA. Second, MBOA uniquely integrates an optional gradient feedback term from a validation batch, hybridizing metaheuristic search with first‑order optimization to accelerate convergence on deep learning hyperparameter tuning tasks.

Even more striking is the proposed model’s precision of 98.95%, which is almost four points higher than DeBERTa’s 95.12%, the highest among the transformers. This indicates that the proposed model produces extremely few false positives, a critical advantage in applications where incorrect classification carries a high cost. The recall of 98.34% and F1‑score of 98.64% further confirm that the model maintains an excellent balance between capturing relevant documents and avoiding errors.

Among the transformer baselines, RoBERTa achieves the highest accuracy at 94.71% and recall at 94.74%, while DeBERTa attains the best precision (95.12%) and the highest F1‑score among the transformers at 94.98%. BERT follows closely with 93.87% accuracy and 93.55% F1, while DistilBERT lags behind at approximately 92.3% across all metrics. Despite the respectable performance of these well‑tuned transformers, none approaches the near‑99% precision of the proposed model. This consistent and substantial advantage underscores the effectiveness of combining TF‑IDF feature extraction with an MBOA‑optimized Bidirectional GRU architecture. While transformers are powerful general‑purpose models, the proposed specialized pipeline, tailored specifically for topic classification, proves superior on this benchmark, demonstrating that careful architectural design and hyperparameter optimization can surpass even state‑of‑the‑art transformer models. When compared against modern transformer‑based architectures, the proposed model demonstrates a clear and substantial advantage. The strongest transformer baseline, DeBERTa, reaches an accuracy of 94.97% and an F1‑score of 94.43%, while RoBERTa attains 94.16% accuracy and 94.52% F1‑score. BERT and DistilBERT lag further behind, achieving 93.23% and 92.63% accuracy respectively. The proposed model exceeds DeBERTa by approximately 4% points in both accuracy and recall, a margin that is practically significant in text classification tasks where even one‑percentage‑point improvements are highly valued. This consistent superiority suggests that for this specific topic classification task, a well‑optimised recurrent architecture with bidirectional processing can outperform general‑purpose transformer models that have been fine‑tuned under identical conditions.

The performance gap becomes even more pronounced when comparing the proposed model against standard deep learning architectures. The best performing traditional recurrent model is the GRU, which achieves an accuracy of 89.92% and an F1‑score of 89.68%, followed by the LSTM at 88.83% accuracy and 88.20% F1‑score, and the CNN at 86.13% accuracy and 86.62% F1‑score. The proposed model surpasses the GRU by nearly 9% points in accuracy and F1‑score, a dramatic improvement that cannot be attributed to architectural differences alone, as the baseline GRU already incorporates recurrent processing. This leap in performance underscores the critical role of the MBOA hyperparameter optimization, which fine‑tunes the BiGRU architecture far beyond what manual tuning or default configurations can achieve.

Examining precision and recall separately reveals further insights into the models’ behaviors. The proposed model achieves a precision of 98.32% and a recall of 98.79%, indicating that it is slightly more effective at identifying true positives than at avoiding false positives, though both values are exceptionally high. Among the transformers, RoBERTa shows a precision of 94.87% and a recall of 94.19%, while DeBERTa exhibits a precision of 94.57% and a recall of 94.29%. The slight imbalance in the transformer models (higher precision than recall) suggests they are somewhat conservative in their predictions. In contrast, the baseline GRU shows the opposite pattern with recall (89.58%) slightly lower than precision (89.79%), indicating a mild tendency toward false negatives. The proposed model’s nearly equal metrics confirm its balanced decision boundary.

In summary, the experimental results provide compelling evidence that the proposed MBOA‑optimized BiGRU pipeline delivers superior classification performance across all metrics, significantly outperforming both transformer architectures and conventional deep learning models. The margins of improvement are consistent and substantial: approximately 4% points over DeBERTa, 9% points over GRU, and more than 12% points over CNN. These findings strongly support the effectiveness of integrating TF‑IDF feature extraction with a bidirectional GRU whose hyperparameters are automatically refined by the MBOA algorithm. The balanced precision and recall further demonstrate that the proposed model is not biased toward either false positives or false negatives, making it a robust and reliable choice for real‑world topic classification applications.

Conclusion

The automated classification of written documents to topics is a significant task in natural language processing (NLP). While advancements in traditional machine learning models and contemporary deep learning models have been evident in recent years, the fundamental problem of providing optimal feature extraction, hyperparameter extraction and model architecture remains the limiting factor in achieving peak performance. This research captures a scalable framework for topic classification that includes combining several critical components to reach state-of-the-art results. The proposed system starts with complete data pre-processing that includes file format conversion, text normalization, and the removal of stop words to reduce noise. For feature extraction, the Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) method was employed to effectively vectorize the text, transforming unstructured documents into a structured numerical format that captures the significance of terms relative to the entire corpus. The core of the classification model is a Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (BiGRU) neural network. This architecture was selected for its ability to process sequential text data in both forward and backward directions, capturing richer contextual information than unidirectional models. To improve the potential of this BiGRU architecture, a novel Modified Builder Optimization Algorithm (MBOA) has been proposed. This metaheuristic algorithm was specifically designed to automate and optimize the model’s hyperparameters. The performance of this integrated system was rigorously evaluated on two standard benchmark datasets, including the BBC News dataset and the larger AG News dataset. The proposed MBOA-optimized BiGRU model demonstrated exceptional performance, establishing a new state-of-the-art on both datasets. On the BBC News dataset, the model achieved an accuracy of 98.62%, a precision of 98.71%, a recall of 98.59%, and an F1-Score of 98.65%. Similarly, on the more diverse AG News dataset, it attained an accuracy of 98.23%, a precision of 98.41%, a remarkable recall of 98.82%, and an F1-Score of 98.61%. Moreover, on Yahoo! Answers dataset, the model could achieve the values of 98.41%, 98.95%, 98.34%, and 98.64% for in accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score, respectively. These results significantly outperformed a wide range of baseline models, deep learning architectures, and even advanced transformer-based models. Despite its success, this study has certain limitations. The model’s performance must also be validated on more informal and noisier text genres, such as social media posts and user reviews. In the future, a variety of possibilities can be investigated. First, MBOA-BiGRU pipeline can be tested on other NLP tasks such as sentiment analysis and spam detection. Second, other pre-trained word embeddings (e.g. Word2Vec, GloVe) can be used to enhance the feature representation.

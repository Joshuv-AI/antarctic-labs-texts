Impact of moisture condition, shade-taking device, zirconia color, and thickness on color difference values: an in vitro study
Authors: Pongsuwee Poatrakan, Atchariya Wattanavisut, Mali Palanuwech
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThe purpose of this in vitro study was to investigate the effect of zirconia color, thickness, and moisture condition on color difference values (ΔE00) using two shade guides.Materials and methodsForty-two zirconia specimens were divided into three color groups: A1, A2, and A3 (n = 14 each). Each color group comprised two thickness groups (1.5 and 2.0 mm; n = 7 each) and underwent testing over 50 V anodization titanium background specimen (n = 7) in wet and dry conditions, yielding 12 experimental groups. All the specimens were examined using a spectrophotometer and compared with the two control groups (zirconia and vita shade guides, n = 1) to establish ΔE00. The ΔE001 (zirconia shade guide) and ΔE002 (vita shade guide) values were calculated for the first and second hypotheses, respectively. ΔE00 values = 1.8 were considered 50% clinically acceptable threshold (AT). The data collected were statistically analyzed using analysis of variance and appropriate post-hoc tests (P = .05).ResultsThe wet/dry condition, zirconia color, and thickness significantly affected the observed ΔE00 (P <.05) for both shade guides. The dry condition using 1.5- and 2.0-mm zirconia revealed values same as or greater than the estimated mean when compared with the wet condition (P < .05) for both shade guides. In contrast to the vita shade guide, all the mean and 95% confidence interval (CI) values of ΔE001were below 50% AT, except 1.5-mm zirconia with A1 and A3 shades in dry condition.ConclusionsThe use of a 1.5-mm and A1–A3 shade zirconia over yellow-anodized titanium potentially results in acceptable color matching with 95% CIs using a combination of wet condition and zirconia shade guide.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-026-07859-w.

Introduction

Recently, pre-shaded zirconia blanks have been introduced to eliminate the risk of veneering porcelain cracking and delamination [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. They are classified into three categories: homogeneous yttria-stabilized tetragonal zirconia polycrystal (Y-TZP) (monolithic) single-layer, monolithic multilayer, and heterogeneous Y-TZP multilayer zirconia [4, 5, 6]. The layers of monolithic multilayer zirconia differ in pigment percentage, whereas those of heterogeneous Y-TZP multilayer zirconia differ based on yttria concentrations (mol% Y-TZP). Multilayer zirconia, which is typically darker, more colored, and more opaque at the cervical area, and lighter, less colored, and more translucent at the incisal area, is better arranged to mimic the natural tooth compared with the monolithic single-layer zirconia [5, 6, 7]. Additionally, 4 mol% Y-TZP zirconia is significantly stronger, and is not weakened by alumina airborne particle abrasion. It is slightly less translucent compared with a 5 mol% Y-TZP zirconia [8]. Therefore, the 4 mol% Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia is the material of choice for implant-supported fixed prostheses with high fracture resistance, low degree of bacteria adhesion, and good esthetic properties [4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9].

Natural beauty is increasingly emphasized by current trends in smile enhancement. Color is recognized as one of the five parameters included in the white esthetic score, which is a visual analog scale used to assess the esthetics of implant-supported crowns [10, 11]. Precise shade selection by patients and proper communication with dental technicians in order to achieve accurate color replication of implant-supported fixed prostheses are keys to the success of esthetic prostheses by clinicians. Shade selection is generally performed by the Vita Classical shade guide (VSG: Vita Zahnfabrik, Bad Säckingen, Germany), which is one of the most commonly used shade guides in dental clinics since the 1950 s [12, 13, 14, 15]. Pre-shaded zirconia blanks are claimed by their manufacturers in color matching to the VSG [4, 5]. Therefore, the pre-shaded multilayer zirconia requires minimal or no external coloring, thereby eliminating operator-dependent variability [16]. Color matching between the multilayer zirconia and VSG has not been investigated by previous studies.

The shade selection of natural tooth and restorative materials in patients is usually performed in wet conditions as it is a convenient and practical procedure performed inside the naturally moist oral cavity of the patient. Furthermore, water in the inter-prism spaces of enamel is replaced with air in the dry condition, yielding decreased translucency of the enamel and increased luminosity of the tooth [17]. In contrast to enamel, water can neither diffuse into the zirconia nor spread out from it. The degree of color differences between moist and dry conditions using the CIELAB formula was lower for zirconia than that for enamel [17, 18]. This may be because the translucency of zirconia can be altered by changes on the external surface [4, 5, 19]. To the best of the authors’ knowledge, the effect of wet zirconia surface on the esthetic outcomes has not been extensively studied, warranting further investigation.

The aim of this in vitro study was to evaluate esthetic outcomes based on the color difference values of zirconia with varying color, thickness, and surface moisture conditions over the yellow-anodized Ti using the zirconia shade guide (ZSG) and VSG. The null hypotheses of this study were as follows: (1) The zirconia color, thickness, and surface moisture condition would not affect the measured color differences, when a corresponding ZSG specimen color is used as a control. (2) The zirconia color, thickness, and surface moisture condition would not affect the measured color differences, when a corresponding VSG specimen color was used as a control.

Materials and methods

Experimental design and specimen preparation

Overall, twelve zirconia groups (a combination of three colors (A1, A2, and A3) with two thicknesses (1.5 and 2.0 mm), and two moisture conditions (wet and dry)) and a Ti group were prepared as twelve experimental groups, whereas twelve shade guide groups (six zirconia groups (a combination of three colors (A1, A2, and A3) with one thickness (12.0 mm), and two moisture conditions (wet and dry) and six VITA groups (A1, A2, and A3) with two moisture conditions (wet and dry)) were prepared as twelve control groups. Twelve experimental groups (using three zirconia colors, two zirconia thicknesses, and two moisture conditions) and six control groups (using three colors of the zirconia shade guide (ZSG) with two moisture conditions (wet and dry)) were used for color difference analysis in the first hypothesis (ΔE001). Additionally, twelve experimental groups (using three zirconia colors, two zirconia thicknesses, and two moisture conditions) and six control groups (using three colors of the VITA Shade Guide (VSG) with two moisture conditions (wet and dry)) were used for color difference analysis in the second hypothesis (ΔE002) (Fig. 1). Based on the results of an unpublished pilot study conducted by the author, a sample size of four or more was recommended to achieve α = 0.05 with a power of > 0.95. Consequently, a sample size of seven was used.

Argen Z Multilayer HT Zirconia (Argen Z blocks, 4Y-TZP Monolithic Multilayer, A1–A3; The Argen Corporation, USA) was used to fabricate 21 square-shape zirconia specimens (11.5 × 11.5 × 2.0 mm) with a beveled top-right corner, which were divided into three color groups: A1, A2, and A3 (n = 7 each) (Supplementary Table S1); and 24 square-shape zirconia specimens (11.5 × 11.5 × 1.5 mm and 11.5 × 11.5 × 12.0 mm; n = 7 and 1, respectively) with a beveled top-left corner, which were divided into three color groups: A1, A2, and A3 (n = 8 each) (Supplementary Table S1). The 1.5- and 2.0-mm zirconia groups that underwent testing over 50 V anodization titanium background specimens (n = 7) in wet and dry conditions were used as the experimental groups. The 12.0-mm zirconia group was used as the corresponding ZSG control group. A three-dimensional (3D) design program (Microsoft program design; Microsoft Corporation, USA) and milling machine (Roland DWX-520DCi milling machine; Roland DG Corporation/DGSHAPE Corporation, Japan) were used to fabricate the specimens. The specimens were polished by a goat hair brush (Komet Dental; Gebr. Brasseler GmbH & Co.KG Corporation, Germany) and diamond polishing paste (Dia Glace diamond polisher; Yeti Dental, Germany), cleaned by a cleansing machine (Biosonic UC125H; Coltène/Whaledent Inc., USA) for 1 min in distilled water (LEE CIER HUAD; Lee Cier Huad Ltd. Part., Bangkok, Thailand) at room temperature, placed on a glass plate, air-dried by a dust blower, and glazed (Yamamoto liquid; Shofu Inc., Japan) [20]. The specimen thickness was re-examined after glazing with a digital thickness gauge (Calipretto S, Renfert, Germany). Specimens lacking an acceptable thickness (± 0.01 mm) were replaced with new specimens, and then stored in a moisture-free container.

For the corresponding VSG control group, three VITA Classical shades (VITA classical, A1–A3; Zahnfabrik H. Rauter GmbH & Co. KG, Germany, n = 1) were cleaned using the cleansing machine for 1 min, placed on a glass plate, air-dried by a dust blower, and then stored in a moisture-free container.

Titanium grade 5 (Arum Metal Disk, ARUM Dentistry; ARUM DENTISTRY Co., Ltd, Germany/Korea) was used to fabricate 7 background Ti square-shaped specimens (11.5 × 11.5 × 4.0 mm) using 3D modeling software (Microsoft program design; Microsoft Corporation, USA) and a heavy metal milling unit (ARUM 5X-500; ARUM DENTISTRY Co., Ltd, Germany/Korea). All specimens were ground with 600-grit silicon carbide paper on a polishing machine (NANO-1000 S; Pace Technologies, Arizona, USA) followed by 800-, 1,000-, and 1,200-grit silicon carbide paper [21]. The specimens were then polished by a felt cloth combined with 3-micron polishing powder followed by 1-micron polishing powder. Specimens lacking an acceptable thickness (± 0.01 mm) were replaced with new specimens, cleaned by the cleansing machine for 1 min in distilled water at room temperature, placed on a glass plate, and then air-dried by a dust blower. The specimens were colored using an anodization machine (Calia Pro Anodized machine; Max Dental Co., Ltd, Korea) at an electric voltage of 50 V for 1 min, rinsed with distilled water, and dried with oil-free air for 20 s at room temperature [21, 22].

Color characterization

Color measurements were obtained using a spectrophotometer (Ultrascan Pro, Hunterlab, Virginia, USA) after calibration according to the manufacturer’s instructions using the proprietary software (Inclusion Included, Ultrascan VIS, Hunterlab, Virginia, USA) by one operator (P.P.) based on simple random sampling. A custom black plastic sheet (diameter 38.0 mm, thickness 1 mm) with a hole (diameter 8.0 mm) and a line with two dots above the hole for positioning of the incisal surface of the VSG was used to position the specimen in the VSG control groups. A custom rectangular cuboid jig (100.0 × 100.0 × 57.5 mm) with a cuboidal cavity (10.0 × 12.0 × 11.5 mm) from putty silicone (Siligum putty; DMG Chemisch–Pharmazeutische Fabrik GmbH, Germany) was used to position the specimens in the experimental and ZSG control groups. A black box (Ultrascan Pro; Hunterlab, Virginia, USA) was used to cover all the experimental and control groups during the measurement according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

The average of the three measurements was calculated for each control and experimental group. For the VSG control group, a VSG specimen was placed at the center of the hole of the plate, which exposed the center of the artificial VITA tooth. Consequently, a reflectance port with diaphragm (diameter 7.0 mm) was aligned centrally on the polished surface of the VSG specimen. For the ZSG control group, a 12.0-mm ZSG specimen was placed at the bottom of the cavity of the jig. Consequently, the reflectance port was aligned centrally on the glazed surface of the ZSG specimen. After the measurement, the VSG and ZSG specimens were placed for subsequent measurement. For the experimental groups, a background Ti specimen was placed at the bottom of the jig cavity. To achieve optical contact, three drops of distilled water were applied to the background specimen, which was covered with a zirconia specimen [23]. Consequently, the reflectance port was aligned centrally on the glazed surface of the zirconia specimen. After the measurement, the Ti background and zirconia specimens were placed in three intervening distilled water drops for subsequent measurement. In addition, three drops of distilled water using a dropper (Mizuho dropper bottles 0824, Mizuhokasei; TRUSCO NAKAYAMA Co., Ltd, china) located at the center of the specimen were applied onto the exterior surface of the VSG, ZSG, and zirconia specimens before measurement for the wet condition.

The CIEDE2000 color differences (ΔE00) formula was more suitable for zirconia than the CIELAB (ΔEab) formula [24]. Therefore, ΔE₀₀ between the experimental and control groups with the same shade and moisture condition (wet/dry) were calculated from CIEDE2000 values using Eq. 1, wherein L, C, H, RT, S, and k represent lightness/darkness, chroma, hue, rotation term, weighting factor, and parametric factor, respectively. In this study, kL, kC, and kHwere each set to 1 [25, 26]. Furthermore, the 50% perceptibility threshold (50% PT) and 50% clinical acceptability threshold (50% AT) were set at 0.8 and 1.8, respectively [27]. The ΔE001 (ZSG) and ΔE002 (VSG) values were calculated for the first and second hypotheses, respectively. The 12.0-mm specimens were not intended to simulate clinical restorations, but rather to serve as an optically stable reference. In an unpublished pilot study conducted by the author, the mean ± standard deviation (SD) of the ΔE₀₀ value measured by calculating the color difference for the control specimens (options mode with an average method at n = 3) against black and white was 0.03 ± 0.01, which is below the 50% PT; therefore, the 12.0-mm zirconia specimen was recommended as the control group (Supplementary Table S1 and S2).

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Python 3.9 and R programming language (R Core Team, 2025) with the statsmodels and scikit-posthocs libraries. A separate three-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was conducted for each shade guide (ΔE001and ΔE002) to evaluate the main effects of zirconia color, thickness, and wet/dry condition, as well as their interaction effects on the ΔE₀₀ values. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to check if the residuals of the model were normally distributed. Levene’s test was used to verify that the variances across the different experimental groups were equal [28]. Given the violation of these assumptions, the Games-Howell test was employed for post-hoc comparisons. The level of statistical significance was set at α = 0.05. Partial Eta Squared (ηp²) was calculated to determine the effect size for all main and interaction effects. Prior to conducting the ANOVA, the assumptions of normality and homogeneity of variances were assessed.

Results

Results from the three-way ANOVA revealed that the zirconia color, zirconia thickness, and wet/dry condition on zirconia surface; interaction between the two factors; and interaction among the three factors significantly affected the mean color difference values of high translucent multilayered zirconia for ZSG (ΔE001) and VSG (ΔE002) (P <.05; Tables 1 and 2, respectively). Descriptive statistics, including the mean and SD values of ΔE001 and ΔE002 for all the experimental groups, are detailed in Tables 3 and 4, respectively, and Fig. 2. The Games-Howell post-hoc test for multiple comparisons revealed that the zirconia color and wet/dry condition on the zirconia surface significantly affected the estimated mean color difference values of HTMZ for ZSG and VSG (P <.05, Tables 3 and 4, respectively) in each zirconia thickness. Additionally, the mean and 95% confidence interval (CI) ΔE001 and ΔE002 values after Games-Howell post-hoc test are presented in Tables 5 and 6, respectively, providing a measure of precision for the mean of each group. Furthermore, the mean change in the lightness (ΔL), red-green values (Δa), and yellow-blue values (Δb) of ΔE001 and ΔE002 are presented in Figs. 3 and 4, respectively.

Discussion

In this in vitro study, we applied different color, thicknesses, and surface moisture conditions of monolithic multilayer zirconia over the yellow-anodized Ti implant abutment in a simulated clinical setting of an implant-supported fixed prosthesis to achieve superior esthetics. To accomplish this, three factors were controlled to reduce the gap in the ΔE00 between the implant-supported zirconia prosthesis and the shade guides (ZSG and VSG). Our observations showed that the zirconia color, thickness, and surface moisture condition using ZSG and VSG significantly affected the mean ΔE00 values; therefore, the null hypotheses were rejected.

This study revealed that the perceived color of the zirconia is not an intrinsic, static property but rather a dynamic outcome influenced by a system of interacting variables. This discussion synthesizes the findings to provide a cohesive interpretation, focusing on the significant clinical implications of two properties, viz. moisture condition and modulating role of the measurement device.

First, the moisture condition (wet/dry) affects the ΔE00 of 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia. A primary finding of this investigation is the substantial and statistically significant main effect of the wet/dry condition on ΔE00 values across both measurement systems. The large effect sizes observed (ηp² = 0.348 for ZSG; ηp² = 0.632 for VSG) confirm that the hydration state of the zirconia specimen is a dominant factor in color perception [28]. The estimated mean value for 1.5-mm desiccated zirconia (dry condition) was higher than that in wet condition (P <.05) for both shade guides (Tables 3 and 4), suggesting greater color shifts and instability. However, the estimated mean value for 2.0-mm desiccated zirconia was equal to that in wet condition (P <.05) for both shade guides (Tables 3 and 4). The mean color difference (ΔEab) between the wet/dry conditions for 0.5-mm multilayer zirconia ranged from 0.40 to 0.77 [18], similar to those between the wet/dry conditions of 1.5-mm multilayer zirconia (ΔE001), which ranged from 0.09 to 0.53 (Table 3; Fig. 2). The wet surface of zirconia in this study outperformed desiccated zirconia by reducing color mismatches, which involve lower ΔE001 and ΔE002 values (Tables 3 and 4, and Fig. 2) and Δlightness (Fig. 3), similar to the property of a thin glazed layer of zirconia [18, 29, 30, 31]. This is likely attributable to changes in the index of refraction at the material’s surface and within its porous microstructure upon drying, which alters light scattering and absorption [18, 29–34]. When incident light encounters the dry surface of the specimen, it is divided into reflected, transmitted, and absorbed light [21, 32]. In contrast, when incident light encounters the wet surface of the specimen, it is divided into reflected, refracted, transmitted, and absorbed light [21, 32, 33]. The denser the transparent medium, the higher its index of refraction and the slower the light travels through it, the incident light travels slower in water than in air [34]. As incident light passes through one transparent medium (air) to another transparent medium (water), the change in speed bends the light. Different wavelengths of light are slowed at different rates, which causes them to bend at different angles, resulting in refraction-induced image deformation [33, 34]. Additionally, on the desiccated surface, with apparent roughness and tiny porous defects, the light undergoes diffuse reflection, such that the rays scatter in many different directions. However, on the wet surface, which is smoother, the light undergoes specular reflection, such that the rays reflect at a consistent angle, creating a glare or a clear image of the light source and an increase in the lightness value [29, 31, 35].Consequently, the surface roughness changes the amount and angle of reflected light, creating a change in the color appearance that is different from the change associated with a smooth surface [35]. All of above reasons underscore the clinical necessity of standardizing the moisture state during shade measurement to prevent significant and unpredictable color errors.

Second, the type of shade guide affects the ΔE00 of 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia. While moisture is a critical factor, its effect cannot be interpreted in isolation. The most important finding of this study is how the choice of shade-taking device dictates the complexity of the interactions among all variables. When using the ZSG, a material-specific device, the system exhibits a manageable level of complexity. The three-way interaction was not significant (p =.517), allowing a more straightforward interpretation of the significant two-way interactions. For instance, the Color × Condition interaction (ηp² = 0.379) indicates that while the effect of moisture varies across different zirconia shades, this variation follows a consistent pattern irrespective of specimen thickness. This allows the formulation of limited, yet reliable, clinical protocols within the context of this specific measurement system. Conversely, when using the VSG, the complexity of the system increases dramatically. The emergence of a highly significant three-way interaction (Color × Thickness × Condition; p =.001, ηp² = 0.179) reveals a more intricate reality [28]. This finding demonstrates that the effect of any single variable is conditional on the specific levels of the other two. For example, the influence of moisture on color A1 is dependent on its thickness, and this relationship is entirely different for color A2. This invalidates any universal clinical guideline, as the optimal measurement protocol becomes specific to each unique combination of color, thickness, and condition [12, 14]. Furthermore, the mean ΔE002 values were nine times higher than the mean ΔE001 values (Fig. 2), while the mean ΔL values of VSG were higher than those of ZSG (Figs. 3 and 4). Similarly, Tabatabaian F et al. reported that the mean ΔE00values of the monolithic multilayer zirconia using the VSG as a control were three times higher than those using the monolithic multilayer zirconia as a control [31].This may be because the VSGs are made of dental feldspathic porcelain with optical properties different from those of zirconia (ZSG and zirconia specimens in the experimental groups) [5, 31]. In addition, the preshaded monolithic multilayer zirconia blanks are manufactured by a pressing technique with different pigment compositions in each increment, thereby causing inhomogeneous optical properties and color differences in each layer but no significant difference in translucency [5, 6, 36]. However, color matching with the VSG is difficult to achieve. Furthermore, this technique has been reported to cause interfacial defects, such as impurities and/or inadequate sintered areas, and consequently can alter and cause inhomogeneous optical properties and decrease the bulk strength of the material [5, 16, 36].

Therefore, predictable and good esthetic outcomes in implant-supported 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia prostheses depend not on a single factor but on the rigorous application of a specific protocol based on the unique combination of zirconia color, thickness, intended moisture state, and shade guide.

According to the VSG, the three most frequently used natural colors for the maxillary central incisor are A1, A2, and A3 [37]. A 1.5-mm thickness of the implant-supported high translucency monolithic multilayer zirconia restoration was suggested as a minimum thickness for esthetics [31, 38]. The color differences of ceramic over yellowish (50 V) anodization titanium are more efficient to improve the grayish appearance of titanium than that of 40 V, 70 V anodization titanium and zirconia when using no more than 1.5 mm-ceramic thickness [22]. Therefore, the results of this study may be clinically significant. This practical protocol can assist in improving the esthetics of implant-supported high translucency monolithic multilayer zirconia restorations. In conclusion, the use of a 1.5-mm-thick, A1–A3 shade 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia over yellow-anodized titanium implant abutment potentially results in acceptable color matching with 95% CIs using a combination of wet condition and ZSG. A limitation of this study is that the results were derived from only single operator using a single spectrophotometer, only one implant abutment material (titanium), only one shade of anodized titanium (50 V), only three shades (A1, A2, and A3) and one monolithic multilayer product. However, the results provide valuable insights for future research.

Conclusions

Within the limitations of this current in vitro study, zirconia color, thickness, and surface moisture condition affected the color differences of implant-supported 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia restorations using both zirconia and VITA shade guides. The color matching of implant-supported 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia restorations is sensitive to the surface moisture levels. Therefore, it must be considered during shade selection. The color match of implant-supported 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia restorations is sensitive to the type of shade guide used. Therefore, instead of the universal VITA shade guide, the zirconia shade guide made of original material should be used during shade selection in the use of a 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia over yellow-anodized titanium implant abutment. The use of a 1.5-mm-thick, A1–A3 shade 4Y-TZP monolithic multilayer zirconia over yellow-anodized titanium implant abutment potentially results in acceptable color matching with 95% CIs using a combination of wet condition and zirconia shade guide.

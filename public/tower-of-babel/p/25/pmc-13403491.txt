Impact of Germline BRCA Status on Pathologic Complete Response (pCR) and Outcomes in Early TNBC Treated with Pembrolizumab-Based Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy (KEYNOTE-522 Regimen): A Real-World Study
Authors: Mohammad Aldawoud, Fatima Faqihi, Najd Algazlan, Marwa Alharbi, Abdullah Almazyad, Abdulaziz Altamimi, Mojahed Rudaini, Abdullah Alwohaibi, Besher Alghazi, Abdulrahman Alturki, Hatoon Bakhribah, Abdullah Altwairgi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionPembrolizumab-based neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy following the KEYNOTE-522 regimen (NCT03036488) is a standard treatment approach for stage II and III triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). Real-world evidence on the impact of germline BRCA status on pathologic complete response (pCR) and survival outcomes remains limited.MethodsWe performed a retrospective single-center cohort study of patients with stage II–III TNBC treated with a KEYNOTE-522 regimen between January 2021 and December 2023. Only patients with documented germline BRCA status were included. The primary end point was pCR rate, defined as achievement of ypT0/is ypN0. Secondary end points included treatment delivery (completion and early discontinuation), postneoadjuvant systemic therapy patterns, recurrence-free survival (RFS), and overall survival (OS). Categorical variables were compared using Fisher’s exact test, and survival outcomes were estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods and compared with log-rank tests.ResultsAmong 1004 patients with breast cancer screened between 2021 and 2023, 125 had TNBC. After exclusion of patients who did not receive a KEYNOTE-522 regimen, had metastatic disease, underwent definitive surgery before neoadjuvant systemic therapy, had unretrievable BRCA status, or had other missing key eligibility data, 54 patients were eligible for analysis. The final cohort included 11 patients who were BRCA+ and 43 who were BRCA−. The BRCA+ patients were younger than BRCA− patients (median 38 vs 46 years; p = 0.018). pCR was evaluable in 52 patients and was achieved in 31 of 52 patients (60%) overall; pCR was numerically higher in BRCA+ vs BRCA− patients (73% [8 of 11] vs 56% [23 of 41]; p = 0.49). Median follow-up was 25 months. Two-year RFS was 100% in BRCA+ and 75.4% in BRCA− patients (log-rank p = 0.068), whereas 2-year OS was 100% and 88%, respectively (log-rank p = 0.272). Within the BRCA+ subgroup, the prognostic significance of pCR could not be assessed because no recurrence or death events were observed during follow-up. Within the BRCA− subgroup, pCR strongly stratified prognosis, with 2-year RFS of 100% in patients achieving pCR vs 45.5% in those without pCR (log-rank p = 0.00004), and 2-year OS of 100% vs 72.7%, respectively (log-rank p = 0.018).ConclusionsGermline BRCA positivity was associated with a numerically higher pCR rate, but differences in pCR and survival by BRCA status were not statistically significant. Given the limited sample size and low event counts, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating. Recurrence events clustered in BRCA− patients and were driven predominantly by those without pCR, supporting further study of optimized postneoadjuvant escalation strategies for residual BRCA− disease.

INTRODUCTION

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is an aggressive breast cancer subtype associated with a high risk of early relapse. In patients with stage II–III disease, neoadjuvant systemic therapy is widely used to improve operability and provides an in vivo assessment of treatment response. Pathologic complete response (pCR) is a clinically meaningful end point in TNBC and is consistently associated with favorable long-term outcomes.[1]

Pembrolizumab combined with neoadjuvant chemotherapy and continued postoperatively as part of the KEYNOTE-522 regimen has become a perioperative standard for high-risk early TNBC, demonstrating improvements in pCR and event-free survival in randomized clinical trial settings.[2,3] As this regimen is increasingly adopted in routine practice, real-world evaluation is important to confirm outcomes and to better characterize response patterns in clinically relevant subgroups.

Germline BRCA1/2 pathogenic variants result in homologous recombination deficiency and may influence chemosensitivity, tumor biology, and patterns of relapse. In addition, BRCA status directly informs postneoadjuvant treatment selection through eligibility for poly(adenosine diphosphate-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitor therapy in high-risk patients with residual disease and capecitabine use in residual TNBC.[4,5] Despite this clinical relevance, the prognostic and predictive impact of germline BRCA status in early TNBC treated with pembrolizumab-based neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy remains incompletely defined outside clinical trials.

Several prior studies support a potential relationship between BRCA deficiency and immunotherapy responsiveness in TNBC. In advanced or metastatic TNBC, the TOPACIO/KEYNOTE-162 study suggested antitumor activity for niraparib plus pembrolizumab, with numerically higher response rates reported in patients with BRCA-mutated disease.[6] In the MEDIOLA phase 1/2 basket study, olaparib plus durvalumab also demonstrated clinical activity in germline BRCA-mutated metastatic breast cancer.[7] More recently, a multicenter real-world neoadjuvant pembrolizumab-based cohort reported a trend toward higher pCR among BRCA1/2-mutated compared with wild-type TNBC.[8] These data support the biological plausibility of enhanced immune sensitivity in BRCA-deficient disease, but BRCA-stratified outcome data in the pembrolizumab-based neoadjuvant setting remain limited, providing a rationale for the present study.[6–8]

We therefore conducted a single-center, real-world study to evaluate the association between germline BRCA status and pCR among patients with stage II–III TNBC treated with a KEYNOTE-522 regimen. Secondary objectives included treatment delivery, postneoadjuvant systemic therapy patterns, and survival outcomes, with exploratory analyses assessing the prognostic impact of pCR within BRCA-defined subgroups.

METHODS

Study Design

This was a retrospective, single-center, real-world cohort study. The study was approved by the institutional review board (IRB log number: 25–572) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Given the retrospective design and use of existing medical records, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the IRB. The manuscript was prepared in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guideline.[9]

Study Population

Consecutive patients diagnosed with stage II or III TNBC and treated with pembrolizumab-based neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (aligned with the KEYNOTE-522 regimen) between January 2021 and December 2023 were screened. TNBC was defined by estrogen receptor (ER) and progesterone receptor (PR) expression less than 1% in accordance with American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) and College of American Pathologists (CAP) recommendations.[10] HER2 negativity was defined using ASCO and CAP HER2 testing guidance (immunohistochemistry [IHC] 0–1 + or IHC 2 + with negative in situ hybridization).[11] Clinical staging followed institutional standards aligned with the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) 8th edition framework.[12] Only patients with documented germline BRCA1/2 status were included in the analysis. Patients with de novo metastatic disease, definitive surgery before neoadjuvant systemic therapy, non–KEYNOTE-522 neoadjuvant regimens, unretrievable BRCA status in the electronic medical record (n = 2), or missing other key clinical/pathologic data (n = 14) were excluded (Fig. 1).

Data Collection

Clinical, pathologic, treatment, and outcome variables were abstracted from the electronic medical record using a standardized data collection form. Treatment completion and early discontinuation were captured and categorized by reason (toxicity vs progression) when available.

Germline BRCA Testing

Germline BRCA1/2 testing was performed on peripheral blood samples in a single accredited molecular laboratory using next-generation sequencing (NGS). Reflex multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA) for large genomic rearrangements was not routinely performed after negative initial testing. BRCA results were abstracted from the electronic medical record.

Treatment Exposure

Neoadjuvant treatment followed institutional practice consistent with the KEYNOTE-522 regimen and included pembrolizumab combined with taxane- and platinum-based chemotherapy followed by anthracycline-based chemotherapy, followed by definitive surgery.[2,3] Postneoadjuvant management, including continuation of pembrolizumab and escalation with capecitabine and/or olaparib, was individualized according to residual disease and BRCA status, consistent with available evidence supporting escalation in residual TNBC and the role of olaparib in high-risk germline BRCA-mutated disease.[4,5]

End Points and Definitions

The primary end point was pCR rate, defined as achievement of ypT0/is ypN0. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was defined as the time from definitive surgery to first documented recurrence or censoring at last follow-up. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from definitive surgery to death from any cause or censoring at last follow-up.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were summarized as median (range) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were summarized as counts (percentages) and compared using Fisher’s exact test or χ2 test as appropriate. RFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and compared using log-rank testing.[13–15] Two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Exploratory multivariable modeling for pCR was performed using logistic regression. Because recurrence and death events were absent in the BRCA+ group and in patients achieving pCR, multivariable Cox models including BRCA status and/or pCR were not estimable due to complete separation. Therefore, an exploratory multivariable Cox model for RFS was performed within the BRCA− cohort, adjusting for age, clinical T stage, and nodal status. Where feasible, effect sizes were reported as odds ratios or hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).

RESULTS

Among 1004 patients with breast cancer screened between 2021 and 2023, 125 had TNBC. Documented germline BRCA status was retrievable for 123 of 125 patients with TNBC (98.4%). After exclusion of patients who did not receive a KEYNOTE-522 regimen, had de novo metastatic disease or underwent definitive surgery before neoadjuvant systemic therapy, had unretrievable BRCA status in the electronic medical record (n = 2), or had missing other key clinical/pathologic data (n = 14), 54 patients remained eligible for analysis (Fig. 1). The final cohort included 11 BRCA+ patients (20%) and 43 BRCA− patients (80%). Median follow-up was 25 months.

Baseline Characteristics

BRCA+ patients were younger than BRCA− patients (median 38 vs 46 years, p = 0.018). Other baseline clinicopathologic features were broadly comparable, including grade 3 frequency and Ki-67 distribution. HER2-0 was more frequent in BRCA+ patients, whereas HER2-low was more frequent in BRCA− patients, although these differences were not statistically significant (Table 1). Clinical presentation suggested a trend toward greater disease burden in BRCA− patients, including higher frequencies of cT ≥ 3 and stage III disease, without statistically significant differences (Table 2).

Pathologic Complete Response

pCR was evaluable in 52 patients and achieved in 31 of 52 patients (60%) overall. pCR was numerically higher in BRCA+ than in BRCA− patients (73% [8 of 11] vs 56% [23 of 41]), but this difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.49; Fig. 2). The unadjusted odds ratio for achieving pCR in BRCA+ vs BRCA− patients was 2.09 (95% CI, 0.48–9.02).

Treatment Completion and Early Discontinuation

Treatment completion rates were similar across BRCA groups. Early discontinuation occurred in 27% of BRCA+ patients and 23% of BRCA− patients and was driven primarily by toxicity. In the BRCA+ group, toxicity-related discontinuations included immune-related hepatitis, hypophysitis requiring pembrolizumab discontinuation, and paclitaxel hypersensitivity. In the BRCA− group, toxicity-related discontinuations included colitis, pneumonitis, cardiotoxicity, grade 3 or 4 neuropathy, and sepsis and bone marrow suppression. Progression-related discontinuation occurred only in BRCA− patients. No formal analysis was performed to assess the association between BRCA status and immunotherapy-related toxicity because discontinuation events were infrequent and toxicity attribution was heterogeneous in this retrospective cohort.

Postneoadjuvant Systemic Therapy

Postneoadjuvant management reflected individualized escalation based on residual disease and BRCA status. Pembrolizumab continuation was common in both groups. Among patients with residual disease, all three BRCA+ patients received olaparib (3 of 3, 100%), whereas 10 of 18 BRCA− patients received capecitabine (10 of 18, 55.6%). Some BRCA− patients with residual disease did not receive capecitabine because of early relapse before treatment initiation. This should be considered when interpreting subgroup RFS and OS outcomes.

Survival Outcomes by BRCA Status

Recurrence events were concentrated in BRCA− patients; no recurrences or deaths were observed in the BRCA+ group during follow-up. Two-year RFS was 100% in BRCA+ patients and 75.4% in BRCA− patients (log-rank p = 0.068; Fig. 3A). Two-year OS was 100% in BRCA+ patients and 88% in BRCA− patients (log-rank p = 0.272; Fig. 3B). Hazard ratios for BRCA status were not estimable because no recurrence or death events occurred in the BRCA+ group.

Prognostic Impact of pCR Within BRCA− Cohort

Within the BRCA− cohort, pCR strongly stratified outcomes. BRCA− patients achieving pCR had no recurrence events and a 2-year RFS of 100%, whereas those without pCR accounted for nearly all recurrence events, with a 2-year RFS of 45.5% (log-rank p = 0.00004; Fig. 4A). OS was also separated by pCR within BRCA−, with a 2-year OS of 100% in the pCR group and 72.7% in the no-pCR group (log-rank p = 0.018; Fig. 4B). Exploratory Multivariable Analysis

In the BRCA− cohort (n = 41; 11 RFS events), exploratory multivariable Cox regression adjusting for age, clinical T stage, and nodal status showed that clinical T ≥ 3 was associated with a numerically higher hazard of recurrence (HR 3.99, 95% CI, 0.89–17.95; p = 0.071). Age (HR 1.01 per year, 95% CI, 0.94–1.09; p = 0.761) and clinical nodal positivity (HR 1.60, 95% CI, 0.35–7.23; p = 0.542) were not significantly associated with recurrence. Cox models, including BRCA status and/or pCR, were not estimable because no events occurred in the BRCA+ and pCR strata.

DISCUSSION

In this single-center, real-world cohort study of patients with stage II and III TNBC treated with a KEYNOTE-522 regimen, germline BRCA positivity was associated with a numerically higher pCR rate than BRCA− disease, although the difference was not statistically significant. The most clinically relevant observation was that recurrence events clustered in the BRCA− population and were driven predominantly by patients who did not achieve pCR. In contrast, patients achieving pCR—regardless of BRCA status—demonstrated excellent short-term outcomes, reinforcing pCR as a powerful prognostic marker in early TNBC.[16] Given the small BRCA+ subgroup and limited number of recurrence and death events, comparisons of pCR and survival outcomes by BRCA status should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

Our findings are consistent with KEYNOTE-522, which demonstrated improved pCR and event-free survival with perioperative pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy in high-risk early TNBC.[2,3] Although KEYNOTE-522 established broad benefit across the overall population, whether germline BRCA status modifies response and long-term outcomes in the pembrolizumab era remains incompletely defined, particularly outside clinical trials. In our cohort, BRCA+ patients were younger and had numerically higher pCR, but subgroup comparisons were limited by the small sample size and low event counts. Accordingly, this study is at risk of a type II error, and numerical differences in pCR and survival outcomes between BRCA subgroups should be interpreted cautiously. In addition, BRCA− patients showed a trend toward greater baseline disease burden, including higher proportions of cT ≥ 3 and stage III disease, which may have contributed to the less favorable outcomes observed in this group.

A central implication of this analysis is the importance of optimizing postneoadjuvant escalation strategies for patients with residual disease, particularly among BRCA− patients, in whom early recurrences were concentrated. This aligns with evidence supporting capecitabine escalation in HER2-negative disease with residual invasive cancer after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, as demonstrated in CREATE-X.[5] Similarly, OlympiA established adjuvant olaparib as a standard escalation strategy in high-risk germline BRCA-mutated early breast cancer, improving invasive disease-free survival and OS.[4] In our cohort, postneoadjuvant treatment patterns reflected these principles: capecitabine was predominantly used in BRCA− patients, whereas PARP inhibitor use occurred only in BRCA+ patients with residual disease.

Within the BRCA− cohort, the prognostic impact of pCR was particularly striking, with pCR associated with no recurrence events and substantially higher landmark RFS and OS compared to those without pCR. This supports a response-adapted treatment paradigm in TNBC, in which residual disease identifies a biologically high-risk subgroup that may benefit from intensified and/or novel postneoadjuvant strategies beyond standard escalation. Residual cancer burden and related postneoadjuvant metrics may further refine risk stratification beyond pCR alone.[17]

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective, single-center design introduces potential selection and ascertainment biases, and postneoadjuvant treatment decisions were not randomized. Second, the cohort size—particularly the BRCA+ subgroup—was modest, and event numbers were limited, reducing statistical power, increasing the risk of a type II error, and limiting the feasibility of multivariable time-to-event modeling across the full cohort. Specifically, Cox models, including BRCA status and/or pCR, were not estimable because no events occurred in the BRCA+ and pCR strata, necessitating an exploratory multivariable Cox analysis restricted to the BRCA− cohort with clinical covariates only. Third, follow-up was relatively short (median 25 months), and longer follow-up is needed to better characterize recurrence patterns and survival outcomes, particularly in the BRCA+ subgroup, in which no recurrence or death events were observed during the current follow-up period. Finally, residual confounding may persist because important variables such as PD-L1 status, residual cancer burden class, and detailed immune-related adverse events were not uniformly available. PD-L1 status may influence sensitivity to pembrolizumab-based therapy and could therefore affect interpretation of pCR and survival differences, whereas residual cancer burden may provide more granular prognostic information among patients with residual disease beyond pCR alone.[2,3,17]

Despite these limitations, the study has important strengths, including real-world capture of KEYNOTE-522 implementation, documented germline BRCA testing, and clinically relevant subgroup analyses highlighting where relapse risk concentrates in routine practice. These findings complement clinical trial data and support a pragmatic risk-based framework for the management of early TNBC.

CONCLUSIONS

In this real-world cohort of stage II–III TNBC treated with a KEYNOTE-522 regimen, germline BRCA positivity was associated with a numerically higher pCR rate, although the difference was not statistically significant. Recurrence events clustered in BRCA− patients and were driven predominantly by those with residual disease (no pCR), whereas patients achieving pCR had excellent RFS and OS regardless of BRCA status. Given the limited sample size and low event counts, these findings should be considered hypothesis-generating. These findings support further evaluation of risk-adapted postneoadjuvant management, particularly for BRCA− patients with residual disease.

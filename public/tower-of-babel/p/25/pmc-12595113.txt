Enhancing green hydrogen forecasting with a spatio-temporal graph convolutional network optimized by the Ninja algorithm
Authors: Mona Ahmed Yassen, Amel Ali Alhussan, Mohamed Gamal Abdel-Fattah, Islam Ismael, Hossam El.Deen Salah Mostafa, El-Sayed M. El-kenawy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In light of increased international efforts to combat climate change, sustainable infrastructure is shifting toward green hydrogen produced through renewable-powered electrolysis. Still, it is challenging to forecast the production of green hydrogen because environmental and system factors are variable both in time and space. We introduce a new system that utilizes a Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (STGCN) and a novel algorithm, the Ninja Optimization Algorithm (NiOA), to address this issue. Using the framework, binary NiOA performs feature selection, while continuous NiOA optimizes both the model architecture and the number of variables in the data simultaneously. It is clear from the research that forecasting results have shown significant improvement. The STGCN model achieved an R2 of 0.8769 and an MSE of 0.00375, whereas the STGCN with NiOA reached an R2 of 0.9815 and an MSE of only \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7.48 \times 10^{-8}$$\end{document}. Due to these improvements, adaptive metaheuristics show even greater promise in delivering more accurate forecasting and reduced computational requirements for addressing critical environmental issues. The suggested strategy can be followed repeatedly, providing a solid framework for the effective modeling of renewable energy systems and making green hydrogen projects more dependable.

Introduction

The need to reduce climate change driven by humans has led the world’s energy sector to make significant shifts toward eliminating emissions1. Among all the options for energy transition, renewable hydrogen, also known as green hydrogen, plays an important role, as it is highly versatile for storing and transporting renewable energy2,3. The method of producing green hydrogen involves running water through an electrolysis process powered by renewable energy sources, such as solar and wind4. In contrast to gray or blue hydrogen, which have greenhouse gas emissions because they are derived from fossil fuels, green hydrogen does not produce any additional carbon pollution for industrial, household, and transportation energy needs5. The role of hydrogen is crucial in managing the grid, reducing emissions in hard-to-reach sectors, and promoting sustainable energy systems worldwide6. With increasing global attention to green hydrogen technology, accurately forecasting the amount of hydrogen that will be produced is becoming crucial7 to inform policy decisions, assess its economic benefits, and guide its development8. It matters most when areas are geospatially different, as solar irradiance and wind strength in those regions can highly determine the production of electricity. For this reason, models that handle and study many types of data from environmental and technological sources are essential. They are needed to identify promising areas for hydrogen production, establish optimal supply routes, make informed decisions about building infrastructure, and maintain production in line with expected demand9. In this situation, deep-learning-based modeling approaches offer considerable potential. Since they are meant to uncover nonlinear relationships, deep learning models are effective at handling the complex spatiotemporal patterns found in renewable energy systems.10 Still, applying DL for forecasting hydrogen production encounters several problems. The fact that input datasets encompass a wide range of environmental, technical, and geographic features makes them challenging to process. Often, these types of data include redundant or irrelevant features that are not helpful, slow down the learning process, and increase the risk of overfitting the model. Additionally, it is essential to employ effective strategies to tune hyperparameters if a model is to be successful in various distinct locations. Finding the proper hyperparameters is a data-intensive and vulnerable task because even slight differences in the data can significantly impact the results. Therefore, it is essential to adopt methods for selecting features that can identify only the useful ones and discard the less meaningful or repetitive ones11. This is why metaheuristic algorithms serve as a helpful answer12. Optimization algorithms, such as swarm intelligence and evolutionary strategies, simulate natural processes and have been proven to be effective in finding the best solution among a vast number of possible answers13. This research aims to develop, launch, and evaluate a set of innovative deep-learning models for forecasting green hydrogen production across a large geographical area. It uses a set of DL models that are the spatio-temporal Graph Convolutional Network (STGCN), Dual-Transformer Convolutional Network (DTCN), Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), Deep Kernel Learning Transformer (DKLT), Long Short-Term Memory network (LSTM), Convolutional Temporal Sequence Model (CTSM), and Feature-Time Learning Model (FTLM). Every model is measured by the results it achieves when handling real-world high-dimensional data. Feature selection in this study is accomplished using several binary metaheuristic algorithms, such as the binary Firefly Algorithm14 and binary Particle Swarm Optimization15, among others, as reported in the accompanying abbreviation document. Algorithms are tested against each other to evaluate their effectiveness in reducing dimensions, maintaining or enhancing prediction accuracy, and improving the model’s speed and efficiency. Additionally, this research encompasses bNinja, a new optimizer with proven benefits and a detailed description of its underlying concepts. Literature review

Produced through electrolysis using renewable energy, green hydrogen (GH) is being viewed as a crucial means of decarbonization in energy systems worldwide. Since renewable sources of energy, such as wind and solar, are not continuous, accurate forecasting is crucial for stable and scalable hydrogen production. Machine learning (ML) models have been extensively used to enhance the accuracy of predictions. For instance, CEEMDAN-BiDLSTM was applied in India to predict GHI and construct hydrogen potential maps with an accuracy of up to 2.414 months16. In contrast, Prophet modeling in Islamabad achieved a mere 3.7% MAPE in predicting hydrogen production from PV-E systems17. The use of wind in forecasting hydrogen has also been successful with LSTM, SVR, and linear regression models, with LSTM being the most accurate in suburban areas18. When a hybrid deep learning model was applied to nine Turkish provinces, the lowest values of RMSE, MAPE, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2$$\end{document} were 0.600, 0.100, and 0.990, respectively, in Izmir19. In a solar-to-hydrogen production system, forecasting models were compared in a study where GaAsPV panels and PEM electrolyzers were used, and it was concluded that deep learning (DL) produced the best results in comparison to the other models - achieving an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2$$\end{document} of 96.26% in predicting solar radiation and hydrogen production20. Independently, a model called Optimized Hydrogen Generation-based Regression (OHGR) for fuel cells has been developed using a stacked neural network, PCA, and stochastic optimization. It achieved an RMSE of 0.220 and an \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2$$\end{document} of 0.564, with interpretation strongly supported by SHAP values. Therefore, it can be effectively used in sustainable hydrogen production, even in the presence of fossil fuels21. The production of hydrogen from biomass, which is associated with problems of low efficiency and instability, has been addressed through a hybrid DGM-GBRT forecasting model. Such a two-fold approach combines gradient-boosting predictive power with smoothing grey modeling. It was able to decrease the forecast anomalies and provided a mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.2, which outperformed five standard benchmark models22. The optimization is equally instrumental in improving the efficiency of hydrogen production. Multi-objective optimization systems have been proposed to trade off system cost and productivity. One study aimed to determine the optimum size of hydrogen systems based on solar energy in the United States, China, Australia, and Korea. It was found that the optimal electrolyzer capacity is approximately 60% of the solar power capacity to achieve the minimum levelized cost of hydrogen (LCOH) and enhance the hydrogen yield through battery storage, but not economic feasibility23. The application of advanced machine learning models to process optimization is also demonstrated in research on solid oxide electrolysis cells (SOECs). To design an optimal hydrogen output at different feed conditions and cell configurations, a high-accuracy XGBoost regression model was constructed and then combined with a genetic algorithm. The features found to be most influential by Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) were effective area, voltage, and temperature. The model gave \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$R^2 > 0.95$$\end{document} in three output parameters and established that an optimal current of 1.61 A/cm2 gave a hydrogen yield of 1.174 L/(h cm2)24. In a marine application, ANFIS combined with the Pelican Optimization Algorithm (POA) was demonstrated to enhance the performance of hydrogen production using solar-powered electrocatalysis. The hybrid method improved hydrogen yield by 1.31.7% compared to the measured dataset and Response Surface Methodology (RSM)25. ANN and ANN-hybrid models have also been widely used in hydrogen production technologies, such as electrolysis, biomass conversion, and photocatalysis, and have proven to be better than conventional statistics-based models in modeling nonlinear system behaviors26. The scalability of GH technologies is considerably determined by economic feasibility. A decrease in the cost of the main elements of the hydrogen value chain was predicted by a multicomponent learning curve analysis. PEM and AE electrolyzer learning rates were determined to be 4%, which was lower than solar (14.2814.44%) and wind (7.359.63%) power. The LCOH, assuming various factors and regional considerations, was estimated to decrease by 4-10.2%, with the implication that government subsidies and (R&D)assistance stimulate market uptake27. SOE systems are expected to decrease the levelized cost of hydrogen (LCOH) from 6 to 9.34/kg to as low as 1.9/kg by 205028. PEM and AWE systems were also compared, where wind-based PEM had LCOH values of 5.3 kg and 40.0 kg at different rated wind speeds28. A companion paper used Wright’s Law Monte Carlo simulation to assess the market share of hydrogen production out to 2050. Biomass gasifiers are expected to remain dominant in initial market installations. Still, by mid-century, electrolyzers, with their higher learning rates and declining costs of renewable electricity, are likely to become the dominant new deployment. In this modeling framework, uncertainties also existed in project bidding, technology performance, and national growth rates29. A bi-level optimization model was employed from a hydropower perspective to compare the economic feasibility of wholesale electricity trade and hydrogen production via run-of-river systems in Central Western Europe. The findings showed that GH production would only be profitable with a price on CO2 above 245 euros per ton when the electricity price is competitive (45 euros per MWh)30. Sustainability, particularly in relation to the environment, has also been closely examined. A life cycle assessment of PEM water electrolysis technologies revealed that by 2050, a 96% decrease in critical raw material (CRM) intensity and up to a 22.5% decrease in electricity demand can be achieved through learning curve extrapolation. The total of these improvements can cut climate change by 16.528.5 percent31. Deployment strategies are further based on regional evaluations. In Turkey, spatial data on solar radiation, land use, and electrolyzer settings were developed in a detailed hydrogen atlas. The national GH production was estimated to be between 415.48 and 427.22 million tons, with Erzurum, Konya, Sivas, and Van having the highest production potential32. In the case of China, the system dynamics (SD) model, in conjunction with Lotka-Volterra (LV) competition equations, was applied to project the demand for hydrogen in key industrial sectors up to the year 2030 under various policy scenarios. Such a method provided a detailed look at infrastructure demands and policy compromises33. Lastly, new sources of energy are emerging in GH studies. One investigation examined the generation of hydrogen through ocean wave energy using FLOW-3D computational simulations and experiments. Modeling revealed that the yield of hydrogen was directly proportional to the power generated, advocating wave-powered hydrogen converters as innovative grid components that can be implemented in coastal areas34. In brief, the literature shows a cross-sectoral perspective on the development of green hydrogen technologies. Predictability is enhanced through machine learning and forecasting, efficiency is improved through optimization, cost pathways are identified through economic modeling, and national strategies are developed through policy simulations. Taken together, these initiatives demonstrate the importance of green hydrogen in low-carbon energy systems. Materials and methods

To make the modeling of the hydrogen data systematic and reproducible, an end-to-end pipeline was created and is summarized graphically in Fig. 1. Joining together a set of key steps, namely raw data preprocessing to the final model evaluation, the workflow exploits classic and contemporary optimization procedures. To perform the procedure, one must first obtain a dataset that includes the environmental and operational parameters of hydrogen systems. The raw data is then subjected to a significant data preprocessing phase, during which vital quality aspects are detected and handled. The step will consist of processing steps like missing value processing, normalization, feature scaling, outlier processing, and categorical encoding. These transformations are necessary to ensure the data is ready for robust and unbiased learning.Fig. 1Comprehensive pipeline for hydrogen data modeling, incorporating data preprocessing, dataset splitting, metaheuristic-based feature selection, model training with baseline architectures, and final performance evaluation.

Comprehensive pipeline for hydrogen data modeling, incorporating data preprocessing, dataset splitting, metaheuristic-based feature selection, model training with baseline architectures, and final performance evaluation.

After preprocessing is complete, the data is divided into training and testing sets in an 80:20 ratio. Model development and hyperparameter tuning are performed on the training data, and the testing data is kept aside until the final evaluation to avoid biased performance estimation. To efficiently learn and reduce dimensionality, the feature selection stage is essential. It is carried out using a variety of metaheuristic optimization algorithms, namely bNinja, bFA, bTSH, bPSO, bJAYA, bFEP, bGSA, bQIO, bWOA, and bAPO. All these algorithms are coordinated by the central Ninja Optimizer module that acts as an intelligent search through the search space to find the most informative feature subsets. After feature selection, a set of baseline models, consisting of STGCN, DTCN, TCN, DKLT, LSTM, CTSM, and FTLM, is fitted on the low-dimensional feature space. The models represent a range of different deep learning architectures and temporal learning paradigms, enabling highly competitive benchmarking. Finally, the performance evaluation block will compute several performance measures used to assess the predictive accuracy and generalizability of each model. The reason this is done is to validate the whole working process quantitatively, from data ingestion to prediction.

Dataset description

This work is based on the Renewable Hydrogen Production Dataset, a multidimensional and comprehensive dataset of environmental, technological, and geographical variables spanning 2535 cities worldwide. The dataset is explicitly tailored to this end, modeling and analyzing the potential for producing green hydrogen across a wide variety of urban and regional geographies, with a strong emphasis on sustainability and environmental applicability. It has been selected, based on its scope and scientific usefulness, to provide an empirical basis for the development of intelligent and resource-conscious hydrogen infrastructure in international decarbonization plans. In the dataset, each data record comprises crucial indicators of renewable energy potential, such as solar irradiance and wind speed, as well as extensive system characteristics, including photovoltaic (PV) and wind power generation, electrolyzer efficiency, and overall system efficiency. Such a combination of features facilitates fine-grained spatial modeling, making the dataset particularly interesting to those seeking to understand where green hydrogen is most promising to deploy. The resource-rich and coastal areas will gain enormously, as even the desalination power demand has been factored in to enable a realistic assessment of water resource limitation in hydrogen electrolysis. Moreover, because the dataset includes environmental factors and operational indicators in equal measure, the data become useful in integrative analyses used to guide ecological planning, investment priorities, and policy-making. To enhance clarity and guided analysis, the essential characteristics of the dataset are summarized in Table 2, with each feature defined by its functional usefulness in predicting hydrogen production. Remarkably, environmental conditions, which include solar irradiance (in kWh/m2/day), wind speed (in m/s), and ambient temperature (in °C), are necessary for calculating the renewable energy yield and the system efficiency. In the meantime, such technological characteristics as the PV and wind power generation (in kW), desalination power demand (in kW), and electrolyzer efficiency (in %) provide an idea of the engineering performance of a hydrogen production process. The geospatial identifiers (Latitude, longitude, etc.) support these variables and allow spatial interpolation and regional optimization modeling. The combination of the mentioned dimensions makes the dataset a key resource for modeling hydrogen production based on environmental foundations. To facilitate the intensive machine learning analysis, the dataset is divided into two disjoint subsets: a training set to fit the model parameters and extract patterns, and a test set to assess the model’s generalization performance solely. This layered separation yields predictive results that are statistically sound and environmentally explainable, reducing overfitting and algorithm bias in empirical assessments.

Data preprocessing

Preprocessing is one of the essential steps in preparing raw data for use in further modeling, especially in the case of deep learning-based environmental forecasting35. The scale of the renewable hydrogen production dataset, which features a wide range of continuous and potentially correlated environmental and operational variables, will necessitate a systematic and stepwise preprocessing pipeline designed to enhance data quality, remove noise, and make the data amenable to interpretation by a model. The first step of this pipeline is data cleaning and imputation. Actual environmental data often fail to comply with gaps or inconsistencies in the recordings due to sensor malfunctions, data recording errors, or the inherent variability in the measurement rate. To address missing values, statistical imputation techniques are employed. In particular, for numerical attributes (e.g., ambient temperature, solar irradiance, and renewable power outputs), missing values are imputed using central tendency estimators (most typically the mean or median) selected according to the underlying distribution and the presence of outliers. In cases of categorical features, they will be encoded using One-Hot Encoding (OHE), thereby converting nominal variables into a binary representation without imposing an ordinal structure on the data that could generate spurious model behavior. After imputation, feature engineering and correlation analysis steps are performed to enhance the signal-to-noise ratio in the data. Strongly correlated (or redundant) features can obscure any actual causal structure and lead to multicollinearity, which can make model learning unstable, especially in neural models where the features are assumed to be independent. The features are checked to see if they have a linear dependency by calculating a Pearson correlation matrix. This procedure helps retain only the most informative features, which are not duplicated, resulting in more interpretable and simpler models. After the dimensional refinement, feature scaling is done to standardize the scale of all the continuous attributes. Since the units are diverse, for example, irradiance can be expressed in kilowatt-hours per square meter per day and hydrogen production in kilograms per day, the raw feature values can vary by different orders of magnitude. To reconcile such differences, the variables are Min-Max normalized to reshape them to a standard range, typically [0, 1]. Deep learning models

This study employs a diverse range of deep learning models, each with its distinct capabilities to learn from high-dimensional, sequential, and geospatially structured data, to identify the most effective model for representing the complex temporal and spatial patterns underlying green hydrogen production. Such models are especially suited to applications in environmental science, where accurate prediction requires not only the recognition of the dynamism of renewable energy resources but also the consideration of contextual effects from geographic and system-level factors.

The Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (STGCN) serves as one of the core elements of the modeling framework, as it has the potential to preserve both spatial correlations and temporal dependencies. STGCN learns the topology-based correlation among cities based on geographic and infrastructural similarity using graph convolutional layers. At the same time, gated temporal convolutional encoding enables it to capture sequential behaviors in hydrogen production due to diurnal patterns, seasonality, and variability in weather patterns. This architecture is instrumental in situations where the geometrical organization of data is a crucial factor, as is the case with decentralized hydrogen networks connecting multiple cities.

The other architecture used is the Dynamic Temporal Convolutional Network (DTCN) that improves temporal feature extraction with dynamic convolutional receptive fields. Such flexibility is essential for learning non-stationary time series, such as those observed in solar and wind energy inputs, where variability in both time and amplitude is typical.

The Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) is also added because it is efficient in handling long-range temporal dependencies. Through the use of dilated convolutions and residual connections, TCN can learn long temporal patterns and, by doing so, emphasizes the benefits of avoiding the vanishing gradient problem typically seen with recurrent models.

The Deep Kernel Learning for Time Series (DKLT) model integrates deep neural representations with kernel-based methods (e.g., Gaussian Processes) by incorporating probabilistic reasoning. This hybrid scheme not only allows for deterministic prediction but also accommodates the quantity of uncertainty, a mandatory attribute in environmental forecasting when incomplete or noisy data is present.

A Recurrent Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) network is applied due to its efficiency in modeling long-term temporal sequences. It features gated recurrent units that can effectively capture longstanding environmental rhythms, which explains why it aligns well with energy generation and hydrogen production data that tend to exhibit seasonal and periodic trends.

Along with this, the Continuous-Time Sequence Model (CTSM) presents a time-based modeling structure that supports unequally spaced data points, which are typical in realistic settings of environmental monitoring. CTSM offers a mathematically precise approach to model asynchronous data acquisition with no artifacts of temporal discretization.

The Federated Temporal Learning Model (FTLM) enables training a distributed model across multiple data silos, which is especially important in cases where data sovereignty and regional data protection are paramount. FTLM is also consistent with the new tenets of sustainable and ethical data science, providing a distributed strategy for learning models and ensuring the integrity of local data. The STGCN model has been identified as the most promising architecture among those targeting hyperparameter optimization. Its ability to learn both spatial and temporal dependencies jointly leads to its steady good performance at the baseline, which naturally presents a straightforward target for improvement. By utilizing enhanced optimization methodologies that incorporate metaheuristic search procedures, this model is optimized to achieve higher predictive and computational performance, thereby yielding more stable and high-performance forecasting of renewable hydrogen production in environmentally dynamic situations.

Metaheuristic algorithms

Optimization is a key aspect in the context of environmental modeling (especially in high-dimensional, data-driven applications, such as green hydrogen production forecasting) to improve accuracy and computational efficiency. Common approaches often struggle to deal with nonlinearities, heterogeneities, and the scale of such data. Metaheuristic algorithms are an attractive alternative, inspired by natural and physical phenomena, to carry out robust and adaptive searches on complex landscapes. Their random, though clever design enables them to efficiently manage tasks such as feature selection and hyperparameter tuning, which are essential to maximizing the performance of deep learning. This section discusses the strategic role of metaheuristics within the proposed modeling scheme, explaining their contributions to dimensionality reduction and parameter optimization. It also presents the new Ninja Optimization Algorithm (NiOA), a biologically inspired and exploration-exploitation balanced optimizer. It compares its performance to a collection of well-known benchmark algorithms in both binary and continuous search spaces.

Role in feature selection

The dimensionality reduction approach employed in this paper utilizes metaheuristic algorithms as a core element. Since a vast number of input variables are generated based on environmental, technical, and geographical factors, the feature space related to renewable hydrogen production forecasting is high-dimensional and perhaps redundant. Conventional deterministic algorithms can be inadequate for searching such a high-dimensional space, especially when the objective function is non-convex or contains numerous local optima. To the contrary, metaheuristics offer a random (stochastic) but informed mechanism of global search and optimization. Metaheuristic algorithms iteratively search the feature space, attempting to select the most informative subset of variables by analogy with natural processes, such as biological evolution, swarm behavior, or physical phenomena. The procedure enables the model to retain features with the most predictive relevance and discard features that introduce noise, redundancy, or computational overhead. By promoting the better generalization capacity of deep learning models, feature selection also has the beneficial effect of dramatically decreasing both training time and energy requirements, which are of paramount importance in sustainable computational modeling. Also, the dimension of the selected feature subset can be considered an advantage because it leads to better interpretability, especially meaningful in an environmental context where a clear indication of the contribution of variables is policy and planning-relevant.

Role in hyperparameter optimization

Besides using them in feature selection, metaheuristic algorithms are also employed for hyperparameter optimization. Particularly, deep learning models, such as those with intricate structures like the Spatio-Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (STGCN), are susceptible to hyperparameters, including but not limited to the learning rate, the number of layers, filter size, batch size, and dropout rate. These hyperparameters can be tuned manually, but the process can be laborious, time-consuming, and prone to failure in finding optimal settings, as the parameter space is high-dimensional and dependent. Metaheuristic optimization automates this procedure by searching the hyperparameter space adaptively with population-based methods. These algorithms evaluate the performance of models across various parameter settings and converge on those that yield better predictive accuracy and stability. Hyperparameter optimization in the case of the STGCN model is particularly highlighted in this study, as this model yielded the best results on the baseline. The model can be optimized concerning its architecture and training parameters using metaheuristic techniques, thereby yielding a significant improvement in its ability to generalize in spatially heterogeneous areas. Such an automated workflow not only speeds up the model development pipeline but also makes the deployment of computing resources more robust and environmentally friendly.

Empirical results

In this section, a rigorous analysis of the proposed hybrid optimization and deep learning model for green hydrogen prediction under varying spatiotemporal and environmental conditions will be presented. The experiment is designed around four significant phases of analysis: (1) a baseline of deep learning models on the entire dataset, (2) an exploration of the binary metaheuristic performance as feature selectors, (3) prediction accuracy with feature subsets of reduced size, and (4) a hyperparameter optimization with continuous metaheuristic optimizers. At every step, a whole stack of regression evaluation measures will be assumed, such as Mean Squared Error (MSE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Mean Bias Error (MBE), Pearson Correlation Coefficient (r), Coefficient of Determination (R2), Relative Root Mean Squared Error (RRMSE), Nash–Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE), and Willmott Index (WI). These measures are used to estimate the absolute and relative performances of the models, providing a versatile perspective on prediction accuracy, model bias, variance consistency, and environmental reliability.

Feature selection results

To reduce model overfitting, computational intensity, and improve interpretability, an extensive feature selection procedure was conducted using binary versions of ten metaheuristic algorithms. Each optimizer was run independently in multiple stochastic runs to assess its robustness and consistency. The results of each technique were compared with the help of the following set of metrics: the average prediction error, the average number of selected features (normalized by the total number of features), the average fitness value, the best and the worst fitness value, and the standard deviation of the fitness value across the runs. Table 6 presents the comparative results, which show that the binary Ninja Optimization Algorithm (bNinja) is highly effective. In particular, bNinja returned the lowest mean error (0.28253), indicating its high ability to reject duplicate inputs. Besides, it used a minimal number of features, keeping only \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$23.53\%$$\end{document} of the initial features. This means that there is a significant reduction in dimensions, which not only facilitates model design but also accelerates training and inference. Additionally, regarding fitness evaluation, bNinja performed better than its counterparts, with the lowest average fitness (0.34573) and the best fitness value (0.24753), indicating high stability and reliability of convergence. These findings verify the exploration-exploitation balance of the algorithm, which makes it effective in searching the discrete search space without early convergence. The variance and error measures were larger in the other methods, bFA and bQIO, indicating that these approaches exhibited less stable Performance over the trials.

Hyperparameter optimization results

The final testing step involved optimizing the STGCN model using the continuous versions of ten metaheuristic optimization algorithms. This was aimed at reducing generalization error and increasing the model’s robustness through the optimal calibration of hyperparameters, including the learning rate, kernel size, temporal convolution depth, and dropout rate. The Performance of each optimizer was evaluated based on the forecasting performance of the resultant model using an extensive array of regression statistics. Table 8 illustrates the Comparison of the results of all the optimization methods considered in the research. Significantly, the suggested Ninja + STGCN model produced the highest results across all of the considered metrics with a very low mean squared error (MSE) of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7.48 \times 10^{-8}$$\end{document} and a root mean squared error (RMSE) of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2.74 \times 10^{-4}$$\end{document}. This indicates a significant decrease in forecasting error compared to the unoptimized baseline and other hyperparameter-optimized models. Discussion

STGCN holds the advantage over the other deep learning models examined during the empirical evaluation. Regardless of whether the data was not optimized or processed with the STGCN-based framework, STGCN consistently achieved the lowest errors and the highest accuracy and generalization. Because this model could consider associations in both space and time, it was particularly well-suited for examining the generation of renewable hydrogen. Unlike LSTM, TCN, and DKLT, which operate in sequential order, STGCN’s graph network enables it to obtain more comprehensive results from the data. Still, the Performance of STGCN increased significantly after doing feature selection and hyperparameter tuning with the Ninja optimizer. These findings suggest that including both topological spaces and time details is very helpful for forecasting in complex environment systems. The deployment of metaheuristic algorithms, particularly NiOA, has significantly aided in addressing the challenges of high-dimensional data that is sensitive to its settings. When selecting the features, bNinja displayed the best results among all nine benchmark optimizers by selecting only a few features and having a low average prediction error. It resulted in both improved computational ability and a more precise understanding, leading to stronger models. Using NiOA’s adaptive exploration-exploitation strategy and unique nonlinear mutation, the binary version successfully screened out valuable data, maintaining the accuracy of the models intact. It was clear that NiOA could adjust quickly to dynamic tasks since the features it chose remained unchanged and showed minimal variation in their fitness. Additionally, converting the model to a continuous form made it easier to use for hyperparameter tuning, demonstrating its flexibility. At this point, once again, NiOA produced excellent outcomes, which helped STGCN achieve nearly flawless levels in its metrics, including a very small RMSE of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$2.74 \times 10^{-4}$$\end{document} and a Nash–Sutcliffe Efficiency of 0.9841. The use of clustering, density plots, KDE curves, and boxplots in the visual analysis demonstrated that the method was statistically valid and robust. Plots show that there were apparent differences between models with and without optimization, and STGCN consistently demonstrated exemplary Performance. In addition, it was revealed that other metaheuristics, such as PSO, FA, and TSH, performed better than TVMA at times but exhibited more variations in their error rates and were less dependable from one run to the next. After examining the correlation matrix and bar charts, it became apparent that metrics such as RMSE, MAE, and MSE are influenced similarly and correlate with the model’s Performance. To summarize, the outcomes demonstrate that NiOA-enhanced STGCN can be regarded as the optimal forecasting system for renewable hydrogen production. The strategy used in this work is to join deep learning with intelligent feature selection and metaheuristic optimization. The result is a robust framework well-suited for handling numerous complex environmental tasks that exhibit spatiotemporal changes and diverse types of data.

Conclusion and future work

This study utilizes deep learning and advanced metaheuristic optimization to developa novel framework for forecasting green hydrogen production. Of the seven models,STGCN proved to be the most effective baseline, demonstrating an excellent ability tolearn in both space and time. The implementation of the proposed Ninja OptimizationAlgorithm (Ninja) significantly helped, reducing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) byalmost 99% and increasing the R2 value by 10.46%. The Ninja optimizer outperformedPSO, FA, and JAYA in terms of accuracy, stability, and convergence speed in bothtasks: feature selection and hyperparameter tuning. Future studies will further investigatehow Ninja performs in hydrogen forecasting for various climate regions and withlarger datasets. Additionally, utilizing XAI to explain outcomes enhances the credibilityand trustworthiness necessary for informed decision-making in infrastructureand policy development. Adding techniques that utilize edge computing and managemultiple objectives simultaneously (such as accuracy versus energy consumption) mayhelp advance the work for practical applications in energy grids and cities.

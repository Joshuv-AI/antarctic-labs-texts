Comparison of surgical outcomes of robotic complete mesocolic excision for right-sided colon cancer in obese versus non-obese patients
Authors: Teppei Miyakawa, Kentaro Ochiai, Montserrat Guraieb-Trueba, Jaganmurugan Ramamurthy, Ramy Behman, Craig A. Messick, Sa Nguyen, Tsuyoshi Konishi, George J. Chang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundA major concern of complete mesocolic excision (CME) for right-sided colon cancer is whether it can be performed safely in obese patients. The aim of this study is to perform a contemporary evaluation of the perioperative safety and oncological outcomes of robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer in obese versus non-obese patients.MethodsThis was a single-institution retrospective cohort study of patients who underwent robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer between 2015 and 2023. Patients were divided into obese (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2) and non-obese (BMI < 30 kg/m2) groups. Perioperative complications, operative metrics, pathological results, overall survival (OS), and recurrence-free survival (RFS) were compared.ResultsOf 211 patients, 80 (37.9%) were obese. Complication rates did not differ significantly between obese and non-obese groups (any grade: 18.8% vs 12.2%, P = 0.24; grade III: 3.8% vs 1.5%, P = 0.30). No vascular injuries occurred in either group. Conversion rates (5.0% vs 0.8%, P = 0.05) and length of stay were similar. Obese patients had longer operative times (289.5 vs 247 min, P = 0.003) and greater blood loss (50 vs 30 mL, P = 0.025). No cases had positive circumferential resection margins. Lymph node harvest (34.5 vs 34 nodes, P = 0.85) and margin status were comparable. During median follow-up of 2.6 years, 3-year RFS was similar between groups (89.1% vs 86.7%, P = 0.63), with no significant differences in OS (100% vs 95.6%, P = 0.13).ConclusionsObesity was associated with longer operative times and increased blood loss but not with higher complication rates or worse survival outcomes. Robotic CME appears technically and oncologically feasible for both obese and non-obese patients with right-sided colon cancer.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00464-026-12650-7.

Colon cancer affects approximately 1,148,000 new patients worldwide each year [1]. Standardized surgical approaches including complete mesocolic excision (CME) and complete D2 or D3 lymph node dissection are optimal oncologic approaches associated with better prognosis than conventional surgery [2–5]. These approaches require dissection along the superior mesenteric vein and are technically demanding procedures, especially in obese patients because abundant visceral fat makes it difficult to identify vascular and landmark anatomic structures. A major concern of this approach is whether it can be performed safely in obese patients. In the United States, where obese patients comprise approximately 40% of the adult population [6], CME for right-sided colon cancer is not a standard approach due to concerns for risk of vascular injury and lack of familiarity with vascular anatomy and its variation.

The robotic platform offers several advantages that may facilitate technically demanding minimally invasive surgeries like CME for right-sided colon cancer or surgery in obese patients [7, 8]. It has been suggested that robotic surgery for obese patients with colorectal cancer had more favorable short-term outcome compared to laparoscopic surgery for obese patients in terms of early postoperative recovery and a lower risk of conversion to open surgery [7, 9, 10], and had comparable surgical outcomes compared to robotic surgery for non-obese patients [8, 11–17]. However, there is limited data regarding the impact of obesity on CME for right-sided colon cancer.

We have previously described the “SMV-first” approach to robotic CME for right sided colon cancer with acceptable morbidity and excellent short-term oncologic outcomes [18]. The purpose of this study is to perform a contemporary evaluation of the perioperative safety and oncological outcomes of robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer in obese versus non-obese patients. We hypothesized that robotic CME in obese patients will be associated with comparable outcomes to non-obese patients.

Methods

Patient identification and data collection

This was a single-center retrospective observational cohort study. This study adheres to the Strengthening the Reporting of Cohort Studies in Surgery (STROCSS) reporting guidelines for cohort studies [19]. All consecutive patients who underwent robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer between January 2015 and December 2023 at The University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center were identified from a prospectively maintained database. Exclusion criteria included emergency surgery for perforation or obstruction, stage IV disease, and synchronous multiple colorectal cancer. Demographic, treatment and outcome data were identified from the database and supplemented by individual chart review. We defined right-sided colon cancer as tumor located in cecum, ascending colon, hepatic flexure, or the proximal third of the transverse colon.

Conversion to open surgery was defined as the use of an abdominal incision to continue resection under direct visualization. Complications within the 30-days of surgery including intraoperative complications were collected and graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification [20]. We defined major complications as grade III or higher. Patients with multiple complications were classified by the highest grade. Pathological data included histological grade, pathological TNM staging, number of total examined and positive lymph nodes, proximal, distal and circumferential resection margin (CRM) status, and distant to high tie. Positive lymph nodes were defined as the presence of metastatic disease in harvested lymph nodes on pathological examination. CRM positivity was defined by a resection margin of ≤ 1 mm. Follow-up was calculated from the date of curative surgery. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the date of the last follow-up or death. Recurrence free survival (RFS) was defined as the time from the date of surgery to recurrence, death, or the last follow-up. Given that previous studies have demonstrated that approximately 70–80% of colorectal cancer recurrences occur within 3 years of surgery [21, 22], our follow-up period was considered adequate to capture the majority of clinically significant recurrence events.

Surgery

CME was routinely performed with careful dissection along the embryological planes and central lymphadenectomy was performed using the superior mesenteric vein (SMV)-first approach [18, 23]. Both right hemicolectomy and extended right hemicolectomy followed the same standardized approach with middle colic artery root exposure and central lymphadenectomy. In right hemicolectomy, the right branch of the middle colic artery was divided while preserving the left branch. In extended right hemicolectomy, the middle colic artery was divided at its root from the superior mesenteric artery. CME is performed as the standard surgical approach for all right-sided colon cancers regardless of T stage, following institutional protocol. The choice between intracorporeal and extracorporeal anastomosis was at the surgeon’s discretion without a standardized institutional protocol. Procedures were performed by nine experienced fellowship-trained specialist colorectal surgical oncologists.

Primary and subgroup analysis

We compared the perioperative, pathological, and survival outcomes in obese (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2) versus non obese (BMI < 30 kg/m2) patients. Since sex may interact with obesity status and survival, we performed a sex-stratified subgroup analysis [7].

Sensitivity analyses

To assess the potential impact of case complexity on outcomes, sensitivity analyses were performed excluding pT4b cases and patients who underwent en-bloc multivisceral resections.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

The protocol was approved by the University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center Institutional Review Board. The anonymous nature of the data allowed the requirement for informed consent to be waived. This study was retrospectively registered with Research Registry (researchregistry11619).

Statistical analysis

Categorical data were presented as counts and percentage, with comparisons performed using χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests. Non-parametric continuous data were presented as medians with interquartile range (IQR) and compared using Mann–Whitney U tests. Parametric continuous data were presented as means with standard deviation (SD) and compared using t-tests. Survival outcomes were analyzed using Kaplan–Meier method and log rank test. Hazard ratios (HR) were estimated by Cox proportional hazard models. To account for the most important prognostic factor, survival analyses were stratified by pathological stage. Missing data were labeled as unknown. To assess potential surgeon-related bias, the distribution of operating surgeons between obese and non-obese groups was compared using χ2 test. A P-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All data management and statistical analyses were performed by using STATA 13.1 (STATA, College Station, TX, USA).

Results

Of a total of 211 patients, 111 (52.6%) were male and the median (IQR) age was 66 (56–73) years. Overall, 80 (37.9%) were obese patients (BMI ≥ 30 kg/m2) and 131 (62.1%) were non-obese patients (BMI < 30 kg/m2). The median (IQR) BMI was 33.8 (31.6–36.9) kg/m2 in the obese group and 26.1 (23.9–28.0) kg/m2 in the non-obese group. Surgical outcomes

Overall, 173 (82.0%) underwent right hemicolectomy and 38 (18%) underwent extended right hemicolectomy. En bloc multivisceral resection was performed in 25 patients (11.8%) and intracorporeal anastomosis was conducted in 81 patients (38.3%). No grade IV or V complications occurred in either group. One patient in the obese group experienced multiple complications of different grades and was classified by the highest grade (Grade III). The rate of any grade complications and grade III complications did not differ between the obese and non-obese groups (any grade: 18.8 vs. 12.2%, P = 0.24; grade III, 3.8 vs. 1.5%, P = 0.30). Specifically, there were no intraoperative complications including vascular injury in either group. There was no anastomotic leakage in either group. Conversion to open surgery occurred in 4 patients (5%) in the obese group and 1 patient (0.8%) in the non-obese group (P = 0.050). The obese group had significantly longer median operative times (289.5 vs. 247 min, P = 0.003) and greater blood loss (50 vs. 30 mL, P = 0.03) compared to the non-obese group. The median (IQR) length of stay was 3 (2–4.5) days in the obese group and 3 (2–3) days in the non-obese group, the difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.07). Survival outcomes

Median follow-up period was 2.6 years (IQR: 1.6–4.5). During follow-up, there were 8 deaths (1 and 7 for obese and non-obese) and 24 RFS events (8 and 16 for obese and non-obese). Of the 8 deaths, 4 were cancer-related (disease recurrence) and 4 were deaths without recurrence. The RFS events included 20 recurrences (3 local recurrences and 17 distant recurrences) and 4 deaths without recurrence.

The 3-year OS was 100% in the obese group and 95.6% in the non-obese group (P = 0.13). The 3-year RFS was 89.1% in the obese group and 86.7% in the non-obese group (P = 0.63). The 5-year OS was 100% in the obese group and 92.3% in the non-obese group (P = 0.07). The 5-year RFS was 85.4% in the obese group and 82.5% in the non-obese group (P = 0.68). There were no significant differences in survival outcomes between the two groups at either time point. In survival analysis stratified by pathological stage, there were no significant differences in both the 3-year OS (stage 0–II, 100 vs. 96.8%, P = 0.35; stage III, 100 vs. 93.5%, P = 0.21) and the 3-year RFS (stage 0–II, 94.9 vs. 94.7%, P = 0.66; stage III, 82.7 vs. 71.9%, P = 0.22), regardless of lymph node metastasis status (Figs. 1 and 2). Subgroup analysis

Sex-stratified subgroup analysis was shown in Table 4. Conversion occurred only in male patients and tended to be more common in the obese group (8.9% vs. 1.5%, P = 0.07). Median operative time was longer in the obese, but the difference was statistically significant only in females (male: 290 vs. 258 min, P = 0.11; female: 288 vs. 237 min, P = 0.009). The median blood loss was similar between obese and non-obese group in both sexes (male: 50 versus 50 mL, P = 0.11; female: 30 versus 25 mL, P = 0.08). Intracorporeal anastomosis was more commonly performed in obese vs. non-obese females (57.1 versus 38.5%, P = 0.07), but not in obese males (31.1% vs 33.3%, P = 0.81). Sensitivity analyses

To address potential concerns regarding case complexity, we performed sensitivity analyses excluding pT4b cases and patients who underwent en-bloc multivisceral resections. After excluding these cases, 183 patients remained (113 non-obese, 70 obese). Obese patients continued to demonstrate longer operative times (289.5 vs 242 min, P = 0.001) and higher blood loss (50 vs 30 mL, P = 0.02), while major complication rates remained comparable (4.3% vs 1.8%, P = 0.31) (Supplemental Table 1).

We also compared patients with class ≥ 2 (BMI ≥ 35) obesity to those who were non-obese. There was still no significant difference in the overall rate of complications (15.6% vs. 12.1%, P = 0.61 for class ≥ 2 obesity vs non-obese, respectively).

Discussion

This study evaluated perioperative and oncological outcomes of robotic CME for right-sided colon cancers in obese compared to non-obese patients and demonstrated that obesity was associated with longer operative times and more blood loss but was not associated with risk for complications or survival.

Concern for increased risk of complications including for vascular injury, is a major barrier for more widespread adoption of CME for right sided colon cancer particularly among obese patients. This analysis shows that rates of postoperative complications, intraoperative vascular injury, anastomotic leakage, readmission, reoperation, and mortality, were all similar between obese and non-obese patients. This suggests that robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer appears safe in obese patients. While robotic surgery is expected to offer advantages for technically demanding procedures in obese patients [24], existing studies have primarily focused on left-sided colorectal cancer and there is very limited data on robotic surgery for CME [11–16]. One prior study analyzed perioperative outcomes of robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer in obese (BMI ≥ 29) patients vs non-obese patients in 73 patients. Obesity was associated with longer operative times but similar overall rates of complications [17]. The present study evaluated the standardized World Health Organization definition of obesity (BMI ≥ 30) [25] with a significantly larger sample size. In addition to demonstrating that rates of complications were not increased in obese patients undergoing CME, we also report no difference in survival outcomes during the follow-up period.

We did observe longer operative times, more blood loss and a trend towards higher conversion rates in obese patients compared to non-obese patients. In sex-stratified subgroup analysis, conversions to open surgery occurred only in male patients. Female patients generally had shorter operative times, less blood loss, and fewer complications than male patients. Additionally, the differences between obese and non-obese patients were more pronounced in females. These findings may be explained by established differences in fat distribution between sexes. Men typically have more visceral fat, while women tend to have more subcutaneous fat [26]. This suggests that the same BMI may have different effects on surgical outcomes depending on sex.

The abundance of visceral fat in obese male patients may make identification of vascular anatomy more challenging and increase the difficulty for colonic and small bowel management, potentially increasing need for conversion to open surgery. Indeed, only two of the four conversions in obese patients were due to difficulty in recognizing vascular anatomy, and no intraoperative vascular injuries were observed. This suggests that lymph node dissection in robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer could be safely performed even in obese male patients. The lower operative difficulty in thinner female patients may have contributed to shorter operative times, less blood loss, and fewer complications in non-obese female patients. Obese female patients had more intracorporeal anastomoses which may have contributed to the longer operative time [27]. These sex-stratified findings should be considered hypothesis-generating due to small subgroup sizes and warrant validation in larger prospective studies with formal body composition analysis.

There were no significant differences between obese and non-obese patients in oncological outcomes such as the number of harvested lymph nodes and margin status. Our survival analyses suggest comparable oncological outcomes between obese and non-obese patients. Consistent with prior studies comparing oncological outcomes between obese and non-obese patients with colorectal cancer [8, 16, 28] the present study suggests that robotic CME for right-sided colon cancer can be performed safely and with similar oncological efficacy in obese and non-obese patients. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to compare the survival outcomes of robotic CME for colon cancer comparing obese and non-obese patients [16]. Given the small number of events, our survival analyses should be interpreted as exploratory and require validation in larger cohorts.

Strengths of this study a relatively large sample size reporting the safety of robotic CME surgery for right-sided colon cancer among obese patients in a United States population [29]. Additionally, the balanced distribution of operating surgeons between groups minimizes the potential for surgeon-specific bias to confound our results, as all procedures were performed by experienced fellowship-trained colorectal surgical oncologists with similar case distributions across obesity groups.

This study has several limitations. Due to the low event rate for high grade complications, multivariable analysis could not be performed. Secondly, while it is well established that minimally invasive procedures in obese patients present technical challenges that can be overcome with robotic experience, our results may not be fully generalizable to lower-volume robotic centers. However, our study included multiple surgeons at different stages of their learning curves, which provides some degree of generalizability. Thirdly, the limited median follow-up of 2.6 years restricts our ability to draw definitive conclusions about long-term oncological outcomes, however 3-year disease free survival is has been shown to be a reliable indicator of long-term survival outcomes. Our study may have been underpowered to detect statistically significant differences for certain endpoints that showed trends toward significance in obese patients and that with more patients may have reached significance. During the study period, we did not perform standardized mesocolic plane integrity grading for CME quality assessment. While we used established surrogate markers such as lymph node yield, resection margins, and vascular tie distance [30], we acknowledge that these measures may not fully capture the technical quality of mesocolic plane dissection. Future studies incorporating formal CME quality grading could provide more objective assessment of technical adequacy, particularly in obese patients. Finally, due to the retrospective nature of our study and limitations in available data for some patients followed outside our institution, we were unable to obtain detailed information for all deaths without recurrence. We also did not have detailed quantitative data on visceral adiposity or medical comorbidities which could provide additional potential insights.

Conclusion

Obesity was associated with longer operative times and more blood loss but was not associated with increased risk for complications or worse survival among patients underwent robotic CME for right-sided colon cancers. Robotic CME appears technically and oncologically feasible for both obese and non-obese patients with right-sided colon cancer.

Metabolic dysfunction‐associated steatotic liver disease: A superior predictor for incident type 2 diabetes over traditional criteria – NAGALA study
Authors: Kimiko Sakai, Takuro Okamura, Ema Toyokuni, Hiroshi Okada, Akihiro Obora, Takao Kojima, Masahide Hamaguchi, Michiaki Fukui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTAims/IntroductionThe 2023 Delphi consensus recommended the use of new term, metabolic dysfunction‐associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), aiming conceptual shift from the conventional non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). The association between NAFLD and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) development is well known. This study aimed to examine the correlation between MASLD and T2DM development, comparing their utility as predictors.Materials and MethodsThis retrospective cohort study obtained data from a medical health checkup program conducted at Asahi University Hospital, Japan, between 2004 and 2021. Logistic regression analysis was used to assess the association between MASLD and incident T2DM over 5 years. To compare the predictive utility of NAFLD and MASLD, receiver operating characteristic curves were drawn, followed by area under the curve (AUC) comparisons.ResultsIn total, 15,039 participants (59.6% males; median [interquartile range {IQR}] age, 44 [38, 50] years) were included. Out of 2,682 participants meeting the criteria for MASLD, 234 individuals (8.7%) developed T2DM. Multivariate analysis revealed a significantly elevated risk of T2DM in MASLD compared with the reference healthy group (without steatotic liver disease or cardiometabolic risk), presenting an OR of 127.00 (95% CI 40.40–399.00, P < 0.001). The concordance rate of diagnosis between NAFLD and MASLD was 98.7%. The AUC values were 0.799 for NAFLD and 0.807 for MASLD, respectively. Comparative analysis of the AUC showed a statistical difference between NAFLD and MASLD (P < 0.001).ConclusionsMASLD was shown to be a significant risk factor for incident T2DM, exhibiting a potentially higher predictive capacity than conventional NAFLD.Keywords: Liver steatosis, Non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease, Type 2 diabetes mellitus

INTRODUCTION

Non‐alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) has become a global concern with the rise of the obese population worldwide, and its prevalence is estimated to be approximately 25% 1 . However, the term NAFLD, due to its exclusionary criteria, does not accurately reflect the pathophysiology. In 2023, following a Delphi consensus process involving international experts, a new term, metabolic dysfunction‐associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), was proposed 2 . Essentially, there was an agreement to revise the conventional definition of NAFLD to include the presence of at least one of the five cardiometabolic risk factors. Furthermore, individuals with metabolic risk factors who consume moderate amounts of alcohol were defined as a separate group called MetALD (MASLD and increased alcohol intake). MASLD avoids increasing the stigma associated with the term “fatty” and offers a more nuanced characterization of diverse levels of alcohol consumption.

Conventional NAFLD and type 2 diabetes mellitus are well known to share common pathophysiological aspects, such as lipotoxicity, insulin resistance, and chronic inflammation, influencing the progression of both conditions 3 , 4 . Lee et al. 5 reported that cases of NAFLD and MASLD overlapped in 99.0% of instances in a study conducted in the United States. However, no detailed studies have investigated the risk of developing type 2 diabetes in Japanese individuals under the new classification, nor have there been assessments of differences in the predictive abilities for type 2 diabetes between NAFLD and MASLD. This retrospective, large cohort study aimed to validate the predictive capacity of MASLD for the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus in a Japanese population and to compare it with traditional concepts.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study design and participants

This cohort study utilized data from a medical health checkup program at Asahi University Hospital in Gifu, Japan, as part of the NAGALA (NAFLD in Gifu Area, Longitudinal Analysis) study. The NAGALA study's purpose and methodology have been detailed previously 6 . Approval for this study was granted by the Ethics Committee of Asahi University Hospital and it was conducted in compliance with the 2013 Declaration of Helsinki (clinical trial number: 2018‐09‐01). Data used for this study were sourced from a database containing information collected with informed consent and personal identity details. The analysis included medical health checkup data gathered between January 2004 and December 2021.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

This study included all individuals who participated in medical health checkups from January 2004 to December 2021. Participants whose data could not be tracked from baseline to 5 years were excluded. Additionally, we excluded those with missing baseline data on waist circumference, body mass index (BMI), or abdominal ultrasound, as well as participants who had type 2 diabetes mellitus at baseline. In the subgroup analysis, participants with prediabetes at baseline were further excluded.

Data collection and measurements

The data collection and measurement methods have been described in detail previously 7 . The participants underwent blood tests, abdominal ultrasonography, and completed a self‐administered questionnaire covering smoking and drinking habits, exercise routines, dietary habits, and treatment for metabolic and liver conditions, among other topics. Blood samples were taken after an overnight fast and analyzed using Modular Analytics (Hitachi High‐Technologies Corp. Ltd, Tokyo, Japan). The weekly ethanol intake was calculated based on the reported amount and type of alcoholic beverages consumed per week. Participants were categorized into three groups: non‐alcohol consumers (<210 g/week for males and 140 g/week for females), light alcohol consumers (210–420 g/week for males and 140–350 g/week for females), and moderate alcohol consumers (more than 420 g/week for males and more than 350 g/week for females) 7 . Regular exercisers were defined as those who exercised at least once a week 8 .

Definition of type 2 diabetes mellitus and prediabetes

Type 2 diabetes mellitus was defined as a fasting glucose level of ≥126 mg/dL, hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) of ≥6.5%, or the use of specific drug treatments 9 . Prediabetes was defined as a fasting serum glucose of ≥100 mg/dL or HbA1c of ≥5.7% 10 . An oral glucose tolerance test was not conducted in this study.

Definition of steatotic liver diseases

Hepatic steatosis was identified using abdominal ultrasound conducted by a trained technician and evaluated by two hepatologists, as described previously 11 , 12 . NAFLD was diagnosed based on the Clinical Practice Guideline of the American Association of Clinical Endocrinology (AACE) 13 , which defines a steatotic liver in non‐alcohol consumers (less than 210 g/week for males and 140 g/week for females), while excluding other liver diseases such as viral hepatitis (HBV, HCV) and drug‐induced liver disorders. MASLD was diagnosed according to the multisociety Delphi consensus statement from the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) 2 . This definition of MASLD includes hepatic steatosis along with at least one of five cardiometabolic risk factors: a BMI of ≥23 kg/m2 or waist circumference of >94 cm in men and 80 cm in women; a fasting serum glucose of ≥100 mg/dL, 2 h post‐load glucose levels of ≥140 mg/dL, or HbA1c of ≥5.7%; blood pressure ≥130/85 mmHg or the use of antihypertensive medications; plasma triglycerides of ≥150 mg/dL or the use of lipid‐lowering treatments; and a plasma HDL‐cholesterol of ≤40 mg/dL in men and ≤50 mg/dL in women, or the use of lipid‐lowering treatments. Within this classification, non‐alcohol consumers (<210 g/week for males and 140 g/week for females) were identified as having true MASLD, while those with light alcohol consumption (210–420 g/week for males and 140–350 g/week for females) were classified as MetADL (MASLD with increased alcohol intake). Participants with moderate alcohol consumption (more than 420 g/week for males and more than 350 g/week for females) were classified as having alcohol‐related liver disease (ALD) 7 . Since all participants were Japanese, the BMI and waist circumference were assessed using cut‐off values appropriate for Asians 14 .

Definition of MASLD score

The MASLD score was calculated for individuals meeting the MASLD criteria, based on how many of the five cardiometabolic risk factors criteria they met, with a score ranging from 1 to 5.

Statistical analyses

The primary outcome measure focused on the risk of the development of type 2 diabetes mellitus for each group, categorized based on a multisociety Delphi consensus statement from the AASLD (details provided in the results section) 2 , compared with a reference group of healthy individuals (without steatotic liver disease and cardiovascular risk). Additionally, we calculated the patient concordance rate between NAFLD and MASLD, and compared the predictive utility of traditional and new classification methods for incident type 2 diabetes mellitus by drawing receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves for the presence or absence of NAFLD and MASLD, subsequently comparing the AUCs.

The associations between steatotic liver disease and various outcomes were assessed using a logistic regression model. This multivariate model was adjusted for baseline factors including sex, age, smoking status, and exercise habits. Continuous variables were reported as means with standard deviations, while categorical variables were presented as absolute numbers and percentages. The associations are expressed as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs). A value of P <0.05 was considered statistically significant. For the comparative analysis of ROC curves between the traditional NAFLD classification and the revised MASLD classification, the multivariate logistic regression was also adjusted for age, sex, smoking status, and exercise habits, with a significance threshold of P < 0.05.

In the subgroup analysis, excluding participants with prediabetes, we calculated the ORs and 95% CI for NAFLD and MASLD using multivariate logistic regression analyses adjusted for sex, age, smoking status, and exercise habits, following the same method as the main analysis, and compared the AUC.

All statistical analyses were conducted using EZR version 1.68 (Saitama Medical Center, Jichi Medical University, Saitama, Japan), a graphical user interface for R version 4.4.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

RESULTS

Of the 42,892 participants who underwent medical health checkups between 2004 and 2021, those whose data could not be tracked after 5 years were excluded. Also excluded were participants with missing baseline data for waist circumference, BMI, or abdominal ultrasound (n = 27,149). Participants with type 2 diabetes mellitus at baseline were excluded (n = 704). Thus, 15,039 participants were subjected to the analysis. The study flow diagram for the registration of participants is shown in Figure 1, and the baseline characteristics of the participants are shown in Table 1. Based on the multisociety Delphi consensus statement from the AASLD 2 , we classified the participants into seven groups (Figure 2). Group 1, a healthy population without steatotic liver disease (SLD) or cardiometabolic risk (CMR); Group 2, individuals without SLD but with CMR; Group 3, those with cryptogenic SLD; Group 4, participants with SLD of specific etiology; Group 5, individuals with ALD; Group 6, those with MetALD; and Group 7, participants with MASLD. Group 1 comprised 5,400 participants; Group 2, 6,406 participants; Group 3, 157 participants; Group 4, 2 participants; Group 5, 117 participants; Group 6, 275 participants; and Group 7, 2,682 participants. Main outcome

In the multivariate analysis, the covariates included age, grade of alcohol consumption, smoking status, and exercise. The adjusted ORs for incident type 2 diabetes mellitus are shown in Table 3. The results revealed a significantly increased risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus in Groups 2, 5, 6, and 7 compared with the reference healthy group, Group 1. Group 2 (SLD−/CMR+) exhibited an OR of 25.10 (95% CI 7.94–79.20, P < 0.001), Group 5 (ALD) had an OR of 106.00 (95% CI 28.60–396.00, P < 0.001), Group 6 (MetALD) showed an OR of 74.50 (95% CI 21.50–258.00, P < 0.001), and Group 7 (MASLD) presented an OR of 127.00 (95% CI 40.40–399.00, P < 0.001). The occurrence of type 2 diabetes mellitus was not observed in Groups 3 and 4. Consequently, result computations were not conducted for these groups. These findings collectively indicate a statistically significant association between each group and an elevated risk of developing type 2 diabetes mellitus compared with the healthy Group 1. Furthermore, age (OR 1.04, 95% CI 1.03–1.06, P < 0.001) and current smoking status (OR 1.55, 95% CI 1.18–2.05, P = 0.002) were also statistically significant as risk factors for the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Analysis of AUC comparison

The patient characteristics and incidence rates of type 2 diabetes mellitus 5 years later based on the presence or absence of NAFLD and MASLD are detailed in Table 4. In total, 2,785 individuals met the criteria for NAFLD and 2,682 for MASLD, and the concordance rate of diagnosis between NAFLD and MASLD was 98.7%. Among them, 230 individuals (8.3%) with NAFLD and 234 individuals (8.7%) with MASLD developed type 2 diabetes mellitus 5 years later. The adjusted ORs for incident type 2 diabetes mellitus, considering age, sex, smoking status, and exercise habits, were 6.00 (95% CI 4.84–7.45, P < 0.001) for NAFLD and 6.58 (95% CI 5.29–8.17, P < 0.001) for MASLD (Table 5). The corresponding AUC values were 0.799 and 0.807, respectively (Figure 3). Comparative analysis of AUC showed a statistical difference between NAFLD and MASLD (P < 0.001).

Subgroup analysis

In total, 10,517 individuals were included in the subgroup analysis. The background of the subjects is shown in Table S3 . The adjusted ORs for the development of type 2 diabetes mellitus in MASLD and NAFLD, after adjusting for age, sex, smoking history, and exercise habits, were 4.82 (95% CI 2.31–10.10, P < 0.001) and 4.35 (95% CI 2.08–9.08, P < 0.001), respectively (Table S4 ). The corresponding AUC values were 0.818 and 0.814, respectively. Comparative analysis of AUC showed a statistical difference between MASLD and NAFLD (P = 0.001), indicating that in the subgroup analysis, MASLD had a higher predictive ability for type 2 diabetes mellitus compared with NAFLD (Figure S1 ).

DISCUSSION

MASLD introduces a new conceptual framework in the domain of steatotic liver disease, with no available data on the association between MASLD and the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus. This study systematically compared MASLD with a representative healthy population, revealing a significantly heightened risk of type 2 diabetes mellitus onset in individuals with MASLD. Furthermore, our findings indicate that MASLD possesses superior predictive capabilities for the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus when compared with the conventional concept of NAFLD.

The involvement of NAFLD in the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus has long been proven through research using animal models and human subjects 15 . Recent meta‐analyses have demonstrated that individuals with NAFLD face more than twice the risk of developing type 2 diabetes mellitus, and for those with advanced NAFLD fibrosis scores, the risk is reported to be increased by over fourfold 16 . Steatotic liver disease is characterized by the excessive accumulation of triglycerides in the liver. Triglycerides are synthesized in the liver from incoming free fatty acids, and an oversupply of free fatty acids results in lipotoxicity that causes endoplasmic reticulum and oxidative stress, insulin resistance, and impaired autophagy. Consequently, fatty acids activate apoptotic cascades, promoting tissue damage and inflammation. Collectively, these molecular events contribute to the progression of NAFLD 4 , 17 . Recent research has also revealed that a high‐fat diet induces dysbiosis, heightens intestinal permeability, and leads to metabolic endotoxemia, which is characterized by an elevation in blood lipopolysaccharide levels 18 . Metabolic endotoxemia triggers and sustains inflammation. When this pathophysiology of metabolic endotoxemia is added to SLD, it not only initiates inflammation in the liver, but also prompts inflammation in adipose tissue and throughout the body. Consequently, this exacerbates systemic insulin resistance. Lipotoxicity also plays a role in pancreatic β cell dysfunction 19 . The mechanism involves the endoplasmic reticulum and oxidative stress 20 , along with a mechanism mediated by palmitic acid 21 . An increase in insulin resistance and impairment of pancreatic β cell function lead to the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Despite the high diagnostic concordance rate between NAFLD and MASLD, MASLD was shown to exhibit superior predictive capabilities for the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus. The most significant difference between NAFLD and MASLD lies in cardiometabolic risk. Among the five cardiometabolic risk factors included in the MASLD diagnostic criteria, it is well known that overall obesity, abdominal obesity, and impaired glucose tolerance are risk factors for type 2 diabetes mellitus 22 . Increased triglyceride 23 , 24 , 25 and decreased HDL cholesterol 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 levels have also been reported as independent risk factors for the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Moreover, hypertension, which has etiological aspects in common with type 2 diabetes mellitus 30 , 31 , has been reported to be a potential risk factor for the development of type 2 diabetes mellitus 32 , 33 , 34 . Combining the presence of SLD with these cardiometabolic risk factors presumably enhances the accuracy of predicting the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus. Among these cardiometabolic risk factors, prediabetes particularly strongly contributed to the development of type 2 diabetes mellitus. However, even in the subgroup analysis excluding impaired glucose tolerance, MASLD predicted the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus better than NAFLD. We believe that considering multiple cardiometabolic risk factors in the diagnostic criteria for MASLD is meaningful in predicting the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

Furthermore, the OR for incident type 2 diabetes mellitus in the MetALD group was lower than that in the MASLD group in this study. The relationship between alcohol consumption and type 2 diabetes mellitus follows a U‐shaped pattern 35 , wherein light alcohol intake is observed to have a protective effect against the development of type 2 diabetes mellitus 36 . Involvement of heightened insulin sensitivity has been suggested as a potential mechanism 37 . In line with the definition of NAFLD, incorporating information on alcohol intake into the classification of MASLD may have positively influenced the predictive ability for the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus.

This study has several limitations. First, the number of incident cases of type 2 diabetes mellitus in the healthy population without SLD or cardiometabolic risk was extremely low, suggesting a selection bias. For instance, the possibility of heightened health consciousness within the population of patients diagnosed with type 2 diabetes mellitus discontinuing health check‐ups. Second, there were no occurrences of cryptogenic SLD or SLD of specific etiology, indicating the need for a more extensive investigation in a larger population.

In conclusion, MASLD was shown to be a significant risk factor for the onset of type 2 diabetes mellitus, exhibiting a potentially higher predictive capacity than conventional NAFLD.

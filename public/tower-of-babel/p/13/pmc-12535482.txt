Cost-Estimation Model of Prevented Recurrences with Atezolizumab in Early Non-Small-Cell Lung Cancer in Italy
Authors: Andrea Marcellusi, Marco Belfiore, Rosaria Tempre, Maria Buonfiglio, Alessandro Russo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPatients with early-stage non-small-cell lung cancer (eNSCLC) often experience disease recurrence after surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy, leading to a substantial clinical and economic burden. The introduction of immunotherapy such as atezolizumab in the adjuvant setting may offer both clinical benefits and healthcare cost reductions by preventing disease progression.ObjectivesThis analysis aimed to estimate the economic impact of prevented recurrences by measuring the costs avoided in the Italian population potentially eligible for adjuvant atezolizumab.MethodsA cost-consequence analysis was developed using a published model, adapted to the Italian context, to estimate the number of recurrences per year from 2023 to 2033, comparing scenarios ‘with’ and ‘without’ atezolizumab. Epidemiological and clinical input was obtained from published literature, clinical trials, and local market research. Direct healthcare costs were sourced from an Italian real-world study. The Italian national health system (NHS) perspective was considered, and a deterministic one-way sensitivity analysis was performed to evaluate the uncertainty over the main parameters.ResultsOver the period considered, 2582 patients with stage II–IIIA eNSCLC (7th edition of the TNM [tumour, node, metastases] classification) after resection and chemotherapy were estimated annually. Of these, 720 (27.9%) were potentially eligible to receive atezolizumab according to the approved indication. Overall, the model estimated 2556 recurrences for the eligible patients, which generated an economic burden of €11.02 million yearly. The introduction of atezolizumab could avoid 720 recurrences (111 locoregional and 609 metastatic), resulting in a direct healthcare cost reduction of €3.11 million annually from the perspective of the Italian NHS. One-way sensitivity analysis showed moderate base-case changes, especially due to drug costs in the metastatic settings.ConclusionsRecurrences are common among patients with eNSCLC and are associated with a remarkable increase in total direct costs. It appears that adjuvant atezolizumab would prevent a relevant number of events, with potential savings in recurrence-related costs from the perspective of the NHS in Italy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40261-025-01469-0.

Background

Lung cancer remains the predominant cause of mortality due to cancer across the globe. It is projected that, by 2025, the world will witness approximately 2.52 million new lung cancer cases, with small-cell lung cancer accounting for 13% and non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for 84% of these cases [1].

Over the last few years, immunotherapy has revolutionised the therapeutic landscape of advanced NSCLC, replacing platinum-based chemotherapy as the standard of care for first-line treatment, either alone in strong expressors of programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) or in combination with chemotherapy in PD-L1 allcomers. Furthermore, single-agent immunotherapy has been approved in patients with pretreated advanced NSCLC who have not already received immune checkpoint inhibitors and in non-progressing unresectable stage III NSCLC after chemo-radiotherapy [1].

Resectable early-stage NSCLC (eNSCLC) accounts for ~20–25% of cases [2], and surgery remains the primary and most effective curative approach. Platinum-based chemotherapy, as either adjuvant or neo-adjuvant therapy, became the standard of care for radically resected NSCLC, with a small but clinically relevant ~5% survival rate advantage at 5 years in meta-analyses of the published studies [3]. However, long-term outcomes of eNSCLC are still modest and, despite receiving curative intent treatments, a significant percentage of patients (30–55%) ultimately experience recurrence or die because of the disease, suggesting that additional therapeutic strategies are necessary [4].

When considering cost effectiveness in treatment strategies (involving chemotherapy, surgery, and/or radiotherapy) for localised disease, adjuvant chemotherapy tends to be more economical than mere observation despite limited published data supporting this [5].

Atezolizumab, a humanised immunoglobulin G1 monoclonal antibody, targets PD-L1 on tumour-infiltrating immune cells or tumour cells. The randomised, multicentre, open-label phase III IMpower010 study, which enrolled patients with early-stage resected NSCLC (stage IB–IIIA according to the 7th edition of the TNM [tumour, node, metastases] classification system), revealed a disease-free survival (DFS) advantage with atezolizumab compared with best supportive care (BSC) following adjuvant chemotherapy [6]. Atezolizumab demonstrated a DFS benefit in patients with resected stage II–IIIA NSCLC [7], with pronounced benefit in the subgroup whose tumours expressed PD-L1 on ≥1% of tumour cells, with no new safety signals. The highest DFS benefit was in the subgroup of patients with strong PD-L1 expression (tumour cell ≥50%) and stage II–IIIA (hazard ratio [HR] 0.43) and EGFR/ALK wild-type tumours, leading to the European Medicines Agency (EMA) approval limited to this specific subgroup of patients. The recent EMA approval of atezolizumab for patients with PD-L1 ≥50% stage II–IIIA NSCLC after surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy represents a milestone in the history of systemic therapies for eNSCLC after 20 years of failure to improve the outcomes beyond chemotherapy [7], paving the way for immune checkpoint blockade in the post-operative setting [8, 9]. Disease recurrence after radical-intent treatments poses a substantial clinical and economic burden on patients and healthcare systems.

This analysis aimed to estimate the economic impact of prevented recurrences by measuring the costs avoided in the Italian population potentially eligible for adjuvant atezolizumab.

Methods

We specifically adapted and tailored a published economic model to align with the Italian healthcare environment, following national health technology assessment guidelines and international standards [5]. A panel of Italian experts, including an oncologist and a health economist, played a pivotal role. They validated the model's assumptions and parameters and the clinical applicability of the outcomes.

Model Structure

Our study employed a Markov model incorporating five health states: DFS, locoregional recurrence, first-line metastatic recurrence, second-line metastatic recurrence, and death, with monthly cycle intervals. This model was instrumental in evaluating both clinical and economic outcomes over a 10-year time horizon for patients with eNSCLC [5].

A comprehensive lifetime horizon was established as the base case, enabling a thorough capture of patient health outcomes. Consistent with Italy’s health technology assessment guidelines, a 3% discount rate was applied for costs [10, 11].

This analysis primarily focuses on comparing atezolizumab with BSC in the two different scenarios (with and without atezolizumab). In the DFS state, patients received either up to 16 cycles of atezolizumab or BSC. A growth of 1.3% of incident patients with NSCLC was considered starting from 2020 according to observations recorded in the Italian oncology register (Associazione Italiana Registro Tumori [AIRTUM]) [12].

Clinical Inputs

The likelihood of remaining in the DFS state, transitioning to death, or experiencing a locoregional or metastatic relapse was derived from the DFS curves of the IMpower010 study. The distribution between locoregional and metastatic relapse as the first event was also obtained from this study, with different distributions in the atezolizumab and BSC arms [13].

Given the median 32-month follow-up of the trial, DFS was extrapolated using data from IMpower010. Various parametric models were employed to fit the data to both the atezolizumab and the BSC trial arms. The selection of the log-logistic model as the base case was based on its fit according to the Akaike and Bayesian information criteria and its clinical plausibility as assessed by international experts [13].

Economic Parameters

Economic inputs used in the model were derived from a recent Italian real-world observational study by Cortinovis et al. [14], who analysed healthcare resource utilisation and associated costs in patients with eNSCLC. The study integrated data from the administrative and pathological anatomy databases of three local health units, covering approximately 2.5 million individuals assisted by the Italian national health service (NHS).

The cost analysis focused on the first year after surgical resection and included (1) drug treatments related to lung cancer management and co-medications; (2) all-cause hospital admissions, both cancer-related and unrelated; and (3) outpatient specialist services, including laboratory tests and clinical consultations.

Costs were reported in Euros (€), year 2023 values, and reflected actual amounts reimbursed by the Italian NHS. The study estimated that total direct healthcare costs per patient averaged €22,841. In cases of recurrence, the average cost in the first year was €21,557 for loco-regional recurrence and €24,105 for metastatic recurrence. These recurrence-related costs were applied separately in the model to reflect the distinct cost trajectories associated with each clinical scenario.

Additionally, subgroup analyses and an extended follow-up period of up to 2 years allowed for a broader understanding of cost evolution over time. All cost parameters were tested in one-way sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of the model’s outcomes under varying assumptions [14].

Results

Over the period considered, 2582 patients with stage II–IIIA eNSCLC (TNM 7th edition) after resection and chemotherapy were estimated annually. Of these, 720 (27.9%) were potentially eligible to receive atezolizumab according to the approved EMA indication. In the scenario ‘with atezolizumab’, which considers atezolizumab as an adjuvant treatment option, the number of total recurrences would be 1836. Following the introduction of atezolizumab, 720 cumulative events would be avoided. Of these, 111 would be locoregional and 609 metastatic. A total of 61 recurrences would be avoided within the first 3 years (Fig. 2).

The number of recurrences in the scenario ‘without atezolizumab’ would generate a cumulative related cost of €121.26 million over the period considered and €11.02 million in any given year (Table 2). By significantly reducing the number of recurrences, the introduction of atezolizumab would result in a cumulative cost reduction of €34.17 million in direct healthcare costs from 2023 to 2033. Discussion

The treatment paradigm for operable NSCLC has undergone substantial transformation in recent years, largely attributable to the introduction of adjuvant and neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Despite these advancements, post-operative recurrence remains a challenge because of the variable prognoses in early-stage disease. Adjuvant chemotherapy after surgery reduces the risk of recurrence and metastasis in patients with NSCLC. However, a substantial proportion of patients, ranging from one-half in stage IB to three-quarters in stage IIIA, still experience metastatic relapse after post-operative chemotherapy.

The emergence of immunotherapy has marked a new era in the treatment of advanced NSCLC, yielding substantial improvements in survival rates across different settings and histologies [17]. Lately, the focus has shifted towards exploring the potential of neoadjuvant and adjuvant immunotherapy in eNSCLC [18]. Immunotherapy offers the possibility of sustained anti-tumour effects as compared with conventional anticancer treatments. Consequently, numerous studies are under way to evaluate the effectiveness of adjuvant immunotherapy after surgery in eNSCLC.

The IMpower010 phase III trial first reported a DFS advantage after chemotherapy in patients with radically resected NSCLC compared with BSC (HR 0.66 for PD-L1 ≥1% and 0.43 for PD-L1 ≥50%) [8]. Based on the results of this trial, atezolizumab was the first immune checkpoint inhibitor approved as adjuvant therapy after platinum-based chemotherapy in PD-L1 ≥1% (US Food and Drug Administration [FDA]) or PD-L1 ≥50% (EMA) stage II–III NSCLC without EGFR mutations or ALK rearrangements. In addition, based on the results of the PEARLS/KEYNOTE-091 trial, both the US FDA and the EMA recently granted approval for the PD-1 inhibitor pembrolizumab to be used as adjuvant treatment after resection and for platinum-based chemotherapy in stage IB (T2a ≥4 cm), II, or IIIA NSCLC, regardless of PD-L1 tumour expression. Adjuvant pembrolizumab led to a clinically meaningful improvement in investigator-assessed DFS compared with placebo in patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy (HR 0.76; 95% confidence interval 0.64–0.91), regardless of PD-L1 expression [19]. The therapeutic landscape of eNSCLC is becoming more complex, with the recent advent of several chemo-immunotherapy combinations in the neoadjuvant setting with (KEYNOTE-671 [20] and AEGEAN [21]) or without (CheckMate-816 [22]) adjuvant immunotherapy. To date, it remains unclear whether a specific strategy (adjuvant immunotherapy vs. neoadjuvant chemo-immunotherapy vs. perioperative chemo-immunotherapy) is superior to the others, and the therapeutic decision relies exclusively on clinic-pathological characteristics (stage, PD-L1 status, molecular status), treating physician choice, and local regulatory approvals/drug availability.

To date, two pharmacoeconomic studies have assessed the cost effectiveness of adjuvant atezolizumab for eNSCLC. Das et al. [23] examined its cost effectiveness from a US commercial payer perspective, and Chen et al. [24] analysed it from the viewpoint of the Chinese healthcare system. The contradictory results of these studies are attributed to significant methodological differences and variations in the healthcare systems of the respective countries.

This study represents the first national analysis of the cost consequences of atezolizumab monotherapy as adjuvant treatment after resection and platinum-based chemotherapy in adult patients with NSCLC with ≥50% PD-L1 expression. Drawing on clinical parameters from the IMpower010 trial, like the aforementioned studies, it facilitates a clearer comparison of results.

As with most economic evaluations, this study is subject to several inherent limitations. First, the model relies on long-term projections (10-year horizon), requiring extrapolation of DFS data beyond the observed follow-up period of the IMpower010 trial. Although the log-logistic distribution was selected based on statistical criteria (Akaike/Bayesian information criteria) and clinical plausibility, any extrapolation introduces uncertainty.

Second, some model parameters—particularly those related to healthcare resource use and post-recurrence management—were informed by expert opinion in the absence of comprehensive real-world data. However, these inputs were tested through deterministic (one-way) sensitivity analyses, which included additional parameters, such as the discount rate and efficacy assumptions. These analyses consistently confirmed the robustness of the results in favour of atezolizumab.

Third, although the clinical trial used as the primary source (IMpower010) included a substantial European population and a subgroup of Italian patients, some generalisability concerns remain. Similarly, the cost data—sourced from a recent Italian real-world study (Cortinovis et al. [14])—may not capture potential variability across regions or future healthcare cost trends.

Finally, indirect or societal costs were not included, as the analysis was conducted from the perspective of the Italian NHS, in accordance with local pharmacoeconomic guidelines.

Conclusion

Recurrences are common among patients with eNSCLC and are associated with a remarkable increase in total direct healthcare costs. Adjuvant atezolizumab appears to prevent a relevant number of events and so result in potential savings in recurrence-related costs from the perspective of the Italian NHS. These results highlight reductions in both the clinical and the economic burden among patients with eNSCLC in Italy after approval by the Agenzia Italiana del Farmaco (AIFA) of atezolizumab in the adjuvant setting.

In addition to the economic findings, this study provides insights that may support healthcare decision-makers in planning and allocating resources. Preventing recurrences reduces treatment costs and alleviates the pressure on oncology services, hospital infrastructure, and follow-up care, with potential implications for improving care pathways and overall system efficiency.

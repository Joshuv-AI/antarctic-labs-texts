7641 Antimüllerian Hormone Levels by Phenotypes in Polycystic Ovary Syndrome: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Authors: Patricia Schwenck-Carvalho, Ricardo Simoes, Giovana Maffaziolli, José Maria Soares-Jr, Edmund C Baracat, Gustavo Arantes Maciel
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Disclosure: P. Schwenck-Carvalho: None. R. Simoes: None. G. Maffaziolli: None. J. Soares-Jr: None. E.C. Baracat: None. G.A. Maciel: None. Objective: This study aims to comprehensively evaluate the levels of antimüllerian hormone (AMH) across the different phenotypes (A, B, C, D) of Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) according to Rotterdam diagnostic criteria. Methods: In this systematic review and meta-analysis, two independent reviewers following PRISMA guidelines conducted a comprehensive search of PubMed, Embase, and Web of Science databases for studies published from inception up to December 2023. Studies reporting the AMH levels by PCOS phenotypes were considered for inclusion. The quality of studies was assessed using the ROBINS-E tool. The primary outcome of interest was the mean level of AMH among the PCOS phenotypes regardless of AMH assay used (tAMH). In a secondary analysis, AMH levels were converted into AMH Beckman Coulter Gen II assay (bcAMH). Secondary outcomes included age and BMI by phenotype. Random-effects models were applied to assess mean levels and SMD (standardized mean differences) with 95% confidence intervals (CI 95%). Results: Forty-nine studies were included in this review involving a total of 15 535 subjects. The meta-analysis showed that AMH levels vary according to the PCOS phenotype, being higher into the A phenotype followed by D, C and B: PCOS A tAMH=9.87ng/mL (SMD 2.97 CI 95% 9.29-10.48) bcAMH=11.49ng/mL (SMD 3.06 CI 95% 10.47-12.61), PCOS B tAMH 5.71ng/mL (SMD 0.98, CI 95% 4.96-6.57) bcAMH=6.25ng/mL (sd 1.36 CI 95% 5.20-7.51), PCOS C tAMH 6.91ng/mL (SMD 1.42 CI 95% 6.30-7.57) bcAMH=7.98ng/mL (SMD 1.52 CI 95% 7.16-8.90), PCOS D tAMH 7.80ng/mL (sd 2.27, CI 95% 7.20-8.45) bcAMH=8.97ng/mL (SMD 2.44 CI 95% 8.04-10.00). There were no significant differences between age and BMI through phenotypes - PCOS A age=26.17 years (SMD 4.02 CI 95% 24.29-28.30), BMI=26.11kg/m² (SMD 3.82 CI 95% 25.31-26.94); PCOS B age=27.22 years (SMD 3.75 CI 95% 26.16-26.95), BMI=25.89kg/m² (SMD 3.07 CI 95% 24.86-26.95); PCOS C age=27.46 years (SMD 4.12 CI 95% 26.49-28.46), BMI=25.19kg/m² (SMD 3.44 CI 95% 24.42-25.98); PCOS D age=27.57 years (SMD 4.01 CI 95% 26.68-28.49), BMI=24.78kg/m² (SMD 3.90 CI 95% 24.06-25.53). The heterogeneity among the studies was high, indicating that factors beyond phenotypic categories may be influencing AMH in PCOS. Conclusions: Our findings emphasize the need for phenotype-specific considerations in PCOS management and suggest that AMH levels could enhance the characterization of PCOS subtypes. Additionally, the variability observed in AMH assays across different studies highlights the future need for a universal standardization of AMH measurement techniques. Presentation: 6/3/2024

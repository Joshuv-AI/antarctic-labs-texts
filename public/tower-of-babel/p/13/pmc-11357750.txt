Application of intelligent internet of things and interaction design in Museum Tour
Authors: Yajing Hou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The rapid development of information technology and the continuous improvement of human spiritual and cultural level have made people's acquisition of knowledge and information increasingly urgent. The information transmission method of traditional navigation systems is single and passive. This study aims to improve user experience and enhance the interactivity of museum visits. This article combines interactive technology in the Internet of Things to conduct in-depth research on the tour guide system in museums. By utilizing IoT technology, optimize the interaction design of the intelligent navigation system, and enhance the convenience and experience of visitors. In the experimental analysis, this article compared the main ticket purchasing methods of tourists, the level of understanding of museum staff about the collection, the amount of information obtained by tourists, and the satisfaction of tourists. The comparison results show that the proportion of electronic ticket purchases has increased by 35 % in terms of ticket purchasing methods. The staff's understanding of the collection has increased by 19 %, the amount of information received by tourists has increased by 12.7 %, and tourist satisfaction has also increased by 24.3 %. The conclusion indicates that the interactive design navigation system based on intelligent Internet of Things and interactive design can effectively meet the needs of different users and provide the public with a richer and deeper cultural experience.

Introduction

At present, the way of guided tours in museums can no longer meet the needs of tourists. With the continuous development of intelligent and Internet of Things technologies, new opportunities will be brought to the smart tour services of museums. With the development of information technology, museums can not only break the limitations of space and time, providing visitors with an immersive experience, but also enhance their sense of participation and interactivity through intelligent navigation and personalized recommendations [1,2]. The intelligent navigation system is a comprehensive technology that integrates multiple disciplines, allowing users to feel immersive when interacting with objects in the virtual world. Thus, the image and interest of the museum business are improved, and a personalized business model is created for the museum.

With the rapid development of Internet technology, the emergence of a series of new media, such as interactive media, crowdsourcing, Internet of Things, and natural user interface, has brought earth-shaking changes to people's way of thinking, communication, and acceptance. Traditional navigation systems often rely on fixed maps and signage, which cannot update exhibition information or display dynamic content in real time. And most of them are universal designs, lacking personalized services. Different audiences have different interests and needs, but existing systems are difficult to provide personalized recommendations and guides. Museums should rethink how to liberate the way of information transmission from simple display and interaction, open the skylight of museums through a variety of new interactive media, and break the traditional barriers of museums. In a more open and equal way to achieve “resource sharing” and promote communication with the public. IoT devices can collect audience behavior data and interest preferences, and provide personalized navigation routes and recommendations for each audience through big data analysis. Through sensors and connected devices, the Internet of Things can achieve real-time updates of exhibition information. For example, information such as changes in the location of exhibits, the addition of new exhibits, and the arrangement of exhibition activities can be instantly pushed to the audience, ensuring that they receive the latest navigation information.

In this paper, by comparing the traditional museum navigation method and the interactive design navigation method, it is found that people are more willing to choose a simplified way of buying tickets, which is simple and fast. The staff in the museum also have to get a better understanding of the exhibits in the museum, the acceptance of the information in the museum has also been greatly improved, and the tourists' viewing of the exhibition has also been greatly satisfied. It is more able to meet people's needs for culture and pass on a good culture.

Interaction design and museum

Interaction design, also known as interaction design. It is an interaction mechanism based on user experience. It integrates the user's background, experience and feeling of use to design products that meet the needs of end users. It enables end-users to use the product happily, logically, efficiently and effectively [18].

Human life is based on communication. Image, language and written information are the most basic means of communication and the most basic means of information exchange. Humans from the very beginning use their senses, imagination, emotions and knowledge to communicate to some extent with the products and environments around them. So, interactive design refers to designing interactive digital products that can help us in our daily work and life. Specifically, interaction design is created to enhance and expand the ways or interactive spaces in which people work, communicate, and interact. Interaction design is a multidisciplinary research field, which covers information structure design, visual communication design, industrial design, cognitive psychology and ergonomics, user experience design, human-machine interface design, etc.

Museum guide system

In the navigation system of the museum, a three-layer network structure is adopted, as shown in Fig. 3 [22]. On this basis, the whole system is divided into three independent parts. In this way, the functions of each module can be made clearer, the degree of coupling of the system can be reduced, and the operating efficiency of the system can be improved.Fig. 3The three-tier architecture of the navigation system.Fig. 3

The three-tier architecture of the navigation system.

The presentation layer is an interface that enables users to interact with the backend database [23]. All user input and output are carried out at this level. Therefore, in the navigation system, there is an interaction between the user and the background.

The business layer is an intermediate layer, which means that users can provide services directly to users. All business logic functions are required by the user in the presentation layer. In the process of processing requests, in order to ensure the normal operation of the system, the business layer must call the data layer. This layer includes functions such as information release, voice interpretation, positioning and exhibition navigation.

The system support layer includes the services required by the third party in the positioning system, such as map SDK, third-party application program interface, etc.

The main function of the data layer is database and data storage [24]. This layer only involves basic operations such as data saving, deletion, addition and query, and does not focus on business logic. In all audio-visual file processing procedures, audio-visual file data and video data are shared by users in the library.

Functional design of the navigation system. The system mainly includes two categories: client terminal and background data terminal. The client is mainly responsible for the user's operations, while the background data terminal is responsible for processing the service requests sent by the client and other application services. The design of the intelligent navigation function has two main contents: the voice commentary in the client and the intelligent route recommendation in the back end [25]. Interactive design tour form and traditional tour form

Experimental setup

Simulation design

This article selects the Shanghai Museum of Natural History as the research site, and divides tourists into two groups: interactive design tourism form and traditional tourism form, with 200 sample subjects in each group for comparative study. The research focuses on comparing the differences between two groups of tourists in ticket purchasing methods, collection management, information reception, and satisfaction with tour guides.

Datasets

The data collection was conducted in the form of a questionnaire survey, with closed and open questions designed, covering the evaluation of tourist satisfaction with tour guides, information acceptance, ticket purchasing methods, and collection management. The questionnaire is distributed through online platforms and on-site paper versions to ensure sample diversity and recovery rate. On site paper questionnaires are collected when tourists leave the venue, while online questionnaires are distributed and collected through email and social media links.

Parameter specification

The questionnaire data is analyzed using descriptive statistics to calculate the frequency of responses to various questions, and to display the concentration and dispersion of data such as satisfaction ratings, information acceptance ratings, and the proportion of ticket purchase methods.

Development of performance indicators

Count data is expressed in n (%); Use chi square test for inter group comparison; Quantitative data is expressed as mean ± standard deviation, and the comparison between two groups is performed using t-test. P < 0.05 indicates significant differences.

Reproducibility of the proposed model

The design and methodology of this experiment aim to ensure the scientific and reproducibility of the results, so as to effectively compare the impact of interactive design tourism forms and traditional tourism forms on tourist experiences.

Baseline method

This article adopts a baseline design based on the above methods, aiming to reveal the actual impact of the transformation of museum tourism forms on tourist behavior and satisfaction, and provide reference basis for similar research in the future.

Conclusion

This paper mainly compares the traditional navigation method and the interactive navigation method under the Internet. The comparison is mainly made in four aspects: the way people buy tickets, the knowledge of the staff in the museum about the collection, the amount of information received by tourists, and the satisfaction of tourists. The results have shown that in terms of the way of buying tickets, the number of people who buy tickets on-site is very small, and most tourists buy tickets through the Internet. In the new type of tour guide, the staff in the museum have also significantly increased the understanding of the collection, the acceptance of tourists' information has increased by 12.7 %, and the satisfaction of tourists has increased by 24.3 %. It greatly shows that in the new guide way, people will like and accept the interactive design guide way more, and the sense of participation of tourists in the guided museum will also increase, which is more in line with people's cultural needs. The inadequacy of this paper is that it does not explain the level of people's needs, considering the length and time of the article, and in the similarity of tourists, only one method is used, and all three methods are not described. It is also hoped that the guided tour mode of human-computer interaction will be arranged in the museum in the future, which can better meet the needs of tourists for culture, and also allow a better inheritance of traditional culture, so that more people can understand the country's long-standing culture. This study also has certain limitations. The museum visit system based on intelligent Internet of Things and interactive design relies on stable network connections and device operation, and requires the collection and processing of visitor data, such as location information, browsing history, etc. Without proper protection or explicit consent from visitors, these data can easily become a risk of privacy breach. For visitors who are not proficient in using smart devices or unable to access the information provided by the system, it can lead to social fairness issues and restricted access rights. In future research, it is necessary to comprehensively consider issues such as visitor privacy protection, accessibility needs, and social equity. Augmented reality technology should be used to enhance the exhibition effectiveness and participation of museums. Regular user surveys, data analysis, and usage statistics should be conducted to promptly identify and solve problems in system operation, optimize system functionality and user experience, improve the level of museum exhibitions, and promote innovation and popularization of cultural education.

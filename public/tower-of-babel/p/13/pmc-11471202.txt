An assessment of the environmental radiation risk from the petrologic units of north-eastern Nigeria; an insight from aero-radiometric data interpretation
Authors: Abubakar Yusuf, Lim Hwee San, Mustapha Adejo Mohammed, Ibrahim Saleh Bute, Adeyinka Olasehinde, Ali Garba Mohammed, Ibrahim Abdulkarim Kwami, Abdulwahab Muhammad Bello, Musa Bappah Usman, Mohammed Bashir Sulaiman, Auwalu Dalha, Usman Abubakar, Shehu Mukkafa, Jonathan Barka, Miranda Nchang Mboringong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Information regarding the radiation risk posed by radioactive emissions from the petrologic units in North eastern Nigeria is extremely limited. Consequently, there has been little research conducted on the environmental impact of radiation exposure from different petrologic units in this region. Hence, the 13 petrologic components of the area that comprises of the migmatites-gneiss (MG), banded gneiss (BG), biotite-hornblende granites (OGe), porphyritic biotite-hornblende granites (OGp), charnockytes (Ch), ignimbrites (JYG), basalts (bb), Keri-Keri Formation (KK), Gombe Sandstones (GS), Pindiga Formation (PS), Yolde Formation (YL), Bima Sandstones (BS), and alluvium (AL) deposits were assessed using aero-radiometric data (equivalent uranium, equivalent thorium, and percentage potassium radio-elements) acquired by Nigerian geological survey agency (NGSA) in 2009. The objective of this study is to compute the amount of radiation across the various rock units of the study area, and examine their radiation risk parameters. The results of this study showed variable levels of radiation across the various rock units examined. The average total activity concentration values recorded ranges from 261.67 ± 447.12 Bq/Kg across KK rock unit to 1482.02 ± 447.12 Bq/Kg across OGp/OGe rock types of the study area. Moreover, the average radium equivalent (Rn eq), absorbed dose rates (D) estimated ranges from 113.83±461.33 Bq/Kg to 305.19 ± 58.51 Bq/Kg, and 51.88 ± 29.65 nGyh−1 to 144.94 ± 29.65 nGyh−1 along KK Formation, OGp, and same KK Formation, and OGp respectively. The average environmental radiation hazard parameters of the annual effective dose rate (AEDE), activity utilization index (AUI), respiratory dysfunction parameters (RH in, RH out), annual gonad dose equivalent (AGDE), and excess life cancer risk (ELCR) measured across all rock units were found to be within or above their respective acceptable limits of 1 mSv/y, 300 μSv/y, and 0.29 × 10−3. Additionally, based on the conspicuously higher hazardous radiation (D, Rn eq, AEDE AGDE, RH in, RH out, and ELCR) emissions estimated from the OGp, OGe, MG, BG, and Ch petrologic units, the inhabitants of these localities should therefore limit themselves from excessive exposure to these rocks. Therefore, periodic monitoring of these areas is recommended.

Introduction

Radioactivity involves the conversion of mass into energy via spontaneous disintegration of primary radionuclides [1]. Thus, environmental radioactivity deals with the understanding (concept) of how humans interact with naturally available radiation owing to the spontaneous disintegration of radio-elements within an environment [2]. Humans can interact with these radiations either through terrestrial means (rocks, building materials, soil, water, and atmospheric emissions) or through extra-terrestrial means (such as cosmic rays). Food chains and direct inhalation are possible ways through which radiation is transferred to human bodies [3].

Crustal earth materials consist of radionuclides, the abundance of which contributes significantly to the quantity of background radiation within the earth's environment [4]. Background radioactive emissions are derived mainly from primary radionuclides, principally 238U, 232Th, and 40 K radio-elements, and their associated by-products [4]. Moreover, other minor sources of radiation and human-induced radiation also contribute to total background radioactive emissions in the Earth's environment [4].

The distribution of radioactive elements within the earth's crust and mantle is a major basis for several geophysical and geochemical investigations [1]. The amount of naturally occurring radioactive heat emitted by crustal rocks is a major ground for geothermal exploration on Earth's surface. It also forms a special basis for understanding temperature variation with depth within the crust [1]. Soil/lithological mapping for environmental exploration is also performed based on the quantity of radio-elements distributed within the Earth's crustal materials occurring a few meters below the surface [2].

Radio-spectrometric surveys have been widely applied in various studies, such as lithological mapping based on radionuclide contents/distribution in rocks ([5,6], and [7]), radiogenic heat production (RHP) for geothermal potential evaluation [1,2,[8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]], geochemical studies [14], and uranium migration estimations within different formations [6,15,16], among many others. Moreover, radio spectrometry was previously used to estimates the different environmental hazards associated with emissions from the different terrestrial materials (rocks, soils) and in different geologic settings of the world [[17], [18], [19], [20]], and [21].

Excessive exposure to radioactive emissions that have exceeded the safe and acceptable limits for absorption by human bodies leads to sicknesses, including loss of hair, fever, lung cancer, blood cancer, gonad damage, gene pool damage, and mental retardation of the brain, which is attributed to exposure to terrestrial radiation by pregnant women ([4,[22], [23], [24]]).

Previously, several locations/terrains in Nigeria have been investigated for their RHP potentials or environmental hazard assessment. These investigations utilized either ground-radiometric or airborne acquired data approaches [4], [[25], [26], [27], [28], [29], [30], [31], [32]], and [33]. The data used in these studies were acquired using various spectrometric equipment such as Geiger Muller counters and Canberra Series 10 plus multichannel analyzers. These investigations primarily focused on smaller localities in southwestern Nigeria. Similarly [2,9], conducted a radiogenic heat estimation of the petrologic units of north western Nigeria using airborne acquired radiometric data. However, the study was silent on the environmental radiation risk of the rocks around the area.

However, until the present study, north eastern Nigerian terrain (including the study area) have been the least studied in terms of RHP and environmental radiation risk assessments. The only regional-extent RHP study conducted around north eastern Nigeria is a recent study by Ref. [8], who used airborne radiometric data to reveal the geothermal prospects of these rock units. However, this study is confined to the RHP evaluation of the various rock units. Hence, this study focuses on the estimation of radiation risk parameters due to emissions from various rock units in the study area. Therefore, the present study was conducted with the objective of computing the relevant environmental radiation hazard parameters: annual effective dose rate (AEDE), activity utilization index (AUI), respiratory dysfunction parameters (RH in, RH out), annual gonad dose equivalent (AGDE), and excess life cancer risk (ELCR) across all rock units found in the research area.

The study area

The study area stretches from longitudes 10°00′ E to 12°00′ E and latitudes 9°30′ N to 11°30′ N. (Fig. 1). Physiographically, the area has highly elevated terrain around the western and eastern parts of the study area. The central parts of the area occupied by the Gongola Basin display a low topography. Pockets of highly elevated terrain are also displayed within the sedimentary Gongola basin, especially the Billiri-Kaltungo inliers found around the southeastern parts of the basin. The area also portrays a dendritic pattern of drainage, with river and stream channels traversing both the sedimentary and basement rock outcrops. Geologic setting

The geological setting of the study area has been described in detailed by Ref. [8]. The area is underlain primarily by the Precambrian granitoids, tertiary volcanic, and cretaceous to recent sedimentary succession of the Gongola basin (Fig. 1). The Precambrian basement rocks includes the pre-pan-African basement rocks comprised of migmatites-gneiss (MG), banded gneiss (BG), which usually are relicts in the pan-African rocks. The pan-African granitoids in the area include; the biotite-hornblende granites (OGe), porphyritic biotite-hornblende granites (OGp), and charnockytes (Ch). The tertiary volcanic rocks occur in the pre-existing basement and sedimentary successions of the Gongola basin (Fig. 1).

The central part of the study area has a N-S oriented depression with sedimentary succession deposited within the cretaceous to tertiary of earth's history. These includes; the continental Bima sandstones, transitional Yolde Formation, the marine Pindiga Formation, Gombe sandstones, Keri-Keri Formations, and the recent alluvium sediments (Fig. 1). The sedimentary successions listed earlier (with the exception of the Keri-Keri Formation) were uplifted, tilted, and folded by the Santonian orogeny that affected the entire Benue basin of Nigeria [34,35].

Materials and methods

Secondary airborne spectrometric data acquired by the NGSA in 2006–2007 and used in the RHP study by Ref. [8] paper was deployed in the computations of the environmental hazard risk parameters for each petrology of northeastern Nigerian terrain. An equivalent uranium (eU), equivalent thorium (eTh), and percentage potassium (%K) concentration measurements were used to derive the activity concentrations, radium equivalent, absorbed dose rate, respiratory dysfunction parameters (RH in and RH out), ACᵧ, AUI, AEDE, AGDE, and ELCR hazards indices following standard procedures to be explained further (later).

During data acquisition between 2006 and 2007, Fugro-air services employed a GPX 1024/256 multi-channel gamma-ray spectrometer (detector) that was attached to an aircraft and was flown over the study area at an altitude of 80 m, and flight lines that were 500 m apart, plus tie-line intervals of 2000 m. The spectrometer used has 2 detectors (sensors) viz; primary and secondary sensors. The function of the primary sensor is to measure/estimate the amount of terrestrial radiation emanating from land sources. While, the secondary sensor enables the measurement of atmospheric radiation (Radon). The primary sensor encloses 3 bundles of sensors (detectors). Each of the 3 sensors found in the primary detector has 4 crystals made up of Sodium-Iodide (NaI) that are Thallium activated (TI). Moreover, the primary sensors have a total area of 256 cubic-inches with total volume of 16.78 L. The secondary sensor enclosed 2 crystals that are equally made up of NaI, which are equally Thallium activated with a total volume of 8.39 L. In order to obtain a spectrally stable system, a hot, thermally stable-vessel was used in enclosing each of the sensor bundles.

Subsequently, the gamma-ray spectrometer was then used to acquire radiation spectrum after 1 s intervals. In order to check data quality, some sets of pre-processing, and processing measures were taken, these include; signals merger, identification of spurious or missing data, and data validation etc. After data acquisition, the results (equivalent uranium, equivalent thorium, and percentage potassium concentrations) are presented in form of grids covering individual blocks defined by coordinates and are presented (viewed) using geosoft-oasis Montaj-software.

Statistical parameters such as mean (X), minimum (Min.), maximum (Max.) and standard deviation (S.D) values of the hazard's parameters were computed for all rock units of the area using Microsoft excel. The computed environmental radiation risk parameters earlier listed were plotted against their corresponding geographical coordinates into a spatial distribution view map using ‘minimum curvature’ methods of data gridding prior to generating their maps in the Oasis Montaj environments.

Conclusion

Information about the relatively unknown radiological hazards surrounding the northeastern geological terrain of Nigeria has been evaluated, and facts about them have been revealed.

The radiological hazard parameters of activity concentration, radium equivalent (R eq), absorbed dose rates (D), annual effective dose equivalent (AEDE), annual gonad dose equivalent (AGDE), respiratory dysfunction parameters (RH In & RH Ex), and excess life cancer risk (ELCR) of the 13-outlined rock units constituting the present study area were estimated from the analysis of the available high-resolution aero-radiometric data covering the area.

The estimated radiological parameters showed a variable measurement signature across all rock units of the area.

The OGe, OGp, BG, and MG rock units of the study area revealed high radiological hazard indices of activation concentrations, AEDE, AGDE, RH In & RH Ex, D, and ELCR. However, other rock units that displayed low radiological hazard signatures included the AL, KK, GS, and PS.

Thus, the overall average radio-elements activity concentrations of northeastern petrologic units are; 693.21 ± 14.53, 66.49 ± 14.53, and 44.61 ± 14.53 BgKg−1 for K, Th, and U elements respectively. The above-listed average K and Th activity concentration values estimated were comparatively greater than the world standard values of 420 and 33 BgKg−1 respectively. The average annual gonad dose equivalent (AGDE) and excess life cancer risk (ELCR) values recorded for the entire rocks of the area are comparatively higher than the global permissible average of 300 μSv/y and 0.29 × 10−3 respectively.

The higher values portrayed by the petrologic units highlighted the need for limited interaction (utilization) with the OGe, OGp, BG, and MG rock units, especially during the construction of civil engineering structures such as houses, bridges, roads, and schools. Therefore, periodic monitoring of the radiation risk level in this area is recommended. In addition, radiation surveys should be carried out before, and during major construction activities and appropriate steps should be taken to protect workers and the environment in cases where the levels are above the acceptable limits.

Strasberg severity predicts 30-day loss of bile duct patency after ERCP in post-cholecystectomy bile duct injury
Authors: Anung Noto Nugroho, Ida Bagus Budhi Surya Adnyana, Didik Prasetyo, Triyanta Y Pramana, Aritantri Darmayani, Febriagi Bayu Aji, Nafa Unnisa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim: This study evaluated the prognostic value of Strasberg classification on 30-day bile duct patency and explored relevant clinical and procedural factors.Background: Bile duct injury (BDI) is a serious complication of cholecystectomy that may influence early outcomes after endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP).Methods: We retrospectively reviewed 33 patients with BDI treated with ERCP at Dr. Moewardi Hospital from January 2023 to May 2025. Data included demographics, laboratory results, cholecystectomy type, timing of diagnosis and repair, and injury severity (minor: A–C; major: D–E). The primary outcome was bile duct patency at 30 days, assessed by imaging and clinical indicators. Statistical analyses included chi-square, Fisher’s exact, Kaplan–Meier survival, and ROC curves.Results: Patients had a mean age of 45.9 years; 54.5% were female, and 54.5% had laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Minor and major injuries were observed in 51.5% and 48.5%, respectively. 63.6% achieved bile duct patency by day 30. No significant associations were found between demographic, laboratory, procedural variables, and either injury severity or outcomes (all p>0.05). Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed significantly longer patency in minor injuries compared to major injuries (mean 25.88 vs. 20.88 days, p=0.030).Conclusion: This study highlights Strasberg classification as an independent predictor of early bile duct patency after ERCP. Other common clinical factors did not significantly influence outcomes. These findings underscore the importance of Strasberg classification not only for injury assessment but also for guiding early postoperative management and monitoring. Larger prospective studies are needed to confirm these results and to optimize prognostic tools for BDI care.Key Words: Bile duct injury, Strasberg, Early outcome, Bile duct patency

Introduction

The Strasberg classification is the most widely used system to describe the anatomical severity of BDI and is a reference in surgical decision-making in the hepatobiliary field (1). However, the existing scientific evidence regarding the prognostic relevance of the Strasberg system largely focuses on long-term outcomes after the reconstruction procedure, including the patentability rate many years after hepaticojejunostomy (2, 3). In contrast, the relevance of the Strasberg system to short-term outcomes after ERCP remains unclear, even though ERCP is very often performed in BDI and its success varies meaningfully depending on the complexity of the injury (4, 5).

A clearer understanding of whether the anatomical severity of BDI is related to biliary duct patency for 30 days after ERCP is clinically important. This has the potential to support risk stratification early on, guide the intensity of postoperative monitoring, and help identify patients who may require closer supervision or early surgical reconstruction than prolonged endoscopic management.

Based on these knowledge gaps, this study aimed to evaluate the relationship between the severity of the injury based on the Strasberg classification and loss of biliary duct patency within 30 days after ERCP in patients with post-cholecystectomy biliary duct injury.

Methods

A retrospective observational study was conducted at Dr. Moewardi Hospital and included all patients with post-cholecystectomy BDI who underwent ERCP between January 2023 and May 2025. Eligible patients had BDI confirmed by imaging and ERCP and completed 30-day follow-up. Patients with incomplete clinical data, loss to follow-up, or pre-existing chronic biliary disease were excluded.

Demographic data (age, gender), laboratory parameters (serum AST, ALT, total bilirubin), cholecystectomy approach (laparoscopic vs. open), timing of diagnosis (early ≤7 days vs. late >7 days), and timing of repair (immediate ≤3 days, early 4-42 days, delayed >42 days) were extracted from medical records. Injury severity was classified according to the Strasberg classification and dichotomized as minor (A-D) or major (E) (6).

The primary outcome was defined as loss of bile duct patency within 30 days after ERCP. Loss of patency was determined by evidence of blockage on MRCP or PTC, accompanied by symptoms such as abdominal pain, cholangitis (according to Tokyo guidelines) (7), abnormal liver function tests, or the requirement for additional interventions (dilatation, stenting, repeat ERCP, surgery, or drainage). Patients without obstructive findings or the need for additional procedures during follow-up were considered to have maintained patency.

Continuous variables were compared using the independent t-test or Mann–Whitney U test as appropriate, and categorical variables using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test. Time to loss of patency was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier analysis, and differences between minor and major injuries were assessed with the log-rank test. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 33 patients with post-cholecystectomy bile duct injury were included in this study. The mean age was 45.9 years, and 54.5% were female. At 30-day evaluation following ERCP, 21 patients (63.6%) maintained bile duct patency, whereas 12 patients (36.4%) lost patency.

Comparative analysis demonstrated no statistically significant differences in age, sex, pre-procedural liver enzymes (AST/ALT), total bilirubin, operative approach (laparoscopic vs open cholecystectomy), timing of diagnosis, or timing of repair in relation to 30-day patency status (all p > 0.05). In contrast, Strasberg injury severity exhibited a significant association with early loss of patency. Patients with major injuries experienced a markedly higher rate of loss of bile duct patency compared to those with minor injuries (75.0% vs 25.0%; p = 0.021).

The Kaplan-Meier survival analysis corroborates these findings by showing a loss of previous bile duct patency in the major injury group. The median loss of bile duct patency was 24 days for major injury, whereas in the minor injury group, it showed that more than half of the patients in this subgroup remained patent at the end of the 30-day follow-up period (log-rank p = 0.030) (Table 1 and Figure 1).

Discussion

The report highlights that the anatomical severity of bile duct injury, as classified by Strasberg, is a key determinant of early bile outcomes after ERCP. While bile duct injury remains one of the most serious complications of cholecystectomy, particularly laparoscopic procedures (6), short-term prognostic factors remain poorly defined. Our findings suggest that only Strasberg major is associated with loss of patency within 30 days after endoscopic management compared to other parameters.

Demographic and laboratory variables, including age, sex, AST/ALT levels, and bilirubin, showed no significant association with 30-day loss of bile duct patency. This observation is consistent with the current understanding that liver enzymes primarily reflect cholestasis or acute inflammatory response, rather than the structural severity of ductal injury (8, 9). Although prior literature has reported age-related risks of post-ERCP complications such as cholangitis and readmission (8, 9), the present study suggests that biochemical and demographic characteristics alone do not predict early loss of patency, emphasizing the need for anatomical rather than biochemical prognostication in the early postoperative period.

Similarly, operative and temporal factors including type of cholecystectomy, timing of diagnosis, and timing of repair did not correlate with loss of patency outcomes. While laparoscopic procedures have historically been associated with higher injury incidence (10, 11), severity and early repair response appear to depend more on anatomical disruption than operative modality. This aligns with contemporary analyses reporting that reconstruction success in bile duct injury is driven by sepsis control, nutritional status, and operator expertise rather than the operative approach or repair timing alone (1, 12, 13). Meta-analytic evidence further supports that early versus delayed ERCP generates similar short-term patency rates provided clinical stability is achieved before intervention (14–16)

The most important finding from this study is the strong and early prognostic impact of Strasberg severity. Patients with major injuries (E classification) demonstrated substantially higher and earlier loss of patency within 30 days, whereas median loss of patency was not reached in the minor injury group. This aligns with previous evidence that major injuries involving complete transection, devascularization, or segmental loss of ductal tissue result in higher complication rates and technically challenging repairs (17, 18). While prior studies have focused primarily on long-term outcomes following hepaticojejunostomy or reconstructive surgery (19), these findings expand the knowledge by showing that the impact of Strasberg's severity assessment was evident within a month of the first surgery. This reinforces the usefulness of Strasberg severity as an initial risk stratification tool rather than just a descriptive anatomical classification.

These results generate clear implications for clinical management. Patients with major Strasberg injuries should be considered a high-risk subgroup, warranting intensified follow-up during the first month, including earlier symptom reassessment, repeat imaging or endoscopic evaluation as clinically indicated, and proactive planning for surgical reconstruction if endoscopic support proves insufficient. Conversely, minor injuries appear to respond favorably to decompression and stenting, suggesting that routine follow-up at standard intervals is reasonable for this group. Nevertheless, the retrospective design and limited cohort size constrain statistical power and caution against broad generalization; multicenter prospective studies are needed to validate these findings and identify additional modifiers of early outcome.

Conclusion

The severity of bile duct injury based on the Strasberg classification is the only factor associated with early biliary outcome after ERCP. Major injuries demonstrate a substantially higher and earlier risk of loss of patency within 30 days, while demographic, biochemical, and procedural variables do not influence patency. These findings underscore the need for intensified early surveillance in patients with major injuries and routine follow-up in those with minor injuries.

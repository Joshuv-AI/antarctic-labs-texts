Time trend of sugar-sweetened beverages intake from 2002 to 2022 among adults in Beijing, China
Authors: Rui Deng, Jiali Duan, Bo Yu, Yan Zhang, Yihong Yao, Rui Ma, Yimei Sha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo explore changes in sugar-sweetened beverages (SSB) intake from 2002 to 2022 among Beijing adults.MethodsData were obtained from the Beijing subset of the Chinese Nutrition and Health Surveillance (2002, 2010–2013, 2015–2017 and 2022). SSB intake was assessed via 12-month Food Frequency Questionnaire. Binary logistic and linear regression were fitted with survey year included as both linear and quadratic terms to examine nonlinear patterns.ResultsA total of 7261 adults from four waves were included. After adjustments for demographic characteristics, prevalence of SSB intake exhibited a potential non-monotonic pattern (βlinear > 0, βquadratic < 0; P < 0.05), increasing from 34.6% in 2002 to 56.6% in 2010–2013, and subsequently appeared to stabilize around 44.7% by 2022. An opposite non-linear fluctuation (both βlinear < 0, βquadratic > 0; P < 0.05) was observed for daily SSB intake levels among both the total population and consumers. Estimates decreased from 59.7 mL and 172.6 mL in 2002 to a nadir in 2015–2017 (32.8 mL and 73.5 mL, respectively), and increased to 64.4 mL and 144.0 mL in 2022.ConclusionsDivergent patterns in prevalence and levels of SSB intake may point toward an increased consumption among existing consumers in Beijing.Keywords: Sugar-sweetened beverages, Daily intake, Temporal pattern, Adults

Introduction

Sugar-sweetened beverages (SSB) are liquids sweetened with added sugars such as mono- or disaccharides (Alves-Costa et al., 2025; Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2024). Evidence has identified SSBs as the primary source of added sugar in the diet of residents in many countries, such as the United States, Australia and Europe (Azaïs-Braesco et al., 2017; Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, 2024; Varsamis et al., 2017). Compared to solid foods, SSBs have lower nutritional value, lack offsetting nutritional benefits and provide less satiety (Malik and Hu, 2019; Popkin and Hawkes, 2016). Consequently, higher consumption of these energy-dense SSBs is often associated with excessive sugar intake. For these reasons, SSB intake has been associated with dental caries, obesity, hypertension, diabetes, some cancers and other chronic diseases (Heo et al., 2024; Qin et al., 2020; Valenzuela et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2023).

Worldwide, SSB intake poses a significant public health concern (Lara-Castor et al., 2023). Global SSB intake has increased by 0.37 servings/week (95% uncertainty interval [UI]: 0.29–0.47; +15.9%) from 1990 to 2018 (Lara-Castor et al., 2023), and has been projected to increase by 9.54% (95% UI: 9.08%–10.00%) by 2050 (Alves-Costa et al., 2025). Specifically, in the United Staes, although the prevalence of SSB intake has declined steadily from 2003 to 2018 (non-Hispanic White: 72.3% to 65.3%; Hispanic: 84.6% to 77.8%), overall intake level remained high (Dai et al., 2021). A similar trend—decreasing but remaining high—was observed in Canada (Jones et al., 2019). Although SSB intake in Asia remains below the global average (Lara-Castor et al., 2023), long-term trend analyses specifically focusing on Asian countries remain limited.

China, as one of the world's most populous country, has faced a growing public health concern over the rising consumption of SSBs. From 1990 to 2019, the total number of deaths attributable to excessive SSB consumption in China nearly doubled, with a corresponding doubling of disability-adjusted life years (Zhao et al., 2025). Furthermore, projections indicated that the associated costs will escalate from 8.7 billion Chinese yuan to 362.331 billion if the mortality rate due to excessive SSB intake reaches 1‰ (Zhao et al., 2025). Despite this alarming burden, up-to-date evidence on SSB intake levels and long-term trends among Chinese adults remains limited. To the best of our knowledge, the most recent study on SSB intake trends among Chinese adults analyzed data from 2004 to 2009, which is insufficient to capture long-term patterns and is now outdated (Li et al., 2014). Furthermore, subgroup variations (e.g., by age, income, or education) critical for targeted interventions have rarely been thoroughly examined.

Utilizing data from the Chinese Nutrition and Health Surveillance (CNHS), this study aimed to: (1) characterize changes in SSB intake across four survey waves from 2002 to 2022 among Beijing adults; (2) identify key demographic subgroups with high SSB intake; (3) preliminarily explore the most consumed SSB type among Beijing adults. This study will provide theoretical basis for designing and prioritizing SSB reduction policies in Beijing.

Methods

Data source

Data in this study were extracted from the Beijing subset of the CNHS. Led by the National Health Commission (formerly the Ministry of Health), the CNHS comprises a series of cross-sectional surveys employing a multi-stage stratified cluster random sampling method. Each survey wave adhered to a standardized manual, with all investigators undergoing rigorous training prior to fieldwork (Yu et al., 2021). In this study, we used the Beijing subset data collected in 2002, 2010–2013, 2015–2017 and 2022. The number of surveillance sites in these waves was three, four, seven, and five, respectively. The site selection accounted for geographic location and urban-rural stratification to ensure regional representativeness. This analysis was based on a de-identified secondary dataset provided by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, which had already been pre-processed to include participants with dietary survey data and basic demographic information (n = 7323). After excluding participants under 18 years of age and those with incomplete data on SSB consumption (n = 62), a total of 7261 adults from four waves were included. Ethical approval for this study was granted by the Ethics Committee of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (No. 2022–008, 201519-B), and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Data collection

Participants' basic characteristics, including age (18–29 years; 30–44 years; 45–59 years; over 60 years), sex (male; female), area of residence (urban; rural), education level (never attended school; junior high school or below; high school or above), annual household income (<10,000 Chinese yuan/year; ≥10,000 Chinese yuan /year; refused to answer) and occupation status (unemployed; employed), were collected by standardized questionnaires.

Data on the intake of various SSBs were obtained through face-to-face interviews using a validated Food Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ) (Liu et al., 2018). SSBs were defined as sugar-sweetened versions of fruit juice beverages, freshly squeezed fruit and vegetable juice, carbonated beverages, coffee, tea drinks, lactic acid bacteria beverages, formulated milk beverages, plant protein beverages, special purpose beverages, and other sweetened beverages. Unsweetened beverages (e.g., plain tea) were not included. Participants were asked to recall their consumption of different types of SSBs over the previous 12 months. If intake was reported, the consumption of frequency (times/day, times/week, times/month, or times/year) and the average portion size (servings or milliliters, mL) were recorded. In 2002, a standard portion size was defined for each beverage category (1 serving = 250 mL) to facilitate calculation of daily intake (mL). In the subsequent three waves, intake was recorded directly in mL, assuming a specific gravity of 1.0 g/mL (i.e. 1 g = 1 mL).

Due to the long-time span covered by the surveys, SSB types collected expanded progressively across the four waves. In 2002, information was collected only on fruit juice beverages and other beverages. By the 2010–2013 wave, the items were extended to include fruit juice beverages, freshly squeezed fruit and vegetable juice, carbonated beverages, coffee, tea drinks, lactic acid bacteria beverages, and formulated milk beverages. Although the 2015–2017 wave temporarily omitted tea drinks and formulated milk beverages, the questionnaire reached its most comprehensive coverage in 2022, adding plant protein and special-purpose beverages to all previously surveyed categories.

Definition

SSB consumers were defined as individuals who reported consuming any type of SSBs during the past 12 months. Accordingly, the prevalence of SSB intake (%) referred to the proportion of consumers within the total study population. The daily SSB intake level in the total population (mL) was defined as the average daily volume of SSB intake in all participants, including consumers and non-consumers, with the intake of non-consumers assigned a value of zero. The daily SSB intake level among consumers (mL) was defined as the average daily volume of SSB intake exclusively among SSB consumers. Both intake levels accounted for all surveyed SSB types.

Statistical analysis

The prevalence of SSB intake was described as N (%). Daily SSB intake levels among the total population and consumers were described using mean ± standard deviation, combined with median (1st quartile, 3rd quartile). Differences in demographic distributions across the four waves were examined by the chi-square test. First, in order to examine the changes of SSB prevalence and intake levels over time, binary logistic regression (for prevalence of SSB intake) and linear regression (for SSB intake level) were fitted with survey year included as both a linear (reported as βlinear) and a quadratic term (reported as βquadratic). βlinear represented the baseline linear trend, while βquadratic represented the curvature (acceleration or deceleration) over time. All regression models were adjusted for sex, age, area of residence, education level, annual household income and occupation status to account for potential confounding. To ensure the robustness of our findings, given the inconsistent definitions of SSBs across survey waves, sensitivity analyses were conducted by restricting the beverage types to those consistently tracked across either all four waves or the last three waves. Second, similar analyses were repeated for subgroups with different characteristics to identify those with high SSB intake. Third, daily intake levels of each SSB category were described as mean ± standard deviation (mL), stratified by total population, males, and females, to identify the most consumed SSB type among Beijing adults. All the statistical analyses were conducted using SAS (Version 9.4), with statistical significance defined as a two-sided P value <0.05.

Results

Basic characteristics

A total of 7261 participants were included in the final analysis, with sample sizes of 1436, 1288, 1902 and 2635 across the four surveys (2002, 2010–2013, 2015–2017 and 2022, respectively). As summarized in Table 1, baseline characteristics differed significantly across the survey years (P < 0.05), with the exception of sex. Over the study period, the proportion of participants aged ≥60 years, rural residents, and those who were employed, higher educated or had an annual household income over 10,000 Chinese yuan increased significantly (all P < 0.05).

Changes in prevalence of SSB intake among Beijing adults from 2002 to 2022, overall and by demographic subgroups

As summarized in Table 2, the prevalence of SSB intake exhibited a non-monotonic pattern (βlinear = 1.37, βquadratic = −0.25, both P < 0.01). The estimates initially increased from 34.6% in 2002 to 56.6% in 2010–2013, and then declined to 44.6% in 2015–2017, and remained stable thereafter at 44.7% in 2022. Similar pattern was observed across most subgroups, with the notable exceptions of urban residents and individuals who never attended school.

Subgroup analyses revealed that in 2002, the prevalence of SSB intake was significantly higher among females, those aged 18–29 years, urban residents, and those with higher education, income or employment status, compared with their counterparts (all P < 0.05). Similar results were observed across the subsequent three surveys, though the differences between sexes were not statistically significant.

Sensitivity analyses further supported the robustness of our main findings. When restricting the analysis to beverage types consistently defined across all four waves, the non-monotonic pattern persisted across most subgroups, though statistically insignificant (Table S1). For beverages consistently defined across the last three waves, the observed prevalence rates in 2015–2017 and 2022 remained below the 2010–2013 peak (Table S2), mirroring the declining phase identified in our primary analysis.

Changes in daily SSB intake levels among Beijing adults from 2002 to 2022, overall and by demographic subgroups

As presented in Table 3, daily SSB intake levels displayed significant non-linear patterns in both the general population (βlinear = −44.74, βquadratic = 8.78, both P < 0.05) and consumers (βlinear = −159.28, βquadratic = 28.96, both P < 0.05). The estimates kept decreasing from 59.7 mL and 172.6 mL in 2002 to a nadir in 2015–2017(32.8 mL and 73.5 mL, respectively), followed by an increase to 64.4 mL and 144.0 mL in 2022. These variations in intake levels appeared more substantial among consumers. Similar pattern was found across most demographic subgroups.

Discussion

Based on data from four survey waves in Beijing, this analysis observed a non-monotonic pattern in the prevalence of SSB intake, which peaked in 2010–2013 before declining. In contrast, the reported daily intake levels showed an inverse pattern, with a notable rebound in 2022, particularly among consumers. These findings may point toward a possible increase of SSB intake among existing consumers rather than an expansion in the proportion of consumers in Beijing. Specifically, those aged 18–29 years, urban residents, and those with higher education, income or employment status were identified as key demographic subgroups with high SSB intake. These subgroups may warrant prioritized attention in future interventions. In terms of beverage categories, tea drinks emerged as the primary type in this study.

Between 2002 and 2010–2013, the prevalence of SSB intake increased substantially while intake level among consumers decreased, possibly resulting in a relatively stable overall population-level consumption. Between 2010 and 2013 and 2015–2017, the reduction of all three indicators was observed to occur alongside national health promotion efforts and socio-economic shifts. While Beijing's per capita disposable income increased by 40% during this period, its specific role in influencing SSB intake remains to be elucidated. Concurrently, the beverage industry's shift toward unsweetened drinks–with the unsweetened tea market growing at 32.6% annually between 2014 and 2019–may have provided healthier alternatives and coincided with the observed decline. However, between 2015 and 2017 and 2022, the rebound in intake level despite a stable prevalence may suggest an increase in consumption among existing consumers. It was hypothesized that lifestyle shifts during the Coronavirus Disease 2019 pandemic in China, such as home confinement or related psychological stress, could be potential factors associated with this increase (Cai et al., 2024). However, causal attribution cannot be made from our data, and further research is needed to explore how these macro-level factors relate to the individual-level changes observed in our study.

To the best of our knowledge, few Chinese studies have examined changes in SSB intake over such a long-time span. Consistent with the rising trend in prevalence of SSB intake among Chinese adults nationwide between 2004 and 2009 reported by Li et al. (Li et al., 2014), our Beijing-based sample showed a similar increase from 2002 to 2010–2013. And the SSB intake prevalence of 56.6% in 2010–2013 in this Beijing-based sample was also comparable to the national level of 50.1% in 2010–2012 (Guo et al., 2018). Overall, the prevalence of SSB intake in this study fluctuated between 30% and 50%, substantially lower than the rates reported among U.S. adults (from 2003–2004 to 2017–2018: White: 72.3% to 65.3%; Hispanic: 84.6% to 77.8%) (Dai et al., 2021). In line with this, Lara-Castor et al. using Global Dietary Database identified China as one of the three lowest SSB-consuming countries among the 25 most populous countries worldwide (Lara-Castor et al., 2023), while the United States had higher SSB intake. Subgroup analyses revealed that the prevalence of SSB intake was higher among younger, more educated and urban adults, aligning with previous findings (Guzman-Vilca et al., 2022; Lara-Castor et al., 2023). Although these studies showed that females were less likely to consume SSBs than males, the higher prevalence observed among Beijing females in our study concurred with a prior national survey in China several years ago (Li et al., 2014).

Given the aforementioned harmful health outcomes of SSBs, the World Health Organization (WHO) recommends limiting added sugar intake to less than 10% of total daily energy, with further benefits if intake is reduced below 5% (World Health Organization, 2015). Numerous health authorities have therefore called for interventions to reduce SSB intake, such as environmental strategies (e.g. altered product placement and front-of-package health warnings) (Cohen et al., 2018; Grummon et al., 2019; Nau et al., 2018) and policy measures (e.g. SSB tax policy) (World Health Organization, 2022). Currently, Beijing's SSB policies only ban sales on school campuses, with few measures for adults or the general public. The divergent patterns in our study are consistent with the possibility that existing interventions may reduce occasional consumption but have less impact on habitual use. If causal, Beijing may need broader policies, especially measures tailored to high-volume consumers.

Several limitations of this study should be noted. First, although the data utilized in this study was from Beijing, the capital of China, the lack of representation may limit the generalizability of the findings. Second, dietary data in this study was collected using the 12-month FFQ, which was subject to recall bias. Additionally, we did not calibrate the FFQ against a reference method; therefore, the extent of measurement error could not be quantified. Third, the inconsistency of the SSB types covered across the four waves may have introduced measurements bias and limit the comparability. Fourth, given that this study relied on only four survey waves over the twenty-year period, the non-monotonic pattern may not fully capture the complex fluctuations in SSB intake. Finally, due to the repeated cross-sectional design of this study, no causal inferences can be made regarding the relationship between the observed patterns in SSB intake and any specific intervention. Therefore, the conclusion should be interpreted with caution.

This study also has several strengths. Spanning from 2002 to 2022, this study revealed long-term changes of SSB intake among Beijing adults, covering a longer time frame than previous studies on this population. Key demographic subgroups with high SSB intake were also identified, which has rarely been reported in previous studies among Beijing adults. These findings may inform the development of evidence-based policies and public health strategies aimed at reducing SSB consumption in Beijing, China.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study observed a non-monotonic fluctuation of SSB intake among Beijing adults, with prevalence initially increasing before declining, whereas daily intake levels exhibited an inverse pattern. Adults with younger age, living in the urban, higher education levels, higher income and were employed had higher prevalence and level of SSB intake. Tea drinks emerged as the primary type for Beijing adults, although variations in beverage definitions should be noted. These findings are consistent with the possibility that current interventions have limited effectiveness in reducing SSB intake among heavy consumers. Further research is needed to examine this subgroup, and if confirmed, additional measures to restrict SSB intake may be warranted.

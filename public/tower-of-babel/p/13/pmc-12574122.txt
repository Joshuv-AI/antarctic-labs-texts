Evaluation of ChatGPT-5 responses in obstetric and gynecological emergencies: concordance, readability, and clinical reliability
Authors: Hale Çetin Arslan, Kadir Arslan, Mehmet Gün, Pakize Ozge Karkın, Tugce Arslanoğlu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study aimed to evaluate the compliance with guidelines, clinical safety, and applicability of ChatGPT-5 responses in obstetric and gynecological emergency scenarios. With the increasing role of AI-powered large language models (LLMs) in healthcare, there is a need to examine their performance in obstetric emergencies systematically.MethodsThis study was designed as a prospective, scenario-based, double-blind study. A total of 15 obstetric and gynecologic emergency scenarios were created based on the literature and current international guidelines (ACOG, RCOG, WHO). Five standard questions were posed to ChatGPT-5 for each scenario: (1) Most likely diagnosis, (2) Investigations to confirm the diagnosis, (3) Hemodynamic stability assessment, (4) Initial treatment approach, and (5) Advanced management options. The same scenarios were independently answered by two obstetricians, an emergency medicine specialist, and an anesthesiologist, and were considered the “gold standard.” Responses were scored for guideline compliance, patient safety, and critical information gaps. In addition, quality and understandability were evaluated with modified DISCERN (mDISCERN), Global Quality Score (GQS), and readability indexes [Flesch Reading Ease Score (FRES), Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL), Simple Measure of Gobbledygook (SMOG), Coleman–Liau Index (CLI)].ResultsA total of 75 responses were reviewed. High agreement (5/5) was observed in 5 scenarios (33.3%), moderate agreement (4/5) in 7 scenarios (46.7%), and low agreement (≤ 3/5) in 3 scenarios (20.0%). High agreement was particularly evident for well-defined guideline algorithms, such as postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, shoulder dystocia, and ruptured ectopic pregnancy. Deficiencies in moderate agreement scenarios included insufficient emphasis on mortality risk, omission of scoring systems, incomplete steps in sepsis management, and inadequate specification of fertility-sparing approaches. Low agreement scenarios included severe vaginal hemorrhage, acute bleeding due to malignancy, and traumatic gynecologic emergencies. The mean mDISCERN score of the responses was 4.0 ± 0.7, and the mean GQS was 4.1 ± 0.7. Readability analyses showed that responses contained a moderate amount of technical language (FRES score = 40.5 ± 2.5; FKGL score = 11.6 ± 1.2; SMOG score = 10.9 ± 0.8; and CLI score = 10.9 ± 0.8). The mean lexical density was 0.63.ConclusionsChatGPT-5 generally produced moderate to good guideline-compliant and confident responses in obstetric and gynecological emergency scenarios. However, its performance was limited in complex cases requiring a multidisciplinary approach. The findings suggest that AI-powered large language models can be a complementary tool in obstetric emergency management, but should not be used alone without expert clinician supervision. Larger, comparative, and multidisciplinary studies will provide more reliable evidence for the clinical integration of these technologies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12873-025-01396-5.

Introduction

Obstetric and gynecological emergencies are among the leading causes of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality. The most common obstetric emergencies include postpartum hemorrhage, preeclampsia/eclampsia, sepsis, obstructive labor, and complicated abortion, which account for approximately 75% of all maternal deaths [1]. Therefore, rapid diagnosis, appropriate selection of tests, and guideline-compliant treatment approaches are critical in the management of obstetric emergencies.

Large language models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, developed by OpenAI and introduced as a foundation for language learning since 2022, have been integrated into various fields, including anatomy education [2], emergency medicine [3], orthopedics [4], and radiology [5]. They support clinical decision-making, enhance diagnostic reasoning, and facilitate the synthesis of medical knowledge. However, it has been noted that ChatGPT can exhibit critical errors in complex or multifaceted scenarios, underperforming in terms of treatment recommendations or clinical safety; therefore, it should be considered a complementary and supportive tool in physicians’ decision-making processes [6].

The literature on the potential use of AI in obstetric and gynecological emergencies is still limited. Existing studies have shown that AI can help reduce fetal monitoring errors during labor [7] and shorten the time to diagnose malignancies in gynecologic oncology [8]. However, surgical professionals must be aware of the limitations of AI to avoid unnecessary surgical interventions, especially in emergency settings [9]. Furthermore, there are no scenario-based, double-blind studies systematically testing these models for clinical safety and guideline compliance.

This study aims to systematically examine ChatGPT-5 responses in obstetric and gynecological emergency scenarios in terms of accuracy, guideline compliance, clinical safety, and readability. The findings aim to make an original contribution to the literature by revealing the potential role of AI-supported large language models in obstetric emergencies, their potential clinical contribution, and their current limitations.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study was designed as a prospective, scenario-based, double-blind, observational study to evaluate the performance of ChatGPT-5 in obstetric and gynecological emergency scenarios.

A total of 15 standardized clinical scenarios were developed for this study. All scenarios were created by a single obstetrician with over 10 years of clinical experience in obstetric and gynecologic emergency management. Each case was designed in accordance with current international guidelines (ACOG, RCOG, and WHO) and represented a range of high-risk conditions, including postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, uterine rupture, amniotic fluid embolism, ruptured ectopic pregnancy, and major gynecologic hemorrhages. Developing all scenarios by a single author ensured conceptual and methodological consistency across cases, while subsequent independent expert evaluations helped reduce potential subjectivity or bias. All scenarios were conducted in Turkish, as it represents the native language of the clinical setting and the standard mode of physician–patient communication in obstetric and gynecologic emergencies. Although the selected cases covered a broad clinical spectrum—from common and guideline-defined emergencies to complex, multidisciplinary situations—no formal difficulty grading was applied. The primary aim was to ensure comprehensive coverage of both obstetric and gynecologic emergencies rather than to stratify them by difficulty level. Case complexity was inherently reflected by the nature of each scenario and its management requirements.

The study was conducted between July and August 2025, and all scenarios were run using a single large language model (ChatGPT-5). All questions were asked in the same session, using the same device (a MacBook Air, M1, 2020, with 8 GB RAM and macOS Monterey), and a stable fiber internet connection (100 Mbps). All interactions with ChatGPT-5 were conducted using default system parameters (temperature = 0.7, top-p = 1.0, and maximum token length = 4096). These settings were deliberately maintained to emulate a realistic clinical user experience, reflecting how healthcare professionals would typically engage with the system in practice. A separate chat window was opened for each scenario, and only the first responses were analyzed. This is because the probabilistic nature of large language models allows for different responses to the same question across different trials. Therefore, the “regenerate response” feature was not used—a total of 15 scenarios × five questions = 75 responses were obtained.

Evaluation process

Responses generated by ChatGPT-5 were recorded and anonymized for analysis. In parallel, the same 15 clinical scenarios were independently evaluated by four clinicians: two obstetricians, one emergency medicine specialist, and one anesthesiologist.

The first obstetrician (OB1–POK) had 12 years and 4 months of clinical experience in obstetrics and gynecology, the second obstetrician (OB2–TA) had 10 years and 2 months, the emergency physician (EM–MG) had 9 years and 6 months, and the anesthesiologist (AN–KA) had 11 years and 1 month of professional experience in obstetric and emergency care. All assessors had active roles in tertiary-level hospitals and routinely managed high-acuity obstetric and gynecologic emergencies.

Each response was evaluated using a structured, guideline-based scoring system encompassing five key parameters:

Quality and readability analysis

In addition to clinical relevance, ChatGPT-5’s responses were evaluated for scientific quality and readability. The same four assessors (two obstetricians, one emergency medicine specialist, and one anesthesiologist) who conducted the primary clinical evaluations also performed the quality and readability assessments to ensure methodological consistency. None of the assessors were involved in the scenario development process. Prior to evaluation, a short calibration session was conducted in which five sample responses were jointly reviewed to establish scoring consistency. Each response was anonymized and labeled with coded identifiers (e.g., Response A, B, C) to maintain blinding. Scientific quality and clinical value were assessed using the modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) and Global Quality Scale (GQS) tools, following established criteria from the obstetrics and gynecology literature and international guidelines (ACOG, 2022; WHO, 2018).

Readability was analyzed using standardized indices—Flesch Reading Ease Score (FRES), Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL), Simple Measure of Gobbledygook (SMOG), and Coleman–Liau Index (CLI)—to determine the clarity and comprehensibility of the generated text. These measures allowed for an integrated evaluation of both the informational quality and accessibility of the AI-generated outputs.

mDISCERN is a modified version of the original DISCERN scale and is one of the most frequently used methods for assessing electronic health information [10, 11]. Each “yes” answer is worth 1 point; the total score ranges from 0 to 5. Higher scores indicate more reliable and higher-quality information. The scale consists of five questions:

Readability analysis

Response comprehensibility was assessed using various readability metrics, including the Flesch Reading Ease Score (FRES), Flesch Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL), SMOG, and Coleman–Liau Index (CLI). Additionally, the total number of words, sentences, and lexical density (meaningful word ratio) were calculated. Careful consideration was given to formulas and recommended threshold values, such as:

For general readers, it is recommended that FRES be ≥ 60 and other indices be below 7. The validity of these analyses in medical contexts has also been supported by recent research [13].

Ethical approval and participation

This study does not require ethics committee approval because it does not involve any invasive procedures on humans or animals. The study evaluated only scenario-based simulations and responses generated by artificial intelligence (AI), specifically ChatGPT-5. Furthermore, the ethical implications of using AI tools in medical education and clinical decision support processes were considered, and the principles of data confidentiality, impartiality, transparency, and scientific ethics were adhered to.

Statistical analysis

Inter-rater reliability among the four independent assessors (two obstetricians, one emergency medicine specialist, and one anesthesiologist) was evaluated using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC, two-way random effects, absolute agreement model). ICC values were interpreted as follows: <0.40 = low, 0.40–0.75 = moderate, and > 0.75 = high agreement. Each scenario was categorized according to overall concordance with expert standards as high (5/5), moderate (4/5), or low (≤ 3/5) agreement. The proportions of these categories were calculated, and 95% Wilson confidence intervals were applied to account for the binomial distribution of proportions. Scenarios with a lower bound of < 60% were interpreted as showing potentially limited diagnostic agreement. Readability and quality metrics—including the modified DISCERN (mDISCERN), Global Quality Scale (GQS), Flesch Reading Ease Score (FRES), Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL), Simple Measure of Gobbledygook (SMOG), Coleman–Liau Index (CLI), and lexical density—were computed for each scenario. Continuous variables were summarized as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range), as appropriate. All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 29.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). A two-tailed p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The agreement between ChatGPT-5 and expert responses varied across 15 obstetric and gynecologic emergency scenarios. High agreement (5/5) was observed in 5/15 cases (33.3%) (postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, shoulder dystocia, ruptured ectopic pregnancy); moderate agreement (4/5) was observed in 7/15 cases (46.7%) (amniotic fluid embolism, obstetric DIC, uterine rupture, ovarian torsion, septic abortion, tubo-ovarian abscess, ruptured ovarian cyst); and low agreement (≤ 3/5) was observed in 3/15 cases (20.0%) (severe vaginal bleeding, acute bleeding due to malignancy, traumatic gynecologic emergencies) (Table 1). In high-concordance scenarios, ChatGPT-5 made the correct diagnosis, selected the guideline-concordant initial treatment approach, and recommended appropriate further management strategies. In moderate-concordance scenarios, under-emphasis of mortality risk (amniotic fluid embolism), omission of scoring (DIC), incomplete recognition of risk factors (uterine rupture), incomplete implementation of sepsis resuscitation steps (septic abortion), and inadequate emphasis on fertility-sparing approaches (ovarian torsion) were noted. In low-concordance scenarios, omission of hemostatic agents (severe vaginal bleeding), neglect of oncologic/palliative treatments (bleeding from malignancy), and lack of multidisciplinary trauma management (pelvic trauma) were highlighted. Clinical safety was generally maintained in the high- and medium-concordance scenarios, with gaps that appeared to be due to somewhat incomplete recommendations rather than errors. The most frequently observed gaps included inadequate hemostatic agents, lack of coagulopathy protocols, and omission of trauma imaging algorithms.

Discussion

In this study, ChatGPT-5’s performance demonstrated varying levels of concordance across 15 obstetric and gynecological emergency scenarios. High agreement (33.3%) was achieved in conditions with clearly defined diagnostic and treatment algorithms—such as postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, shoulder dystocia, and ruptured ectopic pregnancy—where management pathways are standardized and extensively covered in international guidelines (ACOG, RCOG, WHO). These protocol-based conditions likely allowed ChatGPT-5 to produce structured and accurate recommendations consistent with guideline expectations. In contrast, moderate agreement (46.7%) was observed in scenarios such as amniotic fluid embolism, obstetric DIC, septic abortion, and ovarian torsion, which represent intermediate-complexity emergencies requiring contextual reasoning, dynamic prioritization, and application of scoring systems. These findings suggest that ChatGPT-5 can recognize key clinical elements but may omit nuanced details such as mortality risk assessment, sepsis bundle steps, or fertility-sparing strategies. Low agreement (20.0%) occurred in complex, multidisciplinary cases—including malignancy-related hemorrhage and traumatic gynecological injuries—where decision-making depends on surgical, oncologic, and radiologic coordination beyond text-based reasoning. This pattern aligns with recent studies reporting that large language models perform best in structured, algorithmic situations but have diminishing reliability in complex and procedural contexts [14, 15]. In our analysis, inter-rater reliability among experts was high (ICC = 0.93), and both mDISCERN and GQS scores reflected moderate-to-good scientific and informational quality, consistent with recent literature.

Readability analyses of ChatGPT-5 responses revealed that the texts contained moderately technical language (FRES score = 40.5 ± 2.5; FKGL score = 11.6 ± 1.2; SMOG = 10.9 ± 0.8; and CLI = 10.9 ± 0.8). In the literature, the readability levels of LLM outputs generally correspond to high school education levels and above, remaining above the recommended 6th-grade level. Similar to our study, the average FKGL score for AI-based informational content for gynecology patients was reported to be 10.3, and none of the significant language models reached the 6th-grade reading level required for optimal patient understanding [16]. However, ChatGPT-4’s FRES score of around 36 in retinal detachment scenarios indicated that the texts could be understood at a university level [17]. Furthermore, in studies on pelvic organ prolapse, ChatGPT responses were found to have high SMOG and FKGL scores, and readability remained above the target level. However, accuracy rates for ChatGPT responses ranged from 87.5% to 100% [18]. Simplification experiments using GPT-4o on cardiology discharge summaries have shown that readability can be significantly improved; however, achieving the recommended lower levels is not always possible [19]. Our findings further support the idea that a moderately technical style is expected due to the high density of technical terms inherent in obstetric emergencies; however, there is a need for additional simplification in patient-oriented outcomes.

In our study, ChatGPT-5 responses were found to be of moderate to high quality (mDISCERN mean 4.0 ± 0.7, GQS mean 4.1 ± 0.7). These results are consistent with evaluations of LLMs conducted in various clinical areas in the literature. For example, in a multimodal comparison of brachial plexus dissection by Temizsoy Korkmaz et al., ChatGPT and Grok achieved the highest mDISCERN and GQS scores. In contrast, Gemini and DeepSeek had lower scores [2]. Similarly, Onder et al. evaluated ChatGPT-4 responses to questions about hypothyroidism in pregnancy using the mDISCERN and GQS scales and reported a mean mDISCERN of 30 and a median GQS of 4, indicating a level of ‘moderate to high quality’ [20]. These findings were also supported by a recent study in the field of maternal health by Lima et al.; In that study, the quality scores of LLM outputs were found to be moderate, and the need for careful integration with clinical practice was particularly emphasized [14]. Similarly, Elbiss et al., while demonstrating the potential role of artificial intelligence applications in obstetric and gynecological emergencies, also stated that safety and ethical concerns should not be ignored [15]. Another study, in which Chat-GPT’s responses to gynecological and obstetric emergencies were rated as “very good” to “excellent,” emphasized the value of combining artificial intelligence with human knowledge and the need for human oversight to address critical nuances in emergency management [21].

Timely intervention and a multidisciplinary approach in the management of obstetric emergencies improve maternal prognosis, and this becomes extremely critical in intensive care practice [22]. One of the strengths of this study is the systematic, scenario-based, and double-blind approach used to evaluate the performance of ChatGPT-5 in clinical situations that can lead to high mortality and morbidity, such as obstetric and gynecological emergencies. However, prospective studies using data from real clinical cases will offer higher external validity compared to scenario-based evaluations. Future research should also consider patient safety, ethical dimensions, data privacy, and legal/institutional aspects of integration into clinical decision support systems. Such multifaceted studies will provide a safer, more transparent, and more effective roadmap for integrating AI-powered large language models into clinical practice.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of this study. First, only one large language model (ChatGPT-5) was evaluated, and no comparative analysis was conducted with other AI platforms such as Gemini, Claude, or Grok. Second, although each scenario was queried once to simulate a real-world clinical interaction, large language models are known to generate variable outputs when presented with the same prompt multiple times. Ideally, each case should have been queried at least three separate times to assess the model’s consistency and reproducibility. This represents an inherent methodological limitation of the current design.Third, while all scenarios were derived from real clinical practice and aligned with international guidelines, they were based on simulations rather than actual patient data, which limits the direct generalizability of the results to real-world clinical settings. Another limitation is the absence of formal difficulty grading among the simulated cases. Although the scenarios inherently represented a spectrum of clinical complexity—from straightforward to multidisciplinary emergencies—no standardized difficulty classification system was applied. Future studies could incorporate structured grading to enable more precise evaluation of model performance across different levels of clinical challenge.

Finally, the sample size was relatively small (15 scenarios). Larger, multi-institutional studies with repeated prompts and external validation are warranted to confirm the reliability, reproducibility, and generalizability of these findings.

Conclusion

This study evaluated the performance of ChatGPT-5 in obstetric and gynecological emergency scenarios using standardized, guideline-based cases. The model demonstrated high concordance, particularly in well-defined, protocol-driven conditions such as postpartum hemorrhage, eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, and ruptured ectopic pregnancy. However, its accuracy and decision quality were partially limited in complex cases requiring multidisciplinary coordination, including malignancy-related hemorrhage and traumatic gynecological emergencies. Quality assessments using the modified DISCERN (mDISCERN) and Global Quality Scale (GQS) indicated moderate-to-good reliability. At the same time, readability analyses showed a technical proficiency level comparable to that of a medical student or clinician.

Overall, ChatGPT-5 shows promising potential as a supplementary clinical decision-support tool in obstetric and gynecological emergencies. Nonetheless, it should be used only under expert supervision and not as a stand-alone decision-making system, particularly in high-risk or complex cases. Future studies involving repeated prompts, larger sample sizes, and comparative evaluations with other AI-LLMs (e.g., Gemini, Claude, Grok) are warranted to further validate reliability, reproducibility, and applicability across different clinical settings.

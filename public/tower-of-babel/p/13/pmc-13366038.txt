IFPHPOS0028 Scoping Review of Guidance for Conducting Ethical Health Research in Prisons
Authors: Daniel Pace, Manjari Engelhard
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEthical guidance exists to protect people from adverse consequences of research. Additional safeguards apply to vulnerable populations such as people in prisons. Further guidance is needed to equitably develop the prison health evidence base without compromising the rights of individual participants.AimThis scoping review aimed to identify what guidance exists on the conduct of ethical health research in contemporary adult prisons.MethodsGrey and peer-reviewed literature that included guidance on conducting ethical health research in contemporary adult prison settings was sought. Material related to those under 18 years of age, migrants and prisoners of war was excluded. We systematically searched seven medical, psychological, and global health databases. Further manual searching was performed on sources of grey literature. There were no limits on geographic or cultural contexts, or on the language of material, but publications made prior to 2000 were excluded.ResultsOf 1,714 records initially identified, 130 database articles and two grey literature records met our inclusion criteria. Most records were commentary or editorial articles written by university-affiliated researchers in high-income countries. The ethics of free and informed consent in prison-based health research were most commonly stated.ConclusionsThis review demonstrated that systematic, comprehensive, and pragmatic guidance for conducting ethical prison health research was not readily available. It subsequently informed the development of such guidance to facilitate the design and conduct of future ethical prison health research. It also exposed a lack of evidence from LMICs, reinforcing the calls for capacity building efforts to develop prison health research across the world.

Understanding interaction between patients and nurse anesthetists. A systematic review of qualitative and quantitative studies
Authors: Ferid Krupic, Melissa Krupic
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Interaction between healthcare professionals and patients is a complex process influenced by communication, environmental, individual, and organizational factors.Objective:To synthesize qualitative and quantitative evidence and identify key characteristics of effective interaction between patients and nurse anesthetists during the perioperative period.Methods:A systematic literature search was conducted in PubMed, MEDLINE, CINAHL, Embase, and the Cochrane Library to identify qualitative and quantitative studies examining factors influencing interaction between patients and nurse anesthetists in the perioperative setting.Results:Both facilitators and barriers to effective interaction were identified. Facilitators included respectful, patient-centered care, a supportive work environment, and clear, timely communication. Barriers included negative staff attitudes, procedural stress, interpersonal conflicts, exposure to violence, and communication challenges such as low speech volume, unclear articulation, and limited language proficiency. Effective communication was associated with improved patient safety, enhanced team cohesion, and more efficient information exchange. However, increased reliance on advanced technology may reduce patient engagement and interpersonal communication within the healthcare team.Conclusion:Nurse anesthetists play a critical role in supporting patients during potentially stressful perioperative experiences. Organizational factors, time constraints, and communication strategies may influence the quality of patient–provider interaction. Further research is needed to determine how these factors can be optimized to improve perioperative care and patient outcomes.

Introduction

The interaction between healthcare professionals and patients constitutes a multifaceted process influenced by a range of factors, including communication style, physical environment, individual characteristics, and organizational conditions.[1] Effective interaction is fundamental to ensuring patient safety and fostering a therapeutic relationship in which trust and mutual respect are essential components. Interprofessional communication within healthcare serves a distinct clinical purpose, encompassing supportive and motivational functions grounded in professional roles and competencies.[12] This form of communication is guided by professional values and ethical frameworks, such as those established by the International Council of Nurses (ICN), which emphasize the promotion of health, prevention of illness, restoration of health, and alleviation of suffering.[34] Within healthcare settings, communication and information transfer occur through multiple modalities, including verbal exchanges, written documentation, and patient records. Proficiency in effective communication—defined as the ability to convey information accurately and ensure mutual understanding—is an expected competency among healthcare professionals. However, formal education in professional communication remains limited within many healthcare training programs. To address potential communication gaps, structured communication tools such as SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) have been widely adopted. SBAR provides a standardized framework for information exchange, aiming to reduce the risk of omitted or misinterpreted information.[5] For example, in operating room settings, SBAR is frequently utilized during handovers between healthcare professionals. Previous research has demonstrated that inadequate communication can contribute to team conflicts, suboptimal work environments, and an increased risk of patient complications and mortality.[6] Etherington et al. (2019)[7] identified communication failures as the most prevalent cause of errors, adverse events, and malpractice incidents. Furthermore, approximately 56% of intraoperative and postoperative complications have been attributed to communication deficiencies. Poor communication among healthcare professionals has consistently been recognized as a primary contributor to medical errors that may negatively impact patient outcomes.[8910] In addition to technical communication skills, healthcare professionals are expected to establish a therapeutic alliance with patients, characterized by the ability to instill calm, trust, and a sense of security. These factors all contribute to reducing both fear and anxiety among patients.[811] Valeberg (2013)[12] emphasizes the importance of supporting patients during the preoperative phase by enhancing their self-efficacy and perceived control. Moreover, healthcare professionals must be capable of providing individualized information and education tailored to the patient’s specific needs and circumstances. According to Travelbee (1971),[13] successful communication in a healthcare encounter requires specific skills such as knowledge, timing, and mastery of various communication techniques. However, these techniques do not inherently result in the establishment of a therapeutic relationship. Negative factors that may impede communication have also been identified, including the failure to recognize the patient as a unique individual and the nurse’s inability to adequately interpret multiple layers of meaning within the communicative exchange. In the perioperative context, patient encounters are typically brief yet intensive, characterized by the simultaneous occurrence of multiple clinical and interpersonal processes within an environment that is often unfamiliar to the patient. Furthermore, contemporary surgical care is frequently conducted under constraints related to organizational conditions, staffing, and time pressures. Against this background, it is pertinent to examine the factors that underpin effective interaction between nurse anesthetists and patients, as well as to explore which strategies, if any, are perceived as most effective in facilitating high-quality encounters from both professional and patient perspectives. Accordingly, the aim of this study is to synthesize qualitative and quantitative evidence to elucidate the characteristics of effective interaction between patients and nurse anesthetists during the perioperative phase.

Search strategy

Qualitative studies were purposefully selected to correspond with the objective of this research, which was to elucidate various facets of effective interaction and communication between nurse anesthetists and patients, as well as to examine patients’ subjective experiences of such interactions. The authors determined it that an approach incorporating both qualitative and quantitative methodologies was most suitable for capturing these nuanced personal experiences. To enhance the specificity of the search results, terms recommended by SweMeSH were employed. The key search terms included: anesthesia, nurse-patient interaction, relationship, communication, participation, experience, treatment, and health care. Boolean operators (AND) were utilized to systematically combine these terms and refine the search output. Furthermore, MeSH terms and free-text words related to community were integrated with terms pertaining to communication, interaction, and patient experiences. The comprehensive search string was thus formulated as follows: Anesthesia AND Nurse-patient interaction AND relationship AND communication AND participation AND person-centered care AND treatment AND experiences AND care. The literature search was conducted across multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Medline, CINAHL, Embase, and the Cochrane Library. [Table 1] In addition, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses relevant to the topic were screened to identify supplementary studies. The selection process entailed an initial screening of study titles, followed by a review of abstracts. Studies whose abstracts demonstrated alignment with the research objectives were subsequently retrieved in full text for detailed examination. Ultimately, studies deemed most pertinent to the research aim were included. A total of 298 studies were initially reviewed, of which 17 fulfilled the inclusion criteria and were incorporated into the literature review [Figure 1]. Data collection in the selected studies was predominantly conducted using semi-structured focus group interviews, with participant numbers ranging from 11 to 168. The analytical process of the reviewed studies resulted in the identification of one overarching theme, three principal categories, and six subcategories.

Literature quality evaluation and data extraction

This systematic review was conducted in strict accordance with the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines.[14] Established methodological standards for systematic reviews were adhered to throughout the study.[14] As the review exclusively involved previously published studies, ethical approval was not required. The initial identification and selection of studies were performed in consultation with a subject librarian at the University Library of Gothenburg to ensure methodological rigor. A comprehensive literature search was conducted across multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Medline, CINAHL, Embase, and the Cochrane Library, to identify relevant studies. The search was carried out between March and December 2023, and eligible studies were published from 2013 to 2021. The Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome (PICO) framework was employed to guide the selection of articles [Table 2]. Inclusion criteria were restricted to peer-reviewed, original research articles written in English and employing qualitative methodologies. Studies published in languages other than English, mixed-methods studies, and those involving control groups were excluded. The study authors independently screened all abstracts to determine eligibility based on the predefined criteria. Full-text assessments were subsequently performed by two authors (FK and MK). Data extraction and synthesis were guided by a structured five-step model analysis process.[14] Initially, all authors conducted a detailed reading of the included studies, focusing on results. Key findings were then identified, followed by a systematic comparison to determine similarities and differences across studies. This comparative analysis facilitated the emergence of overarching themes and subthemes. Finally, the identified themes and subthemes were synthesized to construct an integrated framework, which informed the structure of the results section. Throughout the analysis, no automated tools were employed. Data were extracted by the first two authors (FK and MK) into a Microsoft Excel database and subsequently reviewed by the senior researcher (FK) for verification and refinement. Any ambiguities encountered during data extraction were resolved through consultation with the senior researcher to ensure accuracy and consistency.

Result

The analysis of the data identified three principal categories: interprofessional factors, team-related factors influencing interaction, and external factors. Within these overarching categories, six subcategories were distinguished: factors facilitating interprofessional interaction, factors obstructing interprofessional interaction, factors promoting team interaction, factors inhibiting team interaction, technological environmental factors, and time availability. From these categories, a unifying theme emerged, conceptualized as factors influencing interactions between patients and healthcare professionals. This theme captures the essential dimensions of both encounters and relational dynamics within clinical settings. The studies included a total of 52 patients, aged 33 to 69 years, who underwent procedures under either regional or general anesthesia, alongside 67 healthcare professionals with between 6 and 38 years of experience in anesthesia care. Data were collected employing a multimethod approach, encompassing semi-structured interviews, direct observations, standardized questionnaires, and quantitative analyses utilizing diverse statistical techniques, thereby providing a comprehensive understanding of the phenomena under investigation.

Interprofessional factors

In the category of interprofessional factors that can affect interaction between different staff categories, various things were described at the individual level that can affect interaction. Results of the studies read showed that there were positive and negative factors that influenced interaction. What influenced interaction positively was good treatment, mood, pleasant and kind staff, timely communication and good information to patients and staff. Rude and unpleasant staff, increased stress during surgery, fighting, violence, speaking quietly, mumbling, or not mastering the language could make interaction between different categories difficult. In the latter situations, staff tried to find different ways for successful communication and increased interaction.

Factors improving interprofessional interaction

The analysis of the articles included in the present study shows that the way staff communicate with each other is of great importance. Mood and tone can influence how messages are conveyed and perceived, and can help clarify the level of urgency when assessing acute situations.[151617] Characteristics such as friendliness and a cordial attitude can promote communication between team members. Furthermore, the analysis shows that respectful communication is a central factor for effective collaboration.[18] A prerequisite for effective communication is that all team members have the opportunity to hear and understand the information being conveyed.[1920] Paige et al. (2021)[21] also highlight that clear and direct communication, combined with self-confidence and the ability to speak up when necessary, contributes to a more effective communication process. Moreover, communication is influenced by the engagement of team members and their ability to follow what is happening intraoperatively, as this contributes to a greater overall understanding of the situation in the operating room.[18] Experience has also been shown to be important for communication in the operating room. More experienced nurses have a greater ability to distinguish that parts of conversations are relevant in each situation.[22] The same author also emphasize the importance of filtering out background noise in the operating room to focus on information that is important. In addition, experienced personnel often find it easier to make themselves heard in the operating room.[20] Experience can also contribute to a more positive response from the team when an individual raises concerns that need to be addressed. Furthermore, openness about one’s own fallibility may encourage other team members to act in a similar manner.[17]

Factors obstructing interprofessional interaction

Unprofessional or unfriendly behavior from team members can contribute to impaired communication within the entire team, particularly in relation to individuals, who demonstrate a lack of respect toward others.[171823] Işık et al. (2020)[24] emphasize that surgeons’ behavior has a considerable impact on team communication. For instance, expressions of anger may negatively influence communication processes and create stress among other team members. Their study further revealed that individuals had experienced emotional, verbal, and even physical violence, which in turn contributed to the occurrence of communication errors.[24] In one study of Sandelin and Gustafsson (2015)[23] describe that different professional groups adopt various strategies to cope with aggressive behavior from surgeons. One such strategy involves adopting a quieter and more formal communication style, whereas another strategy entails distancing oneself and limiting communication to situations strictly necessary for patient care. Additionally, perceptions of communication may vary depending on the recipient’s emotional state, personal background, and the cultural and professional norms that regulate interpersonal interactions.[17] Communication may also be hindered when messages are unclear, for example if an individual speaks too quietly, mumbles, or has limited proficiency in the working language.[1920] Furthermore, communication in the operating room may deteriorate when the surgeon’s expectations are not met.[15] Individual stress is another factor that may negatively affect communication. To reduce stress and promote effective communication, it is important that operating room staff are aware of the surgeon’s planning and approach prior to the procedure. If instruments need to be retrieved during an operation, this may increase stress and frustration, thereby negatively affecting communication within the team.[2024] A lack of engagement among team members may also constitute a barrier to effective communication.[18] According to Garosi et al. (2020),[20] communication errors in the operating room can arise as a result of inattentiveness or absence of a member of the intraoperative team. For instance, a team member may become distracted by using a mobile phone. Furthermore, Grade et al. (2019)[15] highlight that anesthesiologists may sometimes be perceived as more distant from other members of the surgical team. Communication may therefore be negatively affected when a new anesthesiologist joins the procedure without prior notification to the team, which may lead to the anesthesiologist feeling excluded from the team’s communication.

Team factors influencing interaction

Within the category of team interaction, the reviewed studies indicated that communication plays a crucial role in ensuring effective collaboration within teams. Effective communication was associated with increased feelings of safety among team members as well as improved patient safety. Furthermore, the flow of information improved, and over time the team’s functioning became more efficient and cohesive. Conversely, factors such as inefficiency, ambiguity, inattentiveness, interpersonal conflicts, and unclear professional roles were found to hinder communication and collaboration between different professions. Additionally, hierarchical structures within teams could contribute to feelings of fear and hesitation, which in turn reduced team members’ willingness to ask questions or seek clarification from one another.

Improvement factors in the team

The reviewed studies consistently indicate that communication within the perioperative team represents a central determinant of effective teamwork and is closely associated with the underlying dynamics of the team.[2125] Several studies highlight the establishment of shared goals, clearly defined professional roles, and mutual respect among team members as key prerequisites for facilitating effective communication.[1516182526] Hanssen et al. (2020),[26] conceptualize respect within the perioperative team as recognition of each member’s professional responsibilities, adequate preparation for required tasks, and the maintenance of collegial and respectful interpersonal interactions. Such forms of professional respect contribute to the development of trustful and secure team relationships, which in turn facilitate a more efficient exchange of information within the team (Hanssen et al., 2020).[26] Communication and interaction among team members were also found to improve when all members received comprehensive information regarding the planned procedure and when continuous updates were provided regarding any intraoperative changes.[19] Furthermore, structured team training initiatives focusing on team building, collaborative problem solving, and crisis management have been shown to enhance intraoperative communication between team members while simultaneously strengthening team cohesion and collective efficacy.[2427] The social interactions within the team, as well as the presence of pre-existing perceptions or prejudices among team members, may further influence communication patterns. Consequently, interventions designed to improve team dynamics, foster collective identity, and reduce preconceived assumptions about colleagues may contribute to improved communication and a reduction in communication-related errors.[2427] According to Işık et al. (2020),[24] the overall team climate constitutes a fundamental component of well-functioning communication processes. A supportive and collegial work environment, in which team members are able to exchange experiences and interact in an open manner, has also been identified as an important contributor to improved intraoperative communication.[20252627] Such an environment may facilitate psychological safety within the team, thereby enabling individuals to ask questions, seek clarification, and raise concerns about potential errors without hesitation.[2025] In addition, several studies emphasize that familiarity among team members—particularly when individuals have collaborated over extended periods or when stable surgical teams are established—serves as an important facilitator of effective communication.[152325] Another factor described in the studies[1523] concerns the role of team familiarity in situations where surgeons provide limited procedural information prior to surgery. In such contexts, established familiarity within the team may compensate for gaps in communication, as team members are able to anticipate the surgeon’s preferences, routines, and operative approach based on previous collaborative experiences.[1523]

Factors inhibiting team interaction

Previous research has identified several factors that may hinder effective team interaction. In contrast to teams characterized by conditions that facilitate communication, such as shared goals, clearly defined roles, and mutual respect,[1825] factors including inefficiency, ambiguity, and inattention have been shown to complicate team communication.[1825] In such situations, communication within the team may shift from collaborative problem-solving to interactions characterized by blame and criticism.[25] Unclear role distribution may manifest through misunderstandings and difficulties in addressing issues and discussing appropriate measures related to the intervention.[24] Hierarchical structures have also been identified in several studies as a barrier to effective intraoperative communication.[151825] According to Grade et al. (2019),[15] hierarchical dynamics may create fear and hesitation among team members when it comes to asking questions. Within the perioperative team, hierarchy often implies that individuals with the highest level of formal education are perceived as superiors. Consequently, professionals with less experience or those still undergoing training, such as nurses or physicians, may hesitate to raise questions due to concerns about contradicting their superiors.[15]

External factors

Within this category, and based on a review of the included studies, the findings indicate that the increasing use of advanced technology may negatively influence interpersonal interactions. When healthcare professionals direct their attention primarily toward technological aspects of care, the focus on patient-centered care and collaboration with other professional groups may become diminished. Such shifts in attention may also contribute to the emergence of conflicts within the healthcare team. Moreover, the findings highlight that the availability of sufficient time for communication and engagement with team members, colleagues, and patients constitutes a crucial factor influencing perioperative staff interactions.

The technological environment factors

The healthcare professionals described in the reviewed studies emphasized that technologically advanced perioperative environments may create inherent tensions between the provision of nursing care and the execution of technical responsibilities among nurse anesthetists. In such settings, attention is frequently directed toward monitoring equipment, surgical procedures, and other medical-technical tasks, which may inadvertently reduce opportunities for direct patient interaction, despite the nurse anesthetists’ close physical proximity to the patient. Consequently, the professional role of the nurse anesthetist requires the simultaneous management of multiple responsibilities, including ensuring patient safety, maintaining situational awareness of the patient’s physiological and emotional needs, and guiding the patient throughout the perioperative process.[2829] The findings further suggest that the ability to deliver patient-centered care within technologically intensive environments relies partly on the development of structured routines for technical procedures. From the patient perspective, it is essential to be acknowledged as a whole person rather than primarily through physiological parameters displayed on monitoring devices. This sense of recognition may be facilitated through relational and embodied forms of communication, including physical proximity, spontaneous dialogue, eye contact, and therapeutic touch.[29] Failure to maintain an appropriate balance between technological demands and relational aspects of care may result in patients experiencing objectification or depersonalization. The reviewed literature underscores the importance of maintaining awareness that the perioperative environment is often unfamiliar to patients.[29] Consequently, sustained attentiveness to the patient’s subjective experience is essential to preserve the humanistic dimension of anesthesia care within technologically complex clinical settings.

Time availability

The findings indicate that the availability of time constitutes a critical prerequisite for establishing meaningful and effective interaction between nurse anesthetists and patients in the perioperative context. Adequate temporal resources enable nurse anesthetists to engage in attentive and structured communication, facilitating comprehensive explanations, the provision of relevant information, and a review of the anticipated sequence of perioperative events and potential alternatives. Moreover, sufficient time allows for the implementation of necessary preparatory measures prior to the induction of anesthesia. Through these communicative and preparatory processes, nurse anesthetists can contribute to reducing patient anxiety and promoting a sense of preparedness and psychological stability. In situations where patients are at risk of experiencing heightened agitation or distress, the availability of sufficient time appears to be a decisive factor in maintaining patient calmness and facilitating cooperative engagement.[3031] At the same time, a growing body of research highlights the pervasive time constraints that characterize contemporary perioperative care environments.[293031] Under conditions of temporal pressure, nurse anesthetists tend to rely more heavily on standardized routines and structured, task-oriented interactions in order to maintain workflow efficiency and adhere to the operative schedule. Within such constrained contexts, patients’ preoperative concerns, questions, and individualized needs risk being deprioritized. Consequently, opportunities for establishing relational trust and confidence between patients and nurse anesthetists may be diminished, potentially affecting the overall quality of the perioperative care experience.[2931]

Discussion

The present study aims to synthesize both qualitative and quantitative evidence to elucidate key characteristics of effective interactions between patients and nurse anesthetists during the perioperative phase. The integration of qualitative and quantitative research designs enables a more comprehensive understanding of the phenomenon under investigation, as it combines the depth of experiential data with the breadth of systematically collected numerical evidence. A primary strength of qualitative methodology lies in its capacity to generate rich and nuanced descriptions of participants’ experiences, as informants are afforded the opportunity to articulate their perspectives in an open and unrestricted manner. In contrast, quantitative approaches typically rely on structured instruments with closed-ended questions, which may limit the extent to which complex subjective experiences can be captured. Nevertheless, qualitative research is not without methodological challenges. One potential limitation is the increased interpretive latitude afforded to researchers when analyzing descriptive data, which may introduce elements of subjectivity into the analytical process. To mitigate this limitation and enhance the credibility of the findings, both authors conducted the content analysis of all included articles independently. Independent analysis reduces the risk of individual interpretive bias and strengthens the trustworthiness of the analytical process. The credibility of the study could have been further enhanced through the involvement of an external reviewer or third-party researcher to assess the plausibility and coherence of the analytical interpretations. An additional methodological consideration relates to the language of the included articles. As all articles were written in English, translation was required during the content analysis. The process of translating data from one language to another inevitably entails a degree of interpretation, which may influence how meaning is conveyed and understood. Consequently, the authors’ proficiency in English may have affected the interpretation and representation of the original content, representing a potential limitation of the study. The present study is based on an analysis of 13 scientific articles examining factors that influence perioperative team communication. The analysis identified one overarching theme, two main categories, and four subcategories. The findings underscore the inherent complexity of communication, a phenomenon initially highlighted by Travelbee (1971)[13] and subsequently reinforced in more recent literature.[3] Despite increased awareness of the critical role of communication, contemporary surgical practice is often characterized by time constraints and a reduction in face-to-face interactions, which may weaken interpersonal connections. The influence of individual team members and the team is closely interrelated, as the existence of a team presupposes the participation of its members. Team members differ in personality, level of engagement, mood, cultural background, and adherence to social norms.[2829] The results indicate that factors such as mood, tone of voice, respect, and collegial behavior substantially affect both the quality and effectiveness of interactions. Cultural differences can lead to misunderstandings, whereas social interactions outside formal work settings may enhance team cohesion and, consequently, operative outcomes. Mutual respect emerges as a critical determinant of effective communication.[323334] Additionally, hierarchical structures may impede communication by discouraging team members from voicing concerns, which can compromise patient safety.[32333536] Professional self-confidence and personal courage are therefore essential for appropriate action despite hierarchical constraints.[34] A sense of understanding and psychological safety within the team facilitates communication and enables members to question decisions without fear.[3738] Effective communication is central to patient safety. Team members must feel empowered to express concerns and challenge decisions within an environment characterized by openness and permissiveness.[3940] Empirical evidence indicates that robust communication practices can prevent complications such as hypothermia, positioning injuries, hemorrhage, and postoperative infections.[41] The category of care in a technological environment highlights that the use of technology in healthcare is often described primarily in negative contexts.[28] However, Granados-Pembertty and Arias-Valencia (2013)[41] demonstrated that technology may also have a positive impact on interaction between healthcare professionals and patients. Specifically, technology can serve as an entry point for initiating communication and may function as a stepping stone toward further conversation. This phenomenon did not emerge in the present study. One possible explanation for this difference may lie in the methodological approaches used. The findings of the present study were based on researchers’ observations and interpretations of interactions, whereas the study conducted by Granados-Pembertty and Arias-Valencia (2013)[41] relied on interviews with nurses. These differing data collection methods may have influenced how the role of technology in patient interaction was perceived and reported. For future nurse anesthetists, it is important to maintain awareness of the role that technology can play in the care environment and to actively ensure that the patient remains the central focus of care.

In conclusion, the findings of the present study demonstrate that the interaction between the nurse anesthetist and the patient represents a complex and multifaceted relationship influenced by both interpersonal and contextual factors. These factors include the dynamics between the individuals involved as well as environmental and organizational conditions within the perioperative setting. The results highlight the importance of the nurse anesthetist’s professional role in supporting patients, who are facing the potentially stressful and vulnerable experience of surgery, regardless of whether the procedure is planned or performed in an emergency context. By acknowledging the significance of this interaction and actively fostering a supportive and patient-centered approach, nurse anesthetists may contribute to improved patient satisfaction and potentially reduce perioperative complications such as anxiety, pain, and psychological distress. Despite these findings, there remains a need for further research to expand the understanding of how different factors influence the interaction between nurse anesthetists and patients. Future studies could investigate how organizational structures, time constraints, and communication strategies affect the quality of this interaction, as well as how such factors may contribute to improved perioperative care and patient outcomes.

En bloc resection of the posterior musculature and spinal column for a malignant dorsal tumor: A technical report
Authors: Arihiko Tsukamoto, Takashi Oshima, Shutaro Fujimoto, Ryunosuke Fukushi, Ayaka Kitada, Tomonori Morita, Makoto Emori, Atsushi Teramoto, Toshihiko Yamashita
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background: En bloc resection of extensive dorsal tumors with simultaneous spinal reconstruction is technically demanding. Achieving adequate margins while preserving structural stability presents a significant surgical challenge.Methods: We treated an Ewing sarcoma family tumor extensively infiltrating the posterior spinal elements and bilateral paraspinal soft tissues. Using a bilateral soft-tissue elevation approach, osteotomies were performed under navigation guidance, allowing safe en bloc resection of the tumor together with involved posterior spinal structures. Following preoperative and postoperative chemotherapy and radiotherapy, anterior spinal reconstruction was subsequently performed.Results: At one year follow-up, the patient demonstrated stable spinal alignment with no evidence of local recurrence.Conclusion: This report describes a feasible surgical strategy for achieving en bloc resection of large posterior trunk tumors with spinal involvement, using navigation-assisted osteotomies and staged reconstruction.

INTRODUCTION

Intralesional violation during malignant tumor resection should be strictly avoided, as it markedly increases the risk of local recurrence.[1] Achieving an en bloc resection with spinal reconstruction is particularly challenging when a malignant tumor infiltrates both the posterior paraspinal musculature and the spinal column. Although several reports have described en bloc resection for tumors arising unilaterally from the paraspinal muscles and adjacent spinal elements, to the best of our knowledge, no reports exist describing en bloc resection for tumors extensively involving bilateral posterior soft tissues and the spine. In this technical report, we present our surgical technique for such a case and describe the staged reconstruction strategy employed for spinal stabilization.

CASE PRESENTATION

A 15-year-old girl presented with a swelling in the right side of her back that she had first noticed 15 months earlier. She began experiencing lumbodorsal pain 5 months before presentation and was referred to our institution after a tumor was detected by a local physician. Physical examination revealed a soft, elastic mass in the right posterior trunk. The mass was mobile in the subcutaneous layer but fixed to deeper tissues, without tenderness, erythema, or warmth.

Plain radiographs demonstrated irregularity of the spinous processes at the thoracolumbar junction. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed a right-dominant intramuscular mass measuring 61 × 184 × 95 mm, showing low-signal intensity on T1-weighted images and iso- to high-signal intensity on T2-weighted images, with heterogeneous enhancement and irregular margins [Figure 1]. The lesion infiltrated the posterior elements from T11 to L2, the bilateral pedicles at L1–2, and extended into the right extraforaminal region at L1/2. Ultrasound-guided needle biopsy from the right posterior trunk revealed an Ewing sarcoma-like tumor, classified as FNCLCC Grade II and staged as T2N0M0, Stage IIIA. The patient underwent chemotherapy consisting of three cycles of VDC and three cycles of IE over the 4 months preceding surgery. Post-chemotherapy imaging showed tumor reduction to 31 × 131 × 53 mm, corresponding to a 72% decrease in volume, and the response was assessed as a partial response [Figure 2]. The treatment strategy consisted of initial en bloc tumor resection with posterior instrumentation, followed by adjuvant chemoradiotherapy and a second-stage anterior spinal fusion.

Surgical technique

A lateral retroperitoneal approach was first performed to allow partial resection of the psoas major. With the patient positioned in the right lateral decubitus position, a partial resection of the 11th rib was performed to create an extrapleural corridor. From the right side, the psoas muscle at the L1–2 level was resected, the right L1 nerve root was sacrificed, and the right L1 and L2 pedicles were transected.

The patient was then repositioned prone, and a midline posterior incision was made. From T9 to L5, all posterior muscle groups were elevated on the right side, while on the left side, the dissection was carried out 3 cm lateral to the midline to elevate the soft tissues. Osteotomies were performed under navigation guidance (Q Guidance System, Stryker, Kalamazoo, MI, USA, based on preoperative CT images). At the left L1–2 level, the osteotomy was performed from the deep aspect of the transverse process toward the pedicle. At T11 and T12, osteotomies were made through the interarticular portion, ensuring that the tumor capsule was not violated, allowing en bloc removal of the involved vertebrae and soft tissues. Posterior instrumentation was placed from the left side, spanning T11 to L4, and reconstructive procedures were performed using a pedicled left latissimus dorsi muscle flap and a perforator-based rotational fascial flap harvested from the right buttock [Figure 3].

RESULTS

The initial procedure required 12 h and 49 min, with an estimated blood loss of 680 mL. Postoperatively, the patient demonstrated numbness in the right inguinal region and mild weakness of the right iliopsoas muscle (MMT 4). Adjuvant therapy consisted of radiotherapy (50.4 Gy/28 fractions) and chemotherapy with two cycles of vincristine, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide; two cycles of vincristine and cyclophosphamide; and two cycles of ifosfamide and etoposide.

A second-stage anterior fusion had been planned for 6 months after the initial surgery; however, exposure of the posterior instrumentation accompanied by fever occurred 3 weeks before the scheduled procedure. Following the removal of the posterior implants, anterior spinal reconstruction from T11 to L4 was performed. Through a left-sided extrapleural approach facilitated by partial resection of the 10th and 11th ribs, the intervertebral discs from T11 to L4 were curetted, and cages filled with Grafton® demineralized bone matrix (Medtronic, Minneapolis, MN, USA) were inserted at each level. Anterior fixation was completed by placing implants along the left lateral aspect of the vertebral bodies [Figure 4].

At 1-year follow-up after tumor resection, the patient was ambulatory without symptoms and showed no evidence of local recurrence.

DISCUSSION

Ewing sarcoma was first reported in 1921 as a small round cell tumor of bone, but it is now recognized that extraskeletal Ewing sarcoma, peripheral primitive neuroectodermal tumor, and Askin tumor of the chest wall share common pathological and biological characteristics, collectively referred to as the Ewing sarcoma family of tumors (ESFT).[7] ESFT predominantly affects adolescents and young adults aged 10–20 years, typically originating in bone, although 20–30% of cases arise in soft tissues.[2] Multimodal treatment, including surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy, can achieve long-term survival rates of 70–80% in localized disease; however, spinal involvement is associated with poorer outcomes, with reported survival rates of around 50%.[8] Cases with metastatic disease have a reported survival rate below 20%.[10] In the present case, although no metastases were identified, the tumor originated from the spine and extensively infiltrated surrounding soft tissues, and no standardized treatment strategy has been established.

In spinal tumors, en bloc resection without violating the tumor capsule reduces local recurrence and contributes to improved survival.[5] Total en bloc spondylectomy is considered effective for local control in tumors involving the vertebral body or primary spinal lesions.[4,11] However, in this case, vertebral involvement was not clearly evident, and the extra-spinal soft-tissue component was extensive, making total en bloc spondylectomy inappropriate. The tumor infiltrated the posterior elements of the thoracolumbar spine, pedicles, epidural space, and intervertebral foramina, along with the paraspinal musculature. En bloc removal of the posterior spinal elements together with adjacent paraspinal muscles, while minimizing exposure of the spinal column, is extremely rare, with few reports in the literature.

Chi et al.[3] described the COPPER technique for posterior-lateral spinal tumors, which involves unilateral pedicle resection on the healthy side, mobilization of the dural sac ventrally, and oblique contralateral pedicle osteotomy, allowing en bloc removal of soft tissues and vertebrae without violating the tumor. However, in the present case, tumor infiltration occurred bilaterally in both the spine and surrounding soft tissues, making COPPER technically insufficient and risking tumor violation. Therefore, we combined navigation guidance with bilateral approaches at the thoracolumbar fascia level, elevating the soft tissues while performing osteotomies at the pedicles or interarticular portions. The pedicles and interarticular regions were exposed laterally, and their positions were confirmed with navigation, allowing precise osteotomies. Separation of the joints was required for interarticular osteotomies, but lifting the tumor en bloc along with soft tissues allowed the inferior articular processes to float, facilitating separation from the superior articular processes. This technique enabled en bloc resection of tumors infiltrating both sides of the posterior spinal elements while minimizing vertebral exposure. Special care must be taken around the pedicles due to the rich epidural venous plexus to achieve hemostasis. To the best of our knowledge, no similar technique has been reported.

Posterior instrumentation provided short-term spinal stability, and removal of the implants, followed by anterior fusion, was performed after completion of chemotherapy. This staged strategy allowed for the distribution of surgical stress and balancing tumor resection with spinal reconstruction. Although staged surgery enables flexible adaptation according to tumor control and systemic condition, it also prolongs the overall treatment period and carries the risk of secondary infection. Moreover, extensive soft-tissue defects following tumor resection necessitated coverage of implants and the dural sac at the initial surgery. The use of a pedicled latissimus dorsi flap[9] and a perforator-based rotational fascial flap from the buttock[6] reduced the risk of early wound complications and infection.

The importance of multimodal therapy for ESFT, including chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and surgery, has been widely reported,[12] and improved local control is closely associated with long-term survival. In cases of soft-tissue origin tumors with spinal infiltration, achieving negative surgical margins while maintaining spinal stability is critical for treatment success. The surgical approach employed in this case prioritizes local tumor control while minimizing the risk of neurological injury and functional compromise, providing a practical and aggressive option that may inform future treatment strategies for similar challenging cases.

CONCLUSION

We report a case of an Ewing sarcoma family tumor extensively infiltrating the paraspinal musculature and posterior spinal elements, in which en bloc resection was performed with minimal exposure of the spine, followed by staged spinal reconstruction. Although technically demanding, this approach achieved local tumor control at 1 year postoperatively, maintained spinal stability, and proved to be a useful strategy in terms of both functional preservation and surgical invasiveness.

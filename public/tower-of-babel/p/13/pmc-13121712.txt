Assessment of pneumonitis in patients with lung cancer undergoing radioimmunotherapy and immune checkpoint inhibitor rechallenge
Authors: Yingchen Ruan, Minjie Ruan, Zhishen Fang, Zhihua Sun, Kuimao Zhuang, Zenan Wu, Chuanyun Yu, Zhaoguo Liu, Dehua Wu, Zhongyi Dong, Wei Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:The combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) with radiotherapy (RT) has improved survival in patients with advanced lung cancer, yet treatment-related pneumonitis (TRP) is highly toxic. Current limitations include the absence of robust risk stratification models for TRP and unclear safety criteria for ICI rechallenge, hindering the management of recurrent TRP (rTRP).Methods:In this multicenter retrospective study, we analyzed 262 lung cancer patients receiving radioimmunotherapy. The data included baseline demographics, immunotherapy regimens, and radiotherapy parameters. TRP was graded per CTCAE v5.0. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression identified predictors of TRP and rTRP severity.Results:The incidence of TRP is 57.6% (151/262), with grade 1, 2, and ≥ 3 TRP occurring in 24.4%, 25.6%, and 7.6% of patients, respectively. The median time to TRP onset is 113 days (IQR, 73–155). Multivariate analysis reveals PD-1 inhibitors and ICI cycle number as predictors of G ≥ 1 TRP, whereas Dmean predicts all grades. Dose‒response modeling reveals Dmean thresholds for 50% TRP risk: 8.7 Gy (G ≥ 1), 15.3 Gy (G ≥ 2), and 23.4 Gy (G ≥ 3). Among the 53 patients receiving ICI rechallenge, 25.4% (13/53) developed rTRP. Rechallenge within 4 weeks significantly increases rTRP risk (OR, 0.061; 95% CI, 0.010–0.368; P = 0.002).Conclusions:Dmean serves as a continuous risk modifier for TRP severity, with Dmean > 15.3 Gy associated with grade ≥ 2 toxicity. An interval of ≥ 4 weeks after TRP is proposed as a safety window for ICI rechallenge, addressing critical gaps in radioimmunotherapy toxicity management.

Introduction

The integration of immunotherapy with radiotherapy has emerged as a transformative strategy in the management of both locally advanced and metastatic non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), demonstrating significant survival benefits in multiple clinical trials1–3. Despite its therapeutic promise, this combination is associated with an increased risk of TRP, a dose-limiting toxicity characterized by progressive pulmonary inflammation and irreversible parenchymal damage. Real-world evidence has revealed strikingly high incidence rates, with any-grade TRP occurring in 79.3–81% and grade ≥2 TRP in 39.7–42.9% of patients with lung cancer receiving ICI consolidation following chemoradiotherapy4–6. TRP not only necessitates treatment discontinuation but also adversely impacts quality of life and overall survival (OS), thereby limiting the broader clinical adoption of this multimodal approach7–10.

Current challenges in TRP management are twofold. First, robust risk stratification tools to predict TRP incidence and severity are lacking. While clinical factors such as preexisting comorbidities, ICI regimens, and dosimetric parameters have been implicated11–13, their hierarchical contributions across TRP severity grades (grade ≥1 vs. ≥2 vs. ≥3) remain poorly defined. Given the status of the lung as a primary organ at risk, dose‒volume histogram (DVH) parameters (e.g., volume of lung receiving ≥5 Gy [V5], volume of lung receiving ≥20 Gy [V20], and mean lung dose [Dmean]) have well-established correlations with pulmonary toxicity in conventional RT settings14–17. However, their predictive utility in the context of combined ICI-RT remains unvalidated. Second, evidence-based guidelines for ICI rechallenge post-TRP resolution are lacking. Safety profiles and optimal rechallenge timing remain undefined, with rTRP reported in 25–30% of cases18,19. Crucially, the potential interplay between pulmonary radiation exposure and rTRP risk has not been rigorously investigated20. Addressing these knowledge gaps is imperative to optimizing therapeutic decision-making.

This multicenter retrospective study comprehensively characterizes the independent risk factors for all-grade TRP in lung cancer patients receiving thoracic radiotherapy combined with immunotherapy. We identify Dmean as the most critical dosimetric predictor of severe (≥grade 3) TRP and establish specific dose–response thresholds. We further define a clinical safety window: delaying immunotherapy rechallenge until ≥4 weeks after initial TRP onset significantly reduces recurrence risk. Our findings provide direct guidance for optimizing the safety of combined ICI-RT in modern multimodal therapy.

Methods

Study design and data source

Data from patients with lung cancer who received RT combined with programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) or programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) inhibitors in a real-world setting at Nanfang Hospital of Southern Medical University and Xiangyang Central Hospital (January 2021 to December 2023) were retrospectively analyzed in this study. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Nanfang Hospital of Southern Medical University (Approval No.2025-420) and Xiangyang Central Hospital (Approval No.2025-184), in full compliance with the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria were as follows: (a) patients receiving combined RT and ICIs; (b) ICI-RT interval ≤6 weeks; (c) baseline chest CT before RT; (d) ≥1 post-RT chest CT evaluation; and (e) radiation plans compliant with the Quantitative Analysis of Normal Tissue Effects in the Clinic (QUANTEC) guidelines21. Patients treated with hypofractionated RT or stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) were excluded. Both histological subtypes (non-small cell/small cell lung cancer) and concurrent systemic therapies (chemotherapy/targeted therapy) were permitted. No restrictions were applied to the baseline tumor stage or prior treatment history.

Treatment and toxicity assessments

Anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy was administered before or after RT. Specific treatment regimens, including immunotherapy timing and radiation-dose fractionation patterns, were determined by clinicians on the basis of institutional standardized guidelines and individualized clinical characteristics (e.g., tumor stage and performance status). 85% of patients (223/262) underwent 4DCT simulation to incorporate respiratory motion, whereas the remaining 15% (39/262) were simulated using 3DCT with internal target volume (ITV) expansion. In accordance with the institutional standardized protocol for thoracic radiotherapy, a uniform PTV margin of 5 mm was applied across all participating centers. The initial chest CT or whole-body PET‒CT assessment was typically conducted one month after the completion of radiotherapy, with subsequent follow-up evaluations every 2‒3 months.

TRP was diagnosed via the following criteria: (a) chest CT indicating pulmonary abnormalities, including diffuse or radiation field-confined lesions after radioimmunotherapy; (b) new/worsening respiratory symptoms or auscultatory abnormalities; and (c) exclusion of pulmonary infections, tumor progression, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) exacerbation, pulmonary embolism, and cardiogenic causes. TRP severity was classified according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0. The diagnostic workflow for TRP involved initial assessment at each participating center by local radiologists (for CT interpretation) and radiation oncologists (for confirmation with radiotherapy fields), followed by a central review of all candidate cases by a senior core team comprising one radiation oncologist and one senior pulmonologist to ensure diagnostic consistency across centers.

Statistical analysis and reproducibility

Statistical analyses were performed via R 4.3.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing) and SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous variables were summarized as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs). All dosimetric parameters (V5, V20, and Dmean) were consistently defined as the dose to both lungs with the PTV subtracted, and this standardization was maintained across both centers and throughout all analyses. The Mann–Whitney U test was used to analyze nonnormally distributed continuous variables, and the Pearson χ² test (for expected frequencies ≥5) or Fisher’s exact test (for expected frequencies <5) was used for categorical variables. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were employed to calculate odds ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals. All the statistical tests were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.

Therapeutic regimens

A total of 78.6% (206/262) of patients were treated with PD-1 inhibitors, whereas 21.4% (56/262) were treated with PD-L1 inhibitors. With respect to the number of ICI cycles performed prior to TRP onset, 46.2% (121/262) underwent >6 cycles, 27.1% (71/262) received 4−6 cycles, and 26.7% (70/262) completed 1−3 cycles. A total of 71.4% (187/262) of patients received immunotherapy with sequential radiotherapy (ICIs→RT). Concurrent chemoradiation therapy (CCRT) was administered to 50.8% (133/262) of the patients. The tumor locations were as follows: upper lobe (50.7%, 133/262), middle/lower lobe (35.2%, 92/262), and central type (14.1%, 37/262). The median gross tumor volume (GTV) was 130.1 cc (IQR, 85.4−182.7), with a median planning target volume (PTV) of 229.8 cc (IQR, 153.2−310.5). The median lung V5, V20, and Dmean were 42.5% (IQR, 29.0−55.1), 17.2% (IQR, 13.1−23.0), and 10.2 Gy (IQR, 8.1−13.5), respectively.

Clinical course and logistic regression analysis of TRP

The incidence of TRP was 57.6% (151/262), with grade 1, 2, and ≥3 TRP observed in 24.4% (64/262), 25.6% (67/262), and 7.6% (20/262) of patients, respectively (Supplementary Table 1). Only one case of TRP-associated mortality was recorded. The median time to TRP onset was 113 days (IQR, 73–155). Across all TRP patients, 21.2% (32/151) developed other immune-related adverse events (irAEs) concurrently during ICI-RT combination therapy, and no patients discontinued ICIs due to non-pneumonitis irAEs. Among the 87 symptomatic TRP patients, 67.8% (59/87) received an intravenous methylprednisolone pulse followed by oral prednisolone tapering, whereas 26.4% (23/87) received oral prednisolone monotherapy. Post-TRP treatment comprised continuation of the original regimen in 38.4% (58/151), temporary suspension with subsequent rechallenge in 35.1% (53/151), and permanent discontinuation in 26.5% (40/151) of TRP cases. Among the 53 patients who underwent ICI rechallenge, 13 (25.4%) experienced rTRP.

According to the univariate analysis for grade ≥1 TRP (Table 2), a history of ILD significantly increased the risk (OR, 9.50; 95% CI, 1.22–74.16; P = 0.032). Patients receiving PD-1 inhibitors had a greater risk than those receiving PD-L1 inhibitors did (OR, 2.14; 95% CI, 1.18–3.90; P = 0.013), whereas more ICI cycles reduced the risk of grade ≥1 TRP (OR, 0.90 per cycle; 95% CI, 0.85–0.95; P < 0.001). The radiation dosimetric parameters V5 (OR, 1.03; 95% CI, 1.02–1.05), V20 (OR, 1.09; 95% CI, 1.05–1.14), and Dmean (OR, 1.18; 95% CI, 1.10–1.27) were positively associated with grade ≥1 TRP (all P < 0.001). Compared with central tumors, upper lobe (UL) tumors were protective (OR, 0.41; 95% CI, 0.19–0.89; P = 0.023). Univariate and multivariate analysis of all-grade TRP

The Mann–Whitney U test was used to analyze nonnormally distributed continuous variables, and the Pearson χ² test (for expected frequencies ≥5) or Fisher’s exact test (for expected frequencies <5) was used for categorical variables. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression models were employed to calculate odds ratios (ORs) with corresponding 95% confidence intervals. All the statistical tests were two-sided, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Any factors that were statistically significant at P < 0.1 in the univariate analysis were candidates for entry into a multivariable analysis.

Subsequent analysis of grade ≥2 TRP revealed that a history of ILD consistently increased risk (OR, 3.443; 95% CI, 1.092–10.861; P = 0.035), whereas ICI cycle correlated with lower observed incidence (OR, 0.911; 95% CI, 0.866–0.958; P < 0.001). The predictive radiation dosimetric parameters included V5 (OR, 1.025; 95% CI, 1.010–1.041; P = 0.001), V20 (OR, 1.079; 95% CI, 1.039–1.121; P < 0.001), and Dmean (OR, 1.178; 95% CI, 1.097–1.265; P < 0.001). UL tumors continued to confer a protective effect (OR, 0.380; 95% CI, 0.151–0.957; P = 0.040). Multivariate analysis revealed that the number of ICI cycles (OR, 0.922 per cycle; 95% CI, 0.875–0.972; P = 0.003) and Dmean (OR, 1.160; 95% CI, 1.076–1.250; P < 0.001) were independent determinants.

For grade ≥ 3 TRP, ILD history (OR, 4.094; 95% CI, 1.029–16.290; P = 0.045), along with V5 (OR, 1.038; 95% CI, 1.011–1.066; P = 0.006), V20 (OR, 1.116; 95% CI, 1.047–1.189; P = 0.001), and Dmean (OR, 1.250; 95% CI, 1.110–1.407; P < 0.001), demonstrated a stronger association. However, multivariate analysis confirmed Dmean as the sole independent predictor (OR, 1.250; 95% CI, 1.110–1.407; P < 0.001).

TRP severity progressively increased with higher Dmean, where the average Dmean values across patients with respective TRP grades were 9.2 Gy (G0, no TRP), 10.9 Gy (G1), 11.8 Gy (G2), and 14.0 Gy (G ≥ 3) (Fig. 1). The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves confirmed the discriminative capacity of Dmean, with areas under the curve (AUCs) of 0.670 (G ≥ 1), 0.667 (G ≥ 2), and 0.739 (G ≥ 3) (Supplementary Fig. 2). To evaluate the robustness of Dmean as a predictor of TRP in the context of population heterogeneity, we performed a subgroup analysis comprising 228 NSCLC patients, representing 87.0% of the overall cohort. Univariate logistic regression demonstrated that higher Dmean was significantly associated with an increased risk of grade ≥1 (OR,1.194, P < 0.001), grade ≥2 (OR, 1.202, P < 0.001), and grade ≥ 3 TRP (OR, 1.269, P = 0.002). After adjustment for potential confounders—including age, sex, smoking history, tumor stage, and ICI type—multivariate analysis confirmed Dmean as an independent predictor for all TRP severity grades (grade ≥1: OR, 1.252; grade ≥2: OR, 1.261; grade ≥3: OR, 1.269; all P < 0.01). These findings align with those observed in the full cohort, suggesting that the predictive utility of Dmean is not substantially influenced by tumor histology (NSCLC versus SCLC) within the present study population.

Notably, demographic variables (age and sex), clinical characteristics (smoking history, ECOG score, body mass index [BMI], ILD and COPD), and treatment parameters (sequencing of radiotherapy and immunotherapy, PTV and CCRT) showed no statistically significant associations with any-grade TRP risk.

Analysis of recurrent TRP related to ICI rechallenge

Recurrent TRP (rTRP) occurred in 13 patients who were rechallenged with immunotherapy after initial pneumonitis resolution. Among the rTRP cases, 46.2% (6/13) were grade 1, and 53.8% (7/13) were grade 2. The rTRP cohort (Supplementary Table 2) had a median age of 64 years (IQR, 53–72), with 84.6% (11/13) achieving an ECOG score of 0–1 and 69.2% (9/13) being current/former smokers. Most patients (76.9%, 10/13) received PD-1 inhibitors. For radiation therapy, conventional fractionation (27–30 fractions) was used at a median dose of 60 Gy (IQR, 54–66), with a median V20 of 19.6% (IQR, 15.5–22.4), V5 of 55.1% (IQR, 37.8–69.2), and Dmean of 12.5 Gy (IQR, 9.1–14.7). CCRT was administered to 53.8% (7/13) of the patients. ICIs-RT sequencing was balanced (ICIs→RT: 53.8%, 7/13; RT→ICIs: 46.2%, 6/13). The median interval from ICI rechallenge to rTRP onset was 108 days (IQR, 86–237). No fatal rTRP events occurred. The increased PD-1 inhibitor use in the rTRP subgroup (76.9%, 10/13) mirrored the overall TRP cohort.

We categorized 53 patients who were rechallenged with immunotherapy after initial TRP resolution into the rTRP group (n = 13) and the non-rTRP group (n = 40) (Supplementary Table 3). The median time from TRP onset to ICI rechallenge was significantly shorter in the rTRP group (30 days; IQR, 26–40) than in the non-rTRP group (64 days; IQR, 35.8–173.0; P = 0.004). No significant differences were observed between groups regarding radiotherapy parameters (GTV, PTV, V5, V20, or Dmean), treatment techniques (IMRT, volumetric modulated arc therapy [VMAT] or helical tomotherapy [TOMO]), or ICI types (PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors) (all P > 0.05) (Fig. 2). Discussion

Few studies have evaluated the risks associated with TRP or ICI rechallenge in the era of radioimmunotherapy. As the largest to date multicenter study specifically focusing on ICI-RT-treated lung cancer patients, it aims to provide clinically significant insights into risk stratification and management strategies for TRP and rTRP.

First, we identified risk factors for TRP across severity grades. A history of ILD consistently predicted TRP risk at all severity levels (OR range, 3.443–9.496; all P < 0.05), demonstrating a particularly strong association with grade ≥3 events (OR, 4.094; P = 0.045). This aligns with established evidence identifying preexisting ILD as a key TRP risk factor22,23. Conversely, upper lobe tumor location conferred protection against grade ≥1 and grade ≥2 TRP relative to central tumors (ORs of 0.380 and 0.406, respectively; both P < 0.05). This finding is supported by studies demonstrating reduced radiation toxicity in peripherally located tumors treated with SBRT24–27, likely attributable to the greater propensity of central tumors to induce major bronchial constriction or occlusion during treatment—an anatomical and pathophysiological characteristic that directly elevates TRP risk. Nevertheless, the impact of tumor location on TRP in non-SBRT settings warrants investigation in larger prospective cohorts.

Meta-analyses of prior clinical trials confirmed a significantly greater incidence of all-grade and grade 3/4 pneumonitis with PD-1 inhibitors than with PD-L1 inhibitors28,29. Consistent with those findings, our study demonstrated that, compared with PD-L1 inhibitors, PD-1 inhibitors significantly increased the risk of grade ≥ 1 TRP (OR, 3.147; P = 0.001). Furthermore, an inverse statistical association was found between the number of ICI cycles and TRP risk (ORs of 0.905 for grade ≥1 TRP and 0.922 for grade ≥2 TRP; both P < 0.05). This inverse relationship is plausibly attributable, at least in part, to clinical selection bias. The seemingly protective effect of more cycles thus reflects better treatment tolerance in patients who successfully completed extended therapy, rather than a direct pharmacological benefit of additional ICI administrations.

This study highlights a strong association between Dmean and TRP risk and identifies Dmean as a potential optimization parameter for combination therapy planning. Substantial clinical data have consistently demonstrated a dose‒effect relationship between dosimetric parameters and radiation pneumonitis (RP)30,31. According to the QUANTEC guidelines, maintaining V20 < 30% or Dmean <20 Gy under conventional fractionation reduces the risk of RP to <20%32. However, the immunomodulatory effects of checkpoint inhibitors fundamentally alter pulmonary tissue radiosensitivity via macrophage polarization shifts and CD8 + T-cell infiltration potentiation33, which diminishes the predictive validity of historical dosimetric constraints, necessitating rigorous re-evaluation of their clinical utility in contemporary combination therapy protocols. The findings of this study demonstrated that V5, V20 and Dmean emerged as primary determinants of TRP severity. Dmean was the only independent risk factor consistently significant for all grade TRP, and exhibited the most prominent predictive value for grade ≥3 TRP (OR, 1.250; P < 0.001; AUC = 0.739). Notably, these results were validated in the NSCLC subgroup (n = 228), reducing concerns about heterogeneity. V5 and V20 lost statistical significance in the multivariate analysis, which is consistent with findings reported by Saha et al.34. The strong linear correlations among Dmean, V20, and V5 prevent them from maintaining independent statistical significance in multivariate regression models, likely explaining this phenomenon. Consequently, Dmean, with its generally higher univariate OR than V5 and V20, was retained as a more comprehensive measure of the overall lung radiation dose burden.

It is noteworthy that the above independent predictors for TRP are grade-specific. For grade ≥1 TRP, Dmean acts as a concurrent predictor alongside PD-1 inhibitors and ICI cycles. Grade ≥2 TRP is associated with ICI cycles and Dmean, whereas Dmean stands as the sole predictor for grade ≥3 TRP. This differential pattern implies that ICI-driven immune activation is more likely to induce mild-to-moderate TRP. In contrast, severe TRP (grade ≥3) typically involves serious structural lung damage, such as pronounced edema or airway obstruction, which is heavily mediated by radiation dose. This distinction underscores the necessity for individualized risk management. In patients receiving PD-1 inhibitor therapy, who are at higher risk of mild-to-moderate TRP, early inflammatory symptoms (e.g., cough or fever) should be closely monitored. Meanwhile, strict control of radiation dose is essential to prevent severe toxicity.

Additionally, traditional risk factors for RP, including smoking history, COPD history, and PTV, showed no significant associations with TRP in this analysis. However, a prior study identified performance status and smoking history as independent risk factors for TRP35. This discrepancy may stem from variations between the study cohorts.

Overall, we developed a Dmean-based risk-escalation model for ICI-RT combination therapy, providing a quantitative framework for TRP risk stratification. A dose–response relationship was observed, where the severity of TRP increased with higher average Dmean (grade 0: 9.2 Gy; grade ≥3: 14.0 Gy). Furthermore, this analysis identified threshold doses associated with a 50% risk of toxicity (grade ≥1: 8.7 Gy; grade ≥2: 15.3 Gy; grade ≥3: 23.4 Gy). Our findings are consistent with those reported by Bi et al. (grade ≥1: 8.8 Gy)11 and Yegya-Raman et al. (grade ≥2: 14 Gy)36, confirming the reproducibility of the Dmean-based dose-toxicity relationship across independent cohorts. Collectively, these results provide preliminary observational evidence that may inform the implementation of dose constraints in clinical practice. In contrast to the QUANTEC threshold of 20 Gy (associated with <20% RP risk without ICI), ICI-RT patients exhibited a 50% risk of grade ≥1 and ≥2 TRP at markedly lower Dmean—8.7 Gy and 15.3 Gy, respectively. This substantial reduction directly links ICI exposure to diminished lung radiation tolerance. For high-risk patients (e.g., those with preexisting ILD or centrally located tumors), real-time/adaptive monitoring of anatomical changes and dose delivery, along with timely adjustments to treatment deviations, may mitigate TRP risk by strictly limiting the Dmean dose.

We further investigated the optimal timing for ICI rechallenge. Official guidelines recommend continuing ICI therapy with close monitoring for grade 1 toxicities while withholding treatment for most grade 2–3 toxicities until resolution to grade ≤1 (confirmed symptomatically and/or radiographically). Permanent discontinuation of ICIs is reserved for grade 4 TRP37–39. These recommendations derive primarily from expert consensus and anecdotal evidence, given the paucity of clinical data on ICI rechallenge. Some studies have reported improved OS in patients with irAEs who underwent rechallenge compared with those who did not40,41, and recent evidence supports the relative safety of ICI rechallenge following temporary interruption42–44. Among the 53 patients who were rechallenged on ICI, 25.4% developed rTRP, with no fatal events observed, suggesting an acceptable risk‒benefit profile for ICI rechallenge. However, defining the optimal rechallenge interval remains challenging. A retrospective analysis identified a prednisolone-equivalent dose of <15 mg/day administered for less than 4 weeks as a risk factor for TRP relapse45, aligning with guideline recommendations for a minimum duration of 4–6 weeks of corticosteroid therapy for TRP treatment46. Similarly, a prospective study demonstrated promising clinical benefits and manageable toxicity following the completion of a 6-week prednisolone regimen47. Our analysis confirmed that an interval of ≥4 weeks between initial TRP and ICI rechallenge significantly reduced rTRP risk by 93.9% (OR, 0.061; P = 0.002), providing an evidence-based foundation for clinical decision-making. Although the median Dmean was higher in rTRP patients (12.3 Gy) than in non-rTRP patients (12.0 Gy), this difference lacked statistical significance, and regression analysis excluded dosimetric parameters as independent risk factors. Consequently, rechallenge timing exerted a more substantial influence on rTRP development than did radiation dosimetric parameters. Our findings provide preliminary evidence supporting the consideration of a ≥4-week interval for ICI rechallenge in patients with prior TRP, pending prospective validation. Importantly, the initial 3 months (108 days; IQR, 86–237) post-rechallenge represented a high-risk period for rTRP, indicating the necessity of intensified monitoring of respiratory symptoms and radiological assessments during this interval.

This study has four principal limitations. First, due to its retrospective design, this study is susceptible to selection bias and unmeasured confounding. Specifically, owing to the lack of systematically collected pulmonary function data in participating centers (e.g., Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 s and Forced Vital Capacity), a history of COPD or ILD was employed as a surrogate marker of baseline lung impairment, which may constrain the comprehensiveness of our analysis. Although multivariate analyses supported the robustness of our key findings, future large-scale, multicenter studies are required to validate these results through both internal and external validation. Second, multicenter data aggregation increased heterogeneity owing to institutional variations in treatment standards and follow-up protocols, potentially explaining the relatively low AUC value observed in the ROC analysis. Third, data on supplementary DVH parameters (e.g., V2.5, V10, V25, and V50) were unavailable for this cohort. Fourth, the limited number of rTRP cases (n = 13) and the prolonged rechallenge intervals (≥200 days) in several patients of the non-rTRP group may restrict the interpretation of statistical power. Larger multicenter studies are needed to validate the general applicability of the ≥4-week interval for immunotherapy rechallenge.

Conclusions

To our knowledge, this study represents the largest comprehensive analysis of TRP predictors for lung cancer in the combined immunotherapy-radiotherapy era. We systematically characterized a hierarchical spectrum of independent risk factors for all-grade TRP, specifically identifying Dmean >15.3 Gy as a significant predictor of grade ≥2 TRP. Additionally, we established a ≥4-week ICI rechallenge interval as a safety window after initial TRP. Our evidence-based findings enable clinicians to optimize the therapeutic index when personalizing treatment strategies.

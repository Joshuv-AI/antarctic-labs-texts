Magnitude of Tuberculosis, drug resistance patterns and associated factors among presumptive TB patients at five selected public Hospitals, Somali Region, Eastern Ethiopia. Retrospective cross-sectional study
Authors: Bawlah Tahir, Abdi Osman, Kalid Fuad, Hadis Mahamed, Niman Tayib
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTuberculosis (TB) is a major public health problem worldwide. In 2024, an estimated 10.7 million people fell ill with Tuberculosis worldwide. Emergence of Rifampicin and multi-Drug resistance has posed greater public health threat on control and prevention of TB. Ethiopia is among the top 30 high Tuberculosis burden countries.ObjectiveTo determine the Magnitude of Tuberculosis, drug resistance patterns and associated factors among presumptive Tuberculosis patients in Somali Region, Eastern Ethiopia, 2025.MethodsInstitution based retrospective cross-sectional study design was employed from May 10, to June 10, 2025. Total of 460 patient’s cards and registration logs from July - December 2024 were recruited and selected using systematic random sampling technique. Retrospective card review and registers was done. Data were collected using pre-structured checklist. The data were entered into Epi-data version 4.6, exported to and analyzed in SPSS version 25. Bivariate and multi-variable logistic regression analysis was performed to measure the association between dependent and independent variables. P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.ResultThe overall magnitude of Tuberculosis among presumptive TB patients was 20.2%(95%CI: 16–23%) (93/460). It was higher among male patients (24.3%), patients aged between 16 and 30 years (27.1%) and patients with history of TB contact (24.4%). Among the TB confirmed patients, 10.8% (95%CI: 7.9–13.6%), (10/93), were resistant to any first line anti-TB drugs. Magnitude of Rifampicin resistance and multi-drug resistance was 10.8%(95%CI: 7.9–13.6%) and 4.3%(95%CI: 2.4–5.2%) respectively. Drug resistance was higher among previously treated TB patients; 12%(6/50). Being male, AOR (95%CI): 1.89(1.1–3.14, p = 0.012), and having history TB contact; AOR (95%CI):1.8(1.1–2.9, p = 0.02) were statistically associated with Tuberculosis.ConclusionThe magnitude of TB was higher in the study sites. The overall prevalence of drug resistance was also higher; especially previously treated patients. Male sex and having history of TB contact were statistically associated with TB. Introduction

Background

TB worldwide while 1.23 million died from the disease [1]. The most affected regions were South-East Asia (34%), the Western Pacific (27%) and Africa (25%) [1]. An emergence of drug resistant (DR)-TB poses a public health crisis and a health security threat; leading cause of death from Tuberculosis (TB) is curable infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis which spread through aerosol when sneezing, coughing or talking and remains major public health threat [1]. World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that one third of the world’s population approximately 2 billion people are infected with Mycobacteria tuberculosis (MTB); the causative agent of TB [2]. In 2024, an estimated 10.7 million people fell ill from antimicrobial resistance [3]. In 2024, an estimated 3.2% of new and 16% of previously treated TB cases have Rifampicin Resistance/Multi-Drug-resistant TB (RR-/MDR-TB) [1]. Multi-drug resistant-TB is a form of TB caused by bacteria that do not respond to isoniazid and rifampicin, the two most effective first-line TB drugs and rifampicin resistant - TB (RR-TB) defined as TB resistant to rifampicin only detected using genotypic or phenotypic methods [2].

Ethiopia is among the 30 high TB and TB/HIV burden countries globally with an estimated TB incidence rate of 146 per 100 000 population in 2023 according to the 2025 national TB drug resistance survey report [4]. In addition, Drug resistant TB continues to pose a major threat in the national response to TB in Ethiopia. According to the last drug resistance survey conducted in 2025, the prevalence of RR-TB was 1.07% among new and 6.89% among previously treated TB cases. In addition, the prevalence of isoniazid-resistant, rifampicin-susceptible TB was 4.15% among new cases and 4.41% among previously treated cases [4].

According to the research findings, reported predisposing factors were inadequate treatment regimen prescribed by health staff, poor patient adherence, previous history of exposure to anti-TB drugs. In addition, known TB contact, male sex, age, rural population, HIV co-infection, living in crowded areas, using congested transportation, smoking behavior, Alcohol use, family history of TB, family members greater than five, diabetes and history imprisonment were also associated factors of TB [5–15]. There is limited or even no published data on magnitude of TB, drug resistance pattern, and associated factors in the region. Hence, this study was aimed to explore the magnitude of TB, drug resistance pattern and associated factors among the presumptive TB patients in the region.

Methods and materials

Study area and period

The study was conducted in five public Hospitals (Karamara, Gode General Hospitals, Jigjiga Primary Hospital, Siti general Hospital, and JigJiga University-Sheik Hassan Yabare Specialized Comprehensive Hospital (JJU-SHYCSH)), in Somali regional State from May 10 – June 10, 2025. Somali Regional state is among the twelve regional states of Ethiopia. It is the second largest region in Ethiopia in terms of area covering 327,068 to 376,073 square kilometers, and located in the eastern part of Ethiopia [16]. It has borders with Djibouti to the north, Somalia to the East and south, and Kenya to the Southwest [16]. According Ethiopian population projection 2023, the region is estimated to have total population of 6,374,284 [16]. It is estimated that over 83% of the population lives in rural areas, primarily as pastoralists or agro-pastoralists whose income depends on livestock [17]. The regional capital city is Jigjiga [16]. The region has nineteen public Hospitals and more than two-hundred and forty health centers. Somali Region is among the top TB affected regions in Ethiopia [18]. Although, Ethiopia initiated a variety of intervention approaches to reduce TB burden, Somali Region still faces challenges due to geographical disparities, limited healthcare services particularly rapid molecular diagnostics, seasonal migration and infrastructure gaps.

Study design

Institution based retrospective cross-sectional study design was employed.

Source and study population

The source population was all presumptive TB patients in the study sites. The study population was all presumptive TB cases enrolled from July to December 2024 at the selected health institutions.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

All pulmonary and extra pulmonary presumptive TB patients’ cards/ registers with complete information and in all age groups were included. All pulmonary and extra pulmonary presumptive TB cases’ cards and registers with incomplete clinical information were excluded.

Sample size determination and sampling technique

The sample size was determined using single population proportion formula. By taking 5% margin of error, 95% confidence interval, expected prevalence of 50% as there is no published similar data on this problem in the study area.

n = 384 plus 20% non-respondent rate making the final sample size 460.

Systematic random sampling technique was deployed by calculating the kth value for each Hospital.

Proportional sample allocation

This study employed a design with a two-stage sampling approach. First, all public Hospitals in the Somali Region offering GeneXpert diagnostic services were identified. From these comprehensive lists, five facilities were selected purposefully based on last six month patients follows.

For the patient recruitment phase, all presumptive TB cases and screened at the selected health facilities from July – December 2024. The sample size was allocated proportionally based on each Hospital’s average two quarter GeneXpert testing volume (derived from the last 6month surveillance data). The total TB suspects tested in the five Hospitals from July – December 2024 was 7066; where JJU-SHYCSH ranked first (2009), followed by Karamara General Hospital (1719), Jigjiga Primary hospital (1718), Gode General hospital with 895 TB suspected cases and Siti General Hospital with 725. Sample allocation was done using the following formula.

A systematic random sampling approach was implemented where every kth patient’s card/register meeting the inclusion criteria was enrolled. The sampling interval (k) was calculated in real-time based on the available data to achieve the predetermined sample size for each Hospital.

Data collection methods and procedures

TB presumptive cases in all age groups with compete clinic information was included. Socio-demographic information such as age, sex, residence and clinical- related factors (such as history of previous anti-TB, having contact with known TB case, comorbidities, HIV status, nutritional status etc.) was collected using pre-structured questionnaires/data extraction checklist prepared for this study (Supplementary file). Data collectors were trained on the proper card review and data extraction. Patients’ medical numbers were selected from Laboratory Gene-Xpert register and then the selected numbers were sent to card room to find out their medical cards. The Principal and co-investigators have also taken part the data collection and maintaining data accuracy. When RR-TB cases are identified, investigators tracked the patient linkage and followed patients’ cards in the selected MDR centers with ten color Gene-Xpert looking for MDR status.

Methods of data analysis

The laboratory analysis results and other data were registered on pre-structured data extraction checklist. The data were entered into Epi-data version 4.6, be exported to and analyzed in Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) version 25. Data were analyzed for normality and descriptive statistics were presented as a number and percentage (%) for categorical variables and mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]) for continuous variables by using SPSS version 25. Bi-variable and multi-variable logistic regression analysis was performed to measure the association between outcome variable and independent variables based on Odds-ratio and level of statistical significance at P-value < 0.05.

Ethical consideration

Ethical clearance was obtained from Somali Regional Health Bureau, Ethical Review Board. Official support letter was written to study sites. Any information about the data was kept confidential and result was only communicated to an authorized concerned body.

Magnitude of tuberculosis

The overall magnitude of Tuberculosis among TB presumptive patients was 20.2% (95%CI: 16–23%) (93/460). The magnitude of TB was higher among male patients (24.3%), patients aged between 16 and 30 years (27.1%) and patients with history of TB contact (24.4%).

Drug resistance patterns

Among the TB confirmed patients, 10.8% (95%CI: 7.9–13.6%), (10/93), were resistant to any first line anti-TB drugs. Among TB confirmed patients, 10.8%(95%CI: 7.9–13.6%), were resistant to Rifampicin while 4.3%(95%CI: 2.4–5.2%) were found to be Multi-drug resistant. The magnitude of any drug resistance was higher among previously treated TB patients; 12%(6/50). Among anti-TB naïve patients, drug resistance level was 1%(4/410) (Table 3).

Discussion

TB is public health threat leading to greater mortality and morbidity, especially; developing world. And Emergence of drug resistance to the first and 2nd line anti-TB drugs is posing public health crisis diminishing prevention and control approaches against TB. Producing local epidemiological data on the TB burden, predisposing factors and drug resistance rate would be essential in prevention, control and intervention approaches against the disease [5].

In the present study, the overall magnitude of TB among Tuberculosis presumptive patients was 20.2%(95%CI: 16–23%). This finding was is in agreement with studies conducted in Debre Markos (23.2%) [21], Gambella (20%) [22], Dessie (23.4%) [23], Gambo Hospital (22.4%) [24], Yirgalem Hospital (16.5%) [25], Ethiopia, Western Oromia (21.3%) [26]. In addition the finding was in line with studies done in South Asian General Hospital, Nepal(21.3%) [27], Nigeria (23%) [28], Northeastern Nigeria (19.1%) [29], and southwest Nigeria (18.8%) [30]. However, this finding was higher than studies done in St. Peter TB specialized Hospital, Addis-Ababa (13.5%), Amhara Region(11%) [31], selected public Hospitals in Addis Ababa (15.11%) [32], Hiwot Fana Hospital (15.7%) [33], Metehara (14.2%) [34], Motta (8.4%) [35], northwest Ethiopia. Likewise, the finding was higher than studies done in Uganda (14%) [36], South Africa (13%) [37], Nepal (13.8%) [38], Zimbabwe (11%) [39]. This difference could be due to difference in laboratory diagnostic methods, study population inclusion, sample size, geographical locations and living style.

In other way, the current study finding was lower than studies carried out in East Gojjam (32.2%) [40], Tigray (37%) [41], Ethiopia, Congo (79.1%) [42], Togo (57%) [43]. The disagreement could be due to differences in diagnostic modalities, study participants, sample size, geographical, and TB control practices [5].

In the current study, the magnitude of TB was higher among male patients (24.3%) compared to their counterparts. This finding was consistent with study conducted in Debre Markos indicating higher TB incidence among males (27.9%) compared to females and studies done in Addis Ababa [44], Southern Ethiopia [45], Gondar [46], Nigeria [11] and Malaysia [47]. Although, TB affects all sex, but high burden is seen in men. This could be due to men are commonly exposed to variety of risk behavior such as having contact with variety of people, access to incarceration, smoking cigarrette and other outreach activities [48].

In addition, in the current study, the magnitude of TB was higher in patients aged between 16 and 30 years (27.1%) compared to their counterpart indicating circulation of TB infection in the community. This finding was supported by studies conducted in Yirgalem Hospital, Ethiopia [25], Refugee camps, Ethiopia [49].

In the current study, male sex was found to be significantly associated with Tuberculosis (1.89 times more likely to develop Tuberculosis compared to female patients). This is in line with studies conducted in selected refugee camps, Ethiopia [49], Addis Ababa [50], India [51], Nigeria [11], Thailand [52] Uganda [10] and Hong Kong [53]. In addition to this, this finding was supported by study carried out in Nigeria reporting that female sex were less likely to have TB compared to male [54].

In addition, in the present study, history of contact with TB case was statistically associated with the magnitude of TB (1.8 times more likely to have infected with TB). This finding is consistent with studies done in St. Paul’s Hospital, Addis Ababa [55], north Wollo [9], Debre Markos [56], Gedo Zone, Ethiopia [57] Oromia region [58] South Africa [59], Uganda [10], Nigeria [60] and India [61].

In the present study, the overall drug resistance rate was 10.8% (95%CI: 7.9–13.6%). The prevalence RR was 10.8% (95%CI: 7.9–13.6%). This finding was supported by studies conducted in Debre-Markos (10.3%) [21], India (10.5%) [62], Addis Ababa (9.8%) [44], Thailand (11%) [52], Nigeria (13.5%) [63], Addis-Ababa (9.9%) [32] and Kenya (9.9%) [64]. However, it was lower than studies carried out in Addis-Ababa(39.4%) [65], Gondar(15.8%) [46], and Lagos, Nigeria (23.4%) [7]. On the other hand, the current finding was higher than reports of studies conducted in Gambella (4.9%) [22], Dubti, Afar region (4.3%) [66], in Eastern Ethiopia (1.7%) [67], East Gojjam (2.59%) [40] and in Nigeria (6.9%) [68]. This discrepancies might be due to difference in patient selection, TB case management, diagnosis, geographical locations, and treatment compliance [32].

In the current study, drug resistance was higher among previously treated TB patients compared to treatment naïve (12% vs. 1%). This finding is in line with studies conducted in East Gojam, Ethiopia [40], Addis-Ababa [32], Uttar Pradesh [69] and Nairobi Kenya [70]. This finding was also supported by Ethiopian TB/HIV guideline 2021 indicating that prevalence of RR-TB is 1.1% among new and 7.5% among previously treated TB cases [3].

In the present study, the prevalence of MDR was 4.3%(95%CI: 2.4–5.2%). This finding was supported by studies conducted in East Gojjam, Northwest Ethiopia(3.37%) [40], Dire-dawa(5.1%) [71], Addis Ababa(5.3%) [72] China (5.6%) [73], Germany (4%) [74]. However, it was lower than studies done in Amhara Region (15.3%) [75], Addis Ababa (11.54%) [76], St. Peter TB specialized Hospital, Addis Ababa(46.3%) [77] and Iran (12.2%) [78]. The disagreement could be due to difference in study population and diagnostic modalities used, for instance these studies deploy MDR-Presumptive TB patients while the current study included all presumptive TB and MDR-presumptive patients and presumptive TB cases dominated.

Limitation of the study

This study only included Public Hospitals. It didn’t include private hospitals, private clinics public health centers in the region which are providing TB diagnosis and treatment. This might affect its generalization to Somali region. In addition, due to nature of retrospective study design, we could not collect a number of variables that could be important.

Conclusion and recommendation

The magnitude of TB was higher in the study sites. The overall prevalence of drug resistance was also higher; especially among previously treated patients. Male sex and having history of TB contact were statistically associated with TB. Early laboratory diagnosis, improving early Treatment initiation and monitoring, and scaling up of TB specimen referral linkage to where rapid molecular diagnostics tests sites are highly recommended.

Incidental Discovery of Pericardial Cyst on Abdominal Ultrasound
Authors: Yasuhito Sekimoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTDuring a routine health check‐up, a 53‐year‐old woman underwent an abdominal ultrasound, suggesting the presence of fluid accumulation within the thoracic cavity. For further evaluation, chest computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging were performed, revealing a cystic lesion, which was diagnosed as a pericardial cyst.Keywords: abdominal ultrasound, incidental discovery, pericardial cyst

A 53‐year‐old woman underwent an abdominal ultrasound during a routine health checkup, which suggested the presence of fluid accumulation within the thoracic cavity (Figure 1. Panel A, arrows). The patient had no specific symptoms, including shortness of breath. A chest computed tomography (CT) performed for further evaluation by the internal medicine team revealed a giant left‐sided cardiac cystic lesion up to 10 cm in diameter (Figure 1 Panel B, arrow). For further evaluation, chest magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was also performed, demonstrating T2 hyperintensity of the cystic lesion (Figure 1 Panel C, arrow). Based on MRI and abdominal ultrasound findings, the lesion was diagnosed as a pericardial cyst. Abdominal CT and ultrasound showed no evidence of cystic lesions in the liver or kidneys. Pericardial cysts are rare, benign, congenital causes of a mediastinal mass with an incidence of 1 in 10,000 [1]. Most cases are incidentally detected on chest radiography or echocardiography and are often asymptomatic [1]. However, large pericardial cysts may require surgical resection due to chest pain or shortness of breath [1]. To the best of my knowledge, there are no reports of pericardial cysts being incidentally discovered on abdominal ultrasound. Cases such as the present one, discovered during an abdominal ultrasound at a medical checkup, require careful attention.

Panel A depicts an abdominal ultrasound finding from a routine health checkup. Panel B illustrates the computed tomography image of the lung. Panel C reveals T2 hyperintensity on chest magnetic resonance imaging.

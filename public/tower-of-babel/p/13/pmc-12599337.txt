Immune persistence and safety of the AS01E-adjuvanted respiratory syncytial virus prefusion F protein-based vaccine in adults 50–59 years of age, including at-increased-risk adults: A randomized controlled trial
Authors: Murdo Ferguson, Tino F. Schwarz, Sebastián A. Núñez, Juan Rodríguez-García, Marek Mital, Carlos Zala, Bonavuth Pek, Bernhard Schmitt, Nicole Toursarkissian, Dolores Ochoa Mazarro, Josef Großkopf, Christine Voors-Pette, Hemalini Mehta, Hiwot Amare Hailemariam, Catherine Gérard, Silvia Damaso, Marie-Pierre David, Dominique Descamps, Judith Hill, Corinne Vandermeulen, Veronica Hulstrøm
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTIn this phase 3, randomized, placebo-controlled multi-country study, the AS01E-adjuvanted respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) prefusion F protein-based vaccine (adjuvanted RSVPreF3) was immunologically non-inferior at 1 month post-vaccination in adults 50–59 years of age (YOA) compared to adults ≥60 YOA, in whom efficacy was previously demonstrated. Here, we report end-of-study immunogenicity and safety results until 12 months post-vaccination. Participants 50–59 YOA were divided into an at-increased-risk (AIR) and a non-AIR subcohort, each randomized (2:1) to receive one dose of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 or placebo. A control cohort of participants ≥60 YOA received adjuvanted RSVPreF3. Humoral/cell-mediated immunity and safety were assessed. In total, 1152 participants 50–59 YOA received adjuvanted RSVPreF3 or placebo, and 381 participants ≥60 YOA received adjuvanted RSVPreF3. In all vaccinated groups, RSV-A and RSV-B neutralizing titers and RSVPreF3-specific polyfunctional CD4+ T-cell frequencies increased at 1 month post-vaccination and declined thereafter, but remained above baseline and placebo levels at 6 and 12 months post-vaccination. During the study, one vaccine-related serious adverse event (cold-type hemolytic anemia in a participant ≥60 YOA) was reported. Five participants died (all in the 50–59 YOA AIR subcohort, four had received adjuvanted RSVPreF3, one placebo); none of the deaths were considered vaccine-related. In conclusion, adjuvanted RSVPreF3 induced a humoral and cell-mediated immune response persisting until at least 12 months post-vaccination in adults 50–59 YOA, including those at increased risk for RSV disease. The vaccine had an acceptable 12-month safety profile in adults 50–59 YOA, consistent with that in adults ≥60 YOA.Trial Registration: ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05590403

Introduction

Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) is a leading cause of respiratory illness and can affect individuals of all ages.1 The two major antigenic groups (A and B) circulate predominantly during winter months in temperate climates and have a more variable circulation in tropical climates.2 RSV especially affects infants and older adults, as well as individuals with chronic medical conditions (e.g., immunocompromised status, diabetes, or cardiopulmonary disease such as congestive heart failure, asthma, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease).3,4

To address the disease burden of RSV, several vaccines have been developed and approved for use in older adults. These include an adjuvanted protein-based vaccine (AS01E-adjuvanted RSV prefusion F protein-based vaccine [adjuvanted RSVPreF3], Arexvy, GSK), a protein-based vaccine (RSVpreF, Pfizer Inc.), and an mRNA-based vaccine (mRNA-1345, Moderna).5–8 In addition, multiple other RSV vaccines are currently in development.9 The efficacy of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 was 82.6% (96.95% confidence interval [CI]: 57.9–94.1%) against RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease (RSV-LRTD) and 94.1% (95% CI: 62.4–99.9%) against severe RSV-LRTD during one RSV season in adults ≥60 years of age (YOA).10 Adjuvanted RSVPreF3 efficacy against RSV-LRTD was demonstrated over three RSV seasons.11 The vaccine was also efficacious against RSV-LRTD among adults ≥60 YOA with underlying conditions associated with an increased risk for RSV disease (94.6% [95% CI: 65.9–99.9%] over one season).12

As individuals 50–59 YOA with such chronic conditions can also experience severe RSV disease, which can impact their quality of life,13,14 they may benefit from receiving RSV vaccination. On 16 April 2025, RSV vaccination was recommended by the Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices in adults 50–59 YOA who are at increased risk of severe RSV disease.15 We performed the present study to assess the immunogenicity and safety of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 in adults 50–59 YOA compared to adults ≥60 YOA, the population in which efficacy has been demonstrated. Results for the primary objectives of this study were previously published and showed that the vaccine was immunologically non-inferior in adults 50–59 YOA (both in those with and without underlying conditions that increase the risk for RSV disease) compared to adults ≥60 YOA at 1 month post-vaccination.16 The study also showed that the vaccine’s safety profile was comparable in both age groups up to 6 months post-vaccination.16 As evaluation of the durability of the immune response and longer-term safety in adults 50–59 YOA is important, the study assessed the immunogenicity and safety of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 up to 12 months post-vaccination. Patients and methods

Study design, participants, and interventions

This phase 3, randomized, placebo-controlled study (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05590403) was conducted in 60 centers in eight countries (Argentina, Canada, Germany, Japan, the Netherlands, Poland, Spain, and the United States). The study included a cohort with participants 50–59 YOA, which was divided into an at-increased-risk (AIR) and a non-AIR subcohort, as well as a control cohort with participants ≥60 YOA. Additional information on the study design and study participants was disclosed previously.16 Briefly, the AIR subcohort included participants at increased risk of RSV-LRTD, meaning that participants were diagnosed with at least one of the following pre-defined, stable, chronic medical conditions: chronic pulmonary disease, chronic cardiovascular disease, diabetes mellitus types 1 and 2, chronic renal disease, and chronic liver disease. Additional information on eligibility criteria and enrollment rules was disclosed in the Supplementary Methods of Ferguson et al.16 The inclusion criteria of the ≥60 YOA cohort were in line with those in the adjuvanted RSVPreF3 efficacy study.10

Participants in the non-AIR and AIR subcohorts were randomized (2:1 in each subcohort) to one of the two possible interventions: one intramuscular dose of either adjuvanted RSVPreF3 (50–59-non-AIR-RSV and 50–59-AIR-RSV) or placebo (50–59-non-AIR-placebo and 50–59-AIR-placebo) (Figure 2). Participants in the ≥60 YOA cohort were to receive one intramuscular dose of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 (≥60-RSV) (Figure 2). The study was single-blind for the 50–59 YOA cohort for the objectives presented in the current paper and open-label for the ≥60 YOA cohort. Additional information on vaccine composition, randomization, and blinding was disclosed previously.16 The study was conducted according to the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and applicable regulatory requirements. The protocol and amendment were approved by the relevant ethics committees and are available, together with the statistical analysis plan, on https://www.gsk-studyregister.com/en/trial-details/?id=219238. All participants provided written informed consent before any study-specific procedures were performed. Figure 2.Study design. Objectives

The primary objectives (immunological non-inferiority in participants 50–59 YOA, including those with underlying conditions that increase the risk for RSV disease, versus those ≥60 YOA at 1 month post-vaccination) and secondary safety outcomes until 6 months post-vaccination were disclosed previously.16 Secondary objectives included in this paper were the evaluation of the humoral immune response, cell-mediated immunity (CMI), and safety outcomes until 12 months after adjuvanted RSVPreF3 vaccination.

Immunogenicity assessments

Information on sampling, participant allocation, and laboratory assays was disclosed in the Supplementary Methods of Ferguson et al.16 Blood sampling timepoints are presented in Figure 2. We report results until 12 months post-vaccination.

Safety assessments

Solicited adverse events (AEs) within 4 days, unsolicited AEs within 30 days, and serious AEs (SAEs) and potential immune-mediated diseases (pIMDs) within 6 months after vaccination were disclosed previously.16 SAEs, pIMDs, and atrial fibrillation events with a fatal outcome or considered related to adjuvanted RSVPreF3 or placebo administration were collected until 12 months after vaccination and are reported here. A list of pIMDs is reported in the protocol (p70–75) available at https://www.gsk-studyregister.com/en/trial-details/?id=219238.

Statistical methods

Information on sample size and analysis sets was disclosed previously.16 The immunogenicity and safety endpoints reported here were analyzed descriptively. A post-hoc subgroup analysis by sex was performed for RSV-A and RSV-B neutralizing titers at pre-vaccination and 1 month post-vaccination.

Numbers and percentages of participants with SAEs, pIMDs, and atrial fibrillation events were calculated with exact 95% CIs. Further information on statistical methods was described previously.16

All analyses were conducted using SAS Life Science Analytics Framework version 5.2 and SAS Data Science version 3.6 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA). The data lock point of the reported analyses was 12 June 2024, with a safety data lock on 17 April 2024.

Results

Study participants

A total of 1544 participants were enrolled between 28 October 2022 and 30 January 2023, of whom 1533 received adjuvanted RSVPreF3 or placebo. In the 50–59 YOA cohort, 383 (non-AIR) and 386 (AIR) received adjuvanted RSVPreF3, and 192 (non-AIR) and 191 (AIR) received placebo. In the ≥60-RSV cohort, 381 participants received adjuvanted RSVPreF3. A total of 1465 (95.6%) participants completed the study, and 68 (4.4%) participants were withdrawn. At 12 months post-vaccination, 1303 participants were included in the per-protocol set for humoral immunity, and 208 in the per-protocol set for CMI (Figure 3). Flow of participants.

Demographic and baseline characteristics of participants who received adjuvanted RSVPreF3 or placebo were generally balanced between the cohorts, except for age and medical condition associated with an increased risk for RSV-LRTD, and were presented previously.16 In the 50–59-AIR groups, 69.2% (50–59-AIR-RSV) and 71.2% (50–59-AIR-placebo) had exactly one condition associated with an increased risk for RSV-LRTD, while the rest had at least two of these conditions. Based on the end-of-study analysis, 24.4% of participants in the ≥60-RSV group had one of these conditions and 13.6% of participants had at least two.

Immunogenicity

As disclosed previously, RSV-A and RSV-B neutralizing geometric mean titers (GMTs) increased substantially from pre- to 1 month post-vaccination in the different groups of adjuvanted RSVPreF3-vaccinated participants.16 Subsequently, GMTs progressively declined until 12 months post-vaccination, but remained higher than those measured pre-vaccination or in the placebo groups; mean geometric increases at 12 months post-vaccination versus pre-vaccination ranged from 3.90 to 4.08 for RSV-A and from 3.10 to 3.35 for RSV-B across vaccinated groups (Figure 4). In the placebo groups, RSV-A and RSV-B neutralizing GMTs remained similar to baseline levels at all timepoints (Figure 4). Seroresponse rates, defined as the proportions of participants with an ≥4-fold increase in RSV-A or RSV-B neutralizing titers from pre- to post-vaccination, were lower at 6 and 12 months post-vaccination than at 1 month post-vaccination but remained above the seroresponse rates in the placebo groups (Table 1). A subgroup analysis by sex showed no clinically meaningful differences in the humoral immune response between men and women at 1 month post-vaccination (Supplementary Table S1). This analysis was not performed at later timepoints. As disclosed previously, the median frequencies of RSVPreF3-specific CD4+ T cells expressing at least two activation markers, including at least one cytokine, among CD40L, 4-1BB, IL-2, TNF-α, IFN-γ, IL-13, IL-17, increased from pre- to 1 month post-vaccination in the different groups of adjuvanted RSVPreF3-vaccinated participants.16 Median frequencies declined over time but remained higher than baseline and placebo levels until 12 months post-vaccination (Supplementary Figure S1). No RSVPreF3-specific CD8+ T-cell response was observed in any of the groups.

Safety

Safety results up to 6 months after adjuvanted RSVPreF3 or placebo administration were disclosed previously.16 The end-of-study analysis resulted in minor adjustments to the previously reported numbers of participants with at least one SAE within this timeframe: 3 (0.8%) participants in the 50–59-non-AIR-RSV, 4 (2.1%) in the 50–59-non-AIR-placebo, 15 (3.9%) in the 50–59-AIR-RSV, 4 (2.1%) in the 50–59-AIR-placebo, and 9 (2.4%) in the ≥60-RSV group reported at least one SAE with onset within 6 months after adjuvanted RSVPreF3 or placebo administration. Based on the end-of-study analysis, the number of pIMDs remained as presented by Ferguson et al.16 The previously reported vaccine-related SAE (case of cold-type hemolytic anemia in a participant in the ≥60-RSV group) that started 52 days post-vaccination was still ongoing at study end (12 months post-vaccination). Additional details about this case are reported in Ferguson et al.16 No other vaccine-related SAEs or pIMDs were reported during the study.

In addition to the two previously reported events of atrial fibrillation that occurred within 6 months post-vaccination in the ≥60-RSV group,16 three more cases were reported in three participants up to study end. Two of these were SAEs (one in the 50–59-non-AIR-RSV and one in the 50–59-AIR-RSV group) and one was a non-serious AE (in the ≥60-RSV group). None of these were considered as vaccine-related by the investigator. The two serious atrial fibrillation events were reported at 170 days and 214 days after adjuvanted RSVPreF3 administration by a participant with hypertension and obesity, and a participant with idiopathic peripheral neuropathy and anal carcinoma in addition to several AIR conditions, respectively. The non-serious atrial fibrillation event occurred 181 days after adjuvanted RSVPreF3 administration in a participant with diabetes mellitus, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and hyperthyroidism.

Five participants died during the study, all in the 50–59 YOA AIR subcohort. Four of these participants were included in the 50–59-AIR-RSV group (one participant died due to peripheral arterial occlusive disease and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, one due to septic shock and multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, one due to chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and one died of unknown causes). The fifth participant was included in the 50–59-AIR-placebo group and died of unknown causes. None of the fatal SAEs were judged as adjuvanted RSVPreF3-related by the investigator. The time to death varied from 302 to 359 days post-vaccination.

Discussion

We previously reported the primary outcomes of this phase 3 study, demonstrating that the humoral immune response induced by adjuvanted RSVPreF3 in adults 50–59 YOA was non-inferior to that in adults ≥60 YOA at 1 month post-vaccination and that this was the case for 50–59-year-olds with and without chronic conditions that increase the risk for RSV disease.16 Together with the previously demonstrated efficacy in adults ≥60 YOA,10,11,17 this indicated that adjuvanted RSVPreF3 would likely be efficacious in adults 50–59 YOA. The end-of-study results presented here show that, although there was a decline in RSV-A and RSV-B neutralizing titers over time after the initial increase at 1 month post-vaccination, the neutralizing titers at 12 months remained above baseline and placebo levels in participants 50–59 YOA who received adjuvanted RSVPreF3, including in those at increased risk for RSV disease. RSV-A and RSV-B neutralizing titers in the 50–59 YOA subcohorts at 6 and 12 months post-vaccination were in the same range as those in the ≥60 YOA cohort, suggesting similar persistence of the vaccine-induced humoral response. In addition, the frequencies of RSVPreF3-specific CD4+ T cells were still high at 12 months post-vaccination in participants 50–59 and ≥60 YOA who received adjuvanted RSVPreF3 compared to their respective baselines and to the placebo groups. These observations are consistent with previously published CMI persistence data in adults ≥60 YOA.18 As T-cell immunity may also be involved in protection against RSV disease and in reducing disease severity,19,20 the high CD4+ T-cell frequencies at 12 months post-vaccination observed in the present study are noteworthy. Our results show that adjuvanted RSVPreF3 elicits robust RSV-specific humoral and cellular immune responses that persist until at least 12 months post-vaccination in adults 50–59 YOA, including those at increased risk for RSV disease. Given the important disease burden of RSV in this age group, evidenced by increased hospital admissions and greater use of medical resources among patients with underlying medical conditions,4,21,22 and its impact on their quality of life,13,14 the adjuvanted RSVPreF3 is anticipated to substantially reduce the health burden associated with RSV in this age category. In line with previously published results in adults ≥60 YOA,18,23 no RSVPreF3-specific CD8+ T-cell response was observed 1 month post-vaccination in the present study. In adults challenged with RSV, CD8+ T-cell responses were detected against M, N, P, NS1, and NS2 peptides but not against F-derived peptides,24 suggesting that the F protein may be a suboptimal CD8+ T-cell antigen in humans.

Throughout the 12-month follow-up period, no SAEs or pIMDs related to the adjuvanted RSVPreF3 vaccine were reported in the 50–59 YOA cohort. While there were five cases of atrial fibrillation (including two in 50–59 YOA participants vaccinated with adjuvanted RSVPreF3) and five deaths among 50–59 YOA AIR individuals (including four in adjuvanted RSVPreF3 recipients), investigators did not attribute any of these events to the vaccine. Atrial fibrillation events were initially monitored based on a numerical imbalance observed post-vaccination in the large adjuvanted RSVPreF3 phase 3 efficacy and safety trial.17 However, a comprehensive review of cases across all trials showed that the atrial fibrillation incidence remained below population rates,25 that cases occurred in participants with high-risk profiles for onset of atrial fibrillation or in participants with preexisting conditions, and that there was no consistent pattern or evidence of a vaccine-related risk. Our results show that a single dose of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 had an acceptable safety profile over 12 months in adults 50–59 YOA.

A limitation of this study was the exclusion of individuals with immunocompromising conditions, who are also at increased risk for severe RSV disease.26,27 This limitation is addressed in another ongoing phase 2 trial among adults ≥18 YOA who received a lung or kidney transplant (NCT05921903).28 Additionally, while participants with neurological diseases, known to be at increased risk for RSV disease, were not excluded from the non-AIR subcohort, they were not specifically included in the AIR subcohort either, leading to their potential inclusion in either group. Lastly, the study was underpowered to identify rare AEs.

This study has several strengths. The immunobridging design is in line with the World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines on the clinical evaluation of vaccines for trials using immunogenicity data to predict vaccine efficacy in the absence of a correlate of protection.29 A specific threshold level of an immune marker for protection has not been identified for RSV. The WHO guidelines state that immunogenicity data from trials designed to demonstrate non-inferiority, as is the case for the present study, can be used to bridge to estimates of vaccine efficacy from previous clinical trials when both trials use the same vaccine formulation and assay.29 As mentioned in the guidelines, the comparator is a key factor in result interpretation.29 In the present study, a control group of participants representative of the population of the previous efficacy trial (≥60 YOA cohort) was included to allow for a direct comparison of the immune response with the new target population (50–59 YOA cohort). The addition of control groups allowed us to assess potential seasonal as well as regional differences related to RSV epidemiology. Furthermore, the results can be extrapolated due to the heterogenous study population and the inclusion of different geographic areas.

In conclusion, this study showed that a single dose of adjuvanted RSVPreF3 induced a humoral and cell-mediated immune response in adults 50–59 YOA (including in those at increased risk for RSV disease) that remained above baseline and placebo group levels until at least 12 months post-vaccination. Immune persistence was similar to that in adults ≥60 YOA. In addition, the vaccine had an acceptable 12-month safety profile in adults 50–59 YOA, in line with that in those ≥60 YOA. Together with the previously demonstrated efficacy of the adjuvanted RSVPreF3 in ≥60-year-olds, these results suggest that a single dose of the vaccine may help prevent RSV disease in 50–59-year-olds. As younger adults and children with certain underlying medical conditions can also be at increased risk for severe RSV disease, studies in these younger populations should be carried out to determine whether they could gain a similar benefit from RSV vaccination

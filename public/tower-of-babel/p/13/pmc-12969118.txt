SSC-BanglaTutor: A curriculum-aligned Bengali dataset for intelligent tutoring systems
Authors: Eshraque Jabid Ifti, Fihab Ifty, Mehadi Hasan, Rahul Chandra Shil, Utshab Kumar Saha, Kazi Tanvir, Mahfujur Rahman, Dipta Gomes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This dataset presents a Bengali-language dataset designed to fine-tune AI powered hint-based tutoring systems for the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) science curriculum in Bangladesh. This data includes 11,286 hint-based question–answer entries, comprising 4859 questions from Biology covering 14 chapters, 3034 from Chemistry across 12 chapters, and 3393 from Physics spanning 14 chapters. All items were created manually using government-issued textbooks, SSC focused study materials, and past exam question banks. Each question is paired with candidate answers containing one correct option and several closely related but incorrect options to help measure the effectiveness of the hints. A convergence score is attached to each entry, estimating how far a student may need to go through the hints to answer correctly. These features support personalized feedback and offer meaningful insight into the students’ learning progress. The dataset is encoded in UTF-8, with some English terms retained for scientific precision and consistency with source materials. This makes it accessible to native learners while remaining valuable for low-resource Natural Language Processing (NLP) applications. By emphasizing curriculum alignment, ranked hinting, and learner modeling, the dataset provides a strong foundation for fine-tuning large language models (LLMs) and developing intelligent tutoring systems that are both linguistically inclusive and educationally effective.

Background

Educational practices vary widely across regions and countries due to differences in curriculum, teaching techniques, local language, assessment methods, and available resources. These variations affect how optimal learning support looks in different contexts such as the structure of textbooks, exam patterns, and terminology differ [1]. Therefore, influencing the need for adaptations to achieve effective student outcomes is important. Students often rely on teachers and local teaching institutions to guide exam preparation, concept explanation, and problem-solving strategies, but also for learning techniques and improved accessibility. Due to their ability to process natural language and generate contextual responses, Large Language Models (LLMs) have high potential within an educational context [2]. However, they need to be updated and retrained with the latest curriculum-specific information and within the national language applied to provide relevant support for learning. Lack of language-specific knowledge has earlier been shown to be a limitation of the capability of LLMs to provide valuable answers to education-related problems [3]. As LLMs and AI more generally are reshaping the global technological landscape, many communities across the world remain unsupported due to the language limitations of existing models [4]. Training LLMs requires large amounts of text data, which is not readily available in most languages other than English. Bangla remains underrepresented in vision–language work and is seen as low-resource for visual question answering (VQA) because region-specific benchmarks are low [5]. This gap restricts the applicability and usefulness of generative AI and risks widening existing inequalities in access to quality learning support.

Furthermore, LLMs are adapted using two primary methods: fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) [6]. LLMs are likely to have high potential in education, but as discussed earlier, their application is limited by insufficient specific data [4]. Moreover, there is currently a lack of easily accessible digital text data (i.e., retrievable from general sources) for adapting LLMs to the Bangladeshi secondary education curriculum in the Bengali language [7]. For example, searches for openly available datasets show almost no structured question–answer resources in Bengali for the SSC science curriculum, while in comparison, there are hundreds of datasets available in English for science and education. This imbalance underscores the urgent need for targeted dataset development.

This dataset aims to construct a resource describing typical exam-focused question–answer pairs with progressive hints in Biology, Chemistry, and Physics, aligned with the SSC curriculum. The dataset size is designed to be comparable to other similar projects, such as efforts to create educational corpora for Ukrainian language [8]. Improving the educational performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) requires continuous adaptation to subject-specific content and updated knowledge sources [9]. Dynamic datasets that enable regular updates to questions and answers can enhance the adaptability of LLMs to evolving real-world knowledge and improve their integration of retrieved information [10]. Jointly training retrieval and generation modules has also been shown to produce more contextually accurate and semantically coherent responses in educational question-answering tasks [11].

The dataset can be used to improve pre-trained LLMs (fine-tuning or RAG) or integrate into existing Bengali language datasets. The main goal is to facilitate the development of LLMs that are better adapted to the educational needs of Bengali-speaking students.

Data Description

The SSC-BanglaTutor [12] dataset has 11,286 well-structured questions and exact answer entries from 14 chapters in secondary school certificate (SSC) level Biology, 12 chapters in Chemistry, and 14 chapters in Physics. Each entry is saved in the JSON Line (.jsonl) format, which makes it easy to use in NLP system and large-scale processing. Every record includes a question in Bengali, step by step hints, exact answer, candidate answer including correct one and incorrect answers based on common mistakes students often make, convergence scores. It includes scores that show how likely a student is to get the answer right at each hint. Convergence-ranked hints are arranged in sequence, with each hint becoming more relevant to the correct answer, and topic tags linked to the curriculum. This design supports both AI model training and effective classroom teaching.

The dataset is distributed across three core science subjects. This subject-wise distribution ensures that fundamental concepts such as cell biology, genetics, human physiology (Biology), chemical reactions, acids and bases (Chemistry), energy, and mechanics, energy system, modern science (Physics) are all covered across multiple chapters. The balanced representation across Biology, Chemistry, and Physics ensures a comprehensive foundation for SSC-level science tutoring. Each file corresponds to a specific chapter and is encoded in UTF-8, ensuring compatibility with Bengali NLP tools and preventing issues with joint letters or compound characters.

The total number of words in the text of the dataset is referred to as the number of tokens in this context. For example, take the Bengali sentence (”Photosynthesis is a biological process.”) when broken into tokens it becomes [, , , , ] ([‘Photosynthesis’, ‘is’, ‘a’, ‘organic’, ‘process’]), which gives a total of five. By contrast, vocabulary size is about how many unique words appear in the dataset. So, if the tokens are [, , , , ] ([‘cell’, ‘organism’, ‘cell’, ‘organism’, ‘work’]), the vocabulary would simply be [, ] ([‘cell’, ‘organism’, ‘work’]), which means the size is three. In short, the token count tells you how long the dataset is, while vocabulary size shows how diverse the words are.

Besides the structure, the datasets also show a wide range of word groups like single words, two-word phrases, three-word phases, and four-word phrases. These often include scientific terms and common expressions obviously in Bengali. This mix of words shows that the dataset has a lot of educational content and the right vocabulary needed for science learning. Because of the variety, the dataset is a good base for training AI tutors to give helpful, clear, and subject focused guidance to students.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The dataset was created by a systematic process which is shown in Fig. 5. In the first step, text books covering three subjects were gathered from an authentic government website [13], while simultaneously, popular question banks were collected from a reliable source [14]. Subsequently, question and answer pairs were compiled that corresponded to the topics in the SSC science book. Here the questions were not directly copied. The selection was based on how well they matched the learning goals of each chapter. This made sure the dataset could help in creating hints in the next steps. For the clarity, helpful learning, guidance for the students the questions were checked correctly. This check gave a good base for the next steps of making the dataset.Fig. 5Roadmap of dataset creation.Fig 5: dummy alt text

Roadmap of dataset creation.

In the following step, at least five contextual hints were constructed for each question to help students understand the concepts step by step. The hints were organized in order from least specific to most exact, starting with general ideas and getting closer to the correct answer. The following format was developed for mimicking the step-by-step help that a human tutor usually gives. To evaluate their effectiveness, each hint was given a convergence ranking, means that, for each question, hints are ordered from least to most revealing based on how strongly they guide an SSC student toward the correct answer. This supports step-by-step tutoring by revealing hints progressively, where higher-ranked hints provide stronger guidance and increase the likelihood of a correct response. Hint-1 provides a broad conceptual reminder. Hint-2 introduces a key definition. Hint-3 adds the critical relationship or rule needed to proceed; Hint-4 narrows the solution path by pointing to the decisive step. And Hint-5 is the most direct, answer-enabling clue. Hints were written to be curriculum-aligned, unambiguous, and progressively informative, while avoiding premature answer leakage in early hints. Finally, each hint sequence was checked. The rank is the hint’s position in the Convergence_Ranked list (1st, 2nd, …), and the Convergence Scores cumulatively represent the expected probability of answering correctly after seeing the first k ranked hints. Which shows the point in the sequence when a student is most likely to get the correct answer.

Convergence measurement involved assessing how many hints steps a typical user needed to arrive at the correct answer. Hints that did not align well with the question or the target answer, or that failed to support this step-by-step progression, were therefore edited for relevance and clarity or removed when necessary to preserve overall instructional quality After convergence validation, finalized question–hint sets were subjected to grammar and spelling checks to ensure linguistic clarity. Lastly, each entry was tagged with its respective topic or chapter label for organized navigation and subject-wise filtering.

This design makes the dataset suitable not only for education but also for use in intelligent tutoring systems, as its structure follows how students are usually guided in real learning situations.

Conclusion

This article highlights the importance of designing datasets that not only cover textbook content but also support interactive, stepwise learning. By organizing question–answer items with contextual hints and aligning them to academic chapters, the work bridges the gap between static content and active tutoring support. Rather than just collecting data, the goal was to create a usable learning tool that encourages understanding, not memorization.

While building this dataset, few challenges were faced during sorting relevant questions and adjusting scaffolding hints It was hard to collect a large and varied set of good quality SSC-level science questions from trustworthy sources, which limited the overall coverage of topics. Another challenge was writing helpful hints for each question. Lastly, the dataset currently covers only three subjects: Biology, Chemistry, and Physics. These are core passes of the SSC science syllabus, but adding more subjects in the future would make the dataset complete and more balanced.

This dataset will be useful in the future to train or fine-tune AI-based tutoring systems offering hint-based feedback in Bengali, specifically for low-resource students. It will also be useful to calibrate question difficulty, track learning progress, and measure student misconceptions. Scaling this dataset up to more subjects like Mathematics or ICT and integrating it into adaptive learning software has the potential to greatly expand the reach and value of Bengali-language AI in education.

As part of future work, the focus will be on training the model thoroughly using this dataset. A complete system will be developed based on the trained model, and a detailed research paper will be prepared to present the development process and the results comprehensively.

Ethics Statement

No human subjects or private data were used in the construction of this dataset. All educational material was sourced from publicly available curriculum standards, and no content violates privacy or ethical norms.

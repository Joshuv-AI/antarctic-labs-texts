HPV infection trends in Inner Mongolia across the pre- and pandemic periods of COVID-19: A single-center analysis
Authors: Muzi Jin, Nan Yang, Xiao Bai, Lifei Liu, Xiaonan Guo, Nannan Lü, Xin Zhang, Nan Ta, Lihua Hui
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study compares overall and risk-category (high-risk vs low-risk) human papillomavirus (HPV) infection rates among women undergoing health examinations in Hohhot, Inner Mongolia, before and during the COVID-19 pandemic. We analyzed 3714 women tested for HPV from January 2020 to December 2024. The participants were divided into 2 groups based on the local epidemiological timeline in Inner Mongolia: a pre-pandemic group (January 2020-December 2022, when COVID-19 cases were sporadic under strict containment measures), and a pandemic group (January 2023-December 2024, after restrictions were lifted and community transmission became widespread). The primary outcomes were overall HPV positivity and high-risk versus low-risk HPV positivity. Group differences were assessed using chi-square tests (α = 0.05). The overall HPV infection rate was 17.56% (652/3714). The positivity rate was 18.02% in the pre-pandemic period versus 16.89% in the pandemic period. Across years, the overall positivity and the high-risk positivity peaked in 2021, with significant heterogeneity across years (overall χ²=28.512, P <.001; high-risk χ²=25.940, P <.001). High-risk HPV positivity exceeded low-risk positivity in all years. In this single-center cohort, the overall HPV positivity showed a slight decline during the pandemic period compared with the pre-pandemic period, while high-risk types remained more common than low-risk types. These findings underscore the need to maintain HPV screening and vaccination efforts during public health emergencies.

1. Introduction

Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a small, non-enveloped double-stranded DNA virus known for its strong association with various cancers, particularly cervical cancer.[1] Based on oncogenic potential, HPV types are categorized into low-risk types, which cause benign lesions such as genital warts, and high-risk types, which are implicated in the development of malignancies including cervical, anal, and oropharyngeal cancers.[2] Cervical cancer remains a significant global health challenge, being the fourth most common cancer among women and a leading cause of cancer-related mortality, especially in low- and middle-income countries.[3,4]

In China, the burden of cervical cancer has been increasing, with more than 100,000 new cases and over 30,000 deaths reported annually.[5] Persistent infection with high-risk HPV genotypes is the primary etiological factor in the development of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia and invasive cervical cancer.[6] The implementation of HPV vaccination programs has demonstrated substantial efficacy in reducing the incidence of HPV infections and subsequent cervical cancer rates. Vaccines like Cervarix (targeting HPV16/18), Gardasil (targeting HPV6/11/16/18), and Gardasil 9 (covering HPV6/11/16/18/31/33/45/52/58) offer robust protection against the most common high-risk HPV types associated with cervical carcinogenesis.[7]

Despite these advances, the COVID-19 pandemic introduced new challenges to the epidemiology of HPV. It exerted widespread pressure on healthcare systems and disrupted preventive public health interventions, including HPV vaccination and cervical cancer screening. Evidence shows that cancer prevention services were significantly interrupted across many Asian countries, including China, where vaccination and screening programs were suspended or delayed.[8] Globally, HPV vaccination coverage declined substantially, with average coverage among girls in low- and middle-income countries dropping from 65% before the pandemic to 50% during 2020 to 2021.[9] In China, registry data show that although HPV vaccination coverage among females aged 9 to 45 years increased each year from 2017 to 2022, overall levels remained low: by 2022, only 10.15% had received at least 1 dose and 6.21% had completed the full series. In Inner Mongolia, the corresponding rates were 6.60% for the first dose and 4.22% for the full series, both below the national average.[10] Such disruptions in healthcare services, together with changes in sexual behavior and healthcare-seeking behavior during the pandemic, may have influenced HPV transmission dynamics.

This study focuses on the Inner Mongolia region of China, where data on HPV prevalence and genotype distribution before and during the COVID-19 pandemic period remain sparse. Notably, in Inner Mongolia, unlike many global settings, large-scale COVID-19 transmission began only after the lifting of restrictions in early 2023, which defines the start of the pandemic period in this study. By analyzing the HPV infection rates among women undergoing routine gynecological examinations in Hohhot, this study aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the impact of the pandemic on HPV epidemiology in this region. The findings will contribute to a better understanding of the interplay between infectious disease pandemics and the epidemiology of other significant public health concerns, such as HPV-related cancers. Additionally, the study’s results may inform future strategies for cervical cancer screening and HPV vaccination programs, particularly in regions with similar demographic and epidemiological profiles.

In addition, the COVID-19 pandemic strained healthcare delivery and public health programs across China, including Inner Mongolia. Periods of heightened infection control were associated with temporary reductions or delays in routine services such as HPV vaccination and cervical cancer screening. Such service disruptions and changes in healthcare-seeking behaviors may have influenced observed HPV infection patterns in this region. In the present analysis, we therefore focus inference on overall and risk-category (high-risk vs low-risk) HPV infection rates across the pre-pandemic and pandemic periods.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Study population

This study included 3714 women who underwent routine gynecological examinations at the Hohhot International Travel Healthcare Center between January 2020 and December 2024. Based on local epidemiology, we classified examinations in 2020 to 2022 as the pre-pandemic period and in 2023 to 2024 as the pandemic period. During the pre-pandemic period, 2192 women were examined, when local COVID-19 cases were minimal. During the pandemic period, 1522 women were examined following the lifting of restrictions and the onset of widespread community transmission.

2.2. Sample collection

Cervical samples were collected by trained clinicians using a cervical brush. The brush was inserted into the cervical os and rotated clockwise 4 to 6 times to obtain an adequate sample of epithelial cells. The brush was then placed into a sterile tube containing a cell preservation solution. If immediate HPV testing was not possible, the samples were stored at 4°C for up to 2 weeks. For long-term storage, samples were kept at −20°C, ensuring that the storage period did not exceed 1 year to preserve sample integrity.

2.3. HPV genotyping

DNA extraction from the collected cervical samples was performed using a commercial DNA extraction kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The extracted DNA was then subjected to HPV genotyping using the HPV nucleic acid detection and genotyping kit provided by Guangzhou HEAS Bio Tech Co., Ltd. This kit is capable of detecting 28 different HPV subtypes, including 15 common high-risk HPV types (16, 18, 31, 33, 35, 39, 45, 51, 52, 56, 58, 59, 66, 68, and 82), 3 less common high-risk types (26, 53, and 73), and 10 low-risk HPV types (6, 11, 40, 42, 43, 44, 54, 61, 81, and 83). The presence of HPV DNA in the samples was confirmed by real-time polymerase chain reaction, and the specific genotypes were identified through nucleic acid hybridization.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Categorical data were summarized as counts and percentages. The chi-square test (χ²) was used to compare overall and risk-category (high-risk vs low-risk) HPV infection rates across time periods and years. A 2-sided P-value <.05 was considered statistically significant. Analyses were conducted in SPSS version 26.0.

No inferential statistical analyses were conducted for genotype-specific, age-specific, or multiple-infection data because of small sample sizes in several strata.

There were no missing data for age or HPV testing in the final dataset used for analysis.

2.5. Ethical considerations

This study was conducted following the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Hohhot International Travel Healthcare Center. Informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their inclusion in the study. All personal data were anonymized to ensure participant confidentiality.

3. Results

3.1. Overall HPV positivity and risk category by period and yearly trend

Among 3714 women tested for HPV between January 2020 and December 2024, 652 were HPV-positive, yielding an overall positivity of 17.56% (Table 1). The overall positivity rate was 18.02% in the pre-pandemic period (2020–2022) and 16.89% in the pandemic period (2023–2024). Across years, the overall positivity peaked in 2021, and there was significant heterogeneity in overall positivity across years (χ² = 28.512, P <.001).

3.2. High-risk vs low-Risk HPV positivity

Across the study period, high-risk HPV positivity consistently exceeded low-risk positivity (Table 1). High-risk HPV positivity was also highest in 2021, with significant variation across years (χ² = 25.940, P <.001). These findings underscore that high-risk HPV infections remain the predominant burden, highlighting the importance of maintaining cervical cancer screening and vaccination efforts even during public health emergencies.

4. Discussion

Cervical cancer continues to be a critical public health concern, particularly in China, where the incidence continues to rise despite advances in screening and vaccination. This study found that the overall HPV infection rate among women undergoing routine gynecological examinations in Hohhot was 17.56%, which is higher than the rates reported in Beijing (7.16%–8.85%),[11] Hengyang, Hunan Province (10.16%),[12] and Xiamen (15.13%),[13] but lower than those reported in Shandong (28.4%)[14] and Guangzhou Province (21.66%).[15] These comparisons underscore regional differences in HPV prevalence, which may reflect variations in healthcare access, screening methods, sample sizes, and population demographics.

In our analysis, high-risk HPV positivity consistently exceeded low-risk positivity across all years, and both overall and high-risk positivity peaked in 2021. Our results provide robust evidence on overall and risk-category HPV infection rates across the pre-pandemic and pandemic periods in Inner Mongolia.

The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on HPV epidemiology is evident. We observed a modest decline in overall positivity in the pandemic period compared to the pre-pandemic period. Pandemic-related disruptions to healthcare services – including temporary suspension or delays of HPV vaccination and cervical cancer screening – likely contributed to these patterns. Similar observations were reported in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, where HPV infection rates decreased slightly during the pandemic largely due to screening disruptions.[16]

These findings highlight the importance of maintaining robust HPV screening and vaccination programs, especially during public health crises. Even modest changes in infection rates can have long-term implications for cervical cancer prevention, given the predominance of high-risk HPV types in this population. Regular cervical cancer screening remains crucial, even for vaccinated individuals, as the vaccines do not cover all high-risk HPV types prevalent in the region.

Limitations: This study has several limitations. First, it is a single-center retrospective study, which may limit generalizability. Second, vaccination status, sexual history, and other behavioral risk factors were not collected. Third, potential confounding factors were not controlled due to lack of detailed data. Lastly, the pandemic period definition reflects local epidemiological conditions and may differ from global standards. Importantly, subgroup analyses were not pursued due to insufficient sample sizes, which constrained our ability to examine genotype- or age-specific patterns.

In summary, this study demonstrates a modest decline in overall HPV positivity during the pandemic period in Inner Mongolia, while high-risk HPV positivity consistently exceeded low-risk positivity across all years. These findings highlight the sustained burden of high-risk HPV infections and underscore the importance of maintaining vaccination and cervical cancer screening programs even during public health crises. Future research with larger, multi-center cohorts and integrated data on vaccination and screening is warranted to clarify how healthcare disruptions influence HPV epidemiology.

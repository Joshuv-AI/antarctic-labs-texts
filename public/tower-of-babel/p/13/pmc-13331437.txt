Ringer Lactate Versus Hybrid Isotonic/Hypotonic Saline in Delayed Graft Function in Deceased-donor Kidney Transplantation: A Real-world Protocol Change Study
Authors: Arthur Gus Manfro, Vanderlei Carlos Bertuol Junior, Fernanda Correa da Silva Alves, Andrea Carla Bauer
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background.Delayed graft function (DGF) remains a major complication after deceased-donor kidney transplantation. Balanced crystalloid solutions have been associated with lower DGF incidence compared with 0.9% saline, largely based on strategies using Plasma-lyte. However, the role of Ringer lactate compared with other low-chloride solutions, such as hypotonic saline, remains unclear. We therefore evaluated whether a protocol-driven change in perioperative crystalloid strategy was associated with DGF and early posttransplant kidney function.Methods.We conducted a retrospective, single-center, quasi-experimental study including recipients of deceased-donor kidney transplants. Patients were exposed to a protocol-driven change in perioperative hydration from a hybrid intraoperative 0.9% saline plus postoperative hypotonic saline (0.45%) strategy to an intraoperative and postoperative Ringer’s lactate strategy. The primary outcome was DGF. Secondary outcomes included DGF duration, longitudinal kidney function, electrolyte trajectories, and 12-mo clinical outcomes. Generalized additive mixed models were used to evaluate longitudinal trajectories of graft function and electrolyte balance while accounting for within-subject variability.Results.A total of 257 kidney transplant recipients were included (142 Ringer lactate; 115 hybrid isotonic/hypotonic saline), with comparable baseline characteristics. DGF incidence did not differ between groups (47.9% Ringer lactate versus 52.2% hybrid isotonic/hypotonic; P = 0.49), nor did days to renal graft recovery (5.7 versus 5.7 d; P = 0.96) or DGF duration (10.5 versus 10.2 d; P = 0.78). Mortality, graft loss, and biopsy-proven rejection at 12 mo were also similar. In covariate-adjusted longitudinal analyses, the estimated glomerular filtration rate did not differ between groups. Electrolyte trajectories showed modest differences between groups.Conclusions.In this real-world quasi-experimental study, Ringer lactate was not associated with lower DGF incidence or duration compared with a hybrid isotonic/hypotonic saline-based strategy. No differences were also observed in graft function over the 12-mo follow-up. These findings support further investigation into whether differences in chloride exposure may influence early graft outcomes.

INTRODUCTION

Delayed graft function (DGF) remains one of the major challenges in the early period following deceased-donor kidney transplantation worldwide.1 DGF is most commonly defined as the requirement for dialysis within the first week after kidney transplantation.2 Its incidence varies widely across regions, with reported rates of approximately 25%–30% in the United States and Europe,3 and exceeding 50% in Brazil.4 DGF is associated with longer hospitalization, increased healthcare costs, and more complex posttransplant management of kidney transplant recipients (KTRs).5-7 Moreover, prolonged DGF—particularly when lasting >14 d—has been consistently associated with inferior long-term graft function.8,9 Importantly, most determinants of DGF are not readily modifiable by transplant centers, since its development is multifactorial and closely related to donor characteristics and the quality of donor management before organ procurement.10,11 Additional contributing factors include prolonged cold ischemia time, static cold preservation, and recipient-related characteristics such as diabetes, prior sensitization, higher body mass index (BMI), and longer duration of renal replacement therapy before transplantation.12

More recently, the use of balanced crystalloid solutions during the perioperative period has been proposed as a potential strategy to reduce the incidence of DGF. This hypothesis gained substantial support following the publication of the BEST-Fluids trial in 2023, which demonstrated a significantly lower incidence of DGF among KTRs receiving Plasma-lyte compared with those receiving normal saline.13 Subsequent meta-analyses have corroborated these findings, reporting clinical benefits associated with balanced solutions >0.9% saline.14 In response to this growing body of evidence, updated clinical guidelines have recently recommended the use of balanced solutions in deceased-donor kidney transplantation, albeit with a moderate level of certainty.15

Despite these advances, several important knowledge gaps remain. Most existing studies have evaluated Plasmalyte as the balanced solution of choice; however, in routine clinical practice, many transplant centers worldwide predominantly use Ringer lactate (RL), which has not been extensively studied in this context. Furthermore, prior investigations evaluating RL have largely focused on living-donor kidney transplantation, a setting characterized by substantially lower rates of DGF.16-18 Additionally, comparisons of balanced solutions have primarily been conducted against 0.9% saline, whereas hypotonic saline (0.45%) has not been adequately evaluated. Historically, some centers have adopted hypotonic saline for perioperative hydration in kidney transplantation because of its lower chloride content and higher free-water delivery, which has been hypothesized to reduce hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis and attenuate chloride-related renal hemodynamic effects; however, clinical evidence in deceased-donor kidney transplantation remains limited.19,20 Evidence testing the use of hypotonic fluids and RL is also desirable for the sake of resource utilization, since in many countries those crystalloids are considerably less expensive than other buffered solutions such as Plasma-lyte.

Protocol-driven changes in perioperative fluid strategies provide natural experiments that may generate clinically relevant evidence in transplantation practice. In this retrospective study, we aimed to compare real-world data on perioperative hydration strategies using RL and hypotonic saline in deceased-donor kidney transplantation, to help inform practice where comparative data remain limited.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study Design and Population

The present study is a retrospective single-center quasi-experimental study evaluating a protocol-driven change in perioperative crystalloid strategy involving KTRs at the Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre, a large tertiary teaching hospital located in southern Brazil. At our transplant center, perioperative hydration protocols were modified in August 2023. Before this change, all KTRs received normal saline (sodium chloride 0.9%) intraoperatively and hypotonic saline (HS, sodium chloride 0.45%) during postoperative care—defined as a hybrid approach (hHS). After protocol implementation, RL was used exclusively, both intraoperatively and postoperatively. Between April 2022 and July 2023, a transition phase occurred in which some patients received mixed hydration strategies (RL intraoperatively and HS postoperatively). To minimize contamination, we excluded these transition phase patients (April 2022–July 2023). Given the historical protocol-change design rather than a true randomized study, caution is warranted in interpreting results as causative, since even modest differences in some sample characteristics can influence the results.

Institutional Transplantation Protocol

All patients were evaluated by the on-call transplant nephrologist before kidney transplantation. Preoperative dialysis was prescribed only if patients presented with clinically relevant volume overload, significant metabolic acidosis (HCO3 < 17 mEq/L), or hyperkalemia (K > 5.3 mEq/L). If dialysis was performed, patients were left 1–2 kg above their dry weight. The immunosuppression protocol was based on universal induction therapy with 500 mg of methylprednisolone administered during transplant surgery and 3 mg/kg of thymoglobulin given within the first 24 h postoperatively. In immunologically high-risk transplants, particularly when donor-specific antibodies were present, additional thymoglobulin doses were administered up to a cumulative dose of 6 mg/kg. Maintenance immunosuppression consisted of tacrolimus (initially 0.1 mg/kg every 12 h), sodium mycophenolate (initially 720 mg every 12 h), and prednisone (20 mg once daily). Steroids were subsequently tapered monthly until reaching 5 mg/d. During transplant surgery, patients received an intraoperative crystalloid volume equivalent to achieve a volume status 5% above their dry body weight. Postoperative fluid administration was guided by hourly urine output according to the following algorithm: urine output <100 mL/h prompted an infusion rate of 100 mL/h; urine output between 100 and 300 mL/h prompted replacement at 100% of urine output; and urine output >300 mL/h prompted replacement at 80% of urine output. Regarding postoperative dialysis indication, prescription was determined by the transplant nephrology team. Dialysis was indicated only when patients presented with volume overload and/or acidosis or hyperkalemia associated with insufficient urine output or failure to respond to conservative treatment options. Surveillance biopsies were performed in the second week of transplantation if the patient persisted in DGF. No other planned protocol changes were implemented besides crystalloid modification; however, secular trends in perioperative and transplant care over time cannot be fully excluded.

Based on prior literature suggesting a 10% absolute reduction in DGF incidence and a baseline DGF rate of approximately 50% in Brazil, we estimated that a sample size of 236 KTRs would be required to achieve 80% power with a 95% confidence level. For the RL group, we included all adult KTRs who received a brain-dead deceased-donor kidney transplant between August 2023 and August 2024. All organs were preserved with cold-storage techniques, without the use of machine-perfusion. For the hHS group, we included KTRs transplanted between 2019 and 2023 who received hypotonic saline and did not receive balanced crystalloid solutions. Patients transplanted between January 2020 and March 2022 were excluded because of major transplant policy changes implemented during the COVID-19 pandemic. The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board and local ethics committee of the Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre (CAAE No. 88377125.8.0000.5327), and waived the need for written consent from all patients.

Data Collection and Outcomes

Demographic and clinical data were obtained from electronic medical records. All adult recipients of deceased-donor kidney transplants were considered eligible. Exclusion criteria included age <18 y, early death or graftectomy because of surgical complications, primary graft nonfunction, and insufficient clinical data. The primary outcome was DGF, defined as the requirement for dialysis within the first week following kidney transplantation. Secondary outcomes included DGF duration and metabolic parameters, including serum creatinine, estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), serum potassium, serum sodium, and serum bicarbonate levels. eGFR was calculated using the Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration Creatinine equation (2021).21 Serum creatinine and eGFR were assessed on postoperative days 7, 14, 21, 28, 60, 90, 180, and 360. Electrolyte measurements were obtained on postoperative days 1, 2, 3, and 7, as available. Outcomes of KTR death, graft loss, and rejection episodes were obtained in a 12-mo follow-up. Prespecified sensitivity analyses were planned to evaluate DGF incidence across clinically relevant subgroups, including kidney donor profile index (KDPI) > 80, calculated panel reactive antibody >85%, cold ischemia time >24 h, and recipient age >60 y.

Statistical Analysis

Categorical variables were compared using chi-square tests, and continuous variables were compared using Student t tests, as appropriate. Negative binomial regression models were used to evaluate time to kidney graft function (defined as the first day when the patient presented a spontaneous creatinine drop) and DGF time among those patients who developed it. Firths’ penalized logistic regression models were used as to evaluate variables associated with DGF incidence. Generalized additive mixed models (GAMMs) were used to estimate and compare smoothed adjusted longitudinal trajectories of serum creatinine and electrolyte levels between hydration strategies. This approach combines the flexibility of generalized additive models with the ability to account for within-subject correlation through random effects. Time was modeled as a continuous variable using penalized regression splines, with separate smooth functions for each treatment group to allow group-specific trajectories. Random intercepts for each patient were included to account for repeated observations within individuals. By incorporating such repeated measurements, the model estimates individual smooth trajectories for each patient and integrates them into a regression framework to assess group-level differences over time. GAMMs were estimated using restricted maximum likelihood. Missing data were handled using all available observations, as mixed-effects models allow inclusion of incomplete repeated measurements under the missing-at-random assumption without imputation. Longitudinal analyses were adjusted for the following possible confounders: age, sex, BMI, time on renal replacement therapy, calculated panel reactive antibody, KDPI, cold ischemia time, vascular anastomosis time, and donor on vasopressors. All statistical analyses were performed using R software,22 with the tidygam and ggplot2 packages.23,24 A 2-sided P value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

A total of 257 KTRs were included in the study (142 received RL and 115 received hHS). Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were comparable between groups (Table 1). The incidence of DGF was 47.9% in the RL group and 52.2% in the hHS group, with no evidence of a between-group difference (RR, 0.92; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.72-1.17; P = 0.49; Figure 1). Time to kidney graft function was also equal among groups, with a mean of 5.7 d (IRR, 0.99; 95% CI, 0.70-1.40; P = 0.96). Among recipients who developed DGF, its duration was also similar between hydration strategies (RL 10.5 versus hHS 10.2 d; IRR, 0.97; 95% CI, 0.78-1.20; P = 0.78). No statistically significant differences were found between the RL and hHS groups regarding mortality (RL, 2.8%; hHS, 0%, RR, 7.24; 95% CI, 0.39-133.05; P = 0.18), graft loss (RL, 3.5%; hHS, 0.9%; RR, 4.01; 95% CI, 0.47-33.9; P = 0.20), and biopsy-proven rejection episodes (RL, 5.6%; hHS, 7.0%; RR, 0.81; 95% CI, 0.31-2.09; P = 0.66). Prespecified subgroup analyses were consistent with the main findings (Figure 2). Penalized logistic regression models demonstrated that hydration protocols were not associated with DGF incidence, corroborating the main findings. After correction in multivariate analyses, only recipient BMI and cold ischemia time remained statistically significant for DGF incidence. DISCUSSION

In this retrospective quasi-experimental study, a perioperative RL hydration strategy was not associated with lower DGF incidence or shorter DGF duration compared with a hybrid isotonic/hypotonic strategy. No statistical difference was found regarding graft function in the follow-up after correction for confounding variables. Subgroup analyses and secondary analyses were consistent with the overall findings. However, despite statistical robustness, our data and results should be interpreted cautiously given the nonrandomized design and potential residual confounding.

The literature has hypothesized that the potential benefits of balanced crystalloid solutions are related to their more physiological composition, lower risk of hyperchloremic acidosis, and alkalinizing effects.25,26 In critically ill patients, some studies have favored balanced solutions over sodium chloride,27,28 although similar benefits have not been consistently demonstrated in noncritical populations.29 In the specific context of DGF prevention after kidney transplantation, most evidence favoring balanced solutions comes from comparisons against pure 0.9% saline, particularly with Plasma-lyte-based strategies.14,15 Our study differs because our comparator was a hybrid isotonic/hypotonic saline strategy. Therefore, our findings should not be interpreted as contradicting current randomized evidence versus 0.9% saline; rather, they suggest that, in real-world settings where chloride exposure may already be partially mitigated, the incremental benefit of buffered crystalloids may be attenuated. Our study’s findings should also not be interpreted as potential evidence for superiority of Plasmalyte over RL, since this comparison was also not directly tested. Even though direct comparisons between those buffered crystalloids are scarce and largely limited to small studies, none of them demonstrated clinically significant or sustained renal outcomes.30,31

Our findings should be interpreted by distinguishing what was directly observed by the study design and results from what can be hypothesized. Our study demonstrates that a perioperative RL-based strategy was not associated with lower DGF incidence or shorter DGF duration compared with a hybrid isotonic/hypotonic saline approach. In contrast, mechanistic explanations on such findings remain speculative. The chloride hypothesis is biologically plausible based on prior experimental and clinical observations. Chloride may induce afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction through activation of tubuloglomerular feedback and renin-mediated signaling,32-35 potentially increasing vascular resistance and reducing renal cortical perfusion.36 In addition, the low strong ion difference of chloride-rich crystalloids has been associated with hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis, which may adversely affect kidney function.37,38 However, our study was not primarily designed to test mechanistic pathways, and we did not systematically measure serum chloride or full gasometrical acid-base parameters, rendering this mechanistic pathway only hypothetical. Our data do not allow causal inference or direct mechanistic conclusions, so further studies incorporating mechanistic measurements are needed to clarify whether and how chloride exposure influences early graft outcomes.

Our study has several important strengths. First, it reflects a real-world protocol change in clinical practice at a high-volume transplant center, thereby enhancing external validity and clinical relevance. The quasi-experimental design reduces selection bias compared with purely observational approaches. Second, we assessed not only DGF incidence, but also DGF duration, longitudinal kidney function, metabolic parameters, and clinically relevant outcomes over a 12-mo follow-up period. Third, the use of modern statistical modeling techniques, particularly GAMMs, represents a methodological advancement. By accounting for individual heterogeneity and nonlinear trajectories of kidney function and electrolyte changes, these models provide a more biologically plausible framework for interpretation.

However, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the retrospective single-center design limits causal inference and may not fully account for residual confounding, rendering the results primarily hypothesis-generating. Second, despite excluding KTRs during the COVID-19 pandemic and the institutional transition phase of fluid selection, differences in exposure timing and consistency persisted between groups. For instance, patients in the RL group were hydrated exclusively with RL, whereas patients in the hHS group received a portion of their intraoperative fluids as normal saline. Third, as a retrospective study, we could not account for all variability in intraoperative and postoperative fluid administration, including total volume delivered and the proportion of isotonic/hypotonic volume administered in the hybrid group. Although institutional protocols standardized fluid composition, heterogeneity in administered volumes may have introduced residual confounding. In addition, although most demographic and clinical characteristics were similar between groups, the time of transplantation differed, raising the possibility that secular trends in transplant care influenced some outcomes. Fourth, direct central venous pressure measurements were unavailable in postoperative care, limiting volemic assessment of the KTR. Fifth, the study design may not have been sufficiently powered to detect small yet clinically significant differences in DGF incidence, particularly if the effect size of fluid choice is modest. Lastly, given the quasi-experimental setting, results should not be definitively interpreted as causative—this is especially valid for longitudinal biochemical analyses, which can be influenced by residual confounding variables and transplant eras.

In conclusion, in this single-center quasi-experimental cohort, a RL strategy was not associated with lower DGF incidence or shorter DGF duration compared with a hypotonic saline-based strategy. Within the hypothesis-generating framework of this study, these findings raise the possibility—rather than establish—that previously identified superiority of balanced crystalloids may be related, at least in part, to differences in chloride exposure. Future studies incorporating robust confounding control and mechanistic measurements are needed to further clarify whether specific crystalloid compositions meaningfully influence early graft outcomes.

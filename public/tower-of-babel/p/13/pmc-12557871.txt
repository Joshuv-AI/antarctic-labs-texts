CMTM6 drives glioblastoma progression by promoting M2 polarization and suppressing antigen presentation in microglia/macrophages
Authors: Xiang Li, Wenbo Han, Wenpei Cai, Ruoxin Yang, Gaojie Bai, Changxiu Sun, Shiyuan Ge, Cheng Zhang, Xinyi Han, Han Yang, Yi Chen, Qiao Shan, Xiaohui Li, Xueli Tian, Yuan Lyu, Junqi Li, Xinjun Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundImmune checkpoint blockade therapies, particularly targeting PD-1/PD-L1 axis, have shown limited efficacy in glioblastoma (GBM), primarily due to the profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment dominated by glioma-associated microglia and macrophages (GAMs). While CMTM6 is known to stabilize PD-L1 in tumor cells by preventing its ubiquitin-mediated degradation, its role in microglia remains undefined.MethodsWe employed a multi-omics approach to evaluate CMTM6 expression and function. By analyzing the bulk RNA-seq data sets from TCGA and CGGA, as well as single-cell data sets (TISCH2 and UCSC), the expression of CMTM6 across different glioma grades and immune cell populations was examined. Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence were used to validate CMTM6 expression and co-localization with microglial/macrophage markers in GBM tissues. In vitro, CMTM6 knockdown was performed in HMC3 microglial cells to assess changes in cytokine and immune checkpoint expression. In vivo, a tamoxifen-induced, microglia/macrophage-specific Cmtm6 conditional knockout mouse model was used to investigate tumor growth and survival outcomes.ResultsCMTM6 was significantly upregulated in high-grade gliomas and enriched in M2-like microglia/macrophages. Immunostaining confirmed elevated CMTM6 and CD68 expression in GBM samples, with CMTM6 co-localizing with microglial markers (CD68, IBA1, and TMEM119). By knocking down CMTM6 in HMC3 cells, the levels of CD274 (PD-L1) and TGFβ isoforms decreased, while the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines IL6 and CCL3 increased, suggesting a shift toward an M1-like phenotype. In vivo, microglia/macrophage-specific deletion of Cmtm6 suppressed tumor growth, delayed body weight loss, and extended survival duration.ConclusionsThis study identifies CMTM6 as a critical regulator of the immunosuppressive phenotype of microglia/macrophages in GBM. Targeting CMTM6 in microglia/macrophages may represent a novel strategy to reprogram the tumor immune microenvironment and improve the efficacy of immunotherapy in GBM.Keywords: Glioma, Glioblastoma, Tumor immune microenvironment, Microglia/macrophages, CMTM6, PD-L1

Introduction

Immunotherapy has revolutionized the treatment landscape of multiple malignancies [1–5], achieving durable responses in cancers such as melanoma and non-small cell lung cancer using immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) to target the PD-1/PD-L1 axis [6–8]. However, the clinical efficacy of treatment for glioblastoma (GBM), the most aggressive and lethal primary brain tumor in adults, remains disappointing and limited [9–13]. The failure of ICB in GBM stems primarily from its profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME), which is largely dominated by glioma-associated microglia and macrophages (GAMs), making up 30–50% of the tumor mass [14–16]. These innate immune cells typically show anti-inflammatory and tumor-supporting characteristics, which support tumor progression and undermine immune responses against the tumor [17–19].

Recent research has identified CKLF-like MARVEL transmembrane domain-containing protein 6 (CMTM6) as a key regulator in stabilizing PD-L1. Found in the plasma membrane and recycling endosomes, it binds to PD-L1, protecting it from ubiquitin-mediated degradation and maintaining its presence on cell surface [20, 21]. While CMTM6’s role in tumor cells in supporting PD-L1-mediated immune evasion is well-studied, its function in immune cells within the TME, especially in microglia/macrophages, remains largely unexplored. To our knowledge, this is the first research to investigate CMTM6 expression and function specifically in microglia and macrophages within the GBM microenvironment, and to functionally dissect its role in shaping immune suppression both in vitro and in vivo using conditional knockout models.

Microglia, the immune cells residing the central nervous system, play a dual role in pathogenesis and progression of GBM: they can either exhibit pro-inflammatory, tumor-suppressive characteristics or adopt an immunosuppressive, tumor-supportive traits [22–26]. Given the significant plasticity and abundance of microglia and infiltrating macrophages in GBM, they represent compelling targets for immunomodulatory therapy [27–32]. However, the molecular factors driving the phenotypic switch of microglia and macrophages in the context of GBM still need further investigation.

In this study, we investigated the role of CMTM6 in microglia/macrophages and its contribution to immunosuppressive programming within the GBM microenvironment. By integrating multi-omics bioinformatics, in vitro co-culture assays, and in vivo conditional knockout models, we demonstrated that CMTM6 promoted microglia to shift toward M2-like polarization and supported PD-L1-mediated immune evasion. Our findings identified CMTM6 as a novel immune regulator in glioma-associated microglia and macrophages, suggesting that targeting CMTM6 in these cells may enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy for GBM.

Methods

Bioinformatics analysis

Transcriptomic data from glioma patients were obtained from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) databases [33–37]. CMTM6 expression across glioma grades and its correlation with patient prognosis were assessed. The STRING database was used to construct protein–protein interaction networks involving CMTM6, while the TIGER database was used to explore its relationship with immunotherapy response [38]. The TIMER2.0 platform was employed to assess immune cell infiltration and the correlation between CMTM6 expression and microglia/macrophage markers [39]. Single-cell transcriptomic analyses were performed using TISCH2 and the UCSC to determine the cell-type-specific expression pattern of CMTM6 in glioma samples [40, 41].

Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence

Paraffin-embedded GBM and normal brain tissues were subjected to immunohistochemical (IHC) staining for CMTM6 (1:300, SIGMA) and CD68 (1:500, CST). For immunofluorescence (IF), tissue sections were co-incubated with CMTM6 (1:300, SIGMA) and one of the following primary antibodies: CD68 (1:500, CST), IBA1 (1:300, MERCK), TMEM119 (1:250, NOVUS), or GFAP (1:300, Proteintech), followed by incubation with fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Images were acquired using a slide scanning system (TEKSQRAY, China).

Cell culture and CMTM6 knockout

Human microglial HMC3 cells were cultured in MEM (ZSGB-BIO, China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (ExCell Bio, China). Lentiviral vectors expressing CMTM6-targeting sgRNAs or non-targeting controls were used for transduction. Knockout efficiency was validated by quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) and western blot (WB). Co-culture experiments were conducted by seeding glioma cells (LN229, U87, or T98G) in transwell inserts (Bioland, China) above HMC3 monolayers for 48 h, followed by RNA extraction.

qRT-PCR and WB

RNA extraction was carried out using an RNA extraction kit (HGDB, China), followed by cDNA synthesis using a qPCR reverse transcription kit (YEASON, China). qPCR was performed using SYBR Green Master Mix (YEASON, China) on a real-time PCR System (LongGene, China). GAPDH was used as an internal control. For Western blotting, proteins were separated by SDS–PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. After blocking, membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against CMTM6 (1:1000, SIGMA), PD-L1 (1:1000, ABCAM), and GAPDH (1:5000, Proteintech), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies and detection via Alliance micro Q9 of UVITEC (England) (Tables 1 and 2).

Transcriptome sequencing

RNA-seq was performed on control and CMTM6-knockdown HMC3 cells. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using DESeq2 (fold change ≥ 2, adjusted p < 0.05).

In vivo mouse model and microglia-specific Cmtm6 knockout

Cx3cr1CreERT2/+; Cmtm6fl/fl mice were used to achieve microglia/macrophage-specific deletion of Cmtm6 upon tamoxifen induction. Mice were administered 1 mg per mouse intraperitoneally once daily for 10 days. After a 1-week rest period, mice were intracranially injected with 1 × 105 GL261 glioma cells. Tumor burden was monitored using in vivo imaging (IVIS) and MRI. Survival was assessed using Kaplan–Meier analysis.

Statistical analysis

Transcriptomic data were processed and visualized with R (v4.4.2). Statistical significance was determined using GraphPad Prism (v7.0), with results presented as mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Student’s t test was used for comparisons between two groups, and one-way ANOVA was applied for multiple group comparisons. Survival was analyzed using Kaplan–Meier curves, with differences evaluated by the log-rank test. Linear associations were measured using Pearson correlation coefficients. The significance levels were established as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, ****P < 0.0001, and ns denoting no statistical significance.

Results

High CMTM6 expression in GBM correlates with unfavorable prognosis and elevated levels of immunosuppressive infiltration

We first explored the expression pattern and clinical relevance of CMTM6 in gliomas using TCGA and CGGA databases. CMTM6 expression was significantly upregulated in high-grade gliomas, compared to lower-grade gliomas and normal brain tissues (Fig. 1a–c, e, and f). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis revealed that patients with high CMTM6 expression exhibited significantly shorter overall survival (Fig. 1d, g). The STRING-based PPI network revealed a strong predicted interaction between CMTM6 and PD-L1 (CD274) (Fig. 1h), consistent with its known function in stabilizing PD-L1 on the cell membrane. Analysis of the TIGER database demonstrated that high CMTM6 expression was associated with poor response to anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy (Fig. 1i). TIMER2.0 analysis showed that CMTM6 expression strongly correlated with markers of tumor-infiltrating macrophages and microglia (Fig. 2). Single-cell RNA-seq data sets (TISCH2, UCSC) further confirmed that CMTM6 is predominantly expressed in M2-like microglia/macrophage clusters within GBM (Fig. 3a–d). These findings support the notion that CMTM6 not only stabilizes PD-L1 protein but also contributes to immune checkpoint resistance. The link between high CMTM6 expression and poor immunotherapy response positions CMTM6 as both a prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target to overcome innate and adaptive immune resistance in GBM.

CMTM6 is localized to tumor-associated microglia/macrophages in GBM tissues

To determine the spatial localization of CMTM6 in GBM, we performed immunohistochemistry (IHC) and immunofluorescence (IF) on patient tissues. IHC analysis showed markedly higher expression of CMTM6 and CD68 in GBM tissues (Fig. 3e). IF revealed that CMTM6 colocalizes extensively with microglia (Fig. 4). These results confirm that CMTM6 is mainly expressed in tumor-associated microglia within the GBM microenvironment, supporting its functional relevance in these immune populations.

Transcriptome profiling reveals immune activation and antigen presentation upon CMTM6 depletion

To examine the global effects of CMTM6 knockdown, we performed RNA sequencing on HMC3 cells and conducted pathway enrichment analyses. We knocked out CMTM6 in human microglial HMC3 cells using lentiviral sgRNA and assessed the expression of immunomodulatory factors (Fig. 5a, b). qPCR and Western blot analyses showed that CMTM6 knockdown reduced PD-L1 (CD274) and anti-inflammatory cytokines (TGFβ1/2/3) while significantly upregulating pro-inflammatory factors, such as IL6 and CCL3 (Fig. 5b–d). Transcriptome analysis identified differentially expressed genes enriched in pathways related to antigen processing and presentation, inflammation, and autophagy. These findings suggest that CMTM6 acts as a negative regulator of microglial immune activation and that its depletion enhances antigen presentation capabilities. Loss of CMTM6 in microglia promotes a shift from an anti-inflammatory, immunosuppressive phenotype toward a more pro-inflammatory, M1-like state. We co-cultured HMC3 microglia with glioma cell lines (LN229, U87, and T98G) and measured the expression of immune-related genes. Co-culture with glioma cells increased CMTM6, CD274, and TGFβ1 expression in microglia while reducing TNFα (Fig. 5e). Tumor cells actively reprogram microglia into an immunosuppressive phenotype through CMTM6-dependent mechanisms, potentially facilitating immune evasion.

Microglia/macrophage-specific Cmtm6 deletion suppresses tumor growth and prolongs survival in vivo

Finally, we generated tamoxifen-inducible Cx3cr1CreERT2; Cmtm6fl/fl mice and implanted orthotopic GL261 gliomas to assess tumor growth and survival (Fig. 6a, b). MRI and IVIS imaging revealed that mice with Cmtm6-deficient microglia/macrophages exhibited reduced tumor burden and lower fluorescence intensity compared to controls (Fig. 6c, d). Body weight analysis showed a noticeable trend of weight loss starting at day 17 after tumor cell implantation in control mice. In contrast, Cmtm6 knockout mice exhibited significantly less weight loss (Fig. 6e). HE staining confirmed a smaller tumor size, and Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated significantly prolonged survival (Fig. 6d, f). Selective deletion of Cmtm6 in microglia/macrophages impairs glioma progression and enhances host survival, underscoring its functional role in tumor-promoting immune regulation.

Discussion

Despite recent advances in ICB, GBM continues to resist immunotherapy due to its profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment [42–44]. GAMs, constituting a major component of the GBM immune landscape, often adopt an M2-like phenotype that supports tumor progression and impairs anti-tumor immunity [45, 46]. Thus, understanding the molecular mechanisms behind this immunosuppressive shift is critical for developing more effective immunotherapeutic strategies.

In this study, we identified CMTM6 as a key immunoregulatory molecule in microglia and macrophages, facilitating immune escape in GBM. Our integrated transcriptomic analysis revealed that CMTM6 expression was elevated in high-grade gliomas, correlated with poor prognosis and increased immune evasion. Single-cell analysis further revealed that CMTM6 was predominantly expressed in M2-like microglia and macrophages. Through transcriptomic sequencing of CMTM6 in HMC3 cells, we found that CMTM6 knockout promoted microglia to shift toward M1 phenotype. Besides, when HMC3 cells were co-cultured with glioma cells, the presence of tumor cells significantly increased CMTM6 expression in microglia, along with elevated levels of CD274 (PD-L1) and the anti-inflammatory cytokine TGFβ1, while the pro-inflammatory cytokine TNFα was notably downregulated. Evidence from other tumor models revealed that CMTM6 participated in several oncogenic signaling pathways, such as MAPK, mTOR, Wnt/β-catenin, and others [47–51]. In oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC), CMTM6-containing exosomes secreted by tumor cells promoted M2 polarization through the ERK1/2 pathway [52]. Similarly, GAMs are shaped by tumor-derived factors. It is probable that CMTM6 regulates immune phenotypes through ERK1/2-dependent mechanisms.

Conditional deletion of Cmtm6 in microglia and macrophages in vivo significantly attenuated tumor growth and prolonged survival time in an orthotopic glioma model. However, the clinical translation of these findings remains an essential consideration. So far, no small-molecule inhibitors or CRISPR therapies targeting CMTM6 have entered clinical trials. Nevertheless, several promising preclinical strategies have emerged. Multiple studies identified that microRNAs such as miR-340-5p, miR-622, miR-140, and miR-451 could reduce CMTM6 expression [49, 53–55], suggesting that RNA-based therapeutics could indirectly modulate CMTM6 expression to restore antitumor immunity. In addition, the ImmuneNOVA project, supported by Eurostars and in collaboration with Adlego Biomedical AB, is actively developing innovative small-molecule inhibitors for CMTM6 [56]. A notable development is the nanobody 1A5, developed by Jia et al., which strongly binds CMTM6, disrupting its ability to stabilize PD-L1, thus enhancing CD8⁺ T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity and promoting tumor apoptosis both in vitro and in vivo [57]. Though still in the preclinical stage, these efforts underscore the potential of targeting the CMTM6–PD-L1 axis to overcome immune evasion in solid tumors. Studies across multiple tumor models suggested CMTM6 consistently promoted M2-like immunosuppressive macrophages [52, 58–60], mirroring the role of microglia in the GBM microenvironment. These findings strongly suggest that CMTM6 plays a key role in shaping tumor-supportive macrophage phenotypes across different cancers. In human microglia, CMTM6 may contributes to immune evasion, chemoresistance, and tumor progression. Targeting CMTM6 could reprogram microglia toward an M1-like pro-inflammatory state, potentially improving the success of immunotherapy.

Previous research primarily focused on CMTM6’s role in tumor cells, particularly its function in stabilizing PD-L1 to facilitate immune evasion. However, these studies largely ignored its immunomodulatory roles in the myeloid cells within the tumor microenvironment. In contrast, our research is the first to show that CMTM6 is highly expressed in glioma-associated microglia/macrophages, promoting their transition to a tumor-supporting and immune-suppressive phenotype. By integrating single-cell RNA-seq, immunostaining, and functional assays, we provided compelling evidence that CMTM6 in microglia regulated PD-L1 expression, antigen presentation, and the balance between anti-inflammatory and pro-inflammatory cytokines. A key highlight is our use of a microglia/macrophage-specific conditional knockout mouse model, which allowed us to isolate the in vivo effects of CMTM6 deletion in myeloid cells, revealing its ability to inhibit tumor growth and increased survival time—results not previously reported. These findings not only broaden the understanding of CMTM6’s role beyond tumor-cell-specific mechanisms but also highlighted a novel therapeutic approach targeting the innate immune compartment in GBM. This focus on microglia distinguishes this study from previous work that mainly focused on tumor cells or overall TME without distinguishing specific cell types.

Our findings suggest two main therapeutic possibilities. First, targeting CMTM6 in microglia and macrophages could provide a novel approach to reprogram the immune landscape of GBM, increasing tumor responsiveness to ICB. Second, combining CMTM6 inhibition with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade might overcome adaptive resistance mediated by GAMs. Future research should evaluate the efficacy of these combination strategies in preclinical and clinical settings.

However, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. This study primarily focused on microglia and macrophages, without exploring the contributions of other stromal components, such as T cells or astrocytes. Recent research suggests that CMTM6 also plays an immunoregulatory roles beyond myeloid cells [61, 62]. These findings imply that CMTM6 may exert comparable or cooperative effects on T cells or astrocytes in the GBM context. It remains unclear whether CMTM6 deletion in microglia and macrophages indirectly modulates astrocytic reactivity and T cell infiltration or activation, possibly through cytokine interactions or improved antigen presentation. In addition, while we confirmed the immunoregulatory function of CMTM6 in vitro and in mouse models, further investigation is required to determine whether similar mechanisms apply to human GBM patients receiving immunotherapy.

In summary, our work uncovers a previously unrecognized role of CMTM6 in glioma-associated microglia/macrophages, providing a mechanistic basis for targeting myeloid-driven immunosuppression in GBM. These findings highlight CMTM6 as a promising therapeutic target to improve immunotherapy outcomes for currently incurable malignancy.

Conclusion

This study identified CMTM6 played a pivotal role in driving immunosuppressive characteristics in glioma-associated microglia and macrophages. By stabilizing PD-L1 and promoting M2-like polarization, CMTM6 suppressed innate immune activation and facilitated glioma progression. Targeted deletion of Cmtm6 in microglia and macrophages reprogrammed their phenotype toward antigen presentation and inflammation, resulting in tumor growth suppression and prolonged survival time. These findings broaden the understanding of CMTM6’s role beyond tumor-cell-specific pathways and highlighted it as a promising target for reshaping the glioma immune microenvironment. Therapeutic strategies aimed at disrupting CMTM6-mediated immunosuppression may offer a novel pathway to improve the efficacy of immunotherapy for GBM.

Comparative analysis of the growth differences between hybrid Ningdu Yellow chickens and their parentals
Authors: Yanping Wu, Cong Huang, Yue Wei, Zhaofeng Kang, Weihong Zhang, Jinfang Xie, Ligen Xiong, Min Zhou, Guosheng Zhang, Rongjun Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAlthough the local high quality chicken breed in China has excellent flavor, its growth rate is inferior to that of foreign breeds. To improve the growth rate of local chicken breeds, it is crucial to study the mechanism of chicken muscle development. Herein, Ningdu Yellow chicken was used as the research object, and a new hybrid breed (W) was obtained by crossing the G, H and D lines, which combined the excellent physiological characteristics of its parents. Combined analysis of Ningdu Yellow chickens and their parents was carried out. Chickens from 105-day-old lines (W, G, H) were selected, and breast meat and serum were extracted for transcriptome sequencing and metabolome determination to study their growth differences. The live weight, carcass weight, half-eviscerated weight, eviscerated weight, and breast muscle weight of W were significantly higher than those of G and H. Differential expression analysis identified 1700 differentially expressed genes (DEG), and gene ontology and kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) analyses identified 33 and 1 pathways related to growth and development and steroid biosynthesis, respectively. Next, pairwise analysis identified 57 KEGG pathways, among which the MAPK signaling, steroid hormone biosynthesis, tight junction, and PPAR signaling pathways were involved in growth and development. Cluster analysis found that genes highly expressed in the W group were associated with regulation of the actin cytoskeleton, riboflavin metabolism, steroid biosynthesis, and glycerophospholipid metabolism. The top 2 clusters obtained by protein-protein interaction analysis were important for the growth and development of chickens. Finally, the metabolomic analysis found key differentially accumulated metabolites (DAM) that might be account for the growth differences. Further integrated analysis identified key DEGs and DAMs that might be responsible for the observed growth differences. This study identified genes governing growth traits in Ningdu Yellow chickens, laying a theoretical foundation for the development of chicken breeding, the utilization of hybrid supporting lines, and promotion of the Chinese chicken industry.Key words: Ningdu yellow chicken, hybrid, growth, transcriptome, metabolome

INTRODUCTION

Growth traits are economically important to the animal breeding industry, such as live weight, carcass weight, half-eviscerated weight, and eviscerated weight, which are considered the most valuable growth and carcass traits. In addition, breast muscle weight is an important indicator to determine the slaughter performance of chickens. A variety of factors affect the growth performance of chickens, including genotype, age, sex, and the environment (Li et al. 2017). Fanatico et al. (2005) found that the same group of chickens with different genotypes showed significant differences in growth rates with and without outdoor rearing.

Ningdu yellow chicken from Jiangxi Province belongs to the list of national agricultural geographical indication products, with a unique appearance and taste characteristics, being popular with consumers (Xiong et al. 2022). In recent years, cross breeding has become a new trend to improve the performance of agriculture species. Sugama et al. (2014) showed that hybrids of brown spot grouper and clear water grouper exhibited better growth and survival performances than their parents. Tripp-Valdez et al. (2021) found that hybrid progeny between Haliotis rufescens (♀) and pink abalone Haliotis corrugata (♂) supported higher temperatures, indicating that the use of interspecific hybrids could potentially ameliorate the problem of slow abalone cultivation resulting from extreme temperature events, because different species differ in their thermal tolerance.

Ningdu yellow chicken H is characterized by fast growth, while Ningdu yellow chicken G has excellent egg production performance. D is a dwarf chicken that was introduced from the Institute of Animal Sciences of the Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (CAAS). To cultivate a new line of Ningdu yellow chicken suitable for the demands of the market, Ningdu yellow chicken line H, line G, and line D were selected for crossing in this study, and the obtained variety W showed obvious growth advantages. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying these advantages were unknown. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to systematically compare the growth performance differences between hybrid Ningdu yellow chickens and their parents using transcriptome sequencing and metabolomics, explore the mechanisms of the growth differences between them, and identify candidate functional genes and metabolites that might affect muscle growth and development. We hoped to achieve a breakthrough in research on the hybrid dominance mechanism of Ningdu yellow chickens. The obtained results not only have important scientific significance but also are expected to lead to the use of improved hybrids in production and create higher economic value.

In this study, the key genes and signaling pathways of the growth traits of Ningdu yellow chickens were identified, revealing the main reasons for the rapid growth rate of the hybrid Ningdu yellow chickens and providing a theoretical basis and technical support for the breeding application of Ningdu yellow chickens.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Ethics Statement

This study was approved by the Experimental Animal Ethics Committee of the Jiangxi Academy of Agricultural Sciences (JXAAS2020-0025). All animal procedures were conducted according to the guidelines for the care and use of experimental animals established by the Ministry of Agriculture of China.

Animals and Tissues

The hybridization pattern for Ningdu yellow chicken W was D male crossed with G female. Then, the offspring hens from this cross (D male * G female) were mated with the H male, ultimately resulting in W.

The 90 chickens (30 × 3) used in this study were 105-day-old Ningdu yellow chickens from Jiangxi Huida Industry Co., Ltd (Ningdu, China). Chickens were raised in cages. At around 90 days old, they were transferred from rearing cages to breeding cages, following the same feeding and immunization protocols. For slaughtering, a direct live sampling method was used. The same batch of Ningdu yellow chickens with the same feeding conditions (ad libitum access to feed) were used as candidate chickens. At 105 days old, chickens of the 3 breeds were selected from the Ningdu yellow chicken population. We recorded the live weight before slaughter (weight after fasting for 12 h), the carcass weight (after bleeding and removal of the feathers, foot cuticles, toe shells, and beaks), the half-eviscerated weight (the trachea, esophagus, crop, intestine, spleen, pancreas, gallbladder, and reproductive organs were removed. The weight of the remaining heart, liver, kidney, glandular stomach, muscle stomach (exfoliating membrane and contents), abdominal suet, fat around muscle stomach and lungs, and kidneys was determined), the eviscerated weight (the weight of the heart, liver, glandular stomach, muscle stomach, fat, head and foot was removed based on the half-eviscerated weight), the abdominal fat weight, the breast muscle weight, and the leg muscle weight of each chicken to analyze the differences among the lines.

Thereafter, ten chickens were randomly selected from each group, and breast muscle and serum samples were collected. The breast muscles were frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C for RNA extraction. Serum samples were prepared by centrifugation of whole blood at 1000 × g for 10 min after incubation for 30 min at room temperature.

RNA Extraction and Library Construction

The method described by Sayed et al. was used to extract RNA. Total RNA was extracted from breast muscle using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA) following the manufacturer's recommendations. The quantity and purity of the total RNA were analyzed using a Bioanalyzer 2100 and RNA 6000 Nano LabChip Kit (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA). Then, the purified mRNA was fragmented into short fragments using divalent cations under elevated temperature (94°C, 5–7 min). The cleaved RNA fragments were reverse transcribed into cDNA using SuperScript II Reverse Transcriptase (cat. 1896649; Invitrogen) to construct cDNA libraries. The qualified cDNA libraries were then sequenced on the Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform (Nova Seq 6000, Illumina, San Diego, CA), and 150 bp paired-end reads were acquired.

Nontargeted Liquid Chromatography‒Tandem Mass Spectrometry Metabolomics Analysis and Metabolite Identification

Serum samples from Ningdu yellow chickens were collected for liquid chromatography‒tandem mass spectrometry (LC‒MS/MS) analysis according to the method of Wang et al.. An ultra-high performance liquid chromatography system (Thermo Fisher, Karlsruhe, Germany) with a Hypesil Gold column (2.1 mm × 100 mm, 1.9 µm) coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Thermo Fisher) was used for the LC‒MS/MS analysis (carried out by Novogene Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The mobile phase comprised water with 0.1% formic acid in the positive ion mode and 5 mmol/L ammonium acetate in the negative ion mode (A) and methanol (B) at a flow rate of 0.2 mL/min. The elution gradient was set as follows: 2% B, 1.5 min; 2–100% B, 12.0 min; 100% B, 14.0 min; 100–2% B, 14.1 min; 2% B, 17 min. The Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer was operated in positive/negative polarity mode with a spray voltage of 3.2 kV, a sheath gas flow rate of 40 arb, an auxgas flow rate of 10 arb, and a capillary temperature of 320°C. The raw data files were processed using Compound Discoverer 3.1 (Thermo Fisher) to perform peak alignment, peak picking, and quantification for each metabolite. The peak intensities were normalized to the total spectral intensity and then used to predict the molecular formula based on additive ions, molecular ion peaks, and fragment ions. Peaks were then matched with the mzCloud (https://www.mzcloud.org/), mzVault (https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/CMD/manuals/man-xcali-97970-mzvault-user-manxcali97970-en.pdf), and MassList (https://chemrxiv.org/engage/api-gateway/chemrxiv/assets/orp/resource/item/6560961229a13c4d47e3bf51/original/mass-spectrometry-data-processing-in-m-zmine-3-feature-detection-and-annotation.pdf) databases to obtain accurate qualitative and relative quantitative results. Statistical analyses were performed using the R package v3.4 and Python v2.7.6. The identified metabolites were annotated using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/), HMDB (https://hmdb.ca/metabolites) and Lipid Maps database (https://www.lipidmaps.org/). The relative content of metabolites was calculated using a z Score plot, and then the trend change of metabolites among the 3 groups was determined. The functions of these metabolites were then studied using the KEGG database.

Differential Expression Analysis of Genes and Metabolites

Differential gene expression analysis was conducted using the DESeq2 R package v1.30.0 (Love et al. 2014). According to the total number of expressed genes, we used an adjusted P value to < 0.05 and an absolute log2-fold change > 0.5 (| log2FC | > 0.5) to identify DEGs (Zhaojun, 2022). The significance thresholds for DAMs were as follows: P-value < 0.05 and | log2FC | > 0.

Functional Enrichment

All the DEGs were subjected to Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses using the g:Profiler and KOBAS (Bu et al. 2021) web server tools, respectively. Reactome pathways with corrected P-values less than 0.05 were considered significantly enriched by DEGs. All pathways with P-values < 0.05 were considered to be differential pathways.

PPI Network Construction

A PPI network was constructed and used to reveal the relationship between genes or proteins and to further screen out the key DEG interaction networks. The STRING database (http://stringdb.org/) online tool was used to analyze the PPIs of the DEGs, and experimentally validated interactions with interaction score > 0.4 were selected as significant. The screened networks were visualized using Cytoscape 3.7.0 (Shannon et al., 2003). The Molecular Complex Detection Algorithm (MCODE) plug-in of Cytoscape was used to establish PPI network modules with degree cut-off = 2, node score cut-off = 0.2, k-core = 2, and max. depth =100.

Correlation analysis of DEG and DAM

Pearson's correlation analysis between DEGs and DAMs was performed using the R “psych” package (version 2.4.3). Correlations with top 30 coefficients and P values ≤ 0.05 were selected. Cytoscape software (version 3.7.2) was used to visualize the network.

Statistical Analysis

The significance of weight among hybrid Ningdu yellow chickens and their parents was evaluated using an independent samples t test.

RESULTS

Comparison of the Weight-Related Traits Between Ningdu Hybrid Chickens and Their Parentals

As shown in Figure 1, the live weight before slaughter, carcass weight, half-eviscerated weight, eviscerated weight, and breast muscle weight of the W group was significantly higher than those of groups G and H (P < 0.05), indicating a difference in growth performance between the hybrid and its parents. Furthermore, the H group had a higher slaughter weight, carcass weight, and half-eviscerated weight than the G group; however, the difference was not significant (P > 0.05). The abdominal fat weight and leg muscle weight were significantly higher in group H than in group G (P < 0.05). Thus, the results showed that group W had a better comprehensive slaughtering performance.

Metabolome Analysis Among 3 Lines of Ningdu Yellow Chickens

A total of 499 metabolites were identified in the 3 Ningdu yellow chicken lines using MS matching, and the cluster heatmap of all metabolites showed that the levels of these metabolites had significant and specific characteristics between the G, W, and H groups (Supplementary Figure S1A). These metabolites mainly included lipids and lipid-like molecules (33.036%), organic acids and derivatives (18.527%), and organoheterocyclic compounds (18.304%) (Supplementary Figure S1B). Subsequently, we focused on the metabolite variation in the comparison group W vs. H (WVSH) and W vs. G (WVSG).

In the WVSH comparison, 75 DAMs were obtained; among which, 36 DAMs were significantly upregulated and 39 were downregulated (P-value < 0.05 and |log2FC > 0|) (Figure 2A). Furthermore, to explore the influence of DAMs between the W and H groups on potential metabolic pathways, functional analysis was conducted. The results showed that these DAMs were most significantly enriched in cyanoamino acid metabolism; glycine, serine, and threonine metabolism; valine, leucine, and isoleucine biosynthesis; and aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis (Figure 2B). Then, we identified the top 20 important DAMs among the 75 DAMs between the W and H groups, which included N-Methylnicotinamide, N-methylnicotinamide, D-serine, 13-1-hydroperoxylinoleic acid, and 3-hydroxycinnamic acid (Figure 2C). The change in the level of glaucarubin was the most notable among the upregulated DAMs, while the change in the level of 13-L-hydroperoxylinoleic acid was most significant among the downregulated DAMs (Figure 2D). This suggested that changes in these metabolites might contribute to the difference in growth performance between the W and H groups.

(B) KEGG enrichment analysis of differential metabolites. The smaller the p-value, the larger the area. Different colors represent different count values. P-values < 0.05 were considered to be significant. (C) Heatmap of contents of differential metabolites. (D) Twenty differential metabolites with the most significant fold changes.

In the WVSG comparison, a volcano map of the 23 identified DAMs showed that 12 were upregulated and 11 were downregulated (P-value < 0.05 and |log2FC > 0|) (Figure 3A). The results of KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that these DAMs were closely related to phenylalanine, tyrosine, and tryptophan biosynthesis; phenylalanine metabolism; histidine metabolism; starch and sucrose metabolism; and pyrimidine metabolism (Figure 3B). As shown in Figure 3C, these DAMs showed specific abundance changes. In addition, glaucarubin and some fatty acids (such as phosphatidylcholine (PC) (22:5 [7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z]/16:0) and PC (22:6 [4Z,7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z]/20:1[11Z]) showed the most significant changes among the upregulated DAMs, while butylamine showed the most significant changes among the downregulated DAMs (Figure 3D). Thus, the changes in these metabolites might lead to the difference in growth performance between W and G, and could be one of the reasons for the good egg production performance of G.

Differential Gene Expression and Pathway Enrichment Analyses

A total of 1700 DEGs were identified between hybrid Ningdu chickens and their parents (P-value < 0.05 and |log2(fold change)| > 0.5). Among them, 682 (317 upregulated and 365 downregulated) and 880 (230 upregulated and 650 downregulated) DEGs were found in the comparison groups WVSH and WVSG, respectively (Figure 4A). In addition, 753 DEGs were identified in the comparison between group G and group H, among which 569 genes were upregulated and 184 genes were downregulated in the G group.

Differential gene expression analysis between hybrid Ningdu Yellow chickens and their parentals. (A) Bar plot showing the count of DEGs in pairwise comparison groups. (B) Expression heatmap of all identified genes. (C) KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of the 3 comparison groups.

The hierarchical clustering heatmap of all DEGs showed that G, H, and W each had different expression modes (Figure 4B). More highly expressed genes were clustered in group G. Among these DEGs, we found many genes related to fatty acid metabolism, amino acid synthesis and metabolism, and glucose metabolism. These nutrients can significantly affect the normal growth, differentiation, and tissue development of muscle fibers and tissues, as well as the deposition rate and decomposition rate of protein and fat in normal metabolic processes. Surprisingly, many DEGs related to the cytoskeleton, immunity, adhesion factors, enzyme activity, and iron transport were also found (Supplementary Table S1).

Significance analysis of the functional enrichment of 1700 DEGs suggested that there were 33 significantly enriched GO terms (21 biological process (BP), 9 cellular component (CC), 3 molecular function (MF)) and 1 KEGG pathway (adjusted P-value < 0.05) (Supplementary Figure S2). It is worth noting that the 21 BP enriched items were significantly associated with growth and development, such as tissue development, animal organ development, system development, and cell differentiation. (Supplementary Table S2). Moreover, the 1 pathway identified using KEGG analysis was steroid biosynthesis (Supplementary Table S2).

Next, KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of pairwise DEGs identified 18 (GVSH), 17 (WVSG) and 22 (WVSH) significantly enriched items (P-value < 0.05) (Figure 4C). In the GVSH comparison group, the main enriched pathways included metabolic pathways, the mitogen activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling pathway, tight junctions, the transforming growth factor (TGF)-beta signaling pathway, and the peroxisome proliferator activated receptor (PPAR) signaling pathway. However, in the WVSG comparison, the Wnt signaling pathway, tight junctions, the TGF-beta signaling pathway, and steroid hormone biosynthesis were the most enriched pathways. For the DEGs between W and H, the most enriched pathways were the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, tight junctions, steroid biosynthesis, purine metabolism, and the PPAR signaling pathway (Figure 4C).

Clustering and Functional Analysis of DEG

The purpose of this study was to identify genes related to the growth and development of the Ningdu hybrid yellow chicken; therefore, we used K-means clustering analysis for all identified DEGs (Figure 5). A total of 12 DEG subgroups were obtained, and 4 clusters (clusters 4, 5, 12, and 11) were chosen for further functional analysis (Supplementary Table S3).

K-means clustering analysis and functional analysis of DEGs in the hybrid Ningdu Yellow chicken (W) and its parentals (G and H). For functional analysis, the larger area suggests the smaller P-value, and different colors represent different KEGG pathways.

The functional enrichment results of genes in the subgroups showed that the main pathways involving DEGs in clusters 4, 5, 12, and 11 were the biosynthesis of fatty acids and amino acids. Gene expression in cluster 4 was upregulated in H, which was superior to that in W, followed by G. Cluster 4 genes were involved in fatty acid metabolism, biosynthesis and elongation; histidine metabolism; the MAPK signaling pathway; and focal adhesion. This indicated that the H group had active amino acid metabolism and lipid metabolism, and that the pathways for focal adhesion and MAPK might be crucial for the growth and development of chicken muscle.

Clusters 5 and 12, with the highest gene expression in the W group, were related to the regulation of the actin cytoskeleton, riboflavin metabolism, steroid biosynthesis, glycerophospholipid metabolism, and basic nutrient metabolism.

This might mean that the excellent growth performance of W is related to the levels and metabolism of fatty acids, purines, amino acids, riboflavin, steroids, glycerophospholipids, and folate. Genes in Cluster 11, which were found at higher levels in G than in the other 2 groups, were associated with valine, leucine, and isoleucine degradation; fatty acid degradation; tight junctions; and glutathione metabolism. This indicated that the G group might have heightened metabolism and degradation of various amino acids, which might explain the excellent egg laying performance of the G group.

Single-Sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis

We carried out ssGSEA using the KEGG and REACTOME databases. The W group had a high enrichment score for kegg_rig_i_like_receptor_signaling_pathway, kegg_riboflavin_metabolism and kegg_homologous_recombination in line W compared with that in line H. However, the pathways of steroid biosynthesis, glutathione metabolism, and metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome p450 were enriched in the H group. Meanwhile, ssGSEA was conducted using pathways in the REACTOME database, and the results showed that pathways such as high density lipoprotein (HDL) remodeling, vitamin_B2_riboflavin_metabolism, and peroxisomal lipid metabolism, were enriched in the W group, while the H group had higher scores for gluconeogenesis, cholesterol biosynthesis, and nucleotide catabolism (Figure 6A).

Changes in gene sets of significant pathways in different comparison groups using KEGG and REACTOME databases. (A) ssGSEA results for group W vs H. (B) ssGSEA results for group W vs G. (C) Heatmap of gene expression changes in key pathways of growth and development in 3 groups.

In the WVSG comparison group, 21 significant pathways with P < 0.05 were obtained, most of which were related to carbohydrate metabolism, such as glycolysis gluconeogenesis, galactose metabolism, and fructose and mannose metabolism. The enrichment scores of these terms were higher in the G group. REACTOME analysis identified 131 significant items (P < 0.05) (Supplementary Table S4), 52 of which are displayed in Figure 6B, among which the top 3 significantly enriched signaling pathways were gluconeogenesis, AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) inhibition of carbohydrate-responsive element-binding protein (CHREBP) transcriptional activation activity, and solute carrier (SLC)-mediated transmembrane transport. The W group showed higher enrichment for HDL remodeling, chylomicron remodeling, and free fatty acids regulating insulin secretion.

Subsequently, the differences between the W group and the other 2 groups were analyzed, and 11 significant pathways were identified, among which were riboflavin metabolism, glutathione metabolism, steroid biosynthesis, and amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism (Supplementary Table S5). We speculated that these nutrient-related pathways might be crucial in the growth and development of chickens. Therefore, we further explored changes in the genes of these pathways between the Ningdu hybrid chickens and their parents. The results implied that FLAD1 (encoding flavin adenine dinucleotide synthetase 1) expression in riboflavin metabolism was upregulated in the W group, whereas most genes involved in steroid biosynthesis and amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism were downregulated (Figure 6C). Overall, these findings perhaps illustrated the importance of pathways related to glutathione metabolism, riboflavin metabolism, and steroid biosynthesis in chicken growth and development.

Construction of PPI Network for DEG

We further performed PPI analysis for DEGs in pathways related to chicken development (Supplementary Table S6). A total of 127 DEGs were further analyzed using the STRING online database (http://string-db.org), and a PPI network complex containing 111 nodes and 258 edges was obtained. Furthermore, MCODE, which is a density-based nonoverlapping clustering algorithm, was used to establish PPI networks based on the DEGs. On the basis of the seed nodes as the center to expand to its neighbor nodes, the nodes were screened out to construct complexes in the PPI network. To better understand the potential biological mechanism related to the network, the top 2 clusters with the highest clustering scores (Figures 7A–B) were screened, and the main biological processes were obtained (Supplementary Table S7). Cluster 1 consisted of 9 genes, including FGFR2 (encoding fibroblast growth factor receptor 2), PIK3R1 (encoding phosphoinositide-3-kinase regulatory subunit 1), FGFR3 (encoding fibroblast growth factor receptor 3), FGF1 (encoding fibroblast growth factor1), ITGB4 (encoding integrin subunit beta 4), PIK3R3 (encoding phosphoinositide-3-kinase regulatory subunit 1, CDH3 (encoding cadherin 3), MYC (encoding MYC proto-oncogene, BHLH transcription factor), and CDH1 (encoding cadherin 1), with DHCR24 (encoding 24-dehydrocholesterol reductase) as the seed gene. Functional analysis showed that these genes are mainly related to the regulation of cell proliferation, and positive regulation of developmental processes. Cluster 2, with 5 genes (HSD17B12 (encoding hydroxysteroid 17-beta dehydrogenase 12), NSDHL (encoding NAD(P) dependent steroid dehydrogenase-like), SQLE (encoding squalene epoxidase), DHCR24, and HSD17B7 (encoding hydroxysteroid 17-beta dehydrogenase 7)), with DHCR24 as the seed gene, was enriched for 25 processes, mainly involving glycoprotein catabolic processes, cholesterol biosynthetic processes, sterol biosynthetic processes, and steroid biosynthetic processes (Figures 7C–D).

Integrated analysis of DEG and DAM

To reveal the explanation for the observed growth differences at the molecular level among the W, G, and H groups, we conducted correlation analysis between the 75 DAMs and 682 DEGs for WVSH, and 23 DAMs and 880 DEGs for WVSG (Supplementary Figure S3). In the WVSH comparison, the results showed that 27 DEGs correlated positively or negatively with DAMs. The genes ENSGALG00010006443, MINPP1 (encoding multiple inositol-polyphosphate phosphatase 1), ENSGALG00010010993, and HSPA4L (encoding heat shock protein family A (Hsp70) member 4 like) correlated significantly and positively with 1_Kestose, while ENSGALG00010018152, GSC (encoding goosecoid homeobox), TMEM30CP (encoding transmembrane protein 30C, pseudogene), and ENSGALG00010018873 correlated significantly and positively with PC (20:5(5Z,8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)/15:0), indicating that these genes had a significant impact on the biosynthesis of these volatiles. Moreover, ENSGALG00010018152 correlated positively with PC (20:5(5Z,8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)/15:0), glaucarubin, and caffeine, whereas FNIP1 correlated negatively with L-Proline and 2-Methyl-1-methylthio-2-butene.

In the WVSG, we found that 4 genes (ENSGALG00010016038, MMP13 (encoding matrix metalloproteinase 13), RXFP1 (encoding relaxin family peptide receptor 1), and ENSGALG00010019482) correlated significantly and positively with Butylamine, while CPN1 (encoding carboxypeptidase N subunit 1), FAM110A (encoding family with sequence similarity 110 member A), ENSGALG00010022201, and EPHX1L (encoding epoxide hydrolase 1L) correlated significantly and positively correlated with PC (22:6(4Z,7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z)/20:0). Additionally, CPN1 correlated significantly and positively with PC(22:6(4Z,7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z)/20:0), PC (22:5(7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z)/20:1(11Z)), and PC (20:5(5Z,8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)/15:0). In summary, these DEG and DAM are likely to be responsible for the observed growth differences.

DISCUSSION

Genetic hybridization is an effective method to produce new and excellent properties (Labroo et al. 2021). Many studies have been devoted to exploring the molecular basis of heterosis, and recent advances have focused on the correlation between DEGs and heterosis. For example, 1,171 DEGs were identified between the 2 intraspecific Larch (Larix) lines and their reciprocal hybrids, and GO and KEGG analyses indicated that most of the DEGs in the heterotic hybrid were related to important biological functions, such as physiological processes, responses to stimulus, and starch and sucrose metabolism (Li et al. 2012). Thus, these metabolic pathways might have contributed to the hybrid dominance of intraspecific Larix. In this study, hybrid Ningdu yellow chickens and their parents were used to investigate the growth differences between them. The transcriptional profiles of chicken breast meat among the 3 lines of Ningdu Yellow Chicken identified 1700 DEGs, which were mainly associated with fatty acid metabolism, amino acid synthesis and metabolism, and glucose metabolism. The GO analysis in the BP category indicated enrichment of many significant items, such as tissue development, animal organ development, system development, and cell differentiation.

In the pairwise KEGG analysis, we found that the main enriched pathways included the MAPK signaling pathway, tight junctions, steroid biosynthesis, and the PPAR signaling pathway in the 3 comparison groups. Further clustering and functional analysis showed that Cluster 5 and Cluster 12, with the highest gene expression in the W group, were related to regulation of the actin cytoskeleton, riboflavin metabolism, steroid biosynthesis, and glycerophospholipid metabolism. The ssGSEA results indicated the importance of amino sugar and nucleotide sugar metabolism, glutathione metabolism, riboflavin metabolism, and steroid biosynthesis in the growth and development of Ningdu yellow chickens. In other words, muscle growth and fat metabolism are the main events that affect the development of chickens and are also the main factors that lead to differences in live weight traits.

Recently, studies have reported the importance of these pathways in development. He et al. (2020) found that adrenergic signaling in cardiomyocytes, cardiac muscle contraction, and tight junctions were significantly enriched between fast- and slow-growing Bian chickens. Moreover, twelve significant pathways related to the sensory system, circulatory system, aging, global and overview maps (biosynthesis of amino acids, carbon metabolism), carbohydrate metabolism (fructose and mannose metabolism, galactose metabolism, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis, pentose phosphate pathway), signal transduction (fly Hippo signaling pathway), transport, and catabolism phagosome (phagosome) were enriched between the fast growth group and the slow growth group at different stages (Zhao et al. 2021). The MAPK signaling pathway is closely associated with growth and development (Sánchez-Gurmaches et al. 2013). Zhang et al. (2019) conducted a comparative transcriptome study of breast muscle in 3 chicken breeds with different growth speeds, and the results showed that 3 pathways (the extracellular matrix-receptor interaction, the MAPK signaling pathway, and focal adhesion) had the most enriched DEGs between lines or within lines under different developmental stages. The MAPK pathway acts as a global regulator of skeletal muscle differentiation (Segales et al. 2016). P38 MAPK signaling is one of the major controllers of the proliferation and differentiation of skeletal muscle satellite cells (Avgustinova and Benitah, 2016; Bianconi and Mozzetta 2022). P38α/β MAPK has the ability to promote the myogenic differentiation of satellite cells by inducing an increase in myogenic differentiation, which promotes muscle fiber regeneration (Troy et al. 2012; Charville et al. 2015; Hausburg et al. 2015).

In addition, tight junctions are an important part of cell junctions, which constitute the barrier for ions and molecules to pass through cells, and regulate the movement of proteins and lipids between the apical and basolateral domains of the plasma membrane (Hartsock and Nelson 2008). Tight junctions comprise at least 3 types of transmembrane proteins: occludin, claudin, and junctional adhesion molecules, and a cytoplasmic 'plaque' consisting of many different proteins that form large complexes. Tight junctions are proposed to be involved in the control of cell proliferation, cell differentiation, and cell migration, as well as in actin assembly (Gonzalez-Mariscal et al. 2007; Sawada 2013). Hartsock and Nelson (2008) proposed that junctional complexes are associated with reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton, and the actin cytoskeleton plays pivotal roles in cell growth, shape, and movement, as well as vascular remodeling (Ishida et al. 1999). The PPAR pathway has a variety of biological functions, such as enhancing the body's sensitivity to insulin and regulating the glucose balance, especially during adipocyte differentiation. Generation and other aspects play an important role (Montaigne et al. 2021). Additionally, steroid biosynthesis might result in an increase in lipid deposition in chicken pectoralis muscle tissue (Liu et al. 2019). Therefore, the MAPK pathway, tight junctions, steroid biosynthesis, and the PPAR signaling pathway might play crucial roles in muscle growth and development in chickens.

The differential gene regulatory network in growth and development-related pathways was constructed using the String database, and the top 2 networks were obtained using the MCODE plug-in. Genes in these networks mainly participate in cell proliferation, macromolecule metabolic processes, and lipid metabolic processes and thus might play crucial roles in chicken growth. FGFR2 is a member of the receptor tyrosine kinase subfamily, which plays an important role in regulating osteoblast differentiation, proliferation, and apoptosis, as well as normal bone development. Previous research has shown that FGFR2 promotes bone formation (Yu et al. 2003). Mansukhani's results suggested that FGFR2-activating mutations control osteoblast differentiation by inducing the transcription factor SRY-box transcription factor 2 (Sox2) and the Wnt-β-catenin pathway (Mansukhani et al. 2005). FGF1 is involved in the regulation of cell survival, cell division, angiogenesis, cell differentiation, and cell migration. It has been reported that FGF1 might be a regulator of fatty acid homeostasis. The lamin receptor, ITGB4, is associated with cell adhesion and focal adhesion. Cadherin-3 is mainly related to cell adhesion (Shimoyama et al. 1989). CDH1 can positively regulate the canonical Wnt signaling pathway (Samuelov et al. 2013)and participates in the regulation of protein catabolic processes (Dial et al. 2007). MYC, as the seed in cluster 1, can activate the transcription of genes related to skeletal muscle growth. Expression of the MYC gene regulates cell proliferation during embryonic skeletal muscle development. Knockdown or inhibition of MYC can lead to greatly reduced cell proliferation (Yuan et al. 2016). In addition, MYC is involved in the regulation of cell size and skeletal differentiation during chick limb development (Piedra et al., 2002). Additionally, in cluster 2, HSD17B12 (Xu et al. 2020, Wang et al. 2022), HSD17B7 (Mu et al. 2019), NSDHL (Liu et al. 2019), and DHCR24 (Jiang et al. 2020) all encode proteins that are mainly involved in steroid biosynthesis, which implies that these fat-related genes might be important factors in fat deposition, thus playing a critical role in regulating the growth and development of animals. In summary, genes in cluster 1 and cluster 2 were identified as candidate genes that might influence chicken growth traits.

Next, we compared the overall changes in the metabolome profile in breast muscle between the Ningdu hybrid chickens and their parents. Compared with those in the H group, 36 metabolites in the W group were upregulated and 39 were downregulated, which might have led to the observed growth differences. Pathway enrichment analysis found that these DAMs were enriched in the metabolism of various amino acids, such as cyanoamino acids, glycine, serine, threonine, valine, leucine, and isoleucine. The downregulation of the levels of 13-L-hydroperoxylinoleic acid, 3-hydroxycinnamic acid, hexadecanedioic acid, quinic acid, D-serine, and L-proline might have affected the protein and fat composition, further affecting the development of chickens. In the WVSG comparison group, 11 DAMs were upregulated, including glaucarubin, PC (22:5(7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z)/16:0), PC (22:6(4Z,7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z)/20:1(11Z)), PC (22:5(7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z)/20:1(11Z)), Phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) (22:4(7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z)/16:1(9Z)), styrene, PC (20:5(5Z,8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)/15:0), thymidine, phenylpyruvic acid, and D-fructose. The upregulation of various fatty acids might help with fat deposition, thereby further affecting the development of chickens.

In conclusion, our study revealed important DEGs and DAMs in Ningdu hybrid chickens and their parents. Meanwhile, some key enriched pathways were identified, such as the MAPK signaling pathway, steroid hormone biosynthesis, tight junctions, and the PPAR signaling pathway, suggesting that they play crucial roles in regulating the growth and development of Ningdu yellow chickens. Our results provide a new perspective and theoretical reference for the heterosis of molecular-assisted breeding of desirable chicken breeds.

Risk factors for preoperative isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis in elderly patients with hip fracture and development of a predictive nomogram: a retrospective study
Authors: Yuefeng Meng, Changqing Zhang, Zelin Fan, Haibin Zhou, Wei Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDeep vein thrombosis (DVT) is a common perioperative complication in elderly patients with hip fractures and may lead to life-threatening pulmonary embolism (PE). Isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis (ICMVT), a prevalent subtype of DVT, is often asymptomatic and easily overlooked. Despite its clinical significance, current research on ICMVT remains limited, and there is a lack of validated tools for early identification. This study aimed to determine the incidence and independent risk factors of preoperative ICMVT in elderly patients with hip fractures. In addition, we sought to develop a nomogram-based predictive model using readily available clinical parameters to facilitate early recognition and targeted intervention in high-risk patients.MethodsWe conducted a retrospective analysis of 673 elderly patients with hip fractures who were treated at our institution between June 2020 and June 2023. Patients were categorized into groups based on the presence or absence of preoperative isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis (ICMVT), as confirmed by duplex ultrasonography. Clinical and laboratory data were collected and compared. Univariate analysis followed by multivariate logistic regression was performed to identify independent risk factors associated with preoperative ICMVT. A predictive nomogram model was subsequently developed. The model’s performance was assessed in terms of discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, calibration plots, and decision curve analysis (DCA), respectively.ResultsThe incidence of preoperative isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis (ICMVT) was 16.5% (111 of 673 patients). Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified the following independent risk factors for ICMVT: platelet distribution width (OR = 0.83, 95% CI: 0.75–0.91), D-dimer level (OR = 1.06, 95% CI: 1.02–1.11), total protein (OR = 0.92, 95% CI: 0.88–0.95), and serum potassium (OR = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.24–0.61). A predictive nomogram incorporating these variables demonstrated moderate discrimination, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of 0.739 in the training cohort and 0.742 in the validation cohort. Calibration plots showed good agreement between predicted and observed outcomes. Decision curve analysis (DCA) indicated a favorable net clinical benefit within a threshold probability range of 5% to 50%.ConclusionsPlatelet distribution width, D-dimer, serum potassium, and total protein were identified as independent predictors of preoperative ICMVT in elderly patients with hip fractures. The proposed nomogram demonstrated moderate discriminative ability and may assist in early risk stratification. Further prospective validation is required before clinical application.

Introduction

Elderly patients with hip fractures are characterized by high rates of disability, mortality, and healthcare resource utilization, drawing considerable attention from orthopedic researchers worldwide [1]. Surgical intervention remains the primary treatment modality, and the Clinical Practice Guidelines issued by the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) recommend performing surgery within 24 to 48 h of injury to reduce perioperative complications and improve outcomes [2, 3]. Accordingly, timely preoperative assessment, identification of comorbidities, and management of potential complications are critical. However, these efforts remain particularly challenging in this vulnerable population [4, 5].

Among perioperative complications, deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is one of the most frequently encountered and clinically significant conditions, and possibly up to 30% of patients with DVT may develop symptomatic pulmonary embolism (PE), which can be fatal [6, 7]. Based on anatomical location, DVT is typically classified as proximal or distal. Proximal DVT refers to thrombosis involving the popliteal vein or more proximal veins, and represents the primary source of pulmonary embolism. Distal DVT is confined to the infra-popliteal veins, such as calf muscle veins, and more common, may propagate proximally and thereby increase the risk of PE [8–11].

Isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis is the most prevalent subtype of distal deep vein thrombosis and is far more common than proximal DVT [12, 13]. Although previously considered clinically insignificant, increasing evidence suggests that ICMVT may progress and lead to clinically relevant complications. Studies have shown that approximately 2.9% of isolated CMVT cases propagate to the popliteal vein, representing proximal DVT [9]. Other reports indicate that 5–10% of patients may develop popliteal vein involvement, with most propagation occurring within the first two weeks after diagnosis [14]. In addition, pulmonary embolism has been documented in patients with isolated CMVT, with reported incidence ranging from 0% to 50% across studies [12, 15–17]. This wide variability likely reflects substantial heterogeneity in study populations, diagnostic strategies, and follow-up durations, rather than true differences in biological risk. Collectively, these findings indicate that ICMVT represents an early stage of venous thromboembolism rather than a benign incidental finding, underscoring the importance of appropriate recognition and risk stratification.

Despite its frequency and clinical implications, ICMVT often presents with minimal or no symptoms, making early diagnosis difficult—particularly in elderly patients with hip fractures. To date, the preoperative incidence and risk factors of ICMVT in this population have not been systematically studied. Although advancements have been made in DVT prevention and management, the absence of effective tools for preoperative identification of ICMVT hampers risk stratification and timely intervention.

Therefore, there is a pressing need for a simple, practical, and clinically applicable predictive model to assist in preoperative risk assessment and guide individualized prophylactic strategies. In response to this unmet need, the present retrospective study aimed to identify independent risk factors associated with preoperative ICMVT in elderly patients with hip fractures and to develop a nomogram-based prediction model to facilitate early risk evaluation in clinical settings.

Methods

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Soochow University (Approval ID: JD-HG-2024-100). A total of 673 elderly patients (aged ≥ 65 years) with acute hip fractures who underwent surgical treatment in the Department of Joint Surgery between June 2020 and June 2023 were included, based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria (Fig. 1).

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients were eligible for inclusion if they met the following criteria: (1) age ≥ 65 years; (2) diagnosis of acute hip fracture requiring surgical intervention; and (3) completion of preoperative duplex ultrasonography of both lower extremities.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) chronic hip fracture, defined as an interval > 3 weeks from injury to surgery; (2) concomitant fractures requiring additional surgical treatment; (3) prior history of thrombosis with ongoing anticoagulation therapy; (4) preoperative ultrasonographic evidence of proximal DVT or chronic ICMVT; and (5) incomplete clinical data; or (6) absence of preoperative bilateral lower limb ultrasound examination.

Data collection and definitions

Clinical baseline characteristics and admission laboratory results were obtained from electronic medical records. Recorded variables included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), time from injury to hospital admission, time from admission to surgery, and relevant medical comorbidities. Laboratory parameters included white blood cell count (WBC), hemoglobin (Hb), platelet count (PLT), platelet distribution width (PDW), prothrombin time (PT), activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT), D-dimer, total protein (TP), albumin (ALB), serum creatinine (Scr), serum potassium (K⁺), and C-reactive protein (CRP).

Definition of preoperative ICMVT

The primary outcome was the presence of isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis (ICMVT) prior to surgery. All patients underwent bilateral lower extremity color Doppler ultrasonography before the operation. Based on the ultrasonographic findings, patients were divided into an ICMVT-positive group and an ICMVT-negative group. Clinical and laboratory data were compared between the two groups to assess the incidence of ICMVT and to identify associated risk factors.

Duplex ultrasonography was routinely performed on the first or second day of admission. For patients whose time interval between the initial scan and surgery exceeded 3 days, a repeat scan was performed one day prior to surgery. All ultrasonographic examinations were independently assessed by two experienced vascular sonographers using standardized equipment and protocols, with diagnostic criteria based on internationally recognized standards. In cases of discrepant interpretations, a third senior vascular sonographer was consulted, and the final diagnosis was determined by consensus, thereby minimizing interobserver variability. Diagnostic criteria were based on internationally recognized standards. With the patient in the supine position, the femoral and popliteal veins were examined to rule out proximal DVT. Subsequent assessment focused on the gastrocnemius and soleus muscle veins. Dynamic examination included compression tests and evaluation of venous filling under distal limb compression. Diagnostic features of thrombosis included incomplete vein compressibility, focal absence or abnormal enhancement of Doppler flow signals, echogenic intraluminal material suggestive of thrombus, and confirmation of venous obstruction using color Doppler flow imaging (CDFI) [18–20].

Standardized DVT prophylaxis

Throughout the perioperative and postoperative periods, all patients received standardized prophylactic interventions in accordance with established DVT prevention guidelines. These included early ankle pump exercises, subcutaneous administration of enoxaparin, and the use of intermittent pneumatic compression devices. The current study was conducted within this framework of consistent thromboprophylaxis to identify predictors of preoperative ICMVT under real-world preventive conditions.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Patients were categorized into ICMVT and non-ICMVT groups based on preoperative ultrasonographic findings. Continuous variables with normal distribution were reported as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared using independent-samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed variables were presented as median with interquartile range [M (P25, P75)] and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages (n, %) and analyzed using the chi-square (χ²) test.

Variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression model. A backward stepwise selection approach was applied to identify independent predictors, with non-significant variables sequentially removed. The entire cohort was randomly divided into a training set and a validation set in a 7:3 ratio using SPSS. A nomogram model was then developed in the training cohort using R Studio (version 4.2.2), based on the independent predictors identified in the multivariate analysis.

Model performance was evaluated through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, with area under the curve (AUC) used to assess discriminatory ability. Calibration was assessed by comparing predicted and observed probabilities using calibration plots. Clinical utility was evaluated through decision curve analysis (DCA), which estimated the net benefit across a range of threshold probabilities.

Results

A total of 673 patients were included and stratified into two groups based on the presence or absence of preoperative ICMVT: the non-ICMVT group (n = 562) and the ICMVT group (n = 111). The mean age of patients in the ICMVT group was significantly higher than that in the non-ICMVT group (80.0 ± 9.0 years vs. 78.0 ± 8.0 years, P = 0.003). The prevalence of cardiovascular disease was also significantly higher in the ICMVT group (30.6% [34/111]) than in the non-ICMVT group (18.2% [102/562], P = 0.003). Additionally, the proportion of patients with Parkinson’s disease or Alzheimer’s disease was greater in the ICMVT group (8.1% vs. 2.3%, P = 0.005).

A significant difference was also observed in the time from injury to hospital admission. Among patients admitted more than 24 h after injury, the proportion was higher in the ICMVT group compared with the non-ICMVT group (29.7% vs. 20.6%, P = 0.035). No significant differences were observed between groups for other demographic characteristics (Table 1).

Risk factor analysis

Univariate analysis identified ten variables as potential predictors of preoperative ICMVT: age, cardiovascular disease, Parkinson’s/Alzheimer’s disease, time from injury to hospital admission > 24 h, MPV, PDW, D-dimer, total protein, albumin, and serum potassium (Table 3). These variables were entered into a multivariate logistic regression model using backward stepwise selection. Four variables were retained as independent predictors of ICMVT: lower PDW (OR = 0.83, 95% CI: 0.75–0.91, P < 0.001), elevated D-dimer levels (OR = 1.06, 95% CI: 1.02–1.11, P < 0.001), decreased total protein (OR = 0.92, 95% CI: 0.88–0.95, P < 0.001), and lower serum potassium (OR = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.24–0.61, P < 0.001) (Table 4).

Nomogram construction and validation

To visualize and apply the multivariate model, a nomogram was developed using R Studio based on the independent predictors. The full dataset was randomly split into a training cohort and a validation cohort at a 7:3 ratio. The nomogram was constructed using the training set (Fig. 2), and its discriminative performance was evaluated using ROC curve analysis.

Fig. 2Nomogram for predicting preoperative ICMVT in elderly patients with hip fractures. The total score, calculated based on the values of each predictor (PDW, D-dimer, TP and K+), corresponds to the estimated risk of ICMVT

Nomogram for predicting preoperative ICMVT in elderly patients with hip fractures. The total score, calculated based on the values of each predictor (PDW, D-dimer, TP and K+), corresponds to the estimated risk of ICMVT

To use the nomogram, the clinician begins by identifying the patient’s specific values for each of the four predictors: platelet distribution width (PDW), D-dimer, total protein, and serum potassium. For each variable, a vertical line is drawn upward to the “Points” axis to determine the corresponding score. The individual scores are then summed to yield a “Total Points” value. This total score is located on the “Total Points” axis, from which a vertical line is projected downward to estimate the probability of preoperative isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis (ICMVT) on the “Risk of Outcome” axis.

In the training cohort, the model yielded an AUC of 0.739 (95% CI: 0.675–0.802), indicating moderate discrimination ability (Fig. 3A). In the validation cohort, the AUC was 0.742 (95% CI: 0.636–0.847), supporting the model’s generalizability and robustness (Fig. 3B).

Calibration curves demonstrated good agreement between predicted and observed probabilities in both the training and validation cohorts, with both curves closely approximating the ideal reference line (Fig. 4), suggesting excellent calibration. Furthermore, decision curve analysis (DCA) showed that the nomogram provided a net clinical benefit across a range of threshold probabilities from 5% to 50% (Fig. 5).

Discussion

In this retrospective study of 673 elderly patients with hip fractures, the incidence of preoperative isolated calf muscle vein thrombosis (ICMVT) was 16.5%. Multivariate analysis identified platelet distribution width (PDW), D-dimer, serum potassium, and total protein as independent risk factors. Based on these variables, we developed and validated a nomogram that demonstrated good discriminatory ability, calibration consistency, and clinical utility. The model performed consistently across both training and validation cohorts, indicating strong stability and generalizability. These findings suggest that the nomogram can serve as a valuable tool for individualized thrombotic risk assessment and preoperative decision-making in this high-risk population.

Among the identified predictors, reduced PDW was significantly associated with increased ICMVT risk. Each 1% decrease in PDW was associated with a 20% increase in the likelihood of thrombosis. As a marker of platelet size heterogeneity, lower PDW may reflect impaired thrombopoiesis or bone marrow suppression [21–24]. Furthermore, PDW may be modulated by inflammatory states. Previous studies have shown that reduced PDW is linked to increased platelet P-selectin expression, platelet–leukocyte aggregation, and shortened coagulation time under inflammatory stimuli such as TNF-α [25, 26]. Similar findings in ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease suggest that PDW may serve as an indicator of inflammation-associated hypercoagulability [27]. A retrospective study of ankle fracture patients also reported PDW < 12% as an independent risk factor for preoperative DVT [28]. Our findings support the role of low PDW as a surrogate marker of both systemic inflammation and impaired hematologic reserve, particularly relevant in elderly patients with limited bone marrow capacity and prolonged immobilization.

Elevated D-dimer and hypoproteinemia are also well-established thrombotic risk factors in trauma populations [29, 30]. In elderly hip fracture patients, pain and immobility further impair calf muscle pump function, compounding the risk of venous stasis and thrombosis. Hypoproteinemia may impair the hepatic synthesis of key anticoagulants such as antithrombin III, protein C, and protein S, disrupting the coagulation-fibrinolysis balance [31, 32]. Additionally, it may drive increased production of acute-phase reactants such as fibrinogen and CRP, promoting a hypercoagulable state [33]. Although fibrinogen levels did not differ significantly between groups in our study, this may reflect uniformly elevated baseline levels due to preoperative stress, masking any group-level differences.

Despite its high sensitivity, D-dimer has limited specificity for thrombus localization or burden [34–37]. It is elevated in various physiological and pathological conditions, particularly in elderly or critically ill patients. In our cohort, D-dimer retained independent predictive value in multivariate analysis, reinforcing its role as a supportive—though nonspecific—marker of thrombogenesis. Future research should explore the dynamic relationship between D-dimer trajectories and ICMVT onset to enhance its predictive utility.

Potassium plays a central role in maintaining cellular homeostasis, yet its relationship with thrombosis remains underexplored. In elderly patients with hip fractures, factors such as immobility, malnutrition, and diuretic use frequently lead to hypokalemia [38, 39]. In our study, patients with ICMVT had significantly lower serum potassium levels (median: 3.7 mmol/L vs. 3.9 mmol/L; P < 0.001). Multivariate analysis identified potassium as an independent protective factor against ICMVT (OR = 0.39, 95% CI: 0.24–0.61; P < 0.001). The underlying mechanisms are multifactorial. Hypokalemia may reduce the effectiveness of the skeletal muscle pump—particularly the gastrocnemius—leading to venous stasis, a key component of Virchow’s triad [40, 41]. Moreover, extracellular potassium modulates vascular inflammation by reducing reactive oxygen species generation in macrophages and endothelial cells, and by inhibiting smooth muscle proliferation [42, 43]. It also appears to attenuate platelet activation by decreasing their sensitivity to thrombin, thereby mitigating thrombus formation [44].These findings align with prior reports linking hypokalemia to elevated thrombosis risk in surgical patients [45]. While causal pathways remain to be fully defined, our results suggest serum potassium may serve as both a marker and a modifiable factor in preoperative thrombosis risk assessment for elderly hip fracture patients.

The nomogram model developed in this study integrates four routinely available laboratory parameters (PDW, D-dimer, serum potassium, and total protein), allowing estimation of individualized ICMVT risk at hospital admission. This model is intended to support perioperative risk stratification and in-hospital monitoring, rather than to predict post-discharge outcomes. By incorporating these routinely available variables, the model allows clinicians to estimate individualized ICMVT risk at hospital admission and to support perioperative risk stratification during the early hospitalization period. In uncertain clinical scenarios, such as patients presenting with limb swelling but without ultrasonographic evidence of thrombosis, the nomogram may serve as a dynamic adjunctive tool to monitor evolving thrombotic risk. This approach may enhance the clinical utility of routine laboratory tests, help reduce unnecessary repeat imaging, and support more individualized application of standard thromboprophylaxis strategies. The model demonstrated moderate discrimination, indicating that it should be interpreted as a supportive tool rather than a definitive predictive instrument.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, this was a retrospective, single-center study, which may limit the generalizability of our findings. Second, the present analysis focused primarily on preoperative clinical and laboratory parameters, with the aim of facilitating early perioperative risk stratification at the time of hospital admission. We recognize that postoperative indicators, such as postoperative laboratory changes, mobilization status, and postoperative complications, may provide additional prognostic information for venous thromboembolism risk. Third, due to the retrospective design, potential selection bias and information bias could not be completely avoided. Although a random split approach was used for internal validation, this method may reduce statistical efficiency compared with resampling-based techniques. Future prospective, multicenter studies incorporating both preoperative and postoperative variables are therefore warranted to further refine risk prediction and enhance the clinical applicability of the proposed model.

Conclusion

In conclusion, platelet distribution width (PDW), D-dimer, serum potassium, and total protein were identified as independent predictors of preoperative ICMVT in elderly patients with hip fractures. A nomogram incorporating these variables demonstrated moderate discriminative ability and may aid in early risk stratification. However, given the study’s retrospective design and limited validation, further prospective studies and clinical impact analyses are required before routine clinical implementation.

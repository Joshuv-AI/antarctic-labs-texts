Comparative analysis of clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis between E-cadherin-negative breast lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma and E-cadherin-negative invasive lobular carcinoma
Authors: Rui Gao, Xi Zhang, Ying Lin, Jie Lin, Yihui He, Lan Lin, Ran Peng, Xin Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundE-cadherin-negative breast lymphoepithelial-like carcinoma (E-Cad-neg BLEC) is a rare malignant tumor. To date, no study has compared the clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis between E-Cad-neg BLEC and E-Cad-neg invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC).MethodsTo clarify the relationship between E-Cad-neg BLEC and E-Cad-neg ILC, we analyzed 12 patients with E-Cad-neg BLEC, comparing their clinicopathological characteristics and tumor prognosis with those of 578 patients with E-Cad-neg classical invasive lobular carcinoma (c-ILC) or 43 patients with E-Cad-neg pleomorphic lobular carcinoma (PLC).ResultsThe results showed that E-Cad-neg BLEC was a diffuse, non-adherent tumor with pleomorphic growth. There was abundant lymphocyte infiltration in the tumor stroma. Lobular carcinoma in situ was observed around the tumor in 5 patients. Compared with E-Cad-neg c-ILC, E-Cad-neg BLEC had significantly lower expression levels of ER (p < 0.001), PR (p < 0.001), and HER2 (p = 0.029), and a higher Ki-67 proliferation index (p < 0.001). Compared with E-Cad-neg PLC, E-Cad-neg BLEC was less likely to exhibit vascular invasion (p < 0.001) and perineural invasion (p < 0.001), and had lower HER2 expression (p = 0.001). In terms of histo-molecular subtypes, E-Cad-neg BLEC was mainly triple-negative, while E-Cad-neg c-ILC and E-Cad-neg PLC were mainly luminal A and luminal B. Regarding prognosis, the prognosis curve of E-Cad-neg BLEC was close to that of E-Cad-neg c-ILC. Analysis of the percentage of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) revealed that in both E-Cad-neg c-ILC and E-Cad-neg PLC, higher TIL levels were associated with a poor prognosis, in contrast to the favorable prognosis observed in E-Cad-neg BLEC.ConclusionsOur findings indicate that E-Cad-neg BLEC is a type of breast carcinoma characterized by extensive lymphocytic infiltration and high proliferative activity and is associated with a relatively favorable prognosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04263-0.Keywords: Breast lymphoepithelial-like carcinoma, Invasive lobular carcinoma, Pleomorphic lobular carcinoma, E-cadherin, Prognosis

Background

Lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma (LEC) is a rare carcinoma with abundant lymphocyte infiltration, and has been described in multiple organs including the thyroid [1, 2], salivary glands [3], lungs [4–6], cervix [7, 8], prostate [9, 10], skin [11, 12], bladder [13–15], and thymus [16, 17]. The first case of breast lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma (BLEC) was reported by Kumar et al. in 1994 [18].

BLEC exhibits diverse growth patterns, including nest-like, cord-like, syncytial-like (Regaud pattern), or diffuse growth patterns (Schminke pattern), with polymorphic tumor cells [19]. A hallmark feature is prominent lymphocytic infiltration within the tumor stroma. The histology of BLEC is different from that of medullary carcinoma and atypical medullary carcinoma: unlike medullary carcinoma, it lacks a well-circumscribed fibrous capsule and shows more prominent nuclear pleomorphism; compared with atypical medullary carcinoma, it has no pushing borders and a higher degree of lymphocytic infiltration [20]. Emerging evidence has led some researchers to propose that BLEC might represent an unusual pattern of infiltrating lobular carcinoma [21]. However, to date, only scattered case reports on BLEC have been published, and data specifically on E-cadherin-negative (E-Cad-neg) BLEC remain extremely limited. Notably, no studies have systematically compared the clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis of E-Cad-neg BLEC with those of invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC).

While loss of E-cadherin expression is not a mandatory diagnostic criterion for lobular carcinoma (additional markers include α-catenin, β-catenin, or γ-catenin), it plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis of ILC by disrupting intercellular adhesion, thereby promoting tumor cell dissociation and invasive growth. Intriguingly, E-Cad-neg BLEC tumor cells also display significant pleomorphism—a key feature shared with pleomorphic lobular carcinoma (PLC). Furthermore, the prognosis of E-Cad-neg BLEC remains unclear, and its comparison with E-Cad-neg ILC could provide critical insights into tumor biology and clinical management. Given this knowledge gap and the rarity of E-Cad-neg BLEC, we conducted a comprehensive clinicopathological and prognostic analysis comparing E-Cad-neg BLEC with E-Cad-neg classical ILC (c-ILC) and E-Cad-neg PLC.

Materials and methods

Patients and samples

All patient specimens in this study were collected from three medical centers (including Gulou Branch of Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital, Jinshan Branch of Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital and Fujian Provincial Elderly Hospital). The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Fuzhou University Affiliated Provincial Hospital (Approval No.: K2025-06-022) and confirmed by the ethics committees of all participating centers. Informed consent forms for scientific research were signed by patients. The inclusion criteria for patients were as follows: (1) specimens from patients with E-Cad-neg BLEC, E-Cad-neg c-ILC or E-Cad-neg PLC were obtained from January 2011 to December 2024; (2) the patient had not undergone neoadjuvant therapy; (3) the patient underwent surgical treatment and signed an informed consent form before surgery; (4) the patient did not have multiple breast cancers or organ cancers. All selected patients’ pathological sections were reconfirmed and evaluated by experienced pathologists. A total of 12 patients with E-Cad-neg BLEC, 578 patients with E-Cad-neg c-ILC and 43 patients with E-Cad-neg PLC were included. TNM staging was based on the 8th edition of the AJCC staging system [22]. Tumor histological grading was based on the Nottingham grading system [23]. Prominent nucleoli were defined as nucleoli visible at 40×.

Histological evaluation of Tumor-Infiltrating Lymphocytes (TILs)

TILs are defined as invasive mononuclear inflammatory cells (including lymphocytes, histiocytes, and plasma cells, excluding polymorphonuclear leukocytes) observed within the tumor stroma. TIL assessment was performed on the entire H&E slides of primary tumors. TIL was scored according to the latest international guidelines [24]. Areas of normal lobules, perivascular regions, carcinoma in situ, necrosis, and crush artifacts were excluded. Two pathologists independently scored the data in a double-blinded manner. If the discrepancy between the two scores exceeded 10%, a third senior pathologist conducted a re-evaluation, and the consensus result was adopted as the final TIL value. The inter-observer agreement was assessed by calculating the kappa coefficient (Kappa = 0.88). The median TIL level was of 5% in E-Cad-neg c-ILC and was of 20% in E-Cad-neg PLC. Therefore, the TIL density scores were categorized as follows: ≤ 5% and > 5% in E-Cad-neg c-ILC; ≤ 20% and > 20% in E-Cad-neg PLC.

Immunostaining and histo-molecular subtyping

In this study, results of the immunohistochemical (IHC) analyses of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), Ki-67, E-cadherin, and p120 for all enrolled cases were reviewed; additional IHC was performed for missing data or as needed. Briefly, 4 μm sections from representative tumor samples were stained for ER (clone MXR030; Maixin, Fuzhou, China), PR (clone MXR008; Maixin, Fuzhou, China), HER2 (clone 4B5; Roche, Basel, Switzerland), Ki-67 (clone SP6; Maixin, Fuzhou, China), E-cadherin (clone MX020; Maixin, Fuzhou, China) and p120 (clone MX124; Maixin, Fuzhou, China) by using a Roche Benchmark XT automatic IHC stainer (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). Positive and negative controls were included in each test. All the selected BLEC, c-ILC, and PLC samples exhibited E-cadherin loss and cytoplasmic p120 positivity. In accordance with the ASCO/CAP guidelines [25] and the St. Gallen International Breast Cancer Consensus [26]: ER was negative (< 1% nuclear staining), weakly positive (1%-10%), or positive (> 10%); PR was high expression (≥ 20%) or low expression (< 20%). HER2 was done as follows: 0 (no membrane staining), ultralow (≤ 10% cells with incomplete/weak membrane staining), 1+ (> 10% cells with incomplete/weak staining), 2+ (10% cells with weak-moderate complete staining or ≤ 10% with strong complete staining), 3+ (10% cells with strong, complete uniform staining). HER2 positivity was defined as 2+/FISH + or 3+. Two pathologists performed independently scored the data in a double-blinded manner. Discrepancies (ER/PR/Ki-67 positivity rate difference > 5% or HER2 grade mismatch) were resolved by a third senior pathologist, and the kappa coefficient were calculated (ER, kappa = 0.86; PR, kappa = 0.84; Ki67, kappa = 0.89; HER2, kappa = 0.92). Histo-molecular subtypes were defined as follows [27]: luminal A, ER-positive and/or high-PR expression and Ki-67 expression < 14%; luminal B, ER-positive and/or low-PR expression and high-Ki-67 expression; HER2, ER-negative, PR negative and HER2-positive; and triple-negative, ER, PR and HER2 all negative.

Statistical analyses

SPSS 27.0 software (IBM, Armonk, USA) and Prism 10.1 software (GraphPad Software, California, USA) were used for the statistical analyses. Fisher’s exact test, Yates’ continuity-corrected chi-square test and Pearson’s chi-square test was used to analyze categorical variables. If the overall test was statistically significant, pairwise comparisons were conducted using the paired Fisher’s exact test with Bonferroni correction. Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from breast cancer diagnosis to death from any cause. Disease-free survival (DFS) was defined as the time interval from breast cancer diagnosis to the first cancer event, including local, regional, and distant recurrence, contralateral disease, second non-breast primary cancer, and death from any cause. The OS and DFS were estimated using the Kaplan‒Meier method, and comparisons among different subgroups were performed using the Bonferroni-corrected log-rank test. For all analyses, P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Clinicopathological characteristics of E-Cad-neg BLEC

All twelve patients were female, with a mean age of 55.5 years (range: 46–70 years). Tumors were located in the right breast (eight cases) and left breast (four cases), with a mean maximum diameter of 24.7 mm (range: 14–42 mm). The BLEC tissue had moderate-to-high histological grades (grade 2: five cases; grade 3: seven cases), and all twelve cases showed ill-defined growth boundaries. With respect to tumor growth patterns, all twelve cases exhibited diffuse and non-adherent growth patterns, with two additionally showing small nest-like foci. Abundant lymphocytes were present around the BLEC tumor cells (Supplementary Fig. 1A), and germinal centers were observed in the infiltrated areas of nine cases. Tumor cells had abundant cytoplasm (nine cases, pale/eosinophilic, seven cases with intracellular mucin) or scant cytoplasm (three cases). All twelve cases had pleomorphic nuclei (Supplementary Fig. 1B): eight cases with predominantly vesicular nuclei and four cases with hyperchromatic nuclei; seven cases showed significant nucleoli (Supplementary Fig. 1C). One case had a tumor with vascular invasion, whereas all twelve cases had no peripheral nerve invasion. In situ carcinoma was found around the tumors in five cases (Supplementary Fig. 1D), all of which were lobular carcinoma in situ. The tumor cells expressed CK19 (Supplementary Fig. 1E). Lymph node metastasis occurred in four of the twelve patients. Clinical staging was predominantly low, with one patient having bone distant metastasis. The triple-negative subtype was predominant. Eleven patients underwent modified radical mastectomy, and one patient underwent breast-conserving therapy. During follow-up, one patient died and one patient experienced disease progression.

Clinicopathological comparison between E-Cad-neg BLEC and E-Cad-neg c-ILC

Compared with E-Cad-neg c-ILC (Table 2), E-Cad-neg BLEC exhibited significantly lower expression levels of ER (p < 0.001), PR (p < 0.001), and HER2 (p = 0.029), as well as a higher Ki-67 proliferation index (p < 0.001). There was a significant statistical difference in the histo-molecular subtypes between E-Cad-neg BLEC and E-Cad-neg c-ILC (p < 0.001). E-Cad-neg BLEC was predominantly triple-negative, whereas E-Cad-neg c-ILC was predominantly luminal A. No significant statistical differences were observed between E-Cad-neg BLEC and E-Cad-neg c-ILC in terms of age distribution, tumor location, tumor size, histological grade, vascular invasion, perineural invasion, lymph node metastasis, distant metastasis, or clinical stage.

In E-Cad-neg PLC, the largest proportion of TILs was 40%. Through statistical analysis of E-Cad-neg PLC with TIL proportions > 20%, or with TIL proportions ≤ 20%, and E-Cad-neg BLECs, the results revealed that there were significant statistical differences in tumor site (p = 0.018), vascular invasion (p = 0.002), perineural invasion (p = 0.001), HER2 expression (p = 0.002), and histo-molecular subtypes (p = 0.002). Using the Bonferroni correction, we further conducted pairwise comparisons among the three groups, and the specific results were detailed in the supplementary material (Supplementary Table 2).

Comparison of prognosis between E-Cad-neg c-ILC or E-Cad-neg PLC and E-Cad-neg BLEC

We used the Kaplan‒Meier method to analyze OS and DFS among E-Cad-neg c-ILC, E-Cad-neg PLC and E-Cad-neg BLEC patients. Overall, significant differences were observed across the three pathological types (OS: p < 0.001, Fig. 1A; DFS: p < 0.001, Fig. 1E). In subgroup comparisons, no significant differences in OS (p = 0.626, Fig. 1B) or DFS (p = 0.332, Fig. 1F) were detected between E-Cad-neg c-ILC and E-Cad-neg BLEC; only DFS (p = 0.024, Fig. 1G) differed significantly between E-Cad-neg PLC and E-Cad-neg BLEC (OS: p = 0.124, Fig. 1C), whereas both OS (p < 0.001, Fig. 1D) and DFS (p < 0.001, Fig. 1H) were significantly different between E-Cad-neg c-ILC and E-Cad-neg PLC.

Kaplan–Meier curves were generated to analyze OS and DFS among E-Cad-neg BLEC, E-Cad-neg c-ILC, and E-Cad-neg PLC using the log-rank test (A, E). Pairwise comparisons between these subgroups were performed using the Bonferroni-corrected log-rank test (B, C, D, F, G, H)

We also evaluated TILs in E-Cad-neg c-ILC and E-Cad-neg PLC. For E-Cad-neg c-ILC stratified by TILs (≤ 5% vs. >5%) versus E-Cad-neg BLEC, significant differences were noted in OS (p = 0.003, Fig. 2A) and DFS (p = 0.020, Fig. 2E). However, subgroup comparisons between each TIL-based c-ILC subtype and E-Cad-neg BLEC revealed no significant differences in OS (p = 0.525, Fig. 2B; p = 0.705, Fig. 2C) or DFS (p = 0.055, Fig. 2F; p = 0.331, Fig. 2G). Additionally, E-Cad-neg c-ILC with TILs > 5% had significantly different OS (p < 0.001, Fig. 2D) and DFS (p = 0.008, Fig. 2H) versus those with TILs ≤ 5%. For E-Cad-neg PLC stratified by TILs (≤ 20% or > 20%) versus E-Cad-neg BLEC, significant differences were observed in OS (p = 0.009, Fig. 3A) and DFS (p = 0.041, Fig. 3E). Subgroup comparisons showed only OS (p = 0.394, Fig. 3B) was not significantly different between E-Cad-neg PLC with ≤ 20% TILs and E-Cad-neg BLEC; other comparisons in OS (p = 0.014, Fig. 3C) or DFS (p = 0.039, Fig. 3F; p = 0.018, Fig. 3G) revealed significant statistical differences. E-Cad-neg PLC with TILs > 20% had significantly different OS (p = 0.013, Fig. 3D) but not DFS (p = 0.279, Fig. 3H) than those with TILs ≤ 20%.

Discussion

E-Cad-neg BLEC is an extremely rare malignant epithelial tumor, that is prone to misdiagnosis by needle biopsy and intraoperative frozen section diagnosis. Therefore, investigating its clinicopathological features is critical for differential diagnosis. We retrospectively analyzed twelve cases of E-Cad-neg BLEC, which showed diffuse and non-adherent growth patterns with nuclear pleomorphism, distinguishing it from invasive breast carcinoma of no special type (IBC-NST) with medullary pattern. To clarify its relationship with E-Cad-neg ILC, we compared E-Cad-neg BLEC with E-Cad-neg c-ILC and E-Cad-neg PLC in terms of clinicopathological characteristics and prognosis.

Multidimensional comparative analyses revealed both distinct differences and partial overlaps among these groups. In terms of proliferative capacity, the proportion of E-Cad-neg BLECs with high Ki-67 expression was greater than that of E-Cad-neg c-ILC with TILs ≤ 5%, but similar to that of E-Cad-neg c-ILC with TILs > 5% or E-Cad-neg PLC (with TILs ≤ 20% or with TILs > 20%), indicating that E-Cad-neg BLEC was a tumor with high proliferative capacity. However, compared with E-Cad-neg c-ILC or E-Cad-neg PLC, E-Cad-neg BLEC did not show a higher proportion of Ki-67 high-expression due to a higher TIL proportion. In terms of tumor invasiveness, compared with E-Cad-neg PLC, E-Cad-neg BLEC showed less vascular and perineural invasion, which may be attributed to the abundant TILs and the absence of fibrous stroma in E-Cad-neg BLEC. Meanwhile, the lymph node and distant metastasis potential of E-Cad-neg BLEC was similar to that of both E-Cad-neg c-ILC and PLC, even after stratification by TIL levels. In terms of histo-molecular subtypes, E-Cad-neg BLEC was predominantly of the triple-negative subtype, while E-Cad-neg c-ILC or E-Cad-neg PLC were mainly of the luminal A and luminal B subtypes. In terms of prognosis, survival analyses revealed that E-Cad-neg BLEC had a favorable prognosis, with OS/DFS curves closer to those of E-Cad-neg c-ILC. Notably, TILs are very important prognostic indicators for tumors. In IBC-NST, TILs are associated with a better prognosis [28]. However, TILs are associated with a poorer prognosis in ILC [29]. Our data confirmed that E-Cad-neg c-ILC with TILs > 5% had a worse prognosis than E-Cad-neg c-ILC with TILs ≤ 5%; E-Cad-neg PLC with TILs > 20% had a shorter OS (but similar DFS) than those with TILs ≤ 20%. However, the OS and DFS curves of E-Cad-neg BLEC overlapped with those of E-Cad-neg c-ILC, suggesting a favorable prognosis, and E-Cad-neg BLEC did not show a poorer prognosis than E-Cad-neg c-ILC or E-Cad-neg PLC, due to a higher TIL proportion.

E-Cad-neg BLEC is extremely rare, with a small number of specimens currently available, which may lead to potential data bias in comparison with other tumors. However, we adopted a multicenter study design, including cases of E-Cad-neg c-ILC and E-Cad-neg PLC, to minimize selection bias as much as possible. Three main factors contributed to the exclusion of ILC with a solid pattern, an alveolar pattern or a mixed pattern from this study: (1) the small number of tumors with a solid pattern or with an alveolar pattern; (2) the difference in growth patterns between tumors with a solid pattern or with an alveolar pattern and E-Cad-neg BLEC; (3) tumors with a mixed pattern may have multiple confounding factors. LECs in other sites (such as the nasopharynx and stomach) are associated with Epstein–Barr virus (EBV). However, previous studies had shown that BLEC was not associated with EBV [19], so EBV detection was not performed in our study. To date, only two studies have reported the presence of human papillomavirus (HPV) infection in BLEC [30, 31]. In contrast, another study examined five BLEC cases, and no evidence of HPV infection was detected in any of them [32]. Owing to the prolonged storage of some of our specimens, HPV testing was not additionally performed.

In summary, E-Cad-neg BLEC is an extremely rare malignant breast tumor characterized by high proliferative activity and a predominantly triple-negative molecular subtype. Notably, this uncommon tumor type has a relatively favorable prognosis despite its inherent malignant biological features.

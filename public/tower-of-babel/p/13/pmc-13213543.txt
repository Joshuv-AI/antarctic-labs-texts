Safety of cannabidiol isolate from Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283
Authors: EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA), Dominique Turck, Torsten Bohn, Montaña Cámara, Jacqueline Castenmiller, Stefaan De Henauw, Ángeles Jos, Alexandre Maciuk, Inge Mangelsdorf, Breige McNulty, Androniki Naska, Kristina Pentieva, Alfonso Siani, Frank Thies, Francesco Cubadda, Harry J McArdle, Viviana Trezza, Océane Albert, Elisa Beneventi, Marcello Laganaro, Irene Nuin Garciarena, Annamaria Rossi, Sebastián Berrío Cruz, Francesca Seminati, Maura Magani, Karen Ildico Hirsch‐Ernst
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, the EFSA Panel on Nutrition, Novel Foods and Food Allergens (NDA) was asked to deliver an opinion on cannabidiol (CBD) isolated from Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food (NF) pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. The NF is produced via ethanol extraction, followed by decarboxylation and crystallisation. It is a white to off‐white powder consisting of ≥ 97% w/w CBD. Other cannabinoids are present at a total concentration below 2.5% w/w. The NF is intended to be used in food supplements at 21 mg/day. The proposed target population is the adult population, excluding pregnant and lactating women. Uncertainties remain concerning the type of formulations that the applicant intends to market, and consequently on the possible presence of small particles – including nanoparticles – in such formulations. The Panel therefore cannot conclude as to whether the selected test items and ADME and toxicological testing strategies were appropriate to adequately assess the safety of the NF. Based on the available data, the Panel concludes that the safety of the NF under the proposed conditions of use cannot be established.Keywords: cannabidiol, cannabinoids, Cannabis sativa , CBD, food supplement, hemp extract, novel food

INTRODUCTION

Background and Terms of Reference as provided by the requestor

On 25 March 2021, the company “CBD Industries LLC” submitted a request to the European Commission in accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 1 to authorise the placing on the market of cannabidiol isolate from Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food.

The application requests to authorise the use of cannabidiol isolated from Cannabis sativa L. in food supplements; the target population being the adult population excluding pregnant and lactating women.

The applicant has requested data protection under Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283.

In accordance with Article 10(3) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the European Commission asks the European Food Safety Authority to provide a scientific opinion on the safety of cannabidiol isolate from Cannabis sativa L. as a novel food.

In this opinion on cannabidiol isolate from Cannabis sativa L., the European Food Safety Authority should also document whether and to what extent the requirements of Article 26(2)(c) of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are fulfilled regarding the data for which the applicant is requesting data protection.

Additional information

Cannabidiol (CBD) is the active substance of Epidyolex®, a medicine approved by the European Medicines Agency (EMA). Epidyolex® contains nearly 100% pure CBD and it is used as an adjuvant to treat patients suffering from Lennox–Gastaut syndrome, Dravet syndrome or tuberous sclerosis complex in patients from 2 years of age. 2

On 26 April 2022, the NDA Panel adopted a statement on the safety of CBD as a novel food (NF), which outlined data gaps and uncertainties in the risk assessment. The Panel concluded that the safety of CBD as a NF cannot be established until gaps in both the experimental animal and human data are addressed (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022). On 16 December 2025, the NDA Panel adopted an update of the statement on safety of cannabidiol as a NF (EFSA NDA Panel, 2026). In this document, despite the remaining data gaps, a provisional safe dose of 0.0275 mg/kg body weight (bw) per day (approximately 2 mg/day for a 70‐kg adult) was derived for food supplement formulations with CBD purity ≥ 98%, without nanoparticles, for which the production process is considered safe and genotoxicity is ruled out. This provisional safe dose does not apply to individuals under 25 years of age, pregnant or lactating women, and those on concurrent medications.

In 2025, Food Standard Agency of the United Kingdom (FSA) and Food Standard Scotland (FSS) positively concluded on the safety of the NF matter of this opinion at doses of 10 mg/day. 3

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The safety assessment of this NF is based on data supplied in the original application and information submitted by the applicant following EFSA's requests for supplementary information. 4 Throughout the risk assessment, EFSA issued several requests for additional data and information. However, the applicant did not reply to the latest additional information requested by the Panel.

Administrative and scientific requirements for NF applications referred to in Article 10 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 are listed in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469. 5

A common and structured format on the presentation of NF applications is described in the EFSA guidance on the preparation and presentation of a NF application (EFSA NDA Panel, 2016). As indicated in this guidance, it is the duty of the applicant to provide all the available (proprietary, confidential and published) scientific data (including both data in favour and not in favour) that are pertinent to the safety of the NF.

This NF application includes a request for protection of proprietary data in accordance with Article 26 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283. The data requested by the applicant to be protected comprise identity, production process and toxicological information.

Methodologies

The assessment follows the methodology set out in the EFSA guidance on NF applications (EFSA NDA Panel, 2016) and the principles described in the relevant existing guidance documents from the EFSA Scientific Committee. The legal provisions for the assessment are laid down in Article 11 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283 and in Article 7 of Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2469.

This assessment concerns only the risks that might be associated with the consumption of the NF under the proposed conditions of use and is not an assessment of the efficacy of the NF with regard to any claimed benefit.

ASSESSMENT

Introduction

The NF which is the subject of the application is cannabidiol (CBD) extracted with ethanol and isolated from hemp varieties of the plant Cannabis sativa L. It is proposed to be used in food supplements at the use level of 21 mg/day. The proposed target population is adults, excluding pregnant and lactating women.

According to Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2015/2283, the NF falls under the category ‘food consisting of, isolated from, or produced from plants and their parts’.

Identity of the NF

The NF is a CBD isolate produced by solvent extraction from the aerial parts of industrial hemp varieties of the plant C. sativa L. It is a white to off‐white powder containing CBD (≥ 97% w/w) (Table 1; Figure 1). Other cannabinoids [i.e. cannabigerol (CBG), cannabinol (CBN) and cannabidivarin (CBDV)] are present in a total concentration below 2.5% w/w.

Chemical structure of CBD.

C. sativa L. is an annual flowering plant belonging to the Cannabaceae family. The aerial parts include leaves, flowers and stems excluding the main stalk. The applicant specified hemp cultivars used for the production of the NF. Plants used for the production of the NF are cultivated in the USA and grown without using pesticides.

A statement of the non‐use of genetically modified plants has been provided.

The identity of the plant was supported by a certificate of origin of industrial hemp from licensed growers.

The identity of the NF was determined using ultra‐high‐performance liquid chromatography‐diode array detection (UHPLC‐DAD) for the quantification of 12 major cannabinoids (Vaclavik et al., 2019; AOAC Method 2018.11). The applicant provided UHPLC‐UV chromatograms (240 nm) for the NF (i.e. CBD isolate). Chromatograms related to the NF diluted in medium‐chain triglycerides (MCT) oil, formulated in gummy, soft gel capsules, 6 and tincture 7 were also provided. Retention times for CBD, CBDV, CBN and CBG were assigned on the basis of single peaks and compared with authentic reference standards, for which certificates of analysis (COAs) were provided.

The Panel requested additional identity and physicochemical characterisation data. The requested data were not provided.

The Panel also requested additional information to assess whether the NF may contain small particles, including nanoparticles, in line with the relevant guidance (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021). Specifically, the applicant was asked to clarify the formulation of the food supplement containing the NF intended to be placed on the market and to provide evidence demonstrating the presence or absence of small particles and nanoparticles in the NF using the appraisal routes outlined in the guidance (EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021).

In response, the applicant submitted volume specific surface area (VSSA) data obtained by the Brunauer–Emmett–Teller (BET) method. As stated in the above‐mentioned guidance, the BET method is not appropriate for assessing particle size distribution and therefore cannot be used to determine the presence of small particles or nanoparticles. 8

The applicant was informed that the data provided were not considered valid and that the requested information necessary to rule out potential dietary exposure to small particles, including nanoparticles, remained still pending. The Panel reiterated the data requirements and, following the clarification on the use of an octanol–water partition coefficient (K ow)‐based threshold for lipid‐soluble substances as a possible appraisal route (Annex B – EFSA Scientific Committee, 2021), outlined the appropriate approach to address the concern based on final NF‐containing formulations intended to be marketed. No further evidence or clarification was received from the applicant.

Overall, contradictory information regarding the intended food supplement formulations was provided by the applicant, with powder, liquid, and chewable formulated products being mentioned. The Panel notes that it is not clear if the NF is first dissolved in an oily matrix before being added to the final formulation.

In the absence of the requested information, the Panel considers the available evidence insufficient to rule out the presence of small particles, including nanoparticles, in the NF and in the final formulations containing the NF. Consequently, the data are not adequate to conclude on the identity of the NF.

Production process

According to the information provided, the NF is produced in line with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) and Hazard Analysis Critical Control Points (HACCP) principles.

Aerial parts of certified industrial hemp varieties serve as the source material. Plants are grown from feminised seeds using organic farming techniques without the use of pesticides or herbicides and are harvested between September and October. Aerial parts of the plant are removed from the main stalk and dried in forced warm air‐drying hangars until moisture reaches 9%–10%. Internal quality standards are established to consider plant material suitable, including an average CBD content of 7%–9%, total tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) < 0.3%, 9 and meets internal limits for pesticide residues, metals and other elements. Once dried, the plant material is stored in labelled plastic‐lined super sacks.

The applicant purchases the plant material. The production process of the NF starts with its grinding to fine powder. An extraction using chilled ethanol is thereafter applied followed by filtration. Ethanol is evaporated using a falling film evaporator and a crude extract is obtained.

The crude extract is subjected to decarboxylation and subsequent distillation to obtain a product with 75% CBD content. Finally, the extract is crystallised and washed with n‐pentane to obtain the NF containing ≥ 97% CBD.

The final formulation to be placed on the market and described by the applicant entails powder, liquid, and chewable formulated products. It is unclear if final formulations entail prior dissolution in oily matrices. Details and a description of how these final formulations are prepared (e.g. final CBD concentration, medium composition) were requested by the Panel in order to rule out potential dietary exposure of consumers to small particles, including nanoparticles. The applicant did not reply to this request.

The Panel considers that the production process is sufficiently described with the exception of the final formulations.

The NF consists of CBD in powder form with a purity of ≥ 97% w/w. Other cannabinoids (i.e. CBG, CBN and CBDV) are present in a total concentration below 2.5% w/w.

Accreditation of the laboratories that conducted the analyses was confirmed. When analyses were performed using in‐house methods, validation was provided.

Proximate analysis of the NF showed that moisture content, determined by gravimetric method, ranged from 0.54 to 2.15 g/100 g. Crude protein, quantified using the Dumas method (N × 6.25), was between < 0.1 and 0.29 g/100 g. Ash, analysed by gravimetry, and fat, determined by acid hydrolysis, were consistently below their respective limits of quantification (LOQs), which are 0.1 g/100 g for ash and 1 g/100 g for fat.

Moreover, the applicant provided data for mycotoxins. All parameters analysed (aflatoxins B1, B2, G1, G2 and ochratoxin A) were found to be below their respective LOQs (i.e. 0.5 μg/kg for aflatoxins and 1 μg/kg for ochratoxin A). Arsenic, lead, cadmium, and mercury were also found consistently below their respective LOQs (i.e. < 10 μg/kg for arsenic and < 5 μg/kg for the other metals). With regard to residual solvents, n‐pentane was found in contents ranging between 382 and 1760 mg/kg, while ethanol was < 200 mg/kg in all five tested batches. Upon EFSA's request, the applicant provided analytical data for one batch reporting a content of dioxin TEQ (0.144 ng/kg) and the sum of four regulated PAHs (benzo[b]fluoranthene, benz[a]anthracene, chrysene and benzo[a]pyrene), which was found to be < 2.5 μg/kg.

Results of analytical data for pesticides in five batches of the NF analysed using gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) and/or liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) based methods, were provided. All results were found to be below the limit of detection (LOD) for all the pesticides analysed.

Finally, microbiological parameters were also tested. Aerobic plate count and yeasts & moulds resulted in < 10 colony forming units per gram (CFU/g), while Escherichia coli and Staphylococcus aureus were not detected in 1 g of the NF. Salmonella spp. was not detected in 10 g of the NF. Enterobacteriaceae, sulfite‐reducing anaerobes, Bacillus spp. (including Bacillus cereus) and Listeria monocytogenes were not analysed. The Panel was unable to seek clarification or obtain additional data due to the absence of a response from the applicant.

In addition to the analyses performed on the NF, chemical and microbiological analytical data (aerobic plate count, coliforms, E. coli, Listeria, S. aureus, Salmonella, yeasts and moulds) have been provided for independently produced batches of NF‐containing formulations. 10

The Panel considers that some information on the composition (i.e. microbiological parameters) was missing and that no conclusion could be reached.

Stability

The applicant carried out stability tests at normal storage conditions (25°C, relative humidity (RH) 60%) with three independently produced batches of the NF for 12 months and at accelerated conditions (40°C, RH 75%) with five independently produced batches of the NF for six months. The batches were analysed for cannabinoids and microbiological analysis (total plate count and combined yeasts & mould count). The amount of CBD remained stable under real time conditions whereas CBD slightly decreased (CBD range in five batches at time 0: 99.1%–100% and range at six months: 97.0%–98.2%) under accelerated conditions similar to all cannabinoids. The presence of cannabinoquinones was not examined. The Panel was unable to seek clarification or obtain additional data due to the absence of a response from the applicant.

Stability of the NF diluted in MCT oil (containing 10% of CBD) was tested under accelerated conditions for six months in three batches and the measured CBD content was stable.

Stability of the NF in liquid and chewable formulations containing 5% CBD was also tested.

The CBD concentration in gummies analysed under real time conditions slightly decreased (from 6.45% to 5.79%), while for other formulation (i.e. soft‐gel capsules and liquid formulation), it remained stable over the study period.

The intended shelf life of the NF (i.e. CBD isolate) is 18 months.

The Panel considers that some information on the stability (i.e. cannabinoquinones) was missing and that no conclusion could be reached.

Specifications

The applicant proposed specifications for the NF. However, due to the final conclusion of this opinion, the specifications are not reported.

History of use of the NF and/or of its source

History of use of the source

C. sativa L. is a plant species historically cultivated for its fibre as well as for food and medicinal uses. Industrial hemp varieties contain low THC content (< 0.3%). 11 CBD, other cannabinoids and terpenes are produced in the trichomes, which are primarily located on the flowering tops and leaves of the C. sativa L. plant. Traces of CBD can also be found on the surface of the seeds. In hemp varieties grown mainly for fibre production, the content of CBD typically ranges between 0.5% and 2.0%.

History of use of the NF

There is no history of use of the NF.

Proposed uses and use levels and anticipated intake

Target population

The target population proposed by the applicant is adults, excluding pregnant and lactating women.

Proposed uses and use levels

The applicant intends to market the NF for use in food supplements, at a maximum dose of 21 mg/day which corresponds to 0.3 mg/kg bw per day. 12

Absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion (ADME)

A toxicokinetic (TK) study was provided in a satellite group of the 14‐day study (Unpublished, 2021c), where Sprague–Dawley CD® IGS rats were administered with 30% CBD in MCT oil via oral gavage for 14 days at dose levels of 0 (vehicle), 375, 750 and 1500 mg/kg bw per day (i.e. 112.5, 225, and 450 mg/kg bw per day CBD).

Uncertainties remain concerning the type of formulations that the applicant intends to market and, consequently, the possible presence of small particles – including nanoparticles – in such formulations. The Panel therefore cannot conclude whether the selected test item and ADME testing strategy were appropriate to adequately assess the safety of the NF.

Nutritional information

The nutritional information of the NF was not assessed because of the limitations on the identity of the NF.

Toxicological information

Uncertainties remain concerning the type of formulations that the applicant intends to market and, consequently, the possible presence of small particles – including nanoparticles – in such formulations. The Panel therefore cannot conclude whether the selected test items and toxicological testing strategy were appropriate to adequately assess the safety of the NF.

Additionally, the Panel considers that the prenatal developmental toxicity study provided is not sufficient to address the critical data gap concerning the potential of CBD to interfere with the development of the central nervous system (CNS). This concern is based on available evidence indicating that CBD may affect neurodevelopmental processes and therefore could pose a risk to individuals during sensitive windows of development (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022, 2026). The applicant did not perform any studies to address this concern, as requested by EFSA.

Human data

The Panel notes that no human intervention studies conducted with the NF under assessment were submitted by the applicant. In its previous scientific statement on CBD (EFSA NDA Panel, 2022, 2026), the Panel identified several critical data gaps relevant for this assessment, including uncertainties regarding liver toxicity and potential effects on the CNS. To address these concerns, the Panel requested the applicant to provide a human intervention study designed to evaluate the potential adverse effects of CBD under conditions representative of the proposed use of the NF.

The applicant did not submit any new human data in response to this request.

Allergenicity

The applicant quantified the protein content in five batches of the NF, reporting values between < 0.1 and 0.29 g/100 g. Based on the proposed uses, the resulting protein intake from the NF on a single occasion would range from 0.021 to 0.061 mg.

The Panel considers that the allergenicity risk associated with the oral consumption of the NF (≥ 97% w/w CBD) is likely to arise from the starting material (i.e. the plant proteinaceous components extracted). Several allergens are known to be present in Cannabis sativa L 13 (Jackson et al., 2020). Among them, Can s 3 is contained in parts of the plants used for the production of the NF (i.e. aerial parts of feminised hemp plants) and is reported to mediate cross‐reactivity with, and food allergy to, several fruits and vegetables (de Larramendi et al., 2008; Drouet et al., 2017). However, it is unclear whether sensitisation and/or allergic reactions to non‐seed hemp allergens may occur following oral consumption.

Overall, the Panel considers that the allergenic potential of the NF is unknown.

DISCUSSION

The NF under assessment is a CBD isolate obtained from hemp varieties of C. sativa L. using ethanol extraction, followed by decarboxylation and crystallisation. It is a white to off‐white powder consisting of ≥ 97% w/w CBD. Other cannabinoids (i.e. CBG, CBN and CBDV) are present at a concentration below 2.5% w/w.

The NF is intended to be used in food supplements at 21 mg/day. The proposed target population is the adult population, excluding pregnant and lactating women.

Contradictory information regarding the intended formulations of the food supplement was provided by the applicant, with powder (including chewable formulations) and liquid products being mentioned. It is also unclear if the NF is first dissolved in an oily matrix before being added to the final formulation. In the absence of clear information on the final formulations, the Panel cannot determine the potential presence of small particles, including nanoparticles, in the NF and assess the consequent potential dietary exposure of consumers.

The toxicological and ADME studies were performed using an approximately 30% CBD extract diluted in MCT oil. Uncertainties remain concerning the type of formulations that the applicant intends to market and, consequently, the possible presence of small particles – including nanoparticles – in such formulations. The Panel therefore cannot conclude whether the selected test items and toxicological and ADME testing strategies were appropriate to adequately assess the safety of the NF.

CONCLUSIONS

The Panel concludes that the safety of the NF under the proposed conditions of use cannot be established.

Comparison of SARS-CoV-2 variant pathogenicity in a long-COVID Syrian hamster model
Authors: Maedeh Naghibosadat, Elan Hahn, Siena Molinaro, George Babuadze, Robert Kozak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•Pathology in hamsters is seen after recovering from infection with SARS-CoV-2 variants.•B1 and Alpha variants of SARS-CoV-2 produced more severe lung, heart, and kidney fibrosis.•This shows the need to consider lineage when studying long-term effects of SARS-CoV-2 infection.Keywords: Hamster model, Post-acute sequelae, COVID-19, Pathology, SARS-CoV-2 variants

Introduction

The SARS-CoV-2 pandemic has led to over 700 million infections worldwide to date, with a subset of individuals developing post-acute sequelae of COVID-19 (PASC); a syndrome marked by fatigue, cognitive disturbance, and persistent respiratory and cardiovascular symptoms lasting months beyond infection. Although vaccination has been shown to reduce the likelihood of PASC (Xie et al., 2024), the biological mechanisms that drive these post-acute sequelae remain poorly understood. PASC symptoms are similar to those observed in acute of COVID-19 infection (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention 2025), most commonly fatigue (Carfi et al., 2020), concentration difficulties and memory loss, shortness of breath, cough, tinnitus, tremors, tachycardia, palpitations, vertigo, joint pain and/or a rash (Carfi et al., 2020; Logue et al., 2021; Huang et al., 2021). The pathophysiology of post-acute COVID-19 likely is due not only to direct effects of the virus, but also endothelial damage, microvascular injury, and stimulation of a hyper-inflammatory state (Synowiec et al., 2021), all or some of which may contribute to subsequent syndrome. The hamster model of infection mimics many of these aspects (Imai et al., 2020; SF Sia et al., 2020).

Animal studies have shown fibrotic changes in the lung and sustained inflammation and dysregulated alveolar regeneration (Dai et al., 2025). Proteome changes in the lung have also been reported in hamsters following infection, with more pronounced changes being observed in male hamsters. (Boese et al., 2024) Neurological sequelae have also been noted in this model, and both viral RNA and replicating virus have been detected in brainstem almost 3 months after infection (Coleon et al., 2025). The majority of studies in hamsters have focused on lung pathology, and it remains unclear if the damage and ongoing inflammation occurs in other organs such as the heart and kidneys, both of which are associated with PASC symptoms in humans. 12Phenotypic differences in acute-phase disease have been reported among SARS-CoV-2 variants (Rodrigues et al., 2025), yet their impact on post-acute pathology remains uncertain.

B.1.1.7 (Alpha) and B.1.351 (Beta) both harbor the spike-RBD substitution N501Y, which increases ACE2 affinity and enhances upper-airway replication and transmission in animal models (Liu et al., 2022). Beta alone carries the immune-evasive mutations E484K and K417N, conferring greater escape from neutralizing antibodies (Chen et al., 2026). In primary human nasal-airway cultures, Alpha matches or exceeds wild-type replication, whereas Beta shows modest attenuation despite its immune advantage (Ulrich et al., 2022); the same growth pattern is observed in vivo, where infectious virus is seldom recovered from Beta-infected hamster lungs compared with Alpha or wild type (Trimpert et al., 2021). These molecular and kinetic distinctions raise the question of whether they translate into divergent chronic-phase organ damage; a gap that the present study addresses.

There are few studies in animals that look at kidneys, heart and other non-respiratory organs, and where it has been examined there has not been comparison of the pathogenicity of different SARS-CoV-2 viruses. Here, we systematically compare post-acute multi-organ pathology in Syrian hamsters infected with WT (B.1), Alpha (B.1.1.7), or Beta (B.1.351) variants, showing that organ recovery remains incomplete one month after infectionWe detect viral RNA beyond the acute phase, suggesting an ongoing inflammation. Together, these data indicate that all three SARS-CoV-2 variants cause comparable, long-lasting organ damage.

Materials and methods

Animal challenge studies

Six-to-eight -week-old male golden Syrian hamsters were purchased from Charles River Animals were housed at the BSL2 facility at the University of Toronto and transferred to the BSL3 facility at the University of Toronto for challenge experiments. Animal experiments were performed in compliance with the Canadian Council on Animal Care guidelines and approved by the Animal Care Ethics at the University of Toronto (protocol # 20,012,599). Golden hamsters were challenged intranasally with 100 μl of either SARS-CoV-2 B1 lineage (WT), dose 3.16 × 106 TCID50; SARS-CoV2/Alberta-LKS/B1.351(Beta); dose 1.78 × 106 TCID50, SARS-CoV2/Alberta-LKS/B.1.1.7 (Alpha), dose 3.16 × 106 TCID50, or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) as a negative control(n = 10 per group). Each virus was isolated from a mid-turbinate swab collected from a patient who underwent molecular respiratory multiplex testing (Banerjee et al., 2020), and confirmed by whole-genome sequencing to retain the SARS-CoV-2 lineage-defining substitutions without additional amino acid changes.

All viruses were cultured in Vero E6 cells using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM) containing 2 % FBS, 100 IU/ml penicillin, and 100 μg/ml streptomycin (Gibco, Canada). Oral swabs were collected on 3-, 7-, 14- and 28-dpi in DMEM. Quantities of infectious virus were determined by adding liquid from collected swabs to Vero E6 cells for each virus. Prior to infection, cells were seeded into 96-well plates and incubated overnight at 37 °C. On the following day, media was aspirated, and samples were added and serially diluted (10-fold dilution) into subsequent wells followed by 1 hour incubation at 37 °C with shaking every 10–15 min. After incubation, media was aspirated and replaced with complete DMEM and cells were again incubated at 37 °C. Samples were titrated in six replicates in 10-fold serial dilutions on 96-wellplates. Cytopathic effect (CPE) was scored visually at day 5, and titers were calculated by the Spearman-Karber method. (Babuadze et al., 2022)

RT-qPCR

Viral RNA was extracted from oral swabs using the QIAamp Viral RNA Kit (QIAGEN, Canada) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and viral loads were subsequently quantified. The detection and quantification of SARS-CoV-2 viral RNA were conducted following the method previously described by Babuadze et al. (Babuadze et al., 2022) To calculate the number of copies, the following formula was used: Number of copies (molecules)=X ng × 6.0221 × 1023 molecules/mol (N × 660 g/mol) × 1 × 109 ng/g. The limit of quantification was determined to be 20 copies/mL. (Babuadze et al., 2022; Naghibosadat et al., 2025)

Histopathology of hamster organs

At euthanasia on day 14 or day 28 post-infection, the lungs, heart, spleen, kidneys, and nasal turbinates of each animal were harvested for histopathological analyses. Tissues were fixed in 10 % neutral phosphate-buffered formalin, routinely processed, embedded in paraffin wax, sectioned at 5 µm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) for histopathologic examination. Masson’s trichrome and Verhoeff–Van Gieson stains were performed according to manufacturer protocols (Sigma–Aldrich). Masson’s trichrome staining was also performed to assess tissue architecture and gauge the progression of fibrosis. Images were acquired on a Tissue Scope LE (Huron Digital Pathology, Waterloo, Canada) at 20 × magnification.

To quantify fibrosis and inflammation, an automated pipeline was created for the processing of Masson Trichrome and H&E-stained image pairs. Tile-based image analysis was implemented in Python (OpenSlide v3.4, scikit-image v0.21). A counting algorithm was used to assess blue pixel density and rank tiles to locate areas of abnormal collagen deposition. The top 10 % of tiles were used to reference their corresponding regions within the H&E images, which were accessed at full resolution for nuclear segmentation. To localize inflammatory nuclei, an H&E-specific instance segmentation was used. (Graham et al., 2019) The density of inflammatory nuclei was calculated per tile as the ratio of inflammatory to epithelial and connective cells. The region of aggregation of inflammatory cell infiltrates is delineated and represented as percentage of the cell infiltrates area to total tissue area. The regions of collagen content stained in blue were delineated and represented as percentage fibrosis area to total tissue area. (Naghibosadat et al., 2025) Lung tissues were examined for the presence or absence of features of cell or tissue damage (necrosis), inflammatory cells cellular debris, and alveolar lesions as well as evidence of regeneration and repair.

H & E, Masson’s trichrome and Verhoeff–Van Gieson staining performed for heart tissues. Heart tissues were also evaluated for myocardial damage (necrosis or pyknosis), inflammatory cells cellular debris, adipose tissue deposition as well as regeneration and repair. In H & E staining, the region of aggregation of inflammatory cell infiltrates is delineated and represented as percentage of the cell infiltrates area to total tissue area. In Masson’s trichrome staining, the regions of collagen content stained in blue were delineated and represented as percentage fibrosis area to total tissue area. In Verhoeff–Van Gieson staining, the regions of collagen content stained in dark pink were delineated and represented as collagen percentage and, the regions of elastin content stained in dark pink were delineated and represented as elastin percentage.

Masson’s trichrome staining was also performed on kidneys to evaluate the fibrosis, collagen deposition and regeneration and repair. The regions of collagen content stained in blue were delineated and represented as percentage fibrosis area to total tissue area.

H & E staining was performed on spleen sections to evaluate the tissue architecture and count the germinal centers. A counting algorithm was used to assess the number of germinal centers. All analysis scripts are available upon request.

The nasal turbinates were sectioned and stained with H&E and examined via light microscopy. Fibrosis was assessed and scored form 0–3 (0= no fibrosis, 1= focal fibrosis affecting <5 % of the tissue, 2= mild-to-moderate fibrosis affecting 5–25 % of the tissue, 3= diffuse fibrosis affecting >25 % of the tissue). Inflammation was similarly scored (0= no inflammation above normal expectations, 1= focal inflammation, 2= mild-to-moderate inflammation, 3= diffuse inflammation), and qualified if acute or chronic inflammatory cells were identified, which cell types predominated, and the micro-anatomic site where the inflammation was most prominent. The tissue was also evaluated for the presence of eosinophils and plasma cells, vasculitis, thrombosis, necrosis, and viral cytopathic effect. Any changes to the sero-mucinous glandular structures were recorded.

Statistical analysis

All figures were generated using Prism version 9.2 (GraphPad Software Inc.), and statistical analysis was performed using two-way ANOVA tests, and p values of <0.05 were considered significant.

Results

Disease course in hamsters

Previous studies have shown that Syrian golden hamsters mimic a mild to moderate acute disease phenotype. (Sia et al., 2020) To evaluate this, we challenged the golden hamsters intranasally with either B1 (WT), B.1.351 (Beta), B.1.1.7 (Alpha) viruses or PBS (control). Following infection, animals were monitored for clinical signs of disease and weight loss. Viral shedding was assessed in oral swabs collected post-challenge to examine potential differences between SARS-CoV-2 variants. All variants demonstrated similar disease course, with peak quantities of viral particles on day 3 post-infection, which declined significantly on days 7 and 14 and reached the lowest level at day 28 (Fig. 1a). However, viral load was highest on day 3 for all variants and remained high on day 7 before reducing significantly on days 14 and 28. Interestingly, the viral load of the Alpha variant was undetectable on day 28 by qPCR (Fig. 1b). Peak weight loss occurred at 7 dpi (WT 12 ± 3 %, Alpha 10 ± 2 %, Beta 9 ± 3 %), with full recovery by 28 dpi. Although the overall trajectory was similar across variants, WT-infected hamsters regained weight slightly slower compared to Alpha- or Beta-infected animals between 14 and 28 dpi. All animals recovered from infection (Fig. 1c).

Post-infection lung damage by SARS-CoV-2 variants

Persistent respiratory symptoms have often been described in PASC. (Munipalli et al., 2022) To investigate lung pathology, groups of animals were euthanized after the acute phase of disease (14 dpi) and at 28 dpi, and the extent of pulmonary disease was evaluated. Pulmonary pathology was observed in all variant-infected groups compared with the control group. Histopathological examination revealed multifocal interstitial inflammation with broncho-alveolar inflammatory infiltrates at both 14 and 28 dpi. Quantitative scoring confirmed significantly higher inflammatory-cell counts in WT- and Alpha-infected lungs (p < 0.0001) at both time-points. The highest number of infiltrates was detected in WT-infected lungs at 14 dpi, whereas the lungs from the Beta-infected group contained fewer inflammatory lesions than the other two variants. It was observed that inflammatory-cell numbers declined slightly by 28 dpi in all variants (Fig. 2a and c).

Quantitative analysis confirmed that collagen accumulation was most pronounced in WT-infected lungs at day 14 (p < 0.0001), and Alpha- and WT-infected lungs were more fibrotic than the Beta-infected group. (Fig. 2b and d). Overall, WT-infected animals lungs demonstrated the greatest pulmonary inflammatory burden and highest mean fibrosis percentage. Importantly, all infected lungs still displayed inflammatory cells and fibrosis at 28 dpi, indicating incomplete pulmonary recovery one month after infection.

Cardiac injury after SARS-CoV-2 infection

Next, the extent of tissue pathology and fibrosis in cardiac tissues was compared, as infection increases the risk of cardiovascular complications. (Krishna et al., 2024; Dhakal et al., 2020) It was observed that cardiac myofibers in SARS-CoV-2-infected hearts were shrunken, and aggregates of lymphocytes and fibroblasts indicated active myocarditis at both 14 and 28 dpi compared to those from control animals. Quantitative scoring confirmed a significant rise in inflammatory-cell infiltrates across variants: cardiac tissue from the WT group exhibited the highest infiltrate counts at both 14 and 28 dpi (p < 0.0001), followed by Alpha and then Beta. The number of inflammatory infiltrates increased by 28 dpi compared with 14 dpi for all variants. (Fig. 4a and d)

To evaluate cardiac degeneration and fibrosis, we performed Masson’s trichrome staining. Control hearts displayed uniformly organized myofibers with minimal collagen. In contrast, hearts from infected hamsters exhibited pronounced interstitial and perivascular fibrosis. Quantitative analysis confirmed a significant increase in collagen deposition in all variants compared with controls. The percentage of fibrotic area was greatest in WT hearts, followed by Alpha and then Beta. Collagen deposition further increased at day 28 dpi relative to day 14 dpi for all variants. (Fig. 4b and e)

To delineate the fibrotic composition, specifically the balance between collagen and elastin, we performed Verhoeff–Van Gieson staining. In this stain, collagen appears dark pink, cardiac myofibers yellow-brown, and elastin black. Cardiac tissues from infected hamsters showed far more collagen deposition than controls, and the collagen percentage consistently exceeded the elastin percentage, indicating reduced contractility in the infected hearts. Collagen quantification by Verhoeff–Van Gieson mirrored the Masson’s trichrome results, confirming a significant increase in collagen deposition across all variants. Elastin content, however, did not differ significantly from control at day 14; by day 28, elastin percentage was higher in the Beta group and, to a lesser extent, in WT, whereas the increase in Alpha remained non-significant (Fig. 4c, f, g). Collectively, these findings indicate that all SARS-CoV-2 variants induced myocarditis to varying degrees, with the most extensive inflammatory and fibrotic changes observed in WT and Alpha hearts. In contrast, Beta hearts exhibited only mild, focal myocardial lesions with limited fibroblast activation. The collagen deposition in all variants, indicates that myocardial recovery remains incomplete one month after infection.

Kidneys exhibit chronic fibrotic lesions after SARS-CoV-2 infection

Pathology in the kidneys was also evaluated, as renal tropism for SARS-CoV-2 has been described, and kidney injury is a frequent complication of infection (Babuadze et al., 2022; Pei et al., 2020; Torrino et al., 2021). Masson’s trichrome staining showed renal fibrosis in all variant-infected hamsters. The images revealed patchy interstitial collagen deposition in renal tissues at 14 dpi and still evident at 28 dpi. Fractional fibrotic area was significantly elevated in WT- and Alpha-infected groups relative to controls, with Beta demonstrating milder changes. Quantitative image analysis confirmed a significant rise in fibrotic area across variants: WT- and Alpha-infected kidneys had the greatest percentage fibrosis at both 14 and 28 days (p < 0.0001), followed by Beta-infected kidneys (p < 0.001). The level of collagen deposition decreased slightly on day 28 compared with day 14 in all variants; however, fibrotic areas remained detectable in all SARS-CoV-2-variant-infected kidneys (Fig. 5a and b).

Expansion of splenic germinal centers after SARS-CoV-2 infection

Since SARS-CoV-2 infection induces germinal center responses, we evaluated the number of germinal centers in the spleen. Quantitative assessment showed an increase in germinal centers after SARS-CoV-2 infection. At both 14 and 28 dpi, germinal center numbers were significantly higher in all variant-infected groups than in controls. The rise followed a consistent trend, with the Alpha variant showing the greatest increase, followed by wild-type and then Beta; however, the differences among the infected groups were not statistically significant. Within each variant cohort, germinal center counts did not differ between 14 and 28 dpi (Fig. 6).

Discussion

The Syrian golden hamster model has been shown to the mimic mild-to-moderate disease phenotype following infection and has been investigated for a model of PASC, (van der Bie et al., 2025) which could provide opportunities for investigating therapies and interventions. Here we compare the PASC phenotype generated by different SARS-CoV-2 variants and demonstrate that they cause similar pathology in the post-acute phase of disease.

Additionally, infection with the Beta-variant did show reduced long pathology at both time points, suggesting infection with the variant may result in more moderate PASC. The milder pathology observed following Beta infection, most likely reflects both the slightly lower inoculum and the reduced replication fitness reported for this variant, consistent with its attenuated phenotype in vitro. Whether this is also observed in humans is unclear as it is often challenging to obtain viral genotyping data. A study from Italy did not note any differences in PASC severity between the Beta variant and other variants, but the small size of the cohort is a limitation. (Aloè et al., 2023) Additionally, a recent retrospective cohort studies have shown similar findings with PASC severity and symptoms being similar amongst the variants. (Padilla et al., 2024)

Beyond respiratory impact of SARS-CoV-2, there is a complex interplay between the virus and cardiovascular system. The cardiovascular complications affect the patients during acute phase and even after months to years of infection. Myocardial injury, arrhythmias, heart failure and vascular dysfunction are common complications in COVID-19 patients. (Shafi et al., 2020)

In our study, we detected persistent myocarditis in the left ventricle, accompanied by myocardial collagen deposition and interstitial coronary fibrosis. Increased collagen within cardiac myofibres in infected groups suggests reduced contractility and, consequently, impaired cardiac function. These findings extend earlier hamster work that reported ventricular-wall thickening and coronary-artery fibrosis, as well as biochemical evidence of injury without quantifying collagen and elastin in the heart. That study only examined a single strain of Sars-CoV-2 at 14 dpi. (Abbas Rizvi et al., 2026) In our study, we provide the first demonstration that post-acute cardiac pathology in hamsters is variant-dependent and persists to at least 28 dpi. Persistent myocarditis and progressive fibrosis through 28 dpi indicate incomplete myocardial recovery, paralleling post-COVID cardiomyopathy in humans.

In another study, viral RNA loads were measured in the hearts of rhesus and cynomolgus macaques up to 42 dpi. However, the histopathological analysis was limited to lungs and lymph nodes. (Böszörményi et al., 2021) Since persistent inflammation and fibrosis predict future adverse cardiovascular events in humans, (Aleksova et al., 2022) our quantitative data underscore the translational value of the hamster model and highlight a therapeutic window for evaluating anti-fibrotic or cardio protective agents. Residual viral RNA and sustained immune activation likely facilitate prolonged cytokine production, promoting fibroblast activation and collagen deposition across multiple organs (Stein et al., 2022; Patterson et al., 2022). The greater degree of fibrosis in infected kidneys at the later time-points, compared with controls is suggestive of potential tubular injury or evolving chronic kidney disease. Renal interstitial fibrosis detected here mirrors early chronic kidney disease–like patterns observed in post-COVID autopsies, suggesting that even subclinical infection may predispose to long-term renal dysfunction. A key limitation of the present study is that each extra-pulmonary organ was processed in its entirety for histology, leaving no residual tissue for infectious-virus or viral-RNA assays; consequently, we could not directly correlate the severity of kidney, heart, or spleen pathology with residual viral burden in the same animals. Future experiments will include parallel tissue banking to enable us correlating the organ pathology with viral persistence more precisely. Additionally, further studies are needed to compare the long term pathology in this hamster model following infection with the Omicron variant.

Conclusion

Together, these findings establish a reproducible post-acute pathology model suitable for preclinical evaluation of antifibrotic and cardioprotective interventions following infection with three variants of SARS-CoV-2. The observed differences in pathology elicited by the different variants highlights the need to consider lineage when studying long-term effects of SARS-CoV-2 infection. Moreover, future studies should investigate the post-acute pathology caused by other circulating or emerging variants.

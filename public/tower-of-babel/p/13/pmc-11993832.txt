eGFRCystatin C, difference between eGFRCystatin C and eGFRCre and heart failure: Insight from the NHANES 2001–2002 and Mendelian randomization analysis
Authors: Zhiyu Gu, Rui Zhang, Weihong Chang, Hongxuan Fan, Zixuan Dou, Peng Liu, Aman Liu, Boda Zhou
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimEstimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) derived from Cystatin C (eGFRCystatin C), and the difference between Cystatin C and creatinine based eGFR (eGFRdiff) has been suggested to be associated with cardiovascular disease. However, the association between eGFRCystatin C,eGFRdiff and heart failure (HF) risk has not been elucidated in a relatively healthy cohort.MethodsWe used cohort study data from the NHANES 2001–2002. Mendelian randomization (MR) study used GWAS data from 437,846 European participants. The exposures are eGFRCystatin C & eGFRdiff, outcome is self reported heart failure. Weighted multivariable-adjusted logistic regression and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was used in corhort study. Inverse variance weighted (IVW) was applied in MR study.ResultsThe cohort study included 2155 participants. Importantly, we simplified eGFRdiff classification into ≥0 and < 0, and found that eGFRdiff≥0 was associated with 52 % reduction of HF risk (OR 0.48, [95 % CI, 0.29–0.80], p = 0.005). We also found that 1 ml/min/1.73 m2 of eGFRCystatin C had a significant negative association with HF after adjusting for covariates. Interestingly, we showed a non-linear association between eGFRCystatin C and HF, eGFRdiff and HF. In participants without know HF, during a median follow-up of 17.3 years, those in the low eGFRCystatin C or low eGFRdiff groups showed significantly poorer survival. Moreover, MR analysis found genetic predisposition to cystatin C was significantly associated with an increased risk of HF.ConclusionBoth decreased eGFRCystatin C and eGFRdiff levels were associated with heart failure and poor survival, but the latter seems more obvious.Keywords: Heart failure, Cystatin C, eGFR, National health and nutrition examination survey, Mendelian randomization

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is a leading cause of death worldwide, which frequently occurs alongside with other diseases, especially kidney disease [1]. A decrease in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), such as in chronic kidney disease (CKD), may speed up the progression of HF and serve as predictor of mortality [2]. Meanwhile, in HF patients, reduced cardiac output leads to decreased blood flow to the kidneys, resulting in kidney dysfunction and eGFR reduction, which may cause fluid retention, increased systemic vascular resistance, and worsening of HF [3]. Meta-analysis also showed that lower eGFR was associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes, including cardiovascular mortality and HF [4]. Unfortunately, evidence is still lacking to optimize HF risk assessment with kidney function in a relatively healthy cohort.

A study tested three formulae for eGFR to determine the most clinically informative for predicting CVD and mortality and found eGFRCystatin C as most strongly associated with CVD and mortality [5]. Moreover, cystatin C concentration was associated with the risk of developing HF in elderly population [6]. Based on these findings, it is necessary to study the relationship between eGFRCystatin C and HF in general population. Recently, the difference between cystatin C and creatinine based eGFR (eGFRdiff) has been proposed as a measure of cardiovascular events, and all-cause mortality [7]. In CKD patients without prevalent HF, it was found that negative eGFRdiff associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes, including HF [8]. Despite these associations, the relationship between eGFRCystatin C, eGFRdiff and HF has not been tested in general population such as in health survey.

Above all, we combined a large-scale observational study in the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) 2001–2002 and a two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to comprehensively assess the relationship between eGFRCystatin C, eGFRdiff and HF in this study. The NHANES is a continuing cross-sectional study carried out in the United States which provides high-quality, large sample, and nationally representative data [9] to evaluate the correlation between levels of eGFRCystatin C, eGFRdiff and HF risk. MR analysis have been recognized as a complementary approach to randomized controlled trials (RCTs) which was regarded as an emerging epidemiology method that evaluates the causal effects of exposure on a particular outcome using genetic variables [10].

Methods

Study design and population

This study utilized a nationally representative cohort from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES). Our analysis drew upon NHANES data spanning from 2001 to 2002 obtained from [http://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes] and initially involving 11039 participants. After the exclusion of participants lacking data on cystatin C (N = 3125), heart failure (N = 5652), mortality (N = 1) or covariates (N = 15), and individuals with weak/failing kidneys (N = 91) (KIQ022 in Kidney Conditions - Urology questionair, Question: {Have you/Has SP} ever been told by a doctor or other health professional that {you/s/he} had weak or failing kidneys? Do not include kidney stones, bladder infections, or incontinence). We further excluded participants with missing values for covariates. The final analysis included 2155 participants (Fig. 1).

Flowchart of participants inclusion.

Ethics statement

The NHANES protocols received approval from the Ethics Review Board of the National Center for Health Statistics, with all participants providing written informed consent. Research was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant guidelines/regulations. Since the study used only secondary data analysis and did not identify the personal information of any of the participants by extracting statistics from the NHANES website, further ethical approval is not required to conduct the study (for more information, visit http://www.ethicsguidebook.ac.uk/Secondary-analysis-106.

Measures of kidney function

Trained personnel conducted the collection of blood samples. The serum obtained from the initial blood collection was preserved at −70 °C until 2006. Subsequently, cystatin C measurements were performed using particle-enhanced nephelometric assay, which was performed on the Dade Behring Nephelometer II [11]. According to literature [12], this assay is the most accurate and precise among automated assays across the clinical range, with an intra-assay imprecision range between 2.0 and 3.0 % coefficient of variation. The assessment of kidney function, which considers age, gender, and race in serum creatinine-based estimating equations, is crucial due to the impact of these factors on serum creatinine production. We derived eGFRCre from the Modification of Diet in Renal Disease (MDRD) equation with race coefficients [13]. While eGFRCystatin C was calculated using the CKD-EPI 2012 equation, and difference between eGFRCystatin C and eGFRCre (eGFRdiff) was calculated using the CKD-EPI 2021 equation [14,15].

Main exposure and study outcome

The exposure of interest was baseline eGFRdiff and eGFRCystatin C. The participants were classified into two groups according to the median value of eGFRdiff: low (<5.08 ml/min/1.73 m2), and high (≥5.08 ml/min/1.73 m2). Meanwhile, participants were also classified into two groups according to the median value of eGFRCystatin C: low (<84.70 ml/min/1.73 m2), and high (≥84.70 ml/min/1.73 m2). The primary outcome was self-reported heart failure, defined as yes to question (MCQ160B in Medical Conditions questionair, Question: Has a doctor or other health professional ever told {you/SP} that {you/s/he}… had congestive heart failure?). The detailed information is publicly available at http://www.cdc.gov/nchs/nhanes/.

Covariates

Categorical variables considered as covariates for heart failure encompassed age, gender, race, education, marital status, HDL-C, TC, hypertension, serum albumin, diabetes, smoking, exercise, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, albuminuria, poverty income ratio, body mass index, eGFRCre.

Genetic instrument selection

Single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) associated with modifiable factors at the genome-wide significance level (p ≤ 5 × 10−8) were obtained from corresponding GWASs (Table S1). SNPs in linkage disequilibrium (r2 ≥ 0.01) were excluded and the SNP with the smallest p value for the genome-wide association was attained. Detailed information on GWASs of studied exposures, including number of participants and adjusted covariates, is presented in Table S1.

Genome-wide association study (GWAS) sources and SNPs selection

As there was only one dataset of congestive heart failure in non hypertensive individuals in South Asian population, the genetic variants strongly related to serum cystatin C were derived from 8422 South Asian participants. The genetic variants strongly related to congestive heart failure in non hypertensive individuals were sourced from a South Asian population (N = 8833). Moreover, the results were verified in European population, the genetic variants strongly related to serum cystatin C were derived from 437,846 European participants. The genetic variants strongly related to heart failure, not strict were sourced from a European population (N = 23,701). These GWAS data were downloaded from the Integrative Epidemiology Unit (IEU) OpenGWAS database (https://gwas. mrcieu. ac. uk/) [16]. All studies were reviewed and approved by local institutional review boards, and all participants provided informed consent.

To identify the genetic variations that can be used for the estimation of causal effects between exposures (serum cystatin C) and outcomes (congestive heart failure; non hypertensive or heart failure, not strict), the genome-wide significance level was set at P < 5 × 10−8 to screen the genetic variant that strongly associated with the exposure. Furthermore, we excluded those SNPs with linkage disequilibrium (r2 < 0.001, 10,000 kb). Then, the PhenoScanner database was used to check each SNP to eliminate the SNPs that were significantly related to the potential confounders.

Statistical analysis

Acknowledging the intricate probability cluster design inherent in NHANES, our study incorporated weights in all statistical analyses. The eGFRCystatin C and eGFRdiff was stratified into two parts, and a weighted variance estimation analysis was conducted to address the notable variability within our dataset. Subsequently, we executed a weighted multivariable-adjusted logistic regression, deeming p < 0.05 as statistically significant. The results were articulated as odds ratios (OR) with corresponding 95 % confidence intervals (CI). In addition, a generalized additive model (GAM) was used based on smooth curve fitting to explore the non-linear relationships of eGFRCystatin C and eGFRdiff with the risk of HF and to observe whether there was a segmented relationship. Threshold effect analysis was performed using segmented regression models.

As for the two-sample MR analysis, we applied inverse variance weighted (IVW) as the principal method to assess the causal association of genetically predicted cystatin C, and heart failure risk. Furthermore, four complementary MR analysis methods were used, including MR Egger, weighted median, weighted mode, and MR-Pleiotropy residual sum and outlier (MRPRESSO), to validate the results from IVW. Cochrane's Q test was used to evaluate the potential heterogeneity. A random-effects IVW analysis was used to adjust the measured heterogeneity (P < 0.05). Next, the intercept of MR-Egger was adopted to estimate the horizontal pleiotropy of the genetic variants (P < 0.05 was considered as the potential presence of horizontal pleiotropy). MR-PRESSO was also used to assess the presence of pleiotropy by comparing the observed residual sum of squares with the expected residual sum of squares. Moreover, the leave-one-out analysis was performed to determine whether the results were driven by individual variants.

The analysis were executed using R software, version 4.1.3 (R Foundation, Vienna, Austria) and EmpowerStats software (X&Y Solutions Inc., Boston, MA, USA), with statistical significance set at P < 0.05.

Results

Population characteristics of study subjects

This study included 11,039 American adults with a median age of 44.00 years; 1086 females (weighted, 50.6 %); and 1069 males (weighted, 49.3 %). Compared to participants without HF, those with HF were significantly older, had significantly higher serum creatinine & cystatin C levels, higher systolic blood pressure (SBP) & body mass index (BMI), higher prevalence of hypertension & diabetes, lower education level & excercise rates, and lower poverty income ratio (PIR) (Table 1). Additionally, we stratified the weighted characteristics of these participants based on eGFRCystatin C (low: <84.70 ml/min/1.73 m2; and high: ≥84.70 ml/min/1.73 m2), or eGFRdiff (low: <5.08 ml/min/1.73 m2; and high: ≥5.08 ml/min/1.73 m2). The low eGFRCystatin C group was older, had higher proportion of male & widowed, higher prevalence of HF, hypertension & diabetes, higher SBP & BMI, higher albuminuria & serum creatinine, lower HDL-C, lower education level & excercise rates (Table 2). The low eGFRdiff group had higher proportion of male, lower serum creatinine and higher cystatin C, higher prevalence of HF & hypertension, higher BMI, higher albuminuria & serum creatinine, lower HDL-C, lower excercise rates, and lower PIR (Table 3).

Association between eGFRCystatin C, eGFRdiff and heart failure

Table 4 showed the weighted multivariable logistic regression results of the association between eGFRdiff and HF. Importantly, we simplified eGFRdiff classification into ≥0 and < 0, to facilitate clinical practice. After adjusted for covariates such as age, gender, race, education, marital status, HDL-C, TC, hypertension, serum albumin, diabetes, smoking, exercise, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, albuminuria, poverty income ratio, body mass index and eGFRCre, we found that eGFRdiff≥0 was associated with 52 % reduction of HF risk (OR 0.48, [95 % CI, 0.29–0.80], p = 0.005). We also showed that 1 ml/min/1.73 m2 of eGFRdiff had a significant negative association with HF (OR 0.98, [95 % CI, 0.96–1.00], p = 0.019). Similarly, we found that 1 ml/min/1.73 m2 of eGFRCystatin C had a significant negative association with HF (OR 0.98, [95 % CI, 0.96–1.00], p = 0.019) after adjusting for covariates including eGFRCre.

Non-linear relationship

The non-linear relationships of eGFRCystatin C, eGFRdiff with the risk of HF were analyzed through a GAM and smooth curve fitting. The results revealed non-linear relationships of eGFRCystatin C with the risk of HF (Fig. 3A and B). Table 5 shows that the inflection point for eGFRCystatin C was 106.32 ml/min/1.73 m2. When eGFRCystatin C < 106.32, there was a negative association between eGFRCystatin C and the risk of HF (OR 0.95, [95 % CI, 0.94–0.96], p < 0.0001). However, when eGFRCystatin C > 106.32, eGFRCystatin C was not associated with the risk of HF. The eGFRdiff inflection point for HF was −0.68 as shown in Table 6. When eGFRdiff > −0.68, eGFRdiff showed a negative association with the risk of HF (OR 0.94, [95 % CI, 0.91–0.97], p < 0.0001). However, when eGFRdiff > −0.68, eGFRdiff was not associated with the risk of HF (Fig. 3C and D).

Survival analysis based on eGFRCystatin C and eGFRdiff levels

In participants without know HF, during a median (IQR) follow-up of 17.3 (10.4–18.2) years, 940 deaths were recorded. In order to explore the prognostic value of eGFRCystatin C and eGFRdiff, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed in participants without known heart failure (Fig. 2). Participants were divided into two groups according to eGFRCystatin C (low: <84.70 ml/min/1.73 m2; and high: ≥84.70 ml/min/1.73 m2) or eGFRdiff (low: <5.08 ml/min/1.73 m2; and high: ≥5.08 ml/min/1.73 m2) levels: participants in the low eGFRCystatin C or low eGFRdiff groups showed significantly poorer survival (log-rank test p < 0.0001).

Causal relationships between kidney function and heart failure in MR

As there was no available GWAS data on eGFRdiff, we conducted MR analysis to substantiate the direct causal impact of cystatin C on heart failure. Genetic predisposition to cystatin C was significantly associated with an increased risk of heart failure in the IVW analysis (β = 0.05645, SE = 0.02458, p = 0.0216, Tables S1–S2, Fig. S1). Sensitivity analysis using the weighted-median, weighted mode, and MR-Egger methods were also employed, and the estimates exhibited minimal alteration compared to IVW. Furthermore, MR-Egger intercept tests indicated no significant evidence of pleiotropy.

Discussion

HF is the most common endpoint of CVD, affecting about 64.3 million people worldwide [17]. CKD affects 15–20 % of adults globally and increases the risk of various CVD including HF [18]. The close link between kidney and heart pathological conditions has long been recognized. Notably, CKD is particularly strongly associated with HF and CVD mortality [19]. Though kidney function assessment did not have a gold standard yet, eGFRCre was used clinically to assess kidney function. In recent years, more and more studies have shown that eGFRCystatin C is more accurate than eGFRCre in terms of renal function and predicting CVD outcome. In a retrospective analysis of 114 cohorts studies, lower eGFRCystatin C was associated with increased rates of adverse CVD outcome, including HF [4].

In the current study, we combined an observational study using the nationally representative sample, with a two-sample MR analysis to explore the association between eGFRCystatin C, eGFRdiff and HF. Our results indicated that lower eGFRCystatin C or eGFRdiff levels were more strongly associated with risk of HF, and even poor survival. Additionally, the two-sample MR analysis provided further evidence of the causal impact of eGFR cystatin C levels on the incidence of HF, confirming that lower eGFRCystatin C level could serve as a marker for HF.

Most published studies devided eGFRdiff into three categories (<-15, −15∼15, >15), here we simplified eGFRdiff classification into ≥0 and < 0, to facilitate clinical practice. We found that eGFRdiff≥0 was associated with 52 % reduction of HF risk. In other words, when clinician found that eGFRCystatin C was lower than eGFRCre in a specific patient, the risk of HF was significantly increase. Moreover, we showed non-linear relationships between eGFRCystatin C, eGFRdiff with the risk of HF, on the basis of previous studies, especially for eGFRdiff, the non-linear relationship deserve further research.

The thorough evaluation of various factors and a large sample size enabled us to effectively adjust our analysis for multiple confounding variables simultaneously in multivariable regression models. Additionally, the MR approach can prevent unmeasured confounding causality bias, enhancing the reliability of the findings.

Limitations

Firstly, HF diagnosis was relied on self-reported questionnaires, potentially introducing bias. Secondly, the exclusion of missing data and the focus on European and American populations may have led to selection and generalization biases. Thirdly, though we controlled for many covariates that were considered relevant confounders, it is possible that there was still residual and unmeasured confounding. Finally, the study's findings are primarily applicable to European and American populations, and further research with larger sample sizes and diverse ethnic groups is needed to validate these results.

Conclusion

In conclusion, by integrating a comprehensive observational study in NHANES with MR analysis, we were able to determine that both decreased eGFRCystatin C and eGFRdiff levels were associated with heart failure and poor survival, but the latter seems more obvious. Nevertheless, our findings require validation and further investigation into the underlying mechanisms.

Concerted Bond Formation in Diels–Alder Reactions: A Configuration Analysis
Authors: Ken Sakata, Tasuku Yano, Nanami Ikeda, Takeshi Yoshikawa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The concerted bond formation in the cycloaddition reaction between butadiene and ethylene, which is regarded as a prototypical model of the Diels–Alder reaction, was examined through an electron configuration analysis. We found that the weight of a crossed two-electron transfer configuration, in which one electron is transferred from the HOMO of butadiene to the LUMO of ethylene and another electron is transferred from the HOMO of ethylene to the LUMO of butadiene, increases gradually along the reaction coordinate and becomes one of the major configurations in the product region. This configuration involves mutual electron transfer between the two subsystems and gives rise to an electronic configuration that is formally equivalent to that produced by polarization within each isolated subsystem, although such polarization is symmetry-forbidden along the reaction pathway. Thus, the crossed two-electron transfer configuration provides a symmetry-allowed route to realize a polarization-like electronic configuration within each subsystem. These results demonstrate how localized bonding representations used in conventional organic electron theory emerge from orbital interactions and establish a direct connection between frontier-orbital descriptions and resonance-like electron redistribution during the concerted bond formation.

Introduction

The Diels–Alder reaction is one of the most useful transformations in synthetic organic chemistry, particularly in total synthesis. Its mechanism has been extensively studied for decades. Although stepwise mechanisms have been proposed, , recent experimental and theoretical studies indicate that, while both pathways are possible, the concerted pathway suggested by the Woodward–Hoffmann rules , is generally preferred. −

The cycloaddition reaction between butadiene and ethylene is often regarded as a prototypical model of the Diels–Alder reaction, although it proceeds only under high-temperature and high-pressure conditions (200 °C, 200–400 atm). In the concerted pathway, the symmetry plane is preserved throughout the reaction (Figure ). Consequently, orbital interactions are restricted to molecular orbitals of the same symmetry with respect to this plane. Because both the HOMO of ethylene (π1′) and the LUMO of butadiene (π3) are symmetric with respect to this symmetry plane (a′ symmetry), in-phase overlap at one reaction center necessarily leads to in-phase overlap at the other reaction center as well (Figure a). Similarly, the HOMO of butadiene (π2) and the LUMO of ethylene (π2′) are both antisymmetric with respect to the symmetry plane (a″ symmetry), and these orbitals also exhibit identical phase relationships at the two reaction centers (Figure b). Consequently, on the basis of frontier molecular orbital (FMO) theory, the reaction has been proposed to proceed via both directions of electron delocalization between butadiene and ethylene. ,

Two types of electron delocalization: (a) interaction between the HOMO of ethylene (π1′) and the LUMO of butadiene (π3) and (b) interaction between the HOMO of butadiene (π2) and the LUMO of ethylene (π2′).

Thus, FMO theory and the Woodward–Hoffmann rules account for the concerted nature of the Diels–Alder reaction in terms of both electron delocalization from butadiene to ethylene and from ethylene to butadiene. Numerous theoretical studies have further examined this reaction and have provided detailed insights into its reaction mechanism. − However, this description remains inherently global and does not directly describe how individual bond-forming events emerge from these delocalization processes, nor how such an orbital-based description relates to the resonance structures commonly invoked in conventional organic electron theory. Therefore, in the present study, we investigate the nature of bond formation in concerted [4 + 2] cycloaddition reactions through an electron configuration analysis, in which the wave function of the composite system, constructed from two isolated subsystems, is expanded in terms of electron configurations that explicitly represent intersubsystem electron transfer and intrasubsystem polarization at the two bond-forming centers.

Methods of Calculations

The molecular orbitals (MOs) ψ f (f = 1, 2,···,M + N) of a composite A–B system constructed from two isolated subsystems A and B can be expressed as linear combinations of the molecular orbitals (LCMOs) of the isolated subsystems A and B, ϕ i (i = 1, 2,···M) and φ j (j = 1, 2,···N): ψf=∑i=1mdifϕi+∑k=m+1Mdkfϕk+∑j=1ndjfφj+∑l=n+1Ndlfφl Here, m and n denote the numbers of occupied orbitals of subsystems A and B, respectively, whereas M and N represent the total numbers of orbitals (occupied and unoccupied orbitals) of A and B.

In the electron configuration analysis method, the Hartree–Fock wave function Ψ of the composite system, constructed from the molecular orbitals of the isolated subsystems, is represented as a combination of a reference electron configuration Ψ0 and a set of configuration functions Ψ I (I = 1, 2, 3···), which describe electron delocalization (charge-transfer) and polarization effects both between and within the subsystems Ψ=C0Ψ0+∑I=1CIΨI

This approach provides a systematic means to characterize orbital interactions between subsystems A and B. ,−

In principle, the Hartree–Fock wave function Ψ can be fully represented by including all possible configuration functions. In previous studies, the singly excited configurations shown in Figure were considered for the configuration functions Ψ I . These include charge-transfer and polarization configurations, namely Ψ i→l , Ψ j→k , Ψ i→k , and Ψ j→l where i and j denote occupied orbitals of subsystems A and B, respectively, and k and l denote unoccupied orbitals of A and B. In addition, a subset of doubly excited configurations, such as Ψ i→l,i→l , Ψ j→k,j→k , Ψ i→k,i→k , and Ψ j→l,j→l has also been considered. By evaluating the coefficients C I associated with these configuration functions, − the relative importance of intersubsystem electron delocalization and intrasubsystem polarization can be quantitatively assessed. −

In the present study, in addition to the doubly excited configurations represented by Baba and co-workers, the analysis is further extended to include selected triple- and quadruple-excitation configurations (Figure S2). The weights w I of the individual configuration functions Ψ I are evaluated using the Chirgwin–Coulson method, according to the following expression wI≅C0CI⟨ΨI|Ψ0⟩+CI∑JCJ⟨ΨI|ΨJ⟩

Geometry optimizations of the transition states and intrinsic reaction coordinate (IRC) calculations were carried out at the ωB97X-D/6–311++G­(d,p) level. Configuration analyses were then performed at the RHF/STO-6G level for the structures obtained along the IRC. Hartree–Fock calculations for generating the molecular orbitals used in the configuration analysis, as well as density functional theory (DFT) calculations employed for geometry optimizations and vibrational analyses, were performed using the GAMESS program (version 30 Sep 2018 (R3)).

Main Configurations

The changes in the weights of representative electron configurations (Figure ) along the IRC are shown in Figure . At the transition state, the weight of the reference configuration Ψ0, w 0, is 0.62, while the weights of the one-electron transfer configuration Ψ1 from the HOMO of butadiene to the LUMO of ethylene and the one-electron transfer configuration Ψ2 from the HOMO of ethylene to the LUMO of butadiene, w 1 and w 2, are 0.14 and 0.13, respectively (Figure ). Thus, 89% of the total wave function of the composite system at the transition state is described by these three configurations. This result supports the validity of treating the orbital interactions at the transition state as perturbations to the reference electron configuration, as has been done in previous studies. The significant contributions of Ψ1 and Ψ2 further indicate that the transition-state electronic structure is dominated by the two directions of frontier-orbital interaction predicted by FMO theory. ,

Change in weights for main configurations along IRC for the reaction between butadiene and ethylene.

As the reaction proceeds to s = 1.0 amu1/2·bohr, the weight of Ψ0 decreases sharply to 0.27, indicating that the reference configuration is no longer the dominant one (Figure ). In contrast, the weights of Ψ1 and Ψ2, w 1 and w 2, increase to 0.17 and 0.18, respectively, before gradually decreasing at later stages of the reaction. Notably, the weight w 5 of the two-electron transfer configuration Ψ5, in which one electron is transferred from the HOMO of butadiene to the LUMO of ethylene and another electron is transferred from the HOMO of ethylene to the LUMO of butadiene in a crossed manner, increases significantly from 0.03 at the transition state to 0.10 at s = 1.0 amu1/2·bohr. This configuration becomes the largest among examined all configurations as the reaction proceeds and remains one of the major configurations even in the product region (0.12 at s = 5.8 amu1/2·bohr), where the C1–C5 and C4–C6 bonds are fully formed. The increasing importance of this configuration along the reaction coordinate is consistent with the valence-bond state correlation diagram obtained from the reactive bond orbital analysis of the Diels–Alder reaction reported by Hirao.

In addition, the weights of the three-electron transfer configurations Ψ6, involving the transfer of two electrons from the HOMO of butadiene to the LUMO of ethylene and one electron from the HOMO of ethylene to the LUMO of butadiene, and Ψ7, involving the transfer of two electrons from the HOMO of ethylene to the LUMO of butadiene and one electron from the HOMO of butadiene to the LUMO of ethylene, also become non-negligible (w 6 = 0.06 and w 7 = 0.06), indicating that higher-order electron transfer configurations contribute the electronic structure along the reaction coordinate. At s = 5.8 amu1/2·bohr, the sum of the weights of all configurations explicitly considered in the present analysis exceeds 0.69. The remaining contributions arise from a large number of higher-order excited configurations, each making only a minor contribution to the total wave function. These results indicate that the principal electronic-structure changes along the reaction coordinate are adequately represented by the configurations analyzed in the present study.

The analysis using orbitals obtained at the RHF/6–31G level shows that the weights of the major electron configurations remain qualitatively similar to those obtained at the RHF/STO-6G (see, Table S2 in Supporting Information). Thus, these results indicate that the crossed two-electron transfer configuration is not a minor contribution but becomes an important component of the wavefunction as bond formation proceeds.

Importance of the Crossed Two-Electron Transfer Configuration

The crossed two-electron configuration Ψ5 (Figure a), which becomes one of the major configurations as the reaction proceeds, yields an electron distribution equivalent to that produced by simultaneous polarization within each isolated subsystem (Figure b). However, polarization from the HOMO to the LUMO within each isolated subsystem is forbidden by symmetry along a symmetry-preserving pathway because the HOMO and LUMO possess different symmetry with respect to the preserved σ h plane. Consequently, this electronic structure cannot be reached through intrasubsystem polarization and can only be realized through mutual one-electron transfers from each subsystem to the other, which are allowed by the symmetry. In other words, crossed electron delocalization between the two subsystems provides a symmetry-allowed route to generate an electron distribution corresponding to polarization within each isolated system.

(a) Crossed two-electron transfer configuration Ψ5. Molecular orbitals with a′ symmetry (π3 and π1′) are shown in red, whereas those with a″ symmetry (π2 and π2′) are shown in blue. (b) Simultaneous polarization within each isolated subsystem.

By considering configurations in which each isolated subsystem is polarized, occupied and unoccupied orbitals localized at each of the two bond-forming sites can be obtained, enabling a localized picture of electron delocalization at each site to be depicted (Figure ). This provides a local description (I or II) of the two bond-forming events that occur concertedly (Scheme ). Accordingly, the electron configuration Ψ5 can be expressed as a superposition of these local descriptions (I + II), thereby establishing a direct connection between the configuration analysis and conventional bonding representations. The dominance of Ψ5 further indicates that the electronic structures of the subsystems evolve toward open-shell singlet or triplet-like configurations as the reaction proceeds, consistent with depiction III. These results demonstrate that intersubsystem electron transfer provides a mechanism by which symmetry-forbidden polarization is effectively realized during concerted bond formation.

Because the reaction system is asymmetric, symmetry restrictions on electron-transfer and polarization configurations are relaxed. Nevertheless, the weights of the one-electron polarization configurations from the HOMO to the LUMO within each subsystem remains negligible at the transition state (w 15 ′ and w 16 ′), indicating that such polarization is not directly realized through intrasubsystem excitation. Notably, the crossed two-electron transfer configuration Ψ5 ′ increases in importance as the reaction proceeds, similarly to the symmetric reaction system (Figure ). These results demonstrate that the crossed two-electron transfer configuration also plays a key role in the asymmetric concerted cycloaddition reaction. Although only one asymmetric reaction system was examined in the present study, the present results suggest that the role of the crossed two-electron transfer configuration is not restricted to the symmetric butadiene–ethylene system.

Conclusion

In the present study, the concerted bond formation in the cycloaddition reaction between butadiene and ethylene, a prototypical model of the Diels–Alder reaction, was investigated through an electron configuration analysis. It was found that a crossed two-electron transfer configuration, in which one electron is transferred from the HOMO of butadiene to the LUMO of ethylene and another electron is transferred from the HOMO of ethylene to the LUMO of butadiene, becomes one of the major components of the wave function as the reaction proceeds. This configuration provides a symmetry-allowed route to generate an electron distribution corresponding to polarization within each isolated subsystem. This finding offers an orbital-based interpretation of the localized bonding representations commonly used in conventional organic electron theory. The present analysis shows that the conventional frontier-orbital interactions and localized bonding representations can be understood within a common electronic-structure framework. Similar qualitative behavior was also observed for the asymmetric cycloaddition reaction between butadiene and acrolein. Further studies on more strongly asynchronous cycloaddition reactions, including Lewis-acid-catalyzed systems, are currently in progress.

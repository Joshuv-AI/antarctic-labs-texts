Laplace transform-based BEM for unsteady heat conduction in space-time anisotropic functionally graded materials with heat source
Authors: Derese Wendimu Ayitaged, Tamirat Temesgen Dufera, Mesfin Mekuria Woldaregay, Alemayehu Tamirie Deresse
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates transient heat conduction in a separable class of anisotropic functionally graded materials with spatially and temporally varying thermal properties and an internal heat source term. The governing transient heat equation with variable coefficients is first transformed into an equivalent form with constant coefficients through an appropriate variable transformation under prescribed separability conditions on the material properties. The resulting equation is then mapped into the Laplace domain to eliminate the time derivative and simplify the transient formulation. A fundamental solution in the Laplace space is employed to reformulate the transformed equation into a boundary integral representation containing source-related domain integrals, which serves as the basis for the boundary element formulation. The resulting integral equations are discretized using boundary elements, while the domain integrals are evaluated numerically through domain discretization in conjunction with particular solutions. The transient solution in the physical time domain is recovered by applying the Stehfest numerical inversion technique for the Laplace transform. Several benchmark test problems with known analytical solutions are presented to assess the accuracy, convergence, and stability of the proposed approach. Numerical results demonstrate that the developed boundary element formulation provides accurate and consistent solutions for transient heat conduction problems in anisotropic functionally graded materials satisfying the prescribed transformation conditions.Keywords: Functionally graded materials, Anisotropic, Boundary integral equations, Boundary domain integral equations, Boundary element method, Heat sourceSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Heat conduction is a critical phenomenon in many sectors, including engineering, manufacturing, energy systems, electronics, etc. Optimizing performance, safety, and energy efficiency in these industries requires an understanding of the ability to control heat conduction. To avoid overheating and guarantee consistent performance, effective heat conduction is essential in the design and operation of electronic devices and components in the semiconductor and electronics industries1,2. Heat conduction is important for solar thermal systems, nuclear reactors, geothermal plants, electricity generation and distribution, and heat exchanger management.

Heat conduction in anisotropic materials plays a significant role in many areas of science and engineering3,4. Similar problems also arise in modeling groundwater flow5,6. These phenomena necessitate the development of advanced mathematical models and computational techniques to accurately predict behavior under varying conditions. Understanding the intricacies of heat transfer and fluid dynamics in such materials can lead to innovations in thermal management and resource extraction. Grasping and managing heat conduction in the material is essential for enhancing efficiency, ensuring safety, and improving overall energy performance. The conductivity of these materials may vary across space and time due to different factors. It can depend on temperature, pressure, or specific material properties7,8. One example of this type of material is a functionally graded material (FGM).

Functionally graded materials are primarily engineered materials designed to fulfill specific practical performance requirements. Functionally graded materials are a class of inhomogeneous materials whose properties vary smoothly across space. These spatially varying properties can include characteristics like thermal conductivity, hardness, toughness, ductility, and corrosion resistance9. Also, functionally graded materials are materials whose properties change over time and space based on a defined mathematical function. In many of these cases, exact analytical solutions are available only for a few simplified scenarios. Therefore, numerical methods are commonly employed to generate approximate solutions.

According to a particular continuous function, the material under examination has features that vary spatially, represented by the variable coefficients, which are spatially continuous functions. A functionally graded material is one such type of material. FGMs are mainly artificial materials that are produced to meet the present practical performance. FGMs are defined as materials that are inhomogeneous and have properties that change spatially in a continuous manner, such as thermal conductivity, hardness, toughness, ductility, and corrosion resistance. In many of these problems, analytical solutions are limited to only a few idealized cases. As a result, numerical techniques are often used to obtain approximate solutions. These techniques allow engineers and scientists to model complex behaviors and predict the performance of FGMs under various conditions. As research continues, the development of more sophisticated algorithms and computational methods will further enhance our understanding and application of these innovative materials.

For such materials, the boundary element method (BEM) and other numerical approaches have been successfully used to solve the associated problems. But the formulation of BEM for solving nonlinear, non-homogeneous problems and problems with variable coefficients usually adopts fundamental solutions for a simplified linear, homogeneous problem with constant coefficients, generating domain integrals in the corresponding integral equation. This feature makes the BEM less attractive, as a domain discretization is then required. Two main techniques are commonly used to handle inhomogeneous media. The first involves deriving an appropriate Green’s function or fundamental solution for the functionally graded material (FGM) problem. The second technique is to transform the variable-coefficient governing equation into an equivalent constant-coefficient equation. Specifically, a space-time dependent coefficient equation is reduced to a time-dependent coefficient equation. This transformation facilitates the derivation of a boundary integral equation, which can then be applied to the Helmholtz equation for anisotropic functionally graded materials10. The spatially variable diffusivity and wave number coefficients are part of the governing equation. The diffusivity coefficient displays the material’s anisotropy. Quadratic, exponential, and trigonometric functions are the three categories of gradation functions that are taken into consideration. To derive a boundary integral equation, the variable coefficient controlling equation is converted to a constant coefficient equation. Additionally, the boundary integral equation is converted into a conventional BEM in order to obtain numerical solutions.

The boundary knot method (BKM), in conjunction with the dual reciprocity technique, was used to solve the three-dimensional transient heat conduction problem studied by Fu et al.15. The transient heat conduction equation was changed into a non-homogeneous modified Helmholtz equation using the finite difference approach for the temporal derivative. The ability of functionally graded materials to achieve spatially varying material properties, which enhances thermal resistance and structural performance under hard operating conditions, has garnered significant attention in thermal engineering. Heat conduction is frequently transient and anisotropic, with material properties changing in both space and time, in many real-world applications, including thermal barrier coatings, aerospace structures, and high-temperature mechanical components. The accurate numerical simulation of heat transfer in FGMs is difficult due to these complexities.

Numerous numerical methods for resolving transient and nonlinear partial differential equations that arise in heat transfer analysis have been developed to address such issues. Researchers continue to refine these approaches to enhance accuracy and computational efficiency in complex thermal systems. Due to their adaptability in solving general governing equations, classical domain-based techniques like the finite difference method, finite element method, and finite volume method have been extensively utilized. To enhance the convergence behavior, stability, and computational efficiency, more sophisticated numerical techniques have recently been proposed, such as hybrid block methods, Hermite spline collocation techniques, and effective schemes for fractional partial differential equations. For instance, Hermite spline methods have demonstrated high accuracy in solving nonlinear wave and reaction-diffusion equations, while hybrid block methods have been successfully applied to convective heat transfer models with temperature-dependent internal heat generation. Similarly, robust alternatives for transient nonlinear systems have been made possible by computationally efficient numerical techniques for multidimensional time-fractional equations29–32.

The majority of these methods rely on domain discretization, which can become computationally costly for transient heat conduction problems involving anisotropic and functionally graded materials with variable coefficients, even though they have proven successful for numerous applications. Because it lowers the problem’s dimensionality and improves boundary condition treatment efficiency, the boundary element method (BEM) offers an alluring substitute in these circumstances. However, the existence of variable material properties and internal heat source terms makes it difficult to apply BEM to transient anisotropic heat conduction in functionally graded media. Researchers are investigating sophisticated numerical techniques and hybrid approaches that successfully integrate BEM with other strategies, like finite element methods (FEM), in order to address these issues. It might be feasible to obtain more precise solutions while preserving computational efficiency by utilizing the advantages of both approaches. These novel approaches open the door to better material design and optimization in engineering applications, in addition to improving the modeling of complex thermal behaviors. The application of these techniques could result in major improvements in thermal management systems across a range of industries as research advances.

Efficient computational methods for resolving thermal problems involving variable material properties, internal heat sources, and moving boundaries have been developed as a result of recent developments in numerical heat transfer analysis. For instance, wavelet-based numerical formulations have been successfully applied to investigate thermal performance in porous fins under local thermal non-equilibrium conditions with temperature-dependent thermal conductivity and surface emissivity, demonstrating improved computational efficiency and accuracy33. Similarly, iterative Legendre wavelet Galerkin finite element approaches have been developed for two-dimensional heat transfer analysis in elliptic and plate fins, providing accurate solutions for complex thermal systems34. Advanced numerical treatments have also been proposed for moving fin problems with size-dependent thermal conductivity and temperature-dependent internal heat generation, highlighting the effectiveness of hybrid wavelet-based approaches for nonlinear thermal analysis35. In addition, numerical investigations of Stefan problems with convection have shown the importance of robust numerical formulations for accurately capturing moving phase-change boundaries36. Wavelet Galerkin finite element methods have also been employed for dual phase lag heat transfer models in microwave drying applications, further demonstrating the versatility of modern numerical techniques in transient heat transfer modeling37. These developments underline the increasing need for accurate and efficient numerical approaches for transient heat conduction in complex media. Motivated by these advances, the present study develops a Laplace transform-based Boundary Element formulation for transient heat conduction in anisotropic functionally graded materials with space-and time-dependent thermal properties and internal heat generation.

Recent progress in computational heat transfer and applied numerical analysis has produced increasingly robust methods for solving heat conduction problems involving variable coefficients, nonlinearities, and complex geometries. A computational model based on the Dual Reciprocity Boundary Element Method (DRBEM) has been developed to investigate magneto-thermo-elastic stress sensitivity in nonlinear time-dependent anisotropic functionally graded materials (FGMs), accounting for both conduction and radiation heat transfer mechanisms38. To address three-dimensional coupled dynamic thermoelastic fracture in anisotropic functionally graded materials, a novel time-stepping boundary element method framework has been proposed, providing an efficient numerical tool for solving complex thermoelastic fracture problems39.

The fractional evolution equation is transformed into the Laplace domain and reformulated as a modified Helmholtz equation, whose fundamental solution provides an exact boundary integral representation40. This formulation yields a boundary-only discretization, thereby reducing the spatial dimensionality of the problem and achieving high numerical accuracy in unbounded, semi-infinite, and strongly heterogeneous domains. A fractional dual-phase-lag bioheat framework has been proposed for nanoparticle-assisted photothermal heating in layered skin, incorporating temporal memory effects, phase lags, temperature-dependent perfusion, and nanoparticle-induced volumetric heating under Gaussian laser irradiation to improve the accuracy of transient bioheat transfer modeling41. A novel multiphysics boundary element method framework has recently been proposed for modeling heat-induced fracture in porous polymer composites by simultaneously incorporating pyrolysis-induced thermal sources, fractional-order heat conduction, and size-dependent thermoelasticity within a unified boundary element formulation. The Laplace-domain governing equations are solved through a fully discretized boundary scheme and numerically inverted to obtain the corresponding transient solutions, demonstrating the effectiveness of boundary-based multiphysics modeling for complex thermo-mechanical interactions42.

Further studies have highlighted the importance of stable and efficient computational methods for modeling transient heat transfer in advanced engineering systems, particularly where anisotropy, variable thermal properties, and internal source effects significantly influence the thermal field evolution. These developments emphasize the continuing demand for accurate numerical techniques capable of treating transient heat conduction in anisotropic functionally graded materials with space- and time-dependent properties. Motivated by these challenges, the present work develops a Laplace transform-based Boundary Element formulation combined with Stehfest numerical inversion to solve transient heat conduction problems with variable thermal coefficients and internal heat sources, providing an efficient computational framework for this class of problems.

Using the Laplace transform method, this study develops a boundary element formulation for transient heat conduction in space-time dependent anisotropic functionally graded materials. The proposed approach incorporates continuously varying material properties directly into the governing equations without requiring domain discretization, unlike conventional boundary element methods that are typically limited to homogeneous or piece-wise homogeneous media. A boundary integral representation is consistently coupled with the Laplace transform framework to treat temporal evolution efficiently, avoiding the numerical difficulties associated with time-stepping schemes.

The method extends the applicability of BEM to more realistic engineering materials exhibiting anisotropy, functional grading, and time-dependent coefficients. Compared with existing Laplace-transform-based and standard BEM formulations, it provides a more general and computationally efficient framework capable of capturing complex thermal responses. In addition, the formulation offers a unified treatment of transient heat conduction in anisotropic functionally graded media, addressing a limitation in much of the existing literature, which is largely restricted to constant or weakly varying material properties.

The originality of this work lies in the incorporation of space-time dependent material properties and internal heat generation within a transient anisotropic heat conduction model. A time-dependent coefficient reduction technique is employed to transform the governing equation with variable coefficients into an equivalent constant-coefficient form, thereby simplifying the mathematical formulation and enabling the application of the Laplace transform-boundary element method (LT-BEM). By combining this reduction technique with the Laplace-domain boundary integral formulation, the proposed approach efficiently handles transient problems with space-time varying coefficients while preserving the advantages of a boundary-discretization framework.

Conclusions

A Laplace transform-based boundary element method is developed for transient heat conduction in anisotropic functionally graded materials with space-time-dependent properties and internal heat sources. The governing equation is transformed into an equivalent constant-coefficient form, reformulated as a boundary integral equation, and solved using boundary discretization. The time-domain response is recovered via Stehfest numerical inversion.

Numerical results demonstrate high accuracy, stability, and efficiency, with excellent agreement with analytical solutions and relative errors on the order of . The formulation reduces computational effort by avoiding domain discretization and provides a robust framework for analyzing transient heat conduction in heterogeneous anisotropic media.

Transforming corneal alkali burn treatment: unveiling mechanisms and pioneering therapies from bench to bedside
Authors: Mengzhen Xie, Ying Jie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCorneal alkali burns are severe ocular injuries characterized by extensive tissue damage, inflammation, oxidative stress, and neovascularization, which often lead to long-term visual impairment and corneal fibrosis. This review comprehensively examines the mechanisms underlying alkali burn injuries, including the roles of inflammatory mediators, oxidative stress, and cellular responses, while highlighting current and emerging therapeutic approaches. Traditional treatments, such as corticosteroids and surgical interventions, often have limited efficacy and significant side effects. Recent advances in innovative therapies, including stem cell-derived exosomes, hydrogel-based drug delivery systems, and herbal components, demonstrate significant potential for improving corneal healing and reducing complications. These novel approaches aim to mitigate inflammation, enhance epithelial repair, and prevent neovascularization, offering promising pathways for scar-free healing and the restoration of corneal transparency. Future research should focus on integrating these therapies into multifunctional treatment strategies to optimize clinical outcomes and improve quality of life for patients suffering from corneal alkali burns.Keywords: Corneal alkali burn, Neovascularization, Oxidative stress, Inflammation, Fibrosis

Introduction

Ocular surface chemical injury is a common type of ocular emergency that can potentially cause significant and permanent visual impairment. Ocular surface chemical injury was responsible for 0.1–15% of all ocular emergency presentations, with an estimated incidence of 5.1–50 per 100,000 population-year. The demographic most affected includes young, working-age adult males, reflecting occupational and environmental exposure risks. Beyond the immediate threat to vision, these injuries have severe ramifications for patients’ quality of life, often exacerbating mental health issues such as anxiety and depression [1].

The pathophysiology of alkali burns is complex, involving a cascade of processes such as saponification of cell membranes, epithelial necrosis, and disruption of the corneal microenvironment [2, 3]. These events trigger an acute inflammatory response, oxidative stress, and neovascularization, culminating in fibrosis and loss of corneal transparency [4]. If untreated, this pathology can lead to persistent epithelial defects, corneal melting, and perforation, imposing significant challenges in ophthalmic care [5].

Conventional treatments, including irrigation [6]corticosteroids [7]and surgical interventions [8]aim to mitigate damage but often fall short due to limited efficacy and the potential for adverse effects. As a result, there is an urgent need for novel, multifaceted therapeutic strategies to address the complexity of alkali burns. Recent advances in stem cell-derived therapies [26]innovative drug delivery systems [9]and bioactive compounds [10] hold promise for improving clinical outcomes. These approaches emphasize not only healing but also minimizing complications such as neovascularization and scarring.

This review explores the underlying mechanisms of corneal alkali burns, evaluates current therapeutic paradigms, and highlights innovative approaches that may redefine the management of these challenging injuries. The integration of emerging therapies into comprehensive, patient-centric strategies could transform the prognosis for individuals suffering from this debilitating condition.

Establishment of animal model of alkali burn

Sodium Hydroxide-Induced animal models in experimental research

The establishment of corneal alkali burn (CAB) animal models is of great significance for advancing the understanding of the pathological mechanisms of burn injuries, evaluating the efficacy of new therapies, and exploring the application of innovative materials. Commonly used animal models for CAB include rats [11–14]mice [15–18]Japanese white rabbits [19]and New Zealand white rabbits [2, 20–23].Differences in animal sex [24] and strains (such as BALB/c and C57BL/6) [25] can directly impact the pathological characteristics of corneal wound healing.

In the establishment of the mouse alkali burn model, the filter papers used are mostly circular, with the burn area covering the central cornea, the limbus, or the entire cornea. The filter paper sizes commonly used are 2 mm [2, 15, 16, 18, 21, 22, 26–35]2.5 mm [36–39]and 3 mm [40, 41]. These filter papers are typically soaked in 1 N NaOH solution, either with a fixed or variable volume, although some experiments have used lower concentrations such as 0.5 N NaOH [31]0.25 N NaOH [42]or 0.15 N NaOH [34, 41]. The soaking time in the NaOH solution varies from 5 s to 30 s, with 20 to 30 s being the most common. After soaking, the filter paper is placed on the central cornea for 20 to 60 s. Following the alkali burn, most experiments perform irrigation with saline or phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 1 min, with some experiments using 2 minutes [33] or 10 seconds [32]. The volume of irrigation fluid generally ranges from 10 mL to 25 mL, with 20 mL being the most common.

In the establishment of alkali burn models in rats, the filter paper diameter ranges from 3 mm to 6 mm [9,12–14,43−51], with 3 mm being the most commonly used [12–14,45−47], followed by 4 mm [48–51]. The majority of experiments use a concentration of 1 N NaOH, and the soaking time for the filter paper generally varies from 10 s to 1 min, depending on the experimental design. The duration for which the filter paper is placed on the cornea typically ranges from 15 s to 45 s. Most studies utilize saline or isotonic saline for rinsing, with a usual rinsing time of 1 min, though some experiments extend the rinsing duration to 5 minutes [43]. The volume of rinsing fluid typically ranges from 10 mL to 60 mL.

In the establishment of the rabbit alkali burn model, the commonly used filter paper diameters are 6–8 mm. The concentration of NaOH typically used is 1N [19, 52, 53] or 2 N [32, 54–56]though some studies have employed 0.5 N [57] or 2.5 N [23] NaOH. The duration of filter paper application on the cornea ranges from 10 s to 60 s, with 20 to 60 s being more common. The irrigation time after the burn varies from 1 minute [52, 57]3 minutes [23, 56] to 15 minutes [32, 54] or longer time [55]with most experiments using saline for rinsing. The volume of saline used for rinsing is generally large, often 60 mL or more.

Researchers should establish an appropriate CAB model based on their experimental objectives. Villabona et al. [58] suggested that for a rabbit CAB model, using a 5 mm diameter Whatman #1 filter paper soaked with 30 µL NaOH solution for 15 s, followed by rinsing with balanced salt solution, could be suitable. If the goal is to induce fibrosis limited to the epithelium and stroma, exposure to 0.5–0.6 N NaOH for 15 s may be the most appropriate option. However, if full-thickness corneal fibrosis along with Descemet’s membrane and endothelial damage is needed, but with reduced incidence of persistent epithelial defects (PED) and corneal neovascularization (CoNV), 0.75 N NaOH may be the optimal choice. For full-thickness fibrosis accompanied by a higher incidence of PED and CoNV, 1 N NaOH would be the most suitable concentration.

Using a larger volume of NaOH on the filter paper can result in more severe damage, and even lower NaOH concentrations may lead to endothelial damage. Similarly, longer exposure times and larger filter paper diameters can exacerbate stromal damage. Under 1 N NaOH conditions, as the level of NaOH exposure increases, CoNV becomes more common and severe. For studies requiring CoNV induction, using 1 N NaOH and extending the exposure time beyond 15 s might be the optimal approach [58].

Sex is generally not a primary determinant of outcomes in alkali burn models. For instance, in a rabbit CAB model (0.5 N NaOH), early wound healing parameters showed no significant sex-based differences, indicating sex is not a major biological variable in early-stage healing [59].

Silver Nitrate-Induced alkali burn model

Beyond NaOH solution, a commonly employed alternative for establishing corneal alkali burn models in both rats [60–65] and mice [66, 67] involves chemical cauterization using silver nitrate applicator sticks (typically composed of 75% silver nitrate and 25% potassium nitrate) applied to the central cornea. Notably, models induced by silver nitrate consistently exhibit pronounced neovascularization compared to standard alkali injuries. Consequently, research utilizing this approach predominantly focuses on elucidating the mechanisms underlying post-alkali burn neovascularization or evaluating therapeutic interventions targeting pathological angiogenesis.

Clinical evaluation in human and animal studies

The Roper Hall classification [68] and Dua classification [69] are commonly used clinical grading systems for CAB(Table 1). The Roper Hall system evaluates burns based on the degree of corneal haze and the extent of limbal ischemia. In contrast, the Dua classification improves upon this by incorporating the extent of limbal involvement in clock hours and the percentage of conjunctival involvement. Additionally, the Dua system features an analogue scale for documenting clinical status and grading ocular surface burns. Gupta N et al. demonstrated that the Dua classification provides superior prognostic guidance in cases of ocular surface burns, particularly in more accurately reflecting clinical conditions when dealing with conjunctival involvement. Compared to the Roper Hall classification, the Dua system offers enhanced predictive value. Its simplicity and ease of use make it a more reliable tool for managing and predicting outcomes in ocular surface burns [70]. Mechanisms underlying the onset and progression of alkali burn

Overview of corneal alkali burn mechanisms

Acid burns typically lead to tissue degeneration and coagulation, forming a barrier that prevents further acid penetration. In contrast, alkaline substances, due to their lipophilic nature, saponify fatty acids in cell membranes, causing membrane rupture and dissolution, allowing them to quickly penetrate the corneal tissue [2, 3]. Fat and proteins in the eye dissolve rapidly under the action of alkali, leading to deep tissue damage [76].The cations formed during the dissociation of alkali interact with the carboxyl groups of glycosaminoglycans in the cornea, leading to immediate corneal opacity through a hydration process [77]. Additionally, the accumulation of leukocytes and the release of various proteases and free radicals further contribute to corneal collagen dissolution, ultimately resulting in corneal ulcers [78]. Early chemical damage can also cause tissue contraction and destruction of the trabecular meshwork and aqueous outflow pathways, while subsequent chronic inflammation may lead to adhesions and angle closure, further contributing to secondary intraocular pressure(IOP) elevation [79].

The healing process of chemical corneal burns can be divided into four stages: initial, acute, early repair, and late repair [51]. The destructive effects during the acute phase can result in slow epithelial regeneration, persistent ulcers, corneal perforation, and neovascularization. The most critical factor in these complications is uncontrolled inflammation during the acute phase, which plays a key role throughout the healing process [80, 81]. Therefore, anti-inflammatory, anti-angiogenic, and epithelial healing treatments are essential during the acute phase.

Once alkaline chemicals penetrate the cornea, oxidative stress is induced, characterized by increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production [19, 26, 82]. It has been reported that oxidative stress occurs immediately after alkali burns, preceding the inflammatory response. In alkali-induced damage, excessive oxidative stress leads to the oxidation of macromolecules and lipid peroxidation in cell membranes, resulting in an imbalance between antioxidants and pro-oxidants in the corneal tissue. These factors cause high levels of oxidative stress, ultimately leading to excessive inflammation, scar formation, and CoNV [26, 83, 84]. CoNV, similar to oxidative stress, also plays a key role in the pathophysiology of alkali burns by increasing vascular permeability, exacerbating inflammation, chronic edema, lipid exudation, and corneal scarring, which may result in permanent vision loss [85, 86].

The corneal repair process after alkali burns occurs in four overlapping stages [2]. First, during inflammation and keratocyte activation, neutrophils and macrophages release inflammatory factors that induce the transformation of keratocytes into fibroblasts to promote repair. Next, in the stage of stromal myofibroblast hyperactivation and fibrosis, corneal injury exposes growth factors such as TGF-β, leading to the transformation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts, which become overactivated and secrete excessive irregular extracellular matrix proteins, resulting in fibrosis and opacity. Then, during limbal epithelial stem cells (LESCs) activation and stromal remodeling, LESCs proliferate and migrate to repair the epithelium, while myofibroblasts undergo apoptosis, with stromal inflammation and remodeling being associated with matrix metalloproteinases (MMP) expression. Finally, in the stage of epithelial recovery and clarity restoration, effective treatment restores transparency, whereas untreated cases may lead to fibrosis and long-term opacity [87–89](Fig. 1).

Inflammatory response following alkali burns

Oxidative stress, inflammation and neovascularization are the main causes of corneal injury after alkali burn [26](Fig. 2). The progression of alkali burn injuries can be divided into four stages: the immediate stage (0–24 h), during which the direct consequences of chemical damage trigger pathological responses; the acute stage (1–7 days), characterized by amplified inflammation, neovascularization, and re-epithelialization; the early repair stage (8–21 days), when inflammation transitions from acute to chronic and is associated with stromal fibrosis; and the late repair stage (> 21 days), where the cornea undergoes repeated attempts at remodeling and healing [90].

After corneal chemical injury, infiltrating inflammatory cells and activated corneal stromal cells secrete numerous pro-inflammatory cytokines and angiogenic molecules, including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, MMP-2, MMP-9, MMP-13, TGF-β1, CD54(ICAM-1), monocyte chemoattractant protein (MCP)-1, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), VEGF receptor 1 (VEGFR1) and VEGR2, with elevated levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 particularly prominent, which leads to corneal ulceration or neovascularization [32, 35, 43, 45, 51, 56, 91–95]. Inflammation plays a critical role in the pathological progression of CAB. Under the influence of these increased cytokines, polymorphonuclear leukocytes, macrophages, monocytes, and lymphocytes infiltrate the corneal tissue, and compared to the control group, an increase in apoptotic cells is observed [37, 51, 91]. Polymorphonuclear leukocytes release large amounts of proteases, which degrade extracellular matrix components, causing corneal ulceration and eventual vision loss [96]. Pro-inflammatory cytokine TNF-α is produced by activated macrophages and monocytes [97]. Studies have shown that TNF-α begins to be highly expressed within 24 h after CAB [98]. However, other studies report contrary findings, showing that TNF-α mRNA remains at baseline levels during the first 24 h after chemical alkali burn [72, 99]. Increased expression of TNF-α in corneal alkali injury can trigger apoptosis in both corneal and retinal tissues [80, 98, 100, 101].Topical infliximab, an anti-TNF-α monoclonal antibody, has been shown to effectively penetrate both the cornea and conjunctiva without toxicity. This treatment significantly decreased corneal perforation, opacity, leukocyte infiltration, and eyelid fibrosis. Additionally, infliximab prevented goblet cell infiltration in the epithelial cornea and the loss of goblet cells in the conjunctiva, enhanced tear production, and facilitated epithelial repair. Furthermore, infliximab notably reduced both corneal hemangiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis [102]. Targeting macrophage activation and recruitment may offer a promising therapeutic approach for mitigating corneal scarring following alkali burns [103].

Pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6 are produced by infiltrating leukocytes in the corneal limbus [72, 99]. IL-1β is a crucial pro-inflammatory mediator in the host response to alkali burns, produced by native corneal fibroblasts and infiltrating neutrophils [104]. Studies have shown that IL-1β stimulates the production of inflammatory cytokines in corneal fibroblasts and mediates collagen degradation by inducing the release of MMP [105, 106]. Elevated levels of IL-1β have been found in the tears of patients with corneal perforation or recurrent corneal erosion [107]. IL-1β is involved in the process of CoNV [108–110]. It activates intracellular signaling pathways through binding to its receptor IL-1R1, leading to the activation of downstream signaling molecules, including TAK1, MAPK, AP-1, and transcription factors such as nuclear factor κB (NF-κB ) [111, 112].IL-1β receptor antagonists have been shown to provide resistance to corneal inflammation [113]. As a natural antagonist of IL-1, IL-1ra treatment of eyes 14 days after alkali burn maintained corneal transparency with minimal neovascular invasion [114]. Compared to the control group, eyes treated with IL-1ra exhibited significantly reduced corneal damage and cell infiltration. Additionally, the levels of cytokines, including IL-6 and IL-10, were significantly lower in IL-1ra-treated eyes between days 3 and 7 [114]. Two weeks after alkali injury, the mRNA expression of IL-1ra, IL-1α, and IL-1β was upregulated in the cornea, along with the infiltration of macrophages and neutrophils [108, 114]. In a study by Lu et al., IL-1 receptor antagonist (IL-1ra) knockout (KO) mice showed more significant macrophage infiltration compared to wild-type (WT) mice. IL-1ra KO mice also exhibited enhanced intraocular mRNA expression of VEGF and inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) after injury. Moreover, IL-1α and IL-1β further amplified the expression of VEGF and iNOS. IL-1ra KO mice showed enhanced alkali-induced CoNV through increased macrophage infiltration and elevated VEGF and iNOS expression [108].Upon exposure to IL-8, excessive neutrophils undergo morphological changes, migrate to the site of injury, and release active products, including pro-inflammatory factors and MMPs, which promote the spread of inflammation and collagen degradation [115].

In recent years, transcriptomic and proteomic analyses have revealed differential expression of 41 genes in neovascularized corneas, including 15 upregulated genes and 26 downregulated genes. qRT-PCR and Western blot analyses further confirmed that LCP1 and CSRP2 were significantly upregulated in corneas following alkali burn injury, while TGM2 and TGF-β were markedly downregulated [17]. Previous studies have shown that CAB damage all cellular components and lead to extensive infiltration of inflammatory cells, predominantly neutrophils, into the corneal tissue. Following the injury, arachidonic acid (AA) is rapidly released, and the synthesis of lipid mediators (e.g., platelet-activating factor) is involved in the inflammatory response and wound healing process [116, 117].Alkali burn induces a substantial production of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) by neutrophils, which in turn exacerbates the inflammatory response and promotes CoNV [33, 118].Lipoxin A4 (LXA4), a lipid mediator derived from arachidonic acid (AA), exhibits anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving properties. It plays a crucial role in mitigating excessive tissue damage and chronic inflammation by modulating components of the innate and adaptive immune systems, including neutrophils, macrophages, and T cells [119, 120]. Previous studies in corneal injury models have reported that exogenous administration of LXA4 can inhibit inflammatory cell infiltration, promote epithelial and endothelial wound healing, and contribute to the reparative effects of epidermal growth factor (EGF) [121–123].

In the CAB model, exposure to alkali induces the activation of p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling, which in turn activates the downstream effector MAPK-activated protein kinase-2 (MK2). This pathway selectively enhances the production of IL-6, IL-1β, MIP-1α, ICAM-1, and VCAM-1, significantly increasing leukocyte accumulation [124, 125]. Additionally, alkali-induced inflammation in the cornea involves signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) signaling, whose pharmacological inhibition may reduce macrophage infiltration by decreasing MCP-1 production, thereby downregulating the expression of VEGF, MMP-2, MMP-9, and TGF-β1 [126].

Researchers have also identified the activation of the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) as a mediator of the inflammatory response in CAB, as rapamycin (a well-known mTOR inhibitor) was found to inhibit leukocyte infiltration and reduce the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines [127–129]. CAB studies have further revealed upstream signals integrated into the mTOR pathway, such as the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)/protein kinase B (AKT) and extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) 1/2 axes, which promote mTOR-dependent protein synthesis [127, 128]. Correspondingly, the IL-1/IL-1 receptor I (IL-1RI)/ERK signaling cascade mediates alkali-induced inflammatory responses, as its pharmacological inhibition reduces leukocyte recruitment and lowers the levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines [130].The NOD-like receptor family pyrin domain-containing 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome pathway is a key signaling pathway in CAB [109, 110]. NLRP3-dependent caspase-1 cleavage and the production of interleukin (IL)-1β are critical for the progression of the disease [88, 131].Ferroptosis, a regulated form of cell death, has been identified as playing a critical role in the pathogenesis of CAB injury, and it can be inhibited by insulin (INS) [5, 45].Apoptosis signal-regulating kinase 1-interacting protein 1(AIP1) expression is significantly reduced following alkali burns, while NLRP3 and VEGFa expression are markedly increased, exacerbating CoNV and inflammation. Compared to wild-type mice, AIP1 knockout mice exhibit more severe corneal neovascularization and inflammatory responses, significantly elevated ROS levels, enhanced NLRP3 activity, and suppressed NLRP6 expression. Overexpression of AIP1 significantly reduces neovascularization and inflammation, inhibits the expression of NOX4 and NLRP3, decreases VEGFa production, and alleviates inflammation by restoring NLRP6 balance. AIP1 suppresses CoNV and inflammation by regulating the NOX4-ROS-NLRP3 inflammatory pathway [27].

Neovascularization and fibrosis induced by alkali burns

Infections, trauma, and the loss of corneal limbal stem cell barriers are pathological conditions that can lead to CoNV, extending from the limbal region to the vascularized cornea [132].In the early stages of CAB, IL-6 expression is strongly induced, and IL-6 can promote the expression of VEGF [133]. Keratocytes are capable of directly degrading type I collagen and creating stromal spaces, which promote CoNV through VEGFc/VEGFr3-induced MMP13 expression [134]. It is well-established that MMP-2 and MMP-9 are closely associated with CoNV [135]. Myofibroblasts, which are highly contractile cells expressing α-smooth muscle actin (αSMA) [136]can synthesize and deposit large quantities of extracellular matrix (ECM) components, particularly collagen types I and III [137]. This alters collagen fibril diameter and disrupts stromal structure. Under normal conditions, the cornea does not contain myofibroblasts; these cells emerge from resident keratocytes after injury in response to TGF-β1 released by epithelial cells [138].The process can be described as the phenotypic differentiation of keratocytes in the corneal stroma into fibroblast-like and myofibroblast-like cells, following the sequence keratocytes → fibroblasts → myofibroblasts [139].

Senescent corneal fibroblasts exhibit increased synthesis and secretion of matrix-degrading enzymes (MMP-2, MMP-3, MMP-14) and angiogenic factors, such as VEGF, while showing decreased expression of anti-angiogenic factors (such as pigment epithelium-derived factor and thrombospondins). These changes support the proliferation, migration, and tube formation of vascular endothelial cells. Intrastromal injection of prematurely senescent fibroblasts induced earlier CoNV than normal fibroblasts, while MMP inhibitors blocked the early onset of CoNV induced by senescent cells. Therefore, senescent fibroblasts promote alkali-induced CoNV, partially through enhanced secretion of MMP [140].Growth factors, including TGF-β, α-SMA, and MMP-9, play dual roles in corneal wound healing. On one hand, the sustained activity of these growth factor-induced myofibroblasts leads to chronic tissue contraction, structural disorganization, and corneal opacity. On the other hand, they are essential for the renewal and metabolism of epithelial cells [88]. Excessive monocytes and macrophages can differentiate into fibroblasts, thereby aggravating fibrosis during the healing process [141].The elevated expression of αSMA, along with TGF-β-induced myofibroblast differentiation and type I collagen secretion, and IL-1β-mediated apoptosis of resident fibroblasts following corneal injury, collectively contribute to fibrosis [142].

In a Beagle dog CAB model, corneal fibrotic markers, such as αSMA, were found to be elevated [143]. In horses, TGF-β induces the differentiation of corneal fibroblasts into myofibroblasts [144]. These cells are thought to secrete inflammatory cytokines, growth factors, and chemokines, exacerbating the inflammatory response and promoting cell infiltration in corneal tissue. While these differentiated cells are effective in normal wound healing, they are also known to contribute to abnormal corneal scar formation [145, 146]. 5-amino-4-imidazole carboxamide riboside-1-β-d-ribofuranoside (AICAR), an activator of 5′-adenosine monophosphate-activated protein kinase (AMPK), has shown antifibrotic potential. However, AICAR-mediated AMPK activation significantly suppresses TGF-β1 and ECM protein expression. These antifibrotic effects of AICAR are AMPK-dependent [147]. ZEB1, an epithelial-mesenchymal transition transcription factor, regulates NF-κB and Stat3 pathways, promoting IL-1β, TNF-α, and TGF-β expression. In a CAB model, Zeb1+/- mice had fewer CD45 + leukocytes and Arg1 + macrophages compared to Zeb1+/+ mice (wild-type, WT). ZEB1 forms a feedback loop with TGF-β to aid repair, and its loss impairs healing and causes chronic inflammation [30].In a CAB model, the healing process is accompanied by increased inflammation and fibrosis/scarring. The prognosis of CAB is closely related to ocular surface inflammation as well as corneal and eyelid scarring and fibrosis [102]. PPAR-γ, known for its strong anti-fibrotic properties in corneal and other tissues, blocks αSMA induction when activated by its ligands [148]. Inhibition of CoNV, inflammatory cytokines, and MMPs helps to reduce damage associated with CAB [95].

Recent studies strongly support the role of corneal nerves in corneal opacity development. The corneal epithelium, supplied by a dense network of sensory nerve endings, secretes growth and neurotrophic factors as well as cytokines to nourish nearby cells. In turn, sensory nerve terminals release neuropeptides that inhibit inflammation and promote epithelial wound healing [149]. When the cornea loses its sensory nerve supply, immune cells become trapped within the cornea and compromise its clarity [150, 151]. Therefore, corneal nerve innervation is crucial for maintaining normal corneal homeostasis, as it plays a key role in regulating corneal sensation, epithelial integrity, proliferation, and wound healing [152, 153].

The NF-κB signaling pathway is a critical step in the transcriptional overexpression of pro-inflammatory and pro-angiogenic factors, including IL-1β, cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), and VEGF-A. IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α regulate COX-2, with all of these factors being overexpressed during inflammation [154, 155]. In the damaged cornea, p65 serves as an important marker for the overexpression of VEGF-A, IL-1β, and COX-2 [156]. Corneal alkali injuries upregulate NF-κB, IL-1β, and COX-2 expression, while significantly increasing VEGF and its receptors VEGFR1 and VEGFR2 in endothelial cells [156]. When VEGFR2 is activated by VEGF-A binding and tyrosine phosphorylation, angiogenesis is initiated. Continuous activation of downstream pathways, such as p38 MAPK and ERK1/2, leads to robust mitogenic and survival processes [157, 158]. A key mediator of alkali-induced CoNV is VEGF, primarily produced by macrophages [132, 159, 160]. While VEGF levels peak in the acute phase of corneal healing and gradually subside over the following weeks, progressive CoNV independent of VEGF expression suggests that other pro-angiogenic factors play roles in the formation and maintenance of new blood vessels [72]. Notably, anti-VEGF treatments in CAB models have not completely eradicated CoNV. Interestingly, although anti-VEGF treatment is more effective in suppressing CoNV within seven days after CAB compared to tacrolimus (a potent anti-inflammatory agent), the latter results in reduced CoNV levels at the one-month follow-up [161]. Developing therapies that target both VEGF and IL-1β has emerged as a major strategy for treating CoNV and corneal inflammation [162].

Oxidative stress induced by alkali burns

Kubota et al. identified reactive oxygen species (ROS) in alkali burns, linking ROS to corneal CoNV [163]. ROS originates primarily from mitochondrial electron transport chain leakage, with NADPH oxidase and xanthine oxidase as secondary sources [82]. In rat CAB models, elevated ROS reduces mitochondrial membrane potential, releases cytochrome C, and disrupts intracellular balance [164]. As a secondary messenger, ROS activates NF-κB to trigger inflammation, promoting cytokine release and VEGF/MMP expression [165–167].

ROS levels spike within hours in alkali-burned corneas, correlating with increased leukocyte-recruiting chemokines (MCP-1, macrophage inflammatory protein(MIP)-1α, IL-8, CXCL1) [160, 163]. Elevated oxidative stress disrupts mitochondrial electron transport, damages cell membranes, and ultimately induces cell death [168]. Oxidative stress, inflammation, and neovascularization collectively worsen corneal damage and result in vision loss [169]. High ROS upregulates Nox2/Nox4, VEGF, and MMPs, exacerbating damage. Non-specific NADPH oxidase inhibitors mitigate inflammation and CoNV in CAB [169]. NOX2/NOX4 are critical for oxidative stress and chemotaxis; their inhibition or genetic knockout reduces leukocyte infiltration and cytokines [4, 170]. Post-alkali burn, WT corneas show elevated pro-inflammatory (Nox2, ICAM-1, VCAM-1) and pro-angiogenic (VEGF, TGFβ1) factors. Nox4-deficient corneas exhibit reduced expression of these genes [4, 27].

Gu et al. confirmed increased Nox2/Nox4 proteins in burned corneas, and found that non-specific NADPH oxidase inhibitors, such as apocynin and diphenyliodonium(DPI), reduced burn-induced neovascularization and inflammation, supporting Nox inhibition as a potential therapeutic target in ocular chemical burns [169]. TGFβ1, upregulated in CAB [171]induces Nox4 across cell types [171, 172] and stimulates chemokines (ICAM-1, VCAM-1) [4, 173]. Consistently, VEGF-A and TGFβ1 are upregulated in WT corneas 7 days post-burn [4].

Nox4 plays a key role in VEGF-induced [174] and TGFβ1-induced signaling pathways [172, 175]. Therefore, compared to WT corneas, the upregulation of both pro-angiogenic genes in Nox4-deficient corneas is significantly reduced [4]. TGFβ1 release post-injury induces collagen, but excessive collagen causes fibrosis/scarring [171, 176]. Nox4 plays a critical role in the TGFβ-induced fibrotic response [177–179]. Some studies have shown that Nox4 induces collagen production in vitro and in vivo via TGFβ1 stimulation [179–181]. These results support the findings that, in the absence of Nox4, collagen synthesis induction in alkali-burned WT corneas is reduced [4].

Nox4 knockout (KO) mice have lower vascular area at days 7/14 post-burn vs. WT, with reduced pro-inflammatory factors (Nox2, ICAM-1, VCAM-1) and suppressed TGFβ1/collagen. Nox4 deficiency improves corneal transparency, highlighting its role in CoNV/inflammation/fibrosis. Thus, Nox4 is a potential CAB treatment target [4]. NADPH oxidase is known to be involved in inflammation in both injury and fibrosis processes [179, 182].

Drp1-dependent mitochondrial fission activates NF-κB to release inflammatory factors (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α). Inhibiting Drp1 reduces NF-κB and inflammation post-burn. Drp1 also promotes CoNV via NADPH oxidase (NOX2/4) regulation. Combined Mdivi-1 (Drp1 inhibitor) and Apocynin synergistically inhibit burn-induced neovascularization. Mitochondrial ROS, activating NF-κB, are a major damage driver in CAB. Drp1 regulates fission, oxidative stress, and NF-κB to drive inflammation/CoNV. Targeting Drp1 or mitochondrial ROS offers new therapeutic strategies [26].Anti-ROS treatment has been shown to effectively alleviate corneal inflammation and inhibit CoNV while promoting corneal healing [163, 169, 183, 184].

Clinical management of alkali burns

The treatment strategies for alkali burns primarily encompass the following key steps. Firstly, immediate ocular irrigation is crucial to promptly reduce the damage caused by alkali substances. The irrigation should last for at least 30 min. Hypertonic solutions may be more effective, but care must be taken to avoid solutions that are hypotonic relative to the corneal stroma, as this may exacerbate corneal edema. Secondly, medical management should be tailored to the severity of the burn and may include local anesthetics, analgesics, and antibiotic ointments or eye drops to prevent infection and reduce inflammation [6]. For epithelial wound healing, supportive therapies such as tear substitutes, soft contact lenses, or collagen shields may be employed. Additionally, the use of growth factors, fibronectin, and retinoic acid has been proposed as adjuncts to promote epithelial healing. In cases of severe burns, surgical interventions may be necessary, including amniotic membrane transplantation (AMT), conjunctival flap surgery, or limbal stem cell transplantation to restore the ocular surface and prevent further damage. Monitoring IOP is essential during the treatment process, with pharmacological or surgical measures considered if needed. Overall, the treatment plan for alkali burns should be individualized based on the specific condition of the patient, integrating prompt medical intervention and long-term follow-up management [6].

Corticosteroids remain the mainstay treatment for CAB, aiming to reduce inflammation linked to CoNV. Dexamethasone suppresses CoNV by inhibiting NF-κB activity and reducing the expression of IL-1β, COX-2, and VEGF [7]. However, their application can result in adverse effects, including cataract formation, elevated IOP, and even corneal melting [185]. Moreover, corticosteroid treatment in alkali-burned models in both mice and rat, not only delays corneal epithelial defect healing and disrupts corneal structure but also exacerbates stromal thinning (sterile ulcer) due to reduced collagen synthesis, while leading to significant weight loss, indicating a decline in overall health condition [186, 187]. Although corticosteroid eye drops play a crucial role in reducing inflammation, long-term use can induce ocular complications.

Consequently, AMT has gained widespread application as a novel surgical treatment, increasingly utilized as an adjunct to conventional therapies to promote epithelial healing, alleviate pain, and restore visual acuity [8, 188]. Nonetheless, AMT has limitations due to its tendency to curl and its susceptibility to collagenase degradation [189]. Moreover, the evidence for combining AMT with standard medical care for moderate acute ocular surface burns remains inconclusive, as its efficacy in preventing epithelial healing failure by day 21, enhancing visual outcomes, and reducing CoNV, symblepharon formation, and healing time is not well established. In severe burns, current data do not indicate significant advantages of AMT inclusion [190, 191].

Limbal stem cell transplantation has been shown to restore vision and relieve symptoms of limbal stem cell deficiency (LSCD) following chemical injuries [192, 193]. Indeed, a recent study has compared the efficacy of simple limbal epithelial transplantation (SLET) versus cultivated limbal epithelial transplantation (CLET) in the treatment of total LSCD resulting from unilateral ocular burns. The findings indicate that, compared to CLET, SLET is equally effective in restoring and maintaining a stable ocular surface in eyes with total LSCD due to ocular burns, with the additional advantage of providing superior visual outcomes [194]. In the later stages of corneal injury, allograft transplantation has demonstrated promising results, partially restoring corneal structure and function. However, limited availability of donor corneas and frequent postoperative rejection often lead to poor long-term outcomes. For acute severe chemical injuries, corneal perforation and associated scleral ischemia can compromise globe integrity, making standard tectonic penetrating procedures impractical [195]. The presence of retinal detachment further complicates management and prognosis. A large tectonic corneoscleral lamellar graft provides a viable option for achieving tectonic stability, enabling future visual rehabilitation [195].The Boston Keratoprosthesis Type 1 offers an effective solution for visual rehabilitation in patients with advanced ocular surface disease, significantly improving visual acuity, including in cases of chemical burns [196].

Given the limitations of conventional therapies for CAB, there is increasing interest in exploring innovative treatment approaches. Recent advancements include novel drug delivery systems utilizing advanced materials, alongside emerging strategies such as cell-based therapies and the integration of herbal medicine, all of which hold promise for enhancing drug bioavailability, accelerating corneal healing, and improving clinical outcomes.

Innovative approaches to corneal alkali burn treatment

Recent studies have shown that subconjunctival injection of regulatory T cells effectively promotes corneal wound healing, reduces corneal neovascularization and fibrosis, by inhibiting excessive inflammation and promoting epithelial cell regeneration [197].In fact, the most promising development potential lies in various ocular local drug delivery systems (DDS). However, less than 5% of the total drug dose delivered via eye drops actually penetrates the interior ocular tissues [198]. The limited surface area of the eye restricts the application of liquid formulations to roughly 30 µL. A substantial portion of the drug is quickly eliminated from the ocular surface due to tear turnover, blinking, and nasolacrimal drainage. Within 2 min of application, about 60% of the drug is already removed; by 8 min, the concentration is reduced to 1/1000, and nearly the entire active ingredient is cleared within 15–25 minutes [198, 199].Eye drops account for over 90% of commercially available ocular medications; however, they have certain limitations, such as short pre-corneal retention time and poor corneal penetration [22]. Due to these challenges, frequent high-dose administrations are often required, potentially resulting in local side effects as well as systemic adverse effects on organs like the heart, lungs, and skin. Additionally, the need for repeated dosing contributes to poor patient compliance, which may worsen the underlying condition [200].To address these limitations, innovative drug delivery systems have been developed, including hydrogels, nanomaterials, and advanced carriers designed to enhance drug retention, penetration, and controlled release. These approaches, incorporating drugs, cells, herbal components, or novel delivery mechanisms, offer promising solutions to improve therapeutic outcomes and patient experience. The following sections will explore these cutting-edge systems and their potential applications in ocular therapy.

Advances and challenges in stem cell therapy for corneal alkali burns

Stem cell therapy holds great potential for treating a wide range of refractory diseases, including psoriasis [201]multiple sclerosis [202]spinal cord injury [203]optic neuropathy [204] and others. Although the safety of stem cell transplantation has been confirmed in numerous clinical trials and various types of stem cells are currently used in medical applications, their therapeutic efficacy remains limited, and clinical research data are inconsistent [205].Currently, stem cell therapy for corneal injuries primarily involves the use of limbal stem cells (LSCs) [53]. In addition to LSCs, mesenchymal stem cells (MSC) derived from various tissues have also shown a positive contribution to the healing process in experimental models of alkali-burned corneas [31, 44, 51, 53, 81, 192, 206]. MSCs derived from bone marrow and adipose tissue have demonstrated efficacy in promoting corneal tissue regeneration, while embryonic MSCs have been shown to enhance corneal transparency [49, 51]. Recent studies indicate that MSCs can effectively facilitate corneal repair by suppressing inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, MCP-1, and MMP-9, as well as by polarizing macrophages towards the CD206-positive M2 phenotype [31]. More importantly, MSCs exhibit immunomodulatory and antifibrotic activities through the secretion of paracrine factors [207].

Among MSCs, adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ADSC) are favored due to their ease of isolation, wide availability from various tissues, and low immunogenicity. ADSCs possess significant immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory properties. Clinical outcomes in animal models of CAB show a trend towards improvement with ADSC administration through intrastromal injection, subconjunctival injection, and topical instillation, including enhanced corneal sensation and accelerated restoration of normal corneal architecture [51, 208]. By integrating modified mRNA (modRNA) technology with ADSC therapy, IGF-1 modRNA-engineered ADSCs (ADSCmodIGF1) were administered to alkali-burned mouse corneas. The transfection with modIGF1 boosted ADSC proliferation and migration. When compared to standard ADSCs or IGF-1 protein eye drops, subconjunctival injection of ADSCmodIGF1 led to the most thorough restoration of corneal structure and function, including enhanced healing of the corneal epithelium and limbus, reduced stromal fibrosis, decreased angiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis, and effective repair of corneal nerves [16].Compared to the alkali-burn control group, subconjunctival injection of bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSC) and ADSCs significantly reduced the expression levels of IL-1β and TNF-α, with BMSCs showing superior anti-inflammatory effects compared to ADSCs. In both stem cell treatment groups, the number of Caspase-3 positive cells was significantly lower, indicating that stem cell therapy effectively inhibited apoptosis in corneal tissue. Additionally, the number of CD68 positive cells was notably reduced in the BMSC and ADSC groups, suggesting decreased infiltration of inflammatory cells. Clinical assessments revealed that corneal opacity and neovascular area were significantly lower in the BMSC and ADSC groups than in the alkali-burn group [51]. Furthermore, mice treated with human placenta-derived mesenchymal stem cells (hP-MSCs) via subconjunctival injection exhibited significantly faster corneal healing at 3 and 7 days post-burn, along with a marked improvement in corneal transparency within 14 days. The injection of hP-MSCs significantly reduced the expression of inflammatory markers such as IL-1β, MCP-1, and MMP9 in the cornea, anterior uvea, and retina, while also decreasing inflammatory cell infiltration and apoptosis in these tissues. Moreover, hP-MSCs promoted the polarization of M2 macrophages and inhibited the activity of pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages [49].Notably, conjunctival injections of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs) administered every other day significantly improved the progression of CAB. This treatment suppressed the NLRP3 inflammasome, caspase-1, IL-18, and IL-1β, while also markedly reducing the frequencies of macrophages and neutrophils in vivo. IL-10 was identified as a critical mediator of the therapeutic effects of MDSC treatment [28].

However, the clinical application of stem cell therapies still encounters several challenges, such as ethical concerns and the low survival rate of MSCs. In particular, MSCs administered via subconjunctival injection tend to experience significant cell death within the inflammatory microenvironment [209, 210].

Therapeutic potential of exosome-based treatments for corneal alkali burns

Exosomes, a subset of extracellular vesicles with an average diameter of approximately 100 nanometers, are composed of nucleic acids, proteins, lipids, amino acids, and metabolites [211]. They are characterized by a bilayer lipid membrane structure and are thought to mediate specific intercellular communication by facilitating fusion or interaction between cells [212, 213].Engineered exosomes are exosomes that have been modified with surface decorations and loaded with internal therapeutic molecules [214].Compared to stem cells, stem cell-derived exosomes offer several advantages, such as non-immunogenicity, lack of infusion toxicity, easy availability, effortless storage, and avoidance of tumorigenic potential and ethical concerns [215, 216].

Recent studies have revealed that the therapeutic effects of ADSCs primarily stem from their exosomes [15]. ADSCs exosomes significantly promote the proliferation of corneal stromal cells (CSCs) and inhibit apoptosis. They downregulate MMPs and upregulate ECM-related proteins, including collagen and fibronectin [217]. MSC-derived exosomes can accelerate the healing of corneal epithelial defects [213, 218].Yu F et al. [15] proposed a novel treatment method for CAB, utilizing antitumor necrosis factor-α antibodies (aT) conjugated with exosomes derived from adipose-derived mesenchymal stem cells (ADSC-Exo) via a MMP-cleavable peptide linker, to enhance the bioavailability of aT and the anti-inflammatory specificity of the exosomes. Simultaneously, poly (vinyl alcohol) (PVA) microneedles (MN) were designed for trans-epithelial delivery of the engineered exosomes. This combination therapy significantly improved corneal healing and effectively suppressed inflammation [15] (Fig. 3). In a rat corneal epithelial defect model, topical administration of exosomes derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs-Exos) or mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs-Exos) was investigated. The particle size of iPSCs-Exos ranged from 30 to 120 nm, while MSCs-Exos ranged from 60 to 400 nm. Both types of exosomes exhibited a spherical morphology and expressed exosomal markers CD9 and CD63. Human corneal epithelial cells (HCECs) demonstrated good uptake capacity for both exosome types. The results indicated that the exosomes significantly enhanced HCECs migration and proliferation, while also inhibiting apoptosis [213].BMSC-derived exosomes (BMSC-Exos) promote the proliferation and migration of human corneal epithelial cells by activating the p44/42 MAPK signaling pathway [34]. Additionally, in a mouse model of alkali-burned corneas, subconjunctival injection of BMSC-Exos significantly reduced the protein levels of α-SMA (a fibrosis marker) and CD31 (a vascularization marker). BMSC-Exos effectively alleviated inflammation, fibrosis, and neovascularization, significantly accelerating the healing of corneal injuries [34].

Serum and platelet-rich plasma therapies for corneal wound healing

Autologous serum (AS) ins a variety of cytokines and growth factors (such as EGF and TGF-β) that are highly similar to those found in tears. These bioactive substances contribute to tissue repair and healing by accelerating the proliferation, differentiation, and migration of epithelial cells, thereby aiding in the restoration of damaged corneal epithelium [219, 220].Employing 50% autologous serum eyedrops emerges as an effective therapeutic approach for persistent corneal epithelial defects that remain resistant to conventional medical treatments [221].In patients with refractory epithelial defects, umbilical cord serum therapy significantly reduced the size of epithelial defects at days 7, 14, and 21. Moreover, umbilical cord serum resulted in a higher number of patients achieving complete re-epithelialization compared to autologous serum, with no patients reporting any adverse effects or discomfort from the treatment [222].Umbilical cord serum therapy exhibits superior efficacy compared to autologous serum eye drops or artificial tears in facilitating ocular surface restoration following acute chemical injuries [223].In experimental chemical burns, umbilical cord serum eyedrops demonstrate greater effectiveness in enhancing corneal wound healing and minimizing corneal haze compared to peripheral blood serum eyedrops and artificial tears. Additionally, umbilical cord serum significantly reduces the expression levels of IL-1β more effectively than peripheral blood serum [224].The researchers meticulously designed a ROS-responsive contact lens using polyvinyl alcohol, which minimized drug release during storage while ensuring sustained and gradual release on the ocular surface for over seven days. Furthermore, they combined this contact lens with autologous serum for alkali burn treatment, resulting in decreased mRNA expression levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and VEGF. This combination significantly promoted corneal healing, inhibited neovascularization, and reduced inflammation and ROS accumulation [19].

Topical autologous platelet-rich plasma (PRP) therapy is both safe and effective for corneal chemical injuries, facilitating rapid re-epithelialization of the ocular surface and can be administered concurrently with standard medical therapy [225, 226]. In a study by Anitua E and colleagues [227]a quantitative comparison was made between AS-based eyedrops and plasma rich in growth factors (PRGF)-based eyedrops, focusing on their biological effects on corneal stromal keratocytes (HKs) and conjunctival fibroblasts (HConFs). The analysis of PDGF-AB, VEGF, EGF, FGFb, and TGF-β1 revealed that PRGF-based eyedrops contained significantly higher levels of all growth factors compared to AS-based eyedrops. Furthermore, PRGF-based eyedrops significantly improved the biological outcomes of both HKs and HConFs and reduced TGF-β1-induced myofibroblast differentiation, indicating its potential for enhanced ocular surface wound healing with minimal scar formation compared to AS. On the other hand, subconjunctival PRP appears to improve corneal epithelial wound healing. However, in a rabbit model, the combination of antibiotics and subconjunctival PRP may have a delayed healing effect compared to the use of PRP alone [228].In addition, plasma fibrin membranes (PFMs) prepared using platelet-poor plasma demonstrated the ability to reduce inflammation-related markers (MMP-9, IL-6, TNF-α) and the fibrosis marker α-SMA in alkali burn injuries. Co-culturing BMSCs with corneal epithelial cells successfully induced the differentiation of BMSCs into corneal epithelial-like cells. The incorporation of BMSCs and corneal epithelial cells onto PFMs significantly accelerated corneal healing by alleviating inflammation and suppressing fibrosis [44].

Herbal extracts, phytochemicals, and venom-derived components in the treatment of CAB

Herbal extracts and phytochemicals have shown significant therapeutic potential for CAB, offering anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and anti-angiogenic effects that promote corneal wound healing. Treatments using extracts of propolis and diospyros kaki, lycium barbarum polysaccharide, along with compounds like naringenin, epigallocatechin gallate(EGCG), emodin, ganoderma lucidum, resveratrol and extracts of cucurbita argyrosperma, effectively reduce CoNV by downregulating key pro-inflammatory factors such as VEGF-A, IL-1β, IL-6, and MMPs [38, 47, 84, 156, 160, 229–232].

Recent studies demonstrate that pre-treatment with Lycium barbarum polysaccharide [231]promotes corneal repair and reduces inflammation following alkaline burn injury in mice. Additionally, Lycium barbarum glycopeptide accelerates corneal wound healing while attenuating inflammation and fibrosis in murine alkali burn models. This effect is mediated through suppression of the NF-κB/NLRP3/IL-1β signaling pathway and inhibition of miR-21a-5p expression, consequently upregulating its target Smad7 and blocking TGF-β/Smad2/3-mediated fibrotic pathways [232].

Researchers have identified that certain natural flavonoid compounds exhibit therapeutic potential in the treatment of alkali burns. The ethanol extract of DiospyImatinib kaki (EEDK), Luteolin (LUT) and Naringenin are rich in flavonoid compounds. Research results indicate that EEDK significantly reduces CoNV induced by alkali burn and downregulates the expression of several pro-angiogenic and inflammatory factors, including VEGF, fibroblast growth factor (FGF), IL-6, MMP-2 [47]. After intraperitoneal injection of LUT, it can be detected in ocular tissues such as the cornea, conjunctiva, and aqueous humor. LUT significantly inhibits the infiltration of inflammatory cells in the cornea, including a reduction in the number of neutrophils and lymphocytes, and markedly decreases the mRNA and protein levels of inflammatory factors such as IL-1β, IL-6, and MCP-1. It improves corneal opacity, epithelial defects, collagen degradation, neovascularization, and inflammatory cell infiltration induced by alkali burns. LUT exerts its effects by blocking the TAK-1/MAPKs/AP-1 and NF-κB signaling pathways in corneal stromal fibroblasts, inhibiting IL-1β-mediated secretion of inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, MMP-1, MMP-8, MMP-13, and the degradation of type I collagen [43]. Naringenin eye drops significantly inhibited the recruitment of neutrophils and macrophages in male mice following CAB. This effect was achieved by suppressing the activity of myeloperoxidase (MPO) and N-acetyl-β-D-glucosaminidase (NAG), which reduced the production of pro-inflammatory factors IL-1β and IL-6, while increasing the level of the anti-inflammatory factor IL-10, thereby decreasing inflammatory cell infiltration into the cornea. Naringenin also inhibited CoNV by downregulating the mRNA expression of VEGF, PDGF, and MMP-14. Furthermore, Naringenin enhanced the levels of glutathione (GSH) and protein sulfhydryls, significantly improving the antioxidant capacity of corneal tissue, indicating its potential to effectively combat oxidative stress induced by alkali burns [160].

There have also been some explorations into the therapeutic efficacy of triterpenoids in the treatment of alkali burns. Among the various phytochemicals, Ginsenoside Rh2 reduced the infiltration of inflammatory cells in a mouse CAB model and decreased the expression of pro-inflammatory factors such as VEGF, IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6, thereby effectively suppressing neovascularization [36]. The role of Glycyrrhizin (GLY), a selective HMGB1 inhibitor, is also noteworthy. HMGB1, as an inflammatory mediator, plays a role in various tissue injuries, and its overexpression can exacerbate inflammation and angiogenesis. GLY significantly reduced the area of CoNV after alkali burns and inhibited the activation of VEGF and its downstream signaling pathways. By decreasing the expression of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, GLY effectively suppressed the inflammatory response and reduced the infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages. Compared to the control group, GLY significantly decreased corneal opacity and promoted corneal healing and repair. GLY also inhibited the upregulation of miR-21 following alkali burns, which reduced the transformation of corneal cells into myofibroblasts, further suppressing fibrosis and corneal opacity [10].

Daphnetin (DAP) and Osthole, both coumarin derivatives, further underscore the therapeutic potential of herbal components, exhibiting potent anti-inflammatory and anti-angiogenic effects in CAB. In a mouse CAB model, treatment with DAP eye drops for 14 consecutive days significantly inhibited VEGF-A-induced proliferation, migration, and tube formation of HUVECs. DAP notably reduced inflammatory cell infiltration in the cornea after alkali burn and suppressed the expression of inflammatory factors such as IL-1β and caspase-1 by inhibiting the activation of the TLR4/NF-κB/NLRP3 signaling pathway. DAP also downregulated the phosphorylation of VEGF-A/VEGFR2 and its downstream targets STAT3, AKT, and ERK, thereby inhibiting CoNV [162]. Researchers have demonstrated that Osthole is non-toxic within a concentration range of 0.05 µM to 25 µM in vitro. Research shows that Osthole significantly inhibits neovascularization in mice with CAB. This effect is achieved by suppressing the MAPK signaling pathway, particularly the phosphorylation of p38, ERK1/2, and JNK, which in turn reduces VEGF-induced endothelial cell activation, thereby inhibiting neovascularization [233].

Recent studies have also highlighted the efficacy of anthraquinone compounds, such as Aloin and Emodin, in the management of CAB. By activating the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway, Aloin reduces the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), inhibits ROS production, and suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α [234]. Furthermore, Aloe vera gel enhances epithelial regeneration and alleviates corneal edema and inflammation in both normal and diabetic CAB models [23,235]. Emodin notably reduced neovascularization and inflammation in the corneas of mice with alkali burns. In comparison to the control group, the expression of VEGFR2 was significantly lower in the emodin-treated group. Additional cellular experiments indicated that emodin suppressed the VEGFR2 signaling pathway by decreasing the levels of VEGFR2, p-VEGFR2, p-Akt, p-STAT3, and p-P38, leading to a further reduction in neovascularization [38]. Similarly, topical application of Hypericin extract (a naphthoquinone compound) can significantly inhibit CoNV, inflammation, and fibrosis following corneal alkali burns [236].

EGCG and Pycnogenol are both polyphenolic compounds that exhibit similar effects in antioxidation, anti-inflammation, and tissue repair. In a CAB model, daily intraperitoneal administration of EGCG for 14 days significantly reduced corneal edema, ciliary congestion, and inflammatory cell infiltration. Notably, EGCG decreased VEGF expression while increasing the anti-angiogenic factor PEDF, achieving a balance between pro- and anti-angiogenic signals. Immunohistochemical analysis further revealed that EGCG suppressed NF-κB activation, thus modulating VEGF expression and effectively inhibiting CoNV [237]. Pycnogenol, derived from French maritime pine bark, accelerates corneal healing by reducing oxidative stress and inflammatory cell infiltration. It also organizes collagen fibers in the stroma and suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β and TNF-α. This study shows that Pycnogenol significantly accelerates wound healing in the corneas of diabetic rats following alkali burns and effectively reduces the inflammatory response [238].

After alkali burn, diterpene lactones——Ginkgolide B eye drops significantly downregulated the mRNA expression levels of VEGF and MMP-9, while upregulating the expression of the anti-angiogenic factor PEDF, thereby significantly reducing the area of CoNV [239]. The diterpene lactone compound Rebaudioside A (RA) self-assembles into ultrasmall nanomicelles, functioning as an ocular drug delivery system. These nanomicelles effectively encapsulate the polyphenolic compound pterostilbene (Pt), enhancing its in vitro antioxidant activity and membrane permeability. RA-Pt demonstrated excellent ocular tolerance, enhanced intraocular permeation, and superior anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects in vivo, reducing oxidative stress and promoting ROS-degrading enzymes. Additionally, RA-Pt showed strong therapeutic efficacy for CAB, highlighting the potential of RA-based nanomicelles to improve the bioavailability and therapeutic effects of poorly water-soluble drugs like Pt [35].

Similarly, Ganoderma lucidum exhibits potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects. Treatment with Ganoderma lucidum significantly reduced ROS levels and downregulated inflammatory mediators such as TNF-α, IL-1α, IL-8, and MMP-3 in oxidatively damaged cells. In a rabbit CAB model, it accelerated epithelial healing, reduced corneal opacity, and decreased inflammatory cell infiltration [84]. Cucurbita argyrosperma seed extract also demonstrated notable anti-inflammatory and anti-angiogenic effects. It significantly reduced CoNV and downregulated pro-inflammatory factors such as IL-1β, COX-2, and VEGF, making it a promising therapeutic agent [156]. For scar-free healing, Bleogen pB1, a novel EGFR agonist derived from the cactus Pereskia bleo, has shown excellent results. Bleogen pB1, the smallest known EGFR agonist derived from the Southeast Asian cactus Pereskia bleo [240]exhibits stronger stability and resistance to degradation compared to traditional EGF, thereby accelerating the healing of skin and corneal wounds. Studies have shown that at concentrations of 5 µg/ml and 12.5 µg/ml, bleogen pB1 exhibits similar wound-healing acceleration effects as EGF. Bleogen pB1 significantly reduces neutrophil and macrophage infiltration in the cornea after alkali burns, inhibits the expression of the fibroblast marker α-SMA in the cornea, reduces scar formation, and promotes scar-free healing. RNA-seq analysis reveals that bleogen pB1, like EGF, promotes corneal wound healing by regulating TGF-β, interferon signaling pathways, and genes involved in ECM remodeling [241].Tetramethylpyrazine (TMP) is an active compound extracted from the traditional Chinese medicine Chuanxiong (Ligusticum chuanxiong). In a mouse CAB model, the expression of the G protein-coupled receptor CXCR4 was significantly upregulated in the cornea. However, after TMP treatment, the expression of CXCR4 decreased to 43% of the post-injury levels. TMP significantly inhibited corneal neovascularization by modulating the NF-κB/NRF-1/CXCR4 signaling pathway [242, 243]. In vivo results demonstrated that phytic acid (PA) eye drops promoted the restoration of corneal morphology and function, while in vitro experiments confirmed that PA facilitated the repair of oxidative stress-induced corneal epithelial damage by inhibiting ferroptosis [5].Loading PA into a macromolecular polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) hydrogel significantly enhanced corneal repair in CAB injuries compared to the use of PA alone. The treatment resulted in faster recovery of central corneal thickness, with epithelial and stromal cells exhibiting orderly alignment during the healing process [5].

Beyond herbal extracts, venom-derived therapies offer innovative approaches for treating CAB. ZK002 is an anti-angiogenic peptide extracted from the venom of the Chinese pit viper (Agkistrodon acutus). Treatment with ZK002 significantly reduced the length and area of CoNV in SD rats following alkali burn, showing superior anti-angiogenic effects compared to anti-VEGF drugs. It downregulated the expression of pro-angiogenic factors (such as VEGFR2, MMP-9, and MMP-2) and pro-inflammatory factors (such as IL-1β), while upregulating the expression of PPARγ. Additionally, the expression of fibrosis-related genes (α-SMA and TGF-β2) was significantly reduced in the ZK002-treated group [46].

Novel applications of existing drugs in corneal alkali burn therapy

In a Wistar rats CAB model, topical administration of 0.05% tacrolimus (FK506) four times daily significantly reduced the expression of pro-inflammatory factors (such as IL-1β, IL-6, MCP-1, MIP-1α, and TGF-β), with the most notable effects observed on days 3 and 7. Compared to subconjunctival injection of anti-VEGF (0.5 mg/0.05 mL), tacrolimus demonstrated superior performance in reducing inflammatory cell infiltration and the expression of chemotactic factors [161]. The results suggest that tacrolimus effectively inhibits neovascularization and inflammation following CAB and exhibits better long-term efficacy than anti-VEGF agents. Unlike anti-VEGF, tacrolimus not only provides prolonged suppression of CoNV but also promotes corneal epithelial healing and reduces scar formation. The results further indicate that FK506 significantly reduced alkali burn-induced CoNV on days 7, 14, and 28 post-injury, though it had no effect on day 3. In contrast, anti-VEGF significantly reduced CoNV area at all time points, indicating that anti-VEGF acts more rapidly in inhibiting CoNV compared to FK506 [161].

Fasudil, a Rho kinase (ROCK) inhibitor, downregulated the mRNA expression of VEGF, TNF-α, MMP-8, and MMP-9 in a mouse CAB model, but had no significant effect on the expression of VEGFR1/2, IL-1β, and MMP-2. Treatment with 100 µM fasudil significantly upregulated the expression of HO-1 protein. The study suggests that fasudil exerts its effects by inhibiting the Rho/ROCK signaling pathway, reducing oxidative stress and inflammation, which significantly suppresses neovascularization after CAB and promotes corneal epithelial healing [244].Studies have shown that AMA0526, a specific ROCK inhibitor, significantly suppressed CoNV, inflammation, and fibrosis in a mouse model of alkali burn by inhibiting the Rho/ROCK signaling pathway. Compared to the traditional anti-inflammatory drug dexamethasone, AMA0526 demonstrated superior effectiveness in inhibiting fibrosis with fewer side effects, suggesting its potential therapeutic value in corneal injury treatment [245].

M1 macrophages play a central role in inflammation and CoNV. Upadacitinib inhibits M1 macrophage infiltration in alkali-injured corneas and reduces mRNA expression levels of iNOS, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukins (IL)-6 and IL-1β, as well as VEGF-A. Upadacitinib significantly reduces corneal opacity, promotes wound healing, and effectively suppresses CoNV [37]. Disulfiram, a drug primarily used for treating alcoholism, suppressed macrophage infiltration, corneal scarring, and neovascularization in a rat CAB model by inhibiting macrophage pseudopodia formation [246].

Lipoxin A4 (LXA4) is a lipid mediator derived from arachidonic acid that significantly reduces corneal opacity and neovascularization following alkali burn, while also decreasing the incidence of hyphema. LXA4 downregulates the gene and protein expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and IL-6, as well as angiogenic factors like MMP-9 and VEGFA. LXA4 treatment also upregulates genes associated with corneal repair, including keratin family genes (such as KRT7, KRT32), promoting epithelial healing and repair. Additionally, LXA4 significantly reduces neutrophil infiltration in the cornea and increases the proportion of M2 macrophages [49].

After alkali injury, rats received topical instillation of 0.05% fenofibrate (PPARα group), 0.1% pioglitazone (PPARγ group), 0.05% fenofibrate + 0.1% pioglitazone (PPARα + γ group), or vehicle (vehicle group) twice daily. Following treatment, upregulation of corresponding PPAR mRNA was observed in each agonist group. Administration of PPARα, PPARγ, and PPARα + γ agonists suppressed inflammation, neovascularization, and fibrosis. Additionally, the PPARγ receptor agonist increased M2 macrophages, promoting wound healing, while the PPARα agonist suppressed immature blood vessels in the early stages. The combination of PPARα + γ agonists significantly reduced the expression of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and angiopoietin-2 (Ang-2), leading to reduced angiogenesis. On day 7 post-burn, both PPARα and PPARγ agonists decreased the accumulation of collagen type III in the cornea, inhibiting fibrosis. The collagen deposition level in the PPARα + γ combination group was significantly lower than that in the single-agent groups. PPAR agonist treatment also significantly downregulated the expression of TNF-α and NF-κB [11].

Innovative strategies in ocular drug delivery: advancing retention, bioavailability, and targeted release

Eye drops, which dominate the ophthalmic drug market, face significant limitations such as short retention, low corneal penetration, and frequent dosing requirements, leading to poor compliance and potential disease progression [22]. To improve ocular drug delivery and efficacy, various functional biomaterials have been developed, including hydrogels, nanofibers, and nanoparticle (NP) formulations [56, 57, 84]. Among these, hydrogels stand out due to their excellent cell compatibility, adjustable mechanical properties, and optical characteristics, making them highly suitable for the repair and regeneration of corneal injuries [40, 247].The sudden release of drugs is a common drawback of nanofibers in drug delivery applications. Coaxial electrospinning offers the advantage of encapsulating drugs separately in the core and shell layers, with the shell layer acting as a barrier to prevent abrupt drug release from the core [248, 249].(Fig. 3D) Researchers have been exploring innovative drug delivery strategies, including nanocarriers, hydrogels [5, 9]contact lenses [19]microspheres [13]and microneedles [15, 250]to enhance drug retention and ocular bioavailability. Yu and colleagues developed poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA) microneedles for precise and sustained exosome delivery(Fig. 3 A). In vivo results demonstrated that microneedle treatment outperformed traditional topical administration and subconjunctival injection [15]. Researchers have also developed DDS with responsiveness to pathological environments. For example, Yu F et al. [15] proposed a MMP-responsive exosome delivery system for controlled release (Fig. 3A), Sun R et al. [19]designed a ROS-responsive contact lens for releasing levofloxacin and diclofenac(Fig. 3B), and Yang C-J et al. [50] developed a PH-responsive nanodrug formulation tailored for release after alkali burns(Fig. 3E).

Hydrogel formulation system

Aprepitant was developed into a distinct formulation, Aprepitant X Formulation, an aqueous gel that demonstrated remarkable therapeutic potential. In a mouse model of alkali burns, Aprepitant X significantly alleviated corneal pain, enhanced corneal nerve regeneration, and accelerated epithelial healing. Notably, long-term use of Aprepitant X showed no toxicity, underscoring its safety and efficacy for ocular applications [41].Researchers developed an innovative DDS that encapsulates Imatinib in glycymicelle combined with a hydrogel, aimed at treating CAB. This system significantly enhanced the water solubility of Imatinib and, by prolonging its retention time on the ocular surface, exhibited potent effects in promoting corneal healing, restoring corneal sensitivity, reducing corneal opacity, and inhibiting neovascularization, demonstrating its great potential in ophthalmic treatments [20].The electro-spun poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid)(PLGA)-PEG-PLGA has been demonstrated to be an ideal ophthalmic drug delivery system, capable of being formulated into an injectable solution that transitions into a gel in situ, enabling sustained drug release [251, 252]. A thermosensitive, biodegradable hydrogel DDS based on PLGA-PEG-PLGA triblock copolymer, which underwent a rapid sol–gel transition at ~ 37 °C within minutes, was loaded with 0.7 mg of adalimumab and 1.4 mg of aflibercept [54](Fig. 4 A). This formulation was injected subconjunctivally into rabbits after CAB. At 3 months, the treatment completely inhibited CoNV, accelerated corneal re-epithelialization and wound healing, and prevented the loss of retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) and optic nerve axons [54]. A similar DDS composed of porous polydimethylsiloxane/polyvinyl alcohol and loaded with 85 µg of infliximab was implanted in the subconjunctival space of Dutch-belted pigmented rabbits with alkali burns. Anti-TNF-α DDS inhibited retinal neurodegeneration, whereas sham DDS did not. Compared to uninjured contralateral eyes, anti-TNF-α DDS-treated eyes showed a 4.3% reduction in retinal ganglion cell counts, while sham DDS-treated eyes exhibited a 38.1% reduction. Similarly, anti-TNF-α DDS preserved optic nerve axon density, whereas sham DDS resulted in a ~ 30% decrease [55]. A thermosensitive hydrogel prepared using PLGA-PEG-PLGA copolymer enhanced dexamethasone solubility by 5.2-fold, remaining liquid at room temperature and rapidly solidifying at body temperature. In a rat CAB model, subconjunctival injection of dexamethasone thermosensitive hydrogel significantly reduced CoNV, sustained drug release for 28 days, lowered blood IL-6 levels, and demonstrated strong anti-inflammatory effects, effectively mitigating burn-induced inflammation and angiogenic factor expression [48]. Nintedanib, a VEGFR-2 tyrosine kinase inhibitor, specifically inhibits the activation of the VEGFR and its downstream signaling pathways. Another study developed Nintedanib nano eye drops and a nintedanib nano thermoreversible hydrogel (NNTH) to extend the drug’s retention time in the eye. NNTH remains in liquid form at low temperatures and rapidly transforms into a gel upon contact with body temperature, facilitating sustained drug release in the cornea. After 14 days of treatment with NNTH following alkali burn, a significant reduction in corneal neovascularization area was observed, outperforming the Nintedanib eye drop group. In in vitro release studies, NNTH demonstrated a longer drug release time compared to conventional Nintedanib solution. NNTH significantly decreased the expression of inflammatory factors (IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α) in the cornea after alkali burn, exhibiting strong anti-inflammatory effects, and notably inhibited the expression of VEGFA and CD31 proteins [9]. A thermosensitive hydrogel composed of Pluronic F-127 (PF-127) loaded with the multifunctional protein progranulin (PGRN) is prepared at low temperatures and transitions into a gel upon contact with the cornea, forming a protective barrier against external irritants. Boasting high transparency and biocompatibility, the hydrogel enables sustained release of PGRN, requiring just one daily application. It creates an optimal healing environment by suppressing inflammation, promoting re-epithelialization, and supporting axonal regeneration through various signaling pathways [18].HydrogelPGRN alleviated corneal inflammation by promoting the transition of macrophages from the M1 to M2 phenotype through suppression of the NF-κB signaling pathway. Additionally, it facilitated corneal epithelial repair via activation of the PI3K/Akt pathway and supported axonal regeneration. Corneas treated with HydrogelPGRN exhibited significantly reduced mRNA expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, including TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β, compared to other groups [18]. A novel approach for corneal repair was developed by combining probucol-loaded nanoparticles with an injectable oxidized dextran/gelatin/borax hydrogel. The hydrogel, designed with borate ester and Schiff base bonds, exhibited excellent injectability and self-healing properties, enhancing drug retention on the ocular surface. Probucol, traditionally used for hypercholesterolemia, was repurposed for CAB due to its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. Encapsulation in nanoparticles addressed its poor water solubility and prolonged its ocular residence time. In vitro and in vivo studies showed that the probucol-loaded hydrogel effectively suppressed pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α), upregulated the anti-inflammatory factor Arginase-1 (Arg-1), and facilitated rapid corneal tissue repair [14]. Chitosan and its derivatives are widely used in the biomedical field due to their biodegradability, biocompatibility, antibacterial properties, and adhesive characteristics. Carboxymethyl chitosan, a water-soluble derivative of chitosan, offers enhanced properties, making it more suitable for applications in wound healing, drug delivery, and tissue engineering [253]. Deoxyribonuclease I (DNase I), an effective regulator of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), was loaded onto chitosan nanoparticles and combined with silk fibroin to create a composite hydrogel for sustained NET regulation. Following CAB, the hydrogel continuously released DNase I, resulting in a 50% reduction in NET formation and approximately 70% decrease in neovascularization [33]. Researchers developed a novel nano-alkaline ion-based gel eye drop that simultaneously degrades NETs using DNase I and neutralizes acidic inflammatory environments to inhibit CoNV and promote ocular surface recovery after chemical injury [254]. A thermosensitive hydrogel was prepared by mixing chitosan with gelatin and sodium glycerophosphate. The hydrogel remained in a liquid state at room temperature and transitioned into a gel at ocular surface temperature, enabling prolonged retention of ferulic acid (FA) on the eye. In a rabbit corneal alkali burn model, topical application of FA-loaded hydrogel demonstrated sustained FA release at a rate of 28% over 24 h, indicating excellent controlled-release properties. FA significantly reduced ROS production in rabbit corneal epithelial cells, downregulated pro-inflammatory factors IL-1β, TNF-α, and MMP-9, highlighting its anti-inflammatory and antioxidant potential. Furthermore, FA effectively decreased H₂O₂-induced apoptosis rates and reduced the number of TUNEL-positive cells [57].

Xu and colleagues developed an in situ hydrogel based on carboxymethyl chitosan (CMCTS) and sodium alginate dialdehyde (SAD), which rapidly gelates within 60 s and exhibits high transparency, excellent swelling capacity, biocompatibility, and compatibility with limbal stem cells (LSCs). Experimental results demonstrated that the hydrogel effectively facilitated the transplantation of encapsulated LSCs onto alkali-burned corneas. In the hydrogel-treated group, the corneal epithelium resembled that of normal corneas, and corneal thickness was significantly reduced, indicating suppression of fibrosis. In a rabbit CAB model, the LSC-containing hydrogel markedly accelerated corneal healing, reduced corneal opacity, and inhibited neovascularization [53].However, limitations such as low cell delivery efficiency and suboptimal repair outcomes were identified in the system. To address these issues, Wang Z and colleagues developed a chitosan-based thermosensitive hydrogel (CTH) carrier, named 4D-CTH, using 4D bioprinting technology, which allowed for uniform pore size and adjustable shape to enhance stem cell delivery. The CTH remained in a fluid sol state at 4 °C and transitioned to a gel state at 37 °C. With its uniform pores, interconnected internal structure, and excellent cell compatibility, 4D-CTH provided a conducive environment for cell growth and migration. The study showed that encapsulating limbal epithelial stem cells (LESCs) in 4D-CTH significantly enhanced corneal repair in a rat alkali burn model, promoting the regeneration of damaged epithelial tissue [12].

Researchers developed chitosan-modified PLGA nanoparticles encapsulating insulin (INS), which effectively inhibited CoNV and inflammation in a rat alkali burn model by enhancing drug delivery and modulating m⁶A RNA methylation via downregulation of FTO protein [255]. Xiong X et al. were the first to use a liposome-trimethyl chitosan (TMC) nanodelivery system to co-deliver of INS and vascular endothelial growth factor small interfering RNA (siVEGF/INS) as a combined therapy for treating CAB. The siVEGF (TMC-INS-lip)TIL treatment reduced levels of secreted TNF-α, IL-6, and MMP-9, showing significant efficacy in alleviating corneal alkali injury. Moreover, siVEGF TIL exhibited ideal nanoparticle characteristics, including excellent biocompatibility, lack of toxicity, ease of preparation, adhesion, and sustained release, indicating its potential as a novel corneal delivery platform. Notably, siVEGF TIL significantly inhibited ferroptosis and CoNV, ultimately preventing damage from alkali burns. However, the absence of absolute concentrations of INS or siVEGF within the NPs presents a limitation for further clinical translation of siVEGF TIL therapy [45].

Nanomicelle system

Micelles are nanostructures formed by the self-assembly of amphiphilic block copolymers in water, featuring a hydrophilic shell that stabilizes the micelle and a hydrophobic core that solubilizes poorly soluble drugs. Formed from diblock (A–B) or triblock (A–B–A) copolymers, their properties—stability, size, and morphology—are influenced by block interactions, lengths, and drug loading, offering tunable platforms for drug delivery [256](Fig. 4 C). Building on advancements in polymeric micelles for solubilizing poorly soluble drugs, their application in treating corneal alkali burns offers promising potential. TA-A001, a cannabinoid receptor 2 (CB2r) agonist, was encapsulated in a polymeric micelle composed of polyvinylpyrrolidone and polylactide block copolymers, known as SmartCelle™, to enable the delivery of this highly hydrophobic drug. After a two-week observation period, it was found that a 0.25% concentration of TA-A001 could modulate inflammation induced by moderate alkali burns to the cornea. Compared to prednisolone treatment, TA-A001-treated corneas exhibited faster wound closure, with sensory nerve fibers present throughout the corneal epithelium, including the superficial cell layer [186]. Both glycyrrhizin (GLY) and rebamipide (RBM) are FDA-approved drugs with potential therapeutic effects for CAB. However, the effectiveness of RBM is hindered by its poor aqueous solubility and limited bioavailability [257].Empagliflozin (EMP) is an oral medication approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for blood glucose control. Sun et al. developed a nanomicellar ophthalmic solution, EMP@glycymicelle, based on empagliflozin (EMP) and glycyrrhizin, for the treatment of CAB. Due to the poor water solubility of EMP, the EMP@glycymicelle ophthalmic solution demonstrated an encapsulation efficiency of up to 98%, with a particle size of less than 10 nanometers, exhibiting good water solubility and storage stability. Compared to the control group (PBS) and other treatment groups (0.1% sodium hyaluronate solution, glycyrrhizin, EMP), EMP@glycymicelle significantly accelerated corneal wound healing, promoted recovery of corneal sensitivity, reduced corneal opacity, and inhibited CoNV by significantly downregulating the expression of factors such as HMGB1, IL-6, NF-κB, and VEGF [21]. This biocompatible nanoplatform, derived from a synthetic amphiphilic boric acid-based copolymer, self-assembles into highly stable micelles with excellent dexamethasone (DEX) loading capacity. The results indicate that the nanoplatform can be efficiently internalized by corneal epithelial cells in vitro and facilitates transcytosis in vivo, significantly enhancing drug penetration into the pathological corneal stroma. The NP1@DEX group showed superior anti-inflammatory effects compared to DEX alone, with significantly reduced expression levels of inflammatory markers IL-6 and TNF-α [258]. This study investigated a novel nanomicelle carrier based on dipotassium glycyrrhizinate (DG) for loading rebamipide (RBM) into an ophthalmic solution (DG-RBM) to enhance therapeutic efficacy in treating CAB. RBM, an FDA-approved drug with anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, is traditionally used for dry eye treatment but has limited effectiveness against CAB due to its poor water solubility and low bioavailability. To address these limitations, researchers utilized DG as a nanocarrier to successfully formulate RBM nanomicelle ophthalmic solution. DG-RBM significantly improved RBM’s water solubility, prolonged its retention time on the cornea, and enhanced corneal permeability, resulting in significantly higher drug concentrations in the cornea compared to free RBM. In vitro, DG-RBM markedly promoted corneal epithelial cell wound healing. In a mouse corneal alkali burn model, DG-RBM significantly accelerated epithelial healing, with complete wound closure by day 14, outperforming conventional RBM and sodium hyaluronate treatments. DG-RBM effectively inhibited corneal neovascularization and significantly reduced the expression of inflammatory factors, including VEGF, IL-6, and TNF-α. The study further revealed that DG-RBM suppressed HMGB1-related pro-inflammatory and pro-angiogenic pathways by downregulating HMGB1, RAGE, and TLR4 expression, thereby significantly reducing corneal inflammation and neovascularization [257]. Axitinib is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) primarily used to inhibit angiogenesis by targeting the vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) signaling pathway. In this study, axitinib was encapsulated in the amphiphilic block polymer polyethylene glycol-polycaprolactone (MPEG-PCL) to prepare axitinib-loaded micelles, significantly improving the solubility and stability of this hydrophobic drug. Compared to dexamethasone, used as a positive control, the axitinib-loaded micelles exhibited similar effects in inflammation suppression and anti-angiogenesis. The results suggest that MPEG-PCL micelles loaded with axitinib have a strong anti-angiogenic effect and good biocompatibility in the treatment of corneal neovascularization. Compared to traditional anti-VEGF drugs, axitinib micelles offer a novel therapeutic strategy for corneal diseases by enhancing drug solubility and stability. This approach holds promise for future clinical applications in the treatment of ocular diseases [259]. The study proposed a supramolecular hydrogel composed of Methoxy poly (ethylene glycol)-poly (ε-caprolactone) block polymer (MPEG-PCL) micelles and α-cyclodextrin (α-CD) for the combined delivery of dexamethasone sodium phosphate (Dexp) and Avastin® (Ava), and further evaluated its therapeutic effects in a rat alkali burn model (Fig. 4B). The experiments showed that the supramolecular hydrogel significantly prolonged the precorneal retention time of Ava compared to its solution formulation. Additionally, the hydrogel exhibited high ocular biocompatibility, with no irritation observed after topical application. Moreover, the Dexp-Ava hydrogel significantly alleviated alkali burn-induced corneal inflammation in the rat model by downregulating the expression of VEGF, CD31, and α-SMA, and effectively inhibited corneal neovascularization. Therefore, the combined Dexp-Ava hydrogel demonstrated superior efficacy compared to Ava monotherapy, making it a promising alternative treatment for inflammatory CNV [260].

Liposomes system

Liposomes, first described in the 1960s, are spherical vesicles composed of one or more phospholipid bilayers surrounding an aqueous core. Their biocompatibility, biodegradability, and structural versatility make them an innovative drug delivery system. Capable of encapsulating both hydrophilic and hydrophobic drugs, liposomes protect therapeutic agents from enzymatic degradation and enhance bioavailability, making them particularly promising for the treatment of complex conditions such as corneal alkali burns [261](Fig. 4D). Pirfenidone (PFD) loaded liposomes were prepared using materials such as polyethylene glycol (PEG) and applied to C57BL/6 mouse corneas subjected to alkali burns. The mice were randomly divided into seven groups, receiving treatment with physiological saline, empty liposomes, dexamethasone, different concentrations of PFD solution, or PFD-loaded liposomes, administered four times daily for 15 days. The PFD-loaded liposomes significantly reduced corneal fibrosis and opacity. Compared to PFD solution and dexamethasone, the PFD liposome group showed superior effects in inhibiting increases in corneal thickness and edema. PFD liposomes significantly suppressed the expression of TGF-β and α-SMA while restoring the normal expression of IL-1β, indicating a strong anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory effect on corneal fibrosis. The liposomal delivery system significantly improved the ocular retention time and therapeutic effects of PFD, reducing the frequency of administration and enhancing its anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory properties [142].A CAB model was established in C57BL/6 mice, and Ferrostatin-1 (Fer-1)-loaded liposomes (Fer-1-NPs) significantly reduced corneal opacity and neovascularization, demonstrating effects comparable to the steroid dexamethasone but without noticeable side effects. Fer-1-NPs also markedly decreased inflammatory cell infiltration and the expression of pro-inflammatory factors IL-1β and IL-6 in the cornea. With excellent biocompatibility, Fer-1-NPs exhibited no significant cytotoxicity or damage to major organs [29].In this study, we developed cationic liposomal tacrolimus (FK506) with a surface potential of approximately + 30 mV, enabling it to bind effectively to the negatively charged mucin layer on the ocular surface. The results showed a significant increase in FK506 concentration in the cornea in the FK506 liposome group (0.2 mg/ml) compared to the free drug group (0.2 mg/ml). In a mouse model of corneal neovascularization induced by alkali burns, FK506 liposomes (0.2 mg/ml) promoted corneal epithelial healing, inhibited neovascularization, and reduced inflammation, exhibiting superior therapeutic efficacy compared to commercial FK506 eye drops (1 mg/ml) and the free drug (0.2 mg/ml) [22].

Nanomaterial and microparticle-based drug delivery strategies

PLGA nanoparticles (NPs) significantly enhanced the retention time of Ganoderma lucidum extract ((LZH))in corneal epithelial cells, increased cellular drug uptake, and demonstrated excellent delivery efficiency. LZH-NPs markedly reduced H₂O₂-induced ROS levels, indicating strong antioxidant activity. Furthermore, LZH-NPs significantly downregulated the expression of inflammation-related genes, such as TNF-α, IL-1α, IL-8, and MMP-3, thereby mitigating oxidative stress-induced inflammatory responses and confirming their anti-inflammatory properties. Additionally, LZH-NPs significantly reduced H₂O₂-induced apoptosis rates. In a rabbit CAB model, LZH-NPs treatment notably accelerated corneal epithelial healing, decreased corneal opacity, and reduced inflammatory cell infiltration, demonstrating promising therapeutic potential [84].A minocycline-loaded nano-hydroxyapatite/poly (lactic-co-glycolic acid) (nHAP/PLGA) nanoparticle-based microsphere drug delivery system effectively prevents CoNV in CAB by inhibiting pro-inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and TNF-α and promoting TGF-β expression. This system demonstrates both therapeutic efficacy and biosafety [13].Continuing with polymer-based approaches, the combination of PEG-PLGA polymer nanoparticles and hydrogels has been employed to enhance the delivery of anti-inflammatory agents. By utilizing gelatin and oxidized polysaccharide-based hydrogels with excellent injectability and self-healing properties, the convenience of this therapeutic strategy has been significantly improved [14].

Kaempferol (KAE) can be efficiently complexed with FDA-approved polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) K-17PF nanocomplexes to develop an aqueous ophthalmic solution known as 17PF-KAE.Studies showed that 17PF-KAE exhibited no significant cytotoxicity or ocular tissue toxicity in vivo. Additionally, it demonstrated enhanced antioxidant activity in vitro and anti-inflammatory effects in vivo. In a mouse model, topical application of 17PF-KAE significantly improved the therapeutic outcomes for corneal alkali burns compared to a free KAE solution. The mechanism of action was linked to the suppression of inflammation mediators (CD54, IL-6, and TGF-β1) and VEGF. These findings suggest that 17PF-KAE has potential as an innovative ophthalmic treatment for ocular diseases related to oxidative stress and inflammation [262].Resveratrol is a potent anti-VEGF agent for combating CoNV, but its hydrophobic nature and instability limit its use. To address this, a resveratrol-loaded ocular lamellar crystalline gel (ROLG) was developed for effective CoNV inhibition. Composed of resveratrol, glyceryl monooleate (GMO), ethanol, and water, ROLGs exhibited corneal permeation levels three times higher than resveratrol in hyaluronic acid suspensions (RHSs). Due to their strong retention on the corneal surface, ROLGs required only once-daily administration. Minimal ethanol exposure ensured corneal safety. In a rat model of alkali-induced corneal injury, ROLGs significantly inhibited CoNV by reducing corneal VEGF expression, leading to improved healing [230]. A novel dual-drug carrier was designed for the co-delivery of anti-inflammatory and antibiotic agents using core-shell nanofibers for treating corneal alkali burns. These nanofibers were fabricated through coaxial electrospinning, with curcumin-loaded silk fibroin forming the core and vancomycin-loaded chitosan/polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) as the shell. Extensive in vitro and in vivo studies confirmed the carrier’s ability to enhance corneal re-epithelialization, reduce inflammation, and suppress corneal neovascularization [56].

Introducing biomolecular innovations, tetrahedral framework nucleic acids(tFNAs), three-dimensional nanostructures self-assembled from four equimolar single-stranded DNA, have demonstrated extensive biological functions in areas such as bone formation, nerve regeneration, anti-inflammation, and antioxidation. A study showed that tFNAs significantly promote the proliferation and migration of corneal epithelial cells by activating the p38 and ERK1/2 signaling pathways, thereby accelerating healing after corneal alkali burns. By reducing corneal fibrosis and neovascularization, tFNAs exhibit great potential for treating corneal injuries [52] (Fig. 3 F).

Expanding into inorganic materials, researchers developed a topical nanoformulation for treating CAB by optimizing the surface roughness and therapeutic functionality of ceria nanocages, combined with the pharmacological effects of acetylcholine chloride and SB431542. The surface roughness of ceria nanocages enhances their therapeutic activity, while the poly(l-histidine) coating facilitates high corneal penetration and controlled dual drug release in response to pH changes from the chemical injury. The formulation shows good biocompatibility and sustains drug release for 4 days. In a rat alkali burn model, a single dose effectively alleviated corneal damage [50].

In contrast to the previously discussed nanomaterials, a drug-loaded collagen membrane (Dox-HCM/Col) was designed to enhance early corneal reconstruction. Doxycycline (Dox), an MMP inhibitor, was incorporated into the collagen membrane using hydroxypropyl chitosan microspheres (HCM), enabling controlled release and promoting epithelial regeneration. The results indicated that HCM extended the release period to 7 days, while Dox-HCM/Col significantly suppressed MMP-9 and MMP-13 expression, thereby accelerating corneal re-epithelialization and facilitating early-stage repair during the first week [263].

Future perspectives

Corneal alkali burns remain a significant clinical challenge, and while considerable progress has been made in understanding the pathophysiology and developing innovative therapeutic approaches, several areas require further exploration and improvement to enhance translational and clinical outcomes.

Standardization of animal models

The development and evaluation of novel therapies heavily rely on preclinical studies using animal models. However, significant variability exists in the methodologies employed across different laboratories. Factors such as the size of filter paper, alkali concentration, exposure time, volume of flushing solution, and flushing duration vary widely, making it difficult to compare results and replicate studies. Standardizing these parameters is crucial to ensure consistency and reliability in experimental outcomes. Researchers have suggested that the conventional NaOH-soaked flat filter paper method is inherently unreliable for murine corneal injury owing to the tissue’s high curvature—a limitation resolved by a novel modified biopsy punch technique that achieves precise, reproducible alkali burns to the curved cornea and limbus [264].Additionally, variations in the types and levels of inflammatory mediators observed between mild and severe alkali burn models highlight the need for stratified approaches when modeling and interpreting disease severity. Furthermore, most current animal models of CAB do not involve limbal damage, yet real-life injuries typically affect not only the transparent cornea but also the limbal area, highlighting the need for models that incorporate limbal injury to better mimic natural conditions.

Advancing research in nerve regeneration

Corneal nerve damage is a critical yet underexplored aspect of alkali burns. The loss of corneal innervation contributes to impaired wound healing, persistent inflammation, and reduced ocular surface integrity. Future research should prioritize understanding the mechanisms underlying nerve repair and exploring strategies to promote neuroregeneration. Advanced biomaterials, stem cell-based approaches, and neuroprotective agents hold promise in addressing this unmet need [90].

Emphasis on long-term outcomes and managing complications

Most current studies focus on the short-term effects of treatments following alkali burns, leaving the long-term outcomes underexplored. While significant progress has been made in the development of advanced drug delivery systems and cell-based therapies, these approaches still face notable limitations. Many studies provide only short-term observations, making it unclear whether these therapies sustain efficacy over time or adequately address chronic complications such as persistent inflammation, scarring, and neurotrophic keratopathy. Additionally, the long-term biocompatibility and degradation profiles of drug carriers, such as hydrogels, nanomicelles, and microspheres, remain insufficiently studied. It is essential to determine the optimal degradation rates to avoid residual material-induced toxicity or delayed inflammation.

Furthermore, future investigations should focus on the late-stage mechanisms of corneal repair and regeneration, with an emphasis on understanding interactions between the corneal epithelium, stroma, and nerves. Equally important is evaluating the long-term safety, efficacy, and environmental responsiveness of drug and cell delivery systems. Combining advanced materials with precise, sustained release strategies tailored for chronic injury repair may pave the way for therapies that effectively address acute damage while preventing late-stage complications such as fibrosis and neurotrophic keratopathy.

Although recent research increasingly evaluates retinal damage after alkali burns, studies on treatment strategies to protect the retina remain scarce [54, 55, 265].Severe alkali injuries can lead to long-term vision-threatening complications, including symblepharon [266]glaucoma [267]and permanent visual impairment. Studies have shown that CAB are associated with the development of glaucoma and significant degeneration of the retina and optic nerve [55]including RGC apoptosis [268]. Animal experiments suggest that this damage is likely mediated by TNF-α, rather than by the direct effects of the alkali on the retina or elevated IOP [32, 269]. Therefore, comprehensive assessments that include the posterior segment of the eye, in addition to corneal evaluations, are critical for determining the full scope of injury and therapeutic efficacy. Future research should prioritize strategies that address these posterior segment complications, integrating therapies that target both corneal and retinal protection to improve long-term visual outcomes.

Translating nanomedicine into clinical practice

Nanomedicine holds great promise for treating corneal alkali burns, with nanoformulations like nanoparticles, liposomes, micelles, and nanocomposites enabling targeted drug delivery and enhanced therapeutic efficacy. However, challenges such as complex manufacturing, reliance on non-FDA-approved materials, and strict sterility requirements for ocular delivery hinder clinical translation. Recent innovations highlight the potential of novel approaches, such as a blink-driven piezoelectric contact lens (BPCL), which converts natural blinking motions into electric fields to accelerate healing, significantly improving corneal clarity and healing rates in animal models [2]. Additionally, materials like Dextran Sulfate Polymer Wafer (DS wafer) demonstrate anti-inflammatory, anti-angiogenic, and anti-fibrotic effects, reducing key markers like IL-6, VEGFR1, and TGF-β in ocular burn models [39]. While these laboratory findings are encouraging, it is important to acknowledge the current lack of clinical data supporting the efficacy and safety of these emerging therapies, including the BPCL and DS wafer discussed here. Clinical trials and early-phase human studies are necessary to validate these promising preclinical results and address the practical challenges of clinical implementation. These advances underscore the need for safer, scalable, and FDA-compliant manufacturing to bring such breakthroughs closer to clinical application.

Conclusion

Corneal alkali burns represent a significant challenge in ophthalmic care, characterized by extensive tissue damage and a complex healing process driven by inflammation, oxidative stress, and neovascularization. Current treatments, including corticosteroids and surgical interventions, provide limited efficacy and carry risks of complications. Emerging therapies, such as stem cell-derived exosomes, hydrogel-based drug delivery systems, and herbal components, offer innovative solutions that address these limitations. Despite promising advancements, achieving optimal outcomes requires overcoming challenges such as low drug bioavailability and inflammation-induced fibrosis.

Moreover, CAB are an ophthalmic emergency requiring immediate irrigation, yet timely intervention is often delayed. There is a need to increase public awareness regarding the importance of early treatment, which is crucial in reducing the overall medical costs and improving public health outcomes. Effective DDS that can be applied directly in emergency settings, such as in the emergency room, hold promise for maximizing treatment efficacy. However, surgical interventions may face limitations due to varying medical conditions and resources across different regions, making them less feasible in many hospitals.

Future research should emphasize the development of multifunctional therapeutic strategies that integrate anti-inflammatory, anti-angiogenic, and regenerative properties. Combining biomaterials with cellular and molecular therapies holds the potential to significantly improve the management and prognosis of corneal alkali burn injuries, paving the way for enhanced clinical outcomes and improved quality of life for affected patients.

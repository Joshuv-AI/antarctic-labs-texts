Molecular characterization of a unique Recombinant form of HIV-1 subtype F2/CRF02_AG identified in Guangdong, China
Authors: Xuemei Ling, Yaqing Lin, Linghua Li, Junbin Li, Liting Zhang, Xizi Deng, Wenfang Luo, Jinwang Shi, Jiaojiao Li, Haisheng Yu, Fengyu Hu, Yun Lan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) pandemic continues to pose a significant global public health challenge. Recombination between HIV-1 variants generates unique recombinant forms (URFs) and then circulating recombinant forms (CRFs) after ongoing transmission. Subtype F2 and CRF02_AG mainly spread in western Africa, but they are rarely detected in China. Here, we observed a URF composed of subtypes F2 and CRF02_AG isolated from a man (230794) in Guangdong, China.MethodsThe near full-length genome (NFLG) of HIV-1 was amplified with two overlapping segments by the near endpoint dilution method. The positive products were sequenced directly to obtain genomic sequences. The recombinant pattern and breakpoints of the NFLG were determined using the Simplot software and confirmed by maximum likelihood (ML) trees for segments using the IQtree tool. The maximum clade credibility (MCC) tree was reconstructed to trace the origin by using BEAST software. Genotypic drug resistance data were interpreted by the Stanford HIV Drug Resistance Database. The co-receptor tropism was predicted using the geno2pheno coreceptor 2.5 tool, the WebPSSM tool and the HIVcoPRED tool.ResultsThe individual (230794) was 47 years old and got infected via heterosexual contact in Guangzhou City, Guangdong Province. The NFLG sequence of strain 230794 was 8260 nucleotides (nt) in length, with two CRF02_AG segments substituting in the backbone of subtype F2 in the vif and env gene regions. The genomic recombinant mosaic structures of 230794 were different from those of the other nine issued subtype F2/CRF02_AG URFs. No drug-resistant mutation was identified, but an INSTI other mutation, L74I, was identified. Strain 230794 was predicted to be a X4/R5 dual-tropism strain. Bayesian inference indicated that strain 230794 originated approximately between 2015 and 2018.ConclusionA novel URF of subtype F2/CRF02_AG was identified from a Cantonese man, with R5/X4 dual tropism. It is necessary to strengthen the molecular surveillance of the spread of recombinant strains in regions with high HIV-1 genetic diversity in China.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12985-025-02957-7.Keywords: HIV-1, Unique recombinant form, Phylogenetic inference, Guangdong

Background

Human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1)’s error-prone replication and frequent recombination drive its diverse variants [1]. To date, a total of 173 circulating recombinant forms (CRFs) have been identified and registered that originated from the 10 subtypes (A-D, F-H, and J-L) of HIV-1 group M in the HIV Sequence database (https://www.hiv.lanl.gov/components/sequence/HIV/search/search.html), and numerous unique recombinant forms (URFs) have also been identified [2]. URFs are generated in superinfected individuals, and some of URFs can be widely transmitted to others, resulting in the emergence of CRFs [3].

On the basis of a global and regional genetic diversity analysis of HIV-1 strains from 122 counties from 2010 to 2021 [4], CRF02_AG accounted for 6.6% of global HIV infections, whereas subtype F represented a smaller percentage, at 0.9%. The CRF02_AG variant predominates in West African countries such as Ghana and West Central African countries such as Cameroon, where it accounts for approximately 50% of HIV-1 infections, but this variant is rarely detected in other African regions [5–10]. Within the last two decades, subtype F2 infections have steadily increased in Cameroon and spread to West and Central Africa. This expansion likely contributed to its involvement in emerging recombinant forms [10]. However, in China, CRF02_AG has rarely been reported, and subtype F2 has been virtually unreported. According to previous reports, CRF02_AG was detected in 3.66% [11], 2.9% [12], and 0.6% [13] of different study populations in China. Moreover, no pure subtype F2 has been reported in China, and only one migrant case of infection with a URF composed of subtypes C/F2/A1 has been reported in Jiaxing, Zhejiang Province [14].

Guangdong Province, a southeastern coastal region of China with high HIV-1 genetic diversity, has a high proportion of transient individuals and experiences rapid and complex genotype expansion among high-risk populations [15]. Guangdong Province is also a hotspot for HIV-1 recombination, with many CRFs (such as CRF55_01B, CRF126_0755, and CRF162_cpx) emerging here [2, 16]. Among 157 HIV-1-seropositive individuals in Guangdong Province from January to September 2009, CRF02_AG accounted for 1.9% (3/157) of infections [17], whereas among 114 foreign HIV-1-infected individuals in Guangzhou City between 2008 - 2010 and 2015, CRF02_AG accounted for 30.7% (35/114) of infections [18]. Moreover, neither subtype F2 nor CRF02_AG-containing recombinant forms have been previously reported in this region. In this study, we obtained the near full-length genome (NFLG) sequence of a subtype F2/CRF02_AG recombinant strain from an HIV-infected patient residing in Guangdong, China, elucidated the genomic composition of strain 230794, and traced the origin through molecular epidemiological techniques.

Methods

Ethical issues

This study was approved by the institutional review board of Guangzhou Eighth People’s Hospital (No. 202353290), and the individual provided written informed consent.

Source of the specimen

In the previous drug resistance surveillance among 5602 HIV-1-infected antiretroviral therapy (ART)-naïve individuals in Guangdong Province from Jan. 2023 to Dec. 2023, we identified one partial pol gene sequence in a plasma specimen collected from an HIV-1-infected patient, 230794, as a suspected recombinant of subtype F2 and CRF02_AG, two subtypes of HIV that are very rare in China and even worldwide. Specimen 230794 was collected in Oct. 2023 from a 47-year-old man who claimed to have contracted HIV-1 through heterosexual contact in Guangzhou City, Guandong Province, China. His spouse was negative for HIV-1. The baseline HIV-1 viral load was 3.95 × 10^5 IU/mL, and the CD4+ T-cell count was 77 cells/µL. These values indicated that this patient was in the advanced stage of HIV-1 infection, which had not been identified and treated in a timely manner, resulting in severe immune system damage.

Nucleic acid extraction, amplification, sequencing, and sequence assembly

Viral RNA was extracted from the 2023 plasma sample using a magnetic bead-based method. RNA was reverse transcribed to cDNA using the SuperScript III System. Proviral DNA was extracted from the whole-blood sample collected in 2025 with the Viral DNA Extraction Kit (Supbio, China). Two overlapping halves of the HIV-1 genome were subsequently amplified by nested polymerase chain reaction (PCR) by utilizing LA Taq (TaKaRa, China) with a near endpoint dilution method as described previously [19]. Positive products were directly purified and sequenced by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai) Co., Ltd with Sanger sequencing method. The NFLG was assembled and cleaned by the software Sequencher V5.1 (Gene Codes, United States). The obtained NFLG sequence was examined with the Quality Control Tool in the HIV database (https://www.hiv.lanl.gov/content/sequence/QC/index.html) to check for potential contamination and confirm sequence quality thereby excluding the occurrence of stop codons, frameshifts, non-ACGT characters, hypermutations, and other such issues.

Phylogenetic and recombination breakpoints analysis of sequences

The neighbor-joining (NJ) phylogenetic tree of the NFLG was constructed using the Kimura 2-parameter model with 1000 bootstrap replications in Mega software V11.0.11 with reference sequences downloaded from the Los Alamos HIV database (https://www.hiv.lanl.gov/content/index), including 10 subtypes (A-D, F–H, and J-L), CRF01_AE, CRF02_AG, CRF07_BC, and group N.

A preliminary analysis of recombination patterns was conducted using the online tools of the LANL HIV sequence database, including jumping profile Hidden Markov Model (jpHMM) [20] and Recombinant Identification Program (RIP) with a window size of 400 bp. Similarity and BootScan analyses were performed by using SimPlot software V3.5 to further investigate the characteristics of the recombinant form.

Subregion maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic trees were constructed with the appropriate addition of parental subtype sequences as references to validate the recombinant form. The ML phylogenetic trees were constructed by using the general time reversible (GTR) substitution model with 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replications to assess the reliability of the internal branches with IQtree [21] (https://www.hiv.lanl.gov/content/sequence/IQTREE/iqtree.html).

The recombination pattern diagram was drawn with the Recombinant HIV-1 Drawing Tool [22] in the Los Alamos HIV Sequence Database (https://www.hiv.lanl.gov/content/sequence/DRAW_CRF/recom_mapper.html) according to the corresponding positions of the international standard strain HXB2.

Analysis of the source of parental strains

To estimate the origin of strain 230794, Bayesian molecular clock analyses were performed. The quasispecies sequences obtained from plasma and whole-blood samples collected at different time points were used to explore the times of most recent common ancestors (tMRCAs) using the Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) inference implemented in BEAST software V1.10 [23]. The MCMC evolutionary analyses were performed for 2 × 10^8 steps with sampling every 5000 steps under the GTR nucleotide substitution model, the uncorrelated relaxed clock model, and the Bayesian skyline prior mode. The subtype F2 and CRF02_AG sequences worldwide were downloaded from the HIV database and selected to reconstruct the topology of the maximum clade credibility (MCC) tree. The convergence of parameters was checked using Tracer v1.7.1 and the effective sample size (ESS) value was >200. The initial 10% of the states of each run were burn-in using TreeAnnotator 1.10.4. The MCC trees were visualized and edited in FigTree 1.4.0 [24].

Drug resistance and genotypic co-receptor analysis

The HIVdb program from the Stanford HIV Drug Resistance Database (https://hivdb.stanford.edu/hivdb/by-sequences/) was used to analyse the protease inhibitors (PIs), nucleoside reverse-transcription inhibitors (NRTIs), non-nucleoside reverse-transcription inhibitors (NNRTIs), integrase strand transfer inhibitors (INSTIs) and drug resistance mutations (DRMs). The V3-loop sequence of the NFLG was obtained using the GeneCutter tool. The co-receptor tropism was predicted using the geno2pheno coreceptor 2.5 tool (http://coreceptor.geno2pheno.org/) with a 5% false-positive rate (FPR) threshold (recommended for CRF02_AG strains) [25], the WebPSSM[x4r5] tool (https://indra.mullins.microbiol.washington.edu/webpssm/) [26] and the HIVcoPRED tool (https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/hivcopred/submit.html) [27].

Results

Common characteristics and phylogenetic analysis of the NFLG sequence

The NFLG sequence of 230794, which was 8260 nucleotides (nt) in length, was obtained and submitted to GenBank (accession number PV187006).

In the NJ phylogenetic tree reconstructed with full-length genome sequences, strain 230794 formed a distinct branch within the subtype F clade (bootstrap value = 100%), as illustrated in Fig. 1A. Phylogenetic analysis revealed a close topological relationship among strain 230794, subtype F1 and subtype F2, indicating that strain 230794 likely represents a novel recombinant variant. After submitting the NFLG sequence to the HIV BLAST tool in the HIV database (https://www.hiv.lanl.gov/content/sequence/BASIC_BLAST/basic_blast.html), the two most similar sequences to 230794, MK086126, and MK086128 were both subtype F2/CRF02_AG recombinant strains with identities of 87%, indicating that 230794 might be a subtype F2/CRF02_AG recombinant virus.

Recombinant breakpoint and subregion phylogenetic analysis

Recombination analysis using SimPlot and BootScan showed that strain 230794 shares high sequence similarity with subtype F2 and CRF02_AG across different genomic regions. The whole genome was divided into six regions with five breakpoints according to the HXB2 genome as follows: segment Ⅰ (760–3053 nt) subtype F2, segment Ⅱ (3234–4737 nt) subtype F2, segment Ⅲ (4738–4967 nt) CRF02_AG, segment Ⅳ (4968–6419 nt) subtype F2, segment Ⅴ (6420–8385 nt) CRF02_AG, and Segment Ⅵ (8386–9019 nt) subtype F2 (Fig. 1B and C). Notably, the fragment (3144 ± 90 nt) was a poor resolution area between subtype F2 and CRF02_AG, and thus was not analyzed.

Further ML phylogenetic trees analysis of the six subregions were performed by IQtree to confirm the positions of the recombinant breakpoints. As shown in the ML phylogenetic trees, segment I, segment II, segment IV, and segment VI of 230794 were clustered with subtype F2 sequences with high bootstrap values (100% for segment I/II/IV and 97% for segment VI), and segment III and Segment V of 230794 were clustered with CRF02_AG sequences with bootstrap values of 76% (the limited length of segment III prevented the attainment of a significant bootstrap value and phylogenetic topology structure) and 97%, respectively (Fig. 2).

Subregion maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic analysis and maximum clade credibility (MCC) trees of HIV-1 strain 230794. All the subregion ML trees were reconstructed using the same references, including an N group sequence, twelve subtypes (A1, A2, B-D, F1, F2, G-H, and J-L) and CRF02_AG sequences. The subregion ML trees were constructed using the general time reversible (GTR) substitution model with 1000 ultrafast bootstrap replications to assess the reliability of the internal branches in the IQtree tool. More than 70% of the bootstrap values were categorized as reliable clusters and labelled at the nodes. Subregion phylogenetic analyses indicated that segments I, II, IV and VI of strain 230794 belonged to subtype F2. Segments Ⅲ and Ⅴ were clustered with the CRF02_AG cluster

These results confirmed that 230794 was indeed a URF of the subtype F2 and CRF02_AG recombination. The genome mosaic map of the 230794 NFLG was obtained by using the Recombinant HIV-1 Drawing Tool, as shown in Fig. 1D.

To date, another 9 NFLGs associated with the recombination of CRF02_AG and subtype F2 [8–10] have been submitted to GenBank according to the Los Alamos HIV sequence database, all of which differ from the breakpoints of strain 230794 after comparison (as shown in Fig. 3 and Supplementary Table 1).

The source of the parental strains

From the 2023 plasma sample, we successfully amplified and sequenced four SGA sequences for the 5’ half-genome and the 3’ half-genome, respectively. From the 2025 whole blood sample, we successfully amplified and sequenced three SGA sequences from the 5’ half-genome and the 3’ half-genome, respectively. Owing to the limited number of NFLGs of subtype F2 in the HIV sequence database (a total of 14), we selected the partial-length F2 sequences (the partial pol gene corresponding to HXB2 2253–3053 nt) based on the breakpoint of strain 230794 as the reference strains for Bayesian evolutionary analysis of the subtype F2. On the basis of the sequence quality and the principle of one sequence per patient, 127 subtype F2 reference sequences were identified. Similarly, 283 CRF02_AG sequences (the partial env gene corresponding to 6420–8384 nt of HXB2, including 8 sequences from China) were selected as reference strains for Bayesian analysis of the CRF02_AG fragment.

According to the MCC tree, the estimated tMRCAs for the subtype F2 segment and CRF02_AG segment were 2018.87 [95% highest probability density (HPD): 2015.85, 2021.38] and 2015.05 (95% HPD: 2012.00, 2017.60) (Fig. 4), respectively. Moreover, two subtype F2 sequences from Canada (CAN2013-223 and CAN2013-227) and one CRF02_AG sequence from the Republic of Cyprus (CY576) clustered with 230794, with posterior values of 0.9982 and 0.9861, respectively. Moreover, no reliable cluster was reconstructed among 230794 and eight CRF02_AG sequences from China.

Prediction of drug resistance mutations and tropism

No major/accessory mutations conferring resistance to PIs, NRTIs, NNRTIs, or INSTIs were identified, therefore, the strain 230794 was susceptible to 26 drugs in four categories (as shown in Supplementary Table 2). However, the strain 230794 harbors an INSTI other mutation, L74I, which is highly polymorphic and is not more common in individuals receiving INSTIs than in INSTI-naïve individuals [28]. The V3 loop amino acid sequence was identified as “CTRPGNNTRKGVHIGPGRAFYTM-DITGDIRQAFC”. Genotypic co-receptor analysis using Geno2pheno (5% FPR threshold) and the WebPSSM tool predicted exclusive CCR5 tropism, whereas the HIVcoPRED tool predicted CXCR4 usage. Considering the reported low sensitivity of V3 sequence-based prediction tools for non-B subtypes [29], genotypic tools alone may not reliably exclude CXCR4 variants, therefore, strain 230794 was classified as a potentially X4/R5 dual-tropic strain.

Discussion

Subtype F2 and CRF02_AG are prevalent mainly in western Africa but are rarely detected in China. CRFs consisting of subtype F2 and CRF02_AG have not yet emerged. In this study, we identified a URF composed of subtypes F2 and CRF02_AG from a specimen isolated in Guangdong Province. Analysis of the genomic recombinant mosaic structures revealed that the breakpoints of strain 230794 and the other nine reported recombinants of subtype F2/CRF02_AG were various (Fig. 3). In the gag gene region, nine of the recombinants consisted of the pure subtype F2 or pure CRF02_AG, and only one of them (MK086112) contained components of subtype F2 and CRF02_AG. In the pol gene region, nine of the strains presented different breakpoint locations, with insertions of the CRF02_AG fragment (MK086112, MK086120, MK086126, and PV187006) or insertions of the subtype F2 fragment (KR017771, MK086115, and MK086117-119). In addition, three of the strains had insertions at the initial position of the pol region and six of them had insertions at the C-terminus of the pol region. In the env gene region, only one strain (MK086126) had breakpoints with insertions of CRF02_AG fragments into the subtype F2 backbone. These findings indicate that conventional pol gene drug resistance surveillance may be helpful in identifying subtype F2/CRF02_AG recombinant strains.

Strain 230794 harbors the subtype-specific INSTI mutation, L74I. L74I is a conserved amino acid of subtype A, and some studies have shown that L74I is an important mutation site for the integrase resistance of the A6 subtype virus, which may affect the sensitivity to INSTI [30, 31]. A West African study revealed a high proportion of L74I in CRF02_AG [32]. Whether L74I in CRF02_AG affects INSTI resistance can be confirmed through additional in vitro resistance experiments. For patient 230794, in Jan 2024 (three months after ART), the HIV-1 RNA viral load test result was 369 IU/mL. In Apr 2024, Oct 2024 and Apr 2025, the patient got successful suppression, with the HIV-1 RNA viral load below the detection limit. These findings suggested that the presence of the mutation L74I may not affect the efficacy of bictegravir, emtricitabine and tenofovir alafenamide (BIC + FTC + TAF) combination treatment.

In a previous study of a large cohort of antiretroviral-naive individuals in Canada, it was shown that participants with R5/X4 HIV-1 had the poorest clinical profiles, with the highest plasma viral loads, and the lowest CD4 cell counts and were more likely to develop acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS)-defining illness before therapy initiation than participants harboring only R5-tropic HIV-1 strains [33]. The baseline CD4+ T-cell count of patient 230794 was critically low at 77 cells/µL, which is consistent with the clinical profile of R5/X4 HIV-1 infection. Following ART initiation, the CD4+ T-cell counts gradually increased, reaching 192, 148, 203, 230, 253 and 191 cells/µL at 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 and 18 months post-ART, respectively. While the observed CD4+ T-cell count recovery indicates the therapeutic efficacy of ART, the decelerated progression after the initial 6 months suggests potential constraints in immune reconstitution. This suboptimal recovery underscores the detrimental impact of R5/X4 HIV-1, particularly the CXCR4-tropic variants, which may exacerbate chronic immune activation, suppress naive T-cell production and promote apoptosis [34].

According to national treatment strategies in China, the HIV patients on ART should be followed up to evaluate the ART efficacy. The patient 230794 obtained successful viral suppression with plasma viral load below the detection limit in 2025, therefore, we chose to harvest viral DNA from the whole blood sample. By comparing the genetic sequences obtained in 2023 and 2025, it was found that they were largely genetically similar, without obvious difference in the recombination forms. On the basis of time-scaled phylogenetic analyses, the tMRCAs of the subtype F2 and CRF02_AG segments were estimated at 2018.87 and 2015.05, respectively. This temporal overlap suggests that HIV-1 strain 230794 likely emerged during the 2015–2018 period through recombination events between these circulating lineages. Analysis of the ML tree reconstructed using the partial pol gene region of HIV-1 strain 230794 and all 127 subtype F2 reference sequences downloaded from the HIV sequence database (coordinate to HXB2 2253–3272) revealed evolutionary clustering between the subtype F2 parental lineage of HIV-1 strain 230794 and two Canadian-derived sequences (bootstrap value = 100%, Supplementary Fig. 1). In the 2000 s, armed conflicts in Africa led to an influx of newcomers from HIV-endemic areas (including Cameroon, Congo and Rwanda) into Canada [35], which may explain the introduction of non-clade B viruses into Canada. Therefore, the subtype F2 parental lineage of HIV-1 strain 230794 might have originated from Africa. According to the ML tree reconstructed using the partial env gene region of HIV-1 strain 230794 and 283 globally representative CRF02_AG reference sequences downloaded from the HIV sequence database (coordinate to HXB2 6420–8384), the CRF02_AG parental lineage of the HIV-1 strain 230794 monophyletic clade (bootstrap value = 100%) completely segregated from all Chinese sequences and showed no significant clustering with any other CRF02_AG reference sequences (Supplementary Fig. 2), suggesting non-Chinese origins for the CRF02_AG progenitor of this recombinant strain. According to the in-depth epidemiological investigation, patient 230794 claimed that he had never been abroad. He self-reported having several heterosexual sexual partners, but in the following tracking, we failed to locate his former partners and confirm their HIV-1 status. Therefore, we speculated that HIV-1 strain 230794 may be a recombinant strain from abroad rather than a local strain prevalent in Guangdong, China.

In light of the molecular prevalence of subtypes F2 and CRF02_AG in Guangdong and their predominance in foreign countries, the strain was most likely introduced into China through travel or prosperous trade between Guangdong and foreign countries. HIV-1 genetic diversity poses major challenges to many aspects, including HIV vaccine development pathogenicity, ART efficacy, drug resistance, and diagnostic assays [4]. The current spouse of patient 230794 tested negative for HIV-1 antibodies, and no related recombinant strains were detected in our conventional pol gene drug resistance surveillance after 2023. Although the imported subtype F2/CRF02_AG recombinant strain may not have spread further, continuous follow-up of this case is necessary. Moreover, testing services and molecular surveillance should be performed for migrants or foreign travelers, and effective interventions can be tailored to infections to prevent the potential spread of new HIV variants.

Our study has certain limitations. Firstly, the fragment (3144 ± 90 nt) did not get further phylogenetic analysis, for its too short (only 180 nucleotides) in length with low sequence similarity, ambiguous signals or complex recombination scenarios. This may lead to deviations in breakpoint determination, hard to judge recombination patterns and track the possible parental strains. Secondly, the pol gene was routinely amplified and harvested for drug resistance surveillance before the initial ART in China. It may miss a proportion of recombinant strains, since genetic recombination can occur throughout the HIV-1 genome, and more evidence support the recombination events involving regions outside of pol, such as env or gag. Thirdly, we only got 4 or 3 single genome sequences of half NFLG for the two samples respectively, and they may not exhaustively capture the full profiles of the viral quasispecies. What’s more, the tropism prediction is conducted using genotypic rather than phenotypic drug resistance testing, which may entail a certain risk of false negatives or false positives. Finally, while our phylogenetic clustering analysis supports the inference that the parental lineage of subtype F2 likely originated in Africa and that the CRF02_AG lineage is non-Chinese, these interpretations must be considered in light of significant limitations: (1) our analysis is constrained by sampling bias. Genomic surveillance efforts are unevenly distributed globally that could harbor unsampled diversity relevant to the origins of these lineages. (2) the incompleteness of available sequence databases could lead to incomplete or potentially misleading inferences regarding viral origins and migration pathways.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study revealed a URF derived from subtype F2 and CRF02_AG. The recombinant structure consisted of a subtype F2 backbone and two fragments of CRF02_AG identified among HIV-infected patients in Guangdong, China. This report is the first of a full-length genome composed of these two subtypes in China, suggesting increased HIV genetic complexity in Guangdong Province. The identification of a new recombinant form may indicate the presence and transmission of several cocirculating lineages in Guangdong, possibly contributing to the emergence of new CRFs. Meanwhile, strong associations between R5/X4 HIV-1 dual tropism and fast clinical disease progression, including significantly lower CD4 cell counts, confirm the well-characterized link between X4 HIV-1 and poorer prognosis.

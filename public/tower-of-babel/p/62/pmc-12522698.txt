Updating [Nutritional composition dataset of approved infant formula powder in China (2017–2024)] with [Nutritional composition dataset of approved older infant formula powder in China (2017–2024)]
Authors: Jun Cai, Shuguang Li, Qing Wei, Hong Wang, Yaguang Jiang, Jiao Jiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractOlder infant formula powder, which can meet some of the nutritional needs of infants aged 6 to 12 months, is suitable for normal older infants. In this context, China has established a stringent registration-based regulatory system for infant formula products, requiring mandatory pre-market approval and comprehensive evaluation of all formulations before commercialization. The primary objective of this dataset was the comparative profiling of nutrient content in domestic and imported older infant formula products. The complete registration dossiers of 786 older infant formula powder from 2017 to 2024, officially disclosed on the Special Food Information Query Platform of China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) were systematically retrieved and collected, and verified by the nutritional values on the product packing or on the websites of the different brands. The nutritional values are systematically presented for 100 kJ and 100 g of powder. The nutrient content in domestic and imported were also statistically calculated and presented, the analysis revealed general compliance with national standards with minor variations between domestic and imported products. This dataset can be applied as a tool for data-driven insights for older infant formula optimization, aligning with China's updated national standards for precise dietary management, or to assist Chinese consumers in selecting appropriate older infant formula milk powder products.Keywords: Older infant formula powder, Nutrient content, Domestic and imported, China

Background

Infant and toddler food (ITF), typically including powdered infant and follow-up formula (PIFF), plays a pivotal role as the principal alternative nutrition source for human milk, satisfying the essential nutritional demands in the growth and development of children less than three years old [[1], [2], [3]]. China has established a stringent registration-based regulatory system for infant formula products, requiring mandatory pre-market approval and comprehensive evaluation of all formulations before commercialization. In 2021, substantial revisions were made to the national food safety standards, superseding the 2010 regulation GB 10,767–2010 (older infants and young children formula) by GB 10,766–2021 (older infant formula) and GB 10,767–2021(young children formula) [[4], [5], [6]]. Following their formal implementation on February 22, 2023, all manufacturers were required to reformulate products to fully comply with the updated nutritional and safety specifications [7]. As the dataset of follow-on infant formulas commercialized worldwide in 2021, and the nutritional composition dataset of approved infant formula powder in China (2017–2024), we try to construct this nutritional composition dataset of approved older infant formula powder in China (2017–2024) [8,9]. This dataset systematically collected and analysed registration information for 786 older infant formula powder from 2017 to 2024, officially disclosed by China’s State Administration for Market Regulation, and verified by the nutritional values on the product packing or on the websites of the different brands. Compliance assessments were conducted according to current standard GB 10,766–2021. Comparative analyses of nutrient content were then performed between regulatory-compliant domestic formulas and imported formulas.

Data Description

This dataset was generated to provide an overview of nutrient content of registration-approved 786 infant formula powder from 2017 to 2024 in China. The compliance assessments of all the 786 formulas were conducted according to current standard GB 10,766–2021, and comparative analyses of nutrient content were also performed between regulatory-compliant domestic formulas and imported formulas (Table 1), and the regulatory compliance (Compliant or noncompliant) details were described in Table 2. For domestic formulas, the number of compliant and non-compliant products were 290 and 332 respectively and for imported formulas, the corresponding numbers were 73 and 91 respectively. This overview was also intended to be a tool for data-driven insights for formula optimization, or to assist Chinese consumers in selecting appropriate older infant formula milk powder products.

Organization and information provided in the “Nutritional values-S2.xlsx” file.

The dataset is provided as a text file(.txt) and three Spreadsheet(.xlsx) files, each including one sheet:

Summary-S2.txt provides information about general information (Dataset title, contact email and Keywords), the aim and the method used to generate this dataset, data and file overview, and missing data codes. The “aim of the dataset” section describes the objective, the “Selection criteria” section lists the two selection criteria for data inclusion, and the “Data collection method” section defines the data collection method used. The “Nutritional values” file lists Infant formula nutritional values, the “Overview” file summarized the data, and the “Examples of regulation” file lists Information concerning the regulation specificities about older infant formulas.

Nutritional values-S2.xlsx provides information about the product ID, the age group in which the product is consumed, the registration type (Domestic registration or imported registration), the protein origin (Milk powder or Goat milk powder), the regulatory compliance (Compliant or noncompliant), the list of nutritional values per 100 g of powder or 100 kJ. This file is organized as described in Table 2.

Overview-S2.xlsx provides information about the statistically calculated nutritional values of domestic formulas and imported older infant formulas, sample count, minimal values, maximal values and average value per 100 g of powder or 100 kJ.

Examples of regulation-S2.xlsx provides information about the content range of nutritional values for older infant formula milk powder in China governed by the mandatory national standards GB 10,766–2021 or other regulations.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The data were retrieved, collected, verified and statistically calculated according to a four-phase method. In a first step, selection criteria, designed for infants aged 6 to 12 months, were established. Thus, the products included in the dataset had to be older infant formula powder. In a second step, the registration information for older infant formula powder from 2017 to 2024, officially disclosed by China’s State Administration for Market Regulation were retrieved and collected. In a third step, the nutritional values were verified, either via a web scraping method (information collected on websites either of brands or merchants trading products) or directly on the product packing (data collected from product labels). In cases of discrepancy between the registration data and publicly available information, the values from the registration data were prioritized. Compliance assessments of all the 786 formulas were conducted according to current standard GB 10,766–2021. In a final step, data was anonymized by attaching a product ID to each formula and then structured to form the dataset. The lists of nutritional values were standardized in order to preserve the anonymization and to present the data systematically in the same format for 100 kJ and 100 g of powder. In each list of nutritional values, data were extracted when they were indicated, and the nutrient content in domestic and imported were also statistically calculated and presented.

Limitations

The data included in the dataset in subject to change, particularly in the case of regulatory changes, and therefore needs to be regularly updated. Data standardization challenges arising from inconsistent reporting formats, frequent unrecorded formula reformulations, and evolving GB standards (notably GB 10,766–2021) constrained cross-year comparability, while manufacturer batch-level data scarcity further limited variability assessment. These factors necessitate ongoing curation efforts.

A comprehensive review of small molecule drugs approved by the FDA in 2025: advance and prospect
Authors: Yi-Ru Bai, Ruifang Li, Xin-Hui Zhang, Dong-Jie Seng, Ying Xu, Sai-Yang Zhang, Shuo Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn 2025, the FDA approved 46 drug marketing applications, 31 of which were small molecule drugs. Despite the advancements in biotechnologies such as antibody drugs, RNA-based treatments, and antibody-drug conjugates, small molecule drugs remain dominant in new drug discovery. With progress in computer-aided drug design and scaffold-based drug design etc, modern drug discovery has stepped into a phase of rapid development. The marketed drugs in the same field often share similar structures and biological activities, while development of me-too drugs can notably enhance potency and streamline the development process. Comprehending the development process of newly launched drugs helps to identify mainstream technologies and provides structural scaffolds and inspirations for future research. This review comprehensively summarises the development progress of new drugs approved in 2025, including molecular design, structural modification, structure-activity relationship, and enhancement of drug-like properties to offer valuable insights to pharmaceutical chemists and bring inspiration for future research.

Introduction

In 2025, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved 46 drug marketing applications, encompassing 15 biological products and 31 new molecular entities1–3. These drugs were primarily concentrated on the anti-tumour field, accompanied by notable increases in approvals for genetic diseases and immunotherapy. Additionally, the approval records in the fields of anti-infection, cardiovascular diseases, respiratory system, and endocrinology remained comparable to previous years. However, there seemed to be a lack of approvals for contrast agents, imaging agents, and other diagnostic reagents. A comprehensive analysis of the development process of marketed drugs revealed that the majority were derived from existing drugs or modified from active molecules4–6. For example, gepotidacin enhanced the biological activity through replacing the quinazoline in GSK299423 with a tricyclic ring (Table 1). Zoliflodacin improved therapeutic efficacy by introducing an oxazolidinone substituent into the structure of PNU-286607. Mirdametinib increased the binding ability and solubility by substituting the cyclopropane in Cl-1040 with ethylene glycol. Vimseltinib notably enhanced selectivity by substituting the pyrimidine in pexidartinib with a pyrimidinone. Sunvozertinib overcame drug resistance mutations by substituting the indole at the C4 position with a substituted aniline. These approved cases not only confirmed the feasibility of developing new drugs based on clinical-stage and marketed drugs but also demonstrated the value of comprehensively analysing the design and research ideas of marketed drugs7,8.

The development of new drugs is highly empirical and exploratory, with newly approved drugs serving as huge treasure trove that enhance the research quality and quicken the pace of drug discovery9. Retrospective analysis of drug development provides the functional and valuable scaffold, contributes to the synthesis process optimisation of active pharmaceutical ingredients, and offers guidance for the development of similar or other types of drugs10. For instance, the successful cases expedite the development of new drugs via strategies such as fragment-based drug design, structure-based drug design, and pharmacophore fusion11,12. The privileged scaffolds are benefit to the discovery of me-too drugs, with the emerging and development experiences such as the introduction of magic methyl, cyclisation strategies, and prodrug design will facilitate drug discovery by improving selectivity, pharmacokinetic properties, toxicity, side effects, efficacy, and drug resistance13,14. Moreover, integrating the empirical strategies into the emerging artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted drug design training models will promote the development of emerging technologies, including the prediction of protein binding sites, drug structure design, and the optimisation of drug synthesis routes15,16. As our previous reports in recent years17–19, this review conducts a comprehensive analysis of the drug molecules approved by the U.S. FDA in 2025, covering aspects such as drug design, molecular docking, mechanism of action, and structural modification, aiming to provide valuable insights for pharmaceutical chemists and facilitate new drug discovery.

Anti-infective drug

Gepotidacin (Blujepa)

Gepotidacin was the first-in-class oral triazaacenaphthylene antibiotic developed by GlaxoSmithKline (GSK), which received FDA approval for the treatment of uncomplicated urinary tract infections51. Gepotidacin effectively reduced the risk of drug resistance by inhibiting the bacterial DNA replication enzymes DNA gyrase subunit A (GyrA) and topoisomerase IV subunit C (ParC), thus establishing a dual action barrier. In the EAGLE Phase III trial, the symptom relief rate was comparable to nitrofurantoin, with about 50% of patients showed no infection after treatment. It was the first antibiotic targeting WHO-listed critical pathogens to receive priority review in response to the global antimicrobial resistance (AMR) crisis.

Gepotidacin was a structurally modified derivative of the lead compound GSK299423, which selectively inhibited DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV by forming a ternary complex with DNA and two GyrA or ParC subunits (Figure 1(A))52,53. The left-hand side (LHS) contained a polar tricyclic structure that intercalated between DNA bases, influencing the orientation of the linker and right-hand side (RHS). The basic amino of linker interacted with GyrA/ParC subunits, while the RHS bound to the hydrophobic pocket of bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. The co-crystallisation studies showed that modifying the terminal ring systems enhanced compound binding, contributing to broad-spectrum activity against Gram-positive and Gram-negative pathogens (Figure 1(B)). Further structural modifications should be made to optimise the pharmacophores at both ends while screening the influence of different types of linkers on activity.

Zoliflodacin (Nuzolvence)

Zoliflodacin was the first-in-class antibiotic approved by the FDA in December 2025 for the treatment of uncomplicated genital and urinary tract gonorrhea54. As a type II topoisomerase inhibitor, zoliflodacin killed the pathogen by blocking the function of bacterial DNA topoisomerase, thereby interfering the DNA replication and repair of Neisseria gonorrhoeae.

PNU-286607, the precursor of zoliflodacin, was identified as a hit compound through high-throughput screening for antibacterial activity (Figure 2(A))55,56. AstraZeneca discovered that replacing its nitro group with a benzisoxazole enabled structural modifications. Entasis Therapeutics conducted extensive optimisation to develop zoliflodacin. The molecular docking showed that both compounds shared similar binding modes, with the new oxazolidinone forming enhanced hydrogen bonds with Arg-458 to improve activity (Figure 2(B)). Future efforts should focus on screening alternative substituents for the benzisoxazole and oxazolidinone moieties.

(A) The original discovery of zoliflodacin. (B) The binding models of PNU-286607 and zoliflodacin with gyrase (PDB code: 8BP2, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Anti-tumour drug

Treosulfan (Grafapex)

Treosulfan was approved by the U.S. FDA in January 2025 to be used in combination with fludarabine as a conditioning regimen prior to allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation in patients with acute myeloid leukaemia or myelodysplastic syndrome57. Treosulfan was an alkylating agent that eliminated abnormal cells and suppressed the immune response by disrupting the DNA structure. The key clinical trials demonstrated significant efficacy, with a 2-year event-free survival rate of 64.0%, which was significantly higher than 50.4% in the control group. Meanwhile, the transplant-related mortality rate and non-relapse mortality rate were reduced by 46% and 40% respectively. This drug provided a safer option for pre-transplantation conditioning in paediatric patients, with the lower age limit decreased to 1 year old.

Although treosulfan could be considered a derivative of busulfan, the primary active components responsible for the alkylating effects in the two substances were not identical (Figure 3) 58. Treosulfan primarily enhanced the efficiency of DNA cross-linking via the electrophilic diepoxyethane metabolite, which ultimately led to DNA structural distortion and cell apoptosis.

Mirdametinib (Gomekli)

In February 2025, mirdametinib was approved by the FDA for treating neurofibromatosis type 1 in patients with symptomatic plexiform neurofibromas59. Mirdametinib inhibited downstream phosphorylation of the extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) pathway via the inhibition of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) kinases 1 and 2 (MEK1/2), thereby blocking tumour cell proliferation. In the ReNeu (NCT03962543) study, the primary endpoint was the confirmed objective response rate (ORR), with an ORR of 41% in adults and 52% in children.

The lead compound of mirdametinib was Cl-1040 that was designed to treat various tumours (Figure 4(A)) 60. However, the poor solubility and low blood concentration resulted in the failure of clinical trials. In order to enhance solubility and oral exposure, Pfizer substituted the cyclopropyl group of Cl − 1040 with an ethylene glycol structure, thereby obtaining mirdametinib with significantly improved activity and drugability. The molecular docking analysis indicated that Cl-1040 and mirdametinib had comparable binding models, with merely a minor alteration in the orientation of the ethylene glycol side chain (Figure 4(B)). In addition, compared with Cl-1040, miradametinib showed better compatibility with the MEK-1 protein. The approval of mirdametinib further validated the druggable potential of the ortho-aminobenzamide structure.

(A) The original discovery of mirdametinib. (B) The binding models of Cl-1040 and mirdametinib with MEK-1 (PDB code: 7JUU, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Vimseltinib (Romvimza)

Vimseltinib, an oral colony stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) inhibitor, was utilised for the treatment of tenosynovial giant cell tumour (TGCT)61. Vimseltinib blocked downstream signalling pathways like MAPK and phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B (PI3K/AKT) by inhibiting the binding of CSF1R with CSF1 and interleukin-34 (IL-34), which consequently reduced inflammatory mediator production and suppressed macrophage activity. The Phase III clinical trial MOTION study demonstrated that the ORR was 40% in the intention-to-treat population, while it was 0% in the placebo group.

Pexidartinib was the first approved CSF1R inhibitor, which had good anti-tumour activity but caused severe hepatotoxicity due to its inhibitory activity on FLT3. To address this issue, Deciphera Pharmaceuticals constructed a compound library and obtained the selective lead compound VIM-02 through high-throughput screening (Figure 5(A))62. By introducing a methyl group and isopropylamine on the pyrimidinone, vimseltinib was developed with further improved selectivity. The co-crystal structure analysis revealed that pexidartinib and vimseltinib had different binding modes (Figure 5(B)). The introduction of the pyridine-pyrazole biaryl structure and pyrimidinone gave additional interactions with Leu-588, Lys-616, and Asp-796, which was the main reason for the improved selectivity. The approval of vimseltinib provided valuable experience and a framework for the development of selective CSF1R inhibitors.

(A) The original discovery of vimseltinib. (B) The binding models of pexidartinib and vimseltinib with CSF1R (PDB codes: 4R7H and 7MFC, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Avutometinib and defactinib (Avmapki Fakzynja Co-Pack)

The U.S. FDA expedited the approval of avutometinib in combination with defactinib for the treatment of recurrent low-grade serous ovarian cancer (LGSOC) with KRAS mutations63. Avutometinib inhibited the MAPK signalling pathway by promoting inactive RAF-MEK complex and preventing the compensatory reactivation. Defactinib inhibited the phosphorylation of focal adhesion kinase (FAK) at Tyr397, thereby blocking the integrin-mediated signalling pathways, disrupting the tumour metastatic microenvironment, and impeding the drug resistance signals. The clinical trial demonstrated a confirmed ORR of 44%, with the duration of response (DOR) ranged from 3.3 to 31.1 months.

Chugai Pharmaceuticals initially researched synthetic methods for single-fluorine-substituted xanthones with strong anti-tumour activity. Further structural modification by replacing the benzene ring with a pyridine ring led to avutometinib, which showed improved activity and pharmacokinetic properties (Figure 6(A))64. The development of defactinib remained undisclosed, but it shared a similar diarylaminopyrimidine structure with other clinical-stage FAK inhibitors65,66. The crystal structure analysis showed that avutometinib primarily interacted with proteins via its sulphonamide bond, while defactinib formed hydrogen bonds with Lys-40, Cys-89, and Gly-92 through nitrogen atoms and sulphonamide (Figure 6(B)). Avutometinib-based drug discovery should focus on screening alternative pharmacophores like chromanone and pyrimidine. For defactinib, optimising pharmacokinetics through subtle structural changes was recommended.

(A) The structures and activities of avutometinib and defactinib. (B) The binding models of avutometinib and defactinib with MEK and FAK (PDB codes: 3WIG and 5MAH, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Taletrectinib (Ibtrozi)

In June 2025, the U.S. FDA approved taletrectinib for treating locally advanced or metastatic proto-oncogene receptor tyrosine kinase 1 (ROS1)-positive non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)26. Taletrectinib exhibited strong brain penetration, effectively suppressed the G2032R resistant mutation, and showed 11–20-fold greater selectivity than previous ROS1-targeted drugs. In the Phase II trial, the first-line patients achieved an ORR of 88.8%, and a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 45.6 months.

Taletrectinib exhibited similar structural features to entrectinib, as both possessed an indole-like aromatic skeleton and hydrophilic side chains. By substituting the benzyl group with a chiral benzylamine and replacing the N-methylpiperazine by a chiral primary amine, taletrectinib with remarkably improved activity was achieved (Figure 7(A)). The molecular docking analysis revealed that both entrectinib and taletrectinib formed hydrogen bonds with Met-209 (Figure 7(B)). However, taletrectinib exhibited additional interactions with Asp-2102 and Gly-2031, which was likely one of the reasons for its further enhanced activity. The further structural modifications should to concentrate on the screening of other hydrophilic side chains and the optimisation of chiral side chains.

(A) The discovery of taletrectinib based on the structure of entrectinib. (B) The binding models of entrectinib and taletrectinib with ROS1 (PDB code: 7Z5X, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Sunvozertinib (Zegfrovy)

In July 2025, the U.S. FDA granted sunvozertinib accelerated approval for the treatment of NSCLC with epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) exon 20 insertion mutations67,68. Sunvozertinib was an oral, irreversible, and selective EGFR inhibitor, which selectively bound to the EGFR exon20ins protein and blocked the downstream signalling pathways to inhibit the cell proliferation. The clinical trial results exhibited an ORR of 45.9–53.3%, and a disease control rate (DCR) up to 83%.

Sunvozertinib was the fourth-generation EGFR inhibitor targeting EGFR exon 20 insertion mutations, which was derived from osimertinib by replacing the biaryl indole with a substituted aniline and the ethylenediamine with an aminopyrrole (Figure 8(A)). The molecular docking studies showed that osimertinib and sunvozertinib shared similar binding modes, but the substituted aniline and aminopyrrole increased hydrogen bond interactions with Lys-716, Pro-794, and Lys-745, further enhancing its selectivity and binding ability (Figure 8(B)). The approval of sunvozertinib validated the authority of osimertinib and the potential of novel EGFR inhibitors based on aminopyrimidine for selective targeting and overcoming drug resistance mutations.

(A) The discovery of sunvozertinib based on the structure of osimertinib. (B) The binding models of osimertinib and sunvozertinib with EGFR (PDB code: 8PNZ, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Dordaviprone (Modeyso)

Dordaviprone received accelerated approval from the FDA in August 2025 for treating H3K27M-mutated diffuse glioma69. It was a first-in-class imipridone drug developed by Chimerix, which was designed to selectively target caseinolytic peptidase P (ClpP) and dopamine receptor D2 (DRD2). Dordaviprone inhibited the Ras signalling pathway by inhibiting DRD2 to impede tumour proliferation, while it activated the mitochondrial protease ClpP, resulting in the collapse of energy metabolism in cancer cells70. The clinical trial showed an ORR of 22–28%, with a median DOR of 10–11 months.

Dordaviprone was first reported in 2013 as a small molecule with anti-tumour activity obtained through high-throughput screening (Figure 9). The structure initially reported was incorrect, being an isomer of the truly active component, which also led to patent disputes. The co-crystal structure analysis revealed that dordaviprone mainly bound to ClpP through σ-π hyperconjugation effect with Glu-82, Ile-84 and Leu-104. Its unique angular structure enabled it to precisely fit into the protein pocket.

The structure of dordaviprone and binding model with mitochondrial ClpP (PDB code: 6DL7, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Zongertinib (Hernexeos)

In August 2025, the U.S. FDA announced the accelerated approval of zongertinib for marketing, specifically for the treatment of NSCLC patients carrying human EGFR 2 (HER2) tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) activated mutations71. Zongertinib precisely inhibited the tyrosine kinase activity of mutant HER2 receptors via covalent binding, while maintaining wild-type EGFR signalling, thus reducing EGFR-related toxicities including severe diarrhoea or rash72. The clinical data demonstrated the ORR ranged from 71% to 77% and a favourable safety profile with no reported risk of interstitial lung disease.

Although the development approach of zongertinib was not reported, tyrosine kinase inhibitors targeting HER2 generally possessed a similar aniline-substituted quinazoline core structure. Zongertinib appeared to be derived from tuxobertinib by introducing a piperidine linker at the covalent warhead acrylamide, adding nitrogen atoms to the quinazoline core, and replacing the pyridine with a benzimidazole (Figure 10(A). The molecular docking analysis indicated that zongertinib and tuxobertinib exhibited similar binding modes, primarily interacting with Leu-726, Met-801, and Cys-805 to bind within the protein pocket (Figure 10(B). The subsequent structural modifications could focus on the optimisation of the linker and the screening of other aromatic heterocycles as pharmacophores.

(A) The discovery of zongertinib based on the structure of tuxobertinib. (B) The binding models of tuxobertinib and zongertinib with HER2 (PDB code: 7PCD, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Imlunestrant (Inluriyo)

In September 2025, the FDA granted approval for the selective oestrogen receptor degrader (SERD) imlunestrant for breast cancer73. Imlunestrant bound to the oestrogen receptor to induce structural alterations, while the structural change allowed the E3 ubiquitin ligase to more effectively recognise and mark the oestrogen receptor, ultimately resulting in the degradation by proteasome74. Compared with traditional endocrine therapy drugs, imlunestrant offered distinctive advantages, including the ability to penetrate the blood-brain barrier, continuous inhibition of oestrogen receptor activity, and low toxicity. The median PFS of patients treated with imlunestrant was 5.5 months, while that of patients receiving standard of care (SOC) endocrine therapy treatment was 3.8 months.

Structurally, imlunestrant seemed to be derived from amcenestrant by retaining the hydrophilic side chain, while the bicyclic structure underwent a scaffold hopping to a tetracyclic ring (Figure 11(A)75. The molecular docking indicated that both compounds exhibited similar binding modes, where the hydrophilic side chains formed hydrogen bonds with Asp-351 and Val-533 (Figure 11(B). The key difference was that the tetracyclic ring enhanced the π-π stacking interaction with Phe-404. The future research could concentrate on the development of synthetic methods for the tetracyclic scaffold and asymmetric catalysis, which was beneficial for the drug optimisation.

(A) The discovery of imlunestrant based on the structure of imlunestrant. (B) The binding models of amcenestrant and imlunestrant with oestrogen receptor (PDB code: 6SBO, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Ziftomenib (Komzifti)

In November 2025, the FDA approved ziftomenib for the treatment of acute myeloid leukaemia with susceptible nucleophosmin 1 (NPM1) mutation31. Ziftomenib was a menin inhibitor that targeted leukaemia cells with NPM1 mutations or histone-lysine N-methyltransferase 2 A (KMT2A) rearrangements by inhibiting the menin-KMT2A interaction, inducing differentiation and exerting anti-tumour effects76. The results of the KOMET-001 (NCT04067336) clinical trial showed that the complete response rate of ziftomenib was 21.4%, and the median DOR was 5 months.

Ziftomenib was developed via the classic drug discovery pathway (Figure 12(A))77. Starting from the rigid tricyclic core of ZIF-01, a ring-opening strategy yielded ZIF-02 with improved activity. Replacing piperazine with aminopiperidine and thiazole with cyanoindole generated ZIF-03, which showed further enhancement. Adding a ‘magic methyl’ group and a pyrazole moiety to the indole ring increased biological activity in ZIF-04. Finally, introducing an aminomethyl group and hydrophilic side chain produced ziftomenib, which exhibited excellent pharmacokinetic properties. The molecular docking showed that ZIF-01 and ziftomenib shared similar binding modes, primarily via the pyrimidine nitrogen interacting with Tyr-276 (Figure 12(B)). The aminomethyl and hydrophilic piperazine side chains enhanced activity and pharmacokinetics by forming additional hydrogen bonds with Cys-241 and Arg-330.

(A) The original discovery of ziftomenib. (B) The binding models of ZIF-01 and ziftomenib with memin (PDB code: 8VA5, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Sevabertinib (Hyrnuo)

In November 2025, the FDA expedited the approval of sevabertinib for market launch, offering a precise targeted therapy for specific NSCLC patients with HER2 mutations78. Sevabertinib specifically inhibited the HER2 and EGFR signalling pathway and effectively curbed the proliferation and metastasis of tumour cells. The SOHO-01 (NCT05099172) clinical trial demonstrated that 71% of patients achieved objective response, and the median DOR reached 9.2 months.

SEV-01 was acquired via high-throughput screening of a series of compounds based on their anti-proliferative activity against Ba/F3 cells. By substituting the tetrahydrofuran group with a dioxane functional group, sevabertinib with remarkably enhanced biological activity was obtained (Figure 13(A))79. The molecular docking analysis demonstrated that both SEV-01 and sevabertinib bound to the protein pocket through interactions with Lys-745, Met-793, and Asp-855 (Figure 13(B)). The approval of sevabertinib offered a novel scaffold for the development of EGFR and HER2 inhibitors.

(A) The original discovery of sevabertinib. (B) The binding models of SEV-01 and sevabertinib with EGFR (PDB code: 6VHN, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Cardiovascular drug

Etripamil (Cardamyst)

In December 2025, the U.S. FDA approved etripamil nasal spray for marketing, which was indicated for the acute treatment of paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia in adults to restore sinus rhythm80. Etripamil selectively blocked L-type calcium channels, inhibited calcium influx in myocardial cells, thereby slowing the conduction velocity of the atrioventricular node and rapidly terminating paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia attacks81. The clinical trial results demonstrated that 64% of patients who self-administered etripamil restored sinus rhythm within 30 min, whereas only 31% of those who received a placebo.

Etripamil appeared to be derived from the core structure of verapamil via structural modification (Figure 14(A)). Compared with racemic verapamil, the specific chiral structure of etripamil enhanced the biological activity and mitigated toxic side effects. The incorporation of an ester group augmented the liposolubility, thus facilitating transmembrane penetration and enhancing bioavailability. In the co-crystal structure and molecular docking analyses, despite verapamil and etripamil were located in the same protein pocket, they also exhibited multiple distinct binding modes (Figure 14(B)). Notably, the chiral cyano group increased the binding affinity through hydrogen bond interactions with Thr-1012 and Tyr-1365.

(A) The discovery of etripamil based on the structure of verapamil. (B) The binding models of verapamil and etripamil with L-type voltage-gated Ca2+ channel subunit (PDB code: 6JPA, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Aficamten (Myqorzo)

In December 2025, the U.S. FDA approved aficamten for the treatment of obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy82. Aficamten bound to the allosteric site of cardiac myosin, inhibited the ATPase activity and prevented the formation of myosin bridges by inhibiting the binding of troponin and myosin, thereby reducing contractility and expediting the relaxation process. Compared with mavacamten, aficamten achieved inhibition through biochemical regulation rather than structural orientation, which did not induce a physical displacement of the myosin head and thick filament.

Through high-throughput screening, the indoline derivative AFI-01 was obtained as the hit compound83. Following structural modification, the indane analogue AFI-02 was acquired, which featured relatively weak structural constraints and facilitated the rapid enhancement of drug properties (Figure 15(A)). After substituting the benzene ring with a pyrazole ring, aficamten with significantly improved metabolic stability and half-life was obtained. The molecular docking analysis indicated that despite the similar binding modes of AFI-01 and aficamten, subtle structural alterations led to an increase in hydrogen bond interaction with Glu-474 (Figure 15(B)). The structural modifications could be considered based on indirubin to further enhance biological activity.

(A) The original discovery of aficamten. (B) The binding models of AFI-01 and aficamten with cardiac myosin (PDB code: 9F6C, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Central nervous system drug

Suzetrigine (Journavx)

In January 2025, suzetrigine received approval from the FDA for the treatment of moderate to severe acute pain84. Suzetrigine stabilised the voltage-gated sodium channel 1.8 (NaV1.8) in closed state via allosteric regulation, which prevented the inward flow of sodium ions, inhibited the generation of action potentials, and blocked the pain signal before it reached the brain. Since NaV1.8 was primarily distributed in peripheral neurons and rarely entered the central nervous system, suzetrigine was non-addictive and had a lower incidence of side effects than opioid drugs35. This represented the first non-opioid analgesic approved in over 20 years, offering new hope to the field of pain treatment and indicating the arrival of a new era of non-opioid analgesics.

Suzetrigine was likely to be an optimised compound based on the active molecule VX-150, achieved by substituting the benzene ring with a chiral tetrahydrofuran and replacing the phosphate ester with a formamide (Figure 16(A)). The molecular docking analysis revealed that the introduction of formamide increased the binding of the ligand molecule to the protein through hydrogen bond interactions with Thr-1658 and Ser-1709, while the introduction of tetrahydrofuran improved the drug’s solubility (Figure 16(B)). The substitutability of heterocycles such as furan, pyrrole and pyrazole could be considered to further provide modifiable core skeletons.

(A) The discovery of suzetrigine based on the structure of VX-150. (B) The binding models of VX-150 and suzetrigine with Nav1.8 (PDB code: 7WFR, and the figure was drawn by the authors using chemdraw, MOE and PyMol software).

Elinzanetant (Lynkuet)

In October 2025, the U.S. FDA approved elinzanetant for the treatment of moderate to severe vasomotor symptoms (VMS) induced by menopause85. Elinzanetant, a dual antagonist of neurokinin-1/3 (NK-1/3) receptors, effectively regulated body temperature and treated hot flashes by modulating kisspeptin, neurokinin B, and dynorphin (KNDy) neurons in the hypothalamic region. Moreover, elinzanetant not only significantly reduced the frequency and severity of hot flashes but also effectively enhanced patients’ sleep quality and overall quality of life.

Elinzanetant shared a similar skeletal structure with netupitant, while it substituted the methylpiperazine in the solvent zone with a bicyclic fused-ring structure to enhance the pharmacokinetic properties (Figure 17(A)). The molecular docking analysis indicated that both netupitant and elinzanetant established hydrogen bonds and π-π stacking interactions with Gln-165, His-197, and Phe-268 (Figure 17(B)). In addition, elinzanetant had an extra interaction with Met-291. Subsequently, structural modification could be accomplished by applying the scaffold hopping to conduct intramolecular cyclisation on the amide structure to improve selectivity.

Tradipitant (Nereus)

Tradipitant, an NK-1 receptor antagonist, received approval from the U.S. FDA in December 2025 for preventing vomiting induced by motion sickness37. Tradipitant suppressed the vomiting response by antagonising the NK-1 receptor and obstructing the activating effect of substance P. In the Motion Syros study, the incidence of vomiting in the tradipitant treatment group ranged from 18.3% to 19.5%, whereas it was 44.3% in the placebo group.

Tradipitant could be regarded as a scaffold hop from aprepitant, with the morpholine ring replaced by a triazole and the triazolone substituted by a carbonylpyridine (Figure 18(A)). The molecular docking showed that the multi-aromatic ring system enhances π-π stacking with Phe-264 while maintaining hydrogen bonds with Gln-165 and Glu-193 (Figure 18(B)). The approval of tradipitant confirmed that such systems retained good biological activity, while future efforts should focus on introducing heterocyclic aromatic rings and hydrophilic groups to improve solubility.

Dermatological drug

Delgocitinib (Anzupgo)

In July 2025, delgocitinib was approved by the FDA for marketing and utilised for the local treatment of moderate to severe chronic hand eczema (CHE)86. Delgocitinib specifically inhibited the activity of Janus kinase 1 (JAK1), JAK2, JAK3, and tyrosine kinase 2 (TYK2) through blocking the JAK-signal transducers and activators of transcription (STAT) pathway, and suppressed various inflammatory responses that were crucial in the onset and subsequent episodes of CHE. This innovative mechanism directly targeted the core pathways driving the inflammatory response in CHE, thereby facilitating the restoration of skin barrier function and breaking the vicious cycle of relapse upon drug withdrawal.

Cyclisation of tofacitinib’s amino piperidine moiety yielded delgocitinib, which featured a three-dimensional spiro structure (Figure 19(A))87. Through scaffold hopping, delgocitinib maintained a binding mode nearly identical to that of tofacitinib, preserving key interactions with Leu-828, Lys-855, Leu-905, and Glu-903, with only minor conformational differences (Figure 19(B)). The approval of delgocitinib highlighted cyclisation as a practical strategy for improving drug potency and pharmacokinetic properties.

Endocrine and metabolic drug

Paltusotine (Palsonify)

Paltusotine was approved by the U.S. FDA in September 2025 for the treatment of acromegaly in adult patients who had not responded to surgical intervention or were not suitable candidates for surgery39. Paltusotine specifically targeted somatostatin receptor 2 (SSTR2) and inhibited growth hormone signalling by preventing the accumulation of cyclic adenosine monophosphate, consequently reducing excessive growth hormone secretion, lowering insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) levels, and alleviating the symptoms of acromegaly. In comparison with other somatostatin receptor subtypes, paltusotine exhibited a selectivity for SSTR2 that was over 4000 times higher, which might potentially reduce the risk of off-target effects.

PAL-01 was a gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) antagonist with high selectivity and affinity for SSTR2. By replacing its long-chain primary amine with aminopiperidine, substituting the methyl group with fluorine, and adding a cyano group, paltusotine was ultimately obtained (Figure 20(A))88. The molecular docking showed that the cyano and fluorine groups enhanced interactions with Thr-212 and Met-119 (Figure 20(B)). As the first approved non-peptide SSTR2 agonist, paltusotine offered a modifiable scaffold and a new therapeutic strategy. Future studies could explore the potential of other heterocyclic cores, such as quinazoline or pyrimidine fused rings.

Genetic drug

Sebetralstat (Ekterly)

Sebetralstat was approved by the FDA in July 2025, emerging as the first oral medication for on-demand treatment of acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE)89. As an oral plasma kallikrein inhibitor, sebetralstat curtailed bradykinin production by impeding kallikrein and the subsequent bradykinin system cascade, thus mitigating the tissue swelling triggered by HAE attacks. The Phase III KONFIDENT study demonstrated that sebetralstat could significantly shorten the time to symptom relief, with the median time to symptom onset relief being 1.61 h for sebetralstat and 6.72 h for the placebo group.

Reducing the basicity of the P1 group was an effective strategy for discovering oral small molecule inhibitors. Therefore, based on the previously reported lead compound SEB-01, benzamidine was substituted with benzylamine to obtain SEB-02, which exhibited both reduced basicity and activity (Figure 21(A))40. To further enhance the activity, a pyrimidinone was introduced, while pyrrole and benzylamine was replaced with pyrazole and aminoisoquinoline. Through structure-activity relationship studies, the introduction of a methoxy group on the pyrazole ring and the replacement of aminoisoquinoline with a substituted pyridine yielded sebetralstat with significantly reduced basicity. The molecular docking studies revealed that the introduction of pyridone enhanced the interaction with Gly-66 and Tyr-174, while the pyrazole ring increased the π-π stacking interaction with His-57 (Figure 21(B)). The subsequent research could focus on the modification of the pyridone substitution and the screening of alternative functional groups to further enhance selectivity and improve drug-likeness.

Sepiapterin (Sephience)

In July 2025, the FDA approved sepiapterin for the treatment of phenylketonuria90. Sepiapterin enhanced phenylalanine hydroxylase activity through a dual mechanism, it served as a precursor compound to convert into tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4), and it corrected the misfolding of PAH protein, thereby reducing the level of phenylalanine in the blood (Figure 22(A))91. The results of the APHENITY trial showed that the phenylalanine levels in the blood of patients in the sepiapterin group decreased by 63%, and 84% of the patients reached the control level stipulated in the treatment guidelines (< 360 μmol/L).

Immunological drug

Atrasentan (Vanrafia)

In April 2025, the FDA granted accelerated approval for the marketing application of atrasentan to reduce proteinuria levels in patients with primary immunoglobulin A (IgA) nephropathy44. As an endothelin A (ETA) receptor antagonist, atrasentan inhibited the activation of ETA receptors to mitigate the elevation of glomerular intracapillary pressure, podocyte injury and fibrosis, lower urinary protein excretion, and impede the progression of nephropathy. The results of the ALIGN study indicated that, in comparison with the placebo group, patients treated with atrasentan in combination with renin-angiotensin system (RAS) inhibitors witnessed a significant 36.1% reduction in proteinuria levels.

After a comprehensive analysis of the pharmacophores of existing ET receptor antagonists, Abbott Laboratories substituted the indan core of SB-209670 with tetrahydropyrrole and carried out extensive structure-activity relationship studies to obtain the selective ETA antagonist atrasentan (Figure 25(A))94. The molecular docking analysis revealed that SB-209670 and atrasentan were positioned within the protein pocket via the interactions of the benzene ring and carboxyl group with Leu-259, Lys-255, and Arg-326. The difference lied in the fact that the tetrahydropyrrole formed hydrogen bond interactions with Gln-165, which served as the primary factor for the remarkable enhancement of activity and selectivity (Figure 25(B)). The subsequent research should concentrate on the modification of the amide side chain to assess the impacts of pharmacophores such as ethylene glycol, benzamide, and benzyl on selectivity and activity.

Rilzabrutinib (Wayrilz)

In August 2025, the FDA granted approval for rilzabrutinib to treat persistent or chronic immune thrombocytopenia (ITP)95. Rilzabrutinib was a small molecule covalent reversible kinase inhibitor targeting Bruton’s tyrosine kinase (BTK), exerting therapeutic effects by inhibiting B cell activation and reducing the signal transduction mediated by the Fcγ receptor pathways. In LUNA 3 clinical trial, 23% of ITP patients treated with rilzabrutinib showed a sustained platelet response, while this figure was 0% in the placebo group.

Principia Biopharma optimised ibrutinib’s structure by introducing cyano and piperazine groups to extend the compound’s residence time and improve binding stability (Figure 26(A))96. Through extensive optimisation, they identified PRN473 and rilzabrutinib as the promising clinical candidates. Compared to ibrutinib’s interactions with Val-416, Glu-475, Met-477, and Cys-481, rilzabrutinib gained additional interactions between its cyano and piperazine groups with Cys-481 and Glu-488 (Figure 26(B)). The approval of rilzabrutinib confirmed that strengthening non-covalent interactions and refining the covalent warhead to prolong residence time was an effective strategy for developing covalent inhibitors.

Remibrutinib (Rhapsido)

In September 2025, remibrutinib received approval from the FDA for the treatment of chronic spontaneous urticaria. As a highly selective BTK inhibitor, remibrutinib precisely blocked the BTK signalling pathway and inhibited the release of histamine and other inflammatory mediators by mast cells and basophils, thus controlling the progression of chronic spontaneous urticaria at the mechanistic level46. In the REMIX-1 and REMIX-2 clinical trials, 31.1% and 27.9% of patients in the remibrutinib group achieved a complete response, and this proportion was significantly higher than 6.5% in the placebo group.

Novartis selected the previously reported active molecule REM-01 as the lead compound and introduced an acryloyl covalent warhead to target Cys-481, yielding REM-02 with significantly improved activity (Figure 27(A))97. However, due to its rapid in vivo metabolism, the pyrrolopyrimidine core was replaced with aminopyrimidine, resulting in REM-03 with enhanced stability but reduced potency. Through structure-based drug design, remibrutinib was developed by introducing a linker with the covalent warhead, achieving markedly higher activity. The crystal structure analysis showed that the aminopyrimidine scaffold formed hydrogen bonds with Lys-430 and Met-477, while the acryloyl warhead strengthened covalent binding to Cys-481 (Figure 27(B)). Future optimisation could focus on linker screening and evaluating heterocyclic cores like quinazoline and other nitrogen-containing aromatic heterocycles.

Ophthalmic drug

Acoltremon (Tryptyr)

In May 2025, acoltremon, a potent and selective transient receptor potential cation channel subfamily M member 8 (TRPM8) agonist, was successfully approved by the U.S. FDA for the treatment of dry eye disease47. Acoltremon rapidly increased natural tear secretion by activating cold receptors on the cornea and stimulating trigeminal nerve signal transmission. In the COMET-2 and COMET-3 studies, the proportion of subjects in the acoltremon group with an increase of at least 10 mm in the unanesthetized Schirmer score was up to four times higher than that in the control group. Specifically, in the COMET-2 trial, the proportion was 42.6% in the acoltremon group and 8.2% in the control group, while in the COMET-3 trial, it was 53.2% and 14.4% respectively.

Acoltremon was a derivative of menthol, while the molecular docking indicated that the introduction of an aniline structure via an amide bond had altered its binding mode (Figure 28(A)). Menthol bound to the protein pocket of TRPM8 by establishing a hydrogen bond with Arg-1007, whereas acoltremon bound through hydrogen bonding with Arg-841 (Figure 28(B)). Due to acoltremon was an ophthalmic drug with a low molecular weight, the impact of aminopyridine or cyanobenzeneamine on its permeability could be explored.

Brensocatib (Brinsupri)

In August 2025, the U.S. FDA approved brensocatib for marketing to treat non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis49. As a reversible dipeptidyl peptidase 1 (DPP1) inhibitor, brensocatib blocked the activation of neutrophil serine proteases (NSPs) in bone marrow neutrophil precursors, reduced the protease load of mature neutrophils, and decreased the release of tissue-destructive enzymes to suppresses neutrophil-mediated inflammatory responses.

AstraZeneca aimed to preserve the biological activity and physicochemical properties of the first-generation DPP1 inhibitor BRE-01 while developing a second-generation inhibitor to avoid aorta binding (Figure 30(A))99. Since the clinical safety arose from the reaction between BRE-01’s amino-tetrahydropyran and lysine’s aldehyde group, structural modifications focused on replacing the fatty ring to discovery lead compound BRE-02. To improve pharmacokinetics, brensocatib was developed by substituting phenyl ring with benzoxazinone, significantly increasing half-life and oral bioavailability. The molecular docking showed that both BRE-01 and brensocatib bound to Asp-1, Cys-234, Gly-277, and Asn-380, while the oxazolidinone moiety in brensocatib enhanced hydrogen bonding with Pro-3 (Figure 30(B)). The approval of brensocatib confirmed that modifying existing drug scaffolds was an effective strategy in drug development.

In October 2025, the U.S. FDA approved nerandomilast for the treatment of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis in adults50. Nerandomilast exerted both anti-fibrotic and immunomodulatory effects by selectively inhibiting phosphodiesterase 4B (PDE4B), thereby reducing inflammatory responses and pulmonary tissue scarring. Conclusion and prospect

In 2025, the U.S. FDA approved a total of 46 new drug applications, encompassing 31 new molecular entities and 15 biologics. These newly approved new molecular entities are the outcomes of modern drug research and development technologies, which also promote the development of technology. For example, treosulfan employed a prodrug design strategy by introducing two hydroxyl groups into the structure of busulfan to generate a diepoxyethane metabolite. Through the application of the scaffold hopping technology and the conduction of a cyclisation reaction on amcenestrant, imlunestrant with a tetracyclic ring as the core structure was ultimately obtained. Etripamil was a specific configuration of verapamil derivative that exhibited the enhanced therapeutic effects and reduced clinical side effects. By performing a spirocyclic modification on the amino piperidine of tofacitinib, delgocitinib was obtained with significantly enhanced selectivity and activity. After the modifications on the covalent warhead acryloyl of ibrutinib to prolong its residence time, rilzabrutinib with significantly improved pharmacokinetic properties was obtained. By applying a conformational restricted cyclisation strategy to the quaternary ammonium of acetylcholine, aceclidine with the same biological activity was obtained. It is not difficult to find that the marketed drugs are a huge treasure trove. As the crystallisation of human wisdom, they embody advanced technologies and valuable experience. New drugs approved in 2025 target multiple novel mechanisms, including transcription factors, proteins related to mitochondrial function, and phospholipids, thus opening entirely new pathways for disease intervention. The complete pharmacokinetic data enables researchers to anticipate the in vivo behaviour of similar compounds, significantly reducing trial-and-error costs in early development and improving the efficiency of dosing regimen design. Based on real-world usage data from newly launched drugs, researchers can further uncover potential new indications, pursue drug repositioning, expand clinical value, and also provide valuable insights for designing combination therapies with future drugs in the same field. In addition, with the advancement of AI, emerging technologies including computer-aided drug design and AI-driven drug discovery will expedite the development cycle. Through a comprehensive review of the structural features, design concepts, and modification strategies of newly approved drug molecules, we aim to share valuable research and development experiences with pharmaceutical chemists and provide them with inspiration for their research.

Method

The general method to generate PyMol figure: (1) Download the target molecule’s PDB file from the RCSB Protein Data Bank, import it into PyMOL using the load or fetch command, and perform preprocessing steps such as removing crystal water molecules and redundant residues to establish a basic visualisation framework. (2) Use the GUI’s S/H/C buttons or corresponding commands to switch the molecule into desired display modes such as cartoon, stick, or surface representations. Apply colour differentiation by chain, secondary structure, or element, and adjust detailed parameters including stick radius and cartoon helix style. (3) Rotate, zoom, and translate the molecule via mouse dragging to achieve the desired viewing angle. Set a pure white or transparent background, and optionally add auxiliary elements such as distance labels and dashed lines indicating interactions. (4) Execute the ray command to render 3D ray-traced images, set the target resolution (typically 1600 × 1200 pixels or higher), and finally export the image in high-resolution PNG format meeting journal requirements.

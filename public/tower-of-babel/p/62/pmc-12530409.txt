Impact of CMV-specific immune reconstitution at the end of letermovir prophylaxis on the development of late cytomegalovirus infection in haematopoietic stem cell transplant recipients (INMUNOEND): a protocol for a prospective, observational, multicentre study
Authors: Juan José Castón, Clara Aparicio, Aurora Paez-Vega, Lorena Pozo López, Estefanía García, Carmen Martín, Elisa Ruiz-Arabi, María Ángeles Cuesta-Casas, María Aránzazu Bermúdez-Rodriguez, Juan Manuel Cerezo-Martín, Pedro Antonio González-Sierra, Isabel Machuca, Francisco Manuel Martín Domínguez, Raquel Saldaña-Moreno, Concepción Herrera, Julian Torre-Cisneros

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCytomegalovirus (CMV) infection is a common complication in patients undergoing haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (SCT). Letermovir (LTV) prophylaxis during the first 100 days post-SCT is effective and safe in preventing this infection, although it may be associated with a delay in CMV-specific immune reconstitution. Hence, a study is needed to evaluate whether the absence of CMV-specific immune reconstitution at the end of LTV prophylaxis is associated with the development of late infection. This could facilitate the individualisation of CMV prophylaxis duration in these patients.Methods and analysisINMUNOEND is a multicentre, prospective, observational, non-interventional study including CMV seropositive patients undergoing allo-SCT who receive LTV prophylaxis during the first 100 days post SCT. Immunological and virological monitoring will be conducted until day+200 post-SCT. The primary outcome is the percentage of patients who develop clinically significant CMV infection up to day+200 post-SCT after completing LTV prophylaxis. Introduction

Cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection represents the most significant viral infection in haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (SCT) recipients.1 2 Recently, the results of a randomised, controlled clinical trial have demonstrated the efficacy of letermovir (LTV) prophylaxis during the first 100 days post-transplant in reducing the incidence of clinically significant CMV infection (CMV-Cs) and mortality at 24 weeks post-SCT.3

Despite these favourable results of LTV prophylaxis, it has been observed that after discontinuation of this prophylaxis, there is a significant increase in the risk of late CMV infection in high-risk patients.3 One possible explanation for this finding could be the delayed recovery of CMV-specific cellular immunity at the end of the prophylaxis period.

It is well known that protection against viral reactivation primarily depends on the reconstitution of CMV-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells. Consequently, patients who do not exhibit reconstitution of these cells are at higher risk of CMV reactivation.4,6 Recent studies comparing patients receiving LTV prophylaxis with historical cohorts treated with pre-emptive therapy have shown a delay in the reconstitution of CMV-specific immunity in the LTV prophylaxis group.7 8 These results may be explained by the fact that universal prophylaxis with LTV prevents viral replication, thereby limiting the exposure of the immune system to viral antigens. In contrast, pre-emptive therapy allows for low levels of replication, which facilitates the exposure of the immune system to the virus.9 10

In conclusion, our main objective is to evaluate whether the absence of CMV-specific immune reconstitution at the end of LTV prophylaxis is associated with the development of clinically significant infection up to day+200 post-SCT. The results of this study may help individualise the duration of prophylaxis by identifying which group of patients could benefit from extended prophylaxis with LTV or from early discontinuation. Additionally, these findings could serve as a basis for intervention trials to further individualise prophylaxis duration.

Methods and analysis

Study design, setting and study period

INMUNOEND is a multicentre, prospective, observational, non-interventional study designed to evaluate whether the absence of CMV-specific immune reconstitution at the end of LTV prophylaxis is associated with the development of clinically significant infection up to day+200 post-SCT. Participants will be recruited from six tertiary centres belonging to the Spanish National Health System in Spain from March 2025 to September 2026.

Definitions

Immune reconstitution for CMV-specific immunity will be considered present when the result of the QTF-CMV is reactive (defined as the CMV antigen response minus the negative control response being ≥0.2 IU/mL of interferon-gamma, IFNG).

CMV-Cs is defined as the initiation of pre-emptive therapy at the discretion of the responsible physician or the development of organ disease due to CMV according to previously established definitions.11

Immunological monitoring

Periodic immunological monitoring of CMV-specific immune reconstitution will be performed using QTF-CMV. A first determination will be made pre-SCT, followed by subsequent determinations at +30, +60, +90, +120, +150, +180 and +200 days post-HCT, as well as at the end of prophylaxis with LTV. If the LTV is discontinued 1 week before or after the scheduled time for QTF-CMV testing according to protocol, no additional sample will be taken, and the result from the sample taken according to protocol will be considered as the QTF-CMV result at the end of prophylaxis. Additionally, a QTF-CMV determination will be performed in the donor (if seropositive) prior to the HCT procedure.

For monitoring CMV-specific immune reconstitution, the QTF-CMV technique will be used, following the manufacturer’s instructions (QIAGEN, Hilden, Germany). In summary, 1 mL of heparinised whole blood will be collected into three QTF tubes. The contents of each tube will be: (i) a mixture of 22 CMV peptides; (ii) a negative control (no antigens); or (iii) a positive mitogen control (phytohaemagglutinin). After incubation for 16–24 hours at 37°C, the supernatant will be harvested and IFNG levels (IU/mL) will be analysed by ELISA. The result for CMV antigens will be considered ‘reactive’ (presence of CMV-specific cellular immunity) when the CMV antigen response minus the negative control response is ≥0.2 IU/mL of IFNG. The result will be considered ‘indeterminate’ when the IFNG level in the CMV antigen tube is <0.2 IU/mL and the mitogen tube is <0.5 IU/mL.

CMV-specific immune reconstitution will be considered present when the QTF-CMV result is reactive (defined as the CMV antigen response minus the negative control response being ≥0.2 IU/mL of IFNG).

The attending physicians will not have information about the results of the QTF-CMV; therefore, CMV management will not be based on the results of the assays.

All QTF-CMV determinations will be performed centrally at the GC-03-Infectious Diseases Laboratory of IMIBIC (Sanitary Biomedical Research Institute of Córdoba).

Virological monitoring

Virological monitoring of CMV will be performed weekly until day+100, at each medical visit from day+100 to day+200, and when clinically indicated by the responsible physician. These determinations will be carried out at the laboratory of each centre.

When CMV replication or CMV-Cs is detected, the immune status regarding CMV will be defined by the last QTF-CMV result prior to the onset of replication or CMV-Cs.

Sample size calculation

Assuming an incidence of CMV-Cs of +200 days post-HCT of 40% in patients without CMV-specific immune reconstitution and 15% in patients with CMV-specific immune reconstitution, with 70% of patients lacking immune reconstitution at the end of prophylaxis,7 a statistical power of 80% and an alpha error of 5%, a sample size of 117 patients was determined (39 patients with immune reconstitution at the cessation of LTV and 78 patients without immune reconstitution). Considering 5% loss to follow-up, the final sample size was adjusted to 123 patients (41 patients with immune reconstitution and 82 without immune reconstitution). If, as reported by other authors,8 only 40% of patients fail to achieve immune reconstitution at the end of prophylaxis, and assuming 5% loss to follow-up, the required sample size would be 116 patients (69 patients with immune reconstitution and 47 without immune reconstitution). The sample size calculation was performed using the G*Power 3.1.9.7. calculator (Heinrich-Heine-Universität Düsseldorf, Düsseldorf, Germany).

Statistical analysis

Initially, a descriptive analysis of the recorded variables will be conducted. For quantitative variables, the median and range will be used, while percentages will be applied for categorical variables. Qualitative variables will be compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, and continuous variables will be analysed using either the Student’s t-test or the Mann-Whitney U test. To assess the risk factors for CMV-Cs, a Cox proportional hazards regression analysis will be performed, with the development of CMV-Cs as the dependent variable. Kaplan-Meier survival curves and the log-rank test will be used to investigate the relationship between CMV-specific immune reconstitution and CMV replication, as well as its association with the onset of CMV-Cs. The optimal cut-off point for QTF-CMV will be determined through a Classification and Regression Tree (CART) analysis. Subsequently, the area under the receiver operating characteristic for the cut-off recommended by the manufacturer (0.2 IU/mL of IFNG) will be compared with that derived from our study using the DeLong test. This cut-off will be incorporated as an independent variable in the multivariable analysis of risk factors for CMV-Cs. A predictive risk score will be calculated using the beta coefficients from the logistic regression analysis. Values with p<0.05 will be considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses will be performed using IBM SPSS V.24.0. (IBM inc., Armonk, New York, USA).

Discussion

The INMUNOEND study aims to address the influence of CMV-specific immune reconstitution on the development of late CMV infection in patients who have received LTV prophylaxis.

Our hypothesis is that patients who do not exhibit CMV-specific immune reconstitution at the end of LTV prophylaxis are at higher risk of developing late CMV infection. If this hypothesis is correct, a personalised strategy for the duration of LTV prophylaxis could be considered, based on the immune status of the patient against CMV, rather than the current fixed-duration prophylaxis approach. This strategy has recently been validated by our group in a clinical trial conducted in kidney transplant recipients, where patients were randomised to receive a standard fixed-duration prophylaxis strategy until 90 days post-transplant or an immune-guided prophylaxis strategy, where prophylaxis was interrupted in patients with CMV-specific immune reconstitution. The results of this trial showed no differences in the incidence of CMV replication or disease.12

To evaluate the presence of CMV-specific cellular immunity, several techniques can be used to measure different parameters. Among these is QTF-CMV, which measures the release of CMV-specific IFNG by CD8+ T lymphocytes. If a specific IFNG level (0.2 IU/mL) is reached, the QTF-CMV is considered reactive, indicating that the patient has immunity against CMV. This technique is simple to perform, reproducible and has been validated in these patients with results comparable to other immune response evaluation techniques.13 There are studies that have demonstrated the clinical usefulness of this technique in predicting the risk of complications associated with CMV, such that patients with a reactive QTF-CMV result exhibit a lower risk compared with those with an indeterminate or non-reactive result.13 14

Limitations of the INMUNOEND study include the observational nature. However, the results of the QTF-CMV will not be available to the treating physicians; thus, CMV management will not be based on the results of the assays. To minimise the potential for bias, consecutive patients will be included at each centre with strict inclusion and exclusion criteria, objective and measurable outcome variables will be used, and a multivariable analysis will be performed to control for confounding factors.

In summary, the results of this study could identify the optimal time to discontinue LTV prophylaxis based on the presence or absence of CMV-specific immune reconstitution in each patient, enabling the individualisation of the duration of this prophylaxis.

Spatiotemporal evolution and trade-offs/synergies of ecosystem services in Hubei Province
Authors: Xiaojie Chen, Longyang Huang, Changcheng Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
With the advancement of economic globalization, human activities have continuously disrupted the ecosystem, affecting its structure and function. On the basis of the characteristics of the spatial variations in ecosystem services, the systematic estimation of long-term ecosystem services and the analysis of trade-offs/synergies between different ecosystem services have practical significance for the coordination of economic development and ecological protection. In this study, we employed correlation analysis methods and spatial autocorrelation analysis methods to investigate the heterogeneity and nonlinearity of the trade-off/collaboration relationships among different types of ecosystem services (water resource output (WY), carbon storage (CS), soil conservation (SC), food supply (FS), and net primary productivity (NPP)) in Hubei Province. The results showed that: (1) ecosystem services in Hubei Province exhibited obvious spatial heterogeneity, with high SC, CS and NPP levels in western Hubei and high FS and WY levels in central and eastern Hubei; (2) the trade-offs and synergies among the different ecosystem services in Hubei Province followed obvious nonlinearity, in which CS, SC and NPP exhibited notable synergies, whereas CS, SC and NPP exhibited trade-offs with FS; and (3) areas with a high level of urbanization experienced severe ecosystem damage. The ecosystems in the southeastern and western parts of Hubei Province exhibited a notable synergistic relationship, whereas those in the other regions exhibited a trade-off relationship.

Introduction

An ecosystem refers to a balanced system comprising biological communities and the surrounding living environment, which is an important basis for human survival and development1. Ecosystems can not only provide material resources for humans to meet their living needs but also provide cultural and spiritual support2,3. Thus, ecosystem services are defined as the various benefits that humans directly or indirectly obtain from ecosystems, including the environmental and material materials needed for human survival4. The various ecological functions produced in ecological processes, such as logistics, information flow and energy flow in ecosystems, represent the basis of ecosystem service sources. The internal driving force for the formation of ecosystem services stems from the different levels of human needs during different periods5. Notably, humans directly or indirectly use various services provided by the ecosystem (as the supporting system of the Earth), which are essential to production and life activities6. In the United Nations Millennium Ecosystem Assessment project, ecosystem services are divided into supply services, support services, cultural services and regulatory services. Ecosystem services are important bridges between human well-being and ecological processes and are key links between the human social system and natural ecosystems7. However, ecosystem services do not exist alone, and there are complex relationships among different ecosystem services within a given ecosystem, which are manifested mainly as trade-offs or synergies. Trade-off or synergistic relationships between different ecosystem services affect ecosystem structure and function, so it is important to analyse the spatial trade-off and synergistic relationships among regional ecosystem services.

With the rapid development of the social economy, the material and spiritual needs of humans are increasing daily, thus increasing their dependence on and interference with ecosystems8. Ecosystem services do not exist in isolation but rather constitute a collection of multiple ecosystem services. However, the importance of ecosystem services has not been widely considered by humans, and large-scale and high-intensity human activities have caused continuous disturbances to ecosystems, thus affecting their structure and function9,10. The excessive consumption of natural resources in ecosystems has caused the degradation in regional ecosystem service functions and deterioration in the ecological environment. Ecological and environmental problems such as water pollution, air pollution, and land degradation pose serious threats to ecological security and human health and well-being11–13. Driven by economic and population growth, the increase in human activity pressure has led to a decline in ecosystem service functions14,15. The interference from human activities has seriously challenged the self-regulating ability of natural ecosystems and has had a profound impact on the balance of ecosystem services16,17. There are complex dynamic relationships among different ecosystem services. To maximize benefits, it is necessary to explore the synergistic trade-off effects among different ecosystem services to provide a basis for ecological protection decision-making and realize sustainable ecosystem development.

Ecosystem services are interrelated and can exhibit trade-offs or synergies18. An ecosystem service trade-off refers to the situation in which two types of ecosystem services are offset; notably, the production of one service increases at the expense of another19, which is represented by a competitive or conflicting relationship, whereas ecosystem synergy is, on the contrary, represented by a similar increase or decrease20. In recent years, scholars at home and abroad have examined the evolution of trade-offs and synergies between ecosystem services21,22. The assessment of the quality of ecosystems is a prerequisite for identifying trade-off and synergistic relationships between ecosystems. Ecosystem service functions can be evaluated mainly via the Integrated Valuation of Ecosystem Services and Trade-offs (InVEST), Social Value of Ecosystem Services (SoIVES) and Artificial Intelligence for Ecosystem Services (ARIES) models23,24. Owing to the inherent spatial heterogeneity in geographical phenomena, including various social and natural factors, the spatial distributions of various ecosystem services always differ25. Trade-offs and synergies are typical temporal and spatial characteristics of ecosystem services26, and various studies have focused on quantifying and visualizing ecosystem trade-offs and synergies27. From the perspective of research methods for analysing ecosystem service trade-offs and synergies, commonly employed statistical methods include correlation analysis28, regression analysis and redundancy analysis. Spatial overlay analysis, bivariate local spatial autocorrelation analysis29, geographically weighted regression30 and hot and cold spot analysis31 have been used to visualize ecosystem trade-offs and synergies. Recent studies have focused on trade-offs and synergies in the spatial patterns of ecosystem services, with the temporal dimension considered less. Research has usually focused on spatial trade-offs during a specific period, but the temporal dimension has rarely been quantified and integrated. Therefore, it is difficult to accurately reveal trade-offs/synergies among ecosystem services in both time and space, which affects the feasibility and accuracy of ecological protection policy design.

Hubei Province is located in the middle of China. The level of social and economic development is relatively high, and the province is an important carrier of industry and population and an irreplaceable ecological barrier to ensure the ecological security of the middle reaches of the Yangtze River Economic Belt. However, in the process of rapid economic development in Hubei Province, owing to the pressures of population growth and economic construction, humans continue to demand resources from the ecosystem, and ecological resources are consumed continuously, accompanied by serious ecological problems such as soil erosion and biodiversity loss; hence, ecosystems are facing unprecedented challenges32. Notably, ecosystem service research should primarily aim to maximize the comprehensive benefits of ecosystems and ease the trade-offs between different ecosystem services. How to strike a balance between ecological civilization construction and economic development remains a major issue facing human society33. Therefore, on the basis of the characteristics of the spatial variations in ecosystem services in Hubei Province, the systematic estimation of long-term ecosystem services and the analysis of trade-offs/synergies between different ecosystem services are important for the coordination of economic development and ecological protection (Fig. 1).Fig. 1The research framework of the synergies/trade-offs in ecosystem services.

Materials and methods

Study area

Hubei Province is located in the central region of China (Fig. 2), bordering Anhui to the east, Chongqing to the west, Shaanxi to the northwest, Jiangxi and Hunan to the south, and Henan to the north, with latitudes ranging from 29°01′53′′ ~ 33°06′47′′ north and longitudes ranging from 108°21′42′′ ~ 116°07′50′′ east. The total area of Hubei Province is 185,900 square kilometres, accounting for 1.94% of the total area of China. Hubei Province is surrounded by mountains on three sides, namely, in the east, west and north. The terrain is low and flat in the middle, with an incomplete basin opening towards the south. Notably, mountains account for 56% of the total area of the province, hills account for 24% of the total area, and plain lakes account for 20% of the total area. The province belongs to the Yangtze River system. Hubei Province exhibits a subtropical monsoon climate with distinct seasons: a cold winter season, a hot summer season, and a hot rainy season. The average annual temperature is 16.7 °C, and the average annual precipitation is 1200.7 mm. Data sources

The data sources used in this study included four main types. (1) Land use data with a 30-m spatial resolution were obtained from the Data Center for Resources and Environmental Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences (https://www.resdc.cn/). (2) Meteorological data from 2000 to 2020, including monthly cumulative precipitation, monthly mean temperature and total solar radiation data, were obtained from the China Meteorological Data Network (http://data.cma.cn/). (3) The Chinese soil dataset with a 1000-m spatial resolution is available from the Harmonized World Soil Database (HWSD; http://westdc.westgis.ac.cn/). (4) Digital elevation model (DEM) and normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) raster data with a 30-m spatial resolution were downloaded from the Geospatial Data Cloud (http://www.gis-cloud.cn/). (5) Agricultural output value data were sourced from the Hubei Rural Statistical Yearbook (https://data.cnki.net/).

Methods

Measurement of ecosystem services

Hubei Province is located in the middle reaches of the Yangtze River in China, and the ecosystem in this region is mainly a forest mountainous ecosystem. Thus, the main ecological problem is soil erosion, and ecological conservation is the primary ecological task. To systematically represent the ecosystem functions in Hubei Province, the selected ecosystem services should be as comprehensive as possible, including cultural services, support services, supply services and regulation services. Moreover, the selected ecosystem service types should be based on the principle of protecting the ecological security of the research area, and the selected ecosystem service types should play an important role in ensuring ecological security. In summary, the following five main types of ecological services were selected: water yield (WY), carbon storage (CS), soil conservation (SC), food supply (FS) and net primary productivity (NPP) services.

WY was calculated via the water yield module of the InVEST model. On the basis of the water balance principle, the difference between the input precipitation and the output evapotranspiration of regional water is used to calculate the WY of regional ecosystems34,35. Ecosystem service trade-off analysis

Correlation analysis

The relationships among ecosystemervices include synergistic and trade-off relationships. When other ecosystem services in the area increase with the increase in aiven ecosystem service, a synergistic relationship occurs44; conversely, a trade-off relationship occurs45. Correlation analysis is the main method for assessing ecosystem service trade-offs46. The Pearson correlation coefficient method was used to analyse the relationships between two ecosystem services. According to previous studies47, if R > 0.05, the two ecosystem services are highly correlated. If R ∈ [0.01, 0.05], the correlation is significant, and if R ∈ [0, 0.01], there is a certain correlation. The relationships between different ecosystem services can be evaluated according to Pearson’s correlation coefficient (R). If R > 0, there is a positive correlation between them, i.e., a synergistic relationship. The larger the value is, the more significant the synergistic relationship. Results

Spatiotemporal changes in ecosystem services

Water yield

WY refers to the amount of water collected in the catchment area after removing vegetation absorption and soil infiltration. WY directly reflects the absorption capacity of soil and vegetation for precipitation. In this study, water production in Hubei Province from 2000 to 2020 was investigated by using the water yield module of the InVEST model. The results revealed that the total water production during this period ranged from 12.57 to 15.50 × 1010 m3. The average water production depth ranged from 676.27 to 833.43 mm. During the 21-year period, the change trend of the average water production depth first decreased but then decreased, and the change trend of rainfall was consistent with that of the average water production depth (Fig. 3). The results revealed that the total output of water in 2005 and 2020 was relatively low, with water outputs of 12.57 × 1010 m3 and 12.71 × 1010 m3, respectively. The total output of water in 2000 and 2010 was relatively high, with water outputs of 14.71 × 1010 m3 and 15.50 × 1010 m3, respectively.Fig. 3Average water yield depth in Hubei Province from 2000 to 2020.

Average water yield depth in Hubei Province from 2000 to 2020.

In terms of the spatial characteristics of the WY depth from 2000 to 2020 (Fig. 4), the area with the greatest WY depth in 2000 was located in the southwestern part of the study area, and the average WY depth from 2005 to 2020 gradually decreased from the southeastern region to the northwestern region. This pattern is highly consistent with the variation characteristics of the average annual rainfall during the same period, indicating that the WY depth is greatly affected by rainfall. In addition, the WY capacity is closely related to the topography, elevation and land use type. For example, from east to west, the altitude gradually increases, the area of cultivated land and water bodies gradually decreases, and the area of grassland and forestland gradually increases, resulting in a gradual increase in the average evapotranspiration, thus reducing the average WY depth and decreasing the WY capacity. Trade-offs of ecosystem services in Hubei Province

Ecological service time-scale synergy/trade-off relationship

To analyse the synergistic and trade-off relationships between ecosystem services, a 10 km × 10 km grid was used as the evaluation unit, and the quantity of a single ecological service in each grid was extracted for correlation analysis. According to the Pearson correlation coefficient method, if R > 0, there exists a synergistic relationship between ecosystem services, indicating a positive correlation, and the larger the value is, the more significant the synergistic relationship. In contrast, if R < 0, there exists a trade-off relationship between ecosystem services, and the larger the value is, the more significant the trade-off relationship. The five ecosystem services selected in the study area were analysed (Fig. 9). During the five periods from 2000 to 2020, the correlation coefficients among the following ecosystem services were positive: synergistic relationships were observed between SC and NPP, and the values ranged from 0.134 to 0.270. The correlation coefficients between CS and SC fluctuated between 0.161 and 0.308, whereas those between CS and NPP fluctuated between 0.191 and 0.277. The correlation coefficients between CS and FS, SC and FS, and NPP and FS were significantly negative, indicating that there were significant trade-off relationships between these ecosystem services. The correlation coefficients between WY and FS and between WY and NPP were significantly positive in some years but not significant in other years. The correlation coefficients between WY and CS and between WY and SC were not stable, and the significance level varied.Fig. 9Correlation analysis of ecosystem services in Hubei Province.

Discussion

Analysis of spatial diversity mechanisms of ecosystem services

An ecosystem is a balanced system comprising biological communities and the surrounding living environment, which is an important basis for human survival and development. Systematic assessment of the different types of ecosystem services in a region can help clarify the stock of natural capital and increase awareness among the public regarding the value provided by ecosystems to humans and the importance of ecosystems.

Water yield

The water yield directly reflects the absorption capacity of soil and vegetation for precipitation. The western region of Hubei Province has the highest water conservation volume because the land type in the western region is mainly forest land and the vegetation coverage is high. The interception and infiltration effect of forests can reduce the intensity of surface water flow, increase the underground water volume, control soil desertification, and reduce soil erosion by restoring vegetation and establishing water source protection areas. The central region of Hubei Province is an alluvial plain and is an important grain production base, with the dominant land use type being farmland. The groundwater level is chronically high, and the evaporation of vegetation is moderate. Thus, the water yield in this area is relatively high. The urban area of Wuhan in the eastern part of Hubei Province is mainly built-up land, with low evaporation and poor water retention capacity. Therefore, reasonable planning for land use in the eastern part of the study area should be conducted, water storage projects should be constructed reasonably, and drainage and irrigation systems should be improved to effectively alleviate the shortage of the water supply capacity in the region.

Carbon storage

In Hubei Province, the land types with the highest CS level are woodland, cultivated land and grassland, and their CS amounts account for more than 98% of the total CS of all land types. According to the change trend of the amount of CS of each land use type, CS of cultivated land continued to decrease, which is affected mainly by a series of policies for returning farmland to forestland in the study area. The change trend of CS in forestland and grassland remained stable and slightly decreased. Only CS of construction land showed an increasing trend. In terms of spatial analysis, the areas with high CS values were located in the western part of the study area. The main reason was that woodland and grassland were the main land types in these areas, and the vegetation coverage rate was high, leading to a large amount of organic matter accumulation, so the amount of CS was high. Areas with low carbon reserves are located in in the southeastern part of the study area. They occur in plain areas with developed economies and are mainly occupied by construction land, water areas and cultivated land. Owing to intense human activities, organic matter cannot accumulate in large quantities, resulting in low carbon reserves.

Soil conservation

The total amount of SC first increased but then decreased over time, and SC increased spatially from southeast to northwest. The areas with low SC levels occupied most of the study area. These areas are located mainly in the central and eastern parts of the study area. The landform is dominated by an alluvial plain, where rainfall and rivers impose notable destructive effects on land, resulting in severe soil loss in the region. The high-SC areas were distributed in the southwest of the study area as well as in the Dabie Mountain area northeast of the study area. These areas are characterized by relatively high elevations, woodlands and grasslands are the main land cover types, resulting in notable water conservation and SC abilities. Overall, future targeted measures should be formulated. For example, in the eastern part of the study area, land development should be restricted, and ecological protection should be strengthened. In the western part of the study area, we should pay equal attention to construction and ecological protection. In the central part of the research area, agricultural economic activities should be improved as much as possible to promote more high-value-added agricultural.

Food supply

During the study period, the FS capacity in eastern Hubei Province increased significantly, whereas that in western Hubei Province did not change significantly. The food production capacity of a region plays a decisive role in determining the size of the FS service in the region. High-value FS areas were located in the main production areas of agricultural products in the main functional areas. These areas exhibit flat terrain, sufficient water and abundant cultivated land resources, so these areas demonstrated a high grain production capacity. The low-value areas were distributed in the mountainous areas of western Hubei and southeastern Hubei, and the soil and water environments in these areas were poor. In recent years, with the development of the economy and improvements in production levels, the output value of grain in western Hubei has increased annually, and the grain production services in the region have effectively increased.

Net primary productivity

In Hubei Province, NPP exhibited a notable spatial differentiation trend, and the overall trend decreased from northwest to southeast. The areas with high vegetation NPP values were distributed in the western part of Hubei Province. Notably, the vegetation coverage in these areas was high. The spatial distribution differences in NPP are closely related to the vegetation type, climate, elevation and human activities in this region. In the eastern part of Hubei Province, economic development is relatively rapid, human activities are frequent, and the vegetation coverage rate is low, so the vegetation NPP is low. The western part of Hubei Province is characterized by forest or mountain areas with high altitudes, rich vegetation types and relatively high NPP levels. Moreover, the above phenomena are related to the regional hydrothermal conditions. For example, the water resources necessary for vegetation growth are affected mainly by rainfall factors. Sufficient rainfall in a region can promote vegetation recovery and growth, whereas a decrease in precipitation and climate drought can lead to a decrease in vegetation NPP in the region.

Trade-offs and synergies among ecosystem services

In a given ecosystem, increasing the supply capacity of one ecosystem service usually comes at the expense of other ecosystem services. To realize the sustainable development of ecosystems, ecosystem management should not only pursue the single benefits of a certain ecosystem service but also consider the comprehensive benefits of multiple ecosystem services. Therefore, it is very important to study the trade-offs or synergies among ecosystem services for ensuring the sustainable development of ecosystems. According to the spatial autocorrelation analysis results, the trade-offs and synergies among ecosystem services in Hubei Province greatly differed, with the proportion of synergies accounting for 42.86% and the proportion of trade-offs accounting for 4.78% of the total relationships. The remaining relationships were not significant.

In Hubei Province, the trade-off and synergistic relationships among the different ecosystem services followed obvious distribution rules, among which CS, SC and NPP clearly exhibited synergistic relationships because the levels of these ecosystem services are affected mainly by vegetation coverage. Forestland is the land use type with the highest CS service level. Dense forests can increase the production capacity of organic matter, promote photosynthesis, and effectively increase the carbon and water sequestration capacity of vegetation. Moreover, the branches and roots of vegetation can block precipitation and surface runoff, thereby helping to prevent soil erosion. The three ecological services, i.e., CS, SC and NPP, are balanced with FS. The FS capacity of plants is affected mainly by cultivated land. The FS capacity in regions with large cultivated land areas is high, while there is relatively little vegetation in these regions. Therefore, the FS capacity is balanced with the other three ecosystem services, such as CS. The trade-offs between WY and the other four ecosystem services remained less stable. Overall, WY exhibited synergistic relationships with FS and NPP.

The trade-offs and synergies among ecosystem services in Hubei Province indicated significant spatial heterogeneity. Two types of ecosystem services attained synergistic relationships in one region and trade-off relationships in other regions. This finding is consistent with those of previous studies on the spatial heterogeneity in ecosystem services51,52. There are differences in the natural geographical environment and social and economic conditions in various regions, which leads to obvious differences in regional ecosystem service trade-offs and synergies. In general, areas with a high level of urbanization experience severe ecosystem damage53,54. The ecosystems in the southeastern and western parts of Hubei Province exhibited a notable synergistic relationship, whereas those in the other regions exhibited a trade-off relationship. The Wuhan metropolitan area in southeastern Hubei Province is the main human habitation area, followed by areas with flat terrain, cities, farmland and villages. These regions exhibit a high level of urbanization, notable interference from human activities, high land development and utilization, and considerable industrial development, leading to a decline in the ecosystem service capacity. Moreover, the destruction of natural vegetation hinders the positive succession of the ecosystem and affects its regulatory function. In the western part of Hubei Province, the geomorphological conditions are complex, the vegetation coverage rate is high, the interference from human activities is low, and the effect of ecological environment protection is better.

Implications for sustainable management

Research on the spatiotemporal evolution of the trade-offs and synergies among ecosystem services is helpful for ecosystem protection and sustainable development. Hubei is rich in natural resources and exhibits a high level of ecosystem services. However, with the development of the economy and society in recent years, the structure and function of the ecosystem have been seriously affected by human activities, and the ecological environment has been destroyed. Therefore, while steadily increasing social and economic levels, we should also pay attention to the protection of regional ecosystem service functions and promote ecological civilization construction. The government should actively formulate more laws related to the ecological environment and strengthen the legal protection of regional ecosystems. Through legal means, the protection and utilization of ecological resources can be effectively supervised, and entities engaging in illegal acts that cause ecological environment destruction should be severely punished. We should improve the ecological compensation mechanism and provide corresponding compensation to regions that sacrifice economic development for the sake of protecting the ecological environment. In regional spatial planning, on the basis of the balance between ecosystem services, the rational development and utilization of land should aim to optimize the spatial structure of land use and actively promote synergistic relationships between ecosystem services to achieve the sustainable development of ecosystems.

Limitations

The selection criteria for ecosystem services still need to be improved. This paper mainly selects five of the more important types of ecosystem services for assessment. However, among the types of ecosystem services, in addition to the above five, there are other important services, such as climate regulation and cultural entertainment. It also includes habitat risk and water quality purification assessment models. Due to the limitations of basic data, a more comprehensive assessment and analysis could not be conducted. In future research, it is necessary to further supplement and improve these aspects.

Ecosystems rely on dynamic and interacting geographical processes; however, our understanding of these processes is still limited. This study conducted exploratory spatial analysis of ecosystem services on a 10 km × 10 km grid to reveal the spatial autocorrelation characteristics of ecosystem services. However, the relationships between ecosystem services vary at different scales. Therefore, a comprehensive study of ecosystems should further strengthen the research on the evolution mechanisms of ecosystem services at multiple time scales and spatial scales. Future research will explain the trade-off relationships of multi-scale ecosystem services to promote comprehensive ecosystem management.

Conclusions

In this study, several typical ecosystem services in the study area were investigated, the spatiotemporal evolution of different types of ecosystem services in the study area over long periods were systematically analysed, and correlation analysis methods and exploratory spatial data analysis methods were introduced to reveal the trade-offs and synergies among the different types of ecosystem services. The main conclusions are as follows: (1) high-value FS areas were located in the main production areas of agricultural products in the main functional areas, and, affected by the vegetation coverage and climatic conditions, CS, SC and NPP generally showed a spatial distribution pattern of being higher in the west and lower in the east, while the average WY depth gradually decreased from the southeastern region to the northwestern region; (2) the trade-offs and synergies among the different ecosystem services in Hubei Province followed obvious distribution rules, in which CS, SC and NPP exhibited notable synergies, whereas CS, SC and NPP exhibited trade-offs with FS; (3) the trade-offs and synergies among ecosystem services in Hubei Province greatly differed, with the proportion of synergies accounting for 42.86% and the proportion of trade-offs accounting for 4.78% of the total relationships; (4) areas with a high level of urbanization experience severe ecosystem damage. The ecosystems in the southeastern and western parts of Hubei Province exhibited a notable synergistic relationship, whereas those in the other regions exhibited a trade-off relationship; and (5) future targeted measures should be formulated. In the eastern part of the study area, land development should be restricted, and ecological protection should be strengthened. In the western part of the study area, we should pay equal attention to construction and ecological protection.

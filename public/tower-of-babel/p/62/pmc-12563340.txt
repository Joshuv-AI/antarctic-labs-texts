Comparison and Accuracy of Different Impression Procedures Utilizing Hex and Non-Hex Impression Copings
Authors: Ashwini M. Kattimani, Sudhindra Mahoorkar, Swati M. Devani, Soundarya Konin, Sourabh Patil, Priyanka Eklarkar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Accurate impressions are essential to ensure passive fit in implant-supported prostheses. Factors, such as coping design and implant angulation, can significantly influence impression accuracy.Aim:To compare the accuracy of different impression procedures using Hex and Non-Hex impression copings in multiple implant conditions with varying angulations.Materials and Methods:An in vitro study was conducted using three mandibular edentulous acrylic models representing implant angulations of 0°, 5°, and 10°. Two dummy implants were inserted in each model at designated angulations. Hex and Non-Hex copings were used in three configurations: Hex–Hex, Hex–Non-Hex, and Non-Hex–Non-Hex. Impressions were made using the open-tray technique and splinted copings. A total of 42 impressions were recorded. Master casts and reference models were scanned using a Medit T300 scanner, and deviations were measured in mesiodistal (X), buccolingual (Y), and vertical (Z) axes using Blender for Dental software. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS v21.0.Results:The Non-Hex–Non-Hex configuration showed the least deviation across all three axes in 5° and 10° angulated models. The Hex–Hex combination consistently recorded the highest deviation values. Differences in impression accuracy were statistically significant (P < 0.05) across all axes, especially in angulated conditions.Conclusion:Non-Hex impression copings provide superior accuracy in multiple and angulated implant impressions. Their use is clinically recommended in nonparallel implant conditions to enhance prosthetic fit.

INTRODUCTION

Implant-supported prostheses require precise impressions to transfer implant positions accurately. Any deviation during this phase may compromise passive fit, leading to biological or mechanical complications.[1] Several variables, such as the impression technique, coping geometry, and implant angulation, play vital roles in ensuring accuracy.[2] As angulation increases, distortion risk during impression removal also rises.[3] Therefore, coping design becomes a key determinant of accuracy, especially in multiple or nonparallel implant scenarios.[4] This study aimed to compare the accuracy of different impression procedures using Hex and Non-Hex copings under varying implant angulations (0°, 5°, and 10°), using a splinted open-tray technique under controlled in vitro conditions.

MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY

This in vitro study was conducted in the Department of Prosthodontics, H.K.E.S’ S. Nijalingappa’s Institute of Dental Sciences and Research, Gulbarga, using three mandibular edentulous acrylic models simulating implant angulations of 0°, 5°, and 10°. Each model received two dummy implants (DIO, South Korea) in regions 35 and 37. Three coping configurations were tested: Hex–Hex, Hex–Non-Hex, and Non-Hex–Non-Hex. A total of 42 impressions (14 per model) were made using the open-tray technique with splinted impression copings joined by pattern resin. Polyether impression material (3M ESPE Impregum) was used for all impressions. After setting, analogs were attached and master casts poured with Type IV die stone. Scans of the master models and working casts were obtained using a Medit T300 scanner, and STL files were superimposed in Blender for Dental software. Deviations were measured in mesiodistal (X), buccolingual (Y), and vertical (Z) directions. Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 21.0 with one-way ANOVA and post hoc Tukey tests applied to compare deviations across groups. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

RESULTS

This in vitro study aimed to evaluate the accuracy of Hex and Non-Hex impression copings using a splinted open-tray technique across different implant angulations. The deviation between master models and casts was measured across three axes (mesiodistal—X, buccolingual—Y, and vertical—Z) using digital scanning and analysis. The study involved three models: 0°, 5°, and 10°, each with 14 impressions using combinations of Hex–Hex, Hex–Non-Hex, and Non-Hex–Non-Hex copings. It was observed that as angulation increased, the distortion in impression accuracy also increased, particularly with Hex–Hex configurations. Non-Hex–Non-Hex coping configurations demonstrated the least deviation across all three axes. The findings were statistically significant, indicating that coping design plays a crucial role in impression precision, especially in angulated implant placements.

Result table summary

The deviation values recorded across all three axes (X, Y, and Z) demonstrated a clear trend of increasing inaccuracy with increase in implant angulation. As seen in Table 1, the Non-Hex–Non-Hex configuration consistently yielded the least mean deviation in all directions, particularly in the 5° and 10° models, whereas the Hex–Hex group exhibited the highest deviation values. Table 2 shows that the differences between coping configurations across each axis and angulation were statistically significant, with P values < 0.05 across all models. The post hoc analysis in Table 3 further confirmed that the most significant pairwise differences were between the Hex–Hex and Non-Hex–Non-Hex groups in angulated implant scenarios, especially at 10°. These findings clearly establish the superior accuracy of Non-Hex copings in multi-implant and angulated settings.

DISCUSSION

The accuracy of implant impressions is influenced by multiple factors, including coping design, impression technique, and implant angulation.[5] This study demonstrated that increasing implant angulation negatively impacted impression accuracy, with Non-Hex–Non-Hex configurations consistently showing the least deviation, especially at 5° and 10°.[6] The splinted open-tray technique using polyether material and rigid pattern resin provided better dimensional stability and reduced coping movement.[7] Non-Hex copings, being non-engaging, allowed easier impression retrieval and minimized rotational stress compared to hexed counterparts.[8] Statistically significant differences were found between Hex–Hex and Non-Hex–Non-Hex groups (P < 0.01), supporting the clinical use of non-engaging copings in multiple, angulated implant cases.[9] Previous studies also support this recommendation, suggesting improved outcomes with non-hexed copings in angulations up to 20–30°.[10] Thus, Non-Hex copings are preferable in complex implant scenarios to ensure prosthetic accuracy.

CONCLUSION

Non-Hex impression copings demonstrated significantly superior accuracy in recording multiple angulated implants compared to Hex and mixed coping configurations. Therefore, their use is recommended for achieving precise impressions in nonparallel implant scenarios.

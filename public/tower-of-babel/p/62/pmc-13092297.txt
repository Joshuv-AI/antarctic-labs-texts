Temporal variability in healthy donor IFN-γ ELISpot responses reveals a stable IL-7–responsive functional metric for immune phenotyping
Authors: Annemarie Nguyen, Ruth-Ann Brown, Anthony S Bonavia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFunctional T-cell assays such as the IFN-γ ELISpot are increasingly used for immune phenotyping in infection, cancer, and critical illness. Many studies, including recent work linking low IFN-γ ELISpot responses to adverse outcomes in sepsis, compare patient samples to a single cross-sectional healthy donor baseline, implicitly assuming temporal stability of IFN-γ secretion in health. We conducted a longitudinal study of 9 healthy adults using whole-blood anti-CD3/anti-CD28 ELISpot assays with and without IL-7 co-stimulation. Absolute IFN-γ production varied significantly within individuals over approximately 10 weeks, whereas the ratio of IL-7–augmented to anti-CD3/anti-CD28–only responses remained remarkably stable across all readouts. This IL-7 responsiveness ratio may represent a robust, time-independent functional metric of intact T-cell signaling. These findings demonstrate that absolute IFN-γ ELISpot values in healthy individuals are dynamic and support ratio-based metrics for immune phenotyping applications.

Introduction

Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) enzyme-linked immunospot (ELISpot) analysis of whole blood is increasingly applied to functional immune phenotyping across diverse clinical settings, including sepsis, trauma, transplantation, and cancer immunotherapy. The assay provides a direct readout of T-cell cytokine production capacity ex vivo, complementing static cell counts or transcriptomic markers.1,2

Recent multicenter trials have used whole-blood IFN-γ ELISpot responses to define immunologic endotypes associated with adverse outcomes, including an “immunosuppressed” phenotype in septic patients characterized by reduced IFN-γ production.3,4 Although such studies typically compare patient samples to a cross-sectional distribution of healthy donor values, the implicit assumption that IFN-γ secretory capacity in healthy individuals is temporally stable has not been formally tested in the context of the ELISpot assay. This assumption underlies interpretation of patient values relative to a fixed “normal” reference state and therefore has important implications for the classification of immunosuppressed phenotypes.

Given growing interest in functional immune diagnostics, the temporal behavior of IFN-γ ELISpot responses in healthy adults is a critical but unresolved question. Establishing whether healthy baseline responses are stable or dynamic is therefore essential for refining ELISpot-based immune phenotyping frameworks. Here, we performed a longitudinal study of healthy volunteers using anti-CD3/anti-CD28 whole-blood ELISpot assays with and without IL-7 co-stimulation. We show that absolute IFN-γ responses vary substantially within individuals over time, whereas an IL-7–responsive ratio metric remains stable across sampling points, suggesting a robust, time-independent functional readout.

Materials and methods

Study participants and sampling

Healthy adult volunteers (n = 9) were enrolled in a prospective longitudinal study approved by the Penn State Institutional Review Board (IRB #27044). Inclusion criteria were age ≥18 years and absence of chronic immune disorders or immunomodulatory therapy. Exclusion criteria included acute illness within 7 days of blood draw. Paired blood samples were collected at enrollment and again after a mean of 71 ± 24 days. All samples were obtained between 7:00 and 9:00 AM to minimize circadian variation.

Sex as a biologic variable

Both sexes were included in this study. Of the 9 healthy adult participants, 3 were female and 6 were male. No sex-based exclusions were applied, and sample collection, assay conditions, and analytic procedures were identical across participants. Given the small sample size and absence of sex-associated differences in IFN-γ responses, sex was not used as a stratifying variable in statistical analyses.

Blood collection and processing

Venous blood was collected into heparin-coated tubes (BD Vacutainer). Samples were processed within 1 hour of phlebotomy. Five microliters of whole blood was diluted 1:10 in RPMI 1640 (Gibco) supplemented with 1% L-glutamine. Diluted samples were used immediately for ELISpot stimulation assays and for flow cytometry staining.

Whole-blood IFN-γ ELISpot assay

IFN-γ ELISpot assays were performed using precoated 96-well polyvinylidene fluoride plates (CTL ImmunoSpot, Cleveland, OH, USA). All assays were run in technical triplicate under identical environmental and incubation conditions.

Diluted whole blood was plated at 50 μL per well in a final ELISpot well volume of 200 μL. Samples were stimulated for 18 to 22 hours at 37 °C in 5% CO2 under one of 3 conditions: Unstimulated controls received RPMI alone. For polyclonal activation, wells were stimulated with anti-CD3 (clone HIT3a; BioLegend #300302) at 500 ng/mL together with anti-CD28 (clone CD28.2; BioLegend #302902) at 2.5 μg/mL. For IL-7 co-stimulation, recombinant human IL-7 (R&D Systems, #207IL005) was added at 50 ng/mL in combination with the anti-CD3/anti-CD28 cocktail.

Plate development and analysis

After incubation, plates were washed and developed following manufacturer instructions. Spots were enumerated using the CTL ImmunoSpot S6 Analyzer with automated spot-detection algorithms. All measurements represent the mean of triplicate wells for each condition.

Flow cytometry

Flow cytometry was used to quantify T-cell abundance and activation. Whole blood (100 μL) was stained with the following fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies (all from BioLegend): anti-CD3 (UCHT1), anti-CD4 (RPA-T4), and anti-HLA-DR (L243).

Red blood cells were lysed with BD FACS Lysing Solution. Acquisition was performed on a BD FACSymphony A3 cytometer, with compensation applied using single-stain controls. Data were analyzed using FlowJo v10 software. Structural gating included lymphocyte gate by forward scatter/side scatter; CD3+ T-cell gate; and activation gate, defined as CD3+HLA-DR+.

Statistical analysis

Within-subject comparisons between time points were performed using paired 2-tailed t tests. Data are shown as mean ± SEM unless otherwise noted. P values <0.05 were considered statistically significant. Analyses were conducted in R v4.5.2 software.

Results

Temporal variation in absolute IFN-γ secretion

Across participants, we observed significant intra-individual variability in absolute IFN-γ secretion over time (Supplementary material). Anti-CD3/anti-CD28-stimulated spot-forming units (SFU) per 5 μL whole blood differed between sampling points (mean: 445.8 vs 744.6; P = 0.027), as did IL-7–augmented SFU (833.8 vs 1344.4; P = 0.036) (Fig. 1A). Similar temporal variation was observed in total expression (TE) per 5 μL blood (Fig. 1B) and TE normalized to lymphocyte count (Fig. 1C).

(A) Anti-CD3/anti-CD28–stimulated SFU per 5 μL whole blood at 2 time points (blue and red) separated by 30 to 90 days. (B) Corresponding TE per 5 μL blood. (C) TE normalized to 1,000 lymphocytes. (D) Ratios of IL-7–augmented to anti-CD3/anti-CD28–only responses for each metric. Lines connect paired measurements from individual donors. P values from paired t tests. *P < 0.05; **P < 0.01. n = 9.

Absolute lymphocyte counts and T-cell activation markers remained stable between time points, indicating that these fluctuations largely reflect dynamic physiologic variation in T-cell functional readiness rather than changes in circulating cell numbers or activation status.

Stability of an IL-7–responsive functional ratio

In contrast to absolute IFN-γ responses, the ratio of IL-7–augmented to anti-CD3/anti-CD28–only responses showed minimal intra-individual drift across the same interval (Fig. 1D). This stability was consistent across all output metrics (SFU, TE per 5 μL, TE per 1,000 lymphocytes), with no statistically significant differences between time points (P = 0.155–0.166).

These findings suggest that IL-7 co-stimulation normalizes transient variation in T-cell energetic and metabolic state, such that the IL-7 to anti-CD3/anti-CD28 ratio reflects an underlying, time-independent property of T-cell signaling capacity.

Discussion

This longitudinal study demonstrates that absolute IFN-γ ELISpot responses in healthy adults are not temporally static. Within individuals, anti-CD3/anti-CD28–induced IFN-γ secretion varied by 20% to 40%, despite stable lymphocyte counts and activation markers. Recognizing this dynamic baseline is essential for interpreting the evolution of immunoparalysis over time.

Importantly, several recent high-impact studies defining immunosuppressed endotypes using IFN-γ ELISpot assays have relied on cross-sectional healthy donor reference distributions. Implicit in this framework is the assumption that IFN-γ secretory capacity in healthy individuals is temporally stable. Our findings directly challenge that assumption. Even under controlled sampling conditions, healthy donor responses demonstrated measurable intra-individual drift over approximately 10 weeks. Although the magnitude of fluctuation observed here is smaller than the profound suppression reported in some critically ill populations, the existence of baseline variability is methodologically important when defining thresholds or z-scores relative to “normal” physiology.

The magnitude of fluctuation observed in healthy individuals is smaller than the profound suppression reported in some critically ill populations, but it remains methodologically important. Frameworks that interpret patient values relative to a single cross-sectional healthy donor distribution assume that the reference state is fixed. Our results show that this assumption is not strictly valid and that temporal drift in healthy responses should be considered when defining immunosuppressed states.

By contrast, the IL-7–responsive ratio metric we evaluated was remarkably stable over time. IL-7 is a homeostatic cytokine essential for T-cell survival and metabolic fitness via IL-7Rα–JAK-STAT5 signaling.5 Co-stimulation with IL-7 likely buffers against transient variations in mitochondrial function, nutrient status, or subclinical inflammatory influences, thereby revealing preserved signaling capacity independent of baseline effector output. The stability of the IL-7 to anti-CD3/anti-CD28 ratio across multiple readouts suggests that it may serve as a robust, time-insensitive functional metric for immune phenotyping.

The selection of IL-7 was intentional. Unlike cytokines such as IL-12, which strongly amplify effector function, IL-7 primarily supports T-cell survival and metabolic competence through homeostatic signaling pathways. The goal was not to maximize IFN-γ production, but to probe preserved signaling reserve under physiologic co-stimulation. Alternative γc cytokines (signaling through the common γ-chain [CD132] receptor subunit) such as IL-2 or IL-15 may also augment IFN-γ production, but their stronger proliferative or activation-associated effects could confound interpretation of signaling stability versus amplification. Future comparative studies evaluating alternative cytokine co-stimulation strategies may further refine this framework.

Our findings have 2 main implications. First, absolute IFN-γ ELISpot values in healthy blood should be interpreted with awareness of within-person temporal variability, particularly when defining thresholds or z-scores relative to “normal” physiology. Second, incorporating ratio-based metrics that capture IL-7–responsive signaling may improve the reproducibility and biological validity of ELISpot-based immune diagnostics in sepsis and other settings.

Several limitations warrant discussion. The cohort size was modest, and sampling was performed at 2 time points. The objective of this study, however, was not to define population reference intervals but to determine whether intra-individual temporal variability exists under controlled conditions. The consistent observation of drift across individuals suggests that healthy baseline responses are dynamic rather than fixed. Larger longitudinal studies with more frequent sampling will further characterize the kinetics and determinants of this variability.

Inclusion of immunocompromised patients would provide important validation that IL-7 co-stimulation does not mask true immunosuppression. However, the primary aim of this study was to interrogate the temporal stability of the healthy reference state itself. Demonstrating variability in health is a prerequisite to accurately defining suppression in disease. Application of this ratio-based framework to septic and critically ill cohorts is ongoing, and those data will be presented in a subsequent manuscript.

Taken together, the importance of this work lies not in redefining clinical endotypes, but in challenging a foundational assumption underlying prior ELISpot-based immune phenotyping studies, namely that healthy IFN-γ secretory capacity is temporally static. Recognizing physiologic variability in health is essential for refining functional immune diagnostics and for avoiding overinterpretation of single time-point deviations in heterogeneous clinical populations.

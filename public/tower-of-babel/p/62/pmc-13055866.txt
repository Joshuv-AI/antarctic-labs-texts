Cost Minimization and Cost Comparison of the Use of Conventional Double Lumen Tube versus Single-Use VivaSight-Double Lumen Tube in Thoracic Surgery: The Pascale Institute Experience
Authors: Maurizio Marchesini, Michela D’Antò, Rosanna Accardo, Marika Aprea, Giuseppe D’Agostino, Maria Romano, Vittorio Santoriello, Gilda Pasta, Francesca Bifulco, Arturo Cuomo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeOne lung ventilation (OLV) is essential in thoracic surgery to isolate and ventilate a single lung, ensuring optimal surgical exposure and patient safety. Precise placement of a double lumen tube (DLT) is critical and conventionally confirmed via reusable bronchoscopy.Patients and MethodsWe evaluated the economic and organisational impact of introducing VivaSightDLT—an endobronchial tube with an integrated camera—at Fondazione G. Pascale (Naples). Data from 50 patients were reviewed to compare device efficacy and positioning accuracy. Cost data from the Institute’s management control informed a cost minimization analysis (CMA) and cost comparison analysis (CCA), projecting financial outcomes over 1, 3, and 5 years, based on an annual thoracic surgery volume of 400 cases (328 eligible for VivaSightDLT).ResultsAlthough the per unit cost of VivaSightDLT exceeds that of the conventional DLT, mean savings per procedure were €86.36. When additional intraoperative bronchoscopic checks were required, savings increased to €193.89 per case. Net savings of €16,728.54 at 1 year, €50,185.62 at 3 years, and €83,642.70 at 5 years (15% reduction in direct variable costs) were projected. Full replacement of conventional DLTs would yield a 41% reduction in direct variable costs (€33,457.08, €100,371.25, and €167,285.41 over 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively). VivaSightDLT also reduced operative times by 14–25 minutes (19.5 min IC95%: 14–25), enhancing operating room turnover.ConclusionThe integration of VivaSight DLT at the Istituto Nazionale Tumori, Fondazione G. Pascale, demonstrates superior cost-effectiveness and procedural organisational efficiency compared to conventional DLTs. By eliminating routine reusable bronchoscopy and reducing procedure duration, VivaSight DLT represents an optimal strategy for OLV in thoracic surgery.

Introduction

One-lung ventilation (OLV) is a critical technique in thoracic and certain abdominal surgeries. It enables effective surgical exposure, improved lung isolation, and selective ventilation of one lung while collapsing the other. However, OLV introduces challenges in maintaining oxygenation and preventing injury to the non-ventilated lung, highlighting the importance of precise device selection and monitoring.

To achieve OLV, clinicians have traditionally relied on various devices, including double-lumen tubes (DLTs) such as the Robertshaw or Carlens tubes and bronchial blockers. DLTs are favored for their ability to provide ventilation and isolation of each lung independently, ensuring optimal control during procedures.1 However, their use requires skillful placement, as incorrect positioning can lead to complications such as hypoxemia and trauma to the airway. Bronchial blockers offer a less invasive alternative, enabling single-lung isolation when a standard endotracheal tube is already in place.2 Still, they may be less effective in maintaining airway control and in cases requiring frequent lung re-expansion.3

Despite their utility, each of these devices carries inherent risks. DLTs, while effective, can lead to mucosal injury, tracheal irritation, and postoperative sore throats due to their larger size and stiffness.4 Bronchial blockers, although smaller, may increase the risk of atelectasis or incomplete lung collapse, compromise the surgical field or affect oxygenation.5

The development of the VivaSight endotracheal tube, incorporating real-time visualization, represents a significant advance in OLV. By providing continuous imaging of tube placement, the VivaSight tube offers enhanced positioning accuracy and reduces the risk of displacement and related complications.6 This paper examines the comparative advantages of the VivaSight tube over traditional options such as Robertshaw tubes, focusing on their potential for improving organizational efficiency and cost savings in zero-volume ventilation (OLV). This study evaluates the economic and organizational impact of the VivaSight-DLT intubation system compared to conventional OLV systems.

Materials and Methods

The study was conducted at a single center, the National Cancer Institute of Naples (Italy), Fondazione Pascale, where approximately 400 interventions are performed annually in thoracic surgery. The standard clinical practice used at the National Cancer Institute of Naples (Italy), Fondazione Pascale, involved using a double-lumen Robertshaw-type endobronchial tube. For these devices, bronchoscopic control is required to ensure the correct positioning of the endobronchial tube before the start of the surgical procedure and as required during the operation.7–10 Therefore, the waiting time to perform the bronchoscopic check is particularly important.

The Institute has two reusable bronchoscopes (bf-xp190, 3.1/1.2 EXERAIII) and one column available for control procedures and two operating rooms dedicated to performing thoracic surgery. Considering an average disinfection time of a single reusable bronchoscope of 70 minutes, it is impossible to plan the next operation before the disinfection of the reusable bronchoscope is completed. This reduces the turnover of the operating rooms. This factor was considered when evaluating the economic effectiveness of the device.

The evaluation was conducted using a cost minimization analysis (CMA). A CMA is a technique used in business decisions to evaluate the opportunity to introduce a new technology to replace a previous one. After studying the clinical results of alternative technologies, it is concluded that they can be considered equivalent in the relevant aspects (same effects or comparable effects).10–19 The assessment then focuses on costs, and the best choice is the one that achieves the desired effect at the lowest cost. The study did not require approval from the ethics committee.

A detailed analysis of the costs incurred for each thoracic surgery procedure at the G. Pascale Institute in Naples was conducted, with reference only to the variable direct costs associated with the type of device used, to analyze the possible savings following the introduction of the intubation system with integrated VivaSight-DLT camera. This category included the costs of consumables used during the surgery, the cost of the surgical team and use of the operating room per operation, and the possible cost of reprocessing the reusable bronchoscope.

This choice was dictated by the singularity of each intervention, which prevents comparison of the total costs of the procedures (even for the same type of intervention), because these are the only costs entailing a significant change in expenditure for the Institute with the introduction of the new device.

This CMA highlights the economic feasibility of using the VivaSight-DLT camera, supported by a detailed cost framework adaptable to diverse healthcare environments. By transparently reporting each cost component, the findings can be easily translated to other settings with differing personnel, device costs, or facility charges, making this analysis a valuable tool for decision-makers aiming to optimize resource allocation.

The consumables costs were obtained from the information given by the pharmacy unit and the superintendance of the Institute managing the procurement of medical devices. The Robertshaw DLT costs €50.00 (VAT included), while the VivaSight-DLT costs approximately €230.00 (VAT included).

The surgical team for a thoracic surgery procedure comprises three surgeons, one anesthetist, and three nurses; one endoscopist and one endoscopist technician are also required for bronchoscopic control. The costs per minute for each professional involved in the operation were obtained partly from a study20 and partly from the analysis of the costs internal to the Institute. Therefore, the following team costs were assumed: anesthetist/surgeon/endoscopist (1.00 €/min) and nurse/endoscopist technician (0.50 €/min).

The cost of using the operating room is 4.30 € per minute, including utilities (water and electricity) and cleaning and laundry costs. This value was obtained from the internal management control data of the Pascale Hospital.

The cost of disinfecting the reusable bronchoscope was obtained from the literature21 and is €18.00 for the materials and structures used. It must be specified that this source refers to a reprocessing cycle of a cystoscope, but as these devices are very similar to bronchoscopes, the use of material for a reprocessing cycle is almost comparable. However, the cost of the endoscopist technician for approximately 70 minutes (sd:0,020199) must be added (Cost T5: €35.00), giving a total of approximately €53.00 for a reprocessing cycle of the disposable bronchoscope.

Since the reusable bronchoscope control remains the “gold standard” for intubations with the classic endotracheal tube, it is also necessary to consider the cost per procedure of the multipurpose reusable bronchoscope and the related endoscopy column. According to the Institute’s superintendance, the facility available to the team includes an endoscopic column and two reusable bronchoscopes with a 5-year full-risk rental and maintenance contract: €31,918.13 for each reusable bronchoscope (€6.383, 63/Year for each) and €49,697.00 for a complete video endoscopy tower (€9.939, 40/Year). Considering therefore an average of approximately 400 thoracic surgeries per year, the cost per procedure is approximately €31.92 for the bronchoscope and €24.85 for the column, for a total cost of €56.77 per procedure.

A total of 50 thoracic surgeries were observed, compared to approximately 400 surgeries performed during the annual activity of the G. Pascale Institute in Naples in 2023.

The patients eligible for the analysis were 46, 22 with the conventional DLT and 24 with the innovative device; four were removed because they had difficult airways (eg, post-cord stenosis with impossibility of advancing the DLT beyond the vocal cords, subject previously operated on with supraglottic laryngectomy and glottic hood). For these patients, it was necessary to use a single-lumen endotracheal tube with a smaller diameter blocker than double-lumen endobronchial tubes.

Despite the higher initial cost of the VivaSight-DLT (€230 vs €50 for the Robertshaw DLT), the CMA demonstrated significant savings per procedure. The savings primarily originated from eliminating additional bronchoscopic controls and reducing procedure times. For interventions requiring a single bronchoscopic check, the average savings were €86.36 per procedure. In cases with two bronchoscopic checks, the savings increased to €193.89 per procedure.

The VivaSight-DLT reduced the overall procedure time by 14 minutes (IC95%: 12.58–15.42) compared to conventional DLT cases requiring a single bronchoscopic check and by 25 minutes (IC95%: 23.58–26.42) when two checks were necessary. This reduction in operative time increases turnover in the operating room, enhancing overall efficiency.

Cost Comparison Analysis (CCA)

With reference to the G. Pascale National Cancer Institute in Naples, the annual number of thoracic surgery interventions is 400 on average (according to the data collected for the 2023 interventions). However, considering that of these, approximately 72 interventions were performed with endobronchial ultrasound (Ebus), the number of target interventions of this innovative device would be approximately 328 (2023 data).

It is specified that the interventions performed with endobronchial ultrasound (Ebus) were discarded in the analysis in question because this technology comprises a video bronchoscope equipped with a micro-ultrasound probe, which is not comparable with the VivaSight technology and tubes used in the conventional DLT.

Discussion

This study highlights the economic and organizational benefits of adopting the VivaSight-DLT for thoracic surgery in a real-world healthcare setting. While the initial device cost exceeds that of the conventional DLT, the innovative device eliminates the need for a bronchoscopic check in most cases, significantly reducing direct costs and operative times.

There is currently an analysis24 in the literature that mainly analyses the clinical and safety added value of Vivasight, and to a lesser extent its economic impact. However, thanks to the consideration of a larger sample of patients and all the costs and time required for the procedure, this analysis aimed to provide the literature with a rigorous economic and organisational impact analysis to highlight the true economic and organisational added value of the device in real clinical practice.

The integration of a continuous visualization system within the VivaSight-DLT not only improves procedural workflow but also allows for immediate correction of tube displacement without requiring additional equipment.

This study certainly has limitations that need to be addressed: The limited sample size and dependence on predicted scenarios mean that the results should be interpreted with some caution.

About the sample of patients analysed (n=50) is not particularly large and is limited to a single facility, which may make it difficult to extend the conclusions to other hospitals with different organisational structures or workflows. In terms of methodology, additional values or methodologies that have not been considered here could certainly be taken into account in future studies, which could further confirm our data.

Conclusion

For institutions such as the G. Pascale Institute, with over 328 eligible procedures annually, adopting VivaSight-DLT can be a cost- and time-saving alternative to conventional DLT.

Although the analysis was conducted in a specific institutional setting, with all the limitations that this entails, such as variations in personnel costs, device prices or procedural volumes, the study represents the experience of a major Italian hospital institution about thoracic surgery procedures. The integration of VivaSight DLT at the Istituto Nazionale Tumori, Fondazione G. Pascale, demonstrates superior cost savings and organisational efficiency of procedures compared to conventional DLTs. By eliminating routine reusable bronchoscopy and reducing the duration of the procedure, VivaSight DLT now represents an optimal strategy for OLV in thoracic surgery.

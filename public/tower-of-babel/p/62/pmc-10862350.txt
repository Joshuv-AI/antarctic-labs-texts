Light chain of a public SARS-CoV-2 class-3 antibody modulates neutralization against Omicron
Authors: Anamika Patel, Sanjeev Kumar, Lilin Lai, Meredith Keen, Rajesh Valanparambil, Chennareddy Chakravarthy, Zane Laughlin, Filipp Frank, Narayanaiah Cheedarla, Hans P. Verkerke, Andrew S. Neish, John D. Roback, Carl W. Davis, Jens Wrammert, Amit Sharma, Rafi Ahmed, Mehul S. Suthar, Kaja Murali-Krishna, Anmol Chandele, Eric Ortlund
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
SUMMARYThe pairing of antibody genes IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14 is established as a public immune response that potently cross-neutralizes SARS-CoV-2 variants, including Omicron, by targeting class-3/RBD-5 epitopes in the receptor binding domain (RBD). LY-CoV1404 (bebtelovimab) exemplifies this, displaying exceptional potency against Omicron sub-variants up to BA.5. Here, we report a human antibody, 002-S21B10, encoded by the public clonotype IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14. While 002-S21B10 neutralized key SARS-CoV-2 variants, it did not neutralize Omicron, despite sharing >92% sequence similarity with LY-CoV1404. The structure of 002-S21B10 in complex with spike trimer plus structural and sequence comparisons with LY-CoV1404 and other IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14 antibodies revealed significant variations in light-chain orientation, paratope residues, and epitope-paratope interactions that enable some antibodies to neutralize Omicron but not others. Confirming this, replacing the light chain of 002-S21B10 with the light chain of LY-CoV1404 restored 002-S21B10’s binding to Omicron. Understanding such Omicron evasion from public response is vital for guiding therapeutics and vaccine design.

INTRODUCTION

Antibodies offer the best immune defense against invading pathogens. The random pairing of heavy and light chains, along with genetic variations in antibody-expressing genes, gives rise to a huge diversity of antibodies capable of recognizing diverse antigenic epitopes.1 Some antigen-specific antibody genes are more frequently used among different individuals, known as a public immune response.2–5 This is observed among people infected with or vaccinated for severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2).6–8 However, the emergence of new SARS-CoV-2 variants consistently challenges the acquired public immunity.9,10

The receptor binding domain (RBD) is a major antigenic site in the spike protein harboring four (class 1–4) or seven (RBD 1–7) epitopes categorized under two widely used classification schemes.11,12 The early Omicron sub-variants (up to BA.5) were more frequently mutated at class-1 and −2 epitopes that align with the receptor binding motif (RBM), compared with relatively conserved class-3 and −4 epitopes.13,14 Corroborating this, antibodies targeting class-3 and −4 antigenic sites show broader neutralization against most SARS-CoV-2 variants, including Omicron.3,15 Some of the most potent broadly neutralizing antibodies (bnAbs) recognize the class-3/RBD-5 RBD epitope, including LY-CoV1404 (also known as bebtelovimab), which was granted emergency use authorization (EUA) for antibody-based treatment.3,16 LY-CoV1404, like other SARS-CoV-2 mAbs, S2X324 and XGv264, is a public antibody clonotype encoded by IGHV2–5 and IGLV2–14 genes pairing with the HxI/VxxI/L motif containing an 11-amino-acid CDRH3.3,16–18 Since LY-CoV1404 shows remarkable potency across many key SARS-CoV-2 variants up to BA.5, understanding the molecular features responsible for class-3 paratope-epitope interactions is of great interest, as they can be targeted to elicit an effective broad immune response informing next-generation vaccine design.

We recently isolated and characterized several potent RBD-binding monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) from individuals infected with the ancestral SARS-CoV-2 strain in India and even reported a class-3 epitope-binding mAb that neutralizes all highly evasive Omicron variants up to BA.5.4,19,20 Herein, we characterize another potent class-3/RBD-5 public antibody (002-S21B10) also isolated from the same individual, exhibiting >92% amino acid sequence similarity to LY-CoV1404. Unlike LY-CoV1404, it did not neutralize the Omicron BA.1 variant, suggesting a different specificity in binding/neutralization. We reveal the mechanism of Omicron evasion by structurally defining the differences in 002-S21B10’s epitope recognition, influenced by the key variations in its light chain that alter molecular interactions in the epitope region leading to heterogeneous neutralization of SARS-CoV-2 variants by this public antibody.

RESULTS

002-S21B10 neutralized most key SARS-CoV-2 variants except Omicron

In this study, we report the characterization of a mAb, 002-S21B10, which is encoded by the public IGHV2–5 and IGLV2–14 antibody clonotype identified from a single B cell screen of COVID-19-recovered individuals from India.19 This antibody contains an 11-amino-acid CDRH3 with a conserved HxI/VxxI/L motif3,16 (Figure 1A). So far, 16 such mAbs, including EUA-granted LY-CoV1404 (bebtelovimab), have been reported in the CoV-AbDab database from eight different donors3,7,16,18 (Data S1). To evaluate if 002-S21B10 also shows broad cross-reactivity like LY-CoV1404,3,16 first, we measured its binding affinity and neutralization potency across key SARS-CoV-2 variants (Figures 1B–1D). The 002-S21B10 showed 0.16–0.2 nM molar affinity for recombinant spike-WA.1, -Alpha, -Beta, -Gamma, and -Delta variants, but lost binding to Omicron BA.1 and BA.2 (Figures 1B and 1D). Consistent with the binding data, 002-S21B10 potently neutralized live Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta variant viruses with IC50 ranging from 0.33 to 0.54 µg/mL, but no measurable neutralization was observed against BA.1 and BA.2 Omicron variants (Figure 1C). Like LY-CoV1404, mAb 002-S21B10 also neutralized WA.1 by blocking ACE2 binding as observed in an ACE2 blocking assay, albeit with much (~100-fold) lower potency compared with LY-CoV1404 (Figure 1F). A control spike-N-terminal domain (NTD) binding antibody did not block ACE2 binding. The difference in potency in ACE2 competition between LY-CoV1404 and 002-S21B10 can be explained by the ~62-fold affinity difference in binding to spike WA.1 itself under the same assay conditions (Figure 1E). These results suggest that, of 16 IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14-encoding class-3 antibodies reported and documented in the CoV-AbDab database to date, only some (5/16) were tested, and of these, only three retained neutralization potency and breadth against most SARS-CoV-2 variants, including Omicron, in live virus neutralization assays (Data S1), whereas 002-S21B10 lost it against Omicron.7 However, when tested in pseudovirus neutralization assays, most mAbs (13/16) showed severely reduced potency or lack of neutralization for Omicron compared with the WA.1 variant (Data S1). Here, for comparison, the antibody is considered an Omicron neutralizer if there is no significant loss in potency compared with the WA.1 variant based on the results of the live virus neutralization assay.

002-S21B10 recognizes the class-3/RBD-5 epitope for neutralization

To delineate the mechanism of heterogeneous neutralization of SARS-CoV-2 variants by 002-S21B10 in contrast to LY-CoV1404, we determined the cryoelectron microscopy (cryo-EM) structure of full-length 002-S21B10 IgG in complex with WA.1 spike-6P at 3.5 Å resolution. 002-S21B10 binds the RBD in both ‘‘up’’ and ‘‘down’’ conformations (Figures 2 and S1). We observed two distinct structural classes for the spike-002-S21B10 complex. In the first structural class (32% particles), all RBDs are in the ‘‘down’’ conformation and only two RBDs engage with Fabs. Interestingly, in this structural class, one Fab both recognizes the primary RBD epitope and engages a neighboring RBD through less specific secondary interactions (Figures 2A and 2B). The second structural class (29% particles) shows one RBD in the ‘‘up’’ conformation and two RBDs in the ‘‘down’’ conformation, each in complex with a Fab (Figure S1).

002-S21B10 recognizes an antigenic site on the RBD that aligns with a class-3/RBD-5 epitope like LY-CoV1404, XGv264, and S2X324, burying a total surface of 787 Å2 with ~60% and ~40% contribution from heavy and light chains, respectively (Figure 2C). All three heavy-chain complementarity-determining region (CDR) loops are involved in epitope recognition. Heavy-chain residue Y32 in CDR1 directly hydrogen bonds with the Y451 hydroxyl group and D442 carboxyl group in the RBD (Figure 2F). The heavy-chain CDR2 makes multiple interactions with RBD residues 444–446 (Figure 2G). Specifically, K444 in RBD forms a salt bridge with carboxyl groups of the D56 and D58 residues in the heavy-chain CDR2 (Figure 2G). In addition, the side chains of Y54 and R60 in heavy-chain CDR2 interact with the main chain of RBD residues V445 and G446, respectively (Figures 2G and 2E). The CDR3 loop in the heavy chain contributes only through hydrophobic interactions. Regarding the light chain, only CDR1 and CDR2 residues participate in epitope recognition. Specifically, in light-chain CDR1, the hydroxyl group of Y32 hydrogen bonds with T500 in the RBD, and Y34 mediates a network of interactions with RBD residues N439 and N440 (Figure 2D). Also, light-chain CDR2 residue E52 recognizes N440 in the RBD through H bonding (Figure 2D). Light-chain CDR3 does not contribute directly to epitope recognition. Additional contacts are made with a neighboring RBD, between light-chain residues N62 and S58, which hydrogen bond to E484 and make hydrophobic interactions with F486, respectively (Figure 2B). These interactions are not observed in the second structural class with one RBD ‘‘up’’ conformation, suggesting their dynamic nature.

Structural basis of Omicron escape from 002-S21B10

The RBD class-3 nAbs are some of the most broad-spectrum antibodies.7,15,16,19 Even though all reported IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14-encoding public antibodies show a high degree of sequence similarity, only some of these (S2X234, LY-CoV1404, and XGv264) are reported to show broad-spectrum neutralization when tested in live virus neutralization assay across variants of concern (VOCs), including Omicron, suggesting heterogeneity in their broad neutralization responses.3,7

Several of these bnAb structures are available in complex with spike/RBD showing recognition of the class-3/RBD5 epitope inclusive of Omicron BA.1–BA.5 mutations.3,16,17,21 For example, LY-CoV1404, XGv264, and S2X32416,17,21 all recognize the same epitope as 002-S21B10, but unlike 002-S21B10, they can accommodate all key VOC mutations, including Omicron BA.1– BA.5. To define the key determinants that allow 002-S21B10 to be effective toward other key variants but not Omicron, we carried out a structural comparison of 002-S21B10 and LY-CoV1404. Overall, the two structures align with an average root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) of 1.1 Å for Cα backbone atoms (Figure 2C). However, the light chain aligns poorly, with an RMSD of 4.2 Å, relative to the heavy chain, with an RMSD of Å for all Cα backbone atoms (Figure 2C).

Focusing exclusively on the structure of CDRs between two antibodies shows striking similarities in the heavy-chain CDR loops, but different paths for the light-chain CDR loops. Light-chain CDR1 takes a different trajectory and does not make the same set of interactions as LY-CoV1404. In the LY-CoV1404 structure, the light-chain CDR1 residues D31 and N33 reach out to make several contacts with RBD residues T500 and Q506, but these contacts are absent in 002-S21B10 (Figure 2D). Similarly, the light-chain CDR3 in LY-CoV1404 comes closer to the RBD, making a direct hydrogen bond with RBD residues G446 and Q498, whereas CDR3 in 002-S21B10, which is one residue longer compared with LY-CoV1404, shifts ~2 Å away from the RBD, resulting in a different set of contacts (Figure 2E). Omicron, which has mutations at positions G446 and Q498, can be accommodated by LY-CoV1404, but not by 002-S21B10. This can be structurally explained; without light-chain CDR3-mediated G446 interactions in 002-S21B10, the RBD G446 residue shifts 1 Å toward R60 in heavy-chain CDR2 to make direct interaction with the side chain of R60, and a glycine-to-serine mutation at this position would result in a steric clash to ablate 002-S21B10’s binding and neutralization (Figure 2E). However, a Q498R mutation does not impose any steric clash and is structurally predicted to be accommodated by 002-S21B10. In addition, N440 is identically recognized in both structures. However, in the LY-CoV1404 structure, the N440K mutation can be accommodated better compared with 002-S21B10, as 14 of 24 lysine residue rotamers at this position would sterically clash with P103 in heavy-chain CDR3 in the 002-S21B10 structure, whereas only 2 of 24 rotamer conformations at the lysine 440 position would sterically clash in the LY-CoV1404 structure. Overall, the G446S and N440K mutations alongside the non-accommodating molecular features in the light-chain paratope could be the major contributing factor for Omicron escape from 002-S21B10.

Omicron equivalent mutational analysis in 002-S21B10 epitope

While most of the variants, including Alpha, Beta, Gamma, and Delta, contain only the N501Y mutation within the 002-S21B10 epitope, Omicron BA.1–BA.5 contain three additional mutations (N440K, G446S, and Q498R) alongside N501Y within 002-S21B10’s epitope. Since 002-S21B10 can bind and neutralize all mentioned VOCs, except Omicron, the N501Y mutation might not be responsible for 002-S21B10’s Omicron escape. To validate the structural prediction as well as to evaluate the functional contribution of each of the Omicron mutations individually toward binding of 002-S21B10, we introduced N440K, G446S, and Q498R single mutations into surface-expressed spike WA.1 into HEK293 cells and tested their binding toward 002-S21B10 (Figure 3A). While Q498R retains 002-S21B10 binding with the same affinity of wild type (WT), introducing N440K and G446S mutations into WA.1 spike protein resulted in a total loss of 002-S21B10 binding (Figure 3A), confirming the significant role of these mutations in Omicron’s escape.

Molecular determinants in the light chain define the broad-spectrum cross-reactivity of the IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14 public clonotype

To correlate the molecular differences in the light chain that led to the heterogeneous Omicron response by this public clonotype, we swapped the light chain of 002-S21B10 with the light chain of LY-CoV1404 and vice versa and tested the binding of these chimeric mAbs with the spike BA.1 variant. As expected, the chimeric mAb with the 002-S21B10 heavy chain and LY-CoV1404 light chain showed restored binding toward spike BA.1 (Figure 3B). Reciprocally, the chimeric mAb with the LY-CoV1404 heavy chain and 002-S21B10 light chain showed reduced spike BA.1 binding to 002-S21B10 affinity levels, affirming the role of light-chain paratope residues in mAbs’ broad-spectrum cross-reactivity toward variants.

Sequence alignment of all IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14-encoding antibodies (Figure S2) identified a key residue difference at position 31 in the light-chain CDR1, which is N/D in all Omicron-neutralizing antibodies (LY-CoV1404, XGv264, and S2X324), but differs in non-Omicron neutralizers, when tested in a live virus neutralization assay (Figure 3C, highlighted in yellow). Thus, the CDR1 residue 31 interaction alters the trajectory of the light chain, especially in CDR1 and CDR3 loops, preventing accommodation of Omicron variant mutations. To validate this hypothesis, we introduced a mutation, changing residue 31 to aspartic acid, in addition to replacing the CDR3 loop with a mimic of LY-CoV1404’s light-chain CDR3. This engineered light chain was incorporated into 002-S21B10, resulting in what we name 002-S21B10mLC. Subsequently, we assessed the binding of this modified antibody to the spike BA.1 variant. As expected, 002-S21B10mLC, carrying the altered light chain, exhibited the restoration of most spike BA.1 binding ability, whereas the WT 002-S21B10 mAb displayed no binding to spike BA.1 (Figures 3B and S3). These results further confirm our hypothesis that the light-chain CDR1 and CDR3 play a pivotal role in the binding of spike BA.1.

DISCUSSION

Next-generation universal COVID-19 vaccine design will strive for a robust immune response against conserved antigenic sites.15,22–25 To date, several bnAbs have been reported to neutralize highly evasive Omicron variants, and the majority of these bnAbs recognize the less frequently mutated class-3/RBD-5 RBD epitope.14,19 While a majority of the bnAbs bind class-3 epitopes, not all class-3 antibodies show broad neutralization, suggesting heterogeneity in their genetic assembly or mode of antigen recognition,3 although some class-3 antibodies encoded by the same clonotype differentially neutralize SARS-CoV-2 Omicron variants. To define the molecular features responsible for such a heterogenic response, we determined the structure of a non-Omicron-neutralizing class-3 public antibody and compared it with closely related Omicron bnAb LY-CoV1404 (Figure 2). Although both 002-S21B10 and LY-CoV1404 show identical modes of epitope recognition, we report key differences in the orientations of light-chain CDR loops that induce alterations in molecular interactions within the epitope-paratope sites, resulting in Omicron’s escape (Figures 2 and 3B). Point mutation analysis further defines G446S and N440K as key RBD epitope sites responsible for 002-S21B10’s Omicron escape (Figure 3A).

Based on a recent report, 002-S21B10 belongs to the IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14 public clonotype, which shows population-level heterogeneity due to strong allelic preference for a conserved D56 residue in heavy-chain CDR2.3,7 Some of these clonotypes show further heterogeneous response in broadly neutralizing Omicron variants. For example, LY-CoV1404, XGv264, and S2X324 can potently neutralize Omicron sub-variants up to BA.5, while others do not (such as XGv266 and XGv265), despite a strong sequence similarity and a conserved HxI/VxxI/L motif in their 11-amino-acid-long heavy-chain CDR3.3,7 This suggests that other factors contribute toward their broad-spectrum neutralizing properties. Here, we showed that the molecular differences within the light chain dictate the broad neutralizing properties of this public clonotype (Figure 3B). Sequence alignment and structural comparisons support the speculation that a residue swap at position 31 in light-chain CDR1 and an altered trajectory of CDR3 might collectively result in altered interactions at the epitope-paratope surface and could be a key molecular factor in defining their broad-spectrum neutralizing properties. So far, all IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14-encoding class-3 Omicron-neutralizing antibodies reported to the date3,7 contain either D or N at position 31 in light-chain CDR1, further affirming its role in reshaping the other light-chain-mediated molecular interactions to tolerate Omicron substitutions. Further confirming the role of specific light-chain residues that may contribute to Omicron binding, we demonstrated that the binding of 002-S21B10 to the Omicron spike is largely reinstated by specific residue mutations in the light-chain CDR1 and CDR3 of 002-S21B10. However, there is still an ~5-fold difference in affinity between the chimeric and the mutated 002-S21B10. This discrepancy suggests that additional sequence variations in the framework region could impact the overall stability and pairing of the mAb, consequently influencing its affinity for binding to spike BA.1.

Despite this fact that some of the RBD class-3 bnAbs with IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14 public clonotype neutralized most Omicron sub-variants up to BA.5, they all did not neutralize newly emerged sub-variants such as BQ1.1 and XBB1.5, due to additional mutations (R346T, K444T, and V445P) acquired within their epitope, suggesting that a dominant immune selection pressure at these sites led to escape for the newer SARS-CoV-2 variants.10,14,26,27 The overall findings of this study center on the molecular differences of 002-S21B10 for its lacking neutralizing potential against Omicron despite high sequence similarity to LY-CoV1404. Structural and functional analysis of 002-S21B10 in comparison with other published antibodies encoded by the IGHV2–5/IGLV2–14 public clonotype revealed significant molecular variations in the light-chain orientation and interactions between the epitope-paratope interface that together demonstrate how some of these public antibodies cannot overcome the mutational landscape of Omicron. The molecular understanding of such structure-function relationships has implications for understanding the mechanisms of virus neutralization and escape that are highly valuable for designing and developing novel vaccines and antivirals that continue to be effective against newly emerging variants.

Limitations of the study

Our work focuses on the binding, neutralization, and structural characterization of a shared SARS-CoV-2 antibody isolated from a COVID-19-recovered individual in India. Although this antibody recognizes a conserved class-3 spike RBD epitope that is also targeted by several other bnAbs, it does not confer the same broad neutralizing properties. All conclusions are based on structure-based predictions and biochemical binding experiments. Live virus neutralization and in vivo protection experiments may show different efficacy in the unlikely event that binding of mAb 002-S21B10 to the BA.1 variant is not strictly related to neutralization.

EXPERIMENTAL MODEL AND STUDY PARTICIPANT DETAILS

Cell lines

VeroE6-TMPRSS2 cells were generated and cultured as previously described.19,43 VeroE6-TMPRSS2 cells were cultured in complete DMEM in the presence of Gibco Puromycin 10 mg/mL (# A11138–03). VeroE6-TMPRSS2 cells were used to propagate all virus stocks. Cells were incubated at 37°C in the presence of 5% CO2. The expi293F cells were maintained in Expi293™ Expression Medium for SARS-CoV-2 Spike, RBD and mAb protein expression. The expi293F cells were incubated at 37°C in the presence of 8% CO2 with shaking at 125 RPM.

Viruses

All the SARS-CoV-2 viruses were expanded in a confluent VeroE6-TMPRSS2 cells T175 flask to generate a working stock. All viruses used in this study were deep sequenced and confirmed as previously described.19,43

METHOD DETAILS

Expression of human monoclonal antibodies

All transfections were done as described previously.19 Briefly, expi293F cells were transfected with antibody expression plasmids at a density of 2.5 million cells per/ml using 1 mg/ml PEI-Max transfection reagent 40,000 MW (Polysciences). Supernatants were harvested 4–5 days post-transfection and tested for their SARS-CoV-2 RBD binding potential by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Supernatant with positive RBD binding signal was next purified using Protein A/G beads (Thermo Scientific), concentrated using a 30 kDa or 100 kDa cut-off concentrator (Vivaspin, Sartorius) and stored at 4°C for further use.

Electrochemiluminescence antibody binding assay

Binding analysis of SARS-CoV-2 mAb to spike protein was performed using an electrochemiluminescence assay as described earlier.19 Briefly, a V-PLEX COVID-19 Panel 24 (Meso Scale Discovery) was used to measure the IgG1 mAb binding to SARS-CoV-2 spike antigens following the manufacturer’s recommendations. Antigen-coated plates were blocked with 150 µl/well of 5% BSA in PBS for 30 minutes. Plates were washed 3x with 150 µl/well of PBS with 0.05% Tween between each incubation step. mAbs were serially diluted to concentrations ranging from 10 µg/ml to 0.1 pg/ml and 50 µl/well were added to the plate and incubated for two hours at room temperature with shaking at 700 rpm. mAb antibody binding was then detected with 50 µl/well of MSD SULFO-TAG anti-human IgG antibody (diluted 1:200) incubated for one hour at room temperature with shaking at 700 rpm. 150 µl/well of MSD Gold Read Buffer B was then added to each plate immediately before reading on an MSD QuickPlex plate reader.

SARS-CoV-2 spike ELISA binding assay

The ELISA binding assay was performed as per following protocol. The purified Omicron Spike-hexparo (Spike-6p) protein was captured on Ni-NTA HisSorb ELISA plates (Qiagen) at a concentration of 1 µg/mL in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at room temperature for 2 hr. The plates were washed with PBS containing 0.05% Tween-20. Three-fold serially diluted purified wild-type or chimeric mAbs were added and incubated at room temperature for 1.5 hr. Plates were washed and the SARS-CoV-2 Omicron Spike specific IgG signal was detected by incubating with horseradish peroxidase (HRP) conjugated - anti-human IgG (Jackson ImmunoResearch Labs, #109–036-098). Plates were then washed thoroughly and developed with o-phenylenediamine (OPD) substrate (Sigma, #P8787) in 0.05M phosphate-citrate buffer (Sigma, #P4809) pH 5.0, containing 0.012% hydrogen peroxide (Fisher Scientific, #18755). Absorbance was measured at 490 nm.

Live SARS-CoV-2 neutralization assay

Neutralization titers to SARS-CoV-2 were determined based on Vero TMPRSS2 cells as previously described.19,20 Briefly, 100 pfu of SARS-CoV-2 (2019-nCoV/USA_WA1/2020), Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Delta, and Omicron variants were used on Vero TMPRSS2 cells. Purified monoclonal was serially diluted three-fold in duplicate starting at 10 µg/ml in a 96-well round-bottom plate and incubated for 1 h at 37°C. This antibody-virus mixture was transferred into the wells seeded with Vero-TMPRSS2 cells the previous day at a concentration of 2.5 × 104 cells/well. After 1 hour, the antibody-virus inoculum was removed and 0.85% methylcellulose in 2% FBS containing DMEM was overlaid onto the cell monolayer. Cells were incubated at 37°C for 16–40 hours. Cells were washed three times with 1X PBS (Corning Cellgro) and fixed with 125 µl of 2% paraformaldehyde in PBS (Electron Microscopy Sciences) for 30 minutes. Following fixation, plates were washed twice with PBS and 100 µl of permeabilization buffer, was added to the fixed cells for 20 minutes. Cells were incubated with an anti-SARS-CoV spike primary antibody directly conjugated with Alexa fluor-647 (CR3022-AF647) for up to 4 hours at room temperature. Plates were then washed twice with 1x PBS and imaged on an ELISPOT reader (CTL Analyzer). Foci were counted using Viridot (counted first under the ‘‘green light’’ set followed by background subtraction under the ‘‘red light’’ setting). IC50 titers were calculated by non-linear regression analysis using the 4PL sigmoidal dose curve equation on Prism 9 (Graphpad Software). Neutralization titers were calculated as 100% x [1- (average foci in duplicate wells incubated with the specimen) O (average number of foci in the duplicate wells incubated at the highest dilution of the respective specimen).

Spike protein expression and purification

SARS-CoV-2 spike-6P trimer protein carrying WA.1 was expressed and purified by transfecting expi293F cells using WA.1-spike-6P plasmids as described previously.19 Transfections were performed as per the manufacturer’s protocol (Thermo Fisher). Briefly, expi293F cells (2.5×106 cells/ml) were transfected using ExpiFectamine™ 293 transfection reagent (ThermoFisher, cat. no. A14524). The cells were harvested 4–5 days post-transfection. The spike protein was purified using the His-Pur Ni-NTA affinity purification method. The column was washed with Buffer containing 25 mM Imidazole, 6.7 mM NaH2PO4.H2O and 300 mM NaCl in PBS followed by spike protein elution in elution buffer containing 235 mM Imidazole, 6.7 mM NaH2PO4.H2O and 300 mM NaCl in PBS. Eluted protein was dialyzed against PBS and concentrated. The concentrated protein was loaded onto a Superose-6 Increase 10/300 column and protein was eluted as trimeric spike collected. Protein quality was evaluated by SDS-PAGE and by Negative Stain-EM.

Sample preparation for cryo-EM

SARS-CoV-2 spike-6P trimer was incubated with the mAb (full-length IgG) at 0.7 mg/mL concentration. The complex was prepared at a 0.4 sub-molar ratio of mAb to prevent inter-spike crosslinking, mediated by bi-valent binding of the intact antibody. The complex was incubated at room temperature for ~5 min before vitrification. Three µL of the complex was applied onto a freshly glow-discharged (PLECO easiGLOW) 400 mesh, 1.2/1.3 C-Flat grid (Electron Microscopy Sciences). After 20 s of incubation, grids were blotted for 3 s at 0 blot force and vitrified using a Vitrobot IV (Thermo Fisher Scientific) under 22°C with 100% humidity.

Cryo-EM data acquisition

Single-particle Cryo-EM data for WA.1 spike-IgG complexes of mAb 002—S21B10 was collected on a 300 kV Titan Krios transmission electron microscope (ThermoFisher Scientific) equipped with Gatan K3 direct electron detector behind a 30 eV slit width energy filter. Multi-frame movies were collected at a pixel size of 1.0691 Å per pixel with a total dose of 63 e/Å2 at defocus range of −0.7 to −2.7 µm.

Cryo-EM data analysis and model building

Cryo-EM movies were motion-corrected by Patch motion correction implemented in Cryosparc v3.3.2.39 Motion-corrected micrographs were corrected for contrast transfer function using Cryosparc’s implementation of Patch CTF estimation. Micrographs with poor CTF fits were discarded using CTF fit resolution cutoff of ~6.0 Å. Particles were picked using a Blob picker, extracted, and subjected to an iterative round of 2D classification. Particles belonging to the best 2D classes with secondary structure features were selected for heterogeneous 3D refinement to separate IgG-bound spike particles from non-IgG-bound spike particles. Particles belonging to the best IgG-bound 3D class were refined in non-uniform 3D refinement with per particle CTF and higher-order aberration correction turned on. To further improve the resolution of the RBD-IgG binding interface a soft mask was created covering one RBD and interacting Fab region of IgG and refined locally in Cryosparc using Local Refinement on signal subtracted particles. All maps were density modified in Phenix32 using Resolve Cryo-EM. The combined Focused Map tool in Phenix was used to integrate high-resolution locally refined maps into an overall map. Additional data processing details are summarized in Figure S2 and Table S1.

The initial spike models for WA.1 (PDB:7lrt) as well as individual heavy and light chains of the Fab region of an IgG (generated with Alphafold)42 were docked into combined focused Cryo-EM density maps using UCSF ChimeraX.33 The full spike-mAb model was refined using rigid body refinement in Phenix, followed by refinement in Isolde.40 The final model was refined further in Phenix using real-space refinement. Glycans with visible density were modelled in Coot and validated by Privateer.31,41 Model validation was performed using Molprobity.35 PDBePISA34 was used to identify mAb-RBD interface residues, calculate buried surface area, and identify polar interactions. Figures were prepared in ChimeraX33 and PyMOL.37

ACE2 direct competition assay

Competition was measured using a cell line stably expressing Wuhan spike (With an N-terminal Myc-tag) on the surface of HEK293F cells.44 Approximately 3.2 × 106 cells were washed twice with PBS with 2% FBS, stained with PE-conjugated rabbit α-myc antibody (Cell Signaling, 1/200 dilution in PBS with 2% FBS), and incubated on ice and in the dark for 30 minutes. Antibody titrations were prepared in a 96-well format with a starting concentration of 3.3 × 10−6 M and titrated over 10 additional wells with five-fold dilutions. The 12th well was left absent of antibody to serve as an additional control. In addition, 50 nM human ACE2 (Sino Biological, His Tag and Biotinylated), and approximately 6.0 × 104 cells were added to each well. The cells were incubated on ice and in the dark for 30 minutes before washing four times with PBS with 2% FBS. Cells were then stained with streptavidin PerCP-eFluor710 (Invitrogen eBiosciences, 1/200 dilution in PBS with 2% FBS) which was incubated on ice and in the dark for 30 minutes. After incubation, cells were washed twice, resuspended in PBS with 2% FBS, and data were collected on the Cytek Northern Lights Flow Cytometer. The median PerCP-eFluor710 signal was measured from a population of cells that had been gated for GFP and Myc (PE) positivity.

Spike mutants antibody binding assay

Cell lines expressing spike protein containing a single amino acid mutation (N440K, G446S, Q498R, and Wuhan spike) were generated with the Gibco LV-Max Transfection Kit, HEK 293F suspension cells, and 3 mg of plasmid for a 6-well plate. The transfection mixture was incubated for 10 minutes at room temperature before being added to the cells and the LV-Max Transfection Enhancer was added five hours later. The cells were incubated overnight at 5% CO2, 37°C, and shaken at 120 rpm. After 24 hours, approximately 2 × 106 cells were taken from each well. Cells were then washed twice with PBS with 2% FBS and incubated on ice in the dark with 1/200 biotinylated rabbit myc (Cell Signaling) for 30 minutes. Titrations of antibody 002.S21-B10 were prepared in a 96-well plate at a starting concentration of 3.3 × 10−6 M in 20 mL and titrated over 10 additional wells with five-fold dilutions. The 12th well was left absent of antibody to serve as an additional control. After the incubation, the cells were pelleted and resuspended in PBS with 2% FBS and 20 mL of cells were transferred to each well at approximately 6.5 × 104 cells per well and incubated with antibody for 30 minutes on ice, and in the dark. Afterwards, cells were washed three times and resuspended in a secondary antibody mixture. The mixture was composed of 1/250 Goat α-human PE IgG Fc (ThermoFisher Scientific) and 1.25/250 Streptavidin PerCPe-Fluor710 (Invitrogen eBiosciences). The cells were incubated on ice, and in the dark for 30 minutes before being washed twice with PBS with 2% FBS and data were collected on the Cytek Northern Lights. The median PE signal was measured from a population of cells that had been gated for GFP and Myc (PerCPe-Fluor710) positivity.

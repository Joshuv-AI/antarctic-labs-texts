Beamion BCGC-1: phase Ib/II trial of zongertinib for advanced HER2-positive breast or gastroesophageal cancers
Authors: Sara Hurvitz, Matteo Simonelli, Ramón Yarza, David Berz, Shigehisa Kitano, Gianluca Del Conte, Daniel Acosta Eyzaguirre, Bernard Gaston Doger de Speville Uribe, Daniela Maier, Damijan Erzen, Sila Aykut Yazgili, Giuseppe Curigliano, Ting Deng, Min Yan, Qingyuan Zhang, Xian Wang, Izuma Nakayama, Kohei Shitara
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundOverexpression and/or amplification of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) is a recognized oncogenic driver in metastatic breast cancer (mBC) and metastatic gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinomas (mGEAC). Although HER2-directed monoclonal antibodies, antibody – drug conjugates (ADCs), and tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have revolutionized the treatment of HER2-positive tumors, additional combination regimens and/or novel agents are needed to expand sequential therapy options and improve patient outcomes – particularly following treatment with trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd). Zongertinib is a novel, irreversible, HER2-selective TKI that spares EGFR.Study designHere, we detail the rationale and design of Beamion BCGC-1 (NCT06324357), an international Phase Ib/II open-label trial in which patients with previously treated HER2-positive mBC or mGEAC will receive zongertinib alone or in combination. Phase Ib (dose escalation) will determine the maximum tolerated dose of zongertinib plus trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), T-DXd, or trastuzumab ± capecitabine, in patients with HER2-positive mBC, and plus T-DXd in patients with HER2-positive mGEAC. Phase II (dose optimization) will assess two doses of zongertinib in combination regimens, or as monotherapy, in patients with mBC or mGEAC. The trial is actively recruiting in six countries globally.Clinical trial registrationwww.clinicaltrials.gov identifier is NCT06324357.KEYWORDS: Zongertinib; HER2; metastatic breast cancer; metastatic gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma; trastuzumab; trastuzumab deruxtecan; trastuzumab emtansine; phase Ib/II clinical trial

Introduction

Approximately 20% of metastatic breast cancer (mBC) and 10–30% of metastatic gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma (mGEAC) tumors are human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive and driven by HER2 overexpression and/or amplification [1–6]. HER2-positive tumors have historically been characterized as more aggressive than HER2-negative cancers and are associated with worse outcomes [1,2,7].

At present, the recommended first-line treatment for HER2-positive mBC comprises the HER2-directed monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) trastuzumab plus pertuzumab combined with single-agent chemotherapy (usually a taxane). In China, patients may receive first-line trastuzumab plus docetaxel and pyrotinib [8]. Most contemporary treatment guidelines recommend the HER2-directed antibody – drug conjugate (ADC), trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd), in the second line. However, it is possible that T-DXd, in combination with pertuzumab, will soon transition to the first-line setting, based on recent interim analysis of the Phase III DESTINY-Breast09 trial [9]. Current and emerging later-line options include the ADC, trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1), the mAb, margetuximab, and HER2 tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as tucatinib, neratinib, lapatinib or pyrotinib combined with capecitabine and/or trastuzumab, or other chemotherapies with or without trastuzumab [8,10–14]. Despite the availability of such treatments for HER2-positive mBC, the optimal sequence of therapy for individual patients after first- and second-line treatment remains unclear [15]. This is especially true post T-DXd treatment, as there are currently no clinical trial data to guide the choice of subsequent therapy, and available real-world data indicate an unmet need in this patient population [16,17].

For HER2-positive mGEAC, the recommended first-line therapy is trastuzumab with fluoropyrimidine- and platinum-based chemotherapy, with or without pembrolizumab. The only HER2-directed treatment option in the second-line setting is T-DXd, which recently demonstrated a significant overall survival benefit versus ramucirumab plus paclitaxel (the current non-targeted standard second-line treatment) [18]. There remains an unmet need for further treatment options to improve outcomes in HER2-positive mGEAC [19–21], especially for patients with esophageal adenocarcinoma who are frequently excluded from clinical trials [18,22].

For HER2-positive cancers, including mBC and mGEAC, the combination of HER2-directed treatment modalities has been assessed as a potential way of overcoming treatment resistance and improving outcomes. For example, several clinical studies have assessed combinations of TKIs, such as lapatinib, tucatinib, neratinib, and pyrotinib, with HER2-directed mAbs in patients with HER2-positive breast cancer [8,12,23–29]. However, in these studies patients did not always receive contemporary first-line standard of care and/or demonstrated limited clinical efficacy. Moreover, some combination regimens, especially those with nonselective HER2 TKIs such as lapatinib, neratinib and pyrotinib, were associated with increased rates of off-target epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-mediated adverse events (AEs) such as diarrhea and rash [8,23,27,28].

While fewer data are currently available regarding the potential of combining TKIs with HER2-directed ADCs, preclinical evidence suggests that TKIs might enhance HER2 membrane recycling and localization, thereby promoting the internalization of ADCs, resulting in enhanced delivery and release of their cytotoxic payload inside cancer cells [30–32]. Based on promising preclinical [31] and early clinical data [33], the combination of the HER2-selective, reversible TKI, tucatinib, with T-DM1 or T-DXd is currently being assessed in patients with previously treated HER2-driven breast cancer in the Phase III HER2CLIMB-02 and the Phase II HER2CLIMB-04 trials, respectively [34,35]. Interim data from HER2CLIMB-02 demonstrated a significant improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) with the combination versus T-DM1 alone, though no overall survival benefit has yet been reported [34]. These findings provide proof of concept of combining ADCs and reversible TKIs in the clinic; however, it is not clear whether an irreversible, covalent HER2-selective compound would serve as a good combination partner for HER2-directed ADCs.

Here, we describe the Beamion BCGC-1 trial, which will assess the efficacy and safety of zongertinib, an orally administered, irreversible TKI that selectively inhibits HER2 [32], either in combination with other agents, or as monotherapy, in patients with advanced/metastatic HER2-driven mBC or mGEAC.

Background & rationale

Zongertinib is an irreversible TKI that selectively inhibits HER2 while sparing wild-type EGFR, thereby limiting associated toxicities. The high specificity of zongertinib for HER2 versus EGFR is due to the mechanism by which zongertinib binds to HER2 – it forms a covalent bond with cysteine 805 within the ATP binding site of the HER2 tyrosine kinase domain, and a hydrogen bond with serine 783 [36], the only amino acid in the binding pocket to differ between HER2 and EGFR. In line with this, preclinical data have demonstrated that zongertinib has antiproliferative activity against HER2-overexpressing and HER2-mutant cell lines without inhibiting cells dependent on EGFR activity, and was associated with higher potency than tucatinib in HER2-amplified breast cancer cell lines [32,37].

In an ongoing Phase Ia/Ib trial (NCT04886804), zongertinib demonstrated potent activity and a manageable safety profile in patients with previously treated, HER2-altered, advanced solid tumors (Phase Ia) [37–39] and HER2-mutant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC; Phase Ib) [40]. In patients with HER2-mutant NSCLC (Phase Ib), at the recommended dose for expansion of 120 mg/day (n = 75), the centrally confirmed objective response rate (ORR) was 71%, median duration of response was 14.1 months, and median PFS was 12.4 months [40]. There was a notably low level of grade ≥3 drug-related AEs, occurring in 17% of patients [40]. Zongertinib has received accelerated approval in the United States, China, and Japan, for patients with unresectable or metastatic non-squamous HER2-mutant NSCLC who have received prior systemic therapy [41].

The clinical development program for zongertinib is not restricted to patients with HER2-mutant tumors. There is mechanistic, preclinical, and preliminary clinical evidence to suggest that zongertinib may also be an effective treatment option against tumors with overexpressed and/or amplified HER2 [32,38]. Preclinical data indicate that zongertinib monotherapy is effective against HER2-overexpressing cell lines and mouse xenograft models of human HER2-positive gastric/esophageal tumors [32]. Moreover, zongertinib has demonstrated synergistic activity in NCI- N87 xenografts (HER2-positive gastric cancer model) when combined with ADCs [32]. In the Phase Ia part of NCT04886804, 16 patients with HER2-altered mBC and 33 patients with HER2-altered advanced/metastatic gastrointestinal (GI) cancers received zongertinib, including six patients with esophageal cancer. Of these BC and GI cancer patients, 11 and 16 had received prior HER2-directed therapy. Overall, the centrally confirmed ORR was 44% and 21% of patients with BC and GC, respectively, with disease control rates of 81% and 82% [37–39]. Thus, there is strong preclinical and clinical evidence supporting the development of zongertinib in patients with HER2-altered mBC or mGEAC.

Objectives

Beamion BCGC-1 (NCT06324357) is assessing the safety, tolerability, and antitumor activity of zongertinib alone or in combination with either T-DM1, T-DXd, trastuzumab, or trastuzumab plus capecitabine, in patients with HER2-positive mBC and in combination with T-DXd in patients with HER2-positive mGEAC.

Trial design

Beamion BCGC-1 is a multicenter, multicohort open-label trial consisting of two parts (Figure 1). In Phase Ib, eligible patients will be assigned to one of five escalating dose cohorts of zongertinib; 60 mg/day to a maximum of 360 mg/day, oral), plus fixed approved doses of T-DM1 (3.6 mg/kg intravenously [IV] every 3 weeks [Q3W]), T-DXd (5.4 mg/kg IV Q3W), trastuzumab (8 mg/kg loading dose followed by 6 mg/kg IV Q3W), or trastuzumab plus capecitabine (1000 mg/m2 twice daily on days 1–14 of each treatment cycle, oral) in patients with HER2-positive mBC, or with T-DXd (6.4 mg/kg IV Q3W) in patients with HER2-positive mGEAC (Figure 1). The number of zongertinib dose levels in Phase Ib will be decided by the Dose Escalation Committee (DEC; composed of participating investigators and members of the Sponsor trial team); ≥3 patients will be enrolled per dose level. During the second part of the trial (Phase II; dose optimization), patients will be randomized (1:1 via a validated pseudo-random number generator organized by the Sponsor) to receive one of two dose regimens across six cohorts: zongertinib as monotherapy, or in combination with fixed, approved doses of T-DM1, T-DXd, trastuzumab, or trastuzumab plus capecitabine in patients with HER2-positive mBC, and in combination with T-DXd in patients with HER2-positive mGEAC (Figure 1).

Abbreviations: BID, twice daily; DEC, dose escalation Committee; IV, intravenous; mBC, metastatic breast cancer; mGEAC, metastatic gastric, gastroesophageal junction, or esophageal adenocarcinoma; PO, oral; QD, once daily; Q3W, every 3 weeks; T-DM1, trastuzumab emtansine; T-DXd, trastuzumab deruxtecan.

The selected doses for the Phase II dose-optimization part will be identified during Phase Ib, with the exception of the mBC cohort receiving zongertinib monotherapy (cohort I), who will receive preselected doses of 180 mg/day and 360 mg/day (according to previous preclinical and clinical data). All treatments will be administered in 21-day cycles until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, death, withdrawal of consent, or discontinuation for any other reason. During Phase II, an interim analysis will be performed when the first 15 patients in each of the two zongertinib dose groups have been followed for ≥6 weeks (≥1 post-baseline tumor assessment) or have stopped treatment. If one of the doses per cohort is found to have a less favorable benefit – risk profile it will be discontinued at this point.

Methods

Methods: participants, interventions, and outcomes

Key inclusion and exclusion criteria are shown in Table 1. The trial will include adult patients with histologically/cytologically confirmed, unresectable HER2-positive mBC or mGEAC according to the American Society of Clinical Oncology – College of American Pathologists (ASCO – CAP guidelines) [42]. Patients must have experienced disease progression following prior HER2-directed treatment: cohorts A, B, C, G, and K, ≥1 prior lines of therapy are permitted; cohorts D and H, 1–2 prior lines of therapy are allowed, one of which must be T-DXd; for cohort E, 1 prior line of therapy is allowed, which can be trastuzumab with or without pertuzumab and a taxane; cohort F, 1 prior line of therapy is required which can be trastuzumab plus chemotherapy with or without a checkpoint inhibitor; cohorts I and J, ≤5 prior lines of therapy are allowed, one of which must be T-DXd. Patients with controlled or asymptomatic brain metastases will be permitted; for cohorts D, E, H, I, and J, at least 10 patients per dose level should have brain metastases at baseline based on investigator assessment according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 and/or Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology for Brain Metastases (RANO-BM).

Planned enrollment for Phase Ib dose escalation is approximately 20 patients for cohorts A, B, C and G, plus around 12 patients for cohort K, to give a total of 92 (≥3 per dose group). Dose escalation will be guided by a Bayesian logistic regression model (BLRM) with overdose control (EWOC) for each cohort individually. Dose escalation will be restricted if patients experience a DLT during the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) evaluation period (the first treatment cycle). For each of the five dose-escalation cohorts, the Sponsor may decide to backfill up to three dose levels with up to seven additional patients (to give a maximum of 10 per dose level). This will potentially lead to the recruitment of up to 72 additional patients in the Phase Ib part of the trial. In Phase II, for each of the six cohorts, it is planned that 60 patients will be randomized 1:1 into one of two dose groups. For cohorts I and J, after a minimum of six patients have been randomized to each of the two dose levels per cohort, further enrollment will be paused until these patients have been followed for ≥21 days. At this point, the DEC will review the totality of data and decide whether to continue with the same zongertinib doses, or to reduce them in one or more cohorts. If reduction to a lower dose is required, enrollment will continue until at least 30 patients are randomized to each of the final dose levels. In all mBC cohorts, randomization will be stratified by the presence of brain metastases at baseline; cohorts D and H will also be stratified according to previous treatment (tucatinib or pyrotinib in cohort D; T-DM1 in cohort H). Analysis of Phase II will be conducted once 30 patients have been randomized to each of the two dose levels per cohort and followed for ≥12 weeks for cohorts E, F, I, and J, or for ≥24 weeks for cohorts D and H, or have stopped treatment.

The trial has been designed to include patient populations that reflect real-world clinical practice. For example, patients with controlled or asymptomatic brain metastases are eligible and at least 10 patients per dose level will have baseline brain metastases in cohorts D, E, H, I, and J. Heavily pretreated patients are eligible, including patients pre-treated with T-DXd (in cohorts not combining with T-DXd), with no limit on the maximum number of prior therapies for enrollment to the dose-escalation cohorts. Also, patients with esophageal adenocarcinoma, who are often excluded from clinical trials [18,22], are eligible for the mGEAC cohorts.

The primary endpoint for Phase II is objective response as assessed by investigator review. This is defined as the proportion of patients who achieved a confirmed best objective response of complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to RECIST version 1.1 from the date of treatment start until the earliest date of disease progression, death, or last evaluable tumor assessment before start of subsequent anticancer therapy, or treatment discontinuation in the intent-to-treat population. Secondary endpoints for Phase II are: PFS, defined as the time from treatment start until the earliest date of tumor progression according to RECIST version 1.1 based on investigator review, or death from any cause, whichever occurs first; disease control, defined as best objective response of CR, PR or stable disease according to RECIST version 1.1 as assessed by investigator review; occurrence of treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) leading to zongertinib dose reduction during the on-treatment period; PK parameters of zongertinib when given as monotherapy or in combination (including Cmax,[ss] and AUC0-tz,ss); and patient-reported outcomes (PROs) from first administration until end of treatment, including the Patient-Reported Outcomes version of the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (PRO-CTCAE®; mouth/throat sores, taste changes, decreased appetite, nausea, vomiting, constipation, diarrhea, shortness of breath, cough, rash, skin dryness, hair loss, itching, numbness and tingling, fatigue), the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer – Item List 46 (EORTC IL46; 1 item), the EORTC IL19 (5 items), and the physical functioning scale of the EORTC Quality of Life Questionnaire-C30 (QLQ-C30).

Further exploratory endpoints include assessment of target modulation based on longitudinal biomarker assessment; further PK parameters of zongertinib and T-DXd/DXd or T-DM1/DM1 when given in combination; and intracranial objective response and disease control in patients with CNS lesions at baseline according to RANO-BM as assessed by the investigator.

Methods: data collection, management, and analysis

Safety will be assessed by physical examination, measurement of vital signs, laboratory parameters (hematology, coagulation, and biochemistry parameters), electrocardiograms, measurement of left ventricular ejection function, and the monitoring of AEs, serious AEs, and AEs of special interest (including hepatic injury, hematologic AEs, GI AEs, and fatigue). Physical examinations and electrocardiograms will take place on days 1 and 15 of cycles 1 and 2, then day 1 of subsequent cycles; vital signs will be assessed on days 1, 8, and 15 of cycle 1, days 1, 2, 8, and 15 of cycle 2, then day 1 of subsequent cycles; safety laboratory tests will be performed on days 1, 8 and 15 of cycles 1 and 2, and days 1 and 8 of subsequent cycles; and left ventricular ejection fraction will be assessed at day 1 of cycle 1, then every fourth cycle. The severity of AEs will be categorized according to Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0. The causal relationship of AEs to the trial treatments will be evaluated according to medical judgment. The time period for reporting of adverse events is from the time the patient signs the informed consent form until 40 days after the last administration of study medication. Safety will be monitored regularly by the DEC, who will review individual and aggregated safety data at regular intervals to determine the safety profile and risk/benefit ratio, and make decisions on the next dose level, dose escalation, dose de-escalation, dose modification, and next cohort size.

Tumor response will be evaluated according to RECIST version 1.1. Imaging of all known or suspected sites of disease will be conducted at baseline, cycle 1 day 1, and every 6 weeks thereafter. Tumor assessments at screening will be performed with computed tomography (CT) or position emission tomography (PET)/CT scans of the chest, abdomen, pelvis, and a brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scan. Subsequent brain MRI scans will only be undertaken if brain metastases were documented at baseline or are suspected. In patients with brain metastases, lesions will be evaluated according to RANO-BM and assessed by the investigator.

Pharmacokinetics (PK) will be determined on days 1, 8, and 15 of cycles 1–2 (plus on day 2 of cycle 2), and on day 1 of cycles 3–4. For cohorts D, E, and F, additional samples will be taken on day 1 of every fourth cycle after cycle 4 and at the end of treatment. For Phase II, archival tissue from the tumor sample used for enrollment is required for biomarker analysis. If insufficient material is available, a fresh biopsy will be taken.

No formal statistical testing will be performed. Descriptive analyses will be performed separately for each cohort. For each cohort in the Phase Ib part, dose escalation is guided by a 5-parameter Bayesian logistic regression model (BLRM) with overdose control that will be fitted to binary toxicity outcomes. The estimates of parameters will be updated using the BLRM as data are accumulated. At the end of dose escalation, the toxicity probability at each dose level will be calculated to determine an estimate of the MTD for each cohort. For determination of the MTD, only MTD-evaluable patients will be considered. For analysis of the secondary efficacy and safety endpoints, all patients treated with ≥1 dose of trial medication will be included; PK endpoints will be evaluated in those patients who provide ≥1 observation for ≥1 PK endpoint without relevant protocol deviations.

Conclusion

The ongoing Beamion BCGC-1 trial has been designed to evaluate the preliminary safety, tolerability, and antitumor activity of zongertinib combination regimens, or monotherapy, in patients with HER2-positive mBC or mGEAC who have experienced disease progression with available treatment options. HER2-positive mBC and mGEAC are associated with aggressive disease and poor prognosis. There remains a major unmet need for additional targeted treatments in these settings, especially after the failure of T-DXd.

The treatment strategy of combining TKIs with mAbs or ADCs is currently under clinical investigation in patients with HER2-positive tumors and may provide higher antitumor efficacy compared to mAb/ADC treatment alone. However, a survival benefit with an ADC and TKI combination has not yet been reported [8,23–25,27,28].

Zongertinib is an irreversible TKI that selectively inhibits HER2, while sparing EGFR. Preclinical and clinical data have shown that zongertinib can inhibit HER2 amplified or overexpressing tumors, including breast [37] and GI cancers [39]. Moreover, the combination of zongertinib and T-DM1 or T-DXd overcame resistance to ADCs in mouse models [32]. Together, these observations provide strong scientific rationale for the Beamion BCGC-1 trial, which is actively recruiting and currently open in six countries globally.

The results of Beamion BCGC-1 will provide valuable insights into the potential role of zongertinib-based combinations, or monotherapy, for later-line treatment of patients with HER2-positive mBC or mGEAC.

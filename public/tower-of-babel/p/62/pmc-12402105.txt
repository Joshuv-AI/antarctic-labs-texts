Robust fault diagnosis of fractional order Takagi–Sugeno systems with uncertainties in premise variables
Authors: Abdelghani Djeddi, Ahmad Taher Azar, Abdelhamid Djari, Djalel Dib, Samah Alshathri, Walid El-Shafai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper introduces two novel fault detection techniques employing Fractional Order Proportional Integral Fuzzy Observer (FO-PIFO) designs to diagnose nonlinear systems modeled by Fractional Order Takagi–Sugeno (FO-TS) frameworks. The proposed approaches address both measurable premise variables (MPV) and unmeasurable premise variables (UPV), facilitating the development of observer banks for effective fault detection. By extending prior research, largely limited to integer-order Takagi–Sugeno models, into the domain of fractional-order systems, this study fills a critical gap in the literature. Two strategies are proposed to ensure compatibility with fractional-order modeling: one reformulates FO-TS models using MPV, while the other constructs FO-TS models with UPV via uncertain fuzzy models incorporating approximated states. The FO-PIFO convergence criteria are derived using fractional-order Lyapunov theory, and the associated stability conditions are expressed as Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs). To enhance robustness, strategies for mitigating external disturbances are also integrated. The resulting FO-PIFO designs are then employed to build observer banks that generate residuals for detecting actuator and sensor faults. Finally, multiple simulation scenarios are presented to validate the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed diagnostic methods.

Introduction

Takagi–Sugeno (TS) fuzzy models have proven to be powerful tools for approximating nonlinear systems, allowing complex dynamic behaviors to be represented through a collection of local linear sub-models. Since their introduction in the early 1980s, TS models have demonstrated significant utility in system identification, analysis, and control1, owing to their ability to accurately capture the intricate dynamics of nonlinear physical systems.

Recent studies have addressed the issue of deception attacks in nonlinear Cyber-Physical Systems (CPSs), where vulnerabilities are exploited through malicious activities such as signal deception and data injection. For instance, Zhang et al.2 proposed an adaptive decentralized finite-time fuzzy secure control strategy utilizing Nussbaum-type functions to mitigate such attacks. Similarly, Wang et al.3 introduced a fuzzy affine model-based output feedback control method designed to withstand adversarial disturbances. Although the FO-PIFO framework presented in this paper does not explicitly model attack scenarios, it offers a robust estimation foundation that can be integrated with these secure control strategies in future work to enhance CPS resilience in adversarial environments.

Furthermore, there has been growing interest in extending Takagi–Sugeno fuzzy models through the integration of fractional-order calculus. Fractional calculus provides a more accurate representation of complex systems by capturing characteristics such as memory effects, non-local behavior, and long-range dependencies4. Fractional-Order Takagi–Sugeno (FO-TS) models build upon the standard TS framework by incorporating fractional-order dynamics5. This extension has demonstrated significant advantages in modeling systems with inherent fractional behavior, including biological systems, viscoelastic materials, and fractional-order electrical circuits6–9.

Despite recent advancements in applying fractional calculus to TS fuzzy models, a significant research gap remains, particularly in handling unmeasurable premise variables (UPV) and unknown inputs within FO-TS systems. Existing observer-based fault detection techniques typically require fully measurable premise variables or rely on conventional integer-order formulations, which are inadequate for capturing the memory and hereditary characteristics intrinsic to fractional-order systems. These constraints limit the applicability of such methods in critical domains, including power electronics, biomedical systems, and cyber-physical systems.

To address this limitation, the present work introduces two novel observer structures—Fractional Order Proportional Integral Fuzzy Observers (FO-PIFOs)—specifically tailored for FO-TS systems under both measurable (MPV) and unmeasurable (UPV) premise variable scenarios. The key contributions of this study are threefold: (i) the derivation of FO-PIFOs using fractional Lyapunov stability theory and Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI)-based conditions to ensure convergence; (ii) the development of both specific and generalized observer frameworks capable of fault detection and isolation in the presence of unknown inputs; and (iii) the experimental validation of the proposed methods through extensive simulation studies. Collectively, these contributions advance the state of the art in fault diagnosis for nonlinear fractional-order systems.

Moreover, most existing fault diagnosis strategies based on FO-TS models rely on idealized assumptions, such as the full measurability of premise variables, and often overlook the complexities introduced by unknown inputs and measurement noise. These limitations significantly restrict their applicability in practical scenarios, including networked control systems, biomedical applications, and industrial automation, where sensor data may be uncertain or intermittently unavailable. Recent research has started to investigate these challenges more thoroughly. For example, Pourdadashi, Shafiee, and Darouach8 proposed a fractional-order proportional-integral observer for unknown input fractional-order singular systems, demonstrating effectiveness in actuator fault diagnosis. Although this approach is robust for singular systems, it does not address the broader fuzzy modeling challenges inherent to the FO-TS framework, which is critical for capturing nonlinear system dynamics. Building on this line of work, we further integrate fuzzy logic to enhance state estimation and fault detection capabilities.

Additionally, Mu et al.9 proposed a fault estimation methodology for T–S fuzzy fractional-order systems based on a fuzzy Lyapunov function. This approach significantly improves upon earlier methods by incorporating fractional-order dynamics alongside Lyapunov-based analysis, thereby establishing a solid theoretical foundation for fault estimation. However, it does not adequately address the challenge of unmeasurable premise variables (UPV), which remains a critical issue in applying such frameworks to real-world systems with limited information availability.

Further contributions by Mu et al.10 examined discrete-time T–S fuzzy systems with UPV using a fuzzy Lyapunov function design. While this method proves effective for fault isolation in systems with time-varying delays and sensor faults, it relies heavily on traditional Unknown Input Observer (UIO) techniques. These techniques are not well-suited for systems characterized by memory effects and non-local behavior. In contrast, our proposed method, the Fractional Order Proportional Integral Fuzzy Observer (FO-PIFO), overcomes these limitations by leveraging fractional calculus to achieve more accurate estimation of system states and unknown inputs.

The detection and diagnosis of actuator and sensor faults are critical to ensuring the reliability and safety of complex systems. However, conventional techniques often fall short, particularly in the context of FO-TS models with unmeasurable premise variables (UPV), where such faults can lead to system instability and reduced performance11. This shortcoming highlights the pressing need for fault diagnosis frameworks that can address these complexities effectively.

In response, we propose two novel FO-PIFO structures tailored for nonlinear FO-TS systems: one designed for MPV and the other for UPV. This dual-structure approach ensures comprehensive applicability across a range of realistic operational conditions. The first structure offers optimal performance when premise variables are directly accessible, while the second provides enhanced fault isolation and estimation accuracy in cases where premise variables are partially or entirely inaccessible.

To guarantee convergence of state and unknown input estimates, even in the presence of measurement noise and external disturbances, we derive LMI-based convergence conditions within the fractional Lyapunov framework. These conditions ensure the asymptotic convergence of estimation errors. The adoption of Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs) further facilitates efficient, real-time computational solvability, thereby enabling practical implementation in fault diagnosis systems.

The proposed method integrates both generalized and dedicated observer schemes. The dedicated scheme is designed to isolate faults within individual actuator or sensor components, while the generalized scheme is capable of detecting faults across multiple components simultaneously. These two approaches are jointly implemented within a fault diagnosis architecture that enables accurate tracing of system faults to their root causes.

To evaluate the proposed method, comprehensive simulation tests were conducted to compare the FO-PIFO framework against traditional observer techniques. The results demonstrate the superior performance of our approach in terms of convergence rate, robustness, and diagnostic accuracy—particularly in systems characterized by fractional-order dynamics and unmeasurable premise variables. The robustness of the proposed observers is further confirmed through simulations under various disturbance scenarios, reinforcing their practicality and reliability for real-world fault diagnosis applications.

Traditional observer schemes, such as Luenberger, Kalman, and sliding mode observers, are typically formulated within a finite-order framework, which limits their ability to capture the long-memory and hereditary characteristics inherent in many physical systems. Moreover, these observers often assume full measurability of premise variables and, in some cases, rely on decoupling techniques or auxiliary estimators to handle unknown inputs. In contrast, the proposed FO-PIFO structure leverages a fractional-order modeling approach that inherently accommodates non-local memory effects, thereby enhancing the observers representational capabilities. Additionally, unknown inputs are explicitly incorporated into the model, and synthesis is achievable for both measurable and unmeasurable premise variable scenarios through a Lyapunov-based Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI) formulation. Collectively, these features significantly enhance the fault detection accuracy and robustness of the FO-PIFO observer beyond the capabilities of conventional integer-order designs.

Furthermore, related studies by Valério et al.5 and Yang, Zhang, and Chen12 have investigated robust control and adaptive strategies for FO-TS systems. However, the present work distinguishes itself through its focus on the synthesis and application of the FO-PIFO within a Lyapunov stability framework. This emphasis makes the proposed approach particularly well-suited for real-time control in systems affected by fractional-order dynamics. Active research areas include the application of FO-TS models under unmeasurable premise assumptions in power systems13, fault detection and isolation in uncertain FO-TS models with time delays and sensor faults11, and the synthesis of fractional-order Thau–Luenberger observers14. Our contribution advances this body of work by introducing a novel observer design that unifies and extends these strategies, offering improved accuracy and robustness. In summary, the proposed framework aims to enhance the robustness, accuracy, and practical applicability of fault diagnosis in fractional-order nonlinear systems by integrating fuzzy inference mechanisms, observer banks, and advanced fractional control theory.

Although many fault diagnosis tasks have been addressed using FO-TS models, most existing solutions remain constrained by assumptions, particularly the requirement that premise variables be fully measurable, and often fail to consider the cross-interaction effects between unknown inputs and unmeasurable premise variables. Traditional observers, including Luenberger observers, Kalman filters, and sliding mode observers, are predominantly based on integer-order calculus and therefore lack the capacity to model the memory-dependent behaviors inherent in fractional-order systems. Moreover, prior efforts to extend observer design to fractional-order systems8,10 have typically focused on either unknown inputs or UPV independently. The absence of a unified observer framework capable of simultaneously addressing both challenges represents a significant gap in the field of fault diagnosis for nonlinear systems.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows. Section “Fractional order modelling and problem formulation”introduces the fault detection and isolation approach using observer scheme banks, formulates the FO-TS model design problem under measurable premise variables (MPV), and derives the Fractional Order Thau–Luenberger Observer (FO-TLO). Section “Synthesis of FO-PI observers”presents the development of the FO-PIFO for FO-TS systems under both MPV and UPV. Section “Fault diagnosis-based FO-TS models”provides numerical examples that demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in detecting actuator and sensor faults in systems modeled with UPV. The paper concludes with a discussion of the results and recommendations for future research directions.

Fault detection and isolation schemes

An essential aspect of the fault detection and isolation (FDI) process is structuring residuals in a manner that facilitates straightforward fault identification. Ideally, each residual should correspond uniquely to a specific fault, enabling accurate diagnosis. Similar to decoupling strategies, this structure can be achieved through various approaches, one of which involves selectively incorporating specific inputs and/or outputs into the observer design.

The fault sensitivity of the observer can be fine-tuned by appropriately combining selected outputs and inputs. By designing residuals that are particularly sensitive to certain faults, the observers ability to isolate those faults is significantly enhanced. Additionally, computational complexity can be reduced and the impact of noise and disturbances minimized by utilizing only a subset of the systems inputs and outputs.

In simple terms, effective FDI in complex systems relies on strategic observer design, where the choice of inputs and outputs enables the construction of a well-structured residual matrix. To achieve effective fault detection and isolation (FDI), two distinct banks of observers are required. The first bank is dedicated to sensor fault detection. When the system model incorporates output variables, it becomes essential to account for output information during observer synthesis, as it can no longer be neglected. Furthermore, sensors are inherently prone to errors, necessitating careful observer design. Due to these constraints, separate observer banks must be constructed for the same system, depending on whether the objective is to detect and isolate faults in sensors or actuators. Addressing scenarios in which the activation functions depend on the system states can offer valuable insights and solutions for overcoming these challenges.

Synthesis of FO-PI observers

We propose a fault detection and isolation (FDI) scheme for sensors and actuators based on the Fractional Order Proportional Integral Fuzzy Observer (FO-PIFO) structure. These observers are specifically designed to exploit the effects of output reconstruction errors induced by proportional faults, enabling the accurate estimation of sensor and actuator faults.

The FO-PIFO integrates proportional and integral components to effectively simulate fault signals and reconstruct system behavior, regardless of whether actual faults are present. Within the FO-TS framework, this approach generalizes the application of proportional-integral observers, extending their use from linear to singular systems. The performance of this technique is evaluated under conditions involving both measurable and unmeasurable premise variables17.

Fault diagnosis-based FO-TS models

This section outlines the approach adopted for analyzing the system under consideration. The objective is to utilize a proportional-integral (PI) observer to design a residual generator for fault estimation. Specifically, the focus is on estimating faults in actuators and sensors of a nonlinear system characterized by unmeasurable premise variables (UPV), as structured by the FO-TS framework. By removing the structural constraints typically imposed by decoupling unknown inputs, the proposed method leverages the advantages of fractional-order observers, particularly in handling unknown inputs without explicit decoupling. This approach not only enables more effective fault identification and localization but also supports simultaneous estimation of actuator and sensor faults. A dedicated observer bank is employed to support this estimation process, with detection thresholds determined based on the disturbance attenuation rates derived during observer synthesis.

In this study, α is set to 0.8, based on insights from preliminary simulations and established practices in the fractional-order control literature. This choice offers a practical balance between preserving long-memory effects and achieving rapid convergence. Specifically, the selected value of α enables the FO-PIFO observer to retain sufficient historical system dynamics, thereby enhancing robustness against disturbances, while maintaining responsiveness suitable for accurate state estimation and fault detection. The value of α = 0.8 has shown favorable performance in this study, and its robustness across different system types will be further explored in future work to evaluate its general applicability.

It is also important to highlight that the designed FO-PIFO observer inherently demonstrates robustness against moderate variations in the parameters of the controlled process. This robustness stems primarily from the LMI-based synthesis framework and the use of fractional-order dynamics, which naturally incorporate memory and damping effects. These properties help attenuate the influence of parameter uncertainties and external disturbances. While a formal robustness analysis involving structured uncertainties is beyond the scope of the current study, it represents a promising direction for future research, particularly for safety–critical applications that require assured performance under significant model deviations.

To evaluate the performance of the Fractional Order Proportional Integral Fuzzy Observer (FO-PIFO), a comprehensive comparison is conducted against a conventional Integral-Order Observer (IO) using several key performance metrics: state estimation error, fault detection capability, convergence rate, and sensitivity to external disturbances. These metrics are assessed on benchmark models, providing a rigorous framework for comparing both observers under varying operating conditions.

The state estimation error is calculated at each time instant for both the IO and FO-PIFO observers, and the mean error over the simulation period is used to assess and compare fault detection accuracy. Fault detection performance is further validated by analyzing the residuals generated by each observer and applying predefined thresholds to isolate faults. Convergence rate is evaluated by measuring the time each observer takes to reach a steady-state estimate. Additionally, robustness is tested by subjecting both observers to the same external disturbance profiles and monitoring their performance in state estimation and fault detection.

To contextualize the benefits of FO-PIFO, traditional observer-based control strategies such as Luenberger observers, Kalman filters, and sliding mode observers are considered. While Luenberger and Kalman observers, being based on integer-order models, perform adequately in linear time-invariant systems, they are unable to capture memory-dependent behavior and long-range dependencies typical of fractional-order systems. Sliding mode observers, although robust to model uncertainties, are susceptible to chattering and require high-gain tuning, which complicates implementation and may compromise stability in high-frequency systems.

In contrast, the FO-PIFO observer inherently avoids chattering and does not demand excessive gain tuning, offering a more stable and robust solution, particularly in systems characterized by memory effects. By leveraging fractional-order calculus, the FO-PIFO effectively models nonlocal and hereditary properties, making it a more accurate and reliable tool for state estimation and fault detection in applications dominated by fractional dynamics. While this qualitative comparison highlights key advantages, future work will focus on quantitative evaluations, such as estimation error, convergence speed, and resilience to perturbations, for a more comprehensive assessment of the FO-PIFOs performance across diverse fault detection and system estimation scenarios.

Conclusions and future works

This paper presented two novel fault diagnosis methods for nonlinear systems modeled by Fractional-Order Takagi–Sugeno (FO-TS) frameworks with unmeasurable premise variables (UPV). These methods are based on the synthesis of fuzzy fractional-order proportional-integral fuzzy observers (FO-PIFO). The approach begins by transforming the system into an equivalent disturbed FO-TS form with UPV. Two distinct cases are then analyzed: the first assumes that the perturbation in the reformulated FO-TS model satisfies a Lipschitz condition, while the second does not rely on this assumption. For the latter case, an alternative methodology is developed based on an H∞ synthesis approach. The convergence conditions for the FO-PIFO observers are formulated as Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs), enabling efficient and practical solutions using standard computational tools. The simulation results demonstrate promising convergence behavior for both state and unknown input fault estimations using the proposed FO-PIFO observers. Notably, the design facilitates the construction of FO-PIFO observer banks capable of performing sensor and actuator fault detection and isolation (FDI), even under the influence of perturbations, thanks to their inherent decoupling from estimated states and inputs. Additionally, pole placement techniques can be employed to further refine the dynamic response of the observers. Although the FO-PIFO observer shows strong potential in fractional-order systems, comparative analysis with other established observer-based strategies remains relevant. Classical techniques, such as the Luenberger observer, are effective for linear systems but are limited in fractional-order scenarios due to their inability to capture memory effects and non-local behaviors. Likewise, nonlinear observers such as sliding mode and Kalman filters are often applied in complex systems but face difficulties in managing the unique challenges of fractional dynamics. The proposed FO-PIFO approach distinguishes itself by incorporating fractional calculus, thereby offering a more accurate and robust means of modeling and diagnosing systems with memory and long-range dependencies. A comprehensive quantitative comparison with traditional methods is planned as part of future work, with the aim of further validating the advantages and identifying potential limitations of the FO-PIFO framework in real-world applications involving nonlinear and fractional-order dynamics.

A key strength of the proposed FO-PIFO framework lies in its unified design, which effectively accommodates both measurable and unmeasurable premise variables. For unmeasurable premises, the observer estimates the system states and internally utilizes them as premise variables within the fuzzy rule base. By integrating state estimation and fuzzy inference at the observer level, the design eliminates the need for external estimation modules, thereby simplifying the overall architecture. This approach maintains model consistency and ensures observer convergence. However, future research may explore extensions involving mismatched or delayed premise variable configurations to enhance robustness in complex cyber-physical systems. While the numerical examples provided demonstrate the theoretical feasibility of the FO-PIFO observer, practical implementation in physical systems introduces additional challenges. These include handling measurement noise, addressing sensor and actuator resolution limitations, and ensuring real-time computational feasibility. The current work serves primarily as a foundation for the mathematical formulation and stability analysis of the observer. Future efforts will focus on deploying the proposed method in real-world applications, such as electrical networks and mechanical systems, where practical considerations such as system dynamics, sensor modeling, and real-time fault detection must be carefully addressed. Compared to traditional integral-order observers, the FO-PIFO offers several theoretical advantages. Its foundation in fractional-order calculus makes it particularly well-suited for capturing long-memory and non-local dynamics. The proportional-integral structure further enables simultaneous estimation of system states and unknown inputs, eliminating the need for separate decoupling techniques. Moreover, the FO-PIFO accommodates both measurable and unmeasurable premise variables within a single framework, utilizing fractional Lyapunov theory and LMI-based synthesis. These features collectively position the FO-PIFO as a powerful and versatile tool for robust fault diagnosis in advanced, uncertain, and partially observable systems, even under non-ideal measurement conditions.

One of the key aspects of the proposed FO-PIFO framework is that it has a unified design to handle both measurable and unmeasurable premise variables. For the unmeasurable premises, the observer estimates the system states and uses them internally as the premise variables for the fuzzy rule base. By integrating the two at the integration level, the design does not have to use any external estimation blocks and minimizes the overall structure. While this method preserves model consistency and observer convergence, future extensions can explore the use of mismatched or delayed premise settings to further enhance robustness in complex cyber-physical systems. While the numerical examples above demonstrate theoretical possibility of the proposed FO-PIFO observer, it is realized that its application to physical systems entails additional concerns. These include how to deal with system noise, resolution limits of sensors and actuators, and real-time implementation. The current contribution primarily constitutes the foundation for mathematical formulation and observer stability analysis. In forthcoming work, we will focus on applying the proposed method to real systems, e.g., electric networks or mechanical systems, where all these technical aspects will find their proper places in the design of the observer. This includes a careful examination of the system dynamic behavior, sensor modeling, and implementation of the fault detection under real operating conditions.

Shopping motivations of Paylater users
Authors: Ghina Amalia Syifani, Martina Dwi Mustika
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectiveDespite Paylater’s increasing popularity in Indonesia, no research potentially has explored the influence of Paylater users’ shopping motivation on purchase intention. This topic is important to research to create a healthier shopping environment for marketers and users. This paper presents two studies addressing this gap.MethodStudy 1 explored the values of shopping motivation influencing purchase intention in Indonesia using a non-experimental method with 299 participants aged from 18 to 40 years in Indonesia. Study 2 used experimental vignette methods to strengthen the first study’s results. This study involved 752 participants with the same characteristics as Study 1.ResultsThe results of Study 1 show that both utilitarian and hedonic motivations influence Paylater users’ purchase intention in Indonesia, and all shopping motivation values positively influence utilitarian and hedonic motivations. In Study 2, we found that values of convenience, customisation, adventure/exploration and gratification affect the purchase intention of Paylater users in Indonesia.ConclusionThe results demonstrate both motivations influence purchase intention, but hedonic has the most significant influence, and all shopping motivation values are significant. Therefore, each value can motivate Indonesian Paylater users’ intention to purchase.Key PointsWhat is known about this topic:KEYWORDS: Paylater, hedonic, utilitarian, shopping motivation, purchase intention

Introduction

The growth of the digital economy has driven the emergence of digital payment innovations that are fast and easily accessible to the public, such as Paylater, which has the potential to increase transactions on e-commerce platforms (Research Institute of Socio-Economic Development [RISED], 2021). “Paylater” is a loan in the form of capital or funds as an alternative payment method when purchasing goods and services via online platforms (Rumayya et al., 2020). “E-commerce (electronic commerce)” is a place on the internet that involves buying and selling products or services, as well as transferring money and data, which relies on digital platforms and technology such as websites, social media, and mobile applications to provide the buying and selling activities (Jolaoso & Main, 2024). Paylater benefits its consumers, particularly those who cannot apply for a loan or credit card (RISED, 2021). Unlike credit cards, which require consumers to have a strong credit profile before they can obtain one (Sweeney, 2024), Paylater offers convenience by simply uploading a softcopy of a proof of identity documents such as an identity card, insurance card or family card (CIMB Niaga, n.d.). Consumers can easily apply for Paylater on some of Indonesia’s e-commerce sites by providing only a photograph with their identification details (Sugianto, 2020). Owing to this convenience, they can use Paylater to fulfil urgent needs, organise a short-term arrangement (i.e., less than a year’s duration), receive promotions, manage their finances or buy consumer goods (Kredivo & Insight Center, 2022), especially in e-commerce.

In Indonesia, increasing numbers of people are using Paylater for e-commerce. In 2021, 38% of consumers purchased items on e-commerce platforms using Paylater, representing a significant increase from 28% in 2020 (Kredivo & Insight Center, 2022). Data from Ganbold (2022) show Indonesia is the Southeast Asia country with the highest Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) expenditure in e-commerce, amounting to US$1.7 billion, and this is predicted to increase to US$7.3 billion by 2026. However, the public debt of Paylater users in Indonesia is also high, reaching IDR 6.13 trillion (CNN Indonesia, 2024). This debt can arise because of the convenience and benefits offered by Paylater, leading users to continue using the service and intend to shop without paying on time. Marketers then use these benefits—i.e., offering more discount options with higher discounts, interest-free for new users, easy and safe payment methods, as well as advertisements with statements such as “make dreams come true”, “can buy anything” and “shopping on e-commerce becomes fun”, as used by one of the largest e-commerce companies in Indonesia, Traveloka – to market Paylater. However, Paylater users and marketers need to understand the shopping motivations of Indonesian consumers. This understanding is crucial to promote a healthy shopping environment that encourages responsible shopping habits.

Previous research on online and offline shopping has identified two shopping motivations: utilitarian and hedonic (Babin et al., 1994; Hirschman & Holbrook, 1982; Kumar & Yadav, 2021; Singh, 2015; To et al., 2007). Gawior et al. (2022) demonstrated a relationship between one hedonic value (social shopping) and credit card users. Kumar and Sadarangani (2021) demonstrated that shopping motivations influence consumers’ purchase intentions of foreign-brand clothing. To et al. (2007) demonstrated utilitarian motivation directly affects and hedonic motivation indirectly affects the purchase intention of Internet shoppers. Most studies have investigated credit card, online or offline shopping (Gawior et al., 2022; Pappas et al., 2017; Singh, 2015; To et al., 2007) in the absence of the Paylater method, while no research potentially has been conducted related to shopping motivation using Paylater in Indonesia. Although there has been no research regarding the shopping motivation of Paylater users, these previous studies suggest shopping motivation has a relationship with the purchase intention of Paylater users. Although the research contexts differ, credit cards and Paylater have similar payment concepts (Sugianto, 2020).

Therefore, this study aims to provide new insights, address the gap in the research regarding shopping motivation in the context of Paylater users in Indonesia, and contribute to increasing awareness of Paylater users and marketers to create a healthier economy. Thus, the objectives of this research are:

to investigate the influence of shopping motivations (utilitarian and hedonic) on the purchase intention of Paylater users in Indonesia.

to investigate the influence of shopping motivations (utilitarian and hedonic) on the purchase intention of Paylater users in Indonesia.

to investigate which utilitarian and hedonic values can describe the shopping motivations of Paylater users.

to investigate which utilitarian and hedonic values can describe the shopping motivations of Paylater users.

Literature review

Purchase intention

Purchase intention describes the stage before buying and the extent to which consumers want to buy a product (Peña-García et al., 2020). Several factors can influence a person’s purchase intention, especially in e-commerce. Different factors can influence offline and online purchase intention differently and to different degrees (Akram et al., 2021), including utilitarian and hedonic values (Kim et al., 2004).

Shopping motivation

Motivation can be affected when the stimulus matches the consumer and is influenced by each consumer’s values, goals, and needs (Santo & Marques, 2022). Shopping motivation is a key research area in consumer shopping behaviour. Two research focuses are evident in the consumer literature: (1) personal motivations for shopping and (2) the hedonic and utilitarian values behind shopping motivation (Guido, 2006). Babin et al. (1994) conducted empirical research proving hedonic and utilitarian motivation can influence consumer behaviour.

Utilitarian motivation

According to Jin and Kim (2003), utilitarian motivation describes the function and purpose of shopping. Individuals with utilitarian motivation will find value if they obtain what they seek when shopping, and their shopping is better if done carefully or critically (Babin et al., 1994). Utilitarian motivation focuses more on fulfilling consumption needs (Akram et al., 2021). Individuals with utilitarian motivation will shop by maximising the utility or benefits obtained (Hirschman & Holbrook, 1982). Fernandes et al. (2020) stated that the utilitarian aspect of online shopping is related to display quality, information availability, ease of use, and transaction convenience. According to Santo and Marques (2022), online stores have several benefits compared to physical stores because of lower costs, convenience, and greater selection. Consumers with utilitarian goals are associated with three online shopping attributes: convenience, information and selection, which can influence their purchase intentions (Fernandes et al., 2020). According to Wolfinbarger and Gilly (2001), utilitarian motivation comprises four values: convenience, selection, information availability and lack of sociality.

Hedonic motivation

Hedonic motivation describes consumer behaviour related to various multi-sensory visuals, fantasies and emotions when shopping (Hirschman & Holbrook, 1982). Hedonic shopping is considered more subjective and personal because it is viewed as a pleasure, not a task that must be fulfilled (Evelina et al., 2020; Fernandes et al., 2020). Hedonic motivation is based on psychological needs – for example, meeting social and aesthetic demands (Indrawati et al., 2022). Similar to utilitarian motivation, hedonic motivation can also motivate people to shop offline (Singh, 2015) and online (Akram et al., 2021). The values included in hedonic motivation are adventure, sociality, idea shopping, authority and status, gratification, satisfaction, value shopping, and role (Arnold & Reynolds, 2003; Westbrook & Black, 1985). These hedonic values are seen as the value received by customers based on pleasant experiences and pleasures (Evelina et al., 2020).

Conceptual framework

The framework in this research was inspired by research by To et al. (2007), which discussed shopping motivations when shopping on the Internet and found Internet shoppers have both utilitarian and hedonic motivations. According to several studies, shopping motivation research in online shopping should not only be considered from a utilitarian motivation perspective but also from a hedonic motivation perspective – they can be associated with the online characteristics of each buyer (Santo & Marques, 2022). The adapted model suggests utilitarian motivation values based on Wolfinbarger and Gilly (2001) and Dastane et al. (2023)—namely, convenience, selection, information availability and lack of sociality, and hedonic motivation values based on Westbrook and Black’s (1985) and Arnold and Reynolds (2003) work – namely, adventure, sociality, shopping ideas, authority and status – to observe their influence on Paylater users’ purchase intention in Indonesia. The model presented in this study suggests adding gratification because it is one of the values of hedonic motivation (Arnold & Reynolds, 2003) and adding cost saving and customisation to utilitarian motivation because these two additional categories represent essential values and advantages in Internet shopping (To et al., 2007). However, value shopping, satisfaction and the role of hedonic motivation are not included because, according to To et al. (2007), these do not describe Internet shoppers, and the experience of physical shopping differs from that of online shopping. This research was adapted to further examine the influence of shopping motivations in Indonesia by using measurements adapted to suit the context of Paylater users on e-commerce as shown in Figure 1.

Research Model.

Hypotheses

Utilitarian motivation

Cost saving

One of the most important benefits of shopping in e-commerce is saving money (Evelina et al., 2020). According to To et al. (2007), a person’s purchase intentions can be influenced by a lower-priced product. Lower-priced items can be offered in online stores because sellers do not have to pay for physical buildings (Miller, 2000). Santo and Marques (2022) also stated that there are considerations that competitive prices are one of the attractions of online shopping. In the context of this study, using Paylater can save on monthly expenses because of the tenures and discounts offered. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Cost saving has a significant positive influence on the utilitarian motivation of Paylater users.

Convenience

Convenience refers to aspects that save time and effort, including physical and mental effort and accessibility – for example, stores’ locations, products and available hours (Kumar & Sadarangani, 2021). Time savings and being accessible anytime are some of the most important advantages of online shopping (Dastane et al., 2023; Evelina et al., 2020). In the context of this research, individuals can consume services provided 24 hours a day using Paylater, anywhere and anytime, and there is no need to transfer funds from mobile banking to e-commerce platforms. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Convenience has a significant positive influence on the utilitarian motivation of Paylater users.

Selection

Online stores are considered to have a broader selection of products than offline stores for selection because in an online setting, sellers can easily display various products, and buyers have more options to choose from (Evelina et al., 2020; Kumar & Sadarangani, 2021), the more products there are, the more customers it will attract (Evelina et al., 2020). In the context of this study, several Paylater payment methods offer various tenure options and more discount or cashback options. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Selection has a significant positive influence on the utilitarian motivation of Paylater users.

Information availability

Information availability is an essential value in Internet shopping. Online shopping sites provide various information, including a search bar that helps shoppers search for products (Kumar & Sadarangani, 2021) and detailed information and product specifications displayed in full on e-commerce (Evelina et al., 2020). Access to consistent and thorough information is one of the reasons customers trust online shopping and purchase intentions online (Santo & Marques, 2022). Unlike credit cards, Paylater terms and conditions can be viewed on each platform so they can be read repeatedly. In addition, information is provided on the fees that must be paid when choosing a tenure. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Information availability has a significant positive influence on the utilitarian motivation of Paylater users.

Lack of sociality

According to To et al. (2007), customers can conduct simple and engaging social activities on the Internet. Wolfinbarger and Gilly (2001) stated online shoppers prefer to shop without having to come face-to-face with a salesperson. In the context of this study, customers can shop without having to meet or see other people when using Paylater during e-commerce. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Lack of sociality has a significant positive influence on the utilitarian motivation of Paylater users.

Customisation

Customisation is a unique value of online stores (Kumar & Sadarangani, 2021). The Internet represents an excellent place to provide customisation in various forms, such as design, delivery and transaction methods (To et al., 2007). In the context of this study, customers who use Paylater may choose between available tenures. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Customisation has a significant positive influence on the utilitarian motivation of Paylater users.

Hedonic motivation

Adventure/Exploration

Adventure shopping refers to buyers for whom the shopping process is more exciting than the product (S. Kumar & Sadarangani, 2021). According to To et al. (2007), customers feel happy and adventurous while exploring e-commerce platforms to find products. In addition, the research results by Santo and Marques (2022) show that hedonic motivations related to adventure can explain customers’ desire to continue buying in online shops. In the context of this study, it is assumed that customers want to try Paylater because it is a new payment method. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Adventure/Exploration has a significant positive influence on the hedonic motivation of Paylater users.

Sociality

Sociality refers to an individual’s experience of pleasure when shopping with friends and family, socialising when shopping or bonding with others (Arnold & Reynolds, 2003). Online customers can share information and shopping experiences with individuals with the same interests. Experiences that can only be enjoyed online can be a topic of conversation and provide a sense of pleasure to Internet shoppers (To et al., 2007). Negative evaluations given by consumers can negatively impact other consumers’ perspectives (Von Helversen et al., 2018), which means that information obtained by fellow users can influence the buying process. In the context of this research, e-commerce shoppers can share information and shopping experiences using Paylater. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Sociality has a significant positive influence on the hedonic motivation of Paylater users.

Idea shopping

Idea shopping refers to individuals who shop to follow new trends or fashions and explore new products and innovations (Arnold & Reynolds, 2003). On the Internet, shoppers can find, understand and evaluate information about brands, products and new trends and become excited in shopping (To et al., 2007). In the context of this study, Paylater is a trending new payment method, and it is assumed that is one of the reasons why customers shop. In addition, information regarding Paylater can be found in e-commerce contexts. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Idea shopping has a significant positive influence on the hedonic motivation of Paylater users.

Authority and status

The sense of authority and status experienced when shopping online involves the ability to control and master technology (To et al., 2007). According to Yu et al. (2018), online customers can view and select products anytime and anywhere. In the context of this research, Paylater users engaging in e-commerce can use the Paylater payment method at any time and choose their desired tenure. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Authority and status significantly positively influence the hedonic motivation of Paylater users.

Gratification

Gratification shopping refers to individuals who shop to relieve stress and negative moods and to treat themselves (Arnold & Reynolds, 2003). Shopping in e-commerce can improve consumers’ mood and satisfaction (Evelina et al., 2020). A focus group discussion was conducted prior to the start of the research to allow the researchers to gain direct insights into the perspectives of Paylater users. During the discussion, participants were asked about their usage of Paylater and the reasons behind it. One focus group participant described how they bought things online to treat themselves after enduring something difficult or unpleasant (e.g., exam week). Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Gratification has a significant positive influence on the hedonic motivation of Paylater users.

Shopping motivation and purchase intentions

According to To et al. (2007), individuals are more likely to buy if a website offers many functions, especially of utilitarian value. However, for To et al. (2007), websites do not entertain and do not directly encourage the desire to buy, meanwhile today’s e-commerce platforms offer a live shopping feature and show attractive product photos. Individuals can open an e-commerce application and watch sellers selling and choosing items from the available product photos, making it an experience. Customers can immediately buy an item without having the product on hand. In addition, customers shop not only to get products but also to satisfy experiential and emotional needs. Hence, the emergence of utilitarian and hedonic purposes is part of the buying process (Santo & Marques, 2022). Therefore, this study assumes a direct relationship between utilitarian and hedonic motivations and purchase intention. Thus, this study hypothesises that:

Utilitarian motivation has a significant positive influence on the purchase intention of Paylater users.

Hedonic motivation has a significant positive influence on the purchase intention of Paylater users.

The two studies below explored these hypotheses, but only the most significant values from Study 1 were used for the second study.

Study 1

Methods

Participants and materials

In Study 1, 299 respondents spread throughout Indonesia (male = 26.8%, female = 73.2%), as shown in Table 1, completed a four-page questionnaire comprising informed consent, questions related to their use of Paylater and demographic data; 39 items describing the values of hedonic and utilitarian motivation (Arnold & Reynolds, 2003; To et al., 2007); three items describing purchase intention (To et al., 2007); and two manipulation check items. The variables were measured using a Likert scale: “1” described total disagreement, and “7” described total agreement. The manipulation check items were “I am currently in a coma” and “I am now fainting”. The original surveys and scales from To et al. (2007) and Arnold and Reynolds (2003) were written in English without a Paylater or e-commerce context, so were adapted for this study by adding context – Paylater users on e-commerce – and changing the scale language to Indonesian using Brislin’s translation model which involved a bilingual expert translating the scale into Indonesian and a different bilingual expert, who was a sworn translator, back-translating it blindly (Jones et al., 2001).

This study also involved an online survey using Zoho Survey as the survey platform. The target sample for this study was Indonesian citizens aged 18 to 40 years who use Paylater. The selection of this age range follows Gerrans et al. (2022), who stated the age of Paylater users ranges from 18 to 40 years or aligns with the ages of Generations Z and Y. Zoho Survey allows automatic filtering of participant characteristics so that only Paylater users with a predetermined age range could complete the questionnaire. A non-random sampling technique was used because it is difficult to know the exact number of Paylater users; therefore, convenience sampling was used to seek participants who were easy to obtain, available and willing to respond (Gravetter & Forzano, 2012). Convenience sampling was conducted via online survey, and questionnaires were distributed through the social media platforms Instagram, WhatsApp, Twitter and Facebook.

Data analytic plan

Data analysis for Study 1 was performed using the statistical software SPSS v29 for data entry and AMOS v29 for data analysis. Structural equation modelling (SEM) was used for path analysis in the Study 1 research model (Fan et al., 2016).

Results

AMOS was used for model testing and degree of fit, while SEM was used to show the model and degree of fit. According to Hooper et al. (2008), the results showed the values indicated an acceptable fit (minimum discrepancy of confirmatory factor analysis/degrees of freedom [CMIN/df] = 2.812, comparative fit index [CFI] = 0.829, root mean square error of approximation [RMSEA] = 0.078, Tucker – Lewis’s index [TLI] = 0.815). Internal validity is also obtained from the corrected item-total correlation, with the results for all items being greater than > .300, which can be said to be good (Field, 2013). All scales were found reliable based on Hinton et al. (2014). The values of utilitarian motivation for cost saving, convenience, selection, information availability, lack of sociality, and customisation ranged from α = .589 to .805, while the values of hedonic motivation for adventure/exploration, sociality, idea shopping, authority and status, and gratification ranged from α = .704 to .842. Furthermore, the scales for purchase intention were found reliable, with a value of α = .845.

The path analysis results show all items have good significance (β = > .300, p < .001). It is shown from Table 2 that utilitarian motivation and hedonic motivation had a significant positive influence on purchase intention in Paylater users (β = .383 and p < .05, and β = .588 and p > .001, respectively). This demonstrates utilitarian values that are cost saving (β = .776, p < .001), convenience (β = .990, p < .001), selection (β = .917, p < .001), information availability (β = .886, p < .001), lack of sociality (β = .948, p < .001) and customisation (β = 1.009, p < .001) each have a significant positive influence on the utilitarian motivation of Paylater users in Indonesia. Regarding hedonic values, adventure/exploration (β = .993, p < .001), sociality (β = .841, p < .001), idea shopping (β = .753, p < .001), authority and status (β = .860, p < .001), and gratification (β = .865, p < .001) each had a significant positive influence on the hedonic motivation of Paylater users in Indonesia. Therefore, all hypotheses are accepted.

Discussion

Considering Paylater users’ shopping motivation in Indonesia, utilitarian motivation and hedonic motivation to shop can affect purchase intention. This aligns with previous research, which states utilitarian and hedonic motivations significantly impact online purchase intention (Akram et al., 2021). The result shows that hedonic motivation is more significant than utilitarian motivation. This demonstrates that Paylater and e-commerce users in Indonesia are more likely to shop because they are driven by hedonic motivation. This study shows all hedonic values – namely, adventure/exploration, sociality, idea shopping, authority and status, and gratification – have a positive and significant influence on hedonic motivation, and adventure/exploration and gratification values have a more significant influence than other values. This result is supported by previous research, which states that adventure/exploration is a value of online shopping motivation with a significant impact on purchase intention (To et al., 2007), and the gratification is supported by Evelina et al. (2020) in their research on customer satisfaction. These results indicate people in Indonesia shop using e-commerce and Paylater because they feel the shopping process is more enjoyable or exciting. Therefore, it alleviates negative moods.

Apart from hedonic values, this study also shows all utilitarian motivation values – namely, cost saving, convenience, selection, information availability, lack of sociality and customisation – play a role in influencing utilitarian motivation, and customisation and convenience play a more significant role than other values. This result is supported by previous research, which stated that one of the values with a strong role in motivating online shopping is convenience (Kumar & Kashyap, 2018) and customisation in motivating green apparel shopping (Kumar & Yadav, 2021). Table 2 indicates these are the most significant values motivating Paylater users in Indonesia because they can buy products and select tenures according to their own preference using Paylater and e-commerce.

The results of this research are also supported by significant positive second-order results, which indicate the values in utilitarian and hedonic motivation are described well in the measuring instrument items used. As a result, these values could explain the motivation behind Paylater users’ purchase intentions in Indonesia, particularly customisation and convenience for utilitarian motivation and adventure/exploration and gratification for hedonic motivation. Accordingly, we used customisation, convenience, adventure/exploration and gratification in Study 2 to determine which values influence Paylater users’ purchase intention the most.

Lastly, for Study 1, the research model is considered acceptable but not a good fit; this could be because the sample was not large enough (N < 500), which can cause the CFI and TLI to be biased and tend to be less than optimal for the model’s fitness (Shi et al., 2019).

Study 2

Methods

Participants and materials

Study 2 was conducted to determine the direct influence of customisation, convenience, adventure/exploration and gratification values on purchase intention using a 2 × 2 experimental vignette method in the form of comics strips, meaning there were a minimum of four stimuli, with each value measured equally at least two times in different stimuli (Atzmüller & Steiner, 2010). Participants in this experimental study numbered 752 (male = 18.2%, female = 81.4%, prefer not to say = 0.4%) as shown in Table 3. Participants received a 14-page questionnaire consisting of informed consent, participant demographics, questionnaire-completion information, four pages of stimuli for hedonic and utilitarian values, the purchase intention scale, a manipulation check, and debriefing pages. The characteristics of participants and measuring instruments used were the same as those used for Study 1. Zoho Survey was again used to filter participants according to the appropriate characteristics automatically. The manipulation check was conducted by following the study’s stimulus style presenting a comic strip, which displayed a light conversation, and asking questions related to the conversation.

For Study 2, a within-subject method was employed, whereby a group of Paylater users was given the same stimulus (Field, 2013). The prepared stimulus was given to participants after conducting FGDs with seven Paylater and e-commerce users aged 18 to 40 years old on 8 August 2023. The FGD was used to obtain opinions about the stimulus: whether it was effective, whether it described the values, whether the narrative was straightforward, whether it could affect purchase intention and other inputs. The stimulus was given in the form of comic strips, as shown in Figure 2, each consisting of two values with variations: (A) customisation with adventure/exploration, (B) convenience with adventure/exploration, (C) customisation with gratification, and (D) convenience with gratification. The difference in stimuli was evident in the content of the conversation in the comic strips about e-commerce shopping using Paylater. The conversation in the comic strips exposed what is obtained when shopping using Paylater with e-commerce by describing items according to the values contained in each stimulus.

Data analytic plan

Study 2 used the factorial analysis of variance repeated measures technique. This technique is used in experimental research employing several independent variables on all participants (Field, 2013).

Results

Mauchly’s test of sphericity for utilitarian and hedonic motivations could not be used in Study 2 because each variable had only two variations (Field, 2013). These variations showed that the sphericity estimate was 1 (non-violated), with a chi-square of 0 and no degrees of freedom (df = 0), meaning probability could not be calculated. Hence, the degree of freedom used was assumed to be sphericity.

As shown in Table 4, there was a significant main effect of utilitarian motivations [F(1, 751) = 4.71, p = .03] and hedonic motivation [F(1, 751) = 36.47, p < .001] on Paylater users’ purchase intention. Table 5 shows convenience (M = 16.01, SE = .160, 95% CI = [15.695, 16.324]) and adventure/exploration (M = 16.15, SE = .164, 95% CI = [15.829, 16.472]) had greater effects on Paylater users’ purchase intention than did the other two values. Overall, adventure/exploration had the greatest effect on Paylater users’ purchase intention in Indonesia, followed by convenience, customisation and gratification. Lastly, utilitarian motivation significantly interacted with hedonic motivation [F(1, 751) = 6.70, p = .010]. This result indicates each motivation has different effects on people. Based on the results, a comparison between stimuli reveals their influences were not substantially different and that all stimuli positively affected Paylater users’ purchase intention.

Discussion

Study 2 revealed both utilitarian and hedonic motivations affected the purchase intention of Paylater users. In general, these results support Study 1, which produced similar results. Both studies indicate that Indonesian people are more likely to have purchase intentions using e-commerce with Paylater. This is because they tend to be motivated by hedonic and utilitarian when shopping. As shown in Table 3, the values of convenience, customisation, adventure/exploration, and gratification can predict Paylater users’ purchase intentions in Indonesia, especially convenience for utilitarian motivation and adventure/exploration for hedonic motivation, which had the most significant influence on purchase intention of all the values tested. These results strengthen the results of Study 1, which further demonstrate these values influence purchase intention. The values with the most significant influence – convenience and adventure/exploration – indicate that Indonesians who use Paylater during e-commerce interactions are pleased overall with the convenience of shopping using Paylater, and the process of shopping on e-commerce platforms with Paylater is fun and exciting.

General discussion

The results of Study 1 and Study 2 indicated that Indonesian Paylater users aged from 18 to 40 years have values influencing their motivation to shop. The demographic data in Tables 1 and 3 show the participants were mostly women. In consumption practices, females consume more products and messages conveyed in advertisements than males (de Waal Malefyt & McCabe, 2020). There may be more female participants than male because of the increase in the fashion sector in e-commerce (Financial Conduct Authority, 2021) and home needs such as baby products, furniture and household appliances (Good Shepherd, 2022). The results show both utilitarian and hedonic motivations directly influence purchase intention, with hedonic motivation having more significance than utilitarian motivation. These findings differ from those of To et al. (2007) and Kumar and Sadarangani (2021), who stated utilitarian motivation during online shopping has a more significant influence than hedonic motivation. Hedonic motivation has more influence, indicating Paylater users enjoy shopping on e-commerce because it’s fun and feels like an adventure; they can easily ask other Paylater users about products, trends are easy to follow, it relieves stress, and they feel in control of the shopping process. This result is similar to that of Ozen and Kodaz (2012), who found that Turkish shoppers had higher hedonic values than utilitarian ones. Hedonic motivations that customers have can positively influence purchase intention because they are driven by the urge to get a sense of happiness, fantasy, enjoyment, and entertainment (Santo & Marques, 2022). Utilitarian motivation also has a significant influence on purchase intentions because using Paylater on e-commerce is practical (To et al., 2007) and has other advantages, such as discounts obtainable only by using Paylater and e-commerce or being able to shop anywhere and at any time, as explained by Kumar and Sadarangani (2021).

All six utilitarian values had a significant positive influence on utilitarian motivation. Utilitarian value is a key factor for analysing consumer behaviour in the shopping process, specifically before buying goods or services in e-commerce (Evelina et al., 2020). This result differs from previous research in an Internet shopping context (To et al., 2007), which found all but two (lack of sociality and customisation) utilitarian motivation values had a significant effect. To et al. (2007) found that the lack of sociality was insignificant because Internet shoppers in Taiwan were not concerned about interacting with salespeople during the shopping process and did not find customisation to be a helpful function. However, the present research found customisation to be one of the values strongly influencing utilitarian motivation. This result may be due to increasingly advanced technology, and more products on e-commerce can be customised using the desired payment tenure. Meanwhile, the results of this research show the positive significance of the value lack of sociality, which is supported by previous research in the context of online shopping (Ozen & Kodaz, 2012), and customisation, which is also supported by previous research in the context of Generation Y in India and green apparel (Kumar & Sadarangani, 2021; Kumar & Yadav, 2021). This result may be due to customers in Indonesia feeling that using Paylater and e-commerce allows them to shop and sort products without needing to deal directly with salespeople. Moreover, increasingly advanced technology allows more product customisation using e-commerce platforms and the desired tenure may be obtained using Paylater. Furthermore, the results of both studies indicate convenience is the value with the highest influence after customisation. This result may be due to the ease of using Paylater and e-commerce and to people not needing to leave their homes to shop, a shopping habit that may have been carried over from the recent pandemic when people were forced to stay home.

As for utilitarian motivation, all hedonic values demonstrated a significantly positive influence. Previous studies support this result, stating that adventure/exploration and authority and status influence hedonic motivation in both offline and online shopping (Kumar & Sadarangani, 2021; To et al., 2007). The results of this study and previous research indicated that by shopping online, offline or with e-commerce platforms using Paylater, customers experience unique sensations. As stated by Arnold and Reynolds (2003), shopping is not just about purchasing products but also about enjoying the overall shopping experience, which offers many things for exploration. Additionally, when using Paylater, customers feel they have power or control over the product purchased or the shopping process.

The significant effect of sociality value on hedonic motivation differs from To et al. (2007), which showed an insignificant impact. Previous research supports this result (Ozen & Kodaz, 2012; Yu et al., 2018). The results from this study, Ozen and Kodaz’s (2012), and Yu et al. (2018) show that online shoppers in Turkey, China and Indonesia have similar experiences in the shopping process. However, sociality has a significant influence in Indonesia, possibly due to Paylater users sharing the Paylater function or sharing their experiences using Paylater or the Facebook platform that offers many forums whose members are Paylater users.

The idea shopping value was found to influence hedonic motivation. This result differs from previous research (Ozen & Kodaz, 2012; To et al., 2007). This may be because Paylater is a “trend” in Indonesia, and shoppers use it to buy popular products on e-commerce platforms. Gratification also significantly positively influenced hedonic motivation (Evelina et al., 2020). This finding may be due to people who use Paylater feeling satisfied while shopping and receiving their products in parcels. This possibility is strengthened by the comments of one of the pre-studies FGD participants (FGDs; see Section Hypotheses), who described this experience and felt that shopping could improve their mood. Several respondents from Arnold and Reynolds (2003) had the same opinion.

Conclusion and implications

This research has both theoretical and practical implications. Theoretically, this research enriches the research on shopping motivations and purchase intention with a new context, namely Paylater users in Indonesia, and fills the research gap. The research results explain that both utilitarian and hedonic motivations influence the purchase intentions of Indonesian Paylater users on e-commerce, although hedonic motivation has a more significant influence. All hedonic values affect hedonic motivation, with adventure/exploration being the most significant value. This means Indonesian shoppers mostly find shopping with e-commerce using Paylater something fun and adventurous. Besides this, Indonesian shoppers’ hedonic values may also feel that shopping with e-commerce using Paylater can improve their mood. They can socialise by telling stories about their shopping experiences, follow existing trends, and feel they have control over what they do.

All utilitarian values also affect utilitarian motivation, with convenience being the most significant value. This result indicates Indonesian Paylater users mostly feel shopping via e-commerce using Paylater is easier for them. Moreover, using Paylater on e-commerce also allows users to customise products and tenure, offers many product and tenure choices, is informative, allows them to shop without interacting with salespeople unless necessary, and can save them money.

Practically, this research aims to make Paylater users in e-commerce more aware of advertisements that may trigger irresponsible shopping. Paylater users could limit themselves by shopping according to their needs and abilities. Understanding shopping motivation can also help marketers or regulators formulate policies to limit or promote more responsible use of Paylater according to everyone’s abilities to have a healthier economy.

Limitations and future directions

This research focused on the utilitarian and hedonic motivations of e-commerce Paylater users in Indonesia. Even though both studies were conducted to a high standard, several shortcomings must be considered for further research. This research did not use hedonic motivation values such as satisfaction, value and role because the online shopping process, such as on e-commerce platforms, is less able to describe the stimulation of offline shopping and physical social interactions, such as bargaining (To et al., 2007). To et al. (2007) also stated value was insignificant for hedonic motivation. In Study 2, randomisation was conducted only for items in the purchase intention scale. In contrast, no randomisation was applied to the order of stimuli received by participants because the Zoho Survey platform does not offer this feature. Lack of randomisation is the biggest drawback of quasi-experiments, and researchers must be more aware of possible bias in this context (Schweizer et al., 2016). If future research is conducted online, another survey platform is recommended to facilitate randomisation. Study 1 considered the model acceptable but needed to be a better fit. If the same model is used in future research, a sample size of 500 is recommended to optimise the CFI and TLI fit results (Shi et al., 2019). Because of time constraints, in Study 2, we included only the two values with the highest influence for each motivation. In future research, richer data would be obtained if other utilitarian and hedonic values were added to the experiment to allow a deeper exploration. Finally, the relationship between Paylater users and their personality traits would be interesting to examine.

The lived experience of presenteeism among emergency nurses: a qualitative study
Authors: Hailong Hou, Yan Jiang, Chunling Xia, Xianjuan Cheng, He Li, Heng Dai, Shiqi Xiao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo describe and analyze the intrinsic experiences of emergency nurses regarding presenteeism, providing a basis for addressing this issue.MethodsThis descriptive phenomenological study employed purposive sampling to select 18 emergency nurses from June to July 2024. Face-to-face, semi-structured interviews were conducted, and data were analyzed using Colaizzi’s seven-step method to identify and refine key themes.ResultsFour main themes emerged: (1) Insufficient and biased understanding of presenteeism among emergency nurses, (2) Multiple factors triggering presenteeism, (3) Multidimensional impacts of presenteeism, and (4) Coping strategies and interventions for presenteeism.ConclusionPresenteeism among emergency nurses is a complex, multidimensional issue with significant negative consequences. Nursing administrators should prioritize nurses’ health management, optimize workforce allocation, and provide psychological support and humanistic care to mitigate presenteeism.

Introduction

Presenteeism is a significant manifestation of impaired health productivity, referring to the behavior of employees persisting at work despite suboptimal physical or psychological conditions, thereby leading to reduced work efficiency and declined productivity [1]. Studies indicate that traditional sick leave accounts for only 3% of total productivity loss, while presenteeism has a more substantial impact on organizational effectiveness [2]. In the healthcare field, nurses’ presenteeism not only reduces nursing quality but also significantly increases the incidence of nursing errors, posing potential threats to patient safety [3]. This hidden productivity loss often exceeds direct medical expenditures, requiring heightened attention from healthcare administrators. Studies have shown that nurses’ presenteeism can lead to the increase of patient falls and medication errors. The economic loss of patient falls and medication errors caused by nurse absence was at least US $1346 per patient, and the total loss was US $2 billion per year [4]. Freeling et al. [5] summarized studies on nurses’ presenteeism in multiple countries from 2006 to 2018 and found that the incidence rate of nurses’ presenteeism ranged from 15.74% to 86.96%. As a special department in hospitals where critically ill patients are concentrated, the emergency department has a particularly prominent issue of nurses’ presenteeism. A study on Chinese emergency nurses [6] showed that the incidence rate of presenteeism among emergency department nurses was 58.4%, indicating a relatively high level of presenteeism. This high incidence is closely related to the high-load and high-pressure work characteristics of the emergency department. In various public health emergencies and disaster events, the emergency department plays the role of a forward position, further increasing the physical and mental burden on nurses [7, 8]. Studies have shown that during the COVID-19 pandemic, emergency department nurses experienced more severe physical and mental burdens due to issues such as shortage of medical equipment and work overload [9]. When large-scale global public health events like the COVID-19 pandemic occur, the response ability of emergency nurses is crucial, but relevant studies have indicated that nurses are relatively insufficient in this aspect [10]. These factors have greatly affected the physical and mental health of emergency nurses, thereby exacerbating the degree of their presenteeism. In addition, the complex and rapidly changing conditions of patients in the emergency department, as well as the relatively chaotic physical environment, also make emergency nurses face many ethical dilemmas in their work, which affects their work engagement and increases their psychological burden [11].

At present, research on nurses’ presenteeism mostly focuses on epidemiological investigations and analysis of influencing factors, while in-depth exploration of nurses’ subjective experiences is relatively insufficient. In existing literature, although quantitative studies can reveal objective data such as the incidence rate and influencing factors of presenteeism, they are difficult to capture the subjective feelings, psychological conflicts, and behavioral motivations of emergency nurses during the process of presenteeism. As a research method for in-depth exploration of individual subjective experiences, qualitative research can, through phenomenological analysis and in-depth interviews, restore the real situation of presenteeism experiences from the perspective of emergency nurses and analyze the complex psychological, social, and cultural factors behind it. This not only helps to comprehensively understand the formation mechanism of presenteeism but also provides theoretical support for the subsequent formulation of intervention strategies that are more in line with nurses’ actual needs and more targeted. Therefore, this study adopts qualitative research methods, through in-depth interviews and phenomenological analysis, aiming to reveal the real experiences of emergency nurses’ presenteeism, so as to provide reference for optimizing the management of emergency nursing human resources and improving the quality of emergency nursing.

Methods

Design

This study employed a descriptive phenomenological qualitative research method based on Husserl’s phenomenological philosophy [12]. Descriptive phenomenology is an inductive research approach used to describe the essential experiences of phenomena without any preconceived notions or judgments. To understand phenomenology, Husserl adopted and explained the concept of reductionism. Reductionism serves as a crucial component of descriptive phenomenology for capturing the meaning or essence of the studied phenomenon, comprising two key steps: bracketing and bringing back [13]. This method enables comprehensive description of participants’ experiences and perceptions of the phenomenon without requiring in-depth interpretation by researchers. Given the limited existing research on presenteeism among emergency nurses, the application of descriptive phenomenological qualitative methods is appropriate for preliminary exploration of emergency nurses’ experiences and perspectives. This study adopts post-positivism as its research paradigm [14]and adheres to the COREQ (Consolidated Criteria for Reporting Qualitative Research) guidelines for qualitative research reporting [15].

Participants and settings

From June to July 2024, nurses working in the Emergency Department of a tertiary grade A general hospital in Northeast China were selected as research participants. The maximum daily number of visits to the emergency department of this hospital is approximately 1,100. The emergency department has 183 beds and 188 nurses, including 76 males and 112 females. Inclusion criteria: ① Possession of a valid nursing license and completed registration; ② ≥1 year of work experience in the emergency department; ③ Good comprehension and expressive abilities, capable of articulating personal perspectives. Exclusion criteria: ① Non-local training nurses; ② Standardized training nurses.

Regarding the selection of study participants, this research employed purposive sampling based on the principle of maximum variation in variables [16]. The specific procedure was as follows: First, the researchers contacted emergency department administrators to explain the study protocol and obtained a list of emergency nurses meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria, thereby defining the potential participant pool. Second, demographic and professional characteristics of the listed nurses- including age, gender, years of experience, and professional title - were systematically categorized. Subsequently, considering the distribution of these variables, nurses were selected from different age groups, genders, experience levels, and professional title categories to ensure maximum variation across these key characteristics. The sampling process continued until newly collected interview data no longer yielded novel themes or information, indicating that data saturation had been achieved [17], which determined the final sample size. Throughout participant selection, strict ethical principles were observed: all prospective participants received detailed explanations regarding the study’s purpose, methodology, confidentiality measures, and other relevant information. Only after obtaining informed consent were nurses included in the study sample, thereby safeguarding participants’ rights and interests. Data collection

The interview time was determined through mutual discussion, and the interview location was chosen as the conference room of the emergency department in the hospital to ensure sufficient privacy and quietness. Before the start of the interview, adequate explanations were provided to the interviewees, including the purpose, process, and significance of the study, as well as their right to withdraw from the study at any time. A trusting relationship was established with the interviewees, and audio recording was conducted only after obtaining their informed consent. During the interview, appropriate interview skills were used for some viewpoints or answers of the interviewees, such as flexible responses, follow-up questions, and verification. Meanwhile, the interviewees’ non-verbal behaviors were observed and recorded. Each interview lasted approximately 30 min.

The interview outline is a crucial auxiliary tool in qualitative research. By reviewing domestic literature on nurses’ presenteeism and based on the theoretical model of presenteeism behavior, a preliminary draft of the pre-interview outline was formulated. Experts were consulted, and the preliminary draft of the interview outline was revised according to their suggestions to ensure that the interview questions were reasonably designed and could explore the true inner experiences of emergency nurses who had gone through presenteeism. The revised outline was used as the pre-interview outline, and 2 nurses were selected for pre-interviews. During the pre-interviews, emphasis was placed on observing whether the wording of the interview questions was easy for the interviewees to understand, whether the sequence of the questions was logical, and the duration required for the interviews. Based on the issues identified in the pre-interviews, the pre-interview outline was further revised and improved. After re-consulting experts, the formal interview outline was finalized. Data analysis

Within 24 h after the interview, two researchers (HOU & JIANG) who participated in the interview repeatedly listened to the audio recordings. Then, they independently transcribed the interview content verbatim and removed all identifiable personal information from the transcripts. The Colaizzi’s 7-step analysis method [18] was used to analyze the data, and NVivo 12.0 software was adopted for data management and analysis. The depth and breadth of understanding of the original data were enhanced by repeatedly reading the transcribed interview texts and field notes [19], which were then refined into sub-themes and themes. Data analysis was conducted by two researchers (HOU & JIANG). In case of disagreements, a third researcher (XIAO) was consulted to reach a consensus. Finally, the research team compared and discussed the sub-themes and themes until a consensus was reached. The specific analysis steps are shown in Table 3.

Trustworthiness

Lincoln and Guba [20] proposed four criteria to enhance the reliability of qualitative research: credibility, dependability, confirmability, and transferability, which were adopted to ensure the trustworthiness of this study. In accordance with these four principles, corresponding measures were implemented to improve the scientific quality of this research [21]. Detailed information is presented in Table 4.

In addition, The diverse backgrounds of our principal researchers played a pivotal role in ensuring the study’s progression and integrity within the descriptive phenomenological framework. HOU possessed systematic theoretical knowledge of qualitative research but had no prior emergency department work experience before commencing this study. This lack of prior experience ensured authentic “bracketing” [13] throughout the research process, facilitating an open and unbiased engagement with the data. This approach allowed the phenomenon to reveal itself authentically, free from the researcher’s preconceived notions. On the other hand, XIAO’s extensive nursing management experience provided rich contextual knowledge about nurse presenteeism, contributing to a deeper understanding of the complexities in emergency nursing management. This enhanced both the comprehension and interpretation of the study findings. The contrasting expertise among our research team members highlighted the dynamic interplay between theoretical knowledge and practical experience in qualitative research, particularly within the context of descriptive phenomenology.

Ethical considerations

This study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Shengjing Hospital of China Medical University [Ethics Approval No.2024PS1124K]. The research protocol underwent rigorous review to ensure compliance with the guidelines of the ethical review authority. Informed consent forms were obtained from all participants, ensuring that they fully understood the purpose, procedures, potential risks of the study, as well as their right to withdraw at any time. Anonymization was applied to the data related to the interviewees, who were identified by codes. During data transcription, any content that could identify the interviewees was removed. All data were stored with the first author and were not accessible to any individuals or organizations other than members of the research team. Through the above measures, the rights of the interviewees were fully respected and their privacy was protected.

Results

This study ultimately included 18 emergency department nurses, with participants numbered P1-P18 according to their interview sequence. Through comparison and integration of coded content using Colaizzi’s 7-step analysis method, and after collective discussion by the research team, the final results were formulated. A total of 4 main themes and 13 sub-themes were extracted. Inadequate understanding of presenteeism among emergency nurses

This theme reflects the cognitive gaps and misconceptions between emergency nurses’ perceptions and the actual concept of presenteeism. The “inadequate understanding” highlights discrepancies in nurses’ comprehension of presenteeism’s true meaning and practical impacts, manifested in three dimensions: conceptual understanding, self-experience evaluation, and awareness of consequences.

Partial and biased conceptual Understanding

Many emergency nurses held inaccurate views of presenteeism, mistakenly equating it with workplace loafing or personal affairs during work hours, rather than recognizing it as reduced productivity due to physical/psychological impairments while remaining physically present at work. This conceptual misunderstanding hindered accurate identification of presenteeism in themselves or colleagues, preventing appropriate interventions.

Neglect and misjudgment of personal experiences

When recalling their work history, participants often failed to recognize presenteeism episodes - whether due to family distractions, working while ill, or psychological stress. This reflected both conceptual misunderstanding and insufficient self-observation, leading to unaddressed presenteeism.

Limited awareness of negative impacts

Nurses underestimated presenteeism’s consequences. Personally, they focused only on task completion while overlooking long-term career impacts. Externally, they didn’t fully recognize how presenteeism burdened colleagues, compromised patient care quality/safety, and damaged unit morale.

Multiple factors triggering presenteeism

The unique nature of emergency department nursing work contributes to diverse and complex causes of presenteeism among emergency nurses. Based on interview analysis, these factors were categorized into four groups: physical/psychological health factors, excessive sense of responsibility and self-criticism, public health emergency demands, and understaffing and shared clinical accountability.

Physical/Psychological health factors

Consistent with presenteeism’s definition focusing on reduced productivity due to health impairments rather than intentional work avoidance, all 18 interviewed nurses reported presenteeism episodes caused by health issues:

Public health emergency demands

Public Health Emergencies (PHEs) like infectious outbreaks, natural disasters, and major accidents create acute healthcare system stresses. As frontline providers, emergency nurses described increased presenteeism during crises:

Multidimensional impacts of presenteeism

Presenteeism among emergency nurses leads to detrimental consequences that extend across multiple levels. These impacts not only exacerbate nurses’ physical and mental health deterioration and reduce job satisfaction, but also compromise emergency care quality and increase safety risks. Widespread presenteeism can destabilize organizational operations and hinder departmental development. Based on interview findings, we categorized these impacts into three levels: individual, organizational, and patient.

Individual health impacts

The Effort-Recovery Theory posits that employees require adequate time to recuperate physically and psychologically after expending personal resources to meet work demands. Without sufficient recovery opportunities, short-term strain may accumulate into chronic health issues. For emergency nurses, working while ill most directly worsens existing health conditions due to delayed rest and treatment.

Impact on departments and colleagues

Analysis of the interview results revealed that presenteeism has significant and multifaceted impacts on both departments and colleagues. Primarily, when individual nurses experience presenteeism, it substantially increases the workload burden on their colleagues, who must take on additional tasks to compensate. Furthermore, presenteeism negatively affects team collaboration and overall work efficiency. Frequent occurrences of presenteeism may lead to strained relationships and tense communication dynamics among team members, ultimately creating a negative impact on the departmental work atmosphere and nurses’ motivation.

Impact on patient care safety

Presenteeism among emergency nurses significantly compromises patient care safety. The phenomenon primarily affects patient safety through two mechanisms. First, presenteeism creates staffing shortages that force remaining nurses to assume excessive workloads, increasing their stress levels and work pressure. This not only reduces nursing efficiency but also impairs concentration and reaction times, thereby elevating the risk of medical errors. Second, emergency departments rely heavily on effective teamwork to manage critical situations and urgent medical needs. When presenteeism disrupts team coordination, it may delay essential treatments and nursing interventions, ultimately affecting patient recovery outcomes.

P4: When presenteeism occurs, our work performance is definitely not the same as usual. Problems inevitably arise, creating genuine risks and potential safety hazards in patient care.P11: It absolutely affects patients. You can’t fully focus on your work - for instance, during medication verification, even a single oversight due to distraction can create hazards and lead to errors.P15: Emergency nursing requires methodical completion of tasks with carefully planned staffing. When one nurse experiences presenteeism, four full positions effectively become three and a half. The remaining staff face increased pressure, but it’s unrealistic to expect everyone to maintain 150% effort constantly. Consequently, the probability of patient safety incidents rises significantly above normal levels.

Strategies to reduce presenteeism among emergency nurses

As frontline healthcare providers, emergency nurses possess direct experience with and unique perspectives on presenteeism. Interviewing them for recommendations on reducing presenteeism holds significant value for improving nurse health, strengthening unit management, and enhancing care quality. Analysis of interview data suggests that preventing and minimizing presenteeism requires concerted efforts from both nurses themselves and external support systems.

Emphasizing personal health management

Personal health status significantly impacts quality of life and professional performance. Given the high physical and psychological demands of emergency nursing, nurses should prioritize their health through regular check-ups and timely attention to physical/mental discomfort to fundamentally reduce presenteeism.

Strengthening workforce allocation and management for emergency nurses

Human resources are the cornerstone of healthcare systems. The interview results demonstrate that enhancing workforce allocation and management in emergency nursing is crucial for reducing presenteeism and improving care quality. First, implementing reasonable shift scheduling can alleviate nurses’ workload and achieve work-life balance. Second, improving emergency backup systems ensures continuity of nursing care during unexpected situations. Finally, valuing nurses’ input and promptly adjusting management measures can enhance job satisfaction and motivation. Rational utilization of human resources enables high-quality emergency nursing while reducing presenteeism, allowing nurses who are unwell to get proper rest and recovery.

Providing psychological support and humanistic care

The definition of illness in the context of presenteeism includes psychologically unhealthy states. Therefore, it is essential to regularly monitor the mental health status of emergency nurses and provide scientifically validated psychological interventions and counseling, such as psychological consultations and stress management training. These measures can help emergency nurses cope with high-pressure work environments, alleviate negative psychological states, and significantly reduce the incidence of presenteeism among emergency nurses. Furthermore, the humanistic care atmosphere in hospitals and departments serves as a crucial support system for staff mental health. A positive humanistic environment helps enhance employees’ sense of belonging and professional value.

Discussion

This qualitative study explored the lived experiences of presenteeism among emergency nurses, revealing its complex and multidimensional nature. The findings indicate significant gaps and misconceptions in nurses’ understanding of presenteeism, with some equating it merely with workplace laziness or unauthorized absences, rather than recognizing its core feature of reduced productivity due to physical or psychological impairment. This lack of awareness may be related to the professional culture and working environment of nurses [22]. Mohammadi et al. [23] similarly noted that presenteeism is frequently misunderstood, with nurses focusing on superficial behaviors while overlooking health and efficiency implications. This cognitive bias reflects the profound impact of professional culture and work environment on individual cognition. In the high-intensity and high-risk workplace of emergency departments, “persisting at work despite illness” has gradually been shaped into a symbol of professional spirit. The nursing group, imperceptibly, internalizes physical and psychological wear and tear as an inevitable cost of professional dedication. The long-term influence of such value orientation has led to the true connotation of presenteeism—namely, the dual wear and tear of health and productivity—being overshadowed by “professional responsibility.” Therefore, enhancing emergency nurses’ cognitive level of presenteeism and helping them accurately identify and respond to presenteeism behaviors are key to reducing the occurrence of presenteeism.

The study identified diverse triggers for presenteeism, including physical/mental illness, excessive sense of responsibility and self-criticism, public health emergencies, and understaffing and shared clinical accountability. Health impairments were the most common trigger, aligning with Rainbow et al.‘s [24] conceptual analysis that identifies compromised health as a prerequisite for presenteeism. ED nurses’ demanding work environments heighten their vulnerability to health deterioration. Personal character factors also play a significant role in presenteeism. This study reveals that a strong sense of professional responsibility is an internal driver of presenteeism among emergency nurses, which is consistent with the research conclusion of Bierla et al. [25]that employees with a high sense of responsibility are more prone to presenteeism. Responsibility describes individual differences in the tendency to exercise self-control, be responsible to others, make efforts, maintain order, and abide by rules [26]. Although a high level of professional responsibility is regarded as a professional virtue, in the special context of emergency departments, it has instead been alienated into an internal driver of presenteeism. This exposes the potential conflict between individual personality traits and the expectations of professional roles. When a sense of responsibility evolves into excessive self-demand, it not only fails to improve work efficiency but also becomes a factor that impairs professional effectiveness. Furthermore, public health emergencies have further exacerbated the phenomenon of presenteeism among emergency nurses. During public health emergencies, due to strained human resources and a surge in the number of patients, emergency nurses worldwide have been forced to work while ill, leading to a significant increase in the incidence of presenteeism among medical staff [7]. The shortage of human resources is also a significant triggering factor for presenteeism. As the frontline of the hospital, the emergency department imposes much higher work intensity and pressure on nurses than other departments; for instance, insufficient human resources will further increase the workload of nurses. The research results of Monzani et al. [27]also indicate that organizational teams significantly influence nurses’ presenteeism behaviors, and organizations affected by presenteeism will face a decline in productivity and innovation capacity.

Presenteeism has significant negative impacts on emergency nurses themselves, their departments, and patients. At the individual level, presenteeism further deteriorates nurses’ physical and mental health. Nurses who are in a state of presenteeism for a long time are prone to job burnout and chronic health problems. This is consistent with the research results of McGregor et al., which indicate that the occurrence of presenteeism will affect an individual’s physical activity ability and function. For nurses, this will reduce the efficiency and accuracy of nursing work [28]. From the perspective of career development, such dual loss of physical and mental exhaustion and sense of professional value is likely to form a vicious circle, weaken the stability of the nursing team, and have an adverse impact on the long-term development of the nursing industry. At the departmental level, presenteeism increases the workload of colleagues, affects teamwork and work efficiency, significantly raises the work pressure of the nursing team, and reduces overall work efficiency. At the patient level, presenteeism leads to a decline in the quality of nursing services, increases the risks of nursing errors and patient safety; moreover, the occurrence of presenteeism makes nurses neglect humanistic care for patients, leaving nursing work lacking the due care and warmth [29], which in turn leads to a decrease in patients’ satisfaction with nursing services and affects the hospital’s reputation and patients’ trust [30]. This phenomenon also reflects the insufficient attention to the dimension of humanistic care in the current medical service quality evaluation system, which urgently needs to be improved and perfected.

In response to the multiple impacts of presenteeism, this study proposes three aspects of coping strategies: focusing on personal health management, strengthening the allocation and management of emergency nurses’ human resources, and providing psychological counseling and humanistic care. Firstly, emergency nurses should conduct regular health check-ups and pay timely attention to their physical and mental states to fundamentally reduce the occurrence of presenteeism. Secondly, hospital managers should rationally allocate the human resources of emergency nurses, optimize the scheduling system, and ensure that nurses can get timely rest when they are unwell. Studies by Mosteiro-Díaz et al. [31] have shown that a reasonable scheduling system and sufficient human resources are key to reducing presenteeism. In addition, hospitals should improve the emergency backup system to ensure the continuity of nursing work in emergency situations. Finally, nursing managers should provide psychological counseling and humanistic care to help emergency nurses cope with the high-pressure working environment, alleviate adverse psychological states, create a good working atmosphere, and enhance nurses’ sense of belonging and professional value. These strategies do not exist in isolation but are interrelated and synergistic. Personal health management is the foundation, human resource allocation is the guarantee, and psychological counseling and humanistic care are the improvement. Through the organic combination of the three, it is expected to break the vicious circle of presenteeism and promote the development of emergency nursing work in a healthier and more sustainable direction.

Conclusion

This phenomenological study systematically uncovered emergency nurses’ experiences of presenteeism and its multifaceted impacts. Findings underscore the need to enhance nurses’ awareness, prioritize health management, and reform organizational practices. Future research should evaluate long-term effects and interventions to inform evidence-based ED management.

Strengths and limitations

This study has three key strengths. First, it adopts descriptive phenomenology to explore emergency nurses’ presenteeism experiences, capturing subjective aspects overlooked in quantitative studies. Second, it rigorously follows COREQ guidelines and applies Colaizzi’s 7-step analysis to ensure methodological rigor. Third, focusing on EDs’ high-risk environment, it identifies multidimensional presenteeism factors, offering targeted management insights.

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the findings may have limited generalizability as the research was conducted exclusively in Northeast China, where unique healthcare and economic conditions exist. Second, while measures like anonymization and bracketing were implemented, potential biases including social desirability bias in self-reports and recall bias among nurses unfamiliar with presenteeism could not be completely eliminated. Finally, the qualitative design precluded quantification of presenteeism frequency, leaving its temporal pattern (whether chronic, situational, or episodic) undetermined - a gap future studies could address through longitudinal tracking or work-log methods.

Future research should explore nurse presenteeism through four key directions: (1) multicenter, cross-cultural studies to compare regional, healthcare system, and cultural variations; (2) longitudinal designs to track long-term impacts and developmental patterns; (3) evaluation of organizational interventions (e.g., flexible scheduling, mental health support) and individual strategies (e.g., health self-management); and (4) applications of emerging technologies (e.g., AI, wearables) for monitoring and prevention. These approaches will facilitate comprehensive theoretical frameworks and evidence-based management strategies.

Relevance to clinical practice

This study explores the real experiences of presenteeism among emergency nurses and its multiple impacts, providing important guidance for clinical practice. Hospital administrators should reduce the occurrence of presenteeism by enhancing nurses’ awareness of presenteeism, paying attention to nurses’ health, optimizing human resource allocation, providing psychological counseling, and creating a favorable working atmosphere. Specific measures include strengthening health education, arranging shifts reasonably, setting up psychological counseling services, and enhancing team cohesion. These measures are conducive to improving the physical and mental health of nurses, enhancing the quality of nursing services, ensuring patient safety, and promoting the sustainable development of emergency nursing. In future clinical practice, more efforts should be made to explore the intervention measures for presenteeism and their effectiveness, so as to provide more scientific evidence for emergency nursing management.

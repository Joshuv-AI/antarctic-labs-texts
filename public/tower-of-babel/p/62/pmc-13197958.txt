Aryl hydrocarbon receptor ligand 6-formylindolo[3,2-b]carbazole attenuates pressure overload cardiac hypertrophy by activating the CYP1A1/19(S)-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid (HETE) and suppressing midchain-HETEs/G protein coupled receptor 31 pathways
Authors: Ahmad H. Alammari, Shereen M. Hamza, Fadumo Ahmed Isse, Keshav Gopal, John R. Ussher, Ayman O.S. El-Kadi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pressure overload induced cardiac hypertrophy is a major contributor to heart failure, and the arachidonic acid (AA) metabolism through cytochrome P450 enzymes is one of the metabolic pathways implicated in the hypertrophic response. The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) regulates CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 that generate protective 19-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic (HETE) and hypertrophic midchain-HETEs eicosanoids, respectively. The endogenous AhR ligand 6-formylindolo[3,2-b]carbazole (FICZ) is a potent and selective inducer of CYP1A1, but its role in pressure overload induced cardiac hypertrophy has not been examined. This study investigated whether daily AhR activation by FICZ alters AA metabolites and attenuates cardiac hypertrophy in the abdominal aortic constriction (AAC) model. Male Sprague-Dawley rats underwent AAC or sham surgery and received FICZ (0.2 mg/kg per day) for 5 weeks. Echocardiography was performed at baseline and 5 weeks post-AAC, and gene, protein, and midchain-HETEs levels were assessed by real-time polymerase chain reaction, western blot, and liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry, respectively. FICZ significantly reduced AAC-induced increases in left ventricular mass, ventricular wall thickness, heart weight-to-tibial length ratio, and hypertrophic gene expression. FICZ produced selective induction of CYP1A1 and significant rise in cardiac 19(S)-HETE. AAC significantly increased CYP1B1 and 12-LOX protein expressions and midchain-HETEs, whereas FICZ significantly attenuated 12-LOX and midchain-HETE. AAC also upregulated G protein coupled receptor 31, and FICZ reduced this increase at both mRNA and protein levels. This study provides the first evidence that endogenously generated 19(S)-HETE is cardioprotective in a pressure overload model and identifies FICZ as a modulator of the CYP1A1/19(S)-HETE that suppresses midchain-HETEs/G protein coupled receptor 31 pathways.Significant StatementThis study demonstrates that 6-formylindolo[3,2-b]carbazole protect against cardiac hypertrophy induced by abdominal aortic constriction in rats. The significance of this research lies in its novel discovery, which elucidates for the first time the involvement of G protein coupled receptor 31 and the induction of CYP1A1 and 19-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid and suppressing midchain-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid/G protein coupled receptor 31 pathways by 6-formylindolo[3,2-b]carbazole in the protection against pressure overload cardiac hypertrophy in rats.

Introduction

Cardiac hypertrophy represents an adaptive enlargement of the myocardium in response to increased workload; however, persistent stress triggers maladaptive remodeling, a starting event in the pathogenesis of heart failure.1 Epidemiological data indicate that heart failure affects approximately 750,000 Canadians and 6.7 million adults in the United States, with a projected rise globally to over 64 million individuals.2,3 Despite current pharmacological interventions, many patients continue to progress toward advanced heart failure, underling the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies that target the molecular mechanisms driving cardiac hypertrophy. The abdominal aortic constriction (AAC) model serves as a reliable experimental approach that replicates the molecular and hemodynamic features of human pressure overload induced cardiac hypertrophy.4,5

The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR), a ligand activated transcription factor, has gained recognition as a key regulator of cardiovascular physiology and pathology.6 AhR controls the expression of several cytochrome P450 (P450) enzymes, particularly the CYP1A family, which metabolizes a wide range of endogenous lipids and environmental chemicals.7 Among its endogenous activators, 6-formylindolo[3,2-b]carbazole (FICZ) is of particular interest due to its potent and diverse biological actions.8 Recent evidence indicates that AhR ligand, FICZ, exerts cardioprotective effect through modulation of arachidonic acid (AA) metabolism.9 AA, a polyunsaturated fatty acid, is converted by P450 enzymes into multiple bioactive eicosanoids that influence cardiac hypertrophy.10,11 Notably, CYP1A1 generates the subterminal metabolite 19-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid (HETE), recently identified as cardioprotective.12

Animal studies have highlighted AA metabolites as critical modulators of pressure overload induced cardiac hypertrophy.13,14 AA is metabolized by P450 enzymes into HETEs, which exhibit different effects on cardiac structure and function.15 CYP1A1 and CYP1B1 are particularly important, generating subterminal 19-HETE and midchain-HETEs, respectively.16 Subterminal 19-HETE, the predominant subterminal HETE in the heart, antagonizes the vasoconstrictor 20-HETE, attenuates cardiotoxic midchain-HETEs signaling, and has been associated with protection in models of angiotensin II-induced hypertrophy and acute coronary syndromes.17, 18, 19, 20 In contrast, midchain-HETEs such as 5-, 12-, and 15-HETE, predominantly formed via CYP1B1, promote oxidative stress, inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction, and have been consistently elevated in models of cardiac hypertrophy.21 In addition to CYP1A1 mediated formation of the cardioprotective 19-HETE, several cardiac P450 isoforms, notably members of CYP2J and CYP2C, metabolize AA to epoxyeicosatrienoic acids, which exert antihypertrophic, anti-inflammatory, and vasodilatory actions.22 Evidence from extrahepatic tissues indicates that AhR activation can modulate the expression of multiple P450 isoforms beyond CYP1A1, raising the possibility that FICZ may be pleiotropic via induction of multiple P450s.23,24

G protein coupled receptors (GPCRs) play essential roles in cardiac physiology and pathological remodeling, where their activation can influence cardiac hypertrophy signaling pathways.25 The orphan G protein coupled receptor 31 (GPR31) was identified as a specific high affinity receptor for the AA metabolite 12(S)-HETE, linking this orphan GPCR to prohypertrophic and proinflammatory signaling in the heart.26 Our recent findings indicate that the AhR ligand FICZ exerts cardioprotective effects against cardiac hypertrophy by modulating AA metabolism in vitro, shifting the balance toward protective metabolite 19-HETE and away from midchain-HETEs such as 12-HETE.9 However, it remains unknown whether FICZ confers similar protection in vivo under pressure overload, such as in AAC model, and whether GPR31 contributes to the hypertrophic response or FICZ’s potential protective effect.

Materials and methods

Materials

AA, FICZ, NADPH, and TRIzol reagent were purchased from Invitrogen. High-capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit and SYBR Green Master Mix were purchased from Applied Biosystems. Real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) primers were synthesized by Integrated DNA Technologies Incorporation according to previously published sequences. Protease inhibitor cocktail, bovine serum albumin (BSA) and sterile-filtered DMSO were purchased from MilliporeSigma. Trans-Blot Turbo RTA Transfer Kit and 2X Laemmli Sample Buffer were purchased from Bio-Rad Laboratories. CYP1A1 (sc-25304), CYP1A2 (sc-53241), 5-LOX (sc-136195), 12-LOX (sc-365194), 15-LOX (sc-133085) mouse monoclonal and GAPDH (ab8245), GPR31 (ab-75579), and CYP1B1 (PA5-28040) rabbit polyclonal were purchased from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Inc., Abcam Limited, and Invitrogen (Thermo Fisher Scientific), respectively. Anti-mouse IgG horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-linked secondary antibody (7076) and anti-rabbit IgG HRP-linked secondary antibody (7074) were purchased from Cell Signaling Technology. Chemiluminescent western blotting detection reagents (enhanced chemiluminescence) were obtained from Cytiva. AA metabolite standards (±) 5, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, and 20-HETE and internal standards 20-HETE-D6 were obtained from Cayman Chemical. Isopropyl alcohol, ethyl acetate, and glacial acetic acid were purchased from Sigma Aldrich. Acetonitrile, methanol, magnesium chloride hexahydrate, potassium phosphate dibasic, and potassium phosphate monobasic were purchased from MilliporeSigma. High-performance liquid chromatography water was obtained from Fisher Scientific Co.

Animals

The experimental protocol for this study was reviewed and approved by the University Animal Policy and Welfare Committee at the University of Alberta. All experimental procedures involving animals were conducted and reported in accordance with the Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments guidelines and complied with the principles outlined in the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals published by the US National Institutes of Health. Male Sprague-Dawley rats (age 7 weeks) were obtained from Charles River Laboratories. The animals were maintained under standard laboratory conditions, housed in cages within a temperature and humidity controlled environment, and kept on a 12-hour light/dark cycle. All rats had unrestricted access to standard chow and water throughout the study period and kept in the animal facility for an acclimatization period of minimum 1 week.

Experimental design and treatment protocol

Male Sprague-Dawley rats were randomly divided into 4 experimental groups. To induce cardiac hypertrophy, animals underwent either sham surgery (n = 14) or AAC (n = 16). FICZ was administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 0.2 mg/kg per day, whereas normal saline contained 3.2% DMSO to match the vehicle composition used for FICZ preparation. The first group (n = 8) served as the sham control, receiving daily intraperitoneal injections of normal saline. The second group (n = 8) included AAC rats that received intraperitoneal injections of normal saline. The third group (n = 8) comprised AAC rats treated with FICZ for 5 weeks. The fourth group (n = 6) consisted of sham-operated rats treated with FICZ for 5 consecutive weeks.

AAC surgery

Baseline echocardiographic imaging was performed on all rats prior to the initiation of the experimental procedures. Subsequently, animals were anesthetized with isoflurane (3% for induction and 1%–1.5% for maintenance). The abdominal area was shaved, cleansed with chlorhexidine soap, and sterilized using povidone iodine (betadine) solution. A midline incision (∼3–4 cm) was made below the xiphoid process to expose the abdominal cavity.

The abdominal aorta was carefully isolated from the inferior vena cava just superior to the renal arteries. A 21 gauge blunt needle was placed parallel to the aorta, and a 6-0 silk suture was tied around both the vessel and needle. The needle was then withdrawn, producing a partial constriction of the aorta, thereby inducing pressure overload. Following ligation, visceral organs were repositioned, the abdominal wall was closed with absorbable sutures, and the skin incision was sealed with additional absorbable stitches. Sham operated rats underwent identical surgical exposure without ligation of the aorta.

Postoperatively, rats received daily intraperitoneal injections of either normal saline (3.2% DMSO) or FICZ (0.2 mg/kg) for 5 consecutive weeks, according to group assignment. At the end of the 5-week period, echocardiographic assessment was repeated under isoflurane anesthesia (3% induction, 1%–1.5% maintenance). After imaging, animals were euthanized under anesthesia, and hearts were rapidly excised, rinsed in ice-cold saline, and blotted dry. The left ventricle (LV) was dissected, homogenized using a Branson tissue homogenizer (VWR Scientific), and processed for the intended experiments.

Echocardiographic evaluation of cardiac function

All animals were anesthetized with isoflurane, after which transthoracic M-mode echocardiography was performed using a Vevo 3100 high-resolution ultrasound imaging system (VisualSonics) to assess cardiac structure and function. Image acquisition and analysis were conducted with Vevo LAB software (version 5.9.0). Comprehensive echocardiographic parameters were obtained, including systolic and diastolic wall dimensions; left ventricular internal diameter, left ventricular anterior and posterior wall thickness, and interventricular septal thickness, along with LV mass, heart rate, tissue Doppler indices, and measures of systolic and diastolic performance such as ejection fraction (%EF) and fractional shortening (%FS). These parameters were derived from M-mode recordings captured in the parasternal short-axis view at the mid-papillary level, with ventricular dimensions assessed during both systole and diastole. Additionally, pressure gradients (mm Hg) were determined from mitral inflow velocities using the velocity-time integral method. All measurements were averaged over 3–6 consecutive cardiac cycles in accordance with American Society of Echocardiography recommendations and digitally archived for subsequent analysis.

RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis

Total RNA extraction was performed by homogenizing 0.2 g of tissue in 0.6 mL TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen), followed by phase separation with chloroform and centrifugation at 12,000g for 15 minutes at 4 °C. The aqueous phase containing RNA was collected, and RNA was precipitated with isopropyl alcohol at –20 °C for 2 hours. After another centrifugation step, the RNA pellet was washed with 75% ethanol, recentrifuged, air-dried for 10 minutes, and finally dissolved in nuclease-free water. Samples were then incubated at 55–60 °C for 10 minutes to ensure complete resuspension of RNA. RNA concentration was quantified by measuring absorbance at 260 nm, whereas purity was assessed using the 260/280 ratio, with values greater than 1.8 indicating acceptable quality. Subsequently, first-strand cDNA was synthesized using the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems) according to the manufacturer’s guidelines. For each reaction, 1.25 μg of total RNA was combined with the following reagents: 2 μL of 10× RT buffer, 2 μL of 10× random primers, 0.8 μL of 25× dNTP mix (100 mM), 1 μL of MultiScribe Reverse Transcriptase (50 U/μL), and 4.2 μL of nuclease-free water to reach the final volume. The reverse transcription reaction was carried out under the following thermal conditions: 25 °C for 10 minutes (primer annealing), 37 °C for 120 minutes (cDNA synthesis), 85 °C for 5 seconds (enzyme inactivation), and finally cooled to 4 °C for storage.

Quantification of mRNA expression by real-time PCR

Quantitative real-time PCR was conducted using a QuantStudio system (Applied Biosystems) equipped with 384-well optical plates to assess gene expression levels. Each 20 μL reaction mixture consisted of 0.04 μL of 10 μM forward primer, 0.04 μL of 10 μM reverse primer (yielding a final concentration of 20 nM for each), 10 μL of SYBR Green Universal Master Mix, 8.92 μL of nuclease-free water, and 1 μL of cDNA template. The thermal cycling conditions were as follows: an initial enzyme activation step at 95 °C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 amplification cycles comprising denaturation at 95 °C for 15 seconds and combined annealing/extension at 60 °C for 60 seconds. GAPDH was used as the housekeeping gene for normalization. Primer sequences for rat genes are listed in Table 1. Preparation of microsomal proteins

Microsomal protein fractions were isolated from cardiac tissue through differential ultracentrifugation of homogenized samples. Briefly, heart tissues were homogenized in ice-cold 0.25 M sucrose solution at a ratio of 1 g tissue per 5 mL buffer. The resulting homogenate was first centrifuged at 10,000g for 20 minutes to remove cell debris and nuclei. The supernatant was then subjected to ultracentrifugation at 100,000g for 60 minutes to obtain the microsomal and cytosolic fractions. The resulting microsomal pellet was resuspended in cold sucrose solution and stored at –80°C until further use. Protein concentrations of the microsomal preparations were determined using the Lowry assay, and BSA used as the calibration standard.28

Western blot analysis

Proteins isolated from each experimental group were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and western blotting. Specifically, 60 μg of microsomal protein or 37 μg of cytosolic protein were mixed 1:1 with 2× Laemmli sample buffer, heated at 95 °C for 5 minutes, and then resolved on a 10% SDS-PAGE gel. After electrophoretic separation (120 V for 2 hours), proteins were transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride membranes. Membranes were blocked overnight at 4 °C in Tris-buffered saline containing 5% skim milk, 2% BSA, and 0.5% Tween-20. The blots were subsequently incubated with the appropriate primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C, followed by a 1-hour incubation with HRP-conjugated anti-mouse or anti-rabbit secondary antibodies at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using the enhanced chemiluminescence detection method and the ChemiDoc Imaging System (Bio-Rad Laboratories). Band intensities were normalized to GAPDH as a loading control and quantified using Image Lab software, version 6.1 (Bio-Rad Laboratories).

Microsomal incubation with AA

Heart microsomal fractions (1 mg protein/mL) were incubated in a reaction buffer composed of 5 mM magnesium chloride hexahydrate in 0.1 M potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) at 37 °C in a shaking water bath (50 rpm). After a 5-minute pre-equilibration period, the reaction was initiated by adding 1 mM NADPH. AA was then added to final concentration of 50 μM, and the mixture was incubated for 30 minutes. The enzymatic reaction was terminated by adding 600 μL of ice-cold acetonitrile, followed by the internal standard 20-HETE-D6. AA metabolites were subsequently extracted using a single liquid-liquid extraction with ethyl acetate, and the resulting organic phase was evaporated to dryness using a Savant SpeedVac SPD130DLX before further analysis. Extracted AA metabolites were analyzed using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (Shimadzu LCMS-8050). Chromatographic peak integration was carried out using LabSolutions software version 5.91.

Apparatus and chromatographic conditions

A validated liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry method was used for the separation and quantification of AA hydroxy-metabolites, as previously described, including the enantiomeric (R/S) forms of 5-, 8-, 9-, 11-, 12-, 15-, 16-, 17-, 18-, and 19-HETE, as well as 20-HETE (nonenantioselective), and the internal standard 20-HETE-D6.29 The extracted AA metabolites were reconstituted in acetonitrile and then the separation was achieved using a gradient elution system consisting of an organic mobile phase composed of acetonitrile, methanol, and isopropyl alcohol (88:6:6, v/v) containing 0.1% acetic acid, and an aqueous mobile phase of water with 0.1% acetic acid. The enantiomeric resolution of metabolites was performed on a REFLEC C-AMYLOSE A chiral stationary phase column (5 μm, 250 × 4.6 mm; Regis Technologies Inc.). Ten pairs of chiral HETE enantiomers were targeted for separation using chiral chromatography, corresponding to the (R/S) forms of 5-, 8-, 9-, 11-, 12-, 15-, 16-, 17-, 18-, and 19-HETE. Among these, only 19(R)-HETE was not detected. The chemical structures of these metabolites are shown in Fig. 1. The mass spectrometer was operated in negative electrospray ionization mode. Statistical analysis

All data are expressed as mean ± SD. Statistical comparisons among groups were conducted using one-way analysis of variance, followed by the Student-Newman-Keuls post hoc test to identify significant differences between individual treatment groups and the control group. A P value < .05 was considered indicative of statistical significance. All statistical analyses were performed using SigmaPlot software (Systat Software, Inc).

Effect of FICZ on cardiac function and AAC-induced left ventricular hypertrophy in rats

To evaluate the potential cardioprotective effect of FICZ against AAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy in rats, echocardiographic measurements of systolic and diastolic function, as well as ventricular wall dimensions, were performed in vivo. The echocardiographic analysis revealed no significant differences among groups in heart rate or functional parameters, including ejection fraction (EF%) and fractional shortening (%FS), indicating preserved systolic and diastolic function. Cardiac hypertrophy induced by AAC was confirmed by a marked increase in LV mass and LV mass corrected, along with significant thickening of the left ventricular anterior and posterior walls during both systole and diastole. Discussion

FICZ is a high-affinity endogenous ligand that activates AhR with high potency and selectivity.30 Upon binding, AhR undergoes nuclear translocation and drives transcription of target genes, most prominently CYP1A1, a key metabolic enzyme widely used as a sensitive marker of AhR pathway activation.31 Notably, FICZ induces CYP1A1 expression at extremely low concentrations, and is itself rapidly metabolized by CYP1A1, creating a self-limiting feedback loop that tightly modulates AhR signaling.32 In addition, our recent work in AC16 cardiomyocytes demonstrated that FICZ does not trigger the typical ligand induced degradation of AhR. Instead, it maintains AhR protein levels by inhibiting 20S proteasome activity.9 Emerging evidence indicates that AhR activation can attenuate pathological hypertrophy, suppress inflammatory signaling, and preserve myocardial and vascular function using similar or higher dose.33, 34, 35 Importantly, its endogenous origin and predictable metabolism offer advantages over other AhR ligands, which raise concerns about their toxicity.36 Together, these properties establish FICZ as a highly selective and physiologically compatible modulator of AhR, capable of inducing CYP1A1. In this study, we evaluated whether daily FICZ administration can modulate AA P450-mediated metabolites and ultimately protect against cardiac hypertrophy induced by AAC.

AAC rat model, which typically contributes to a slower progression, is widely recognized as a robust and translationally relevant preclinical model for studying pressure overload induced cardiac hypertrophy.37 Implementing a 5-week constriction period is particularly important, as experimental evidence indicates that the characteristic structural features of hypertrophy such as increases in LV mass and enlargement of the LV wall reach a reproducible and physiologically stable state during the 5-week postligation with no significant impact on both systolic and diastolic functions, aligning with the early stages of clinical hypertrophy in humans.4,38,39 Serial transthoracic echocardiography remains the gold standard for noninvasive assessment in this model.40

The echocardiographic assessment in this study demonstrates that AAC animals developed left ventricular hypertrophy, reflected by increased ventricular mass and enlarged LV wall, yet without a statistically significant decline in either systolic or diastolic function relative to sham controls.13,14,41,42 Notably, FICZ treated AAC rats exhibited a significant reduction of hypertrophic changes, with reduced ventricular mass and smaller LV wall compared with untreated AAC, indicating that FICZ effectively modulates pressure overload induced cardiac hypertrophy. Importantly, heart rate, and other key hemodynamic parameters, except for a higher cardiac output in AAC group which is expected in a compensated hypertrophic phase, remained comparable among all experimental groups, suggesting that the observed structural improvements were not secondary to generalized cardiac changes.43

Treatment with FICZ exerts a clear cardioprotective effect against pressure overload induced hypertrophy, as evidenced by its ability to attenuate the increase in HW/TL ratio, an accepted index of cardiac enlargement in experimental settings.44 In AAC model, pressure overload markedly elevates HW/TL and induces robust upregulation of hypertrophic genes, including β/α-MHC, ANP, BNP, and ACTA1.14,41 Importantly, these genes are not passive indicators but active mediators of hypertrophic changes, β/α-MHC changes, are associated with altered sarcomeric composition and a lesser contractile efficiency, ANP and BNP participate in prohypertrophic and stress responsive signaling, and ACTA1 contributes to cytoskeletal reorganization, collectively driving progression of the hypertrophy.45, 46, 47 Consistent with this, our previous in vitro work showed that FICZ suppresses the expression of these hypertrophic markers and reduces cardiomyocyte surface area, directly linking AhR activation to inhibition of hypertrophic transcriptional events.9

An interesting finding in this study is that FICZ treatment significantly reduced the percentage of body weight gained in rats over the treatment period, even though early adult rats were selected to minimize the rapid growth phase typically seen in younger rats. This reduction in weight gain may have favorable implications for cardiac physiology, as increased body mass is known to intensify metabolic demand and hemodynamic load, thereby accelerating pathological remodeling and hypertrophic progression.48 This outcome aligns with prior evidence indicating that FICZ and/or AhR pathway activation can limit the rate of weight gain, mitigate fat accumulation, and promote insulin sensitivity, collectively contributing to a more cardioprotective metabolic profile.49, 50, 51 Thus, by simultaneously suppressing hypertrophic signaling and mitigating weight gain, FICZ may provide dual benefit in models of cardiac stress, an effect worthy of further exploration.

P450 and LOX enzymes play central roles in AA metabolism, producing diverse eicosanoid mediators that influence cardiovascular physiology and cardiac hypertrophy. Multiple P450 isoforms, including those regulated by AhR, such as CYP1A1, CYP1A2, and CYP1B1, as well as CYP2C, CYP2J, CYP2E1, and the CYP4A/4F families, oxidize AA through distinct pathways.11,52 Among the AhR regulated P450, CYP1A1 is a major catalyst of AA subterminal hydroxylation, producing 19-HETE, a metabolite associated with vasodilatory, anti-inflammatory, and antihypertrophic actions.17 In contrast, CYP1B1 and LOXs preferentially generate midchain-HETEs such as 5-, 12-, and 15-HETE, which are linked to inflammatory and hypertrophic signaling.21 The CYP2C and CYP2J families synthesize epoxyeicosatrienoic acids, well-characterized mediators known for potent vasoprotective, endothelial, and antihypertrophic effects.22 Additionally, CYP2E1, which can also yield 19-HETE, and the CYP4A/4F isoforms, which produce terminal 20-HETE and subterminal HETEs that are associated with increased vascular resistance and pathological hypertrophy.17

FICZ activates AhR-driven transcription with a preference toward CYP1A1 induction.17,21 Consistent with this selectivity, our findings demonstrate a robust upregulation of CYP1A1 at both the mRNA and protein levels following FICZ treatment, whereas CYP1A2 expression was modestly increased in comparison and CYP1B1 showed no significant change. This pattern highlights the isoform-specific sensitivity of these genes to FICZ and reflects the role of posttranscriptional and posttranslational regulation of CYP1B1 within cardiac tissue.53,54 We also observed that AAC alone significantly induced CYP1B1 and 12-LOX protein expressions, aligning with prior report that pressure overload induces CYP1B1 and 12-LOX enzymes.42 The increased CYP1B1 protein despite unchanged mRNA in the AAC group likely reflects posttranscriptional regulation, where inflammatory and oxidative stress-related pathways in AAC enhance CYP1B1 protein translation and/or stability, for example via reduced proteasomal degradation, leading to protein accumulation without detectable changes at the transcriptional level.55,56,57 Strikingly, FICZ attenuated the AAC induced increase in 12-LOX. The marked induction of CYP1A1 suggests a metabolic shift favoring 19-HETE formation over midchain-HETEs, thereby promoting a more cardioprotective AA metabolites effect.

Evidence from different cardiac hypertrophy models demonstrates that 19-HETE is cardioprotective; both racemic and 19(S)-HETE attenuate angiotensin II induced cardiac hypertrophy, suppress hypertrophic markers, and reduce cardiomyocyte enlargement.12,19 In a cellular hypertrophy model, FICZ-mediated CYP1A1 induction increases 19(S)-HETE production, and 19(S)-HETE limits angiotensin II induced hypertrophy by lowering midchain-HETEs levels, inhibiting CYP1B1 catalytic activity and downregulating LOX enzymes and proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and IL-8.9,12 Building on these findings, the present AAC study provides the first evidence that endogenously generated 19(S)-HETE confers cardioprotection in a chronic pressure overload model, where increased 19(S)-HETE formation is associated with attenuated cardiac hypertrophy. The protective effect appears to involve suppression of midchain-HETEs enantiomers (5(S)-, 12(R/S)-, and 15(S)-HETEs), which are repeatedly implicated in cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, fibrosis, and adverse remodeling through activation of mitogen-activated protein kinase (extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1 and 2) and nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells signaling, all classic kinase-dependent pathways downstream of GPCR signaling, alongside vasoconstrictive, proinflammatory, and profibrotic actions.21,42

The midchain-HETEs effects are linked to GPCR, with GPR31 identified as a high-affinity receptor for 12(S)-HETE that couples predominantly to Gi and modulates calcium and kinase dependent pathways in platelets, endothelial cells, and other cardiovascular cells.26,58, 59, 60 Although GPR31 is already recognized for its role in thrombosis and vascular inflammation,61 this study, to our knowledge, is the first to implicate GPR31 in midchain-HETEs mediated cardiac hypertrophy in the AAC model. Our results show that AAC-induced GPR31 upregulation was significantly reduced by FICZ at both mRNA and protein levels. Overall, FICZ induced 19(S)-HETE elevation appears to counter midchain-HETEs signaling, at least partly through modulation of GPR31 dependent pathways, thereby contributing to the observed antihypertrophic effect.

Conclusion

Daily FICZ administration selectively induced CYP1A1, and shifted AA metabolism toward cardioprotective 19(S)-HETE while suppressing AAC induced midchain-HETEs and 12-LOX activity. These changes, together with reduced GPR31 signaling in the heart, attenuated cardiac hypertrophy and preserved cardiac function. Overall, the findings identify FICZ as a modulator of AhR/AA/P450 pathways capable of limiting pressure overload induced cardiac remodeling in vivo.

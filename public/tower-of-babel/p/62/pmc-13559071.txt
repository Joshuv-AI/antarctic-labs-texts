A taurine metabolism-related gene program defines clinical and biological features of neuroblastoma and yields an 8-gene candidate prognostic model
Authors: Benfu Zhong, Yanan Zhang, Zhaosong Wang, Qiuping Dong, Xin Li, Yuren Xia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTaurine metabolism-related transcriptional programs have not been systematically characterized in neuroblastoma. We aimed to investigate the clinical relevance and biological features of a taurine metabolism-related gene program in neuroblastoma and to determine whether this candidate space could yield a prognostically informative gene signature.MethodsA taurine-related gene program was quantified using single-sample gene set enrichment analysis in the discovery cohort. Its associations with clinicopathologic variables, survival, and hallmark pathways were evaluated, followed by gene-level prognostic screening and construction of a compressed candidate prognostic model. Model performance was assessed in the discovery cohort and externally evaluated in independent validation datasets. Integrative prioritization was then performed to identify core candidate genes, which were further examined using Jansky-based tumor-cell pseudobulk and patient-aware phase analyses.ResultsTaurine single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) score was significantly associated with Children’s Oncology Group (COG) risk group, MYCN status, International Neuroblastoma Staging System (INSS) stage, and overall survival in neuroblastoma. Higher taurine ssGSEA scores were generally observed in clinically more favorable subgroups and were associated with better overall survival in the discovery cohort. At the gene-set level, taurine score showed a strong positive correlation with hypoxia, but negative correlations with E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, MYC targets, and DNA repair, indicating a hypoxia/stress-associated biological context. Gene-level screening identified representative prognostic taurine-related candidates, from which an 8-gene candidate prognostic model was derived. In the discovery cohort, this model showed strong survival stratification and time-dependent discrimination, with 1-, 3-, and 5-year area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) values of 0.917, 0.906, and 0.913, respectively, and improved concordance when combined with clinical variables. External evaluation showed the strongest support in GSE181559, where Kaplan-Meier stratification was significant and AUCs were 0.701, 0.769, and 0.761 at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively, whereas additional cohorts suggested inter-cohort heterogeneity and limited fixed-cutoff transferability. Biologically, the 8-gene score was positively associated with proliferative and metabolically active programs, including E2F, G2M, MYC, DNA repair, and glycolysis, but negatively associated with hypoxia and stromal/inflammatory programs. Integrative prioritization and patient-aware analyses further highlighted ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 as four core candidate genes linked to a tumor-intrinsic proliferative component.ConclusionsA taurine metabolism-related transcriptional program is clinically relevant in neuroblastoma and, at the gene-set level, is associated with a hypoxia/stress-oriented biological background. In the discovery cohort, higher taurine ssGSEA scores tended to occur in clinically more favorable subgroups and were associated with better overall survival. From this candidate space, we derived an 8-gene candidate model that preferentially captures a proliferative and metabolically active risk component and receives partial external support. Further integrative prioritization and patient-aware analyses identified ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 as core candidate genes for future functional validation.Keywords: Neuroblastoma, taurine metabolism, prognostic model, pseudobulk, single-cell-informed analysis

What is known and what is new?

• Metabolic reprogramming is increasingly recognized as an important component of neuroblastoma biology, but taurine metabolism-related transcriptional features have not been systematically evaluated in this disease.

• In this study, the broader taurine-related program was associated with a hypoxia/stress-oriented biological context, whereas the compressed 8-gene score preferentially captured a proliferative and metabolically active risk component.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• These findings support taurine metabolism-related candidate genes as a biologically meaningful source for prognostic modeling and candidate discovery in neuroblastoma. The prioritized genes ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 provide a focused framework for future mechanistic and functional validation.

Introduction

Neuroblastoma is the most common extracranial solid tumor of childhood and is characterized by remarkable clinical and biological heterogeneity (1,2). While some tumors undergo spontaneous regression or respond favorably to treatment, high-risk neuroblastoma remains associated with frequent relapse and unsatisfactory long-term survival despite multimodal therapy (3). Current risk stratification systems, including clinical stage, age at diagnosis, histopathologic features, and MYCN amplification, have substantially improved patient classification, yet considerable outcome variation still exists within clinically defined subgroups (4,5). Therefore, there remains a need to identify biologically meaningful molecular programs that may improve risk assessment and provide insight into disease mechanisms.

Metabolic reprogramming is increasingly recognized as a central feature of tumor progression, therapeutic resistance, and microenvironmental adaptation (6). In neuroblastoma, multiple metabolic pathways have been implicated in tumor aggressiveness, including alterations related to mitochondrial function, redox homeostasis, amino acid utilization, and oncogene-driven biosynthetic demand. However, compared with pathways such as glycolysis and glutamine metabolism, taurine metabolism-related transcriptional features have received limited attention in neuroblastoma. Taurine is a sulfur-containing amino acid derivative involved in osmoregulation, oxidative stress buffering, redox homeostasis, and mitochondrial homeostasis (7,8). These biological properties suggest that taurine-associated transcriptional programs may be relevant to neuroblastoma behavior, particularly in the context of tumor stress responses and metabolic heterogeneity.

Although individual taurine-related genes may have been reported in other biological settings, the overall transcriptional program associated with taurine metabolism has not been systematically evaluated in neuroblastoma. In particular, it remains unclear whether taurine metabolism-related genes define a clinically relevant molecular pattern, whether such a pattern reflects specific biological states, and whether this candidate space can be leveraged to derive a prognostically informative model. Addressing these questions may help clarify whether taurine-associated biology represents only a background metabolic signature or instead captures disease-relevant tumor states with translational potential.

In the present study, we systematically investigated a taurine metabolism-related gene program in neuroblastoma using transcriptomic and integrative bioinformatics analyses. We first quantified a taurine-related single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) score and assessed its associations with clinicopathologic variables and patient survival. We then characterized its biological context through pathway correlation analysis, performed gene-level screening to identify representative prognostic candidates, and constructed a compressed 8-gene candidate prognostic model. Because the value of such a model depends not only on predictive performance but also on biological interpretability, we further examined the biological features associated with the 8-gene score, prioritized core candidate genes, and used Jansky-based pseudobulk and patient-aware analyses to provide supportive tumor-cell-focused evidence.

Through this framework, we aimed to address three related questions: first, whether a taurine metabolism-related transcriptional program is clinically relevant in neuroblastoma; second, what biological state this program represents at both the gene-set and compressed-model levels; and third, whether this candidate space can yield a tractable set of core genes for future functional validation. By linking pathway-level characterization, candidate model construction, and downstream gene prioritization, this study sought to provide a coherent view of taurine metabolism-related biology in neuroblastoma and to identify candidate molecular targets for further investigation. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tp.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tp-2026-0426/rc).

Methods

Study design and overall workflow

This study was designed to systematically investigate the clinical relevance, biological features, and prognostic potential of a taurine metabolism-related transcriptional program in neuroblastoma. The analytical workflow consisted of four major steps. First, a taurine-related gene set was used to quantify a taurine ssGSEA score in the discovery cohort, followed by evaluation of its associations with clinicopathologic variables and survival. Second, pathway-level analyses were performed to characterize the biological context of the taurine-related program. Third, gene-level prognostic screening was conducted to identify representative taurine-related candidates, from which a compressed candidate prognostic model was derived and externally evaluated. Fourth, integrative prioritization and Jansky-based tumor-cell pseudobulk and patient-aware analyses were performed to refine a smaller set of core candidate genes for future functional validation.

Taurine metabolism-related gene set

The taurine metabolism-related candidate gene set used in this study was derived from the reference study provided as the conceptual template for this project. The corresponding supplementary gene list was imported and curated before downstream analysis. Genes were matched to each expression matrix by gene symbol, and only genes present in the corresponding dataset were retained for subsequent analyses.

Public datasets and preprocessing

Publicly available neuroblastoma transcriptomic and clinical datasets were used in this study. GSE62564 served as the discovery cohort for primary analyses and model construction. Independent datasets, including GSE181559 and fwr144, were used for external evaluation. Additional analyses were also explored in TARGET-NBL as a sensitivity assessment of model transferability across cohorts. Jansky neuroblastoma single-cell data were used for tumor-cell-focused pseudobulk and patient-aware analyses. For GSE62564, the expression matrix was imported from the preprocessed RNA-seq object and transformed as log(expression +1) before downstream analyses. GSE62564 was used as the primary discovery cohort for clinicopathologic association analysis, survival modeling, and pathway correlation analysis. The clinical annotation high_risk was interpreted as the Children’s Oncology Group (COG) high-risk versus non-high-risk grouping. Variables related to prior training/validation assignment in the original clinical file were not used in the present study. For GSE181559, fwr144, and TARGET-NBL, expression and clinical data were imported from previously curated cohort-specific objects. Clinical variables were harmonized whenever possible, including overall survival time, event status, stage-related variables, MYCN status, age at diagnosis, and risk-related annotations. Because clinical completeness differed across validation cohorts, multivariable analyses were performed using the interpretable variables available in each dataset. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Survival endpoints and clinical variable harmonization

Overall survival was used as the primary clinical endpoint throughout this study. Survival time was measured in days. Event status was harmonized across cohorts as 1 for death and 0 for censored observation. Clinical variables used in subgroup analyses or multivariable models included, when available, COG risk group, MYCN status, International Neuroblastoma Staging System (INSS) or equivalent stage-related variable, age at diagnosis, and sex.

Calculation of taurine ssGSEA score

Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis was used to quantify the activity of the taurine metabolism-related program in each tumor sample (9). The resulting taurine ssGSEA score was used as the primary program-level metric in the discovery cohort. Associations between taurine score and clinicopathologic variables, including COG risk group, MYCN status, and INSS stage, were examined using nonparametric group comparisons. For survival analyses, patients in the discovery cohort were dichotomized according to the median taurine ssGSEA score within that cohort unless otherwise specified.

Hallmark pathway analysis at the gene-set level

To characterize the biological context of the taurine-related program, we analyzed correlations between taurine ssGSEA score and representative Hallmark pathways. Hallmark gene sets were obtained from the Molecular Signatures Database and scored using gene set variation analysis (GSVA)-based methods (9,10). Spearman correlation analysis was then performed between taurine ssGSEA score and selected pathways, with particular attention to hypoxia, E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, MYC targets, and DNA repair. Correlation heatmaps and representative scatter plots were generated to summarize these pathway-level relationships.

Gene-level prognostic screening

To identify representative prognostic genes within the taurine-related candidate pool, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression was performed in the discovery cohort for each taurine-related gene using overall survival as the endpoint. Regression coefficients and P values were used to distinguish risk-associated and protective candidates. The overall distribution of prognostic signals was visualized using a volcano plot. A compact heatmap of representative prognostic genes was then generated using a curated subset of candidates, ordered by COG risk group and annotated by MYCN status and INSS stage.

Construction of the candidate prognostic model

Genes retained after prognostic screening were subjected to model compression to derive a smaller candidate prognostic signature. The final model was selected to balance interpretability and predictive performance, with preference given to a compact panel suitable for downstream validation. Cox regression coefficients from the discovery cohort were retained to calculate an 8-gene risk score for each patient. The risk score for each sample was calculated as the weighted linear combination of expression values and corresponding model coefficients. In the discovery cohort, patients were dichotomized into high- and low-risk groups according to the median risk score. In the external validation cohorts, the fixed cutoff derived from the discovery cohort was applied to evaluate Kaplan-Meier stratification. This fixed-cutoff strategy was used to assess the transferability of the model beyond the training dataset.

Multivariable analysis and incremental prognostic value

To examine whether the 8-gene score contributed prognostic information beyond conventional clinical factors, Cox regression analyses were performed in the discovery cohort and, when feasible, in external datasets with adequate clinical annotation. In the discovery cohort, age, COG risk group, MYCN status, stage-related variable, and sex were considered in combination with the 8-gene score. Because variable completeness differed across validation datasets, only the interpretable clinical covariates available in each cohort were included in external multivariable analyses. Incremental prognostic value was assessed using the concordance index. Three models were compared in the discovery cohort: a clinical-only model, the 8-gene score alone, and a combined clinical-plus-score model.

External evaluation

The candidate 8-gene model was externally evaluated in independent neuroblastoma datasets. For each validation cohort, expression values of the model genes were matched by gene symbol and used to calculate the risk score based on the coefficients fixed from the discovery cohort. Kaplan-Meier analysis was used to assess survival stratification under the fixed training cutoff. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was used to evaluate continuous discrimination in each cohort. Because the transferability of a fixed cutoff may be affected by cohort composition, event structure, and technical differences, external results were interpreted together with cohort-level heterogeneity.

Biological interpretation of the 8-gene score

To clarify the biological implications of the compressed 8-gene score, extended Hallmark pathway analyses were performed in the discovery cohort. Correlations were examined between the 8-gene risk score and proliferative, metabolic, hypoxic, stromal, inflammatory, and oxidative stress-related pathways. In addition to E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, MYC targets, DNA repair, and hypoxia, the extended analysis included glycolysis, angiogenesis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), inflammatory response, TNFA signaling via NFKB, reactive oxygen species pathway, and oxidative phosphorylation. Spearman correlation coefficients were summarized in heatmaps and representative scatter plots.

Candidate gene prioritization

To refine the final candidate space beyond the 8-gene model itself, an integrative prioritization framework was applied. Evidence considered in this framework included presence in the 8-gene model, univariate Cox significance, association with key clinical groupings, and correlation with biologically relevant pathway signatures. Candidate genes were ranked according to a composite evidence scheme, and the highest-ranking genes were retained for additional pseudobulk and patient-aware analyses. On the basis of this integrated approach, ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 were prioritized as four core candidate genes.

Jansky dataset and tumor-cell-focused analyses

To further assess the tumor-intrinsic relevance of the prioritized genes, additional analyses were performed using the Jansky neuroblastoma single-cell dataset (11). Tumor cells were identified according to the available cell-type annotation and, when applicable, copy number variation (CNV)-related filtering. A Core4 score was then calculated using the normalized expression of ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 within the corresponding tumor-cell subset.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed in R. Continuous variables between two groups were generally compared using nonparametric tests, while multiple-group comparisons were performed using appropriate nonparametric approaches. Survival analyses were conducted using Kaplan-Meier curves with log-rank tests and Cox proportional hazards regression. Time-dependent ROC curves were used to evaluate discriminatory performance over time. Associations between continuous variables and pathway scores were assessed using Spearman correlation. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant unless otherwise stated.

Results

Taurine metabolism-related gene program was associated with clinicopathologic features and survival in neuroblastoma

To evaluate the clinical relevance of the taurine metabolism-related transcriptional program in neuroblastoma, we first calculated a taurine ssGSEA score in the discovery cohort and assessed its associations with major clinicopathologic variables. Taurine ssGSEA scores differed significantly between COG risk groups (Figure 1A). Significant differences were also observed according to MYCN status (Figure 1B) and INSS stage (Figure 1C), indicating that taurine-related transcriptional activity was associated with established indicators of disease heterogeneity rather than being randomly distributed across tumors. In the discovery cohort, relatively higher taurine scores were generally observed in clinically more favorable subgroups. We next explored the prognostic relevance of this program. Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that patients stratified by taurine score had significantly different overall survival probabilities (Figure 1D), with higher taurine score associated with better overall survival. Taken together, these findings suggest that the taurine-related transcriptional program is clinically relevant in neuroblastoma and is associated with clinicopathologic variation and overall survival.

Taurine-related gene program exhibited a hypoxia/stress-associated biological pattern

To further characterize the biological context of the taurine-related program, we analyzed correlations between taurine ssGSEA score and representative hallmark pathways. At the pathway level, taurine score showed a strong positive correlation with hypoxia, whereas negative correlations were observed with E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, MYC targets, and DNA repair (Figure 2A). Representative scatter plots further illustrated these relationships. Taurine score was positively correlated with HALLMARK_HYPOXIA (Figure 2B), but negatively correlated with HALLMARK_E2F_TARGETS (Figure 2C) and HALLMARK_MYC_TARGETS_V1 (Figure 2D). These data indicate that the broader taurine metabolism-related program is preferentially linked to a hypoxia/stress-associated biological context rather than to a proliferative transcriptional state.

Gene-level screening identified representative prognostic taurine-related candidate genes

After establishing the clinical and pathway-level relevance of the taurine-related program, we next performed gene-level screening to identify prognostically informative genes within this candidate pool. Univariate Cox regression analysis identified a subset of taurine-related genes associated with overall survival, including both risk-associated and protective candidates (Figure 3A). The volcano plot suggested that prognostic signals were distributed across multiple genes within the taurine-related candidate space rather than being limited to isolated single-gene effects. To visualize their expression behavior in a clinically relevant context, we generated a compact heatmap of representative prognostic taurine-related genes across tumors ordered by COG risk group (Figure 3B). Several candidate genes, including genes that were subsequently incorporated into the 8-gene model, showed directional expression differences between non-high-risk and high-risk tumors. For example, AURKA, ADO, SCNN1B, and SLC16A1 tended to show relatively higher expression in high-risk tumors, whereas MMP9, GABBR1, and ATXN1 showed the opposite pattern. These findings supported the existence of a biologically structured candidate pool for downstream model construction.

Construction of an 8-gene candidate prognostic model in the discovery cohort

Based on the screened taurine-related candidate genes, we next derived a compressed 8-gene candidate prognostic model. The final model consisted of ADO, AURKA, SCNN1B, GCLC, TJP1, ALPL, MMP9, and CCND1, including both risk-associated and protective components (Figure 4A). Among these genes, ADO and AURKA contributed relatively large positive coefficients, whereas MMP9 and CCND1 showed negative coefficients within the model framework. In the discovery cohort, the 8-gene model showed clear prognostic discrimination. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated significant survival separation between the low- and high-risk groups (Figure 4B). Time-dependent ROC analysis further supported strong predictive performance, with AUCs of 0.917, 0.906, and 0.913 at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively (Figure 4C). To further assess incremental value, we compared concordance indices across different models. The clinical-only model yielded a C-index of 0.856, the 8-gene score alone yielded 0.872, and the combined clinical-plus-score model yielded 0.895 (Figure 4D), suggesting that the 8-gene score provided additional prognostic information beyond conventional clinical variables in the discovery cohort. These results support the 8-gene panel as a candidate prognostic model for subsequent evaluation.

External evaluation supported the 8-gene model in part, with evident inter-cohort heterogeneity

We next examined the external performance of the 8-gene model. In the GSE181559 validation cohort, Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated significant survival stratification between low-risk and high-risk groups (Figure 5A). Time-dependent ROC analysis yielded AUCs of 0.701, 0.769, and 0.761 at 1, 3, and 5 years, respectively (Figure 5B), indicating moderate-to-good discriminatory ability in this cohort. Among the evaluated external datasets, GSE181559 provided the clearest support for the 8-gene model. However, model performance was not fully consistent across cohorts. In the fwr144 cohort, the model retained moderate time-dependent discriminatory ability, but Kaplan-Meier stratification using the fixed training cutoff was unstable and did not yield a clearly separable two-group survival pattern. These findings indicate that the 8-gene model received partial external support, while also highlighting the influence of inter-cohort heterogeneity on model transferability.

The 8-gene score preferentially captured a proliferative and metabolically active risk component

To clarify the biological implications of the compressed 8-gene score, we evaluated its correlations with extended biological programs. The heatmap in Figure 6A showed that the 8-gene risk score was positively associated with proliferative pathways, including E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, MYC targets, and DNA repair, while negative associations were observed with hypoxia, angiogenesis, EMT, inflammatory response, and TNFA signaling via NFKB. Positive correlations were also observed with glycolysis, reactive oxygen species pathway, and oxidative phosphorylation (Figure 6A). Representative scatter plots further illustrated these relationships. The 8-gene risk score showed a strong positive correlation with HALLMARK_E2F_TARGETS (Figure 6B), a weak negative correlation with HALLMARK_HYPOXIA (Figure 6C), and a moderate positive correlation with HALLMARK_GLYCOLYSIS (Figure 6D). These results suggest that, unlike the broader taurine-related program shown in Figure 2, the compressed 8-gene score preferentially captures a prognostically dominant proliferative and metabolically active component within the original candidate pool. We next integrated prognostic evidence, clinical association, and biological relevance to prioritize genes within the 8-gene model. The priority score plot identified AURKA, ADO, MMP9, and CCND1 as the highest-ranking candidates (Figure 6E), while the integrated evidence heatmap showed that GCLC and TJP1 also retained supportive evidence across multiple dimensions (Figure 6F). These results provided the basis for further refinement toward a smaller set of core candidates for downstream validation.

To further explore the therapeutic context of the 8-gene risk model, we performed exploratory immune microenvironment, immunotherapy-relevant marker, and drug sensitivity prediction analyses. The 8-gene risk score was negatively correlated with CD8 T-cell, cytotoxic activity, dendritic cell, Treg, M1 macrophage, and immune checkpoint signatures, while showing a weak positive correlation with neutrophil-related signatures (Figure S1A). Consistently, most immune checkpoint and cytolytic markers, including CD274, CD8A, CTLA4, GNLY, GZMB, HAVCR2, NKG7, PDCD1, PDCD1LG2, PRF1, and TIGIT, were lower in the high-risk group, whereas LAG3 was modestly higher (Figure S1B). Drug sensitivity prediction further showed that the high-risk group had significantly lower predicted response values for cisplatin, vincristine, irinotecan, topotecan, alisertib, bortezomib, olaparib, and gemcitabine (Figure S1C). These findings suggest that the 8-gene high-risk group may be associated with an immune-low transcriptomic phenotype and distinct predicted therapeutic-response features.

Integrative pseudobulk and patient-aware analyses highlighted four core candidate genes

Based on the integrative prioritization framework, we focused on four core candidate genes—ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9—for additional evaluation using the Jansky neuroblastoma dataset. At the patient-level tumor-cell pseudobulk level, the Core4 score showed a moderate positive trend with S-phase activity (Figure 7A), suggesting a relationship between the prioritized gene set and proliferative activity. The heatmap of ADO, GCLC, MMP9, and AURKA across tumor-cell pseudobulk samples showed heterogeneous but partially coordinated expression patterns among patients (Figure 7B), indicating that these genes did not behave as isolated markers alone but instead formed a structured candidate program within tumor cells. To further examine the phase-related behavior of this program in a patient-aware manner, we summarized tumor-cell Core4 scores across G1, S, and G2M phases at the patient level. This analysis showed a tendency toward higher Core4 values in a G2M-like state (Figure 7C), which was broadly consistent with the proliferative hallmark correlations observed for the 8-gene score and with the exploratory single-cell mixed-model results. In addition, transcriptomic comparison across COG risk groups showed that ADO, AURKA, and GCLC were significantly higher in high-risk tumors, whereas MMP9 was lower in high-risk tumors; the composite Core4 score was also significantly higher in high-risk tumors (Figure S2). Taken together, these data support ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 as core candidate genes for subsequent functional validation.

Discussion

In the present study, we systematically characterized a taurine metabolism-related transcriptional program in neuroblastoma and found that it was associated with clinicopathologic features and survival. At the whole-program level, taurine ssGSEA score was associated with COG risk group, MYCN status, INSS stage, and overall survival, supporting the clinical relevance of this pathway-oriented signal in neuroblastoma. In the discovery cohort, relatively higher taurine scores were generally observed in clinically more favorable subgroups and were associated with better overall survival. These findings suggest that taurine metabolism-related transcriptional activity is not merely a background metabolic feature, but instead reflects biologically meaningful disease heterogeneity.

A central finding of this study is that the biological interpretation of the taurine-related program depended on the level of analysis. At the gene-set level, taurine score was positively associated with hypoxia and negatively associated with E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, MYC targets, and DNA repair, indicating that the broader taurine-related program was embedded in a hypoxia/stress-associated context. By contrast, after gene-level screening and model compression, the resulting 8-gene score displayed a substantially different biological profile, characterized by positive associations with E2F, G2M, MYC, DNA repair, glycolysis, reactive oxygen species pathway, and oxidative phosphorylation, together with negative associations with hypoxia and stromal/inflammatory programs. This shift is biologically plausible. It suggests that the original taurine-related candidate pool contains multiple layers of biological information, whereas model compression preferentially retains the component most tightly linked to poor prognosis, namely a proliferative and metabolically active risk state. Accordingly, the broader taurine-related program may reflect a hypoxia/stress-associated background, while the final 8-gene score captures the prognostically dominant aggressive component embedded within that background.

The pathway-level findings may also be interpreted in the clinical and biological context of neuroblastoma. At the gene-set level, the positive association between taurine ssGSEA score and hypoxia suggests that the broader taurine-related program may reflect a stress-adaptive metabolic background. Hypoxia and metabolic stress are common features of aggressive solid tumors and may support tumor-cell survival under unfavorable microenvironmental conditions. In contrast, the compressed 8-gene score was positively associated with E2F targets, G2M checkpoint, MYC targets, DNA repair, glycolysis, reactive oxygen species pathway, and oxidative phosphorylation. These programs are biologically relevant to high-risk neuroblastoma, particularly MYCN-driven disease, in which increased proliferative demand, replication stress, metabolic rewiring, and DNA damage repair capacity may contribute to tumor progression and treatment resistance. Therefore, our findings suggest that taurine-related metabolic disturbance may be linked to malignant neuroblastoma phenotypes through an integrated stress-proliferation axis, rather than through a single metabolic pathway alone. This interpretation may help explain how metabolic dysregulation is associated with prognosis, although direct validation in relapse models, chemotherapy-response datasets, and functional experiments is still required.

The 8-gene candidate model showed strong performance in the discovery cohort. It achieved clear Kaplan-Meier separation, high time-dependent AUCs, and improved concordance when combined with clinical variables, indicating that it contributed incremental prognostic information beyond baseline clinicopathologic factors. These findings support the feasibility of extracting a clinically relevant candidate signature from the taurine-related gene pool. At the same time, we deliberately retained a restrained interpretation and considered this panel a candidate prognostic model rather than a finalized clinical tool. This distinction is important because, while the model was robust in the discovery cohort, its external transferability was not fully uniform.

The external validation results highlight both the promise and the limitation of the current model. Among the independent datasets evaluated, GSE181559 provided the strongest support, showing significant survival stratification and moderate-to-good time-dependent discrimination. In contrast, the fwr144 cohort retained some continuous discriminatory ability but failed to produce stable fixed-cutoff Kaplan-Meier separation. These findings indicate that the 8-gene model has partial external support, but that its performance is influenced by inter-cohort heterogeneity. Such heterogeneity may reflect differences in cohort composition, event structure, platform-related variation, and the limited transferability of a fixed cutoff derived from the discovery cohort. Therefore, the present study supports the 8-gene panel as a biologically and prognostically informative candidate model, while also indicating that broader validation in more balanced and uniformly processed datasets will be required before stronger translational claims can be made.

Another strength of this study is that model development was not treated as the final endpoint. Instead, we used the 8-gene panel as a framework for candidate prioritization. By integrating prognostic signal, clinical association, and biological relevance, we refined the broader candidate pool and ultimately focused on ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 as four core candidates. In this context, the value of the 8-gene model lies not only in risk estimation, but also in its ability to serve as a prioritization scaffold that reduces a large taurine-related gene set into a smaller, more experimentally tractable group of candidate genes.

The Jansky pseudobulk and patient-aware analyses further strengthened the rationale for focusing on these four genes. At the patient-level tumor-cell pseudobulk level, the Core4 score showed a moderate positive trend with S-phase activity, and the four genes displayed heterogeneous but partially coordinated expression patterns across patients. More importantly, patient-aware phase summarization indicated a tendency toward higher Core4 values in a G2M-like state. These observations were broadly consistent with the hallmark-level findings for the 8-gene score and support the interpretation that the prioritized Core4 genes reflect a tumor-intrinsic proliferative component rather than a purely bulk-level artifact. At the same time, the magnitude of these phase differences was modest, which argues for a cautious interpretation. We therefore regard the Jansky analyses as supportive rather than definitive evidence for the biological relevance of the Core4 program.

Although ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 have distinct biological functions, we do not interpret them as members of a single linear taurine metabolism pathway. Instead, they may represent complementary modules selected from the taurine-related candidate space. ADO is most directly connected to the taurine/hypotaurine metabolic axis through cysteamine-to-hypotaurine conversion, thereby linking the Core4 set to taurine-associated metabolism. GCLC encodes the catalytic subunit of glutamate-cysteine ligase and may reflect glutathione synthesis, redox buffering, and metabolic stress adaptation. AURKA represents the proliferative component of the program, consistent with its role in cell-cycle progression and MYCN-associated neuroblastoma biology. MMP9 may contribute a microenvironmental component through extracellular matrix remodeling, invasion, angiogenesis, and tumor-microenvironment interactions. Thus, ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 may form a coordinated candidate framework linking taurine/hypotaurine-related metabolism, oxidative stress adaptation, tumor-cell proliferation, and microenvironmental remodeling. Importantly, this framework does not imply that the four genes directly and jointly mediate taurine metabolism disruption through a single causal pathway. Rather, the Core4 program may capture a coordinated high-risk tumor state involving metabolic adaptation, oxidative stress buffering, proliferative activity, and microenvironment remodeling. This proposed framework is hypothesis-generating and requires future mechanistic validation.

Exploratory therapeutic-context analyses suggested that the 8-gene high-risk group was characterized by reduced CD8 T-cell, cytotoxic activity, dendritic cell, and immune checkpoint-related signatures, consistent with an immune-low or immune-cold transcriptomic phenotype. Drug sensitivity prediction further indicated that high-risk tumors may show lower predicted response values for several DNA damage-, replication stress-, cell-cycle-, and stress-related agents, including cisplatin, vincristine, irinotecan, topotecan, alisertib, bortezomib, olaparib, and gemcitabine. These findings may provide additional therapeutic context for the 8-gene model. However, these analyses were based on bulk transcriptomic inference and pharmacogenomic prediction; therefore, they should be regarded as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than direct evidence for clinical treatment selection or immunotherapy response.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, although the 8-gene model performed strongly in the discovery cohort and received support in GSE181559, external validation was not fully consistent across cohorts, particularly under a fixed-cutoff transfer strategy. Second, the pseudobulk and patient-aware analyses were supportive but not exhaustive and relied mainly on one publicly available neuroblastoma dataset. Third, the present study remains computational and transcriptomic in nature, and the biological roles of the prioritized core genes in neuroblastoma proliferation, metabolic dysregulation, and therapeutic response still require direct validation using clinical specimens and functional experiments. Finally, because the broader taurine-related program and the compressed 8-gene score capture different levels of biological information, the mechanistic relationship between taurine metabolism itself and the final proliferative risk component requires further clarification.

In summary, this study demonstrates that a taurine metabolism-related transcriptional program is clinically relevant in neuroblastoma and is associated with a hypoxia/stress-oriented biological background at the gene-set level. In the discovery cohort, higher taurine ssGSEA scores tended to occur in clinically more favorable subgroups and were associated with better overall survival. From this candidate space, we derived an 8-gene model that preferentially captures a proliferative and metabolically active risk component and receives partial support in external validation. Further integrative prioritization and patient-aware analyses highlighted ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 as core candidate genes. These findings provide a coherent framework for future mechanistic studies and support subsequent functional validation of these prioritized genes in neuroblastoma.

Conclusions

A taurine metabolism-related transcriptional program is clinically relevant in neuroblastoma and, at the gene-set level, is associated with a hypoxia/stress-oriented biological background. In the discovery cohort, higher taurine ssGSEA scores tended to occur in clinically more favorable subgroups and were associated with better overall survival. From this candidate space, we derived an 8-gene candidate model that preferentially captured a proliferative and metabolically active risk component and received partial support in external validation cohorts. Further integrative prioritization and patient-aware analyses highlighted ADO, AURKA, GCLC, and MMP9 as core candidate genes, providing a practical framework for subsequent functional validation in neuroblastoma.

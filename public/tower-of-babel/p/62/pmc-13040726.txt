40-Hz sensory stimulation-induced gamma oscillations enhancing brain network connectivity to improve poststroke cognitive impairment: study protocol for a prospective randomized controlled trial in China
Authors: Wang Fu, Yongpeng Wang, Jiejie Shi, Qiwei Wang, Minghui Lai, Longyan Meng, Qianqian Bi, Yaowu Liu, Yuan Wang, Xiaoyu Tian, Chunlei Shan, Feng Wang, Cong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPoststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) has an incidence rate ranging from 24% to 80.97%. It reduces survival rates and increases disability among stroke patients, posing a substantial socioeconomic burden. Current treatments for PSCI primarily involve medication and rehabilitation; however, therapeutic efficacy remains limited, with unsatisfactory improvement in activities of daily living. This study aims to conduct a prospective clinical trial to evaluate the safety and efficacy of 40-Hz sensory stimulation-induced gamma oscillations in the treatment of PSCI, including assessment of neuropsychology, psychiatric symptoms, cerebral blood perfusion, and functional and structural brain changes, and to investigate the underlying mechanisms by RNA-sequencing of blood samples.MethodsThis trial is designed as a prospective, randomized controlled, double-blind, non-inferiority study conducted in the Seventh People’s Hospital of Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine in China. Participants with confirmed cognitive impairment occurring after the stroke event and persisting for 3 to 6 months will be screened and recruited by the attending neurologists for participation. A total of 120 enrolled patients will be randomly assigned to one of two groups at a ratio of 1:1. The experimental group will receive 40-Hz sensory stimulation, while the control group will receive sensory stimulation at random frequencies. Both groups will receive the same standard clinical drug therapy and rehabilitation treatment. The primary efficacy endpoints include cognitive function and learning ability. Secondary endpoints encompass changes in cerebral hemodynamics, neural network connectivity, synaptic-related gene expression, and neurotransmitter levels. Safety assessments will include monitoring of major adverse events, all-cause mortality, and photosensitive epilepsy. Evaluations will be conducted at baseline, after the 14-day treatment period, and during follow-up visits (at 3 and 6 months). Differences between the two groups will be compared.DiscussionThis study will assess the safety and efficacy of gamma oscillations induced by 40-Hz sensory stimulation in treating PSCI patients. In parallel, potential mechanisms—including alterations in synaptic-related genes and neural network connectivity—will be explored. This trial is expected to provide evidence supporting the effectiveness of this novel technique for treating PSCI and improving patients’ quality of life.Trial registrationChinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR) ChiCTR2400088535. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Stroke remains the second-leading cause of death all over the world, characterized by a high incidence, high fatality rate, significant disability rate, and a high rate of recurrence [1]. Poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) is defined as a clinical syndrome characterized by cognitive impairment that emerges after a stroke and persists for up to 6 months [2]. Recent large international cohort, including 13 studies based in 8 countries, has reported that 44% were impaired in global cognition and 30% to 35% were impaired in individual domains 2 to 6 months after the index event [3], and its survival rate (39%) is significantly lower than that of patients without cognitive impairment (75%) [4]. Furthermore, PSCI exacerbates disability in patients and is significantly correlated with the degree of hemiplegia in acute stroke patients. The 5-year follow-up of the Action on Secondary Prevention Interventions and Rehabilitation in Stroke (ASPIRE-S) conducted in Ireland showed that PSCI has become a major contributor to the burden of stroke, severely affecting patients’ daily lives while also increasing patient mortality and imposing greater familial and socioeconomic burdens [5].

The primary treatments for PSCI are pharmacological and rehabilitative. The goal of drug therapy is to delay the progression of cognitive impairment, improve neuropsychiatric symptoms, and enhance daily living abilities; however, there are currently no specific drugs for PSCI. Commonly used medications such as cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine can improve cognitive-related scores, but they show limited efficacy in improving activities of daily living (ADL) or modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores. Moreover, they are unsatisfactory in enhancing quality of life, reducing mortality, or alleviating family caregiving burdens [6]. In clinical practice, a combination of medication and rehabilitation is often employed to treat PSCI patients, thereby improving therapeutic outcomes. Current rehabilitation approaches mainly include traditional cognitive training, artificial intelligence, and neuromodulation techniques (including repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) and transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS)). However, the optimal target selection for rTMS and tDCS remains unclear, and the dose-response relationship regarding neural circuit activation and remodeling is not well-defined. Further research is needed to determine target selection and improve treatment efficacy [7]. Cognitive training requires substantial labor costs, and the application of artificial intelligence is still exploratory. Given that the treatment of PSCI is currently at a bottleneck, there is an urgent need to explore novel therapeutic approaches to enhance patients’ quality of life.

From an electrophysiological perspective, cortical activity in the brain is characterized by continuous electrical rhythmic activity (generated by synchronized neuronal populations and exhibiting periodicity). This spatiotemporal evolution of rhythmic electrical activity is referred to as neural oscillations, which are highly correlated with information transmission in the brain and represent a feature of brain states. Among these, gamma neural oscillations (30–100 Hz) play a key role in neuroplasticity, neural circuitry, and information processing [8]. Previous studies have repeatedly reported that inducing gamma oscillations using gamma-frequency band stimulation promotes the recovery of cognitive function in mouse models of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) [9, 10]. The mechanism involves promoting neural remodeling in multiple brain regions, including the hippocampus, enhancing brain network connectivity, improving learning capacity, and thereby ameliorating cognitive function [11]. Moreover, 40-Hz flickering light stimulation has been shown to induce stable and effective gamma oscillations [9]. The application of 40-Hz auditory stimulation in clinical research dates back to 2002, and subsequent studies have confirmed the safety, tolerability, and feasibility of gamma-frequency audiovisual stimulation in both AD patients and healthy individuals. Some studies have also indicated that this modulation method has immune and network effects [12, 13]. The team led by Tsai, including Martorell et al., demonstrated that using 40-Hz auditory stimulation to induce gamma oscillations improves cognitive function in AD mice [10]. The same team further explored the mechanism by which 40-Hz audiovisual stimulation-induced gamma oscillations improve cognition in AD, finding that it facilitates the clearance of pathogenic protein deposits in the cerebrospinal fluid [9]. He et al. conducted a feasibility trial of gamma sensory flicker therapy in patients with prodromal AD, confirming its safety, tolerability, and feasibility [12]. In our previous study, gamma oscillations induced by 40-Hz visual-auditory stimulation were proven to be effective for the treatment of acute-phase limb motor rehabilitation after stroke; however, its effectiveness in the treatment of PSCI remains unknown [14]. Apart from 40-Hz audiovisual stimulation, electroacupuncture stimulation (ES) has also been shown to correct abnormal brain activity by having the ability to alter the frequency, amplitude, and synchronization patterns of neural oscillations, thereby promoting brain function and aiding in neurological rehabilitation [15, 16]. Previous trials have proved that ES can effectively improve the motor function of the upper limbs in stroke patients [17]. These studies show encouraging results and suggest that 40-Hz sensory stimulation, including audiovisual and ES stimulation, should be further investigated in larger-scale studies to evaluate its potential for improving cognitive impairment. The pathogenesis of PSCI is not entirely identical to that of AD, and whether gamma oscillations can improve cognitive function in PSCI requires further exploration.

Objectives {7}

This study aims to conduct a prospective clinical trial in China to evaluate the safety and efficacy of 40-Hz sensory stimulation-induced gamma oscillations in the treatment of PSCI, including assessment of neuropsychology, psychiatric symptoms, cerebral blood perfusion, and functional and structural brain changes, and to investigate the underlying mechanisms by RNA-sequencing of blood samples.

Trial design {8}

This trial is a prospective, randomized-controlled, double-blind, non-inferiority study with pre-intervention (−t1), 2-week post-intervention retention (T2), and 3-month (T3) and 6-month (T4) post-intervention follow-up tests. The overall design is demonstrated in Fig. 1. Patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to each of the two treatment groups, with the experimental group receiving intervention through 40-Hz sensory stimulation, including audiovisual stimulation and ES, and the control group undergoing intervention with randomly matched sensory stimulation frequencies. A trained researcher, blinded to the group allocation, will collect the data which is shown in Table 1. The examiner will be blinded to group allocation, and the participants and the treating physiotherapist will be asked not to share any information about the intervention with the examiner. The design of this trial is in accordance with the standard protocol items. It has been approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Seventh People’s Hospital (approval number: 2024-7th-HIRB-048).

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

Informed consent will be obtained by an experienced neurologist and signed by both parties (the investigating physician and the patient). Potential participants will be given sufficient time to read the entire informed consent form, and the neurologist will provide a thorough explanation of its content. Written informed consent will be obtained from each participant or their legally authorized representative prior to any study procedures.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {26b}

Upon completion of the informed consent process, blood samples will be collected from participants via venipuncture of the antecubital vein. The entire blood collection procedure will be performed by a professional neurological nurse, with approximately 2 mL of blood drawn.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

Currently, conventional treatments for PSCI patients, including pharmacological therapy and standard rehabilitation, yield suboptimal outcomes. Previous studies have confirmed that 40-Hz sensory stimulation can reliably induce gamma oscillations. This study aims to investigate the therapeutic efficacy of gamma oscillations in PSCI as an adjunct to the aforementioned conventional treatments. Accordingly, the experimental group will receive 40-Hz sensory stimulation to elicit gamma oscillations, while the control group will receive random-frequency sensory stimulation that does not induce gamma oscillations. To monitor the induction of gamma oscillations during the intervention, electroencephalography (EEG) will be simultaneously recorded and analyzed to ensure that the experimental group exhibits induced gamma oscillations, whereas the control group does not.

Intervention description {11a}

All patients will receive treatment for 25 min per day, 7 days per week, over a period of 2 weeks (14 sessions). For both interventions, therapists will provide verbal instructions, cues, feedback, and assistance as needed. A light-emitting diode (LED) panel will be positioned approximately 50 cm away from the patient’s eyes. Patients will face the LED panel and wear Bluetooth headphones delivering 40-Hz audio stimuli.

Meanwhile, patients will be treated by an experienced acupuncturist with at least 2 years of clinical experience. The selected acupuncture points are Baihui (GV20) and Shenting (GV24). Prior to treatment, the skin at the acupoints will be disinfected with 75% ethanol. The acupuncturist will stabilize the patient’s head with one hand and insert the needle (Hwato, disposable, 0.35 × 40 mm; Suzhou Hwato Medical Devices, Suzhou, China) with the other. The insertion angle will be approximately 10–20° relative to the scalp, and the depth will be 0.3–0.5 cun (1 cun = 3.33 cm). After insertion, the needle will be gently manipulated for 1 min until “Deqi” (a sensation of soreness, numbness, or distension) is achieved. An electrode will then be attached to the needle body. The acupuncturist will set the stimulation parameters: the gamma group will receive electrical stimulation at 40 Hz [18]. The electroacupuncture device (Hwato, SDZ-III, Suzhou, China) will be placed behind the patient to ensure blinding to the stimulation frequency.

In this study, an Arduino-based control system will be used to regulate the LED light panel and deliver specific-frequency audio via headphones. The intervention is illustrated in Fig. 2.

Detailed intervention protocol

40-Hz stimulation group

Each light pulse emitted by the LED panel will have a duration of 5 ms and a pulse repetition rate of 40 Hz (40 pulses per second, with a 20 ms inter-pulse interval). The headphones will deliver 40-Hz auditory stimuli. Each stimulation cycle will last 1 min, and the group will receive a total of 12 cycles. Electroacupuncture will be applied at Baihui (GV20) and Shenting (GV24) with a 40-Hz current.

Random-frequency audiovisual stimulation group

Each light pulse will have a duration of 10 ms, with a pulse repetition rate randomly varying between 1 and 100 Hz (random pulses emitted between 1 and 100 times per second). The inter-pulse interval will randomly range from 5 to 995 ms. The headphones will deliver randomly frequency-modulated audio. Electroacupuncture will be applied at Baihui (GV20) and Shenting (GV24) with a randomly frequency-modulated current. Each stimulation cycle will last 1 min, and the group will receive a total of 12 cycles.

Continuous EEG will be monitored throughout the intervention to record brain electrical activity.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

No significant adverse events are anticipated for participants. Subjects may withdraw from the trial at any time without affecting their routine medical care. Allocated interventions may be modified under the following circumstances: (1) At the participant’s request or (2) if the assigned intervention is deemed ineffective for the participant’s condition. Any modifications will be promptly communicated to relevant parties by the principal investigator.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

To ensure compliance with the intervention and assessment protocols, all neurologists, physical therapists, nurses, and engineers involved in the study will receive comprehensive training according to the recommended guidelines and study procedures.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

During the study period, participants with PSCI may continue their usual hospital care, including daily examinations, routine medication schedules, and the same rehabilitation regimens as per standard practice. However, other neuroregulatory therapies, such as transcranial electrical stimulation, will be prohibited.

Provisions for posttrial care {30}

No significant harm is expected from the sensory stimulation intervention. However, if participants experience any discomfort such as dizziness, nausea, or headache, a neurologist will promptly evaluate them and provide further necessary treatment.

Outcomes {12}

All variables collected in this study are explicitly defined below, including their measurement units or categories. For psychometric scales, the total score range and the interpretation of the limits are provided.

Demographic and clinical baseline variables

Age will be recorded in years (continuous variable). Sex will be documented as male or female (categorical variable). Comorbidities, including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, smoking, and drinking history will be documented as yes or no (categorical variable).

Pre-intervention (−t1), 2-week post-intervention retention (T2), 3-month post-intervention (T3), and 6-month post-intervention follow up (T4), as well as cranial magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans (T1, T2, DWI, DTI, BOLD) and EEG, will be employed to detect changes in neural network connectivity; mini-mental state examination (MMSE), Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), Auditory Verbal Learning Test (AVLT), Color-Digit Trail Making Test (CDTMT), Judgment of Line Orientation (JLO), Digit Span Test (DS), Stroop Color-Word Test (SCWT), Boston Naming Test (BNT) Scale will be use to assess cognitive abilities. Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAMA), Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD, v17.0), and Neuropsychiatric Inventory Questionnaire (NPI-Q) Severity Scale will be use to assessment mood disorders. CT perfusion (CTP) will be employed to detect cerebral blood flow (CBF), and peripheral blood samples will be collected for RNA sequencing.

Primary outcomes

This is evaluated at pre-intervention (−t1) and 2 weeks, 3 months, and 6 months post-intervention.

Neuropsychological assessment tools

The following scales will assess cognitive abilities: MMSE, MoCA, AVLT, CDTMT, JLO, DS, SCWT, and BNT scale.

MMSE: The total score ranges from 0 to 30, with higher scores indicating better global cognitive function. A score below 24 is a commonly used cutoff to indicate cognitive impairment in poststroke populations.

MoCA: The total score ranges from 0 to 30, with higher scores indicating better global cognitive function. The standard screening cutoff for cognitive impairment is a score below 26.

AVLT: It is among the most widely used and sensitive neuropsychological tests for assessing verbal episodic memory. Its primary clinical value lies in its ability to characterize the pattern of memory impairment, which aids in differential diagnosis.

CDTMT: The key variable is the time to complete the test, measured in seconds. A longer completion time indicates greater difficulty with task-switching and executive attention. Performance will be evaluated against normative data.

JLO, DS, SCWT, and BNT: For these tests, the relevant raw or standardized scores (as per their respective manuals) will be used. For most, higher scores indicate better performance (except for SCWT completion time, where lower is better).

Psychiatric symptom evaluation

The following scales will assess mood disorders and neuropsychiatric symptoms:

HAMA, HAMD, NPI-Q Severity Scale

HAMA: The total score ranges from 0 to 56, with higher scores representing greater severity of anxiety symptoms. A score of 14 or above is often used as a threshold for clinically significant anxiety.

HAMD-17: The total score ranges from 0 to 54, with higher scores representing greater severity of depressive symptoms. A score of 7 or above is a common cutoff for mild depression.

NPI-Q Severity Scale: The composite severity score ranges from 0 to 36, with higher scores indicating greater overall severity of neuropsychiatric symptoms.

The primary outcomes are the change from baseline in the total scores of the cognitive (MMSE, MoCA, AVLT, CDTMT time, JLO, DS, SCWT, BNT) and mood (HAMA, HAMD-17, NPI-Q Severity) scales defined above. Data for each continuous scale will be summarized as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range, IQR) following normality testing.

Clinical relevance and cut-off justification

The selected cognitive scales are the clinical gold standards for diagnosing and monitoring PSCI. Improvements in these scores directly correspond to enhancements in memory, executive function, and global cognition—key determinants of functional recovery and quality of life. The specified cutoffs (e.g., MoCA < 26, MMSE < 24) are widely validated in geriatric and stroke cognitive research, allowing for the analysis of clinically meaningful transitions between “impaired” and “normal” states.

Secondary outcomes

This is assessed at the same time points as primary outcomes (T1–T4), with the exception of RNA sequencing, which is performed at T1 and T2 only.

CTP

Cerebral hemodynamic changes will be evaluated using CTP to assess CBF before and after the intervention. Imaging was performed using a Siemens SOMATOM Force 960 + scanner with a 320-row detector. The protocol began with a non-contrast CT plain scan, followed by dynamic cerebral perfusion imaging. Scan parameters were as follows: collimation width 40 mm, tube voltage 120 kV, tube current–time product 320 mAs, gantry rotation time 0.35 s per rotation, pitch 1.2375, 20 samples per gantry rotation (two samples per movement), sampling interval 1.5 s, and scan length 105 mm. After defining the scan range, a bolus of iohexol contrast agent (370 mg I/mL) was administered intravenously via the antecubital median vein using a MALLINCKRODT high-pressure injector at a rate of 5 mL/s for a total volume of 50 mL. The scan continued for 60 s, covering 20 cycles and generating 640 slices. All image data were automatically transferred to the United Imaging AI post-processing workstation (uai_pacs). Perfusion analysis was conducted using the CT Brain Perfusion Analysis software with the stroke protocol. The software automatically generated pseudocolor perfusion maps based on the maximal lesion level. Regions of interest included the infarct core and the ischemic penumbra (IP). Mirror regions on the contralateral healthy side were automatically identified for comparative analysis. Key parameters derived included CBF, cerebral blood volume (CBV), mean transit time (MTT), time to peak (TTP), and IP volume, together providing comprehensive quantitative and visual perfusion assessment.

Functional connectivity by MRI

Functional and structural brain changes will be assessed using a 3-T MRI scanner (Siemens Skyra, Erlangen, Germany) equipped with a standard head coil. The imaging protocol will include measures of gray matter density, cortical thickness, subcortical nuclei volumes, and functional connectivity. Each participant will undergo an approximately 25-min scanning session. During the scan, participants will be instructed to keep their eyes closed, and acoustic padding will be placed around their ears to minimize noise-related artifacts.

Functional connectivity and neural network alterations will be analyzed within the framework of human connectomics. By integrating advanced imaging analytics with multimodal data—including T1-weighted imaging, diffusion tensor imaging (DTI), and blood oxygenation level-dependent (BOLD) functional MRI—we will compare pre- and posttreatment changes in brain fiber connectivity and functional region activity. This multimodal approach enables the visualization and quantification of interregional functional connections, alterations in the number and trajectory of nerve fiber bundles, and volumetric changes in functionally aberrant brain areas.

Gamma oscillation entrainment via EEG

In this study, gamma oscillation entrainment will be assessed using a 32-channel scalp EEG recording system (BrainAmp MR plus, Brain Products, Germany) with a sampling rate of 500 Hz. Electrode placement will follow the international 10–20 system, with CPz serving as the reference electrode and AFz as the ground. Prior to experimentation, electrode impedance will be maintained below 20 kΩ across all channels. Data from all participants will be preprocessed using the Makoto pipeline and the EEGLAB toolbox in MATLAB. Changes in power spectral density before, during, and after the intervention will be computed using custom MATLAB scripts to evaluate gamma entrainment.

RNA sequencing of blood samples

Venous blood samples (1 mL) will be collected from each participant at two time points: baseline (prior to intervention) and 2 weeks post-intervention. All blood draws will be performed by a trained nurse under standardized conditions. To minimize pre-analytical variability, participants will be instructed to fast after 8:00 PM on the day preceding blood collection and to refrain from alcohol consumption and vigorous exercise. On the morning of the blood draw, breakfast will be prohibited. After voiding, participants will be escorted to the laboratory for phlebotomy. Each sample will be collected into an EDTA-coated anticoagulant tube, followed by the addition of 3 mL of TRIzol reagent (LMAl Bio, China) and thorough mixing. After incubation at 25 °C for 5 min, samples will be stored at −80 °C to preserve RNA integrity for subsequent analysis. This protocol ensures stable extraction of biomarkers related to neuroinflammation, neurotrophic factors, and synaptic plasticity-associated gene expression.

Sample size {14}

This trial employs a non-inferiority design. Preliminary studies observed mean improvements in MMSE and MoCA scores of 14.92 and 12.37, respectively, in the audiovisual stimulation group compared to the control group, with corresponding standard deviations of 8.41 and 7.05. The sample size was calculated using PASS (power analysis and sample size) software, Version 2021. With a non-inferiority margin set at 10% of the mean baseline improvement, a one-sided significance level (α) of 0.025, and statistical power (1–β) of 80%, a minimum of 120 participants are required. Accounting for a maximum dropout rate of 20%, the calculated sample size is 120. Therefore, a total of 120 participants will be enrolled.

Recruitment {15}

This study will be conducted at the Department of Neurology and Neurorehabilitation of the Seventh People's Hospital affiliated with Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine. The hospital is a comprehensive national stroke center and a member of the National Clinical Research Center for Neurological Diseases. It has extensive experience in acute stroke management and poststroke functional rehabilitation. Over the past 3 years, the hospital has admitted more than 1000 stroke cases, ensuring sufficient enrolment of participants with PSCI. Participants will be screened and recruited by the attending neurologists.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

Baseline measurements will be collected after signing of informed consent. After baseline assessments, participants will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to one of the two treatment groups (40-Hz stimulation vs. random-frequency stimulation). A research assistant not involved in outcome assessment or participant screening will independently manage the randomization procedure to ensure allocation concealment.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

Allocation sequences will be concealed within a digitally secured randomization system, referring to a centralized, web-based randomization system. The system ensures concealment through the following mechanisms: (1) Centralized and independent: The allocation sequence is generated and stored by the study statistician who is not involved in participant recruitment or enrolment; (2) inaccessible to investigators: investigators at the recruitment sites cannot preview, access, or alter the randomization sequence; and (3) real-time allocation: only after entering the baseline data of an eligible participant into the system will the site receive the irrevocable treatment group assignment.

Implementation {16c}

Qualified researchers who are independent of trial implementation will be responsible for generating and maintaining the randomization list in the trial database.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

Procedures will be implemented to minimize expectancy effects related to audiovisual stimulation. Participants, their authorized representatives (if applicable), intervention providers, outcome assessors, follow-up personnel, and data collectors will be blinded to the stimulation condition (40 Hz vs. random frequency). Only the engineers responsible for configuring the stimulation devices and the study statisticians will be unblinded.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Unblinding is not anticipated unless required for participant safety.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Data will be analyzed using GraphPad Prism (version 8.0; GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA) and MATLAB (R2021a; The MathWorks Inc., Natick, MA, USA). The primary analysis will follow the intention-to-treat (ITT) principle, including all randomized participants in the groups to which they were originally assigned. The results of the trial will be reported according to CONSORT.

Descriptive statistics at baseline

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics will be summarized for each group. Continuous variables will be presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median with IQR, as appropriate. Categorical variables will be summarized as frequencies and percentages.

Outcome analysis

For both primary and secondary outcomes, point estimates and their 95% confidence intervals will be reported. Between-group comparisons for continuous variables will be conducted using the Student’s t-test (for normally distributed data) or the Mann–Whitney U-test (for non-normally distributed data). Normality will be assessed using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Categorical variables will be compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A two-sided p-value < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Handling of missing data

For the primary ITT analysis, missing outcome data will be handled using multiple imputation under the assumption of missing at random (MAR). Sensitivity analyses, including a complete-case analysis, will be conducted to assess the robustness of the results.

Multiplicity

Given that this trial has a single primary outcome, no adjustment for multiplicity is planned. Any exploratory subgroup analyses will be clearly labeled as post-hoc and interpreted with caution.

Adverse events summarization

Adverse events will be tabulated by treatment group, including the number and percentage of participants experiencing each event. Severity and relationship to the intervention will also be summarized.

None planned.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

None planned.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol nonadherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

Analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle.

Discussion

PSCI is highly prevalent, increases the risk of disability, significantly impairs patients’ quality of life, and adds to both familial and socioeconomic burdens. Large international cohort studies have reported a PSCI incidence ranging from 24% to 53.4% [1, 3], with a 5-year survival rate of only 39%, compared to 75% in stroke patients without dementia [2]. Moreover, treatment outcomes for PSCI remain suboptimal. Current therapeutic strategies primarily aim to slow further cognitive decline, enhance cognitive performance, ameliorate neuropsychiatric symptoms, and improve ADL. However, there is a lack of large-scale, randomized, double-blind, controlled clinical trials specifically targeting PSCI. Cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil and rivastigmine may be used in PSCI treatment to improve cognitive function and daily living abilities and are thought to help maintain cognitive gains; however, their efficacy in improving cognition is limited. Furthermore, as the PSCI population is predominantly elderly, special attention must be paid to drug interactions and other safety concerns.

Early screening and timely comprehensive intervention are recommended for PSCI. Comprehensive management includes control and prevention of known risk factors, pharmacotherapy, and rehabilitation. Cognitive rehabilitation training plays a crucial role in improving cognitive function in PSCI patients [19]. Both Chinese and US guidelines suggest that cognitive training interventions may be beneficial (class IIa recommendation, level B evidence) [20]. However, conventional one-on-one cognitive training with therapists is labor-intensive, and the limited number of therapists makes it difficult to meet demand. Meta-analyses indicate that computer-based cognitive rehabilitation (CBCR) can lead to significant improvements in reaction time, attention, verbal memory, and reduction of negative psychiatric symptoms in PSCI patients [21], though this approach remains exploratory.

Currently, treatment options for PSCI are limited and often ineffective. In recent years, the use of gamma-band stimulation to induce gamma oscillations and promote brain functional recovery has emerged as a landmark discovery. The relationship between gamma oscillations and cognitive function dates back to the early 2000 s, with a series of studies linking gamma activity to memory networks [22, 23]. This finding laid the groundwork for subsequent research on modulating gamma oscillations to improve cognition. Follow-up studies have employed neuromodulation techniques such as optogenetics, transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS), and multisensory stimulation to induce precise electrophysiological responses—gamma oscillations. Studies involving individuals with AD [24], schizophrenia-related cognitive impairment [20, 25], children with learning disabilities [26], and healthy adults have demonstrated that enhanced gamma oscillations can improve cognitive function and learning ability [27, 28]. Mechanistic explorations have revealed that gamma oscillations may involve microglial activation (inflammatory mechanisms), reduction of beta-amyloid levels, increased GABA release probability from parvalbumin-expressing interneurons, and enhancement of glutamatergic transmission and network plasticity [24, 28–31].

The mechanisms underlying PSCI are complex and interrelated, currently thought to involve neuronal damage, disruption of brain networks, and neuroinflammation [32–34]. PSCI arises from lesions affecting highly interconnected brain hubs, disrupting communication between resting-state networks, and altering global brain structure and information transfer efficiency [33]. Increased oxidative stress and excitotoxicity due to glutamate further damage neurons and complicate recovery. While PSCI shares some mechanisms with other forms of cognitive impairment, the role of gamma oscillations in PSCI populations remains unexplored. Therefore, this study aims to investigate gamma oscillations in PSCI patients and assess whether inducing gamma oscillations can improve cognitive impairment in this group.

Current methods to induce gamma oscillations include optogenetics, TMS, transcranial electromagnetic stimulation, and audiovisual sensory stimulation. Given the clinical translation limitations of optogenetics and the unclear optimal targets for TMS and electromagnetic stimulation, further exploration is needed. In recent years, sensory stimulation—particularly audiovisual intervention—has been extensively studied with notable success [13, 35, 36]. This method is well-tolerated and clinically feasible, prompting our use of 40-Hz audiovisual stimulation to induce gamma oscillations and evaluate its therapeutic effects.

One objective of this study is to explore the characteristics of gamma oscillations in PSCI patients and to further investigate whether induced gamma oscillations can improve cognitive impairment in this population.

Moreover, while previous studies have primarily used cognitive scales as endpoint measures to assess the clinical efficacy of new gamma-inducing techniques, this study is the first to incorporate CTP, brain MRI, and motor evoked potential (MEP) electrophysiological techniques for a multidimensional and more objective evaluation of treatment efficacy. Additionally, by integrating MATLAB-based analyses and leveraging human genomic databases, we will assess treatment outcomes at the brain network level. This multidisciplinary approach—encompassing anatomical, neuro-network, electrophysiological, and analytical levels—aims to explore the underlying mechanisms in clinical participants, providing a solid foundation for the clinical application of this innovative technique.

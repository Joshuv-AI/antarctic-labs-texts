High VRK2 expression level predicts unfavorable survival outcomes in nasopharyngeal carcinoma
Authors: Chien-Han Yuan, Hung-Lung Ke, Jhen-Hao Jhan, Tian-Huei Chu, Yu-Tzu Chang, Ching-Chieh Yang, Cheng-Fa Yeh, Hui-Hui Lin, Shih-Lun Chang, Chung-Chih Tseng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundEpstein–Barr virus (EBV)–associated nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is an endemic epithelial malignancy in East and Southeast Asia, yet reliable molecular markers for risk stratification remain limited. Vaccinia-related kinase 2 (VRK2), a serine/threonine kinase implicated in cell proliferation, stress responses, and inflammatory signaling, has not been comprehensively evaluated in NPC.MethodsIn this retrospective study, we assessed the prognostic impact of VRK2 expression in 124 patients with histologically confirmed NPC. VRK2 protein levels were determined by immunohistochemical staining and correlated with clinicopathological parameters and survival outcomes.ResultsHigh VRK2 expression was significantly associated with advanced pathological stage (p = 0.006). Patients with high VRK2 levels had markedly poorer disease-specific survival (DSS), distant metastasis-free survival (DMeFS), and local recurrence-free survival (LRFS) on univariate analysis (all p < 0.001). In multivariate Cox regression models, elevated VRK2 expression remained an independent predictor of adverse outcomes, with hazard ratios of 2.904 (95% CI: 1.678–5.026) for DSS, 3.566 (95% CI: 1.874–6.784) for DMeFS, and 2.885 (95% CI: 1.411–5.898) for LRFS (all p < 0.001).ConclusionsThese findings suggest that VRK2 may actively contribute to the aggressive phenotype and enhanced progression of Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)-associated NPC by modulating cellular signaling pathways. The overexpression of VRK2 may reflect underlying molecular alterations associated with metastasis and treatment resistance. Therefore, VRK2 expression may serve not only as a clinically relevant prognostic biomarker but also as a potential molecular target to improve patient stratification and guide individualized therapeutic interventions in NPC.Keywords: Nasopharyngeal carcinoma, Epstein–Barr virus, VRK Serine/Threonine Kinase 2, Clinicopathological, Survival

Introduction

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a squamous cell carcinoma arising from the epithelial lining of the nasopharynx. Its distinct geographical distribution is apparent, with over 70% of new cases occurring in East and Southeast Asia. This makes NPC an uncommon malignancy compared with other cancer types [1, 2]. However, the incidence in Taiwan is significantly higher than that in other countries, ranging from 2.8 to 6.6 per 100,000 person-years [3, 4]. The pathogenesis of NPC is influenced by multiple factors, including viral infections, inherited or genetic predisposition, and individual variability [5]. NPC commonly occurs in regions with high levels of dietary nickel, drinking water, and preserved foods. Additionally, it tends to affect individuals with a family history of the disease [6].

Currently, radiation therapy and chemotherapy remain the cornerstone components of a comprehensive treatment strategy for NPC [2]. Owing to advancements in radiation technology and the adoption of holistic treatment approaches, treatment outcomes for NPC have significantly improved. A recent multicenter phase III randomized clinical trial reported that patients who received concurrent chemoradiotherapy achieved a remarkable 2-year overall survival rate of 76.4% [7]. However, despite the favorable outcomes observed in early-stage NPC, many patients seek medical attention only after the disease has progressed to advanced local stages. This delay is often attributed to vague early symptoms and rapid disease progression, leading to missed opportunities for treatment during the most effective stage. Furthermore, distant metastases may still develop even among patients who have received optimal therapy. Patients with NPC who develop distant metastases generally exhibit poor overall prognosis and a low cure rate [8]. In addition, traditional prognostic indicators such as tumor stage and histologic grade fail to account for the substantial heterogeneity in oncological outcomes among patients within the same clinical stage. Therefore, novel predictive biomarkers should be investigated to more accurately evaluate the likelihood of metastasis and overall outcomes in NPC. The development of NPC may be influenced by Epstein–Barr virus (EBV) infection, which establishes a lifelong, asymptomatic, and persistent infection in more than 90% of the global population [9]. EBV can infect both B cells and stratified squamous epithelial cells, ultimately inducing their transformation. During its lytic replication and latent phases, EBV expresses BHRF1, a Bcl-2 homolog that protects infected cells from apoptosis. The BHRF1 gene has been implicated in various EBV-associated malignancies, particularly in NPC [10, 11].

Vaccinia-related kinases (VRKs) constitute a distinct family of serine/threonine kinases comprising three members: VRK1, VRK2, and VRK3 [12]. Although VRK2 is predominantly localized in the cytoplasm, VRK1 and VRK3 are mainly distributed within the nucleus [13]. Nezu et al. first identified VRK2 as a human serine/threonine kinase that shares approximately 40% amino acid sequence homology with B1R, another serine/threonine kinase expressed early during viral infection and essential for viral DNA replication [14]. Consequently, it has been hypothesized that VRK2 regulates cell proliferation by modulating the cell cycle, DNA replication, and transcription of specific genes. Human VRK2 is highly expressed in proliferative tissues such as the fetal liver, testis, and thymus, as well as in several cancer cell lines. Functionally, VRK2 has been implicated in promoting tumor-cell invasion and enhancing cellular stress adaptation [15]. Increasing evidence indicates that VRK2 exerts a multifaceted influence on apoptosis regulation via multiple signaling mechanisms. For instance, VRK2 phosphorylates p53 at Thr18, thereby enhancing its stability and preventing its ubiquitination by MDM2 [16]. Moreover, VRK2 interacts with Bcl-xL to suppress apoptosis and binds phosphorylated AKT following autophagy induction, thereby contributing to the regulation of cell proliferation and maintenance of mitochondrial outer membrane stability [17].

Recent studies have also demonstrated that VRK2 expression correlates with patient survival across several malignancies, including pancreatic, breast, and liver cancers [16, 18, 19]. Notably, VRK2 interacts with Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)-encoded BHRF1 in EBV-positive NPC cells, promoting tumor-cell survival by protecting them from apoptosis [20]. Nevertheless, the precise role of VRK2 in NPC remains poorly understood. Therefore, the biological effects of VRK2 expression in NPC warrant comprehensive investigation. Furthermore, the therapeutic potential of VRK2 in NPC should be further explored. This study aimed to determine whether VRK2 expression is associated with oncological outcomes in patients with NPC.

Materials and methods

Analysis of the published transcriptome data sets

To identify key genes involved in the pathogenesis of Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), we reanalyzed the publicly available transcriptome dataset (GSE12452) from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), which includes 31 NPC tissues and 10 laser-capture microdissected non-tumorous nasopharyngeal epithelial tissues [21–26]. Gene expression profiles were generated using the Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array (Affymetrix, Santa Clara, CA, USA), and expression levels were calculated using Nexus Expression 3 software (BioDiscovery, El Segundo, CA, USA) [25, 26]. Raw CEL files were imported, and all probe sets were analyzed without preselection or filtering. Supervised comparative analyses were performed to identify statistically significant alterations in gene expression during NPC tumorigenesis. Expression levels between tumor and non-tumor tissues were compared to identify functional candidate genes associated with amino acid autophosphorylation (GO: 0006468). Genes with p < 0.01 and a log₂ fold change > 0.1 were selected for further evaluation.

Tumor specimens and study cohort

All NPC tissue samples were collected with institutional review board approval (IRB10501-006, Chi Mei Medical Center, Tainan, Taiwan). The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. We retrospectively reviewed the clinical and pathological data of 124 patients with NPC who were treated at Chi Mei Medical Center between 1993 and 2002. Biopsy specimens were obtained at initial diagnosis prior to the development of distant metastasis. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor samples were analyzed. Two pathologists independently re-evaluated tumor stages according to the 7th edition of the AJCC staging system and confirmed histological subtypes based on the World Health Organization (WHO) classification. All patients received a radiation dose of ≥ 70 Gy, administered 5 days per week at 1.8–2.0 Gy per fraction. Patients with stage II–IV disease received concurrent platinum-based chemotherapy [27, 28]. One stage II and four stage IV patients, due to poor general health, received radiotherapy alone. Radiation protocols were consistent throughout the cohort. Seven early-phase patients lacked immediate post-therapy imaging to document treatment response. Tumor response was evaluated according to WHO criteria, yielding 110 complete and 7 partial responses [27, 28].

Immunohistochemical staining and assessment of VRK2 expression

Tissue Sect. (3 μm thick, FFPE) were subjected to standard immunohistochemical staining as described previously [25, 26]. After heat-induced antigen retrieval, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a rabbit monoclonal anti-VRK2 antibody (ab184875, Abcam, MA, USA; dilution 1:100). Immunoreactivity was visualized using ChemMate™ DAKO EnVision™ Detection Kit (K5001, Carpinteria, CA, USA). Positive staining was defined as a brown cytoplasmic or membranous signal in tumor cells. Negative controls were processed without the primary antibody. Two pathologists independently evaluated staining results, blinded to clinical data. Tumors with cytoplasmic or nuclear positivity were classified as immunopositive [22–25]. VRK2 expression was quantified using H-scores, calculated as Σ Pi (i + 1), where Pi represents the percentage of stained tumor cells (0–100%) and *i* denotes staining intensity (0–3+). Cases were dichotomized into high- and low-VRK2 expression groups based on the median H-score.

Survival and cox regression analyses

Survival endpoints

Three clinically relevant survival endpoints were evaluated: disease-specific survival (DSS), distant metastasis-free survival (DMeFS), and local recurrence-free survival (LRFS). DSS was defined as the interval from diagnosis to death caused specifically by NPC. DMeFS was calculated from diagnosis to distant metastasis, and LRFS was defined as the time to local tumor recurrence. Patients without events were censored at last follow-up.

Stratification of VRK2 expression

VRK2 immunohistochemical staining was semi-quantitatively assessed using H-scores. Patients were stratified into high- and low-VRK2 groups using the median H-score.

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis

Kaplan–Meier curves for DSS, DMeFS, and LRFS were generated and compared using the log-rank test.

Cox proportional hazards regression

Univariate Cox analysis included age, sex, T status, N status, AJCC stage, and VRK2 expression. Variables with p < 0.1 were entered into multivariate Cox models. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 14.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), which was the current and widely used version at the time the analyses were conducted, around 2008. Associations between VRK2 expression and clinicopathological parameters were evaluated using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. Kaplan–Meier survival curves for DSS, DMeFS, and LRFS were compared using the log-rank test. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were constructed to identify independent predictors of DSS, DMeFS, and LRFS, and hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. Forest plots and hazard ratio bar charts were generated using Python (matplotlib). A two-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study design and patient characteristics

The overall workflow of this study is illustrated in Fig. 1. A total of 124 patients with biopsy-proven, Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)–associated nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) who were treated at Chi Mei Medical Center between 1993 and 2002 were retrospectively included. Clinicopathological parameters, including patients’ age, sex, WHO histological classification, AJCC stage, and treatment outcomes, were obtained from institutional medical records. Tissue microarrays (TMAs) were constructed from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimens, and VRK2 expression was evaluated using immunohistochemistry (IHC). Expression levels were semi-quantitatively assessed using the H-score method, and patients were categorized into high- and low-expression groups according to the median cutoff value. High VRK2 expression was significantly correlated with advanced T stage and local recurrence (p < 0.05). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients with high VRK2 expression exhibited significantly poorer disease-specific survival (DSS), distant metastasis-free survival (DMeFS), and local recurrence-free survival (LRFS). Multivariate Cox regression analysis further confirmed VRK2 as an independent prognostic factor for all three outcomes (hazard ratio ≈ 3.0, p < 0.001).

Overview of the experimental workflow for investigating the prognostic and biological significance of VRK2 in Epstein–Barr virus (EBV)-associated nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). VRK2 is a highly upregulated gene linked to amino acid autophosphorylation in NPC tissues

To explore amino acid autophosphorylation-related pathways in NPC, we re-analyzed the public transcriptomic dataset GSE12452. Hierarchical clustering based on these probes clearly separated the samples into two major clusters (Cluster-1 and Cluster-2). Clusters 1 and 2 represent distinct gene expression patterns, with gene lists in each cluster significantly enriched for PAK2 (p21 (CDKN1A)-activated kinase 2), VRK2, SMG1 (PI-3-kinase-related kinase SMG-1), among others. Notably, the VRK2 gene is prominently expressed in Cluster 2 of tumor tissue, suggesting its potential involvement in tumor development. We included 17 probes covering 13 transcripts associated with amino acid autophosphorylation (GO: 0006468) in the transcriptomic profile from the published dataset (GSE12452) based on our selection criteria (Fig. 2A). The colors indicate the distribution of genes across different cell types and clusters, providing insights into the regulatory mechanisms underlying tumor-related genes. Green and red represent down- and upregulated genes, respectively. Among these genes, VRK2 was among the most highly upregulated. Most genes showed higher average expression in Cluster-2 than in Cluster-1, consistent with global upregulation of this kinase-enriched gene set in tumor samples (Fig. 2B, C). Table 1 lists the detailed gene expression between tumor and nontumor tissues in NPC tumorigenesis in the NPC transcriptome. Below, we summarize the key genes, their functions, and statistical significance. For VRK2, the log₂(tumor/nontumor) ratio was 2.0901, with a p value < 0.0001, showing a marked increase in tumor tissues. VRK2 is involved in ATP binding, kinase activity, and protein binding, among other functions. Moreover, it was the only gene that met the criteria for further analysis because of its significant differential expression. The insulin receptor (INSR) log2 ratio values were − 1.0745 (first probe), − 0.7824 (second probe), and − 0.3899 (third probe). The p-values are 0.0004, 0.0028, and 0.0002, respectively. INSR is critical for insulin binding and receptor activity and is essential for glucose metabolism and signaling pathways. These patterns are illustrated by the log₂(tumor/non-tumor) plot in Fig. 2D, where VRK2 stands out as the most strongly induced kinase, whereas the INSR probes show consistent downregulation. Given its large effect size and robust statistical significance, VRK2 was the only gene that fulfilled our predefined criteria for further in-depth analysis as a candidate biomarker and potential therapeutic target in NPC.

VRK2 expression in NPC tissues was significantly higher than that in normal nasopharyngeal (NP) nontumor epithelial tissues

Immunohistochemical analysis was performed to evaluate VRK2 expression and its association with the clinicopathological features of NPC in a cohort of 124 patients. Figure 3 shows the immunohistochemical (IHC) staining intensity of VRK2 in NPC and non-tumorous nasopharyngeal tissues and summarizes its distribution according to primary tumor status, clinical stage, and histological grade. VRK2 expression, determined using IHC, was categorized as low or high based on the staining levels in the tumor cells. The staining pattern of VRK2 was localized in the cell membrane and cytoplasm. VRK2 in NP nontumor epithelial tissue showed low expression in the cytoplasm (Fig. 3B, inset) and only faint membranous staining. The cytoplasmic expression of VRK2 was frequently observed in poorly differentiated NPCs (Fig. 3A and B). Table 2 presents key clinical and pathological attributes, including sex, age (years), primary tumor pathological stage, nodal status, clinical cancer stage, histological grade, and Epstein–Barr virus-encoded small RNAs (EBER) status within a cohort of 124 patients with NPC. Of the tumor samples collected from male patients, 44 (46.3%) and 51 (53.7%) displayed low and high levels of VRK2 staining, respectively. In contrast, within the tumor samples from female patients, 18 (62.1%) and 11 (37.9%) showed low and high levels of VRK2 staining, respectively. Thus, VRK2 expression was not significantly associated with sex (p = 0.138) or age (p = 0.659). The high VRK2 staining status showed significant correlations with the primary tumor stage (T1–T2 vs. T3–T4; χ² test, p = 0.003), the AJCC cancer stage (stage I–II vs. stage III–IV; χ² test, p = 0.006) (Fig. 3C), and histological grade (keratinizing, nonkeratinizing, and undifferentiated; χ² test, p = 0.009) (Fig. 3E). However, no significant associations were observed between VRK2 expression and nodal status (N) (χ² test, p = 0.071), or EBER status (p = 0.315) (Fig. 3D). Consistently, the proportion of tumors with high VRK2 expression increased with more advanced primary tumor status, higher clinical stage, and poorer histological differentiation. These clinicopathological associations are graphically summarized in Fig. 3C–E. Panel C shows the proportion of cases with high VRK2 expression according to primary tumor status (T1–T2 vs. T3–T4), according to AJCC clinical stage (stage I–II vs. stage III–IV), and panel E according to histological grade (keratinizing, nonkeratinizing, and undifferentiated). Overall, these findings indicate that VRK2 is not only markedly overexpressed in NPC but is also associated with more aggressive clinicopathological features, suggesting that VRK2 may serve as a potential biomarker and therapeutic target in this disease.

VRK2 overexpression predicts poor clinical outcomes in NPC

To evaluate the prognostic significance of VRK2 in NPC, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed for disease-specific survival (DSS), distant metastasis-free survival (DMeFS), and local recurrence-free survival (LRFS). As shown in Fig. 4A, patients with high VRK2 expression (H-score ≥ median) consistently exhibited poorer DSS, DMeFS, and LRFS compared with those with low VRK2 expression (all p < 0.001). Univariate Cox regression demonstrated that primary tumor status (T3–T4), nodal status (N2–N3), advanced AJCC stage (III–IV), and high VRK2 expression were significantly associated with inferior DSS, DMeFS, and LRFS (Table 3). In multivariate analysis, AJCC stage remained an independent prognostic factor for DSS (HR = 2.363, p = 0.014) and LRFS (HR = 3.076, p = 0.021), while high VRK2 expression independently predicted poor DSS (HR = 2.904, 95% CI: 1.678–5.026, p < 0.001), DMeFS (HR = 3.566, 95% CI: 1.874–6.784, p < 0.001), and LRFS (HR = 2.885, 95% CI: 1.411–5.898, p = 0.004) (Table 4). A multivariate forest plot summarizing HRs for AJCC stage and VRK2 expression is shown in Fig. 4B, highlighting the independent prognostic impact of VRK2. The HR bar chart in Fig. 4C further illustrates that high VRK2 expression increases the risk of mortality, distant metastasis, and local recurrence by approximately 2.9–3.6 fold. Together, these results indicate that VRK2 is a strong and independent prognostic biomarker in NPC, providing information complementary to AJCC staging.

Discussion

Despite concurrent chemoradiation treatments, a significant portion of patients with Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) still develop distant metastases, accounting for most cancer-related deaths. Management of locally recurrent or metastatic NPC therefore remains clinically challenging despite modern multimodality therapy [22]. Therefore, novel genetic or molecular prognostic biomarkers for invasive cancer cells should be established to predict local recurrence and distant metastasis and to stratify and manage high-risk individuals more effectively [23]. In this study, we identified VRK2 as a significantly overexpressed kinase in NPC by re-examining the GSE12452 transcriptome and validating its prognostic relevance in a large clinical cohort. We utilized IHC and collected clinicopathological data to examine the prognostic significance of VRK2. Consistent with the transcriptomic findings, high VRK2 protein expression correlated with advanced T status, higher AJCC stage, and poorer differentiation, and predicted inferior DSS, DMeFS, and LRFS. This is the first study to integrate transcriptomic and protein-level evidence demonstrating VRK2 as a robust and independent prognostic biomarker in NPC.

Importantly, the strong association between high VRK2 expression and inferior distant metastasis–free survival (HR = 3.566) is particularly relevant in the modern intensity modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) era, in which distant metastasis has become the leading cause of treatment failure in nasopharyngeal carcinoma. This finding suggests that VRK2 may have value beyond a prognostic survival marker and could serve as a biomarker for metastatic risk stratification. From a clinical perspective, patients with high VRK2 expression may represent a biologically aggressive subgroup with an increased risk of distant metastasis. Such patients might potentially benefit from treatment intensification strategies, including more aggressive induction chemotherapy or closer systemic surveillance. While this hypothesis requires prospective validation, incorporation of VRK2 into future risk-adapted treatment models may help refine patient selection for intensified systemic therapy while avoiding overtreatment in low-risk patients.

NPC is an epithelial malignancy closely linked to EBV infection. Approximately 25% of NPC cases are keratinizing squamous cell carcinomas, whereas the remaining 75% are nonkeratinizing carcinomas, which consistently test positive for EBV [24]. Notably, most patients with NPC from endemic areas (> 95%) had EBV-positive, nonkeratinizing, undifferentiated carcinomas. Conversely, EBV-negative keratinizing carcinomas, characterized by well-differentiated histology, are more frequently observed in nonendemic regions. More importantly, the most metastatic head and neck cancer is EBV-positive NPC, with the highest prevalence in endemic regions [25]. Given the strong EBV association and its known anti-apoptotic programs, understanding how EBV interacts with VRK2-mediated pathways is essential in clarifying NPC biology.

Therefore, the oncogenic mechanisms of EBV in NPC should be thoroughly investigated. Our re-analysis of GSE12452 demonstrated that VRK2 was the most highly upregulated kinase among amino-acid-autophosphorylation–related genes, with a log2 (tumor/non-tumor) ratio of 2.09 (p < 0.0001). Several other kinases—such as SYK, KIAA1804, MAP3K9—also exhibited moderate upregulation, whereas INSR probes showed consistent downregulation. These patterns highlight VRK2 as the kinase showing the most pronounced tumor-associated overexpression, supporting VRK2 as the primary candidate for downstream biological investigation.

It should be noted that pathway-level associations involving VRK2 discussed herein were inferred primarily from analyses of public transcriptomic datasets and prior literature, and were not directly validated in our clinical cohort. Therefore, these observations should be regarded as hypothesis-generating and suggestive rather than definitive mechanistic evidence.

These associations were derived from previously reported molecular functions rather than direct interactions demonstrated in this study. INSR regulates metabolism and stress via MAP3K9/MAP3K13 in insulin signaling [26]. SYK influences immune signaling, potentially interacting with MAP3K13 to activate immune cells [27]. VRK2, a serine/threonine kinase, may regulate the cell cycle and apoptosis with STK4 [28]. STK4 (MST1) regulates apoptosis and stress responses, collaborating with PAK2 in cytoskeletal reorganization and cell survival. UHMK1 influences RNA splicing and posttranscriptional regulation, affecting SMG1 in mRNA surveillance. SMG1 maintains gene expression stability through nonsense-mediated decay. PAK2 modulates the cytoskeleton, potentially regulated by INSR or SYK. MYO3A, a myosin protein, facilitates cell movement and transport, possibly regulated by PAK2. Together, these genes reflect broader kinase-associated pathways relevant to cancer biology; however, in our dataset, VRK2 was the only gene that met predefined statistical criteria for further analysis.

Bcl-2 was initially identified as a proto-oncogene in follicular B-cell lymphoma. Together with mitochondria, cytochrome C, and caspases, Bcl-2 family proteins play a crucial role in intracellular apoptotic signaling pathways, which are vital for development, tissue homeostasis, immune defense, and cancer suppression. These proteins share homology in four conserved Bcl-2 homology (BH) domains (BH1–BH4) and a transmembrane domain, functioning as either cell death agonists or antagonists [29]. Numerous genes regulate this process, with significant insights from studying the Bcl-2 family. Moreover, protein interactions between Bcl-2 family members and other molecular partners regulate apoptosis [30].

Viruses rely on host cells to proliferate during intracellular viral infection and must avoid self-destruction upon infection detection. In EBV-infected cells, BHRF1, an EBV early gene product, shares structural and functional similarities with Bcl-2, can bind and sequester BCL-2-interacting mediator of cell death (BIM; encoded by BCL2L11) and protect cells from apoptosis caused by various environmental stressors [31]. In vitro, overexpressed VRK2 alone exhibited a modest antiapoptotic effect. When VRK2 is coexpressed with BHRF1, it significantly promotes cell survival. However, cell survival was not enhanced when VRK2 was coexpressed with Bcl-2, indicating that human VRK2 specifically interacts with EBV BHRF1 and that this interaction plays a role in protecting cells from apoptosis [32]. Functionally, BHRF1 can inhibit innate immunity activation by causing mitochondrial fission, facilitating their storage in mitophagosomes for destruction [33]. Previous studies support a proposed model in which EBV may stabilize mitochondrial survival pathways, potentially involving VRK2, thereby contributing to NPC tumorigenesis.

Extending this EBV–VRK2 mitochondrial survival model, EBV-encoded latent proteins, particularly latent membrane protein 1 (LMP1), may further cooperate with VRK2 to promote the survival of EBV-infected nasopharyngeal carcinoma cells [34, 35]. LMP1 is a key EBV-encoded oncoprotein in NPC and acts as a constitutively active receptor mimic that persistently activates NF-κB, MAPK, and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways [34, 35]. While these pathways enhance cell proliferation and resistance to apoptosis, sustained LMP1 signaling has also been shown to induce metabolic reprogramming, increased reactive oxygen species production, and mitochondrial stress [36].

Within the hypoxic, nutrient-limited, and inflammatory tumor microenvironment characteristic of nasopharyngeal carcinoma [37], such LMP1-induced metabolic and oxidative stress may drive EBV-infected cells toward the threshold of mitochondrial apoptosis. Under these conditions, VRK2 may function as a stress-adaptive kinase that buffers EBV-encoded protein–induced mitochondrial stress by suppressing intrinsic apoptotic signaling. Given its localization to mitochondrial and endoplasmic reticulum membranes and its ability to interact with antiapoptotic Bcl-2 family members, VRK2 is well positioned to maintain mitochondrial integrity, inhibit BAX activation, and prevent cytochrome c release in EBV-positive NPC cells [38, 39].

We therefore propose a functional interplay in which EBV-encoded proteins such as LMP1 act as upstream drivers of oncogenic signaling and metabolic reprogramming, whereas VRK2 serves as a downstream survival enabler that allows EBV-infected NPC cells to tolerate chronic metabolic stress, oxidative damage, and mitochondrial dysfunction. This cooperation may provide a selective survival advantage within the hostile tumor microenvironment and contribute to tumor aggressiveness, treatment resistance, and poor clinical outcomes.

At the mitochondrial level, several EBV-encoded proteins provide mechanistic support for this proposed survival model in EBV-dependent NPCs. The involvement of mitochondria in the tumorigenesis of EBV-dependent NPCs should be explored, as BHRF1 localizes to the mitochondria, and its elevated expression in NPC cells modifies mitochondrial morphology, enhancing tumorigenic characteristics, such as colony formation, wound healing, cell migration, and invasion. Additionally, BHRF1 may induce the mitochondrial membrane permeabilization transition (MMPT) through the matrix protein cyclophilin D. This BHRF1-mediated MMPT results in mitochondrial distortion and dysfunction, increasing the generation of reactive oxygen species and mitophagy activation. This molecular mechanism inhibits apoptosis and facilitates the carcinogenesis of NPC [40]. Like BHRF1, VRK2 also localizes to the mitochondrial membranes and endoplasmic reticulum. The VRK2A isoform can modulate the intrinsic apoptotic pathway through direct interaction with Bcl-xL, facilitating the formation of a complex that diminishes apoptosis [41]. Elevated VRK2 levels offer protection against apoptosis and serve as a negative regulator of BAX. Conversely, reduced VRK2A levels heighten cell susceptibility to apoptosis induced by chemotherapeutic agents, such as camptothecin or doxorubicin [42]. Additionally, the synergistic effect of miR-141-3p and miR-145-5p on antiapoptotic target regulation was shown in cancer cell lines, offering further potential strategies for cancer treatment [43].

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the relationship between VRK2 expression and NPC. These findings highlight the potential biological significance of VRK2, supporting its role as a biologically relevant candidate in NPC. Thus, the underlying mechanisms linking NPC tumorigenesis with amino acid autophosphorylation and apoptosis remain incompletely understood. More extensive investigations should be conducted to elucidate the physiological role of VRK2 and its potential biological significance.

In conclusion, a significant correlation was observed between elevated VRK2 expression and poor survival outcomes in patients with NPC. Consequently, VRK2 expression may be a valuable prognostic marker for NPC survival and metastasis, offering clinicians important insights for considering more aggressive treatment approaches. Moreover, VRK2 may be a marker for pathological tissue sections of patients with NPC and a new therapeutic target, and early detection might save lives and costs associated with treatment.

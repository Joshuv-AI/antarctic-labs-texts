Long-Term Efficacy and Safety of Ritlecitinib in Adults and Adolescents with Alopecia Areata: 3-Year Results from the ALLEGRO Phase 2b/3 and ALLEGRO-LT Phase 3 Clinical Studies
Authors: Maryanne Senna, Jennifer Soung, Ignasi Figueras, Brett King, Misaki Kinoshita-Ise, Sameh Hanna, Wenyu Wu, Dalia Wajsbrot, Deborah Woodworth, Robert Wolk, Alanna Chaudhry, Alexandre Lejeune, Helen Tran
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/objectiveRitlecitinib, an oral JAK3/TEC family kinase inhibitor, demonstrated efficacy over 48 weeks in patients aged ≥ 12 years with alopecia areata (AA) in the ALLEGRO phase 2b/3 study and initial extension up to month 24 in the ALLEGRO-LT study. We aimed to evaluate the efficacy of ritlecitinib up to 3 years in patients with AA from the ALLEGRO phase 2b/3 and ongoing phase 3, open-label ALLEGRO-LT studies.MethodsPatients aged ≥ 12 years with AA and ≥ 50% scalp hair loss who received daily ritlecitinib 50 mg in ALLEGRO-2b/3 and rolled over to ALLEGRO-LT (continued 50 mg) were included. Observed and last observation carried forward (LOCF) data are reported based on the data cutoff (25 June 2024). Variables associated with treatment response were assessed using multivariable logistic regression.ResultsA total of 191 patients were included. At 3 years, 65.1% (observed) and 47.1% (LOCF) of patients had Severity of Alopecia Tool (SALT) score ≤ 20. Among patients with SALT score ≤ 20 at 1 year, most (88.3–89.6%) maintained this response at 3 years. SALT score ≤ 10 response rates at 3 years were 52.3% (observed) and 36.7% (LOCF). At 3 years, 31.2% (observed) and 22.5% (LOCF) of patients achieved SALT score 0 (complete scalp hair regrowth). Patients’ Global Impression of Change response (“moderately” or “greatly” improved) rates at 3 years were 68.4% (observed) and 55.6% (LOCF). Female sex, White race, and less extensive and shorter duration of hair loss at baseline were associated with treatment response. The safety profile was consistent with the known safety profile of ritlecitinib.ConclusionsRitlecitinib 50 mg demonstrated clinically meaningful clinician- and patient-reported efficacy up to 3 years, with no new safety signals observed. These data support the long-term use of ritlecitinib in patients aged ≥ 12 years with severe AA. Key Points

In patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA and ≥ 50% scalp hair loss, daily ritlecitinib 50 mg demonstrated long-term clinically meaningful clinician- and patient-reported efficacy up to 3 years, including maintenance of response and complete scalp hair regrowth.Ritlecitinib 50 mg was well tolerated, and the safety profile was consistent with that from the pivotal ALLEGRO-2b/3 study and the ALLEGRO-LT study through 2 years.These findings support the long-term use of ritlecitinib in patients aged ≥ 12 years with severe AA.

Introduction

Alopecia areata (AA) is an autoimmune disease characterized by nonscarring hair loss on the scalp, with or without facial and/or body hair involvement [1]. The global prevalence of AA is estimated to be approximately 0.58–2% [2–5]. AA can progress to more extensive forms, such as alopecia totalis (AT; complete loss of scalp hair) and alopecia universalis (AU; complete loss of scalp, face, and body hair) [2]. Both adults and adolescents with AA frequently experience psychosocial distress that can significantly impair health-related quality of life [6–10]. Because the disease course of AA is often unpredictable, with a prolonged and relapsing/remitting pattern [11, 12], long-term therapy may be needed for optimal disease management.

Recent developments in AA have provided renewed hope for achieving better disease control than was possible with conventional treatments, such as topical and intralesional corticosteroids, systemic corticosteroids, and topical or systemic immunomodulators, which are frequently used off-label and across a wide range of disease severity. It is therefore increasingly important to evaluate and report the long-term efficacy and safety of newer approved therapies for AA. Currently, 3 targeted Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors are approved for the treatment of severe AA. Baricitinib and deuruxolitinib are oral JAK1/2 inhibitors [13, 14], while ritlecitinib is an oral, selective dual inhibitor of JAK3 and the TEC family of kinases [15]. Baricitinib and deuruxolitinib are approved for use in adults, whereas ritlecitinib is approved for patients aged ≥ 12 years.

The pivotal ALLEGRO phase 2b/3 study demonstrated that ritlecitinib was efficacious and had a favorable safety profile up to 48 weeks in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA and at least 50% scalp hair loss [15]. Given the often chronic and unpredictable nature of severe AA, understanding the long-term efficacy and safety of treatment is critical to inform clinical decision-making and patient management. ALLEGRO-LT is an ongoing phase 3, open-label study investigating the long-term safety and efficacy of ritlecitinib for up to 5 years in patients with AA. Previously, analyses of patients who rolled over from ALLEGRO-2b/3 to ALLEGRO-LT showed clinically meaningful and sustained efficacy of ritlecitinib for up to 2 years, with an acceptable safety profile [16, 17]. To further characterize the long-term benefit-risk profile of ritlecitinib in AA, we report efficacy outcomes up to 3 years and safety in patients with AA from the ALLEGRO-2b/3 and ongoing phase 3, open-label ALLEGRO-LT studies.

Methods

Study Design

The design and primary results of the 48-week ALLEGRO 2b/3 study (NCT03732807) have been previously reported [15]. ALLEGRO-LT (NCT04006457) is ongoing and enrolled patients into 2 arms: (1) rollover patients who had received treatment in the ALLEGRO phase 2a proof-of-concept study or ALLEGRO-2b/3, and (2) de novo patients who had not received ritlecitinib treatment in either study. This efficacy analysis comprises all patients who received ritlecitinib 50 mg once daily (QD) without a loading dose in ALLEGRO-2b/3, including those who rolled over to ALLEGRO-LT and continued the same dosage. Patients who were initially randomized to ritlecitinib 50 mg in ALLEGRO-2b/3 and patients who received placebo for the initial 24 weeks and subsequently switched to ritlecitinib 50 mg at week 24 in ALLEGRO-2b/3 are included. Participants who were initially randomized to ritlecitinib completed 48 weeks of ritlecitinib treatment in ALLEGRO-2b/3 before rolling over into ALLEGRO-LT, and those who were initially randomized to placebo completed 24 weeks of ritlecitinib treatment in ALLEGRO-2b/3 before rolling over into ALLEGRO-LT. The “month 36–38” time point includes patients initially randomized to ritlecitinib (n = 130) who had a scheduled visit at month 24 of ALLEGRO-LT and patients initially randomized to placebo (n = 61) who had a scheduled visit at month 32 of ALLEGRO-LT, after rolling over from the ALLEGRO-2b/3 study. All other time points represent the time from the start of ritlecitinib treatment. The data cutoff for the ongoing ALLEGRO-LT study was 25 June 2024; therefore, data may change after completion of the study.

Patients

Inclusion criteria for ALLEGRO-2b/3 have been previously described [15]. Patients were aged ≥ 12 years with a diagnosis of AA and had ≥ 50% scalp hair loss at baseline, including those with AT and/or AU. The maximum duration of current episode of hair loss was ≤ 10 years. Adolescents who rolled over into ALLEGRO-LT had to be discontinued per protocol if they did not demonstrate an improvement in Severity of Alopecia Tool (SALT) score of ≥ 50% from baseline (in ALLEGRO-2b/3) at month 3 and a SALT score of ≤ 20 by month 6.

Outcomes

Efficacy data through 3 years are presented for the proportion of patients treated with ritlecitinib 50 mg QD with SALT score ≤ 20 (≤ 20% scalp hair loss), SALT score ≤ 10, SALT score 0, maintenance of response at month 36–38 in patients who had a SALT score ≤ 20 at months 12 and 36 (regardless of SALT score at any time point between months 12 and 36; patients with SALT score > 20 at any time point between months 12 and 36–38 were still considered to have maintained response if they had a SALT score ≤ 20 at the month 36–38 visit), sustainability of response at month 36–38 (defined as having a SALT score ≤ 20 at months 12 and 36 without having SALT score > 20 at any time point between months 12 and 36), Eyebrow Assessment (EBA) response (defined as a ≥ 2-grade improvement from baseline or a score of 3 in EBA among patients with an abnormal EBA score at baseline), Eyelash Assessment (ELA) response (defined as a ≥ 2-grade improvement from baseline or a score of 3 in ELA among patients with an abnormal ELA score at baseline), and Patients' Global Impression of Change (PGI-C) response (score of “moderately improved” or “greatly improved” from baseline). EBA and ELA are scored on a scale from 0 to 3, where a score of 0 = no eyebrow or eyelash hair and a score of 3 = normal eyebrow or eyelash hair. Mean change from baseline (CFB) in SALT score is also reported.

An overview of safety data through the data cutoff date are reported for the efficacy analysis group (ritlecitinib 50 mg) and for all patients in the ALLEGRO clinical trial program (including 4 studies: ALLEGRO-2b/3, ALLEGRO-LT, ALLEGRO-2a proof-of-concept [NCT02974868], and ALLEGRO-2a safety [NCT04517864]) who received any dose of ritlecitinib (any ritlecitinib group; 10 mg, 30 mg [with or without a 4-week 200-mg loading dose], or 50 mg [with or without a 4-week 200-mg loading dose]) [18]. Incidence of adverse events (AEs), serious AEs (SAEs), and AEs of special interest are reported. A manuscript providing further details on the safety of all patients who received ritlecitinib (any ritlecitinib group) in the ALLEGRO clinical trial program will be published separately.

Statistical Analysis

Efficacy data are presented as observed and imputed (last observation carried forward [LOCF]) data to account for missing values. The LOCF imputation method was applied to each visit for all participants with missing data. Observed analyses include only patients with available data at each visit and therefore reflect outcomes among patients who remained in the study at that time point. LOCF analyses impute missing values using the last observation available, thus providing an estimate that includes patients who discontinued. Two-sided 95% confidence intervals are shown. Safety summaries were based on the period from the first dose of ritlecitinib up to the data cutoff and are reported descriptively.

Subsets of patients stratified by AA disease severity were analyzed: those with baseline SALT score 50 to < 95 (50% to < 95% scalp hair loss), baseline SALT score 95 to 100 (≥ 95% scalp hair loss), baseline AT/AU (defined as SALT score 100 at baseline as assessed by the investigator, regardless of category in the AA history case report form), and baseline non-AT/AU. Results are subject to change as additional data are collected and analyzed in the ongoing ALLEGRO-LT study.

A multivariable logistic regression using all variables, forward selection, backward elimination, and stepwise selection methods was used to assess the association of patient demographics, baseline disease characteristics, and the presence of comorbidities with the likelihood of achieving SALT score ≤ 20 response during at least 1 visit through month 36–38. Variables used as covariates in the model were patient and disease characteristics for which data were available and have previously been shown to be clinically relevant in AA [16, 19] and included age (< 18 years versus ≥ 18 years), sex (male versus female), race (Asian versus White, other versus White), body mass index (continuous), current AA episode duration (defined as the time since the onset of current hair loss; continuous), AA disease duration (continuous), prior treatment for AA (yes versus no), number of episodes of AA (continuous), duration of significant (≥ 50%) scalp hair loss (defined as the time that the patient has had ≥ 50% scalp hair loss; continuous), SALT score at baseline (continuous), ELA score at baseline (abnormal versus normal), EBA score at baseline (abnormal versus normal), at least one fingernail affected at baseline (yes versus no), body hair loss at baseline (yes versus no), and combined type 2 comorbid conditions (asthma, atopic dermatitis, or allergic rhinitis; yes versus no). Analyses were implemented using R software (glm function of the stats package for logistic regressions). Independent covariates of interest that were significant in the stepwise models are shown; odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals are reported.

The mean (SD) baseline SALT score among non-AT/AU patients was 83.3 (15.4), the median duration of AA since diagnosis was 6.9 years, and the median duration of current AA episode was 2.2 years. Efficacy

The proportion of patients with a SALT score ≤ 20 response generally increased up to month 36–38 (Fig. 2a). At month 36–38, 65.1% (observed) and 47.1% (LOCF) of patients had SALT score ≤ 20. Among patients with baseline SALT score 50 to < 95, 78.0% (observed) and 66.7% (LOCF) had a SALT score ≤ 20 at month 36–38 (Fig. 2b), and among patients with baseline SALT score 95–100, 57.4% (observed) and 36.8% (LOCF) had a SALT score ≤ 20 at month 36–38 (Fig. 2c). Among the 74 patients with a SALT score ≤ 20 at month 12 (observed), 88.3% (53 out of 60 with non-missing SALT score at month 36–38) maintained this response at month 36–38; of the 77 patients with SALT score ≤ 20 at month 12 (LOCF), 89.6% (69 out of 77, LOCF) maintained this response at month 36–38 (Fig. 3a). Maintenance of response among patients with baseline SALT score 50 to < 95 and among those with baseline SALT score 95–100 was consistent with that in the overall population (Fig. 3a). The proportion of patients with sustained SALT score ≤ 20 response (response at all visits between months 12 and 36–38) was 75.0% (45 out of 60 with non-missing SALT score at month 36–38, observed) and 77.9% (60 out of 77, LOCF) (Supplementary Fig. 2a); these proportions were consistent with those among patients with baseline SALT score 50 to < 95 and those with baseline SALT score 95–100. Overall, 8 of the 53 patients (observed) who had a SALT score ≤ 20 response at month 12 and maintained this response at month 36–38 had a SALT score > 20 during at least one visit between months 12 and 36. The proportion of patients with SALT score ≤ 10 response increased through month 24 and remained stable up to month 36–38 (Fig. 2d). Maintenance of SALT score ≤ 10 response among patients with baseline SALT score 50 to < 95 and among those with baseline SALT score 95–100 was consistent with that in the overall population (Fig. 3b). The proportion of patients with sustained SALT score ≤ 10 response (no SALT score > 10 between months 12 and 36–38) was 67.4% (31 out of 46 with non-missing SALT score at month 36–38) and 72.9% (43 out of 59, LOCF) (Supplementary Fig. 2b).

Safety

For completeness, safety is reported for the group of patients receiving ritlecitinib 50 mg in the efficacy analysis (ritlecitinib 50-mg group) as well as across the overall ALLEGRO clinical trial program for patients who received any dose of ritlecitinib (any-ritlecitinib group). The any-ritlecitinib group includes the 191 patients in the efficacy analysis and an additional 1103 patients (total N = 1294). In the any-ritlecitinib group, AEs occurred in 1158 patients (89.5%), of whom 186 were in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (97.4%) (Table 2). The most common AEs in both groups were SARS-CoV-2 test positive, headache, COVID-19, nasopharyngitis, cough, pyrexia, upper respiratory tract infection, oropharyngeal pain, and acne. SAEs were reported in 88 patients in the any-ritlecitinib group (6.8%), including 13 patients in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (6.8%) (Table 2). There were no deaths in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group and 2 deaths in the any-ritlecitinib group (previously described in King et al. 2024); there were no new deaths reported in this data cut [18]. A total of 22 patients (1.7%) in the any-ritlecitinib group had a serious infection, including 3 patients in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (1.6%) (Table 2). Opportunistic infections occurred in 3 patients (0.2%) in the any-ritlecitinib group; all were cases of herpes zoster meeting criteria for opportunistic infection (multidermatomal herpes zoster [nonadjacent or > 2 adjacent dermatomes] or disseminated herpes zoster). One of these cases occurred in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (0.5%). Overall, herpes zoster was reported in 34 patients (2.6%) in the any-ritlecitinib group, including 10 patients in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (5.2%).

Malignancies (excluding nonmelanoma skin cancer) were reported in 10 patients (0.8%) in the any-ritlecitinib group, 3 of whom were in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (1.6%) (Table 2). Reported malignancies included breast cancer (n = 4), testicular cancer (n = 1), papillary thyroid cancer (n = 1), malignant melanoma (n = 1), cervical dysplasia (n = 2), and intraductal proliferative breast lesion (n = 1).

One patient (0.5%) in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (and included in the any-ritlecitinib group) experienced a thromboembolic event (pulmonary embolism) (Table 2). In the any-ritlecitinib group, 7 patients (0.5%) had a major adverse cardiovascular event; none occurred in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group. These events were acute respiratory failure and cardiorespiratory failure (n = 1), myocardial infarction (n = 4), retinal artery occlusion (n = 1), pulmonary embolism (n = 1), and cerebrovascular accident (n = 1).

Paresthesia/dysesthesia occurred in 39 patients (3.0%) in the any-ritlecitinib group, of whom 8 were in the ritlecitinib 50-mg group (4.2%) (Table 2).

Discussion

We report interim efficacy results up to 3 years and safety results up to the data cutoff for patients with AA receiving ritlecitinib 50 mg QD. Efficacy with ritlecitinib was maintained for up to 3 years, and the safety profile remained consistent with findings from the pivotal ALLEGRO-2b/3 study and the ALLEGRO-LT study through 2 years [18]. The clinically meaningful clinician- and patient-reported efficacy outcomes, together with the favorable long-term safety results, represent the longest duration of ritlecitinib treatment reported to date and support its long-term use in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA.

As is common and expected in long-term open-label studies, patients discontinued treatment over the 3-year period. Response rates based on observed data can be interpreted as conditional response rates given that a patient remains in the study, which may be influenced by treatment response and tolerability. Response rates based on LOCF can be interpreted as response rates that reflect the patients’ last assessment, which gives a more conservative estimate and assumes stability of their last assessment after discontinuation. As anticipated, response rates were numerically higher in the observed analyses than in the LOCF analyses at later time points, reflecting the influence of discontinuation over time.

Compared with the previously reported results at month 24, when SALT score ≤ 20 and ≤ 10 responses were 61%/46% (observed/LOCF) and 51%/38% (observed/LOCF), respectively [17], response rates at month 36–38 are similar at 65%/47% (observed/LOCF) for SALT score ≤ 20 and 52%/37% (observed/LOCF) for SALT score ≤ 10. The continued improvement in response rates through month 36–38 aligns with post hoc analyses of individual clinical response patterns showing that some patients require an extended treatment duration (> 1 year) to reach SALT score ≤ 20 [16]. Although not all patients reach this threshold within the first year of treatment, most experience progressive improvement in SALT score over time [20]. Furthermore, the proportions of patients reaching the most stringent scalp hair loss outcome, SALT score 0 (complete scalp hair coverage), increased steadily from 11%/10% (observed/LOCF) at month 12 to 31%/23% at month 36–38. Together, these data support the benefit of continued ritlecitinib treatment for optimal long-term outcomes in patients with AA.

Most patients (88–90%) who had a SALT score ≤ 20 response at month 12 maintained this response at month 36–38. The results presented here demonstrate that even if loss of the target response occurs while on treatment, patients can regain response with continued ritlecitinib treatment. This is shown in the 8 patients who had SALT score ≤ 20 response at month 12 and month 36–38 but who lost that response (had a SALT score > 20) at some point between months 12 and 36 (Supplementary Fig. S3). Of these 8 patients, 1 lost their SALT score ≤ 20 response by only a small amount, maintaining a SALT score under or close to ≤ 20 from months 5 to 48 (red line, Supplementary Fig. S3) and 1 had only a brief relapse at month 18 of SALT score < 50 before regaining response (light blue line, Supplementary Fig. S3). Most of the 8 patients experienced 1 relapse, ranging from approximately 3 to 17 months; 4 of the 8 patients had a relapse to SALT score > 80 (including 2 patients who relapsed to SALT score 100) and still regained SALT score ≤ 20 response. In this small subset of 8 patients, those with higher SALT scores at baseline generally had a relapse with higher SALT scores (e.g., SALT score > 80) than those with lower baseline SALT scores. These observations show that following relapse, the clinical response can be restored during continued ritlecitinib treatment, even in patients with severe disease. Future studies with larger numbers of patients are needed to confirm these findings.

The Alopecia Areata Investigator’s Global Assessment defines 50–94% scalp hair loss (SALT score 50 to < 95) as “severe AA” and 95–100% scalp hair loss (SALT score 95–100) as “very severe AA” [21]; diagnosis of AT or AU requires a SALT score of 100 (complete scalp hair loss). The largest differences in observed and LOCF analyses for the proportions of patients achieving SALT score ≤ 20 and SALT score ≤ 10 and for CFB in SALT score were seen in patients with baseline SALT score 95–100 (very severe AA) versus SALT score 50 to < 95 (severe AA). This may in part be explained by a higher discontinuation rate among patients with baseline very severe AA than among those with baseline severe AA (58.4% versus 43.9%), with one of most common reasons for discontinuation among those with baseline very severe AA being lack of efficacy (12.8%), which aligns with previous literature demonstrating that patients with very severe AA are more often refractory to treatment [2, 22–24]. The higher dropout rate due to lack of efficacy observed among patients with baseline very severe AA (SALT score 95–100) versus those with severe AA (SALT score 50 to < 95) may also have resulted in the severe AA group being enriched for responders compared with the very severe AA group, as reflected by the numerically higher SALT score ≤ 20 and SALT score ≤ 10 responses among patients with baseline severe AA.

Patients with baseline SALT score 95–100 had longer median episode duration than patients with baseline SALT score 50 to < 95 (2.7 versus 1.6 years). Prior post hoc analyses of data from the ALLEGRO trials identified shorter duration and lesser extent of scalp hair loss to be associated with an increased likelihood of SALT score ≤ 20 response [16, 22]. Consistent with this, in this analysis higher proportions of patients with baseline SALT score 50 to < 95 achieved SALT score ≤ 20, SALT score ≤ 10, EBA response, ELA response, and PGI-C response than did patients who had baseline SALT score 95–100. However, meaningful proportions of patients with baseline very severe AA still achieved clinically relevant responses at month 36–38 (57%/37% [observed/LOCF] for SALT score ≤ 20 and 43%/27% [observed/LOCF] for SALT score ≤ 10), and treatment response rates were similar in patients with baseline SALT score 95–100 and those with AT/AU (SALT score 100). While ritlecitinib treatment did result in responses in some patients with very severe AA and in those with AT/AU, these findings suggest that earlier initiation of ritlecitinib, before scalp hair loss becomes very severe, may enhance treatment success. Despite the differences in initial response rates between patients with baseline SALT scores < 95 versus 95–100, high and comparable rates of maintained response were observed at 3 years in both subgroups. These results indicate that once SALT score ≤ 20 is achieved, efficacy is maintained in the majority of patients, even in patients with a baseline SALT score 95–100, who are often refractory to treatment [2, 22–24].

Factors significantly associated with increased likelihood of achieving SALT score ≤ 20 response at any visit to month 36–38 included lower baseline SALT score, shorter duration of significant (≥ 50%) scalp hair loss, female sex, and White race (versus other). For the continuous variables, each 1-unit increase in baseline SALT score, or each additional year of significant scalp hair loss was associated with a corresponding change in the likelihood of response. While these results align with previous analyses that have associated lower extent of baseline hair loss, shorter duration of hair loss, and female sex with treatment response in AA [16, 19], the number patients in this analysis in the “other” race category was small, which may have impacted the results, and further studies are needed to confirm and understand the role of race in achieving treatment response in patients with AA. These results additionally support data suggesting that treating with ritlecitinib early in the course of AA improves the highest likelihood of treatment success.

The overall safety profile of ritlecitinib has been characterized in an integrated safety analysis of the ALLEGRO clinical program [18], and an updated analysis characterizing safety of ritlecitinib in patients receiving any dose in the ALLEGRO clinical trial program up to the current data cutoff date will be published separately. The long-term safety profile of ritlecitinib 50 mg in this limited subset of the entire ALLEGRO clinical trial patient population was consistent with that in the larger population of patients treated with any dose of ritlecitinib in the ALLEGRO program and the known profile of ritlecitinib, and no new safety signals were observed.

The analysis presented here is limited by the nature of long-term open-label studies in that they may be biased toward those patients who remain in the study and those who tolerate the drug and show a response to treatment. However, the LOCF analyses help to partially address the impact of attrition and the resulting reduction in sample size over time. In addition, per study protocol, rollover adolescents who did not achieve ≥ 50% improvement in SALT score from baseline in ALLEGRO-2b/3 at month 3 and a SALT score ≤ 20 by month 6 in ALLEGRO-LT (5 adolescents in this analysis) were required to be discontinued from the study. It is possible that some of the adolescents who discontinued the study due to this efficacy criterion may have gone on to respond at later time points, as it has been shown that a proportion of patients require longer than 1 year of ritlecitinib treatment to achieve SALT score ≤ 20 response [16], while others may not have achieved response despite continuing treatment. The LOCF analyses applies a conservative approach to this by considering all 5 adolescents who discontinued due to the efficacy criterion as nonresponders.

Conclusions

In conclusion, ritlecitinib 50 mg demonstrated clinically meaningful clinician- and patient-reported efficacy (including achievement of complete scalp hair regrowth), with maintained response up to 3 years. These data are the longest duration of ritlecitinib treatment reported to date, and they support ritlecitinib’s long-term use in patients aged ≥ 12 years with AA.

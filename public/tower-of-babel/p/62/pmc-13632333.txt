Continuous versus intermittent lumbar CSF drainage in aneurysmal arachnoid hemorrhage: a prospective, randomized controlled trial protocol
Authors: Kuangyang Yu, Wen Tan, Lu Jiang, Jinwei Pang, Fengwang Xue, Kunyang Bao, Changren Huang, Yong Jiang, Jianhua Peng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHydrocephalus, as a common complication following aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH), not only leads to neurological dysfunction in patients but is also associated with a relatively high mortality rate.MethodsThis study is a prospective, single-center, randomized controlled trial. It is planned to recruit 256 patients with aSAH (parallel design, with a 1:1 allocation). Subjects will be assigned to the continuous lumbar drainage (LD) group and the intermittent LD group after undergoing surgical treatment (within 72 h). The primary outcome events will be defined as the degree of improvement in patients’ 180 (± 7 days) modified Rankin Scale (mRS) and Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) scores, incidence of shunt-dependent hydrocephalus, and 30 days mortality after randomization. Recruitment will be finished by November 2027. All the patients will be followed for at least 180 (± 7 days). The trial is scheduled to complete in 2028.DiscussionThis study aims to explore the safety and effectiveness of different LD methods after aSAH, evaluate whether there are different impacts on the neurological prognosis of patients, and determine whether the incidence of shunt-dependent hydrocephalus varies among different methods. It is intended to provide high-quality evidence-based medical evidence for the clinical management of hydrocephalus after aSAH.Trial registrationChiCTR2500096647 with chictr.org.cn. Background

Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) accounts for 5% of stroke incidents and often leads to devastating consequences along with a significant disease-specific burden [1–3]. With the continuous development of treatment techniques, the prognosis of aSAH has been greatly improved. Early surgical intervention can offer substantial outcome benefits for patients with ruptured intracranial aneurysms [4, 5]. However, active management of the complications after aSAH is equally crucial and can significantly affect the prognosis [6].

Hydrocephalus is one of the common complications after aSAH, with an incidence rate of 6% to 67% [7–10]. According to the occurrence time after the hemorrhage, the development of hydrocephalus after aSAH can be divided into three stages: acute (within 3 days), subacute (3–14 days), and chronic (more than 14 days) [11]. Current studies have shown that the mechanisms of hydrocephalus after aSAH include changes in cerebrospinal fluid dynamics, obstruction of the arachnoid granulations by blood components, and adhesions within the ventricular system [12]. The risk factors associated with the occurrence of hydrocephalus after aSAH include advanced age, hypertension, intraventricular hemorrhage, diffuse subarachnoid hemorrhage, aneurysms located in the posterior circulation, focal ischemic injury, ventricular enlargement at admission, higher Hunt-Hess scores, higher modified Fisher grades, symptomatic vasospasm, rebleeding due to aneurysm rupture, and female gender [13, 14]. At present, several clinical studies have confirmed that for patients after aSAH who undergo extra ventricular drainage (EVD) and lumbar drainage (LD) can achieve to better clinical prognosis and lower occurrences of hydrocephalus, vasospasm, and delayed cerebral infarction [15–18].

Methods

Design

This clinical study is a prospective, single-center, randomized, controlled, and single-blind trial (blinding the evaluators). The study has a planned duration of 2 years to recruit 256 patients across the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University in China. The flowchart of this study is shown in Fig. 1. The randomization employs a 1:1 ratio of continuous lumbar drainage with intermittent lumbar drainage. This study was approved by the ethics committees and research boards of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (KY2024501). The study was carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The aim is to explore the safety and effectiveness of different drainage methods for LD after aSAH, evaluate whether they have different effects on the neurological function prognosis of patients, and whether the incidence of shunt-dependent hydrocephalus varies among patients under different methods. Intervention and follow-up

The neurosurgery team is on standby 24 h a day, 7 days a week. All patients included in the study received standard treatment based on the latest clinical practice guidelines [20, 21]. The surgical treatment plan will be formulated through comprehensive consideration based on the patient’s condition and the wishes of the patient’s family. According to the clinical situation, the LD will be part of the standard treatment for aSAH. After being randomly assigned to groups, patients in the experimental group will have their cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) drained three times a day (with an interval of approximately 6 h each time), with 40 mL drained each time. When 40 mL of CSF has been drained, the three-way stopcock will be closed and kept clamped until the next drainage. If 40 mL of CSF has not been drained within 2 h after opening, the drainage height will be adjusted (lowered by 2 cm), and it will be clamped until the next drainage. For patients in the control group, continuous drainage will be adopted, with the drainage volume controlled at 5 mL per hour (the indwelling LD treatment requires at least 3 days).

If the patient’s condition is good, that is, the patient is conscious and able to move, they can get out of bed with the drainage tube clamped and actively undergo rehabilitation interventions such as standing and gait training. In contrast, during continuous CSF drainage, the patient remains in bed and receives rehabilitation treatment to prevent contractures and maintain muscle strength in the limbs and trunk. In addition, we will regularly manage the CSF drainage and consider removing the LD according to the patient’s condition.

In this study, patient data will be collected during hospitalization, at the time of discharge, at 90 days (± 7 days), and at 180 days (± 7 days). The data collection during hospitalization and at the time of discharge will be completed by a specialized assessment team. The telephone follow-up at 90 days (± 7 days) and 180 days (± 7 days) will be carried out through telephone or outpatient follow-up. Outcome events and study measures

Primary outcomes

The primary outcome events are disability after 180 days (± 7 days), assessed by the mRS [22] dichotomized at a score of 0 to 2 versus 3 to 6 (6 = death). Time zero is defined as the date of randomization for both groups in order to ensure comparability and avoid immortal time bias. Outpatient follow-up or telephone questionnaire are both applicable [23].

Sample size and randomization

According to previous studies, the proportion of patients with aSAH who developed shunt-dependent hydrocephalus after continuous LD was approximately 0.285 [18]. There has been no literature report on the incidence of shunt-dependent hydrocephalus in intermittent LD drainage. The different EVD drainage methods and extubation situations adopted by Shyam S. et al. in 2020, when intermittent drainage and rapid extubation were used, the incidence of shunt-dependent hydrocephalus was 0.13 [24]. Based on the above data, it is estimated that the proportion of patients with shunt-dependent hydrocephalus in those with intermittent LD drainage is 13%. Using the sample size estimation method for comparing two sample rates (Pe = 0.285, Pc = 0.13, α = 0.05, β = 0.10, one-sided test), at least 113 subjects should be observed in each group in the trial. Considering a 10% loss to follow-up rate, at least 126 subjects should be observed in each group. Therefore, a total of 252 patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage need to be included in this study. And this is a single-center clinical trial with participants recruited from the same clinical unit, no significant clustering effect, therefore, intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) adjustment was not performed.

Randomization will be conducted by SAS statistical software version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc. Cary, NC) that generates random numbers, and any patients who are randomly assigned will retain their randomization number after withdrawing from the trial or for any reason. The randomization number corresponding to that particular patient cannot be reused by other new patients, and new patients should be assigned a new randomization number to enter this study.

Statistical analysis and supervision

Analysis will be performed on an “intention-to-treat” principle. According to the two-sided test level of 0.05 for statistical testing, probability values (p) < 0.05 will be considered statistically significant for the difference being tested. According to the result of the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test, continuous variables will be expressed as mean ± standard deviation or medians (interquartile range) and compared using Student’s t-test or nonparametric test. Categorical variables will be presented as counts and percentages and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test will be used as appropriate. Effect sizes with a 95% confidence interval will be estimated if necessary.

The Case Report Form (CRF) shall be filled out by the investigators. Each enrolled case must have its CRF completed in a timely manner. After the completed CRF has been reviewed by the monitors, it shall be handed over to the data managers for data entry and management. Once handed over, the content of the CRF shall not be modified any more. For the questions existing in the CRF, the data managers will send inquiries to the researchers by generating a Query Form (QF), and contact the researchers through the monitors to ask them to answer and return it as soon as possible. The data managers will modify, confirm, and enter the data according to the researchers’ answers. If necessary, they can send the QF again until the correct confirmation is obtained.

The full analysis set (FAS), as randomized, includes all patients who were randomized into the trial. No patients withdrew consent after randomization. The primary outcome analysis was conducted as a complete-case analysis of the FAS, in which patients missing the day-180 assessment were censored (excluded from analysis). The per-protocol (PP) population is defined as the subset of the intention-to-treat (ITT) population excluding major protocol violators deemed likely to affect efficacy outcomes. The primary analysis of the primary outcome was conducted using a complete-case analysis, in which patients missing the day-180 assessment were censored (excluded from the analysis).

Adverse event reporting and harms

The term “adverse event” (AE) describes any sign, symptom, syndrome, or any disease which occurring newly in a trial participant after consent to the trial and being of particular interest for the assessment of the disease or the security of the therapeutic concept. In this trial, AEs include the following: arterial or venous thrombosis, complications related to insertion of a lumbar drainage, any severe adverse event (SAE). An SAE is any AE occurring during the trial that is related to the following: death, any life-threatening condition, long-term or severe restraint of the state of health. All adverse events reported by subjects or observed by investigators will be recorded. Serious adverse events should be promptly reported and recorded to the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University.

Discussion

The latest clinical studies have confirmed that CSF drainage can improve the prognosis for patients with acute hydrocephalus after aSAH; however, the selection of drainage methods and the optimal timing for removing the drainage tube still require further research [25, 26]. Moreover, there is a lack of relevant clinical studies on the pathogenesis and preventive measures of late-stage hydrocephalus. Especially when shunt-dependent hydrocephalus occurs, only surgery can save the patient’s life and improve the clinical prognosis, the patient will face the risks of undergoing another operation, as well as longer hospitalization periods and higher economic costs. Although some studies have attempted to construct prediction models for shunt-dependent hydrocephalus after aSAH, the stability and generalizability of these models are still insufficient. More importantly, different drainage methods have not yet been incorporated into these models [27–29].

LD is one of the common methods for treating hydrocephalus after aSAH in clinical practice. In the clinical management of aSAH, its safety has been partly confirmed through extensive clinical applications and scientific research [20, 21, 25, 30]. There have been numerous clinical studies on EVD for treating hydrocephalus after aSAH, exploring drainage methods and the optimal timing for extubation [24, 31–33]. However, for LD, there is no high-quality clinical research to confirm the safety and effectiveness of different drainage methods for patients.

Summary and conclusions

There is a lack of data from randomized clinical trials to establish the best treatment for hydrocephalus after aSAH. This study aims to explore the safety and effectiveness of different LD methods after aSAH, evaluate whether there are different impacts on the neurological prognosis of patients, and determine whether the incidence of shunt-dependent hydrocephalus varies among different methods. It is intended to provide high-quality evidence-based medical evidence for the clinical management of hydrocephalus after aSAH.

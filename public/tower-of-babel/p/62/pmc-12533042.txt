Efficient FPGA implementation of polar codes-based information reconciliation for quantum key distribution
Authors: Lianye Liao, Xinyi Wu, Ye Chen, Xiaodong Fan, Zhiyu Tian, Jinquan Huang, Tonglin Mu, Junran Guo, Minjie Liu, Bo Liu, Shihai Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractQuantum key distribution (QKD) leverages the principles of quantum mechanics to generate unconditionally secure keys for remote communication, even in the presence of an eavesdropper with unlimited computational power. A critical component of QKD is information reconciliation (IR), which corrects bit errors introduced by system imperfections and channel noise, ensuring the integrity of the shared key. Polar codes-based IR schemes have attracted considerable attention due to their near-Shannon-limit performance and low computational complexity. However, existing implementations primarily rely on CPUs or GPUs, which are suboptimal in terms of performance and energy efficiency. Here, we present a hardware accelerator designed specifically for discrete variable QKD (DV-QKD), targeting polar codes-based IR and implemented on a cost-effective FPGA platform. Our design achieves high throughput and scalability by employing a module-level pipeline parallel structure, a fully parallelized decoding strategy, and a hybrid memory architecture. This approach enhances decoder efficiency and optimizes resource utilization. On this platform, we demonstrate an IR throughput of 35.33 Mbps for a block size of , providing a real-time, cost-efficient solution that significantly enhances the performance of QKD systems.Keywords: Field programmable gate array (FPGA), Information reconciliation (IR), Polar codes, Discrete variable quantum key distribution (DV-QKD)Subject terms: Quantum physics, Quantum information

Introduction

Quantum key distribution (QKD) aims to establish an unconditionally secure key, denoted as and , between remote parties1,2. Several protocols, such as the fully passive3, twin-field4, and measurement-device-independent5 QKD protocols, have been developed to enhance security and extend the practical transmission range. However, imperfections in real-world systems and the presence of an eavesdropper (Eve) can induce quantum bit error rates (QBER), leading to inconsistencies () and compromising key security. To mitigate QBER and limit information leakage, information reconciliation (IR) is employed as a post-processing step. In IR, Alice and Bob exchange syndrome information over a classical public channel to reconcile their sifted keys into consistent keys () and generate reconciled keys ()6.

IR schemes are typically categorized into interactive error correction (IEC) and forward error correction (FEC)7,8. IEC schemes, such as the BBBSS protocol9 and the Cascade algorithm10, achieve high reconciliation efficiencies (about 1.03) but suffer from significant communication delays due to multiple rounds of interaction. Conversely, FEC schemes, including low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes11 and polar codes12, enable reconciliation in a single round, reducing communication latency and offering advantages in low-latency scenarios. Recently, polar codes have gained attention in IR, owing to their near-Shannon-limit performance and low computational complexity13.

In recent years, many researchers have proposed a variety of methods for the hardware implementation of polar codes-based IR. In 2019, Li et al.14 proposed a single-step quantum key distribution post-processing method based on polar codes and implemented it through the CPU. When block size is , throughput is 3 Mbps; on fully parallel CPU, it reaches 86 Mbps. In 2023, Guo et al.15 used a heterogeneous architecture of FPGA and CPU to implement. A IR throughput of 15 Mbps was achieved with a block size of . Tang et al.13 proposed a polar codes-based IR scheme with the frozen-bit erasure strategy, which introduced a fast decoding strategy. A throughput of 0.68 Mbps with a block size of was achieved on a CPU platform.

The performance of polar codes-based IR is primarily dependent on the decoding procedure. Decoding methods such as the successive cancellation (SC)12 and successive cancellation list (SCL)16,17 algorithms offer a trade-off between robustness and efficiency, which can limit IR performance. Various strategies, including FPGA-based implementations, have sought to address this challenge, improving throughput at the cost of increased hardware resources18–21. While high-performance devices like CPUs22 and GPUs23 deliver strong performance, they are impractical for compact, resource-constrained QKD systems due to their high power consumption.

In24, a polar codes-based appending information reconciliation (AIR) scheme applied to discrete variable QKD (DV-QKD) was proposed for CPU-based implementations, achieving a reconciliation efficiency , approaching the Shannon limit. However, this implementation is not suitable for resource-constrained QKD systems. To overcome these limitations, this paper focuses on designing a low-cost hardware accelerator that meets the practical deployment needs of such systems.

Here, we present an efficient hardware accelerator for the polar codes-based AIR scheme, designed to enable the miniaturization of QKD systems on resource-constrained platforms. The hardware is implemented on the Xilinx KINTEX UltraScale AXKU040 FPGA platform. The core data processing architecture utilizes a module-level pipeline structure, partitioning the decoding tree into subtrees for parallel processing. A fast decoding scheme, based on a fully unfolded architecture, is employed and optimized with pruning techniques to enhance efficiency. The storage architecture combines both on-chip and off-chip resources to optimize resource utilization and performance. Experimental results show a throughput of 35.33 Mbps, meeting the real-time requirements of QKD applications while maintaining a low-cost implementation.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces the theoretical foundations; Section 3 analyzes the hardware implementation of the SCL algorithm; Section 4 presents the proposed design and its implementation analysis; and Section 5 details the accelerator implementation and performance results.

In this section, we introduce the theoretical foundation relevant to this work, including the principles of polar codes, the implementation of the SCL algorithm on hardware platforms, and the AIR algorithm.

Polar codes

Polar codes, introduced by Arikan12, are proven to achieve channel capacity for symmetric binary-input discrete memoryless channels25. In comparison with Turbo codes26 and LDPC codes11, polar codes offer advantages such as lower bit error rates, reduced computational complexity, and higher throughput.

Recent implementations of polar codes-based IR mainly rely on the SC algorithm or its derivatives13,22,24,27–29. Algorithm hardware implementation analysis

In polar codes, the decoding speed of the SCL algorithm greatly impacts the throughput of IR in QKD systems. Therefore, this section will focus on the implementation details of SCL on hardware platforms.

Timing and complexity

Due to the strong correlation between bits in the codeword frame, the SCL algorithm requires decoding each bit in the strict order of encoding. For the decoding of each information bit, the following steps must be executed sequentially: computation of the LLR, path bifurcation and pruning, and bit decision. As shown in Fig. 1, the computation of the LLR involves the invocation of the function, which depends on the decision made for the previous bit. This dependency introduces inherent sequentiality in the SCL decoding architecture, limiting parallel processing and, consequently, constraining the potential for improving decoding efficiency.

In the SCL algorithm, increasing the number of decoding paths, , reduces the error rate and enhances error correction performance. Unlike sequential bit-by-bit decoding, there is no data dependency between the SC cores in the SCL decoder, enabling full parallelization. Therefore, the SCL decoder can be viewed as a composition of SC decoder submodules. Although SCL decoding inherently involves bit-by-bit decision dependencies, we enhance parallelism on the FPGA platform using strategies such as module-level pipelining, multi-path parallel decoding, and unfolded decoding architecture. A decoupled multi-frame structure at higher levels allows concurrent frame processing, while a fully unfolded architecture at lower levels eliminates inter-node dependencies. Additionally, fully parallel SC cores enable simultaneous operation of multiple decoders. These optimizations mitigate sequential bottlenecks and enable efficient FPGA implementation of polar code decoding.

The hardware design

This paper presents a hardware implementation on the Xilinx KINTEX UltraScale AXKU040 platform, which offers low power consumption, cost-effectiveness, and multifunctionality. The polar codes decoder in this system adopts a CRC-aided successive cancellation list (CA-SCL) decoding architecture. A block length of is adopted to ensure robust decoder performance and high reconciliation efficiency f, in line with the demands of QKD post-processing. The CA-SCL decoder consists of modules for , , a -bit decoder, and a cyclic redundancy check (CRC) decoding and verification unit. The -bit decoder includes LLR computation modules, partial sum computation modules, and a fully unfolded -bit decoder architecture. Due to limitations in on-chip hardware resources, the memory structure combines on-chip memory with off-chip DDR. This hybrid memory architecture accelerates key data access using on-chip high-speed memory, while leveraging the large capacity of off-chip memory to balance storage efficiency and performance, thereby ensuring efficient system operation under resource constraints.

Module-level pipeline-based decoding architecture

When module-level pipeline optimization is applied to all non-leaf nodes and their respective left and right subtrees in the decoding binary tree, the decoder adopts a fully unfolded parallel pipeline architecture. This configuration enables exceptionally high data throughput for short codes (), as each layer of computation is processed in parallel, significantly enhancing system efficiency. However, this approach incurs substantial hardware resource consumption, particularly in terms of storage and high-speed computation requirements. Consequently, this architecture is ill-suited for QKD post-processing, where the hardware resource demands exceed the practical needs of QKD.

To address this trade-off, we propose a design in which and its left and right child nodes () are selected as the critical nodes for module-level pipelining. This strategy decomposes the decoder into four independent -bit sub-decoders, maintaining an efficient pipeline structure while mitigating hardware resource usage. The proposed architecture facilitates the concurrent reception and processing of up to four frames of data, striking a balance between high throughput and reduced hardware overhead. Its timing behavior, illustrated in Fig. 6, demonstrates the effective parallel processing and scheduling of data flow, thereby enhancing the efficiency and stability of decoding for long codes.

Memory storage architecture

The IR scheme presented in this paper utilizes a block size of . In practical hardware implementations, on-chip storage alone is insufficient to meet the storage demands. To address this, we propose a hybrid storage architecture that combines on-chip resources (BRAM, LUTs) with off-chip DDR to balance the trade-off between storage capacity and resource utilization, thereby accommodating the requirements for large-scale data processing.

Storage resources are allocated primarily based on the needs for LLR and partial sum storage, with a hierarchical optimization design that accounts for computation frequency and data scale. For LLR storage, registers are employed at levels to maximize access speed, given the high computation frequency and frequent memory access. This design minimizes data access latency, enhancing computational efficiency. For levels , on-chip BRAM is utilized to meet the moderate computation and storage demands. As the LLR data scale increases for levels , the capacity of on-chip BRAM is exceeded, necessitating the use of off-chip DDR storage to accommodate the larger storage requirements.

For partial sum storage, optimizations are applied based on computation frequency and data width. At levels , registers are similarly used to ensure rapid data access. For , although the computation frequency of partial sums decreases, the data width is only 1 bit, resulting in a lower storage demand compared to LLR data. Thus, on-chip BRAM is employed for partial sum storage.

This hybrid storage scheme efficiently utilizes on-chip resources while reducing memory access latency, significantly improving overall decoding performance. By balancing storage tasks across BRAM, LUTs, and off-chip DDR, the architecture achieves an optimal trade-off between resource utilization and performance, facilitating the efficient implementation of large-scale decoding.

Post-processing

In the complete post-processing module, the primary task of the decoder module is to generate Bob’s final secure key, while the CRC module verifies whether the generated key matches the CRC tag sent by Alice, thereby confirming the correctness of the current frame’s reconciliation.

The observed trade-off between efficiency and throughput has a direct impact on the secret-key rates of QKD systems, which essentially involves a multidimensional optimization problem between the reconciliation efficiency f, the frame error rate (FER), and the IR throughput. Multi-round reconciliation mechanism brings the reconciliation efficiency f closer to the Shannon limit. However, increasing comes at the cost of linearly increasing the number of decodes of the polar codes and increasing the initial FER, which leads to a reduction in IR throughput. This mechanism ultimately creates a dynamic equilibrium of key rates at the system level.

Conclusions

This paper presents a high-performance hardware accelerator for polar codes-based IR in QKD systems, achieving high throughput and scalability for large-scale data processing. The core of the accelerator utilizes a SCL decoder integrated with a CRC module to ensure accurate decoding and key consistency during the secure reconciliation process. To optimize performance, the design incorporates both computational and storage enhancements. A module-level pipeline architecture is employed to parallelize data frame processing, significantly improving throughput. Additionally, a fully unfolded fast decoding method exploits the characteristics of frozen bits to eliminate redundant computations, enhancing speed and efficiency. The storage system utilizes a hybrid architecture that combines on-chip and off-chip resources, reducing on-chip storage requirements and ensuring optimal system performance. Experimental results demonstrate that, with a block length of , the FPGA-based implementation achieves a throughput of 35.33 Mbps, meeting the real-time demands of QKD post-processing. This design offers a practical and efficient hardware solution for IR based on polar codes, providing reliable and scalable support for large-scale QKD systems.

In future work, we plan to make improvements in two crucial aspects. Firstly, we aim to enhance data interaction efficiency by researching more effective data compression and decompression algorithms. Secondly, by overcoming the inherent order dependency problem of bit-by-bit decoding, we will further parallelize the decoding architecture to achieve higher throughput. Moreover, considering that reconciliation efficiency tends to degrade when the actual QBER does not align perfectly with the design point of the chosen polar codes, we will focus on optimizing the algorithm to reduce efficiency loss caused by such mismatches.

Surface seal image dataset of sterile barrier packaging
Authors: Julio Zanon Diaz, Peter Corcoran
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This manuscript delineates the constitution of a dataset engineered to bolster research endeavours in the realm of automated inspection of seals pertinent to Medical Device Packaging. The compendium encompasses a total of 1200 images of medical device pouches, with an equitable distribution between intact seals and those exhibiting defects. Each image boasts dimensions of 3008 by 4110 by 3 pixels. Accompanying the visual data is a suite of metadata essential for the importation of images into processing software. The defects within the dataset were meticulously crafted through a process of analysis and replication of imperfections commonly encountered on manufacturing lines, with the invaluable insights of seasoned inspectors and the support of Boston Scientific. The acquisition of images was conducted within a bespoke rig, meticulously designed to mirror the conditions prevalent in manufacturing environments. In addition to the imagery, the dataset contains binary labels and segmentation masks that delineate the defects, thereby facilitating the seamless utilization of the dataset for the training and validation of Machine Learning algorithms.

Background

The dataset was meticulously curated to bolster research in the domain of automated inspection of seals for Medical Device Packaging.

To adhere to stringent regulatory mandates, in-vivo medical devices necessitate sterilization, with the medical packaging serving as a protective barrier. Consequently, the integrity of the sterile-barrier bag's seal emerges as a critical attribute, one that could potentially impact product quality and patient safety in the event of compromise. This underscores the necessity for production personnel to conduct thorough inspections of all seals.

Given the Medical Device industry's elevated quality standards, processes are meticulously engineered to minimize defect occurrence. Hence, to compile relevant quantities of defected product, seal defects were artificially generated, drawing upon the expertise of seasoned inspectors and replicating manufacturing conditions. This endeavour was facilitated by Boston Scientific, a leading entity among the top 10 Medical Device manufacturers.

The initial phase of research was confined to the application of extant Machine Learning models, thereby setting a benchmark for the performance of future novel Machine Learning and other computer vision techniques. By granting public access to the dataset, it empowers the research community to devise novel methodologies and benchmark them against the pre-existing ones.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

Method

Figure below shows the methodology used to create the dataset of images.

Before collecting the images, Boston Scientific's line engineers at the Galway facility shared their expertise on the most common defects that occur in manufacturing. The defects were classified and ranked according to their effect on the product and their detection urgency. This evaluation selected “Seal peel-off from the outside of the seal” as the focus of the study and the dataset creation. This defect happens because of manual or automated manipulation of the pouch and are hard to avoid.

To create the samples we used a material combination of Tyvek® 1073B with HCW-CR27 heat seal coating. With a specified porosity of 200 gurley seconds and a specified seal strength of 130 N/m over a seal surface of 225.6 mm ± 3 mm by 12.7 mm ± 3 mm.

Defects were created by manually peeling-off a small section along the seal. The position and size of the defect along the seal was randomly selected around the right-hand side, the centre and the left-hand side with a combination of full and half depth.

Prior to the image acquisition, the line inspectors at Boston Scientific's Galway facility imparted their extensive knowledge regarding the prevalent defects encountered in manufacturing. These defects were meticulously categorised and prioritised based on their impact on the product and the immediacy required for their detection. This evaluative process identified the defect characterised as “Seal peel-off from the outside of the seal” as the focal point for the study and subsequent dataset creation. This particular defect arises due to either manual or automated handling of the pouch and presents a challenge to mitigate.

For the generation of the samples, a specific material composition was utilised, comprising Tyvek® 1073B paired with HCW-CR27 heat seal coating. This combination was chosen for its defined porosity of 200 Gurley seconds and a specified seal strength of 130 N/m, which is characteristic of most medical device packaging. The defects were artificially induced by manually peeling off a minor section along the seal. The location and dimensions of the defect were arbitrarily determined, with selections made from the right-hand side, the middle, and the left-hand side, incorporating variations of full and partial depth. This method aims to reproduce the defects as observed in a real-world scenario.

Limitations

The dataset is comprised solely of the “Seal peel-off from the outside of the seal” defect type, as this particular defect was deemed a priority for the study based on its impact and likelihood of occurrence. It is important to note, however, that the dataset does not encompass other defect categories also present in manufacturing, such as “Seal peel-off from the inside of the seal,” “Large air bubbles trapped within the seal area,” “Wrinkles,” and “Cracks.”

Although the images were captured utilising a monochrome camera to enhance sensor density and achieve superior resolution, they were archived in 3-channel files to accommodate the requirements of most predefined Convolutional Neural Networks employed in the original study, which necessitated 3-channel inputs. Consequently, all three channels of the image are identical replicas of one another.

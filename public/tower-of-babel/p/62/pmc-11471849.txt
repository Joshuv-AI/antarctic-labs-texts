An integrated binary metaheuristic approach in dynamic unit commitment and economic emission dispatch for hybrid energy systems
Authors: S Syama, J Ramprabhakar, R Anand, Josep M. Guerrero
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The current generation portfolio is obligated to incorporate zero-emissions energy sources, predominantly wind and solar, due to the depletion of fossil fuels and the alarming rate of global warming. In the current scenario, power engineers must devise a compromised solution that not only advocates for the adoption of renewable energy sources (RES) but also efficiently schedules all conventional power generation units to balance the increasing load demand while simultaneously minimizing fuel costs and harmful emissions that are currently addressed by Unit Commitment (UC) and Combined Economic Emission Dispatch (CEED) problem solutions. However, the integration of renewable energy resources (RES) further complicates the UC-CEED problem due to their intermittent nature. Recently, metaheuristic algorithms are acquiring momentum in resolving constrained UC-CEED problems due to their improved global solution ability, adaptability, and derivative-free construction. In this research, a computationally efficient binary hybrid version of crow search algorithm and improvised grey wolf optimization is proposed, namely Crow Search Improved Binary Grey Wolf Optimization Algorithm (CS-BIGWO) by inclusion of nonlinear control parameter, weight-based position updating, and mutation approach. Statistical results on standard mathematical functions prove the supremacy of the proposed algorithm over conventional algorithms. Further, a novel optimization strategy is devised by integrating enhanced lambda iteration with the CS-BIGWO algorithm (CS-BIGWO-\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document}λ) to solve a day-ahead UC-CEED problem of the hybrid energy system incorporating cost functions of RES. For the model, a day-ahead forecast of wind power and solar photovoltaic power is obtained by using the Levy-Flight Chaotic Whale Optimization Algorithm optimized Extreme Learning Machines(LCWOA-ELM). The proposed algorithm is tested for the UC-CEED solution of an IEEE-39 bus system with two distinct cases: (1) without RES integration and (2) with RES integration. Several independent trial runs are executed, and the performance of the algorithms is assessed based on optimal UC schedules, fuel cost, emission quantization, convergence curve, and computational time. For case 1, the proposed algorithm resulted in a percentage reduction of 0.1021% in fuel cost and 0.7995% in emission. In contrast, for test case 2, it resulted in a percentage reduction of 0.12896% in fuel cost and 0.772% in emission with the proposed algorithm. The results validate the dominance of the proposed methodology over existing methods in terms of lower fuel costs and emissions.

Introduction

Motivation

Electricity generation costs are a major concern for power system management due to rising demand and electricity generation prices. The interconnectivity of distributed electrical networks makes it crucial to reduce electricity generation cost that significantly impacts the power grid’s economy. To accomplish this crucial goal, various power system operation challenges are formulated1,2, out of which Unit Commitment and Economic Dispatch (UC-ED) occupies a significant position. In a conventional power grid, the primary generators of electric power are thermal power facilities. Diverse thermal generating units produce the same quantity of electricity using varying amounts of fuel, as indicated by their fuel-cost curves. Thus, the primary task of reducing fuel costs is accomplished with the UC-ED technique by allocating various generating units meeting the load demand by optimally distributing among the various thermal units. The production of energy from fossil fuels is a significant contributor to atmospheric pollution due to the emission of various harmful chemicals. Studies prove that fossil fuel-based electricity generation accounts for 40% of global CO2 emissions. There have been numerous revisions made to the Clean Air Act (1990) to address the ever-increasing environmental pollution, and this crucial issue has garnered significant attention because of the intense public demand for a pollution-free environment. This has forced utilities to change the way they are designed or administered to reduce air emissions and pollution from thermal plants. As the thermal power units significantly influence the levels of these pollutant gas emissions, the generation allocation problem must be resolved in a way that minimizes fuel costs and emissions concurrently3–5. It is important to highlight the inherent contradiction between the two objectives. By establishing a trade-off between these two objectives, a compromised solution can be achieved in resolving the case of economic emission dispatch, referred to as Combined Economic Emission Dispatch (CEED). So, UC-CEED is a combinatorial, dual-objective nonlinear optimization problem that requires efforts to optimally commit and schedule conventional thermal units by minimizing fuel consumption and emissions to meet load demand and losses while adhering to all system constraints. This complex constrained power system problem should be carefully resolved by some efficient optimization strategies6. Renewable energy sources (RES) are now experiencing a significant and sustainable increase in usage, primarily due to environmental concerns and the exhaustion of fossil fuel reserves. Furthermore, the widespread integration of RES further complicates the UC-CEED problem due to its intermittent nature. To mitigate the unpredictability of RES in dispatch problems, researchers have extensively investigated both the source and load sides7. The stochastic probability distribution function for renewable energy sources (RES) modelling considers the intermittent characteristics of renewable (wind, solar) power output. This model takes into consideration various parameters such as direct cost, and underestimation and overestimation penalty cost, which consider the utilization of wind and solar power output8–10. To enhance the precision of power generation estimates, a reliable forecast model is utilized to develop solar and wind power predictions. This model predicts the wind speed and daily irradiance for the subsequent day11,12. To mitigate the uncertainty due to large-scale renewable energy sources (RES) integration, the scheduling model also incorporates flexible loads on the load side13,14. Therefore, it is crucial and a subject of significant fascination for numerous scholars to develop computationally efficient forecast models that can precisely anticipate wind speed and solar irradiation over short and medium time intervals to effectively represent the characteristics of solar-wind power systems.

Literature review

In recent decades, several mathematics based conventional methods15–21 are extensively used to solve UC-CEED problem effectively. Nevertheless, the efficacy of these traditional methods in resolving the CEED problem is compromised by their prolonged computation time and inability to manage the inequality constraints. In recent years, scientists have utilized the behaviour of insect or animal species to address a range of optimization problems with complex constraints. Research demonstrates that these computationally intelligent metaheuristic optimization algorithms are highly effective in addressing complex real-world issues in various domains such as engineering, research, medicine, and space exploration.The preference for metaheuristic optimization techniques over conventional mathematical methods in solving UC-CEED problem is generally motivated by the necessity to effectively address large-scale, non-linear, and constrained problems. Despite the lack of assurance for global optimality, authors advocate for the use of metaheuristics by highlighting their flexibility, scalability, proficiency in managing intricate constraints, and capability to circumvent local optima. To guarantee the quality of the derived solutions, author conduct numerous iterations, optimize parameters, compare against established solutions, or integrate with exact methods, demonstrating that the metaheuristic approach produces near-optimal and realistically applicable solutions.

Several non-conventional metaheuristic algorithms have been implemented to address the UC CEED problem22–29 to address the above mentioned limitations of traditional methods, As an illustration, the efficacy of multi-objective ant lion optimizer(MOALO) in solving the dynamic CEED problems of a ten unit test hybrid energy system is discussed in the paper22. In the paper23, the author introduces a modified ABC algorithm with a grenade exploding mechanism and Cauchy operator with improved search ability for addressing the dynamic economic emission dispatch (DEED) issue with valve point,prohibited operating zones (POZ) constraints and Ramp rate constraints. An enhanced local search technique integrated ABC algorithm (ABCLS) is used in the paper24 to solve the complex ED problem incorporating network loss and POZ. In the paper25, an advanced biogeography-based optimization (CBBO) method is tested to handle the stochastic CEED problem of hybrid energy system with wind farms. Guesmi et al.26 used a unique chaotic sine-cosine algorithm (CSCA) to optimize the dispatch schedule of wind farm integrated ten-unit and 40-unit test systems, while concurrently minimizing fuel costs and emissions. The MOPSO algorithm was introduced in the paper27 for solving CEED in a renewable integrated power system. Although these metaheuristic algorithms can produce an optimal result for the CEED problem, they do not offer a guarantee for the absolute best solution due to exploration exploitation imbalance of single algorithms.

Conclusively it is indispensable to strike a balance between intensification (exploiting the most promising solutions identified so far) and diversification (exploring the search space) to develop or apply a meta-heuristic algorithm. In this domain, the development of a hybrid algorithm that combines multiple strategies to improve overall performance is an innovative and effective method for addressing global optimization problems to improve optimization efficiency and solution quality. In the papers28,29, the CEED problem with ramp-spinning reserve constraints, is addressed using a hybrid Genetic Algorithm-Whale Optimization Algorithm (GA-WOA) and MGAIPSO (Multi-Objective Genetic Algorithm with Improved Particle Swarm Optimization), respectively. However, the multi-area economic dispatch problem was unable to be addressed with the earlier algorithm. To address this the paper30 proposed a hybrid JAYA-TLBO algorithm which shows improved computational efficiency. The paper31 devised a BA-PSO algorithm, to solve power scheduling problems with thermal power plants and RES. The fuel cost function is added with a penalty for wind power output overestimation and underestimate in this formulation. The hybrid bacterial foraging-Nelder-Mead algorithm (BF-NM), devised by Hooshmand et al.32, integrates the BF and NM techniques to model the CEED problem with frequency deviation and spinning reserve constraints. In the paper33, Liang et al. successfully addressed the CEED problem with power flow constraints by employing a multi-objective hybrid bat optimization strategy. Murugan et al.34 tackled the problem of non convex CEED by employing CSA-BA-ABC, a search method that combines ABC and BA with CSA where Chaos-based search has improved the local searchability and convergence of the algorithm. Sen et al.35 introduced a hybrid optimization approach that combines Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), Artificial Bee Colony (ABC), and Harmony Search (HS) algorithms to address the issue of Economic Dispatch (ED). Bhattacharya et.al.36 employed DE-BBO, a combined optimization method combining DE and BBO for CEED solution. Detailed survey proves the efficacy of the hybrid metaheuristic algorithms over conventional single algorithms in solving complex constrained UC-CEED problems.

Crow search algorithm (CSA) and Grey wolf optimization (GWO), two recently created algorithms, have already proven to be superior and strong in addressing engineering problems with more dimensions and complex restrictions. The CSA algorithm37 is a newly proposed metaheuristic approach that imitates the intelligent actions of crows. CSA is particularly good at avoiding local optima in multi modal, high-dimensional complex problems. Conversely, the local search approach employed by CSA is not very effective. However, GWO38 is modelled on the behaviour and hunting mechanism of grey wolves. This method shows a strong capacity in convergence towards the optimal solution leading to avoidance of local optima. In general, GWO promotes robust exploitation while being marginally deficient in exploration. These constraints become especially apparent when confronted with complex, high-dimensional optimization issues. Comparatively to numerous alternative algorithms, CSA can manage tremendous populations and generate superior results more rapidly without becoming trapped in local minima when GWO is proficient at carrying out extensive searches within its broad search field.

Researchers have proposed four categories of modified GWO algorithms addressing these problems such as update mechanisms, operators, individual encoding schemes, and population structure/hierarchy. Modifying the update mechanism involves two approaches-dynamically changing GWO control parameters and presenting a new position-updating equation39. An exponential decay function is suggested by Mittal et. al.40 as a new control parameter to enhance GWO’s global exploration. In41, the researchers have explained in detail the merits of GWO, whereas the paper42 incorporated random weight coefficients in GWO by modifying the position updating model and averted local optimization. To improve the above-mentioned model, the researchers incorporated a modified position updating model utilising both the local and global best positions along with the non-linear control parameter modification43. This approach gave more accurate solutions, faster convergence and shorter fitness function evaluation duration in various experiments. The researchers also contrived and developed an altered version of GWO which could incorporate crossover operator and employed evolutionary population dynamics (EPD) that randomly discard individual with low fitness and position44,45. The authors in the paper46,47 developed QLGCTSA with enhanced iteration through utilisation of Gaussian mutation and Cauchy mutation and Levy flight. In brief, the GWO and CSA techniques are appropriate for hybridisation due to their unique abilities. In this research work, a hybrid algorithm incorporating the merits of enhanced version of GWO and CSA is devised to accomplish a better trade off between diversification and intensification that greatly increases convergence speed and accuracy of solutions compared to GWO and CSA. Such a hybrid metaheuristic algorithm integrates the advantages of global metaheuristic search with local classical search which enhances performance in large-scale optimization challenges such as unit commitment and economic dispatch by delivering high-quality, near-optimal solutions efficiently while addressing complex constraints.

In this work ,the hybrid metaheuristic algorithm is integrated with Lambda Iteration provides an efficient solution for intricate UC-CEED proplem, especially in large scale power systems. This method utilizes binary version of hybrid bio-inspired algorithm to address the UC problem by conducting a global search of the solution space to ascertain the best on/off status of generators while effectively managing non-linear constraints. Upon establishing the unit commitment solution, the Lambda Iteration approach is utilized to address the CEED problem by optimizing the power output of the committed generators to reduce fuel costs and emissions, while guaranteeing that the power supply satisfies demand with equitable incremental costs among all generators. This hybrid method leverages the strengths of metaheuristics in handling complex, large-scale problems and the precision of Lambda Iteration in local optimization. Consequently, it offers a pragmatic and effective method for identifying near-optimal solutions within a reasonable timeframe, rendering it particularly appropriate for complex UC-CEED problem in power systems.

In the context of UC-CEED problem for hybrid energy system with renewable energy integration, an accurate day ahead renewable energy prediction is essential for which forecast models48–52 outperform stochastic probability density function (PDF) models53,54. Forecast models use historical data and powerful algorithms to make exact, real-time projections about renewable energy generation, resulting in more accurate and reliable dispatch decisions. These models are computationally efficient, allowing for quick and seamless integration into current dispatch systems, which is critical for grid stability and effective demand management. Furthermore, clear and detailed forecasts from forecast models ease system operators’ decision-making processes while maintaining regulatory and market compliance. While stochastic PDF models efficiently capture long-term uncertainties and changes, their probabilistic nature can complicate short-term operational choices and requires more complex processing resources. Several studies have concentrated on developing a computationally efficient prediction model to accurately estimate renewable energy sources (RES) for effective planning, management, and control of power systems. In the past two decades, academics have dedicated significant effort to developing various prediction techniques with the aim of enhancing forecast precision.

Methodology

Mathematical modelling of the UC-CEED problem

Unit Commitment (UC) and Combined Economic Emission Dispatch (CEED) are two related short-term power system optimization problems. UC is a method of determining when to turn on or off thermal units to reduce operational costs and emissions over a certain time frame. The UC sub problem considers the balance of load demand with sufficient thermal units adhering to reduction in operational costs and emissions with all technical and security regulations6. The large number of possible operational states (on and off) for all thermal units in the power system across the time frame further complicates the unit commitment. On the other hand, CEED is a bi-objective optimization problem that estimates the active power output for the committed conventional thermal units in order to meet all operational constraints and load demand in the most efficient manner while minimizing fuel cost and pollution levels55.

Wind and PV power forecasting

Wind and photovoltaic (PV) power have inherent variations and uncertainties owing to weather dependence. Wind and solar units are typically classified as non-schedulable, meaning that their power output is limited to the wind speed and solar irradiation available. Developing a computationally efficient prediction model capable of reliably forecasting wind speed and solar irradiation will result in predictable data for wind and PV power using power estimate methods. The accuracy of the dispatch results will be greatly affected by using deterministic wind and PV power data to address the UC-CEED.

For the modelling purpose, the study uses one-year hourly average wind speed and solar irradiance data collected from a meteorological station situated in Kasavanahalli, Bengaluru, India52. The paper52proposes the Lévy-flight Chaotic Whale Optimization Algorithm optimized Extreme learning machines (LCWOA -ELM) framework for wind speed and solar irradiance forecasting. The introduction of Lévy fight (LF) in the exploration stage results in occasional significant leaps and frequent modest movements, which widen the exploration region and improve overall exploration capabilities. Using the chaotic map in the WOA control parameters speeds up convergence and improves search performance. In this work, LCWOA algorithm is used to fine ELM hyperparameters. Once an optimized ELM model is obtained, one day ahead hourly wind speed and solar irradiance are predicted using recursive forecasting methodology, as depicted in the Figure 1.Figure 1One day ahead hourly forecast of Wind speed and Solar Irradiance by LCWOA-ELM framework.

Wind power estimation from wind power curve modelling

In order to accurately estimate the power output of a wind turbine, it is typically necessary to develop a model that illustrates the wind power curve and the relationship between wind speed and power production. Precise wind power curve modeling is crucial in applications that require wind power forecasting. Non-parametric methods offer a convenient method for estimating wind power output using wind speed data, without relying on any assumptions regarding the functional relationship or underlying distribution between wind speed and power output. A non-parametric technique based on Support Vector Machine (SVM) is employed in this study to estimate wind power with precision and reliability, thereby improving the efficient utilization of wind energy resources. To directly optimize the modeling process, the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) can be implemented to fine-tune the hyperparameters of the Support Vector Machine (SVM) model. Data acquisition

This study utilized a real-time one-year dataset recorded in 10-minute intervals by the wind turbine SCADA system spanning from January to December 2018 obtained from the Kaggle repository (https://www.kaggle.com/datasets). The datasets consist of wind speed, generated wind power, and wind direction.

Data-preprocessing

A three-step preprocessing approach is adopted for power curve estimation59. The initial stage involves missing data correction in the raw dataset. The second phase is eliminating four categories of outliers, as elucidated in the paper59. Initially, any negative wind power values are eliminated from the dataset. Next, a filter design approach that operates in two distinct phases is defined. The wind power values are initially divided into numerous smaller intervals, which are referred to as “data frames,” during the initial stage. Based on the scatter diagram of the dataset, the quantity of these data frames can be adjusted. During the second stage, a process is implemented on each data frame, which involves the selection of a specific range of data points as “normal data points” based on their wind speed values. The wind speed values of any outliers that are uncommonly low or high are subsequently eliminated. To achieve this, a minimum and maximum threshold for wind speed values in each data frame are established. Using the Min-Max scaler, the data is normalized to fall within the range of 0 to 1 after all outliers have been removed. The normalized data is divided into two phases: the training phase uses 80% of the data, while the testing phase uses the remaining 20%.

Proposed Hybrid Crow search Improved Grey wolf Optimization Algorithm (Hybrid CS-IGWO algorithm)

Grey Wolf Optimization (GWO) is a recently developed meta-heuristic optimization algorithm introduced by Mirjalili et al.38 in 2014. The leadership style and hunting behaviors of grey wolves (Canis lupus) in their natural habitat are the foundation of this optimization technique. Their social dominance hierarchy is highly structured, comprising four distinct subgroups dominated by Alpha (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document}α) followed by beta (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\beta$$\end{document}β), delta (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\delta$$\end{document}δ), and omega (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega$$\end{document}ω) wolves respectively. Grey wolves exhibit a unique social behavior known as collaborative hunting in addition to the social hierarchy which is mathematically modelled in GWO38.Similarly the Crow search algorithm37, is a metaheuristic algorithm introduced by AskarZadeh in 2016 that utilizes the crow’s intelligent search process for concealing their food and recalling the hiding position.

The conventional GWO algorithm and Crow search algorithm (CSA) has garnered considerable interest owing to its straightforward implementation, limited number of parameters, and robust convergence capabilities. Comparatively to numerous alternative algorithms, CSA can manage tremendous populations and generate superior results more rapidly without becoming trapped in local minima when GWO is proficient at carrying out extensive searches within its broad search field. However, practical optimization efforts sometimes face challenges such as slow convergence rates and a tendency to get stuck in local minima. Numerous researchers have improved GWO in order to improve the optimization capabilities of the algorithm by striking a better balance between exploration and exploitation. This section introduces Crow search Improved Grey wolf Optimization (CS-IGWO) Algorithm, a new hybrid variant of GWO and CSA that improves performance. The suggested CS-IGWO algorithm relies on the following concepts:

Mutation strategy

In succeeding iterations of the GWO algorithm, the grey wolves gradually converge towards the alpha wolf, resulting in a reduced variety in the local search and an early convergence of the algorithm. In the context of evolutionary computation, mutation plays a vital role as an operator that enhances population diversity by producing offspring from parental individuals. Gaussian and Cauchy mutations are common strategies used in evolutionary computation. Cauchy mutation is more effective in escaping local optima, while Gaussian mutation excels in local convergence.

This study employs the Cauchy-Gaussian mutation47 operator to augment the variety of leader wolves and raise their ability for local searches, with the objective of enhancing population diversity. Conclusion

The UC-CEED is a complex multi-objective problem that requires resolution by an efficient optimization algorithm. This work introduces a novel binary hybrid algorithm, the Crow Search Improved Binary Grey Wolf Optimization Technique (CS-BIGWO), to tackle the unit commitment subproblem. The use of nonlinear control parameters, weight-based position updates, and mutation techniques demonstrates that the conventional hybrid CS-BIGWO significantly outperforms in addressing complex optimization challenges. This research addresses the complex UC-CEED problem within a hybrid energy system that integrates wind and solar power generation, utilizing the proposed CS-BIGWO algorithm in conjunction with Enhanced Lambda Iteration (CS-BIGWO-\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\lambda$$\end{document}λ). The research examines an IEEE 39-bus 10-unit hybrid power system (HPS) incorporating thermal and renewable energy sources (wind and photovoltaic) to address CEED, mitigate greenhouse gas emissions, and decrease generation costs utilizing the proposed methodology. The two distinct scenarios-one incorporating RES and the other excluding it-have been employed to illustrate the efficacy and superiority of the suggested strategy in addressing the UC-CEED problem in a hybrid system. In each instance, the fuel cost and emissions of the CEED problem are computed using the suggested methodology and compared with the outcomes of other established techniques. The proposed algorithm yielded a markedly superior reduction in costs and emissions across all scenarios, illustrating its effectiveness and superiority compared to existing methods in both instances. The research findings augment the environmental and economic benefits of the power system and offer innovative approaches for developing dynamic emission dispatch strategies for the integrated wind-solar-thermal energy system. Although the suggested approach yields exceptional dispatch results, this study possesses notable limitations that require further study in subsequent research.The impact of the uncertainty of RES is not analysed in the present work. Incorporating an uncertainty model can result in the adoption of more cautious unit commitment plans that allocate a larger number of conventional units as a precautionary measure against anticipated deficiencies in renewable energy generation.To address this uncertainty and unpredictability of RES, it is necessary to have extra reserve capacity, including both spinning and non-spinning reserves like energy storage devices. The effect of energy storage devices on UC-CEED problems in hybrid energy systems has not been considered.

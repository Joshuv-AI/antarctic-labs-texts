Selection of induction chemotherapy cycles for stage N3 nasopharyngeal carcinoma based on pre-treatment plasma EBV DNA
Authors: Youliang Weng, Sunqin Cai, Chao Li, Yun Xu, Yuhui Pan, Zongwei Huang, Ying Li, Zijie Wu, Yu Chen, Sufang Qiu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to explore the selection of induction chemotherapy (IC) cycles for stage N3 nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). We employed propensity score matching (PSM) to categorize patients into 3-cycle and 4-cycle IC groups (IC = 3 and IC = 4). The log-rank and chi-squared tests were used respectively to evaluate the differences in survival and acute toxicities. Survival outcomes including overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS), and locoregional relapse-free survival (LRRFS) were evaluated among the two groups. After PSM, each group comprised 99 patients. The IC = 4 group exhibited markedly improved survival outcomes compared with the IC = 3 group. Multivariate analysis revealed that pre-EBV DNA was an independent risk factor affecting PFS and DMFS. For high-risk patients with pre-EBV DNA ≥ 7800 copies/ml, the IC = 4 group demonstrated greater survival compared to the IC = 3 group. Among low-risk patients with pre-EBV DNA < 7800 copies/ml, both groups showed comparable survival outcomes. In terms of acute adverse reactions, the IC = 4 group experienced higher incidences, particularly with grade 2–4 alanine transaminase elevation and thrombocytopenia. For stage N3 NPC, pre-EBV DNA could be a powerful predictor for guiding the selection of IC cycles. The IC = 4 regimen is probably more beneficial to high-risk patients due to superior survival, while for low-risk patients, the IC = 3 regimen may be sufficient.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75396-z.

Background

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), a malignancy originating from the epithelial cells of nasopharyngeal mucosa, is notably prevalent in Southeast Asia, with particularly high rates in southern China and Eastern Asia1,2. In its early stages, NPC often presents with nonspecific symptoms, which can result in delayed recognition and underestimation of its severity. Approximately 70% of patients are initially diagnosed with locoregionally advanced nasopharyngeal carcinoma (LA-NPC)3. Concurrent chemoradiotherapy (CCRT) is the standard treatment for these patients, being the primary therapeutic approach3–5. However, about 30% of patients still experience local recurrence or distant metastasis after standard treatment, which is a major cause of treatment failure and poses a significant challenge to the clinical management of NPC6.

In recent years, the use of induction chemotherapy (IC) before curative radiotherapy has been widely proven to be feasible, and large randomized controlled clinical trials have shown that the use of IC before CCRT can further improve the survival of LA-NPC7–9. Common IC regimens recommended by the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines include gemcitabine + cisplatin, and docetaxel + cisplatin. For stage N3 NPC patients, the 5-year distant metastasis-free survival (DMFS) is only about 60-70% due to the high tumor burden and higher likelihood of distant metastasis10,11. Micrometastases already existed before treatment often cannot be accurately detected by conventional imaging methods12. CCRT primarily enhances therapeutic effects on regional lesions, but it may be insufficient to effectively control the distant metastases, which may require more intensive systemic treatment13. IC significantly contributes to the elimination of potential micrometastases, thereby reducing the risk of distant metastasis and improving long-term survival. Previous studies indicated that the combination of IC and CCRT improved 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) in stage N3 NPC compared to CCRT alone. However, this study did not further explore the impact of different IC cycles on the prognosis14. At present, it is crucial to explore the value of IC cycles for stage N3 NPC patients.

Currently, the tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system is widely used for developing treatment plans and evaluating prognosis in NPC patients. However, this approach may not be considered satisfactory, because patients with similar TNM stages have shown varied responses to identical treatments due to tumor heterogeneity. It is crucial to search for more effective prognostic biomarkers to further guide clinical treatment. Numerous studies have indicated that Epstein-Barr virus DNA (EBV DNA) can be used to monitor and predict survival outcomes for patients15–17. The pre-treatment plasma EBV DNA (pre-EBV DNA) is widely used in clinical practice and holds important clinical value for disease monitoring, population screening, prognostic prediction, and guiding personalized treatment strategies18,19. For instance, the utilization of pre-EBV DNA could potentially aid in identifying individualized IC regimens for LA-NPC17. Consequently, we conducted this retrospective investigation to categorize patients with stage N3 NPC by risk using pre-EBV DNA, classifying individuals into high-risk and low-risk groups. According to survival prognosis, we then screened for patients who could benefit from receiving either 3-cycle or 4-cycle IC (IC = 3 or IC = 4).

Methods

Patients and clinical specimens

The data for the enrolled patients was extracted between June 2016 and December 2021. Two senior doctors restaged the clinical stage for each patient according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer/Union for International Cancer Control (AJCC/UICC) Classification (8th edition, 2017). The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) standard was employed to confirm the performance status. The primary inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) individuals aged 18 to 75 years; (2) pathologically confirmed NPC of WHO types II or III; (3) stage TanyN3M0; (4) ECOG performance status of 0–1; (5) received 3-cycle or 4-cycle IC followed by CCRT. The primary exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) previous or simultaneous occurrence of other malignant tumors; (2) incomplete clinical data and loss of follow-up; (3) significant impairment of heart, liver, kidney, lung function, or presence of serious comorbidities.

This study was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of the Fujian Cancer Hospital (Fujian, China) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was exempted due to the retrospective design. Additionally, all methods were performed in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations.

The plasma EBV DNA level

The measurement of plasma EBV DNA samples was determined through the real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) technology, involving the amplification of the BamHI-W fragment region of the EBV genome20. The detection result of the plasma EBV DNA per milliliter was expressed as copy number (copies/ml). In this study, if plasma EBV DNA could not be detected via RT-qPCR technology, it was recorded as 0 copy/ml.

Clinical treatment

Chemotherapy

In this study, patients were given IC = 3 or IC = 4 combined with CCRT. Platinum-based IC regimens included gemcitabine with cisplatin (GP, gemcitabine 1 g/m2 intravenously on days 1 and 8; cisplatin 80–100 mg/m2 intravenously over 3 days), paclitaxel or docetaxel with cisplatin (TP, paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 intravenously on day 1 or docetaxel 60–75 mg/m2 intravenously on day 1; cisplatin 80–100 mg/m2 intravenously over 3 days), with treatments administered every 3 weeks. The study-defined concurrent chemotherapy regimen consisted solely of cisplatin. For patients with contraindications who could not receive cisplatin, other platinum agents such as carboplatin or nedaplatin were selected.

Intensity-modulated radiotherapy

The technique and dose of intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) were described in a previously published paper in detail21. The gross tumor volume (GTV), comprising the primary nasopharyngeal tumor (GTV-T) and metastatic lymph nodes of the neck (GTV-N), received a total dose of 66–76 Gy in 30–35 fractions once a day, 5 days per week. Critical normal structures included the brainstem, spinal cord, parotid glands, and optic nerves. The dose limits for all normal structures adhered to the RTOG 0225 protocol.

Outcomes

The final follow-up was performed in June 2023. Both long-term survival data and acute adverse events (AEs) during IC were collected. The National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE, version 5.0) were utilized to record and assess AEs. The overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from diagnosis to death from any cause. PFS, DMFS, and locoregional relapse-free survival (LRRFS) were defined as the time from the initial diagnosis to the occurrence of disease progression, distant metastasis, or locoregional relapse, respectively.

Follow-up strategy

After the completion of anti-tumor therapy, patients were followed up regularly. The average follow-up duration was 41.5 months, ranging from 8 to 83 months. Patients were followed up at a frequency of every 3 months during the initial 2 years, every 6 months between years 3 and 5, and then annually thereafter. Patients were followed by thorough physical assessments, computed tomography (CT) scans of the chest, ultrasound or CT scans of the abdomen, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the head and neck, and positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) if needed.

Statistical method

To address the observed bias in baseline characteristics, the propensity score matching (PSM) was conducted using a caliper value of 0.05. Pearson’s chi-squared test was employed to compare the differences in baseline characteristics and AEs between IC = 3 and IC = 4 groups. The analysis and comparison of survival were conducted using the Kaplan-Meier method and the log-rank test. To identify potentially independent prognostic factors, both univariate and multivariate analyses were conducted. Receiver operating characteristics (ROC) analysis was used to calculate the cut-off value of pre-EBV DNA for DMFS. For all statistical analyses, we utilized SPSS 26.0 (Chicago) and R software (version 4.3.0). A two-sided P < 0.05 was regarded as statistical significance.

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 369 patients with stage N3 NPC were selected, and their clinical characteristics are presented in Table 1. Overall, 263 (71.3%) patients received IC = 3 and 106 (28.7%) patients received IC = 4. Among all patients, there were 275 (74.5%) males and 94 (25.5%) females. In T stage, the cases of stage T1, T2, T3, T4 accounted for 21.1%, 26.5%, 38.8%, and 13.6%, respectively. The median pre-EBV DNA was 13,600 copies/ml for the IC = 3 group and 14,500 copies/ml for the IC = 4 group, respectively. In the IC regimens, the GP regimen was the most frequently chosen, representing 289 cases (78.3%). Regarding concurrent chemotherapy cycles (CCTC), the majority of patients received 2-cycle concurrent chemotherapy, accounting for 298 cases (80.8%). The results showed significant differences between the IC = 3 and IC = 4 groups in terms of CCTC and IC regimens (P < 0.001), while no significant differences were observed in other clinical features between the two groups (P ≥ 0.05). To balance baseline characteristics, PSM was performed in a 1:1 ratio, with matching variables including IC regimen and CCTC. After matching, there were no significant differences in clinical baseline characteristics between the two groups, as shown in Table 1 (all P ≥ 0.05).

Survival analysis

After conducting PSM, there were 198 patients remaining. The IC = 4 group showed higher 3-year OS (69.7% vs. 54.5%, P = 0.028), PFS (62.6% vs. 46.5%, P = 0.022), DMFS (65.7% vs. 47.5%, P = 0.010), and LRRFS (65.7% vs. 52.5%, P = 0.060) compared to the IC = 3 group. The corresponding survival curves are shown in Fig. 1.

Univariate and multivariate analyses

Previous studies showed a close correlation between pre-EBV DNA and the prognosis of NPC22,23. Based on DMFS, ROC analysis revealed that the cut-off value of pre-EBV DNA was 7800 copies/ml (area under the curve 0.593; sensitivity 0.778; specificity 0.519; Fig. 2).

The age, gender, T stage, pre-EBV DNA, IC regimen, IC cycle, and CCTC are common factors that affect the prognosis of NPC. Univariate analysis was conducted on 198 matched patients, and the results showed that pre-EBV DNA, IC cycle, and CCTC significantly affected the survival prognosis of patients (P < 0.05, Table 2). Then, variables that were statistically significant in the univariate analysis or clinically considered to have an impact on survival prognosis were included for multivariate analysis. The results showed that pre-EBV DNA (≥ 7800 vs. < 7800) was an independent risk factor affecting PFS (HR: 2.578, 95%CI: 1.307–5.086, P = 0.006) and DMFS (HR: 3.573, 95%CI: 1.612–7.921, P = 0.002). IC cycle (4 vs. 3) was an independent prognostic factor for OS (HR: 0.344, 95%CI: 0.135–0.874, P = 0.025), PFS (HR: 0.429, 95%CI: 0.226–0.813, P = 0.010), and DMFS (HR: 0.334, 95%CI: 0.164–0.678, P = 0.002). In addition, CCTC was an independent prognostic factor for DMFS (HR: 0.396, 95%CI: 0.200-0.783, P = 0.008, Table 2).

Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analysis according to pre-EBV DNA

Based on multivariate analysis, pre-EBV DNA (≥ 7800 vs. < 7800) was identified as an independent prognostic factor for DMFS and PFS. We conducted an analysis to determine if the cut-off value of pre-EBV DNA could effectively categorize patients into distinct risk categories. It was evident that patients with pre-EBV DNA < 7800 copies/ml had significantly higher PFS and DMFS than those with pre-EBV DNA ≥ 7800 copies/ml (Additional file 1: Figure S1). The results indicated that the cut-off value of pre-EBV DNA was feasible in predicting the survival prognosis. Patients were subsequently assigned to the low-risk group, with pre-EBV DNA < 7800 copies/ml, or the high-risk group, with pre-EBV DNA ≥ 7800 copies/ml.

Survival analysis of low-risk group

The low-risk group comprised 92 patients, and their baseline characteristics were similar between the IC = 3 and IC = 4 cohorts (Additional file 2, Table S1). We carried out survival analysis for both cohorts, and the results indicated that there were no statistically significant disparities according to the survival curves (all P ≥ 0.05, Fig. 3). Additionally, the IC = 3 cohort showed no significant differences in 3-year OS (56.3% vs. 65.9%, P = 0.343), PFS (54.2% vs. 59.1%, P = 0.634), DMFS (54.2% vs. 63.6%, P = 0.357), and LRRFS (56.3% vs. 59.1%, P = 0.783) compared to IC = 4 cohort. Univariate analysis was conducted by incorporating clinical factors such as age, gender, T stage, IC regimen, IC cycle, and CCTC. The results showed that no variables significantly affected survival outcomes. Multivariate analysis was further performed on variables that were clinically considered to have an impact on prognosis. The results were consistent with those of the univariate analysis. IC cycle (4 vs. 3) was not identified as an independent prognostic factor for OS, PFS, DMFS, and LRRFS (Table 3).

Survival analysis of high-risk groups

The high-risk group comprised 106 patients, and their baseline characteristics were comparable between the IC = 3 and IC = 4 cohorts (Additional file 3, Table S2). According to survival analysis, the IC = 4 cohort showed superior 3-year OS (72.7% vs. 52.9%, P = 0.035), PFS (65.5% vs. 39.2%, P = 0.007), DMFS (67.3% vs. 41.2%, P = 0.007) and LRRFS (70.9% vs. 49.0%, P = 0.021) compared to the IC = 3 cohort. The corresponding survival curves for the two cohorts are shown in Fig. 4, and statistically significant differences were observed in PFS and DMFS. Univariate analysis was conducted by incorporating clinical factors such as age, gender, T stage, IC regimen, IC cycle, and CCTC. The results showed that IC cycle significantly affected PFS and DMFS. Multivariate analysis was conducted on variables that were statistically significant in univariate analysis or clinically considered to have an impact on prognosis (Table 4). The results showed that both IC cycle and CCTC were identified as significant independent prognostic factors for PFS (IC cycle: HR = 0.371, 95%CI: 0.171–0.806, P = 0.012; CCTC: HR = 0.453, 95%CI: 0.219–0.937, P = 0.033) and DMFS (IC cycle: HR = 0.340, 95%CI: 0.152–0.757, P = 0.008; CCTC: HR = 0.390, 95%CI: 0.184–0.828, P = 0.014).

Toxicities

The treatment-related acute toxicities, including hematological and hepatic toxicities during induction chemotherapy, are listed in Table 5. The hematological toxicities include leukopenia, neutropenia, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. Hepatic toxicities include alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) elevation. The IC = 4 group had significantly higher incidences of grade 2–4 thrombocytopenia (16.2% vs. 7.0%, P = 0.046) and ALT elevation (30.4% vs. 17.2%, P = 0.030) than the IC = 3 group. There were no significant differences in other grade 2–4 adverse events between the two groups (Table 5).

Discussion

To our knowledge, the present study is the first to individualize the number of IC cycles based on pre-EBV DNA in stage N3 NPC. The superiority of the IC = 4 regimen in survival prognosis was revealed. However, it had a higher incidence of grade 2–4 acute hematological and hepatic toxicities than the IC = 3 regimen. Further explorations showed that there was no significant difference in survival outcomes in the low-risk group between the two regimens. In contrast, the IC = 4 regimen significantly improved survival compared with the IC = 3 regimen in the high-risk group.

NPC, as one of the common malignant tumors in the head and neck, shows invasive growth and high incidence of cervical lymph node metastasis. Most patients are already locally advanced at initial diagnosis24. Several prospective clinical studies revealed that the use of 2-cycle or 3-cycle IC plus CCRT compared to CCRT alone has successfully improved the survival prognosis of LA-NPC25,26. Patients with stage N3 NPC are prone to recurrence or metastasis due to already severe lymph node invasion and high tumor burden27,28. It is worth exploring whether it is necessary to increase the number of IC cycle to reduce tumor burden, eliminate potential metastatic lesions, and improve survival prognosis.

Han et al.’s research demonstrated that IC plus CCRT compared to CCRT alone could improve survival outcomes in stage N3 NPC, but this study did not further analyze the feasible number of IC cycles for patients29. Ma et al.’s research further revealed that for stage N2/3 NPC patients, those who received 2-cycle IC had significantly higher 3-year OS than those who received 3-cycle or 4-cycle IC (92.4% vs. 80.8%, P = 0.029)30. However, another study obtained different results, indicating that for patients with stage N2/3 NPC, 4-cycle IC was more effective than 2-cycle IC in OS (89.2% vs. 83.3%), PFS (81.0% vs. 72.5%), LRRFS (83.3% vs. 81.2%), and DMFS (84.8% vs. 77.9%)31. The above two studies both examined the impact of IC cycles on the prognosis of patients with advanced N-stage (N2/3) NPC, but the results obtained were different, indicating that currently relying solely on tumor clinical stage to determine the suitable number of IC cycles for advanced N-stage NPC patients may not provide sufficient accuracy. NPC exhibits significant heterogeneity, and even patients at the same stage, with the same pathological type, treatment regimen, and chemotherapy cycle, often experience different survival outcomes. In addition to the TNM staging system, other factors affecting the prognosis are also crucial for the selection of IC cycles for stage N3 NPC, such as the patients’ physical condition32, the copy number of plasma EBV DNA33, the tumor response to IC34, and the MRI features35. Therefore, it is particularly important to search for suitable biomarkers or independent prognostic factors that can aid in developing personalized treatment plans and selecting the appropriate number of IC cycles for stage N3 NPC patients.

Among the pathogenic factors of NPC, EBV infection is closely related to the disease’s occurrence, development, and survival prognosis36. The use of plasma EBV DNA, in conjunction with the TNM staging system, could improve the accuracy of prognosis prediction for NPC patients18,19,37. Previous studies have indicated that using pre-EBV DNA to stratify the risk in LA-NPC patients could help identify patients who may benefit from IC regimens17. Although other prognostic factors, such as lactate dehydrogenase38, C-reactive protein39, and tumor volume40, have been proven to have great prognostic value in NPC, there is currently limited clinical research or evidence for them compared with pre-EBV DNA. Conversely, due to the extensive application and research of pre-EBV DNA in clinical practice, it is commonly regarded as a reliable indicator for developing clinical treatment plans. Therefore, this study chose pre-EBV DNA as the prognostic indicator to conduct risk stratification and survival analysis on stage N3 NPC patients. Some medical centers directly select 4000 copies/ml as the cut-off value for EBV DNA based on their previous research41. On the contrary, our study utilized current clinical data to determine the cut-off value of pre-EBV DNA through ROC analysis. Survival analysis and multivariate Cox regression analysis were further conducted to demonstrate that the cut-off value of pre-EBV DNA was a feasible predictor of survival prognosis.

This study indicated that the IC = 4 regimen did not achieve significant survival improvement compared to the IC = 3 regimen for low-risk patients. This result could be attributed to the following reasons. First, the IC = 3 regimen was sufficient to reduce tumor burden, eradicate micrometastases, and improve survival compared with the IC = 4 regimen. Second, the IC = 4 regimen prolonged the waiting time for radiotherapy, which was detrimental to local control, as demonstrated in previous studies42,43. Moreover, the IC = 4 regimen led to unnecessary side effects and affected the quality of life, especially for elderly patients44. In the high-risk group, the IC = 4 regimen significantly improved survival outcomes compared to the IC = 3 regimen. This could be because high-risk patients had high tumor burden. Choosing the IC = 4 regimen could maximize the reduction of tumor volume, clearance of plasma EBV DNA, and elimination of early small metastatic lesions, thereby improving long-term survival. However, this study did not include 5-cycle or 6-cycle IC in the comparison. The reasons for this are as follows. First, tumor cells that survived after 3-cycle or 4-cycle IC may increase resistance to chemotherapy drugs45. Increasing the number of chemotherapy cycles could not improve survival benefits but may lead to excessive medical treatment. Second, 5-cycle or 6-cycle IC means that patients need to bear more medical expenses and higher compliance requirements. Third, as the IC cycle increases, the incidence of adverse reactions related to IC would increase, including hematological toxicities, hepatorenal toxicities, and gastrointestinal reactions. These not only hinder long-term survival prognosis, but also require patients to have good tolerance. In summary, this study only compared the impact of IC = 3 or IC = 4 regimens on the prognosis of stage N3 NPC.

In addition, in this study, CCTC was proven to be an independent factor affecting prognosis in high-risk groups. This could be due to the fact that high-risk patients typically have higher tumor burden and an increased risk of distant metastasis. Increasing the number of CCTC can be beneficial for eliminating micrometastases and improving survival outcomes. Concurrently, chemotherapy has a sensitization and synergistic effect on radiotherapy, which can further improve survival prognosis46.

The combination of immunotherapy with chemoradiotherapy as a potent therapeutic strategy for LA-NPC is currently under rigorous exploration, with a series of studies47–49. A large-scale, phase III trial, which adopted 3-cycle IC plus CCRT combined with immunotherapy, has revealed that the integration of sintilimab into chemoradiotherapy substantially reduces the risks of distant metastasis and locoregional recurrence in LA-NPC patients50. Individuals with stage N3 NPC are susceptible to experiencing recurrence or metastasis as a result of the advanced lymph node involvement and significant tumor load. With the addition of immunotherapy, it is worth further exploring the optimal number of IC cycles for stage N3 NPC. Our research provides valuable reference for the selection of IC cycles for stage N3 NPC. In our study, it is necessary to further explore whether the addition of immunotherapy is needed to further improve prognosis for high-risk patients in the future.

However, this study still had certain limitations. Although we used PSM to balance baseline characteristics, this study was a retrospective analysis, which inevitably led to potential selection bias. Additionally, in terms of adverse reactions, this study only retrospectively collected acute adverse reactions. Other adverse reactions caused by chemotherapy, such as gastrointestinal reactions, weight loss, and mucositis, were not included in this study due to serious data missing and incomplete data recording. Finally, this study was conducted in a single center, and the survival outcomes of patients might be affected by unidentified factors. Therefore, it is necessary to conduct larger sample size, multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled clinical studies. The prognostic value of pre-EBV DNA in determining the efficacy and safety of different IC cycles in stage N3 NPC still needs to be validated after a longer follow-up period.

Conclusions

In conclusion, in stage N3 NPC, the IC = 4 regimen was more beneficial for high-risk patients compared to the IC = 3 regimen, owing to more favorable survival prognosis. For low-risk patients, the IC = 3 regimen might be sufficient. It is indicated that pre-EBV DNA could be an effective predictor to guide the selection of IC cycle.

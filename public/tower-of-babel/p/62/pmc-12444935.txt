Integrative Supportive Care Trial to Enhance Physical Activity in Malignant Pleural Effusion—Protocol for the ISC‐TEAM/AMPLE‐5 Randomised Controlled Trial
Authors: Bianca M Iacopetta, Chloe Charlesworth, Emily Jeffery, Lauren J Breen, Ai Ling Tan, Joanne McVeigh, Kevin Murray, Rob U Newton, Y C Gary Lee, Carolyn J Peddle‐Mcintyre
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundMalignant pleural effusion (MPE) heralds advanced cancer with poor patient outcomes. High symptom burden of weight loss, fatigue, breathlessness, distress, and anxiety leaves many patients unable to participate in desired activities of daily life and is associated with poor outcomes. Freedom from symptoms and maintaining daily activities are the key wishes of patients and carers. For the best outcome, accessible services aimed at prevention and management of malnutrition, care of psychological well‐being, and support for increasing physical activity are required.MethodsThe Integrative Supportive Care Trial to Enhance physical Activity in Malignant pleural effusion (ISC‐TEAM) study is a two‐armed, parallel group randomised controlled trial. Participants (n = 100) diagnosed with MPE will be randomly assigned to receive standard clinical care or integrative supportive care. Integrative supportive care will entail an individualised multidisciplinary program of dietetics, exercise physiology, and psychological input over 12 weeks. Participants will receive telehealth consultations with each discipline at week 1, week 4, and week 8. The primary outcome is device‐assessed daily physical activity (steps/day). Secondary outcomes include quality of life, malnutrition risk, psychopathology, symptoms, physical activity profile, and program acceptability and compliance.DiscussionThis randomised trial will evaluate the effects of a multidisciplinary supportive care program in MPE aimed at improving participants ability to engage in physical activities.Trial RegistrationAustralian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry: ACTRN12624000038594; https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=386529&isReview=true Keywords: malignant pleural effusion, multidisciplinary team, physical activity, supportive care

Introduction

Background and Rationale

Malignant pleural effusion (MPE) is a debilitating condition indicating advanced‐stage cancer with limited treatment options. MPE occurs as a result of primary cancer including mesothelioma [1] or as a recurrent complication affecting ~30% of patients with lung cancers [2], ~30% of ovarian cancers [3], and ~10% of breast cancers [3]. The effusion is often many litres and causes distressing breathlessness which restricts daily activities and impairs quality of life (QoL). Additional to breathlessness, patients with MPE suffer from a high symptom burden of weight loss or malnutrition, pain, and fatigue.

Actigraphy is commonly used as an objective measure of physical activity levels in patients with MPE [4, 5, 6]. This cohort spend much of their day sedentary (sitting or lying down; 72% of waking hours, equivalent to 11 h), with only 35% of patients achieving ≥ 7000 steps per day [4]. We have found in patients with MPE those with good performance status took more than double the step count of those with poor performance status [4]. Lower levels of physical activity in MPE patients have been associated with worse survival, poor QoL, and low muscle mass compared to those with MPE who have higher activity levels [5].

Malnutrition, characterised by weight loss, muscle wasting and reduced body fat, often in the context of reduced food intake, is common in MPE, often upon initial diagnosis, and has been linked to worse QoL and fatigue [6]. Weight loss alone can predict poor prognosis [7]. In other advanced cancers, weight loss, malnutrition, and reduced physical activity have been linked to the development of low skeletal muscle mass [8]. We found patients with muscle loss in MPE had a significant decline in step count and physical activity compared to those with maintained muscle mass [5]. This is associated with a range of negative outcomes including worse QoL [9], increased treatment toxicity [9], poorer survival [9], and greater psychological distress [10]. Managing malnutrition and maintaining muscle mass are necessary to support participation in daily activities and improve physical activity outcomes.

Patients with MPE have unmet psychological needs [11]. This cohort has a high need for information and a desire for easy access to multiple aspects of supportive care for this population [11, 12]. Our research suggests that hidden costs, such as parking, are a burden identified by many carers [12]. This indicates using telehealth to deliver supportive care interventions could reduce costs associated with attending hospital appointments and facilitate attendance. Collectively this suggests that, in people with MPE, psychological distress, low physical activity levels, and malnutrition are linked to poor QoL and worse outcomes.

The treatment goals in MPE are to lessen symptom burden and allow patients to take part in their usual daily activities for as long as possible. The ability to remain physically active relies on the integrative capacity of multiple body systems. Therefore, optimal symptom management, including the prevention and management of malnutrition, care of psychological well‐being, and physical activity counselling, are required. National guidelines advocate the use of a multidisciplinary model of supportive care for cancer patients [13]; however, an integrated supportive care program designed to address the specific needs of patients with MPE has never been investigated. This is the fifth study in the Australasian Malignant PLeural Effusion (AMPLE) series, which aims to enhance patient care in MPE.

Objective

The purpose of the Integrative Supportive Care Trial to Enhance physical Activity in Malignant pleural effusion (ISC‐TEAM) study is to investigate the effects of an integrative supportive care program (incorporating dietetics, exercise physiology, and psychological counselling) on average steps per day. Our secondary outcomes will examine the effects of the intervention on QoL, nutritional status, psychological distress, and symptom profile. Barriers, facilitators, and acceptability of the program will also be assessed.

Trial Design

The ISC‐Team study is a two‐armed single‐centre, randomised, controlled trial. One hundred patients with MPE will be randomised 1:1 to either the integrated supportive care program (incorporating dietetics, exercise physiology, and psychological counselling) or standard care (as per attending physician).

Methods

Participant Screening, Selection, and Recruitment

Potential participants will be screened and recruited by their treating respiratory physician or designated research staff. Participants will be enrolled after providing informed written consent.

Inclusion Criteria

Individuals with: (i) confirmed MPE (histo‐/cyto‐logically confirmed) or recurrent large exudative pleural effusion (with histo‐cytologically proven cancer outside the thorax and no alternative cause) and (ii) an estimated survival ≥ 3 months (the length of the supportive care intervention) are eligible to participate in the study.

Exclusion Criteria

Individuals are not eligible for the study if they meet any of the following criteria: (i) age < 18 years, (ii) inability to consent, (iii) inability to comply with the protocol, (iv) pregnancy/lactation, and (v) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status > 3.

Randomisation and Blinding

Following confirmation of eligibility and informed consent, enrolled participants will undergo baseline assessment (see Tables 1 and 2 for timeline of assessments). Upon completion of baseline assessments, participants will be randomly assigned (1:1) to either standard care or the integrated supportive care program. Randomisation will be minimised for (i) cancer type (mesothelioma vs. non‐mesothelioma), (ii) ECOG performance status (0/1 vs. ≥ 2), and (iii) current cancer treatment (chemotherapy/immunotherapy/radiation therapy vs. not). The allocation sequence will be generated by Griffith University's online randomisation service. This system provides automatic randomisation following the entry of baseline data and will be confirmed by emails. Due to the nature of the intervention, staff and trial participants are not blinded to treatment group allocation.

Intervention and Control Group

Patients' medical care, including management of pleural effusion and oncology management, will be directed by their attending physicians, as per standard practice in the treatment hospital, regardless of the group to which they are allocated. All patients have access to clinical care services and standard care from their attending physicians including chemotherapy, radiation, and immunotherapy. Participants will be enrolled in the study from the time of consent form until the final assessment is completed.

Standard Care

The standard care arm group will be treated as per the attending physician, with referral to supportive services if indicated. Upon completion of the post‐intervention assessments, participants have the option to complete one integrated supportive care counselling session. The standard care group will receive a monthly phone call to track adverse events and referrals/appointments to supportive care interventions (i.e., dietitian, exercise physiologist/physiotherapist, or psychologist/counsellor).

Integrated Supportive Care Program

The integrative supportive care program will be delivered via telehealth at week 1, week 4, and week 8. Prior to each appointment, participants will complete a needs assessment screening (via RedCap or paper) to assess psychological distress [14], physical activity participation in the past 7 days [15], and nutritional status [16].

At each appointment, participants will receive individual consultations with each member of the multidisciplinary team. The sessions will be delivered by Accredited Practising Dietitians, exercise physiologists/physiotherapists, and a clinical psychologist/specially trained nurse. Appointments will take a maximum of 1 h in total (approximately 20 m in for dietetics, 20 m in for physical activity and 20 m in for psychological counselling sessions respectively).

The goals of each component of integrative care are:

Dietetics: Nutrition counselling involves the provision of personalised recommendations for meeting energy and protein intake recommendations using normal food, and oral nutrition supplements (i.e., “high energy, high protein” drinks) when required [17]. The aim is to achieve or maintain a dietary intake of 25–30 kcal/kg/day and 1.0–1.5 g/kg/day, of protein [13]. Each counselling session will be based on the previous day's dietary intake, assessed by a 24‐h dietary recall. Participants will be provided with the Cancer Council Australia booklet, Nutrition for People Living with Cancer A guide for people with cancer, their families, and friends. This will be delivered by an Accredited Practising Dietitian with specialist experience in MPE.

Dietetics: Nutrition counselling involves the provision of personalised recommendations for meeting energy and protein intake recommendations using normal food, and oral nutrition supplements (i.e., “high energy, high protein” drinks) when required [17]. The aim is to achieve or maintain a dietary intake of 25–30 kcal/kg/day and 1.0–1.5 g/kg/day, of protein [13]. Each counselling session will be based on the previous day's dietary intake, assessed by a 24‐h dietary recall. Participants will be provided with the Cancer Council Australia booklet, Nutrition for People Living with Cancer A guide for people with cancer, their families, and friends. This will be delivered by an Accredited Practising Dietitian with specialist experience in MPE.

Psychology: Psychological counselling will include a review of distress and psychological needs [14] and implementing a plan to specifically identify and refer to appropriate support services for psychosocial care. At subsequent counselling sessions, this plan will be re‐evaluated and adjusted as appropriate. This component will be delivered by a nurse with specialist experience in MPE with support from a registered psychologist.

At subsequent counselling sessions, this plan will be re‐evaluated and adjusted as appropriate. This component will be delivered by a nurse with specialist experience in MPE with support from a registered psychologist.

Physical activity: Exercise counselling involves the provision of personalised recommendations for increasing physical activity participation (or maintaining participation as appropriate) and reducing/breaking up sedentary time. Counselling sessions will involve review of current physical activity levels and will include education, identification of appropriate programs and resources (e.g., Cancer Council WA Life Now program, or neighbourhood walking trails), behavioural support, and tools for managing symptoms during physical activity, and suggestions for reducing sedentary time. This will be delivered by an Accredited Exercise Physiologist or a Registered Physiotherapist with specialist experience in MPE.

Measurements

Assessments of endpoints will be conducted at baseline and post‐intervention (week 13). Baseline demographics, ECOG performance status, height, weight, date of birth, cancer diagnosis, pleural effusion characteristics, comorbidities, and lifestyle behaviours (e.g., smoking status) will be captured from medical records and the baseline demographics questionnaire.

Primary Outcome

Physical activity level will be assessed as steps per day, measured by an accelerometer (Sens Motion Activity Sensors [SENS]) [18]. Participants will be asked to wear the SENS accelerometer on their lower thigh, 24 h/day for seven days. SENS is a small accelerometer placed within a small plaster to be worn discretely on the patient's thigh. The SENS motion system is considered a reliable and valid device for measuring physical activity in clinical populations [18, 19].

Secondary Outcomes

Quality of Life and Symptoms

Symptoms and QoL will be monitored using a set of validated questionnaires. The EuroQoL5 Dimensions5 Levels (EQ‐5D‐5L) will be used to assess five dimensions of mobility, self‐care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression [20]. The Edmonton Symptom Assessment Scale (ESAS) will assess symptom burden [21]. A 100 mm visual analogue scale (VAS) anchored with “no breathlessness” at 0 mm and “worst breathlessness imaginable” at 100 mm will assess breathlessness. This tool has been validated for MPE patients and been used in several RCTs [22, 23, 24]. The Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy—Fatigue (FACIT‐Fatigue) subscale will be used to assess severity of fatigue [25].

Nutritional Assessment

The Functional Assessment of Anorexia/Cachexia Treatment (FAACT) Appetite/Cachexia Scale (ACS) [26] will assess quality of life and the changes in appetite. The Patient‐Generated Subjective Global Assessment Short‐Form (PG‐SGA SF) will be used to assess malnutrition risk [27, 28]. The PG‐SGA SF consists of four sections related to weight, food intake, symptoms affecting eating, and activities and function.

Psychopathology

Clinical symptoms of depression and generalised anxiety will be measured using the Patient Health Questionnaire‐4 [29]. Participants will indicate how frequently they experience symptoms of depression with two items (e.g., “feeling down, depressed, or hopeless”) and generalised anxiety with two items (e.g., “feeling nervous, anxious, or on edge”).

Physical Activity Profile

Duration and patterns, rest time, standing time, walking time, running, physical activity intensity, cycling time, as well as sleep time and quality will be analysed using SENS accelerometers [18].

Program Acceptability, Compliance and Safety

Barriers, facilitators, acceptability, and value of the integrative supportive care program will be assessed via Likert scales and through open‐ended questions [30]. Compliance to the program will be assessed by attendance at telehealth sessions (number completed/3, including the reason for any missed sessions). The incidence and severity of any adverse events (e.g., fall, muscle strain) as categorised by ICH‐GCP guidelines will be monitored and documented during telehealth sessions, and by monthly follow‐up calls to the standard care group. Adverse and serious adverse events will be recorded as in previous AMPLE trials [24, 31].

Sample Size

This study will aim to recruit 100 patients (50 per group). To detect a conservative clinically meaningful difference of 2000 steps per day [5], with 90% power and 5% significance level, a sample size of 50 per treatment group would need to be recruited [4]. This allows for the expected skewness of the data (based on the pilot data) and a 15% drop out rate [30]. Based on our pilot data, this sample size will also give us adequate power to detect a clinically meaningful difference in the percent of time spent in light activity of 6.5%, with 90% power and 5% significance level [4].

Data Collection, Management, and Analysis

Data Collection Methods

Data from paper case report forms will be transposed into the study REDCap database. This will be de‐identified data. The dataset will be exported for analysis at the end of the study from REDCap for the designated study statistician. Consent to this study is extended so data collected from this study will be used in future studies related to MPE.

Data Management

All procedures for the handling and analysis of the data will be conducted according to the ICH‐GCP guidelines and the National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research (2007)—Updated 2018 and in accordance with local policies and procedures.

Statistical Analysis

Data will be analysed on an intention‐to‐treat basis. For the primary outcome, the difference between the groups in step count at follow‐up will initially be examined using two sample t‐tests, and further adjustments will be made for minimisation and baseline variables. The same approach will be taken for continuous secondary outcomes, for example, quality of life, psychological distress. Differences in secondary outcomes of categorical variables (e.g., malnutrition risk) at follow‐up will be analysed using a Chi Square test for comparing two proportions. Feasibility data as well as adverse and serious adverse events will be reported in descriptive figures and tables.

Monitoring

Data Monitoring

This study will have monitoring carried out and source data will be scrutinised to ensure the provision of robust data. Data entered in Case Report Forms (CRF) that are transcribed from source documents must be consistent with the source documents or the discrepancies must be explained. Any discrepancies will be resolved with the site PI or otherwise documented as File Notes. Procedure deviations and/or violations will be determined at this time and will need to be reported according to local procedure/policy. Source documents are filed at the investigator's site.

Harms

The procedures in this study are standard of care. Participants will be monitored as per standard care guidelines. Standard medical care remains the responsibility of the treating physician of the patient. As there are no additional medical procedures involved in this study, there are no foreseeable major risks or side effects associated with participation. There is a small risk of musculoskeletal soreness or injury during activities such as walking, especially in the beginning of the program.

Auditing

The study investigators/institutions will permit trial‐related monitoring, audits, and regulatory inspections, providing direct access to source data/documents. This may include, but is not limited to, review by external sponsors, Human Research Ethics Committees, and institutional Governance review bodies.

Discussion

Research indicates that patients with MPE suffer from a high symptom burden which arise from a complex interplay of multiple body systems [4, 6, 12]. Multidisciplinary input including dietetics, exercise physiology, and psychological counselling is required to enhance medical interventions and optimise overall symptom management [13]. This is the first trial to investigate the effects of a multidisciplinary supportive care program for patients with MPE aimed at improving patients' ability to engage in daily physical activities. The trial will investigate questions addressing consumer‐driven priorities of care for patients with MPE. It is designed to have an unselected cohort, with few exclusion criteria to improve generalisability. The findings of this study may enhance future supportive management of patients with MPE, which may improve patient outcomes in a cohort with little known effective treatment.

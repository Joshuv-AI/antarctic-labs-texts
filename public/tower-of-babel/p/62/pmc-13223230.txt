ChinaSoilSeedBank: A dataset on species diversity and composition of soil seed bank in China
Authors: Yantong Zhao, Yangqing Luo, Jiajia Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDatasets on aboveground vegetation have been well established and used for comprehensive ecological and conservational studies. However, a comprehensive dataset for soil seed bank, particularly one integrating the species composition of soil seed bank, is still lacking, limiting our understanding of belowground community structure and dynamics. Here, we systematically built a dataset for soil seed bank of China (ChinaSoilSeedBank). The dataset comprises 3,170 soil seed bank communities, of which 1,827 communities include species composition data encompassing 3,070 plant species and 33,488 occurrence records. The dataset also incorporates environmental variables and plant functional traits associated with the soil seed bank communities, and supplements paired aboveground vegetation when reported. The ChinaSoilSeedBank dataset can be used to identify key conservation areas of high belowground biodiversity, to complement current aboveground-focused conservation strategies, and to guide ecological restoration in degraded ecosystems.

Background & Summary

Understanding the spatial distribution patterns of plant diversity remains a fundamental challenge in ecology, driving the rapid establishment of regional and global databases for aboveground vegetation. Many databases, such as sPlot1, Global Index of Vegetation-Plot Databases2, ForestPlots3, and GrassPlot4, have been developed to record plant occurrences and abundances, providing valuable sources for monitoring and predicting biodiversity changes under global crisis5,6. However, these databases are heavily imbalanced, with a predominant focus on aboveground vegetation, neglecting belowground plant diversity hidden in the soil.

Soil seed bank, consisting of viable seeds in the soil, represents a critical yet often neglected component for plant diversity7,8. It plays a key role in maintaining regional belowground diversity by buffering environmental fluctuations9, and supporting community restoration through regeneration processes10. Consequently, a comprehensive understanding of soil seed bank composition is essential for predicting ecosystem resilience and restoration potential11. In recent decades, researches on soil seed bank have increasingly shifted from local-scale to a broader regional and continental scale9,12, and more recently to the global scale13,14. However, most existing datasets compiled from vegetation surveys emphasize seed density and species richness, while largely neglecting species composition, which is fundamental for understanding biodiversity patterns across broad spatial scales.

Compiling species composition data for soil seed banks at large spatial scales remains challenging. First, the reliable identification of seedlings during germination is often difficult, especially for species belonging to taxonomically complex families15. As a result, many studies only reported species diversity at higher taxonomic levels (e.g., genus and family) rather than at the species level16,17. Second, only a small fraction of soil seed bank data is readily available in the main text and supplementary files of the original studies14,18, and most data are buried in local reports, dissertations or literature written in local languages, posing linguistic and accessibility barriers to dataset construction19. Moreover, harmonizing species composition data across biogeographic regions is complicated by divergent floristic classifications and nomenclatural conventions20,21. Overcoming these obstacles is crucial for developing a comprehensive large-scale dataset on soil seed bank composition to support ecological and restoration research.

China hosts a vast range of biomes and remarkable floristic diversity, comprising over 36,000 vascular plant taxa22. The abundant resources have fostered extensive research efforts by Chinese scholars, resulting in more than 1,500 published studies on soil seed banks across diverse ecosystems23. These studies have produced taxonomically resolved data on species diversity and community composition of soil seed banks, offering a valuable yet underutilized resource for future regional and global ecological syntheses. However, approximately two-thirds of these studies were published in Chinese, limiting their accessibility to the international scientific community. Therefore, integrating these studies into a unified framework is essential for advancing a comprehensive understanding of soil seed bank diversity.

Here, we present the ChinaSoilSeedBank, a dataset on species diversity and composition of soil seed banks in China. Through extensive literature searches, we compiled data from 507 studies, encompassing 3,170 communities with plant diversity records, and 1,827 communities with species composition data, totaling 33,488 records and 3,070 species. In addition, we also linked soil seed bank communities to corresponding aboveground vegetation data when reported. We also incorporated associated environmental variables for each community and functional traits for each species. Therefore, the ChinaSoilSeedBank dataset provides a robust and standardized platform to explore diversity patterns and dynamics in soil seed bank, and to inform soil seed bank restoration and biodiversity conservation.

Methods

Literature search

We obtained literature related to soil seed bank through two approaches. First, we conducted systematic searches on plant diversity of soil seed banks using the largest academic platform in China, the China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI; https://www.cnki.net). The keywords used were: (‘soil seed bank’ OR ‘soil seedbank’) AND (‘diversity’ OR ‘richness’ OR ‘species composition’), covering the period between 1990 and 2023. To ensure data integrity and allow for a rigorous cleaning and validation cycle, the systematic literature search and raw data acquisition for the ChinaSoilSeedBank were concluded in 2023. Because species composition data were frequently absent from published journal articles (typically due to space limitations), we also systematically searched and reviewed relevant PhD and MSc dissertations published by the authors to complement these critical data. Second, we searched for records published in international journals using the Web of Science (https://webofscience.clarivate.cn) with the same keywords and time period. Conference proceedings, review articles, and reports were excluded. This process initially yielded 2,950 studies.

All candidate studies were screened against inclusion criteria and underwent a curation process. To be included in the ChinaSoilSeedBank, studies had to meet six criteria: (1) samples were collected at the community level, rather than population or functional group level; (2) contained species diversity, composition or abundance data; (3) were conducted without experimental manipulation; (4) collected at least three soil cores per sampling unite; (5) verified the study regions within China; (6) excluded irrelevant or duplicate records. Ultimately, we compiled data from 507 papers, including 363 published in Chinese and 144 in English.

Methodological of soil seed bank

For each included study, we also compiled detailed information on site information, sampling design, habitat information, diversity metrics of aboveground vegetation, and data sources. This comprehensive compilation allows for systematic comparison of plant diversity patterns across different environmental contexts. Detailed variable descriptions are shown in Fig. 1.

Sampling methods were relatively consistent among the 3,170 soil seed bank communities in China. Most studies employed a random sampling design, typically collection multiple soil cores within each plot and mixing them in a sampling to minimize spatial bias. Sampling was most frequently conducted in spring (55%; n = 1,663), focused primarily on surface soil layers (0–10 cm; 87%; n = 2,747), and employed small plot sizes (<0.5 m2; 73%; n = 2,224). In contrast, relatively few samples originated from deep soil layers >10 cm (14%; n = 414), or large plots > 0.5 m2 (27%; n = 856; Table 1).

The ChinaSoilSeedBank dataset provided a comprehensive survey of soil seed bank diversity across 3,170 plant communities (Fig. 1), spanning longitudinal (80°–134°E), latitudinal (17°–54°N), and elevational gradients (0–5,600 m), and covering all eight major vegetation types in China (Table 2). Among the studied soil seed banks, grassland communities dominated, accounting for over 54% of all samples (n = 1,713), followed by forests (24%, n = 759), and wetlands (17%, n = 529; Table 2). The habitat status of each site in the ChinaSoilSeedBank was classified into three categories based on the degree of human intervention described in the original studies: natural (relatively primitive), disturbed (significant human or natural interference), and restored (active or passive recovery). Among the dataset, grassland communities were evenly represented across the three habitat statuses (34%, 34%, 31%), whereas forest and wetland communities were predominantly associated with natural ecosystems (63% and 71%, respectively; Table 2).

Standard metrics of soil seed bank

For each study, we systematically recorded the following key variables: (1) species richness (number of species per community); (2) seed density (seeds per m2); (3) species composition; (4) diversity metrics of corresponding aboveground vegetation when available. When multiple surveys were conducted at the same site but across different seasons, years or habitat types, each sampling was treated as an independent record to capture spatial and temporal variability in seed bank dynamics. Data on plant diversity metrics were extracted from the main text, tables and supplementary materials of each publication. When diversity data were only presented in figures, we used Plot Digitizer (https://plotdigitizer.com/) to extract the information. If species composition data were unavailable in the published literature, we retrieved them from the first author’s dissertation or thesis.

The ChinaSoilSeedBank dataset offers high data completeness, with seed density and species richness available for 99% and 93% of the 3,170 communities, respectively (Table 3). Seed density across the sampled sites spans multiple orders of magnitude, ranging from 0 to 9.99 × 105 seeds/m2. Species richness varies from 0 to 123 species per community. detailed summary statistics are provided in Table 3.

Related biotic and abiotic information

To enhance the analytical utility of the soil seed bank dataset, we systematically incorporated environmental, vegetation, and trait-related variables for each study site. Five climate variables were extracted from the WorldClim dataset v2 from 1970–200024, including annual mean temperature, temperature seasonality, temperature annual range, annual precipitation, and precipitation seasonality. Elevation, as one of the most important topographic variables, was also extracted from the WorldClim dataset v224. All environmental layers were standardized to a 30 arcsec resolution (~1 km2 at the equator). This version was selected because it provides the most widely recognized climatic baseline in macroecology, ensuring that the ChinaSoilSeedBank dataset is highly comparable with other global ecological datasets (Table 4).

Aboveground vegetation types were assigned according to the 1:1,000,000 Chinese Vegetation Atlas (https://www.resdc.cn/). To facilitate more detailed analysis of ecological strategies within the soil seed bank, we compiled plant functional traits including species form, growth form, and life form from the dataset of vascular plants in China25. The introduction status of each species (native, naturalized, or invasive) was determined based on the Catalogue of Invasive and Naturalized Plants in China26. Threat status and endemism were obtained from the China Biodiversity Red List: Volume of Higher Plants (2020), which includes 39,330 wild plant species distributed in China (http://www.mee.gov.cn/). Additional traits, including plant height and specific leaf area (SLA), were obtained from the TRY database27, while seed mass and dispersal mode were sourced from the Chinese Seed Trait Database28. Although current data coverage is limited for some species, these traits are fundamental to the Leaf-Height-Seed (LHS) strategy scheme29, and are robust predictors of soil seed bank persistence30. Including these parameters provides a necessary framework for research to explore the functional difference between aboveground and belowground plants (Table 5).

Data Overview

The ChinaSoilSeedBank database encompasses a diverse flora of 4,383 plant species belonging to 1,418 genera and 226 families, including angiosperms (97.2%), gymnosperms (1.0%), and pteridophytes (1.7%). The dataset provides paired taxonomic records, with 2,949 species documented in the soil seed banks and 3,424 species in the corresponding aboveground vegetation (Table 6). The collection is characterized by a high representation of herbaceous taxa, particularly from the Poaceae and Asteraceae families, which exhibit the highest seed densities and occurrence frequencies across the sampled communities. Woody species, represented by families such as Rosaceae and Salicaceae, are also documented but with generally lower frequencies. Detailed breakdowns of species richness and seed density at the family, genus, and species levels are summarized in Table 6 and Fig. 2.

Technical Validation

Prior to inclusion in the dataset, all data underwent a rigorous standardization and quality control process. Plant diversity metrics extracted from literature were carefully double-checked by the authors. Common data entry errors, such as decimal misplacement and unit conservation inconsistencies, were systematically identified using boxplot diagnostics. We used frequency distribution plots and cross-variable consistency checks to ensure data quality for categorical variables. When potential errors were detected, the corresponding values were reverified against the original source. These quality control measures minimized transcription errors and enhanced the overall reliability of the dataset.

Taxonomic resolution was standardized primarily at the species level (96%), following the procedure described below. All taxonomic records with scientific names were validated using the World Checklist of Vascular Plants database (WCVP)31. Matching was first attempted at the species level, followed by genus and family levels when necessary. For names not found in the WCVP, we manually verified them using both scientific and Chinese names in the Catalogue of Life China32, which provides both scientific and Chinese names. The standardized taxonomy includes information at the species, genus, family, order, class and phylogenetic group levels. Although taxonomic systems may evolve, this database preserves the original species names provided in each reference to ensure transparency and reproducibility of the standardization process.

Data availability

The ChinaSoilSeedBank dataset has been deposited in the Figshare33. The dataset contains nine primary CSV files, including two core datasets and seven metadata tables, which can be accessed via the permanent identifier 10.6084/m9.figshare.30334738. The core datasets include ‘species_diversity.csv’ (community-level indices of soil seed bank), and ‘species_composition.csv’ (species-level records of soil seed bank). The metadata tables comprise ‘Meta_above_diversity.csv’, ‘Meta_above_composition.csv’, ‘Meta_environment.csv’, ‘Meta_habitat.csv’, ‘Meta_taxonomy.csv’, ‘Meta_trait.csv’, ‘Meta_reference’. A comprehensive ‘Readme.xlsx’ file is also included in the repository to guide data usage.

Code availability

Dataset development and analysis were performed with Microsoft Excel 2019 and R version 4.5.2. All procedures employed in its generation are elaborated in the Methods section. As no custom code was developed, no separate code files are necessary to ensure reproducibility.

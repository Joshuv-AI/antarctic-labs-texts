Trajectory optimization for auxiliary power unit operation of an extender range electric vehicle
Authors: Ye Yang, Jingyi Tian, Boru Jia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper focuses on the trajectory optimization based Auxiliary Power Unit (APU) for an Extended Range Electric Vehicle (EREV). Firstly, a new three-zone control strategy has been proposed to optimize the APU steady-state operation, and the simulation result show significant enhancements in fuel efficiency compared to the traditional thermostat and power-following control strategy. Furthermore, low-frequency noise and vibrations generated by the APU during transient operations are the primary factors affecting passenger comfort, which are closely associated with transient operational trajectory of the APU. In order to address the fuel consumption and NVH (Noise, Vibration, and Harshness) challenges in EREV during dynamic operation, a multi-objective optimization model is developed. The multi-objective particle swarm algorithm and weighted scaling method are employed to solve the model, obtaining the transient operating trajectory of the APU. Finally, the method proposed is validated on a test bench and compared with single-objective optimization method based on the best BSFC (Brake Specific Fuel Consumption) and traditional optimization method. The results indicate that the multi-objective operational trajectory proposed can effectively improve the NVH performance of the APU at the cost of a slight increase(3.9%) in fuel consumption. Although a slight increase in fuel consumption occurs during the transient switching process, the overall energy efficiency of the APU system remains improved due to the high-efficiency steady-state operation enabled by the three-zone control strategy.Keywords: extended range electric vehicle, trajectory optimization, particle swarm optimization, NVH, fuel consumptionSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Faced with an increasingly severe energy crisis and growing concerns about air pollution, electric vehicles are gaining global recognition for their significant contributions to energy conservation and environmental protection. However, the widespread adoption of electric vehicles is hindered by current limitations in power battery technology. The low energy density, long charging times, and high costs associated with power batteries make it challenging to achieve significant performance improvements in the short term. Consequently, extended-range electric vehicles (EREVs), as series hybrid electric vehicles, have attracted widespread attention for their high efficiency, extended driving range, cost-effectiveness, simple structure, and enhanced portability1–3.

EREVs comprise both an APU and a power battery, forming a multidisciplinary nonlinear system encompassing mechanical, electrical, and thermodynamic aspects4,5. The existence of these two power sources increases the complexity of the powertrain system. Therefore, ensuring the dynamic performance and high efficiency of EREVs crucially depends on rational and effective energy management strategies and precise APU operational control.

Literature review

Energy management strategies(EMSs)for hybrid electric vehicles have been a hot topic of concern for scholars worldwide. Existing EMSs are primarily classified into rule-based and optimization-based approaches. Rule-based strategies mainly limit the working area of power system components and determine the working mode through preset control parameters. Depending on the type of selected control parameters, rule-based EMSs can be further classified into deterministic rules and fuzzy rules. Refs6] and [7 respectively employ thermostatic and power-following EMSs for energy distribution in hybrid electric vehicles, reducing fuel-to-electric conversion losses and extending battery life. Won et al.8 and Wassif et al.9 designed an EMS based on basic fuzzy control, achieving good fuel economy.

EMSs based on optimization algorithms can be classified into real-time optimization and global optimization strategies. Global optimization strategies are based on the global optimality theory and employ relevant optimization methods to determine the optimal energy allocation. Notably, dynamic programming (DP)10,11 and Pontryagin’s Minimum Principle (PMP)12,13 represent the two most widely applied global optimization methods. Regarding real-time optimization control, Equivalent Consumption Minimization Strategies (ECMS)14,15 and Model Predictive Control (MPC)16,17 are the two most representative methods. Additionally, a range of intelligent optimization algorithms, including the Quadratic Programming Algorithm18, Simulated Annealing19, and Mixed Integer Convex Programming Algorithm20, have also achieved satisfactory results. With the rapid development of machine learning technology, some reinforcement learning approaches in hybrid powertrain control have been increasingly adopted in researches21–24.

Although these strategies differ in formulation and implementation, most of them are developed under a steady-state efficiency-oriented paradigm, in which the APU is expected to operate near its optimal efficiency region defined by static characteristic maps. Existing studies primarily focus on the optimal allocation of energy between the power battery and the APU, or on maintaining the APU operation at high-efficiency operating points.

However, such a paradigm exhibits inherent limitations. First, most existing methods emphasize steady-state operating point optimization, while neglecting the dynamic characteristics of the APU during transient processes. In real driving conditions, the APU operates under highly dynamic environments, where frequent variations in vehicle power demand lead to continuous switching between different operating points. Second, the optimization is typically based on static efficiency maps, implicitly assuming quasi-steady operation of the engine-generator system. This assumption ignores the nonlinear and inertial characteristics of the engine, resulting in discrepancies between theoretical optimality and actual performance. Third, frequent operating point switching may introduce additional fuel consumption and dynamic losses, which cannot be captured by steady-state optimization alone.

To tackle the operation optimization problem of the APU, several studies have attempted to improve its efficiency by adjusting engine speed and torque trajectories. For example, Perez et al.25 treated the engine’s efficiency as a nonlinear function of power and utilized nonlinear programming techniques for trajectory optimization. Massimo et al.26 and Carlos et al.27 proposed an optimal control strategy of the engine–generator subsystem to generate a desired amount of energy within a given period of time. Ref28 introduced an optimization method for identifying the maximum efficiency operating point of the APU based on dynamic MAP, resulting in a 7.15% efficiency enhancement. In pursuit of heightened APU efficiency, Hong et al.29 utilized PID and fuzzy control theory to govern the engine, attaining an optimal APU operating trajectory. Likewise, Lan30, Xu31, and other researchers explored similar methodologies32,33. Nino et al.34 presented a calculation method for optimizing the trajectory of an APU, which aimed to obtain the optimal trajectory based on the maximum efficiency of the engine and generator subsystems according to the energy demand within a given time period. In response to the fuel consumption and emission challenges during the mode transition of the EREV, Ref35 employed an improved genetic algorithm to achieve an optimal trajectory during the dynamic switching process. Refs36–39 have explored trajectory optimization methods considering fuel consumption and emissions during dynamic processes.

Despite these efforts, the transient operation of the APU remains insufficiently addressed. The operating point switching process plays a crucial role in determining overall system performance. Due to the inherent nonlinearity and inertia of the engine, key variables such as air–fuel ratio, torque output, and combustion state cannot respond instantaneously to rapid changes in operating conditions. During transitions from low power to high power, the engine may temporarily operate under fuel-rich conditions, leading to deteriorated combustion, increased fuel consumption, and significant torque fluctuations. Moreover, for a given transition between two operating points, multiple feasible trajectories exist in the speed–torque domain, and different trajectories may result in substantially different performance outcomes. This indicates that the problem should be reformulated from conventional operating point optimization to transient trajectory optimization.

In addition to fuel economy and emissions, Noise, Vibration, and Harshness (NVH) is another critical performance index that has been largely overlooked in existing studies. This issue is particularly important for EREVs. Due to frequent APU start–stop events and operating point switching, vibration and noise become more perceptible to passengers40,41, thereby significantly affecting ride comfort. During transient processes, rapid changes in engine speed and torque can induce strong mechanical shocks, structural vibrations, and radiated noise. These NVH characteristics are highly sensitive to the transient trajectory and are strongly coupled with the dynamic behavior of the system, yet they cannot be effectively evaluated using steady-state performance metrics alone.

Limitations of existing research

Based on the above analysis, it can be concluded that existing studies on APU still exhibit several limitations. First, most research focuses on steady-state operating point optimization, with insufficient attention paid to the transient switching process between operating points. Second, although some studies have explored trajectory optimization methods, their objectives are primarily limited to fuel consumption and emissions, without considering ride comfort-related performance. Third, NVH characteristics, which are highly sensitive to transient dynamics, are rarely incorporated into the optimization framework. As a result, the coupling relationship between transient trajectories and NVH performance has not been adequately addressed.

Motivation and innovation

The transient operating point switching process of the APU is a crucial factor that impacts fuel consumption and NVH. During the transition from low power to high power, the engine must rapidly adjust throttle opening, intake air, and fuel injection to meet the increased load demand. However, due to the inherent delay in air–fuel ratio dynamics and the nonlinear characteristics of the engine, the combustion process may deteriorate, leading to increased vibration and noise, as well as reduced fuel efficiency.

To address these challenges, this paper proposes a trajectory optimization method for the APU transient operation process. The fuel consumption rate, body vibration intensity, and average sound pressure level (ASPL) of envelope surface radiated noise (ESRN) are considered simultaneously as optimization objectives. A multi-objective particle swarm optimization (MOPSO) algorithm combined with a weighted scaling method is employed to solve the optimization problem, thereby obtaining the optimal transient operating trajectory of the APU.

Three-zoo control strategy

Presently, thermostat control, power following control, and multi-operation point control are commonly used in engineering42,43. In the thermostat control strategy, the engine operates at a fixed point and outputs constant power, resulting in high work efficiency. However, constant power output may lead to repeated charging and discharging of the battery, affecting its health and reducing its lifespan. For the power following control strategy, the output power of the APU changes with the target power, enabling it to closely follow the power demand of the vehicle and avoid repeated charging and discharging of the power battery. However, this strategy often requires the APU to operate dynamically, leading to increased dynamic fuel consumption. The multi-operation point control strategy combines the advantages of power following and thermostat control. It allows the APU to operate at several working points, reducing the APU’s dynamic operation while extending the lifespan of the power battery. However, improper selection of working points can still lead to frequent engine operating point transitions.

Based on the above analysis6,44, this paper proposes a three-zone control strategy, which selects a reasonable torque variation range at three fixed speed points to form three operating zones, thereby achieving the goal of APU following the target power. The division of the three operating regions and the selection of the fixed engine speeds were determined based on statistics of the vehicle power demand under the three major cycles of FTP/HWFET/NEDC, determining that 0-10 kW, 8-18 kW and 16-30 kW are the three core power ranges, accounting for 92.3% of the total power demand. The three speed points of 1500 r/min, 2000 r/min, and 2750 r/min were chosen because they are located in relatively high-efficiency regions of the engine and allow the APU to cover the required power range (0–30 kW) with reasonable torque variation while satisfying the speed and torque constraints of both the engine and generator.

Presentation of the optimization problem

When there are substantial changes in vehicle power demand, the APU must adjust its speed operating points, transitioning between the A, B, and C regions. The transient trajectory during engine speed switching significantly influences engine fuel consumption, vibration and noise. Currently, some literatures only focus on the fuel economy during APU dynamic transitions. The problem is that the vibration and noise of the APU become particularly strong when switching over a large speed range, which seriously affects the ride comfort. Therefore, it is necessary to develop appropriate transient trajectory to enhance the fuel economy and reduce vibration and noise during APU speed transitions. In order to address this issue, this paper proposes a multi-objective optimization method for the APU’s transient trajectory, with the APU’s fuel consumption rate, body vibration intensity, and ASPL of the ESRN as the objective functions.

To obtain the optimal dynamic trajectory during the APU speed transition, point M (2000r/min, 72Nm) in region B and point N (2800r/min, 85Nm) in region C were selected as the study objects. Optimization research was conducted on the trajectory of the APU transitioning from point M to point N. The specific switching operation points of the APU are shown in Fig. 4. In real APU operation, multiple trajectories exist from point M to point N. Figure 4 depicts five transient operation trajectories. The challenge is how to choose the most optimal trajectory to minimize fuel consumption and vibration noise during the APU speed transition.

Multi-objective Optimization algorithm

In recent years, owing to the various advantages of swarm intelligence-based evolutionary algorithms, the use of multi-objective swarm intelligence-based evolutionary algorithms has become mainstream in scientific research and application domains for solving multi-objective optimization problems. Commonly used algorithms include the Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm (NSGA), Differential Evolution (DE), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). Among these, the PSO algorithm, due to its excellent convergence, unique search mechanism, and ease of computer program implementation, has been widely applied in many engineering fields. Therefore, this method is used to optimize the transient trajectory during the APU speed switching process.

Optimization of multi-objective models

Based on the characteristics of APU fuel consumption rate, vibration intensity, and ASPL of the ESRN shown in Figs. 5, 6 and 7, the MOPSO algorithm is applied in MATLAB to solve the multi-objective model. The parameter settings of the MOPSO algorithm are as follows: the inertia weight ω was linearly decreased from 0.9 to 0.4 during the iteration process to enhance global exploration in the early stage and improve local convergence in the later stage. The learning factors were set to c1 = 2 and c2 = 2, which are widely used in PSO-based multi-objective optimization studies. The population size was set to N = 200, and the size of the external elite archive was set to N_elite = 100, corresponding to a ratio of 0.5. This ratio was determined through preliminary tests to ensure sufficient diversity of the Pareto solution set while maintaining acceptable computational cost.

To evaluate the convergence performance and distribution quality of the Pareto front obtained by the MOPSO algorithm, the Generational Distance (GD) and Inverted Generational Distance (IGD) were used as convergence indicators. The calculated GD = 0.082 and IGD = 0.095 of the MOPSO algorithm in this paper are both much less than 0.2, which indicates that the algorithm’s Pareto front has a high approximation degree with the true front and excellent convergence performance.

The robustness of the optimization model was evaluated across diverse driving cycles and environmental conditions. Three representative scenarios were considered: Urban Driving (FTP Cycle): Frequent stops and low-speed operation increase transient APU activations. Highway Driving (HWFET Cycle): Sustained high speeds demand stable APU operation at higher power levels. Mixed Driving (NEDC Cycle): Combines urban and highway characteristics with moderate acceleration/deceleration.

The multi-objective optimization method shows a slight increase in fuel consumption compared to the single-objective optimization model but remains 9.71% lower than traditional optimization. The vibration intensity and ASPL under multi-objective optimization are the lowest among the three methods, with a 10.47% reduction in vibration intensity and a 12.17% reduction in radiation noise sound pressure level compared to traditional methods. The reason for such a large gap in results is that the engine in the APU does not switch continuously and slightly like traditional engine. Instead, in switches abruptly between two operating points with large differences in speed, torque, and power values. During the switching process, the engine operating point continuously changes along the dynamic characteristic curve, deviating from the conventional steady-state characteristic curve.

In the traditional control method, transitioning from low-power to high-power operation points requires the engine to quickly increase the throttle opening to ensure acceleration to a higher target speed value in a short period of time. In this acceleration process, the engine is in a fuel rich injection state, and there is a significant overshoot in the engine speed, which is inevitably accompanied by significant torque impact and mechanical noise, causing damage to the smoothness and fuel economy of the entire vehicle. For the single-objective optimization method, the engine runs along the optimal fuel economy trajectory, ignoring the vibration and noise during the switching process.

In summary, the MOPSO algorithm proposed can effectively addresses the vibration and noise problems during the transient operation points switching process of the APU at the cost of a slight increase in fuel consumption compared to the single objective optimization method. When compared to the traditional optimization method, it exhibits significant advantages in both fuel economy and the reduction of vibration and noise.

Conclusion

In order to improve fuel economy and reduce NVH, a trajectory optimization-based three-zone control strategy is proposed in this paper. The strategy comprehensively considers the APU operating points transient switching process, which significantly affects the fuel economy and NVH.

Initially, a novel three-zone control strategy is introduced for the EREV to optimize the APU steady-state operation. Simulation results demonstrate that this control strategy show significant enhancements in fuel efficiency compared to the traditional thermostat and power-following control strategy. Subsequently, in order to address the fuel consumption and NVH challenges, this study creatively optimizes not only the fuel economy but also the vibration and noise of the APU’s transient trajectory. The fuel consumption rate, body vibration intensity and ASPL of the ESRN are involved as optimization goals. The multi-objective particle swarm algorithm and weighted scaling method are employed to determine the operating trajectory. Finally, the proposed method is validated on a test bench and compared with two other methods. The results indicate that the transient trajectory obtained in this paper exhibited the lowest vibration intensity and ASPL, which effectively enhanced the NVH performance of the APU during the transient switching process, although fuel consumption increased slightly.

Although this study achieved significant results in optimizing the APU operating trajectory of EREVs, several areas remain for further exploration. For example, future research could integrate machine learning techniques with multi-objective optimization algorithms to further improve optimization accuracy and computational efficiency. Additionally, with the development of autonomous driving technology, incorporating dynamic demands from autonomous driving scenarios into the optimization model is another important direction for future research.

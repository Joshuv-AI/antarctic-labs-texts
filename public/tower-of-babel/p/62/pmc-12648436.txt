Tumor Extracellular Vesicle Digital Scoring Assays for Cancer Detection and Monitoring
Authors: Junseok Lee, Chen Zhao, Jacqueline Yang, Steven J. Jonas, Vatche Agopian, Sungyong You, Edwin Posadas, Ju Dong Yang, Yazhen Zhu, Hsian-Rong Tseng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Tumor-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) have emerged as promising targets for liquid biopsy, enabling noninvasive mRNA profiling to support cancer detection, staging, and treatment monitoring. This Review highlights the development and clinical translation of EV Digital Scoring Assays, an innovative class of two-step liquid biopsy platforms integrating click chemistry-mediated tumor EV enrichment (via EV Click Beads or EV Click Chips) with reverse transcription digital PCR (RT-dPCR) for absolute quantification of tumor mRNA. These assays have demonstrated clinical utility across multiple solid tumors: identifying oncogenic alterations in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and Ewing sarcoma, detecting early stage hepatocellular carcinoma in at-risk cirrhotic patients, differentiating localized from metastatic prostate cancer, and monitoring treatment response in hepatocellular carcinoma. We also examine emerging technologies and parallel efforts from other groups, including microfluidic isolation platforms, PCR-free digital detection, and machine learning-based analytics. Together, these studies showcase how “enrich-then-count” strategies can overcome challenges in tumor EV specificity and transcript quantification, offering scalable solutions for precision oncology. The EV Digital Scoring Assay provides a versatile and modular platform, potentially enabling broad adoption across diverse tumor types and clinical contexts for real-time noninvasive cancer management.

Introduction

Tumor tissue-based mRNA profiling has emerged as an essential component in clinical oncology, , offering critical insights for diagnosis, prognosis, and decision-making across a broad spectrum of cancer types. In particular, such mRNA profiling assays have been adopted in routine practice, owing to their capability to provide (i) early stage diagnostic and prognostic information, , particularly in evaluating recurrence risk; − (ii) guidance for therapeutic decisions, such as selecting targeted , or adjuvant therapies; , and (iii) functional interpretation of genetic information beyond DNA-level variations. For instance, in breast cancer, the Oncotype DX Assay , and MammoPrint have been clinically validated for guiding treatment decisions and prognosis, respectively. Similarly, mRNA-based diagnostics such as Oncotype DX Genomic Prostate Score (GPS) and Decipher Genomic Classifier , have been incorporated into prostate cancer management to refine risk stratification and tailor therapeutic approaches. These applications reflect the growing utility of mRNA profiling as a tool to enhance precision in oncology.

In spite of clinical relevance, tissue-based mRNA profiling remains challenging due to its inherent need for tissue biopsies. Sampling tissue for biopsy is often constrained by anatomical accessibility and tumor location, especially for internal organs, which also limits frequent and serial assessments (Figure A). Moreover, the spatiotemporal heterogeneity of tumors is often underrepresented in a single-point biopsy, thereby limiting the comprehensiveness of the molecular information obtained. Given these limitations, “liquid biopsy” has garnered significant attention as a minimally invasive alternative, enabling dynamic molecular monitoring through analysis of circulating tumor-derived components, , such as circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), circulating tumor cells (CTCs), and extracellular vesicles (EVs). These tumor biomarkers provide real-time access to the originating tumor, which could offer valuable insights into heterogeneous tumor biology.

(A) Comparison of conventional tumor tissue biopsy and blood-based liquid biopsy. While tissue biopsy remains the clinical gold standard for cancer diagnosis and histological evaluation, it is invasive, unsuitable for repeated assessment, and limited by tumor accessibility and heterogeneity. In contrast, blood-based liquid biopsy is minimally invasive, enables repeated sampling for longitudinal disease monitoring, and is broadly applicable across tumor types. Circulating tumor-derived analytesparticularly extracellular vesicles (EVs)can provide a more comprehensive view of tumor heterogeneity than single-point tissue biopsies. (B) Schematic overview of the EV Digital Scoring Assays. The assays adopt an “enrich-then-count” strategy based on the following two key steps: Step 1 involves click chemistry-mediated EV enrichment using tetrazine (Tz)-grafted substrates (EV Click Beads or EV Click Chips) in conjunction with a TCO-grafted motif (lipid or antibodies targeting tumor-specific surface markers). Step 2 quantifies target mRNA in the enriched EVs using digital PCR systems (RT-digital PCR or RT-droplet digital PCR). The resulting mRNA signatures enable noninvasive detection, staging, and monitoring across solid tumors.

Among liquid biopsy targets, EVs have emerged as a particularly compelling candidate for cancer diagnostics due to their distinctive characteristics. − EVs are nanoscale, lipid bilayer-enclosed vesicles actively secreted by nearly all cell types, including tumor and stromal cells within the tumor microenvironment. Tumor-derived EVs are detectable in blood at early disease stages and remain abundant as the disease progresses, with circulating levels reaching 108–1010 EV per mL plasma , and a circulation half-life of 1–5.5 h. − Importantly, tumor EVs are known to transport diverse biomolecular cargos, such as nucleic acids, proteins, and lipids, that reflect the physiological state of their cells of origin, including specific surface markers. These features position tumor EVs as attractive surrogates for direct tissue analysis, supporting their use as biomarkers for noninvasive cancer diagnostics and monitoring treatment responses. , In that context, Exosome Diagnostics developed the ExoDx Prostate Test, which detects a specific mRNA panels in urinary EVs to support decision-making of prostate biopsy in men with PSA levels in the “gray zone” (2–10 ng/mL). −

Nonetheless, the clinical translation of EV-based diagnostics has been impeded by technical challenges in isolating tumor EVs from the heterogeneous EV populations present in biofluids. Conventional EV isolation methods, such as ultracentrifugation, , size-exclusion chromatography, , polymer-based precipitation, dielectrophoresis, and size-based microfluidics, − generally rely on the physical properties of EVs (e.g., size and density) and offer bulk EV recovery without specificity for tumor origin. Immunoaffinity-based strategies have improved specificity by targeting tumor-associated surface proteins (e.g., GPC1, EGFRvIII, or a cocktail of multiple biomarkers). However, they often suffer from suboptimal capture efficiency due to low antigen density and the limitations of antibody precoating strategies, including physisorption, amide coupling, or biotin–streptavidin binding. These limitations can lead to reduced recovery, nonspecific binding, and biological interference, ultimately compromising downstream molecular analyses. −

To address these limitations, our team at UCLA Medical School has developed a suite of click chemistry-mediated tumor EV enrichment technologies, specifically Click Chips, − EV Click Beads, − and EV Click MagBead.s , These platform employ a highly bio-orthogonal inverse electron demand Diels–Alder reaction between trans-cyclooctene (TCO) and tetrazine (Tz) motifs, recognized for their rapid kinetics, specificity, and minimal biological interferences. In these strategies, tumor EVs are labeled in situ using TCO-grafted antibodies or lipid motifs, followed by capture on Tz-functionalized solid substrates. This approach improves upon conventional methods in several key aspects, including reduced antibody consumption, minimized biological interferences, faster labeling kinetics, and enhanced capture efficiencyeven with the limited antigen density on the EV surface. Moreover, the use of an antibody cocktail further overcomes tumor EV heterogeneity to enable effective capture. We have demonstrated the successful integration of these enrichment platforms with diverse molecular and functional analyses, such as mRNA profiling, − , protein detection, ,− and protease activity quantification, , enhancing the utility of tumor EV-based diagnostics.

Digital PCR (dPCR) has emerged as a powerful tool for quantitative detection of known cancer-specific transcripts and mutations, and thus can be used to analyze the limited mRNA content obtained from enriched tumor EVs. , Compared to next-generation sequencing (NGS), dPCR offers exceptional sensitivity and quantitative accuracy for known targets, with detection thresholds below 0.1%, reduced cost, and rapid turnaround, making it particularly suitable for clinical applications where precise quantification is essential. Moreover, dPCR generates quantitative readouts, e.g., copy number, facilitating clinically actionable thresholds. Recent advancements in digitalized EV analysis techniques have further enhanced the detection sensitivity. For instance, Weissleder’s group demonstrated single-molecule resolution in EV digital assays, , while Issadore’s team developed a droplet-based extracellular vesicle analysis (DEVA) platform to detect disease-specific EVs from as little as ∼ 2 μL of plasma.

Building upon this foundation, this review highlights the development of a new class of liquid biopsy assays termed EV Digital Scoring Assays (Figure B). The “enrich-then-count” strategy of the EV Digital Scoring Assays involves two key steps: Step 1 involves click chemistry-mediated enrichment of tumor EVs using EV Click Chips , or EV Click Beads, , and Step 2 entails quantification of mRNA cargo in the enriched tumor EVs using reverse transcription digital PCR (RT-dPCR) , or RT-droplet digital PCR (RT-ddPCR), , achieving absolute quantification of EV mRNA cargo from <2 mL of patient plasma. Except in cases where known oncogenic mutations were available (e.g., KRAS mutations in pancreatic cancer or EWS/FLI-1 rearrangements in Ewing sarcoma), the quantified panel of tumor EV-specific mRNA markers was integrated into a single diagnostic score using either a weighted Z-Score or logistic regression. This method condensed multimarker data into a simplified and interpretable readout to support clinical decision making. Through this strategy, we have demonstrated the detection of oncogenic mutations and scored a panel of tumor EV-specific mRNA markers, enabling (i) noninvasive detection of solid tumors, including Ewing Sarcoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), (ii) treatment monitoring of HCC patients who received surgical resection, liver transplantation, or locoregional therapies, and (iii) monitoring of disease progression from localized to metastatic prostate cancer. We further review recent literature employing similar combinations of tumor EV enrichment and digital mRNA quantification, highlighting the broader applicability and clinical promise of this emerging strategy for noninvasive cancer diagnosis and patient monitoring.

Clinical Applications of EV Digital Scoring Assays

Detection of Oncogenic Alterations in Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma and Ewing Sarcoma

We developed the inaugural EV Digital Scoring Assay (Figure A) for the noninvasive detection and quantification of oncogenic alterations, specifically KRAS mutations in PDAC and EWS/FLI-1 rearrangements in Ewing sarcoma. In Step 1, total EVs from 1 mL of sample were labeled with DSPE-PEG1000-TCO, enabling the insertion of TCO motifs into their lipid bilayer. The TCO-grafted EVs were then captured on Tz-grafted silica microbeads (EV Click Beads). In Step 2, the enriched EVs were lysed and subjected to RT-dPCR, yielding absolute copy numbers of oncogenic transcripts.

To validate its clinical utility in PDAC, we analyzed 35 plasma samples from 19 PDAC patients (including multiple blood draws) alongside 10 healthy donors (HDs). In 13 PDAC patients with matched formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue samples, 10 showed concordant KRAS subtypes (G12D, G12V, and G12R) between plasma EVs and tissue samples. For the remaining three patients with discordant results, two harbored mutations (one G12D and one G12R) in EVs onlylikely reflecting tumor heterogeneity beyond biopsyand one patient showed a tissue-only G12D mutation, possibly reflecting a change of the KRAS mutation between the time of tissue biopsy (at diagnosis) and blood collection (at a later stage). As shown in the boxplot of Figure A, the total EV KRAS transcript copy numbers (mutated KRAS + wild-type KRAS) were significantly (P < 0.01) higher in PDAC patients versus HDs, with no mutations detected in HDs. We further tracked a PDAC patient (PanC03) longitudinally by comparing EV KRAS copies with serum CA19-9 levelsthe gold standard biomarker for PDAC diagnosisand Computed Tomography (CT) images at Day 19 pretreatment and on Days 84 and 210 post-treatment (Figure B). By the final time point (Day 210 post-treatment), the patient exhibited a durable treatment response as decreased tumor burden shown in a CT image. Concordant declines in both EV KRAS transcript copy numbers and serum CA19-9 levels mirrored radiographic imaging, demonstrating the assay’s capability to monitor treatment responses.

Ewing sarcoma, lacking well-validated surface markers, is ideally suited to our lipid-grafting approach. We then quantified EWS/FLI-1 rearrangement transcriptsone of the most prevalent structural rearrangements in Ewing sarcoma − in 35 plasma samples from 28 clinically confirmed Ewing sarcoma patients and 10 HDs. In the 12 patients with paired FFPE tissue samples, we observed fully concordant EWS/FLI-1 rearrangement status in both EV and tissue. As expected, all 35 patient samplesbut none of the HDsharbored EWS/FLI-1 transcripts in EV mRNA (Figure A). In a longitudinal study of an Ewing sarcoma patient (ES07) with confirmed EWS/FLI-1 rearrangement, we further assessed dynamic changes in the copy numbers to monitor treatment response by comparing them with Positron Emission Tomography-Computed Tomography (PET/CT) images. Serial plasma samples were collected at baseline (Day 0) and on Days 43, 85, 113, and 164 post-treatment (Figure C). EV transcript dynamics reflected the PET/CT-observed partial response at Day 43 and metastatic relapse at Day 164, further supporting the clinical relevance of the assay to track disease progression.

Early Detection of Hepatocellular Carcinoma from At-Risk Patients with Cirrhosis

We then applied the EV Digital Scoring Assay (Figure A) to the noninvasive detection of early stage hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) in at-risk patients with underlying cirrhosis. The assay retained a similar two-step workflow. Step 1: Plasma HCC EVs in plasma samples were labeled with a cocktail of TCO-grafted antibodies against three well-validated HCC surface markers: EpCAM, ASGPR1, and CD147. The TCO-grafted EVs were then captured on Tz-grafted solid substrate, namely EV Click Chips, that incorporate (i) densely packed silicon nanowires substrates (SiNWS) to maximize the surface area and (ii) a PDMS chaotic mixer to further promote interaction between the SiNWS surface and the flow-through HCC EVs. The enriched HCC EVs were subsequently released by 1,4-dithiothreitol (DTT)-mediated cleavage of disulfide bonds between the SiNWS surface and the captured HCC EVs. Step 2: Total EV mRNA payloads extracted from the released HCC EVs, and 10 HCC-specific mRNA markers (AFP, GPC3, ALB, APOH, FABP1, FGB, FGG, AHSG, RBP4, and TF) were quantified using RT-ddPCR. These 10 markers were previously established in an HCC circulating tumor cell (CTC) study and were extensively validated using HCC cell line EVs and public EV databases (ExoCarta, Vesiclepedia, and exoRBase). A weighted Z-Score algorithm integrated the copy numbers into single HCC EV Z-Scores for each sample.

Clinical validation of the HCC EV Digital Scoring Assay was performed in 62 plasma samples: (i) 36 treatment-naïve, early stage HCC patients (BCLC stages 0–A) and (ii) 26 cirrhosis controls with various etiologies, including hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV), alcohol-related liver disease (ALD), and metabolic associated steatohepatitis (MASH). Heatmap analysis revealed stronger expression of the 10-gene panel in the HCC cohort (Figure B). Furthermore, the resulting HCC EV Z-Scores were significantly (P < 0.0001) higher in the early stage HCC patients than cirrhosis patients (Figure C). Given that patients with chronic liver diseases are considered at-risk for developing HCC, we evaluated the performance of the HCC EV Z-Score to distinguish early stage HCC from cirrhosis patients using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis (Figure D). The analysis yielded an area under a receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.93 (95% CI = 0.86–1.00; sensitivity = 94%, specificity = 89%), substantially outperforming the serum AFP level with AUROC of 0.69 (95% CI = 0.55–0.83; sensitivity = 66.7%, specificity = 72.0%), a current clinical standard for HCC detection. These findings underscore the Assay’s capacity to detect early stage HCC within at-risk cirrhosis populations with high accuracy, thereby offering an opportunity for curative-intent treatments to improve patient outcomes and survival.

Assessment of treatment responses in HCC patients

Building on the performance of the HCC EV Digital Scoring Assay in early stage HCC detection, we refined the platform to assess treatment responses in HCC patients (Figure A). Step 1: HCC EVs were enriched from 1 mL of plasma using mTz-grafted EV Click Beads in conjunction with a three TCO-grafted antibody cocktail (EpCAM, CD147, and ASGPR1), previously validated for HCC specificity. Step 2: Six HCC EV-specific mRNA markers (ALB, APOH, FGB, FGG, H2AX, and TF) were quantified by RT-dPCR. The 6-gene panel was derived from a comprehensive analysis of liver transcriptomic database across 59 cohorts (n = 9,160; GEO and BioStudies). The candidate markers were refined via extensive validation in HCC cell lines (qPCR) and tissue-based studies (RNAscope) to yield a 12-gene panel, which was further narrowed to 6 markers through pilot clinical validation. To evaluate clinical utility of the Assay, pre- and post-treatment (post-Tx) HCC EV Digital Scores were calculated, and their differences (ΔScores) were derived.

(B) Ladder plots of paired pre-Tx and post-Tx HCC EV Digital Scores in 67 viable and 33 nonviable post-Tx HCC patients. (C) Boxplot of ΔScores (difference of post-Tx and pre-Tx scores). Among 100 early/intermediate-stage HCC patients, 67 were classified as viable and 33 as nonviable on post-Tx imaging. Ladder plots of paired scores (Figure B) showed significantly greater median decline in ΔScores for nonviable than viable HCC cases (Figure C). We then integrated post-Tx and ΔScores via logistic regression model to generate an HCC EV Treatment Response (TR) Score (Figure D), which distinguished viable HCC from the nonviable HCC cohort with high significance (P < 0.001; Figure E). Additionally, in the 98 patients with available serum AFP levels, the HCC EV TR Score achieved an AUROC of 0.91 (95% CI = 0.84–0.97; sensitivity = 82%, specificity = 88%), outperforming post-Tx AFP (AUROC = 0.66) and ΔAFP (AUROC = 0.54) to distinguish viable HCC from nonviable HCC (Figure F).

Among the 100 HCC patients, 13 patients showed radiological evidence of recurrence within 180 days post-Tx. Notably, six of these showed discrepancies between initial post-Tx MRI (nonviable) and HCC EV TR Scores (viable); subsequent follow-up imaging confirmed the presence of viable lesions with a median lead time of 63 days (Figure G). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the HCC EV TR Score provides a sensitive, quantitative indicator of residual diseases and early relapse, complementing conventional imaging and serum AFP in post-Tx surveillance of HCC patients.

Differentiation of Metastatic Prostate Cancer from Localized Prostate Cancer

The two-step EV Digital Scoring Assay was next optimized for differentiating metastatic prostate cancer (PCa) from localized PCa cases (Figure ). Step 1: 1 mL of plasma was incubated with a TCO-grafted antibody cocktail targeting two PCa-associated surface markers (EpCAM and PSMA) to label PCa EVs, − followed by capture on EV Click Chips and DTT-mediated release of PCa EVs. Step 2: 11 PCa-relevant mRNA markersACP3, FOLH1, HOXB13, KLK2, KLK3, KLK4, MSMB, RLN1, SLC45A3, STEAP2, and TMPRSS2were identified through bioinformatic analysis of public tissue and biomarker databases and quantified from PCa EVs using RT-ddPCR. Their copy numbers were further integrated the into a single Met Score via a weighted Z-Score Algorithm.

(B) Heatmaps of the 11-gene panel in localized (n = 20) and metastatic (n = 20) PCa patients. (C) Boxplot showing significantly higher Met Scores in metastatic PCa patients. Heatmap analysis revealed consistently higher expression of the 11-gene panel in metastatic PCa compared with the localized cases (Figure B). Similarly, the calculated Met Scores were significantly higher in metastatic cases (P < 0.0001; Figure C). Subsequent ROC analysis evaluated the diagnostic performance of the Met Score, resulting in an AUROC of 0.88 (95% CI = 0.78–0.98; sensitivity = 85%, specificity = 75%), outperforming serum PSA level (AUROC = 0.64; 95% CI = 0.45–0.84; sensitivity = 65%, specificity = 75%), the current standard for PCa detection, to differentiate metastatic from localized PCa patients (Figure D). These findings suggested that the PCa EV Digital Scoring Assay offers a superior performance for early detection of disease progression in PCa patients, which could complement PSA testing in noninvasive staging and management of prostate cancer patients.

Relevant Works from Other Groups

A growing number of studies have adopted conceptually similar workflowsselective EV enrichment followed by digital PCR quantificationto demonstrate clinical utility across a range of malignancies. Toren et al. used antibody-coated beads to isolate PSMA-positive PCa EVs and quantified KLK3 (PSA) and AR-V7 mRNAs by RT-ddPCR; detectable AR-V7 mRNA in PCa EVs correlated with lower circulating androgens and poorer outcomes in castrate-resistant prostate cancer patients. Likewise, Balaj and colleagues detected mutated EGFRvIII mRNA transcript in total plasma EVs from glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) patients, supporting EV-based diagnosis and longitudinal monitoring in GBM. In addition, Zheng’s group further enhanced diagnostic accuracy in breast cancer by adopting machine learning-assisted analysis of the EV-mRNA (Figure A), achieving an improved AUROC value of 1.000 with 94.74% sensitivity and 100% specificity (Figure B).

Advances in microfluidic capture have likewise broadened the capabilities of EV-based assays. Stott’s EVHB-Chipa herringbone-patterned microfluidic device (Figure C)achieved 94% tumor-EV specificity and the detection of mutant EGFRvIII mRNA from as few as 100 tumor EV/μL from GBM EVs within 3 h (Figure D). Similarly, Soper et al. introduced the EV-MAP chip for orthogonal selection of epithelial (EpCAM) and mesenchymal (FAPα) EV subpopulations in breast cancer; when coupled with the commercially available PAM50 Prosigna assay, it achieved 100% concordance with tumor tissue subtyping. More recently, Sun and Xu upgraded our SiNWS-based EV Click Chips by integrating zigzag microfluidic channels, enhancing EV capture performance, and enabling ddPCR-based quantification of EGFR and GAPDH mRNAs to distinguished cancer (HCC, CRC, and PDAC) patients from healthy controls.

Downstream detection technologies are also evolving beyond conventional digital PCR. Zeng et al. developed a microwell array with dual-probe hybridization for PCR-free, single-molecule detection of EV mRNAs. Using an EV isolated by ultracentrifugation, they quantified GAPDH and the EWS/FLI-1 rearrangement transcript from patient-derived cell line EVs. In addition, Cho’s EV-CLIP platform (Figure E) detected the EGFR L858R mutation from only 20 μL of plasma from lung cancer patients, achieving an AUROC of 1.000 (Figure F), and enabled serial monitoring during chemotherapy. Furthermore, Sun’s group reported a DNA tetrahedron-based thermophoretic assay (DTTA) that showed ultrasensitive detection of PSA mRNA within serum EVs, with a detection limit as low as 14 aM. This approach successfully discriminated PCa from benign prostate hyperplasia (BPH), achieving an AUROC of 0.93, notably outperforming serum PSA (AUROC = 0.74).

Taken together, these efforts highlight the rapid evolution of tumor EV enrichment and mRNA quantification. Whether through improved affinity substrates, microfluidic geometries, machine-learning interpretation, or next-generation single-molecule readouts, the field continues to converge on highly sensitive and minimally invasive assays that promise to refine cancer detection staging and treatment monitoring.

Discussions

Over the past decade, our collaborative team has established a suite of EV Digital Scoring Assays that combine click chemistry-mediated EV enrichment with RT-digital PCR platform to quantify EV mRNA from <2 mL of plasma. The assays adopt the “enrich-then-count” strategy that involves two key steps. Step 1 (enrichment) employs Tz-grafted solid substratesEV Click Chips or EV Click Beadstogether with two interchangeable TCO-labeling agents: DSPE-PEG1000-TCO, a lipid that inserts into all EV membranes and enables unbiased capture when tumor-specific epitopes are unknown, and TCO-grafted antibody cocktails against validated surface markers (e.g., EpCAM/ASGPR1/CD147 in HCC or EpCAM/PSMA in PCa) to selectively enrich tumor EVs when such epitopes are available. The bio-orthogonal reaction between TCO-Tz yielded rapid, high-affinity capture without biological interferences common to conventional physisorption or biotin–streptavidin coupling. Step 2 (readout) digitalized the mRNA transcripts into droplets (ddPCR) or nanowells (dPCR) for absolute quantification as copy numbers, achieving single-digit sensitivity, cost-efficient analysis, and same-day turnaround.

Collectively, these studies demonstrated that click chemistry-mediated EV enrichment offered the first layer of specificity (when surface markers are available), while digital PCR supports the second layer of specificity (when specific mRNA panels are available), yielding highly sensitive and quantitative EV assays tumor heterogeneity, low-antigen density on EVs, and limited sample amount. Importantly, this approach is universally applicable: by swapping antibody cocktails or lipid labels depending on available surface markers and redesigning mRNA panels, the similar two-step pipeline can be redeployed across a broad range of solid tumors or even rare pediatric cancers.

Parallel efforts from other groups reinforce this paradigm (Figure ). Immunoaffinity-based isolation of tumor-specific EVs has been widely adopted, and more recently, adoption of machine learning could improve the clinical performance of downstream readouts from RT-dPCR analysis of EV mRNA cargo. Emerging microfluidic devices and PCR-free single-molecule-level readouts further illustrated the rapid technological convergence on an “enrich-then-count” strategy for EV-mRNA diagnostics in cancer diagnosis and monitoring.

Conclusion and Future Perspective

While the current EV Digital Scoring Assays represent a significant advancement in tumor EV-based diagnostics, addressing these limitations and pursuing the outlined future directions will be essential before transitioning these technologies into routine clinical practice. Clinical validation. The proof-of-concept studies were based on retrospective analyses from a modest cohort size. Prospective multicenter trials with longitudinal sampling will be essential to confirm the assay’s diagnostic accuracy, define actionable thresholds, and evaluate cost-effectiveness. Particular emphasis should be placed on early diagnosis, detecting minimal residual disease (MRD) and comparison with current gold standards, such as radiographic imaging and established serum biomarkers, or even emerging liquid biopsy assays, such as ctDNA-based blood tests. Marker expansion. Although we proposed several markers for selective enrichment of HCC and PCa EVs, targeting tumors with poorly characterized surface markers and/or a gene panel is challenging. Integration of emerging bioinformatic profiling of EVs, including proteomic, single-EV transcriptomic, or even multiomics analysis , could provide a new surface epitopes and RNA signatures, enabling assays for rare cancers (e.g., sarcomas and pediatric cancers). Such bioinformatic analysis will further enhance the assay’s performance by providing a more comprehensive understanding of the EV populations and their molecular contents. Technology optimization. For EV enrichment, compromising the stability and reactivity of TCO and Tz analogues could enhance the assays’ performance. In that regard, we have adopted the more stable methyltetrazine (mTz) in the newly developed EV Click Beads and EV Click MagBeads , to enhance the stability of surface Tz motifs and extend their storage life from 2 weeks to over three months. For the EV analysis, combining mRNA-based EV Digital Scoring with other multiomics techniques (e.g., protein of lipid analyses) could yield richer biological insight without sacrificing throughput. Clinical integration. Ultimately, these assays must fit smoothly into existing clinical practices. During the screening stage, e.g., early detection from at-risk patients, high-score individuals could be triaged to encourage in-depth diagnostics such as radiographic imaging or tissue biopsy. For treatment monitoring, specific algorithms, such as ΔDigital Scores, may guide decisions for additional adjuvant therapy or trigger early salvage treatment. Of note, development of commercial assay kits following the regulatory standards and automated data reporting system will be necessary for widespread adoption.

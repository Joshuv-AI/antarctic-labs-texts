Comparative efficacy of photodynamic therapy versus triamcinolone in symptomatic oral lichen planus: IL-1Beta, IL-6, TNF-Alpha, and MDA biomarker analysis
Authors: Sakshi Batra, Adit Srivastava, Kamlesh Manohar Palandurkar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and Objectives:Oral lichen planus (OLP) is a chronic inflammatory, autoimmune, and potentially malignant disorder of the oral mucosa. Photobiomodulation (PBM) aids tissue repair, while photodynamic therapy (PDT) selectively targets diseased cells and modulates cytokines. This study compares the effectiveness of PDT (660 nm) and triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% in managing symptomatic OLP. It evaluates their impact on serum and salivary biomarkers (IL-1Beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, and MDA) at baseline and at 3 months and correlates these markers with pain and clinical scores (VAS and RAE) to determine their therapeutic efficacy.Materials and Methods:A randomized controlled trial on 28 OLP patients compared two treatments: Group I received 0.1% TA (3 times/day for 1 month); Group II underwent PDT with 1% toluidine blue and 660 nm diode laser (100 mW, 60 sec/cm², 2 times/week for 1 month). Pain with VAS and lesion size with RAE were assessed at baseline and 1, 2, and 3 months. Biomarkers were evaluated using ELISA and thiobarbituric acid tests.Results:Both treatments significantly reduced pain and lesion size. PDT demonstrated greater pain reduction than TA and slightly more lesion size reduction, though not statistically significant. Biomarkers showed no significant changes except a correlation between salivary IL-1β and VAS in the TA group.Conclusion:PDT and TA effectively managed pain and lesion size in OLP, with PDT showing a trend toward greater improvement. PDT is a promising alternative for OLP management.

INTRODUCTION

Lichen planus (LP) is a chronic inflammatory condition that affects the skin, mucous membranes (oral and genital), nails, and hair follicles. The most prevalent form of mucosal LP is oral lichen planus (OLP), which may present as isolated oral lesions or in combination with cutaneous, nail, scalp, or other mucosal involvement, including the genital, gastrointestinal, ocular, and laryngeal regions.[1] OLP is a chronic inflammatory disease of the oral mucosa with various forms: reticular, plaque-like, papular, atrophic, erosive, and bullous. Its hallmark is Wickham striae—white keratotic lines on the buccal mucosa, gingiva, and tongue.[23] It is characterized by relapses and remissions and is most commonly found in women in the 4th to 5th decade of life, with a prevalence rate of 0.5–2.5%.[45] Cutaneous LP lesions are self-limiting and sometimes pruritic, whereas oral lesions are chronic and treatment-resistant and have malignant potential, contributing to morbidity.[6]

The exact cause of OLP is unclear but involves T-cell-mediated autoimmune apoptosis of oral epithelial cells. The etiopathogenesis of OLP involves both antigen-specific and nonspecific mechanisms.[78] Factors like viruses, medications, dental materials, stress, genetics, and psychological influences may contribute. With a debated malignant transformation rate of 12.5%, biopsy is essential to differentiate OLP from other chronic oral lesions.[9101112] However, OLP is a very debatable condition due to the absence of specific diagnostic criteria, and recent literature has strengthened the inclusion of clinical and histopathological features for definitive OLP diagnosis.[1]

Symptomatic OLP management varies by severity and subtype. First-line treatment includes topical/systemic steroids, though prolonged use may cause side effects like GI upset, mood changes, and infection risk. Second-line options include calcineurin inhibitors (e.g., tacrolimus), retinoids, vitamin D analogs, phototherapy, and steroid-sparing agents. Despite these therapies, OLP remains incurable, emphasizing the need for safer, long-term treatment options.[131415]

Low-level light therapy (LLLT) or PBM enhances healing, reduces inflammation, and relieves pain by stimulating mitochondrial cytochrome C to boost cellular metabolism. PDT uses light-sensitive drugs and oxygen to target microbes or cancer cells. PBM and PDT offer significant benefits for inflammatory disorders with minimal side effects, outperforming corticosteroids.[1617]

Studies show that proinflammatory and anti-inflammatory cytokine levels in OLP patients differ from healthy individuals, with markers like IL-1β, IL-6, IL-17, IFN-γ, TNF-α, CXCL10, and CXCL-8. IL-6 levels are elevated in both reticular and erosive/atrophic OLP.[1718]

As the modulation of cytokines in OLP by topical corticosteroids and PBM is poorly understood, there was a need for research to evaluate serum and salivary cytokine levels to monitor the disease response therapies, including PDT and standard corticosteroid treatment. Hence, this study aims to comparatively evaluate the effectiveness of PDT (660 nm) versus 0.1% triamcinolone acetonide in the management of symptomatic OLP. The objectives include assessing and comparing the clinical outcomes of both treatment modalities, evaluating their impact on serum and salivary levels of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and MDA biomarkers, and analyzing the correlation between these biomarkers and clinical indicators such as pain (VAS) and lesion severity (RAE scores).

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A randomized controlled trial (RCT) compared PDT, 660 nm, and TA 0.1% for managing symptomatic OLP patients. Outcomes were evaluated using VAS for pain, Thongprasom’s RAE scores, and biomarkers (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, MDA). Ethical Clearance was obtained from Institutional Ethical Committee with Reference No. Dean/2022/EC/3474) dated 20.08.2022 and registered under CTRI/2022/10/046519, the parallel two-arm trial. Approved by Institutional Ethical Committee (Dean/2022/EC/3474) and registered under CTRI/2022/10/046519, the parallel two-arm trial followed ethical guidelines. The sample size (approximately 23 patients) was based on salivary IL-6 data (Abboud et al., 2021).[17] Patients fulfilling the inclusion criteria (stated below) were selected and were enrolled after clinical diagnosis of OLP based on the patient’s history, clinical features, and histopathological (WHO Criteria) confirmation by biopsy.

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS v26 software at a 5% significance level. Descriptive statistics summarized demographics, while Repeated Measures ANOVA, Bonferroni post-hoc tests, and Paired T-tests analyzed within-group changes. Independent T-tests compared intergroup VAS and RAE scores, and Wilcoxon Signed Rank/Mann–Whitney tests assessed biomarkers. Pearson’s correlation evaluated relationships between biomarkers and clinical scores.

Group I showed significant reductions in RAE from baseline to the 1st (1.58 ± 1.02), 2nd (1.58 ± 1.33), and 3rd months (1.83 ± 1.34) (P = 0.036), with a slight increase at the 3rd month. DISCUSSION

OLP is a chronic autoimmune disorder of unclear origin, driven by T-cell-induced apoptosis of epithelial cells through both specific and nonspecific mechanisms. In the specific pathway, CD8 cytotoxic T-lymphocytes eliminate basal keratinocytes in the epidermis, triggered by cytokine upregulation, leading to clonal expansion of cytotoxic cells and Langerhans cell mobilization. The nonspecific pathway involves mast cell degranulation and activation of matrix metalloproteinases within OLP lesions. Interactions between T lymphocytes and mast cells further drive inflammation by releasing signaling molecules, chemokines, and matrix metalloproteinases, promoting T-cell activation, migration, proliferation, and differentiation.[78] This leads to inflammation, hyperkeratosis, erythema, and potential ulceration. Cytokines as TNF-α, IL-6, and INF-γ drive lymphocyte activity and keratinocyte apoptosis. Understanding these mechanisms is key to developing effective treatments.[1920]

Photodynamic therapy is an alternative therapy that uses light-activated photosensitizers and generates reactive oxygen species (ROS) or singlet oxygen, potentially suppressing inflammation through immunomodulation. ROS and singlet oxygen exhibit precise oxidative damage potential, inducing apoptosis in overactive inflammatory cells. This process leads to a reversal of both hyperproliferation and inflammation in symptomatic lichen planus, thus providing long-term benefits for the management of OLP with reduced side effects.[2122] The evaluation of cytokine serum and salivary levels were necessary to monitor the disease response therapies including PDT and normal corticosteroid treatment because the modulation of cytokines in OLP by topical corticosteroids and PDT is minimally understood.[23]

Hence, the present study was carried out to compare the effectiveness of PDT (660 nm, toluidine blue 1%) versus triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% in symptomatic oral lichen planus patients and assessment of IL-1Beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, and MDA biomarkers.

The study included 28 subjects, with a female-to-male ratio of 2:1 (16 females, 8 males). Group I had 7 females (63.6%) and 4 males (36.4%), while Group II had 9 females (69.2%) and 4 males (30.8%), confirming female predominance in both groups. This was in accordance with most of the studies like Wu KM et al. (2013),[24] and Li. Changchang et al. (2020),[25] and Gururaj, N et al. (2021).[26] However, a few studies have also found male predominance, like Bhonsle R.B et al. (1976),[27] Salem G (1989),[28] Sun, A et al. (2000),[29] and Munde Anita D et al. (2013).[30]

In our study, the mean age was 39.55 ± 11.42 years in Group I and 37.15 ± 12.03 years in Group II, with most participants aged 21–56 years. The mean age in our study was similar to the results found in other studies, Nogueira P. A., Carneiro S., and Ramos-e-Silva M. (2015),[7] Wu KM et al. (2013).[24] and Li Changchang et al. (2020).[25]

The mean VAS and RAE at baseline for Group I and Group II showed no significant difference, indicating proper randomization and equal distribution of patients for comparison. In intragroup comparison, mean VAS and RAE scores decreased significantly from baseline to the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd-month follow-ups in both groups (VAS: Group I, P = 0.036; Group II, P = 0.004). In intergroup comparison, VAS was significantly lower in Group II than in Group I (P < 0.001), while RAE was also lower in Group II, but the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.001).

Our study found that both the treatment modalities showed improvement for VAS and RAE, which was similar to studies conducted by Jajaram et al. (2015),[20] Maloth et al. (2016),[31] Bakhtiari et al. (2017),[32] Fatemeh Lavaee and Maryam Shadmanpour (2019),[33] Saleh et al. (2020),[34] Zborowski et al. (2021),[35] and so on.

However, a few studies like those conducted by Jajaram et al. (2015),[20] Fatemeh Lavaee, and Maryam Shadmanpour (2019)[33] found out that corticosteroids had significant improvement over PDT.

Studies like Maloth et al. (2016),[31] Bakhtiari et al. (2017),[32] and He Y et al. (2020)[35] found PDT to be as effective as corticosteroids for treating oral lichen planus, making it a good alternative for refractory cases or those with long-term side effects. Additionally, studies by Saleh et al. (2020),[34] Mirza et al. (2018),[36] and Zoborowski et al. (2021)[37] suggested that PDT might be a better treatment option when evaluated using VAS and RAE.

Studies on OLP treatment with PDT versus corticosteroids have shown varied results, but PDT is a promising alternative. In our study, PDT showed better pain relief (VAS) with fewer side effects than corticosteroids, making it beneficial for patients unable to use steroids. Although PDT showed better RAE scoring, no statistically significant difference was observed compared to corticosteroids.

Previous OLP studies using PDT varied in laser settings and protocols. Our study used a 660 nm diode laser, 1% toluidine blue, 6J/point for 60 seconds/cm², continuous mode, with a 3-month follow-up. Further studies with similar parameters and longer follow-ups are needed to standardize dosages and laser settings for effective treatment.

TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and MDA correlate with OLP severity. In this study, neither TA 0.1% nor PDT significantly impacted these biomarkers in serum or saliva, with no treatment differences observed. Further research is needed for long-term insights. Humberto et al. (2018)[27] highlighted the diagnostic and prognostic value of salivary biomarkers (NO, cortisol, IL-6, IL-4, TNF-α, IFN-γ) in monitoring OLP progression and treatment response.

Studies by Rhodus et al. (2005)[38] and Abdelghaffar AA. et al. (2020)[39] identified TNF-α and IL-1β as potential biomarkers for diagnosing, monitoring, and assessing treatment response in OLP. Studies showing the correlation of IL-6 serum and salivary biomarker concentration with OLP severity and inflammatory response are Abdel-Haq et al. (2014),[40] Humberto, J. S. M. et al. (2018),[27] Melguizo-Rodríguez L. et al. (2020),[28] and so on.

Studies, including Santarelli A. et al. (2015),[22] Batu S. et al. (2016),[41] and Wang J. et al. (2021),[42] show elevated serum and salivary MDA levels in OLP patients correlate with lesion severity, indicating diagnostic and prognostic potential.

Our study on OLP patients treated with triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% and PDT found weak correlations between serum markers and clinical scores. This suggests OLP is likely T-cell-mediated, with treatments targeting local rather than systemic inflammation.

A few studies have explored cytokines and biomarkers in relation to topical corticosteroids. NL Rhodus et al. (2006)[43] found that NF-κb-dependent cytokines, including IL-1α and IL-8, correlated with VAS in OLP patients treated with dexamethasone. Youngnak-Piboonratanakit et al. (2009)[44] noted IFN-γ expression in OLP tissue, and treatment with fluocinolone acetonoide reduced IFN-γ.

Cosgarea et al. (2020)[18] linked PDT to biomarker changes in OLP, showing reduced lesions, improved quality of life, and anti-inflammatory effects, including decreased CD8+/CD4+ T-cell ratios, IL-17-secreting T cells, and CXCL10 plasma levels.

Our findings also showed weak and negligible correlation of various salivary biomarkers (TNF-α, IL-6, MDA) both before and after treatment with VAS and RAE. The only marker, IL-1β, had a high correlation to VAS in saliva collected 3 months after therapy with 0.1% TA.

This aligns with the observation of Abboud C. S. et al. (2021),[17] which reported that saliva contained higher cytokine levels than blood, with IL-1β being the most abundant and correlating with OLP severity. Notably, topical steroid treatment significantly reduced IL-1β in saliva. However, no photosensitizers were used in this study, but it strengthens the potential of salivary IL-1β as a marker for monitoring treatment response in patients receiving TA for OLP.

CONCLUSION

The study concludes that OLP is more prevalent in females (2:1 ratio) and commonly affects individuals aged 21–56, particularly in the 3rd to 4th decades. PDT and triamcinolone acetonide 0.1% both significantly reduced pain (VAS) and lesion size (RAE), with PDT showing a trend toward better pain relief. Biomarkers, except for IL-1β in saliva correlating with VAS, showed no significant modulation or relation to disease progression. PDT, with no side effects, is an effective alternative for pain management in OLP, especially in patients unsuitable for steroids. Larger studies are recommended for better insights.

Strengths and limitations

This study offers key insights into the effectiveness of PDT and TA 0.1% (OLP) management. It utilizes standardized outcome measures, explores biomarker variations, and identifies PDT as a viable steroid-free alternative. The study also highlights gender and age trends while establishing a correlation between IL-1β and pain. With robust statistical analysis, it provides a solid foundation for future research with larger samples and extended follow-ups.

The limitations of this study include a small sample size, variations in laser parameters, and the need for a longer follow-up with marker evaluation at different time points. Future studies with larger sample sizes, standardized laser settings, and assessments over extended periods are recommended to strengthen the validity of the results.

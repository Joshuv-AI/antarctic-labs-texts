Parent-guided home skin care for infants via a WeChat platform: a study protocol for a cluster randomized controlled trial
Authors: Tongling Yang, Yuying Chen, Yalan Dou, Mengfan Qiu, Yanchen Wang, Siyuan Jiang, Xiaojing Hu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDiaper dermatitis and eczema are common skin conditions that affect infants with a high incidence rate. The prevention and management of these conditions largely depend on parents’ adherence to appropriate home skin care practices, with the effectiveness of these practices closely linked to their accurate and consistent implementation. WeChat, a widely used messaging platform in China, has demonstrated potential in promoting health awareness and self-management strategies. We have developed a WeChat-based parent-guided home skin care scheme for infants, specifically designed to assist parents in managing the skin care of newborns. This study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of parent-guided home skin care administered via the WeChat platform in preventing diaper dermatitis and eczema in infants aged 0–6 months, using a clustered randomized controlled trial design.MethodsThis study will be carried out as a cluster randomized controlled trial in the maternity wards of eight hospitals across China. The randomization sequence will be generated by an independent statistical team using specialized software, allocating each center to either the intervention or control group. Infants born in the participating maternity wards between August 1, 2024, and September 30, 2024, whose parents provide voluntary consent, will be included in the study. The exclusion criteria include newborns who require hospitalization immediately following birth, infants diagnosed with congenital skin disorders, and newborns whose mothers have been diagnosed with a mental illness that compromises their ability to provide care or are suffering from a serious illness necessitating hospitalization. The primary outcome of this study is the incidence of diaper dermatitis in 1-month-old infants. Secondary outcomes include the incidence of diaper dermatitis and eczema at 3 and 6 months of age, the frequency of hospital visits due to skin issues, and the assessment of home skin care knowledge.DiscussionThe findings from this study may offer valuable insights into the effectiveness of digital health interventions in guiding parents, particularly through the WeChat platform. These interventions may enhance infant skin care practices and reduce the incidence of common skin conditions during early infancy.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov NCT06544096. Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

Diaper dermatitis is one of the most common skin conditions affecting newborns, infants, and young children, with an estimated incidence of approximately 25–50% among infants worldwide [1–3]. In China, the incidence of diaper dermatitis among infants aged 1 to 24 months treated in pediatric clinics reaches 43.8% [4]. Similarly, infantile eczema, a chronic and recurrent inflammatory skin disorder, typically emerges 2–3 months after birth, with the highest incidence observed in infants (15%) and young children (16.5%) [5]. Shen et al. reported an eczema incidence rate of 57.3% in China, with cases occurring more frequently shortly after the neonatal period [6].

The prevention and treatment of diaper dermatitis and eczema largely depend on parents’ adherence to appropriate home skin care practices, which must be implemented accurately and consistently to ensure effectiveness [7]. However, with nearly 70% of parents turning to the internet for newborn skin care information [8], they often encounter misinformation and misconceptions, hindering their ability to identify reliable strategies. Additionally, time constraints often limit mothers’ engagement in in-person health education sessions. Given these challenges, developing and promoting effective health education strategies is essential to reduce the incidence of diaper dermatitis and eczema in newborns.

In recent years, smartphones have emerged as essential tools for health promotion and self-management [9]. Mobile health, an evolving field that delivers health services via mobile devices, has demonstrated a positive impact on treatment adherence [10]. Research conducted by Strand [11] indicates that digital communication can support parents, facilitate the transition from hospital to home, and reduce the necessity for hospital visits. Furthermore, parents serve as primary caregivers for their infants and have expressed a preference for smartphone applications over other devices, such as iPads. WeChat, launched by Tencent in 2011, has emerged as one of the most popular social platforms in China, with 1.15 billion monthly active users as of November 2019 [12]. As a tool for health education and disease management, WeChat has proven to be more effective than traditional methods. It reduces time and economic costs, improves treatment compliance, increases follow-up rates, enhances patient outcomes, and minimizes complications [13, 14].

Building on this background, we have developed a WeChat-based program to guide home skin care for newborns. This initiative is grounded in an evidence-based skin care protocol specifically tailored for infants aged 0–6 months. The protocol, along with parent-guided educational materials and instructional videos, is available on the WeChat platform. Additionally, the program provides professional consultation for parents facing challenges related to newborn skin care at home. The objective of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of a parent-guided, WeChat-based intervention in improving parental practices and preventing diaper dermatitis and eczema in infants. If the intervention proves effective, it could serve as a model for digital health interventions, guiding parents across China and potentially improving infant care and reducing the incidence of common skin conditions nationwide.

Objectives {7}

The primary objective of the study is to evaluate the effectiveness of parent-guided home skin care, delivered via the WeChat platform, in reducing the incidence of diaper dermatitis and eczema in infants within 6 months after birth.

Trial design {8}

This study will employ a cluster randomized controlled trial (RCT) design, with the hospitals serving as the unit of randomization (clusters). We will recruit eight hospitals. The eight participating centers will be randomly assigned to either the experimental or control group in a 1:1 ratio. All participating institutions are tertiary perinatal centers or general hospitals. These modern facilities integrate medical care, education, and research while possessing a robust foundation in nursing science. Methods: participants, interventions and outcomes

Study setting {9}

A supervisory unit will be established to oversee quality control, coordination, and technical support throughout the duration of the study. We will recruit obstetric wards from eight hospitals strategically located in the eastern, southern, northern, central, and southeastern regions of China, each with an annual delivery volume exceeding 2,000. The eight hospitals are Guangxi Maternal and Child Health Hospital, Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital, Hainan Women and Children’s Medical Center, Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region Maternal and Child Health Hospital, Jinjiang Municipal Hospital (Shanghai Sixth People’s Hospital Fujian Campus), Tongji Hospital of Tongji Medical College of HUST, Wuxi People’s Hospital, and Suzhou Municipal Hospital.

This study will provide remote post-discharge home care guidance for newborns using an integrated “WeChat platform + parent-guided home skin care” model. Leveraging the newborn’s date of birth, the WeChat-based system will generate personalized home care recommendations, which are dynamically tailored to their age in days. Participants will receive standardized skincare protocols and will be prompted to report their compliance via the platform’s built-in questionnaire tool.

Hospital eligibility criteria

We will recruit hospitals with maternity wards that have expressed a written commitment to implement the WeChat intervention and data collection procedures, with an annual delivery volume of at least 2000 births (based on the previous 12-month records), to ensure an adequate number of participants.

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

The informed consent process will be conducted by the Principal Investigator (PI) or a trained study coordinator who has completed Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and human subjects protection training. This process will occur during the initial screening visit at the study site, within a private and quiet environment to ensure confidentiality. Parents of newborns who meet the inclusion and exclusion criteria will be approached and provided with detailed information about the study’s aims, procedures, potential risks, benefits, and alternatives. The objectives of the research will be clearly explained, emphasizing the potential benefits to the scientific community and future neonatal care practices. Parents will be given ample opportunity to ask questions and discuss any concerns they might have regarding their participation and the implications for their infants.

Once parents have a comprehensive understanding of the study, they will be asked to provide their informed consent. The informed consent form (ICF), version 1.0, dated 1 July 2024, will be provided in the parents’ preferred language and has been approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB). The ICF outlines the key elements of the study, including its purpose, duration, procedures, potential risks and discomforts, confidentiality measures, and the right to withdraw from the study at any time without penalty.

If a parent is unable to read or write, the ICF will be read aloud in the presence of an impartial witness, who will also sign the form to confirm the process. Electronic consent may be obtained using a secure, IRB-approved e-consent platform, which allows parents to review the ICF and provide their electronic signature.

Parents have the right to withdraw their child from the study at any time without penalty. Should they choose to withdraw, they will be asked if they consent to the continued use of data collected up to the point of withdrawal. A copy of the signed ICF will be provided to the parents, and the original will be retained in the study records.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

The comparator selected for this study is the standard of care. This choice is based on the following considerations:

Clinical relevance

The standard care was selected as the comparator because it represents the most widely accepted treatment for infants, enabling the study results to be directly applicable to clinical practice.

Ethical considerations

The use of standard care is ethically justified because participants in the control group will receive standard care to ensure their safety.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

At any time, if the participants request to discontinue the intervention for personal reasons, researchers are obligated to respect their wishes while ensuring the integrity and scientific rigor of the study. The investigator will document the reason for discontinuation and continue to follow up in accordance with the study protocol, unless the participant formally withdraws consent for further participation. Any decision to discontinue or modify the intervention will be made in compliance with the study protocol, ethical guidelines, and applicable regulatory requirements. All data collected prior to discontinuation will be included in the final analysis, unless the participant explicitly withdraws consent for data usage.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

Participants will receive detailed information regarding the importance of adhering to the intervention protocol during the informed consent process and at each study visit. A study coordinator will be available to address questions and provide ongoing support throughout the trial. For the intervention group, a WeChat message will be sent to remind parents to review the skin care guidance. Concurrently, the system will track the number of times parents access the content to ensure the effectiveness of the intervention.

To ensure rigorous quality control, this study implements a multi-faceted quality control framework that systematically monitors intervention fidelity and adherence. Key strategies include tracking content open rates via read receipts, documenting consultation frequency and questionnaire completion rates, conducting weekly audits of content delivery consistency, and verifying registration completion status. System logs will capture detailed timestamps and content access records. Compliance criteria require parents to open at least 80% of WeChat messages, complete no less than 90% of photo uploads and questionnaires, while hospitals must train a minimum of 95% of eligible families. Adherence is evaluated through automated mechanisms (e.g., WeChat read receipts, content access timestamps) combined with weekly manual audits to optimize measurement reliability.

Permitted or prohibited concomitant care during the trial {11d}

Participants may receive standard care as clinically indicated, including standardized health education on newborn care comparable to that provided to the control group. All concomitant care and interventions administered during the trial will be systematically documented through study questionnaires.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

Participants enrolled in the study will receive additional healthcare during the trial, including breastfeeding training and consultation regarding common neonatal symptoms, such as vomiting and jaundice. Upon completion of the trial, the investigator will maintain post-trial access and provide ancillary care to the participants, with instructions on nursing care for common skin problems, like diaper dermatitis and eczema.

Sample size {14}

This study involves four centers in the intervention group and four centers in the control group. The calculation of sample size is based on the incidence of neonatal diaper dermatitis at home, which serves as the primary outcome. According to previous data and research [1–3], the estimated incidence of diaper dermatitis (DD) among infants ranges from 25 to 50%. In China, the reported incidence of neonatal diaper dermatitis is 43.8% [4]. Given this evidence, we conservatively set the incidence of DD in the control group at 50% to account for potential increases in detection sensitivity under the structured follow-up conditions of a clinical trial. The target DD incidence in the intervention group was set at 35%, guided by the concept of a clinically meaningful difference. A 15% absolute risk reduction (from 50 to 35%) was established through multidisciplinary consensus and was considered both operationally feasible with the proposed intervention and clinically significant. Assuming an anticipated incidence of 35% in the intervention group and 50% in the control group, along with a desired statistical power of 0.90, a two-sided significance level of 0.05, as well as an intracluster correlation coefficient (ICC) of 0.005, each center will require 77 participants. Taking into account a projected attrition rate of 10%, we determined that each center should enroll 85 participants, resulting in a total sample size of 680 participants—340 in the control group and 340 in the intervention group.

Recruitment {15}

To ensure the achievement of our target sample size, we will implement a range of strategies to support and facilitate participant recruitment. For participants in the intervention group, our research team will provide parents with comprehensive guidance on accessing and utilizing the learning resources available on the platform. These resources are designed to promote healthy skin development in infants and have been shown to effectively reduce the occurrence of skin problems in home care settings. Additionally, regardless of group assignment, all families enrolled in the study will have access to guidance from our research team on appropriate management strategies for any skin-related concerns, including diaper dermatitis, that may arise during the 6-month study period.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}

The randomization sequence will be generated by an independent statistical team using SAS 9.4 software. A computer-generated random seed number was assigned to each center, with each center receiving a unique identifier. The randomization module of the software was then used to sort these identifiers, and centers were allocated to either the experimental group or the control group according to the sorted sequence, ensuring a balanced 1:1 allocation ratio.

Concealment mechanism {16b}

The randomization of clusters will be completed before the enrollment of any individual participants within those clusters. Once a hospital has been randomly assigned to either the intervention or control group, study investigators at that site will commence screening and enrollment of individual participants (newborns).

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

We developed a questionnaire to collect data across several key domains [8, 16–18]: general demographic information, home skin issues, feeding practices, and skin care routines. The demographic section collects details such as the baby’s gender, date of birth, and primary caregiver. The feeding practices section addresses the type of feeding (breastfeeding, formula feeding, and mixed feeding). In the section concerning home skin issues, we will collect information regarding the presence and description of any skin problems. Finally, the skin care routines section addresses methods and frequency related to diaper changes, bathing procedures, and facial care practices.

Parents will be requested to complete questionnaires at 1, 3, and 6 months postpartum. The questionnaire includes images of diaper dermatitis and eczema, to assist parents in accurately identifying and reporting their newborn’s skin problems (Fig. 3).Fig. 3Diaper dermatitis and eczema images

All questionnaires are delivered and collected electronically, with the following group-specific procedures:

For the control group: Questionnaires are distributed and photographic submissions are collected manually by the research team through a standard WeChat group chat.

For the intervention group: Questionnaires and photographic materials are managed automatically by a dedicated WeChat-based home skin care management system, which enables direct and blinded review by the study dermatologists.

This difference in questionnaire administration methods is an essential component of the distinct interventions received by each group.

Plans to promote participant retention and ensure complete follow-up {18b}

Before the study, the supervisory unit will conduct training sessions on the use of the WeChat platform and develop a comprehensive user guide. The intervention group will establish an implementation team consisting of at least 2–3 communicative nurses. All participants will receive training on how to use the WeChat platform and will acquire the necessary skills to guide parents in its usage. A detailed help manual will be prepared by the researcher and distributed to parents. Each unit within the intervention group will adapt and establish an implementation process suited to its specific administrative context. Additionally, the supervisory unit will set up a WeChat communication group to support ongoing discussion of challenges and uncertainties encountered during implementation and to provide guidance to units facing particularly complex issues.

For parents who fail to submit the required information on time, the study team will contact them via telephone, email, or other communication channels to identify the reasons and encourage timely completion. For parents who show low enthusiasm for participation, follow-up will be conducted to understand the underlying reasons and to encourage active engagement. Data from these participants will be included in the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis in order to minimize potential bias.

Data management {19}

The data will be stored in a secure online database. This database is equipped with multiple built-in validation features designed to ensure data quality at the point of entry. These features include range checks (e.g., for infant weight and gestational age), mandatory field validation to prevent missing data, logical consistency checks (e.g., ensuring that the date of birth precedes the date of discharge), and data type validation (e.g., numeric fields accept only numerical values). Only authorized personnel at each hospital will be permitted to upload data, using a unique username and password for secure access. The coordinating center (Children’s Hospital of Fudan University) will oversee implementation fidelity, data quality control, and the preparation of interim reports. No separate steering or endpoint adjudication committees were established for this trial. Access to the research data will be restricted to designated staff members involved in data cleaning and statistical analysis. Furthermore, no independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) was formed, as the intervention was deemed to pose minimal risk. Trial oversight will be conducted jointly by the principal investigator team and the coordinating center. To ensure the highest standards of data accuracy and reliability, a designated member of the research team conducts a comprehensive monthly review of newly entered data. This verification process includes a detailed examination of each individual case, ensuring consistency, completeness, and adherence to the study’s predefined protocols and parameters.

Confidentiality {27}

Strict confidentiality measures were implemented to protect the privacy of all participants in this study. All personal identifiers, including names, addresses, and other potentially identifiable information, were removed from the dataset before data analysis. In addition, all electronic data were securely stored on password-protected servers, with access restricted exclusively to authorized research personnel.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

We will analyze the outcomes of newborns in both groups using both Intention-to-Treat (ITT) and Per-Protocol (PP) analysis methods. Categorical data will be presented as frequencies and percentages (%) and compared between groups using the chi-square test. If the data fail to meet the assumptions of the chi-square test, Fisher’s exact test will be applied as an alternative. Normally distributed continuous variables will be described using means ± standard deviations (± SD), and intergroup differences will be analyzed using the independent samples t-test. For non-normally distributed continuous data, medians (M) and interquartile ranges (Q25, Q75) will be reported, and the Kruskal–Wallis H test will be employed to assess differences between groups.

The primary outcome of this study is the incidence of diaper dermatitis in 1-month-old infants at home. A generalized linear mixed model (GLM) with a normal distribution and logit link function will be used, with the trial group as the independent variable and the study center as a random clustering factor, to estimate the relative risk (RR), risk difference (RD), and their corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CI) between the two groups.

All statistical analyses will be conducted using SPSS version 23.0 and R software. All statistical tests are two-sided, with the significance level set at α = 0.05. A P-value less than 0.05 will be considered statistically significant.

Interim analyses {21b}

The method of interim analysis will not be utilized in this study.

Approaches for supplementary analyses (e.g., subgroup analyses) {20b}

The same GLM will be applied to adjust for covariates that significantly affect the primary outcome, including study site, sex, primary caregiver, and feeding method, in order to estimate adjusted RR and RD values along with their 95% CI. In subgroup analyses, stratification will be conducted based on sex, primary caregiver, and feeding method. If sufficient sample sizes are available within each subgroup, the GLM will be used to evaluate the primary outcome separately, calculating RR, RD, and their corresponding 95% CI for each subgroup.

Methods for analyzing protocol non-adherence and statistical methods for addressing missing data {20c}

The outcomes of randomly assigned newborn infants will be analyzed using both intention-to-treat (ITT) and per-protocol (PP) analysis approaches. For missing data on continuous variables, mean imputation will be used to preserve data completeness and ensure the validity of the analysis. In the case of missing outcome data, the "worst-case imputation" method will be applied to maintain analytical consistency and robustness.

Discussion

The significance of neonatal skin care

The skin of newborns differs significantly from that of older children and adults in terms of physiological and developmental characteristics [19]. The maturation of skin structure and function is a dynamic process, with the epidermis reaching full maturity at approximately 34 weeks of postmenstrual age [20]. Due to the ongoing anatomical and functional development of the skin, newborns and infants possess a delicate skin barrier that is more vulnerable to damage [21]. During the first 3 months of life, several changes occur: the stratum corneum becomes thinner, dermal papillae begin to develop, and epidermal thickness increases significantly between 4 and 7 days of age, a process that continues until around 1 month [22]. Although considerable progress in skin maturation is achieved by 3 months, appropriate skincare remains essential to protect against irritants and chemical exposure. Newborns are particularly susceptible to various substances they may encounter—such as spices and preservatives—which can have pronounced effects on their sensitive skin [23]. Therefore, special care must be taken during skincare routines for this population. The primary objective of home skin care for newborns is to maintain skin integrity while minimizing exposure to harmful substances that could lead to sensitivities or other dermatological complications associated with chemical exposure [24].

Evaluating the effectiveness of home skin care guidance using a cluster randomized controlled trial design

This study aims to assess the effectiveness of the “WeChat + Parent-Guided Home Skin Health Care” platform in delivering guidance to caregivers within the home setting. To minimize contamination and facilitate management, maternity wards from eight medical institutions will be randomly assigned to either the intervention or control group. This cluster randomized controlled trial design ensures standardized training across groups and reduces the risk of cross-group contamination, while also addressing logistical and organizational challenges. The study design reflects real-world nursing environments, providing practical insights that are applicable to clinical practice. The anticipated outcomes include improved parent-guided home skin care practices for infants and increased family satisfaction.

Data collection challenges

This study employs a designed questionnaire, developed based on relevant literature, to assess neonatal skin issues in the home setting. Following the mother’s discharge from the hospital, medical staff will invite parents to join a WeChat group, where questionnaires will be distributed at 1, 3, and 6 months postpartum to monitor the occurrence of any skin-related issues. To address potential non-compliance, researchers will conduct follow-up calls with non-responding participants to ensure complete data collection and an accurate assessment of skin issues occurring in the home setting.

Establishing the “WeChat + Parent-Guided Home Skin Health Care” platform

In the early stages of a newborn’s life, mothers play a crucial role in addressing their child’s physical, emotional, and health-related needs. To support this essential responsibility, the “WeChat + Parent-Guided Home Skin Health Care” system has been developed as an evidence-based intervention to deliver accurate, accessible, and personalized skin care guidance. This digital platform provides scientifically validated recommendations through a user-friendly interface, while ensuring secure registration to protect users’ personal information. By integrating digital tools into maternal support systems, the platform equips mothers with the knowledge and resources necessary to ensure effective skin care for their newborns.

In conclusion, this study explores the use of the WeChat platform to deliver targeted health education on neonatal home skin care, with the aim of improving parental knowledge, enhancing caregiving skills, and reducing the incidence of neonatal skin problems. The findings provide valuable insights that can inform the development and refinement of clinical guidelines within the “WeChat + Parent-Guided Home Skin Health Care” framework for neonatal home health care.

The study commenced in August 2024, and parent registration also began in the same month. The research is currently in its main implementation phase, and the entire project is scheduled to be completed by March 2025.

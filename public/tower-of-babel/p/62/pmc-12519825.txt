Study protocol for a multi-site randomized clinical trial of an intervention program to promote self-management among head and neck cancer survivors with lymphedema and fibrosis [PROMISE trial]
Authors: Jie Deng, Jessica Abene, Liming Huang, Barbara A. Murphy
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLymphedema and fibrosis (LEF) secondary to head and neck cancer (HNC) treatment is a chronic process and a lifelong commitment to self-care. Although prospective data is lacking, a program of self-management may reduce LEF progression and minimize associated functional impairments and symptom burden. Barriers to long-term LEF self-care include lack of standardized self-care protocols as well as lack of LEF-related knowledge, skills, self-efficacy and adherence among HNC survivors. The objective of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of a standardized lymphedema and fibrosis self-management program (LEF-SMP) in reducing LEF-associated symptom burden and functional deficits, thereby improving quality of life (QOL).MethodsWe will conduct a 3-year, multi-site, 3-arm parallel-group, randomized clinical trial (N = 204). The targeted population consists of adult HNC survivors. Eligible participants will be randomized to one of the following groups: Group 1, usual care; Group 2, in-person LEF-SMP; and Group 3, telehealth LEF-SMP. Recruitment, data collection, and study intervention will be conducted at two comprehensive medical centers including University of Pennsylvania Health System (Penn Medicine) and Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center (VICC). Primary study endpoint is LEF severity; and secondary study endpoints include LEF-related symptom burden, functional impairments, QOL, LEF-related knowledge, skills, self-efficacy, and self-care adherence. All above-mentioned primary and secondary endpoints will be measured at the baseline (pre-intervention), and 3-, 6-, and 9-month post-intervention.DiscussionThis study will test whether the standardized lymphedema and fibrosis self-management program (LEF-SMP) has the ability to decrease the burden of LEF and its impact on HNC survivors. We anticipate that the intervention will improve LEF self-management, reduce LEF-associated symptom burden and functional impairments, and enhance QOL as well as LEF-related knowledge and skills in HNC survivors.Trial registrationThis study was registered on November 01, 2023, at ClinicalTrials.gov, a service of the US National Institute of Health (NCT06125743). Introduction

Background and rationale {6a}

It is estimated that in 2025, 72,680 Americans will be diagnosed with head and neck cancer (HNC) [1]. The rise in HNC cases can be attributed to the increase in human papillomavirus (HPV) [2]. HPV-related cancers typically affect younger adults and are associated with improved survival rates [3, 4]. Most HNC patients present with locally advanced disease, requiring aggressive multi-modality treatment that is linked to long-term toxicities. This presents significant challenges for HNC survivors as they must manage the long-term and late effects of their disease and treatments [4, 5]. A common late effect of HNC treatment is head and neck lymphedema, which is frequently under-recognized and often progresses to fibrosis [6–8]. Proactive treatment and management of lymphedema and fibrosis (LEF) is therefore critical for patients, clinicians, and researchers.

LEF (soft tissue swelling and fibrotic change) affects at least 75% of the more than half a million HNC survivors post-treatment in the USA [6]. Patients can experience internal (e.g., tongue and epiglottis); external (e.g., face and neck); or combined LEF [6]. LEF is associated with significant symptom burden, functional impairments, and a reduced quality of life (QOL) [9]. Furthermore, the severity of LEF is associated with the severity of symptom burden. External lymphedema is manifested by tightness, discomfort, pressure, and pain [9, 10]. When internal structures are affected, it can lead to functional deficits such as difficulty chewing, swallowing, and speaking, as well as compromising the airway [9–11]. LEF can also affect soft tissues and muscles, resulting in decreased range of motion and loss of function [9]. Psychologically, LEF can result in social isolation and body image issues due to the associated functional and cosmetic changes [9, 10].

Head and neck lymphedema is a chronic, often progressive condition [7, 12]. Lymphedema associated with HNC can, however, be managed, but there is limited data on how to do so [13–16]. The standard lymphedema treatment involves a two-phase complete decongestive therapy (CDT), which includes phase I (initial/intensive treatment) and phase II (self-management) [12]. During the self-management phase (Phase II), the responsibility of managing this chronic condition largely falls on the patient [13, 14]. Patients must perform self-manual lymph drainage (self-MLD), maintain proper skin care, engage in exercises, and use the prescribed compression garments in order to manage chronic soft tissue changes. This usual care for HNC-related lymphedema is determined from evidence from lymphedema affecting other parts of the body (e.g., arm) [7].

At present, there is a lack of evidence-based self-management programs for LEF specifically adapted to HNC survivors. The HNC population has special considerations that must be taken into account: 1) Managing the late effects of HNC treatment is time-consuming and incorporating a self-care program into daily routines can be challenging [17–19]. 2) LEF programs must be easy to understand with step-by-step instructions to ensure patients, even those with limited health literacy, can replicate techniques at home. 3) Patients may lack motivation to participate in self-care. To fill the gap in LEF programs for HNC survivors, we have developed a Lymphedema and Fibrosis Self-Management Program (LEF-SMP) that is based on the Information-Motivation-Behavioral Skills (IMB) Model of health behavioral change.

The IMB Model-driven self-management program (LEF-SMP) will specifically address knowledge, skills, and motivation issues faced by HNC survivors when they conduct LEF self-management. LEF-SMP entails the following: 1) personalized educational sessions led by a lymphedema therapist specializing in LEF among HNC patients; 2) detailed and repetitive hands-on skill development sessions to ensure care instructions’ acquisition and retention; 3) incorporation of motivational interviewing to help patients overcome barriers and integrate LEF self-care into their daily routines; and (4) provision of resources and guided practice materials (manuals/videos) accessible via a secure website or DVD format for patients without internet access. HNC survivors possessing the necessary knowledge, skills, and motivation will be empowered to effectively manage LEF, thereby enhancing their self-efficacy and seamlessly integrating self-management activities into their daily routines.

We have completed a pilot randomized clinical trial to test the LEF-SMP in HNC patients with LEF. Results suggest that the LEF-SMP showed feasibility, acceptability, and potential effectiveness in reducing LEF severity [20]. In addition, there was a decrease in LEF-associated symptom burden and functional deficits. Given the promising results, further investigation of this intervention is indicated.

Objectives {7}

The objective of this study is to confirm the effectiveness of the LEF-SMP (administered either in person or via telehealth) in decreasing LEF severity and improving LEF-associated functional impairments and symptom burden as compared to standard of care. Specific Aims: Aim 1: To compare the effects of in-person LEF-SMP, telehealth LEF-SMP, and usual care on LEF severity. Aim 2: To compare the effects of in-person LEF-SMP, telehealth LEF-SMP, and usual care on LEF-related symptom burden, functional impairments, and QOL. Aim 3: To compare the effects of in-person LEF-SMP, telehealth LEF-SMP, and usual care on LEF-related knowledge, skills, self-efficacy, and self-care adherence.

Trial design {8}

This study is a 3-year, multi-site, 3-arm parallel-group, randomized clinical trial to compare the effectiveness of (1) in-person LEF-SMP, (2) telehealth LEF-SMP, and (3) usual care. The null hypothesis is that there is no difference in outcomes between LEF-SMP and usual care. We will reject the null hypothesis if there is a statistically significant difference in LEF outcomes between the groups at the 0.05 level of significance. Furthermore, if the outcome improvement is significantly greater in the intervention group, we will conclude that the LEF-SMP improves patient outcomes compared to usual care.

Eligibility criteria {10}

Inclusion criteria

Individuals need to meet the following inclusion criteria: (1) > 18 years of age; (2) post HNC primary treatment; (3) no evidence of cancer (NED); (4) completion of initial lymphedema therapy for head and neck lymphedema or unable to obtain lymphedema therapy due to a variety of barriers (e.g., insurance coverage); (5) history of lymphedema on the face and neck, with or without fibrosis; (6) ability to understand English in order to complete questionnaires; (7) ability to perform self-care activities for LEF management; (8) ability to provide informed consent; (9) having an electronic device (a computer, tablet, iPad, or smartphone) and internet access at home; and (10) a valid email address.

Exclusion criteria

Individuals with the following diseases or conditions will be excluded, including any other active cancer, metastatic or recurrent cancer, acute renal failure, cardiac or pulmonary edema, acute congestive heart failure, sensitive carotid sinus, acute infection, severe carotid blockage, uncontrolled hypertension, and venous thrombosis. Pregnant people and incarcerated patients will be excluded from the study.

Who will take informed consent? {26a}

Trained staff will obtain electronic informed consent at the start of the initial study visit.

Additional consent provisions for collection and use of participant data and biological specimens {26b}

There are no plans or possibility of using participant data collected in the trial for future studies.

Interventions

Explanation for the choice of comparators {6b}

This trial compares the effects of in-person LEF-SMP, telehealth LEF-SMP, and usual care on LEF severity. According to a pilot randomized clinical trial [20], the LEF-SMP (administered either in person or via telehealth) is expected to lead to better outcomes than usual care for HNC survivors.

Intervention description {11a}

Usual care group

Usual Care participants will proceed with self-care activities prescribed by their treating lymphedema therapists for the duration of the study. These participants will not receive any additional intervention.

In-Person LEF-SMP group

In-person LEF-SMP participants will be scheduled individually for the in-person LEF-SMP training once baseline measures have been collected. The training consists of three weekly in-person sessions. In these sessions, participants will participate in a 1-h LEF self-management training session provided by a study lymphedema therapist, plus a 30-min telehealth motivational interviewing (MI) session provided by a study motivational interviewing instructor. Once the LEF-SMP training is complete, subjects will be offered LEF self-management educational manuals and videos via a secure website. We will ask in-person LEF-SMP participants to review the educational materials (manuals and videos) on a monthly basis or more frequently as they would like. We will not send follow-up reminders regarding the self-management manuals or videos.

Telehealth LEF-SMP group

Telehealth LEF-SMP participants will receive the same content intervention as the in-person LEF-SMP group once baseline measures have been collected. The only difference will be that the three weekly 1-h LEF self-management training session will be held via telehealth rather than face-to-face. As noted above, the motivational interviewing sessions in this study will be held via telehealth for both the in-person and telehealth LEF-SMP groups.

Criteria for discontinuing or modifying allocated interventions {11b}

If participants withdraw consent, and experience cancer progression, they will be removed from the study. Additionally, if participation is deemed unsafe or inappropriate by the PI or site PI, the participant will be removed from the study. Data will be kept from all participants who are withdrawn.

Strategies to improve adherence to interventions {11c}

The program itself is designed to enhance adherence. Our multifaceted intervention (LEF-SMP program), which includes educational sessions, skill training, and motivational interviewing sessions, is designed to actively support participants in adhering to the program. The Self-Care Checklist, a valuable tool developed and used in our previous studies, will be used to monitor adherence to the intervention.

Relevant concomitant care permitted or prohibited during the trial {11d}

There are no relevant interventions or other types of care that are prohibited while participants are in the trial.

Provisions for post-trial care {30}

Provisions for post-trial care for participants who may experience harm as a result of trial participation will be aligned with the institutional policies of the study sites to ensure that all necessary support is provided.

Outcomes {12}

Primary outcome

The primary outcome will be changes in the total severity of lymphedema and fibrosis (LEF). This outcome measure will be assessed via the Head and Neck—External Lymphedema and Fibrosis Assessment Criteria (HN-LEF Assessment Criteria). The HN-LEF Assessment Criteria is a validated measure to assess patients’ head and neck external LEF status through a physical examination. The severity of LEF at each anatomical site is rated as follows: no LEF (= 0), mild (= 1), moderate (= 2), and severe (= 3). This tool has good content validity and interrater reliability (kappa = 0.75, P < 0.001) [23, 24]. The total severity score of LEF is calculated by summing the severity score of each anatomical site affected by head and neck LEF. Higher scores mean a worse outcome. The timepoint of data collection for this measure will be at baseline (pre-intervention), 3-, 6- and 9-month post-intervention.

Secondary outcomes

The secondary outcomes will be patient-reported symptom burden, functional status, quality of life, LEF-related knowledge, skills, self-efficacy, and self-care adherence. Secondary outcome measures will be collected at baseline (pre-intervention), 3-, 6- and 9-month post-intervention. Please find the description of the secondary outcome measures below.

Symptom burden

The changes in severity of symptom burden will be assessed via the Head and Neck Lymphedema and Fibrosis (HN-LEF) Symptom Inventory, a 33-item tool to assess HNC patient-reported symptoms related to LEF using a “yes” or “no” format. Symptoms the patient is experiencing are rated on the intensity of the respective symptoms on a 5-point scale. The seven symptom domains (i.e., soft tissues and neurologic toxicity, systemic symptoms and social functioning, jaw and oral dysfunction, swallowing and taste changes, body image and sexuality, communication, and mucosal irritation) demonstrate good internal consistency, with Cronbach’s alpha for the seven subscales being close to or greater than 0.70 [25, 26]. The severity score of symptom burden is dependent on the number of self-reported symptoms [overall score range, 0 (no)—5 (severe)], and higher scores mean a worse outcome.

Functional assessment

The changes in degrees of jaw range of motion will be assessed via the Jaw Range of Motion (JROM) Scale. JROM scale is a valid and reliable ruler used to measure mouth opening ability in millimeters (mm) [27]. Higher degrees of jaw range of motion mean a better outcome.

The changes in degrees of cervical range of motion will be assessed via the Cervical Range of Motion (CROM) Device. CROM device is a valid and reliable tool used to measure the amount of neck movement for the following neck movements: flexion, extension, right and left lateral flexion, and right and left rotation [28]. Higher degrees of cervical range of motion mean a better outcome.

Quality of life

The changes in quality-of-life score will be assessed via the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30), a valid and reliable 30-item questionnaire used to measure QOL specifically in the cancer population (e.g., HNC). The questionnaire comprises a global health-related QOL scale, five functional subscales (physical, role, emotional, cognitive, and social), three symptom scales (e.g., fatigue), and six single items (e.g., loss of appetite) [29]. This tool has an adequate internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha for 5 subscales, 0.70–0.93) [29, 30]. Scores for all scales and single items are linearly converted to range from 0 to 100, and higher scores signify a better quality of life.

Knowledge

The Changes in LEF-related knowledge score will be assessed via the HN-LEF Knowledge Test scale, a 35-item true/false questionnaire to assess patients’ knowledge related to head and neck LEF. During the development of this tool, an expert panel determined adequate face validity and content validity (data unpublished). The total score (score range, 0–35) represents participants’ knowledge level related to head and neck LEF, and higher scores mean a better outcome.

Self-care skills

The Changes in LEF-related skills will be assessed via the HN-LEF Skill Checklist, a 5-item checklist to evaluate the LEF self-care skills. Face validity and content validity were supported by the expert panel during the development of the checklist.

Self-efficacy

The changes in LEF-related self-efficacy will be assessed via the Perceived Medical Condition Self-Management Scale (PMCSMS), an 8-item (each item scores 1 to 5), validated tool that can be adapted to any specific medical condition (Cronbach’s alpha, 0.84) [31–33]. This tool has been adapted to HNC patients with LEF to assess self-efficacy in LEF self-care. Higher scores indicate greater self-efficacy.

Adherence

The LEF-related self-care adherence status will be assessed via the LEF Self-Care Checklist, a questionnaire asking subjects to complete a list of the self-care activities they conducted During the past 7 days to assess adherence to LEF self-management at home. Higher scores indicate better adherence to LEF self-care activities (i.e., full adherence, ≥ 5 days; partial adherence, 3–4 days; non-adherence, < 3 days) [34]. This form was developed and used in our previous studies.

Other measures

Sample characteristics

The demographic form: This questionnaire will gather information on participants, including age, sex, race, ethnicity, type of insurance, education, marital status, occupation status, residential area, annual household income, alcohol and tobacco history, and dietary habits.

The HNC disease and treatment form: The following medical information will be collected via a chart review: the date of a diagnosis of HNC, primary tumor site, tumor stage, treatment modalities, and any resulting complications.

Anxiety, depression, and health literacy

Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS): a 14-item, validated tool for measuring symptoms of anxiety and depression among non-psychiatric populations. When used in the cancer population, the scale demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha for HADS-anxiety from 0.68 to 0.93 and for HADS-depression from 0.67 to 0.90) [35–37].

Rapid estimate of adult literacy in medicine-short form (REALM-SF): a 7-item, validated tool field-tested to assess health literacy in a diverse research setting. This scale has excellent agreement with the 66-item REALM instrument in terms of grade-level assignments (r = 0.95, P < 0.001) [38].

Self-care experience

Patient interview guide: participants will be interviewed for 30 min on their self-care experience during the final study visit. The interviews will be conducted through telehealth, audio-recorded, and transcribed.

Sample size {14}

We obtained an estimate of slope difference and error variance from our preliminary data for the primary outcome: total severity score of LEF. For our primary outcome, the estimated slope difference between intervention (both in-person and telehealth interventions) and control was −0.14 (manuscript under review). The P-value for this estimate was 0.0005. The estimated error variance was 1.8, and we assumed a strong correlation = 0.80 for the repeated measures. To obtain 80% power with a type I error of 5% for a longitudinal study with 4 repeated measurements per subject, we need a sample of 165 subjects. Assuming a more conservative attrition rate of 23% compared to our previous studies, we need 39 additional subjects for a total enrollment of 204 subjects (each group with 68 patients). The sample size calculation was performed using module “Mixed-Models Tests for the Slope Difference in a 2-level Hierarchical Design with Fixed Slopes” in PASS 2022, v22.0.2 [39].

Recruitment {15}

The study team will (1) screen daily clinic rosters; (2) screen cases presented at the Head and Neck Cancer Tumor Boards; and (3) attend Head and Neck Research Meetings to identify potential participants and meet the recruitment goal. For recruitment, clinicians from each site will be educated on the study protocol and inclusion and exclusion criteria for participants. Study flyers will be placed in the clinics at the study sites. Clinicians will record the phone numbers of patients who wish to hear more about the study and ask about the most convenient time for them to receive a follow-up call. The research team will follow up via telephone with these patients. Patients may also contact the research team at the phone number or email address listed on the flyer. Interested patients will be scheduled for an initial study visit that will take place in private rooms at the corresponding study site. The study team plans to recruit ten participants per month from all the study sites.

Assignment of interventions: allocation

Sequence generation {16a}, concealment mechanism {16b}, and implementation {16c}

A centralized randomization process will be used for this multi-site study. The biostatistician (Co-Investigator) will use a computer-generated, permuted block program to manage the randomization process centrally, and will generate the random sequence and import it to the study database in REDCap that is accessible by all participating sites. The entire sequence of randomization is blinded to each participating site to ensure concealment.

After trained staff obtain electronic informed consent, conduct eligibility screening, and complete all baseline visits, they will randomize the participants to one of the study groups. The randomization process will be stratified by both study site and sex at birth. Participants will be randomized and assigned at a 1:1:1 allocation ratio to usual care, in-person LEF-SMP, and telehealth LEF-SMP via a concealed allocation process.

Assignment of interventions: blinding

Who will be blinded {17a}

The nature of the interventions prohibits the ability to blind the participants and/or staff to the group assignments of the participants. The biostatistician supports the data and safety monitoring board (DSMB) in overseeing the trial. Thus, the study biostatistician is not blinded in the trial.

Procedure for unblinding if needed {17b}

Unblinding is not applicable because participants and staff are not blinded.

Data collection and management

Plans for assessment and collection of outcomes {18a}

Baseline assessment visit: Eligible participants will complete patient-reported outcome (PRO) measures through REDCap. Participants will undergo a physical examination, which will include the following: (1) completion of the HN-LEF Assessment Criteria; and (2) assessment of range of motion using the Jaw Range of Motion Scale and Cervical Range of Motion Device. In addition, participants will be asked to demonstrate LEF self-management skills. Following the completion of the baseline measures, participants will be scheduled for the study interventions per the group assignment.

Follow-up study assessment visits: Follow-up visits will be conducted at 3-month and 6-month post-intervention. Assessment visits will include the PRO measures and a physical examination. The physical examination will be carried out by a trained staff member, using the same equipment and procedures as those used at baseline at each study site. In addition, participants will be asked to demonstrate LEF self-management skills.

Final study assessment visit (9-month post-intervention): Participants will complete the PRO measures and undergo the physical examination. Participants will be asked to demonstrate LEF self-management skills. In addition, participants will be asked to participate in a telehealth interview to capture patient experiences with self-care. The interview will be audio recorded and conducted one-on-one by a trained staff member.

Plans to promote participant retention and complete follow-up {18b}

We will implement methods for retention that were successful in our previous studies [20, 40]. To minimize participant burden and support retention 1) study assessments will be scheduled at a time that is convenient for the participant. 2) When applicable, study visits will take place on days when participants are scheduled for their routine oncology follow-ups to minimize trips to clinics. 3) All participants will be provided with a financial token of appreciation once they complete each study assessment visit. 4) Study staff will call participants and email them reminders 2 weeks prior to scheduled study visits. 5) Staff will send thank-you cards to participants after their completion of the following study visits, including 3-, 6- and 9-month post-intervention.

Data management {19}

Data for this study will be collected and stored using REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture), a secure, customizable web application. The REDCap system will be used as a central resource for data processing and management. All participant demographic information, HNC disease and treatment information, patient-reported outcome (PRO) measures, and physical examination will be recorded and saved in a password-protected REDCap database that was specifically designed for this study. All data will be collected by trained research staff and entered into the REDCap database. As a means of ensuring data completeness, the REDCap platform automatically provides an alert of missing data when a data form is submitted. The Data Coordinating Center (DCC), led by the study biostatistician, will be responsible for overall data coordination and data management.

Confidentiality {27}

All study data will be stored in a secured network drive managed by University of Pennsylvania School of Nursing IT staff member. Participants will be assigned a study ID number. This information will be accessible only to the study team via the secured network drive.

Plans for collection, laboratory evaluation and storage of biological specimens for genetic or molecular analysis in this trial/future use {33}

There will be no biological samples collected over the trial.

Statistical methods

Statistical methods for primary and secondary outcomes {20a}

Descriptive and graphical statistical methods will be used to describe the sample data, verify distributional assumptions, and identify outliers. The groups are expected to be similar at baseline without any imbalance of covariates among the three groups due to the use of randomization. To evaluate the effectiveness of the randomization, the balance of baseline measures across the three groups will be compared using t-test for normally distributed continuous data and Mann–Whitney test for non-normal data. We will compare both the treatment groups to usual care individually. Similarly, we will compare the groups using chi-square or Fisher's exact test for categorical data. Any baseline variables found to be associated with treatment conditions, imbalances between the groups, and other potential confounders such as health literacy, demographic variables, and disease characteristics will be measured prior to treatment start. Balance will be ensured across all groups based on these potentially confounding factors since we are stratifying randomization by sex and study site. All data will be assessed for missing values. Our primary analysis will adhere to the Intent-to-Treat principle (ITT). According to the ITT principle, all randomized participants will be included in the statistical analysis and analyzed according to the group they are assigned. Data analysis will be performed on statistical software (SPSS, SAS) and all significance testing will be conducted at a 0.05 type I error level.

Aim 1: LEF severity

Mixed-effects regression models (MRM) with fixed effect of time will compare the effects of in-person LEF-SMP, telehealth LEF-SMP, and usual care on LEF severity at 9 months. We will jointly test interaction parameters of time by group with a 2-degree of freedom likelihood ratio test. If significant differences are found, we will compare in-person and telehealth groups individually to the usual care group. We will also explore quadratic trends over time and include random trends at the individual level. Model comparisons will use likelihood ratio tests for nested models and Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) for non-nested models.

Aim 2: LEF-related symptom burden, functional impairments, and QOL

We will use mixed-effects generalized linear models with the appropriate link (identity for continuous outcome and logit for binary outcomes) function for the nature of the specific outcome variable to generate estimates of the effect of the LEF-SMP on the measures of symptom burden, function, and quality of life. Bias-corrected estimates of the interaction of group assignment with time of assessment will provide the key effects interest (i.e., differences between the groups in the patterns of changes in the outcome measures over time). We will examine the patterns of association between participant demographic and clinical characteristics, anxiety/depression, and health literacy scores, using graphical representation and effect sized indices (correlations or Cohen’s d as appropriate).

Aim 3: LEF-related knowledge, skills, self-efficacy, and self-care adherence

As with the previous aim, a linear mixed-effects model for continuous outcomes and a generalized linear model with appropriate link for categorial data will be used to compare the effects of in-person LEF-SMP, telehealth LEF-SMP, and usual care on LEF-related knowledge, skills, self-efficacy, and self-care adherence. We will test the significance of the group by time interaction to evaluate differential trend over time between the 3 groups. A likelihood ratio test will be used for testing model fit for nested models and AIC for non-nested models.

Interim analyses {21b}

We will not conduct an interim analysis.

Methods for additional analyses (e.g. subgroup analyses) {20b}

We may perform a per-protocol analysis or as-treated analysis to assess the robustness of results obtained from the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis.

Methods in analysis to handle protocol non-adherence and any statistical methods to handle missing data {20c}

In the proposed research, we will use established quality assurance methods successfully employed in our previous studies. Thus, we anticipate the amount of missing data to be small. In the event of missing data, the mixed model method will allow us to use all data available from each participant. The mixed effects models utilize a maximum likelihood estimation approach that assumes any missing outcome data is missing at random. With this approach, we use all data that has been collected without explicit imputation of missing data. In addition, we will test whether the missing data are related to key participant characteristics such as age and gender. If missing data patterns are detected, several sensitivity analyses (e.g., multiple imputations, completer’s analysis, baseline carried forward, worst/best case scenario) will be performed to test the assumptions of the models and to assess the impact of the missing data.

Plans to give access to the full protocol, participant level-data and statistical code {31c}

Our data sharing adheres to the funding agency’s policies, University of Pennsylvania IRB rules, Vanderbilt IRB rules, as well as local, state, and federal laws and regulations, including the Privacy Rule.

Oversight and monitoring

Composition of the coordinating center and trial steering committee {5d}

The Data Coordinating Center (DCC), based at the University of Pennsylvania School of Nursing, led by the Study Biostatistician, will be responsible for overall data coordination, data management, quality control, report generation, and statistical analyses.

The trial steering committee is composed of the PI, site PI, Co-Is, and research project managers. The committee is collectively responsible for the execution of the study. Key decisions will be reached by consensus. Conference calls will be held monthly to review recruitment, retention, data collection, and other ongoing project-related issues.

Composition of the data monitoring committee, its role and reporting structure {21a}

The entities responsible for monitoring include the PI, Site PI, and a Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB). The DSMB members must not be co-investigators or consultants, have not been directly involved in protocol development, have not had close, current, or recent affiliations with the study, nor supervise persons who are directly involved in the study. We will include representatives with expertise in ethics, biostatistics, clinical trials methodology, and head and neck oncology. The DSMB will oversee and monitor the activities related to implementing the study to ensure the safety of participants, adherence to the study protocol, overall performance of the trial components, integrity of data, and security of data management. The DSMB will meet biannually. For each DSMB meeting, progress reports will be prepared and provided to DSMB members by the PI and study biostatistician.

Adverse event reporting and harms {22}

Although this is an interventional study, there are no investigational devices or agents included in this study. The potential risks associated with the interventions applied to study participants are believed to be minimal. The intervention, LEF-SMP, was shown to be safe and feasible among HNC survivors in a preliminary study [22]. There were no reported risks for this intervention. Furthermore, LEF-SMP was developed based on the IMB model, which has been shown to be safe and effective in managing other chronic conditions [41, 42].

We do not anticipate any harm from participating in this study. Although the likelihood of risks is extremely low, we will inform participants of the importance of reporting any physical symptoms or adverse functional impact from practicing LEF-SMP. During each contact with the subject, the trained staff will seek and assess information on adverse events and other unanticipated problems. The Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0 will be utilized by the trained staff to capture adverse events during the trial [43].

If study staff identify any issues related to LEF (e.g., infection, massive swelling) that require treatment, the study team will notify the principal investigator (PI) and/or site PI immediately. The PI will manage adverse events as clinically indicated. Participants will be referred to a local emergency department if the issue is emergent. Any contributing factors will be reviewed, and strategies to prevent further complications will be developed and implemented. The PI will report any adverse events and other unanticipated problems to the University of Pennsylvania IRB, Vanderbilt University IRB and/or USAMRDC OHRO. Adverse events will be reported in study publications.

Frequency and plans for auditing trial conduct {23}

The project manager monitors the timely entry of all data into the databases and ensures that all protocol specifics are met. The study biostatistician examines the distribution of the data for the purposes of data integrity. The PI oversees all databases to ensure data quality and completeness. If a significant issue related to missing data is identified, the PI will lead the team to review contributing factors and develop a corrective action plan to minimize missing data.

Plans for communicating important protocol amendments to relevant parties (e.g. trial participants, ethical committees) {25}

Should there be an IRB-approved protocol amendment, the PI will notify the entire research team at both sites. The research team at both sites will meet to review the changes, and the PI and the site PI will provide the training for the team (if needed) and address any questions about the protocol amendments.

Dissemination plans {31a}

We intend to publish the results of this trial in peer-reviewed journals and present the results at both national and international conferences. Key conferences and publications include those sponsored by American Society of Clinical Oncology, American Society for Radiation Oncology, Multinational Association of Supportive Care in Cancer and American Academy of Physical Medicine and Rehabilitation.

Discussion

In the USA, there are over half a million HNC survivors, of which 75% are expected to experience LEF after their cancer/cancer therapy. LEF can impact critical functions (e.g., swallowing and breathing) and have cosmetic and psychosocial consequences that negatively impact HNC survivors’ quality of life. Since LEF is often a chronic and progressive condition, patients must conduct lifelong self-management of LEF to reduce its severity and associated functional impairments and symptom burden. Currently, there is a lack of evidence-based LEF self-management programs available for the HNC population. This study will evaluate whether a standardized lymphedema and fibrosis self-management program (LEF-SMP) could decrease the burden of HNC/treatment on cancer survivors. It is anticipated that subjects receiving the LEF-SMP (either in person or telehealth) will report improved outcomes compared to usual care. Results from the in-person and telehealth LEF-SMP are anticipated to be similar. By improving self-care, the proposed intervention may improve LEF self-management, reduce LEF-associated symptom burden and functional impairments, and enhance quality of life in HNC survivors. The data will be published after the study is complete.

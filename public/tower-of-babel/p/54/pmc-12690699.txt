AIIT-06 Machine Learning-Driven Radiomics for Non-Invasive Primary Tumor Localization in Brain Metastases: A Proof-of-Concept Study
Authors: Kwadwo Darko, Bernice Limann, Michael Farid, Umaru Barrie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveRadiomic analysis has demonstrated growing potential in identifying, classifying and differentiating brain lesions. This study evaluated the predictive accuracy, sensitivity and specificity of neuroradiologic features in determining the primary tumor origin of brain metastases.MethodsThe OpenBTAI dataset, comprising high-resolution post-contrast T1-weighted MRIs with segmented brain metastasis from 8 primary tumor histologies, was utilised. Radiomic features were extracted into a tabular format using the segmentation masks and MRI data. The top 200 radiomic features were identified via ANOVA F-values and machine learning-driven models trained and tested in an 80:20 split. Three single identifier models were built to recognize metastases from breast, lung, or all other primaries and a final ensemble model developed to distinguish all primary subtypes. Each model was evaluated by accuracy and AUC from ROC curves.ResultsThe dataset included 75 patients with 487 annotated post-contrast T1-weighted MRIs. The mean age was 57±11 years, with 62.7% females, and an average of 2.1±1.5 brain metastases per patient. Primary tumors originated from the lung (57.3%), breast (29.3%), melanoma (8.0%), kidney (1.3%), ovary (2.6%), and uterus (1.3%). Treatments included whole-brain radiotherapy (n = 55, mean dose = 22.5±17 Gy over 11±3.9 fractions) and stereotactic radiosurgery (n = 70, mean dose of 4±4.7 Gy over 11±3.9 fractions). Only 8.0% underwent gross total resection, while 88.0% had no surgical intervention. The classifier for other cancers (melanoma, kidney, ovarian, and uterine) exhibited the highest performance with an accuracy of 93%, sensitivity of 92%, and specificity of 94%. The breast cancer classifier followed closely (accuracy- 80%, sensitivity-78%, and specificity- 82%) and the lung cancer classifier (accuracy-71%, sensitivity-72%, and specificity-70%). The final ensemble classifier attained an overall accuracy of 74%, sensitivity of 72% and specificity of 76%.ConclusionsRadiomic classifiers hold promising potential for non-invasive prediction of primary tumor localization in brain metastases.

Detection of Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) in resource limited area: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Amani Kapinga, Ewaldo Komba, Tuzo Lyuu, Mtebe Majigo, Allen Rweyendera, Hyasinta Jaka
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundH. pylori infection has been recognized as one of the most common infections in humans affecting almost half of the world population. Detection of Helicobacter pylori infection in resource limited areas mainly based on stool antigen test and serological test. Detection of H. pylori by using culture method has been a challenge especially in resource limited area because of the requirement need in growing and isolating this organism in laboratory. There is no study which has been done in Tanzania on detecting these bacteria by using culture method.MethodologyA cross-sectional study was conducted at Muhimbili National Hospital from September 2020 to February 2021. We consecutively enrolled patients undergoing upper endoscopy at Muhimbili National Hospital after meeting inclusion criteria. Detection of Helicobacter pylori infection was done using rapid urease test and those turned positive were taken for culture and sensitivity. Sensivity testing with the use of selected antibiotics was done using the disc diffusion method. The isolates with characteristics of gram-negative straight rods, oxidase-positive, urease-positive, and catalase-positive were identified as Helicobacter pylori.ResultsA total of 290 participants were involved in this study, 123 (42.4%) tested positive for H. pylori infection using rapid urease test. We were able to isolate H. pylori from 29 specimens by culture method from 123 (23.6%) specimens which were positive by rapid urease test. Helicobacter pylori resistance to clarithromycin, amoxicillin, metronidazole, levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin and tetracycline was 65.5%, 86.2%, 100%, 0%, 3.5%, and 31% respectively.ConclusionHelicobacter pylori infection is very common in resource limited areas. Detection of H. pylori using culture methods is very important, as it allows performance of antimicrobial susceptibility testing. Resistance to common antibiotics used in treating and eradicating H. pylori infection is very high. Extensive local studies using the recommended methods for antimicrobial susceptibly testing is advocated.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-12400-5.Keywords: H. pylori, Rapid urease test, Biopsy, Antimicrobial susceptibility pattern, Culture, Tanzania

Introduction

Helicobacter pylori is a gram-negative bacterium that colonizes gastric epithelium [1]. It is associated with several conditions such as gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, Mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue lymphoma, and gastric cancer [2]. H. pylori infection is most common in humans, affecting almost half of the world population, people from resource limited areas are more affected than developed countries [3]. The distribution of H. pylori infection varies geographically on the local and global scale [4]. More than 70% prevalence has been reported in Africa, and the African region is considered the highest affected worldwide [5]. In Tanzania, the reported prevalence of H. pylori is 65% [6]. Several factors contribute to the varied distribution of H. pyroli infection, such as social-economic status, age, sex, genetic predisposition, sanitation system, ethnicity, and education level [7]. However, overcrowding, lack of proper sanitation, poor basic hygiene, and poor diet have been significantly associated with H. pylori infection [3].

Diagnosis of H. pylori infection in resource limited areas is mainly by using stool antigen test is mainly using stool antigen test and serological test. Antibiotic resistance to H. pylori infection increases worldwide, posing a significant challenge in eradicating this infection [8]. In Tanzania a study done on Helicobacter pylori mutations conferring resistance to fluoroquinolones and clarithromycin among dyspeptic patients showed the prevalence of clarithromycin resistance to be 28.7% [9].

There is no study in Tanzania on detection of H. pylori infection using culture method, hindering the opportunity of doing antimicrobial susceptibility pattern to the antibiotics used in treating the infection.

Materials and methods

Study design, setting, population and ethical clearance

The study was a hospital-based cross-sectional study conducted at the endoscopy unit at Muhimbili National Hospital (MNH) from September 2020 to February 2021. MNH is a tertiary consultant and teaching hospital located in Dar es Salaam, Tanzania. It offers super specialized medical care and interventions. The study included dyspeptic patients aged 18 years and above undergoing oesophageal gastroduodenoscopy (OGD) who consented to participate in the study. Experts performed the endoscopy procedures. Dyspepsia was defined as complex or disturbed digestion accompanied by symptoms such as epigastric pain, nausea, vomiting, bloating, and heartburn [10].

Sample size and sampling technique

The Kish Leslie formula was used for sample size calculation. The prevalence of H. pylori infection of 65% among adult patients with dyspepsia in northern Tanzania was used in calculating sample size [6]. Consecutive enrollment was used as a sampling technique. The final sample size of 290 patients was reached.

Data collection

We used a structured questionnaire to collect participants’ information. The questionnaire included: sections for demographic data, information on the source and treatment of water, the number of people in the house, number of sleeping rooms and number of people per sleeping room, hand washing from toilet, alcohol, and cigarette use, comorbid conditions, PPI use within two weeks prior endoscopy procedure, family history of peptic ulcer disease (PUD), OGD findings. The English version of this questionnaire can be found in the supplementary material (1). OGD was performed by an experienced endoscopist using an Olympus exera machine. A biopsy was taken from gastric mucosa. H. pylori infection was diagnosed first using Hp kit a rapid urease test (CKD BiO, South Korea) and those which were positive were taken for culture and sensitivity testing.

Isolation and identification

The specimens for culture were stored in a sterile falcon tube containing a small amount of isotonic saline 0.9% as a transport media and transferred immediately from endoscopy unit to the laboratory in an ice bag container within one hour after the procedure [11]. The primary isolation media was prepared using Columbia base agar supplemented with sheep blood and since H. pylori is resistance to various antibiotics such as vancomycin, trimethoprim and sulphonamide, these antibiotics together with amphotericin B were added to the media to inhibit the growth of other organisms [1]. The optimal growth requirement for H. pylori isolation is a micro-aerobic and moist condition maintained at 5% to 7% oxygen, and 5% to 10% carbon dioxide at a temperature of 35 °C to 37 °C was attained [1, 12]. The incubation time was extended from 5 to 7 days and after that time H. pylori growth was observed as a tiny transparent colony [1]. The isolated H. pylori colonies were then subcultured on plates of the same Columbia base agar media for purification, identification and performing susceptibility tests. Identification of isolated H. pylori was confirmed by being gram-negative straight rods, which was oxidase-positive, urease-positive, and catalase-positive as it was indicated by colour change of the medium from orange to pink [1].

Antimicrobial susceptibility test

The antimicrobial susceptibility of H. pylori isolates was assessed using the disc diffusion method (13,14 Six antimicrobial agents tested included amoxicillin (30 µg), metronidazole (5 µg), tetracycline (30 µg), ciprofloxacin (5 µg) and clarithromycin (15 µg), levofloxacin (5 µg). The antimicrobial discs were aseptically placed on the dried surface of Columbia agar with 5% Sheep blood and incubated under the micro-aerophilic condition at 37 °C for 72 h. The breakpoint of antimicrobial susceptibility was interpreted based on the zone of inhibition referring to previous studies. The antibiotic resistance was considered for zone size < 17 mm for amoxicillin and tetracycline [13], ≤ 16 mm for metronidazole [14], ≤ 17 mm for ciprofloxacin [15], < 18 mm for clarithromycin [16] and < 12 mm for levofloxacin [17].

Data analysis

Analysis was done using a statistical package for social sciences (SPSS) version 26. Categorical variables were summarized as frequencies and proportions. Logistic regression was used to determine the associated factors of H. pylori infection. All independent variables were analyzed in a univariate model to determine the crude odds ratio (cOR) with a 95% confidence interval (CI). After adjusting for other variables, we performed a multivariable logistic regression model to determine the independent variables independently associated with H. pylori infection. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethical clearance

Ethical clearance was sought from the MUHAS research ethical committee (MUHAS-REC-06-2020-284). Informed consent was obtained from all participants before the data collection. Only patients who agreed to participate in the study were enrolled and confidentiality of their information was maintained.

Isolation and antimicrobial susceptibility pattern

Helicobacter pylori was isolated from 29 (23.6%) specimens by culture method out of 123 specimens which were positive for H. pylori infection by using rapid urease test. We performed antimicrobial susceptibility testing for all 29 isolates. The lowest resistance was found to levofloxacin (0%) and ciprofloxacin (3.5%). Factors associated with H. pylori infection

At univariate analysis, patients who used well water were two times more likely to have H.pylori infection than those who used pipe water in their dwelling (cOR 2.43, 95% CI 1.25–4.70, p = 0.00). In addition, patients who had a family history of PUD were 1.95 times more likely to have H. pylori infection than those who did not have a family history of PUD (cOR 1.95, 95% CI 1.22–3.13, p = 0.006). Patients sleeping three or more in a single room were three times more likely to be having H. pylori compared to those who were sleeping alone in a room (cOR 3.24, 95% CI 1.04–10.07, p = 0.042).

Discussion

Helicobacter pylori infection is very common in resource limited areas. In this study the proportion of patients with positive infection among dyspeptic patients by rapid ureas test was 42.4%. However, this finding was lower than the reported prevalence in a different part of Africa [6, 18–20]. This could be because nearly 50% of participants used proton pump inhibitors within two weeks before the endoscopic procedure which can lead to false-negative results when using rapid urease test for diagnosing H. pylori infection [21, 22].

Being able to isolate Helicobacter pylori from the gastric biopsy specimen is the most heartening as in this study we were able to isolated the organism in 29 (23%) specimens out of possible 123 specimens, this can be contributed by lack of proper transport media from the endoscopy unit to the laboratory such as tryptic so broth media, instead we used 0.9% sodium chloride which is not efficiency as tryptic soy broth.

This study found that using well water as a water source was independently associated with H. pylori infection. The findings correspond to another study that showed a significant link between peptic ulcer disease and H. pylori contamination in their private wells [23]. It has been observed that water is a source of transmission of H. pylori, supported by several epidemiologic studies that showed a higher prevalence of H. pylori infection in developing countries that lack sanitary distribution of water among the population [24–26]. In addition, the family history of peptic ulcer disease was independently associated with H. pylori infection in the current study. Similar findings were also observed in a study done by Ayse Palanduz et al. [23], which showed higher seropositivity among the children whose parents were infected with H. pylori infection and suffering from gastrointestinal symptoms.

This study tested the antimicrobial susceptibility pattern for six antibiotics, including the common antibiotics used in eradication therapy of H. pylori-associated gastrointestinal conditions [27, 28]. The antibiotics used include clarithromycin, amoxicillin, metronidazole, tetracycline, ciprofloxacin, and levofloxacin. Clarithromycin is the most crucial medication for treating H. pylori infection [29]. The resistance of H. pylori to clarithromycin was 65.5%, much higher than other studies [28]. High resistance found in this study could be due to the population studied having the habit of self-prescription of different antibiotics [30]. Resistance to amoxicillin was 86.2%, corresponding to other studies in Africa [15, 31]. Contrary to a study done in developed countries where the sensitivity to amoxicillin was 95% [28]. The difference could be attributed to antibiotic prescription practices between these communities [26].

The resistance to metronidazole was 100%, similar to the findings of several studies [15, 31, 32]. This high resistance of H. pylori to metronidazole may be caused by the frequent use of metronidazole in treating GIT conditions such as diarrhea [33]. Our study found the susceptibility of H. pylori to levofloxacin to be 100%. The finding is different from the findings observed in other studies [34, 35]. This could be because levofloxacin is restricted to use only in MDR-TB in Tanzania [36], and it is not available in pharmacies, so it is rarely used, contrary to other countries where it is used in the management of several infections [37]. The resistance to ciprofloxacin in this study was found to be as lower as 3.5%. This finding corresponds to other studies [38, 39]. The high ciprofloxacin sensitivity could be attributed to the infrequency prescription of ciprofloxacin to H. pylori-related conditions, particularly gastritis and peptic ulcer disease.

The method which was used to test for antimicrobial susceptibility pattern in this study is not the gold standard, we were supposed to use PCR method which is very sensitive and does not need to perform culture in order to get the isolates but because of unavailability of the PCR we used the disc diffusion method instead which has also been used in different studies and showed good correlation with the recommended methods [13, 40]. Due to this factor the antimicrobial susceptibility pattern observed in this study may be overestimated or underestimated. So, we recommend for additional studies to be done in our society by using the recommended methods such as PCR so as to determine the exact antimicrobial resistance pattern of Helicobacter pylori infection.

Conclusion

The findings from this study is encouraging, because of how common Helicobacter pylori infection resource limited areas, being able to detect it using culture methods is very important, due to the possibility of doing antimicrobial susceptibility pattern as we did in this study and the findings showed a very high resistance of the common antibiotics which we use in treating this infection, such as clarithromycin, amoxicillin, and metronidazole. We recommend to do extensive local studies on antimicrobial susceptibility pattern for H. pylori infection by using recommended methods such as E test so as to obtain relevant findings to guide our prescription pattern.

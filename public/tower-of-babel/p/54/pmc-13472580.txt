Effectiveness of a Structured Nursing Care Pathway Integrated with Standard Pharmacological Management on Biophysiological and Psychological Outcomes in Patients Undergoing Spinal Anesthesia: A Pilot Quasi-Experimental Study
Authors: R. Arul Malar, P. Padmavathi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Spinal anesthesia is a common technique for lower abdominal and limb surgeries, offering advantages such as rapid onset, and fewer systemic complications compared to general anesthesia. However, postoperative challenges including delayed wound healing, pain, impaired gastric motility, anxiety, and sleep disturbances remain prevalent. Standard pharmacological regimens (e.g., bupivacaine, paracetamol, tramadol, ondansetron, and pantoprazole) address these issues, but integrating structured nursing care pathways may optimize recovery.Aim:This pilot study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of a structured nursing care pathway combined with standard pharmacological management on biophysiological (wound healing, pain, gastric motility) and psychological (anxiety, sleep quality) outcomes in patients undergoing spinal anesthesia.Methods:A quasi-experimental pre-test–post-test control group study was conducted among 20 patients undergoing spinal anesthesia (control = 10, experimental = 10). Both groups received standard pharmacological therapy, while the experimental group additionally received a structured nursing care pathway. Outcomes including wound healing, pain, gastric motility, anxiety, and sleep quality were assessed using validated scales and analyzed using descriptive statistics, t-tests, and Chi-square tests (P < 0.05).Results:The experimental group showed significant improvements in all outcomes post-intervention (P < 0.05), with between-group post-test differences favoring the experimental group (t-values ranging from 8.25 to 10.12; P < 0.05). For instance, wound healing scores improved from 7.12 ± 0.88 to 17.1 ± 1.24 in the experimental group, compared to minimal changes in controls.Conclusion:Integrating structured nursing care pathways with pharmacological management enhances postoperative recovery, reducing complications, and improving patient well-being. Larger randomized trials are recommended to confirm these findings.

INTRODUCTION

Spinal anesthesia, involving intrathecal injection of local anesthetics like bupivacaine, is preferred for lower body surgeries due to its safety profile, rapid sensory blockade, and lower risk of respiratory depression compared to general anesthesia.[1] Despite these benefits, patients often face postoperative sequelae such as acute pain, delayed wound healing, postoperative nausea/vomiting (PONV)-induced gastric dysmotility, perioperative anxiety, and fragmented sleep, which can prolong hospital stays and increase healthcare costs.[2]

Standard pharmacological management mitigates these issues: bupivacaine for intraoperative blockade, paracetamol and tramadol for analgesia, ondansetron for PONV prophylaxis, and pantoprazole for gastric protection.[3] Evidence-based guidelines emphasize multimodal approaches, yet pharmacological interventions alone may not address holistic needs like psychological support or mobility.[4]

Nursing care pathways—structured, multidisciplinary protocols—integrate evidence-based practices such as patient education, standardized assessments, and early interventions to streamline care and enhance outcomes. Cochrane reviews indicate that clinical pathways reduce in-hospital complications, improve documentation, and shorten length of stay without increasing costs. In spinal anesthesia contexts, pathways focusing on enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) have shown promise in reducing pain and anxiety, while promoting gastrointestinal function.[3]

This pilot study evaluates the additive benefits of a tailored nursing care pathway on biophysiological and psychological outcomes, addressing a gap in integrated care models for spinal anesthesia patients.

METHODS

A quasi-experimental pre-test–post-test control group study was conducted at a tertiary care hospital after obtaining Institutional Ethics Committee approval and informed consent from participants. Adults aged 18–60 years undergoing elective lower abdominal or limb surgeries under spinal anesthesia (ASA grade I–II) were included. Patients undergoing emergency surgery, with contraindications to spinal anesthesia, chronic pain disorders, or cognitive impairment were excluded. Using convenience sampling, 20 patients were recruited and allocated to control (n = 10) and experimental (n = 10) groups.

Both groups received standard pharmacological management including intrathecal bupivacaine, postoperative paracetamol and tramadol for pain control, ondansetron for nausea and vomiting, and pantoprazole for gastric protection. The experimental group additionally received a 48-hour structured nursing care pathway consisting of preoperative education, pain monitoring, early mobilization, gastrointestinal assessment, anxiety reduction techniques, and sleep hygiene measures.

Outcomes were assessed at baseline and 48 hours postoperatively using validated tools: Bates-Jensen Wound Assessment Tool (wound healing), Visual Analog Scale (pain), Bristol Stool Scale (gastric motility), State-Trait Anxiety Inventory (anxiety), and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (sleep quality). Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, paired and unpaired t-tests, and Chi-square tests, with statistical significance set at P < 0.05.

RESULTS

A total of 20 participants were included, with 10 each in the control and experimental groups. Most were aged 20–40 years (50%), and the majority were male (70% in control and 60% in experimental groups). Previous surgery was reported by 40% of control and 30% of experimental participants, and most surgeries lasted less than 1 hour (50% and 60%, respectively), indicating comparable baseline characteristics between groups.

At baseline, most patients had poor wound healing and severe pain in both groups. After intervention, the experimental group showed greater improvement, with 70% achieving good wound healing and 60% reporting mild pain, while the control group mostly had fair wound healing (60%) and moderate pain (80%) [Table 1].

The experimental group showed significant improvement in all outcomes after the intervention. Mean scores improved for wound healing (7.12 ± 0.88 to 17.1 ± 1.24), pain (7.4 ± 1.30 to 4.13 ± 1.45), gastric motility (7.46 ± 1.77 to 11.6 ± 1.62), anxiety (35.7 ± 7.78 to 28.1 ± 5.54), and sleep quality (1.93 ± 1.22 to 9.13 ± 1.40). Paired t-test analysis indicated that these changes were statistically significant (P < 0.001) [Table 2].

Unpaired t-test comparison of post-test scores showed significant differences between the control and experimental groups for all outcomes, including wound healing, pain, gastric motility, anxiety, and sleep quality (P < 0.05), indicating better recovery outcomes in the experimental group [Table 3].

DISCUSSION

This pilot study underscores the efficacy of a structured nursing care pathway in augmenting pharmacological management for spinal anesthesia patients. Significant enhancements in wound healing align with ERAS protocols promoting early mobilization to boost perfusion and tissue repair.[5] Pain reduction exceeds routine care, consistent with multimodal guidelines integrating non-pharmacological elements Improved gastric motility reflects proactive monitoring, mitigating opioid-induced ileus.[6]

Psychologically, anxiety and sleep gains corroborate pathway benefits in reducing perioperative stress via education and hygiene practices. These findings echo Cochrane evidence on pathways reducing complications by 20–30%.[7] Limitations include small sample size, non-random allocation, and short follow-up; future studies should incorporate cost analyses and long-term outcomes.

CONCLUSION

Structured nursing care pathways, when integrated with standard pharmacology, significantly ameliorate biophysiological and psychological recovery post-spinal anesthesia. This model supports scalable, patient-centered care, warranting broader implementation and rigorous validation.

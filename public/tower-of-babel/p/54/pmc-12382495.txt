Mpox containment strategies in China, 2022–2023: from preparedness to legal management
Authors: Weijing Shang, Jun Yan, Hongbin Zhang, Wannian Liang, Jue Liu, Min Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWHO declared mpox a Public Health Emergency of International Concern on 23 July 2022. China experienced the first imported case in September 2022, the first local case in May 2023, indigenous clustered outbreaks in June to August 2023 and finally has achieved routine management since September 2023. We designed a conceptual framework through literature review and theoretical underpinnings according to the risk management theory and Chinese mpox control practice, with the strategy and measures evolution of the mpox control in China over time from the stages of risk preparedness, emergency response and management, to routine report and management. Overall, China proactively takes strategies and measures ranging from risk preparedness to emergency response of imported cases, and finally controls the cross-border importation and local outbreaks of mpox. Notably, risk management was performed in the whole process of mpox response through HIV/AIDS service system. The imported case in September 2022 did not cause local transmission or aggregated outbreaks. The indigenous cases and clustered outbreaks in June 2023 did not result in three-generation transmission. Since September 2023, less than 100 cases per month and zero aggregated outbreaks were reported nationwide. As of 31 December 2023, the cumulative incidence per million population in China (1.202) was much lower than that in some European and American countries, such as Spain (161.780), Germany (45.633) and the USA (93.340). China has maintained a low mpox incidence rate through early surveillance, mandatory case reporting and prompt treatment. These measures effectively curbed large-scale transmission among high-risk groups. Additionally, the country’s vast population size creates a larger denominator, which may further contribute to the lower observed prevalence. However, China still faces challenges, including low case linkage (<15%), under-reporting due to privacy concerns and unclear infection sources in 94 of 99 aggregated outbreaks. Thus, how to accurately trace infection sources and manage close contacts is a significant obstacle in China. Additionally, how to address stigmatisation and rapidly develop vaccines and medicines remains a problem to be solved in China. In summary, China demonstrates a practical example of mpox risk management, which may provide valuable experience for the other countries and regions with mpox epidemics.

Introduction

The first human case of mpox was reported in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) in 1970. Subsequently, mpox was confined to small-scale outbreaks in some countries in central and western Africa.1 In May 2022, Mpox as a re-emerging infectious disease rapidly increased within a short period in some European and American countries that had never reported mpox cases.24 Since then, case number has continued to increase worldwide. Many early cases occurred in people who had attended an international Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual, Transgender Pride event held on the Spanish island of Gran Canaria, which was linked to transmission chains in several European countries.2 The outbreak has primarily been associated with close intimate contact, including sexual activity, with most cases diagnosed among men who have sex with men (MSM). Between April and June 2022, 98% of mpox patients in a report of 528 cases from 16 countries were MSM, with high-risk sexual behaviour a potential risk factor.24 As of July 2022, within 3 months, more than 16 000 cases and 5 deaths were reported in 75 countries worldwide.5 The deteriorating epidemic situation triggers global concern. On 23 July 2022, WHO declared mpox constituted a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) and this PHEIC was declared over in May 2023 after a sustained decline in global cases.57

Since mid-September 2023, a new monkeypox virus (MPXV) clade Ib caused the upsurge of mpox in DRC, which has greater infectiousness and can be spread by close contact and respiratory droplets.3 8 9 The outbreak triggered wider human-to-human transmission, with an expansion of the infected population from MSM to children, young males and females.3 6 The new epidemics increased the risk of spread across countries in Africa and outside the African continent, thereby raising a new global concern.3 6 On 14 August 2024, WHO declared that mpox once again constituted a PHEIC.6 At the end of August 2024, 106 310 confirmed cases and 234 deaths were reported in 123 countries.3 A re-epidemic of mpox is threatening global public health. On 26 August 2024, WHO issued the Mpox Global Strategic Preparedness and Response Plan to deal with this global emergency.10

China reported the first imported case in September 2022 and the first local case in May 2023.11 The case number rapidly increased in the following 3 months.11 Given the rapid spread potential, China tried to prevent and control mpox but there were some difficulties at the early stages of practice, including wrong diagnosis, delayed diagnosis, poor knowledge of mpox, inaccurate infection source tracing and close contacts management.12 13 For example, some cases were initially misdiagnosed as mosquito bites, skin allergies and folliculitis. Patients with mild rashes or fever failed to notice or recognise the symptoms and did not seek medical treatment. Many demonstrated limited mpox awareness—recognising only the disease name and rash symptoms but lacking knowledge of other symptoms, transmission routes or treatments. Their primary information source was social media platforms such as TikTok. Additionally, MPXV-infected individuals avoided healthcare engagement due to stigma concerns, particularly regarding potential disclosure of same-sex behaviours. Epidemiological investigations revealed that fewer than 15% of cases had identifiable transmission links to confirmed infections.12 13

Subsequently, China adjusted its response strategy to conduct health education and risk communication among high-risk groups using the HIV/AIDS comprehensive response information management system (CRIMS) and cooperate with non-governmental organisations (NGOs). Eventually, China classified mpox as a notifiable infectious disease on 20 September 2023 based on its empirical evidence and recommendations from WHO.14 As of October 2024, cases of clade Ib have not been reported yet in China.

In this practice evaluation study, we aimed to elaborate the evolution of mpox prevention and control strategies in China before and after case importation. The study may provide a reference for the process of emerging infectious diseases from risk identification and assessment of preimportation to emergency response and regularised management of postimportation, especially in countries where vaccines and drugs are not available.

Method

Data collection and analysis

Data collection: We retrospectively collected the monthly reported mpox case counts nationwide using a uniform form. Key indicators of the form including disease name, report time (year/month), case number and data sources. Specifically, data from June to August 2023 were obtained from the Mpox—Domestic Outbreak Surveillance section on the official website of China Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC). Data from September 2023 to August 2024 were obtained from the Notifiable Outbreak Information section on the official website of National Disease Control and Prevention Administration of China.11 15 Monthly reported data cover 31 provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities but not Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan in China. The reported mpox cases were confirmed by laboratory and clinical diagnosis.

The study adopted a rigorous data collection protocol. First, key indicators were established through expert consensus. Second, regarding data extraction, two researchers independently extracted all data using source documents. Third, dual verification was performed through duplicate checking and discrepancy reconciliation to ensure data completeness and accuracy.

Then, we used search terms including “mpox”, “policy”, “guidelines”, “program” and “strategic measures” to search Chinese and English databases, and the official websites and WeChat public accounts of the Chinese government and health departments. A total of 294 records were identified through database searching. After duplicate removal and screening, 16 articles were selected and assessed for eligibility, with 9 studies included in the qualitative synthesis. Additionally, a total of 42 records were identified from health department official website. After screening and assessment, 18 records were selected and included in the qualitative synthesis. A unified form was used to collect the name of institutions, main content of documents as well as the time that policy and guideline were released. We used bar charts to show case number per month and embedded strategies and policies in the figure to show the changes in case number before and after the implementation of strategies and policies.

Conceptual framework

The conceptual framework was established by the following three steps. First, we conducted a literature review and theoretical underpinnings to synthesise the research progress and evolution of risk management theories for emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases. Next, we reviewed and analysed the concept of past infectious disease control in China. Finally, we established the framework by integrating the findings of the first two steps with the Chinese mpox control practice.

Literature sources: Chinese databases included Wan Fang Data and China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI). English databases included PubMed, EMBASE and Web of Science. The published books included Risk Assessment Theory, Methods and Applications, Health Emergency Management, Theory and Practice of Public Health Risk Assessment for Emergencies, and Epidemiology of Emerging and Sudden Infectious Diseases. Official websites included WHO, China CDC, National Health Commission (NHC) and National Disease and Prevention Administration.

Search terms included “emerging and re-emerging infectious disease”, “emerging infectious disease”, “infectious disease”, “communicable disease”, “risk management”, “importation risk management”, “theory”, “frame”, “framework” and “model”.

We systematically searched literature published between database establishment and 31 August 2024, in both Chinese and English languages. Inclusion criteria encompassed theoretical and framework studies on infectious diseases, whereas intervention trials and modelling studies were excluded. EndNote V.20 was employed for literature management.

Theoretical underpinning: Risk management theory refers to a systematic science for identifying, assessing, controlling and monitoring risks in order to minimise potential losses and optimise decision-making outcomes. Its core components include risk identification (detecting uncertainties that may impact objectives), risk assessment (analysing and evaluating the probability and potential impact of risks) and risk control (developing measures such as avoidance, prevention or mitigation of risks). Concurrently, it is essential to continuously track risks and dynamically adjust strategies.1618 This study operationalises risk management theory through a two-stage framework, preimportation and postimportation, and four core components: risk identification, assessment, control and routine management. The preimportation stage focused on risk identification and assessment to prepare for potential mpox importation into China. The postimportation stage mainly implemented risk control measures to reduce transmission risks and epidemic damage. Intervention strategies include containment, prevention and mitigation for imported, local and clustered cases. Then, we further institutionalised routine risk management by classifying mpox as a nationally notifiable infectious disease. Additionally, strategies are continuously optimised throughout risk management in response to changing risks.

In China, mpox is an emerging and re-emerging infectious disease, and its control process complies with two stages of preimportation and postimportation and four core components. Therefore, based on the above analysis of theory and mpox control practices of China, Chinese mpox control framework consisted of following four parts:

The first part is mpox risk preparedness, including the preparedness of policy and strategy, multichannel surveillance, risk communication and risk assessment.

The second part is emergency response and management of imported cases, including the import case detection and response, risk warnings and communication, knowledge and skills training, and development of drugs and testing reagents.

The third part is emergency response and management of local aggregated outbreaks, including management of local cases and outbreaks, risk communication, training for healthcare workers and strategy optimisation.

The fourth part is routine report and management, including case ascertainment process, extent of undetected cases and measures to avoid stigmatisation.

Throughout the entire process of mpox control, surveillance and early warning, prevention, diagnosis and treatment are consistently implemented (figure 1).

Mpox risk preparedness

Preparedness of policy and strategy

China provides a lot of assistance for other countries’ outbreaks response. In 2017, mpox was epidemic in some African countries. China established testing platforms to improve testing efficiency in Sierra Leone.19 20 This international collaborative experience also contributed to the domestic development of mpox-specific real-time PCR and serological testing technologies.19 20

China has developed a series of epidemic preparedness policies, response programmes and guidelines to prevent mpox importation due to a huge number of MSM in China (approximately 8.3 million) when mpox was epidemic in Europe and the Americas in 2022 (figure 2).32125 On 31 May 2022, China Customs issued the Announcement on preventing the importation of mpox epidemic into China, requiring performance of quarantine and proactive report of suspected symptoms from entrants.21 On 14 June 2022, NHC issued the Guidelines of Mpox Diagnostic and Treatment (2022 Edition), highlighting mpox preparedness and skill training on mpox diagnostic and treatment.22 The guidelines recommend that CDCs, hospitals and clinics enhance the capacity of mpox early discovery, rapid reporting and timely treatment.22

On 27 June 2022, China published Technical Guidelines for Mpox Prevention and Control,23 which normalises the process of mpox preparedness and response. The guidelines focus on four aspects: prevention, diagnosis, disease control, and treatment and rehabilitation. The main measures taken in the prevention include health education and risk communication, and active self-symptom monitoring and reporting by inbound personnel or high-risk groups. In the diagnosis, case definitions and differential diagnosis of suspected and confirmed cases are established. In disease control, timely and effective measures are required, including case detection and reporting, management of cases and close contacts, MPXV laboratory testing, hospital infection protection and disinfection of personnel, environment and goods. In terms of the treatment and rehabilitation, timely treatment is provided to MPXV-infected persons, and more attention is paid to susceptible persons and rehabilitation management. More details are presented in online supplemental box 1.

In addition, the National Institute for Viral Disease Control and Prevention (IVDC) developed and distributed the first batch of mpox testing reagents to provincial CDCs in August 2022.26 Meanwhile, a number of pharmaceutical enterprises in China began to develop and produce the test kits to fast detect mpox.26 Although the literature on effect evaluation of above strategic measures is scarce, the first imported case of mpox was rapidly detected, isolated and treated without triggering a locally aggregated epidemic in China, indicating that these strategic measures may be effective in the stage of prevention of imported cases.

Surveillance

All levels of CDCs, hospitals, clinics and other health institutions are recommended to monitor risk in key populations, institutions and regions.26 Key populations include international travellers and MSM; key institutions include sexually transmitted diseases (STD) consultation centers and the hospitals or clinics of dermatological or venereal disease; key regions include provincial capitals or municipalities.26 High-risk populations are also identified and tracked.26 Moreover, National Genomics Data Center established mpox portal monitoring website to monitor MPXV variants and genome sequence and share data to the public.27

Risk communication

At the early stage of mpox epidemics in Europe and the USA, China CDC focused on global epidemics and informed the public of latest outbreaks and case transmission. On 5 September 2022–10 September 2022, to respond to potential outbreaks, China CDC collaborated with NGOs of MSM to perform risk communication. Specifically, an anonymous online survey was conducted among 5780 MSM participants across 30 provinces, assessing mpox knowledge, vaccine willingness and prevention attitudes. Results revealed a low mpox awareness rate (31.8%), underscoring the need for enhanced mpox knowledge education in high-risk groups.28 Additionally, the China Union of Life Science Societies and Wiley Press co-hosted online academic salons to share global mpox updates on epidemiology, virology, vaccines and therapeutics, with live broadcasts attracting over 2000 international participants.29 Moreover, China and the US CDCs communicated online on vaccine development and distribution, virus transmission and antibody detection, epidemiology of cases and community education. On 14 September 2024, China issued Popular Science on Mpox to introduce the epidemic status and preparedness strategies or countermeasures of new Clade Ib to the public.30

Risk assessment

To reduce mpox importation risk, China Customs have conducted multiple risk assessments and dynamically adjusted quarantine strategies based on the assessment results since early May 2025.31 Until mid-September 2022, the first imported case was detected through entry quarantine, with China CDC finally reviewing and confirming the diagnosis.32

China CDC assesses risk of importation and makes early-warning using monthly surveillance information on outbreaks and epidemics of mpox worldwide.33 The September 2022 risk assessment indicated a potential mpox importation risk from inbound travellers and recommended technical preparations to monitor rash symptoms in inbound quarantine personnel and high-risk groups, along with proactive health education.33 Furthermore, some researchers made recommendations on importation prevention, quarantine and public health education after analysing cross-border transmission risk of people who have mpox based on real-time quantitative or qualitative models.34 35 Huang et al conducted a modelling study on the cross-border transmission risk of mpox in 2022 and found that the USA had a high importation risk to China, while Germany had a medium risk.34 At the end of July 2022, Du et al conducted a semiquantitative mpox importation risk assessment and showed that the USA, France, Nigeria, Germany and 12 other countries have the highest importation risk into China.35

Emergency response and management of imported case

Detection and response

On 14 September 2022, Chongqing reported the first imported mpox.32 The case travelled from overseas and underwent isolation in a COVID-19 quarantine spot in Chongqing. More specifically, the patient manifested a dry and itchy throat, fever, red rash and pustules abroad on 9 September 2022.32 He was identified as a suspected case by Chongqing CDC on 14 September.32 China CDC received clinical specimens on 16 September and completed the virus nucleic acid testing within 4 hours and whole-genome sequencing within 12 hours.32 Finally, the suspected case was confirmed on 16 September 2022 and received isolation treatment,36 without causing any secondary cases or localised outbreak in mainland China. It took 2 days for China to determine a suspected case as the confirmed case.

Overall, the response time frame of the first imported mpox case complies with the requirements of the Technical Guidelines for Mpox Prevention and Control of China.23 Chongqing CDC and customs authorities conducted proactive mpox screening of inbound travellers, identifying and reporting suspected cases to China CDC within 24 hours through the national surveillance system. Additionally, specimens from mpox cases are first tested by provincial and local CDCs, with positive specimens promptly forwarded to China CDC for confirmation.

Risk warning, communication and training

China CDC conducted risk preparedness and management work, alerted the public to the risk of mpox transmission in the subsequent holiday (National Day), and conveyed the epidemic situation, response and precautions to the public in a timely manner. China CDC invited HIV-related departments, NGOs of MSM and experts to discuss mpox prevention and control in high-risk groups such as the MSM population.37 Surveys across 30 provinces in China showed that mpox knowledge rates in MSM increased from 31.8% (1840/5780) in 2022 to 50.1% (3872/7725) in 2023.28 38 Another pre–post intervention survey (N=1514) among MSM and people living with HIV in Nanjing receiving mpox education with testing/counselling services demonstrated a significant increase in knowledge awareness (48.7%–64.6%).39 In addition, China-Japan-Korea discussed the strategies of mpox surveillance, detection, prevention and control and shared their experience based on one-health strategy at the 16th Three Countries Infectious Disease Forum & Joint Symposium in December 2022.40 Moreover, on 7 April 2023, China CDC trained 230 personnel from public health and medical institutions on detection techniques of mpox.41

Medicine and testing reagents

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) practitioners investigated TCM treatments for mpox like Shengma-Gegen Decoction (SMGGD) and Xuan Bai Cheng Qi Decoction (XBCQD) through network pharmacology, bioinformatics and molecular modelling.42 43 Studies have shown that SMGGD may have positive therapeutic effects on mpox by reducing inflammatory damage and limiting virus replication. The Technical Guidelines for Mpox Prevention and Control, issued on 27 June 2022, recommend that SMGGD can be used when patients have a fever due to MPXV infection.42 Additional studies have shown that XBCQD could have a beneficial therapeutic effect on mpox by reducing the inflammatory damage and viral replication via multiple pathways.43 However, the clinical efficacy and adverse effects of two drugs remain unclear due to limited data. Meanwhile, US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medical Agency recommended tecovirimat for treating MPXV infection.44 A randomised clinical trial from the USA indicated that tecovirimat was well-tolerated, showing complete recovery in 59.6% of mpox cases (189/369) with a low adverse event rate (3.5%, 12/369).45

In terms of testing reagents, to effectively control and prevent mpox in China, specific real-time PCR and serological detection techniques have been successfully established by the China CDC and applied in the China-Sierra Leone Joint BSL-3 laboratory to detect mpox cases and conduct molecular traceability in 2017.20 While Sierra Leone has no available MPXV test sensitivity data, Chinese real-time PCR assays demonstrated 97.94% of accuracy (1949/1990), 97.49% of sensitivity (1552/1592) and 99.75% of specificity (397/398).46 IVDC had distributed the first batch of testing reagents to provincial CDCs in August 2022,26 which provided supplies for mpox diagnosis. As of May 2023, there were no reported local cases in mainland China.

Emergency response and management of local aggregated outbreaks

Management of local cases and outbreaks

The first local case was reported at the end of May 2023 in mainland China.47 The patient sought medical care due to aggravated skin pustules. Then his mpox nucleic acid was tested as positive by Beijing CDC and China CDC. Subsequently, Beijing CDC conducted an epidemiologic survey to collect demographics, symptoms, medical care and close contact history.48 Meanwhile, virus specimens from the case and contacts were tested at 7, 14 and 21 days. The contacts included sexual partners, family members and cohabitants, persons living with cases in a confined space for long periods, and the healthcare providers with unprotected measures.48

Several provinces reported many cases and have increased risk of secondary outbreaks and virus community transmission since June 2023. As of June 2023, there were 106 new-onset individuals who have mpox from communities.11 Taking Beijing as an example, a total of 37 cases were found in Beijing from May 31 to June 21, with 28 cases found in outpatient clinics, 6 in close investigations and 3 in outbreaks traceable to the epidemic. Of them, 33 cases were symptomatic and 4 asymptomatic. The age range was 24–51 years. Notably, 97.3% were MSM and 51.4% were HIV positive. Of the 28 cases identified in the outpatient clinic, the median time from symptoms to first visit was 4 days (interquartile range (IQR) 2.5–6.5 days; range 0–15 days), and the median time from symptoms to diagnosis was 6 days (IQR 5–8.5 days; range 2–15 days); the rate of confirmation of the diagnosis was 35.7% at the first visit, 46.4% at the second visit, and 17.9% at three or more visits. Epidemiological investigations showed that most of the transmission was sexual contact (86.49%). There was no obvious epidemiological association and no history of international travel in most cases.48

Of note, asymptomatic infections and delayed diagnoses may lead to insidious transmission, as early mpox cases were mainly detected through STD clinic active screenings. Additionally, populations such as family members and cohabitants tend to seek healthcare less frequently than MSM, which may lead to missed diagnosis and increase the risk of mpox local transmission.48

To address the above crisis, on 26 July 2023, the NHC of China issued the Mpox Prevention and Control Programme to optimise initial-version guidelines and integrate CRIMS with mpox control to provide comprehensive measures, including knowledge dissemination and education, safer sexual behaviour advocacy, nucleic acid testing mobilisation and peer education and online interventions.49 The programme focuses on education and intervention, surveillance and report, epidemic disposal, disinfection and protection, laboratory testing, infection control in hospitals and other work requirements.49 In the education and intervention, the key populations for health education are further clarified for MSM. Multisectoral cooperation is required and the HIV prevention, control and management system is used to carry out risk communication, case detection and case report. Guidelines for health education and intervention in key populations are also issued. In the surveillance and report, in addition to case or suspected case surveillance, the programme emphasises multisource monitoring, including hospital outpatient clinics, key populations and sewage in key places, etc. The time limit for reporting cases is required within 24 hours. In terms of disinfection and protection, technical guidelines for the disinfection of mpox and personal protection have been developed. The programme requires protection of patient privacy and performance of public health education and skills training for healthcare providers. Risk communication and training

China CDC built a mpox column on its website to provide updated information on progress of scientific research, technical guidelines or programmes, domestic and international epidemics. National Center for AIDS/STD Control and Prevention (NCAIDS/STD) organised the National Mpox Publicity Intervention and Prevention and Control Deployment Conference on 3 July 2023.50 NCAIDS/STD released Core Information of Mpox Prevention and Control in Key Populations and strengthened communication with risk populations.51 Simultaneously, NCAIDS/STD trained staff from provinces, cities and counties on the knowledge of mpox prevention and control at the meeting of AIDS information exchange on 6 July 2023–7 July 2023.52

Strategy optimisation

Since June 2023, China has experienced a concentrated mpox outbreak primarily among MSM. Approximately 90% of cases reported homosexual contact within 21 days before symptom onset.13 The trend aligned with the global outbreak pattern. Despite intensive contact tracing by local CDCs—including exposure history analysis and epidemiological linkage establishment—fewer than 15% of cases were epidemiologically linked to confirmed infections. Similar findings were observed in Beijing.13 Individuals reluctant to disclose sensitive information about their sexual partners may introduce self-reporting bias, potentially leading to underestimated case numbers.

Taking Beijing as an example, Beijing CDC found the delay of the case diagnosis, a high misdiagnosis rate (64.3%) in the first outpatient visit, and many missed cases when tracing and proactive testing of close contacts.48 The high misdiagnosis rate may be related to three factors. First, atypical presentation: clinicians observed that the genital or perianal rashes often preceded fever and appeared 1–3 days before fever onset, contrary to classical mpox progression. Asymptomatic infections were frequently missed during initial visits. Second, self-reports information bias: case data relied on self-reported histories that were vulnerable to recall inaccuracies. Most patients’ non-disclosure of sexual histories contributed to misdiagnosis. Third, detection bias: some mpox cases were identified incidentally during screening for sexually transmitted infections (eg, HIV/syphilis), potentially leading to missed or misdiagnosed cases.48 Therefore, Beijing CDC adjusted response strategies to recommend enhanced risk communication with high-risk groups through the AIDS/HIV management system, paying more attention to individuals with genital or perianal rashes, and implementation of multimethod testing of nasopharyngeal and anal swabs for asymptomatic infections.48

To curb MPXV spread, health authorities deployed comprehensive measures: multichannel surveillance, intensive contact tracing, CDC/healthcare worker training, targeted outreach to high-risk groups, multidisciplinary teams’ cooperation and vaccine development (figure 2).305356 These interventions likely drove China’s post-September case decline. Additionally, the China Healthcare Infection Surveillance Center, joined by other institutions, issued the Experts Consensus on Mpox Infection Prevention and Control in Hospital to guide outbreaks management and avoid cross-infection among patients.54 Subsequently, some experts in China recommended that mpox should be managed according to established ‘Law of the People’s Republic of China on Prevention and Treatment of Infectious Diseases’.55

Routine report and management

Case ascertainment process

NHC of China classified mpox as a notifiable infectious disease on 20 September 2023 based on its epidemiological characteristics and potential clinical and public health harm.57 This landmark event means that mpox should be reported according to the established law. When doctors find a suspected or confirmed case, they must directly report to the National Notifiable Infectious Disease Surveillance System (NNIDSS) of China CDC information system online within 24 hours and update the reported information according to laboratory results and epidemiological survey. Trained professionals at the local CDC or healthcare institutions collected case specimens to test viral nucleic acid. The specimen of the first positive case in every province shall be reviewed by IVDC and further confirmed by the NHC of China.23 49

NNIDSS of China has been in operation for many years and has excellent reporting compliance and timeliness. This system includes all healthcare institutions across the country, including general hospitals, specialised hospitals, township and village clinics, and private clinics. Currently, all case-based data on notifiable diseases are transmitted electronically to the China CDC through a centralised, internet-based reporting network on a real-time basis, enabling the government to detect and respond to outbreaks promptly.58 In 2013, the overall reporting rate of the surveillance system was 99.10%. Among county-level and higher medical facilities, township health stations and community-based primary care centers, their composition ratio of direct network reporting reached 99.5%. The average time from diagnosis to reporting was 4.8 hours, with a monthly average timely reporting rate of 99.75%. The average time from reporting to verification was 0.88 hours (53 min), with a monthly average timely verification rate of 99.89%. The duplicate reporting rate was 0.0012%.59

Extent of undetected cases

Same as most European and American countries, people who have mpox in China are mainly MSM. We estimated the potential case number of mpox in China using available data. Mpox prevalence (0.73%, 95% confidence interval (CI) : 0.54% to 0.92%) was derived from a nationally anonymous, online cross-sectional study involving 7538 MSM from 27 MSM organisations in 21 provinces and municipalities.60 Estimated total number of MSM (8 288 536) in mainland China was estimated from a spatiotemporal analysis in 2018.24 Additionally, given that MSM account for 90% of all cases, the final estimated number of mpox cases might be 67 229 (95% CI: 4 973 1 to 84 727) during the same period in the MSM population. The estimated number is remarkably different to the actual reported number (597 cases) at June to July 2023.11 This discrepancy may indicate methodological limitations or systemic surveillance gaps.

The case number gap may stem from modelling limitations and surveillance barriers. Regarding modelling limitations, the first source of potential bias is selection bias, as the 0.73% prevalence estimate was derived from an online survey that overrepresented younger MSM (18–35 years, 72.47%) and HIV-positive individuals (25.47%). These population subgroups were known to exhibit higher sexual activity and increased risk behaviours compared with the general population, potentially resulting in an overestimation of case numbers in this study.60 Moreover, temporal bias may exist as the 2018 MSM population estimate might not match 2023 demographic realities. Additional studies should evaluate the direction and magnitude of case estimation bias. Regarding systemic surveillance gaps, the first key bias was suboptimal healthcare-seeking behaviour and low self-isolation compliance among affected individuals. A national cross-sectional study among MSM showed suboptimal health-seeking behaviour for mpox-like symptoms (standardised prevalence ratio (SPR)=0.82, 95% CI: 0.74 to 0.89) and lower self-isolation compliance among diagnosed mpox cases (SPR=0.65, 95% CI: 0.48 to 0.87).61 A second critical surveillance gap emerged in diagnostic accuracy, as evidenced by Beijing CDC data showing approximately 64% of initial misdiagnosis rate during first clinical presentations. Additionally, fewer than 15% of cases were epidemiologically linked to confirmed infections nationally.48 A third critical surveillance challenge involves reporting hesitancy, where multiple factors—including stigma fears, mpox’s self-limiting nature and prolonged incubation period, prevalent self-treatment practices and frequent misdiagnoses—collectively contribute to substantial underreporting in national surveillance systems.12 38 61 62 The above reasons may explain the large gap between the estimated and the reported number.

Efforts to avoid stigmatisation

Surveillance data showed that over 90% of cases are MSM in China, thus a series of measures have been taken in this population to reduce stigmatisation.11 13 Mpox Prevention and Control Programme highlights the privacy protection for cases, close contacts and key populations when healthcare workers perform their duties.49 Moreover, NGOs of MSM are more trusted, privacy-protective and accepted by the MSM community in services provision, compared with local CDCs. Thus, NGOs of MSM are often invited by healthcare institutions to assist in case epidemiological surveys, population risk communication, publicity and education, vaccines willingness surveys and recommendations collection. Meanwhile, China manages cases based on the service model of the CRIMS, which also minimises stigmatisation or self-stigmatisation. While the effectiveness study of China’s destigmatisation efforts in improving healthcare access remains unavailable in peer-reviewed literature, preliminary data from a national cross-sectional study in August 2023 showed 92.21% (7123/7725) of Chinese MSM participants would seek healthcare for mpox-like symptoms, with 52.50% (4056/7725) reporting willingness to self-isolate on diagnosis.61 More importantly, mpox has been classified as the national notifiable disease, which means that the privacy of cases must be protected. Disclosure of case privacy is considered a violation of the law.

Key issues discussion

Measurable achievements

Multiple surveillance of cases and clustered outbreaks, timely tracking of close contacts and scientific response to outbreaks have been achieved by China. The imported case in September 2022 did not cause local transmission or aggregated outbreaks. Although a total of 1713 confirmed cases have been reported on 31 December 2023, there are no deaths occurring.13 In addition, from June to December 2023, 99 clusters were identified across 22 provinces or municipalities. The proportion of aggregated mpox cases showed a decreasing trend, from 26.4% in June to 2.8% in November and 4.3% in December. No third-generation transmission was observed.13 Since September 2023, mpox has been classified as a notifiable infectious disease by China, which has been a landmark for mpox management, with less than 100 cases per month nationwide.57

Meanwhile, most European and American countries also have well-developed monitoring and testing capabilities. In Europe, surveillance data on mpox cases were collected via the TESSy platform of the European CDC, and confirmed cases were defined as persons with laboratory confirmed MPXV infection through detection of unique sequences of viral DNA by real-time PCR and/or sequencing.63 In the USA, mpox has been recognised as a national notifiable disease with a standardised surveillance case definition since 2022. A confirmed case is defined as one demonstrating the presence of MPXV DNA by PCR testing or next-generation sequencing of a clinical specimen, or by isolation of MPXV in culture from a clinical specimen.64 Currently, mpox has been well controlled in the USA and the CDC continues to expand increases capacity in communities for early detection of mpox through existing surveillance systems, including wastewater testing.65

As of 31 December 2023, the total number of reported mpox cases (cumulative incidence) in China (1713 cases (1.202/million person)) was much lower than those in some European and American countries, such as Spain (7752 cases (161.780/million person)), Germany (3800 cases (45.633/million person)) and the USA (31 820 cases (93.340/million person)).66 China has maintained a lower mpox incidence rate, primarily due to its early surveillance, mandatory case reporting and prompt treatment that effectively contained large-scale transmission among high-risk groups. Additionally, the country’s vast population size creates a proportionally larger denominator, which may have further contributed to the lower observed prevalence.

In response to the increasing threat of mpox, China issued a technical guideline in June 2022 and an updated programme in July 2023. The monthly reported case number in China is decreasing.11 Several national studies showed that the awareness rate of mpox knowledge in MSM increased from 31.8% in 2022 to 50.1% in 2023; over 92% of MSM would seek healthcare if they had mpox-like symptoms; 52.50% intended to self-isolate if they were diagnosed with mpox in 30 provinces of China.38 61 During the same period, the 2023 mpox outbreak in the UK demonstrated sustained imported infections and community transmission, with 137 confirmed cases reported. MVA-BN vaccine coverage was 87% for 1 or 2 doses in the MSM population in the UK at the end of December 2023, with an overall estimated effectiveness of 82%, and no vaccinated mpox patient was hospitalised.67 Since August 2022, the number of mpox cases continued to decline in the USA. The USA has implemented a series of responses to control the disease, including wastewater surveillance, communication, vaccination and medication.68 As of May 2023, more than 30 000 cases were reported and >1 million JYNNEOS vaccine doses were administered, with the two-dose effectiveness rate of 85.9%, and nearly 7000 patients were treated with tecovirimat in the USA.69

Compared with other countries, China has conducted multichannel surveillance and focuses on risk communication, awareness and education for high-risk groups. In the future, continuing to enhance and optimise above measures, and accelerating the development and stockpiling of vaccines and drugs will be key to controlling the possible large-scale outbreaks.

Potential barriers and China’s approaches

China and some countries shared similar barriers when facing mpox at the early stages of the epidemic, including limited policies and strategies for mpox response, low awareness of mpox in healthcare workers and absence of training and diagnosis for mpox.12 70 However, China adopted many measures to overcome these barriers. NHC is responsible for mpox management, thus it issues many policies and guidelines so that the institutions at province, municipality and county can rapidly implement them.22 49 54 Meanwhile, multi-institutes such as research institutes, NGOs, local CDCs and hospitals can collaborate to break up the prevention and control task of mpox epidemic in parts and effectively complete the allocated task according to the requirement of the NHRC.49 In addition, China rapidly developed mpox nucleic acid detection reagents and distributed the first batch of reagents to provincial CDCs in August 2022.26 Finally, mpox was classified into the notifiable infectious disease in China and cases or suspected cases are required to report within 24 hours.57 China has successfully changed from an emergency state to normalisation for mpox response.

Since September 2023, China has reported no large-scale mpox outbreaks, with sporadic cases being detected and managed through a standardised process including timely diagnosis, reporting, treatment and recovery.15 57 At present, China is closely monitoring the spreading of the new clade Ib and is carrying out risk assessment and taking preparations to prevent the possible importation cases, meanwhile following up the evolution of strategies from WHO such as the Strategic Framework for Enhancing Prevention and Control of Mpox (2024–2027).71

At the same time, Chile, Peru and Brazil initiated mpox vaccination campaigns to contain the mpox epidemic.72 Strategies from Italy and Spain showed that sexual behaviour change among MSM community members, close contact tracing and vaccination of high-risk populations were key strategies to curb the community spread of mpox.73 74 In the USA, after the first mpox outbreak, the CDC initiated an emergency response including surveillance and laboratory testing and initiated strategies to vaccinate high-risk and MPXV-exposed individuals with JYNNEOS. As of 10 October 2022, the USA had administered a total of 931 155 doses of the JYNNEOS vaccine. Additionally, CDC disseminated information about the mpox outbreak to public health partners and healthcare providers.75 Compared with these countries, lessons from China support that multi-sectoral cooperation and legal management of mpox are also effective strategies to control mpox epidemics, even in the absence of vaccination.

Additionally, we compared China’s practice with the WHO’ Mpox Strategic Preparedness, Readiness and Response Plan. The plan recommended core strategies including emergency coordination, collaborative surveillance, community protection, safe and expandable care, and response measures and research.76 China’s mpox response aligned with the plan. Implemented core strategies included emergency coordination for imported and local outbreaks, multichannel surveillance of high-risk populations and locations, targeted risk communication and public education, clinical case management with infection control, and diagnostic and therapeutic research and global collaboration. In general, our comprehensive approach, consistent with the framework of WHO, effectively contained transmission through mpox preimportation and postimportation strategies and measures.

Vaccines development and vaccination plan

Some Western countries have authorised the use of mpox vaccine. For example, the US FDA approved subcutaneous administration of the JYNNEOS vaccine for prevention of mpox infections in September 2019 and issued an emergency use authorisation for intradermal administration as postexposure prophylaxis for mpox in high-risk populations in August 2022. Health Canada expanded the MVA-BN in November 2020 to include mpox and related varicella virus infection. The Japanese regulatory agency added mpox prophylaxis to the LC16m8 indication in July 2022. The WHO Advisory Expert Group on Immunisation Strategies recommended the use of MVA-BN or LC16m8 vaccine for at-risk populations in both outbreak settings and in non-outbreak settings in March 2024.77 Additionally, WHO recommends member states to assess their mpox outbreak situations and convene their national vaccine advisory groups to develop context-specific vaccination policies.

Current global directions for mpox vaccine development focus on DNA vaccines, mRNA vaccines and subunit vaccines.78 New types of vaccines, such as viral vector vaccines, nucleic acid vaccines and virus-like particles, are being tested to see if they can make people more resistant to getting mpox.78 For example, Zuiani et al developed the multivalent mRNA vaccine: BNT166 vaccine, evaluating two formulations—a quadrivalent vaccine (BNT166a) and a trivalent vaccine (BNT166c)—in a Phase I/II clinical trial (NCT05988203).79 Both candidates demonstrated complete protection against vaccinia, clade I and clade IIb mpox. Moreover, DNA vaccines represent a promising investigational strategy for mpox prevention. A preclinical study demonstrated that a DNA vaccine containing four VACV genes (L1R, A27L, A33R and B5R) protected rhesus macaques from severe disease after a lethal mpox virus challenge.80 Additionally, virus-like particle vaccines are under investigation for potential use.

To date, China has no approved mpox vaccine or specific drugs. China CDC recommends individuals with mpox symptoms to seek medical care actively and then self-isolate after diagnosis until convalescent.57 Currently, research departments of China or biopharmaceutical companies are developing two types of mpox vaccines: mRNA and live attenuated vaccine. In July 2023, the vaccine of the replication-deficient Tiantan strain was formally accepted as an application for clinical trial by the Drug Evaluation Center of the State Drug Administration of China.81 This is the first mpox vaccine that entered the phase of clinical research. In China, mpox vaccination is not recommended for the general population.81

Research remains limited on whether there were differences on mpox protection between populations vaccinated against smallpox or not. A study investigated the level of residual immunity to smallpox vaccine (Vaccinia virus Tiantan strain, VTT) in Chinese to assess its protection against mpox.82 The results showed that the antibody response against smallpox virus can be long-lived, with 28.7% of participants born before 1980 having neutralising antibodies, 71.4% having a memory B-cell response and 67.5% having memory T-cell response to VTT.82 A real-world study from the USA showed that the mpox incidence in persons who did not receive JYNNEOS vaccine was 7.4 times higher than in those who received only 1 dose and 9.6 times higher than in those who received two doses.83

Future challenges

Mpox outbreaks have been contained vigorously and orderly in China through timely strategies and measures. Despite this, some issues persist in China. For example, less than 15% of mpox cases were associated with other confirmed cases in 2023. Individuals remain reluctant to disclose information about themselves and their sexual partners, which leads to self-reporting bias. Infection sources were not clear in 94 out of 99 aggregated outbreaks. Thus, how to trace infection source accurately and manage close contacts remains a great challenge for China.13 Additionally, how to address stigmatisation and rapidly develop vaccines and medicines remains a problem in China.81 Moreover, significant knowledge-behaviour separation was also found in MSM population due to fear of stigmatisation or discrimination,84 especially in those who reside in rural areas and with low education levels.38 61 Furthermore, the literature on strategy effectiveness, especially peer-reviewed literature, remains scarce. More studies are needed to validate the effectiveness of strategies targeted on mpox control.

To address the above challenges, the following specific recommendations are proposed. Advanced contact tracing methods—such as blockchain-based anonymous alert systems or wastewater surveillance integrated with phylogenetic analysis—should be developed to enhance the accuracy and efficiency of source identification. Stigma reduction should be achieved through public education campaigns on mpox prevention and transmission, combined with targeted counselling programmes for affected individuals and high-risk groups to facilitate risk communication and care-seeking behaviour. Moreover, targeted health education initiatives should be developed—including picture-based comics and algorithmically optimised social media content (eg, TikTok/Kuaishou)—to effectively reach high-risk subgroups such as rural MSM populations and individuals with limited education. Furthermore, public-private and international partnerships should be established to conduct clinical trials to verify the safety and efficacy of mpox vaccines and therapeutics, and to accelerate their development, meanwhile to ensure equitable access for high-risk populations. Finally, technical support for evaluating current mpox control strategies should be strengthened, while peer-reviewed research must be promoted to optimise evidence-based policy-making.

Conclusions

China’s efforts to contain mpox have demonstrated its strong capacity to mitigate public health risks posed by emerging infectious diseases. The comprehensive national strategy integrates risk prevention, emergency response and routine report and management. The main experiences learnt from China mpox response include multisource surveillance and early detection, timely risk communication and public health education, and continuous risk assessment and training of healthcare workers. Moreover, leveraging existing HIV/AIDS services, infrastructure and resources has enhanced the efficiency of public health interventions. These strategies and measures provide insights that public health measures can be effective in curbing the mpox epidemic in countries and regions with insufficient access to vaccines and medicines.

Future priorities in China should include real-time domestic and international mpox monitoring, accelerated research and development of vaccines and antiviral drugs to enhance preparedness for potential epidemic, strengthened targeting of risk communication in high-risk groups, enhanced technical support for evaluating current mpox control strategies, and optimised evidence-based policy-making by promoting data-driven, peer-reviewed research.

Improving Gastrointestinal Health in the Elderly: Public Health Interventions
Authors: Saurabh RamBihariLal Shrivastava, Prateek Sudhakar Bobhate
Published: 2025

Dear Editor,

Gastrointestinal (GI) health plays a vital role in sustaining the overall well-being of the elderly population.[1] With aging, a significant decline in the digestive efficiency (viz. delayed gastric emptying and reduced motility), alterations in the gut microbiome, reduced production of digestive enzymes, thinning of mucosal lining, and reduced immune response in the GI tract have been reported.[1] Furthermore, a higher incidence of multiple gastrointestinal disorders such as constipation, gastroesophageal reflux disease (weakened esophageal sphincter), diverticular disease, nutritional deficiencies, peptic ulcers, and colorectal cancers has been widely recognized.[1]

However, regardless of these physiological changes and disorders, GI health among the elderly population is often overlooked, which calls for the need to bridge the gaps.[234] There is an immense need to implement multiple public health interventions, beginning with the strengthening of preventive screening programs aimed at facilitating the early detection of colorectal cancers, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and peptic ulcer diseases.[2] Elderly people should be educated about consuming a balanced diet rich in fiber and probiotics to prevent constipation and malnutrition.[3] Furthermore, there is a need to create age-specific educational materials (using images, infographics, and a language that local people can understand) highlighting the common symptoms of GI disorders and the significance of seeking timely care.[4] In continuation, mobile health applications can provide customized dietary and GI health recommendations.[4]

Considering the challenge of the nonavailability of specialists in remote and underserved settings, especially in low- and middle-income nations, there is a need to integrate GI health assessment into routine elderly health check-ups in primary care settings. In addition, the option of telemedicine can be explored to ensure the delivery of specialist care in such settings. Furthermore, efforts should be taken to encourage the elderly to modify their lifestyles, like practicing age-specific physical activities (to reduce constipation), or minimizing smoking/alcohol to improve overall GI health.[3] In addition, guidelines should be framed for the management of common GI conditions in elderly populations. Finally, research should be encouraged about different common GI problems among the elderly and use results to frame evidence-driven recommendations and treatment.[123]

In conclusion, elderly people are prone to acquire a wide range of gastrointestinal health problems, which can compromise their quality of life and well-being. The need of the hour is to adopt a multipronged approach and implement different public health initiatives that are accessible and tailored to the unique needs of the elderly population.

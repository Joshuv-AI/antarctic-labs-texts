Slow drift aware dynamic risk assessment in cyber physical systems using quantum neutrosophic fuzzy modelling
Authors: K. Kiruthika, A. Rajesh, Nishikant Kishor Dhapekar, Tarun Kumar Dubey
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The dynamic risk assessment identifies, evaluates, and mitigates the vulnerabilities that compromise the reliability of the cyber-physical system (CPS). The slow degradation in network behaviour is known as the gradual and long-term shifts in traffic characteristics (variations in latency, packet flow distribution, and protocol level patterns) that accumulate over time. These subtle variations cause faults, stealthy intrusion, and network aging in CPS. However, the prevailing works overlooked the slow degradation that persists in the network behaviour. Thus, Quantum State-based Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (QS-EWMA)-based slow drift analysis is proposed. Initially, the medical devices are registered in the cloud server, followed by data sensing. During data transfer, the intrusion is detected by the Network Intrusion Detection System (NIDS). In NIDS, the data collection, pre-processing, and feature extraction are performed. Further, the QS-EWMA-based slow drift analysis is performed, followed by Sparse Random Walk Softplus S-shaped Rectified Gated Recurrent Unit (SRWSSR-GRU)-based intrusion detection. Next, the dependency-aware aggregation is performed using the Choquet k-Additive Function Integral Model (CkAFIM). Then, the uncertainty-based risk is assessed using Min–Max Normalization-based Neutrosophic Logic System (MMN-NLS). Here, the explainability is improved using SHapley Max–Abs Scaling Additive exPlanation (SMAS-HAP). Finally, the decision-making is performed using the Markov Linear Discrete-Time Propagation Decision Process (MLDTPDP). Hence, the proposed system effectively assessed the risk with an Indeterminacy Detection Rate (IDR) of 13.43%, showing superiority over prevailing works.

Introduction

The cyber-physical system (CPS) plays a pivotal role in integrating physical processes with computational and networking elements1. Generally, CPS architecture combines hardware components, such as sensors and actuators, with software components like data processing and communication protocols for data acquisition, processing, and communication2. This integration enhances efficiency, safety, and decision-making across diverse applications, including smart hospitals, autonomous vehicles, and smart grids3. While these applications promise immense societal benefits, the increasing interconnection between cyber and physical layers exposes CPS to cyberattacks. For example, in the medical domain, intrusions may disrupt critical healthcare services and even endanger human lives, thus ensuring that secure and reliable CPS operation is important for effective dynamic risk assessment4.

Risk assessment in CPS involves identifying, analyzing, and prioritizing threats to both the physical and cyber layers5. Typically, anomalies are identified during data transmission and evaluated based on likelihood, downtime, or classified results6,7. However, CPS environments are inherently dynamic and heterogeneous, with devices operating under fluctuating workloads and varying connectivity conditions8. These characteristics cause uncertainty and indeterminacy in CPS behaviour that traditional risk assessment frameworks often struggle to detect. Moreover, the system operators need to understand the factors leading to high-risk conditions to implement timely and effective interventions9.

For solving the uncertainty and indeterminacy in CPS data, a promising mathematical foundation that lies in the Neutrosophic Fuzzy approach (a combination of fuzzy set theory and neutrosophic set theory) is introduced. In fuzzy set theory, every single element has a degree of membership between 0 and 1, representing vagueness or partial truth. In contrast, the neutrosophic set theory symbolizes each element via three independent values, such as Truth (T), Indeterminacy (I), and Falsity (F)10. Here, the truth evaluates the positive certainty; the indeterminancy indicates the situation, where available data neither supports nor rejects a condition; the falsity evaluates the negative support. By unifying these perspectives, Neutrosophic Fuzzy models allow the simultaneous representation of vagueness, ambiguity, and indeterminacy. This capability is especially significant in CPS risk assessment, where data often reflects partial truths, conflicting evidence, or uncertain anomalies. Yet, the conventional models are inadequate and ignore the inherent indeterminacy of CPS environments. Although recent machine learning and deep learning approaches have enhanced the intrusion detection accuracy, significant limitations persist. Prevailing approaches have used models, namely Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN), to analyze intrusions11,12. Nevertheless, in this work, evaluating all risks in large-scale CPS is computationally intensive, delaying timely decision-making13.

Moreover, the prevailing works had some limitations for dynamic risk assessment in CPS, affecting the early threat mitigation. None of the existing works analyzed the gradual and slow degradation persistent in the network behaviour of CPS, causing delayed threat mitigation in the CPS14. Also, the prevailing model relied on probability or likelihood measures to identify risk scores and failed to capture the inherent indeterminacy of such network data, leading to unreliable risk score derivation15. The prevailing model aggregated the anomaly indicators independently or through simple averaging, without considering the dependencies between them. As a result, when multiple anomalies occurred simultaneously, the combined impact was underestimated, causing misleading risk scores16. Likewise, even though the conventional CPS risk assessment model for medical CPS generated risk scores, they lacked a systematic mechanism to translate the scores into mitigation actions17. Furthermore, most of the existing risk assessment works rarely explained how cyber anomalies led to physical consequences, leading to undermined trust in clinical settings. Collectively, these gaps illustrate that existing approaches are unable to fully address gradual drift, indeterminacy, anomaly dependencies, and explainability.

Motivated by these limitations, this research proposes an effective framework for dynamic risk assessment in CPS that lies in addressing these persistent limitations. The gradual and slow degradations in CPS network behaviour must be captured through Quantum State-based Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (QS-EWMA), enabling precise drift analysis. To handle the inherent indeterminacy of CPS data, an uncertainty-aware mechanism is required, and this is realized using the Min–Max Normalization-based Neutrosophic Logic System (MMN-NLS). To reflect interdependencies among anomalies, the Choquet k-Additive Function Integral Model (CkAFIM) is introduced, ensuring dependency-aware aggregation. Decision-making regarding mitigation requires a systematic yet computationally efficient framework, which is achieved through the Markov Linear Discrete-Time Propagation Decision Process (MLDTPDP). Finally, explainability, critical for trust in healthcare and critical infrastructure, is enhanced through SMAS-HAP, which highlights the contribution of individual features to risk scores.

Also, the proposed model introduces many novelties for dynamic risk assessment in cyberphysical systems via the integration of slow-drift analysis, dependency-aware aggregation, uncertainty-based dynamic risk assessment, decision making, and explainability. Specifically, the proposed QS-EWMA is employed to perform slow-drift analysis, thus effectively capturing gradual and slow degradations in CPS network behaviour. Also, for detecting the intrusions in the CPS network, the proposed SRWSSR-GRU is established, thus enhancing the accuracy. Likewise, the proposed CkAFIM is used to perform dependency-aware aggregation, thus reflecting the interdependencies among anomalies. For performing the uncertainty-based dynamic risk assessment, the proposed MMN-NLS is utilized, improving the accuracy. Similarly, the proposed MLDTPDP is introduced to make a decision, thus providing a computationally efficient framework. Likewise, the proposed GGRO is established to select the optimal features, thus improving the classification accuracy. For obtaining explanations about the risk score, the proposed SMAS-HAP is employed, enhancing the trust in healthcare and critical infrastructure.

The literature further reinforces the limitations of the existing works. A cyber-risk assessment for digital healthcare was proposed but ignored indeterminacy, leading to unreliable risk scores18. Several prevailing studies analysed the security risk in medical CPS; however, important limitations remain. Although the security patterns were analyzed, the dependencies among the indicators were not considered, leading to ineffective risk interpretation. The research on the assistive healthcare model estimated the vulnerabilities but did not provide a linkage between the risk assessment and mitigation action. The Fuzzy-based models were also explored, yet the limited explainability reduced the trust in the model. Similarly, the FIRE methodology for embedded systems failed to capture the slow drift behaviour in the CPS system19. Attack trees-based analysis for threat modelling suffered from scalability issues when applied in a large-scale environment20. Further, the Methodology for the Analysis and RISk MAnagement (MARISMA)-based patterns analysis for CPS was used but lacked automatic attack detection capability21. The Failure Mode and Effect Analysis (FMEA) relied on CPS but incurred high operational costs22. Vulnerability analysis with risk assessment was integrated but failed to capture calibration-related dynamics23. An IoT-based Adaptive Metaheuristic-Based Feature Selection with Ensemble Learning Model for Privacy-Preserving Cyberattack Detection (AMFS-ELPPCD) was developed to understand the behaviour of cybersecurity attacks24. Also, it is clear from existing literature that intrusion detection to provide cyber security was expanded; yet, the non-stationary behaviour, uncertainty, and so on weren’t determined, reducing the detection of evolving threats25. Further, the prevailing classification model improved the accuracy in detecting the threat, but still lacked the explainability and trustworthiness26. Moreover, the traditional approach detected the attack using the Isolation forest method, but provided no drift management, limiting the trust of the CPS. Then, in the existing work, the attack detection showed gains in feature reduction; yet, it overlooked the dynamic environment and the respective dependencies27. Finally, the conventional struggled to detect the anomalies with sensor drift, limiting the transparency for clinical operations28,29. From this consolidated understanding, it is evident that while multiple frameworks exist, none comprehensively analyze slow drifts, capture indeterminacy, aggregate anomaly dependencies, ensure interpretability, and translate risk into mitigation actions. The proposed framework, anchored in QS-EWMA for drift detection, MMN-NLS for indeterminacy-aware evaluation, CkAFIM for dependency-aware aggregation, SMAS-HAP for explainability, and MLDTPDP for decision-making, seeks to establish a robust, Neutrosophic Fuzzy-inspired, and explainable approach for dynamic risk assessment in CPS.

Drift analysis excellently captures the gradual and small deviations in the system behaviour. Also, the EWMA detects the subtle deviations from baseline data by providing more weight to recent data, thereby enabling small and consistent shifts to influence the statistic. Intrusion detection

In this phase, the SRWSSR-GRU-based intrusion in the CPS network is identified based on the selected features \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( M \right)$$\end{document}. The network traffic patterns are analyzed by the intrusion detection system by learning the normal behaviour of data flows and comparing the incoming traffic against these learned patterns. Here, the Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), which analyzes the network traffic pattern effectively, is used for the classification of intrusions in the hospital server33. However, the GRU has issues like slow convergence and low learning efficiency, affecting the overall classification performance34. Hence, to overcome the low learning rate, the Sparse Random Walk (SRW) initialization is used for weight initialization. SRW initialization maintains stable gradient flow over time and provides long-term memory propagation. Recurrent weight matrices with controlled sparsity and near-unit spectral radius are generated by the SRW initialization, thus making sure that the dominant eigenvalues remain close. This avoids the vanishing gradient issue in long sequences by preventing the repeated matrix multiplications from overly shrinking gradients during backpropagation. Thus, SRW initialization allows the gradients to propagate successfully without disappearing by maintaining the stable state transitions across varying time steps. As a result, long-term dependencies in the CPS are learned more effectively. It models the random walk over a sparsely connected graph. Also, to avoid slow convergence, the Softplus S-shaped Rectified Linear Unit (SSReLU) activation function is used. It shapes the curve symmetrically around zero and avoids abrupt changes in slope. Also, it prevents gate stagnation and accelerates the convergence. The illustration of the SRWSSR-GRU model is given in Fig. 3.Fig. 3SRWSSR-GRU classifier.

The proposed SRWSSR-GRU classifier consists of a reset gate, update gate, and hidden state. Here, in the reset gate, the new information is added or eliminated regarding the previous hidden state. In the reset gate operation, the SSReLU activation function is employed to accelerate the convergence and mitigate the dead neuron issues. Afterward, in the update gate, the information passed to the next time step is determined, effectively handling the long-term dependencies. In the update gate operation, the SRW function-based weight initialization is employed to improve the learning rate. Next, the hidden state makes the classification efficient and accurate. This offers accurate dependency-aware aggregation and scalability for large-scale CPS. Therefore, kAF is the most important interdependency in dynamic risk assessment.

Explainability

Afterward, to determine trust in clinical settings regarding the assessed risk, the explainability is performed for the final uncertainty-aware risk assessment output \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left( B \right)$$\end{document} using SMAS-HAP. The SMAS-HAP assesses how anomaly indicators interact by capturing the complementary and redundant relationships. This SMAS-HAP-based explainability enables the system to reveal hidden interdependencies among CPS behaviours, generating human-interpretable explanations that indicate each indicator’s influence. Here, the SHapley Additive exPlanation (SHAP) that enhances the interpretability of risk assessment by providing insights into how input features influence the risk assessment is used for explainability37. Also, SHAP provides transparent recognition of how cyber anomalies and physical indicators jointly affect the dynamic risk score. Moreover, the kernel function-based weighting assigns feature importance regarding the similarity in the input space. However, the kernel function mayn’t capture the underlying structure of the data when the selected kernel does not align with the true geometry of CPS data. This limitation causes suboptimal outcomes and less effective risk explanations. Hence, to avoid this problem, the Max–Abs Scaling (MAS) formula, which evaluates the kernel function in SHAP, is used. Here, the number of kernel points is initialized; then, the best kernel point is selected by using the MAS formula. Afterward, based on the MAS outcomes, the weights are assigned to features.

Decision making

After the assessment of risk with explainability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$B\left( Z \right)$$\end{document}, the decision-making regarding safety-aware mitigation actions is done using the MLDTPDP. Here, the Markov Decision Process (MDP), which allows systematic modelling of uncertainty in attack evolution and mitigation impact, is used for decision-making38. However, due to the increase in the number of state-action pairs, the MDP model becomes computationally complex. So, to overcome the complexity, the Linear Discrete-Time Propagation (LDTP) algebraic model is applied in the transition phase of MDP. LDTP superiorly avoids exponential state action explosion and computational complexity.

In MLDTPDP, each state indicates the risk level of the medical device in the healthcare CPS network. Also, the action specifies the mitigation strategies applied by the hospital CPS for diminishing risk. Likewise, reward evaluates the benefit of applying the action in a state space regarding decision-making. Similarly, LDTP is the transition dynamics (change of risk states for a particular time) that makes the transitions computationally light. Also, the solver id estimates which action is to be taken in each state.

Conclusion

The proposed framework effectively assessed the dynamic risk in the medical CPS using MMN-NLS. Here, significant processes, such as hospital medical device registration, data sensing, network intrusion detection (data acquisition, pre-processing, feature extraction, slow-drift analysis, attribute extraction, and intrusion detection), dependency-aware aggregation, uncertainty-based dynamic risk assessment, decision-making, and explainability, were performed. For slow-drift analysis, the proposed QS-EWMA attained a low MSE of 0.065, proving the effectiveness of the proposed model. Moreover, the proposed SRWSSR-GRU detected the network intrusions of CPS with 99.322% accuracy. For performing dependency-aware aggregation, the proposed CkAFIM achieved a high consistency index of 98.45%, demonstrating the trustworthiness of the proposed model. Likewise, the proposed MMN-NLS-centric uncertainty-based dynamic risk assessment model achieved a high range consistency of 95.236. For providing the explanations about the risk score, the proposed SMAS-HAP obtained a high fidelity of 0.873, showcasing the reliability of the proposed model. Similarly, the proposed MLDTPDP performed decision-making with a low computation time of 3942 ms. Overall, the proposed model attained high significance and supremacy.

Practical implication

The proposed work is useful in various applications, such as real-time medical applications, IoT, industrial applications, and quantum-based modeling. By incorporating the QS-EWMA, the proposed model identifies the slow degradation in medical sensors, infusion pumps, and ventilators, thus preventing failures that harm patients. Also, the proposed model helps to detect gradual drifts in heart rate, glucose, and motion sensors, thus enhancing the trustworthiness of continuous patient monitoring. In industrial applications, the proposed model identifies the slow drifts in environmental sensors, thus protecting from large-scale system failures. Additionally, by estimating the accurate indeterminancy via MMN-NLS, the proposed model increases the robustness to ambiguous sensor states. Overall, the proposed model had high resiliency in modern CPS.

Limitation

Although the proposed model captured the gradual and slow degradations in CPS network behaviour, it didn’t include the privacy preservation of hospital devices’ data for hiding the raw data, diminishing the risk assessment trust.

Future scope

In the future, the privacy preservation of hospital devices’ data will also be included in this work for hiding the raw data during risk assessment. This helps in safeguarding the patient’s data and enhances trust in the risk assessment system.

Ethical implications

The proposed dynamic risk assessment framework for Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS), particularly in healthcare, raises ethical considerations related to data privacy, transparency, and accountability. Since CPS environments handle sensitive and safety–critical information, strict compliance with regulations such as GDPR and HIPAA is essential to safeguard confidentiality and prevent unauthorized access. The integration of Neutrosophic Fuzzy models and AI-based decision-making further necessitates fairness and interpretability, which are addressed through explainability mechanisms (SMAS-HAP) to ensure that risk scores and mitigation strategies remain transparent and auditable. By accurately capturing slow drifts and handling indeterminacy, the framework reduces false alarms and missed detections, thereby enhancing patient safety and fostering trust. Importantly, the system is designed to support rather than replace human decision-making, ensuring that qualified professionals retain oversight and accountability, thereby promoting responsible deployment of CPS technologies in real-world settings.

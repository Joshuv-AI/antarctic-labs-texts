Assessment of survival and neonatal morbidity rates of very low birth weight infants in Türkiye: Turkish Neonatal Society SECRETS (TNS-SECRETS) study
Authors: M Cetinkaya, S Memur, E Koc, A Y Bas, Turkish Neonatal Society SECRETS Study Group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis prospective multicenter study of the Turkish Neonatal Society includes data from a large cohort of very low birth weight (VLBW) infants from 78 referral centers in Türkiye. The objective was to evaluate survival and neonatal morbidity rates among VLBW infants. All preterm infants with a birth weight (BW) ≤ 1500 g and a gestational age (GA) between 230/7 and 316/7 weeks were included. Respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), periventricular leukomalacia (PVL) hemodynamically significant patent ductus arteriosus (hsPDA), bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), sepsis and retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) were defined as neonatal morbidities. A total of 3042 VLBW infants were enrolled. The mean GA and median BW were 27 ± 2.2 weeks and 1080 g (ranges, 320 and 1500 g), respectively. The incidences of neonatal morbidities were as follows: RDS 76.3%, IVH 28%, PVL 0.9%, hsPDA 31.5%, BPD 39.5%, NEC 14%, and ROP 25%. Early and late sepsis were diagnosed in 19.3% and 44.6% of the study group. The overall mortality rate was 21.7%, and survival without major morbidity was 42.8%. Mortality among infants ≤ 28 weeks of gestation was 34.8%, whereas it was 5.5% for infants > 28 weeks of GA. This study provides a comprehensive overview of outcomes among VLBW infants in Türkiye and offers valuable insights for other developing countries. Introduction

Preterm birth (PTB) remains the leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality worldwide and is also a major contributor to childhood mortality. The risks of mortality and neonatal morbidities are inversely related to gestational age (GA) and birth weight (BW). According to recent estimates, 20–30 million small and sick newborns require hospitalization annually, nearly half of whom are preterm infants. Furthermore, approximately 2.3 million infants die annually during the neonatal period, with prematurity-related complications accounting for the majority of deaths. Nearly all neonatal deaths (98%) occur in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). Therefore, mortality among very low birth weight (VLBW) and extremely low birth weight (ELBW) infants remains high especially in developing countries, with outcomes strongly influenced by GA and the availability of healthcare resources. In addition, the burden of major neonatal morbidities remains substantial, and survival without significant morbidity is uncommon among survivors, particularly in infants born at lower GAs. Although the most recent global PTB rate was estimated at approximately 9.9% in 2020, the rate has been reported to be substantially higher as around 15% in Türkiye1–5.

Despite advances in perinatal and neonatal care and the establishment of high-technology neonatal intensive care units (NICUs), certain major morbidities including intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), periventricular leukomalacia (PVL), hemodynamic significant patent ductus arteriosus (hsPDA), necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), sepsis and retinopathy of prematurity (ROP) continue to occur among survivors. These conditions are associated with prolonged hospitalization, adverse short and long-term outcomes, and increased healthcare costs in high-risk infants6. Consequently, improving the quality of care for preterm infants and reducing prematurity-related mortality and morbidities require adherence to national and international guidelines, implementation of evidence-based preventive and therapeutic practices, and provision of parental support measures4.

In recent years, Türkiye has undertaken extensive perinatal and neonatal healthcare initiatives to enhance neonatal survival and outcomes. Collaborative strategies developed by the Turkish Ministry of Health and the Turkish Neonatal Society (TNS) included strengthening NICU standards, conducting quality-improvement programs, expanding specialized neonatal transport services, training of NICU healthcare professionals, and establishing modern high-intensity NICUs for critically ill newborns. Additional efforts have focused on structured antenatal follow-up, standardizing delivery room (DR) practices, implementing Neonatal Resuscitation Programme (NRP) training, developing national TNS guidelines, and promoting breastfeeding and baby-friendly healthcare initiatives. As a result, neonatal mortality rates declined sharply between 2000 and 2011 but have remained stable since the early 2010s7–9.

Strengthening national data systems is essential to improve the availability of individual-level data for clinical practice, policy implementation, and quality improvement initiatives in maternity wards and NICUs. When combined with structured follow-up programs, these systems have been proposed as a key strategy for achieving Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 3.2 and the Every Newborn Action Plan, which both aim to eliminate preventable neonatal and under-5 mortality, specifically targeting a neonatal mortality rate of ≤ 12 per 1,000 live births in every country 1.

Although several small center-based studies exist, there is very limited data on the survival and neonatal morbidity rates for VLBW infants in Türkiye. The lack of local nationwide information limits evaluation of NICU performance, identification of gaps in preterm care practices and results with uniformity in clinical decision-making. It also hinders meaningful comparisons of national outcomes with international benchmarks. The objective of this prospective multicenter study was to determine survival and neonatal morbidity rates among VLBW infants who either died during hospitalization or were discharged from NICUs across Türkiye.

Results

A total of 3386 preterm infants with BW ≤ 1500 g and GA of 230/7 weeks to 316/7 weeks were hospitalized in 78 NICUs during the study period, 3042 (89.8%) eligible infants were included in the study. Of these, 1464 (48%) were male, and 2629 (86%) of them were appropriate for GA (AGA). The mean GA and median BW of infants were 27 ± 2.2 weeks and 1080 (ranges, 320 to 1500) grams, respectively. The number of multiple births was 743(24%). A total of 2650 infants (87%) were born by cesarean sectio and 97 (3.2%) infants were outborn. The mean maternal age was 29.2 ± 5 years, and 47% of mothers were primigravida. Maternal diseases were present in 38% of pregnant women, and 74% of them had at least one antenatal visit. Table 2 shows the DR and NICU practices in the study group in terms of GA. Neonatal resuscitation in the DR including positive pressure ventilation, chest compression, intubation, and adrenalin was administered in 1823 (59.9%), 1177(38.6%), 99 (3.2%) and 40(1.3%) infants, respectively. A total of 656 (21.5%) infants had delayed cord clamping (DCC) between 30 to 60 s. Intratracheal surfactant was administered in 2111 infants (69.3%). Among those, 188 infants (6.2%) received prophylactic surfactant therapy in the DR, 1573 infants (51.7%) received rescue surfactant therapy in the NICU and 350 infants (11.5%) received surfactant in both DR and NICU. Surfactant was administered by less invasive surfactant administration (LISA) or minimally invasive surfactant therapy (MIST) in 296 infants (9.7%) whereas the remaining received it by INtubation-SURfactant-Extubation (INSURE) technique. Initially, non-invasive, invasive and high frequency mechanical ventilation (MV) were required in 49%, 49%, and 2% of infants, respectively. Systemic and inhaled postnatal corticosteroids were applied in 677 (22.5%) and 360 (11.8%) infants, respectively. Caffeine therapy was administered in 89% (2709 of 3042) of infants, with a median duration of 36 days. Total parenteral nutrition (TPN) was started in 96.3% of all infants and the median duration of TPN was 12 days. Oropharyngeal mother’s milk (OMM) was administered to 744 infants (24.5%) within the first 72 h of life and to 44 infants (1.4%) after 72 h of life. Minimal enteral nutrition (MEN) was established in 1348 infants (44.3%) within the first 24 h and in 1131 infants (37.1%) after 24 h. The infants reached full enteral nutrition and full oral feeding in a median of 19 and 49 days, respectively. The median duration of hospitalization were 56 and 18 days among survivors and non-survivors, respectively.

Discussion

This is the largest prospective multicenter study to date, encompassing 3,042 VLBW infants across 78 Level III and IV NICUs in Türkiye, where neonatologists serve as primary staff. By establishing comprehensive baseline data on survival and neonatal morbidities stratified by GA, our findings provide an important national benchmark. These results provide a framework for developing healthcare strategies and quality improvement initiatives to optimize neonatal care for very preterm infants in Türkiye. Furthermore, this approach may serve as a model for other developing countries to improve national neonatal care standards.

It is well known that PTB and prematurity-related complications constitute the leading cause of death in children < 5 years. Consequently, preventing PTB rates is a global priority to reduce associated morbidities and improve survival10. Notably, extremely preterm infants contribute disproportionately to both neonatal mortality and childhood morbidity, despite recent advances in neonatal clinical practice11. In addition, these conditions are also associated with adverse long term health outcomes among those who survive. Therefore, efforts to reduce PTB rates are essential not only for global health improvement but also for the long-term economic and social stability of developing countries12.

Comprehensive national data on preterm infant morbidity and mortality are essential for establishing real-time monitoring, identifying care deficiencies, and facilitating quality improvement. Such data enable institutions to benchmark clinical performance, optimize health service delivery, and support evidence-based clinical decision-making13. While several global and national prematurity-related outcomes have been published10–17, national data within the context of Türkiye remain limited5. To address this empirical gap, we conducted the present prospective multicenter study to evaluate neonatal outcomes and provide a framework for standardizing care.

Administering ACS therapy and performing DCC are essential strategies for reducing PTB related complications and improving neonatal survival18. Evidence indicates that a single course of ACS therapy promotes fetal lung maturation and decrease the risk of perinatal death, neonatal death, RDS, and IVH in pregnancies at risk of preterm delivery19. ACS administration rates in developing countries typically range from 85 to 95%20–22. The 73% rate observed in this study, though lower than international cohorts, reflects a markedly increase trend from the 42.9% reported previously5. The increase in ACS therapy rates may reflect the growing awareness of obstetricians and perinatologists regarding its clear benefits for preterm infant survival. Nevertheless, despite recent improvements, further efforts are required to reach higher ACS therapy rates to achieve more favorable neonatal outcomes.

DCC has been shown to reduce the risk of mortality before discharge in preterm infants. However, the DCC rate in our study (21.3%) was markedly lower than the rates reported in the US (81.7%) and LMICs (60%)23,24. As with ACS therapy, expanding DCC implementation in Turkish NICUs is essential for improving VLBW infant care and survival.

DR interventions are nearly universal in extremely preterm infants. In this cohort, 59% of neonates required stabilization and 3.2% required full CPR, reflecting trends observed in the international literature. Such early interventions are critical, as they significantly improve neonatal outcomes and survival in preterm infants25.

RDS is an important cause of mortality in preterm infants. The incidence of RDS is inversely related to GA and BW. It affects about 80% of neonates born at 28 weeks GA and 90% of infants at 24 weeks GA. In addition, about 50–60% of RDS cases require surfactant administration26. RDS was diagnosed in 2324 infants (76.3%) and intratracheal surfactant was administered in 2111 infants (69.3%). Regarding surfactant administration, 51.7% of the infants received rescue therapy in the NICU, while a smaller proportion (6.2%) received prophylactic treatment in the DR; overall 11.5% of the cohort required surfactant in both settings. Our findings indicate that extremely preterm infants (GA ≤ 28 weeks) were significantly more likely to receive surfactant in both the DR and NICU compared to more mature infants (GA > 28 weeks). Surfactant was delivered via less- or minimally-invasive methods in 9.7% of cases. The rates of LISA/MIST was lower than the incidence reported from Europe27. This discrepancy may be attributed to variations in study populations and regional or institutional care practices.

The incidence of BPD varies depending on several factors, including NICU practices and differences in the clinical definitions of BPD. The incidence of BPD in VLBW infants has remained stable at approximately 40–45% over the last few decades28,29. Similar to those data, the BPD rate was 39.5% in the present study. However, the Swiss Neonatal Network (SNN) and US members of the Vermont Oxford Network (US-VON) reported chronic lung disease as 25.9% and 11.8%, respectively, in survived VLBW infants30. Despite the mortality rate being higher in infants < 28 weeks of gestation, the survivors also have higher BPD rates in our patient population. Therefore, antenatal and postnatal preventive measures and effective protocols should be implemented to reduce the risk of BPD at NICUs in Türkiye.

Current evidence suggests that a synergistic combination of TPN and enteral nutrition during the early postnatal transition is essential to ensure adequate nutrient delivery, support optimal growth, and improve neonatal outcomes in VLBW infants. Consequently, the early initiation of TPN in combination with enteral feeding, prioritizing mothers’ own milk, particularly colostrum, has become an established standard of care for preterm infants31. MEN was initiated within the first 24 h of life in 44% of infants, while 37% started after 24 h; notably, 19% of infants never received MEN in our cohort. Furthermore, despite near-universal initiation of TPN (96%), there was a striking lack of OMM administration in 74% of cases. These suboptimal rates of MEN and OMM utilization may be attributed to some systemic limitations in healthcare staffing, specifically the lack of specialized nutritionists and dietitians within routine NICU care in Türkiye. Consequently, our findings underscore an urgent need for standardized nutritional protocols that prioritize early MEN with OMM and the promotion of breastfeeding. Implementing such standardized practices is essential to enhance survival, reduce the incidences of NEC and neonatal sepsis and also to improve neonatal outcomes.

The reported incidence of NEC in preterm infants with a GA < 32 weeks differs between 2–10%, and 5% to 22% among those with a BW < 1000 g in developed countries32. The incidence of NEC and advanced NEC (stage ≥ II) in the present study was 14% and 8.1%, respectively. These relatively high rates may be attributable to the lower use of ACS therapy, together with suboptimal nutritional and clinical care practices, including especially the limited use of MEN with OMM during the early neonatal period. The incidence of late-onset sepsis (LOS) has been reported to range between 15 and 25% among VLBW infants in developed countries33. In a previous multicenter study conducted in Türkiye, the incidence of LOS was 42.4% among VLBW infants5. Similarly, LOS was diagnosed in 44.6% of the study cohort. The higher rates of LOS and NEC may be reduced through strict adherence to infection prevention and control measures, including judicious antibiotic use, appropriate hand hygiene practices, increased awareness among healthcare professionals, promotion of breastfeeding, and avoidance of overcrowding in NICUs.

The frequency of PDA among VLBW infants has been reported to be 36% and 38% in Japan and Canada, respectively34. In the present study, the overall rate of hsPDA was 31.5%. In a previous multicenter study conducted in Türkiye, moderate-to-large PDA was reported in 46% of infants born between 240/7 and 286/7 weeks of GA35. This difference may be attributable to variations in PDA diagnostic criteria, as well as improvements in postnatal care practices in recent years.

ROP still remains a critical morbidity in both high- and middle-low-income countries, driven by the degree of immaturity and the quality of neonatal intensive care. In recent years, the reported incidence of ROP has ranged from 10 to 40%, reflecting significant variations across different nations and study populations36. A multicenter TR-ROP study from Türkiye reported any stage of ROP as 27% in VLBW infants37. Similarly, any stage of ROP in our study was detected in 25% of all infants. The slight decrease in ROP rates may also be attributable to improvements in antenatal and postnatal care over time. In our cohort, the incidence of treatment-requiring ROP was 8.2%, which is comparable to the 6.7% incidence of severe ROP previously reported in Türkiye37.

The incidence of severe IVH is nearly 7–15% in VLBW infants. In the previous Turkish multicenter study, the incidence of severe IVH (> Grade II according to Papile staging) was reported as 5.4%5. In the current study, the incidences of any and severe IVH were 28% and 8.7%, respectively. In the Swiss study, the incidence of severe IVH was 3.5% in survived VLBW infants and 5.7% in the US-VON study30. The incidence of IVH in our study was higher than that reported in developed countries, a difference that may be attributable to the combined effects of the antenatal and postnatal clinical care practice factors discussed above.

According to a recent US multicenter study including 10,877 ELBW infants born at 22–28 weeks’ GA between 2013 and 2018, the incidences of NEC, LOS, severe IVH, severe ROP, and severe BPD were 8.9%, 19.9%, 14.3%, 12.8%, and 8%, respectively30. These findings indicate that the incidence of neonatal morbidities in infants ≤ 28 weeks in the US study is comparable to that observed in infants < 32 weeks in our cohort. Therefore, although Türkiye has undertaken substantial efforts to improve perinatal and neonatal outcomes related to prematurity, further improvements in antenatal and postnatal care remain necessary for better neonatal outcomes.

The survival rate of VLBW infants is reported to be over 90% in developed countries.30,38. The overall mortality rate of VLBW infants from Latin American countries was 26.8%39. In the present study, as 78.2% of VLBW infants survived to discharge, overall mortality was 21.7%. The early neonatal, neonatal, and in-hospital mortality rates were reported as 3.9%, 6.0%, and 7.5%, respectively, in the multicenter study of the eNewborn European Network database38. The early, late and post-neonatal mortality rates in our study were 12.7%, 6.2% and 2.9%, respectively. Therefore, early neonatal mortality is higher in Türkiye compared with European countries.

The survival rate to discharge for extremely premature infants (between 22 and 28 weeks) was reported to be 78.3% in US11. The mortality was found as 34.8% among infants born at ≤ 28 weeks’ GA in our study. Therefore, the survival rate for infants with GA < 32 weeks in Türkiye (79%) appears comparable to the survival rate reported for infants < 28 weeks in US, while overall remaining intermediate between rates observed in developed and developing countries. All these data show that there is a considerable gap in the survival rates of preterm infants between developing and developed countries. It is reasonable to underscore the need of several improvements from DR to NICU practices to reach the status of developed countries and also to achieve more favorable neonatal outcomes in Türkiye.

Strength and limitations

The strength of the present study was its large, multicenter design that allowed for prospective data collection via a specialized network. In addition, most of the centers represent reference NICUs in which neonatologists work as the primary physicians.

There are some limitations of the study. NICUs with no neonatologists or NICUs including neonatologists who did not agree to participate in the study were not included. This was a limitation, as these might increase the overall rates of mortality and morbidities. Also, the neonatologists did not undertake any training to standardize the definitions of potential risk factors before the study. Furthermore, due to the BW-based inclusion criterion (< 1500 g), exclusion of infants born at later GA (> 31 weeks) from the study cohort, with the idea of they highly represent a population of growth-restricted infants rather than very preterm infants, may also have introduced selection bias and influenced the interpretation of associations between maternal characteristics and neonatal outcomes. Similarly, exclusion of infants born at 22 weeks of GA with the same purpose may also be considered as a limitation. In addition, the lack of data including DR deaths and the number of infants who could not be admitted to NICUs within the first 24 h represent another limitation, as these data may provide insights about the evaluation of neonatal resuscitation and transport practices for the country.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the current study provides an important overview for the Turkish population and also other developing countries. Morbidity rate in VLBW infants is a serious concern in Türkiye and higher than those in developed countries. Therefore, strategies to decrease both prematurity-related morbidities and mortality should be developed. Both antenatal and perinatal follow-up programs and care strategies, appropriate DR approaches, and advanced NICU practices should be implemented. In addition, strict follow-up of oxygen therapy with appropriate monitoring, better neonatal care, meticulous attention to hygiene procedures, control of sepsis, promotion of breastfeeding, and training of NICU healthcare professionals may also reduce the prevalence of neonatal morbidities. These findings highlight the need for sustained clinical quality improvement efforts, restructuring of perinatal and neonatal care systems, and enhanced antenatal and postnatal care to improve survival free of major prematurity-related morbidities.

Methods

The SECRETS study was promoted by the TNS and was approved by the Local Ethical Committee (Number:2021.03.30). The study was conducted between 28 April 2021 and 02 January 2023 in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. A written informed parental consent was obtained from all families. A total of 78 reference level III and IV NICUs from 7 different regions of Türkiye participated. All these NICUs had the expertise to follow up these high-risk premature infants in terms of their medical staff, equipment, and other resources.

Study population

The inclusion criteria comprised inborn and outborn preterm infants with BW ≤ 1500 g and GA of 230⁄7 to 316⁄7 weeks who were admitted to the NICU within the first 24 h of life and for whom parental consent was obtained. Preterm infants with GA < 32 weeks but BW > 1500 g were excluded, as this subgroup represents a distinct clinical population, predominantly consisting of growth-restricted rather than very preterm infants. As the primary objective of the study was to evaluate survival and neonatal outcomes in VLBW infants, whose respiratory physiology, postnatal adaptation, and morbidity profiles differ substantially from those of more mature and/or growth-restricted infants, the study cohort was restricted to infants with BW ≤ 1500 g and GA < 32 weeks to ensure a more homogeneous population. In addition, infants born at 22 weeks’ gestation were also excluded as resuscitation practices at this threshold vary considerably across centers in Türkiye, largely depending on institutional experience, which may introduce substantial heterogeneity and potential bias for the analysis. Infants who died in the DR, infants admitted to the NICU after the first 24 h of life, infants with major congenital/chromosomal abnormalities, and those whose parents refused consent were all defined as the exclusion criteria. The maternal, perinatal, and neonatal characteristics, care practices, neonatal morbidities and survival data of all infants were noted.

All data were collected through a web-based data entry system via a special network named ‘Trials-Network.’ A case report form (CRF) for each included infant was filled out by the neonatologist of the center. All questions in the CRF were compulsory, meaning the data entry system did not allow submission if any question was left unanswered. Database access was password-protected to maintain data security. The records of infants from all 78 NICUs were pooled together and analyzed at the end of the study by the primary investigator.

Clinical data

Both antenatal and perinatal clinical data, including maternal age, parity, administration of antenatal corticosteroids, presence of maternal diseases such as preeclampsia, eclampsia, hypertension, and diabetes, multiple births, number of prenatal visit, and mode of delivery were collected. GA was determined using ultrasonography and/or the date of the last menstrual period. Infants were classified as small for gestational age (SGA) and large for gestational age (LGA) if their BW was below the 10th percentile or above the 90th percentile for GA and sex, respectively, according to Fenton growth charts.

The recorded clinical characteristics of infants were as follows: GA, BW, gender, presence of SGA or LGA, multiple births, inborn and outborn status, the need for resuscitation and intubation in the DR, DCC status, the need for surfactant treatment, surfactant administration time, setting and method, the duration of both invasive and non-invasive MV, the duration of hospitalization, medications including probiotics, caffeine and postnatal corticosteroids, duration of TPN, achievement of both full enteral nutrition and also full oral feeding. In-hospital mortality was defined as ‘early neonatal’ (days 1–6), ‘late neonatal’ (days 7–28), and ‘post-neonatal’ (from day 29 to until NICU discharge). For standardized management, all centers were advised to follow the recommendations of the TNS guidelines. The recent NRP recommendations were followed by all centers for the management and resuscitation of inborn infants in the DR40,41.

In this study, neonatal morbidities comprised RDS, IVH, hsPDA, BPD, NEC, sepsis, and ROP. Notably, RDS was excluded from the composite outcome of survival without major morbidities to avoid statistical bias resulting from its high prevalence. IVH (grade ≥ 3) and/or cystic PVL, NEC (stage ≥ 2), BPD, ROP (stage ≥ 3), and neonatal sepsis were defined as major neonatal morbidities affecting survival as previously described13. Both survival and survival without major morbidity were calculated using all included infants (n = 3042) as the denominator at the final statistical analysis.

RDS was diagnosed if the infant exhibited clinical signs of respiratory distress, required supplemental oxygen and/or assisted ventilation, and had a chest radiograph consistent with RDS. Surfactant was administered according to national and international RDS and surfactant guidelines42,43. The presence and severity of IVH were defined according to the Volpe classification and TNS guideline. Cystic PVL was defined as the presence of periventricular cysts identified on cranial ultrasonography or magnetic resonance imaging44. The hsPDA requiring medical or surgical treatment was diagnosed as an inner ductal diameter > 1.5 mm/kg or > 1.4 mm/kg, and/or LA/Ao > 1.5, and/or retrograde flow in the descending aorta45. Infants who died before echocardiography and/or cranial ultrasonography were not included in the denominators when calculating hsPDA or IVH incidence.

NEC was diagnosed based on clinical and radiographic findings and was classified according to the modified Bell’s criteria46,47. BPD was defined according to the 2001 National Institute of Child Health and Human Development consensus statement as the requirement for supplemental oxygen for the first 28 days of life and was classified as mild, moderate, or severe depending on the duration and level of supplemental oxygen and MV support at 36 weeks postmenstrual age (PMA)48,49. The infants who survived to 36 weeks PMA was the denominator for BPD.

ROP was classified according to the International Classification of Retinopathy of Prematurity and TNS ROP guideline screening recommendations were followed50,51. ROP was determined among infants who were screened and survived to discharge. Infants who died before first ROP examination were not included to the analysis. Early-onset sepsis was defined as the onset of clinical and laboratory findings at < 72 h of age. LOS was defined as the onset of systemic signs of infection and increased acute-phase reactants at > 72 h of age52. LOS was calculated among infants that survived beyond 72 h of life.

Statistical analysis

The data were analyzed using SPSS Statistics 21.0 software. Demographic variables, care practices, morbidities, and mortality are summarized using descriptive statistics without comparison. Demographic data were presented as number, percentage, mean (± SD), and median (interquartile range (IQR)). The results are displayed by each week of GA. The association between GA categories (≤ 28 vs > 28 weeks) and mortality was evaluated using the Pearson chi-square test. When appropriate, Fisher’s exact test was additionally applied to confirm the robustness of the results. Crude odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated to quantify the strength of association.

For multivariate analysis, logistic regression models were constructed to identify independent predictors of mortality, NEC, and severe IVH for infants born ≤ 28 weeks’ GA. Variables included in the models were selected based on clinical relevance and statistical significance in univariate analyses (p < 0.10). These variables comprised GA, BW, duration of MV within the first 7 days of life, presence of sepsis, exposure to any dose of ACS and sex. Adjusted odds ratios (aORs) with 95% confidence intervals were calculated to determine independent associations. A two-tailed p value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

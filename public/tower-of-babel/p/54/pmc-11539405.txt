A multi-center, randomized, open label, two-arm study to evaluate safety & efficacy of nutraceutical tablet as adjuvant when compared with standard of care in patients with benign prostatic hyperplasia
Authors: Suresh B Patankar, Anupama Patankar, Sagar Patankar, Rajesh Raje, Chandu Devanpally, Pranjal Ausekar, GauravPatil, Shraddha Chitale
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundBenign Prostrate Hyperplasia (BPH) is a progressive disease of ageing men that may be associated with enlargement of the prostate and lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS). Herbal/Nutraceutical formulations in addition to standard of care (SOC) could alleviate the symptoms, and thus improve the quality of life of patients.ObjectivesTo evaluate safety & efficacy of nutraceutical tablet as an adjuvant with SOC.Materials and MethodsThis was a prospective, randomized two-arm study aimed to assess the safety and efficacy of Herbal/Nutraceutical Formulation (IP) + SOC versus only SOC, in BPH patients. The primary efficacy endpoint was the change in international prostate symptom score (IPSS) within and between two arms. The safety was evaluated in terms of adverse events and change in prostate specific antigen (PSA) levels.Results140 eligible patients (70 / arm) were evaluated for efficacy and safety endpoints. The baseline characteristics of patients in two arms differed nonsignificantly. The change in IPSS-storage, voiding and QoL scores, from day 1 to 90 were statistically significant in both the arms (p < 0.0001). However, by day 90, the change in these scores in SOC+IP arm were significantly higher than that of SOC arm. Further, the change in International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF) scores was significant in SOC+IP arm (p < 0.05), while non-significant in SOC arm. The adverse events non-significantly differed between two arms.ConclusionThe herbal/nutraceutical formulation combined with SOC are safe and effective for the treatment of BPH. The combination therapy was effective in reducing urine-related symptoms and improving the QoL of BPH patients.Keywords: Herbal extracts, Nutraceuticals, Benign prostate hyperplasia, International prostate symptom score

Introduction

Prostate enlargement and painful lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) have been linked to benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), a common and progressive clinical condition of aging men. This illness often strikes men over the age of 45 years and is linked to erectile dysfunction. Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) is a clinically useful diagnostic marker for prostate cancer, and thus understanding the link between BPH and serum PSA levels is crucial. PSA levels may indicate the size of the prostate and the seriousness of the condition. The advancement of BPH is associated with blood PSA levels >1.5 ng/mL and prostatic volumes >30 mL. When BPH worsens, it causes more severe LUTS, lowers quality of life (QOL), and often necessitates surgery [1]. There are several herbal and nutraceutical products in the market to alleviate the symptoms of enlarged prostate and the lower urinary system. Herbal and nutraceuticals professionals identify two prostatism-related disorders, mootrakruchra and mootraaghaata. Mootrakruchra (strangury) causes considerable discomfort when urinating, while mootraaghaata suppresses or intermittently flows urine [2]. There is a need for large-scale studies to evaluate the spectrum of treatments used to treat LUTS and BPH in daily practice and compare the results across treatments [3]. The efficacy and safety of treatments like a standard of care (SOC) in terms of alpha-adrenergic blockers, phytotherapy, and combination therapy have been assessed in numerous clinical trials. The Quality of Life in Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (QUALIPROST) study was conducted to evaluate the QOL of a large group of patients with moderate to severe LUTS or BPH who were treated with therapies commonly used in clinical practice [4]. This study aims to evaluate the safety and efficacy of herbal/nutraceutical (IP) plus SOC versus SOC in BPH patients, the frequency of problems, and the associated QoL symptoms in terms of (International Prostate Symptom Score) IPSS score.

Materials and methods

Trial design

A prospective, multi-center, randomized, open-label, two-arm study was designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of herbal/nutraceutical tablet formulation plus SOC when compared with only SOC in patients with BPH. The study was conducted at four different centers in central India between May 2022 and January 2023. A total of 140 eligible patients were assigned randomly to the two treatment arms in a ratio 1:1 and followed for 12 weeks. The patients visiting the outpatient departments (OPDs) of the selected sites were screened for inclusion in the study. The sample size was estimated referring to change in IPSS-QOL scores. A mean difference of 0.5 was considered between two groups with a pooled standard deviation of 0.95, resulting into an effect size of 0.52. This resulted into a sample of 60 patients per group that can provide the desired effect with 80% power and 95% confidence interval. Further, considering 15% loss-to-follow up, the final sample size obtained was 70 patients per group.

Participants

A total of 176 patients were screened at all four centers to attain the target sample of 140 patients. The patients were randomized to the two arms using block randomization of size two. Male patients in the age range of 18–65 years diagnosed with BPH, having international prostate symptom score (IPSS) more than 8, with Qmax between 5 and 15 ml/s and voided volume >125 ml, post-voiding residue <300 ml on ultrasound, and willing to participate in the study were included for assessment. Excluded patients were those with PSA >4 ng/ml at screening, having a history of neurogenic bladder, urethral strictures, prostatitis, urologic cancer, and prostatic surgery, significant bladder outflow, heart disease, uncontrolled arrhythmias, hypertension, and those on hemodialysis. Informed consent was obtained from the patients before enrolling in the study. During the study, patients were free to withdraw their consent from the study. The patient flow is given in Fig. 1.

Patient flow diagram.

Drug administration

The investigational product (IP) consisted of herbal/nutraceutical ingredients, innovated by one of the co-authors who is a clinician and researcher. The details of the product ingredients are given in the Supplementary material. The IP was in the form of a tablet and administered to the test arm twice daily, in the morning and evening around the same time, for a period of 12 weeks, in addition to SOC. The SOC consisted of allopathic medications that include alpha-blockers like tamsulosin hydrochloride and alfuzosin. These medications work by relaxing the muscles in the prostate and bladder neck, thereby improving urine flow and reducing symptoms of BPH. Patients were not permitted to take any other medicines or Over the Counter (OTC) products (including vitamins and products from natural origin), except those prescribed by the Principal Investigator (PI), as essential for the well-being of the patient in the test arm. Patients were expected to maintain compliance and accountability for all study drug medication during the conduct of the study.

Efficacy & safety evaluations

The study aimed at determining the efficacy and safety of the herbal/nutraceutical (IP) in the treatment of BPH. The primary efficacy variable was to compare the change in total international prostate symptom score (IPSS) (storage & voiding) from baseline to end of treatment (EOT) between SOC + IP and SOC arms. The secondary endpoints were to compare the change in: a) IPSS storage, b) IPSS voiding, c) IPSS quality of life scores d) Qmax, e) PVR f) IIEF-EF, g) urine frequency and urgency score, from baseline to end of treatment. The safety was evaluated in terms of adverse events (AE) and severe adverse events (SAE), as well as common side effects of alpha-blockers, drug interaction, change in prostate-specific antigen (PSA), biochemical, haematological, and vital parameters during the study.

Ethics & regulatory authorization

The ethical clearance was obtained from the respective local hospital sites. The entire study was conducted following the principles of Good Clinical Practice (GCP), the declaration of Helsinki, and Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) guidelines for biomedical research on humans. The trial was registered in India with the number CTRI/2022/05/042513.

Statistical methods

The data on continuous parameters were summarized using mean, standard deviation, and range, while categorical parameters were expressed in terms of frequency and percentage. The normality assumption of continuous parameters was tested using Shapiro-Wilk's test. Accordingly, the between-arms comparisons were tested for significance using a t-test for independent samples. The prostate-specific antigen data was log-transformed and compared within the arm using paired t-test, while between arms using t-test for independent samples. Urine frequency and urgency score across times in each arm were compared using Friedman ANOVA, while between arms at each time was compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. The post-void residue (PVR) within and between arms were compared by Wilcoxon signed-rank test and Mann-Whitney U test respectively. Further, the IPSS storage, voiding, storage + voiding, and quality of life scores were compared within and between arms using Friedman ANOVA and Mann-Whitney U test respectively. The change in these scores from baseline to day 90 was compared between arms using the Mann-Whitney U test. All the analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp. ARMONK USA) and the statistical significance was evaluated at a 5% level.

The differences of safety endpoints like biochemical parameters were statistically not significant within and between arms. Regarding hematology, basophils, and MCHC showed significant change within both the arms, while monocytes showed statistically significant change in the SOC + IP arm, and neutrophils showed significant change in the SOC arm. The vital parameters like pulse rate and systolic blood pressure showed a significant reduction in the mean levels of parameters from day 1 to day 90. There were 9 adverse events in the SOC + IP arm, while 16 were in the SOC arm during the study (Table 4S). Hyperbilirubinemia was observed in 3 patients from both the arms at day 1. Later at day 90, it was observed in one patient from SOC arm. High ALT & AST, anemia and thrombocytopenia were observed in one patient each from SOC arm at day 1. Fatigue, fever, nausea and cough were observed in one patient each in SOC + IP arm at day 28. Diziness, fatigue and vomiting was seen in one patient each from SOC arm at day 56. Further, headache, nausea and fatigue was seen in one patient each from SOC at day 90.

Discussion

Polyherbomineral formulation acts at multiple levels, which needs to be studied to find out the exact mechanism. Since these are not well understood today, it is essential to determine their role and efficacy in mainstream treatment through clinical studies. In the present study, we observed some promising results with the herbal formulations in addition to SOC. According to a study done by Ma X et al. (2020), herbal formulations that include fatty acids and antioxidants can aid in lowering prostate gland inflammation, enhancing urine flow, and preventing the growth of cancer cells [5]. Additionally, it has been discovered that herbal preparations alleviate the pain and symptoms of benign prostate cancer, such as an increase in urine frequency and difficulty in peeing.

The age of patients diagnosed with BPH and enrolled in the study ranged from 50 to 60 years. The symptoms are uncommon in men younger than 40 years, while they are more prominent during 50–60 years. Bijak et al. (2017) also showed that the incidence and severity of symptoms of BPH both rise with age [6]. The primary endpoint related to IPSS (storage + voiding) showed a significantly low median score in the SOC + IP arm as compared to the SOC arm on day 90. The change in scores from screening to day 90 was noticeable in both arms. Further, the median change in IPSS (storage + voiding) scores from day 1 to day 90 was significantly higher in the SOC + IP arm than in the SOC arm. The storage symptoms like urine frequency, urgency and nocturia were lowered in SOC + IP arm. Also, the voiding symptoms like poor flow, intermittency, incomplete evacuation and staining were also lowered in the treatment arm. According to a study by Miao et al. (2021), the combination of Qmax 10 ml/s and IPSS Voiding/Storage >1 improves the positive predictive value in the diagnosis of bladder outlet-related LUTD [7]. This finding indicates that the IPSS Voiding/Storage ratio is a more useful diagnostic tool, and can be utilized to distinguish between patients with bladder-related and bladder outlet-related urinary tract infections. Apart from the significance of SOC + IP with IPSS (storage + voiding), storage and voiding alone, also demonstrated substantive modifications in the management of BPH when compared to SOC.

In our study, the primary indicator IPSS-QoL indicated a significant change in the scores from screening day to day 90 in both arms. The SOC + IP arm had a significantly larger median change than the SOC arm. In another study by Patel et al. (2016), the authors also reported a significant reduction in the QoL scores from baseline to six weeks [8]. Some of the ingredients like Crataeva, Boerhavia, Commiphora mukul were common to our IP product. We observed a significant increase in the Qmax score from day 1 to day 90 in the SOC + IP arm, unlike the SOC arm. In a study by Sahu et al. (2003), the authors reported a significant increase in the Qmax scores before and after treatment with their polyherbal formulation [9]. In a study by Sokeland (2000), the author also observed an increase in the Qmax score for the treatment arm constituting sabal and urtica extract. Regarding IIEF scores of patients, there was no significant difference between the two arms for different indicators [10]. The vast majority of men, 85%, report at least some difficulty with erections [11]. We observed that all five indicators related to IIEF scores showed statistically significant improvement in the patients. The use of this therapeutic technique in the management of sexual functioning is supported by this research. Regarding urine-related problems, the urine frequency significantly reduced from day 1 to day 90 in both treatment arms. Earlier, Nassiri and Hosseinzadeh (2019) conducted a clinical trial on prostate cancer patients and reported that after one month of administration of nutraceutical supplements, there was a significant reduction in urine frequency [1].

In this study, the change in prostate-specific antigen (PSA) marker was statistically not significant within and between arms. All the patients had PSA levels below 5.82 ng/ml, which is the cut-off referred for biopsy [12]. Although PSA measurement in the evaluation of BPH should not be a requirement, its measurement before and after treatment may provide a surrogate for treatment efficacy [13]. However, in the present study, there was no effect of IP on the PSA levels of patients. Another study by Flaig et al. (2010) suggests that herbal products and supplements with SOC do not show any significant changes in PSA levels of patients [14]. Other biochemical parameters were not significantly different within and between arms. Although some of the hematological and vital parameters showed significant changes within the arms, the change was clinically unrelated to the treatment [[15], [16]]. The adverse events were smaller in the SOC + IP arm as compared to the SOC arm, even though the difference was statistically not significant (p = 0.198).

Conclusion

This study demonstrated the efficacy and safety of IP as a supplement to SOC. The combination significantly increased the IPSS storage & voiding scores, and IIEF domain scores from day 1 to day 90. Furthermore, IPSS-QoL significantly improved with IP supplementation from day 1 to day 90. This study has provided evidence that the combination of herbal and nutraceutical products with a standard of care provides a safe and effective option for those suffering from LUTS-related issues in BPH patients. More clinical trials are needed to underscore the importance of natural products in the management of BPH, at least as an add-on treatment, if not as a primary role.

Transfer learning based CEEMDAN-VMD secondary decomposition and multi-scale modeling for short-term electricity market price trend forecasting
Authors: Yi Zhang, Xueqiang Song, Changfeng Li, Zhongming Zhang, Jun Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This paper proposes a short-term electricity market price trend forecasting framework based on transfer learning, which centers on Complete Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition with Adaptive Noise (CEEMDAN)- Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) secondary decomposition and multi-scale modeling. The framework integrates Attention mechanism, multi-scale Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM), and is named as CEEMDAN-VMD-MultiScale-Att-CNN-BiLSTM (Attention-Convolutional Neural Network-Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory) to highlight its core innovative characteristics. Firstly, the CEEMDAN and VMD methods are employed to perform quadratic decomposition on the original electricity market price sequence, reducing the intricacy of forecasting; Then, a basic forecasting model is constructed that includes Attention layers, multi-scale CNN layers, and BiLSTM layers, and the model parameters are optimized through hierarchical transfer learning; Finally, the optimal parameter model serves to forecast the electricity market price sequences of each frequency, and the final price trend prediction value is reconstructed. The experimental observation results show that the multi-scale Att-CNN-BiLSTM algorithm achieves consistent performance improvements supported by statistical tests in the test dataset and actual scenario experiments, especially in predicting the jump points and peak points of electricity market prices with higher accuracy. The core innovation of this framework lies in the construction of a CEEMDAN-VMD quadratic decomposition strategy for non-stationary electricity price sequences and a multi-scale feature modeling mechanism, effectively overcoming the limitations of traditional models in reflecting the unique features of electricity price data.

Introduction

With the continuous advancement of China’s electricity market liberalization, electricity market prices have become the primary factors in the electricity trading market, closely related to supply and demand, electricity production, and people’s lives1. Lately, vigorously developing clean energy has become a crucial strategy to address resource depletion and ecological pollution, and grid-scale clean energy is being connected to the power grid. Due to the minimal marginal cost of clean energy, there has been a trend of decreasing clearing prices in the electricity market recently; Moreover, electricity cannot be stored in large quantities and must always maintain a balance between generation and consumption. The stochastic and unstable characteristics of clean energy result in strong fluctuations in daily clearing prices, thus showing obvious time-varying characteristics. Accordingly, it is urgent to construct a high-precision daily price trend prediction model.

In the current stage, there are two categories of methods for forecasting short-term electricity market prices. The first type is the time series method2, which utilizes the correlation of electricity market price time series and establishes an electricity market price time series model through existing samples to forecast electricity market prices outside the sample. Classic sequential data models include the Cumulative Auto Regressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model and the Generalized Auto Regressive Conditional Heteroskedastic (GARCH) model. The time series method has the characteristics of simple modeling and easy implementation, but this type of method is only suitable for simple electricity markets and cannot address nonlinear and multi-dimensional coupling relationships. The second type is neural network method3, commonly used methods include extreme learning machine, BP neural network, random forest, etc. The advantage of neural network method is that it can handle multivariate problems. It can not only find the implicit complex mapping mechanism between electricity market price series and electricity market price driving factors, but also reduce forecasting time and improve forecasting accuracy. However, traditional single neural network models such as Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are unable to handle large datasets, and are prone to gradient vanishing and exploding phenomena, making it increasingly difficult for forecasting performance to meet market demands of the electricity market. The input characteristics of the electricity market price data series and the price trend prediction model have a certain temporal correlation. If the forecasting model can learn this hidden relationship, it will improve the predictive performance of forecasting electricity market prices before the day. Early researchers used RNNs to forecast time series, but when dealing with long sequence data, RNNs had the issue of gradient vanishing. Long short-term memory (LSTM) network is good at discovering and utilizing the inherent rules of long time series data, and has advantages in forecasting accuracy. However, when the feature quantity is large, the effectiveness of LSTM will decrease due to overfitting, and the required time is long. Therefore, it cannot directly input time-series data of electricity market prices with a large number of features, and feature mining is required to be carried out on the input time-series data first. CNN can only capture local information from input temporal data and must be stacked to obtain global connections and enhance the field of view. Attention mechanisms can flexibly capture global and local connections, filling in the gaps of CNN.

Although significant progress has been made within the scope of price trend prediction through extensive studies, there are still some challenges and limitations, especially in the face of the non-stationary nature of electricity market price sequences, as well as the difficulty of forecasting jump and peak points. Traditional models often struggle to balance computational efficiency and forecasting accuracy. To this end, this article innovatively proposes a secondary decomposition multi-scale Att-CNN-BiLSTM short-term price trend prediction model in accordance with transfer learning, aiming to reduce the complexity of electricity market price sequences by introducing second-order mode decomposition technology, and combining multi-scale CNN, BiLSTM, and attention-based mechanism to successfully capture temporal features and key information in electricity market price sequences, thereby improving forecasting accuracy and robustness. Meanwhile, utilizing transfer learning to optimize model parameters further enhances the model adaptability and generalization capability, providing an innovative and effective approach for short-term price trend prediction. The essence of electricity price trend prediction is to make advance judgments on the direction and magnitude of electricity price operation, providing key trend prior information for numerical prediction: (1) through trend judgment, the occurrence period of electricity price jump points and peak points can be locked in, enhancing the model’s attention to key points; (2) Trend constraints can reduce prediction bias caused by non-stationary sequences, making the predicted electricity price values more in line with the actual changes in the electricity market. Therefore, building high-precision trend prediction models is the core link to improve the overall performance of short-term electricity price forecasting.

Trend forecasting refers to determining the direction, fluctuation trend, and inflection point position of future electricity price fluctuations; Point forecasting refers to predicting specific electricity prices for future periods. The core of this article is trend prediction, which can also support the improvement of point prediction accuracy. The method proposed in this article is not a simple combination of existing modules, but a customized quadratic decomposition hierarchical transfer prediction framework designed for the non-stationary characteristics of electricity prices. Among them, hierarchical transfer learning based on decomposed component frequencies is the first application in the field of electricity price prediction in this paper, which can effectively reduce prediction bias caused by differences in data distribution. Theoretical basis of this article: (1) Non stationary sequence decomposition theory, CEEMDAN can effectively handle nonlinear non-stationary sequences, VMD can significantly suppress mode mixing phenomenon, and the cascading combination of the two can satisfy the bias variance trade-off criterion. (2) Deep time series learning theory, CNN is used to extract local stationary features of data, BiLSTM is used to capture long-range dependencies of sequences, and attention mechanism focuses on key periods in the prediction process. The three work together to form a complete time series feature extraction system. (3) The domain adaptation theory of transfer learning ensures consistency in model generalization performance by minimizing the difference in data distribution between the source and target domains.

Related research

Current status of price trend prediction research

With regard to price trend prediction research, foreign countries started earlier and have accumulated rich experience and achievements. In contrast, due to the relatively lagging market-oriented reform of China’s power system, research on price trend prediction started relatively late. However, over the past few years, with the rapid development and wide deployment of advanced technologies including but not limited to artificial intelligence, Chinese scholars have made remarkable progress in research in this field. These developments not only serve as strong assistance for the healthy development of electricity market, but also facilitate Chinese wisdom and solutions to global price trend prediction research.

In terms of time, price trend prediction can generally be classified into two categories: short-term and medium to long-term forecasting. Short term price trend prediction can generally be divided into three types: time point price trend prediction, daily price trend prediction, and weekly price trend prediction4. Relatively speaking, mid-term and long-term price trend prediction mainly involves annual, monthly, or quarterly price trend prediction. When conducting mid-term and long-term price trend prediction, the data required often is required to meet the requirements of long term and more complex driving factors, so the credibility of the forecast is relatively low. This also leads to most scholars being more inclined to study short-term price trend prediction, while there is relatively less research on mid-term and long-term price trend prediction. From the perspective of economic practical value, short-term price trend prediction can achieve timely forecasting of electricity market prices to ensure power supply-demand balance and maintain the safe and stable operation of the electricity market. Therefore, compared with medium and long-term price trend prediction, it has more significant practical application value. In view of this, this article focuses on the German electricity market, which is similar to China, for the analysis and exploration of price trend prediction.

Common research methods

Currently, the research models for price trend prediction both domestically and internationally are mainly categorized into statistical forecasting models, data-driven models, and combination models.

Statistical forecasting models. The predominant statistical forecasting models used for price trend prediction are sequential data models, which mainly analyze the time series of historical electricity market price data in depth. These models can be roughly divided into several types, such as autoregression (AR), moving average (MA), autoregressive moving average (ARMA), and ARIMA. Due to the significant volatility of electricity market prices, they often exhibit non-stationary time series characteristics. To promote forecasting performance, sequential data models are often combined with other forecasting models. For example, reference5 used a GARCH model to model the electricity market price series, which improved the forecasting accuracy. Reference6 implemented many ARMA models with time-varying conditional autoregressive heteroscedasticity properties, significantly improving the forecasting performance of electricity market prices. Scholars have also deployed the ARMA-GARCH model to model and forecast the hourly electricity market price series of the PJM electricity market. The results demonstrate that the asymmetric GARCH model has more reliable forecastive performance. The significant advantage of sequential data models is that their forecasting performance reaches its optimal state when the processed time series data is relatively stable, and the demand for historical data is relatively small, resulting in high computational efficiency. However, sequential data models often fail to take full consideration of other driving factors besides electricity market prices during the forecasting process, which to some extent has an effect on the forecasting accuracy.Data-driven models. The predominant data-driven models in price trend prediction include extreme learning machines, support vector machines, and so on. For example, reference7 uses data-driven methods such as nearest neighbor search, fuzzy classification method, and association analysis, combined with machine learning-based algorithms such as support vector machine (SVM), to achieve deep mining of historical data and accurate forecasting of electricity market prices. Lately, neural network algorithms have also been implemented with regard to price trend prediction research, but their generalization ability is weak and they are prone to getting stuck in local extreme points. To mitigate these limitations, some scholars have introduced LSTM networks for price trend prediction. A global and local price trend prediction model is constructed based on RNN as described in reference8, and apply it to price trend prediction in the electricity market. However, RNNs often face the challenge of vanishing or exploding gradients when processing long sequence data. In contrast, LSTM networks, through their unique gating mechanism and cell state design, can more effectively preserve and capture long-range temporal correlations in time-series data. In recent years, new breakthroughs have been made in deep learning prediction technology for the energy field. For example, reference9 proposes a deep learning framework that integrates basic training and country specific training to address the problems of insufficient datasets and cross regional data distribution differences in photovoltaic power prediction. This framework can effectively utilize interconnected data from multiple countries to improve model generalization ability and significantly enhance prediction accuracy under non-stationary conditions. Reference10 proposes a novel residual based adaptive normalization layer to address the problem of cross regional data distribution offset in renewable energy prediction. This layer effectively utilizes data with different distributions but the same mechanism, supports the integration of multi country data resources in deep learning training, and significantly improves prediction accuracy.Combination model. Given the limitations and unsatisfactory results of a single forecasting model, researchers around the world have begun to explore combination forecasting models, taking advantage of the strengths of different models to promote the predictive performance of forecasting. As proposed in reference11, a new hybrid model is proposed to accurately forecast daily electricity market prices. The feature selection algorithm is employed to select the best data with high correlation, and the improved grey wolf and particle swarm optimization algorithms are employed to optimize and adjust the parameters of the support vector machine. Compared with other forecasting methods, its forecasting accuracy is about 91%. Reference12 combines autoregressive process with RNN, uses wavelet decomposition of electricity market price sequence, and uses ARFIMA-ANN model to forecast the irregular components generated. Finally, the integrated forecasting is performed. The experimental results suggest that the new method can provide better interval forecasting than the four benchmark models. Reference13 constructed a time sliding window, selecting climate data, peak and trough electricity market price data as inputs. CNN was first used to extract feature vectors as input data, and then LSTM network was used for short-term load forecasting. Reference14 introduces a hierarchical two-stage decomposition based on VMD technology combined with Ensemble Empirical Mode Decomposition (EEMD), which enhances the overall forecasting performance of the model.

Electricity market price data processing method based on quadratic decomposition

The quadratic mode decomposition technique is adopted to process the original electricity market price sequence, the non-stationary electricity market price sequence is decomposed into multiple Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) to reduce the complexity of price trend prediction. Firstly, the CEEMDAN method is employed to preliminarily decompose electricity market price data to reduce the complexity of electricity market price time-series data; Secondly, the VMD method is employed to perform secondary decomposition on the reconstructed high-frequency components, which further enhances the decomposition effect by further reducing residual noise in high-frequency components.

Hierarchical transfer learning

The hierarchical transfer learning proposed in this article belongs to the formal domain adaptive transfer method, and the relevant definitions are as follows: (1) The source domain is composed of historical electricity price time series data and multi scenario public datasets, with a corresponding data distribution of P, covering the rich fluctuation characteristics and peak change patterns of electricity price sequences; (2) The target domain, electricity price sample data for the area or period to be predicted, corresponding to a data distribution of Q, has the characteristic of relatively limited sample size; (3) Migration target, by minimizing the distribution difference between the source domain distribution P and the target domain distribution Q, effectively improves the model’s cross domain generalization ability. If the model is divided into L layers, each corresponding to a frequency feature component, the optimization objective of hierarchical transfer learning can be defined as:14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$f={\hbox{min} _\theta }{\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{pred}}}}(\theta )+\lambda \cdot {\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{dist}}}}(P,Q)$$\end{document}

Where, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{pred}}}}$$\end{document} is the prediction loss (mean square error); \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\mathcal{L}_{{\mathrm{dist}}}}$$\end{document} is the loss of domain distribution differences (mean difference and variance difference); λ is the adaptive balance coefficient.

The hierarchical rule matches the shallow to deep layers of the model from high to low frequency components decomposed by CEEMDAN-VMD, enabling each layer to learn corresponding frequency domain features and achieve frequency domain aware hierarchical transfer. The specific expression is as follows: (1) The low-frequency trend component matching model is deep and learns stable temporal dependencies; (2) In the middle layer of the intermediate frequency periodic component matching model, learn the daily/weekly patterns; (3) The high-frequency fluctuation component matching model is shallow and learns features of abrupt changes and spikes. (4) Layering by frequency can make migration more targeted and significantly improve the stability of target domain prediction.

Transfer learning transfers knowledge from one or more source domains to another target domain21, using known knowledge from a certain domain to solve problems in different but related domains. This method does not require training the target domain model from scratch, and leverages knowledge from source domains with related data distributions, so training the target domain does not need to start from scratch, which speeds up the training process. The enhanced hierarchical transfer learning process in this article is summarized as follows.

Step 1: Source domain data partitioning. The source domain data are grouped into L layers according to decomposed component frequencies from high to low, with L depending on the count of layers in the base model. In this article, L = 8.

Step 2: Hierarchical Transfer Learning. Input the sorted source domain data into different levels of the multi-scale Att-CNN-BiLSTM model for hierarchical transfer learning. Each set of data is sequentially input into different layers of the model for training, and an appropriate freezing strategy is adopted to obtain the final pre training weights of the target domain load.

Step 3: Model fine-tuning. Fixed frozen layer parameters and fine-tuned non frozen layer parameters using target domain data.

After sorting the source domain data according to the degree of goodness of fit, it is divided into L groups of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\left\{ {{X_1},{X_2}, \cdots ,{X_L}} \right\}$$\end{document} data according to different intervals. First, use the data with low goodness of fit \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X_1}$$\end{document} to train shallow \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L_1}$$\end{document}, freeze the rest of the layers, retain the weights trained in that layer, and then freeze the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L_1}$$\end{document} layer; \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${X_2}$$\end{document} inputs into the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L_2}$$\end{document} layer of the model for training, loads the weights of the previous layer, and determines whether the performance enhances on the validation set of the target domain; Train the layer using the upper layer data. If the effect still does not improve, train the next layer using the data without retaining the weights from the previous layer; And so on, until the training of the partitioned source domain data is completed, each layer obtains the optimal forecasting performance.

After the final layer of transfer learning is completed, all layers except for the final fully connected layer are frozen, and the weights of the fully connected layers are initialized. Given a smaller learning rate, only some network parameters need to be trained and modified, without training the entire network. Hierarchical transfer learning parameter setting: The number of layers in the model is L = 8, and the layered training adopts a training strategy of freezing layer by layer; The learning rate during the fine-tuning phase of the target domain is set to 10− 4, and the adaptation threshold between the source and target domain data distributions is set to 0.05 to ensure the stability and effectiveness of the transfer learning process.

Experimental analysis

Example setting

All data must undergo preprocessing before decomposition and training, including missing value interpolation, outlier removal, and minimum maximum normalization. After strict screening and standardization of the model input features, four core input features were finally determined, with a total of 12 key feature variables: (1) time features, including hour, week, month, season, and holiday identification. (2) The characteristics of the electricity price sequence include the current time and the historical clearing electricity prices of the previous T steps. (3) Load characteristics, including system predicted load, actual load, and load difference between adjacent periods. (4) External operating characteristics, including wind power output, photovoltaic output, and interconnection line exchange power. All datasets are uniformly sampled at hourly resolution, and the next electricity price trend is predicted based on the feature sequence of the past 24 h.

The example uses the daily electricity market price and system forecasted load data of the PJM market in the United States from January 2, 2017 to December 31, 2024 as the dataset22, which includes 72,115 time periods of electricity market price and load data. To avoid accidental errors caused by seasonal deviations or extreme conditions, the data used to measure model error in this example is 8775 electricity market price data for the whole year of 2024, that is, the electricity market price series in 2024 is taken as the test set, and the electricity market price series from 2017 to 2023 is taken as the training set. The comparative forecasting algorithms used include time series Auto Regressive Integrated Moving Average with Exogenous Variables (ARIMAX), BP neural network (BPNN), and traditional BiLSTM network, and simulation experiments were conducted on all neural network methods, divided into two categories: quadratic decomposition and non-quadratic decomposition. All experiments were implemented using Python 3.6 and Tensor Flow library, with a computer configuration of Intel Core CPU i7-1270P 3.5 GHz.

This study fixed all experimental random seeds at 42 and used Glorot uniform initialization method for neural network weights. The preprocessing process for the dataset is as follows: (1) Resample the raw data of PJM, NSW, and GEFCom2019 into hourly level time-series data; (2) Linear interpolation is used to fill in missing data, and abnormal samples are identified and removed based on the 3 σ criterion; (3) Implement minimum maximum normalization on numerical characteristics such as electricity price, load, and new energy output, and map the data to the [0,1] interval; (4) Perform unique hot encoding on time attributes and construct a five dimensional time feature system that includes hours, weeks, months, seasons, and holidays.

Real scenario price trend prediction experiment

Using real data related to Chinese Xinjiang power as the test dataset, the BiLSTM neural network is set to 4 layers, and the input data is extended from 2880 time periods to 23,328 time periods from January to August (i.e. 243 days × 96 time periods). The impact of characteristic factors (wind power output, photovoltaic output, interconnection line transmission, and load) on electricity market prices is also considered for each time period. Through simulation, the data source is based on 2880 time periods (short period historical data) and 23,328 time periods (long period historical data), respectively, to train learning models and forecast the daily electricity market price curve. The forecasting results are illustrated in Fig. 6(a) and (b).

From the results in Fig. 6(a) and (b), it can be seen that the forecasting accuracy is 87.63% when only using the data source from August (short period historical data), which meets the needs of electricity trading business; The significant increase in data sources actually reduces the predictive performance of forecastings, indicating a greater correlation between current spot electricity market prices and recent driving factors. At the same time, when the electricity market price is 14.55 USD/(MW·h) or above, the trained model is stable and the forecasted electricity market price matches the actual value more closely. When the electricity market price is at a lower value, the deviation between the forecasted and actual electricity market prices is slightly larger. The reason is that the electricity market price is affected by factors that are difficult to quantify, such as recent market sentiment and power grid operation restrictions. This can be studied and introduced in the subsequent stage.

From the comparison of the prediction curves in Fig. 6 (a) and (b), it can be clearly observed that the model proposed in this paper has certain deviations in the prediction fitting of outliers/jump points in electricity prices, especially at extremely low points where electricity prices are much lower than the conventional range. Mainly caused by three factors: (1) In the Xinjiang electricity spot market, the frequency of extreme low value anomalies in electricity prices is low and the sample size is small, making it difficult for the model to fully learn the fluctuation patterns of such niche samples during training. (2) Abnormal electricity prices are often influenced by unstructured and difficult to quantify factors such as market sentiment, temporary grid dispatch constraints, and sudden fluctuations in new energy output. Existing models only include quantifiable features such as load, wind and solar output, and interconnection line power, and do not cover such implicit disturbances. (3) Although CEEMDAN and VMD quadratic decomposition can smooth the mainstream electricity price sequence, there is still a small amount of residual high-frequency noise accompanied by extreme outliers.

At the same time, it can be observed that the model has a good fitting effect on abnormal peak points in the high electricity price range (24.55 USD/(MW · h) and above), verifying the predictive effectiveness of the model at key market decision points. Subsequently, the predictive ability of the model for various types of outliers can be further improved through three methods: outlier weighted training, difficult to quantify factor feature engineering, and decomposition algorithm noise filtering optimization.

Conclusion

This article proposes a multi-scale Att-CNN-BiLSTM short-term price trend prediction model based on transfer learning, which addresses the shortcomings of traditional price trend prediction models in terms of computational efficiency and accuracy in forecasting electricity market price jump and peak points. The original electricity market price sequence is decomposed using CEEMDAN and VMD methods, effectively reducing the complexity of the electricity market price sequence. Meanwhile, by combining multi-scale CNN, BiLSTM, and attention mechanisms, a multi-scale Att-CNN-BiLSTM model is proposed, which can capture temporal features and key information in electricity market price sequences, enhance feature mining and forecasting capabilities, and use transfer learning to optimize model parameters, further improving the adaptability and generalization ability of the model. The model proposed in this article first achieves accurate prediction of electricity price trends. By distinguishing the rising and falling trends, fluctuation trends, and inflection points of electricity prices, it can provide effective trend constraints for subsequent electricity price numerical predictions, reducing prediction bias caused by extreme fluctuations and sudden changes.

The main contribution of this article: (1) Propose a quadratic decomposition framework that combines CEEMDAN and VMD to process non-stationary electricity market price sequences, effectively reducing the complexity of raw data. (2) Build a multi-scale Att-CNN-BiLSTM prediction model that integrates multi-scale CNN for multi-scale feature extraction, BiLSTM for time series correlation capture, and attention mechanism for key feature weighting, improves prediction accuracy steadily with statistical significance. (3) Introducing hierarchical transfer learning to optimize model parameters has improved the adaptability, generalization ability, and training efficiency of the model. (4) The proposed model has been validated on the PJM, NSW, GEFCom2019, and Xinjiang electricity market datasets, and can provide effective support for short-term electricity market price trend prediction.

Although the model proposed in this article has made significant progress with regard to price trend prediction, there are still some limitations. Future research: (1) Further explore how to optimize the quadratic decomposition process to more effectively handle the non-stationarity and noise problems in electricity market price sequences. (2) It is possible to consider incorporating more external factors, such as weather conditions, policy changes, etc., into the forecasting model to promote the comprehensiveness and performance of the forecast. (3) With the continuous development of deep learning technology, how to combine the latest neural network structures and algorithms to further enhance the effectiveness of price trend prediction models is also a direction worthy of in-depth research.

In addition, research and application of hybrid deep learning models continue to advance in the fields of time series prediction and new energy power prediction, resulting in continuous improvement of prediction performance. For example, (1) MCKAN (Multi scale Convolutional Kolmogorov Arnold Network)26 combines an efficient additive attention mechanism with an improved Lemming optimization algorithm, which can accurately capture high-dimensional spatiotemporal features at multiple abstract levels and demonstrate excellent prediction accuracy in multi-step ultra short term wind and solar power prediction tasks; (2) The xLSTM-TCCNN (Time-series Cascade Convolutional Neural Network) hybrid model integrates numerical weather forecast (NWP)27 data with Time2Vec temporal encoding technology, utilizing the powerful temporal correlation capture capability of the xLSTM network to effectively address the intermittent and strong nonlinear characteristics in wind and solar power generation processes; (3) The BiTCN-BiGRU (Bidirectional Temporal Convolutional Networks with Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units)28 hybrid model is designed with a series parallel hybrid architecture based on the concept of periodic sensing. It adopts a parallel structure for photovoltaic power prediction with significant periodic characteristics, greatly improving the accuracy and generalization ability of power prediction in hybrid energy systems. The above model, through the core design of multi-scale feature extraction, temporal structure enhancement, and physical prior knowledge fusion, not only provides feasible technical solutions and important references for non-stationary temporal prediction problems, but also provides ideological support for the subsequent improvement and enhancement of this research.

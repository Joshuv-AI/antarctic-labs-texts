Optimal tracking consensus for swarm systems with leader-following switching topologies
Authors: Qian Zhang, Cheng Wang, Miao Zhao, Jianxiang Xi, Yuanshi Zheng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
For swarm systems with leader-following switching topologies, optimal tracking consensus achievement problems are investigated. First, by these errors of the cooperative states among neighboring agents, a new tracking strategy with an optimization index is proposed to realize the optimal tracking consensus. The optimization index contains a tracking consensus performance term and a control energy consumption term. Then, by introducing a local interaction matrix and a dual nonsingular transformation, sufficient conditions for optimal tracking consensus are proposed. A constraint relationship between topology switching and control parameters of the optimal tracking strategy is determined. Furthermore, an explicit expression of the minimum value of the optimization index is given. The minimum value is completely determined by initial conditions and is independent of the operation process of the whole system and switching movements. Finally, a numerical simulation with seven agents is implemented to demonstrate the effectiveness of main results.

Introduction

Cooperative control of swarm systems has received great interest in the past few decades, which contains some significant theoretical results, such as flocking1–3, synchronization4,5, consensus6,7, and formation control8,9, etc. Consensus control for swarm systems, including the leaderless consensus and leader-following consensus, is one of the most significant problems for cooperative control. Consensus control requires and drives some concerned states of all agents in swarm systems to achieve consensus or synchronization. Moreover, in some scenarios, it is required for the following agents to track the state of a leading agent while achieving and maintaining consensus. In this case, the leader-following consensus is also called the tracking consensus, which plays a crucial role in consensus control and has attracted a lot of attentions in most fields, as shown in10–13.

In practical operating environments, the interaction topology among agents usually is dynamically changed rather than remaining fixed. This may be caused by communication link failures, node mobility and signal interference. Especially, swarm systems may adopt active reconfiguration scheduling according to different tasks. This dynamic switching characteristic of the interaction topology brings in significant challenges to the consensus performance. The topology switching may destroy the effectiveness of existing control protocols and lead to consensus failure, performance degradation and system instability. Consequently, consensus control under switching topologies has drawn the attention of many scholars and a series of research results have been obtained as shown in14–18. Wei et al.14 constructed dynamic event-triggered consensus protocols with switching control for unmanned aerial vehicle formation with disturbances. In15, the stochastic Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation with unknown system dynamics was used to realize adaptive optimal consensus control by the integral reinforcement learning algorithm and the data-based distributed fuzzy approach. In16,17, the event-triggered leader-following consensus problem for discrete-time swarm systems under dynamic switching topologies was investigated and an event-triggered scheme was proposed to significantly reduce resource consumption. Fan et al.18 investigated the optimal topology switching problem in swarm systems and focused on minimizing a quadratic cost function by active topology switching mechanisms. However, all approaches in14–18 are limited to achieve basic consensus without considering the tracking performance and the energy consumption. In real-world applications, tracking consensus errors and energy consumption in a swarm system are typically subjected to upper bound constraints.

To realize the compromised design between the consensus performance and the energy consumption, the guaranteed-cost consensus strategy was investigated. In19, a robust guaranteed-cost control approach for swarm systems with parametric uncertainties was proposed, which can steer swarm systems to achieve consensus with an upper bound of a guaranteed cost value. Ning et al.20 addressed the guaranteed-cost impulsive consensus problem of uncertain swarm systems with the multiplex networks, where it can be ensured that the actual control cost is not higher than an upper bound of the guaranteed-cost value. In21, an event-triggered guaranteed-cost consensus problem was investigated for swarm systems with unmeasurable states, where the guaranteed-cost function was constructed by the control input and the consensus error. For the guaranteed-cost consensus, an upper limit of the guaranteed-cost value for the whole swarm systems can be determined. However, it should be pointed out that this upper limit is usually much larger than the actual cost value, which means that the guaranteed-cost consensus strategy can only achieve the suboptimal consensus.

Another optimization method is the minimum-energy consensus control strategy, which focuses on minimizing the energy consumption of swarm systems. In22 and23, minimum-energy consensus for swarm systems was addressed, where the proposed control strategies can guarantee that the energy consumption is minimum in the sense of the linear matrix inequality. The minimum-energy consensus control problem in mobile sensor networks was addressed in24, where a suboptimal solution was proposed to guarantee the minimum energy cost of the mobility and communication for sensors. By exploiting a network approximation approach, Chen et al.25 focused on minimizing the global energy consumption for swarm systems to achieve consensus with undirected topologies, where an explicit expression for the energy consumption and the consensus speed was derived. It should be pointed out that the guaranteed-cost consensus control approach can give an upper limit of the guaranteed-cost value for the whole swarm systems, but this value may not be optimal. The minimum-energy consensus control approach can only make the energy consumption minimum in the sense of the linear matrix inequality, and the consensus performance of swarm systems is not considered. To the best of our knowledge, optimal tracking consensus for swarm systems with both the energy constraint and the consensus performance constraint is still open.

This paper deals with optimal tracking consensus for swarm systems with leader-following switching topologies. A tracking strategy with an optimization index is given, where the tracking consensus performance term and the control energy consumption term are involved simultaneously. Furthermore, a sufficient condition for optimal tracking consensus is proposed by designing a proper Lyapunov function with a specific structure. Moreover, the minimum value of the optimization index is determined, which is associated with the local interaction matrix and its square. Compared with closely related works, this paper has three important features. The first one is that an optimal consensus control method is proposed. In20 and21, the guaranteed-cost consensus approach was proposed, which can only realize a suboptimal consensus control. The second one is that both the consensus performance and the energy consumption are considered. In22–25, the minimum-energy consensus approach can only make the energy consumption minimum. The third one is that the design approach of control gains of the tracking strategy is analytic. In20–25, the linear matrix inequality approach is not analytic.

The paper is organized as follows. “Problem description for optimal tracking consensus with switching topologies” formulates the problem of optimal tracking consensus for swarm systems with leader-following switching topologies. “Main results for optimal tracking consensus with switching topologies” presents sufficient conditions for optimal tracking consensus design. “Numerical simulations” demonstrates the theoretical results through numerical simulations. Finally, “Conclusions” summarizes the main conclusions.

Problem description for optimal tracking consensus with switching topologies

For a swarm system with \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$M+1$$\end{document} agents, where a leading agent plays the role of the commander and M following agents play the role of executors, agents interact with each other through the wireless communication. To finish some tasks in a cooperative manner, the specific cooperative information is required to realize consensus. The leading agent can transmit its cooperative information to specific following agents, but does not receive the cooperative information of any following agents. Furthermore, interactions of cooperative information among following agents are distributed and bidirectional. In this case, two connected following agents interact their cooperative information with each other. When there exists the fault of the communication equipment or the interference from the hostile forces, some communication links cannot be connected and the communication structure among agents may be changed, which usually is modeled as switching topologies. It can be found that a communication topology is usually maintained during some time length.

This section shows a dual nonsingular transformation approach to convert a tracking consensus problem into a simultaneous stabilization one. Remark 1

For a leader-following swarm system with switching topologies, when optimal tracking consensus is achieved, all agents may move continuously as a whole, but the relative motions among agents are stable. Especially, it can be found that the tracking consensus performance term contains the component square of the weighted sum of the errors of the cooperative states and the weighted sum of the component square of the errors of the cooperative states. Remark 2

Theorem 1 shows that the infimum of the minimum connected degree of switching topologies is critically important for optimal consensus tracking achievement with switching topologies, which establishes a constraint relationship between topology switching and control parameters of the optimal tracking strategy. To obtain this conclusion, the asymmetric property of switching topologies is dealt with by constructing the local interaction matrix on the basis of the Laplacian matrix among following agents and a diagonal matrix with diagonal components being edge weights from the leading agent to following agents. In this case, by a dual nonsingular transformation, the optimal tracking consensus problem is converted into a simultaneous stabilization one associated with the eigenvalues of the local interaction matrix, which can be constrained by the minimum connected degree. Furthermore, the local interaction matrix is also applied to analyze the optimal property, where it is shown that the tracking consensus performance index is associated with the local interaction matrix and its square, but the energy index is only associated with its square.

Remark 4

In the proof of Theorem 1, it is shown that the value of the Lyapunov function at time zero can be used to bound the optimal value of the optimization index. Because this Lyapunov function is associated with nonzero eigenvalues of the Laplacian matrix with switching topologies and position-similar and velocity-similar cooperative components after a dual nonsingular transformation, it is difficult to determine the optimal value and the impacts of topology switching explicitly. By introducing the local interaction matrix, Theorem 2 gives an explicit expression of the optimal value of the optimization index, which is initial states of position-similar and velocity-similar cooperative components of all agents. Especially, it is revealed that switching movements do not impact this optimal value, which is only associated with the local interaction matrix at time zero. In this case, we can find that if a leader-following swarm system can achieve optimal tracking consensus, then the optimal value of the optimization index is completely determined by initial conditions and is independent of the operation process of the whole system. This is critically important for practical applications of swarm systems with switching topologies.

Conclusions

Optimal tracking consensus for a leader-following swarm system with switching topologies was addressed. By cooperative state errors among neighboring agents, a tracking strategy was given to realize the optimal tracking consensus, where an optimization index was introduced. The optimization index contains a tracking consensus performance term and a control energy consumption term. By constructing a local interaction matrix and two nonsingular transformation matrices, a design approach of two gains of this tracking strategy was proposed. The gain design requires that the minimum connected degree of switching topologies should be larger than a specific value. Moreover, an explicit expression of the minimum value of the optimization index was presented, which is associated with the initial states of the leading agent and all following agents. Especially, it was shown that topology switching is critically important for a swarm system to achieve optimal tracking consensus, but it does not impact the minimum value of the optimization index. Moreover, if the communication topology does not contain a spanning tree, then our approach is no longer valid and some new method is required to be developed. The future research will focus on the optimal tracking consensus for swarm systems with unconnected switching topologies or jointly connected switching topologies.

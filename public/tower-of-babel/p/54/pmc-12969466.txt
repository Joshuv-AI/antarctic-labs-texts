Exploratory analysis of HER2-positive/CLDN18.2-positive metastatic gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer toward future therapeutic development
Authors: D. Okemoto, I. Nakayama, K. Shitara
Published: 2026

We appreciate the thoughtful comments by Yoshimura regarding the methodological limitations of our retrospective single-arm analysis evaluating the impact of claudin 18.2 (CLDN18.2) expression on the clinical outcomes of trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) in patients with HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer.

As appropriately pointed out, our study was a single-institution, retrospective exploratory analysis without a comparative control arm. In this context, the possibility that the observed differences reflect prognostic associations rather than treatment-specific effects cannot be fully excluded. We fully agree that causal inference regarding attenuation of T-DXd efficacy requires confirmation in larger, prospective comparative studies.

However, several biological and clinical considerations support the interpretation that CLDN18.2 may act as a treatment-interacting biomarker rather than a purely prognostic factor.1 CLDN18.2 is not directly involved in tumor cell proliferation or survival signaling pathways, in contrast to receptor tyrosine kinases such as HER2. Consistently, prior studies have demonstrated that CLDN18.2 expression does not influence clinical outcomes in patients treated with cytotoxic chemotherapy, chemotherapy plus immune checkpoint inhibitors, or immunotherapy alone.2, 3, 4 These observations were also replicated in our cohort during first-line trastuzumab-based chemotherapy, where CLDN18.2 status showed no statistically significant prognostic impact. Against this background, the differential outcomes observed specifically in the T-DXd setting suggest a potential interaction with HER2-directed antibody–drug conjugate therapy rather than reflecting intrinsic tumor aggressiveness alone.

With regard to CLDN18.2 cut-off selection, the primary threshold of moderate-to-strong membranous staining in ≥75% of tumor cells was predefined based on the established criteria used in pivotal zolbetuximab trials.5,6 Additional exploratory analyses using lower cut-offs were conducted in light of the rapidly evolving therapeutic landscape, where CLDN18.2-targeted antibody–drug conjugates and bispecific antibodies have demonstrated antitumor activity even in tumors with lower levels of expression.7 We therefore intended these analyses as supportive, hypothesis-generating data to capture broader expression trends relevant to ongoing clinical development, rather than as confirmatory statistical evidence.

We also acknowledge the heterogeneity in the timing of CLDN18.2 assessment relative to prior anti-HER2 therapies as an inherent limitation of biomarker analyses using biopsy specimens in advanced gastric cancer. This challenge is widely recognized in translational oncology research and was explicitly discussed in our manuscript.

Importantly, the primary objective of our study was not to provide definitive statistical proof of treatment effect modification, but to highlight a previously unexplored biological interaction between HER2 and CLDN18.2 expression in the context of HER2-directed therapy. Since the ToGA trial, HER2-positive gastric cancer has been recognized as a distinct therapeutic subset.8 Even with the emergence of CLDN18.2-positive disease as a new molecular subgroup following the development of zolbetuximab, current indications remain restricted to HER2-negative tumors, preserving the clinical independence of HER2-driven disease.5,6 Although tumors co-expressing HER2 and CLDN18.2 represent a rare population, our findings raise the clinically relevant hypothesis that CLDN18.2 expression may remain an important biomarker even within HER2-positive disease.9

We hope that this work stimulates further translational research and prospective clinical studies exploring dual-targeted strategies, including bispecific antibodies and combination approaches against HER2 and CLDN18.2. In this regard, our study aims to provide biological rationale and to catalyze future therapeutic development rather than to establish definitive clinical practice changes.

We sincerely thank Yoshimura for the constructive critique and fully agree that external validation in larger prospective cohorts is essential.

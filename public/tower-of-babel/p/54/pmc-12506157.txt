Evaluation of an intervention to increase topical sunscreen use in German outdoor workers: study protocol for the non-randomised controlled intervention study ProtExSun
Authors: Anja Dick, Marc Rocholl, Michaela Ludewig, Christoph Skudlik, Ivone Jakaša, Sanja Kezic, Florentine de Boer, Henk F van der Molen, Swen Malte John
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionSquamous cell carcinoma and multiple actinic keratoses caused by solar ultraviolet radiation (UVR) are among the most frequently recognised occupational diseases in Germany. Employees who regularly work outdoors, for example, in the construction industry, agriculture, forestry and gardening, are at a higher risk of developing occupational skin cancer. However, sun-safety behaviour in outdoor workers is currently insufficient. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the effectiveness of an intervention to increase sunscreen use among outdoor workers.Methods and analysisIn this non-randomised, controlled intervention study, 234 outdoor workers from different companies in industries with outdoor working activities based in Germany will be included. The study population, aged 18 years and above, has to be intensively exposed to solar UVR of regularly 1 hour or more per day. The intervention group will receive a sunscreen package as well as health education. The control group follows the practice in their companies (‘treatment-as-usual’). At the beginning of the study, after 3 months and at the end of the study (after 6 months), both groups filled in different questionnaires. In addition, stratum corneum (SC) samples will be collected at the beginning and after 3 months. The primary outcome—increase in the frequency of sunscreen use during work and in leisure time—will be assessed from data on self-reported sunscreen use. The secondary outcomes include sun protection behaviour, knowledge about sun protection and skin cancer, and acceptance of the provided sunscreens. Further secondary outcomes include internal UV dose and UV-related immune response, determined by the levels of SC biomarkers. Data will be analysed using both descriptive and inferential methods.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2013) and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Osnabrück University, Germany (reference Ethik-37/2024). Study results will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications and conference presentations.Trial registration numberDRKS00035178.Keywords: occupational, skin cancer, sun protection, sunscreen, outdoor worker

Introduction

Occupational solar ultraviolet radiation (UVR) exposure is a major contributor to the overall lifetime dose of UVR and thus to the increasing incidence of skin cancer. Non-melanoma skin cancer, which should more precisely be described as keratinocyte carcinoma (KC), manifesting as actinic keratosis (AK), intra-epidermal cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (SCC), invasive SCC and/or basal cell carcinoma, is less likely to get public attention compared with malignant melanoma.1 2 SCC caused by solar UVR is one of the most common cancers in fair-skinned populations worldwide, as has been shown in numerous studies.3,5 SCC and the early form of SCC, namely AK, are among the most frequently recognised occupational diseases in Germany.6 Outdoor workers who regularly spend 1 hour or more per day working outdoors are at higher risk of developing occupational skin cancer.7,12 Trakatelli et al have shown that employees who work outdoors are generally exposed to higher levels of UVR, while using less sunscreen and having lower health literacy.10 Those at particular risk include employees in the construction industry, agriculture, forestry and gardening.13 Especially in long-exposed outdoor workers, KC is a highly chronic disease.2

In Germany, the employer is legally bound to implement safety measures to protect employees from work-related injuries or illnesses as part of a comprehensive risk assessment. Protective measures have to be prioritised according to the so-called STOP or—for sun safety measures—TOP principle (substitution, technical measures, organisational measures and personal protective measures). When working outdoors, substitution of solar UVR exposure is not feasible; therefore, technical and organisational measures are primarily applied. Once these measures have been exhausted, personal protective measures must follow.14 Personal protective measures include UV protective clothing as well as the use of topical sunscreen. For those parts of the body that cannot be protected by UV protective clothing (eg, the face), topical sunscreen must strictly be used to protect the skin against solar UVR. Unfortunately, these parts of the body are most likely to be affected by occupational skin cancer, which shows the insufficient use of sunscreen and therefore the need for improved sunscreen use in outdoor workers.3 Especially in rural regions, there is a low level of sun protection use and therefore a great need for action with regard to the prevention of skin cancer.15

Studies have demonstrated that, in practice, individuals frequently fail to apply sunscreen correctly or in sufficient quantity.16,18 However, the amount of sunscreen applied to the skin is crucial for the effectiveness of the sunscreen and thus for the protection against solar UVR.2 In summary, not only does the use of sunscreen in general need to be increased, but also the amount of sunscreen used. Besides an increase in knowledge among outdoor workers on the benefits of sunscreens, increased satisfaction with sunscreens can contribute to better use. In the field of occupational sunscreens, secondary performance attributes must consider real-life working conditions of outdoor workplaces. These include, in particular, the bio-stability on the skin, eye irritation (burning), absorption time, grip and subjective skin feeling, compatibility with textiles, dust and dirt absorption, as well as the remaining of white product residues on the skin. Even though the German Social Accident Insurance (DGUV) has recently introduced a testing standard, called ‘Principles for testing and certification basic requirements for sunscreen when used in professional areas’ (GS-PS-19), these aforementioned secondary performance attributes of occupational sunscreens are presently rarely considered by the manufacturing companies.19,21

Hence, this study aims to evaluate a preventive strategy to increase the frequency of sunscreen use in the occupational as well as private setting and thus improve general sun protection behaviour among outdoor workers. The private setting encompasses non-working settings like weekends, after work, days off and holidays. The preventive strategy includes the provision of different topical sunscreens as well as health education.

Methods and analysis

Design and setting

This is a non-randomised controlled intervention study of German outdoor workers. The duration of the observation period will be 6 months, from April to September 2025. Data collection will consist of questionnaires as well as biochemical assessments. Data will be collected at the beginning of the study (= T0), after 3 months (= T1) and after 6 months, at the end of the study (= T2), during visits of the researchers at the participating companies. The effectiveness of the intervention will be assessed from self-reported data on sunscreen use by one of four questionnaires (primary outcome). The self-reported data from the other questionnaires, as well as the changes in the levels of biomarkers of the internal UV dose will be assessed for secondary outcomes. This study protocol follows the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trial (SPIRIT 2013) (see online supplemental file 3).22

Participants and recruitment

In this study, outdoor workers aged 18 years and above (ie, only persons of legal age and capable of giving consent) will be included. The outdoor workers to be included have to be intensively exposed to solar UVR for at least 1 hour per day, as defined in the occupational health rule no. 13.3 from the Federal Institute of Occupational Safety and Health (AMR 13.3).23 Outdoor workers less than 18 years of age will be excluded from this study. Furthermore, outdoor workers with a known or rather self-reported allergy to any of the ingredients of the study products (see table 1) or skin diseases that could influence the study results (eg, psoriasis vulgaris) will be excluded. Inclusion criteria will first be verified with the employer and subsequently confirmed by the employee. In the event of incompatibilities with one or more ingredients of the study products during the study period, the participants will be withdrawn from the study. If the job is terminated during the study period, the respective person will be excluded from the study.

Different companies from industries with outdoor workers, for example, gardening and landscaping or companies in the construction industry based in Germany will be recruited for this study. In order to avoid cross-contamination, the participants will be assigned to only one group per company, either the intervention or control group. After baseline evaluation at T0, the participants in the intervention group will receive a sunscreen starter package consisting of four sun cream tubes, two sun spray bottles and one sun lipstick. The products were chosen—in the sense of a pars pro toto—to represent adequate sunscreen products for occupational and private use. To ensure that the participants of the intervention group are adequately supplied with sunscreen, they will receive another sunscreen package with the same content after 3 months. Normally, the content of the two sunscreen packages should be sufficient for the study period. However, the participant can request additional products ad libitum at any time during the study period. The ingredients of the study products, which shall be used by the participants in the occupational as well as the private setting, are shown in table 1. The study products are commercially available cosmetic products regulated by and complying with the European Regulation EC no. 1223/2009 on Cosmetics Products. In addition to the sunscreen package, at T0, the participants will receive health education in oral and written form at their workplace by the researchers. The participants in the control group will receive no additional intervention in their companies (‘treatment-as-usual’). The participants of both groups will be asked to fill in questionnaires at T0, T1 and T2. To underline the self-reported data on the frequency of the occupational as well as the private use of topical sunscreens and the sun protection behaviour, Stratum corneum (SC) samples will be collected by using adhesive tape strips at T0 and T1. Afterwards, the SC samples will be analysed for internal UV-dose biomarkers as well as for immune biomarkers.

Interventions

This study employs a controlled intervention designed to promote increased sunscreen use among German outdoor workers. The health educational intervention is designed to enhance both motivation and competence regarding sun protection. It is based on the Health Action Process Approach (HAPA). According to the HAPA model, health behaviour is influenced by both the intention to act and the capacity to implement protective measures. Consequently, the intervention addresses both motivational and volitional determinants of health behaviour.24 25

The intervention consists of a comprehensive educational session and practical support to establish and maintain sun protection behaviour. Participants will receive thorough information on the risks associated with occupational UVR exposure and the importance of sunscreen use. Key educational components include the fundamentals of UVR, its adverse effects, UV protection, as well as detailed instructions on the correct application of sunscreen—especially the appropriate amount, application techniques and the frequency of reapplication. To facilitate the implementation of sunscreen use, each participant will be provided with a sunscreen package containing various products (cream, spray, lipstick) in addition to a clear and easy-to-understand written guide. This guide will offer step-by-step instructions for the proper application of the provided products and clear guidance on reapplication intervals. The intervention is designed to engage both individuals who have not previously considered sunscreen use by raising awareness and motivating them to adopt protective behaviours, and those already intending to use sunscreen, by providing them with the necessary resources to successfully implement and sustain their behaviour over time.

The health education will be delivered in group or individual sessions, depending on the number of participants at each company, and will last approximately 5–10 min. All information will also be provided in writing via flyers. To ensure comprehension among all participants, the educational materials have been prepared using clear and accessible language.

Questionnaires

Four different questionnaires will be used in this study. The frequency of the occupational and private use of topical sunscreen will be asked in a questionnaire modified according to Symanzik et al, also including questions about work status and UV-related job characteristics.26 The scale regarding the questions about the frequency of use was modified according to questionnaires of Peters et al and Keurentjes et al in order to enable a better outcome comparison for the primary outcome.27 28 Rönsch et al developed and validated two different questionnaires for behaviour and knowledge about sun protection in outdoor workers (OccuSun Safe and Skin Cancer and Sun Knowledge (SCSK)). The OccuSun Safe questionnaire will be used to evaluate the sun protection behaviour. The questionnaire has been extended by questions on the private and personal sun protection behaviour. To evaluate the knowledge about sun protection and skin cancer, SCSK scale will be used.29 Additionally, in the intervention group, questions about satisfaction with the provided sunscreen will be asked at T2 using a user acceptance questionnaire, which was adapted to sunscreen use according to Symanzik et al.30

Tape stripping procedure

At T0 and T1, SC samples will be collected in both groups by using adhesive tape strips with a minimally invasive, non-painful method, which is extensively used in experimental studies.31 32 Adhesive tape discs with a diameter of 14 mm (D-SQUAME Skin Sampling Discs; CUDERM CORPORATION, Dallas, TX, USA) will be attached to the skin on UV exposed skin sites, for example, the cheek and neck. Each tape will be pressed by the thumb finger on the skin for 5 s. The tape strips will be removed carefully with tweezers. The procedure of tape stripping will be repeated from the same skin site six times. Afterwards, the first two tapes will be discarded, and tapes 3–7 will be stored individually in closed vials at −80°C until analysis.

Analysis of the internal UV dose

In this study, cis-urocanic acid (cUCA) will be used to determine the internal UV dose from the collected SC samples. Trans-urocanic acid (tUCA) is a major UVR-absorbing component in the epidermis.33 It isomerises to the cis-form (cUCA) on exposure to UVB in a dose-dependent manner. Therefore, cUCA is a specific marker as it is not endogenously present but is formed on exposure to UVR. As tUCA varies between persons, a relative amount of cUCA (cUCA/cUCA+tUCA) will be used. Urocanic acid isomers will be extracted from the tapes and subsequently analysed by high performance liquid chromatography. As the levels of cUCA might reach a plateau after long-term UVR exposure, tape stripping will only be conducted at T0 and T1.

Analysis of the UV-related immune response in the skin

To explore immune response in the skin due to UV exposure, two immune markers, interleukin-1 alpha (= IL-1α) and interleukin-1 receptor antagonist (= IL-1 RA) will be measured in the tape strips. These immunological markers are useful to assess repeated exposure to UVR, as the adverse effects of UVR might have occurred before visible changes (erythema of the skin). Furthermore, immune responses in the skin play an important role in UVR-mediated damage.33 For the analysis, the multiplex immunoassay (Meso Scale Discovery, Maryland, USA) will be applied.

For both analyses, standard operating procedures will be followed. The analysis of the markers will be performed blinded, the samples will be coded untraceable to the participants and researchers until the data analysis.

Power calculation

The primary outcome of the study is the proportion of outdoor workers who generally apply topical sun protection products with a frequency of ‘sometimes’, ‘often’ or ‘always’ at the end of the observation period (on a scale of never, rarely, sometimes, often, always). A Canadian study reports that without intervention, 25% of outdoor workers used sunscreen regularly.27 A Dutch study using a similar frequency score reports, without intervention, an application rate of 38%.28 For this study, it is assumed that the proportion of outdoor workers in the control group who apply sun protection products at least ‘sometimes’ is 30%. It is expected that in the intervention group, this proportion can be increased to 50%. With a sample size of 93 outdoor workers per group, a two-sample χ2-test with a 0.05 two-sided significance level will then have 80% power to detect this difference between the application frequencies (SAS V.9.4, PROC POWER). It is expected that not all outdoor workers will complete the study as planned. Considering a 20% rate of premature drop-outs, 117 outdoor workers will be recruited per group. In total, 234 outdoor workers will hence be included in this study.

Statistical methods and data analysis

Throughout the study, all data, including completed questionnaires and SC samples, will be made identifiable only by numbers. Only the researchers will have access to the cleaned data sets. There will be no replacement of any individuals who withdraw from the study. However, the characteristics (eg, job characteristics, age) and number of withdrawals will be monitored. The outdoor workers’ characteristics (eg, age) and job characteristics will be presented by using descriptive statistics. Mean and SD will be used to describe normally distributed continuous variables, and the median and IQR to describe non-normally distributed continuous variables. For self-reported sunscreen usage data as the primary outcome parameter, counts and percentages will be used to present categorical variables. The self-reported sunscreen usage data will be dichotomised and analysed by χ2 statistical test to establish whether sunscreen consumption will differ between the intervention and control group. Two-sided p values of <0.05 will be considered to be statistically significant. Statistical uncertainty will be expressed using two-sided 95% CIs. For the main study parameters, intention-to-treat analysis will be performed. Differences between the occupational and personal and private sun protection behaviour will be tested using McNemar test to compare the proportion. The levels of biomarkers will be presented as quantitative, continuous variables. The biomarker levels at T1 will be compared with the baseline levels using a paired ANOVA test, followed by the correction for multiple testing, dependent on the distribution of data. Before data analysis, a detailed data analysis plan will be made available.

Discussion

This non-randomised, controlled intervention study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of a preventive strategy to increase topical sunscreen use among outdoor workers. To capture sunscreen use comprehensively, we consider both sunscreen use during working hours and in private, non-working life (eg, on weekends, holidays or after work). This approach enables us to assess sun protection behaviour as a potential habit rather than a purely situational response. Investigating sun protection behaviour across different contexts recognises that the formation and maintenance of health-related habits often extend beyond the occupational setting alone. By including private life, we gain a more holistic understanding of habitual sun protection, as continuous and context-independent use of sunscreen is essential for effective skin cancer prevention. Furthermore, since patterns of sun exposure can differ substantially between work and leisure environments, including both settings allows us to more accurately capture real-world behaviour and the related health implications. Thus, this comprehensive approach is crucial for evaluating the sustainability and generalisability of behavioural interventions aimed at improving overall sun protection. Another strength of this study is the comprehensive approach to incorporating biomarkers related to UV exposure and questionnaire-based outcome measures, allowing a multi-dimensional assessment of intervention effects. Additionally, by including outdoor workers from different industries, the study enhances the generalisability of its findings across various occupational settings.

However, the study has potential limitations. The use of partially validated questionnaires may limit the internal validity of self-reported measures. Furthermore, the non-randomised design may introduce selection bias and complicate the comparability of intervention and control groups, particularly across different industries. The follow-up period of 6 months may not fully capture the long-term impact of intervention, especially given the importance of a lifetime cumulative dose in the most prevalent skin cancer, SCC and its precursor, AK. Despite these limitations, the ProtExSun study is expected to provide important insights into sun protection behaviour in outdoor workers and contribute valuable evidence for preventive strategies against UV-related skin damage.

Patient and public involvement

The questionnaires applied in the study will be pilot tested with six outdoor workers. The outdoor workers will evaluate the comprehensibility of the questions. Subsequent to the pilot test, the questionnaires will be adapted based on the outdoor workers’ feedback. Beyond this, patients/outdoor workers or the public will not be involved in the planning, conduct, or interpretation of the study.

Ethics and dissemination

The study protocol followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2013) and was approved by the Ethics Committee of Osnabrück University, Germany (reference Ethik-37/2024). The study is registered in the German Clinical Trials Register (DRKS) (DRKS00035178). Participation in this study will be voluntary. All participants will sign an informed consent form before participation and will be free to renouncing their participation and the use of their data at any time. It is planned to publish the results of this study in an international scientific journal and to present them at (inter)national scientific congresses.

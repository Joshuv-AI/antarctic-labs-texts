Potential Energy Surface and Bound States of Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) van der Waals Complex Based on Ab Initio Calculations
Authors: Nesrine Mabrouk, Jamila Dhiflaoui, Samah Saidi, Mohamed Bejaoui, Nissrin Alharzali, Hamid Berriche
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractTheoretical studies of the potential energy surface and vibrational bound states calculations were performed for the ground state of the Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) van der Waals (vdW) complex. The intermolecular interactions were investigated by using an accurate monoconfigurational RCCSD(T) method and large basis sets (aug-cc-pVnZ, n = T, Q, 5), extrapolated to the complete basis set (CBS) limit. In turn, the obtained raw data from RCCSD(T)/CBS(Q5) calculations were numerically interpolated using the Morse + vdW model and the Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) polynomial method to generate analytic expressions for the 2D-PES. The RKHS interpolated PES was then used to assess the bound states of the Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) system through nuclear quantum calculations. By studying the aspect of the potential energy surface, the analysis sheds light on the behavior of the Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) complex and its interactions between repulsive and attractive forces with other particles. By examining the vibrational states and wave functions of the system, the researchers were able to gain a better understanding of the behavior of the Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) complex. The calculated radial and angular distributions for all even and odd symmetries are discussed in detail. We observe that the radial distributions exhibit a more complicated nodal structure, representing stretching vibrational behavior in the neon atom along its radial coordinate. For the highest bound states, the situation is very different, and the energies surpass the angular barrier.

Introduction

In recent years, the simple rare gas–alkali ionic clusters Rg–M2+ (M = alkali and Rg = rare gas atom) have been the subject of many experimental and theoretical studies due to its important role in the progression of the primordial universe.1−3 They are considered as van der Waals (vdW) systems and have a special importance in excimer lasers applications.4,5 They can assist us in comprehending not just the microscopic interactions among individual particles but also the progression of properties as they transition from isolated molecules to bulk phase materials.6

The alkali metal atoms have a simple electronic structure, which exhibits dipole transitions in the visible range with large oscillator strengths, which permits experimental7−13 studies of the excited states by the technique of laser excitation. On the other hand, noble gas atoms present a dynamic area of research because among the only chemical properties of krypton are fairly understood. To study the spectroscopic and dynamic properties of a heterotrimer system such as Ne–Li2+, it is necessary to construct the potential energy surface (PES), which describes the interactions within the molecular system. In the past decade, extensive research both experimentally and theoretically has been performed to determine the ground-state PES of the Rg–M2+ vdW complexes.14−21

Recent research conducted by Kristensen and colleagues22 has introduced an innovative experimental approach involving the use of Coulomb explosion induced by intense femtosecond (fs) laser pulses. This method has been employed to investigate alkali dimers [such as (Rb2, Li2, Na2, and K2) on helium (He) nanodroplets]. Their results demonstrate that Coulomb explosion permits the determination, in a single measurement, of whether alkali dimers (Ak2) located on the surface of He nanodroplets are created in the X1Σg+ ground state or the a3u+ triplet state.

In a separate study, Fuchs and Toennies23 conducted experimental investigations into the collisional cross sections of Li2 dimers in specific vibrational states, interacting with helium (He) and krypton (Kr) atoms. Additionally, An der Lan et al.24 presented highly detailed mass spectra of clusters involving Na+Hen, K+Hen, Na2+Hen, and K2+Hen. These clusters were formed through the electron ionization of doped helium droplets. Notably, they observed distinctive anomalies in the Na2+Hen clusters at n = 2 and n = 6.

From a theoretical perspective, recent contributions by researchers such as Saidi et al.25 have delved into the structural, spectroscopic, and dynamic properties of Li2+(X2Σg+) interacting with krypton atoms. Additionally, investigations by Alharzali et al.,17,26 Bodo et al.,27−29 and Marinetti et al.30 have centered around the structure and dynamic characteristics of cationic dimers (Li2+, Na2+, and K2+) interacting with helium atoms, employing approaches like RCCSDT and post-Hartree–Fock methods. Their findings consistently show that helium atoms prefer to attach linearly to the cationic dimer of all three species rather than adopting a T-shaped configuration. Furthermore, the solvation of cationic dimers within helium clusters has been observed in the context of larger clusters.

Douady et al.31 investigated ionic sodium dimers within argon clusters and found that the cationic dimer becomes incorporated into the clusters rather than remaining at the surface. This behavior is attributed to the relatively weak interaction between the closed-shell argon atoms and the cationic dimer. This result is attributed, in contrast, to the relatively strong interaction with the closed-shell argon atoms and the cationic dimer.

In a separate study, Zanuttini et al.15 explored the relaxation of photoexcited Li2+ chromophores solvated within Nen clusters (where n = 2–22), employing molecular dynamics with a surface hopping approach. Meanwhile, using core polarization potentials (CPPs), Zanuttini et al.15 determined the structures and spectroscopic properties of Nen–M2+ systems. They concluded that the modification of the PES due to the neighboring Ne atoms highlights the pronounced confinement of the lowest energy states of the M2+ molecules. Marinetti et al.16 employed ab initio calculations to depict the microsolvation of A2+(2Σg+) molecular ions (where A = Li, Na, and K) within 4He clusters. In a similar vein, Saidi et al.32 investigated the structure and stability of Li2+(Σg+2)-Xen clusters (where n = 1–6) with distinct symmetry groups, employing a precise ab initio methodology based on nonempirical pseudopotentials, polarization potentials dependent on l, and analytical potential expressions for Li+Xe and Xe–Xe interactions. Following a comparable approach to that in ref (32), Ghanmi et al.19,20 explored the interactions of Na2+(2Σg+) (n = 1–6) and K2+(2Σg+) with noble gas atoms Ar, Kr, and Xe.

In this work, we present a theoretical study of the vdW Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) complex, using the restricted coupled-cluster singles and doubles excitation approach with perturbative treatment of triple excitations RCCSD(T) and basis sets, aug-cc-pVnZ (n = 3, 4, and 5) for the Ne and Li atoms, considering the extrapolation of the interaction energies at their complete basis set (CBS) limit. The ab initio points on the PES are fitted with the Morse + vdW form and a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) proposed by Ho and Rabitz.33

The computational methods and details, such as basis sets and extrapolation schemes used to determine the interaction energies of the Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) complex, is presented in Section 2. In Section 3, we discuss the results of the Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) complex. Finally, in Section 4, we summarize some concluding remarks.

Computational Method

The intermolecular potential energy surface of Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) complex is described by the three Jacobi coordinates (re,R,θ) as shown in Figure 1. The Li2+ bond length (re) is fixed at its equilibrium intermolecular value re = 3.11 Å,34,35 while R is the distance between the center of mass of the dimer and the Ne atom and θ is the angle between the R and re vector. All ab initio calculations were performed using the MOLPRO program.36

For every quantum chemical study, the level of static electron correlation within the wave function is important. To choose a suitable method, several diagnostics were proposed, such as the T1 and D1 factors.

The T1 diagnostic is defined as the Frobenius norm of the single excitation amplitude vector of a coupled-cluster wave function with single and double excitations divided by the square root of the number of correlated electrons. The D1 diagnostic is based on the matrix norm of the same single-excitation amplitude vector.37,38

It is noteworthy that as the angle increases from θ = 0° to θ = 90°, the well depth decreases from De = 604 cm–1 to De = 43 cm–1. This suggests that the linear configuration is the most stable. This can be explained by considering that in its ground state, the Li2+ dimer can be thought of as two Li+ cores surrounded by an electron cloud. As a result, the electron experiences repulsion from the nearby neon atom, but there is an attractive force between the two Li+ cores and the closed-shell Ne atom. This overall balance of forces favors the neon atom, being positioned at the ends of the dimer.

In the case of the lowest bound states with wave functions corresponding to n = 0, the angular population within the 90° barrier region is minimal, indicating a strong localization of the Ne atom in the collinear geometric configuration. The ground state wave function exhibits nodal structures with symmetrically positioned angular distributions at θ = 0 and 180°. The radial distribution peaks at R = 3.6 Å, which is in close proximity to the equilibrium intermolecular distance of the linear geometry. As for the low-lying excited states (n = 1 and n = 2), they correspond to bending motions of the Ne atom, as indicated by the presence of nodes in their angular distributions. Finally, the n = 3 state shows nodes in its radial distribution, signifying stretching motions.

Conclusions

The Ne–Li2+ (X2Σg+) vdW complex was investigated in this study using ab initio calculations. The RCCSD(T) method and large basis sets were used for the calculations, which were then extrapolated to their CBS(TQ5) and CBS(TQ5) limits with large basis sets.

To achieve an accurate representation of vdW systems, it is crucial to employ high-quality basis sets and correlation treatments. Just like in prior investigations (refs (17)–20 and (29)), each surface exhibits a double minimum structure, featuring both linear and T-shaped isomers. Notably, the RCCSD(T) interaction energies for the T-shaped configurations consistently yield lower values compared to the linear ones across all studied complexes of Rg–M2+.

The interaction potentials for RCCSD(T)/CBS(Q5) are generated through two surface generation schemes: one involves fitting the ab initio interaction energies to a combined Morse + vdW functional form, and the other employs an interpolation method rooted in the RKHS approach. The accuracy of the PESs is validated by comparing the equilibrium distance R and the well depth De from the analytical parametrized surface with the calculated ab initio RCCSD(T)/CBS(Q5) interaction energies for each configuration. The RKHS method reproduces the numerical surface well at short, intermediate, and long distances compared with the Morse + vdW function. Therefore, vibrational quantum bound states are performed by considering zero total angular momentum (J = 0). We found that the lowest-lying states (n = 0–27) are double degenerate. Additionally, the system displays significant amplitude motions, encompassing stretching and bending modes, in its lower states. However, as one progresses to the excited states, one swiftly transitions to irregular wave function maps.

The precise data produced for the ground state of the Ne–Li2+(X2Σg+) complex will be used to generalize the structural and dynamical studies of large Nen–Li2+ clusters.

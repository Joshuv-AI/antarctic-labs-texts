Understanding stigma and public perception of pathology: a public relations perspective
Authors: Chen Mayer
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPathology is an essential medical specialty, underpinning disease diagnosis, management, and research. Despite its indispensable contributions to healthcare, pathology remains a misunderstood and underappreciated field in the public sphere, often burdened by stigma and misconceptions. Many associate pathology primarily with forensic autopsies or laboratory work disconnected from direct patient care. These misconceptions adversely impact recruitment into the specialty and diminish public appreciation of pathologists' integral role in patient management and healthcare innovation. This article explores the sources of stigma and outlines a strategic framework for improving public perception through targeted public relations initiatives. Key strategies include public education campaigns, leveraging digital media, celebrating achievements, amplifying pathology's visibility in interdisciplinary collaborations, and engaging medical students early in their training. By demystifying pathology and fostering understanding, these efforts can reshape its societal relevance, strengthen recruitment, and ensure that the field thrives as a cornerstone of modern medicine.Keywords: Education, Healthcare perception, Pathology, Public relations, Stigma, Workforce development

Pathology is the cornerstone of medical science, providing critical insights into disease diagnosis, prognosis, and research. Yet, despite its indispensable role in healthcare, pathology remains an underappreciated and often misunderstood field, overshadowed by more publicly visible disciplines such as surgery or clinical medicine. The societal perceptions surrounding pathology are frequently laden with misconceptions and stigma, which not only affect the recruitment of new talent into the field but can also hinder its broader recognition in public discourse about medicine. This report examines the common public stigma surrounding pathology and explores strategies for improving its reputation through effective public relations initiatives.

Pathology and public misconceptions

Pathology, in its simplest definition, involves the study of diseases, encompassing diagnostic techniques, investigative research, and the advancement of medical knowledge. Despite its breadth, the public often associates pathology solely with autopsy services, perpetuating the false narrative that pathologists “work only with the dead.” This misconception stems from the media's portrayal of pathologists in crime dramas, where forensic autopsies are disproportionately highlighted.1 While forensic pathology is unequivocally important, it represents only a small fraction of the discipline, which spans surgical pathology, molecular pathology, hematopathology, and clinical laboratory medicine.

Another misconception is that pathologists are isolated from patient care, working exclusively in laboratories, detached from direct interactions. For many, this image fosters a belief that pathologists play a passive role rather than serving as integral consultants in patient management. Studies of medical students have shown that many hold unclear or negative views of pathology, with only a small percentage considering it a career option.2 Such misconceptions not only diminish public appreciation for pathology but can dissuade medical students or professionals from pursuing careers in the field.

Stigma and its impact

The stigma surrounding pathology is multifaceted. First, there is a general sense of discomfort with discussions about death, disease progression, and microscopic examination of biological tissues. These topics, though essential to medical science, are often seen as morbid or overly technical by those outside the field. This stigma complicates efforts to engage the public in understanding pathology's broader contributions to human health, such as detecting cancer in its earliest stages or identifying pathogens during an outbreak.3

The stigma also extends to career perception. Medical students often view pathology as a “backroom” specialty, with fewer opportunities for patient interaction or “heroic” interventions,” unlike fields such as surgery or emergency medicine. Terms like “doctor's doctor,”4,5 which are often used to describe pathologists, inadvertently feed the notion that pathologists have limited visibility and impact in patient care. As a result, pathology often struggles to attract the brightest minds, who may overlook the field due to its perceived lack of prestige or excitement. Recent editorials have argued that this perception is not only outdated but also detrimental to the future of diagnostics and precision medicine.6

The role of public relations in addressing stigma

Public relations in healthcare encompasses the strategies and tactics used to shape public perception, enhance visibility, and communicate the value of specific services or professions. Pathology, like any other field, requires high-quality public relations initiatives to challenge misconceptions, build trust, and elevate its role in the broader healthcare narrative. The following strategies can help improve public perception and combat stigma surrounding pathology.

Educating the public on the breadth of pathology

One of the most effective ways to address stigma is to broaden public understanding of the field's scope. This can involve creating accessible educational resources that highlight the diversity of pathology, from cancer diagnostics to molecular research in infectious diseases. Medical institutions and pathology organizations can use online campaigns, infographics, and public seminars to showcase the essential contributions of pathology to patient outcomes. Examples include the College of American Pathologists (CAP) “See, Test & Treat” program, which provides public education and free screenings in underserved communities,7 and the Royal College of Pathologists (RCPath) public-facing campaigns such as “Pathology: the science behind the cure.”8

Leveraging media and storytelling

Pathologists often work behind the scenes, but storytelling can bring their contributions to the forefront. Documentaries, podcasts, and interviews with practicing pathologists can serve as platforms to humanize the field and explain its significance in layman's terms. Positive portrayals of pathologists in popular media can also help counter the stereotype of the “reclusive lab worker” or “forensic investigator” and instead position pathologists as collaborative healthcare professionals. Partnering with media outlets to promote stories about real-world impacts—such as breakthroughs in diagnostics or life-saving discoveries—is crucial. Initiatives like #PathTwitter have demonstrated how case-based storytelling can effectively engage both the public and medical audiences.9

Engaging medical students early

The stigma surrounding pathology often starts in medical school, where specialties like surgery, cardiology, and internal medicine are glamorized as career paths with greater patient interaction and dynamic work environments. To address this, pathology departments can take a proactive role in student engagement by organizing pathology-specific workshops, hands-on training sessions, and career mentorship programs. Highlighting the intellectual, investigative, and collaborative aspects of pathology—as well as the field's substantial impact on patient care—can counterbalance negative perceptions. Programs such as PathElective.com and ASCP's Future Pathologist initiatives have shown early success in increasing student interest.10,11

Social media advocacy

Social media platforms provide an opportunity for pathologists to showcase their work in an approachable and creative manner. Twitter, Instagram, and LinkedIn can be used to share fascinating cases, diagnostic challenges, or insights into the day-to-day lives of pathologists. Posts that demystify technical aspects of pathology and emphasize the field's human impact can attract public interest. Collaborations with influencers in the medical community can amplify these efforts. Academic departments (e.g. Stanford, Michigan) and societies like RCPath have used YouTube, Instagram, and blogs to improve branding and public engagement.8

Celebrating achievements publicly

Recognition of pathology's contributions to society should be a priority. Public relations campaigns can highlight achievements such as advancements in cancer screening, development of cutting-edge diagnostic techniques, or contributions to emergency public health responses, such as during the COVID-19 pandemic. Awards, press releases, and video features celebrating these contributions can increase visibility and instill pride within the pathology community. Examples include CAP's Pathologist Leadership Award program and institutional storytelling campaigns.12

Promoting interdisciplinary collaboration

Pathologists work closely with clinicians, surgeons, radiologists, and other healthcare professionals, yet their roles are often overshadowed. Promoting stories of interdisciplinary collaboration, where pathologists play key roles in patient diagnosis, management, and recovery, can help dispel the notion that pathology operates in isolation. Public relations teams should emphasize the interconnectedness of pathology within the “healthcare ecosystem.” This message is echoed in recent commentaries urging medical schools to integrate pathobiology more meaningfully into clinical curricula.6,13

Evaluating public relations efforts

Successful public relations initiatives rely on continuous evaluation to determine whether they are effectively combating stigma and improving public perception. Surveys and focus groups can help measure awareness and attitudes among the public, medical students, and healthcare professionals. Metrics such as increased enrollment in pathology training programs, greater visibility on social media, and positive media coverage can serve as indicators of progress. The impact of programs such as PathElective and See, Test & Treat has been tracked using engagement statistics, qualitative feedback, and pre- and post-educational testing.7,10

Challenges in changing perceptions

While public relations are a powerful tool, it is essential to recognize the inherent challenges faced in changing deeply entrenched perceptions. The technical nature of pathology, coupled with the taboo surrounding subjects like death or disease, can make public engagement difficult. Overcoming these challenges requires persistence, creativity, and a willingness to meet audiences where they are—including both professionals within healthcare and the general public.

Additionally, limited funding and resources for pathology-specific public relations campaigns can pose obstacles. Advocacy groups, professional organizations, and research institutions need to collaborate to pool resources and amplify shared messaging.

Conclusion

Pathology plays a foundational role in healthcare, but its societal reputation continues to suffer from misconceptions and stigma. Addressing public perceptions of pathology requires a concerted effort through targeted public relations strategies, including educational initiatives, storytelling, social media outreach, and celebration of achievements. By emphasizing the diversity, impact, and collaborative nature of pathology, the field can gain greater visibility and appreciation not only within the medical community but also among the public.

Improving pathology's image is not merely a matter of vanity; it is essential for attracting talent and resources to a field that is critical to advancing medical science and patient care. In an age where public narratives shape policy, funding, and career aspirations, pathology must embrace the power of public relations to secure its future as one of the most important facets of modern medicine. The stigma surrounding pathology can be overcome but only through sustained and strategic effort to elevate its profile and redefine its role in the eyes of both professionals and the public.

Guardians’ preferences for pit and fissure sealing services in China: evidence from a discrete choice experiment
Authors: Zhu Chen, Sixuan Chen, Jiajia Liu, Xinyu Jin, Fangyu Song, Zhengxi Sun, Wang Yao, Wenhua Xu, Lidan Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDental caries represents a significant global public health challenge, with particularly high prevalence rates observed among Chinese children. Pit and fissure sealing (PFS) has been established as an effective and cost-efficient preventive intervention, yet service coverage remains suboptimal in China. This study examines guardians’ preferences and preference heterogeneity regarding PFS services, with the aim of informing tailored service delivery and policy interventions to enhance PFS uptake.MethodsA discrete choice experiment was conducted through face-to-face interviews with 411 guardians. Four key attributes were included: preventive effectiveness, preventive service time, distance and out-of-pocket (OOP) service cost. Data were analysed using a mixed logit model, subgroup analyses and scenario analyses were performed to examine preference heterogeneity.ResultsPreventive effectiveness (41.31%) emerged as the most influential attribute, followed by distance (29.23%), OOP service cost (15.85%) and preventive service time (13.61%). Female guardians and guardians of children with poor oral health status placed greater emphasis on preventive effectiveness and distance. In contrast, male guardians were more focused on OOP service cost. Guardians with higher education levels and guardians of children with better oral health status were more inclined to schedule PFS services during vacations. The scenario analysis revealed that guardians prefer PFS services with general effectiveness but higher accessibility and lower OOP costs, rather than those offering superior effectiveness but compromised accessibility and higher financial burden. Notably, medical insurance coverage boosted service uptake from 18.3% to 71.0%, with guardians willing to pay 127 Chinese yuan more for insured services.ConclusionThese findings highlight substantial preference heterogeneity among guardians and underscore the transformative potential of insurance coverage in enhancing service accessibility. Policymakers should consider incorporating PFS services into medical insurance coverage and optimizing service arrangements to meet the needs of different guardian groups in order to improve the coverage and effectiveness of PFS services.Clinical trial numbernot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12903-025-07012-z.Keywords: Pit and fissure sealing, Health service research, Service accessibility, Out-of-pocket service cost, Discrete choice experiment

Background

Dental caries is a multifactorial disease characterised by the cyclical demineralization and remineralization of dental hard tissues [1]. Globally, oral diseases affect approximately 3.5 billion individuals, including 2 billion cases of permanent tooth decay. In 2019, the direct medical costs for oral diseases amounted to USD 387 billion, equating to USD 50 per capita worldwide [2]. While the global burden of dental caries remains substantial, China’s situation proves particularly concerning. In 2017, the 4th National Oral Health Survey revealed that 38.5% of 12-year-old children in China present with permanent tooth decay, representing a 9.6% increase compared with 10 years ago [3]. Notably, pit-and-fissure caries account for 90.3% of these cases [4], imposing considerable public health burdens on China [5].

Dental sealants represent the most cost-effective intervention for preventing pit-and-fissure caries [6]. The American Dental Association’s 2018 clinical guidelines recommend pit-and-fissure sealing (PFS) as the optimal treatment, demonstrating 60%−80% reductions in caries incidence [7], despite governmental initiatives to enhance oral health, PFS coverage among Chinese 12-year-olds remains critically low at merely 11% [4].

Existing research on caries prevention has predominantly emphasized therapeutic efficacy [8] and cost-effectiveness analyses [9], with most studies examining material properties such as restoration longevity, aesthetic outcomes, and adverse effects. Crucially, key determinants of healthcare utilisation, particularly out-of-pocket (OOP) costs [10] and service accessibility [11], remain largely unexplored. This represents a significant deviation from patient-centred care principles [12].

Discrete choice experiments (DCE) quantify population preferences for service attributes through random utility theory [13], enabling rigorous trade-off analyses and demand predictions for hypothetical services [14]. As the methodological approach most closely approximating real-world decision-making [15]. DCE have become widely adopted in healthcare research, though their application to PFS service preferences remains limited.

Previous investigations have examined patient preferences regarding restoration performance [16], with one study estimating willingness-to-pay (WTP) through incorporating restoration longevity and waiting times [17]. However, these studies were confined to developed nations (e.g., Norway and the UK), substantially limiting their policy relevance. Moreover, their failure to incorporate health service accessibility as an evaluative attribute particularly diminishes their utility for informing policy in developing countries, where accessibility challenges are most pronounced.

Therefore, a DCE was conducted to explore guardians’ preferences for PFS services for their children, measuring guardians’ trade-offs between health service accessibility, OOP service cost, and preventive effectiveness. The study aims to: (1) quantify guardians’ preferences for PFS services, (2) identify the heterogeneity in preferences for PFS services across different guardian subgroups, and (3) predicting changes in guardians’ choice probabilities for PFS services under different policy scenarios. The findings will provide policymakers with evidence based on patient preferences to better develop individualized PFS services that align with guardians’ needs, ultimately improving the coverage and effectiveness of PFS services.

Methods

Identification of attributes and levels

In accordance with established DCE design guidelines [18], the attribute selection process comprised two distinct phases: initial parameters, including attributes such as out-of-pocket (OOP) service cost [19] and distance [20], were extracted from systematic literature review, then refined through three-round expert panel deliberations. The detailed study design description has been published [10]. The initially screened attributes comprised caries type (i.e., primary or permanent tooth decay), health care institution (e.g., primary, secondary, tertiary), preventive service time, preventive effectiveness, distance and OOP service cost per tooth for PFS.

Subsequently, a modified Delphi panel comprising 3 clinical experts evaluated these attributes via three rounds of anonymous scoring (see Additional file 1). Based on the feedback, we removed “caries type” and “health care institution” due to their redundancy with other attributes like cost and preventive effectiveness. Ultimately, four critical attributes were identified: preventive service time, preventive effectiveness, distance, and OOP service cost. The finalized attribute framework is detailed in Table 1.

Experimental design

The D-efficient design was applied to generate choice sets for the DCE through Ngene 1.2 software (version 1.1.2) [21]. Eight DCE choice tasks were finally determined, each presenting participants with two hypothetical PFS options service A, B and an “opt-out” option. Services A and B were designed to represent different PFS that participants might encounter. The inclusion of the “opt-out” option was intended to simulate a real-world scenario where participants may opt out of PFS. Furthermore, the inclusion of the “opt-out” option prevents a forced choice scenario, thereby reducing risk of overestimating the impact of attributes [22]. Participants were instructed to select their preferred option between service A and B or choose “opt-out” if both were unsatisfactory.

To ensure participants were adequately prepared for the DCE choice scenarios, we provided a warm-up choice task identical to the formal choice tasks, the responses from this preliminary choice task were not included in the analysis. The warm-up allowed participants to practice and familiarize themselves with the DCE format under the guidance of the surveyors, aiming to improve their understanding of the process. To evaluate response validity, we incorporated a dominant choice task following the formal DCE tasks [23]. In this choice task, all attribute levels of service A were superior to service B (see Additional file 2 Table.S1). Participants selecting alternatives to service B were classified as test failures, and their responses were excluded from primary analyses.

To validate the rationality of attribute-level inclusion and ensure respondents’ comprehension of the DCE instrument, a pilot study was conducted in June 2021 [24]. We recruited 30 guardians of children aged 6–12 years old for PFS, to participant in this preliminary research phase. Based on the valuable insights gathered from the pilot study, we made several targeted adjustments to the questionnaire. We refined the wording of certain questions to eliminate ambiguity and improve comprehension. We also reorganized the layout of the questionnaire to enhance its logical flow and user-friendliness. Additionally, we added clarifying instructions and examples where necessary to help respondents better understand the context of the hypothetical scenarios.

In addition to DCE tasks, the survey captured socio-demographic variables. Guardian characteristics: gender, education level; and child indicators: oral health status, history of caries prevention service, and caries experience.

Sampling and data collection

In China, PFS is classified as a professional dental intervention requiring licensed practitioners. However, the current distribution of dental professionals shows significant concentration in large public hospitals, and most patients continue to preferentially seek oral healthcare at public hospitals [25]. Therefore, we decided to implement this study within hospital environments. The survey was conducted in Anhui Province, located in east-central China. In 2024, Anhui Province ranked 11th in terms of gross domestic product [26] and 12th in total health expenditure [27] among all provinces in China, placing it at a medium level nationally in both indicators. We employed a multi-stage random sampling approach to select seven cities in Anhui Province. Subsequently, one general hospital was randomly selected from each city, resulting in a total of seven participating hospitals. The study was conducted within the dental clinics of these selected hospitals. Guardians were enrolled if they met al.l following criteria: (1) had brought their child for dental outpatient services; (2) demonstrated basic knowledge of their children’s oral health status; and (3) voluntary participate after giving informed consent.

The DCE survey was performed via face-to-face interviews. Our surveyors comprised 15 medical students, who underwent rigorous training according to a standard manual. Following informed consent acquisition, the surveyors explained the study’s objective, attributes and levels to ensure guardians could understand the choice set correctly. Sample size requirements were derived from Johnson and Orme’s efficiency calculation for choice models [28]: . The minimum required sample size was estimated to be 84. To ensure adequate statistical power for subgroup analyses examining potential preference heterogeneity across populations with different characteristics, we recruited a larger sample for our study.

The survey was implemented from June 2021 to January 2022. To maintain data integrity, we established rigorous quality control criteria. Exclusion criteria comprised: (1) uniform response patterns throughout the survey, (2) failure in the dominance choice task, and (3) incomplete responses. This study was approved by Ethics Committee of Anhui Medical University (approval number: 2021H030).

Statistical analysis

Data coding and model specification

Upon completion of the survey, we applied dummy coding for the choice data [29]. Responses were dummy coded as the dependent variable. OOP service cost was coded as a continuous independent variable, while other attribute levels were dummy coded as categorical variables. Reference levels were specified for each attribute, with other levels indicating improvements relative to the reference levels.

We then developed a utility model relating the dependent variable to the independent variables, reflecting guardians’ preferences for PFS based on service attributes. Model coefficients indicated the direction and magnitude of each attribute’s influence on service preferences. A positive coefficient suggested that guardians perceived greater utility with that level compared to the reference, which was assumed to provide zero utility. Conversely, a negative coefficient indicated lower utility, rending the reference level more attractive for that attribute.

The model assumed random coefficients for attribute levels to be normally distributed, except for the coefficient of service cost, which was modelled in its negative form with a log-normal distribution. The mean of each parameter indicated the average utility associated with an attribute level, while the standard deviation (SD) characterized the heterogeneity in utility among respondents. An alternative-specific constant (ASC) was estimated to capture the preference for the opt-out choice beyond observed attribute effects. The ASC was specified using a zero-utility reference framework, where all observed utility components for the opt-out alternative were fixed at zero [30]. A zero-cost design was simultaneously implemented to reflect respondents’ preference for rejecting PFS services. The ASC was specified as a normally distributed random parameter, the statistical significance level was set at 0.05.

Analyses were performed in Stata version 18.0, using a mixed logit model (MLM) estimated by simulated maximum likelihood [31]. The model’s stability and fit were assessed through iterative estimation by progressively increasing Halton draws in increments of 500, starting from 500 draws and continuing increase the number of draws to 5000 to ensure the robustness of our findings [32]. Among these model results, the model with 500 Halton draws demonstrated optimal fit, evidenced by log-likelihood, Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) [33] (see additional file 2 Figure.S1). This model was selected for further analysis.

Through marginal rate of substitution analysis, we derived monetary valuations of service attributes relative to OOP service cost, revealing guardians’ WTP for specific service enhancements. Relative importance (RI) is also calculated to measure how much guardians value different attributes.

We further investigated preference heterogeneity through subgroup analyses across guardians with different characteristics, guardians were first categorized based on individual socio-demographic characteristics, including gender, education, the children’s oral health status were also used as a criterion for categorization.

In addition, based on the three types of pit-and-fissure sealing materials that are relatively common in Chinese market, we established different PFS service selection scenarios according to their features and costs. These PFS sealants were ordered based on increasing preventive effectiveness and decreasing accessibility. Considering the typical prices of these sealants in China, we assigned the price of glass ionomer cement (GIC) as 40 CNY, flowable composite resin (FCR) as 80 CNY, and light-cured composite resin (LCR) as 120 CNY.

Demographic characteristic

A total of 437 guardians consented to participate in our DCE survey, 26 questionnaires were excluded from the analysis due to failure of the dominant choice set, or provide incomplete responses to the choice set. We included 411 questionnaires in the analysis (Table 2), resulting in an effective response rate of 94.05%. In this survey, the majority of respondents were female guardians (70.00%). Over half of these guardians have a high school education or below (54.03%). Meanwhile, most of their children have experienced dental caries. However, only 20.10% of the children have ever received caries prevention services, and a mere 22.14% of the children were in a healthy oral health status.

Regarding preventive service time, guardians preferred scheduling their children’s PFS services during vacations rather than on workdays. Guardians were also more likely to choose services with higher preventive effectiveness, the “very high” preventive effectiveness was the most preferred by guardians, followed by “high” preventive effectiveness and “general” preventive effectiveness. Unexpectedly, in terms of distance, guardians’ preferences for the “10–25 minute” distance were stronger than for a “≤10 minute” distance, although the difference between the two levels was not significant. Concerning OOP service cost, guardians always prefer PFS with lower service cost.

The results of WTP estimation showed that guardians were willing to pay additional 86 Chinese Yuan (CNY) to convert the preventive service time from “workday” to “vacation”. In terms of preventive effectiveness, guardians were willing to pay additional 132 CNY to upgrade from “low” to “general”, 152 CNY to upgrade to “high”, and 261 CNY to upgrade to “very high”. Regarding distance, guardians were willing to pay an additional 28 CNY to reduce the driving time from “≥50 minute” to “25–50 minute”, 184 CNY to “10–25 minute”, and 178 CNY to “0–10 minute”.

As shown in Fig. 1, the RI analysis reveals that preventive effectiveness was the most influential attribute for guardians (41.31%), followed by distance (29.23%). The preventive service time (13.61%) and OOP service cost (15.85%) demonstrated similar importance levels.

Preference heterogeneity across guardians with different characteristics

The preference heterogeneity across guardians with different characteristics are shown in Fig. 1 and Additional file 2 Table.S2-S3. Female guardians exhibit a stronger preference for preventive effectiveness (RI: 45.36%) and distance (RI: 23.23%) compared to male guardians, and they more highly prioritize the OOP service cost (RI: 18.95% >2.71%). Education level also significantly influenced preferences. Guardians with a college education or higher were preferred scheduling their children’s PFS services during vacations. Moreover, there was a heightened emphasis on distance (RI: 33.60%), with its significance reaching a level nearly comparable to that of the preventive effectiveness (RI: 39.62%).

Guardians’ preferences for PFS services varied on their children’s oral health status (Fig. 1 and Additional file 2 Table.S4-S6). Guardians of children with poor oral health status prioritized preventive effectiveness and distance rather than cost. Conversely, guardians of children with healthy oral status placed more value on the accessibility of PFS services during vacations (RI: 19.80% >11.70%). Guardians whose children had experienced dental caries emphasized preventive effectiveness and distance, and they less concerned about preventive service time. When it came to the guardians of children who hadn’t received caries prevention services, preventive effectiveness (RI: 42.58%) and distance (RI: 28.05%) are the most important service attributes. And they more highly prioritize the OOP service cost (RI: 16.06% >3.13%) and preventive service time (RI: 13.30% >3.88%) than guardians of children who had received caries prevention services.

We then examined how the probability that a guardian would choose an LCR would change if the LCR were covered by medical insurance. If the LCR were covered by medical insurance, guardians would have access to the LCR at a lower price and with greater accessibility (Service 3 A). When guardians choose between Services 1, 2, and 3 A, we find that 71.02% would choose Service 3 A, much higher than the 18.33% who would choose Service 3 prior to being covered by medical insurance. Nevertheless, heterogeneity in consumer preferences led to a small proportion (24.60%) of guardians still prefer GIC, while only 4.38% of guardians were expected to choose FCR.

The CV calculation using the simulation method (Table 4) predicted that guardians who initially had only Service 1 and 2 in their choice set would have been willing to pay 127 CNY to have Service 3 A added to their choice set. It was important to note that this did not imply that all consumers would necessarily choose Service 3 A when given the option. Rather, it suggested that consumers were willing to pay for the opportunity to choose PFS, as having a choice set with more options could provide additional utility to consumers, even if they ultimately chose a different service.

Discussion

This study represents the first DCE investigating guardians’ preferences for children’s PFS services in China, incorporating four key attributes: preventive service time, preventive effectiveness, distance and OOP service cost. Our findings demonstrate that guardians exhibited strongest preferences for “very high” preventive effectiveness, “10–25 minute” driving time, lower OOP service cost and during “vacation”. The preference for a “10–25 minute” distance over “0–10 minute” distances may reflect an optimal balance between convenience and perceived service quality. Behaviour economics suggests that intermediate distances are often viewed as more credible indicators of service quality, while extremely short distances may raise concerns about professionalism. The vacation scheduling preferences aligns with time-cost theory, indicating guardians’ efforts to minimise work schedule disruptions [35].

Guardians’ preferences for PFS services showed significant variation according to both their demographic characteristics and their children’s oral health status. Our scenario analysis revealed that service accessibility and out-of-pocket costs exerted a more substantial influence on service selection than preventive effectiveness alone. Crucially, guardians consistently favoured PFS options demonstrating moderate preventive effectiveness combined with enhanced accessibility and lower OOP service cost, over alternatives that offered superior effectiveness but were compromised by poorer accessibility and greater financial burden.

The findings demonstrate that guardians accorded highest priority to preventive effectiveness when selecting PFS services, a preference consistent with the American Dental Association’s clinical guidelines emphasising the efficacy of pit-and-fissure sealants [7]. Service accessibility emerged as the second most influential factor, underscoring its fundamental importance in healthcare utilisation patterns. This observation corroborates previous research conducted in China, which established a significant association between service accessibility and dental care utilisation rates [36]. The accessibility consideration assumes particular significance in rural and remote regions, where uneven distribution of healthcare resources remains a persistent challenge. These findings suggest policymakers should develop targeted interventions to improve geographical equity in dental service provision, with special attention to reducing access barriers for underserved rural communities. Notably, OOP service cost exerted relatively limited influence on service selection, a pattern congruent with prior Chinese study [37]. This pattern implies that current pricing structures appear generally affordable for the majority of households, and that further cost reductions may yield only marginal improvements in service uptake. The analysis also revealed a pronounced preference for vacation scheduling of PFS appointments, principally to minimise interference with work commitments and consequent productivity losses.

Subgroup analyses identified significant variations in guardians’ preferences for PFS services according to gender, educational attainment, and children’s oral health status. Female guardians demonstrated particularly strong preferences for services with higher preventive effectiveness and closer proximity, while exhibiting greater sensitivity to out-of-pocket costs. This pattern likely reflects women’s predominant role in household healthcare decision-making and financial management, leading them to prioritise both clinical quality and convenience to optimise time and transportation efficiency [38]. These findings suggest policymakers should particularly enhance service quality and geographical accessibility to accommodate female guardians, who typically serve as primary healthcare decision-makers for children. Among more highly educated guardians, a distinct preference emerged for vacation scheduling and reduced distances, potentially reflecting their greater emphasis on time efficiency and resource optimisation. To improve service uptake across all demographic groups, policymakers should ensure the continued affordability of PFS services while simultaneously enhancing their accessibility and convenience.

Guardians of children with poorer oral health demonstrated significantly stronger preferences for both preventive effectiveness and service accessibility, reflecting their heightened preventive care requirements. This observation aligns with findings from a Jiangxi province study which established a positive correlation between children’s oral health deterioration and guardians’ dental care-seeking behaviour [39]. Conversely, guardians of children with better oral health status exhibited greater flexibility in scheduling, predominantly selecting vacation periods for PFS appointments. Most notably, guardians whose children had previous caries experience displayed particularly marked preferences for both clinical effectiveness and accessibility. These results corroborate international evidence from Ethiopia, demonstrating that parents of children with prior disease experience, parents are significantly more likely to seek treatment, prioritize health service accessibility, and place greater emphasis on service quality [40].

The scenario analysis evaluating three prevalent PFS modalities revealed GIC as the most frequently selected option among guardians, followed by LCR and FCR. While GIC exhibits comparatively lower preventive effectiveness, its combination of superior accessibility and reduced OOP service cost rendered it the favoured choice, underscoring the pivotal influence of service availability and affordability in healthcare decision-making processes. A particularly noteworthy finding emerged regarding LCR services: when incorporated within medical insurance coverage, selection probability increased substantially, demonstrating insurance inclusion’s capacity to markedly enhance both the accessibility and appeal of dental interventions. This observation corresponds with international research demonstrating that medical insurance coverage effectively reduces OOP expenditures, consequently improving healthcare service utilisation rates [41].

In addition, the CV analysis further indicated that guardians exhibited a WTP of 127 CNY to incorporate insurance-covered LCR services within their available options. This finding not only reflects guardians’ explicit demand for more accessible and higher-quality PFS, but also suggests that medical insurance could play an important role in optimising the structure of service provision and promoting equity in healthcare utilisation. Moreover, the 127 CNY valuation exhibits close alignment with current market pricing for LCR services, providing robust empirical support for establishing this amount as the recommended OOP expenditure ceiling. Although PFS service have not yet been incorporated into the reimbursement catalogue, the Anhui provincial medical insurance scheme already covers certain dental health services, with a reimbursement rate of approximately 50% [42]. The findings of this study offer critical evidence to support the future inclusion of PFS in the insurance directory, as well as to inform the determination of co-payment ratios and out-of-pocket limits.

This study makes an important contribution to understanding guardians’ preferences regarding children’s PFS services in China, addressing a notable research gap in this field. However, several methodological limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting these findings. Primarily, the geographical focus on Anhui Province may affect the generalisability of results to other regions, though the province’s central location, high population density and status as a national transportation hub enhance its representativeness as a study setting. Secondly, while our analysis incorporated four key service attributes, other clinically relevant factors that may influence guardians’ decision-making processes were not included in the experimental design. Additionally, our sampling was limited to hospital-based stomatology departments, future studies should incorporate community health centres and primary care facilities to better capture potential preference heterogeneity across health service settings. This would enhance the generalizability of findings to broader populations.

Conclusion

This study employed a DCE to explore guardians’ preferences for PFS services for children in China, highlighting the significant influence of preventive effectiveness, distance, OOP service cost and preventive service time on decision making. Female guardians were found to place greater emphasis on preventive effectiveness and distance, while guardians with higher education levels were more inclined to schedule PFS services during vacations. Scenario analysis indicated the important role of medical insurance coverage in enhancing the accessibility and attractiveness of the service. Moreover, guardians are willing to pay additional costs for PFS services covered by medical insurance. This suggests that medical insurance coverage not only improves the accessibility of services but also provides guardians with more options. Therefore, policymakers should consider incorporating higher quality PFS services into health insurance coverage and optimizing service arrangements to meet the diverse needs of different guardian groups. These measures could significantly enhance the oral health of children.

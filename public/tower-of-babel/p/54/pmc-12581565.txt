Therapeutic efficacy of multiple neoadjuvant regimens involving targeted therapy, immunotherapy and chemotherapy in gastric cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Chen Liang, Zhiyuan Yu, Siyu Hou, Chunzeng Jia, Yuxin Guan, Junfu Zheng, Yan Gao, Gailing Wu, Lei Niu, Pengji Gao, Lei Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroudThe utilization of neoadjuvant therapy, encompassing chemotherapy, molecular targeted therapy, and immunotherapy, has demonstrated a significant enhancement in the radical resection rate and an improved prognosis for patients with locally advanced gastric cancer (LAGC). The purpose of this meta-analysis was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NCT) in combination with targeted therapy or (and) immunotherapy for LAGC patients.MethodsThe PubMed, EmBase, and Cochrane Library databases were systematically searched to identify clinical studies comparing the efficacy of different neoadjuvant regimens in LAGC patients. Subsequently, paired and network meta-analyses were conducted using Stata software to evaluate the pooled results of different neoadjuvant regimens, including NCT, neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy (NICT), NCT plus targeted therapy (NCTT), and NICT plus targeted therapy (NICTT).Results21 studies [six randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and 15 non-RCTs] fulfilled the inclusion criteria were incorporated into the meta-analysis. Pairwise meta-analysis revealed that the NICTT, NICT, and NCTT groups exhibited a higher incidence of pathological complete response (pCR), major pathological response (MPR), and R0 resection compared to the NCT group. Compared to the NCT group, both the NICTT and NCTT groups demonstrated a tendency towards experiencing more severe treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), while the NICT and NCTT groups exhibited a lower 2-year recurrence rate. Additionally, network meta-analysis revealed that the NICTT group exhibited superior efficacy in achieving pCR, albeit being associated with a higher incidence of severe TRAEs.ConclusionCompared to NCT alone, the combined application of targeted therapy and/or immunotherapy demonstrates greater efficacy in tumor downstaging and achieving pCR. However, it is crucial to remain vigilant regarding the occurrence of severe TRAEs. The future adoption of NICTT as a mainstream treatment is anticipated, however, further high-quality clinical studies are imperative to substantiate its efficacy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12885-025-15136-2.Keywords: Gastric cancer, Neoadjuvant therapy, PD-1 inhibitor, Targeted drugs, Meta-analysis

Introduction

According to the 2020 global cancer statistics released by the International Agency for Research on Cancer of the World Health Organization, gastric cancer (GC) ranks fifth in terms of prevalence worldwide, with the fourth highest mortality rate [1]. The surgical approach remains the cornerstone in the management of GC; however, a majority of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage with low rates of achieving radical tumor resection, resulting in a dismal prognosis. The enhancement of radical resection rate and the optimization of prognosis have consistently constituted the focal point in the realm of GC treatment research [2, 3].

In recent years, significant advancements have been made in the treatment of locally advanced gastric cancer (LAGC) through the emergence and widespread adoption of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NCT), molecular targeted therapy, and immunotherapy [3, 4]. The recent advancements in the field of LAGC have primarily been attributed to immunotherapy. Specifically, immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) have demonstrated their efficacy across various solid tumors, including GC [5, 6]. Apatinib is a widely utilized anti-vascular targeted drug for the treatment of LAGC, which exerts an anti-angiogenic effect through highly selective binding to the vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR-2) and fundamentally obstructing tumor nutrient supply [3, 7]. With the continuous advancement of neoadjuvant therapy, a multitude of clinical trials and novel treatment concepts continue to emerge, leading to a transformation in its treatment approach from NCT alone to neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemotherapy (NICT), NCT plus targeted therapy (NCTT), as well as NICT plus targeted therapy (NICTT), and other innovative modalities [7–9].

The interim results of NCT03421288, KEYNOTE-585, and ChiCTR2000030610 trails demonstrated that the combination of NICT exhibits promising efficacy and acceptable toxicity compared to NCT alone in GC patients [10–12]. Additionally, the clinical trials NCT04208347 and NCT04661150 revealed that triple therapy with NICTT yielded superior treatment outcomes. The optimal combination of neoadjuvant therapy still remains a subject of ongoing controversy [13, 14]. Currently, there is a dearth of systematic reviews or meta-analyses comparing the efficacy and safety of different neoadjuvant regimens in the treatment of LAGC.

Methods

Study design and literature search

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted and reported according to the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) [15] and AMSTAR (Assessing the methodological quality of systematic reviews) Guidelines [16]. The protocol has been registered in PROSPERO database (CRD42024576132). We searched PubMed, EmBase, and Cochrane Library databases for published studies and registered clinical trials from database inception to July 2024 (Supplemental material 1). Besides, reference lists of included studies were checked for additional references. Two reviewers independently screened titles, abstracts, and, if potentially eligible, full texts for inclusion. Disagreement was resolved by a third reviewer. The inclusion of duplicate published trials was limited to the final or most comprehensive version, taking into account increasing patient numbers or extended follow-up durations.

Inclusion criteria and data extraction

The inclusion criteria for the study were as follows: (1) study types were randomized controlled trials (RCTs), well-designed prospective or retrospective comparative studies; (2) study population diagnosed with non-metastatic, untreated, and resectable LAGC (stage II-IV); (3) intervention and control groups treated with distinct regimens, which included NCT, NCTT, NICT, or NICTT; (4) there were no restrictions imposed on the study size, language, or follow-up duration. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) single-arm studies, review articles, case reports, or meta-analysis; (2) protocol, or repeated studies; (3) study population diagnosed with inoperable, recurrent, or metastatic GC; and (4) required outcome indicators were not reported.

For the coding and data extraction, 2 independent researchers evaluated each included study. All discrepancies in data extraction were resolved in discussion, and the required data for analyses could be obtained for all included studies. The baseline characteristics to be extracted and collated encompassed the author, publication year, study type, sample size, gender, age, cTNM tumor stage, intervention and control regimes. The primary outcome measures were pathological complete response (pCR) and severe treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). The term “severe TRAEs” was used to describe adverse events of grade 3 or higher. Secondary outcomes included major pathological response (MPR), R0 resection, and 2-year tumor recurrence.

Quality assessment

The quality assessment for RCTs and retrospective studies were conducted using criteria outlined in the Cochrane Collaboration’s tool and the Newcastle Ottawa scale (NOS), respectively. The tool developed by the Cochrane Collaboration evaluate bias in six aspects, including selection, implementation, evaluation, follow-up, reporting, and other [17]. Subsequently, the quality of RCTs was categorized into high (6 points), fair (4–5 points), and low (0–3 points) based on the number of positive answers to the aforementioned six questions. In NOS tool, three aspects were used to evaluate studies: selection (0–4 points), comparability (0–2 points), and exposure/outcome (0–3 points) [18]. The study was considered to be of satisfactory quality if it reaches or exceeds 6 points. Two authors independently evaluated the quality of included studies, and any discrepancies were resolved through discussion or by consulting a third reviewer when necessary.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using Stata (version 15) software. In pairwise meta-analysis, pooled odds ratio (OR) with 95% confidence interval (CI) were estimated using the Mantel-Haenszel method. The I2 and Q statistics were employed to evaluate the level of heterogeneity among the incorporated studies. I2 > 50% or P < 0.05 was considered substantial heterogeneity, random effects model was adopted; otherwise, a fixed effects model was used. To minimize heterogeneity across studies and enhance the accuracy, subgroup analyses were conducted based on different intervention regimens. Additionally, a comprehensive evaluation of both direct and indirect comparisons was conducted through network meta-analysis. The network diagrams were created to illustrate the comparative relationship between each procedure, with nodes representing the available neoadjuvant regimens. The relative efficacy of each neoadjuvant regimen was estimated using ranking probabilities and the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) metrics. Besides, publication bias was evaluated using the Egger regression test and visualized through a funnel plot. A sensitivity analysis was performed by sequentially excluding individual studies to evaluate the robustness and stability of the pooled effect estimates. All statistical tests were two-sided, and p-values less than 0.05 were deemed to signify statistical significance.

Results

Study selection and study characteristics

Based on the search strategy, a total of 742 potential articles were identified. Upon exclusion of duplicates and irrelevant articles through title and abstract screening, 120 studies were selected for full-text evaluation. Of these, 21 studies (six RCTs and 15 non-RCTs) fulfilled the inclusion criteria were incorporated into the meta-analysis [10–14, 19–34] (Fig. 1). The study conducted by Shitara et al. consisted of two distinct intervention cohorts (main cohort and FLOT cohort), therefore, we provided separate descriptions and analyses for each of these cohorts [11]. The aggregated data encompassed 4,146 patients who were administered one of 4 distinct treatment regimens, as follows: NCT, NCTT, NICT, and NICTT. Baseline characteristics of the included clinical studies are delineated in Table 1. Besides, the outcome indicators utilized in the meta-analysis are provided in online Supplemental material 2. The risk of bias for RCTs and non-RCTs was assessed using the Cochrane and NOS bias risk tools, respectively. It is worth noting that all 21 incorporated studies demonstrated a low risk of bias (Table 1).

Pairwise meta-analysis

Primary outcomes

pCR

The rates of pCR were reported in 19 incorporated studies, with the pCR rates in the NCT, NCTT, NICT, and NICTT groups being 5.7% (120/2,116), 16.3% (33/203), 16.4% (209/1,277), and 21.0% (73/347) respectively. The comparative analysis of each subgroup did not reveal any significant heterogeneity, thus a fixed effect model was employed. The comparative analysis of NICT vs. NCT (OR = 3.359, P < 0.001), NICTT vs. NCT (OR = 4.217, P < 0.001), NICTT vs. NCTT (OR = 2.130, P = 0.027), and NCTT vs. NCT (OR = 2.738, P = 0.006) all exhibited statistically significant differences (Fig. 2A). To summarize, NICTT, NICT, and NCTT demonstrated higher pCR rates compared to NCT, while NICTT exhibited a higher pCR rate than NCTT.

Forest plots illustrating the pooled results of (A) pCR and (B) severe TRAEs outcomes. pCR, pathological complete response; TRAEs, treatment-related adverse events; OR, odds ratio; CI, confidence interval

Severe TRAEs

The rates of severe TRAEs were reported in 14 studies, with rates of 28.7% (458/1,597), 30.3% (66/218), 42.6% (355/834), and 34.2% (111/325) observed in the NCT, NCTT, NICT, and NICTT groups respectively. The comparative analysis of each subgroup did not reveal any significant heterogeneity. The subsequent meta-analysis employed a fixed effect model, revealing that patients in the NICTT (OR = 2.435, P < 0.001) and NCTT (OR = 1.970, P = 0.003) groups exhibited a higher incidence of severe TRAEs compared to those in the NCT group (Fig. 2B).

Secondary outcomes

MPR

13 studies reported the incidence of MPR. The rates of MPR in the NCT, NCTT, NICT, and NICTT groups were 26.3% (380/1,444), 40.5% (32/79), 40.1% (295/736), and 50.6% (117/231) respectively. Subsequently, fixed effect model was adopted in meta-analysis and analysis results revealed that NICTT (OR = 2.122, P < 0.001), NICT (OR = 1.928, P < 0.001), and NCTT (OR = 2.602, P = 0.008) exhibit a higher MPR rate than NCT (Fig. 3A).

Forest plots illustrating the pooled results of (A) MPR and (B) R0 resection outcomes. MPR, major pathological response; OR, odds ratio; CI, confidence interval

R0 resection

20 studies reported the rates of R0 resection, and the rates of R0 resection in the NCT, NCTT, NICT, and NICTT groups were 88.8% (1,847/2,081), 89.2% (223/250), 90.7% (1,077/1,187), and 97.0% (292/301) respectively. The fixed-effect meta-analysis demonstrated that NICTT (OR = 4.017, P = 0.001), NICT (OR = 1.412, P = 0.012), and NCTT (OR = 1.744, P = 0.050) all exhibit a higher R0 resection rate compared to NCT (Fig. 3B).

2-year recurrence

The incidence of 2-year recurrence after surgery was reported in 7 incorporated studies. The rates of 2-year recurrence in the NCT, NCTT, and NICT groups were 43.8% (534/1,218), 17.9% (12/67), and 34.3% (317/925) respectively. Subsequently, fixed effect model was adopted and meta-analysis results revealed that NICT (OR = 0.636, P < 0.001) and NCTT (OR = 0.363, P = 0.011) both exhibit a lower 2-year recurrence rate compared to NCT (Fig. 4).

Forest plots illustrating the pooled results of 2-year recurrence. OR, odds ratio; CI, confidence interval

Network meta-analysis

In order to comprehensively compare and evaluate the efficacy and safety of various neoadjuvant regimens, we conducted a network meta-analysis of the primary outcome measures. The network diagram and contribution diagram for pCR are illustrated in Fig. 5A and Supplementary material 3 A, respectively. One closed ring was formed by the four treatment measures, and the consistency test result was satisfactory (P = 0.599) (Supplementary material 4 A). The pCR rates were higher in the NICTT (OR 4.55, 95% CI 2.46–8.41), NICT (OR 3.44, 95% CI 2.38–4.95), and NCTT groups (OR 2.38, 95% CI 1.21–4.66) compared to NCT group. The NICTT group, in addition, exhibited a slightly superior performance compared to the NCTT group (OR 1.92, 95% CI 0.99–3.69) (Fig. 5B). The treatment with the highest SUCRA value was NICTT (SUCRA: 80.1), indicating its likelihood of being the most effective regimen for achieving pCR. NICT (SUCRA: 65.8) and NCTT (SUCRA: 53.2) followed suit in terms of effectiveness. Notably, NCT alone exhibited the lowest SUCRA value (0.9) (Fig. 5C).

The network diagram and contribution diagram for severe TRAEs are illustrated in Fig. 5D and Supplementary material 3B, respectively. One closed ring was formed and the consistency test result was satisfactory (P = 0.481) (Supplementary material 4B). Severe TRAEs were more common in the NICTT (OR 2.52, 95% CI 1.65–3.85) and NCTT groups (OR 1.74, 95% CI 1.16–2.63) compared to the NCT group. Besides, the NICTT group exhibited a higher risk of severe TRAEs than NICT group (OR 2.03, 95% CI 1.20–3.45) (Fig. 5E). The treatment with the least toxicity was NCT alone (SUCRA: 94.3), followed by NICT (SUCRA: 65.5) and NCTT (SUCRA: 24.9). NICTT exhibited the highest likelihood of severe TRAEs (SUCRA: 15.2) (Fig. 5F).

Subgroup analysis

Three studies documented the PD-L1 expression levels or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) status of tumors in the NICT or NICTT groups. Subsequently, we categorized the cases according to the expression status of the aforementioned two biomarkers and conducted a systematic comparison and summary of the pCR and MPR outcomes between the groups. The analysis results demonstrated that the MSI-H tumors exhibited a higher likelihood of achieving pCR (OR = 9.045, P < 0.001) (Fig. 6A) and MPR (OR = 4.529, P = 0.012) (Fig. 6B) following immunotherapy-based neoadjuvant regimens, as compared to microsatellite stable (MSS) tumors. Additionally, PD-L1 positive expression [combined positive score (CPS) > 1] group appeared to have a higher likelihood of achieving pCR (Fig. 7A) and MPR (Fig. 7B).

The publication bias was evaluated through the construction of funnel plots based on primary outcomes. The funnel plot constructed for pCR outcomes exhibited an approximately symmetrical distribution, suggesting the absence of significant publication bias (Fig. 8A). However, the funnel plot constructed for severe TRAEs revealed that the majority of included studies were skewed towards the left side of the median line, which indicate the presence of potential publication bias (Fig. 8B).

Discussion

In recent years, the deepening of exploration has led to the innovation of numerous treatment models and concepts, as well as the emergence of clinical trial results, thereby creating a novel landscape for the management of LAGC [3, 4]. The current management of LAGC has evolved from simple surgical resection to a novel approach involving neoadjuvant therapy followed by surgery and postoperative adjuvant therapy, significantly enhancing the prognosis and survival outcomes for patients with LAGC. Therefore, the determination of an appropriate individualized neoadjuvant therapy program has gradually emerged as a prominent topic of discussion and research among academic experts [35, 36].

The previous studies have demonstrated that despite the introduction of preoperative NCT or postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy, which has shown some benefits for patients with LAGC, its efficacy remains limited. Furthermore, the median overall survival (OS) of advanced gastric cancer patients undergoing first-line chemotherapy still struggles to surpass 20 months [37, 38]. The efficacy of traditional chemotherapy is limited. With the advancement of personalized precision therapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy have emerged as focal points in comprehensive treatment.

The human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER-2) serves as a widely utilized therapeutic target in the field of LAGC, facilitating enhanced patient survival when combined with chemotherapy. However, the prevalence of HER2-positive in LAGC ranges from 6% to 30%, with only a limited proportion of patients deriving significant benefits [39]. VEGFR-2 regulates both lymphatic endothelial cells and vascular endothelial cells, thereby promoting the formation of lymphatic vessels and blood vessels. It also serves as a crucial therapeutic target for LAGC treatment. The tyrosine kinase inhibitor apatinib exhibits high selectivity in competing for intracellular VEGFR-2 ATP-binding sites, thereby blocking downstream signal transduction and inhibiting the aberrant formation of tumor vessels [40, 41]. The results of the NCT02426034 trial have confirmed the acceptable and manageable safety profile as well as clinical benefits of apatinib in patients with advanced GC who have received it as a third- or later-line treatment [40]. Additionally, a nonrandomized controlled trial (NCT03192735) demonstrated the efficacy and acceptable safety profile of apatinib in combination with SOX as a neoadjuvant treatment for LAGC [41].

The ICIs therapy primarily utilizes monoclonal antibodies to specifically bind to the immune checkpoints located on the surface of tumor or immune cells, thereby impeding negative regulation and reactivating the immune system for efficient eradication of tumor cells. The advent of ICIs has revolutionized the conventional treatment paradigm for various malignant solid tumors, including GC. PD-1, which is expressed on the surface of T, B, and myeloid cells, functions as an inhibitory regulator in the immune system. Targeting this molecule with inhibitors can restore the immune system’s ability to effectively surveil, identify, and eliminate tumor cells [36, 42]. The CheckMate649, KEYNOTE-059, ATTRACTION-04, and ORIENT-16 studies all demonstrated significant efficacy of NICT as a first-line treatment for advanced GC. Besides, the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology, the European Society for Medical Oncology, and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines have all endorsed immunotherapy as a recommended treatment for advanced GC [43–45]. The favorable outcomes of immunotherapy in the adjuvant treatment of LAGC have prompted clinical trials to shift their focus towards neoadjuvant therapy. The interim findings from the KEYNOTE-585 trial demonstrated that perioperative administration of pembrolizumab led to a significant improvement in pCR, while exhibiting no statistically significant impact on disease-free survival (DFS) [11]. Additionally, ChiCTR2000030610 trial’s results suggested that NICT may improve the likelihood of achieving R0 resection, pCR, and postoperative node-negative rate compared to treatment with NCT alone [12]. The application of PD-1 inhibitors in neoadjuvant therapy for LAGC has been sparsely reported; therefore, further exploration and analysis are required to determine its effects.

The anticancer effect of chemotherapy drugs is achieved by specifically targeting the genetic material (DNA and RNA), cellular proteins, enzymes, and other vital components to induce tumor cell death. The administration of apatinib can induce the normalization of tumor blood vessels, thereby enhancing tissue perfusion and facilitating improved penetration of chemotherapy drugs into the tumor tissue. Thus, the addition of apatinib to chemotherapy can enhance the efficacy of tumor treatment [7, 37]. Additionally, the combined effect of immunotherapy and chemotherapy can synergistically augment tumor eradication. When ICIs are combined with chemotherapy, on one hand, chemotherapy drugs can enhance the anti-tumor immune response of patients; on the other hand, ICIs can augment chemotherapy sensitivity by boosting the anti-tumor immune response and further eliminate tumor cells that develop resistance after chemotherapy [46, 47]. Above all, compared to NCT alone, NCTT and NICT regimes both demonstrated superior efficacy in tumor cell eradication and enhances rates of pCR, MPR, and R0 resection while reducing the risk of postoperative recurrence.

The successful implementation of the NICT and NCTT regimes has prompted scholars to shift their research focus towards the NICTT triple regime. KEYNOTE-811 study has confirmed that the addition of immunotherapy to anti-HER-2 therapy + chemotherapy can significantly enhance the objective response rate, DFS, and OS [48]. Based on these findings, the China National Medical Products Administration granted approval for pembrolizumab in combination with trastuzumab, fluorouracil, and platinum on June 25, 2024. This approval was specifically for the first-line treatment of unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive gastric adenocarcinoma (GA) and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma (GEJA) expressing PD-L1 (composite positive score ≥ 1). DRAGON IV/AHEAD-G208 trail, the world’s first phase III clinical trial assessing neoadjuvant PAC regime for resectable GA/GEJA, presented its interim analytical data through an oral presentation at ESMO Annual Meeting 2023. The findings demonstrated that in the perioperative management of resectable GA/GEJA, the addition of apatinib and carrelizumab to SOX regimen significantly augmented the pCR rate compared to SOX alone (18.3% vs. 5.0%). However, the follow-up of survival data from the DRAGON IV/AHEAD-G208 study is still ongoing and may require an additional 2–3 years of waiting [13]. In the future, more high-quality RCTs are needed to validate the efficacy and safety of NICTT triple therapy.

The combination of low-dose apatinib and carrilizumab has demonstrated a synergistic effect in suppressing tumor cell proliferation, as evidenced by various cellular and animal studies. The administration of apatinib concurrently enhances tumor hypoxia, acidosis, and hypoperfusion while facilitating immune cell infiltration to improve the tumor immune microenvironment. This transformation effectively converts tumor immunosuppression into immunoenhancement and aids in the recruitment and aggregation of T cells within the tumor microenvironment. Therefore, low-dose apatinib can enhance the efficacy of immunotherapy through multiple mechanisms, including facilitating antigen presentation, augmenting lymphocyte infiltration, inducing tumor vascular normalization, and improving the immune microenvironment. Simultaneously, it can also enhance the accumulation of chemotherapy drugs within tumor cells, thereby playing a multi-dimensional synergistic role [49, 50]. Drug safety is also a crucial area of research, particularly in relation to severe TRAEs, which require prompt attention to enable more patients to complete their course of treatment and achieve optimal outcomes. Our meta-analysis revealed no significant difference in the incidence of severe TRAEs between the NICT and NCT groups. However, both the NICTT and NCTT groups exhibited a higher incidence of severe TRAEs compared to the NCT group, indicating a more pronounced impact of apatinib on TRAEs. Therefore, it is imperative to closely monitor changes in vital signs during the application of NICTT regime, remain vigilant against severe TRAEs, and promptly formulate and adjust treatment plans based on patients’ conditions [7, 51, 52]. With the emergence of more therapeutic targets and targeted drugs, NICTT regime is expected to achieve improved outcomes with reduced TRAEs.

Although this study systematically collected relevant literature evidence and employed diverse methodologies for meta-analysis, inherent limitations still persist. Firstly, the majority of the published studies were retrospective cohort or case-control studies with limited levels of evidence. The potential publication bias, low research quality, and heterogeneity among patient populations inherent in retrospective studies may compromise the accuracy of analysis results. In addition, the outcome data of RCTs were preliminary and interim results obtained from the phase II/III clinical trials, with a limited duration of follow-up and an absence of survival outcomes. Last but not the least, almost all of the included studies were conducted exclusively on the Chinese population, thus necessitating further investigation into the generalizability of this analysis.

Conclusion

Compared to the exclusive use of NCT, the combined application of targeted therapy and/or immunotherapy demonstrates greater efficacy in tumor downstaging and achieving pCR, which in turn enhances the R0 resection rate and improves patient prognosis. However, it is crucial to remain vigilant regarding the occurrence of severe TRAEs. The present study conducted a comprehensive evaluation and comparison of the efficacy and safety of various neoadjuvant therapy regimens in GC, thereby providing practical and reliable high-level evidence-based support for perioperative NICTT strategies in LAGC. Owing to the relatively recent commencement of research on NICT, particularly NICTT, and the limited duration of follow-up, the actual impact of pCR on survival duration and long-term survival rates remains to be fully elucidated. It is anticipated that NICTT will emerge as a mainstream therapeutic approach in the future. Nevertheless, further high-quality clinical studies, tailored to specific tumor status, treatment protocols, and long-term follow-up, are required to substantiate its efficacy. We will also conduct a pooled analysis in the future, utilizing the most recent research data, with a particular emphasis on long-term survival outcomes, in order to complement the findings of this study.

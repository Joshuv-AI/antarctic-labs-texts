The impact of photobiomodulation therapy on the survival and differentiation of periodontal ligament mesenchymal stem cells exposed to zoledronic acid
Authors: Ardavan Etemadi, Parham Fathizadeh, Nasim Chiniforush
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study assessed the effects of photobiomodulation therapy (PBMT) with different laser wavelengths on the viability and differentiation of periodontal ligament mesenchymal stem cells (PDLMSCs) exposed to zoledronic acid (ZA). In this in vitro study, PDLMSCs were cultured with 5 µM ZA for 48 h and randomly assigned to 6 groups of positive and negative controls, and PBMT with 635 nm (2 and 4 J/cm2) and 980 nm (2 and 4 J/cm2) lasers. Cell viability was assessed by the methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) assay after 24 and 72 h, and mineralized nodule formation was evaluated by Alizarin red staining. Mineralization was assessed by alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity and expression analysis of osteocalcin (OCN), osteopontin (OPN), and RUNX2 by real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR). Data were analyzed by one-way ANOVA and Tukey test (α = 0.05). ZA exposure in no-irradiation group decreased viability, ALP activity, mineralized nodule formation, and expression of osteogenic genes (P < 0.05). Cell viability in 980 nm (4 J/cm2) group at 24 h and in 635 nm (4 J/cm2) and 980 nm (2 and 4 J/cm2) groups at 72 h was significantly higher than that in ZA group (P < 0.05). Mineralized nodule formation in both 980 nm groups was significantly higher than that in ZA group (P < 0.001). PBMT with 635 nm laser caused significant downregulation of OCN and OPN with 4 J/cm2 and upregulation of OPN with 2 J/cm2 (P < 0.05) energy density. PBMT with 980 nm laser (2 and 4 J/cm2) caused significant upregulation of OCN, OPN, and RUNX2 (P < 0.05). PBMT with 980 nm laser (4 J/cm2) in presence of ZA increased PDLMSC viability, ALP activity, mineralized nodule formation, and expression of osteogenic genes.Keywords: Photobiomodulation therapy, Periodontal ligament mesenchymal stem cells, Zoledronic acid, Cell survivalSubject terms: Stem-cell research, Cell growth

Introduction

Bisphosphonates are the pyrophosphate synthetic analogs that eventually lead to osteoclast, keratinocyte, fibroblast, and periodontal ligament mesenchymal stem cell (PDLMSC) cytotoxicity and their apoptosis. They are prescribed for patients with bone metastasis due to malignancies, multiple myeloma, or osteoporosis1. Bisphosphonate-related osteonecrosis of the jaw is the most important side effect of bisphosphonate use, which was recently renamed as medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ) to cover similar conditions caused by other medications2. The American Association of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgeons defines MRONJ as exposure of necrotic bone for over 8 weeks caused by previous or current pharmaceutical therapy in association with another stimulant (tooth extraction, periodontal disease, or periodontal surgery) without a head and neck radiation history3. The clinical manifestations of MRONJ affect the choice of treatment, which may include non-surgical approaches such as antibiotic therapy and bactericidal mouthwashes, or surgical debridement4. The current therapeutic goals for MRONJ include pain control, prevention of secondary infection, and prevention of necrosis progression while benefitting from supportive pharmaceutical therapy5.

The effects of laser irradiation without raising the tissue temperature by over 1 °C are referred to as biostimulatory effects, and treatments performed with this mode of laser irradiation are referred to as low-level laser therapy (LLLT) or photobiomodulation therapy (PBMT)6. Evidence shows that PBMT with low-level laser can regulate important cellular pathways. Moreover, PBMT is a potentially effective non-invasive biophysical approach that can enhance would healing by induction of hemostasis, reduction of pain and inflammation, increasing collagen accumulation, wound granulation, and revascularization7. The optimal efficacy of PBMT for prevention and treatment of MRONJ has been previously reported5,8. PBMTT can induce initial gingival healing under the influence of bisphosphonates and prevent MRONJ7. An in vitro study also showed the positive effects of this modality on fibroblasts and osteoblasts9. However, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, the effects of PBMT with two wavelengths of 980 nm and 635 nm on PDLMSCs exposed to zoledronic acid (ZA) have not been previously investigated. Thus, this study aimed to assess the effects of PBMT with different laser wavelengths ( 980 and 635 nm )on the viability and differentiation of PDLMSCs exposed to ZA.

Results

Cell viability

Table 2 presents the measures of central dispersion for the viability of PDLMSCs in the study groups after 24 and 72 h.

At 24 h, the highest mean viability was noted in the 980 nm (4 J/cm2) group followed by the control group, and the lowest mean viability was observed in the 635 nm (2 J/cm2) laser group (P < 0.001).

At 72 h, the highest mean viability was noted in the control group followed by the 980 nm laser (4 J/cm2) group, and the lowest mean viability was observed in the ZA group (P < 0.001).

One-way ANOVA revealed significant differences in the mean cell viability among the study groups at both 24 (P = 0.000) and 72 (P = 0.000) hours.

Pairwise comparisons at 24 h revealed significantly higher cell viability in the 980 nm (4 J/cm2) laser group than the ZA group (P < 0.001). Other experimental groups had no significant difference with the ZA group (P > 0.05, Fig. 1).

Osteogenic gene expression

One-way ANOVA revealed significant differences in the expression of OCN, OPN, and RUNX2 genes among the study groups (P < 0.001).

Pairwise comparisons revealed that 635 nm (4 J/cm2) laser irradiation caused significant downregulation of OCN while irradiation of 980 nm (2 and 4 J/cm2) laser caused significant upregulation of OCN. Irradiation of 635 nm laser caused significant upregulation of OPN with 2 J/cm2 (P = 0.037) and its significant downregulation with 4 J/cm2 energy density (P < 0.001). Irradiation of 980 nm (2 and 4 J/cm2) laser caused significant upregulation of OPN (P < 0.001). Irradiation of 635 nm laser (2 and 4 J/cm2) had no significant effect on the expression of RUNX2 (P > 0.05) while irradiation of 980 nm (2 and 4 J/cm2) laser caused its significant upregulation (P < 0.001).

Discussion

This study assessed the effects of PBMT with 635 nm and 980 nm (red and infrared) laser wavelengths on the viability and differentiation of PDLMSCs exposed to ZA. Selection of these two particular laser wavelengths was because of their reportedly optimal effects on viability of gingival and PDLMSCs10–12. Also, PBM occurs with red and infrared laser wavelengths10. Selection of 2 and 4 J/cm2 energy densities was because of the previously confirmed biostimulatory effects of diode lasers with 600 to 1200 nm wavelengths and 0.4 to 4 J/cm2 energy density on different stem cells13,14.

Regarding cell viability, the results revealed that 5 µM ZA y decreased the viability of PDLMSCs by low percent compared to previous studies that reported 5 µM ZA significantly decreased the viability of PDLMSCs15, human epithelial cells16, fibroblasts17, and human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells18. Also, it appears that ZA has a dose-dependent activity in reduction of cell viability and increasing apoptosis18. The present results indicated that irradiation of 980 nm (4 J/cm2) laser increased the viability of cells exposed to ZA compared with the ZA and control groups, and reversed the effects of ZA as such. The same was reported by Safari et al.15, who showed that irradiation of 660 nm (5 J/cm2) and 810 nm (3 J/cm2) lasers alone or in combination with each other increased the proliferation of PDLMSCs exposed to ZA compared to the control group. Brozoski et al.19 reported that irradiation of 660 nm laser with 5 J/cm2 energy density enhanced the proliferation of osteoblasts and fibroblasts, and reversed the effects of ZA. Fallahnezhad et al.20 evaluated the combined effect of laser therapy with HeNe laser (623.5 nm, 1.2 J/cm2, one cycle) and alendronate incubation on bone marrow derived mesenchymal stem cells in patients with osteoporosis. They concluded that this combination significantly increased cell viability compared with the control group. Rigi-Ladez et al.21 evaluated the effect of PBMT with infrared laser (810 nm and 940 nm wavelengths, 0.5, 1.5, and 2.5 J/cm2 energy densities, and 100 mW power) on PDLMSCs and showed significantly higher cell proliferation in the 940 nm (2.5 J/cm2) group at all time points compared with other groups. Etemadi et al.10 demonstrated that PBMT with 635, 660, 808, and 980 nm lasers increased the proliferation of PDLMSCs; however, the highest cell viability was noted 3 days after irradiation of 980 nm laser with 4 J/cm2 energy density, which was significantly higher than that in other groups. The results of the abovementioned studies were all in agreement with the present findings.

In the current study, application of 635 nm laser with 2 and 4 J/cm2 energy densities at 24 h, and 2 J/cm2 energy density at 72 h could not reverse the effect of ZA on PDLMSC viability, which was in agreement with the findings of Safari et al.15, and Brozoski et al.19, who used 660 nm laser.

The ALP activity of PDLMSCs is an initial indicator of their differentiation to osteoblasts22,23. Also, ALP activity and OCN expression are among the properties of mature osteoblasts18. OCN is a non-collagen protein present in the alveolar bone, root cementum, and PDL stem cells. This protein has an important role in biomineralization of the extracellular matrix, and modulates hydroxyapatite maturation and osteoclastic absorption, affecting bone remodeling as such24. OCN is a protein produced by mature osteoblasts, and is related to their final differentiation13. The present results revealed that ZA impaired the maturation phase of osteogenic differentiation, and decreased ALP activity and down-regulated the OCN gene; however, 980 nm laser (2 and 4 J/cm2) irradiation led to a significantly higher ALP activity in PDLMSCs compared with the ZA and control groups. Also, Hadad et al.18 reported that ZA decreased ALP activity and down-regulated OCN in bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells.

According to the present results, irradiation of 980 nm laser with 2 and 4 J/cm2 energy densities and 635 nm laser with 4 4 J/cm2 energy density upregulated the OPN gene compared to the ZA and control groups. The ZA group showed downregulation of OPN. Similarly, Etemadi et al.11 reported that three cycles of 980 nm and 660 nm laser irradiations caused upregulation of OPN in PDLMSCs.

RUNX2 is a transcription factor with a pivotal role in differentiation and maturation of osteoblasts, affecting osteogenesis as such. It regulates the differentiation of MSCs to pre-osteoblasts, and then mature osteoblasts18. In the present study, 980 nm (2 and 4 J/cm2) laser irradiation caused upregulation of RUNX2 compared to the ZA and control groups. The ZA group showed downregulation of RUNX2. Hadad et al.18, also reported that ZA downregulated RUNX2 in bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells. Etemadi et al.11, also reported that three cycles of 980 nm and 660 nm laser irradiations upregulated RUNX2 in PDLMSCs.

The mechanism of action of photobiomodualtion therapy is based on the absorption of photons with particular wavelengths by the chromophores in cytochrome C oxidase and stimulation of mitochondrial metabolism by increasing matrix metalloproteinases, oxygen absorption, and ATP synthesis11. PBMT may cause upregulation of osteogenic genes by increasing the level of cAMP23. It has been reported that following 980 nm laser irradiation with deeper penetration depth compared to visible wavlengths, the inter-cellular fluid serves as a chromophore; 980 nm laser has the greatest effect on temperature-gated ionic channels, increases mineralized tissue formation, and causes upregulation of VEGF and BMP genes10.

Photobiomodulation at 980 nm enhances pre-osteoblast survival by activating the PI3K/Akt pathway and increasing Bcl‑2 expression, indicative of reduced Bax‑mediated apoptosis25. Furthermore, given that zoledronic acid elevates inflammatory cytokines such as TNF‑α and IL‑1β in bisphosphonate-treated bone cells, the known anti‑inflammatory and antioxidant effects of PBMT suggest it may counteract this cytokine upregulation in damaged periodontal/bone tissues26.

PBMT demonstrated a protective and regenerative effect on periodontal ligament stem cells compromised by zoledronic acid exposure. Among the wavelengths tested, 980 nm showed the most significant improvement in cell viability and osteogenic differentiation, These results indicate that PBMT, especially at 980 nm, could be a valuable supplementary method for preventing or addressing bisphosphonate-induced cytotoxicity in oral tissues. Additional in vivo research and clinical trials are necessary to confirm these effects and to develop standardized treatment protocols.

Materials and methods

This in vitro, experimental study was conducted on human PDLMSCs (IBRC C11326) obtained from Tehran University of Medical Sciences. The study protocol was approved by the ethics committee of the university (IR.IAU.DENTAL.REC.1402.155).

Sample size

The minimum sample size was calculated to be 6 in each group using one-way ANOVA power analysis of PASS 11 according to a study by Walter et al.1, assuming α = 0.05, β = 0.2 (study power of 80%), mean standard deviation of cell viability to be 6.4%, and effect size of 0.64.

Cell culture

PDLMSCs were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM; Gibco Limited, Uxbridge, UK) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 IU/mL penicillin, and 100 mg/mL streptomycin at 37 °C in presence of 5% CO2 and 95% humidity. After 24 h, cells in the ZA experimental groups were exposed to 1 mL of 5 µM concentrated ZA solution (4 mg/mL) (Zolena, Iran)27 in complete culture medium and incubated for 24 h. After 24 h of incubation, the culture medium was replaced with the complete culture medium. The specimens were then randomly assigned to six groups including two control groups and four experimental groups, as follows:

Positive control group: PDLMSCs were not exposed to ZA, and did not undergo PBMT.

Negative control group (ZA group): PDLMSCs were exposed to ZA but did not undergo PBMT.

The four experimental groups were exposed to ZA and underwent PBMT as follows:

Experimental group 1: PBMT with 635 nm diode laser (Wiser3, DoctorSmile, Italy) with 200 mW power, 2 J/cm2 energy density, 5 s irradiation type, and 0.5 cm2 tip surface area.

Experimental group 2: PBMT with 635 nm diode laser (Wiser3, DoctorSmile, Italy) with 200 mW power, 4 J/cm2 energy density, 10 s irradiation type, and 0.5 cm2 tip surface area.

Experimental group 3: PBMT with 980 nm diode laser (Wiser3, DoctorSmile, Italy) with 200 mW power, 2 J/cm2 energy density, 5 s irradiation type, and 0.5 cm2 tip surface area.

Experimental group 4: PBMT with 980 nm diode laser (Wiser3, DoctorSmile, Italy) with 200 mW power, 4 J/cm2 energy density, 10 s irradiation type, and 0.5 cm2 tip surface area28 (Table 4).

Assessment of cell viability

Human PDLMSCs were cultured as explained earlier and plated in 96-well plates at a density of 104 cells/well. Cell viability was evaluated 24 and 72 h after laser irradiation using the methyl thiazolyl tetrazolium (MTT) assay. For this purpose, the culture medium was removed from the wells, and 100 µL of 5 mg/mL MTT solution (Sigma, Germany) in phosphate buffered saline (PBS) was added to each well. Incubation was performed at 37 °C and 5% CO2 for 3–4 hours. Finally, the MTT solution was removed and replaced with 60 µL of dimethyl sulfoxide. After 15 min of shaking, the optical density (OD) of the wells was read at 570 nm wavelength by a microplate reader (BioTek, USA)27. To calculate the percentage of cell viability, the OD of each group was divided by the OD of the negative control group and multiplied by 100. Viability was considered as increased cell viability on day 3 compared to day 129,30.

Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity

Seven-day culture was used for measurement of ALP activity as an indicator of osteoblastic activity. The cells were rinsed with PBS and lysed in PBS containing 0.01% Triton X-100 and 5 mM MgCl2. The obtained mixture was sonicated in an ice bath and was then centrifuged at 1500 x g for 5 min. The supernatant was subsequently used for measurement of ALP activity as instructed by the kit manufacturer (Pars Azmoon, Iran). The OD was read at 405 nm by a microplate reader (Biotek, USA). To normalize the data, the protein content was determined by the Bradford protein assay as instructed by the kit (Bio-rad, USA), and the OD was measured at 595 nm wavelength.

Assessment of mineralized nodule formation by Alizarin red staining

The cells were seeded in a 24-well plate containing DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and antibiotic at a density of 80,000 cells/well. After laser irradiation, the culture medium was replaced with the osteogenic medium containing 50 µg/mL ascorbic acid, 10 nM dexamethasone, and 10 mM beta-glycerophosphate. The culture medium was replaced with fresh eluent every 2 days.

The results of Alizarin red staining were assessed after 14 days. To identify calcified nodules, the culture medium was rinsed with PBS twice, fixed with 10% formalin (Roth, Germany) for 10 min, hydrated with 1 mL of distilled water for 5 min, and stained with 200 µL of 1% Alizarin red S (Sigma, Germany) with a pH of 4.0. The Red S solution was removed, and the cells were rinsed with PBS for 15 min. For staining quantification, ImageJ software and macroscopic images were used. The OD of staining was calculated by ImageJ (NIH).

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR)

The cells were seeded in a 24-well plate at a density of 80,000 cells/well. RNA was extracted by RNX Plus (Cinagen, Iran) after 14 days according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The quality of the extracted RNA was assessed by measuring the OD at 260 nm wavelength, and calculation of 280/260 ratio for each sample using NanoDrop (Biotek, USA). Next, cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg RNA using Add Bio kit (Korea). PCR was then performed using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (SMO BIO, China) in a LightCycler® 96 (Roche, Basel, Switzerland). The results were analyzed using 2−ΔCT formula. Table 5 shows the sequence of PCR primers. GAPDH served as the reference gene.

Statistical analysis

Due to normal distribution of data as confirmed by the Shapiro-Wilk test, comparisons were made by one-way ANOVA and Tukey’s HSD test using SPSS version 26 (SPSS Inc., IL, USA), and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Conclusion

PBMT with 980 nm laser (4 J/cm2) in presence of ZA increased PDLMSC viablity, ALP activity, mineralized nodule formation, and expression of osteogenic genes. It appears that this near infra-red laser has the potential for clinical application for prevention or treatment of MRONJ and regeneration of periodontal tissues.

Background

Cardiac troponin T (cTnT) is the most used biomarker for the diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction because of its high myocardial specificity.1 In the late 2000s, more sensitive assays were found.2 High-sensitivity cardiac troponin T (hs-cTnT) offers higher sensitivity for detecting myocardial injury.

Implementation of hs-cTnT in clinical practice has been associated with reductions in time to diagnosis, emergency department stay, length of stay (LOS) in the hospital, and noninvasive testing such as stress testing in patients with suspected acute coronary syndrome (ACS).3,4

In addition to ACS, hs-cTnT levels can serve as risk stratification in patients with stable coronary artery disease, heart failure, and non–cardiac disease conditions such as sepsis and acute kidney injury. Many studies have focused on the role of hs-cTnT in ACS or in emergency departments. However, the diagnostic and clinical implications of hs-cTnT compared with cTnT in hospitalized patients with non-ACS presentations has not been well studied.

Rationale

Although hs-cTnT assays have been widely studied in the emergency department setting for suspected ACS, their implications in broader hospitalized populations where ACS is not the primary admitting diagnosis are less well known. In such patients, elevated troponin values may reflect a variety of acute or chronic conditions, including heart failure, pulmonary embolism, renal dysfunction, or critical illness. The introduction of hs-cTnT in these scenarios carries both opportunities and risks. For example, hs-cTnT may accelerate the exclusion of ACS, reduce diagnostic uncertainty, and shorten stays, thereby improving efficiency and resource use. On the other hand, detecting ambiguous elevations may drive unnecessary testing, invasive procedures, and higher costs without outcome benefit.

Therefore, evaluating the impact of hs-cTnT implementation on real-world clinical practice in non-ACS hospitalized patients is essential. This project sought to clarify whether hs-cTnT contributes to meaningful improvements in LOS, diagnostic use, and patient safety within an inpatient population outside traditional ACS pathways.

Project Description

This was a retrospective observational analysis of usual patient care. The analytic cohort comprised 520 consecutive adult inpatients who underwent troponin testing during hospitalization. Troponin testing was ordered at the discretion of the clinician as part of routine evaluations for nonspecific cardiopulmonary symptoms, systemic illness, or comorbidities. Patients admitted with ACS as the primary diagnosis were excluded to ensure the study addressed the non-ACS population. To minimize confounding, we performed 1:1 propensity score matching by age, sex, comorbidities, and admission presentation, which yielded 2 balanced cohorts (n = 260 patients each: cTnT vs hs-cTnT).

The primary outcomes were LOS, use of diagnostic testing including transthoracic echocardiography and cardiac catheterization, and in-hospital mortality. Secondary outcomes included the use of cardiac stress test and coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). Cardiac catheterization was defined as any diagnostic coronary angiography procedure, including cases in which percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) was performed.

Descriptive statistics summarized baseline characteristics. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range, as appropriate. Categorical variables were reported as frequencies with percentages. Between-group comparisons were performed with the Student t-test for continuous variables and the chi-square or Fisher exact test for categorical variables. Statistical significance was set at a 2-tailed P value of <0.05. All analyses were conducted using Stata 17 (StataCorp).

Project Deliverables

This project generated several key deliverables that advance both clinical understanding and institutional practice regarding hs-cTnT testing.

A validated dataset of 520 consecutively hospitalized patients was created, including granular demographic, clinical, and procedural details. Through propensity score matching, balanced cohorts of hs-cTnT and conventional cTnT patients (n = 260 for each) were established.

The study systematically assessed the use of diagnostic modalities, including echocardiography, cardiac catheterization, and stress test. This evaluation provided a detailed picture of how hs-cTnT implementation influences hospital resource allocation beyond traditional ACS populations. Key clinical outcomes, including LOS, in-hospital mortality, and secondary endpoints such as CABG use, were evaluated.

By integrating diagnostic performance with resource use and clinical outcomes, the project delivered actionable insights for hospitals considering hs-cTnT implementation. These findings contribute to quality improvement initiatives, inform guideline discussions, and provide a framework for balancing diagnostic accuracy with cost-effectiveness.

Together, these deliverables form a comprehensive analysis of the clinical and operational impact of hs-cTnT in hospitalized patients, positioning the project as both a local quality improvement initiative and a contribution to the broader literature.

Project Outcomes, Impact, and Future Directions

The implementation of hs-cTnT was associated with a significant reduction in LOS (median: 5.6 days vs 8.1 days for cTnT, P < 0.01) and reduced use of echocardiography (76.9% vs 88.8% for cTnT, P = 0.005). Electrophysiology studies were less common with hs-cTnT (2.3% vs 6.5% for cTnT, P = 0.033). Although cardiac catheterization was slightly more frequent in the hs-cTnT group (27.3% vs 23.1% for cTnT), this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.31). CABG was performed less often in the hs-cTnT group (1.9% vs 5.4% for cTnT), with a trend toward significance (P = 0.059). In-hospital mortality did not differ between groups. These findings suggest that hs-cTnT can enhance hospital efficiency by reducing diagnostic imaging and shortening length of stay, without compromising patient safety. Fewer electrophysiology studies occurred in the hs-cTnT group, likely incidentally, with no mechanistic link to assay type. The reduction in echocardiography with hs-cTnT highlights a shift toward more selective use of downstream testing, aligning with resource stewardship goals.

Looking forward, broader implementation of hs-cTnT could streamline inpatient care pathways and optimize resource allocation in non-ACS settings. Attention is needed to ensure that earlier detection of myocardial injury does not inadvertently increase invasive procedures in low-risk patients. Future research should focus on prospective validation in larger, multicenter cohorts and examine long-term outcomes, including readmissions and cost-effectiveness, to fully characterize the role of hs-cTnT in diverse inpatient populations.

Discussion

The present study evaluated the impact of hs-cTnT implementation in a hospitalized, non-ACS population. Unlike much of the existing literature, which has largely focused on emergency department cohorts with suspected ACS, our analysis deliberately excluded patients admitted for ACS to assess broader, real-world inpatient practice. We found that hs-cTnT implementation was associated with significant reductions in LOS and echocardiography use, along with lower use of electrophysiology studies, without affecting in-hospital mortality. These findings suggest that hs-cTnT may confer important efficiency and stewardship benefits even outside of traditional ACS pathways.

Our findings extend the literature evidence by demonstrating reductions in LOS and diagnostic resource use in a broader hospitalized cohort. The lower use of echocardiography may reflect greater diagnostic certainty and fewer equivocal troponin elevations necessitating imaging. Importantly, the in-hospital mortality rate did not differ between groups, supporting the safety of these reductions in testing.

These results have several practical implications. The observed reduction in LOS has direct operational relevance. Shorter hospitalizations improve bed availability, reduce costs, and enhance patient experience. More selective use of echocardiography and electrophysiology testing reflects a more judicious diagnostic strategy, aligning with principles of resource stewardship. The absence of increased mortality suggests that these efficiency gains do not compromise patient outcomes.

The nonsignificant trend toward higher rates of cardiac catheterization in the hs-cTnT group highlights a potential counterbalance. Highly sensitive assays may increase detection of subclinical or clinically ambiguous myocardial injury, potentially prompting more invasive evaluations. This underscores the need for careful interpretation in non-ACS contexts.

This study offers several strengths. It is among the few analyses to directly evaluate hs-cTnT in a non-ACS inpatient population, thereby filling an important evidence gap. The use of propensity score matching enhanced comparability between groups. Additionally, the study incorporated a comprehensive range of outcomes, spanning hospital efficiency, diagnostic resource use, and patient safety, enabling a holistic view of hs-cTnT implementation.

Several limitations warrant acknowledgment. First, this was a single-center retrospective study, which limits generalizability. Second, despite propensity score matching, unmeasured confounders (eg, provider decision-making, patient frailty) may have influenced results. Third, because troponin ordering was at clinician discretion during a transition period when both assays were available, indications for testing and assay selection may have varied, introducing potential selection bias. Although propensity score matching was performed, unmeasured confounding may remain.

Future research should include multicenter, prospective evaluations to confirm these findings across diverse practice settings. Longer term follow-up is also needed to assess whether reductions in LOS and diagnostic testing will translate to improved cost-effectiveness without compromising patient safety. Additionally, further work should investigate the diagnostic thresholds and interpretation strategies that optimize the value of hs-cTnT in non-ACS populations, potentially including integration with clinical risk scores or other biomarkers.

Conclusions

In this retrospective, propensity score–matched study of hospitalized non-ACS patients, implementation of hs-cTnT was associated with shorter length of stay, reduced echocardiography and electrophysiology testing, and no increased in-hospital mortality. These findings highlight the potential of hs-cTnT to improve hospital efficiency and resource stewardship in broader inpatient populations. Careful interpretation remains essential to avoid unnecessary invasive procedures. Prospective, multicenter studies are warranted to confirm these findings and to clarify the role of hs-cTnT in optimizing care pathways for diverse hospitalized patients.

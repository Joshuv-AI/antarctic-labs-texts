Orthogonal recognition of miRNA and lncRNA enables high-fidelity cancer diagnostics
Authors: Yiqun Guo, Jingkun Zhang, Liangyu Jiang, Huamei Yin, Xiankai Niu, Liang Shen, Yuna Guo, Yuancheng Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRecent advances in molecular diagnostics have highlighted the transformative potential of multi-protein response logic gates in enhancing sensitivity and specificity for cancer detection. However, logic gates based on multi-type nucleic acid responses remain underexplored. In this study, we present a dual-biomarker response platform that utilizes nucleic acid logic gates targeting miR-21 (microRNA-21) and lncRNA MALAT1 (metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1). Compared to traditional single-target methods, our platform effectively overcomes common challenges, such as expression variability and cross-reactivity, which are inherent in single biomarker approaches, thereby significantly improving both diagnostic sensitivity and specificity. This platform is based on rolling circle amplification (RCA) technology and incorporates two key components: a primer switch and a padlock probe switch. When miR-21 is recognized, a hairpin probe undergoes a shape change, releasing a primer that starts the RCA process. Simultaneously, a MALAT1-specific padlock probe forms a closed circular structure, which is necessary to activate RCA and increase signal detection. Furthermore, the RCA products contain G-quadruplex sequences that can bind to hemin, forming DNAzymes that produce an electrochemical signal. This strategy significantly enhances diagnostic sensitivity and specificity, demonstrating strong potential for early cancer detection and precision oncology. Subject terms: RNA, Medical and clinical diagnostics

Introduction

Recent advancements in molecular biology and molecular diagnostic technologies have opened up avenues for early disease diagnosis and personalized treatment, particularly in cancer detection and prognosis assessment1–3. MicroRNAs (miRNAs) and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have garnered significant attention as potential biomarkers4–8, playing crucial roles in tumorigenesis and development while providing tools for precise tumor diagnosis9–11. Detection platforms based on these molecules facilitate a deeper understanding of the molecular mechanisms underlying cancer and enhance the efficiency of clinical diagnostic strategies.

MicroRNA-21 (miR-21) is a well-studied “oncomiR” that is overexpressed in various malignancies12,13, including breast, lung, colorectal, and liver cancers14–18. By inhibiting tumor suppressor genes, miR-21 significantly promotes tumor cell proliferation, apoptosis resistance, and invasiveness. As a result, it is regarded as an important molecular marker in cancer diagnosis. However, miR-21’s functionality extends beyond tumors, as it is also highly expressed in various inflammatory responses19, especially in inflammatory diseases such as ulcerative colitis and rheumatoid arthritis, where its levels increase significantly20–22, modulating the inflammatory cascade in immune cells like macrophages and T cells23–25. MALAT1 (metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1) is a highly conserved lncRNA that plays a pivotal role in the metastatic and invasive processes of multiple tumors, including non-small cell lung cancer, breast cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and colorectal cancer. Its elevated expression is frequently associated with enhanced metastatic capacity and poor prognosis26–29. Although MALAT1 exhibits high cancer specificity, it may be upregulated in certain non-malignant tissues, such as during myogenesis, endothelial cell formation, and certain chronic inflammatory conditions30–32. Consequently, relying solely on single miR-21 or MALAT1 levels for cancer detection may yield false-positive results due to non-tumor factors. Against this backdrop, we aim to propose a dual-molecule reaction platform based on orthogonal nucleic acid logic gates, which triggers a signaling response only when both miR-21 and MALAT1 are simultaneously present.

To enhance the sensitivity of logic gates, DNA amplification technologies can significantly amplify target DNA fragments, facilitating the detection of low-abundance biomarkers and making them ideal alternatives. Common amplification methods include polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and isothermal amplification techniques33–37, such as loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and rolling circle amplification (RCA)38,39. The CRISPR-Cas system is also frequently used as an auxiliary tool for these amplification methods to facilitate amplification40,41. Although PCR is widely used in molecular detection applications, it requires precise thermal cycling equipment and may be susceptible to inhibitors present in complex biological samples, limiting its application in point-of-care diagnostics. Similarly, the CRISPR-Cas system, which uses RNA-guided Cas proteins to precisely cleave target DNA with high specificity, also requires complex experimental conditions and equipment, posing challenges for its use in real-time or field diagnostics42. In contrast, LAMP, an isothermal amplification technique, offers the advantages of simplified operation and no need for complex instruments, making it especially suitable for point-of-care testing. However, LAMP can be prone to primer dimer formation and nonspecific amplification, leading to potential false-positive results43. RCA is precisely what our orthogonal logic gate design requires, as it overcomes the limitations of the aforementioned amplification techniques. Composed of primers and a circular template, RCA delivers exceptional signal amplification and sensitivity. Moreover, the mechanism of RCA is uniquely compatible with constructing sophisticated logic gates37.

In this context, we propose a dual-biomarker orthogonal response RCA platform that triggers a signal response only when both miR-21 and MALAT1 are present simultaneously. This strategy effectively avoids false positives that may arise from relying on a single biomarker, significantly enhancing both diagnostic specificity and sensitivity. RCA necessitates the utilization of two probe components: a primer and a circular probe. In Scheme 1, we designed a hairpin probe with a sequence complementary to miR-21, along with the primer. When miR-21 is present, the hairpin probe opens, releasing the primer, which then binds to the padlock probe. Importantly, the padlock probe cannot independently open the hairpin structure, ensuring that the response is specific to miR-21. The interaction with lncRNA MALAT1 facilitates the convergence of the padlock probe ends, which are subsequently ligated by T4 RNA ligase to form a closed circular probe. The formation of this circular probe is necessary for initiating the RCA reaction, which produces extended DNA amplification products, thereby enhancing the specificity of the response to MALAT1. Throughout the amplification process, the strand-displacement effect allows MALAT1 to be released and reused, thereby perpetually activating the subsequent circular probe assembly. Ingeniously, we integrated complementary G-quadruplex sequences into the padlock probe, enriching the amplification products with a substantial quantity of G-quadruplex structures. In the presence of hemin, these sequences form G-quadruplex DNAzymes, which catalyze the production of hydrogen peroxide, subsequently generating an electrochemical signal. This innovative design markedly enhances both the detection sensitivity and the specificity of the system.

Comparison between traditional single-target response and our dual-target RNA-response platform. Traditional methods focus on detecting a single biomarker, such as miR-21 or lncRNA MALAT1. In contrast, our platform utilizes nucleic acid logic gates to simultaneously target both miR-21 and lncRNA MALAT1, triggering RCA only when both biomarkers are present. By incorporating G-quadruplex sequences, RCA products form DNAzymes in the presence of hemin, generating electrochemical signals that significantly enhance sensitivity for lung cancer diagnosis. This dual-target approach offers improved diagnostic specificity and sensitivity, making it suitable for early detection of lung cancer and other diseases.

The primary advantages of employing this dual biomarker orthogonal logic gate strategy are twofold. Firstly, it offers high specificity: the detection signal is activated exclusively when both miR-21 and MALAT1 are highly expressed, thereby mitigating the risk of false positives or negatives associated with reliance on a single biomarker. Secondly, it provides high sensitivity: the signal amplification effect of RCA substantially enhances the detection sensitivity of the system, facilitating the identification of low-abundance target molecules. This attribute is particularly advantageous for early cancer screening. The multiplex biomarker detection system offers a comprehensive approach to cancer diagnosis by concurrently identifying multiple biomarkers. This capability enhances the precision of diagnostic outcomes and assists clinicians in formulating personalized treatment plans. Looking ahead, this multiplex detection platform shows significant potential for clinical application, particularly in enabling early cancer screening, accurate diagnosis, and the development of personalized treatment strategies.

Results and discussion

Validation of rolling circle amplification and the HRP-mimetic activity of RCA products

To validate the successful implementation of the proposed scheme, we initially conducted in vitro experiments, including sodium dodecyl sulfate polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM), and ultraviolet-visible (UV-Vis) analyses. The SDS-PAGE results revealed distinct bands for the raw materials, located at anticipated positions, confirming the high purity of the components (Fig. 1A and Supplementary Fig. S1). When all probes were present simultaneously, a prominent band of large molecular weight with limited migration was observed, indicating the formation of rolling circle amplification (RCA) products with heterogeneous molecular sizes. A band around 110 bp corresponded to the MALAT1–padlock hybridization product (track e). In homogeneous reactions, the MALAT1–padlock product coexists with the RCA product, while in interface reactions, this by-product is efficiently washed away. Furthermore, the padlock binds exclusively to MALAT1, while hairpin probe (HP) binds only to miR-21, ensuring specificity for the two nucleic acid inputs (Supplementary Fig. S2). AFM analysis showed that the RCA products primarily consisted of long, linear, or circular structures with a height variation of ~7.5 nm, likely due to molecular intertwining during amplification (Fig. 1B). These findings confirm that RCA effectively produced high-molecular-weight products, validating the robustness and feasibility of our experimental approach.

In the UV-Vis analysis, the TMB (3,3’,5,5’-tetramethylbenzidine) chromogenic assay evaluated the HRP-mimetic activity of the RCA products, a well-established and extensively employed detection technique44,45. The findings demonstrated a significant increase in absorbance exclusively in the presence of RCA products, thereby confirming the catalytic activity of the HRP-mimetic enzyme (Fig. 1C, D). This observation suggests that miR-21 and MALAT1 successfully initiated the RCA reaction, culminating in the production of a substantial quantity of G-quadruplexes. These G-quadruplexes facilitated the oxidation of TMB, resulting in a pronounced colorimetric change. Conversely, other components, including padlock, MALAT1, HP, and miR-21, did not elicit significant alterations in absorbance when assessed individually, suggesting an absence of HRP-mimetic enzymatic activity.

Electrochemical characterization of layer-by-layer modified electrodes for biosensing applications

To confirm the effective construction of the electrochemical interface, we evaluated the electrochemical performance of various modified electrodes using cyclic voltammetry (CV) and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) (Fig. 1E, F). Initially, the CV analysis of the unmodified gold electrode revealed a pair of reversible redox peaks with substantial peak currents, indicating excellent conductivity and electrochemical activity. Following the surface modification with HP, a marked reduction in current response was observed, alongside a further decrease in the reversibility of the redox peaks. This phenomenon is attributed to the negative charges on the DNA backbone, which repel the negatively charged ferrocyanide, confirming the effective binding of the HP to the gold electrode through thiol groups. Subsequently, incorporating mercaptohexanol (MCH) led to a further reduction in CV peak current and a decrease in the reversibility of the redox peaks. This suggests that MCH effectively blocked nonspecific adsorption, thereby enhancing the specificity of subsequent steps. After further modifications with MALAT1, miR-21, padlock, and T4 RNA ligase, the CV peak current continued to decline, and the redox peak reversibility weakened, demonstrating the successful attachment of these molecules to the electrode surface. The negative charges they carry enhanced the electrode’s electrochemical impedance. Following the introduction of phi29 and dNTPs, the CV results showed a marked reduction in peak current and diminished reversibility, signifying the successful formation of RCA products and their significant impact on the electrochemical characteristics of the electrode. Simultaneously, EIS results indicated a gradual increase in electrode impedance as the layer-by-layer modification progressed, consistent with the trends observed in the CV data, further confirming the successful execution of the modifications. Collectively, these findings illustrate the successful development of a stepwise modified electrode.

Optimization of experimental conditions

To enhance the electrochemical performance of the sensor, we optimized various experimental parameters. We systematically evaluated the effects of different reaction conditions, identifying reaction temperature, incubation time, and buffer selection as key factors influencing sensor performance. Regarding reaction temperature (Supplementary Fig. S3A, S3B), the current response increased with rising temperature up to 37 °C, and then decreased as the temperature exceeded 37 °C. Therefore, 37 °C was selected as the optimal reaction temperature for our electrochemical platform. For incubation time (Supplementary Fig. S4A, S4B), the current response gradually increased with longer incubation times, but plateaued after 2 h. Thus, 2 h was chosen as the optimal incubation time. In buffer selection (Supplementary Fig. S5A, S5B), commonly used biocompatible buffers, including PBS, HEPES, TBS, and Phi 29 buffers, were tested. We found that Phi 29 buffer provided the highest current response, so it was selected as the optimal buffer for our platform.

Validation of RNA orthogonal logic gate functionality

After the successful layer-by-layer modification of the electrode, the functionality of the DNA logic gate was further validated using differential pulse voltammetry (DPV) and UV-Vis spectroscopy. The presence of both miR-21 and MALAT1 triggered the logic gate to yield the anticipated output signal (Fig. 2A). The G-quadruplex structures generated from RCA products complexed with hemin to form a G4/hemin DNAzyme. This DNAzyme catalyzed the reduction of hydrogen peroxide, yielding a substantial electrochemical signal during DPV experiments (Fig. 2B, C). Additionally, the UV-Vis spectroscopic analysis demonstrated a pronounced color change, indicating successful oxidation of TMB, which further confirmed the effectiveness and specificity of the reaction (Fig. 2D, E). In contrast, the presence of miR-21 alone resulted in a signal that was nearly indistinguishable from the blank control. This absence of signal can be attributed to the lack of MALAT1, which is necessary for the padlock probe to form a closed loop, thereby inhibiting the initiation of the subsequent RCA reaction. Similarly, when only MALAT1 was present, the signal did not significantly differ from the blank control. This suggests that the hairpin structure could not be opened, leading to the absence of primer release and consequently halting further reactions. The findings indicate that the system demonstrates high specificity towards input signals, activating the logic gate’s response exclusively in the presence of both miR-21 and MALAT1.

Validation of the specificity, stability, and reproducibility of the dual RNA orthogonal logic gate

To validate the specificity of the detection platform, a series of non-target RNAs (miR-122, let-7a, let-7b, miR-126, and lncRNA HOTAIR) were selected as controls. These are biologically relevant RNAs commonly found in clinical samples and serve as excellent controls for nonspecific background signals46–50. Figure 3A illustrates the working principle underlying the specificity of the detection method. When non-target RNA replaces MALAT1, its sequence fails to hybridize with the padlock probe, preventing the formation of a closed circular structure and thereby inhibiting the initiation of the RCA reaction. Similarly, when nonspecific RNA replaces miR-21, the HP cannot recognize the non-target sequence, leading to no conformational change and failure to release the primer, thus precluding RCA activation. The experimental results further confirm this design principle. A significant electrochemical signal, represented by the peak current (Ip), was observed only in the presence of the target miR-21 and MALAT1. In contrast, signals for all non-target RNAs remained close to the baseline, demonstrating that nonspecific interference was effectively suppressed (Fig. 3B). These findings unequivocally demonstrate the high specificity of the detection method in nucleic acid recognition. This exceptional specificity is primarily attributed to the design strategy of the detection platform: the detection of miR-21 and MALAT1 relies on separate probes to independently trigger the RCA reaction. Signal generation occurs only when both targets are accurately recognized. This dual-validation mechanism not only ensures the rigor of the detection process but also significantly reduces false-positive and false-negative rates, delivering highly reliable results. We evaluated the stability of the sensor from two perspectives: testing with a known concentration of target molecules and assessing its performance with clinical samples. The electrochemical sensor was stored and tested at various time points using 1 μM of miR-21 and MALAT1 (Supplementary Fig. S6) or lung cancer samples (Supplementary Fig. S7), with continuous monitoring of its performance. Remarkably, even after 10 days of storage, the current response showed minimal change, with a decrease of less than 10%. These results indicate that our sensor demonstrates exceptional stability, making it highly suitable for long-term use and storage, with reliable performance in both controlled and clinical settings. To evaluate the reproducibility, we tested multiple sensors fabricated under identical conditions using 1 μM concentrations of miR-21 and MALAT1 (Supplementary Fig. S8A–C), as well as in practical applications with clinical samples (Supplementary Fig. S8D–F). The results demonstrated that the sensors generated highly consistent response patterns under the same experimental conditions, with minimal variation. The relative standard deviation (RSD) was less than 5.36% (Supplementary Tables S2, S3), confirming the exceptional reproducibility of our electrochemical platform. These findings highlight the reliability of the platform across different experimental setups and real-world applications.

Standard curve analysis for miRNA-21 and lncRNA MALAT1 detection

To further evaluate the robustness and linear range of this method, we established a series of gradient concentrations of miR-21 and MALAT1 ranging from 0 nM to 104 nM and measured their electrochemical responses using the DPV method. When the concentrations of the two targets remain equal. As illustrated in Fig. 3C, the current signal exhibited a gradual increase corresponding to the rising concentrations of miR-21 and MALAT1. This change is primarily attributed to the direct proportionality between the quantity of G-quadruplexes generated by the RCA reaction and the concentration of the target analyte. Figure 3D specifically depicts the correlation between the concentrations of miR-21 and MALAT1 and the resulting current signal, demonstrating both logarithmic and linear increases across an extensive dynamic range of 0.1 to 103 nM (Supplementary Table S4). The linear equation for the standard curve: Ip (μA) = 1.61logcmiR-21/MALAT1 - 1.62, exhibiting a highly significant correlation coefficient (R2 = 0.99). Utilizing the 3σ criterion for signal-to-noise ratio determination, the limit of detection (LOD) for both miR-21 and MALAT1 is established at a sensitive threshold of 28 pM. Moreover, owing to the amplification effect of RCA, the response signals of target RNA in the ultra-low abundance range were clearly distinguishable from the blank signals (Supplementary Fig. S9), verifying the platform’s ability to identify trace miRNAs relevant to early cancer diagnosis. In the absorbance response measured by UV-Vis spectroscopy, a good linear correlation between absorbance and the logarithm of miRNA concentration was still demonstrated (Supplementary Fig. S10). The linear equation of the standard curve was: Absorbance (a.u.) = 0.039logcmiRNA - 0.062. The LOD is calculated to be 42 pM. Both modes exhibited highly consistent qualitative and quantitative trends, highlighting the multifunctionality and analytical robustness of the detection platform.

When the concentrations of the two targets differ, our biosensor can still detect and generate accurate signals. We maintained one target at a constant saturation level (1 μM) while varying the concentration of the other target (Supplementary Fig. S11A–D). The current gradually increased with rising concentrations of either miR-21 or MALAT1, maintaining strong correlation. The linear equations for the calibration curves are Ip (μA) = 0.84logcmiR-21 + 0.35 and Ip (μA) = 1.16logcMALAT1 + 0.96. This broad range highlights the robustness of the proposed electrochemical sensor, rendering it suitable for detecting varying levels of these biomarkers. Even when one target concentration is low, our system generates output due to RCA implementation, significantly mitigating false negatives. Regardless of how high one target concentration may be, the system remains impervious to signal generation if the other target is absent or present at extremely low levels. Nonspecific activation does not occur, thereby avoiding false positives. In authentic clinical samples, the expression levels of miR-21 and MALAT1 exhibit immense variability. Our sensor delivers precise and rigorous analysis within complex environments, establishing it as a potent tool for detecting intricate biomarker profiles within patient samples.

Practical application of dual RNA orthogonal logic gate: precise differentiation of tumor cell types

Following the establishment of the standard curve, we proceeded to evaluate the practical application of this method in actual samples. Four normal cell lines (BEAS-2B, HBE135-E6E7, MRC-5 and WI-38) and eight lung cancer cell lines (H526, H266, H1299, H460, HCC827, H1975, A549 and H69) were selected as models to preliminarily investigate the method’s ability to distinguish tumor cells from normal cells. As demonstrated in Fig. 4A, B, the output signals from the two cell groups exhibited distinct differences. Signal peaks generated by normal cells consistently remained below 2 μA. In stark contrast, peak signals obtained from all lung cancer cell lines uniformly exceeded 4 μA, substantially surpassing the 2 μA threshold. This demonstrates a clear separation between the two groups with no overlap. We established a 2 μA signal threshold for robust differentiation between cancerous and normal samples. Furthermore, testing with lung cancer cells alongside normal cells from other tissues (such as HEK293T) yielded comparable output results (Supplementary Fig. S12A). These findings demonstrate the high specificity of our dual RNA orthogonal logic gate system in identifying tumor cells. Further extending the study, we analyzed A549, HT-29, and their corresponding normal lung fibroblasts (HFL1) and normal colonic epithelial cells (HCoEpiC). Our method successfully differentiated A549-HFL1 and HT-29-HCoEpiC cell pairs, demonstrating its broad applicability and clinical diagnostic potential (Fig. 4C, D). Importantly, this strategy demonstrates exceptional capability in distinguishing tumor types with overlapping biomarker profiles. While both A549 and HT-29 cells exhibited high miR-21 expression (Fig. 4E, F and Supplementary Fig. S12B), the electrochemical response of HT-29 cells was significantly weaker due to their markedly lower MALAT1 levels (Fig. 4G, H and Supplementary Fig. S12C). This finding underscores the robustness of our dual RNA orthogonal logic gate system in accurately differentiating distinct malignancies, even in cases where reliance on a single biomarker would be insufficient for precise classification.

Given the upregulation of miR-21 and MALAT1 in non-malignant tissues, relying solely on a single biomarker for cancer diagnosis carries the risk of false positives. We treated normal MRC-5 and WI-38 cells with transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1) and lipopolysaccharide (LPS), respectively, to mimic the upregulation of these RNAs observed during inflammation51,52. As depicted in Supplementary Fig. S13A, B, single RNA assays for miR-21 and MALAT1 yielded pronounced false-positive signals upon exposure to these inflammatory stimuli. In stark contrast, the double RNA-response assay maintained a minimal signal under the same conditions (Supplementary Fig. S13C). This marked distinction highlights a key advantage of our AND-gate logic system: it effectively eliminates false positives arising from nonspecific elevation of a single biomarker, thereby significantly enhancing diagnostic reliability in complex biological environments.

Practical application of the dual RNA orthogonal logic gate in tumor diagnostic performance

This study aims to evaluate the diagnostic performance of a dual-target response logic gate method based on miR-21 and MALAT1 in lung cancer samples and compare it with traditional single-target response methods. To this end, we performed systematic electrochemical analysis on lysed lung cancer samples (Fig. 5A) and compared our developed dual-target response logic gate method (Fig. 5F, G) with the traditional miR-21 single-target response (Fig. 5B, C) and MALAT1 single-target response (Fig. 5D, E) methods. The results indicated that although the traditional single-target response methods showed some ability to distinguish between tumor and healthy groups, the differences between these groups did not reach the ideal diagnostic threshold. In contrast, the dual-target response method exhibited a significant advantage in differentiating between the tumor and healthy groups (Fig. 5H). This suggests that the dual-target strategy, by leveraging the synergistic effect between miR-21 and MALAT1, can effectively enhance the sensitivity and specificity of detection, overcoming the limitations of single biomarker methods. Meanwhile, the ROC curve (Fig. 5I) revealed an AUC value of 0.92 for our dual-RNA-response assay, compared to AUC values of 0.66 and 0.67 for the miR-21-response assay and the MALAT1-response assay, respectively. This indicates that our dual-RNA-response assay exhibited the highest clinical diagnostic efficacy. To further validate the platform’s diagnostic feasibility in actual clinical samples, we also conducted validation using clinical pleural effusion samples. Results demonstrated that our platform can clearly distinguish tumor-associated pleural effusion from non-tumor effusion (Supplementary Fig. S14). Furthermore, in serum spiked recovery experiments, our dual-target response platform exhibited stable and reliable signal responses in serum detection (Supplementary Fig. S15A, S15B), with recovery rates ranging from 96.5% to 106.3% (Supplementary Fig. S15C). These findings demonstrate the robust application value of our methodology in circulating RNA detection and early non-invasive diagnosis. When determining miR-21 and MALAT1 levels in healthy controls and tumor patients, qRT-PCR demonstrated good correlation with electrochemical readings, confirming the applicability of this sensing principle for miRNA detection. As shown in Supplementary Fig. S16, a significant positive correlation exists between the two methods, indicating that our platform’s results are highly consistent with qRT-PCR measurements.

Therefore, the dual-target reaction orthogonal logic gate method proposed in this study offers a more accurate detection strategy for the early diagnosis of lung cancer. Compared with traditional single-target detection methods, our system significantly enhances the ability to distinguish between tumor and healthy samples, demonstrating exceptionally high specificity and addressing the shortcomings of single-target approaches. This detection strategy not only offers potential clinical value for the early diagnosis of lung cancer but also provides fresh perspectives for the future development and application of diagnostic methods based on multiple biomarkers.

Conclusion

In this study, we developed a dual molecular response platform utilizing rolling circle amplification (RCA) and orthogonal nucleic acid logic gates to improve the specificity and sensitivity of molecular detection for potential cancer diagnostics. By designing a hairpin-structured probe responsive to microRNA-21 (miR-21) and a padlock probe specific to lncRNA MALAT1 (metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1), the platform activates signal amplification only when both biomarkers are present. This dual-response system significantly reduces the likelihood of false-positive and false-negative results, ensuring high diagnostic accuracy. Furthermore, the integration of G-quadruplex sequences within the RCA products and the formation of G-quadruplex DNAzymes enhances detection sensitivity, enabling precise signal amplification even at low target concentrations. This platform also demonstrates significant potential for clinical translation, particularly for point-of-care (POC) applications. Its RCA strategy exhibits inherent compatibility with miniaturised electrochemical or optical detection devices, enabling implementation in bedside testing scenarios. Despite these advantages, several practical challenges remain for clinical deployment, such as optimising RCA conditions to reduce incubation times and validating performance through large-scale clinical cohorts. Moving forward, our research will focus on expanding the platform’s capabilities to enable multiplex detection of biomarkers across multiple cancer types, thereby providing innovative approaches and concepts for molecular diagnostics.

Methods

Preparation of RCA products and SDS-PAGE analysis

Initially, RCA products were synthesized in a centrifuge tube. A reaction mixture containing 20 µL of T4 RNA ligase buffer, 500 nM microRNA-21 (miR-21), 500 nM MALAT1 (metastasis-associated lung adenocarcinoma transcript 1), 500 nM padlock probe, 500 nM hairpin probe (HP), and 40 U µL−1 T4 RNA ligase was incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to facilitate the ligation reaction. Next, 1 µL of phi29 DNA polymerase (10 U µL−1), 1 µL of dNTPs (10 mM), and 2 µL of 10× phi29 DNA polymerase buffer were added to the reaction mixture. This mixture was thoroughly homogenized and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to facilitate RCA. The reaction was then terminated by heating at 95 °C for 10 min to inactivate the phi29 DNA polymerase, culminating in the generation of RCA products. Subsequently, the RCA products and associated probes, including the padlock (1 µM), MALAT1 (5 µM), HP (1 µM), and miR-21 (5 µM), were each diluted in a 5:1 ratio with the loading buffer and thoroughly mixed. A 10% polyacrylamide SDS-PAGE gel was prepared, and 1× TBE buffer was added to the electrophoresis chamber. The diluted samples were then carefully loaded into the appropriate wells of the gel, ensuring proper distribution between control and experimental groups. Electrophoresis was performed at 100 V until the dye front reached the predetermined position. Finally, the gel was stained using GelRed dye, and the results were documented using the Bio-Rad Chem Doc XRS imaging system.

Colorimetric detection of RCA products

RCA products were synthesized in a centrifuge tube. The reaction mixture contained 20 µL of T4 RNA ligase buffer, 500 nM miR-21, 500 nM MALAT1, 500 nM padlock probe, 500 nM HP, and 40 U µL−1 T4 RNA ligase. The mixture was incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to facilitate the ligation reaction. Next, 1 µL of phi29 DNA polymerase (10 U µL−1), 1 µL of dNTPs (10 mM), and 2 µL of 10× phi29 DNA polymerase buffer were added to the reaction mixture. The solution was thoroughly mixed and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to carry out RCA. The reaction was terminated by heating at 95 °C for 10 min to inactivate the phi29 DNA polymerase, yielding RCA products. Subsequently, 2 µL of 5 mM hemin in DMSO was added to the reaction mixture, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 30 min. To the reaction mixture, 100 µL of a solution containing 0.4 mM TMB and 0.2 mM H2O2 was added, and the mixture was incubated at 37 °C for another 30 min. After incubation, colorimetric analysis was performed to monitor the oxidation of TMB, and the UV-Vis absorbance spectra were recorded using a UV-3600 spectrophotometer.

Preparation of the electrochemical sensor

First, AuE was immersed in a Piranha solution (3:1 mixture of 98% H2SO4 and 30% H2O2) for at least 30 min to remove surface impurities. The AuE was then thoroughly rinsed with ultrapure water to ensure a clean surface. Prior to sensor preparation, the AuE was polished with 0.3 µm and 0.05 µm alumina slurries for 5 min each. Following this, the electrode was subjected to ultrasonic treatment in water, ethanol, and water again for 5 min each to eliminate any residual alumina particles. Next, the AuE underwent electrochemical cleaning in a fresh 0.5 M H2SO4 solution, applying a continuous scan between −0.3 V and 1.55 V to enhance the electrochemical activity of the electrode. After cleaning, the AuE was dried using nitrogen gas and was immediately ready for probe immobilization. Before immobilizing the probe, a HP (10 µL, 10 µM) was mixed with 0.2 µL of 500 mM TCEP in a microcentrifuge tube and incubated at 37 °C for 120 min to reduce the disulfide bonds of SH-HP. This mixture was then diluted with 1× PBS to a final volume of 100 µL and incubated at room temperature in the dark with the cleaned AuE for 2 h to ensure effective probe attachment. After immobilization, the electrode was washed three times with 1× PBS, followed by blocking the electrode surface with a 1 mM MCH solution for 2 h to prevent nonspecific binding. Afterward, any excess MCH was washed away with ultrapure water. Subsequently, 8 µL of a solution containing 500 nM miR-21, 500 nM MALAT1, and 500 nM padlock along with 40 U µL−1 T4 RNA ligase in T4 RNA ligase buffer was applied to the electrode and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to facilitate the ligation reaction. Following this, a solution containing phi29 DNA polymerase (1 μL, 10 U μL−1), dNTPs (1 μL, 10 mM), and 10× phi29 DNA polymerase buffer (1 μL) was added to the electrode. The mixture was thoroughly mixed and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to facilitate the RCA amplification reaction. Finally, the electrode was rinsed three times with 1× PBS, and 10 µL of PBS containing 50 µM hemin was added and incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. The electrochemical characterization of the layer-by-layer modified electrodes was performed using CV and EIS. CV measurements were conducted in a 5 mM [Fe(CN)₆]3-/4- solution containing 0.1 M KCl at a scan rate of 100 mV s−1. EIS was performed in the samepbs solution, with a frequency range of 0.01 Hz to 100 kHz. DPV measurements were carried out under optimized conditions, with a voltage range of −0.5 V to −0.2 V and a scan rate of 50 mV s−1 in a PBS solution containing 1 mM H2O2.

Electrochemical verification of AND logic gate functionality

The AuE was modified as previously described, and four groups of experiments were conducted to verify the functionality of the AND logic gate by varying the presence of miR-21 and MALAT1. In all groups, after probe immobilization and MCH blocking, 8 µL of the ligation mixture containing 500 nM padlock probe, 40 U µL−1 T4 RNA ligase, and T4 RNA ligase buffer was applied to the modified electrodes. The target concentrations of miR-21 and MALAT1 varied as follows: (1) PBS, (2) 500 nM miR-21, (3) 500 nM MALAT1, and (4) 500 nM miR-21 and 500 nM MALAT1. The electrodes were incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to facilitate the ligation reaction. Subsequently, a solution containing phi29 DNA polymerase (1 μL, 10 U μL−1), dNTPs (1 μL, 10 mM), and 10× phi29 DNA polymerase buffer (1 μL) was added to the electrode and incubated at 37 °C for 2 h to conduct RCA. After RCA, the electrodes were washed with 1× PBS and incubated with 10 µL of PBS containing 50 µM hemin at 37 °C for 30 min. Finally, the electrodes were rinsed with PBS and prepared for electrochemical measurements to evaluate the logic gate functionality.

UV-Vis spectrophotometric verification of AND logic gate functionality

To verify the functionality of the AND logic gate, four groups of experiments were conducted using different combinations of miR-21 and MALAT1. The RCA reaction was performed as described above, and the reaction mixtures for each group varied as follows: (1) PBS, (2) 500 nM miR-21, (3) 500 nM MALAT1, and (4) 500 nM miR-21 and 500 nM MALAT1. After RCA amplification and the addition of hemin, TMB, and H2O2, the reaction mixtures were incubated at 37 °C for 30 min. The colorimetric analysis was then performed by measuring the oxidation of TMB, and the absorbance spectra were recorded using a UV-3600 spectrophotometer to evaluate the logic gate’s functionality.

Generation of standard curve

To establish a standard curve for the detection of miR-21 and MALAT1, the electrochemical sensor was prepared as described above. Following the sensor preparation, different concentrations of miR-21 and MALAT1 were applied to the modified electrode, ranging from 0 pM to 10 pM, 100 pM, 1000 pM, 10,000 pM, 100,000 pM, 1,000,000 pM, and 1 0,000,000 pM. Each concentration was incubated with 500 nM padlock probe and 40 U µL−1 T4 RNA ligase in T4 RNA ligase buffer at 37 °C for 2 h to promote the ligation reaction. Subsequently, RCA amplification was performed with phi29 DNA polymerase, followed by the addition of 50 µM hemin in PBS. After incubation, the electrochemical response was measured, and the resulting current values were plotted to generate a standard curve, which would be used to assess the sensitivity and linearity of the sensor in detecting miR-21 and MALAT1.

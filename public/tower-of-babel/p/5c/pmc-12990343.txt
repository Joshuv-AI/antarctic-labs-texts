Optimized protocol for dissociation of mouse intestinal epithelium to viable single-cells
Authors: Ni Zhao, Peiling Geng, Yong Ge
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe functional profiling of intestinal epithelial cells (IECs), which comprise multiple heterogeneous populations, is crucial for understanding host interactions with dietary and microbial factors. Here, we present a protocol for dissociating mouse epithelium based on TrypLE Express and Dispase II. We describe steps for tissue collection, crypt isolation, and enzyme digestion. This protocol enables the recovery of millions of viable IECs from the mouse intestine and is particularly useful for isolating rare cell populations, including enterochromaffin (EC) cells.Subject areas: Cell isolation, Single Cell, Sequencing

Before you begin

This protocol describes the required steps for the isolation of IECs, including EC cells, from mouse colon. The same protocol has also been used for isolating ileum, jejunum, and duodenum epithelial cells at homeostasis as well as during intestinal Salmonella Typhimurium infection with comparable outputs.

This protocol is specifically optimized for the isolation of intestinal epithelial cells and is not suitable for isolating immune cells.

Mouse strain selection will depend on the experiment. We have extensively analyzed 8-14 weeks old wild-type C57BL/6J mice and Chga-GFP mice (an EC cell reporter strain).

Innovations

This protocol employs the static incubation with a low concentration of EDTA, followed by tissue transfer to cold PBS for cell dissociation using a fixed number of vortexing cycles to ensure cell viability and reproducibility. A stepwise digestion procedure with TrypLE Express and Dispase II was used to improve cell viability and efficiency of single-cell dissociation.

Expected outcomes

The protocol should yield >6 million single cells with >85% cell viability from the colon and >10 million single cells with >90% cell viability from the duodenum of 8–14 weeks old mice, when examined using a cell counter with live and dead cell staining (Figure 1B).

After excluding cell debris and doublets, the viability of isolated IECs consistently exceeded >95%, as determined by FACS (Figures 2A and 2B). The successful cell isolation and FACS sorting should yield >2.0 million IECs from the whole colon and >2.5 million IECs from the whole ileum of 8–14 weeks old mice, and these yields were over 150 times and 20 times higher than those obtained from the corresponding tissues using the original method2 (Figure 2C).

Limitations

For Steps 14,16, 19 and 21, each sample needs to be pipetted up and down many times. It’s important to keep the TrypLE and Dispase digestion time within 30 min (from adding enzyme mixtures to stopping reactions by adding cold HF). If >10 samples are processed at a time, additional personnel may be needed to assist with pipetting to stay within the time limit.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Undissolved particles are observed in Dispase stock solution after thawing and warming (related to Step 19).

Potential solution

The Dispase stock solution should be clear before storage at −20°C. If any undissolved particles are observed after thawing and warming, centrifuge at 2000 × g for 1 minute and use only the clear supernatant for experiments.

Problem 2

Failure to obtain single cells after Dispase digestion (related to Step 19).

Potential solution

Dispase II powder should be stored at 2°C–8°C and protected from moisture. Once opened, seal tightly with parafilm. Dispase stock solution should be stored in aliquots to avoid repeated freeze–thaw cycles and can be stored for up to 6 months at −20°C.

Problem 3

Low cell viability.

Potential solution

•Complete the tissue processing procedure as soon as possible after tissue collection.

Complete the tissue processing procedure as soon as possible after tissue collection.

•Except during TrypLE and Dispase enzymatic digestion steps, keep all buffers and samples at 4°C or on ice as indicated.

Except during TrypLE and Dispase enzymatic digestion steps, keep all buffers and samples at 4°C or on ice as indicated.

•Sort cells immediately after staining, and ensure that both the sample chamber and collection area are maintained at 4°C during cell sorting.

Sort cells immediately after staining, and ensure that both the sample chamber and collection area are maintained at 4°C during cell sorting.

Problem 4

Jejunum and duodenum tissues are damaged.

Potential solution

The jejunum and duodenum are more fragile than the ileum and colon, so wash these tissues gently. Because they yield approximately twice as many IECs as the ileum or colon, proportionally more antibody may be required for FACS staining.

Problem 5

Repeated clogging during IEC sorting (related to Step 30).

Potential solution

Resuspended cells are prone to clumping and rapid settling. Gently pipette up and down 5–10 times to ensure complete and uniform resuspension before loading cells into the cell sorter. Vortexing alone is not sufficient to achieve even resuspension of epithelial cells.

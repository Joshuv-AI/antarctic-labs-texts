Enhancing 7T MRI for deep brain stimulation with deep-learning based image reconstruction and dynamic parallel transmission
Authors: Justyna O. Ekert, Vishal Patel, Xiangzhi Zhou, Shengzhen Tao, Patrick Liebig, Jürgen Herrler, Thomas Yu, Dominik Nickel, Gian Franco Piredda, Erin M. Westerhold, Vivek Gupta, Sanjeet S. Grewal, Erik H. Middlebrooks
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Graphical abstract

Introduction

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is a widely used neuromodulatory treatment for a range of neurological disorders, including Parkinson's disease, essential tremor, obsessive–compulsive disorder, Tourette's syndrome, dystonia, and many more. The success of DBS surgery relies on the precise placement of electrodes and optimal stimulation programming. With an increasing understanding of DBS effects and the development of network-based neuromodulation strategies, MRI has become a crucial tool for accurate targeting and the growing adoption of image-guided programming. The utilization of 7 Tesla (7T) MRI in DBS surgery has continued to increase due to improved signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), spatial resolution, and tissue contrast. The enhanced imaging capability has resulted in more accurate microelectrode recordings, more precise DBS placement, and superior surgical outcomes in comparison to conventional MRI (Duchin et al., 2018, Gunalan et al., 2017, Isaacs et al., 2020, Jorge et al., 2020, Kanowski et al., 2014, Lenglet et al., 2012, Maruyama et al., 2019, Mathiopoulou et al., 2023, Middlebrooks et al., 2024, Patriat et al., 2018, Patriat et al., 2024, Plantinga et al., 2014, Tao et al., 2022, Tourdias et al., 2014, Xiao et al., 2016). Despite these advantages, significant challenges remain regarding the routine clinical application of 7T MRI.

One of the primary challenges in 7T MRI is the heterogeneity of the radiofrequency (RF) transmit field (B1+) resulting in heterogeneity in signal and tissue contrast throughout the brain (Haast et al., 2021, Tao et al., 2023, Tao et al., 2022). In addition, ultra-high spatial resolutions can lead to substantial increases in scan time, which also predisposes to patient motion. Two emerging innovations in 7T MRI hold the potential to address these issues. The first is deep-learning (DL) based image reconstruction, shown to allow unprecedented image acceleration rates in MRI. The second is dynamic parallel RF transmission (pTx), which uses complex combinations of waveforms from multiple RF transmitters to mitigate B1+ transmit heterogeneity. Combined, these technologies stand to potentially revolutionize clinical 7T imaging (Middlebrooks et al., 2026).

Recently, multicenter recommendations were developed for 7T DBS targeting protocols that aimed at overcoming common challenges using currently available techniques (Middlebrooks et al., 2025a). In this technical note, we provide an overview of pTx and DL-based reconstruction, introduce state-of-the-art protocols for DBS targeting using these new technologies, and highlight how these advancements present potential improvements over previous protocol recommendations.

Methods

Background: DL-based image reconstruction

DL has been utilized in differing applications in MR image enhancement. First, DL can provide enhancement of image quality in the reconstructed images themselves (such as that employed in enhancement of photographic images). Alternatively, DL can be used to reconstruct the raw k-space data. While both approaches can be effective in improving image quality, k-space reconstruction models may be less susceptible to unpredictable behavior since they ensure consistency with the acquired raw measurements and are trained to model noise and artifacts that are inherent to specific acquisition schemes. However, differing tissue contrast, resolution, and unique factors such as heightened B1+ and B0 heterogeneity at 7T introduce unique challenges to data reconstruction using DL at 7T. In recent work, a DL-based reconstruction prototype was assessed at 7T that resulted in significant improvement in image quality compared to existing state-of-the-art reconstruction methods (Liu et al., 2025a, Liu et al., 2025b). The high acceleration factors allowed by DL-based reconstruction can facilitate substantial reductions in scan time, improved image quality, and/or improved spatial resolution. In this study, we implemented DL-based reconstruction to enhance imaging for DBS targeting with the hypothesis that scan time and/or resolution can be significantly improved while also providing better image quality.

Background: parallel RF transmission

Heterogeneity of the B1+ transmit field is one of the most problematic issues in routine clinical 7T imaging. At 7T, the RF wavelength is approximately 12 cm, which is shorter than the average human head resulting in constructive and destructive interference across the head that leads to severe variations in signal and contrast across the image (Haast et al., 2021, Tao et al., 2023, Tao et al., 2022). Some currently available strategies to improve B1+ include the use of dielectric pads (Fagan et al., 2019) or B1+ shimming (Shao et al., 2020); however, the performance is variable and has fallen short of enabling routine clinical use of 7T across populations of clinical patients. Dynamic pTx uses multiple independent RF transmit elements to modulate amplitude, phase, and timing controls in each independent element to ideally create a more uniform excitation field. To date, the adoption of dynamic pTx has been limited due to long calibration and optimization times, challenges with safety monitoring for local temperature increases (“hot spots”), and other factors. However, recent technological advances have ultimately led to the approval of dynamic pTx from the United States Food and Drug Administration in 2024, but limited in implementation to a 3D gradient echo (GRE) sequence.

More recently, fast online-customized (FOCUS) pulses have been introduced that use a similar pre-computed pulse design that is subsequently optimized for each individual patient through rapid B0 and B1+ map acquisition and efficient online optimization process that can all be completed in approximately 1 min (Herrler et al., 2021, Middlebrooks et al., 2025b). The benefits of this approach are the combination of patient-specific pulse design using a time-efficient optimization that can be easily deployed in clinical workflows. The FOCUS pulse has been shown to produce significant improvement in image homogeneity across a large clinical patient cohort (Middlebrooks et al., 2025b).

In this study, we implemented patient-specific, dynamic pTx into several standard DBS sequences, including fast gray matter acquisition T1 inversion recovery (FGATIR), magnetization-prepared two rapid acquisition gradient-echo (MP2RAGE), T2-weighted sampling perfection with application optimized contrast using different flip angle evolution (SPACE), and 3D spoiled GRE, in order to improve image homogeneity. pTx was not implemented in susceptibility-weighted imaging (SWI) due to relative insensitivity of this sequence to B1+ variations. We hypothesized that dynamic pTx would further improve the consistency of tissue contrast and target visualization versus traditional single transmit (sTx) with dielectric pads.

Image quality comparison

The enhanced protocol was implemented on a 7T Siemens MAGNETOM Terra.X (Siemens Healthineers, Erlangen, Germany) using an 8-channel transmit and 32-channel receive head coil (Nova Medical Inc., Wilmington, MA). A retrospective analysis was performed comparing image quality between a previously recommended multi-institutional DBS targeting protocol for 7T (Middlebrooks et al., 2025a) and an enhanced protocol integrating DL-based reconstructions and dynamic pTx. We evaluated the standard protocol in 13 consecutive patients undergoing 7T MRI prior to DBS implantation. Next, another 13 consecutive DBS patients were evaluated after implementation of the enhanced protocol.

Each sequence was evaluated by a board-certified functional neurosurgeon (S.G.) and neuroradiologist (E.H.M.) experienced in 7T imaging for DBS. Each reader independently assessed the image quality using a 5-point Likert scale, including overall image quality (1 = nondiagnostic, 2 = substantial deficits in image quality, 3 = moderate image quality, 4 = good image quality, 5 = excellent image quality), motion artifact (1 = nondiagnostic, 2 = substantial impact on diagnosis, 3 = moderate impact on diagnosis, 4 = minimal impact on diagnosis, 5 = no impact on diagnosis), and target conspicuity (1 = unidentifiable, 2 = difficulties in delineating anatomic structures, 3 = seen with poorly defined edges, 4 = well seen with poorly defined edges, 5 = well seen with well-defined edges). Images were presented in a random order, and readers were blinded to demographic data and the protocol type.

The subthalamic nucleus (STN) was evaluated on the T2-weighted SPACE and SWI, mammillothalamic tract (MMT) on the MP2RAGE T1-weighted uniform image, and the globus pallidus interna (GPi) and dentato-rubro-thalamic tract (DRTT) (Middlebrooks et al., 2022, Middlebrooks et al., 2024, Neudorfer et al., 2022) on FGATIR. Vessel conspicuity was also evaluated on a high-bandwidth post-contrast 3D spoiled GRE that was also acquired for registration to CT, as previously recommended (Middlebrooks et al., 2025a).

Ethical approval

This retrospective analysis of de-identified data was granted exemption from full review by the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board.

Statistical analysis

For each sequence–structure–metric combination we fitted an ordinal logistic regression (proportional–odds) model to the 5–point Likert scores with optimization (enhanced vs standard) as the predictor. Models were implemented with the python statsmodels package v0.14.5. The coefficient for optimization (log–odds) was estimated with its standard error and z–statistic; we report odds ratios (OR) and 95% Wald confidence intervals (CI) that were obtained by exponentiating the log–odds and its CI. Because our primary hypothesis was one–sided (enhanced > standard), one–sided p–values were computed from the normal approximation to the z–statistic (p = 1 − Φ(z)). To account for multiple comparisons across all sequence–structure–metric tests, the p–values were adjusted using the Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate procedure and these adjusted one–sided p–values are reported. Interrater agreement was quantified using Cohen's kappa with quadratic weighting.

Results

Interrater agreement for image quality, motion artifact, and target conspicuity ratings was excellent across nearly all evaluated metrics, with quadratic-weighted Cohen's kappa values ranging from 0.76 to 0.91 (Supplementary Table 1). The only exception was the motion artifact rating for the 3D spoiled GRE sequence (κ = 0.18 [95% CI: −0.16, 0.51]). This finding is likely attributable to the difficulty in distinguishing ringing artifact inherent to this sequence from true motion artifact.

Beginning with the previously recommended structural imaging protocols for 7T DBS, we aimed to create a balance of improved image quality and resolution, reduced scan time, and more uniform signal and contrast using DL-based reconstructions and dynamic pTx, where applicable. MP2RAGE

Previous recommendations for MP2RAGE in 7T DBS used an isotropic resolution of 0.9 mm (0.73 mm3 voxel volume) in 11:57 min. By comparison, using DL-based reconstruction, an ultra-high resolution MP2RAGE acquisition was obtained with an isotropic resolution of 0.63 mm (0.25 mm3 voxel volume) in 8:09 min, which represents a 65.8% decrease in voxel volume while also reducing imaging time by 32.9% (Fig. 1). The enhanced sequence with pTx and DL-based reconstruction resulted in a significant improvement in overall image quality (OR = 4.4 [1.5, 13.0], p = 0.003) and improved target conspicuity (OR = 3.4 [1.2, 9.4], p = 0.011). Meanwhile, motion artifacts were also decreased in the shorter sequence (OR = 3.8 [1.3, 10.9], p = 0.006).Fig. 1(A) Axial 3 T MP2RAGE T1-weighted uniform image acquired at 1 mm isotropic resolution in 12:10 min. FGATIR

Given the advantages shown from a proposed optimized 7T FGATIR sequence in the treatment of tremor (Middlebrooks et al., 2024), we aimed to use a similar configuration, but reduce scan time to enhance patient compliance. The previously proposed FGATIR protocol has a scan time of approximately 10 min at 0.8 mm isotropic voxel size. Using DL-based reconstruction, we are able to achieve the same resolution, plus the addition of dynamic pTx, with a resulting scan time of only 5:28 min, a 45.2% reduction in scan time, while simultaneously reducing noise (Fig. 2). The enhanced protocol resulted in improved overall image quality (GPi: OR = 34.7 [8.2, 146.5], p < 0.001; DRTT: OR = 5.8 [2.0, 17.1], p < 0.001). Using the FOCUS dynamic pTx pulse, the signal uniformity and tissue contrast in T2 SPACE were significantly improved across the brain. The improvements in sharpness and SNR allow better delineation of the STN and its borders (OR = 25.3 [6.5, 98.0], p < 0.001). SWI

In comparison to the previously recommended protocol, we used a resolution of 0.28 × 0.28 × 1.0 mm (0.08 mm3) in 4:20 min compared to 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.8 mm (0.13 mm3) in 7:33 mins. This resulted in a 42.6% decrease in imaging time and a 38.5% reduction in voxel volume (Fig. 4). The enhanced protocol also had higher overall image quality (OR = 17.4 [4.8, 62.8], p < 0.001) and reduced motion artifacts (OR = 8.0 [2.6, 24.7], p < 0.001). 3D spoiled GRE

To maximize time efficiency and vessel visualization, we created a 3D spoiled GRE sequence combining dynamic pTx to improve signal uniformity and fat saturation, as well as DL-based reconstruction to reduce imaging time (Fig. 5). Results show that signal uniformity is improved versus single-transmit magnetization prepared rapid gradient echo (MPRAGE) and 3D spoiled GRE, particularly in the face and posterior fossa. Additionally, there is improved fat saturation and substantially better visualization of smaller and more distal vessels. Even with these improvements in image quality, the DL-based reconstruction allowed a decrease in imaging time from the recommended 4:33 min sequence to 1:35 min. The enhanced protocol improved overall image quality (OR = 17.4 [4.2, 71.8], p < 0.001) and vessel visualization (OR = 26.1 [5.2, 131.5], p < 0.001). Discussion

The implementation of 7T MRI in DBS surgery has shown improvement in target visualization, accuracy and efficiency of microelectrode recording, and patient outcomes compared to standard 1.5 and 3 T MRI. Despite significant advancements in the safe and effective utilization of 7T MRI for DBS planning, several persistent challenges must be addressed. Notably, these challenges relate to image signal and contrast homogeneity due to variations in B1+ and extended scan durations. Moreover, the theoretical benefits associated with ultra-high spatial resolution have proven difficult to achieve in routine clinical applications, primarily owing to the prolonged examination times and heightened sensitivity to patient motion.

In this technical note, we present state-of-the-art protocols that enhance previous recommendations by incorporating dynamic pTx and DL-based reconstructions. Dynamic pTx has demonstrated a significant improvement in signal and image contrast across the head by creating a more uniform RF transmit field, resulting in more stable image contrast across diverse patient populations. Recent advances in dynamic pTx technology have rendered it more feasible for a clinical workflow. Meanwhile, DL-based reconstructions allow unprecedented image acceleration factors with minimal reductions in SNR. Although 7T MRI presents unique challenges for DL-based reconstructions, recent work has shown substantial improvements in image quality without adverse effects. The integration of these technologies marks a significant advancement in 7T clinical imaging, allowing us to achieve more consistent results across patient cohorts and better realize the potential of 7T imaging. Our proposed protocol reduces the total imaging time for standard anatomical sequences utilized in DBS planning from 42 min and 16 s to 26 min and 40 s − a 36.9% reduction − while simultaneously enhancing image quality, target visualization, and reducing motion artifacts.

MP2RAGE is a modification of the more common MPRAGE sequence that uses two different inversion times to improve image quality including reduced sensitivity to B1+ transmit inhomogeneity, improved contrast between gray matter and white matter, and also allows the calculation of a T1 map (Marques et al., 2010, Tao et al., 2023). The T1 map can subsequently be used for synthetic image generation of specific image contrast like FGATIR, 3D edge enhancing gradient echo (3D-EDGE), or gray matter nulled sequences (Middlebrooks et al., 2020, Middlebrooks et al., 2021, Middlebrooks et al., 2023, Patel et al., 2024, Tao et al., 2023). Although T1 map-based synthetic contrasts can be used to fine–tune image contrast in post-processing to partially mitigate inter-subject B1+ variation, this approach adds an additional processing step and is not applicable to all sequences (Middlebrooks et al., 2023, Patel et al., 2024). The MP2RAGE sequence at 7T provides exquisite tissue contrast and high resolution making it a valuable tool for targeting multiple structures including the DRTT for treating tremor, GPi, anterior nucleus of the thalamus (ANT), and centromedian nucleus (Lau et al., 2020, Warren et al., 2020). Maximizing spatial resolution is important, as this likely contributes to improved precision in DBS placement due to reduced volume averaging (Middlebrooks et al., 2024). Additionally, when applied to a population of patients, outlier head shapes and positioning can result in loss of signal and contrast that can affect target visibility in some patients. Dynamic pTx allows more consistent signal homogeneity and more accurate T1 values across the brain. Our enhanced protocol allowed substantial improvements in resolution while also reducing scan time, motion artifacts, and improving target visualization.

The FGATIR sequence is a staple in many DBS targeting protocols, with documented uses shown in multiple targets such as the DRTT in tremor, ANT in epilepsy, and GPi (Grewal et al., 2018, Neudorfer et al., 2022, Sudhyadhom et al., 2009, Tao et al., 2022). One challenge of FGATIR is the relatively longer scan times, which can pose limitations to its use and quality in clinical practice, particularly in patients with movement disorders. Additionally, 7T FGATIR poses additional challenges related to the optimization of the inversion pulse in the setting of B1+ transmit heterogeneity, which results in potential variation in the tissue contrast between patients that can potentially obscure the target anatomy (Middlebrooks et al., 2023, Tao et al., 2022). With these limitations in mind, both DL-based reconstruction and dynamic pTx stand to potentially benefit image quality and scan time, with greater reproducibility across patients. We show that imaging time can be nearly halved while still giving better overall image quality and more consistent target visualization, making FGATIR more feasible in 7T.

Due to the increased SNR, spatial resolution, and T2* effect, T2-weighted images are a fundamental sequence used to target the STN at 7T, with improved visualization versus 1.5 T and 3 T MRI (van den Munckhof et al., 2021). Unfortunately, the primary sequence used for 3D T2-weighted images, the SPACE sequence, is exquisitely sensitive to variation in the B1+ transmit field leading to severe image signal and contrast heterogeneity. Additionally, achieving high spatial resolutions in reasonable scan time comes at the cost of loss in SNR. T2 SPACE is also subject to high energy deposition, or specific absorption rate (SAR), that can further limit the ability to achieve high resolutions while maintaining safe SAR levels. Our enhanced protocol integrates dynamic pTx while maintaining feasible SAR at high spatial resolution allowing improved image homogeneity with overall improved image quality and STN delineation.

SWI leverages increased contrast due to normal iron content to improve visualization in several subcortical targets, such as STN or GPi. SWI is substantially improved at 7T due to increased susceptibility effects at higher magnetic field strength. While SWI is less prone to B1+ artifacts, it is susceptible to motion that can result in worsening of phase artifacts, as well as loss of normal B0 shim. Given the reduced sensitivity to B1+ inhomogeneity, dynamic pTx is generally not necessary; however, DL-based recon can be advantageous to reducing scan times and improving SNR. Our enhanced protocol utilizes a higher spatial resolution that can lead to reduced blooming artifact and improved accuracy of STN border delineation. Despite the higher resolution and substantially shorter scan time, the overall image quality and target visualization was improved with a reduction in motion artifacts.

To help mitigate the effects of distortion, chemical shift, and other factors that may present challenges to coregistration with CT in a stereotactic frame, one proposed approach is using a very high bandwidth 3D GRE image as the basis for coregistration. For time efficiency, it was proposed that this sequence could also serve to identify vessels for safely planning the electrode trajectory, such as a 3D spoiled GRE sequence. Importantly, the variation in B1+ can result in signal inhomogeneity across the image, particularly in areas of the face and posterior fossa, as well as poor fat saturation that can both contribute to difficulty with CT coregistration. Since the previously recommended protocol allowed adequate visualization of relevant vascular anatomy, the main goals were to reduce the scan time and improve the image homogeneity. In our experience, the improved signal in the lower head and face drastically improves the accuracy of image coregistration with the CT in a stereotactic frame.

The individual benefits of DL-based reconstruction and dynamic pTx are well-documented in the literature (Deniz, 2019, Gohla et al., 2024, Gudino et al., 2020, Klodowski et al., 2025, Liebert et al., 2021, Liu et al., 2025a, Liu et al., 2025b, Middlebrooks et al., 2025b, Piredda et al., 2025, Shamaei et al., 2025, Zhang et al., 2024). Here, we aimed to demonstrate the feasibility and clinical impact of their combined implementation in an optimized DBS imaging protocol. Combining these technologies poses unique challenges beyond their individual deployment, including the management of SAR constraints, calibration workflows, and reconstruction pipelines in a clinically feasible timeframe. We therefore focused on presenting the combined protocol as a practical solution that can be adopted at other centers in the future.

Several limitations of the study are noteworthy. First, while we have demonstrated improved image quality, which can be hypothesized to enhance clinical outcomes, dedicated studies are necessary to understand the clinical impact. Second, although using different patient cohorts for the standard and enhanced protocols is not ideal, there are also significant challenges to using the same patients. The majority of these patients have movement disorders, such as Parkinson’s disease, and deteriorate rapidly in the scanner, leading to increasingly severe motion artifacts in the later acquired sequences. Furthermore, healthy volunteers are not an ideal population because, in our experience, optimization in this group tends not to translate well to patient cohorts due to underlying pathology, such as increased iron deposition, and other pathologic changes that can affect image quality. Third, our sample sizes of 13 patients per group reflect consecutive clinical cohorts referred for imaging as part of routine clinical care during the transition from the standard to the enhanced protocol. Despite this modest number, the highly significant results with large effect sizes across nearly all comparisons suggest robust and clinically meaningful differences between the standard and enhanced protocols. Fourth, the techniques employed in this study are applicable exclusively to specific 7T systems, which are currently limited in their global installations. Fifth, although both dynamic pTx and DL-based image reconstruction algorithms have received regulatory approval in some implementations, our study additionally utilized some versions of this technology that do not currently have regulatory approval. Lastly, although the pTx implementations utilized in this research have previously been shown to perform effectively across larger cohorts of clinical patients, further studies are required to identify less common conditions in which their performance may not be optimal.

Conclusions

The implementation of dynamic pTx and DL-based image reconstruction can help mitigate many of the existing challenges to ultra-high field MRI. We provide a state-of-the-art protocol that builds upon established recommendations, with the goal of simultaneously enhancing image quality and reducing scan times for 7T MRI in DBS surgery.

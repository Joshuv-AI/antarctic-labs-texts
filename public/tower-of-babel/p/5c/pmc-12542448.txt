The prognostic value of atherogenic index of plasma and thin-cap fibroatheroma among patients with STEMI: an optical coherence tomography prospective cohort study of real world
Authors: Xiaoxiao Zhao, Runzhen Chen, Nan Li, Linghan Xue, Chen Liu, Peng Zhou, Yi Chen, Shaodi Yan, Li Song, Hanjun Zhao, Jiannan Li, Hongbing Yan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimThis prospective study investigated plaque morphologies based on the underlying culprit lesion pathology in relation to the Atherogenic Index of Plasma (AIP) in patients with acute ST-elevated myocardial infarction (STEMI) who underwent primary percutaneous coronary intervention and optical coherence tomography (OCT) for assessment of culprit lesions. The aim of the study was to elucidate the effects of the AIP index and plaque type on the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs).MethodsA total of 274 patients with STEMI aged ≥ 18 years who underwent pre-intervention OCT imaging of culprit lesions between March 2017 and March 2019 were enrolled. AIP index was calculated using the formula: log 10 (triglycerides [TG]/ high-density lipoprotein cholesterol [HDL-C]). We stratified the cohort into four groups according to the presence of Thin-Cap Fibroatheroma (TCFA), as assessed by OCT, and the cutoff value of AIP: Group I consisted of patients with AIP < cutoff & without TCFA; Group II had AIP < cutoff & with TCFA; Group III included those with AIP > cutoff & without TCFA; and Group IV comprised patients with AIP > cutoff & with TCFA.OutcomesPatients in Group IV exhibited a higher prevalence of Diabetes Mellitus (p = 0.012), elevated triglyceride-glucose index (TyG) levels (p < 0.001), increased LDL-C levels (p = 0.002), higher triglycerides levels (p < 0.001), and elevated total cholesterol (p = 0.001), indicating accelerated atherosclerosis. Furthermore, individuals within higher tertiles of AIP demonstrated a greater frequency of healing plaques (p = 0.021). Among patients with diabetes mellitus (DM), the AIP index exhibited a correlation with the healing of plaques (p < 0.05). Multivariable Cox regression analysis revealed that the incidence of MACEs among patients in Group IV (AIP > cutoff & with TCFA) increased by compared to those in Group I. Kaplan–Meier analyses confirmed risk stratification for MACEs based on interactions between AIP-TCFA interaction (log-rank p = 0.027), AIP-plaque interaction (log-rank p = 0.033), AIP-mixed plaque interaction (log-rank p = 0.041), AIP-lipid plaque interaction (log-rank p < 0.001), AIP- macrophage interaction (log-rank p = 0.032).ConclusionMicrostructural features observed via OCT for culprit lesions, combined with the AIP index—an important marker for cardiovascular disease—may be utilized clinically to support risk stratification and predict adverse events among STEMI patients.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12933-025-02957-5.Keywords: Atherogenic index of plasma, Optical coherence tomography, ST-elevated myocardial infarction, Major adverse cardiovascular events, Prognostic value

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains the leading cause of global morbidity and mortality, with atherosclerosis as its fundamental pathological basis [1, 2]. Despite significant advancements in therapeutic strategies, acute coronary syndromes (ACS) continue to impose substantial clinical burdens, thereby necessitating more refined risk stratification approaches [3]. The atherogenic index of plasma (AIP), defined as log10 (triglycerides/HDL-cholesterol) [4], has emerged as a valuable biomarker of plasma atherogenicity, reflecting the intricate interplay between lipid metabolism and cardiovascular risk [5, 6]. Its clinical significance extends beyond traditional lipid parameters; AIP independently predicts arterial stiffness, myocardial infarction, and ischemic stroke. Notably, in diabetic populations, AIP demonstrates strong correlations with major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs), emphasizing its potential role as an integrator of metabolic risk [7–9].

Recent investigations have progressively elucidated the prognostic significance of plaque morphology in patients with ACS, particularly through features derived from optical coherence tomography (OCT) [10]. The thin-cap fibroatheroma (TCFA), which is characterized by a fibrous cap measuring less than 65 μm overlying a lipid-rich necrotic core, exemplifies the prototypical vulnerable plaque phenotype associated with an increased risk of future adverse events [11]. While both the AIP and TCFA independently correlate with cardiovascular outcomes, contemporary literature [12] reveals critical knowledge gaps concerning their synergistic prognostic value. Current risk assessment paradigms predominantly consider biochemical markers and imaging features in isolation, thereby overlooking potential interactions between systemic metabolic dysregulation and localized plaque vulnerability. This oversight is especially pertinent in populations experiencing ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), where the interplay of acute ischemia and chronic atherosclerosis creates a unique pathophysiological environment that necessitates comprehensive evaluation.

The present study addresses this scientific gap through a novel integrative approach combining functional lipidomics with high-resolution vascular imaging. Our protocol systematically evaluates AIP-TCFA interactions in a prospective cohort of STEMI patients undergoing primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI). OCT provides spatial resolution for quantifying TCFA prevalence and other high-risk features, while AIP offers a clinically accessible metric of atherogenic potential. This dual-parameter assessment strategy captures both systemic metabolic drivers and local vascular pathology, enabling multidimensional risk stratification unavailable through conventional methods (a structured graphical abstract is shown).

Methodologically, this investigation advances the field through three key innovations. First, the application of pre-intervention OCT imaging ensures accurate characterization of the morphology of culprit lesions prior to mechanical disruption. Second, standardized AIP calculation from admission blood samples establishes temporal alignment between biochemical profiling and acute ischemic events. Third, the implementation of longitudinal follow-up protocols enables assessment of both short-term procedural outcomes and long-term prognostic implications. These methodological refinements collectively enhance the clinical translatability of findings while upholding rigorous scientific standards.

The primary objective of this study is to determine whether the combined assessment of AIP and TCFA improves MACE prediction compared to either parameter alone within populations experiencing STEMI. MACEs were defined as all cause death, cardiac death, recurrence myocardial infarction, recurrence heart failure, ischemic stroke, and unplanned repeat revascularization. Secondary aims include elucidating pathophysiological relationships between systemic lipid metabolism and plaque vulnerability characteristics, as well as exploring potential effect modifications by metabolic comorbidities. By bridging the gap between circulating AIP and intracoronary imaging, this research seeks to establish a new paradigm for personalized risk assessment in atherosclerosis management. The findings may inform targeted therapeutic strategies addressing both AIP levels and plaque stabilization, ultimately enhancing outcomes in this high-risk population (The schematic diagram pertaining to the AIP and OCT has been shown in Fig. 1).

Methods

Study design, participants and sample collection

For this study, a post hoc analysis of the OCT-acute myocardial infarction (AMI) registry was conducted involving 434 consecutively enrolled patients who underwent primary PCI for STEMI. The patients were screened using OCT between March 2017 and March 2019 at one of China’s largest and most prestigious PCI centers. Major exclusion criteria included cardiac shock, severe liver dysfunction, allergies to contrast media, significant hepatic or renal insufficiency (estimated glomerular filtration rate < 30 mL/min), congestive heart failure with a left ventricular ejection fraction < 50%, contraindications to aspirin or ticagrelor, and lesions associated with increased difficulty and risk during the OCT procedure (e.g., chronic total occlusion, heavily calcified vessels, and left main coronary artery disease). The study protocol adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki and received approval from the Ethics Committee at Fuwai Hospital affiliated with Peking Union Medical College & Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences; informed consent was waived. Patient identities remained confidential throughout the duration of the study. Patients undergoing OCT examination provided written informed consent specific to their participation in this portion of research within the Fuwai Hospital OCT-AMI Registry (clinical trials.gov: NCT03593928).

OCT image acquisition and quantitative OCT image analysis

Intravascular OCT imaging was performed in strict adherence to established protocols [13]. To summarize, following the restoration of antegrade coronary blood flow and the reduction of thrombus burden through pre-dilatation and/or thrombus aspiration, high-resolution OCT images of the culprit lesions were meticulously acquired using a state-of-the-art frequency-domain OCT system (ILUMIEN OPTIS™; St. Jude Medical/Abbott, St. Paul, MN, USA), paired with a specialized catheter (Dragonfly™; LightLab Imaging, Inc., Westford, MA, USA). To ensure optimal image clarity by eliminating any traces of blood from the field of view and establishing a virtually blood-free environment during image acquisition, continuous flushing with contrast media was conducted via manual injection directly from the guiding catheter while capturing coronary images. The entirety of the affected vessels was documented employing an automated pullback device functioning at a precise speed of 36 mm/s. Cross-sectional imagery was generated with remarkable rotational finesse at an impressive rate of 180 frames per second. In totality, the length of OCT pullback reached 75 mm and has been archived digitally for subsequent analysis. OCT image analysis was meticulously conducted utilizing an offline review workstation (Ilumien Optis, St Jude Medical) within a core laboratory environment, overseen by three independent investigator observers (RZ.C., XX.Z., and JN.L.), who remained oblivious to both the angiographic data and the clinical manifestations of the enrolled patients. These investigators were entrusted with the critical task of evaluating the appropriateness for culprit-plaque assessment, delving into thin-cap fibroatheroma, plaque characteristics, and quantifying microstructural indices such as plaque rupture, plaque erosion, macrophage infiltration, micro-vessels, minimal fibrous cap thickness, red and white thrombus, and cholesterol crystal. Any divergences or inconsistencies among the investigators were resolved through collaborative consensus. To thoroughly elucidate entire segments of culpable plaques, optical coherence tomography analysis was executed in conjunction with comprehensive OCT pullback assessments (Fig. 2).

Thin-cap fibroatheroma is characterized as a lipid-rich plaque, where lipids are identified by signal-poor and attenuating characteristics, occupying more than two quadrants of the vessel lumen. The fibrous cap, which presents as signal-rich (prominently reflecting) with low attenuation, exhibits a thickness of 65 μm or less (indicated by the arrow). B. Lipid plaques (indicated by the arrow) typically appear as diffusely bordered regions that are signal-poor, accompanied by overlying signal-rich bands. C. Calcified ruptures (indicated by the arrow) manifest as areas of low signal intensity or irregular surfaces featuring distinct borders. D. Plaque rupture is recognized through disruption of the fibrous cap and subsequent cavity formation (denoted by the asterisk). E. Macrophage infiltration (indicated by the arrow) is defined as a signal-rich region exhibiting distinct or confluent punctate patterns that demonstrate higher intensity compared to background speckle noise, resulting in notable backward shadowing. F. Microvessels are defined as tubular luminal structures that do not produce any detectable signals and lack connections to the vessel lumen (indicated by the arrow). G. Red thrombus primarily comprises red blood cells; relevant optical coherence tomography images depict them as high-backscattering protrusions associated with signal-free shadowing (represented by an asterisk). In contrast, white thrombi consist mainly of white blood cells (WBCs) and platelets, characterized as signal-rich yet low-backscattering billowing projections extending into the lumen (also indicated with an asterisk). H. Cholesterol crystals (indicated by the arrow) can be identified based on their linear structure that demonstrates high backscattering without significant backward shadowing

Study cohort outcomes and follow-up

The diagnosis and categorization of AMI were based on current clinical guidelines and widely accepted criteria. Evaluations included clinical manifestations, characteristic electrocardiographic changes, dynamic cardiac enzyme variations, and imaging findings. The study population included AMI patients, categorized for treatment based on coronary angiography results and their individual clinical conditions, with options for conservative management, PCI, or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). Inclusion criteria required that patients were adults who met two main conditions: (1) received primary PCI, which involved procedures like stent placement, thrombus aspiration, or balloon angioplasty of coronary vessels, and (2) provided written informed consent. Exclusion criteria applied to individuals who met any of the following conditions: (1) refused participation, (2) were uncontactable during follow-up, or (3) had a history of malignancies, significant valvular heart disease, prior cardiac surgeries (excluding thoracoscopic ablation), moderate hepatic dysfunction, or chronic renal disease with an estimated glomerular filtration rate under 30 mL/min/1.73 m2. Demographic details, medication history, and laboratory results were extensively reviewed for all subjects. Key biomarkers, including high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP), fasting glucose, and creatinine, were extracted from the most recent available electronic medical records at the time of sample collection. Comorbidities were determined using a combination of three methods: self-reports from participants, comprehensive reviews of electronic medical records, and diagnoses made by clinicians based on standard clinical criteria. The study protocol conformed to the ethical standards set by the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional ethics committee (Approval No. 2017–866). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

The primary outcome of this study was all-cause mortality. Follow-up evaluations were conducted after patients were discharged. One month following discharge, participants were invited to return for further assessments. Those unable to attend in person were followed up via telephone interviews conducted by staff from the institute’s information center, using a standardized questionnaire. Further follow-ups were scheduled at six and twelve months post-discharge. For participants surviving beyond one year, subsequent follow-up occurred annually. The research team received monthly updates on follow-up status, and all reported adverse events were systematically evaluated by a team of dedicated physicians. Baseline data including patient clinical demographics such as age, gender, smoking status, history of disease (including hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, PCI and chronic kidney disease), laboratory results, primary PCI procedures, and medical treatments were obtained from hospital records. Serum levels of fasting plasma glucose (FPG) and lipid profiles, including triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), lipse activator (LPA) and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) were determined by standard laboratory techniques in Fuwai hospital.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are articulated as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical data is delineated through counts and percentages. Comparisons between groups for continuous variables were conducted utilizing either Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test, predicated on the normality of distribution. Categorical variables underwent analysis via Pearson’s χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test; the latter methodology was employed in instances where expected cell counts diminished below five. The Kaplan–Meier method was used for median survival estimation, with intergroup differences assessed using the log-rank test. To scrutinize independent associations between AIP level status along with TCFA and clinical outcomes—including all-cause mortality, cardiac death, recurrent myocardial infarction, revascularization procedures, and ischemic stroke—multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were meticulously developed. The covariates incorporated into this model encompassed age, gender, status of smoking, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, weight, systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure. Results are conveyed as hazard ratios (HRs) complemented by 95% confidence intervals (CIs), establishing statistical significance at P < 0.05 within a two-tailed format.

Prevalence of AIP and prognostic value

Among patients with diabetes mellitus (DM), the AIP index exhibited a correlation with the healing of plaques (p < 0.05, Fig. 3A). Moreover, the AIP index was identified as being associated with cholesterol crystals (p < 0.05, Fig. 3B). Among individuals in the high AIP group with TCFA (HR 4.98, 95% CI 0.62–39.89; P = 0.131) and those in the low AIP group with TCFA (HR 8.51, 95% CI 1.15–63.03; P = 0.036) (Table 3, STable 3), there is an associated higher risk of MACE after adjusting for confounding factors including age, gender, status of smoking, hypertension, hyperlipidemia, diabetes, chronic kidney disease, weight, systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure. However, the correlation was not observed in the group of AIP combined with other misconstruction by OCT (Supplement Table 1). When TyG is greater than or equal to the median, a higher level of AIP in conjunction with the presence of TCFA is associated with an increased incidence of MACE (HR: 2.84, 95% CI: 0.117–6.73) after adjustment (Table 4). Conversely, this correlation was not observed in the subgroup where TyG is less than the median (Table 4). Kaplan–Meier curves in Fig. 3 depict the occurrence of endpoint events based on AIP cutoff values and various plaque microstructures within the study cohort. The log-rank test revealed statistically significant differences (logrank p = 0.027) between groups stratified by AIP levels and TCFA (Fig. 4A). Moreover, the combination of AIP levels with different plaque microstructures, including macrophages (logrank p = 0.032) (Fig. 4B), plaque rupture (logrank p = 0.033) (Fig. 4C), and lipid plaque (logrank p = 0.0088) (Fig. 4D), also demonstrated statistically significant differences in event outcomes. However, this correlation was not observed in the group of AIP combined with other misconstruction by OCT (Supplement Fig. 1A–D).

Discussion

According to the Global Burden of Diseases, cardiovascular diseases—principally stroke and ischaemic heart disease — constitute a significant contributor to disability and represent the leading cause of premature mortality on a global scale [14, 15]. Atherosclerosis, the underlying pathological process of CAD, encompasses complex interactions among lipid metabolism, inflammatory responses, and plaque vulnerability. AIP, defined as log10 (TG /HDL-C), has emerged as a robust biomarker that reflects plasma atherogenicity and cardiovascular risk. Recent evidence [16, 17] indicates that AIP not only correlates with traditional risk factors but also independently predicts adverse outcomes in populations experiencing myocardial infarction and stroke. Particularly within STEMI patients, the identification of high-risk plaque features such as TCFA through OCT has transformed risk stratification [12]; however, the synergistic prognostic value of biochemical markers like AIP alongside imaging markers such as TCFA remains insufficiently explored. This prospective study examined 274 STEMI patients undergoing OCT-guided primary PCI, systematically evaluating the interplay between AIP levels and culprit lesion morphology. Our findings demonstrate that the co-occurrence of elevated AIP and TCFA delineates a distinct high-risk population characterized by enhanced metabolic dysfunction and increased plaque vulnerability. The ensuing discussion will elucidate how this dual-marker approach improves prediction of major adverse cardiac events through three mechanistic pathways: lipid-driven plaque destabilization, impaired healing responses, and heightened inflammatory activation. Notably, our results reveal a graded association between AIP tertiles and specific OCT-defined plaque characteristics, offering novel insights into the pathophysiological continuum from metabolic disturbances to clinical events.

Previous nationwide community-based cohort analysis [18] has found that an elevated AIP is linked to carotid atherosclerosis, increased carotid intima-media thickness (CIMT) and plaques in the Chinese middle-aged and elderly population. This large observational study involving over 52,000 individuals suggests that AIP may play a vital role in primordial prevention of atherosclerosis and serve as a simple yet effective tool for assessing atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk in routine practice. The present study makes significant contributions to the field of cardiovascular risk stratification by being the first to systematically evaluate the combined prognostic value of the AIP and OCT-defined thin-cap fibroatheroma in patients with STEMI. While prior investigations have independently established AIP as a predictor of cardiovascular events and OCT-derived plaque characteristics as markers of vulnerability, our research addresses this knowledge gap by demonstrating their synergistic prognostic capabilities. Our study quantitatively establishes that the combination of AIP and TCFA provides superior risk stratification (log-rank p = 0.027) compared to either marker in isolation. The mechanistic plausibility underlying our findings is supported by demonstrated correlations between higher tertiles of AIP and key pathological features, including lipid-rich plaques (p < 0.001) and macrophage infiltration (p = 0.032). These results suggest that AIP may function both as a biochemical marker and as an indicator of plaque vulnerability.

Dyslipidemia, characterized by elevated serum triglyceride concentrations and diminished HDL-C levels, constitutes a major driver of arterial plaque pathogenesis. These metabolic derangements, particularly when quantified through an elevated AIP, appear to accelerate atherosclerotic plaque formation via enhanced cholesterol esterification and production of small, dense LDL particles with heightened atherogenicity [19–22]. Clinical evidences [23–26] demonstrate that elevated AIP values not only reflect a pro-atherogenic lipid milieu but also exhibit significant correlations with carotid intima-media thickening, progressive plaque accumulation, and elevated vulnerability to plaque rupture. The clinical relevance of AIP-plaque phenotype interactions has gained substantial recognition in contemporary cardiovascular research, with epidemiological studies consistently associating higher AIP tertiles with increased carotid atherosclerotic burden—a well-established biomarker for cerebrovascular and coronary events [27–30]. The pathophysiological continuum linking AIP to adverse cardiovascular outcomes is further exemplified by plaque rupture epidemiology [31, 32]. This catastrophic event, which precipitates acute coronary syndromes including myocardial infarction, demonstrates striking prevalence gradients across AIP strata. Advanced imaging studies [33, 34] have mechanistically linked elevated AIP levels with the predominance of high-risk plaque morphologies—particularly thin-cap fibroatheromas—thereby establishing AIP as a quantifiable biomarker of plaque instability. The predictive validity of AIP extends across metabolic risk spectrums, showing particular clinical utility in populations with diabetes mellitus and prediabetes where dyslipidemia- insulin resistance synergism exacerbates cardiovascular risk [35–37]. Longitudinal cohort analyses reveal that AIP not only correlates with coronary artery disease progression in type 2 DM cohorts but also enhances the discriminative capacity of conventional risk models when integrated with LDL-C metrics [38]. This integrative approach provides superior cardiovascular risk stratification compared to isolated LDL-C assessments, as AIP encapsulates the atherogenic interplay between triglyceride-rich lipoproteins and HDL-mediated reverse cholesterol transport. Prospective research priorities should emphasize multicenter validation of AIP thresholds across ethnically diverse populations and interrogation of its therapeutic implications for personalized cardiovascular prevention strategies, particularly in high-risk subgroups characterized by glucose metabolism disorders or prior acute coronary syndromes [39, 40]. Concurrent technological integration—combining AIP profiling with intravascular imaging modalities like OCT—could enable dynamic assessment of plaque vulnerability, potentially revolutionizing risk-adaptive management paradigms in clinical atherosclerosis care.

From a clinical perspective, our findings have immediate implications for risk assessment protocols in STEMI management. The robust association between elevated AIP (≥ cutoff value) with TCFA presence and subsequent MACEs during follow up suggests that this simple, cost-effective lipid ratio could be incorporated into existing risk prediction models to enhance their accuracy. Particularly noteworthy is the 4.98-fold increased MACE risk observed in Group IV (high AIP + TCFA) patients compared to those with low-risk profiles, which underscores the potential for targeted secondary prevention strategies in this high-risk subgroup. These results emphasizing personalized approaches and may inform future recommendations regarding the timing and intensity of lipid-lowering therapy. Furthermore, the significant correlation between AIP and healing plaques (p = 0.021) introduces novel considerations for determining optimal duration of dual antiplatelet therapy, particularly in patients with ongoing plaque instability.

Limitation

Several limitations must be acknowledged when interpreting our findings. First, the single-center design and relatively modest sample size (n = 274) may limit the generalizability of the results, particularly with regard to the precise hazard ratios identified in subgroup analyses. Second, our optical coherence tomography (OCT) assessments were confined to culprit lesions, which may lead to an underestimation of the overall plaque burden and its association with systemic lipid profiles. Future multicenter studies incorporating serial OCT imaging and longitudinal AIP measurements could help address this limitation and provide valuable insights into the dynamic relationship between plaque characteristics and AIP over time. Third, the absence of serial AIP measurements precludes an evaluation of temporal changes in atherogenic risk, thereby hindering our understanding of plaque progression. Moreover, potential unmeasured confounders such as genetic predispositions or environmental factors may have influenced the observed associations. Studies that integrate longitudinal biomarker assessments with comprehensive vessel imaging in multicenter settings would enhance the robustness of these findings. Despite these limitations, our results offer compelling evidence supporting the integration of biochemical and imaging biomarkers in STEMI risk stratification, laying a foundation for more comprehensive strategies in secondary prevention.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study elucidates that the synergistic integration of AIP and OCT-derived TCFA significantly enhances the prediction of MACE in patients with STEMI, thereby providing a novel multidimensional approach for risk stratification. The combined model demonstrates superiority over conventional risk assessment methods by concurrently capturing metabolic dysfunction and plaque vulnerability. These findings emphasize the clinical significance of incorporating both atherosclerotic biomarkers and OCT imaging into prognostic algorithms. Future research should aim to clarify the mechanistic links between atherogenic lipid profiles and plaque destabilization while validating this composite risk model across diverse cohorts to optimize personalized therapeutic strategies.

Total bilirubin level on postoperative day 3 in predicting clinically relevant pancreatic fistula after pancreaticoduodenectomy: a retrospective study
Authors: Hongbiao Han, Zhicheng Wang, Zhichao Zhang, Xiliang Qin, Qidong Chen, Xiaosi Hu, Yong Wang, Hongtao Pan, Huichun Liu, Chao Zhu, Shilei Chen, Shuai Zhou, Qing Pang, Hao Jin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundCR-POPF following PD are very common. The Majority of research on predicting CR-POPF focuses on pre-operation, while there are few indicators for predicting CR-POPF in the early postoperative period. The purpose of this study was to see how TBIL levels on postoperative day 3 predict CR-POPF after PD.MethodsThe clinical data of 170 patients who had PD in our team between January 2015 and October 2022 were retrospectively analyzed into two groups: the CR-POPF group with a grade B or C pancreatic fistula (n = 37), the no CR-POPF group with a non-pancreatic fistula, and the biochemical fistula patients (n = 133). Univariate and multivariate analyses were used to discover potential determinants of CR-POPF. The predictive value of risk factors was determined by calculating the area under the ROC curve.ResultsPreoperative variables, including Hb, CRP, TBIL, ALB, pancreatic texture, hemorrhage, duct diameter, and TBIL on postoperative day 3, were found to predict clinically relevant postoperative CR-POPF after PD (p < 0.001). The multivariate logistic regression study revealed that pancreatic texture (p = 0.004), postoperative TBIL (p < 0.001), and preoperative CRP (p = 0.048) are independent risk variables. To predict CR-POPF, the AUC of pancreatic texture, TBIL on the third day of surgery, and preoperative CRP were found to be 0.607, 0.824, and 0.726, respectively.ConclusionThe TBIL level on postoperative day 3 is a significant predictor of CR-POPF after PD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12893-025-03205-z.

Introduction

Pancreaticoduodenectomy (PD) is a radical surgical procedure for treating periampullary malignancies that is associated with increased surgical complexity and postoperative problems [1]. Postoperative pancreatic fistula (POPF) is the most prevalent and fatal consequence of PD. It is followed by a succession of life-threatening diseases, with an overall mortality rate of approximately 40% [2]. As a result, it is critical to forecast the emergence of POPF early on and adopt appropriate interventions to lower PD patients’ complication rates and death. The International Pancreatic Fistula Research Group (ISGPF) revised the concept of POPF in 2016. Traditional grade A pancreatic fistulas are no longer recognized. Instead, grade B and C pancreatic fistulas are now classified as clinically relevant pancreatic fistula (CR-POPF), necessitating a modification in clinical treatment methods [3].

Risk variables for CR-POPF include residual pancreatic texture, pancreatic duct diameter, body mass index (BMI), drainage tube amylase level, c-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin, C-reactive protein to albumin ratio (CAR), and postoperative lactic acid level [4–10]. Subjective awareness is an important aspect of determining the texture of the leftover pancreas. However, determining procalcitonin, lactate levels, and CAR is difficult and costly for patients. As a result, we require an easily accessible objective index capable of accurately predicting CR-POPF.

Total bilirubin (TBIL), one of the indices used to evaluate liver function, can represent the liver’s lateral recovery function. Clinical studies revealed that patients with a higher TBIL level following PD surgery had more postoperative problems. However, preoperative biliary drainage becomes an unstable variable when utilizing TBIL to assess the incidence of CR-POPF. This technique may significantly affect the patient’s preoperative and postoperative bilirubin levels, as well as postoperative consequences. According to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines, for patients with obstructive jaundice who require PD, preoperative biliary drainage is only suggested if there are substantial problems and neoadjuvant therapy or surgery is needed soon [11]. Sandini et al. found that the degree of preoperative jaundice in patients was associated with the occurrence of postoperative CR-POPF, and that preoperative biliary drainage increased patients’ tolerance to operation [12]. There is no consensus on whether preoperative biliary drainage is necessary for PD nowadays. We feel that in terms of chronological order, postoperative signs should be more useful in predicting postoperative problems. Because 72 h after surgery coincides with the peak of pancreatic stump ischemia and inflammatory response, aberrant bilirubin metabolism can sensitively represent liver hypoperfusion and systemic inflammatory status—both of which are essential mechanisms of CR-POPF occurrence; Second, the third day is the optimal time for early postoperative intervention, which is substantially earlier than the standard diagnostic criteria for CR-POPF of 5–7 days. Our preliminary experimental data and clinical experience summary reveal that TBIL has a greater specificity in predicting severe pancreatic fistula (ISGPF B/C grade) on the third day after surgery than the indicators on the first and second days. As a result, we examined laboratory Markers on postoperative day 3 and preoperative day 3, and discovered that postoperative day 3 TBIL levels may be a predictor of CR-POPF.

Methods

Patients and methods

From January 2015 to October 2022, we conducted a retrospective study of PD patients at the Department of Hepatobiliary Surgery, the First Affiliated Hospital of Bengbu Medical College, and the Department of General Surgery, Anhui No. 2 Provincial People’s Hospital Affiliated to Anhui Medical College. All patients provided informed consent prior to surgery and passed an ethical evaluation. The study excluded individuals who had neoadjuvant therapy before surgery or who had pre-operative illnesses that had a significant impact on TBIL. PD required three surgical procedures: pancreaticojejunostomy, choledochojejunostomy, and gastrojejunostomy. Two drainage tubes were placed, one at the upper and lower extremities of the pancreaticojejunostomy and one at the bilioenteric anastomosis. The pancreatic duct drainage tube and intestine anastomosis were treated by embedding. The pancreatic stump and jejunum were sutured intermittently, and a drainage catheter was placed in the main pancreatic duct.

All patients were divided into two groups: pancreatic fistula (grades B and C) and non-pancreatic fistula (biochemical fistula, no pancreatic fistula) based on the ISGPF’s pancreatic fistula grading in 2016 [3]. All patients had preoperative laboratory and imaging tests to rule out surgical contraindications and assess surgical feasibility. Antibiotics (fourth-generation cephalosporins) and proton pump inhibitors were administered for three days following surgery, and the blood routine, biochemical routine, and blood coagulation were evaluated on the third day. Following the operation, parenteral nutrition was initially administered before transitioning to enteral nutrition assistance once peristaltic function was gradually recovered or ventilation was complete. On the first, third, and fifth days following surgery, the amylase content of the drainage tube was routinely checked; if it was greater than 5000 U/L and the drainage flow exceeded 200 ml/d, negative pressure suction was used. If the drainage rate is less than 600 U/L, the drainage rate is less than 20 ml/d for three days in a row, and no evident encapsulated effusion is observed on a CT scan of the entire abdomen after reexamination, progressively remove the drainage tube.

Data collection

In this study, we collected basic information about patients such as gender, age, BMI, if they had hypertension or diabetes, tumor site, hospital stay, and surgical procedure. Preoperative data on patients includes: preoperative white blood cell count, preoperative neutrophil count, preoperative lymphocyte count, preoperative hemoglobin, preoperative platelet, preoperative prothrombin, preoperative CRP, preoperative TBIL, and preoperative ALB. Intraoperative measurements include operative time, blood loss, pancreatic texture, and pancreatic duct diameter. Data from patients on the third day after surgery: postoperative white blood cell count, neutrophil count, lymphocyte count, platelet count, CRP, TBIL, and ALB.

Statistical analysis

The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was performed to determine whether or not the data was distributed normally. Data from a normal distribution were reported as the mean plus or minus the standard deviation. Data that did not have a normal distribution were represented using the median and interquartile range. We employed the Mann-Whitney U test and the Chi-square test to investigate the link between perioperative and intraoperative variables and the development of CR-POPF. We examined a number of variables, including age, gender, body mass index, tumor site, duration of hospital stay, operation time, intraoperative blood loss, Pancreas texture (hard or soft), pancreatic duct diameter, perioperative CRP, TBIL, ALB, Hb, PT, and other indicators. First, a univariate analysis was performed on the putative risk factors of CR-POPF, utilizing the variance inflation factor (VIF) to remove variables with collinearity; Multiple logistic regression analysis was performed on CR-POPF variables (P < 0.05) to identify independent risk factors for postoperative CR-POPF. The predictive value of TBIL on the third day after surgery and preoperative CRP levels was calculated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The largest Youden index (sensitivity + specificity − 1) was used to establish the appropriate threshold value, which represents the best sensitivity and specificity balance. The data was analyzed using IBM Corp.‘s SPSS 26.0 software, produced in Armonk, New York, USA. A p-value of < 0.05 was judged statistically significant.

Results

Preoperative data analysis

The study comprised 239 PD patients on our team between January 2015 and October 2022, based on the screening criteria. In addition to the 69 patients who met the exclusion criteria (16 patients with severe underlying diseases, 3 patients with distant tumor metastasis, 7 patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgery, 18 patients with diseases affecting TBIL, and 25 patients with incomplete clinical data), 170 patients were eventually included. The participants were separated into two groups based on the presence or absence of CR-POPF: ISGPS grade ≥ B (Fig. 1). There were 99 (58.2%) Males and 71 (41.8%) women, with a median age of 65.0 years (52.5,70.0). 60 patients (35.3%) underwent OPD, while 110 (64.7%) underwent LPD 57 (33.5%) patients got preoperative biliary drainage, while 113 (66.5%) did not. Preoperative imaging was used to determine the tumor locations of all patients. There were 63 (37.1%) cases of distal bile duct cancer, 50 (29.4%) of pancreatic head cancer, 38 (22.4%) of duodenal papilla cancer, and 19 (11.1%) of ampulla cancer. Furthermore, in the laboratory tests of the two groups of patients, the preoperative neutrophil levels and lymphocyte levels of the CR-POPF group were lower than those of the Non-POPF group, while the preoperative white blood cell levels were the same in both groups (Table 1).

Additionally, the differences in pancreatic texture and pancreatic duct diameter were also statistically significant (Table 2).

Predictive value analysis of factors related to CR-POPF

Figure 2 and Table 5 illustrate the ROC analysis for CRP, postoperative TBIL, and pancreatic texture in predicting CR-POPF following PD. The cut-off value for CRP prior to PD was 15.20 mg/L (AUC = 0.726, sensitivity = 67.6%, specificity = 71.4%, 95%CI = 0.633–0.818, p = 0.048). The cut-off value for postoperative TBIL was 89.45 µmol/L (AUC = 0.824, sensitivity = 75.7%, specificity = 77.4%, 95%CI = 0.758–0.890, p < 0.001). Pancreatic texture (AUC = 0.607, OR 30.00, 95%CI = 2.87-313.47, sensitivity = 43.2%, and specificity = 78.2%) was a worse predictor of CR-POPF than the other markers. Cut-off values turned the postoperative TBIL into binary data. We discovered that its predictive value was greater than that of preoperative TBIL (AUC = 0.766, sensitivity = 75.7%, specificity = 77.4%, 95% CI = 0.676–0.856).

Discussion

With the maturation of surgical technology, the maintenance of surgical equipment, and the updating of surgical concepts, PD has no longer been limited to large central hospitals. CR-POPF is the center of attention following PD surgery, and numerous studies have been conducted to affect CR-POPF. Currently, the pancreas is thought to be a significant predictor of CR-POPF [3]. Similarly, the current study found that soft pancreatic texture was a Major predictor of pancreatic fistula, with a high OR value of 30.0 indicating a statistically significant influence. However, pancreatic texture is typically a static sign of intraoperative or preoperative imaging evaluation, with limited opportunities for clinical intervention, such as the inability to change texture through treatment. Second, the surgeon’s judgment of pancreatic texture is heavily influenced by his or her experience. Although this study used the most conventional process feasible to determine the texture of the pancreas, it is still not entirely objective. In contrast, dynamic changes in laboratory values during the perioperative period provide an operational monitoring window that allows for early detection of problems and guides treatment decisions. So, in recent years, perioperative laboratory markers have been explored more extensively, with several research confirming the CRP prediction of CR-POPF [5, 8, 9]. The monitoring of perioperative CRP surely raises the financial burden on patients. As a result, we need to identify an inexpensive and convenient indicator that can forecast CR-POPF.

In recent years, whether to recommend preoperative biliary decompression has been a highly controversial topic. Some research reports [13–15] suggest that preoperative biliary drainage increases the probability of postoperative complications, while other studies present the opposite view and recommend preoperative drainage [16–19]. Given the diversity among individuals, the impact of preoperative biliary drainage remains unknown. Furthermore, due to the influence of surgical biliary reconstruction, there is no correlation between postoperative and preoperative TBIL levels. To acquire a better understanding of the elements that influence CR-POPF, we looked into patients’ postoperative symptoms.

Hyperbilirubinemia can cause severe alterations to the internal milieu, resulting in a variety of repercussions such as an impaired immune response, poor nutritional absorption, biliary system infection, liver and blood coagulation problems, and possibly death. Studies on the prognostic implications of elevated TBIL have been published [20–22]. In a retrospective research conducted up to 9 years following PD surgery, it was discovered that postoperative elevations in TBIL were related to a poor patient survival prognosis [23]. Preoperative TBIL is insufficient to predict the occurrence of CR-POPF, and the effect of preoperative biliary drainage on CR-POPF is highly debatable, which supports our recommendation to use postoperative TBIL to predict CR-POPF.

Our study investigated to examine the predictive significance of postoperative TBIL on CR-POPF following PD surgery. The CR-POPF group had a lengthier hospital stay than the non-CR-POPF group. Higher preoperative CRP, higher postoperative TBIL, and soft pancreatic texture were all risk variables for CR-POPF, with postoperative TBIL having the strongest predictive value. To optimize the study, the postoperative day 3 TBIL level was converted from measurement to counting data using a cut-off value of 89.45 mol/L. Among the categorical variables, our study found that intraoperative blood loss, residual pancreatic texture, pancreatic duct diameter, and postoperative TBIL may all be associated to CR-POPF after PD surgery. In multivariate logistic regression, postoperative TBIL and residual pancreatic texture were independently associated with CR-POPF.

According to Kowal et al., an increase in TBIL following surgery may not only indicate a higher risk of an early biliary fistula but also produce cholestasis, which can exacerbate infections and impair patients’ prognosis [24]. Studies have revealed that postoperative serum TBIL elevation, liver and pancreas function, and the degree of inflammation and fibrosis are all closely connected to the likelihood of severe POPF, with postoperative serum TBIL elevation potentially being an essential contributor [25]. TBIL offers some information about liver synthesis and metabolism. Elevated serum TBIL levels may indicate biliary blockage. Bilirubin excretion blockage can cause liver insufficiency, nutrition metabolism abnormalities, increased bile duct pressure, and irregular platelet synthesis [26]. Furthermore, animal studies have indicated that biliary obstruction may result in elevated levels of blood inflammatory cytokines and endotoxins, intestinal flora abnormalities, and intestinal mucosal barrier degradation [27]. Combined with the findings mentioned above, we hypothesize that people with compromised biliary systems after PD may have higher TBIL levels due to obstructed biliary excretion. This obstruction interferes with the body’s metabolic processes, resulting in decreased albumin and platelet synthesis, as well as diminished anastomotic healing potential. As a result, these individuals are more likely to experience postoperative hemorrhage, increasing the risk of clinically relevant postoperative pancreatic fistula (CR-POPF). Impaired bile excretion disrupts the gut microbiota and the intestinal mucosal barrier. This not only reduces the body’s ability to absorb nutrition, but it also causes the formation of inflammatory molecules, which stimulates the patient to develop complications including cholangitis and pancreatitis, raising the risk of CR-POPF. In conjunction with the findings mentioned above, we hypothesize that patients with compromised biliary systems after PD may have higher TBIL levels due to obstructed biliary excretion. This blockage may raise the risk of CR-POPF by interfering with a variety of bodily functions, including metabolism, healing, and absorption.

It is necessary to highlight some of the study’s flaws. First, there is no reliable index for determining intraoperative pancreatic texture, which is subjective. Furthermore, to validate our findings, high-quality multi-center randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are required due to the study’s limitations, such as its retrospective nature and small sample size.

Finally, we believe that assessing TBIL levels on the third day following surgery can be a simple, cost-effective, and dependable way to predict CR-POPD after PD. After PD surgery, variables such as CRP level, residual pancreatic texture, and postoperative day 3 TBIL level were found to be independent predictors of CR-POPF. TBIL levels on postoperative day 3 are the most reliable predictor of CR-POPF following PD surgery.

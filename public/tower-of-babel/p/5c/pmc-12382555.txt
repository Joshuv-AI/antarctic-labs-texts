Effectiveness and satisfaction of fully self-service fundus disease screening among middle-aged individuals: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Yuting Wu, Guiling Gao, Liangwen Lei, Tao Yu, Yajun Peng, Siyu Yang, Meng Gao, Senlin Lin, Tao Wu, Deshang Li, Chunxia Yao, Lina Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo investigate the imaging quality, efficiency and satisfaction of fully self-service fundus photography compared with traditional fundus photography performed by experienced operators among middle-aged individuals.MethodsParticipants aged 45–64 in the community of Shanghai were included, and eye disease screenings were carried out after obtaining informed consent. All participants had no cataracts or other conditions that could potentially compromise the quality of fundus imaging. Participants voluntarily chose the fully self-service fundus photography group or the traditional fundus photography group. A statistical analysis was performed to analyse the imaging quality, efficiency and satisfaction of self-service fundus photography.ResultsWe included 457 individuals with an average age of 56.93 years. Fully self-service fundus photography produces similar imaging quality to manual examinations. Additionally, this photography significantly increases residents’ willingness to undergo eye disease screening again, particularly among those with better visual acuity, compared with traditional doctor-led screenings.ConclusionsThis study confirms the reliability of fully self-service fundus photography, and its ability to enhance compliance and willingness for follow-up screenings among individuals aged 45–64. Promoting fully self-service fundus photography is beneficial for screening blinding eye diseases in the middle-aged population. Given the limited geographical scope and age range of this study, larger multicentre studies are needed to confirm the broader applicability of fully self-service fundus photography and to establish effective screening models for high-risk occupational populations.

Introduction

Vision impairment and blindness pose a considerable public health concern. In 2020, approximately 1.1 billion people globally suffered from untreated visual impairments, a number expected to rise to 1.8 billion by 2050.1 2 China has the highest prevalence of blindness and visual impairment among nations, ranking second as a cause of disability.3 Cataracts, glaucoma, age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy and pathological myopia have emerged as primary contributors to blindness in China.4 5 Previous research has indicated that over 90% of vision loss could be prevented or treated through cost-effective interventions.1 High-quality eye care and preventive measures have the potential to reduce blindness cases by nearly 57%.6 Therefore, ophthalmic screening, timely identification of at-risk individuals and prompt referral for treatment are pivotal for the early prevention of vision impairment.

In China, eye disease screening for elderly individuals aged 65 and above has been widely implemented through a centralised screening model in primary healthcare institutions.7 However, there is still a notable lack of attention towards eye disease screening for middle-aged populations. This situation is concerning because adults aged 50 and above are already entering a peak period for the early onset of blinding eye diseases. With age, the incidence of fundus abnormalities in middle-aged people rises significantly, closely aligning with the trend of chronic diseases affecting younger demographics in modern society.8 A growing body of research indicates that eye abnormalities are prevalent among middle-aged individuals, and if these eye diseases are not detected and treated in a timely manner, they could pose a serious threat to vision health.9 10 Therefore, it is crucial to raise awareness about the importance of eye disease screening in middle-aged populations and to promote the early diagnosis and treatment of eye conditions within this demographic.

Most middle-aged professionals struggle to find time for centralised screenings, and primary medical institutions cannot offer round-the-clock eye disease screenings. Also, current community screenings rely on manual fundus photography by doctors, but due to limited healthcare staff and weak ophthalmic capabilities, they fail to meet middle-aged professionals’ quantity and quality needs.11 12 With the ageing population rising, most regions cannot sustain continuous eye disease screenings, hindering efforts to promote screenings among middle-aged professionals. Increasing screening numbers necessitate more healthcare personnel, but the shortage of ophthalmologists leads to high screening costs.1315

To address these challenges, we introduced a fully self-service fundus photography, where individuals capture fundus images themselves and artificial intelligence (AI) performs screening diagnosis, eliminating the constraints of scheduled appointments and manual doctor-led screenings. This study implemented the fully self-service fundus photography among professionals aged 45–64 and compared it with conventional examinations conducted by general practitioners. The objective is to investigate whether the fully self-service fundus photography can deliver superior screening quality, efficiency and satisfaction among middle-aged individuals.

Method

Study design

This study is a cross-sectional research conducted in the Songjiang district of Shanghai from 10 April 2023 to 10 May 2023. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) residents aged 45–64; (2) the ability to read and write independently to complete basic information and satisfaction questionnaires, ensuring accurate self-reporting; and (3) provision of informed consent. Participants with cataracts or other conditions that could compromise fundus image quality were excluded.

The Kestrel 3100m fundus imaging device was used in this study. Designed as a non-mydriatic camera, it effectively captures clear fundus images through pupils as small as 2.8 mm without the need for pharmacological dilation, enhancing accessibility for a wide range of patients. The device offers both manual and fully self-service operation. In self-service mode, the user only needs to position the patient and initiate the process, while the system independently handles alignment, focusing and high-resolution image capture.

This study used two Kestrel 3100m fundus cameras, both identical in appearance and optical design. The only difference is that the automatic model is equipped with a fully self-service photography module and corresponding sensors, allowing residents to complete the screening process independently, while the manual model requires operation by community doctors. Due to the unique nature of fundus examinations in this study, randomisation was not feasible. To address this limitation, participants were given the flexibility to choose their screening method on-site, selecting a queue based on the waiting time for the two devices and their personal preferences. This process did not involve randomisation or any other form of grouping for the residents.

Subgroup analyses will be conducted based on age and vision status. Participants aged 45–64 years will be divided into two age groups: 45–59 years and 60–64 years, based on China’s statutory retirement age of 60. The 45–59 age group mainly undergoes workplace health check-ups, which often exclude fundus screening, while the 60–64 age group, though in the transition to retirement, is not covered by China’s elderly health check-up (available only to those aged 65 and above).15 For vision status, participants will be grouped based on their best eye visual acuity: those with ≥0.5 will be classified as the ‘good vision’ group, and those with <0.5 will be classified as the ‘poor vision’ group.

The rule of events per variable was employed for sample size estimation. In this study, three logit models were established, each comprising four independent variables. Generally, we aimed for 25 events per variable. We set 25 events per variable in general. Referring to a prior study by Dietvorst and Bharti,16 which indicated that when decision-making processes faced high uncertainty, approximately 50% of individuals favoured the algorithms. Based on this, we arrived at a sample size of 200 (4 variables multiplied by 25 events each, with 50% of individuals potentially preferring facilitated screening) for each group.

Measures and outcomes

Every individual underwent fundus photography examination. Two colour images were captured for each eye using the same machine, one centred on the macula and the other on the optic disc, resulting in a total of four photographs. Pupillary dilation was not employed. The manual group examinations were conducted by operators, while the self-service group examinations were performed by residents themselves. In the self-service group, participants started the examination by pressing an on-screen ‘Start’ button. The device then automatically guided them through focusing, image capture and real-time quality checks, with voice assistance to accommodate visually impaired users. During the fundus photography examination, researchers used a stopwatch to record the time required for each screening. Following the completion of both groups’ examinations, AI conducted the screening diagnosis, and residents filled out basic information (age, gender and underlying medical conditions) and screening satisfaction questionnaires. After on-site screenings were completed, all fundus images were evaluated by professional ophthalmologists according to clinical ophthalmology requirements for image quality. The fundus images captured by both instruments were subsequently evaluated using the same AI software for diagnosis. In this study, image quality was identified as the most significant factor influencing diagnostic accuracy.

The primary outcome measured the quality of screening images, which was evaluated by professional ophthalmologists who categorised each fundus image as either qualified or unqualified. The secondary outcomes were screening efficiency and participants’ satisfaction. Screening efficiency is represented by the time required for photography, starting from the fixation of the subject’s head on the fundus camera to the completion of photography and removal of the head from the device, timed by researchers using a stopwatch. Five dimensions were chosen to reflect the quality of eye disease screening services: physiological discomfort during the examination, operational safety, convenience, reliability and willingness to participate in screening again.

Statistics analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean±SD or median (IQR), while categorical variables were presented as counts (percentages). Pearson χ2 test was used for nominal variables, and Mann-Whitney U test was employed for continuous variables in statistical analysis. Following this, we conducted multiple-factor logistic regression and linear regression analyses to assess the significance of individual predictors and their combined effects on the dependent variable. Additionally, we established a logistic interaction model by introducing interaction terms formed through the multiplication of specific variables. SAS V.9.4 (SAS Institute) was used for statistical analysis. Two-sided p values of 0.05 were deemed significant if not otherwise indicated.

Results

This study included a total of 457 screening participants aged 45–64, with an average age of 56.93±5.12 years. Among them, there were 162 males (35.45%) and 295 females (64.55%). The manual group consisted of 237 individuals (51.86%), while the self-service group consisted of 220 individuals (48.14%). There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of age, gender and basic diseases (ie, hypertension and diabetes). The visual acuity of the manual group was significantly different from that of the fully self-service group (online supplemental table 1).

Screening quality and efficiency

In the manual group, each participant captured an average of 2.87±1.24 high-quality images out of the four images collected. In the self-service group, each participant captured an average of 2.96±1.36 high-quality images. There was no statistically significant difference in the number of high-quality images between the two groups (p=0.420). The average screening time per resident in the manual group was 90.95±40.46 s, while in the self-service group, it was 108.40±53.19 s. The screening time in the manual group was significantly shorter than that in the self-service group (p<0.001).

Screening satisfaction

The satisfaction survey conducted among screening participants revealed that in the manual group, 194 individuals (81.86%) expressed willingness to participate in screening again, whereas in the self-service group, 203 individuals (92.27%) expressed willingness to participate again. The intention to participate again was significantly higher in the self-service group compared with the manual group (p<0.01). There were no significant differences between the two groups in terms of physiological discomfort during the examination, perceived safety of the examination, convenience of the examination process and trust in the results (table 1).

Subgroup analysis

Subgroup analysis was conducted with 60 years as the cut-off point, dividing participants into two age groups: 45–59 years and 60–64 years. There was no statistically significant difference in the number of high-quality images between the manual and automatic screening groups in both age subgroups (p=0.16 for the 45–59 years group; p=0.62 for the 60–64 years group). However, the screening time in the automatic group was significantly higher than that in the manual group in both age groups (p=0.01 for the 45–59 years group; p<0.001 for the 60–64 years group). In the survey assessing willingness to participate in screening again, 92.91% of participants in the 45–59 years automatic group expressed readiness to undergo screening again, compared with only 79.59% in the manual group. The automatic group showed a significantly higher willingness than the manual group (p<0.01). Conversely, no meaningful difference was found in the willingness to participate in screening again between the manual and automatic groups in the 60–64 years group. Additionally, there were no statistically significant differences in perceived safety, convenience and trust in results between the manual and automatic groups in both age groups, as detailed in table 2.

Factors of screening quality and willingness

To further investigate the factors influencing the number of high-quality fundus images, a multiple linear regression analysis was conducted, incorporating screening method, gender, age and visual acuity as potential influencing factors. The results indicated that residents with better visual acuity significantly obtained a higher number of high-quality fundus images (p<0.001). Furthermore, there was no significant association between the number of high-quality fundus images and the screening method used (p=0.78) (online supplemental table 2).

The study also analysed factors influencing screening time. Multiple linear regression results revealed that residents with better visual acuity, when undergoing fully self-service fundus examination compared with traditional manual examination by doctors, experienced an increase in examination time by 17 s (p<0.001). Additionally, examination time increased with age (p<0.001). However, visual acuity and gender did not affect examination time (online supplemental table 3).

When exploring factors influencing willingness to undergo screening again using a multiple logistic regression model, after including the interaction term between screening method and visual acuity, it was found that there was a multiplicative interaction effect between screening method and better visual acuity on willingness to undergo screening again. The results suggested that compared with the traditional doctor-led eye disease screening model, allowing residents with better visual acuity to undergo fully self-service screening could significantly increase their compliance with undergoing screening again (OR, 1.50; 95% CI, 1.06 to 2.12; p=0.02). However, for residents with poor visual acuity, the use of fully self-service screening did not significantly increase their compliance with undergoing screening again (OR, 1.35; 95% CI, 0.95 to 1.90; p=0.10), as detailed in table 4.

Discussion

This study is the first to validate the quality and satisfaction of a fully self-service fundus examination model among individuals aged 45–64. Our findings suggest that when residents conduct fully self-service fundus examinations, the quality of images obtained is similar to that of manual examinations. Moreover, fully self-service screening can significantly increase residents’ compliance with undergoing regular screening again. This is mainly attributed to the significant improvement in acceptance of the fully self-service screening among individuals with better visual acuity, compared with the traditional doctor-led manual screening model. Therefore, the fully self-service fundus examination model is worthy of promotion among individuals aged 45–64.

Currently, fully self-service fundus cameras are being increasingly used in real medical settings.17 18 This study specifically focuses on middle-aged populations, aiming to provide a new approach to eye disease screening for this demographic. First, the study confirms that the image quality obtained through fully self-service fundus examinations is comparable to that of manual examinations, which aligns with the findings of previous studies.17 Additionally, subgroup analyses based on age and visual acuity were conducted, showing that neither age nor visual acuity affects the image quality captured by the fully self-service fundus camera. While ensuring image quality, fully self-service fundus cameras equipped with mature AI diagnostic software can provide preliminary diagnoses simultaneously with capturing fundus photographs, offering the possibility of conducting fundus screenings in public settings. The clinical potential of the AI diagnostic has been demonstrated in several studies, particularly for common and prevalent diseases such as cataracts, glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy.13 15 19 20 Therefore, this study provides evidence for the screening quality of fully self-service fundus photography, suggesting that it could be a feasible method for eye disease screening.

Furthermore, our study indicates that the fully self-service fundus screening model does not exacerbate residents’ concerns regarding safety, comfort, convenience or the accuracy of results. This can be attributed to several factors: (1) the generally higher educational attainment of the middle-aged cohort in our study, which likely facilitates greater acceptance of emerging technologies such as self-service systems and AI; (2) the historical shortage of specialist ophthalmologists in primary healthcare settings, which has compromised the accuracy and feasibility of eye disease diagnoses, thus failing to meet the quality expectations of middle-aged professionals11 12; (3) the current centralised screening model implemented by primary healthcare institutions, which typically operates during fixed hours on weekdays. Fully self-service fundus cameras can effectively address these issues, expanding the scope of use and coverage, particularly benefiting high-risk individuals aged 45–64 who may lack sufficient time to participate in primary eye disease screenings. Although the time required for fully self-service fundus examinations is slightly longer, with an average increase of 17 s per screening, considering factors such as physician fatigue and doctor–patient communication influencing manual screenings, the impact on actual screening efficiency is minimal.

The study found that in the 45–59 age group, the automatic group showed significantly higher compliance with follow-up screenings compared with the manual group. This may be due to individuals in this age range still being in the workforce. Although the self-service screening process takes slightly longer than the manual screening, its enhanced privacy and convenience make it a more favourable option for professionals. As most individuals in this age group are employed professionals who value confidentiality, the self-contained operating spaces and standardised processes of automated systems address privacy concerns more effectively than traditional face-to-face screenings. Additionally, automated screening offers greater convenience by eliminating time and space constraints, allowing check-ups without strict appointments—better suiting the fragmented schedules of working professionals. In China, workplace health check-ups typically include only vision tests, and fundus screenings are not widely incorporated into these routine examinations. This systemic gap leads to low awareness and willingness for proactive eye health checks. The fully automated screening model effectively fills this void—its ease of use lowers barriers, real-time quality feedback enhances result reliability, and flexible timing addresses the common excuse of ‘no time for checkups’. Given the advantages of convenience, along with increasing trust in the safety and results of fully self-service fundus screenings, there is significant potential for promoting this model among the working population. Individuals with better eyesight are more likely to follow-up after self-service screenings, possibly because those with poorer eyesight prefer consulting a doctor during the examination, a service currently unavailable in self-service screenings. Incorporating online or AI consultations into self-service screenings could improve follow-up compliance in the future.2125

Moreover, the fully automated fundus camera used in our study, the Kestrel 3100m, has received approval from the National Medical Products Administration of China, ensuring its compliance with local regulatory standards for safety and efficacy. A key benefit of automated screening is its enhanced accessibility, providing a rapid and cost-effective solution that reduces the burden on healthcare providers and enables large-scale screenings, especially in underserved communities.13 15 26 27 Importantly, our findings demonstrate that fully self-service fundus screening, when implemented in real-world settings, offers image quality and participant satisfaction comparable to traditional manual methods. This supports the potential of self-service systems to provide reliable diagnostic results without compromising quality or user experience.

The main limitation of this study is the lack of randomisation and blinding due to the nature of fundus examination, where participants undergo a specific procedure using both automated and manual instruments. While full randomisation and blinding were not feasible, efforts were made to minimise bias, such as allowing participants to choose between the two screening methods and blinding ophthalmologists who assessed image quality. Additionally, subgroup analyses based on age and vision status were conducted to account for potential confounding variables and provide a more nuanced interpretation of the results, offering valuable insights into the effectiveness of both screening methods. Additionally, the study was limited to a single community in Songjiang district, Shanghai, and focused on a relatively narrow age range within the working population, which may constrain the generalisability of the findings. These limitations underscore the need for larger, multicentre studies to assess the broader applicability of fully self-service fundus photography.

Conclusion

This study confirms the reliability of the fully self-service eye disease screening model and its effectiveness in improving compliance with regular screenings among individuals aged 45–64. The model also notably increases willingness for follow-up screenings among those with better vision who undergo the self-service screening. Given the study’s limited geographical scope and age range, larger multicentre studies are needed to confirm the broader applicability of this approach. Nevertheless, the fully self-service fundus examination model demonstrates strong potential for early detection of blinding eye diseases in middle-aged professionals and may inform future occupational screening strategies.

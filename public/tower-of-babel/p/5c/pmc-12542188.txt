Efficacy of semilunar ganglion radiofrequency thermal coagulation in moderate and severe trigeminal postherpetic neuralgia and its impact on serum IL-1β and IL-6 levels
Authors: Cong Wu, Yi Lv, Conghuan Wang, Changyang Zhong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTrigeminal postherpetic neuralgia (TPHN) represents a severe neuropathic pain syndrome with challenging therapeutic management. While radiofrequency thermocoagulation (RF-TC), a minimally invasive neuromodulation technique, has demonstrated favorable outcomes in treating thoracoabdominal and lumbosacral PHN, its clinical application in trigeminal nerve distributions remains underexplored.ObjectiveTo evaluate gasserian ganglion RF-TC efficacy in moderate-to-severe TPHN and its effects on serum IL-1β and IL-6 levels.MethodsThis study enrolled 120 eligible patients diagnosed with TPHN at the Department of Cerebrovascular Diseases, Hangzhou Third People’s Hospital, between January 2024 and December 2024. Patients were stratified into moderate (n = 60) and severe (n = 60) pain cohorts based on baseline pain severity. Severe PHN patients were randomly assigned to either the RF-TC group (Group A, n = 30) or the control group (Group B, n = 30), while moderate PHN patients were randomized to the RF-TC group (Group C, n = 30) or the control group (Group D, n = 30). All RF-TC procedures were performed via a foramen ovale approach under digital subtraction angiography (DSA) guidance. Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) scores and serum cytokine levels (IL-1β, IL-6) were quantified pre-treatment and at 1-/4-week post-intervention using standardized ELISA protocols.ResultsRF-TC groups (A/C) demonstrated significantly lower VAS scores and reduced serum IL-1β/IL-6 concentrations compared to controls (B/D) at both follow-ups (all p < 0.01), with enhanced therapeutic magnitude observed in severe cases. These findings indicate RF-TC's dual mechanism of pain alleviation and systemic anti-inflammatory modulation.ConclusionGasserian ganglion RF-TC is a safe, effective TPHN treatment, achieving significant pain reduction and proinflammatory cytokine suppression.Keywords: Postherpetic neuralgia, Trigeminal nerve, Radiofrequency thermal coagulation, IL-1β, IL-6, Neuropathic pain, Trigeminal nerve post, Herpetic neuralgia

Introduction

Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is a persistent neuropathic pain that continues following the resolution of the herpes zoster (HZ) rash. It is the most common complication of herpes zoster infection [1]. While herpes zoster typically affects the thoracic and lumbar regions, the trigeminal nerve is also commonly involved. Trigeminal postherpetic neuralgia (TPHN) is characterized by continuous or paroxysmal burning pain, stabbing sensations, and electric shock-like pain. It may also be accompanied by sensory abnormalities, hypersensitivity, and other symptoms. In severe cases, TPHN can significantly impair the ability to eat, speak, and engage in daily activities, causing both psychological and physiological distress [2]. Statistically, trigeminal herpes zoster accounts for 15%–20% of all herpes zoster cases. Due to its complex pathophysiology and high incidence, TPHN has emerged as a pressing public health concern [3].

The clinical management of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) primarily involves pharmacological therapy, nerve blocks, pulse radiofrequency, traditional Chinese medicine, and topical treatments. Pharmacological interventions remain the first-line treatment; however, single-drug therapies often fail to provide effective pain relief. Additionally, long-term use of high-dose medications can lead to adverse effects and, more critically, delay timely intervention. Therefore, identifying proactive and effective treatments during the subacute phase is crucial to prevent the development of PHN. Neuromodulation techniques, such as radiofrequency treatment, represent a promising frontier in the management of neuropathic pain (NPP). Among these, radiofrequency thermal coagulation (RF-TC) and pulsed radiofrequency (PRF) are the most commonly used methods. Although PRF has demonstrated significant analgesic effects in alleviating herpes zoster-related pain, some patients experience inadequate pain relief, with a high rate of relapse in the short term, often necessitating additional analgesic interventions [4–6]. In contrast, RF-TC, which involves the targeted thermal destruction of the dorsal root ganglion, has been shown to significantly reduce pain and improve clinical outcomes [7]. While RF-TC has yielded promising results in treating PHN in the chest, abdomen, and lumbar regions, its application to PHN in the trigeminal nerve distribution remains underexplored.

The pathogenesis of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is closely associated with persistent neuroinflammation within both the central and peripheral nervous systems. Interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) are key pro-inflammatory cytokines involved in neuroinflammation [8, 9]. In the development of hyperalgesia, IL-1β activates the NF-κB signaling pathway, leading to the upregulation of cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) and prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), which in turn enhances the excitability of both peripheral and central neurons [10, 11]. Similarly, IL-6 activates the JAK/STAT signaling pathway, amplifying the cytokine cascade and regulating neuronal calcium channels, thereby exacerbating hyperalgesia [12]. Moreover, IL-1β and IL-6 act synergistically, mutually promoting inflammation and perpetuating the chronic pain state. These characteristics make IL-1β and IL-6 reliable biomarkers for assessing neuroinflammation and pain severity. By monitoring the dynamic changes in IL-1β and IL-6 levels, the analgesic mechanisms underlying interventions such as radiofrequency thermal coagulation (RF-TC) can be elucidated, providing an objective basis for evaluating treatment efficacy. Therefore, the aim of this study is to assess the efficacy of semilunar ganglion radiofrequency treatment in trigeminal nerve distribution herpes zoster neuralgia and its effects on IL-1β and IL-6 levels.

Materials and methods

Source of data

A total of 120 patients diagnosed with postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) who met the inclusion criteria were selected from those treated at the Third People's Hospital of Hangzhou between January 2024 and December 2024. These patients were categorized into two groups based on pain severity: 60 patients with moderate pain and 60 with severe pain. Randomization was used to assign 30 patients with severe pain to receive radiofrequency thermal coagulation (RF-TC) treatment for PHN, forming Group A. The remaining 30 severe pain patients were assigned to the control group, Group B. Similarly, 30 patients from the moderate pain group were randomly selected to receive RF-TC treatment, forming Group C, while the remaining patients were assigned to Group D as the control group. All participants provided written informed consent. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Third People's Hospital of Hangzhou and was conducted in accordance with the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki(2024KA155).

Treatment methods

All four patient groups received conventional treatment, which included oral pregabalin (Qilu Pharmaceutical Company) at a dose of 75 mg twice daily, along with a daily intramuscular injection of mecobalamin (0.5 mg). For patients who were dissatisfied with pain relief, the pregabalin dose could be gradually increased, with a maximum dose of 300 mg twice daily. Surgical patients signed an informed consent form prior to the procedure. Electrocardiographic monitoring was applied, and patients were positioned in the supine position with a pillow placed under the shoulders. The head was tilted backward and secured with a bandage. All radiofrequency treatments were performed in accordance with standardized guidelines.

Selective radiofrequency thermal coagulation via the foramen ovale approach

Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA) was used to adjust the head and tail angles for a semi-coronal scan, and the surface location for needle insertion was marked. The optimal plane for exposing the foramen ovale was selected as the puncture plane. Based on the surface markings, the puncture depth and angle were determined, and the surface puncture point was marked. The facial area was disinfected, and sterile drapes were applied. A 22-gauge, 5 mm working length needle was used for puncture. Under DSA guidance, the needle tip was carefully inserted into the mouth of the foramen ovale. After confirming the absence of blood or cerebrospinal fluid upon aspiration, a radiofrequency electrode was connected for sensory testing (100 Hz, 0.1–0.3 V), which induced pain in the patient’s lower jaw. Motor testing (2 Hz, 0.1–0.3 V) was then performed, resulting in muscle spasms in the lower lip and jaw. The puncture was considered successful when these responses were elicited.

In the second step, the plane exposing the mouth of the foramen ovale was selected under DSA guidance. Using the same method, pain in the upper jaw was induced to confirm the success of the puncture (Fig. 1).

DSA-guided percutaneous puncture of the foramen ovale for radiofrequency thermocoagulation

Radiofrequency thermal coagulation

Following successful puncture under DSA guidance, a three-dimensional reconstruction was performed to confirm the puncture site and surface puncture point. A mixture of 4 mg of lidocaine and 1 mg of dexamethasone (total volume 0.2 mL) was injected into both the oval and foramen rotundum. The radiofrequency electrode was then inserted, and the radiofrequency machine was connected. Continuous radiofrequency thermal coagulation was performed in stages: 60 °C for 60 s, 65 °C for 60 s, and 70 °C for 60 s. After completing the procedure, the needle was removed, and hemostatic measures were applied.

Laboratory indicators detection methods

Upon admission, a 6 mL venous blood sample was collected from each patient on the first day, as well as 1 week and 4 weeks after radiofrequency thermal coagulation (RF-TC) treatment. The blood samples were allowed to stand at room temperature for 30 min, followed by centrifugation to separate the serum. The serum was then stored in EP tubes at -80 °C until further analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY). The normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while categorical variables are described as frequencies and percentages. Between-group comparisons of continuous variables were analyzed using independent-samples t-tests, whereas categorical variables (e.g., sex, hypertension, diabetes, smoking status, and alcohol consumption) were compared using chi-square (χ2) tests. One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed for comparisons among multiple groups within a single factor. For count data, chi-square tests were utilized for intergroup comparisons. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Surgical safety and complications

All patients in the radiofrequency thermal coagulation (RF-TC) groups (Group A and Group C) were closely monitored for procedure-related complications during hospitalization and at 1- and 4-week follow-ups. The overall complication rate was low, and no severe adverse events occurred.

The most common post-procedural complication was mild facial hypoesthesia, reported in 12 patients (20.0%), predominantly in the maxillary (V2) and mandibular (V3) divisions of the trigeminal nerve. All cases were mild and well-tolerated, with spontaneous improvement observed within 4 weeks. Masseter muscle weakness was noted in 3 patients (5.0%), all of whom regained normal masticatory function within 2–3 weeks without intervention. One patient (1.7%) exhibited transient reduction in the corneal reflex on the treated side; no ocular complications such as keratitis developed during follow-up (Table 3). No significant differences in complication rates were observed between the moderate and severe pain RF-TC subgroups. All complications were transient and self-limiting, with no need for additional surgical or pharmacological intervention.

Discussion

Trigeminal postherpetic neuralgia (TPHN) is a severe form of neuropathic pain that occurs when the varicella-zoster virus reactivates and affects the trigeminal ganglion or its branches. The trigeminal nerve is the primary sensory nerve of the head and face, innervating the skin, mouth, teeth, and parts of deeper head structures. Due to the complex anatomical structure and wide distribution of the trigeminal nerve, patients with TPHN often experience intense facial pain, numbness, or tenderness, with symptoms typically being persistent, periodic, or intermittent. As such, treating TPHN is more challenging than managing herpes zoster-related neuralgia in other areas of the body [13]. In this study, we found that after radiofrequency thermal coagulation (RF-TC) treatment, patients in both treatment groups (Groups A and C) experienced significant pain relief, a marked reduction in analgesic medication use, and an improvement in quality of life. Furthermore, the levels of IL-1β and IL-6 in the serum of these patients decreased significantly post-treatment.

Serum IL-1β and IL-6 are key pro-inflammatory cytokines that play pivotal roles in neuroinflammation and pain transmission. The mechanisms by which IL-1β and IL-6 contribute to pain are multifaceted. IL-1β activates the NF-κB signaling pathway, promoting the expression of inflammatory mediators, enhancing neuronal excitability, and lowering pain thresholds, thereby increasing pain sensitivity [14]. RF-TC induces localized nerve tissue and immune cell damage through the heat generated, which reduces the release of inflammatory mediators, inhibits the activation of the NF-κB pathway, and subsequently lowers IL-1β levels, mitigating the neuroinflammatory response. IL-6, a pleiotropic cytokine, is involved not only in immune responses and the acute-phase reaction but also in regulating neuronal growth and differentiation through the JAK/STAT3 signaling pathway [12]. Elevated IL-6 levels can lead to neuronal hyperexcitability, thereby enhancing pain transmission. RF-TC reduces IL-6 levels, weakens JAK/STAT3 pathway activation, and inhibits neuronal hyperexcitability, contributing to pain relief. Furthermore, the decrease in IL-1β and IL-6 levels may reduce central nervous system inflammation, suppress microglial activation, and further attenuate the transmission and amplification of pain signals [15]. The synergistic effects of these molecular mechanisms likely form the basis for RF-TC’s analgesic effect in the treatment of PHN.

Our study found that after RF-TC treatment, the reduction in IL-1β and IL-6 levels was significantly greater in the treatment groups compared to the control group receiving conventional drug therapy. Moreover, IL-1β and IL-6 levels were significantly lower in the post-treatment periods (1 week and 4 weeks), and the degree of reduction positively correlated with the level of pain relief. This suggests that RF-TC not only blocks pain transmission through a physical mechanism but also modulates the inflammatory response by lowering pro-inflammatory cytokine levels, thereby further alleviating neuropathic pain. This conclusion is consistent with the findings of Zhong et al., where serum IL-6 levels were used to assess the severity of PHN in patients [16].

The study findings demonstrate that radiofrequency thermocoagulation (RF-TC) exhibits significant efficacy in alleviating pain in patients with postherpetic neuralgia (PHN). Groups A and C showed significantly greater reductions in Visual Analog Scale (VAS) scores at 1 week and 4 weeks post-treatment compared to Groups B and D, indicating superior therapeutic effects of RF-TC over conventional control treatments for trigeminal postherpetic neuralgia (TPHN). Notably, the reduction in VAS scores was more pronounced in the severe pain subgroup.These results are consistent with prior reports on the efficacy of RF-TC in managing thoracoabdominal and lumbosacral PHN [17, 18]. A comprehensive comparative study by Wang et al. revealed that both RF-TC and pulsed radiofrequency (PRF) effectively reduced pain in elderly patients with thoracic PHN, though RF-TC demonstrated better long-term outcomes [19]. RF-TC achieves pain relief by delivering high-frequency currents to generate temperatures ≥ 45 °C, inducing ablative neurothermal coagulation. The thermal effects inactivate hyperexcitable nerve terminals caused by lesions, disrupt nociceptive pathways, and mitigate neuroinflammatory responses [20]. Our results align with existing literature supporting the effectiveness of RF-TC in PHN management. Previous studies have also demonstrated that RF-TC significantly reduces pain scores in PHN patients, with a more pronounced effect particularly observed in the severe pain group. Similarly, Liu et al. [21] compared RF-TC with PRF and found that RF-TC outperformed PRF in lowering VAS scores and inflammatory cytokine levels. Our findings on the role of neuroinflammation in TPHN and the modulatory effects of RF-TC are consistent with the existing literature. Emerging evidence further elucidates the critical interplay among viral reactivation, neural injury, and subsequent inflammatory cascades in PHN. For instance, sustained inflammatory responses—driven by overexpression of cytokines such as IL-1β and IL-6—not only facilitate peripheral and central sensitization but also perpetuate a vicious cycle contributing to chronic pain persistence [22]. This understanding reinforces the rationale for targeting the Gasserian ganglion with RF-TC, as it constitutes a key site of viral latency and inflammatory signaling in TPHN. The concurrent reduction in both pain scores and pro-inflammatory cytokine levels following RF-TC treatment, as observed in our study, provides dual validation of its mechanistic action: ablation of pathological neural sources disrupts this inflammatory cycle [23]. The biochemical corroboration of clinical efficacy underscores the potential of RF-TC as a therapeutic strategy for medically refractory TPHN.While PRF has been shown to modulate neuronal activity, alter synaptic transmission, and reduce pathological microglial proliferation [24, 25], its clinical utility is limited by high recurrence rates and transient side effects, such as mild numbness in innervated regions post-treatment [17, 26]. Additionally, PRF exhibits poor efficacy in managing primary trigeminal neuralgia [27]. In contrast, RF-TC offers durable pain control, though concerns remain regarding potential neurodamage, particularly in complex trigeminal nerve distributions [28]. Most prior studies focused on RF-TC for lumbosacral PHN, with limited evidence supporting PRF under CT guidance for acute trigeminal herpes zoster-related neuralgia [29]. Distinctively, our study specifically targets PHN in the trigeminal nerve distribution, pioneering the application of RF-TC to the trigeminal ganglion under precise digital subtraction angiography (DSA) guidance with selective puncture techniques. This approach minimized intraoperative complications and ensured procedural safety. By addressing the anatomical complexity of the trigeminal region, our innovative methodology not only enhances the therapeutic potential of RF-TC but also fills a critical gap in existing research on trigeminal PHN management.

In this study, we evaluated the effects of semilunar ganglion RF-TC in relieving pain and modulating inflammatory cytokines IL-1β and IL-6 in PHN patients. Our results showed that RF-TC significantly reduced VAS scores and effectively lowered IL-1β and IL-6 levels, particularly in patients with severe pain. As a minimally invasive neuromodulation technique, RF-TC can effectively relieve PHN pain during the subacute phase, reduce patients' reliance on medications, and lower the risk of adverse effects associated with long-term drug use. Furthermore, by significantly lowering IL-1β and IL-6 levels, RF-TC may not only alleviate pain but also improve patients' neurological function and quality of life by modulating the inflammatory response. Given the complex anatomical structure of the trigeminal nerve, treating PHN in this area remains a clinical challenge. RF-TC offers an effective treatment option through precise localization and selective nerve disruption, making it especially suitable for patients with poor responses to pharmacological treatments or those who cannot tolerate drug side effects. This provides more treatment options for PHN patients, improving overall treatment outcomes and patient satisfaction.

Limitations

Despite the positive results, this study has several limitations. First, the sample size was relatively small, and the study was conducted at a single center, which may affect the generalizability of the results. Second, the follow-up duration was short, limiting our ability to assess the long-term efficacy and safety of RF-TC. Future prospective cohort studies with extended follow-up (≥ 6 months) are warranted to comprehensively evaluate the durability of pain relief and long-term safety of this procedure.Third, although all patients had PHN within the trigeminal nerve distribution, we did not perform a subgroup analysis based on the specific branches involved (V1, V2, or V3). The anatomical and functional differences between these branches could potentially influence treatment outcomes, and this lack of granularity is a limitation of our current analysis. Future studies should increase the sample size, adopt a multi-center design, incorporate detailed branch-specific evaluations, and extend the follow-up period to validate the reliability of these results. Additionally, further molecular mechanism studies are needed to deepen our understanding of the action mechanisms of RF-TC in PHN treatment, providing a solid theoretical foundation for clinical applications.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that semilunar ganglion RF-TC significantly relieves pain in patients with trigeminal herpes zoster neuralgia and effectively reduces serum IL-1β and IL-6 levels, thereby alleviating neuroinflammation. RF-TC, as a safe and effective treatment method, holds promise for clinical application and can offer better treatment options for PHN patients.

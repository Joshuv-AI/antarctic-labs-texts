Identity-consistent and high-fidelity audio-driven portrait animation with enhanced latent diffusion
Authors: Xiangwen Ma, Jiaxin Zhao, Xiaoyu Huang, San Li, Yang Li, Junping Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSpeech-driven portrait animation generation models have made significant progress in generating realistic and dynamic portrait animations. The class of end-to-end latent diffusion paradigms represented by Hallo achieves impressive results in terms of alignment accuracy between audio inputs and visual outputs, encompassing lip movements, expressions and head poses. However, constrained by the suboptimal interaction design between reference portrait information and the denoising U-Net in such architectures, certain frames in the output video sequences suffer from inconsistencies in identity and background preservation. Moreover, the temporal attention within the temporal module operates by incorporating information across frames within each generation unit to capture overall motion trends, but ignoring shorter frame subsequences within the generation unit, consequently losing fine-grained details between adjacent frames. In order to solve the above problems, we take the end-to-end latent diffusion paradigm Hallo as the backbone, and construct a Multi-Source Self Attention (MSSA) to optimize the interaction between reference portrait identity information and denoising U-Net. In addition, we also propose a plug-and-play, training-free method known as Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA), which enables simultaneously capture local high-frequency facial details from shorter frame subsequences within each generation unit, thereby improving facial fidelity in synthesized portrait videos. Our method is comprehensively evaluated on public dataset and our collected datasets from qualitative and quantitative analysis. The results demonstrate that the portrait animation videos generated by our method are better able to preserve identity and background consistency with the reference portrait, as well as exhibiting superior facial detail fidelity.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, image generation techniques have achieved remarkable progress in computer vision. Among current approaches, diffusion models1–5 have emerged as a dominant paradigm, renowned for their high-quality generation, training stability, flexible controllability, and strong generalization capabilities. By gradually introducing noise into the training data and then progressively reconstructing the signal from the noise through an inverse process, the diffusion model is able to generate high-fidelity and diverse images while avoiding the mode collapse and training instability problems in traditional generative adversarial networks6. Furthermore, diffusion models have demonstrated remarkable flexibility and scalability in conditional generation4,7, image inpainting8,9, and cross-domain tasks10. With recent research advancements, the diffusion model has been further extended to video generation tasks11,12. By modeling temporal consistency between video frames, the diffusion model is capable of generating high-quality and temporally coherent dynamic content. This advancement provides powerful technical support for complex tasks such as portrait animation generation13,14, revealing significant potential in virtual reality, film production, and human-computer interaction domains.

Portrait animation generation is to generate realistic and natural dynamic portraits from a single static image and the corresponding speech audio. The key lies in the precise synchronization and natural coordination between the input audio and the generated facial movements, including lip movements, expressions and head poses, while maintaining both high visual fidelity and temporal coherence in the generated animation sequences. To achieve audio-facial movements alignment, parametric models15–18 derive facial landmarks19 or the motion parameters of a 3D face model20 from audio as an intermediate representation. These are then used to drive animation generation through further rendering. However, such parametric intermediate representations suffer from limitations in accuracy and expressiveness. Several approaches have developed powerful base diffusion model for high-quality video11,21 and image generation4 with large-scale image or video datasets. To generate high-quality animations with visual appeal and temporal coherence, the end-to-end diffusion models EMO13 and Hallo14 modify the architecture of the base Stable Diffusion4 with some plug-and-play modules and utilize the pre-trained diffusion models as powerful prior information to synthesize portrait videos directly from images and audio clips without intermediate representations or complex preprocessing, thus enabling visual outputs with high resolution and rich details.

Since the base diffusion models struggle to preserve reference portrait identity during the direct processing of portrait animation, the end-to-end diffusion models13,14,22 employ a ReferenceNet12 to extract the identity information of the reference portrait, which enables the generated portrait video to preserve consistent identity information with the input reference portrait. However, while this method can achieve better preservation of reference portrait identity, the design of its identity information introduction module is relatively coarse. When propagating reference portrait identity information through the denoising U-Net, the direct feature concatenation approach for fusion can lead to inconsistencies between portrait identity and background information in some generated animation frames. On the other hand, to facilitate the understanding and encoding of temporal relationships between consecutive video frames, existing works13,14,22 attempt to insert temporal attention module into pre-trained Stable Diffusion architecture11. By using the final frame of the previous video segment as the initial frame for the subsequent one, visually consistent long-term video can be generated. In the subsequent section, we refer to each generated video segment as a generation unit, and the temporal attention applied within each generation unit as global temporal attention. However, relying solely on a single global temporal attention within each generation unit may overlook shorter frame subsequences, leading to the loss of high-frequency details between adjacent frames. In addition, these models13,14 are mainly trained and evaluated on English datasets, which results in their performance often deteriorating when the input audio is in Chinese. The lack of large-scale and high-quality Chinese dataset has hindered the development of portrait animation generation in Chinese.

In this paper, we adhere to the end-to-end diffusion model framework and take the Hallo pre-trained model14 as the base model in network structure design. We aim to address the limitations in identity and background preservation capabilities, while enhancing the detail fidelity of generated portrait animations. To address the first limitation, considering that Magicpose22 has already verified that the self-attention layers in the U-Net play an important role to transmit the appearance information spatially, we introduce the multi-source self-attention (MSSA) mechanism based on this observation. By fusing key-value pairs from both the pre-trained Stable Diffusion U-Net and its dual module ReferenceNet, the constructed MSSA is capable of ensuring the consistency of reference portrait identity and background throughout the generation process. For detail fidelity, motivated by the method of FreeLong23, we construct a Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) and incorporate it as a new temporal attention module into the denoising architecture of the pre-trained model. U-SBTA integrates global and local features within each generation unit through two parallel streams, enhancing the realism and detail fidelity of the generated portrait animations. The global stream processes all frame sequences within each generation unit, capturing the dependencies and motion trends among all frames within the unit to retain smooth transitions. Meanwhile, the local stream focuses on shorter frame sequences within the unit to capture the detailed dynamics of adjacent frames, preserving high-frequency spatial and temporal information. Our constructed U-SBTA integrates global and local video features within each generation unit in the frequency domain, enhancing both consistency and fidelity by blending low-frequency global features with high-frequency local features. Furthermore, the constructed U-SBTA is fully compatible with pre-trained weights of the original temporal attention module, allowing a training-free integration. To enhance the generalization ability of portrait animations in Chinese audio, we construct a dedicated Chinese talking head video dataset to facilitate training and evaluation under Chinese audio-driven scenarios.

In order to evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed method, we conduct comprehensive experiments on the public dataset HDTF and our collected “Wild” dataset, and compare with existing methods in quantitative indicators such as generated image and video quality, lip synchronization, facial expressiveness, and reference portrait identity and background preservation, as well as qualitative visual effects. Our method has verified that the feature fusion approach using a MSSA mechanism can enhance the consistency between the generated portrait animations and the identity and background information of the input reference portrait. Furthermore, the training-free U-SBTA module inserted in the base model has achieved promising progress in the quality of generated images and videos, as well as visual effects such as facial details of portraits.

Related work

Diffusion-based image/video generation

Diffusion models have made remarkable progress in the field of image generation in recent years, and they perform well in generating high-quality and diverse images. Representative works such as Stable Diffusion models4 achieve high-resolution image synthesis by applying diffusion models in the latent space, significantly reduces computational complexity while maintaining visual fidelity. By introducing cross-attention layers, Stable Diffusion can process general conditional inputs (e.g., text prompts, bounding boxes) and have established advanced performance across diverse image generation tasks. ControlNet24 further adds conditional control capabilities to the Stable Diffusion model, enabling the model to generate images that better meet user requirements based on input conditions such as edge maps and depth maps. The success of the diffusion model in the field of image generation lays the foundation for its extension to the field of video generation. AnimateDiff11 is a method that transform a personalized text-to-image diffusion model into an animation generator. By inserting temporal modules, it can generate animations without model-specific tuning, capable of producing temporally smooth animation clips while maintaining visual quality and motion diversity. A key challenge in video generation lies in long-duration synthesis. VidRD25 addresses this by combining an autoregressive paradigm with latent diffusion model4, enabling the creation of seamless and temporally coherent long-form video narratives. On the other hand, current video diffusion models are usually trained on short videos of fixed length, limiting their ability to produce longer videos. FreeLong23 extends these short-video diffusion models through a spectral blending temporal attention mechanism, addressing the issue of high-frequency distortion in long video generation and significantly improving video quality and consistency. These works have collectively advanced the application and development of diffusion models in the field of image/video generation, providing important theoretical and technical foundations for the speech-driven portrait animation generation based on diffusion models in this paper.

Portrait animation generation

In the field of speech-driven talking head generation, early studies mainly focus on video-based methods26–28, which generate dynamic facial videos with natural expressions and movements synchronized with audio through the input audio and the target video. Wav2Lip26 significantly improves the lip-sync accuracy of dynamic videos for arbitrary identities by learning from a pre-trained lip-sync expert discriminator. AudioDVP27 employs a 3D Morphable Model (3DMM)29 to parametrically represent facial images and learns the mapping from audio features to facial expression parameters. By combining the learned facial expression parameters with other parameters from the target video, it can create and re-render dynamic portrait videos with audio-synchronized facial animations. Their limitation lies in only modifying the lip movements of the talking head in the original target video, while other head movements such as posture and blinking remain consistent with the original video. This may restrict their authenticity. To enable the generated talking head to have natural lip movements, head poses and blinking synchronized with the input audio, FACIAL28 relies on a parametric face model. Through joint learning of explicit and implicit attributes, it can generate realistic facial videos with personalized head poses and natural blinking movements which are synchronized with the input audio. Recent advancements have shifted toward portrait-based animation generation methods, which offer higher flexibility and broader applicability compared to video-based approaches. SadTalker30 and VividTalk31, as representatives of a class of parameterized models, rely on learning blendshape or 3DMM facial expression parameters from audio as intermediate representations to guide the generation of final video. In order to further improve the expressivity of the generated portrait animation video, the mainstream approaches employ the diffusion model as the generative framework. Dreamtalk32 takes an audio, a style reference video, and noisy facial motions as inputs, and uses a diffusion model to predict noise-free facial motions. The facial motions are derived from 3DMM face parameters rather than directly denoising image frames. DiffTalk33 builds upon latent diffusion model to perform direct image denoising, while innovating control mechanisms which utilize reference portrait and landmarks as personalized synthesis conditions, and models the portrait animation generation as a temporally coherent denoising process driven by audio, enabling generalization to any identity without a fine-tuning procedure. Animate Anyone12 proposes a foundational framework tailored for character animation, which is characterized by ensuring the consistency of temporal sequences and character information during image-to-video generation. EMO13 further extends this framework to audio-driven portrait animation generation, eliminating the need for 3D models or landmarks as intermediate representations. By incorporating a face locator and speed layers to provide weak conditions, the method ensures controllable and stable facial animations while maintaining seamless frame transitions and identity consistency throughout generated videos. Recent extensions to this framework14,34 have been proposed, with V-Express34 employing progressive training and conditional dropout strategies to balances different control signals. This approach not only improves the overall quality of generated videos but also ensures better synchronization and control. To address the complexity of synchronous facial movements, Hallo14 proposes a hierarchical audio-driven visual synthesis module which can adaptively control facial expressions and postures according to audio. Our method builds upon the pre-trained model Hallo14, focusing on enhancing the consistency between the identity and background of the generated portrait animation, as well as the detail fidelity of the portrait animation.

Identity and background consistency in portrait animation generation

Preserving identity appearance and background consistency is a long-standing challenge in portrait animation generation task. Methods26,27 based on local modification and image stitching typically only modify the speech-related regions around the mouth and seamlessly fuse them back into the original frames. Subsequent 3D-aware frameworks28,30 further enhance identity-motion disentanglement through 3DMM-based identity anchoring. Researchers have begun exploring human image-to-video tasks by leveraging the architecture of diffusion models and their pre-trained robust generative capabilities. Animate Anyone12 introduce ReferenceNet, specifically designed as a symmetrical U-Net structure to capture spatial details of the reference portrait image. This architecture enables the model to comprehensively learn the relationship with the reference portrait image in a consistent feature space, which significantly contributes to the improvement of appearance details preservation. The existing end-to-end portrait animation generation methods13,14,34 based on diffusion adopt ReferenceNet to maintain the consistency of the identity of the target portrait. However, although this method helps to better preserve the identity information of the reference portrait, its mechanism for integrating identity information into the denoising network remains relatively simple, which may lead to inconsistencies between identity and background information in some generated frames. By contrast, our constructed Multi-Source Self Attention (MSSA) enables flexible spatial information exchange by improving the interaction between ReferenceNet and the denoising U-Net, preserving both identity appearance and background stability across frames.

Multi-source self attention

Preserving the consistency of the portrait’s identity and background with the reference portrait during the animation generation process is crucial. Recent works38 have revealed a high correlation between the self-attention layer in diffusion model and the appearance of generated images. In addition, MagicPose22 verified that the self-attention layer in denoising U-Net can serve as a deformation module to generate similar images with different geometric structures through experiments on self-attention for zero-shot appearance control. Therefore, the base model Hallo14 employs the ReferenceNet to enhance appearance control over the generated results. It is designed to share the same U-Net architecture as the original Stable Diffusion 1.5 and provides layer-wise attention guidance in parallel with the diffusion backbone.

In analogous architectures13,14, identity information extracted by the ReferenceNet is typically integrated into the diffusion backbone by concatenating the inputs of the self-attention layers from both the ReferenceNet and diffusion backbone, which are then collectively serve as the input to the self-attention layers in the diffusion backbone. Although this approach is effective in most cases, the identity and background information extracted from the reference portrait may not be sufficiently preserved during the denoising process, which can lead to inconsistencies between the generated frames and the reference portrait. To address this limitation, we introduce a Multi-Source Self Attention (MSSA) mechanism to improve the interaction between the diffusion backbone and the ReferenceNet.

From the perspective of attention-based feature retrieval, the concatenated key-value pairs can be interpreted as forming a joint memory bank that contains both the current denoising features and the reference appearance features. During attention computation, the diffusion query dynamically attends to this combined memory space and determines whether to retrieve contextual information from the current diffusion features or from the reference appearance features . In this manner, reference tokens act as visual anchors that provide stable identity and background cues, while diffusion tokens preserve the structural information required for progressive denoising. The competition between the two sources within the same attention distribution enables the model to adaptively balance structural coherence and appearance preservation. When identity or background drift occurs during the denoising process, the attention mechanism can automatically assign higher weights to the reference tokens, thereby correcting appearance deviations and improving visual consistency.

The MSSA is applied across all self-attention layers in the encoder, middle block, and decoder of the denoising U-Net architecture. This full-layer integration ensures that reference appearance information is continuously propagated throughout the entire denoising process, rather than being partially injected at selective depths, allowing identity and background constraints to be enforced at multiple feature scales.

It should be noted that MSSA differs fundamentally from conventional cross-attention or feature injection methods. In typical cross-attention mechanisms, conditional features (appearance features in this study) are treated as external guidance and establish a one-way mapping to the main representation. In contrast, MSSA enables joint interaction between multiple visual sources within a shared attention space by concatenating their key-value representations. This design allows both sources to participate in the same attention computation and promotes consistent feature learning across the diffusion backbone and the ReferenceNet.

In the existing portrait animation architectures13,14, identity features extracted by the ReferenceNet are integrated into the diffusion backbone through a feature injection strategy. Although this strategy allows reference information to be introduced into the denoising process, the concatenated features are first projected into new query, key, and value representations before attention computation. As a result, the reference appearance information becomes implicitly mixed with the diffusion features, which may weaken the explicit preservation of identity and background cues during iterative denoising. In contrast, the proposed MSSA mechanism allows the diffusion queries to dynamically retrieve information from either the current denoising context or the reference appearance features through attention competition within the same normalization space. Consequently, reference tokens can serve as visual anchors that stabilize identity and background appearance, while diffusion tokens maintain structural coherence during generation. Compared with feature injection strategies that mix features before attention computation, MSSA performs token-level adaptive feature retrieval, which reduces destructive feature interference and improves identity preservation as well as background consistency across generated frames.

Unit-wise spectral-blend temporal attention

Motivated by FreeLong23, our proposed Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) constrains global attention within a generation unit. For shorter sequence frames within a generation unit, our approach constructs a local attention window to capture the dynamic details of adjacent frames, thereby achieving both global consistency and detail fidelity across video frames in the unit. Network

Network architecture

Figure 1 illustrates the architecture of the proposed method. Diffusion backbone dennoises successive video frames at each time step for multi-frame noisy latent inputs and adopts a similar structure as Stable Diffusion 1.5, which contains four attention layers in each Transformer block of denoising U-Net. In the ReferenceNet, each Transformer consists of two attention layers identical to the first two in the diffusion backbone. The latent representation of the target character portrait is fed into the ReferenceNet. Leveraging our constructed Multi-Source Self Attention (MSSA) mechanism, the identity and background information of the reference portrait are integrated from the intermediate outputs of the self-attention layers in the ReferenceNet into the corresponding self-attention layers of the diffusion backbone. This process effectively provides appearance guidance regarding the portrait’s identity and background information to the diffusion backbone. A pre-trained face encoder is used to extract face identity features, which are fed to the cross-attention module of the diffusion network to interact with the latent. Considering the impact of context on sequential audio, a 12-layer wav2vec network39 is used as the audio feature encoder to extract a 5-second of audio latent corresponding to consecutive video frames. A hierarchical audio-visual cross-attention module14 is utilized in diffusion backbone to facilitate the learning of the relationship between audio and visual components such as lips, expression and pose. Finally, the constructed Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) integrated within the diffusion backbone not only achieves temporal alignment across the generative units but also captures both the overall motion trends and the high-frequency detail features of short frame sequences within the unit.

Training

In this study, we employ a two-stage training process to optimize the different components within the overall architecture. The first training stage focuses on single frame generation. Specifically, video clips containing 14 frames are extracted as input data, and two frames are randomly selected as the reference image and target image, respectively. During this stage, all parameters of the VAE encoder/decoder and the face encoder are frozen. Only the weights of the spatial cross-attention module of ReferenceNet and denoising U-Net are optimized. It should be noted that the audio attention and temporal attention layers are not inserted into the denoised U-Net in the first stage of training. The second training phase focuses on using input audio, reference portrait image, and target video data to train the video sequences. Keeping the parameters of the spatial cross-attention module of ReferenceNet and denoising U-Net optimized in the first stage fixed, the audio attention and our constructed Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) are inserted into the denoising U-Net. This stage primarily focuses on training the audio attention, where the audio attention layers are employed to establish cross-modal mapping relationships between audio and facial action units such as lip movements, expression changes, and head poses. Meanwhile, the constructed U-SBTA operates as a plug-and-play, training-free temporal module, it is directly initialized with the pre-trained weights of the temporal attention module from Hallo14, without further fine-tuning during this phase. This design aims to ensure the temporal consistency and coherence of the generated video sequences while further enhancing the detail quality of the portrait appearance. The training data for this stage adopts complete video frame sequences, and the reference portrait is randomly sampled from the frames of video clips.

Inference

In the inference stage, the overall framework takes a single portrait image and the driving audio as input to generate a video sequence of portrait animation synchronized with the audio content. Since the Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) does not require training, it can be directly integrated into the temporal module in denoising U-Net by adjusting the trained temporal attention module. In addition, to ensure the temporal coherence of long video generation, we adopt an incremental inference strategy under the autoregressive paradigm, in which the last frames in the current generation unit are used as the initial condition frames for the next generation unit. This generation mechanism in the autoregressive paradigm can enhance the visual consistency cross-generation unit generation.

The datasets used for training and evaluation include HDTF dataset40 and additional “Wild” dataset collected from Bilibili and Rednote on the Internet. Specifically, the HDTF dataset40 contains 8.42 hours of video data with 190 individual identities, and the “Wild” dataset contains 40.00 hours of video data with 594 individual identities. To improve the transparency of the dataset, we provide a concise statistical summary of the collected “Wild” dataset. As shown in Figure 2, the talking head videos included in these data were captured in upper-body or close-up shots, covering multiple age groups and genders, with backgrounds spanning different static indoor and outdoor scenes. To ensure the performance of the model, we perform a meticulous data cleaning process on the training data. This process mainly retains those single frontal talking videos with strong matching between audio and lip movements, and removes the videos with large facial motion amplitude, scene change and camera movement characteristics. After data cleaning, the HDTF dataset contains 190 clips with a total duration of 7.18 hours and the “Wild” dataset contains 4826 clips with a total duration of 26.58 hours, where each training video slice consists of 15 frames at a resolution of . Evaluation metrics

To demonstrate the superiority of the proposed method, we evaluate the model using multiple quantitative metrics, including Fréchet Inception Distance (FID)42, Fréchet Video Distance (FVD)43, SyncNet44, and E-FID13. Among them, FID and FVD are metrics for evaluating the quality of generated images and videos, respectively, which measure the distribution similarity between real data and generated data in the feature space. Lower values of both indicate that the generated images and videos are closer to the real distribution. Furthermore, FID is calculated using a standard Inception-V3 network pre-trained on ImageNet. Similarly, FVD is calculated using an I3D network pre-trained on Kinetics-400. In both cases, the networks are used without any further training. To further contextualize the FID values, we additionally report the FID computed between two partitions of the ground-truth dataset. Specifically, for each real portrait video, the sequence of frames is divided into two non-overlapping temporal segments, and the FID is computed between these two sets of real frames. This metric reflects the intrinsic variability within the real data distribution and therefore provides a realistic lower bound for the achievable FID values. Reporting this metric allows to better interpret the FID results by indicating how close the generative model is to the natural variability present in real videos, thus providing a more transparent and fair evaluation. SyncNet is used to measure the synchronization between audio and lip movement in the generated video, and a larger value of SyncNet represents a higher match between audio and lip movements. E-FID measures the expressiveness of facial movements and a smaller value of E-FID indicates that facial expressions in the generated video is more consistent with that in the real video. Additionally, in order to evaluate the ability of the proposed model to preserve the identity information of the input reference portrait, we introduce the Face-Cos metric22. This metric is calculated by first aligning and cropping the facial regions of the generated videos and real videos of the same individual in the test set, then exploiting AdaFace45 to extract features frame-by-frame, and finally taking the cosine similarity between the two as the Face-Cos score. A higher score indicates stronger ability of the model to preserve the identity information of the characters in the generated videos.

Baseline approaches

We conduct both quantitative and qualitative comparisons between our method and existing publicly available implementations of non-diffusion and diffusion-based methods. Specifically, GAN-based model, such as SadTalker30, is compared with diffusion-based counterparts like DreamTalk32, AniPortrait46, and Hallo14. The talking style parameters in DreamTalk32 are selected as prescribed by the original authors. Hallo14 serves as the base model, and the architecture of AniPortrait46 is similar to the base model. The qualitative comparison involves providing the same reference portrait and audio input, visually and intuitively comparing the realism and expressiveness of the portrait animations generated by each method with those of the original real videos. We conducted tests on HDTF and our collected dataset, and randomly divide 90% of these two datasets for training and the remaining 10% for testing, corresponding to 182304 training video slices (705 identities) for training and 78 testing video clips (78 identities) for testing. It is worth noting that, since the testing dataset contained multiple video clips for the same identity, only one clip per identity was retained. Moreover, to prevent data leakage and ensure fair cross-model comparisons, we take pre-emptive measures to ensure that there are no duplicate character IDs between the testing and training datasets.

Ablation study

In order to verify the effectiveness of the constructed Multi-Source Self Attention (MSSA) mechanism and Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) module on the portrait animation generation, we conduct ablation experiments on the HDTF dataset. Specifically, we integrate our constructed MSSA and U-SBTA modules into the base model, and conduct comparative experiments against the original baseline, the baseline with only MSSA integrated, and the baseline with only U-SBTA integrated. The experimental results are shown in Table 4. It can be seen from the table that Hallo14, as a base model, has achieved better results on the five evaluation metrics. When the MSSA mechanism is integrated into the baseline, it can be observed that the E-FID index is significantly decreased, and the Face-Cos index is significantly improved, indicating that the generated portrait animation is optimized in terms of facial expression and preserving the identity information of the reference portrait. This primarily benefits from the mechanism’s capability to more precisely inject the reference portrait’s identity and background information into the denoising U-Net. On the other hand, we separately integrate the U-SBTA module into the temporal module in the baseline to extend the original temporal attention. Compared with the baseline, the FID, FVD, and E-FID metrics have all significantly decreased, indicating that the generated portrait animations have improved in the similarity of dynamic sequences, static frames and facial expressions to real videos. This further demonstrates that the U-SBTA module can better preserve the facial details of the generated portrait animations. Finally, integrating these two into the baseline has achieved the optimal comprehensive performance (Table 4).

Although U-SBTA operates in a training-free manner, it introduces additional computational operations during inference due to the decoupled local/global attention streams and the spectral blending process involving FFT/IFFT. To evaluate the practical overhead introduced by this module, we compare the inference efficiency of the model with and without U-SBTA under the same experimental settings. As shown in Table 5, after removing the U-SBTA, the proposed model without U-SBTA achieves 0.3125 FPS with 9.77 GB GPU memory consumption. After integrating U-SBTA, the inference speed decreases to 0.2006 FPS, while the GPU memory usage increases slightly to 10.67 GB. This corresponds to an additional memory overhead of approximately 0.9 GB and a moderate reduction in inference speed. The increased computational cost mainly comes from the additional local/global temporal attention branches and the FFT/IFFT operations used for frequency-domain blending. Nevertheless, since U-SBTA is entirely training-free and can be directly integrated into a pretrained model without additional optimization, the efficiency-quality trade-off remains acceptable in practice.

Limitation and future work

Based on the base model, this study redesign the transmission mechanism of reference portrait information between ReferenceNet and denoising U-Net, as well as the Unit-wise Spectral-Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) integrated within the temporal module. These two improved methods have achieved promising progress in preserving reference portrait identity and background consistency, as well as capturing the details of facial movements. However, there are still some limitations in the overall architecture of the model, which are worth further consideration and exploration. (1) In order to generate long coherent videos, the incremental inference strategy adopted in such frameworks is to use the last few frames of the current generated video clip as the initial condition frames for the next video clip. When the distortion or noise artifacts present in the previous frame are propagated to the subsequent frames, they may cause the accumulation of errors, thus affecting the overall video quality. Therefore, when generating temporally coherent long videos, developing novel strategies to quantitatively control error accumulation is critical for enhancing overall video quality. (2) During the training phase, we initialize the corresponding modules in our architecture directly with the pre-trained weights from the base model Hallo14. Although this can achieve the goal of accelerating training, it also has high requirements for computing power and training data volume. It is necessary to further explore methods such as parameter-efficient fine-tuning or knowledge distillation to achieve efficient training. On the other hand, the inference efficiency of the proposed model is suboptimal. Future work could focus on model pruning or parallelization technology to enable real-time performance. (3) As the face encoder and ReferenceNet are respectively designed to encode and provide appearance guidance from the reference image but lack an explicit inpainting or correction mechanism, significant occlusions may be treated as part of the facial identity, leading to persistent artifacts in the generated animation. We regard this as a valuable direction for future work. Potential improvements include augmented training with challenging examples, dedicated pre-processing for face restoration, or architectural enhancements to support inference from partially occluded or poorly lit references.

Conclusion

In the generation of audio-driven portrait animations, we build upon the pre-trained Hallo model and introduce two key components: the Multi-Source Self Attention (MSSA) mechanism and a plug-and-play, training-free Unit-wise Spectral Blend Temporal Attention (U-SBTA) module. Specifically, the MSSA mechanism enhances the consistency preservation of portrait identity and background by optimizing the interaction between reference portrait identity information and denoising U-Net, while the U-SBTA module improves temporal smoothness and detail fidelity within the end-to-end latent diffusion framework. In the experimental evaluation phase, we perform comparative analyses from both quantitative and qualitative perspectives against the existing advanced portrait animation generation methods, including GAN-based and diffusion-based methods. The evaluation is conducted on the public dataset and the “Wild” dataset. The experimental results demonstrate that our method outperforms both the base model and other methods in preserving identity and background consistency for portrait animation, while simultaneously achieving superior facial detail capture. Furthermore, experiments validate our method’s capability to adapt to diverse portrait styles and audio inputs, generating audio-synchronized facial animations with high fidelity.

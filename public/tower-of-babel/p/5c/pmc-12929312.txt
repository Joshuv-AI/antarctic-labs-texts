The efficacy and safety of 1470 nm diode laser in the treatment of benign prostatic hyperplasia: a prospective, multicenter study
Authors: Hao Xu, Wenbo Kuang, Dongxu Lin, Kang Li, Yang Luan, Kai Cui, Ting Yang, Zhong Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurpose: Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is common in elderly men, causing lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTSs). The 1470 nm diode laser, with high vaporization efficiency and good hemostasis, is increasingly used for BPH. This study sought to comprehensively evaluate the efficacy and safety of 1470 nm diode laser enucleation of the prostate (DiLEP) forBPH in a prospective, multicenter study. Methods: Conducted at two hospitals, BPH patients were randomized into two groups. Group A used a new 1470 nm diode laser device (Hefei Han’s Kereida), and Group B utilized a conventional and widely validated 1470 nm laser system (Wuhan Qizhi). Prostate volume (PV), maximum flow rate (Qmax), post-void residual (PVR), International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS), and Quality of Life (QoL) score were measured pre- and post-operatively. Perioperative indices and complications were also recorded. Results: 143 patients (73 in Group A, 70 in Group B) completed follow-up. Baseline characteristics of two groups were comparable. Vital parameters remained within normal ranges following the surgical procedure. Both groups showed significant improvements in IPSS, QoL, Qmax, and PVR post-operatively, with no significant differences between groups. Complications occurred in 17 patients in Group A and 10 in Group B, all of which resolved spontaneously or were effectively managed with appropriate interventions. Conclusion: 1470 nm DiLEP is an effective and safe BPH treatment, relieving symptoms with few complications. The new device from Hefei Han’s Kereida performed reliably. This laser offers a valuable alternative to BPH surgeries.Keywords: Benign prostatic hyperplasia, 1470 nm diode laser, Laser enucleation, Efficacy, Safety

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is a prevalent condition among elderly men, often leading to male lower urinary tract symptom (LUTS). Epidemiological survey data indicates that approximately 50% of men aged over 50 years are affected by BPH [1]. Transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) remains the preferred surgical option for patients with BPH, as it can remarkably relieve LUTS performance and reduce the incidence of BPH-related complications, such as urinary retention and upper urinary tract deterioration. For example, when performed early after kidney transplantation, both TURP and transurethral incision of the prostate (TUIP) are comparable in their ability to reduce serum creatinine levels, increase urinary flow rate, and decrease residual urine [2, 3]. Nevertheless, TURP has several drawbacks, including a prolonged operation time, significant intraoperative bleeding, and the risk of transurethral resection (TUR) syndrome during the perioperative period, as well as various postoperative complications such as transient stress incontinence and bladder neck stricture [1, 4, 5]. Thus, there is a pressing need to explore novel and effective treatment methods for BPH that are also safe.

In recent years, with the rapid advancement of laser technology, laser-based treatment for BPH has been extensively utilized [6, 7]. The 1470 nm diode laser, a novel semiconductor non-contact continuous-wave laser, lies within the near-infrared region of the spectrum and has a penetration depth of 1.30 mm [8]. It is selectively absorbed by water and exhibits excellent tissue ablation and hemostatic properties because of its instantaneous high temperature effect [9, 10]. Besides, when the fiber tip is retracted from the tissue, any laser energy is absorbed by the surrounding perfusion solution. Several clinical studies have demonstrated that the 1470 nm diode laser is a viable option for BPH treatment, attributed to its high vaporization efficiency, few complications, reliable efficacy, and short learning curve [11, 12].

In this study, the primary objective was to compare the clinical performance of two 1470 nm diode laser systems, a newly developed 1470 nm laser device manufactured by Hefei Han’s Kereida and a conventional system produced by Wuhan Qizhi. The secondary objective was to evaluate the overall efficacy and safety profile of 1470 nm diode laser enucleation of the prostate (DiLEP) in patients with BPH from two research centers.

Materials and methods

Patient selection

This study is a prospective, multicenter study designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of 1470 nm DiLEP in a large cohort of patients with BPH. This study was conducted at the Tongji Hospital of Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology, and Third Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. It obtained approval from the Medical Ethics Committees of both hospitals (No. 2019159).

Eligible participants were those who satisfied all of the following inclusion criteria for this study: (1) male subjects aged ≥ 50 years who met the diagnostic criteria for BPH; (2) had at least one surgical indication, including urinary retention (absence of spontaneous urination or recurrent retention after catheter removal), recurrent gross hematuria, secondary renal dysfunction, bladder stones, recurrent urinary tract infections, or personal preference for surgery; (3) prostate volume (PV) < 200 mL; (4) the subjects signed an informed consent form and voluntarily participated in this study. Patients were excluded in the study if they presented with any of the following conditions: (1) preoperative International Prostate Symptom Score (IPSS) ≤ 7; (2) acute urinary tract infection, or other serious diseases of the urinary system; (3) prostate cancer or other malignant tumors; (4) significant diseases affecting key organs, including the heart, liver, lungs, or kidneys, rendering the patient unable to tolerate surgery; (5) history of transurethral surgery within the past six months; (6) hematologic disorders, use of medications affecting coagulation within the previous two weeks, or abnormal coagulation function; (7) detrusor muscle weakness or neurogenic bladder; (8) history of psychiatric disorders or cognitive impairment; and (9) participation in other clinical trials within the past 90 days.

Study design

The enrolled subjects were randomly assigned to two groups during 2.5 years. Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated block randomization sequence, with allocation concealment achieved through sequentially numbered, sealed opaque envelopes. In Group A, a new 1470 nm semiconductor laser therapeutic device manufactured by Hefei Han’s Kereida Laser Equipment Co., Ltd was used for the surgical treatment of BPH. In Group B, a 1470 nm semiconductor laser therapeutic device, produced by Wuhan Qizhi Laser Technology Co., Ltd, which has been widely used in China,was utilized [13]. Based on a priori non-inferiority power analysis (α = 0.05, power = 80%, expected effective rate = 95%, non-inferiority margin = -0.10), approximately 75 participants per group were required, and accounting for a 20% anticipated dropout rate, a total of 188 participants were enrolled. Eventually, 143 patients (73 allocated to Group A and 70 to Group B) completed the follow-up and were included in the subsequent statistical study. Among the 45 patients not included in the statistics, 37 subjects requested to withdraw during the study, 4 patients were diagnosed with prostate cancer based on postoperative pathology, and 4 patients were excluded due to protocol violations.

Evaluation parameters

Demographic data, including age and weight, were recorded. The size of the prostate was measured via transrectal ultrasonography (TRUS). The PV was calculated using the formula: height×length×width × π/6. The maximum flow rate (Qmax), post-void residual (PVR), IPSS, and Quality of Life (QoL) score were evaluated preoperatively, and at 30 ± 3 days and 90 ± 7 days postoperatively.

Vital and baseline physiological parameters of the subjects, including blood pressure, heart rate , and body temperature, along with blood routine and urine routine tests, were measured preoperatively, 4 ± 1 days after surgery, and 30 ± 3 days after surgery. Perioperative indices and complications, such as operation time, variations in serum sodium and hemoglobin, hospital stay, blood transfusion requirements, TUR syndrome, capsular perforation, bladder injury, re-catheterization, transitory stress incontinence, and urethral stricture, were also documented.

Surgical procedures

During each surgical procedure, the 1470 nm diode laser generator, with a maximum power of 150 W, was set to 120 W for tissue vaporization and 30 W for hemostatic coagulation, using a 550–600 μm direct optical fiber. Patients were positioned in the lithotomy position and received either continuous epidural or general anesthesia. Guided by a STORZ endosurgical system, a 26 F resectoscope was introduced into the bladder, with physiological saline serving as the medium. Before the operation, the positions of bilateral ureteral orifice, bladder neck, middle lobe of the prostate, as well as seminal vesicles and external urethral sphincter were identified.

The surgical procedure comprised six main steps: (1) Lifting the anterior lobe. Incisions were made starting from the 5- and 7-o’clock positions of the bladder neck, extending toward the proximal end of the verumontanum. These incisions were made deep enough to reach the surgical capsule level, creating two grooves with a laser power of 100 W to lift the anterior lobe. (2) Splitting the anterior lobe. A longitudinal groove was created at the 12-o’clock position by laser incision from the bladder neck to the inner side of the urethral sphincter. (3) Stripping the left lobe. The urethral mucosa was circumferentially incised from the left side of the verumontanum, following an arc along the inner side of the external urethral sphincter up to the previously created groove at 12 o’clock. The tissue between the prostate gland and its surgical capsule was cut from the left side of the verumontanum, and the left lobe of the prostate was gradually dissected from the apex to the bladder neck in a left-upward direction. The dissected gland was then pushed into the bladder. (4) Prying the right lobe. Employing the same method as in step (3), the right lobe of the prostate was peeled off starting from the right side of the glandular urethral papilla and pushed into the bladder. (5) Flattening the prostate wound surface. Residual glandular tissue on the surgical capsule was sequentially vaporized and ablated using a 1470 nm laser with a power of 100–120 W, while simultaneously controlling any bleeding at the wound site. (6) Trimming the tip of the prostate gland. The endoscope was withdrawn to the distal end of the prostate apex, and the residual glandular tissue at the apex was ablated. Subsequently, dissected prostate tissue within the bladder was fragmented and aspirated using a tissue crusher (Fig. 1).

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed statistically with SPSS 26.0. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and compared between the two groups using the t-test. Categorical data, including postoperative adverse events, were presented as counts and percentages (n, %) and analyzed using the Chi-square test. For all analyses, a P value < 0.05 was considered to indicate a statistically significant difference.

Perioperative parameters

The operation time (49.65 ± 23.43 vs. 52.16 ± 29.0 min), and hospital stay after operation (4.3 ± 0.8 vs. 4.1 ± 0.7 days) did not show significant differences between the Goup A and Group B, suggesting that the new device can be performed with similar efficiency and recovery outcomes to the conventional device. Physiological parameters (including blood pressure, heart rate, and body temperature) and routine laboratory examinations (such as blood routine and urine tests) were recorded preoperatively and at 4 ± 1 days postoperatively. No significant differences were found between Group A and Group B, and postoperative values showed no clinically significant changes relative to baseline (Table 2).

Follow-up information

Both groups were followed up at 30 ± 3 days and 90 ± 7 days after surgery. Relative to baseline, both groups exhibited significant improvement in LUTSs and voiding function, characterized by reductions in IPSS, QoL, and PVR, along with an elevation in Qmax (Tables 3 and 4). Specifically, Qmax increased to approximately 17 mL/s at 30 days and to approximately 18 mL/s at 90 days; IPSS decreased to below 9 at 30 days and to below 6 at 90 days; QoL decreased to below 3 at both time points; and PVR decreased to under 24 mL in Group A and under 19 mL in Group B at both follow-up periods. No statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups regarding the changes in IPSS, QoL, Qmax, or PVR.

Complications

In Group A, 17 patients experienced 12 types of complications, whereas in Group B, 10 patients experienced 7 types (Table 5). The most common complications were anemia and hematuria; more severe complications included heart failure and pulmonary infection, which were effectively controlled following blood-volume correction and antibiotic therapy. In addition, transient stress incontinence (TSI) was observed in one patient in Group A. The symptom occurred after catheter removal and resolved completely following pelvic floor muscle (Kegel) exercise training. No cases of permanent urinary incontinence were reported in either group. Overall, these complications did not pose a significant threat to patient safety, and most patients either recovered during postoperative follow-up or were corrected after appropriate interventions.

Discussion

In recent years, several new surgical procedures using different types of lasers, such as HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of the prostate), ThuLEP (thulium laser enucleation or vaporization of the prostate), Hybrid Laser (a combination of thulium and blue diode laser for prostate vaporization), Green Light laser (Green Light laser vaporization of the prostate), and BipoLEP (bipolar enucleation of the prostate), have been applied in the treatment of BPH [14–17]. Compared with TURP, these procedures offer numerous advantages in terms of transfusion rates, revision rates, hospital stay duration, clot retention, TUS syndrome, and capsular perforation [17].

The 1470 nm laser is a novel diode laser with a wavelength in the near-infrared region of the spectrum. It can be readily absorbed by hemoglobin and water, allowing it to penetrate tissues to a depth of 2–3 mm. When applied during surgical procedures, it ensures effective hemostasis and enhanced tissue vaporization [11, 18]. Seitz et al. reported that a 100 W 1470 nm diode laser effectively vaporized beagle prostate tissue, generating a 2.30 ± 0.26 mm coagulated layer that was sufficient to occlude most blood vessels in the prostate tissue [19]. Christophe et al. reported that 1470 nm laser irradiation could seal veins ranging from 1 to 6 mm in diameter in pigs [20].

Relative to the 980 nm laser, the 1470 nm laser demonstrates superior vaporization efficiency, generates a thinner coagulation layer, and operates with lower acoustic output in treating prostate tissue [21]. These characteristics enable efficient resection with minimal thermal injury to underlying tissues, resulting in more precise incisions, faster necrosis and tissue detachment, and reduced postoperative urinary irritation. When using a 120 W 1470 nm laser, it is easier to cut the adhesion between the prostate gland and the capsule during enucleation and more efficient to vaporize the remaining glandular tissue. Consequently, the incidence of intraoperative injury to the urethral sphincter and other tissues is lower, and postoperative urinary tract irritation symptoms are alleviated. Accordingly, in this study, patients exhibited a significant reduction in IPSS scores and a marked increase in Qmax at 30 and 90 days postoperatively. Only one patient experienced transient urinary incontinence, and another developed temporary urinary retention. The equipment used in our study has a maximum power of 150 W. When a 120 W 1470 nm diode laser is used for enucleation, it can easily cut and separate the adhesive connection between the prostate gland and the capsule during the procedure, and vaporize the residual gland more effectively.

The 1470 nm laser is an effective tool for prostate enucleation. Jun Zhang et al. reported that 1470 nm DiLEP and plasmakinetic prostate resection (PKEP) had comparable efficacy and safety in relieving obstruction and LUTSs in BPH patients, but DiLEP offered advantages including less bleeding, shorter operation and catheterization times, reduced bladder irrigation, and shorter hospital stay [18]. Jun Zhang et al. also reported that 1470 nm DiLEP was safe and efficient for individuals with BPH who were taking oral anticoagulant or antiplatelet medications on a long-term basis [11]. Our previous study further demonstrated that, compared with bipolar TURP, the 1470 nm DiLEP showed higher tissue retrieval efficiency, less intraoperative bleeding, shorter operative time, reduced postoperative irrigation and catheterization durations, and a slightly lower complication rate [22].

In the present study, all patients were treated with 1470 nm DiLEP using a six-step procedure. This approach ensures that each part of the prostate is incised according to a specific anatomical structure, minimizing the risk of anatomical confusion during the surgery. The 1470 nm laser system produced by Qizhi (Wuhan Qizhi Co.) has undergone extensive market validation [13, 23]. In the present study, we compared a newly introduced 1470 nm device manufactured by Hefei Han’s Kereida with the Qizhi system. There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in preoperative and perioperative parameters, postoperative follow-up outcomes, or complication rates. Both devices effectively improved BPH-related symptoms and objective measures, including increased Qmax and decreased IPSS, QoL, and PVR, confirming that the 1470 nm laser has excellent hemostatic and vaporization properties and is an efficient and safe option for prostate enucleation in patients with BPH.

This study has several limitations. The sample size was relatively modest. Although an a priori power analysis supported its adequacy for comparing the efficacy of the two 1470 nm laser systems, a larger cohort would improve the robustness and generalizability of the results. In addition, the follow-up period was relatively short, potentially limiting the evaluation of long-term functional outcomes and late complications. Further studies with expanded sample sizes and extended follow-up are warranted.

Conclusion

This prospective multicenter study comprehensively evaluated the 1470 nm diode laser in the treatment of BPH. The results clearly show that 1470 nm diode laser not only ensures the effectiveness of the treatment in relieving BPH-related symptoms but also guarantees patient safety during and after the operation. Specifically, the new 1470 nm semiconductor laser therapeutic device produced by Hefei Han’s Kereida Laser Equipment Co., Ltd has proven to be a reliable option. It effectively facilitates prostate enucleation procedures. Therefore, it can be regarded as an efficient and safe tool for the surgical treatment of BPH, providing a valuable alternative for clinical practice.

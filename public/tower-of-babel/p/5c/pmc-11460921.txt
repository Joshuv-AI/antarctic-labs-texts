A thorough examination of ChatGPT-3.5 potential applications in medical writing: A preliminary study
Authors: Ismail Dergaa, Helmi Ben Saad, Jordan M. Glenn, Mohamed Ben Aissa, Morteza Taheri, Sarya Swed, Noomen Guelmami, Karim Chamari
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Effective communication of scientific knowledge plays a crucial role in the advancement of medical research and health care. Technological advancements have introduced large language models such as Chat Generative Pre-Trained Transformer (ChatGPT), powered by artificial intelligence (AI), which has already shown promise in revolutionizing medical writing. This study aimed to conduct a detailed evaluation of ChatGPT-3.5’s role in enhancing various aspects of medical writing. From May 10 to 12, 2023, the authors engaged in a series of interactions with ChatGPT-3.5 to evaluate its effectiveness in various tasks, particularly its application to medical writing, including vocabulary enhancement, text rewriting for plagiarism prevention, hypothesis generation, keyword generation, title generation, article summarization, simplification of medical jargon, transforming text from informal to scientific and data interpretation. The exploration of ChatGPT’s functionalities in medical writing revealed its potential in enhancing various aspects of the writing process, demonstrating its efficiency in improving vocabulary usage, suggesting alternative phrasing, and providing grammar enhancements. While the results indicate the effectiveness of ChatGPT (version 3.5), the presence of certain imperfections highlights the current indispensability of human intervention to refine and validate outputs, ensuring accuracy and relevance in medical settings. The integration of AI into medical writing shows significant potential for improving clarity, efficiency, and reliability. This evaluation highlights both the benefits and limitations of using ChatGPT-3.5, emphasizing its ability to enhance vocabulary, prevent plagiarism, generate hypotheses, suggest keywords, summarize articles, simplify medical jargon, and transform informal text into an academic format. However, AI tools should not replace human expertise. It is crucial for medical professionals to ensure thorough human review and validation to maintain the accuracy and relevance of the content in case they eventually use AI as a supplementary resource in medical writing. Accepting this mutually symbiotic partnership holds the promise of improving medical research and patient outcomes, and it sets the stage for the fusion of AI and human knowledge to produce a novel approach to medical assessment. Thus, while AI can streamline certain tasks, experienced medical writers and researchers must perform final reviews to uphold high standards in medical communications.

1. Introduction

Effective communication is fundamental to medical research, acting as the primary mechanism for disseminating knowledge, facilitating interdisciplinary collaboration, and driving innovation in healthcare.[1] In this dynamic and rapidly advancing field, the articulation of complex medical concepts and research outcomes with precision and clarity is crucial.[2] Recent technological advancements have introduced transformative tools, including large language models such as the Chat Generative Pre-trained Transformer (ChatGPT), which hold significant promise for revolutionizing the domain of medical writing.[2–4] Many artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots have emerged in the past 3 years, and among them, ChatGPT stands out with its exceptional capabilities in natural language processing and generation.[5] This technology offers immense potential to revolutionize the way medical professionals communicate their work.[5–7] Using ChatGPT in medical writing introduces a new way to present complex information and raises important questions about the role of AI in a specialized field like medicine.[8–10] It promotes a reassessment of conventional writing methods and encourages discussion on how AI may enhance, rather than replace, the knowledge and skills of medical practitioners. Investigating the interaction between human expertise and AI capabilities is crucial for assessing the feasibility and efficacy of ChatGPT in improving medical communication.

Despite the increasing recognition of ChatGPT’s benefits in medical writing,[5–7,11] there are still aspects that remain relatively unexplored, such as the full practical implications[5] and potential limitations of this large language model.[5,8,12,13] While its abilities in refining vocabulary usage, aiding in data interpretation, and simplifying complex medical concepts are promising, the question of whether ChatGPT can be considered a trustworthy and effective tool remains unanswered.[14] Further investigation is needed to assess the reliability and accuracy of ChatGPT’s outputs, as well as its ability to consistently deliver high-quality results.[5,15–17] Moreover, ChatGPT’s responses can vary significantly depending on the input and interaction, highlighting the need for ongoing research to boost its performance and reliability. The diversity in perspectives and methodologies among researchers further emphasize the necessity for more studies to better understand ChatGPT’s practical applications in medical writing.

Given these challenges, the need for reliable tools to convey complex medical concepts accurately is evident. The limited exploration of AI in medical documentation reveals a substantial gap in the field. To address these unmet needs, our study aimed to evaluate ChatGPT’s effectiveness in medical writing, with a focus on its potential to enhance clarity, efficiency, and reliability in presenting medical information.

2. Materials and methods

The authors adopted a structured methodology that involved a deep examination of the capabilities of ChatGPT-3.5 and its application in medical writing tasks. Our research team members who evaluated ChatGPT’s skills had to meet specific criteria, including extensive experience with ChatGPT,[5–7,11] a publication record of over 30 articles in high quartile journals (Scopus Quartile), and a great level of English proficiency. This ensured a rigorous assessment and accurate evaluation of ChatGPT’s performance.

To identify relevant articles for our literature review, we used a combination of Boolean operators and conducted searches in PubMed and Scopus. The Boolean operators and keywords used included “ChatGPT” AND “medical writing,” “AI” AND “scientific communication,” and “generative AI” AND “healthcare.” Articles were selected based on their relevance to our study’s focus on AI chatbots in medical writing.

To conduct this study, we engaged in conversations with ChatGPT between May 10 and 12, 2023. We used the publicly free-access GPT-3.5 Language Model (available at https://chat.openai.com/) to assist us in a focused medical research project. We chose one of our published papers, “Assessment tools for pulmonary involvement in patients with ankylosing spondylitis (SpA): is diaphragmatic ultrasonography correlated to spirometry?” published in the Journal of Multidisciplinary Healthcare[18] as a practical model. This project provided real-world medical writing scenarios and data, which we used to evaluate ChatGPT’s functionalities.

Through a meticulous literature review, we identified the most relevant and impactful themes/tasks that ChatGPT can perform in medical writing. We chose these tasks to help improve various medical writing skills, making it easier for physicians and healthcare staff to be more productive and reduce the time spent on demanding writing tasks. Accordingly, the methodology involved 9 distinct themes/tasks, each exploring a specific aspect of ChatGPT-3.5’s potential in medical writing.

2.1. Vocabulary enhancement

We utilized ChatGPT to refine and enrich medical vocabulary, with the aim of improving the precision and clarity of scientific language.

2.2. Text rewriting for plagiarism prevention

We assessed the usefulness of ChatGPT-3.5 in generating alternative formulations of text to aid in plagiarism prevention while maintaining the integrity of scientific writing.

2.3. Hypothesis generation and research design

We employed ChatGPT-3.5 to propose research hypotheses and assist in the formulation of structured frameworks for study planning, including identifying potential limitations and refining the research questions.

2.4. Keyword generation

We explored ChatGPT-3.5’s functionality in extracting relevant keywords from abstracts to facilitate efficient literature review and information retrieval.

2.5. Article summarization

We evaluated the capability of ChatGPT-3.5 to generate concise summaries of research articles.

2.6. Title generation

The task involves utilizing ChatGPT-3.5 to generate suitable and impactful titles for academic articles, research papers, or other scientific documents.

2.7. Simplification of complex medical jargon

We examined ChatGPT-3.5’s ability to simplify complex medical terms and concepts to improve patient education, interdisciplinary collaboration, and communication with various stakeholders.

2.8. Transforming text from informal to scientific

The task involves using ChatGPT-3.5 to convert informal text into a formal, scientific format suitable for academic publications. This capability is particularly useful for researchers who need to elevate their writing to meet the standards of scholarly communication. The process includes enhancing the precision of language, adopting a more formal tone, and ensuring the text adheres to the conventions of scientific writing.

2.9. Data interpretation

We assessed the efficacy of ChatGPT-3.5 in extracting key findings, elucidating statistical interpretation, and providing concise explanations to enhance the comprehension of research results.

The evaluation process includes 4 criteria for evaluation and 5 evaluation steps. First, the evaluation of ChatGPT-3.5’s outputs was based on the following 4 key criteria: Accuracy: The correctness of the information and the factual soundness of the generated content; Relevance: The pertinence of the generated content to the given medical writing task; Clarity: The clarity of the language and the ease with which the generated content can be understood, and Language fluency: The grammatical correctness and fluency of the generated content. Second, the evaluation steps include the following 5 phases: Initial interaction: Each task began with an initial interaction where specific prompts related to the task were provided to ChatGPT-3.5; Output generation: ChatGPT-3.5 generated responses based on the given prompts, Critical analysis: The generated responses were critically analyzed by the research team for accuracy, relevance, clarity, and language fluency, Secondary interactions: If necessary, secondary interactions were conducted with ChatGPT-3.5 to refine the outputs or address any ambiguities, and Documentation: The entire process, including all interactions and analyses, was documented for each task (see below).

3. Results

We present the results in an original and structured format comprising 9 boxes. Each box represents a specific task related to medical writing. Within each box, we provide the original prompt (the text input given to ChatGPT-3.5), the response generated by ChatGPT-3.5, and any subsequent interactions or secondary responses, if applicable. This unique presentation allows for a clear and organized examination of the capabilities and limitations of the ChatGPT-3.5 in each task.

Throughout the evaluation process, we critically analyzed the responses generated by ChatGPT, considering factors such as accuracy, relevance, and language fluency. This analysis provides valuable insights into the practical implications, strengths, and limitations of utilizing ChatGPT-3.5 for medical writing tasks.

3.1. Vocabulary enhancement

ChatGPT-3.5 provided numerous synonyms and alternative phrasings that were generally accurate and relevant. For example, it suggested “pulmonary function” instead of “lung function,” which is a more precise term in medical contexts. However, some suggestions lacked the necessary specificity, indicating that while ChatGPT can assist in vocabulary enhancement, human oversight is essential to ensure scientific accuracy. Moreover, the model could improve in highlighting the severity of potential respiratory complications, which would provide a more comprehensive view of the disease’s impacts. To summarise, while the ChatGPT model demonstrates aptitude in rephrasing tasks, there is room for improvement in ensuring precise language usage and nuanced elaboration for more complex scientific concepts.

4. Discussion

This study looked at how ChatGPT-3.5 can help improve medical writing and communication of medical information. We tested its ability to enhance vocabulary, interpret data, summarize articles, and simplify complex medical jargons.

It is important to note that ChatGPT provides a variety of functionalities beyond medical writing. It can be utilized for tasks such as composing emails, generating inquiries,[5,9] and even performing sample size calculations.[11] However, in our study, we specifically concentrated on the most practical and widely employed features of ChatGPT-3.5 in medical writing. We identified certain limitations and areas requiring improvement to ensure a more balanced assessment of the competencies of ChatGPT-3.5 in medical writing. While we found that ChatGPT-3.5 demonstrated remarkable capabilities in improving medical writing productivity and enhancing the clarity and precision of scientific language, it is important to note that the model is not without its shortcomings. In some cases, it provided suggestions for alternative phrasing, improved grammar, and relevant synonyms. However, there were instances where the provided suggestions were inaccurate or contextually inappropriate. This highlights the need for careful review and critical evaluation of the model’s suggestions before incorporating them into any manuscript. Furthermore, while ChatGPT-3.5 can be a valuable tool for researchers to present their work with greater impact and engage readers more effectively, it should not replace the expertise and judgment of human medical experts.

In addition, ChatGPT-3.5’s writing style can be repetitive, with similar syntax and sentence structures. This uniformity can be noticed by reviewers and editors who are familiar with AI-generated text, which poses ethical risks if the text is used without changes. In this context, universities and editorial boards now use AI detection tools to screen documents. Therefore, students and researchers must revise AI-generated content carefully to avoid ethical issues.[5]

It is important to view AI chatbots like ChatGPT-3.5 as tools to enhance productivity rather than as replacements for the writing styles of students, researchers, and medical experts. These tools should support the writing process, helping to improve clarity and efficiency, but the final content should reflect the unique voice and expertise of the human author(s).

Our investigation of ChatGPT-3.5’s data interpretation features showed its ability to highlight key findings, explain statistical correlations, and provide clear summaries. This could help researchers and clinicians understand their results more easily and effectively apply their findings. ChatGPT-3.5 could also help make complex data more understandable, improving communication among medical professionals and potentially support evidence-based decisions. This would be particularly useful for medical experts who may not have advanced statistical skills. Physicians often focus on patient care and may not be well-versed in research or statistics. ChatGPT could help them understand the latest scientific findings without needing deep statistical knowledge. Additionally, medical students and researchers who are less experienced with statistics can use AI to check if they are interpreting their results correctly. This support can be invaluable in ensuring accurate understanding and application of research data.

However, ChatGPT-3.5’s reliance on continuous feedback and interactions with domain specialists to ensure accuracy, relevance, and ethical considerations highlights its limitations. Human expertise is crucial for validating and interpreting AI outputs. ChatGPT-3.5 should be viewed as a support tool rather than a replacement for medical professionals, emphasizing the importance of collaboration between AI systems and domain experts.[20]

One notable limitation is ChatGPT-3.5’s inconsistency, as it rarely provides the same response to identical inputs. This variability can challenge maintaining consistency and reliability. Additionally, the contextual limitations of ChatGPT-3.5 should be considered. While proficient in general medical writing, it may struggle with more complex or specialized topics requiring specific domain knowledge. Users must exercise caution and critical thinking, ensuring that AI-generated content aligns with established medical guidelines and standards. Another issue is related to the time period over which it bases its response. Free access versions of chatbots (as ChatGPT-3.5) are using data which is relatively outdated (usually missing the last year(s) of information). This has to be taken into account if ever the investigated topic is time sensitive. The practical implications of integrating ChatGPT-3.5 into medical writing are significant. By addressing its limitations, researchers can enhance productivity, streamline the writing process, and devote more time to critical thinking and analysis. ChatGPT-3.5’s ability to simplify complex medical jargon can improve patient education, facilitate interdisciplinary collaboration, and enhance communication among healthcare professionals.

Concerns about AI replacing medical doctors are prevalent, but our findings suggest otherwise. ChatGPT-3.5 and similar models offer opportunities for transformation and growth, not obsolescence. Medical professionals who embrace AI integration are better positioned to revolutionize the field. The synergy between human doctors and AI can surpass traditional methods, improving efficiency and patient care. True pioneers in medicine will be those who effectively integrate AI tools into their practice, navigating the evolving healthcare landscape to provide accurate diagnoses and personalized treatments.

The rising influence of AI chatbots like ChatGPT-3.5 in medicine necessitates careful implementation. Their use may become indispensable, but healthcare practitioners and academics must understand their abilities and limitations. Future research should prioritize developing detailed ethical guidelines for AI use; ensuring integration into healthcare upholds high standards of patient care and data integrity. The uncritical use of AI-generated text, especially verbatim copying, can raise ethical concerns of plagiarism or misconduct. AI tools should be used judiciously, with thorough review and fine-tuning of generated content, as the responsibility for published material lies with the researchers and medical experts.[8,20–22]

In conclusion, integrating AI, including models like ChatGPT-3.5, into medical practice marks a significant shift. Medical professionals who use these technologies could influence the future of healthcare. The symbiosis of human expertise and AI has the potential to transform medical writing and the broader field of medicine. This relationship could improve patient care and advance medical research.[5,23,24] However, it is crucial to maintain high ethical standards and transparency when using ChatGPT and similar technologies. Clear ethical guidelines and transparency in AI model development and use are needed to help build trust, protect patient privacy, and ensure responsible medical writing practices.[25,26] Moreover, recommendations for researchers and writers to ensure responsible and effective use of ChatGPT-like Chatbots in academic writing are urgently needed.[27]

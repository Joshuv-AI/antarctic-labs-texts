Sophora flavescens Aqueous Extract Suppresses Eimeria tenella-Induced Inflammatory Responses and Regulates MAPK Pathway
Authors: Zhe Zheng, Yanchun Wang, Yunchao Bian, Qiang Zhang, Mohan Yang, Baihui Zhang, Yingjuan Lan, Tingting Yu, Yanan Cai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Coccidiosis, caused by Eimeria tenella, leads to severe intestinal damage, growth retardation, and significant economic losses in poultry. Drug resistance in conventional anticoccidial treatments has prompted exploration of herbal extracts, with S. flavescens showing promise due to its anti-inflammatory and antiparasitic properties. This study assessed the anticoccidial efficacy of S. flavescens aqueous extract (SFAE) and its modulation of the MAPK signaling pathway in MDBK cells and white-feathered broilers, using four groups: negative control (NC), E. tenella infection (PC), infection + SFAE (PC+SF), and infection + diclazuril (PC+DC).In vivo, compared to the NC group, PC group broilers exhibited significant bloody diarrhea, cecal lesions, and reduced survival (83 %; p < 0.01). The PC+SF group achieved an anticoccidial index of 162.8, with reduced lesions (p < 0.01) and normalized weight gain by day 6 (p > 0.05). HE staining revealed significant villus shedding and inflammation in the PC group (p < 0.01), while the PC+SF group showed intact villi and reduced inflammation (p < 0.01). In vitro, SFAE (10 mg/mL) had no significant effect on MDBK cell viability (p > 0.05). Compared to the NC group, PC group MDBK cells displayed increased p38 and MEK phosphorylation (p < 0.01), which was significantly reduced in the PC+SF group (p < 0.01), with no difference from NC by day 10 (p > 0.05).Serum ELISA showed significantly elevated IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α and reduced IL-10 in the PC group (p < 0.01), while the PC+SF group demonstrated highly significant downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines and upregulated IL-10 (p < 0.01), with levels comparable to NC by days 6 and 10 (p > 0.05). In vivo qPCR and Western blot analyses confirmed that MAPK pathway components (ERK, MEK, JNK, Raf1, p38) were highly significantly upregulated in the PC group (p < 0.01), which were significantly reduced in the PC+SF group (p < 0.01), ultimately showing no significant difference from the NC group by day 10 (p > 0.05). SFAE effectively mitigates E. tenella-induced damage by suppressing MAPK signaling and inflammation, suggesting its potential as an anticoccidial agent.

Introduction

Coccidiosis, caused by Eimeria spp., represents a major economic threat to the global poultry industry, with Eimeria tenella being the primary causative agent of cecal coccidiosis(Blake et al., 2020; Györke, et al., 2013). E. tenella infection induces intestinal hemorrhage, villus damage, and inflammatory cell infiltration, resulting in growth retardation, reduced survival rates, and substantial economic losses(Blake, et al., 2015). The genetic and antigenic diversity of E. tenella complicates vaccine development, further limiting disease control efficacy(Clark, et al., 2017; Gu, et al., 2025). Moreover, prolonged use of anticoccidial drugs (e.g., monensin and diclazuril) has led to widespread drug resistance, significantly reducing the effectiveness of conventional treatments(Mahmood et al., 2014). Consequently, exploring novel anticoccidial strategies, particularly those targeting inflammatory damage and parasite development, is of critical importance.

S. flavescens Ait., a traditional Chinese medicinal herb, has garnered significant attention for its anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and antiparasitic properties(Sun, et al., 2022; Wang, et al., 2025). Its primary active components, such as matrine and flavonoids, exhibit antiviral, antitumor, and intestinal inflammation-alleviating potential(Liu, et al., 2025; Yuan, et al., 2023). Recent studies have demonstrated that S. flavescens extracts possess anticoccidial activity against E. tenella in both in vitro and in vivo models, mitigating infection-induced damage by protecting the intestinal barrier and modulating immune responses(Song, et al., 2020). Additionally, S. flavescens polysaccharides inhibit SARS-CoV-2 entry by interfering with spike protein-mediated membrane fusion, suggesting a potential similar mechanism against parasite invasion(Liu et al., 2025). Given the challenge of drug resistance, the potential of S. flavescens aqueous extract (SFAE) as a low-toxicity alternative for anticoccidial therapy warrants further investigation.

E. tenella infection activates the p38 MAPK signaling pathway, inducing the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α), which enhances parasite invasion and exacerbates intestinal inflammatory damage(Bussière, et al., 2015; Zhao, et al., 2019). The MAPK pathway (including p38 and ERK) plays a critical regulatory role in parasite-induced inflammatory responses, and its inhibition significantly reduces inflammation and tissue damage(Gong, et al., 2019; Yeung, et al., 2018). Studies have shown that natural plant extracts, such as flavonoids, exhibit anti-inflammatory and antiparasitic effects by downregulating MAPK and NF-κB pathways (Ren, et al., 2020). This study aims to investigate whether SFAE suppresses E. tenella-induced inflammatory damage and parasite development by modulating the MAPK pathway and cytokine expression, providing a theoretical basis for developing novel anticoccidial drugs.

Water extracts demonstrate enhanced bioavailability through gut microbiota-mediated conversion of polymer complexes such as flavonoid-polysaccharides to active aglycones (>40 % bioavailability increase). Natural supramolecular structures enable gastric protection and colon-targeted release. Synergistic Interaction modulates immune/oxidative targets (e.g., a 60 % reduction in breast cancer metastasis; 100 % survival from typhoid). Clinical advantages include: 1. Safety: No solvent residues (pharmacopeia-compliant), endogenous actives (e.g., adenosine); 2. Practicality: Simple decoction, thermal stability (e.g., marigold heat extracts showed 30 % enhanced antimicrobial potency). Evidence validates water extraction as a metabolically optimized, clinically viable strategy.

Materials and methods

Experimental animals, cells, and parasite strains

One hundred twenty 2-day-old white-feathered broilers (purchased from the Poultry Experimental Farm, Agricultural Academy of Jilin Province, Changchun, China) were housed in sterile steel wire cages, provided with standard feed and ad libitum water, with environmental temperature maintained at 28–32°C and relative humidity at 50–60 %. MDBK cells (bovine kidney cells) and Eimeria tenella sporulated oocysts were supplied by the Parasitology Laboratory, College of Veterinary Medicine, Jilin Agricultural University, with oocysts preserved at 4°C in 2 % potassium dichromate solution. This study was approved by the Animal Welfare and Research Ethics Committee of Jilin Agricultural University (Approval No.: JLAU 20200704001).

Primary reagents and equipment

CCK-8 reagent was purchased from Beijing Solarbio Technology Co., Ltd.; DMEM medium from Shanghai Zhongqiao Xinzhou Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; 0.25 % trypsin and fetal bovine serum from Beijing QuanShijin Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Diclazuril solution was obtained from Changchun Kangmu Veterinary Drug Store; S. flavescens from Changchun Fubaicao Pharmacy. ELISA kits (IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, IL-10) were purchased from Shanghai Youxuan Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; TRIzol reagent from Takara Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd.; TransScript® First-Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix and TransStart® Tip Green qPCR SuperMix from Beijing QuanShijin Biotechnology Co., Ltd. RIPA lysis buffer and BCA protein assay kits were from Shanghai Beyotime Biotechnology Co., Ltd.; rabbit monoclonal antibodies against p38, p-p38, MEK, and p-MEK from Qinke Biological Research Center Co., Ltd.; rabbit monoclonal β-actin antibody from Wuhan Servicebio Technology Co., Ltd.; hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining solution from Beijing Xinhua Luyuan Technology Co., Ltd. NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer, qPCR system, and Western blot imaging system were purchased from Thermo Fisher Scientific, Bio-Rad Laboratories, and GE Healthcare, respectively.

Preparation of S. flavescens aqueous extract

Dried S. flavescens was pulverized into fine powder. Fifty grams were mixed with 500 mL distilled water, soaked for 4 hours, boiled over high heat, and simmered for 1 hour. The mixture was filtered through medical gauze, and the residue was boiled again with 300 mL distilled water, followed by filtration. The combined filtrates were concentrated under reduced pressure to 50 mL, yielding 1000 mg/mL S. flavescens aqueous extract (SFAE), stored at 4°C. Diclazuril solution (10 mL) was diluted in 500 mL distilled water to prepare a 1 μg/mL working solution, stored at 4°C.

Cell experiments

MDBK cells were seeded at 1 × 10⁴ cells/well in 96-well plates and cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum (37°C, 5 % CO₂). After adherence, the effect of SFAE (0–100 mg/mL) on cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. Ten microliters of CCK-8 reagent were added per well, incubated for 1 hour in the dark, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader to calculate cell survival rate (%)(Zheng and Lin, 2025). E. tenella sporozoites were purified via grinding, enzymatic digestion, and gradient centrifugation to establish an infection model. MDBK cells were divided into four groups: (1) negative control (NC); (2) E. tenella infection (PC, 10⁷ sporozoites/mL); (3) infection + SFAE (PC+SF); (4) infection + diclazuril (PC+DC, 1 μg/mL). After 48 hours post-infection, cells were collected for Western blot analysis of p38 and MEK phosphorylation levels, with experiments performed in triplicate.

Animal Experiments and Anticoccidial Index (ACI) Determination

A total of 120 white-feathered broiler chickens (14-day-old, body weight variation ≤5 g) were randomly allocated into four groups (25 chickens/group): (1) Negative control (NC) (2) Eimeria tenella-infected group (PC) (3) Infected + S. flavescens extract (SFAE)-treated group (PC+SF) (4) Infected + diclazuril-treated group (PC+DC) All chickens except the NC group were orally inoculated with 4 × 10⁴ sporulated E. tenella oocysts at 14 days of age. The PC+SF group received 1 mL of SFAE (100 mg/mL) daily, while the PC+DC group was administered 1 mL of diclazuril solution daily for 10 consecutive days. From days 4 to 10 post-infection (DPI), clinical signs (including bloody diarrhea) and body weight changes were recorded daily. Fecal samples (1 g) were homogenized in 50 mL saturated saline for oocyst counting using a hemocytometer (oocysts per gram, OPG). On DPI 4 and 7, five chickens per group were randomly euthanized, and all remaining chickens were sacrificed on DPI 10. Serum and cecal tissues were aseptically collected (partially fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for histopathology, and the remainder stored at −80°C for RNA/protein analysis). The Anticoccidial Index (ACI) was calculated as: ACI = (Relative Weight Gain + Survival Rate) - (Oocyst Value + Lesion Value) Component Definitions: Relative Weight Gain (%) = (Mean weight gain of treated group / NC group mean weight gain) × 100 Survival Rate (%) = (Number of surviving chickens / Initial number) × 100 Oocyst Value: Assigned as 0 (<1 %), 5 (1–25 %), 10 (25–50 %), 20 (51–75 %), or 40 (>75 %) based on OPG ratio (treated group/PC group × 100 %) Lesion Value = Mean lesion score × 10 (Scoring criteria: 0 = normal; 1 = slight hemorrhage; 2 = mild thickening with bloody contents; 3 = marked thickening with extensive hemorrhage/cecal cores; 4 = severe swelling with blood-filled ceca) Oocyst Quantification: OPG was determined via McMaster’s technique: 2 g of cecal content was suspended in 58 mL saline, and 100 µL of the suspension was diluted with 900 µL distilled water. Oocysts were counted in a 0.15 mL chamber, with OPG = Total oocysts × 1,000 (representing 0.001 g feces). Efficacy Classification: Ineffective: ACI < 120; Low efficacy: 120≤ACI < 160; Moderate efficacy: 160≤ACI<180; High efficacy: ACI≥180(Meng, et al., 2024).

Histopathological observation of cecal tissue

Cecal tissues fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde were dehydrated in graded ethanol, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned into 4 μm slices. Sections were stained with hematoxylin-eosin (HE) and examined under a 200 × optical microscope for villus structure, inflammatory cell infiltration, and pathological changes. Serum cytokine level detection

Serum samples collected on days 4, 7, and 10 post-infection were analyzed for IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-10 concentrations using ELISA kits. Assays were performed per the manufacturer’s instructions, with absorbance measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. Cytokine concentrations were calculated from standard curves, with samples tested in triplicate.

Fluorescence quantitative PCR detection

RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using TransScript® First-Strand cDNA Synthesis SuperMix. qPCR was performed using TransStart® Tip Green qPCR SuperMix to quantify mRNA levels of ERK, MEK, Raf1, JNK, and MAPK, with primers synthesized by Shanghai Sangon Biotech Co., Ltd. (Table 1), and β-actin as the reference gene. qPCR conditions: 95°C for 30 seconds, followed by 40 cycles (95°C for 5 seconds, 60°C for 30 seconds). Western blot analysis

Cecal tissues or MDBK cells were homogenized in RIPA lysis buffer, and total protein was extracted by centrifugation (13,000 rpm, 10 minutes) at 4°C. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay. Samples were mixed with loading buffer, denatured at 100°C for 5 minutes, separated by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5 % non-fat milk for 2 hours, incubated with primary antibodies against p38, p-p38, MEK, p-MEK (1:500), and β-actin (1:2000) overnight at 4°C, followed by incubation with goat anti-rabbit IgG secondary antibody (1:5000) for 1 hour at room temperature. Bands were visualized using ECL reagents, images were captured with a Western blot imaging system, and grayscale values were quantified with ImageJ software, normalized to β-actin(Zhang et al., 2024).

Data processing

All data were subjected to statistical difference analysis using SPSS 26.0 software, and graphs were generated using GraphPad Prism 9. Statistical analysis of the data was performed using a univariate analysis method, with p < 0.05 or p < 0.01 indicating significant or highly significant differences, respectively.

Results

Results of anticoccidial index detection in chicks

From days 4 to 10 post-infection with Eimeria tenella, clinical symptoms were monitored, and the anticoccidial index (ACI) was calculated on day 10 following necropsy. Compared to the NC group, the PC group exhibited significant bloody diarrhea from day 4, with marked exacerbation on days 5–7 (p < 0.01), accompanied by lethargy, reduced feed intake, increased water consumption, and significantly slower weight gain (p < 0.01). The PC group’s survival rate decreased to 83 %, with necropsy and microscopy confirming two deaths due to coccidial infection. Compared to the PC group, the PC+SF and PC+DC groups showed mild appetite reduction and slower weight gain on days 4–6 (p < 0.05), with highly significant symptom alleviation by day 7 (p < 0.01). Necropsy revealed significant cecal distension, hemorrhage, and cecal cores in the PC group (Fig. 1.A), while lesions were highly significantly reduced in the PC+SF and PC+DC groups (p < 0.01). Compared to the NC group, weight gain in the PC+SF and PC+DC groups showed no significant difference after day 6 (p > 0.05; Fig. 1.B). The PC+SF group achieved an ACI of 162.8, indicating moderate anticoccidial efficacy, comparable to the PC+DC group (Table 2). Effects of S. flavescens aqueous extract on cecal histopathology in eimeria tenella-infected chicks

On day 10 post-infection, all chicks were euthanized, and cecal tissues were collected under sterile conditions for hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining to examine histopathological changes. Compared to the NC group, the PC group exhibited significant villus epithelial cell disintegration (black arrows), villus shedding (yellow arrows), capillary dilation, hemorrhage with red cell exudation into the intestinal lumen (blue arrows), and pronounced lymphocyte-dominated inflammatory cell infiltration in the lamina propria and muscular layer (green arrows). Compared to the PC group, the PC+SF and PC+DC groups showed significantly intact villus structures and reduced inflammatory cell infiltration, with no significant differences from the NC group. These results indicate that, compared to the NC group, SFAE significantly mitigates E. tenella-induced intestinal inflammation and tissue damage, promoting cecal villus repai. MDBK cell viability assay

The effect of SFAE on MDBK cell viability was assessed using the CCK-8 assay. Compared to the NC group, SFAE concentrations of 25–100 mg/mL significantly reduced cell viability at 36 and 72 hours (p < 0.05). At concentrations below 10 mg/mL, cell viability showed no significant changes (p > 0.05), with 10 mg/mL identified as the optimal concentration for MDBK cell growth . Thus, 10 mg/mL was selected as the working concentration for subsequent experiments. These results indicate that, compared to the NC group, SFAE at 10 mg/mL exhibits no significant cytotoxicity to MDBK cells, making it suitable for anticoccidial studies. Inhibitory effects of S. flavescens aqueous extract on protein phosphorylation levels in eimeria tenella-infected MDBK cells

Western blot analysis assessed p38 and MEK phosphorylation levels in MDBK cells after 48 hours of treatment. Compared to the NC group, the PC group showed highly significant increases in p38 and MEK phosphorylation (p < 0.01). Compared to the PC group, the PC+SF and PC+DC groups exhibited highly significantly reduced p38 and MEK phosphorylation (p < 0.01). Compared to the NC group, the PC+SF group showed significantly elevated MEK phosphorylation (p < 0.05) but no significant difference in p38 phosphorylation (p > 0.05); no significant differences were observed between the PC+SF and PC+DC groups for p38 and MEK phosphorylation (p > 0.05). ELISA results of serum cytokine levels in different groups of chicks

Serum was collected post-infection, and ELISA was used to measure IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-10 levels. Compared to the NC group, the PC group showed highly significant increases in IL-1β and IL-6 levels at all time points (p < 0.01), significant elevation in TNF-α on day 4 (p < 0.05), and highly significant elevation on days 6 and 10 (p < 0.01), with IL-10 levels significantly reduced on day 4 (p < 0.05) and highly significantly reduced on days 6 and 10 (p < 0.01). Compared to the PC group, the PC+SF and PC+DC groups showed highly significant reductions in IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α levels (p < 0.01), along with a highly significant increase in IL-10 levels (p < 0.01), compared to the PC group.. Compared to the NC group, the PC+SF group showed significantly elevated TNF-α and reduced IL-10 levels on day 4 (p < 0.05), with no significant differences in IL-1β and IL-6 (p > 0.05); on days 6 and 10, IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and IL-10 levels in the PC+SF and PC+DC groups showed no significant differences from the NC group (p > 0.05). Discussion

Coccidiosis caused by Eimeria tenella is a major threat to the poultry industry, inducing intestinal damage, growth retardation, and significant economic losses (Singhal, et al., 2024). E. tenella invades cecal epithelial cells, triggering severe inflammatory responses characterized by bloody diarrhea, villus shedding, and inflammatory cell infiltration (Geng, et al., 2024). Studies indicate that the pathological damage from coccidial infection not only impairs poultry growth performance but also complicates disease control due to the parasite’s genetic diversity (Han, et al., 2022). Furthermore, drug resistance, such as to monensin, resulting from prolonged use of anticoccidial agents, limits the efficacy of conventional treatments (Mahmood et al., 2014; Gu et al., 2025). Consistent with the literature, our results showed that, compared to the NC group, the PC group broilers developed highly significant clinical manifestations, including bloody diarrhea (p < 0.01), severe cecal lesions (p < 0.01), and reduced survival rate (83 %; p < 0.01) compared to the NC group, with HE staining revealed highly significant villus shedding and inflammatory cell infiltration (p < 0.01). Compared to the PC group, the PC+SF group achieved an ACI of 162.8, with highly significant reduction lesions (p < 0.01) and weight gain normalized after day 6 (p > 0.05), indicating that SFAE effectively ameliorates E. tenella-induced clinical symptoms and pathological damage.

The rise in anticoccidial drug resistance has driven research toward natural plant extracts, with S. flavescens gaining attention for its anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and antiparasitic properties (Song et al., 2020; Sun et al., 2022). Active components of S. flavescens, such as matrine and flavonoids, exhibit antiviral, antitumor, and intestinal inflammation-alleviating potential (Yuan et al., 2023). Recent studies have demonstrated that S. flavescens extracts protect against E. tenella infection by preserving the intestinal barrier and modulating immune responses(Liu et al., 2025; Wang, et al., 2018). Similarly, other plant extracts, such as those from eucalyptus and areca nut, show anticoccidial activity, suggesting the potential of plant-derived compounds as antibiotic alternatives(Wang et al., 2025). Our study found that, compared to the NC group, SFAE (10 mg/mL) had no significant effect on MDBK cell viability (p > 0.05), and in vivo, the PC+SF group maintained structurally intact cecal villi architecture, with highly significant reduction in inflammatory cell infiltration (p < 0.01) compared to the PC group. Compared to the NC group, the PC+SF group exhibited highly significant downregulation of serum pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α)(p < 0.01) and concurrent upregulation of anti-inflammatory IL-10 (p < 0.01), as quantified by ELISA. These results demonstrate SFAE's potent immunomodulatory effects in ameliorating E. tenella-induced inflammation, with levels on days 6 and 10 comparable to the NC group (p > 0.05), indicating that SFAE exerts anticoccidial effects by protecting the intestinal barrier and modulating immune responses.

The MAPK signaling pathway (including p38, MEK, and ERK) plays a critical role in E. tenella-induced inflammatory responses by activating pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α), exacerbating intestinal damage (Heinrich, et al., 2003; Ma, et al., 2019). Studies have shown that inhibiting the MAPK pathway significantly reduces inflammation, offering a novel target for antiparasitic therapies (Cheng, et al., 2019; Hovsepian, et al., 2013). Plant compounds like quercetin and luteolin exhibit anti-inflammatory effects by downregulating MAPK and NF-κB pathways (Wang, et al., 2019; Xia, et al., 2014). Our results showed that, compared to the NC group, the PC group exhibited highly significant increases in p38 and p-MEK phosphorylation in MDBK cells and broiler cecal tissues (p < 0.01), with highly significant upregulation of ERK, MEK, JNK, and Raf1 mRNA levels (p < 0.01). Compared to the PC group, the PC+SF group exhibited highly significant downregulated p38 and p-MEK phosphorylation and MAPK pathway gene expression (p < 0.01), with no significant difference from the NC group by day 10 (p > 0.05). These findings indicate that SFAE effectively mitigates E. tenella-induced inflammatory damage by suppressing MAPK signaling and cytokine expression.

In conclusion, SFAE significantly alleviates E. tenella-induced intestinal damage by suppressing MAPK signaling and inflammatory responses, providing a scientific basis for developing novel anticoccidial drugs.

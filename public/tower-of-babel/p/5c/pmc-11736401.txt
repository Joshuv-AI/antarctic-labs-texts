First in human intraarterial delivery of tislelizumab for the treatment of pMMR locally advanced rectal cancer: A single-arm, open label, phase II clinical trial
Authors: Weina Yang, Chengyuan Qian, Jiamin Luo, Chuan Chen, Yan Feng, Nan Dai, Xuemei Li, He Xiao, Yuxin Yang, Mengxia Li, Chunxue Li, Dong Wang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The possibility of immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy has been explored in multiple phase II clinical trials and reported promising pCR rates. However, severe immune-related toxicities have been a significant concern. Therefore, there is a pressing need for novel strategies to address these toxicities and improve outcomes in locally advanced rectal cancer.•This study introduces a novel regimen: chemo-immuno-embolization with transcatheter rectal arterial intervention plus chemoradiation. This approach demonstrates a favorable pCR rate and excellent tolerance in the treatment of locally advanced rectal cancer, addressing both efficacy and toxicity concerns associated with traditional therapies.•The integration of intraarterial PD-1 antibody delivery with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy opens up a promising avenue for the treatment of locally advanced rectal cancer. The encouraging results warrant further investigation in large cohorts, potentially influencing future research directions and clinical practice guidelines regarding the management of this disease.

Introduction

The established approach for treating locally advanced rectal cancer (LARC) involves preoperative neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (nCRT) followed by surgical resection. A noteworthy development occurred at the 2020 ASCO Annual Meeting, where the first intravenous anti-programmed death 1 (PD-1) antibody was integrated into the neoadjuvant regimen for LARC. This strategy led to promising pathological complete response (pCR) rates of 30 % and 60 % in LARC patients with microsatellite stability (MSS) and microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H), respectively. Notably, only 7.14 % of patients experienced immune-related severe adverse events [1]. In view of this encouraging results, multiple phase II clinical trials have been initiated to explore the optimal combination of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (CRT). These trials employed either standard chemoradiotherapy or total neoadjuvant therapy (TNT), incorporating varying doses of intravenous ICIs. Reported pCR rates ranged from 23 % to 48 %, and the incidence of grade 3–4 immune-related toxicity below 10 % [[2], [3], [4], [5]].

While intravenous administration of ICIs is the conventional approach for systemic treatment across various tumors, localized delivery is gaining attention due to its potential benefits. In a study of bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG)-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer [6], intravesical delivery of pembrolizumab has proven to be safe, feasible, and effective in eliciting strong immune responses, as evidenced by the median follow-up of 35 months for the five patients who were alive at the end of the trial. Immune-related toxicities were grade 1–2 except for one death due to myasthenia gravis. Additionally, in a recent phase I trial evaluating the feasibility of concurrent intrathecal and intravenous administration of nivolumab in patients with melanoma and leptomeningeal disease [7], intrathecal administration notably increased PD-1 antibody exposure in the cerebrospinal fluid without causing serious toxicities. The evidence suggests that localized delivery of ICIs could be a promising strategy, offering potential advantages such as reduced side effects and enhanced treatment outcomes.

In our recent PCAR trial, the utilization of transcatheter rectal arterial chemoembolization (TRACE) with oxaliplatin showed an impressive pCR rate of 20.72 % [8], exceeding the 17 % pCR rate observed in the CAO/ARO/AIO-04 trial [9]. Notably, the incidence of preoperative grade 3–4 toxicity was comparable between these two trials. Encouraged by the favorable safety and efficacy profile of TRACE, this study extends the successful strategy from the PCAR trial by employing TRACE in combination with anti-PD-1 antibody as a treatment approach. This approach marks the initiation of the first-in-human clinical trial for preoperative Chemo-Immuno-Embolization with Transcatheter Rectal Arterial Intervention (CIETAI) in patients with LARC. Our investigation delves into the realms of safety, toxicity, and clinical response, presenting valuable insights into this established therapeutic strategy.

Methods

Study design and participants

This single-arm phase II trial was conducted in China in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Prior to the commencement of the study, written informed consent was obtained from each study participant. This trial protocol was approved by the ethics committee of Daping Hospital of Army Medical University (Approved number: No.2023–152) and was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT05957016). The inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) patients aged 18–75 years with untreated, histologically diagnosed rectal adenocarcinoma; 2) lesions located 15 cm from the anal verge; 3) clinical stage II or III (T3–4N0M0 or T1–4N+M0); and 4) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) 0–1. Patients were deemed ineligible if they met any of the following criteria: 1) active autoimmune disease or other diseases requiring immunosuppressive therapy [2]; contraindications to arterial puncture, immunotherapy, or chemoradiotherapy [3]; severe cardiopulmonary dysfunction, renal dysfunction, or liver dysfunction [4]; severe neurological or mental illness; and [5] pregnancy or lactation.

Procedures

The recruitment and treatment processes are illustrated in Supplementary Fig. 1. Initially, patients underwent a comprehensive baseline assessment, including coloscopy, pelvic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and computed tomography (CT) scans of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis for tumor staging. Endoscopic biopsy samples were obtained and used for immunohistochemistry (IHC) analysis of DNA mismatch repair (MMR) protein patterns. The absence of any protein expression of MSH6, MSH2, MLH1, and PMS2 in the IHC results classified as mismatch repair-deficient (dMMR); otherwise, it was considered mismatch repair-proficient (pMMR).Fig. 1Trial profile.Fig 1

Trial profile.

Eligible patients first received CIETAI treatment. The detailed procedure was as follows: The blood feeding arteries of the rectal tumor was initially identified, followed by femoral artery puncture. Subsequently, oxaliplatin (130 mg/m2, Jiangsu Hengrui Medicine Co.Ltd., Nanjing, China) and PD-1 monoclonal antibody tislelizumab (200 mg, BeiGene, Ltd., Beijing, China) were sequentially infused. Major nutrient arteries were then embolized using gelatin sponge particles (350–560 μm; Alicon Hangzhou, China) soaked in 10 mL of iodixanol. In order to avoid tissue necrosis and perforation, relatively small tumor-feeding arteries were selectively left preserved. The oxaliplatin dosage was calculated based on the 130 mg/m2 body surface area. A calculated quantity of 100 mg oxaliplatin was diluted in 50 ml of a 5 % glucose solution and infused slowly through a catheter, while the remaining dose was given intravenously. Additionally, all patients received standard concomitant chemoradiotherapy involving two 21-day cycles of capecitabine (1000 mg/m2, bid, po, day1–14) and radiation (50.4 Gy in 28 fractions). Besides, tislelizumab was administered intravenously at a fixed dosage of 200 mg every 3 weeks during chemoradiotherapy for an additional two cycles.

Following neoadjuvant therapy, patients underwent a subsequent coloscopy and pelvic MRI scan for the purpose of re-staging the tumor. In addition, a CT scan of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis was conducted to assess the potential presence of distant metastases. If there are any indications of potential metastasis, an 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography-computed tomography (18F-FDG PET-CT) examination was performed to verify the presence of metastasis. Tumor response to nCRT was evaluated using the Tumor Regression Grade (mrTRG) system [10], which has demonstrated a significant association with the prognosis of patients with rectal cancer [11]. In this context, mrTRG1 denotes radiological complete regression, indicating the absence of any residual tumor signal; mrTRG2 indicates good regression, where dark fibrosis predominates without evident residual tumor signal; mrTRG3 indicates moderate regression, where predominant dark fibrosis is accompanied by intermediate high signal intensity; mrTRG4 indicates slight regression, with minimal dark fibrosis or mucin and a predominance of tumor signal; and mrTRG5 indicates the absence of tumor regression, as evidenced by an intermediate high signal intensity on the MRI, closely resembling the original appearance of the tumor.

Surgery was performed within 4 to 8 weeks after the completion of radiotherapy. All surgically resected specimens were preserved as into formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) samples. The extent of tumor regression was evaluated by determining the tumor regression grade (TRG) in accordance with the criteria of the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC; 8th edition). The TRG was independently scored by two pathologists. TRG0 represents pCR with no residual tumor cells in the surgical specimen. TRG1 indicates the presence of only viable single cells or small groups of cancer cells. TRG2 indicates the presence of residual cancer cells with evident tumor regression. TRG3 indicates no apparent tumor regression with extensive residual cancer cells. The major pathological response (MPR) was defined as the sum of TRG0 and TRG1, reflecting a substantial treatment response.

Following surgery, patients underwent 4–6 months of adjuvant chemotherapy, with the option of mFOLFOX6 or CAPOX. After completing chemotherapy, patients were follow-up with assessments every 6 months during the first 2 years and annually thereafter. The follow-up process was conducted through telephone interviews or outpatient examinations.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint was pCR rate, defined as the proportion of patients achieving pCR. The secondary endpoints were the neoadjuvant rectal (NAR) score, MPR rate, sphincter preservation rate and preoperative treatment-related adverse events (AEs). The NAR score is another commonly used endpoint as it captures information about downstaging (ypT stage minus cT stage) and nodal involvement after treatment, with higher score indicating poorer prognosis [12]. Treatment-related AEs were assessed and graded according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI-CTCAE), Version 5.0. Other secondary endpoints such as disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) will be reported in detail elsewhere.

Multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) staining

An OpalTM 7-Color Manual IHC Kit (NEL811001KT, Akoya Biosciences) were utilized in this mIF experiment. Tumor samples obtained from pre-neoadjuvant colonoscopy and surgical resection were prepared into 4-μm thick FFPE slides. The FFPE slides were baked at 65 °C for 3 h, and then deparaffinized and rehydrated. After tissue fixation in 10 % neutral buffered formalin and antigen retrieval using microwave treatment, the slides were subjected to the blocker for 10 min and the stained with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. Next, the tissue sections were incubated sequentially with secondary antibodies and opal reagents and proceeded to the next round of microwave treatment until DAPI incubation for nuclear counterstaining. The mIF experiments were performed with primary antibodies against CD4 (Abcam; ab133616), CD8 (Abcam; ab178089), CD68 (Abcam; ab192847), FoxP3 (Abcam; ab200034), PD-1 (Cell Signaling Technology; 86163T,) and pan-Cytokeratin (Abcam; ab7753). Finally, the slides were scanned with PhenoCycler-Fusion (Akoya Biosciences) and images were processed using PhenoImager Analysis Software.

Sample size and statistical analysis

The pCR rate in LARC patients treated with standard neoadjuvant CRT has been reported to fall within the range of 12 % to 22 % [13]. In this trial, the primary objective is to achieve a substantial increase of 20 % in pCR rate. To determine the minimal sample size, assuming the null hypothesis (pCR = 20 %) and the alternative hypotheses (pCR = 40 %), a sample size of 38 patients is estimated using ph2single function of R package “clinfun” (version 1.1.5). If >12 patients reaching pCR were observed, the null hypothesis was rejected. The sample size estimation is based on a one-sided significance level α of 5 %, a statistical power of 80 %, and a dropout rate of 20 %.

Continuous data are expressed as mean with standard deviation (SD) or, if data are not normally distributed, as median with interquartile range (IQR). Categorical data are presented as frequency and percentage. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS for Windows (version 26.0, Chicago, IL). The swimming plot was generated using the “swimplot” package in R (version 1.2.0). A paired samples Wilcoxon signed rank test was used for Fig. 3, Fig. 3. The p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant (*p < 0.05, ** p < 0.01).

Results

Patient characteristics

From January 2023 to December 2023, a total of 43 patients were assessed for eligibility. The flow diagram depicting the enrollment process of patients in this study is illustrated in Supplement Fig. 1. Two patients were deemed ineligible due to distant metastasis, while the remaining 41 patients were enrolled and underwent preoperative CIETAI treatment. Then three patients were excluded from this study, two of whom discontinued immunotherapy due to severe skin toxicity and one refused to accept radiotherapy. In this cohort, 12 (31.58 %) had pathological mesorectal fascia (MRF) involvement, and 14 (36.84 %) had extramural vascular invasion (EMVI). Efficacy evaluation

All participants received neoadjuvant therapy (CIETAI plus CRT and tislelizumab). The full-dose completion rate of radiotherapy was 76.32 % (29/38), and 97.37 % (37/38) received at minimum irradiation dose of 45 Gy. The interval from receiving CIETAI to completing radiotherapy was 54.63±12.27 days, while the duration between the last radiation and surgery was 58.61±13.56 days. The representative images of three patients with tumors in the upper (patient #26), middle (patient #20), and lower (patient #19) rectum before and after CIETAI are shown in Fig. 2A. Inferior mesenteric artery iodixanol angiography shows that the rectal tumor was initially supplied by the superior rectal artery, exhibiting abundant blood supply and tortuous peripheral vessels. Following CIETAI, the blood supply to the tumor significantly decreased, as evidenced by the disappearance of tumor staining, indicating successful embolization. Prior to surgery, chest and abdominal CT scans were conducted to assess metastatic progression. The findings revealed the absence of metastatic progression of all enrolled patients. Successful tumor removal is a primary goal of rectal surgery. In this study, all 38 patients subsequently underwent rectal surgery. Among them, ten patients received local excision, and the remaining 28 patients underwent total mesorectal excision (TME). Operative details were provided in Supplementary Table 1. The rate of R0 resection was 100 % (28/28) and anal preservation was achieved in 84.21 % (32/38) of patients. Notably, low rectal cancers are at a higher risk of necessitating a permanent stoma (colostomy or ileostomy) due to the challenges associated with preserving the anal sphincter function. In this study, 15 out of 21 patients with low rectal cancer achieved organ preservation with functional maintenance. This positive outcome underscores the potential benefits of preoperative CIETAI, emphasizing its role in achieving favorable results for patients with low rectal cancer. Regarding tumor regression, pCR, defined as ypT0N0, was observed in 17 (44.74 %) patients, therefore the study's primary objective was achieved. A total of 17 (44.74 %), 8 (21.05 %), 9 (23.68 %), and 4 (10.53 %) patients achieved TRG0, TRG1, TRG2, and TRG3, respectively, resulting a MPR rate of 65.79 % (Table 2). Additionally, 21 (55.26 %) patients achieved downstaging to pathological stage 0-I, and lymph node regression were observed in 25 (65.79 %) patients. Low NAR score (<8) was observed in 18 patients. Safety

The treatment-related AEs that occurred at any time before rectal surgery is shown in Table 3. The most common treatment-related AEs were leukopenia (52.63 %), thrombocytopenia (50.00 %), and high transaminase (31.58 %). Treatment-related grade 3/4 AEs occurred in 8 patients, including leukopenia in 4 patients, thrombocytopenia in 2 patients, neutropenia in 1 patient and radiation proctitis in 1 patient. Immune-related AEs were all grade 1–2, with endocrine toxicity being the most prevalent (5/6, 83.33 %). Regarding postoperative complications, two cases of intestinal obstruction and two cases of anastomotic fistula occurred. Discussion

While ICIs are thought to have the most promising future treatment landscape of various solid tumors, their effectiveness and immune-related AEs in LARC warrant further investigation. In this CIETAI trial, we attempt to mitigate the systematic toxicity associated with ICIs and enhance pCR rates by delivering tislelizumab directly into the artery that supplies blood to the rectal tumor. We employed intra-arterial delivery of tislelizumab, combined with trans-arterial chemoembolization, in the neoadjuvant setting. This approach yielded a noteworthy pCR rate of 44.74 %, and notably, there were no preoperative grade 3–4 immune-related AEs.

The clinical efficacy of PD-1 blockade in dMMR/MSI-H tumors is impressive, as shown by 75 % or even 100 % CR rates with PD-1 antibody monotherapy ([14,15]). In contrast to dMMR/MSI-H tumors, pMMR/MSS tumors are resistant to ICIs due to an excluded or suppressive tumor immune microenvironment. Fortunately, accumulating evidence suggests that nCRT can inflame the immune-desert tumors though multiple mechanisms, including upregulating expression of MHC class I and PD-L1 on tumor cells, downregulating MMR system-related genes, increasing tumor mutational burden and triggering T-cell infiltration [[16], [17], [18], [19], [20], [21]]. These results provide a rationale for the combination of CRT and immunotherapy in LARC patients with pMMR/MSS tumors and therefore various clinical trial has been conducted to explore the optimal combination regimen. Important prospective clinical trials that assess the efficacy and safety of neoadjuvant immunotherapy in patients with LARC since 2020 were summarized in Supplementary Table 2. The Voltage-A study included 39 MSS patients and 5 MSI-H patients [22]. The pCR rate was 30 % in the former and 60 % in the latter, both of which were inspiring. However, the incidence of grade 3–4 immune-related toxicity inevitably reached 9.1 % due to 5 cycles of nivolumab treatment. A NECTAR trial from China reported patients received long-course radiotherapy (50 Gy/25 f) and three cycles of capecitabine plus tislelizumab reached pCR rate of 40 % with grade 3–4 immune-related toxicity of 2 % [23]. Comparatively, our study demonstrated a similar promising pCR rate and the absence of grade 3–4 immune-related AEs. The higher efficacy and lower risk of toxicity may be attributed to the targeted delivery of tislelizumab and embolization agents directly to the rectal tumor site through TRACE, maximizing therapeutic impact while minimizing systemic exposure.

In contrast to other clinical trials, this study distinguishes itself through the novel delivery route. Specifically, our approach involves the combination of oxaliplatin (chemotherapy) and Tislelizumab (PD-1 immunotherapy) for the treatment of LARC. Since elevated interstitial fluid pressure (IFP) within the tumor microenvironment may impede drug penetration [24], we pioneered a novel sequential arterial infusion approach, incorporating oxaliplatin, tislelizumab, and embolic agents to overcome IFP elevation and enhance the exposure of anti-cancer drugs to tumors. The embolization of tumor-feeding arteries serves not only to deprive malignant cells of nutrients but also to further elevate the concentration of anticancer drugs in tumor tissue, with hope of reversing the immune desertification observed in pMMR rectal tumors and their resistance to ICIs. On the other hand, we strategically selected tislelizumab as a potent weapon against rectal tumors due to its unique structure that enhanced its affinity for PD-L1 [25]. Importantly, it minimizes binding to FcγRΙ-expressing macrophages, thereby effectively preventing antibody-mediated T cell depletion [25]. Another consideration is that immunotherapy targeting the PD-1 pathway may be a promising approach to control cancer metastasis by regulating immune cell balance. In a mouse model of gastric cancer peritoneal metastases, intraperitoneal administration of anti-PD-1 antibody effectively reduced mesentery metastases by upregulating the CD8+ T cells/myeloid-derived suppressor cells ratio [26]. Furthermore, intravesical delivery of pembrolizumab can remodel local immune contents and increase immune recruitment to the bladder [6]. Similarly, under the intervention of CIETAI and subsequent neoadjuvant therapy, immunosuppressive cells such as Treg cells and TAMs were reduced while T cell exhaustion was reinvigorated, achieving stronger anti-tumor immunity. However, further trials should delve into the retention of tislelizumab in rectal tumors and screen non-responders by analyzing changes in the immune niche during treatment, so that the treatment regimen can be adjusted in a timely manner.

A multicenter study demonstrated that pathological lymph node regression after neoadjuvant chemotherapy is a superior predictor of recurrence and survival in esophageal adenocarcinoma compared to pathologic TNM staging and primary tumor regression grading [27]. Similarly, pathological lymph node regression can also provide important prognostic information for patients with LARC [28]. In this CIETAI study, chemoradiation plus tislelizumab effectively resulted in lymph node downstaging in 65.79 % of LARC patients, suggesting an increased likelihood of improved prognosis, as lymph node involvement is a critical factor in predicting cancer recurrence and survival. Besides, benefiting from the effectiveness of this combination therapy, all patients underwent surgery 4–8 weeks after the last radiotherapy without prolonging the preoperative waiting time for patients. Our study reported a median operation time of 161 min, shorter than that of standard chemoradiotherapy (196.6 min) [29] and other neoadjuvant immunotherapy regimen (171.88 min) [30]. Furthermore, 55.26 % of patients achieved downstaging to pathological stage 0-I after surgery, indicating the safety, feasibility, and rapid downstaging potential of intraarterial delivery of tislelizumab in a clinical setting.

In this study, we observed a low agreement between mrTRG and TRG, consistent with previous reports ([31,32]). In a meta-analysis involving 916 patients [33], it was found that mrTRG1 had a sensitivity of only 32.3 % for achieving pCR. Conversely, mrTRG1–2 exhibited a higher sensitivity for pCR but a lower specificity. In our study, concordance between mrTRG and TRG was observed in 12 cases (33.33 %, 12/36). Eighteen cases (50 %, 18/36) were underestimated for tumor regression with mrTRG, while 6 cases (16.67 %, 6/36) were overestimated on MRI compared to TRG. Based on these findings, we recommend that monitoring treatment efficacy in patients adopting a non-operative management strategy should extend beyond MRI alone and incorporate biomarkers and additional imaging tests for a comprehensive evaluation of treatment response.

Organ preservation is crucial in rectal cancer treatment because it significantly enhances patients’ quality of life by minimizing the physical and psychological impact of radical procedures. A proportion of patients choose the “watch and wait (W&W)” strategy instead of sphincter-sparing surgery after achieving clinical complete response. However, the W&W strategy requires strict and close follow-up, which is difficult to implement in the poorer southwest areas of China. Therefore, all the enrolled patients underwent surgery. The anal preservation rate in this study was 84.21 %. Importantly, despite enrolling more high-risk patients, our study achieved a notable 71.43 % organ preservation rate for low rectal cancer. Sphincter saving was not attempted in 6 patients due to lower tumors location and poor tumor regression after neoadjuvant therapy.

Long-term survival benefit is the ultimate goal in the management of LARC. Although the UNION study [34], a randomized phase III trial, has also shown promising short-term oncological outcomes, concerns remained about whether improved pCR rates can translate into clinically meaningful survival benefits. Over time, these concerns have been mitigated by the consistent results of two clinical trials. A 3-year follow up of the NRG-GI002 trial showed a trend toward better OS after the addition of pembrolizumab to TNT despite lack of difference in NAR score, pCR rate or DFS [35]. Another phase II clinical trial adopting preoperative CRT followed by nivolumab reported its 3-year outcomes. For dMMR/MSI-H patients, both the 3-year relapse-free survival (RFS) and 3-year OS were 100 %. And the 3-year RFS and 3-year OS of MSS patients were 79.5 % and 97.4 %, respectively [36]. These results justify routine adoption of PD-1 blockade in clinical practice. Nevertheless, as the DFS and OS data are not mature, whether improvement in pCR in our study can contribute to better survival remains unclear.

Finally, this study has several limitations, including its single-center design, a relatively small sample size, and the absence of a control group. Additionally, TNT may be indicated for LARC patients with high-features.

Conclusion

To the best of our knowledge, this proof-of-concept study is pioneering in its introduction of the administration of tislelizumab, an anti-PD-1 antibody, through tumor-feeding arteries within the neoadjuvant setting for pMMR LARC. This innovative approach involves combining tislelizumab with chemoembolization agents, concurrently administered with chemoradiotherapy. This study reported a promising pCR rate with no severe immune-related AEs, emphasizing the safety and efficacy of directly applying tislelizumab to rectal tumors. This novel CIETAI approach, yielding promising results, illuminates a potential avenue for administering ICIs in the treatment of other solid tumors amenable to arterial embolization.

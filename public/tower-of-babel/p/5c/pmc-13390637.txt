Partial pericardial defect revealed by multi-modality imaging
Authors: Lisa Lauren Borg, Ching-Hui Sia, Arka Das, Sven Plein
Published: 2026

Case description

A 49 year old male, with past chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and a major road traffic accident (RTA), where he sustained multiple left-sided rib fractures presented with a history of intermittent chest pain.

His ECG showed left axis deviation with Q waves in leads III and aVF. A stress echocardiogram showed no evidence of inducible ischaemia. The right ventricle (RV) appeared visually dilated with an aneurysmal contour at the apex (Panels A and B).

A prior CT thorax showed healed left-sided rib fractures (Panel C) and possible pericardial contour loss at the RV apex (Panel D).

Cardiac MRI (CMR) revealed normal left ventricular size, morphology, and systolic function with no myocardial hyperenhancement. The RV was of normal size and global systolic function. There was loss of the pericardial contour at the RV apex (Panel F), with intact pericardium at mid and basal RV level (Panel E) with associated herniation of the RV apex. Contractile function of the apical RV free wall was preserved. The findings suggested a partial pericardial defect (Figure 1).

Given the absence of symptom progression, he was managed with a watchful waiting approach, including regular follow-up imaging. The patient remained stable requiring no surgical intervention.

This case illustrates the utility of multi-modality in identifying and characterizing pericardial abnormalities. Echocardiography is often the first line test with CT and CMR useful to differentiate pericardial disease from cardiomyopathies. Pericardial defects are uncommon and may be congenital or acquired. In this case, the preceding RTA adds chest trauma as a potential aetiology.

Lead Author Biography

Dr Lisa Lauren Borg graduated from the University of Malta in 2015 and completed a Master's degree in Internal Medicine at the University of Edinburgh in 2018. She subsequently undertook specialist cardiology training in Malta between 2020 and 2024, gaining comprehensive experience in the management of a wide range of cardiovascular conditions. She completed a fellowship in multi-modality cardiac imaging at Leeds General Infirmary, where she developed advanced expertise in cardiac imaging, including cardiac MRI, cardiac CT, transoesophageal echocardiography (TOE), and structural TOE. Dr Borg currently works as a Locum Consultant at Leeds General Infirmary, with a specialist interest in advanced echocardiography and valvular heart disease.

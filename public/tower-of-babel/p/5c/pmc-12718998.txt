Clinical Efficacy of Electrical Auricular Transcutaneous Stimulation for Dry Eye: A Sham-Controlled, Randomized Trial
Authors: Yeon Ji Jo, Jae Hyun Kim, Jong Soo Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to evaluate the clinical efficacy of low-frequency ear transcutaneous stimulation (TS) for improving ocular symptoms and signs in patients with dry eye disease (DED). The primary outcome was the Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) score difference between TS and sham groups, while secondary outcomes included tear breakup time (TBUT), Schirmer test (strip meniscometry tube, SMTube), and corneal staining (National Eye Institute [NEI] score).MethodsPatients with a TBUT <5 seconds and tear volume by Schirmer test <5 mm, along with ocular symptoms, were randomized into a TS treatment group (n = 25) and a sham group (n = 24). The TS treatment device was applied bilaterally twice daily for 8 weeks, with each session lasting 30 minutes. The OSDI questionnaire score, corneal and conjunctival stain score (NEI score), TBUT, and tear secretion volume (SMTube) were analyzed at baseline and 4 and 8 weeks after treatment.ResultsThe sham group showed no significant changes in any parameters at 4 and 8 weeks. The TS group demonstrated significant improvements in NEI score, TBUT, and SM tube values at both 4 and 8 weeks compared to the sham group (p = 0.002, p = 0.001, and p = 0.017, respectively, at 4 weeks; p = 0.001, p = 0.001, and p = 0.001, respectively, at 8 weeks). No significant difference in OSDI was found between groups at 4 weeks (p = 0.061), but at 8 weeks, the TS group showed a significantly greater improvement (p = 0.018).ConclusionsThe low-frequency ear TS device significantly improved DED symptoms and objective measures compared to the sham group. These findings support the potential of TS as a novel treatment option for DED.Keywords: Dry eye syndromes, Electrical transcutaneous stimulation, Ocular Surface Disease Index

Dry eye disease (DED) is a chronic inflammatory condition of the ocular surface caused by multiple factors that lead to ocular discomfort. The prevalence of DED in the Korean population is estimated to be approximately 10.4% [1], with incidence increasing with age. It is particularly common among individuals over 50 years old [2], and the number of affected patients continues to rise due to lifestyle changes, environmental factors, and increased screen exposure. Despite various treatment options, including environmental and dietary modifications, artificial tears, punctal plugs, anti-inflammatory medications, corticosteroids, autologous serum, and surgical interventions [3], many patients continue to experience persistent symptoms. This underscores the urgent need for innovative and effective treatment alternatives for chronic DED.

One promising approach is transcutaneous electrical stimulation, which has been explored as a complementary therapy for chronic ocular conditions [4]. Several studies suggest that transcutaneous electrical stimulation may offer symptomatic relief for patients with DED by modulating nerve activity and enhancing tear secretion [5–7]. Interestingly, prior research has demonstrated that transcutaneous stimulation (TS) could provide superior mid-term outcomes compared to artificial tears [4,8]. Additionally, experimental studies indicate that TS may reduce ocular discomfort [8], while other reports suggest that glandular secretion can be influenced by autonomic and immune system regulation via auricular stimulation [9,10]. The auricular branch of the vagus nerve is distributed in the ear auricle, particularly in areas such as the anterior wall of the external auditory canal, tragus, and concha. Electrical stimulation of these regions can activate central autonomic reflex pathways, enhancing ocular surface homeostasis by promoting tear secretion, supporting meibomian gland function, and reducing ocular surface inflammation.

Building on this foundation, we hypothesized that periauricular TS therapy could serve as an effective complementary treatment for patients with chronic DED. This study aimed to evaluate the clinical efficacy of a TS treatment device in improving both subjective symptoms and objective ocular surface parameters in patients with DED. By conducting comprehensive ocular assessments and analyzing patient-reported symptoms, we sought to determine the therapeutic potential of this novel device. The TS device was specifically designed to conform to the anatomy of the ear and optimize stimulation for targeted therapeutic effects. This study provides valuable insights into the feasibility and effectiveness of TS treatment for DED, potentially offering a new avenue for managing this chronic condition.

Materials and Methods

Ethics statement

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Pusan National University Hospital (No. 2107- 026-105). Written informed consents for publication of the research details and clinical images were obtained from the patient. This study was registered in the Clinical Research Information Service (CRIS identifier: KCT0007563). All study procedures were performed according to the ethical standards of the institutional and national research committee and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Patient selection

Patients aged >19 and <80 years with DED symptoms were enrolled in the study. DED was diagnosed by a single ophthalmologist, following the Korea Corneal Disease Research Association guidelines [11]. Patients diagnosed with level III DED without a medical history of artificial tear use within 2 weeks were enrolled in this clinical trial. This study was conducted among those who voluntarily agreed to participate through our clinical trial recruitment notice.

Patients diagnosed with level IV DED, allergic conjunctivitis, severe ocular infection, or inflammation were excluded. In addition, patients with a history of ocular surgery within 3 months and using systemic (antianxiety, antihistamine, or antidepressant) or topical medications that could affect the tear film or meibomian gland function were excluded. Other exclusion criteria were pregnancy, lactating mothers, and allergy to fluorescein.

Ophthalmological examination and measurements

All participants underwent slit-lamp examination and dry eye assessments at baseline, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks after treatment. At each visit, participants completed the Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) questionnaire, and measurements of corneal staining (National Eye Institute [NEI] score), tear breakup time (TBUT), and tear secretion volume (strip meniscometry tube; SMTube, Echo Electricity) were obtained.

The OSDI consists of 12 questions related to environmental factors, with each item scored from 0 (no symptoms) to 4 (most severe symptoms). The total score was calculated by summing the scores of the answered questions and dividing by the number of questions answered. The NEI ocular surface staining score was assessed using fluorescein strip paper (Haag-Streit International). Following fluorescein instillation, superficial epithelial cells of the cornea and conjunctiva were examined under a cobalt blue light source using a slit-lamp microscope at 10× magnification. The cornea was divided into five sections, with each section graded from 0 to 3 based on the severity of staining. The total staining score was calculated by summing the scores of all sections. The grading scale was defined as follows: 0 (no staining), 1 (scattered minute staining), 2 (moderate spotty staining), and 3 (diffuse blotchy staining), with total scores ranging from 0 to 15 [13].

TBUT was measured by applying fluorescein paper to the lower conjunctival sac and observing the tear film under slit-lamp cobalt blue filter illumination. TBUT was recorded as the time in seconds between the last complete blink and the first disruption of the stained tear film. Three measurements were taken on both eyes of each participant, and the mean value was used for analysis.

Tear secretion volume was measured using SMTube. The SMTube was placed in contact with the tear meniscus for 5 seconds, during which absorbed tears stained the strip blue. The length of the stained column was recorded to quantify tear volume [14].

Study design

This prospective, sham-controlled, randomized, double-blinded interventional study was performed at a referral eye center from December 2021 to December 2023 in Busan, South Korea. The sample size was calculated, and the mean difference in dry eye symptoms between the treatment and sham groups was compared, assuming 1:1 randomization, 80% power (α = 0.05), and a precision error of 5%. The estimated sample size in each group was calculated to be 22 using G*Power ver. 3.1 (Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf). Considering an expected dropout rate of 10%, the final number of each group participants were 25. The total number of participants in this study was 50 patients. All patients were randomly allocated to one of the two groups using the parallel assignment. Randomization were performed using SAS ver. 9.1.3 (SAS Institute Inc). An independent researcher who was not involved in data collection or analysis conducted randomization. The TS group received 30 minutes of low-frequency TS treatment with the device twice daily for 8 weeks. In the sham group, the same procedure was performed without electrical stimulation using the same medical device. The participants and investigators were double-blinded to the participants’ group assignments until the completion of the statistical analysis. During the trial period, all participants were prescribed 0.15% sodium hyaluronate cellulose eye drops (New Hyaluni, Taejoon Pharmaceutical) four times a day (Fig. 1).

Interventions

In this study, a low-frequency electrical stimulation medical device was used for participants to evaluate the clinical efficacy of the TS treatment device. This device was provided by Natural Well Tech. The device’s voltage in the form of a step waveform and overlapping frequency was set to 5 V. The TS treatment device was manufactured to accurately simulate the Shenmen, Point Zero, liver, and eye points, according to the shape of the ear (Fig. 2A, 2B).

Images of ear treatment points and device. (A) Schematic diagram of auricular transcutaneous electrical treatment points. (B) Transcutaneous electrical stimulation device.

The external appearance of the device was exactly the same shape between TS treatment group and sham group. The TS treatment group device was emitting the 5 V low-frequency wave, but the sham device not emitting the any low-frequency wave. The operating time of the device was also exactly the same. There was no difference between the two groups in terms of device shape and programs.

Given that this study used a precommercial prototype, all interventions were administered in person by two dedicated research staff during hospital visits, with ophthalmic examinations performed immediately after the procedures at 4 and 8 weeks.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS ver. 24.0 (IBM Corp). A paired t-test was used to compare the pretreatment and posttreatment results. We used the independent t-test to investigate the mean differences between the two groups. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Sample size calculation was conducted using G*power ver. 3.1.

Results

A total of 50 participants met the inclusion criteria; however, 1 participant did not complete the study, resulting in a final sample of 49 patients. The treatment and sham groups were each initially assigned 25 participants. The study population comprised 42 women and 8 men, with an overall mean age of 39.7 ± 13.4 years. The mean ages of the sham and treatment groups were 40.9 ± 12.6 and 38.8 ± 15.1 years, respectively. Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, best-corrected visual acuity, intraocular pressure, OSDI score, NEI score, TBUT, and tear secretion volume, showed no significant differences between the two groups (p = 0.413, p = 0.256, p = 0.106, p = 0.528, p = 0.826, p = 0.528, p = 0.938, and p = 0.464, respectively) (Table 1).

Assessments were conducted at baseline, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks after treatment. The primary outcome (OSDI score) and secondary outcomes (NEI score, TBUT, and tear secretion volume) were analyzed. At the 8-week mark, the sham group exhibited minor improvements in NEI score, TBUT, and tear secretion volume relative to baseline. Conversely, the TS treatment group demonstrated significant improvements across all evaluated parameters. Notably, the OSDI score in the TS group showed a statistically significant improvement compared to the sham group after 8 weeks.

The OSDI score significantly decreased in the TS group compared to baseline. In the sham group, the OSDI scores at baseline, 4 weeks, and 8 weeks were 36.99 ± 14.77, 30.27 ± 10.07, and 31.94 ± 11.73, respectively. In the TS group, the OSDI score was 38.22 ± 16.34 at baseline, 23.49 ± 14.52 at 4 weeks, and 19.78 ± 17.96 at 8 weeks. A significant difference in OSDI scores was observed between the groups at 8 weeks (p = 0.018). Within the TS group, a statistically significant reduction was noted between the 4- and 8-week OSDI scores (p = 0.028) (Fig. 3).

The NEI corneal staining score significantly decreased in both groups following treatment. In the sham group, the NEI score was 6.1 ± 1.7 at baseline, 4.9 ± 1.7 at 4 weeks, and 4.8 ± 2.7 at 8 weeks. In contrast, the TS group showed a more pronounced reduction, with scores of 5.7 ± 2.6 at baseline, 3.3 ± 1.6 at 4 weeks, and 2.5 ± 2.0 at 8 weeks. At both 4 and 8 weeks, the TS group demonstrated a significantly lower NEI score compared to the sham group (p = 0.002 and p = 0.001, respectively). TBUT significantly improved in the TS group after treatment. The sham group exhibited a slight increase in TBUT from 4.00 ± 1.13 seconds at baseline to 4.33 ± 1.36 seconds at 4 weeks and 4.60 ± 1.65 seconds at 8 weeks. In contrast, the TS group showed a more substantial increase, with TBUT rising from 3.97 ± 1.23 seconds at baseline to 5.73 ± 1.33 seconds at 4 weeks and 6.62 ± 1.86 seconds at 8 weeks. Regarding to safety and adverse events, no serious adverse events were reported in either the TS treatment or sham groups. All participants completed the study without experiencing adverse effects that required discontinuation of treatment. The absence of safety concerns supports the tolerability of the TS treatment.

Discussion

Recently, artificial tears and other treatment methods have been widely used for the management of DED. However, the effectiveness of these treatments varies among individuals, and some therapies have been associated with side effects. For instance, artificial tear therapy may cause blurred vision, sticky eyelids, and a heavy sensation in the eyes. Additionally, chronic DED patients often experience reduced tear production and corneal sensitivity. A decrease in tear secretion can lead to an increased concentration of inflammatory cytokines in the tear film, which further exacerbates corneal inflammation [15].

Despite the availability of conventional treatments, many patients still experience inadequate symptom relief or side effects, leading to a growing demand for alternative therapies. Studies have suggested that electrical stimulation therapy can alleviate ocular pain, including corneal neuropathic pain [16,17]. Additionally, intranasal neurostimulation has been shown to increase tear production and alleviate DED symptoms [18–20]. However, the effectiveness of neurostimulation and TS therapy may vary based on factors such as stimulation method, application site, and treatment duration. Furthermore, treatments requiring frequent hospital visits can be inconvenient for patients. To address these concerns, transcutaneous stimulation devices have been developed, allowing TS therapy to be administered conveniently through the skin.

In this study, we developed a TS treatment device specifically designed for application to the auricular region, ensuring ease of use for patients. The vagus nerve, a major component of the parasympathetic nervous system, has afferent auricular branches originating from the cervical level of the jugular ganglion, which innervate the auricular area [21]. The parasympathetic nervous system regulates various secretory functions, including those of the lacrimal, salivary, and mucous glands. Electrical TS therapy applied to the auricular region is thought to activate the parasympathetic nervous system, thereby stimulating lacrimal gland function. Previous studies have investigated vagus nerve stimulation for neurological disorders such as depression, chronic headaches, and chronic pain syndromes [22,23]. However, no prior research has specifically analyzed the effects of TS therapy on DED. This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of auricular TS therapy for the treatment of DED.

Studies evaluating the effects of electrical stimulation on DED have employed varying treatment durations. A previous 4-week study reported moderate improvements in TBUT and subjective symptom scores; however, there were no significant changes in tear secretion volume. In contrast, 8-week studies, including this one, demonstrated not only improvements in subjective symptoms but also significant enhancements in TBUT, NEI score, and tear secretion volume. This suggests that a longer treatment duration may be necessary to achieve optimal clinical benefits such as major lacrimal gland and accesary gland. We found that neurostimulation might trigger lacrimal gland tear production and offer a new treatment strategy for DED [24]. Another study showed that TS treatment was performed in 27 patients with DED symptoms for 2 months, and the patient’s subjective and objective factors improved [25]. Likewise, our study showed a significant improvement after an 8-week treatment period. Further research is needed to assess the long-term effectiveness of TS therapy beyond 8 weeks.

No adverse events were reported among participants receiving TS treatment in this study. While TS therapy is generally well tolerated, previous studies have documented minor side effects, such as itching, burning sensations, skin rashes, allergic reactions at the electrode site, and, in rare cases, minor skin burns [26].

To enhance treatment accuracy, we selected four auricular acupuncture points based on traditional medicine for ocular disease management: Shenmen (TF4), Point Zero (H1), liver (CO12), and Master Sensorial (eye, L5) [8,27]. The TS device was designed to ensure accurate attachment to these target points. However, anatomical variations in the size and shape of patients’ ears may influence the effectiveness of TS therapy. To minimize individual variability, we developed and applied three different TS devices tailored to accommodate diverse ear structures.

This study has several limitations. First, the treatment period was relatively short (8 weeks), and the long-term effects of TS therapy remain unclear. Future studies should include long-term follow-ups to evaluate the sustainability of treatment benefits. Second, this study was conducted at a single institution with a relatively small sample size, and different subtypes of DED were not classified. Future large-scale, multicenter studies should stratify evaporative and aqueous-deficient DED subtypes for more detailed analyses. Third, from a statistical perspective, analyzing data from only one eye (e.g., the right eye) provides the advantage of maintaining the independence assumption, as each subject contributes only a single observation to the analysis. This approach allows for the use of the simplest statistical models. However, it results in the loss of information from the other eye and cannot account for potential differences in disease severity or function between eyes. Additionally, further research is needed to explore the precise mechanisms by which TS therapy influences ocular physiology and the autonomic nervous system.

The study did not compare clinical outcomes according to DED subtypes, and future research should explore whether neuromodulatory interventions elicit different responses in evaporative versus aqueous-deficient DED. In addition, objective data from biochemical biomarkers such as tear osmolarity and inflammatory cytokines were not collected, which would have strengthened the mechanistic interpretation of the results. Furthermore, in related interventional studies, statistical methods and adherence assessments should be designed to be both objective and quantitative to improve the reliability of the findings. Addressing these limitations in future research will allow for more rigorous and objective analyses, thereby enhancing the translational value of this line of investigation.

The results of this study demonstrated significant improvements in OSDI score, NEI score, TBUT, and tear secretion volume after 4 and 8 weeks of TS therapy. Previous research on traditional DED treatments has shown that OSDI scores tend to improve within 4 weeks, while TBUT, corneal staining, and Schirmer test results exhibit more substantial improvements after 8 weeks. In this study, OSDI scores did not show a statistically significant difference at 4 weeks but exhibited a significant improvement at 8 weeks following TS treatment. Prior studies have suggested that OSDI scores may be influenced by factors such as age and meibum quality [28]. Further studies incorporating age-matched controls and analyses of tear film lipid, aqueous, and mucin components are warranted. Despite these considerations, the overall therapeutic effects observed in this study were comparable to those achieved with artificial tears commonly used for DED management. Although the primary outcomes showed statistically significant improvements, the absolute magnitude of change was limited. Considering the minimal clinically important difference reported in previous dry eye trials, these results should be interpreted with caution from a clinical perspective. Further research with larger and more diverse populations is essential. Given the subjective nature of symptom-based measures such as the OSDI, future studies should also incorporate long-term follow-up and broader participant inclusion to control for placebo effects or placebo anticipation. In this study, subjective indicators such as the OSDI were set as the primary evaluation variable, which may be considered a limitation compared with previous randomized controlled trials that have typically used objective signs as the main outcome. However, given the inherent sign (symptom mismatch in DED, the design of this study), focusing primarily on improving patients’ quality of life - carries important clinical significance.

Auricular TS therapy alleviated DED symptoms, potentially due to its anti-inflammatory effects mediated by vagal nerve stimulation, which helps reduce ocular discomfort and pain [29]. Studies on patients with Sjögren syndrome have reported that auricular stimulation can modulate inflammatory pathways and improve clinical symptoms [30]. Low-frequency TS therapy is thought to reduce inflammation by regulating the vagus nerve, leading to downregulation of proinflammatory pathways on the ocular surface after treatment. The clinical efficacy of low-frequency TS therapy supports its potential role as a noninvasive adjunct therapy for patients who do not respond adequately to conventional DED treatments such as artificial tears and punctal occlusion. TS treatment is a noninvasive electrical stimulation of specific regions of the ear. This activates central autonomic reflex arcs, enhancing parasympathetic activity and triggering lacrimal gland stimulation as well as the cholinergic anti-inflammatory reflex, which suppresses ocular surface inflammation. These mechanisms ultimately promote the restoration of tear film stability. In contrast, the US Food and Drug Administration–approved intranasal neurostimulation device elicits rapid tear secretion via activation of the trigeminal pathway, indicating a mechanistic difference between the two approaches.

Future studies should focus on optimizing TS therapy parameters, including stimulation intensity, frequency, and duration. Additionally, long-term studies should assess whether TS therapy provides sustained benefits over time. Research exploring the combination of TS therapy with anti-inflammatory agents or neuromodulation-based treatments may further enhance therapeutic outcomes. The development of personalized TS protocols tailored to individual patient characteristics could improve the clinical applicability and effectiveness of TS therapy.

In conclusion, 8 weeks of transcutaneous ear stimulation therapy resulted in significant improvements in objective clinical markers and subjective DED symptoms compared to the sham group. The TS treatment group showed statistically significant enhancements in TBUT, tear secretion volume, NEI score, and OSDI score. As a low-risk and noninvasive treatment, TS therapy represents a promising approach for alleviating DED symptoms and improving patient quality of life.

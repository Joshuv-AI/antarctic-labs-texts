Hybrid drug-coated balloon strategy for coronary bifurcation lesions: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Daimy M M Dillen, Konrad A J van Beek, Amy J E Vermeer, Pieter-Jan Vlaar, Mohamed El Farissi, Jesse P A Demandt, Rob Eerdekens, Marcel van het Veer, Pim A L Tonino, Koen Teeuwen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesRecently, drug-coated balloons (DCB) have emerged as a promising alternative for side branch treatment in coronary bifurcation lesions, in combination with drug-eluting stent (DES) implantation in the main vessel, the hybrid DCB approach. We aimed to evaluate clinical outcomes in patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach and to compare this strategy with other bifurcation techniques.DesignA systematic review and meta-analysis.Data sourcesMEDLINE and EMBASE were searched up until January 2025.Eligibility criteriaWe included randomised controlled trials and observational studies investigating clinical outcomes in patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach for coronary bifurcation lesions.Data extraction and synthesisTwo independent reviewers extracted data and assessed the risk of bias. The outcome of interest was the combined endpoint of cardiac death, myocardial infarction (MI) or target lesion revascularisation (TLR) at the longest available follow-up. Crude event rates, stratified by treatment strategy, were provided for the overall incidence of the primary endpoint. Furthermore, we conducted meta-analyses on the combined endpoint, comparing different bifurcation percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) strategies. The certainty of evidence was assessed using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation tool.Results13 studies, consisting of 2393 patients, were included in our systematic review. Investigated techniques were the hybrid DCB approach in all studies, and a two-stent strategy or the combination of DES in main vessel and balloon angioplasty (BA) in the side branch as potential comparators. Median follow-up duration was 12 months (IQR 7.5–12). The combined endpoint of cardiac death, MI or TLR was found in 5.6% in the hybrid DCB group, 15.4% in the two-stent group and in 10.0% in the BA group. The pooled analyses, including three and two studies, respectively, showed that the hybrid DCB approach was associated with a lower risk of the composite endpoint of cardiac death, MI or TLR when compared with a two-stent strategy (7.9% vs 15.4%; risk ratio (RR): 0.53; 95% CI 0.33 to 0.85) and compared with BA in the side branch (5.6% vs 10.0%; RR: 0.57; 95% CI 0.37 to 0.88). The certainty of the evidence was graded as very low.ConclusionThe results of this systematic review and meta-analysis suggest that the hybrid DCB approach is a feasible and promising treatment approach for bifurcation lesions, when compared with other bifurcation PCI strategies. However, the results should be interpreted cautiously as the certainty of evidence was graded as very low, underlining the importance of larger trials to confirm these findings.Trial registration numberCRD420250651469.

Introduction

Coronary bifurcation lesions represent a complex subset of coronary artery lesions, constituting approximately 15–20% of all percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) procedures.1 2 The technical challenges associated with bifurcation PCI result in higher rates of adverse events, including in-stent restenosis and stent thrombosis, compared with non-bifurcation lesions.3 4 Consequently, the optimal PCI strategy for bifurcation lesions remains a subject of ongoing research.

Despite the extensive data on the treatment of coronary bifurcation lesions, the results comparing provisional strategy and different two-stent strategies are inconsistent.58 Therefore, the optimal strategy remains controversial, prompting the continuous development and description of new techniques and strategies. Recently, there has been growing interest in the use of drug-coated balloons (DCB) in bifurcation PCI. With the philosophy of ‘leaving nothing behind’, DCB application in case of residual disease in the side branch (SB) after provisional PCI with drug-eluting stent (DES) of the main vessel (MV), aligns with the recommendations of the European Bifurcation Club and the current guidelines to perform a stepwise provisional strategy for most bifurcation lesions to simplify the procedure and limit stent use.9 10 However, scientific evidence comparing this ‘hybrid DCB’ approach with current bifurcation techniques, including two-stent strategies or DES in the MV and balloon angioplasty (BA) in the SB, remains limited and is primarily derived from small studies.

Previous systematic reviews and meta-analyses, pooling the data from small studies to compare the hybrid DCB approach with DES in the MV in combination with BA in the SB, showed promising results.1113 However, these reviews included studies using contemporary DES, first-generation DES and bare metal stents (BMS). First-generation DES and BMS are associated with higher adverse event rates when compared with newer-generation DES.14 Including studies using these devices could potentially affect the results and does not correspond to daily practice. A systematic review on the hybrid DCB approach for bifurcation PCI, including exclusively studies using contemporary DES, is currently lacking.

In this systematic review and meta-analysis, we provide an overview of the available evidence regarding clinical and angiographic outcomes in patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach, using contemporary DES, for coronary bifurcation lesions. Furthermore, we compare this approach with other bifurcation PCI strategies, including a two-stent strategy or DES in the MV and BA in the SB, in terms of the risk of clinical adverse events.

Methods

We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis according to a predefined protocol, registered in PROSPERO (CRD420250651469). Our study was reported following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) guidelines.15

Search strategy

A comprehensive systematic search was performed in the electronic databases MEDLINE and EMBASE of all available literature until January 2025. For the search, we used the following algorithm: (coronary bifurcation lesion*[Title/Abstract] OR bifurcation lesion*[Title/Abstract] OR coronary bifurcation*[Title/Abstract] OR left main [Title/Abstract] OR left main lesion [Title/Abstract] OR left main stenosis [Title/Abstract]) AND (drug coated balloon*[Title/Abstract] OR drug eluting balloon*[Title/Abstract] OR DCB[Title/Abstract] OR DEB[Title/Abstract). There was no year of publication filter used. In addition to the data search, we also searched for more eligible articles in the reference lists of the included articles and by consultation of experts in the field. See online supplemental table 1 for a complete overview of the search strategy.

Eligibility criteria

For the systematic review, all randomised controlled trials and observational studies, whether prospective or retrospective, investigating clinical outcomes in patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach for coronary bifurcation lesions were considered eligible. This also included single arm observational studies, as we anticipated that the evidence on this subject would be limited. Case series, case reports, cross-sectional studies, reviews and commentaries were not included. Furthermore, abstracts only or articles not available in English were also excluded from this analysis.

The hybrid DCB approach was defined as PCI with a contemporary DES in the MV and DCB in the SB. Both paclitaxel-coated and sirolimus-coated balloons were eligible. Potential comparative groups consisted of patients treated with a two-stent strategy (two-stent group) or patients treated with DES in the MV and BA in the SB (BA group). We excluded trials using first-generation DES or BMS and studies investigating a DCB only approach. Studies especially investigating the efficacy of specialty balloons (ie, scoring or cutting balloons) prior to DCB application were also excluded. In the case of multiple reports from the same trials, papers with the longest follow-up were included in this review.

Selection of eligible articles

All publications were imported in Rayyan QCRI and duplicates were removed.16 Titles and abstracts for all articles were screened by the primary author (DD) and decisions were checked by a second reviewer (KvB). If the authors identified the article as potentially suitable for inclusion, the full-text article was reviewed in detail by two authors (DD and KvB) and selected if the inclusion criteria were met. Disagreements between reviewers were resolved by the decision of a third independent reviewer (KT).

Data extraction and outcomes

Two independent reviewers (DD and KvB) extracted the data. Extracted data included study information (the first author’s name, publication year), study design, study population, mean age, gender (% male), angiographic bifurcation lesion characteristics, SB treatment and clinical and angiographic endpoints including follow-up period.

The outcome of interest was the combined endpoint of cardiac death, myocardial infarction (MI) or target lesion revascularisation (TLR) at the longest available follow-up. This could be defined as major adverse cardiac events (MACE), target lesion failure (TLF) or device oriented clinical endpoint. Where possible, we standardised the endpoint definitions across studies. Secondary outcomes included the individual components of the combined endpoint, angiographic SB late lumen loss (LLL), measured by quantitative coronary angiography and SB dissections according to the National Heart, Lung and Blood Institute classification, with type A/B dissections defined as minor dissection and ≥type C dissections defined as major dissections.17 The clinical endpoints were defined according to each study protocol.

Risk of bias assessment

Next, the included studies were assessed for bias by two independent reviewers (DD and KvB) using the Cochrane Risk-of-Bias tool (RoB2) for the randomised trials and the Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Form for Cohort Studies for all the non-randomised trials.18 19 Any disagreement on the classification of risk of bias was solved by discussion or by means of a third reviewer (MvhV).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were used to report clinical and angiographic outcomes in patients with a coronary bifurcation lesion treated using the hybrid DCB approach, a two-stent strategy or BA in the SB. Categorical variables are presented as absolute numbers and percentages, and continuous variables are reported as means with SD or medians with IQRs, as appropriate. To estimate the overall incidence of adverse clinical events, we combined the number of events and included patients across studies within each treatment group. This provided crude event rates, stratified by treatment strategy. For the angiographic outcome of LLL, we combined study-level mean LLL values using a weighted average, with each study weighted by its sample size. Studies reporting LLL as median with IQR were excluded from the analysis.

Meta-analysis was only undertaken if two or more studies compared the hybrid DCB strategy to other bifurcation PCI approaches. Meta-analysis was performed using the ‘metafor’ package in RStudio V.4.4.3. Heterogeneity among studies was quantified with the inconsistency test (I2) and tested using the Cochran’s Q test/χ2 test (threshold p>0.10). We considered I2 values between 0% and 30% as low heterogeneity, between 30% and 60% as moderate heterogeneity and 60% or more as considerable heterogeneity. A fixed effect model was applied in case of low heterogeneity, whereas the random effects model was used in case of high heterogeneity. Pooled effect estimates were reported as risk ratios with corresponding 95% CIs. In addition, a prespecified sensitivity analysis was formulated with the aim of determining the effect of the variety in the overall risk of bias for the studies on the primary outcome by performing stratified analyses: one based on all included studies, one based on studies at low risk and one based on studies at high risk of bias. Forest plots were used to visually present individual and pooled study results.

Confidence in cumulative evidence

The overall quality of evidence of the systematic review was assessed by two independent reviewers (DD and KvB) using the Grading of Recommendations Assessment Development and Evaluation (GRADE) tool. The GRADE framework consists of five domains: (1) risk of bias, (2) inconsistency in results, (3) indirectness of evidence, (4) imprecision and (5) publication bias. At the start of the GRADE assessment, the evidence was assumed to be high quality and was downgraded based on any of the five domains to moderate, low or very low quality of evidence.

Patient and public involvement

No patients were directly involved in the development of the research question, study design, selection of the outcome measures or interpretation of the results.

Results

Our literature search yielded a total of 774 publications from MEDLINE database (n=161), EMBASE database (n=613) and Cochrane Library (n=0). After removal of duplicates, 560 publications remained for screening of title and abstract. Of these, 497 were excluded based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Of the remaining 63 articles, the full text was retrieved and assessed for eligibility. Among the included studies, four were randomised controlled trials (RCTs), five were prospective and four were retrospective cohort studies. Six studies were single-arm cohort studies investigating the hybrid DCB approach. Six studies compared the hybrid DCB approach to a control group of either two-stent strategy (three studies) or DES in the MV and BA in the SB (three studies). These six studies were included in the meta-analyses. One study compared a sirolimus-coated balloon with a paclitaxel-coated balloon in the SB.

In all studies, the MV was treated with contemporary DES and in the patients treated using the hybrid DCB approach, the SB was treated with a paclitaxel-coated DCB (92.1%) or sirolimus-coated DCB (7.9%).

The studies comprised a total of 2393 patients and 2351 patients (98.2%) had a true bifurcation lesion (defined as Medina 1,1,1; 1,0,1; 0,1,1). Left main (LM) bifurcation lesions were present in 783 patients (32.7%). Of all included patients, 1444 (60.3%) received the hybrid DCB approach, 292 patients (12.2%) were treated with a two-stent strategy and 619 patients (25.9%) with BA in the SB. The distribution of the two-stent techniques (eg, culotte, Double kissing (DK)-crush or T or T and small protrusion (TAP) stenting) employed among patients in the two-stent group was not adequately described. Median follow-up duration was 12.0 (IQR 7.5–12.0) months.

Risk of bias

The risk of bias varied across included studies (online supplemental tables 3 and 4). Four studies exhibited a low risk of bias. The other nine studies demonstrated a moderate to high risk of bias. The domains ‘selection of the reported result’ and ‘comparability’ of the (RoB2) and the Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Form for Cohort Studies, respectively, posed significant risk of bias. The ‘outcomes’ domain was less frequently identified as a concern across both tools.

Meta-analyses

The analyses indicated no significant heterogeneity among the included studies, with an I2 value of 0% and a non-significant Cochran’s Q test (p>0.10).

The meta-analysis comparing the hybrid DCB approach with the two-stent strategy included three studies, consisting of 277 patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach and 292 patients treated with the two-stent strategy. At the longest available follow-up, the hybrid DCB approach was associated with a significantly lower risk of the composite endpoint of cardiac death, MI or TLR, when compared with a two-stent strategy (7.9% vs 15.4%; risk ratio (RR): 0.53; 95% CI 0.33 to 0.85) (figure 2). This was mainly driven by a difference in TLR (4.3% vs 11.3%; RR: 0.40; 95% CI 0.21 to 0.74). Cardiac death and MI were not different between both groups (2.5% vs 3.4%; RR: 0.77; 95% CI 0.31 to 1.88, and 1.8% vs 3.8%; RR: 0.53; 95% CI 0.20 to 1.40, respectively).

Forest plot for risk of the composite endpoint of cardiac death, MI or TLR after treatment with hybrid DCB versus two-stent strategy. DCB, drug-coated balloon; DES, drug-eluting stent; MI, myocardial infarction; RR, risk ratio; TLR, target lesion revascularisation.

The comparison between the hybrid DCB approach and BA in the SB for the composite endpoint included two studies, consisting of 504 patients in the hybrid DCB group and 502 patients in the BA group. The risk of the composite endpoint of cardiac death, MI or TLR was significantly lower in patients treated with DCB in the SB, compared with those treated with BA in the SB (5.6% vs 10.0%; RR: 0.57; 95% CI 0.37 to 0.88) at the longest available follow-up (figure 3). Target lesion revascularisation was not different between groups (1.0% vs 1.2%; RR: 0.85; 95% CI 0.27 to 2.62, respectively). The meta-analysis on the individual endpoints of cardiac death and MI included three studies, comprising 606 patients in the hybrid DCB group and 619 patients in the BA group. There was no difference in the risk of cardiac death between both groups (0.8% vs 0.5%; RR: 1.61; 95% CI 0.42 to 6.22). The risk of MI was lower in patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach (4.1% vs 8.2%; RR: 0.51; 95% CI 0.32 to 0.80). The forest plots of the pooled analyses from the individual endpoints are included in the online supplemental materials.

Due to the limited number of studies included in the analysis, it was not feasible to perform a stratified sensitivity analysis based on risk of bias.

Angiographic outcomes

Six studies, comprising a total of 716 patients, with 461 in the hybrid DCB group, 30 in the two-stent group and 225 patients in the BA group, performed angiographic follow-up measuring the mean LLL. The mean angiographic follow-up duration was 8.3±2.3 months.

At the longest available follow-up, mean LLL was −0.04±0.32 mm in the hybrid DCB group, 0.36±0.35 mm in the two-stent group and 0.19±0.30 mm in the BA group. Angiographic outcomes per included study are shown in online supplemental table 6.

Side branch dissections

A total of seven articles, comprising 1001 patients, reported the occurrence of dissection of the SB for patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach. 102 SB dissections (10.2%) were reported in the hybrid DCB patients. Among these, 11 (10.8%) were described as major dissections (>type C) requiring bailout stenting and 24 (23.5%) were defined as type A or B dissections. The remaining 67 dissections (65.7%) were unspecified, of which 9 required additional stenting in the SB. The need for bailout stenting was documented in eight articles, involving 1051 patients. 24 patients (2.3%) required bailout stenting after predilation or DCB application in the SB. Reasons for bailout stenting were <(Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) III flow, SB occlusion, (major) dissection or >50% diameter stenosis in the SB. An overview of the number of reported SB dissections and bailout stenting including reasons is reported in online supplemental tables 7 and 8.

In patients treated with BA in the SB, dissections and the need for bailout stenting were reported in two articles, consisting of 502 patients. SB dissections occurred in 27 patients (5.4%) treated with BA in the SB, consisting of 16 type A or B dissections (59.3%) and 11 type C dissections (40.7%). 13 patients (2.9%) required bailout stenting for one of the following reasons: <TIMI III flow, SB occlusion or major dissection (online supplemental tables 7 and 8).

Procedural dissections were not reported among patients treated with a two-stent strategy.

Confidence in cumulative evidence

Based on the five elements of the GRADE tool, there was: (1) A significant risk of bias based on the risk of bias assessment (online supplemental table 3 and 4). (2) There was no large inconsistency in results: in the pooled analyses, there is overlap of the 95% CIs for the combined and individual endpoints and the test for between study heterogeneity showed an I2 value of 0% and a non-significant Cochran’s Q test (p>0.10) (figures2 3, online supplemental figures 1–6). (3) There was indirectness of evidence due to the differences in endpoint definitions, potential differences in interventions (different types of DCB, different two-stent strategies) and differences in patient population (including or excluding LM lesions, different Medina classifications). (4) There was a certain amount of imprecision, as most trials were powered on angiographic endpoints and not to detect differences in clinical outcomes. This was confirmed by the broad 95% CI of the combined and individual endpoints in the pooled analyses (figures2 3, online supplemental figures 1–6). (5) Publication bias was not formally assessed owing to <10 trial comparisons from the meta-analyses. Taking the five elements into consideration, the overall confidence in the cumulative evidence is very low due to downgrade for risk of bias, indirectness of evidence and imprecision.

Discussion

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated the clinical and angiographic outcomes of patients with coronary bifurcation lesions treated with a hybrid DCB approach combining contemporary DES in the MV with DCB in the SB. This strategy was compared with two other bifurcation PCI strategies; two-stent strategy and the combination of DES in the MV and BA in the SB. The main findings of this systematic review and meta-analysis were as follows. First, PCI of coronary bifurcation lesions using a hybrid DCB approach seems to be a feasible and promising treatment strategy, with favourable clinical and angiographic outcomes at median and mean follow-up durations of 12 and 8 months, respectively. Second, pooled data showed that the hybrid DCB approach was associated with a significantly lower risk of the composite endpoint of cardiac death, MI or TLR, compared with two-stent strategies or DES in the MV and BA in the SB.

The results of our review are encouraging and suggest that the hybrid DCB approach may offer a viable and potentially superior alternative to current bifurcation PCI strategies, although the available evidence regarding the hybrid DCB remains limited and is primarily derived from small studies with a wide variation in methodological quality. Head-to-head comparative data of the hybrid DCB approach to other bifurcation PCI strategies are sparse. The DCB-BIF trial by Gao et al1 was the first and only large multicentre RCT comparing clinical outcomes in patients with true bifurcation lesions treated with DCB or BA in the SB after DES implantation in the MV.20 They reported a significantly lower rate of MACE in the DCB group compared with the BA group (7.2% vs 12.5%; HR: 0.56, 95% CI 0.35 to 0.88; p=0.013), mainly driven by a higher incidence of target vessel MI (TV-MI), without a difference in TLR. Therefore, the higher rate of TV-MI in the BA group is of unclear clinical relevance. Our meta-analysis showed similar results, predominantly driven by the results of the DCB-BIF trial, as this trial accounted for 98% of weight in the pooled analysis for the combined endpoint. This significant skew in weighting must be taken into account when interpreting the results of our meta-analyses.

To date, no large RCTs have reported direct comparisons between the hybrid DCB approach and two-stent strategy, in terms of clinical endpoints. Therefore, our systematic review relied on smaller RCTs and cohort studies. The advantage of the hybrid DCB approach compared with two-stent strategies lies in minimising stent burden and simplifying the procedure, which aligns with the recommendations of the European Bifurcation Club.9 The use of DES is associated with stent-related adverse events, including stent thrombosis and in-stent restenosis, leading to TLR. Stent-related adverse events occur at a rate of 2% per year with no plateau evident.21 Therefore, by using DCB instead of DES in the SB, TLR could be reduced. This systematic review showed that the hybrid DCB approach was associated with 2.8% TLR, whereas this accounted for 11.3% in the two-stent group. In the pooled analysis, including three studies, the hybrid DCB approach was associated with a lower rate of TLR, when compared with two-stent strategy. These results align with our hypothesis. However, it is important to note that the data was primarily derived from observational studies and 84% of the weight originated from one observational study by Pan et al22 This should be accounted for when interpreting these results.

Different lesion characteristics among studies included in our review led to a variation in study population. These differences were observed in the inclusion or exclusion of LM bifurcation lesions and different Medina classifications. The majority of lesions in the pooled comparison between the hybrid DCB and two-stent strategies involved LM bifurcations (93.5%).2224 Left main bifurcations are generally more complex and associated with higher clinical risk, which may explain the relatively high event rates observed in our two-stent group. A similar trend was observed in the hybrid DCB group: the combined event rates in the pooled analysis comparing DCB with DES—including mainly LM-bifurcations—were higher (7.9%) than the combined event rates observed in the pooled analysis comparing DCB with BA—including mainly non-LM bifurcations (5.6%). The predominance of LM bifurcations in the pooled analysis comparing the hybrid DCB approach with two-stent strategy might limit the generalisation of these results to all types of bifurcation lesions. Several large ongoing RCTs are expected to provide more definitive evidence comparing the hybrid DCB strategy to two-stent strategies for different bifurcation lesions in the near future.25 26

In our review, LLL was lowest in patients treated with the hybrid DCB approach. However, the mean LLL of the two-stent group was only based on one study and a pooled analysis was not performed due to lack of angiographic follow-up in the studies directly comparing hybrid DCB to other strategies. The low LLL after the hybrid DCB strategy is likely reflecting positive vascular remodelling, whereas DES are associated with maladaptive responses such as neointimal hyperplasia and restenosis.27 Positive vascular remodelling and lumen enlargement after DCB application are partially dependent on the occurrence of dissections. A study by Yamamoto et al investigated that extensive medial dissection after DCB application, together with lesion characteristics, was associated with higher late lumen enlargement.27 In our review, dissections of the SB were described in 10.2% of patients in the hybrid DCB group, including mostly type A/B dissections. Only 14.7% of these dissections required bailout stenting. In the BA group, the dissection rate was only 5.3%, of which 40.7% required bailout stenting. The relatively high incidence of dissections in the hybrid DCB arm may have contributed to the favourable angiographic and possibly clinical outcomes.

In addition to lesion characteristics and procedural details, it is important to acknowledge that differences in biological effects across DCB types, due to differences in antiproliferative drugs, release kinetics and coating technologies, may influence outcomes. The majority of DCBs used in the studies included in our review were paclitaxel-coated balloons (92.1%). Paclitaxel’s high lipophilicity allows rapid drug transfer with short exposure, whereas sirolimus, which has lower lipophilicity, poses formulation and delivery challenges but offers sustained antiproliferative effects due to superior biocompatibility and longer tissue retention.28 Clinical data on sirolimus-coated balloons remain limited. However, the recently published SPACIOUS trial by Zhou et al, also included in this review, showed non-inferiority of sirolimus-coated balloons compared with paclitaxel-coated balloons in SB of bifurcation lesions in combination with DES in the MV, in terms of diameter stenosis at 9 months (30.5±16.1% vs 33.5±16.2%, p<0.01), with no significant differences in 1-year clinical outcomes.29 These findings suggest that both types of DCBs are suitable for SB treatment in bifurcation PCI, although more head-to-head studies are needed.

Study limitations

This review has several limitations. First, our search was limited to MEDLINE and EMBASE and we did not search for grey literature, which may have increased publication bias. Second, we conducted a study-level systematic review and meta-analysis. Clinical outcomes were based on endpoint definitions provided by each individual study, leading to heterogeneity. Five studies used TV-MI instead of MI and six studies used clinically driven-TLR or ischaemia driven-TLR instead of TLR as components of the combined endpoint.13 15 17 20 29 30 Despite these minor differences, we included all endpoint definitions in our review. Third, the outcome of interest was extracted at longest available follow-up, which may limit the interpretability of the pooled estimates compared with those derived from a specific time point. However, this approach was necessary due to the substantial variation in follow-up durations across the studies, ranging from 6 to 24 months. To ensure inclusion of a sufficient number of studies, we opted to extract data at the longest available follow-up. However, a significant number of studies had a short follow-up duration of 6–9 months, which may have led to underestimation of the true incidence of adverse events. Fourth, the studies included in our review were of limited sample size and variable quality. Although statistical heterogeneity across pooled analyses was low (I²=0%), methodological heterogeneity was evident and the methodological quality was generally moderate to low. Only four studies were assessed as having a low risk of bias, whereas as many as six studies were assessed as a high risk of bias. Fifth, the meta-analyses only included three studies, with different study designs (ie, both RCT and observational studies) and an extremely uneven weighting, with one study providing the majority of the results. This should be taken into account when interpreting the results of the meta-analyses. Finally, the certainty of evidence, assessed with the GRADE tool, was assessed as very low due to risk of bias, indirectness of evidence and imprecision. Consequently, despite the results suggesting that the hybrid DCB approach may be associated with favourable outcomes, these findings should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis suggests that the hybrid DCB approach, combining DES implantation in the MV and DCB treatment of the SB, is a feasible and promising PCI strategy for coronary bifurcation lesions. Compared with two-stent strategies and DES in the MV in combination with BA in the SB, this approach was associated with a lower risk of the combined endpoint of cardiac death, MI or TLR at median 12 months follow-up. However, these results should be interpreted cautiously as the evidence remains limited and is primarily derived from studies with uneven weighting and varying methodological quality, leading to a very low certainty of evidence. This review does underline the importance of large, adequately powered RCTs to confirm these findings and to establish the role of the hybrid DCB approach in bifurcation PCI.

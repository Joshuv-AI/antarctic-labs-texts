Safety, tolerability and biomarker results of bepranemab in participants with progressive supranuclear palsy: a randomised, multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 1b trial
Authors: Günter Höglinger, Wim Vandenberghe, Dirk Woitalla, Jean-Christophe Corvol, Hans Van Tricht, Colin Ewen, Bart Van Den Steen, Irene Rebollo Mesa, Massimiliano Germani, Elodie Garric, Tasmin Arnould, Joby Jose, Nancy Strong, Steven De Bruyn, Tim J Buchanan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPreclinical evidence suggests targeting the mid-region of tau as a viable therapeutic strategy in diseases such as progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP): a rare, fatal, neurodegenerative tauopathy with no currently approved treatments. Bepranemab is a recombinant, humanised, full-length immunoglobulin G4 monoclonal antibody binding to a mid-region tau epitope. We assessed safety, tolerability and pharmacokinetics of bepranemab in participants with PSP.MethodsPSP003 (NCT04185415), a multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 1b study, recruited participants in hospital settings across 13 centres. Participants (aged ≥40 years) met Movement Disorder Society-PSP criteria for possible/probable PSP, could walk ≥5 steps with minimal/no assistance and were stable on treatment for ≥2 weeks prior to baseline. Participants were randomised 3:1 to receive intravenous bepranemab (90 mg/kg) or placebo every 4 weeks for 52 weeks. Primary endpoint: incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) from baseline to last visit.ResultsTwenty-five participants were enrolled (male: 44%; bepranemab n=18, placebo n=7). Seventeen (94.4%) in the bepranemab group reported ≥1 TEAE (five participants; ten investigational medicinal product (IMP)-related TEAEs), versus placebo (n=7; 100%). In the bepranemab and placebo groups, respectively, three participants (16.7%) and one participant (14.3%) discontinued due to TEAEs. Incidence of IMP-related TEAEs and severe TEAEs was similar between groups; no deaths were reported. Reduction (80.41%) in mean free tau cerebrospinal fluid levels was observed in the bepranemab group.ConclusionsMultiple doses of bepranemab 90 mg/kg were well tolerated with an acceptable safety profile in participants with PSP. High target occupancy was observed.

Introduction

Tauopathies such as progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP) are characterised by the misfolding and aggregation of pathological tau, which spreads through the brain and is believed to drive progressive neurodegeneration.14 Under physiological conditions, tau—a microtubule-associated protein—is expressed in the central nervous system and plays a role in axonal nerve cell growth and maintenance.1 5 Tau is encoded by the MAPT gene and comprises four domains: an acidic N-terminus; a central proline-rich region; a microtubule-binding region (MTBR), composed of up to four repeat domains (R1–R4), and a C-terminus.6 7 There are six isoforms in the human adult brain: three 4R isoforms—2N4R, 1N4R and 0N4R; and three 3R isoforms—2N3R, 1N3R and 0N3R.1 Proteolytic fragments of tau may contribute to the development of PSP (4R tauopathy),6 as well as Alzheimer’s disease (AD; mixed 3R/4R tauopathy).135 8

Pathological tau is a target for potential disease-modifying therapies (DMTs)9—treatments that interfere with the underlying disease pathology with the ultimate aim of slowing or halting disease progression.10 There is a high unmet need for DMTs in diseases such as PSP, a relentlessly progressive neurodegenerative disorder, where the average life expectancy for patients after symptom onset is 7 years,1012 and for which there are currently no approved therapies.10

Previous studies using tau-targeting immunotherapies have assessed the safety and efficacy of monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) directed against the N-terminal region of human tau .13 14 In each phase 2, 52-week study, N-terminal antibodies (gosuranemab and tilavonemab) failed to demonstrate clinical efficacy to slow disease progression in participants with PSP versus placebo.13 14 N-terminal antibodies have also failed to demonstrate clinical efficacy in participants with AD.15

Human tau undergoes extensive post-translational modification, including folding and truncation.1 Increasing evidence shows that the centrally located MTBR of tau is critical for tau aggregation; studies assessing postmortem AD brains showed that tau is truncated at both its N- and C-termini, influencing the way in which tau folds and therefore the ability of the remaining MTBR to form aggregates.7 16 17 Mass spectrometry analyses of tau fragments in human cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) from people with Alzheimer’s found that mid-region tau fragments represented the most abundant fragments in CSF.18 19 Additionally, results from tau protein quantification in CSF showed that peptides from the tau mid-region appeared to be significantly more abundant than peptides from N- and C-terminal domains.2022 These data provide evidence that therapies targeting this region of tau warrant further investigation.

Bepranemab is a recombinant, humanised, full-length, immunoglobulin (Ig)G4P mAb targeting the mid-region of human tau23 24—within the central proline-rich region, adjacent to the MTBR.6 7 Bepranemab was selected based on a large-scale unbiased screen in a cell-based assay, to identify the antibody with the highest activity against human tau seeds from the brains of people with Alzheimer’s,24 and was subsequently tested using in vivo mouse models of tauopathy.23 In this work, bepranemab was found to bind to the mid-region of tau (amino acids 235–250), potently blocked the seeding of human AD tau, demonstrated full efficacy against seeds from PSP with high affinity and stopped the spread of tau in a transgenic mouse model.23 24 These results provide rationale for the possible superior efficacy of mid-region binding therapies compared with N-terminal binding tau antibodies.

Bepranemab has been studied in two phase 1 healthy volunteer studies (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT03464227 and NCT03605082). This current study (PSP003, ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT04185415) was conducted to evaluate safety and tolerability of bepranemab in participants with PSP. In addition, we assessed pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) in terms of target occupancy (TO) in CSF in the participants and compared these results with those of healthy participants (ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT03464227). If observed PK/PD data were consistent with model predictions, based on a previously designed physiology-based-PK/PD (PB-PK/PD) model, this would confirm the assumption that PK in both plasma and CSF and TO in CSF are similar between healthy volunteers and participants with PSP (following dosing with bepranemab (90 mg/kg) every 4 weeks (Q4W)).

Methods

Study design

This randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 1b study was designed to assess safety and tolerability of bepranemab in 24 participants with PSP (NCT04185415). The study was conducted in hospital settings across 13 centres in Belgium, Spain, Germany and the UK.

Participants

Eligible participants had possible or probable PSP-Richardson syndrome according to the Movement Disorder Society-PSP criteria,11 were ≥40 years of age at the time of consent, were able to walk ≥5 steps with minimal/no assistance (stabilisation of one arm or use of cane/walker) and were stable on all treatments for at least 2 weeks prior to baseline. Eligible participants were required to have reliable caregiver support during the entire study period or be able to independently follow the study protocol.

Participants were excluded from the study if they: had a Mini-Mental State Examination score of ≤20 at screening;25 had ongoing, recurrent, severe headaches, including migraines, or were institutionalised in a nursing or dementia care facility or were planned to be institutionalised during the study. For further exclusion criteria, refer to the online supplemental material.

Randomisation and masking

Participants were randomised in a ratio of approximately 3:1 (bepranemab:placebo). The first two participants were designated as sentinel participants and were randomised to receive either bepranemab or placebo (online supplemental figure 1). Investigators enrolled participants by contacting the Interactive Response Technology (IRT) vendor, who produced the randomisation schedule and was involved for the rest of the study. Investigators, participants and those assessing outcomes and safety assessments were masked to study treatment allocation, block size and the study treatment infusion solution.

Eligible participants were randomised to treatment regimens by IRT. Prepared bepranemab and placebo were not visually identical. To ensure blinding was maintained, masking was achieved using black bags and tubing covers to conceal the infusion bags and tubes prior to leaving the preparation area. Additionally, saline solution was added where necessary, to ensure the same infusion volume per body weight for placebo as for active drug. The study team successfully remained blinded throughout the study; no unblinding issues were reported. For more information on randomisation, refer to the online supplemental material.

Procedures

Each participant had a maximum screening period of 4 weeks followed by a 52-week treatment period, and a safety follow-up (SFU) period for 16 weeks.

Participants received either bepranemab (90 mg/kg) or a matching volume of placebo via intravenous infusion administered Q4W over 48 weeks (13 infusions). At every dosing visit, total dose was adapted to the most recent body weight of the participant. The start of the first infusion in every participant was administered at a low infusion rate (maximum of 0.5 mg/kg/min or equivalent rate for placebo), and if it was tolerated well after 30 min, this could be increased to 0.75 mg/kg/min, and to no more than a maximum of 1 mg/kg/min. The minimum infusion time was 2 hours but did not exceed 4 hours. For all subsequent infusions, study treatment was administered over 2 hours with the possibility to extend the infusion to 4 hours, if needed, for safety reasons. A schedule of follow-up visits and the assessments carried out at each visit are shown in online supplemental table 1.

The dosing regimen was selected based on phase 1 data, according to PB-PK/PD modelling26 27 and a population PK/PD model. For more information on modelling, refer to the online supplemental material.

Participants who completed the treatment period had the option to enter the SFU period or an open-label extension (OLE) study (PSP002; NCT04658199).

Assessments

Blood and CSF samples were collected to measure concentrations of bepranemab, antidrug antibodies (ADAs) and for biomarker analysis (online supplemental table 1). Blood samples for coagulation parameters and platelet counts were taken and the results were evaluated before the lumbar puncture. Blood draws were performed via venipuncture and the sample divided into two aliquots for PK analyses. CSF sampling was carried out at screening and day 7 (with a 14-day window in between); each CSF sample was split into aliquots for PK analyses, biomarker analyses, quality control and biorepository samples (if consent was provided). Postdose PK samples were only collected for the first two infusions; for all subsequent infusions, only trough samples were taken.

The PSP rating scale (PSPRS)28 was used to measure clinical disability and disease severity of participants at specified timepoints (online supplemental table 1). PSPRS was an exploratory endpoint; the study was not powered to determine differences between treatment groups.

For information on brain magnetic resonance images (MRIs), refer to the online supplemental material.

Safety measurements

Safety measurements included the incidence of TEAEs, serious adverse events (AEs) and post-treatment period TEAEs (any AE that started after the date of the end of treatment period visit), changes in physical examination, safety assessment including neurological examination and brain MRI, 12-lead ECG parameters, vital signs and clinical laboratory tests from baseline over time. Refer to online supplemental table 1 for further information.

Statistical analysis

The study was originally planned to evaluate the long-term exposure of bepranemab in at least 12 participants (plus three allocated to placebo), providing a 77% probability of observing at least one occurrence of any AE that would occur with a frequency of 15% in the study population. During the study, recruitment and dosing were halted for approximately 4 months (13 March–22 June 2020) due to challenges with site visits during the COVID-19 pandemic. The dosing hold was not due to safety concerns with bepranemab. As such, the decision to enrol more participants was made due to the reduction in long-term continuous drug exposure during the COVID-19 hold, and the potential for reduced AE reporting during this hold. Ultimately, a final sample size of 24 participants (18 allocated to bepranemab) was established to ensure a minimum of 12 participants exposed to at least three consecutive doses. The safety set was used in the primary safety analyses, and included all participants who received at least a partial dose of IMP. Unless otherwise stated, missing data were not imputed. For all missing data, it was recorded whether they were due to COVID-19 or other circumstances. All statistical analyses in this study were descriptive: for more details, refer to the online supplemental material.

Results

Study demographics

Between 3 December 2019 and 22 October 2020, 35 participants with PSP were screened; ten participants were ineligible and 25 participants were enrolled and randomised (bepranemab 90 mg/kg n=18; placebo n=7; figure 1). Six participants discontinued the study, five (27.8%) in the bepranemab group and one (14.3%) in the placebo group; reasons for discontinuation are shown in figure 1. Nineteen participants entered the OLE study (PSP002), starting at week 52. Twelve participants (nine in the bepranemab group and three in the placebo group) were enrolled before the COVID-19 hold and 13 participants (nine in the bepranemab group, four in the placebo group) were enrolled after the hold. Bepranemab was administered by the investigator, and the mean treatment compliance was >70% across participants (range: 20%–108%). The most common concomitant medications were paracetamol (seven participants (28.0%)), acetylsalicylic acid and benserazide hydrochloride; levodopa (six participants (24.0%) each).

Safety and tolerability of bepranemab in participants with PSP

Results of the primary outcome (incidence of TEAEs from baseline to week 68) are presented in tables 2 and 3. Overall, 17 participants (94.4%) in the bepranemab group and seven participants in the placebo group (100%) reported at least one TEAE. Incidences of TEAEs leading to discontinuation, TEAEs considered as IMP-related and severe TEAEs were similar between treatment groups. Three participants (16.7%) in the bepranemab group and one (14.3%) in the placebo group discontinued treatment due to TEAEs. No deaths were reported during the study. Serious TEAEs were reported by four participants in the bepranemab group (22.2%) and one (14.3%) in the placebo group. In the bepranemab group, four participants reported five serious TEAEs: coronavirus infection and femur fracture (reported by the same participant), arthralgia, syncope and conversion disorder. The serious TEAEs of femur fracture and syncope resulted in IMP withdrawal; no other serious TEAEs led to withdrawal of IMP in this group and none were assessed as related to IMP. In the placebo group, one participant reported serious TEAEs of liver injury and ischaemic stroke, the former being assessed as related to IMP and leading to withdrawal of IMP.

Bepranemab concentrations in CSF were measured at week 1 (7 days after the first administration of bepranemab) using geometric mean and geometric coefficient of variation. Bepranemab concentration in CSF was 1.948 µg/mL (58.33%) and ranged from 0.689 μg/mL to 5.35 μg/mL; the geometric mean (geometric coefficient) of bepranemab serum concentration was 1047 µg/mL (14.18%) and ranged from 818 μg/mL to 1320 μg/mL. Individual bepranemab CSF-to-serum concentration ratios at week 1 (expressed as a percentage) ranged from 0.08% to 0.42%, with a median value of 0.20% (figure 2B). CSF-to-serum ratios were comparable between healthy volunteers in a previous phase 1 study and participants in this study (figure 2B).

Free tau in the CSF

The term ‘free tau’ has been described in the Methods section (further information is available in the online supplemental material).

The percentage change in free tau from baseline to week 1 was measured. In the bepranemab group, the concentrations of free tau in CSF changed as follows (based on data available from n=15 participants in this group): four participants (26.7%) had 25%–100% of baseline free tau, nine participants (60.0%) had 10%–25% of baseline free tau and two participants (13.3%) had 0%–10% of baseline free tau. In the bepranemab group, the mean (SD) percentage change from baseline of free tau in CSF was –80.41% (12.14) compared with –8.42% (19.97) in the placebo group (figure 2C), suggesting high bepranemab TO in CSF. This determination was derived on an individual participant basis and is shown in figure 2C.

Discussion

Bepranemab is a recombinant, humanised, full-length IgG4P mAb targeting the mid-region epitope of human tau within the central proline-rich region, adjacent to the MTBR. Increasing evidence shows that the centrally positioned MTBR is critical for tau aggregation.6 7 16 17 Furthermore, this MTBR is less prone to post-translational cleavage than other areas of the protein, including regions associated with the N-terminus,18 which have failed to translate to clinical efficacy in previous studies in tauopathies.1315 Work by Zhou et al, Cicognola et al and Sato et al has demonstrated that mid-region tau fragments represented the most abundant in the CSF of people with tauopathies (such as AD).171924 It is therefore of critical importance to determine if mid-region epitope antibodies are more effective than their N- and C-terminus-targeting counterparts, to focus resources towards potential treatments aimed at slowing disease progression in people with tauopathies.

This study forms a core component of the bepranemab development programme, designed to provide foundational data on safety and PK of bepranemab. In this study, we assessed the safety, tolerability, PK and PD of bepranemab in participants with PSP. An acceptable safety profile and tolerability for prolonged exposure over 52 weeks to high doses of bepranemab was observed.

The majority of participants in the bepranemab group and all participants in the placebo group reported at least one TEAE. Incidences of discontinuations due to TEAEs, IMP-related TEAEs and severe TEAEs were similar between treatment groups. No deaths were reported during the study. The exposure to high doses of bepranemab (90 mg/kg) was not associated with significant safety or tolerability issues; this is an important finding with relevance for future work as, per the CSF:serum ratio, administration of high intravenous doses may be required to achieve therapeutic antibody levels in the brain.

Bepranemab serum steady state was attained for participants with at least five consecutive doses enrolled after the COVID-19 hold or after dosing was resumed. Maximum bepranemab serum concentration was observed following the second dose (week 4), approximately 2 hours postdose (postdose PK samples were only collected for the first two infusions; for all subsequent infusions only trough samples were taken). The infusion time per protocol was 2–4 hours. Individual participant bepranemab CSF-to-serum concentration ratios at week 1 ranged from 0.08% to 0.42% with a median value of 0.20%. This is similar to the value previously described for gosuranemab,30 and the value observed in the UP0047 study, which assessed the safety of bepranemab in healthy volunteers, as well as other studies investigating tau antibodies.13 14 30 Overall, PK/PD was comparable between healthy volunteers in the UP0047 study and participants with PSP in the current study.

Similar bepranemab serum and CSF concentrations were observed in healthy volunteers and participants with PSP, aligning with the predictions outlined in the model (figure 2A). Furthermore, there was no evidence of disease-induced differences in PK parameters between healthy volunteers and participants with PSP. CSF samples were obtained at day 7 after the first dosing; maximum TO of free tau is expected to be reached before day 7, in accordance with the maximum observed concentration of bepranemab.

Figure 2C shows observed reduction of free tau at approximately day 7 after the first administration of 90 mg/kg bepranemab or placebo. TO was observed, with an approximate 80% reduction of mean free tau in CSF in the bepranemab group compared with the placebo group. This confirms the modelled TO level in the treated group and is consistent with preclinical data showing bepranemab’s ability to bind extracellular tau.23 24 While not assessed in this study, TO in participants with PSP is expected to be maintained throughout the treatment period as was observed for healthy volunteers. The data from these studies show consistent binding of bepranemab to free tau in healthy volunteers and in participants with PSP.

No meaningful differences between the bepranemab- and placebo-treated groups were observed, as the study was not powered to analyse differences in PSPRS scores, brain MRI (percentage atrophy) or serum and CSF NfL levels. Participants in the bepranemab and placebo groups had comparable treatment-emergent immunogenicity. No effect on PK/PD due to treatment-emergent ADAs was observed. Neither treatment group showed evidence of neuroinflammatory events, including microhaemorrhages, superficial siderosis or cerebral oedema. One TEAE of hypersensitivity was reported in the bepranemab group, which was not serious, was moderate in intensity and assessed as related to IMP by the investigator.

PSP003 was a relatively small, short-term safety study—a key limitation is therefore its duration; however, long-term safety of bepranemab treatment (up to 5 years) is being investigated in an ongoing OLE study (NCT04658199). In the future, larger studies should be conducted to investigate treatment effects on selected exploratory endpoints discussed earlier in this paper.

In conclusion, the acceptable safety profile and high TO (approximately 80%) observed with bepranemab warrant further clinical development of this mid-region-binding antibody for the potential treatment of tauopathies in larger and longer studies.

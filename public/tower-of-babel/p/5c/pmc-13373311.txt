Treatment‐Emergent Isolated EGFR C797S Mutation Following First‐Line Osimertinib‐Based Combination Therapy: A Case Report and Literature Review
Authors: Thu Huong Nguyen, Hoang Gia Nguyen, Thu Ha Le
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTOsimertinib is the standard first‐line treatment for EGFR‐mutated non–small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), although acquired resistance remains inevitable. The EGFR C797S mutation is a recognized on‐target resistance mechanism; however, the T790M‐negative/C797S‐only subtype following frontline osimertinib‐based combination therapy is rarely reported. We describe a 44‐year‐old Vietnamese never‐smoking woman diagnosed with stage IV lung adenocarcinoma harbouring EGFR exon 21 L858R mutation. First‐line treatment with pemetrexed–carboplatin plus osimertinib achieved a near‐complete response, followed by maintenance osimertinib monotherapy. After 26 months of disease control, radiologic progression occurred with recurrent bilateral pulmonary metastases and right supraclavicular lymphadenopathy. Repeat biopsy and next‐generation sequencing identified persistent EGFR L858R (VAF 13.92%) and acquired EGFR C797S (VAF 6.19%) without detectable T790M, consistent with a C797S‐only resistance profile. Subsequent gefitinib plus bevacizumab therapy produced a rapid radiologic response with near‐complete resolution of contralateral pulmonary lesions, suggesting retained sensitivity to reversible EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor–based therapy in C797S‐only disease.Keywords: C797S‐only, non‐small cell lung cancer, Osimertinib resistance

Introduction

Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations are the most frequently identified actionable driver alterations in non‐small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), present in 10%–20% of Western patients and up to 45%–50% of Asian patients with adenocarcinoma histology [1, 2]. Osimertinib, a third‐generation (3G) irreversible EGFR TKI, has become the preferred first‐line treatment based on the landmark FLAURA trial, which demonstrated superior progression‐free survival (PFS; 18.9 vs. 10.2 months) and overall survival (OS; 38.6 vs. 31.8 months) compared with first‐generation (1G) TKIs, along with improved central nervous system (CNS) control [3, 4]. The FLAURA2 trial further demonstrated that combining first‐line osimertinib with pemetrexed–carboplatin chemotherapy prolonged PFS compared with osimertinib monotherapy, establishing a new standard option for eligible patients [5].

Despite these therapeutic advances, acquired resistance to osimertinib remains virtually inevitable. Mechanisms of resistance are broadly categorized into EGFR‐dependent (on‐target) and EGFR‐independent (off‐target) alterations. Among the on‐target resistance mechanisms, the EGFR C797S point mutation in exon 20 is the most frequently identified alteration. This substitution disrupts the irreversible covalent binding of osimertinib to the cysteine residue at codon 797 within the EGFR kinase domain, thereby abrogating drug activity [6, 7]. Across different clinical settings, EGFR C797S has been reported in approximately 7%–22% of patients following disease progression on osimertinib therapy [7, 8].

The clinical significance of C797S is critically determined by its allelic relationship with the T790M gatekeeper mutation. Four distinct C797X subtypes have been defined based on this allelic context: (1) in cis—C797S on the same allele as T790M; (2) in trans—C797S on the opposite allele from T790M; (3) cis&trans—both configurations coexisting; and (4) C797S‐only—C797S detected without any co‐occurring T790M [9]. The C797S‐only subtype has distinct biological and therapeutic implications from the other three subtypes. In this setting, C797S arises de novo under direct osimertinib selective pressure, without the intermediate T790M selection step that characterizes the classical resistance pathway seen after 1G/2G TKI therapy followed by osimertinib. Because C797S‐only tumours lack T790M, they are not resistant to reversible 1G EGFR TKIs, which do not form the covalent bond at C797 that osimertinib exploits [10].

Published data on the C797S‐only subtype remain sparse, with most reports focusing on cis and trans configurations. Furthermore, the emergence of C797S‐only resistance following first‐line osimertinib combined with platinum‐based chemotherapy—as employed in the FLAURA2 regimen—has rarely been documented. Here, we report a case of T790M‐negative, C797S‐only resistance developing after first‐line osimertinib plus pemetrexed–carboplatin combination therapy, describe its molecular characteristics, and report a salvage response to gefitinib combined with bevacizumab.

Case Report

Initial Presentation and Diagnosis

A 44‐year‐old never‐smoking Vietnamese woman with no significant past medical history was incidentally found to have a pulmonary mass during a routine health examination in December 2023. She was asymptomatic at the time of diagnosis. Whole‐body positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) demonstrated a primary lung lesion accompanied by bilateral pulmonary metastases and mediastinal lymphadenopathy, consistent with advanced‐stage disease (clinical stage IVA, cT2bN2M1a according to the AJCC 8th edition staging system). Brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed no evidence of intracranial metastasis. Histopathological evaluation of a CT‐guided lung biopsy specimen confirmed papillary adenocarcinoma. Comprehensive molecular profiling using next‐generation sequencing (NGS) identified an activating EGFR exon 21 L858R mutation, while no baseline T790M mutation was detected, consistent with a treatment‐naive setting.

First‐Line Treatment: Osimertinib Combined With Platinum‐Based Chemotherapy

Based on the EGFR L858R mutation status and evidence supporting upfront osimertinib–chemotherapy combinations, the patient was initiated on pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) plus carboplatin (AUC 5) in combination with osimertinib 80 mg/day—a FLAURA2‐analogous regimen. She completed four cycles from December 2023 to April 2024. Restaging CT following four cycles demonstrated a significant reduction in the primary pulmonary lesion and complete radiological disappearance of bilateral pulmonary metastases, indicating a near‐complete response. Given the excellent response and patient preference, she declined continuation of chemotherapy due to fatigue and was transitioned to osimertinib 80 mg/day monotherapy maintenance.

Disease Monitoring and Progression

The patient continued osimertinib maintenance with documented radiological stability at 21 months (October 2025). However, CT chest and neck ultrasound at 26 months (March 2026) identified a new right supraclavicular lymph node measuring 5 mm and new intrathoracic lesions. No brain metastases were identified on updated imaging.

Ultrasound‐guided fine‐needle aspiration biopsy of the right supraclavicular lymph node confirmed metastatic adenocarcinoma. Repeat NGS from the lymph node specimen revealed concurrent EGFR L858R (VAF: 13.92%) and EGFR C797S (VAF: 6.19%). Crucially, T790M was entirely absent in this sample. This molecular profile is definitively consistent with the C797S‐only subtype as classified by Lu et al. [9]: C797S arising in the absence of T790M, representing direct osimertinib‐induced resistance without prior T790M‐mediated resistance.

The relatively low VAF of C797S (6.19%) compared with L858R (13.92%), yielding a C797S:L858R VAF ratio of approximately 0.44, further supports the interpretation that C797S‐carrying clones represent a subpopulation within the tumour at this timepoint, consistent with early or subclonal emergence of C797S‐only resistance.

Salvage Therapy: Gefitinib Plus Bevacizumab

Given the C797S‐only molecular profile—T790M‐negative, with C797S arising without the classical T790M intermediate—the tumour was expected to retain sensitivity to reversible 1G EGFR TKIs, which do not rely on covalent binding at C797. Bevacizumab was added based on established synergy between anti‐VEGF therapy and EGFR TKIs in EGFR‐mutated NSCLC, as well as its potential to suppress angiogenic bypass resistance mechanisms. The patient was initiated on gefitinib 250 mg/day plus bevacizumab 7.5 mg/kg every 3 weeks. Restaging CT after one cycle demonstrated stable primary tumour and complete disappearance of bilateral pulmonary lesions. Discussion

The C797S‐Only Subtype: A Distinct Resistance Entity

The four‐subtype classification of EGFR C797X resistance—in cis, in trans, cis&trans and C797X‐only—established by Lu et al. in a landmark multicenter study of 365 patients [9] provides the essential framework for understanding our case. The C797X‐only subtype, comprising 7.9% of C797X‐mutant cases in that cohort, is defined by the presence of C797S or C797G without co‐detectable T790M. This stands in contrast to the in cis (75.3%) and in trans (6.4%) subtypes, where T790M is present and the allelic relationship between T790M and C797S determines therapeutic sensitivity.

The biological mechanism underlying C797S—only resistance is fundamentally different from T790M—associated C797S subtypes. In the classical second—line osimertinib resistance pathway, osimertinib is deployed specifically to overcome pre‐existing T790M resistance to 1G/2G TKIs and C797S subsequently emerges as a tertiary mutation in T790M—positive clones. In contrast, in first—line osimertinib settings, T790M is not pre‐selected—and C797S emerges directly as the primary acquired resistance mutation under osimertinib pressure, acting independently on the L858R—carrying clone. This de novo C797S emergence without T790M is the hallmark of the C797X—only subtype and explains why T790M is absent [9, 10].

Importantly, Lu et al. demonstrated that C797X‐only patients had shorter PFS on prior osimertinib (median 9.1 months) and the worst PFS among all four subtypes—worse even than in trans patients—underscoring the aggressive nature of this resistance mechanism. The clinical behaviour of our patient (26 months PFS on first—line Osimertinib—based combination therapy before C797S—only emergence) may reflect the additive protective effect of concurrent chemotherapy in the FLAURA2—like regimen, potentially delaying but not preventing C797S emergence.

Recent analyses from the FLAURA2 trial presented at WCLC 2024 further contextualize the present case. Plasma‐based resistance profiling demonstrated that the spectrum of acquired resistance mechanisms was broadly similar between osimertinib monotherapy and osimertinib plus platinum‐pemetrexed, with no novel resistance mechanisms introduced by chemotherapy. MET amplification represented the most frequent genomic alteration (11%–16%), whereas EGFR C797S was detected in approximately 3% of patients receiving osimertinib plus chemotherapy and 10% of those receiving osimertinib alone. Other acquired alterations included uncommon EGFR mutations, ERBB2 alterations, KRAS and PIK3CA mutations, CCND1/E1 and CDK4/6 amplifications and rare oncogenic fusions involving RET, BRAF and ALK. Notably, acquired EGFR T790M was rarely observed, highlighting a major difference from the classical resistance pattern following first‐ or second‐generation EGFR‐TKIs. These findings support the concept that de novo emergence of C797S without concomitant T790M represents a distinct evolutionary pathway of resistance under first‐line osimertinib pressure. Therefore, our case of isolated C797S acquisition after frontline osimertinib‐based combination therapy is biologically consistent with the resistance landscape observed in FLAURA2 and further expands the clinical phenotype of the C797X‐only subtype.

Therapeutic Implications of C797S‐Only: Why Gefitinib Plus Bevacizumab

The selection of gefitinib as the EGFR‐targeting agent was based on the following reasoning: (i) C797S disrupts the covalent bond between osimertinib and C797 of EGFR, rendering 3G TKIs inactive; (ii) however, reversible 1G TKIs such as gefitinib do not rely on this covalent bond and bind competitively to the ATP‐binding pocket, retaining activity against L858R even in the presence of C797S when T790M is absent [10]; (iii) published clinical data on C797X‐only patients treated with 1G/2G TKIs showed a median PFS of approximately 5.0 months [9], supporting biological plausibility; and (iv) no approved 4G EGFR inhibitor is yet clinically available for routine use.

Bevacizumab was added for several reasons: (i) the combination of 1G EGFR TKI plus bevacizumab has demonstrated superior PFS compared with TKI monotherapy in EGFR‐mutated NSCLC in phase III trials (NEJ026: median PFS 16.9 vs. 13.3 months; ARTEMIS/CTONG1509: 17.9 vs. 11.2 months) [11, 12]; (ii) dual EGFR‐VEGF inhibition can suppress angiogenic bypass mechanisms that may co‐contribute to resistance; (iii) bevacizumab has shown activity in combination with osimertinib in phase I/II data and the EGFR—VEGF interplay is well‐established in this disease context [13, 14].

Therefore, based on these findings, the combination of a 1G EGFR TKI and bevacizumab was selected with the expectation of achieving a synergistic antitumor effect. Indeed, the complete disappearance of bilateral pulmonary lesions after only one treatment cycle further supports the validity of this mechanism‐based therapeutic strategy.

Centric stabilization occlusal splints vs. other conservative therapies in the management of temporomandibular disorders: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Abhishek Kr Gupta, Rohit Kr Singh, Vashi Narula
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundTMDs are frequently occurring musculoskeletal disorders that affect the orofacial region, leading to pain and functional limitations. Among conservative treatments, centric stabilization splints are widely used, but their superiority over other noninvasive therapies remains uncertain.ObjectiveTo systematically review and quantitatively evaluate the clinical effectiveness of centric stabilization occlusal splints versus other conservative managements in TMD patients.MethodsLiterature was searched in Cochrane, PubMed, Scopus, WoS, EMBASE, and LILACS from 2000 to 2023. The study included randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that compared centric stabilization splints to other conservative therapies. The main outcome assessed was the reduction in pain, determined through the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS). The quality of the evidence was appraised using the GRADE framework. Meta-analysis employed a random effect model.ResultsThe analysis encompassed eight randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that included more than 400 participants. The combined standardized mean difference (SMD) found that centric stabilization splints had a substantial advantage in reduction of pain (SMD = 0.75; with 95% confidence interval: 0.32 to 1.18; p < 0.01). The evidence was assessed as moderately certain. Sensitivity analysis excluding high-risk studies showed minimal change in effect (SMD = 0.72). Functional outcomes and quality-of-life metrics were also identified as additional advantages.ConclusionCentric stabilization splints demonstrate clinically meaningful short-term pain relief in TMD compared to other conservative treatments. Their use is supported in patients with myogenous TMD, but long-term outcomes require further validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s44445-025-00052-9.

Introduction

TMDs represent a collection of musculoskeletal and neuromuscular disorders impacting the TMJ, jaw muscles, and related anatomical features. They are one of the leading non-dental sources of pain in the orofacial region and typically present with symptoms such as chronic pain, joint noises (clicking, crepitus), restricted mandibular motion, and deviations during mouth opening or closing (Okeson 2003; Schiffman et al. 2014). TMD’s etiology is multifactorial, comprising biomechanical dysfunctions, psychosocial stressors, neuromuscular imbalances, hormonal influences, and anatomical irregularities (Manfredini and Guarda-Nardini 2010; Gupta et al. 2024).

Conservative management is the preferred first-line approach for most TMD cases, particularly those of myofascial or arthrogenous origin. One of the most widely adopted conservative therapies is the use of occlusal splints—custom-fitted intraoral devices that aim to redistribute occlusal forces, alleviate muscle hyperactivity, and reduce joint loading (Dao and Lavigne 1998; Zhang et al. 2021; Rahman 2025). Among the various splint designs, centric stabilization splints (CSS)—typically fabricated from hard acrylic and designed to achieve uniform bilateral occlusal contact in centric relation—are regarded as the gold standard. These splints are believed to promote neuromuscular balance, reduce parafunctional activity such as bruxism, and improve TMJ function (Okeson 2015; Disorders: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis 2025). Despite their widespread clinical use, there is ongoing debate regarding the relative effectiveness of CSS compared to other noninvasive treatments, such as pharmacotherapy, physical therapy, and psychological counseling. While some randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have reported meaningful pain relief and functional improvements with CSS, others have found no significant difference when compared to alternative conservative interventions (Vrbanović and Alajbeg 2019; Alajbeg et al. 2020; Albagieh et al. 2025 Jan 14; Vohra et al. n.d.).

Due to the ongoing ambiguity regarding their relative efficacy, high-quality evidence is mandatory. This meta-analysis seeks to examine the therapeutic efficacy of centric stabilization splints (CSS) compared to other conservative interventions in adults with temporomandibular disorders (TMD). By aggregating data from eligible randomized controlled trials, the objective is to quantify the degree of pain relief associated with CSS and support evidence-based clinical decision-making in the management of TMD.

Methods

Protocol and registration

The PRISMA 2020 standards were followed in this systematic review and meta-analysis. It was registered under Number: CRD4202021106967.

Qualification requirements

To evaluate study eligibility, the PICOS framework was used. The population included adult patients aged 15 to 40 years who had a clinical diagnosis of TMDs based on the RDC/TMD criteria. Any type of occlusal splint therapy—more especially, stability splints and occlusal splints—was the intervention of interest. Comparators included nociceptive trigeminal inhibition (NTI) appliances, pharmaceutical therapies (e.g., amitriptyline), physical therapy modalities (e.g., muscle workouts), and counseling. The primary outcome measured was the change in pain intensity, evaluated using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS). Only randomized controlled trials (RCTs) published in English-language peer-reviewed journals were considered for inclusion. However, when feasible, non-English studies were also included after translation for data extraction and screening purposes. Non-randomized designs, abstracts, reviews, studies without pain outcome measures, animal or in vitro research, and studies involving patients with systemic joint disease (such as rheumatoid arthritis or osteoarthritis) were among the exclusion criteria.

Method of search

A comprehensive search was done in Cochrane, PubMed, Scopus, EMBASE, WoS, and LILACS (2000–2023). In addition to Boolean operators (AND, OR), search phrases included MeSH terms and free-text keywords such as"temporomandibular disorders,""TMD,""occlusal splint,""stabilization splint,"and"splint."A supplemental file containing the entire search strategy has been made available.

Selection of studies

Using database searches, 64 articles in all were found. Eleven full-text papers were evaluated for eligibility after duplicates were eliminated and a preliminary screening of titles and abstracts was completed. Eight of these RCT’s data were used in this qualitative synthesis and meta-analysis after fulfilling all inclusion requirements (Fig. 1).Fig. 1PRISMA Chart

PRISMA Chart

Data compilation and evaluation of bias risk

Two independent reviewers extracted essential data from the selected studies, including study design, number of participants, intervention specifics, comparator information, duration of follow-up, and pre- and post-treatment Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) scores. The disagreements between reviewers were handled by conversation or, when required, contact with a third reviewer. Adverse events were not reported in the included studies. The methodological quality and potential risk of bias for each study were evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2.0 (RoB 2.0) tool, which examines five key domains: randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, outcome measurement, missing data, and selective reporting.

Analysis of statistics

The DerSimonian and Laird method using a random effect model was used for meta-analyses, taking into consideration possible variation amongst studies. Using pooled standard deviations, standardized mean differences (SMDs) were computed for continuous pain outcomes (VAS scores). To visually depict the findings of individual studies as well as estimates of the overall effect, forest plots (Fig. 2) were created. In order to account for tool availability constraints, heterogeneity was assessed visually and, when appropriate, quantified using the I2 statistic.Fig. 2Forest plot

Forest plot

Risk of bias and certainty of evidence

The Cochrane RoB 2.0 technique was used to evaluate the risk of bias in all included studies, taking into account five primary domains: reporting bias, missing data, outcome measurement, randomization, and changes from planned treatments. The degree of evidence certainty for the primary outcome (pain reduction) was assessed using the GRADE (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) system. The evidence was classified as high, moderate, low, or very low based on factors such as indirectness, imprecision, inconsistency, risk of bias, and probable publication bias.

Analysis sensitivity

After excluding publications deemed to have a high risk of bias according to the RoB 2.0 rating, a sensitivity analysis was conducted by rerunning the meta-analysis to examine the robustness of the initial findings. This study aimed to evaluate the relationship between study quality and overall impact size.

Characteristics of Included Studies

This review included 8 RCTs comprising 400 adult participants diagnosed with temporomandibular disorders (TMDs). The included studies spanned a publication range from 2000 to 2023 and exhibited a range of clinical and methodological diversity. The intervention of interest across all studies was occlusal stabilization splints, commonly fabricated from hard acrylic and designed to provide even occlusal contacts in centric relation. The comparators varied considerably and included placebo splints, psychological counseling, pharmacological therapy (notably low-dose amitriptyline), trigger point injections, physical therapy with muscle exercises, and nociceptive trigeminal inhibition (NTI) appliances.

The sample sizes per study ranged from 21 to 80 patients, reflecting small to moderate study populations. Follow-up durations also varied, with some trials evaluating outcomes after as little as one month, while others extended to twelve months. Pain intensity was measured using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS), a well-established and validated instrument for subjective pain measurement. The variation in intervention types and durations provided a broad basis for pooled analysis but also introduced heterogeneity that was addressed in the subsequent meta-analytic approach.

Meta-Analysis Summary

A random effect was performed to measure the overall impact of stabilization splints on pain reduction in patients with TMD. Pooled standardized mean difference (SMD) was 0.75 (95% Confidence Interval: 0.32 to 1.18), with the result reaching statistical significance (p < 0.01). This indicates a moderate to large effect size in favor of stabilization splints compared to other conservative treatments. The findings suggest that patients treated with stabilization splints experienced a clinically meaningful reduction in orofacial pain relative to those receiving alternative interventions. This finding is consistent with prior literature supporting the efficacy of hard occlusal splints, especially in the short-term management of myogenous TMD symptoms.

In Fig. 2, it is summarized that Conti (2012) and Michelotti (2012) reported large, statistically significant effects favoring stabilization splints (SMD > 1.0, CI does not cross zero). Other research revealed non-significant effects with large confidence intervals that crossed zero.

Certainty of evidence (GRADE Assessment)

The GRADE methodology assessed the degree of evidence certainty for the main goal, which is pain reduction. Moderate heterogeneity among trials (I2 = 43%) and signs of publication bias (Egger's test, p = 0.047) were the main reasons for the moderate rating of overall confidence. The overall moderate certainty rating was influenced by several key considerations. Risk of bias was downgraded due to methodological concerns in a few included studies, particularly related to unclear blinding of participants or outcome assessors and selective outcome reporting, which may introduce subjectivity into pain assessments. Inconsistency was evident from moderate statistical heterogeneity, with an I2 value of approximately 43%, suggesting variation in treatment effects across studies likely due to differences in interventions, follow-up durations, or patient populations. However, the direction of the effect remained consistent. Indirectness was not considered a concern, as all included studies directly addressed the adult TMD population and interventions of interest. Imprecision was observed due to the moderate width of the confidence intervals, despite the pooled effect being both statistically significant and clinically meaningful. Furthermore, Egger's test results (p = 0.047) raised suspicions of publication bias, pointing to a possible propensity for research with positive results to be published. Taken together, these factors contributed to downgrading the certainty of evidence from high to moderate.

Sensitivity analysis

To conduct a sensitivity analysis, studies that the RoB 2.0 evaluation determined to have a high risk of bias were eliminated. The purpose of this analysis was to assess the core findings'robustness and ascertain how much the total effect estimate was impacted by study quality. Additional outcomes

Improvements in mandibular range of motion, specifically in maximum mouth opening, were reported in the studies by Gupta et al. (Gupta et al. 2024) and Albagieh (Albagieh et al. 2025 Jan 14), with particularly notable gains observed in patients diagnosed with myogenous TMD. Several studies (Gupta et al. 2024; Dao and Lavigne 1998; Zhang et al. 2021; Okeson 2015; Vrbanović and Alajbeg 2019) also documented reductions in functional limitations, including decreased difficulty with chewing, speaking, and performing daily oral activities. Using approved tools such as the Short Form Health Survey (SF-36) and the Oral Health Impact Profile (OHIP-14), several trials showed moderate increases in quality of life (QoL). However, due to variability in outcome measures and reporting, the QoL data were heterogeneous and could not be pooled for meta-analysis. These outcomes suggest that centric stabilization splints may offer benefits extending beyond pain relief alone.

Subgroup and meta-regression analyses

Subgroup analyses revealed that patients with myogenous TMD experienced greater therapeutic benefits from centric stabilization splints compared to those with arthrogenous TMD (Ozkan and Ozkan 2024). Additionally, splints worn during nighttime only were associated with stronger effect sizes than those worn full-time, suggesting that limited wear may be more effective or better tolerated (Fassih et al. 2025). In terms of follow-up duration, studies with shorter follow-up periods (≤ 3 months) demonstrated more pronounced pain reduction, whereas the effect size tended to diminish over longer durations. Meta-regression analyses, however, did not find this trend to be statistically significant (Zhang et al. 2021; Alajbeg et al. 2020; Ozkan and Ozkan 2024). These results highlight how crucial treatment methodology and patient selection are to optimizing therapy effectiveness.

Discussion

The findings of this meta-analysis and comprehensive review offer important new information on how well centric stabilization splints (CSS) work in relation to other conservative therapy choices for temporomandibular disorders (TMDs). The evidence presented supports the use of CSS as a significantly effective intervention for short-term pain relief, outperforming non-splint therapies such as pharmacologic agents, counseling, and physical therapy (Vrbanović and Alajbeg 2019; Alajbeg et al. 2020; Albagieh et al. 2025 Jan 14; Katyayan and Katyayan 2013; Niemelä et al. 2012; Conti et al. 2016; Michelotti et al. 2012; Ozkan and Ozkan 2024; Díaz et al. 2025; Dinsdale et al. 2025). This discussion situates our findings within the broader clinical and research context, examines potential mechanisms underlying their effectiveness, and identifies key areas for future investigation.

Reducing pain as measured by the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) was the primary objective of all included randomized controlled trials (RCTs). A moderate-to-large effect size favoring the use of centric stabilization splints (CSS) is shown by the pooled standardized mean difference (SMD) of 0.75 (95% CI: 0.32 to 1.18), suggesting clinically significant improvements in orofacial discomfort associated with TMD. This therapeutic benefit was consistent across trials, despite heterogeneity in follow-up durations, comparator interventions, and diagnostic criteria.

Centric stabilization splints, made of rigid acrylic and designed to achieve uniform bilateral occlusal contact in centric relation, function by redistributing occlusal forces, promoting neuromuscular balance, and reducing parafunctional habits such as bruxism. (Al-Moraissi et al. 2020; Rosales Leal and Esteve 2025; Kumar et al. 2025) Several studies included in this review reported accompanying reductions in muscle hyperactivity (as evidenced by EMG assessments), further validating the biological plausibility of their mechanism (Conti et al. 2016; Michelotti et al. 2012).

Unlike studies that have compared multiple splint types, the current analysis deliberately excluded soft and hydrostatic splints to focus exclusively on the efficacy of CSS against other conservative interventions. (González-González et al. 2025) This restriction strengthens the interpretability and clinical applicability of the findings, particularly for practitioners seeking to determine whether CSS should be prioritized over treatments such as low-dose amitriptyline, cognitive counseling, or trigger point therapy.

Our results align with existing literature supporting the use of hard stabilization splints. Hard occlusal splints demonstrated superior pain reduction compared to other noninvasive modalities (Al-Moraissi et al. 2020). While some evidence suggests that multimodal therapy (e.g., splint + physical therapy or behavioral modification) may enhance outcomes, CSS alone still represents a powerful and effective standalone intervention.

In addition to evaluating pain relief, this review also analyzed data related to mandibular range of motion (ROM), functional limitations, and quality of life (QoL). Improvements in mouth opening and oral function were observed, and some studies using OHIP-14 or SF-36 instruments reported enhanced quality of life, highlighting multidimensional benefits beyond pain relief.

From a clinical standpoint, CSS offers multiple advantages. (González-González et al. 2025; Fassih et al. 2025; Mahdi and Al-Fahham 2025) They are non-invasive, cost-effective, reversible, and generally well-tolerated by patients. Given the moderate-to-large effect size and consistent short-term benefits (Bhatia 2025; Ku et al. 2025), CSS may be appropriately positioned as a first-line therapy for patients with myofascial-type TMD.

Subgroup and meta-regression analyses indicated more pronounced effects in patients with myogenous TMD, those using splints during nighttime only, and in studies with shorter follow-up durations (≤ 3 months). These observations underscore the potential importance of patient selection, wear time, and treatment duration in optimizing outcomes, although meta-regression did not reveal statistically significant trends.

While the short-term effectiveness of CSS is well supported, their long-term efficacy remains less certain. Evidence beyond 6–12 months is sparse, and symptom recurrence in the absence of adjunctive management (e.g., behavioral therapy or physical rehabilitation) is possible. This underscores the importance of integrated care models and follow-up planning.

The primary outcome's evidentiary certainty was graded as moderate by the GRADE evaluation. Uncertain blinding practices and selective outcome reporting in a subset of the included trials were the main causes of the identified bias risk concerns. Although there was some moderate variability (I2 = 43%), the general direction of impact was constant. The effect estimate had a moderately wide confidence interval, indicating some imprecision, but it was statistically significant. A visual examination of the funnel plot revealed that it was substantially symmetrical, suggesting only a modest danger of publishing bias, despite Egger's test (p = 0.047) suggesting the presence of potential publication bias.

Moderate heterogeneity was expected since different trials used different comparator therapies and clinical contexts. A random-effects model that adequately took into account between-study variability was used to address this. The total impact size remained strong in spite of this variability, confirming the validity of the results. These findings highlight how crucial it is for future studies to have more rigorous methodology, especially when it comes to open reporting and prospective trial registration. According to study quality ratings, the majority of the included trials had a low risk of bias (Aklar et al. 2025; Gupta et al. 2022; Haseeb et al. 2025).

Another limitation of this review is the retrospective registration on PROSPERO, which may raise concerns about potential bias in the study selection or data extraction process. Although all methodological steps adhered strictly to PRISMA guidelines, prospective registration would have further strengthened the transparency and credibility of the review protocol. However, a few studies lacked blinding, particularly at the participant or outcome assessment level—a limitation that could introduce subjective bias in pain reporting. Future trials should aim for double-blind designs where feasible, adopt uniform splint fabrication protocols, and report core outcome measures using standardized time frames.

Several gaps in the literature were also identified

1. Direct comparator trials: Very few RCTs directly compare CSS with structured counseling, pharmacologic therapy, or physical modalities using standardized methodologies.

2. Functional outcomes: Most studies rely solely on VAS, which, while valuable, does not capture functional limitations, quality of life, or joint mechanics.

3. Psychosocial factors: Variables such as stress, depression, and coping strategies were often unmeasured, despite their known relevance in TMD pathophysiology.

4. Technological innovations: Sensor-equipped splints and digital monitoring offer opportunities for personalization and adherence tracking, yet remain understudied.

This study is improved by a comprehensive search strategy and strict adherence to PRISMA guidelines; however, it should be acknowledged that it has certain drawbacks. The limited number of included studies (n = 8), differences in treatment durations and outcome reporting, and a lack of long-term follow-up data limit the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, subjective pain measures such as the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) have the risk of interpretation bias.

Notwithstanding these drawbacks, the meta-analysis demonstrates that centric stabilization splints (CSS) are a successful, empirically supported treatment for temporary pain management in patients with temporomandibular disorders (TMD), especially those who present with myogenous symptoms. These findings demonstrate the value of CSS in the conservative, non-surgical treatment of TMD. Future research should focus on comparing CSS's effectiveness, looking at long-term outcomes, and analyzing how they fit into multidisciplinary therapy regimens.

Conclusion

Centric stabilization splints deliver clinically meaningful short-term pain relief in the management of temporomandibular disorders (TMDs), with effect sizes exceeding those of other conservative treatments. These findings support the use of occlusal splint therapy as a key element in multimodal, non-surgical TMD management—especially for patients presenting with myogenous symptoms. Future studies should concentrate on improving therapy regimens, elucidating long-term efficacy, and implementing individualized strategies catered to the unique characteristics of each patient and the underlying pathophysiological processes.

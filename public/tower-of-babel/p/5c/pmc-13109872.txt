Proof-of-concept study for a national geriatric hip fracture registry in Brazil

Background

Recent projections by the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics (IBGE) indicate that the country will have approximately 69 million older adults by 2050 (around 30% of the total population), representing about 35% of the population aged over 60 years in Latin America and the Caribbean [1]. The increasing longevity of the Brazilian population has resulted in a rising incidence of osteoporotic fractures, particularly hip fractures in older adults. These injuries are associated with high rates of morbidity and mortality, as well as significant public health costs [2]. It is estimated that proximal femoral fractures lead to a 20% mortality rate within 12 months, in addition to permanent disability in 30% of survivors and loss of independent ambulation in 40% of survivors [3, 4].

Despite the severity of this issue, Brazil still does not have a unified national registry system for hip fractures. Current data are fragmented. Most Brazilian epidemiological studies on hip fractures are regional (single-state or single-hospital series) or limited to short periods. Some teaching hospitals, trauma centers, and orthogeriatric services maintain local registries for quality improvement or research purposes, but these databases are not standardized, are not interoperable, and are not centrally coordinated. As a result, Brazil lacks comprehensive national data on incidence, time to surgery, access to rehabilitation, secondary prevention, and mortality in this population. This gap hinders effective health policy planning and the implementation of secondary prevention measures [5].

In several countries, the creation of national hip fracture registries has contributed to improved care and reduced mortality [5]. A standardized national protocol enables the collection of homogeneous data on patient profiles, treatments provided, time to surgery, complications, and functional outcomes, allowing comparisons across services and regions. In Brazil, implementing a national registry protocol for hip fractures in older adults could identify prognostic variables, assess care quality (e.g. time to surgical intervention, osteoporosis medication prescription rates), and guide healthcare investments. Moreover, a national registry would facilitate adherence to treatment guidelines and the establishment of Fracture Liaison Services (FLS), integrating orthopedics with geriatrics and endocrinology [6, 7].

An additional and timely consideration is the recent international position statement by the Fragility Fracture Network (FFN), which advocates for unrestricted weight-bearing after hip fracture surgery in older adults. Turabi et al. [6] showed that unrestricted weight-bearing contributes to shorter hospital stays, lower complication rates, and better functional recovery, without increasing the risk of fixation failure. This reinforces the importance of implementing evidence-based guidelines and collecting national data to monitor compliance and outcomes. Furthermore, the comprehensive orthogeriatric approach described by Falaschi and Marsh emphasizes that optimal care requires multidisciplinary management, early surgery, rehabilitation, and secondary prevention [8]. Registries are one of the pillars to structure these elements, especially in countries where geriatric medicine is not yet fully established.

Given this context, this article presents a protocol for a National Hip Fracture Registry in Older Adults in Brazil, supporting Brazilian policymakers in implementing a clear and feasible national strategy.

Objectives

This article aims to: (i) present a structured protocol for a national hip fracture registry in older adults in Brazil; (ii) standardize the core variables to be collected, distinguishing those derived from the international Minimum Common Dataset (MCD) [9] and those added for the Brazilian context; (iii) outline the proposed governance and hosting of the national database (site, oversight, and reporting model); and (iv) discuss the expected benefits of such a registry in improving the quality of care, especially regarding surgical timing, rehabilitation, and secondary osteoporosis prevention.

Methods

Development of the registry protocol

The national hip fracture registry protocol was developed based on four complementary components: (i) review of national and international literature on hip fracture registries; (ii) alignment with the Fragility Fracture Network (FFN) Minimum Common Dataset (MCD) [9]; (iii) inclusion of Brazil-specific variables addressing local healthcare characteristics; and (iv) internal expert consensus among the authors.

A targeted review of national and international literature and existing hip fracture registries was conducted to identify key variables and quality indicators [5–9]. The registries considered in the development of this protocol include the National Hip Fracture Database (NHFD) (England & Wales), the Registro Nacional de Fracturas de Cadera (RNFC) (Spain), the Australian and New Zealand Hip Fracture Registry (ANZHFR) (Australia/New Zealand), the Scottish Hip Fracture Audit (SHFA) (Scotland), Rikshöft (Sweden), and the Irish Hip Fracture Database (IHFD) (Ireland). A systematic search was not conducted, as the objective was to develop a pragmatic and implementable registry framework grounded in established international models rather than to conduct a formal systematic review.

The core dataset structure was based on the FFN MCD [9], ensuring international comparability. Additional variables were incorporated to reflect specific characteristics of the Brazilian healthcare system and to enable future epidemiological and clinical analyses. These variables were defined based on clinical experience, previously reported national data [5], and expert consensus among the authors.

To enhance feasibility and scalability, the registry was structured as a tiered dataset. Tier 1 includes core MCD variables, while Tier 2 comprises extended variables relevant to the Brazilian context. This approach allows progressive implementation across different healthcare settings and aligns with indicators used in international registries [10, 11].

Database structure and governance

The registry is proposed as a secure national digital platform coordinated by a steering committee including representatives from the Brazilian Ministry of Health (preferably through the Departamento de Informática do Sistema Único de Saúde (DATASUS) or an equivalent federal health information system), the Brazilian Society of Orthopaedics and Traumatology (SBOT), the Fragility Fracture Network (FFN) Brazil, and academic institutions. This committee will be responsible for maintaining the data collection platform, validating and auditing data, producing national reports, and promoting training for participating centers.

Data collection and application

The protocol is intended for all patients aged ≥ 60 years admitted with proximal femur fractures in both public (SUS) and private healthcare settings. Data collection is performed at admission and at hospital discharge by trained healthcare professionals. Standardized definitions and predefined response options were used to ensure consistency and facilitate data entry [5].

Key indicators such as time to surgery, early mobilization, and mortality were operationally defined to allow benchmarking across institutions. Data confidentiality and compliance with national data protection regulations (LGPD) are ensured.

Registry design

The proposed registry design comprises a national hip fracture registry protocol for older adults in Brazil (HFR-Brazil), developed through review of international registries, published literature, and expert consensus.

The resulting protocol comprises: (i) a tiered dataset aligned with the Fragility Fracture Network Minimum Common Dataset, distinguishing internationally comparable core variables from Brazilian context–specific extensions; (ii) standardized operational definitions for structure, process and outcome indicators; and (iii) a proposed national governance and implementation framework suitable for large-scale adoption.

Together, these elements constitute the final outcome of this study and represent the scientific product generated through the process described in this manuscript.

Results

This study resulted in the development of a structured national hip fracture registry protocol for older adults in Brazil (HFR-Brazil), based on international registry models, literature review, and expert consensus.

The final registry framework consists of a standardized and tiered dataset aligned with the Fragility Fracture Network Minimum Common Dataset (MCD). The dataset was organized into two levels: Tier 1, comprising core internationally comparable variables, and Tier 2, including additional variables tailored to the Brazilian healthcare context.

The proposed dataset includes variables covering key domains such as demographic characteristics, pre-fracture functional status, comorbidities, fracture characteristics, perioperative management, orthogeriatric care processes, complications, and clinical outcomes. Process and outcome indicators were clearly defined, including time to surgery, early mobilization, length of hospital stay, discharge destination, and short- and mid-term mortality.

In addition, the registry incorporates variables specifically designed to reflect national characteristics, such as healthcare sector (public versus private), previous contralateral hip fracture, and follow-up outcomes at 30, 120, and 365 days.

The registry framework also includes a proposed governance structure involving national stakeholders, data validation processes, and standardized data collection protocols to ensure feasibility and scalability across different healthcare settings.

Discussion

This study resulted in the development of a structured and nationally applicable protocol for a Brazilian Hip Fracture Registry aligned with the Fragility Fracture Network Minimum Common Dataset. The principal outcome of this work is the definition of a standardized dataset, together with a governance and implementation framework tailored to the Brazilian healthcare system. By integrating internationally validated core variables with a limited number of carefully justified national adaptations, the proposed registry balances global comparability with local relevance. This approach was developed based on literature review, international registry models, and expert consensus.

From an implementation perspective, the proposed registry is conceived as a progressive national initiative. Initial adoption is expected to occur through voluntary participation by public and private hospitals, coordinated by professional societies and academic centers with experience in hip fracture care and audit. In the medium term, the registry is intended to support engagement with federal health authorities, with the objective of integration into existing national health information systems (e.g. DATASUS/SIH-SUS).

The strategic priority of this initiative lies in its potential to provide reliable national indicators for quality improvement, policy planning, and resource allocation. Professional organizations such as FFN Brazil and the Brazilian Society of Orthopaedics and Traumatology are natural stakeholders to support dissemination, training, and feedback of audit reports. While formal legislation and dedicated funding are beyond the scope of this protocol paper, the framework was explicitly designed to be compatible with future governmental endorsement and scalable national implementation.

Previous Brazilian studies by Cocco et al. [5, 7] have already shown that prolonged hospital stay and delayed surgical treatment are independently associated with higher mortality among older adults with hip fractures. These findings are consistent with international evidence and underscore the need to monitor surgical timing as a national quality indicator. Arliani et al. [14] similarly demonstrated a direct correlation between delayed surgery and increased mortality in older adults with proximal femur fractures, further supporting the inclusion of time-to-surgery and causes of delay as mandatory fields in the registry.

The inclusion of variables related to osteoporosis therapy (current use and prescription at discharge) directly addresses the well-described ‘osteoporosis care gap’. Even after suffering a severe fragility fracture, a considerable proportion of older adults are discharged without pharmacological secondary prevention [7]. Capturing this information at a national level will allow Brazilian health authorities to monitor adherence to secondary prevention and to design targeted interventions (e.g. Fracture Liaison Services, geriatric-orthopedic co-management, or automatic prescriptions embedded in electronic discharge summaries).

One of the major strengths of a national registry is the possibility of quantifying differences between public and private hospitals. Available clinical data in Brazil suggest that older adults treated in Sistema Único de Saúde (SUS) facilities often wait more than 48 h for surgery, mainly due to limited operating room availability, lack of implants, or the need for intensive care beds. In the private sector, surgery is usually performed within 24–48 h, in line with international recommendations [5]. By making time-to-surgery and reasons for delay compulsory fields, the registry will generate objective data to support organizational changes, such as allocating protected trauma-theater slots or creating fast-track pathways for hip fracture patients [14].

International experience demonstrates that national hip fracture registries are feasible and associated with improvements in surgical timing, early mobilization, and mortality reduction. Successful implementation in countries such as the United Kingdom, Spain, Sweden, and Australia/New Zealand supports the relevance of adopting a similar model in Brazil [15–18]. These examples reinforce that standardized data collection can drive quality improvement, even in healthcare systems with regional variability [19]. Recent registry-based and large-scale data studies further reinforce the role of structured databases in improving surgical outcomes and patient safety [20–22].

By incorporating additional process and outcome measures aligned with the FFN Minimum Common Dataset (MCD) [15]—including precise timestamps, orthogeriatric assessment timing, anesthesia and implant details, early mobilization, unrestricted weight-bearing, and 30-/120-day outcomes—the Brazilian registry achieves direct comparability with leading international databases such as the National Hip Fracture Database (NHFD) (England & Wales), the Registro Nacional de Fracturas de Cadera (RNFC) (Spain), and the Australian and New Zealand Hip Fracture Registry (ANZHFR) (Australia/New Zealand) [5, 9–11].

The HFR-Brazil dataset was structured around the MCD [9], which defines the essential variables required for global benchmarking of hip fracture care, while preserving flexibility for national adaptations. Variables marked with an asterisk (*) represent additional data points introduced to capture features specific to the Brazilian healthcare system—such as healthcare sector, contralateral hip fracture history, and postoperative follow-up practices. This integrated design ensures both alignment with international quality standards and sensitivity to local clinical and system-level priorities.

The main limitation of the present proposal is that there is not yet formal institutional support for the implementation of the national registry. The transition from a research-based proposal to a nationwide registry will require political commitment from federal health authorities and engagement from professional societies. Another challenge is hospital buy-in: some institutions may initially resist additional data-entry workload. This can be mitigated by integrating the registry with existing electronic medical records, providing simplified digital forms, and feeding back performance reports to participating hospitals.

Data quality is another potential barrier. To ensure reliable national indicators, data collection must be performed by trained staff, with automated validation rules and periodic audits by the steering committee. Differences in case mix between public and private hospitals, as well as between regions with different levels of health infrastructure, should be explicitly recognized in the analysis plan, with stratified reports. Funding and long-term sustainability will depend on inclusion of the registry in federal budget lines and on demonstrating to policy makers that the registry leads to measurable improvements in mortality, length of stay, and secondary prevention [5, 11]. In busy healthcare environments, some institutions may complete only a subset of variables due to time constraints and competing clinical priorities.

In the Brazilian context, this registry is expected to promote earlier surgical treatment, improve adherence to secondary prevention, and standardize orthogeriatric care pathways across institutions.

Conclusions

This study delivers a national hip fracture registry protocol for older adults in Brazil and provides a standardized dataset and implementation framework for national adoption. A standardized protocol will enable reliable national monitoring, identification of quality indicators, and adoption of preventive and therapeutic strategies targeting secondary prevention and early mobilization. In addition, by explicitly distinguishing internationally validated variables from Brazilian context–specific variables, this proposal responds to the reviewer’s concern and provides a pragmatic and evidence-based roadmap for national adoption. This framework was developed based on literature review, international registry models, and expert consensus.

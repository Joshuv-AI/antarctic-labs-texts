Efficacy and safety of efgartigimod versus intravenous immunoglobulin in early intervention of acetylcholine receptor antibody-positive impending myasthenic crisis: A retrospective cohort study
Authors: Rongjing Guo, Chao Sun, Xiaoxi Huang, Sijia Hao, Qingqing Wang, Zhe Ruan, Ting Gao, Yonglan Tang, Xiangqi Cao, Yu Liu, Zhuyi Li, Ting Chang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Early intervention in impending myasthenic crisis (IMC) is critical to avert life-threatening progression. This study compared the clinical effectiveness and safety of the novel FcRn antagonist efgartigimod versus intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) in IMC management. In this retrospective cohort study, we analyzed 51 acetylcholine receptor antibody-positive (AChR-Ab+) IMC patients who received either efgartigimod (n ​= ​30) or IVIg (n ​= ​21) from June 2023 to November 2024. Efficacy was assessed based on changes in Myasthenia Gravis Activities of Daily Living (MG-ADL) and Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis (QMG) scores over four weeks. From weeks 2–4, the efgartigimod group showed significantly greater improvements in MG-ADL and QMG scores (both P ​< ​0.05), with a similar trend after baseline adjustment. By week 4, the results of the clinically meaningful improvement (CMI) analysis indicated that a higher proportion of patients in the efgartigimod group achieved MG-ADL improvement ≥7 points (90.0 ​% vs. 57.1 ​%) and greater QMG scores improvement. The proportion of patients reaching minimal symptom expression (MSE) was 80.0 ​% in the efgartigimod group compared to 14.3 ​% in the IVIg group, with mean improvement rates of 91.5 ​% vs. 60.7 ​%, respectively. One case of myasthenic crisis-related death occurred in the IVIg group, while no severe adverse events were reported in the remaining patients. Efgartigimod has been shown to effectively alleviate IMC symptoms, prevent progression to myasthenic crisis (MC), and significantly reduce in complications without substantially increasing the economic costs. These findings suggest its potential as a first-line therapeutic option for AChR-Ab-positive IMC patients.

Introduction

Myasthenia gravis (MG) is an acquired autoimmune disorder that affects the neuromuscular junction, leading to muscle weakness, including the ocular, bulbar, facial, respiratory, and extremity muscles [1,2]. The majority initially present with ocular muscle involvement. However, 10–20 ​% of patients experience an acute onset with rapid progression of symptoms such as dysphagia, weakness in swallowing, chewing, limbs, and neck, which may subsequently progress to Myasthenic Crisis (MC) [3,4]. According to 2016 the international consensus guidance for management of myasthenia gravis, this condition is classified as the Impending myasthenic crisis (IMC) [5]. Retrospective data indicate [6] that 41.9 ​% (36/86) of MG with acute onset of dyspnea progress to MC, underscoring the urgency of early recognition and intervention. Current guidelines recommend intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) or plasma exchange (PLEX) as first-line interventions for IMC [7,8]. However, plasma exchange is an invasive procedure, requiring special equipment and personnel with advanced training. Moreover, the supply of IVIg and PLEX, as blood-derived therapies, often fails to meet clinical demands [9]. Additionally approximately 20–30 ​% of patients show insufficient responses to IVIg [10]. Therefore, new therapeutic agents are needed that can rapidly alleviate symptoms and improve patient outcomes.

Efgartigimod, a humanized IgG1 antibody fragment, competitively binds to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). Inhibiting IgG recycling and promoting lysosomal degradation of serum IgG levels thereby significantly reduces pathogenic acetylcholine receptor antibodies (AChR-Ab) levels [11,12]. The ADAPT study demonstrated that efgartigimod rapidly alleviates MG symptoms [13]. Subsequent analysis [14] further revealed its significant effects on improving bulbar muscle function. Furthermore, several case reports [[15], [16], [17]]have found that symptoms in patients with MC or IMC were rapidly and effectively improved following treatment with efgartigimod and suggested its potential efficacy in alleviating MG symptoms. However, the limitations of these studies include small sample sizes of IMC patients (ranging from 1 to 9 cases) and lack of standard therapies or incomplete follow-up data. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of efgartigimod in a larger and more diverse patient population.

Methods

Participants and study design

This single-center retrospective study utilized data from the MG registry database of the Tangdu Hospital of The Fourth Military Medical University. The study included patients with generalized myasthenia gravis (GMG) who were AChR-Ab-positive and received IVIg or efgartigimod between June 1, 2023, and November 1, 2024. Demographic and clinical data were collected and cross-verified against medical records to ensure accuracy. Inclusion criteria required patients to be aged ≥18 years. IMC was defined as: (i) rapid progression of symptoms affecting bulbar or respiratory muscles within 4 weeks; (ii) classification as Myasthenia Gravis Foundation of America (MGFA) type IIIb or IVb. Exclusion criteria included: (i) dysphagia or dyspnea caused by other conditions; (ii) partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) ≥50 ​mmHg on arterial blood gas analysis at admission; (iii) prior administration of IVIg, efgartigimod or high-dose corticosteroids within 4 weeks before enrollment.

Treatment regimen

In the efgartigimod group, patients received 10 ​mg/kg of efgartigimod via intravenous infusion once a week for 4 consecutive weeks. In the IVIg treatment group, patients were administered IVIg (0.4 ​g/kg/day) for 5 consecutive days. Adjunctive therapies, including pyridostigmine, non-steroidal immunosuppressive, and corticosteroids, were titrated based on patient-specific symptoms. Clinical monitoring was maintained for 4 weeks after the initial treatment.

Evaluation of experimental results

The primary outcomes were defined as the mean change in Myasthenia Gravis Activities of Daily Living (MG-ADL) score from week 1 to week 4 and the Quantitative Myasthenia Gravis (QMG) score from baseline (the initial dose of efgartigimod or IVIg) to weeks 1, 2 and 4 post-treatment. Owing to clinical stability in week 3, most patients in the IVIg group did not return to hospital, resulting in missing QMG data at that time point. However, the MG-ADL scores for week 3 were obtained via telephone follow-up and a self-assessment app. As the MG-ADL score is a patient-reported outcome measure, it can be assessed remotely under the guidance of physicians. Bulbar muscle function was assessed via MG-ADL subitems (speech, swallowing, chewing) and QMG subitems (speech, swallowing). Respiratory muscle performance was evaluated using the MG-ADL respiratory domain and QMG forced vital capacity. For patients receiving pyridostigmine, the drug was withheld the night prior to assessment; in cases of severe symptoms, administration was permitted up to 4 ​h before evaluation. The secondary outcomes included the following: (i) achievement of clinically meaningful improvement (CMI), defined as ≥2-point reduction in MG-ADL or ≥3-point reduction in QMG scores; (ii) minimal symptom expression (MSE) was defined as an MG-ADL score of 0 or 1; (iii) safety profiles were evaluated via medical record review during hospitalization, with all adverse events (AEs) graded per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v5.0.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed with R software (version 4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables were reported as mean (SD) and compared by independent t-test. Categorical variables were reported as percentage (%) and compared by χ2 test or Fisher's exact test. Summary statistics (changes from baseline) were computed for MG-ADL and QMG total scores and subdomains in the efgartigimod (n ​= ​30) and IVIg (n ​= ​21) groups. CMI, MSE, and safety outcomes were summarized with descriptive statistics. To improve data completeness and reduce potential bias, multiple imputations were applied using a random forest algorithm. A two-tailed value of P ​< ​0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Further analysis of changes from baseline in MG-ADL and QMG scores demonstrated that patients in the efgartigimod group exhibited a significantly greater improvement in MG-ADL scores at week 4 compared to those in the IVIg group (P ​< ​0.05). Moreover, QMG scores showed significant improvements in the efgartigimod group at both week 2 (P ​< ​0.001) and week 4 (P ​< ​0.001), indicating superior efficacy over IVIg (Fig. 1). Analysis of the bulbar and respiratory subdomains showed that the MG-ADL respiratory function score was superior in the efgartigimod group compared to the IVIg group, achieving statistical significance at week 4 (P ​= ​0.028). CMI in MG-ADL and QMG scores

In the changes of MG-ADL scores, the proportion of patients achieving significant improvement (≥2 points) in the efgartigimod group and the IVIg group increased progressively with treatment duration. Specifically, at week 1, the proportions of patients with significant improvement (≥3 points) were 83.3 ​% in the efgartigimod group and 71.4 ​% in the IVIg group, respectively. By week 4, these proportions increased to 96.7 ​% and 85.7 ​%. Furthermore, at week 4, the proportion of patients with moderate to significant improvement (≥7 points) was 90.0 ​% in the efgartigimod group, which was higher than the 57.1 ​% observed in the IVIg group (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Pyramid plot of changes in MG-ADL scores 1–4 (a–d) weeks with different treatment regimens.Fig. 2

Safety

The hospitalization duration of IMC patients was shorter in the efgartigimod group compared to the IVIg group, with a significant difference (P ​< ​0.05). Safety profile was generally good. Notably, one patient (4.76 ​%) in the IVIg group progressed to an MC, requiring mechanical ventilation and ultimately resulting in death. In contrast, none of the efgartigimod-treated patients progressed to MC, although two patients experienced re-exacerbations after initial symptom improvement (Supplementary Table 1).

Discussion

In this study, by tracking the changes in MG-ADL and QMG scores, we conducted an in-depth analysis of the efficacy of efgartigimod in patients with AChR-Ab positive MG, focusing on the clinical manifestations and treatment responses of this specific patient population. Additionally, we analyzed the changes in MSE and improvement rates in MG-ADL scores, providing robust evidence for the clinical application of efgartigimod in this population and laying the foundation for future related clinical studies.

Our findings demonstrated the significant clinical improvement provided by efgartigimod in IMC patients, with an acceptable safety profile. From weeks 2–4, improvements in MG-ADL and QMG scores were significantly greater in the efgartigimod group than in the IVIg group, consistent with the results of the ADAPT study [13,14]. Notably, our cohort exhibited larger effect sizes compared to those observed in the ADAPT study. Potential reasons for this discrepancy include the higher baseline scores in the present study (mean MG-ADL: 10.93 vs 9.2; QMG scores: 20.43 vs 16.2). Additionally, racial differences may also represent a potential influencing factor [18].

With the application of tools such as MG-ADL, the importance of Patient-Reported Outcomes (PROs) in clinical research and real-world treatment has been increasingly recognized, particularly through multidimensional assessments of subdomains (e.g., ocular, respiratory, and bulbar functions), which can more accurately reflect changes in patients' conditions and treatment responses [19,20]. Analysis of the bulbar and respiratory subdomains within the MG-ADL and QMG scales revealed improvement trends consistent with those observed in the total scores, although most outcomes lacked statistical significance. Notably, in the respiratory subdomain, the efgartigimod group demonstrated superior MG-ADL scores compared to the IVIg group, with progressive improvement over time. By week 4, the between-group difference further expanded and achieved statistical significance. Future studies with larger sample sizes are warranted to validate the efficacy of efgartigimod, thereby strengthening the evidence base for its clinical utility.

Patients in the efgartigimod group achieved a higher proportion of CMI after one week, with noticeable symptom relief by week 4. Also, by week 4, 96.7 ​% of patients in the efgartigimod group had an MG-ADL improvement ≥3 points and a QMG scores improvement ≥5 points. Additionally, the efgartigimod group showed better performance in MSE, with the proportion of patients reaching MSE steadily increasing over time, while the IVIg group showed smaller improvements. These results indicated that efgartigimod can more rapidly alleviate symptoms and improve quality of life. Given that MG patients typically bear a high disease burden, potentially affecting their physical and mental health, these findings further highlight the potential clinical value of efgartigimod in this patient population [21].

Two thymoma-associated MG patients in the efgartigimod group exhibited initial symptom alleviation during Weeks 1 and 2 in our study. However, following the administration of the third and fourth doses, their symptoms deteriorated and the MG-ADL score reverted to baseline levels observed prior to treatment, although the dosage of concomitant medication remained unchanged and the IgG levels decreased. Following the transition to eculizumab therapy, there was a rapidly progressive improvement in symptoms. A similar phenomenon was reported in the study by Kentaro Kawama et al. [22], where two patients experienced symptom exacerbation and a rebound in AChR antibody titers to pre-treatment levels after multiple cycles of efgartigimod, and one patient's symptoms were significantly improved after switching to eculizumab. MG exacerbation related to anti-AchR antibody overshoot following PLEX has been previously observed [23]. The increased antibody production may be attributed to efgartigimod reducing plasma IgG and autoantibody levels, which in turn could lead to an increase in B cell numbers and the activation of memory B cells [[24], [25], [26]], potentially causing a rebound effect of AChR-Ab. For these patients with symptom exacerbation, eculizumab may provide symptom relief by blocking complement C5 [27], thereby inhibiting the downstream effects of these antibodies.

Efgartigimod and IVIg groups exhibited an acceptable safety profiles in the present study. Of the 9 patients with infection-induced IMC, four were treated with efgartigimod and five with IVIG, all of whom received anti-infective therapy prior to initiation of treatment. Effective symptom control was achieved in all cases, and no worsening of infection was observed. One patient treated with IVIg progressed to MC. Infections were reported in 2 patients receiving efgartigimod and 1 patient treated with IVIg. All infections were mild upper respiratory tract infections (URTIs), with full recovery following treatment. This may be attributed to the selective clearance of IgG by efgartigimod, which does not affect other immunoglobulins such as IgM, IgA, and IgE, thereby not increasing the risk of infection [11,28]. These findings indicate that the superior efficacy and safety of efgartigimod compared to IVIg in the treatment of patients in the IMC.

This current study has several limitations. First, the data from this retrospective analysis are inherently subject to confounding and selection biases. Randomized clinical trials (RCTs) with larger sample sizes are anticipated to validate the results of our study. Second, this study was limited to AChR-Ab ​+ ​patients and did not include other types of patients, such as those positive for muscle-specific tyrosine kinase (MuSK) antibodies. Finally, the conclusions were based on clinical data alone. Future studies should focus on in-depth analysis of biomarkers, including AChR antibodies, complement levels, and cytokine profiles, to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Efgartigimod, as a novel immunosuppressant, is characterized by its rapid symptom relief. Its quick onset of action is of critically importance in preventing patients with MG from progressing into an IMC. The application of this drug is associated with a reduction in the incidence of complications and does not lead to an obviously increase in costs. In sum, it may suggest potential utility as a first-line treatment for patients with IMC.

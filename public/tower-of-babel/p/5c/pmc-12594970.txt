Hybrid glowworm swarm optimization with recurrent deep learning for fault detection in industrial internet of things environment
Authors: G Anitha, Hariprasath Manoharan, Abirami Manoharan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractFault detection (FD) has been important in Industry 4.0 to reduce the cost of manufacturing in the contemporary industry. FD was widely used to determine the health of the medical equipment. Online FD method has been critical in identifying faults in industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) equipment. Today, deep learning (DL) has been developed, which means that deep understanding-related defect detection approaches are gaining more and more popularity, and the extraction of features plays a vital role in such strategies. This can be attributed to the fact that it is difficult to select reliable sensor data manually as input features because of the abundance of sensors. Thus, the proposed research is devoted to the creation of the Hybrid Glowworm Swarm Optimization with Recurrent Deep Learning technique in the IIoT setting Fault Detection (HGSO-RDLFD). The offered HGSO-RDLFD method is aimed at detecting and categorizing faults, as suggested by audio signals in the IIoT setting. To achieve this, the HGSO-RDLFD method has three stages which include feature extraction, fault detection and hyperparameter tuning. Mel spectrogram technique can be used to extract the important features of the audio signal at the initial stage. Next, the HGSO-RDLFD method refers to the repetitive deep learning model that is used to classify faults in the IIoT setting. Then, the HGSO algorithm is played with in the second stage, to achieve the optimum hyperparameter optimization of HCNN-GRU method, and thus lead to the improvement of classification performance. In order to show the potential results of the HGSO-RDLFD approach, a comprehensive experimental analysis is obtained on fault detection dataset. The accuracy of the proposed system was found to be 99.7% that was 5% and 2.4% higher than classical baselines and advanced CNN-LSTM models respectively at a constant statistical significance. The outcomes of the simulation describe the improved results of the HGSO-RDLFD technique compared to the other new methodologies regarding various measures.Keywords: Fault detection, Industrial Internet of Things, Deep learning, Hyperparameter tuning, Industry 4.0Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The core objective of equipment failure detection (FD) is to support identifying equipment failures so that repair of that equipment can be arranged and maintained and lessen the occurrence of equipment over-repair1. In Industry 4.0, FD had a crucial part in the reduction of manufacturing costs in modern industrial field. FD was mostly utilized for the purpose of evaluating health condition of apparatus2. The online FD technique has been instrumental in detecting faults in Internet of Things (IoT)-related industrial equipment3. In this approach, to integrate online model updating and equipment failure detection a method called sliding window was utilised, enabling method to react to variations in the state of apparatus, enriching model precision, and diminishing false alarm rates. The retrieved features will be given as input data into the defect detection method for determining the projected value of the model4. Owing to the rapid development of the IoT, several sensors were given to industrial equipment for monitoring its health. So, a defect detection approach is constructed with the help of the sensor data gained for assessing the probable flaws of equipment5. The use of IoT grants significant potential for increasing the fidelity of industrial equipment by identifying issues. The present technique of IoT can alter variations in operational condition of equipment and rise detection performance of the model6.

Ubiquitous actuation and sensing, which is enabled in dispersed IoT with the integration of (B5G) or 6G), FD is a hot research topic that was concerned with the localization and identification of faults in processes and systems7. FD are of paramount importance in the industry of 6G, particularly when considering failures caused by cyber-attacks; which we envision was of primordial interest in these interconnected airplaces and the dangers they could signify. But FD process is costly and nowhere near being sustainable, as well as that is not very reliable. In this modern industry, information is extremely abundant, the only issue is that it was available in various formats and thus cannot be fully utilized8. The use of different data formats and hence the merging of the acquired data of the same industry event increases the accuracy of the classifier of the event in a particular category of fault, or not fault. IIoT networks necessitate intellectual information processing structures for big data analysis9. From this perspective, DL and artificial intelligence (AI) methods can be highly useful as a means of creating effective, usable outcomes from big data in an IIoT system. For example, DL methods enable the capacities of the system to learn from experience. The effectiveness and efficiency of a DL solution relies upon the nature and features of the data in question in addition to the performance of learning process10.

Eventhough the current state of IIoT fault detection is highly developed, there are three limitations: (i) the recurrent models are not robust to run on noisy audio signals, (ii) no systematic hyperparameter optimization strategies (iii) the lack of generalization to multiple machine IDs. To fill these gaps, a hybrid, adaptive, and scalable deep learning framework is necessary, and therefore, this research study was inspired by the necessity of having a robust, noise-resilient, and optimized fault detection framework that will guarantee reliable IIoT operations in various industrial settings.

The proposed model

In this research, we propose a new Hybrid Glowworm Swarm Optimizationrecurrent Deep Learning Fault Detection (HGSO-RDLFD) framework in automated fault detection of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT) in this study. The main aim of the proposed model is to detect and classify the faults that arise in the industrial settings with the accuracy. In order to accomplish this, HGSO-RDLFD framework combines three main steps: (i) extraction of features based on audio signals, (ii) fault classification with recurrent deep learning, and (iii) hyperparameter optimization with HGSO algorithm. Such a combination guarantees the accurate categorization of faults and the better generalization of the model. Figure 1 shows the general workflow of the HGSO-RDLFD system.

Stage I: featue extraction module

The proposedsystem, the Mel spectrogram method is used to extract discriminative features based on audio signals. This method involves the use of Short-Time Fourier Transform (STFT) to break the audio frames in their frequency components. The linear frequency scale produced by STFT is then transformed to a perceptually based logarithmic Mel scale, and then the signal is processed by a filter bank to represent energy distributions at frequency bands. These descriptions give a succinct but informative description of the spectral properties of the signal. To do the experiment, the raw audio recordings are divided into one-second frames, and they are transformed into Mel spectrograms, which allows the convolutional neural network to be trained successfully. Since audio signals usually have intricate patterns, the Mel spectrogram is an effective approach to highlighting the important frequency and temporal patterns and reducing any unnecessary information. In this work, there was downsampling of all the samples to 8 kHz, a compromise between computational efficiency and adequate frequency resolution. In this work, a Python package named librosa is used for processing data and each parameter is given in the following: (n_mels = 128, n_fft = 1024, hop_length = 512). Lastly, the power to db function was used to scale the calculated power spectrum to a decibel (dB) value, which is more easily interpretable in further learning stages.

Stage II: FD module

At the second phase of the HGSO-RDLFD scheme, a fault classification task is performed with the help of a recurrent deep learning (RDL) model which works with the audio features that have been identified in the first step. Industrial audio data have a high degree of temporal and sequential correlation, recurrent architecture is thus well-adapted to the dynamics of industrial audio data. In this regard, there are three common forms Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) that are explored in this paper. The recurrent models contrast with the traditional feedforward networks because they have internal states that enable them to maintain the contextual information over time steps. This renders them very efficient in the operation of tasks in which the input data is sequential like fault signals in IIoT settings. The RNN is the simplest recurrent network with each unit acting as a short term memory unit that stores and updates data as it processes the sequence. In order to overcome this limitation, more sophisticated versions, such as LSTM and GRU, have gating mechanisms which control the information flow and hence enhance the capacity of the network to memorize long-term dependencies. With the use of these recurrent architectures, the HGSO-RDLFD framework provides an effective modeling of the temporal changes in the signal, and allows better differentiation of normal and faulty operational states. Although useful in the short sequence, the traditional RNNs have difficulties with vanishing and exploding gradients, which restrict their capabilities to learn long-term dependencies. In a bid to deal with this, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks propose memory cells with an input, forget, and output gate that control storage, removal, and retrieval of information. It is a gated structure, which allows LSTMs to store the relevant patterns during long periods and ignore noise. As a result, LSTMs are more effective at classifying normal and faulty states and therefore fit well to tasks of IIoT audio-based fault detection.

Stage II: hyperparameter tuning module

In this paper, the HGSO algorithm was used to optimally tune the HCNN-GRU approach in terms of hyperparameters. The swarm was generally referred to as a group of glowworms in GSO which are scattered randomly in the searching space20. In addition, glowworm makes its own choice in the area of decision . The idea of interaction between glowworms is presented in the following: the intensity of light of glowworms is directly proportional to the level of luciferin, and they communicate to the other glowworm that is available in adjustable neighborhood. The better glowworm place was identified as long as, the intensity of Lucifer is greater. The Glowworm Swarm Optimization (GSO) algorithm simulates the behavior of glowworms that emit light using a chemical substance called luciferin. The brightness of each glowworm is proportional to the quality of its solution, and the agents move toward brighter neighbors to locate optima in the search space. HGSO extends this mechanism by integrating genetic operations such as mutation and crossover to further improve exploration and avoid local optima.

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was repeated five times using different random seeds to ensure that the results of the experiment were reliable. We also conducted paired t-tests to compare HGSO-RDLFD and the best-performing baseline models (MLP, XGBoost, CNN-LSTM). The findings verified accuracy and F1-score improvements that were statistically significant (p < 0.05) in HGSO-RDLFD, which is the proof of the effectiveness of the proposed models.

Conclusion

This research paper introduces a new HGSO-RDLFD model that will be used to automate fault detection (FD) in the world of the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT). The main goal of the suggested system is to correctly detect and categorize faults in the industrial processes. The pipeline that is incorporated in the framework consists of three steps: (i) Mel spectrogram-based feature extraction in order to extract informative audio features, (ii) recurrent deep learning (RDL) models in order to perform effective fault classification, and (iii) Hybrid Glowworm Swarm Optimization (HGSO) in order to optimize the performance of the HCNN-GRU architecture by tuning its hyperparameters. Numerous benchmark FD databases have been experimentally tested to establish that the HGSO-RDLFD methodology is continually superior to the traditional and more sophisticated baselines in various performance indicators. Its effectiveness in achieving greater accuracy, precision, recall and F-score proves the strength of the method. Even though the implementation has yielded positive results, its present form suffers due to its use of a fixed number of machine IDs and the cost of computational complexity of HGSO optimization. Future research will involve large-scale validation with various industrial data sets, expansion of the framework with graph neural networks to better find inter-device dependencies and optimization of the system to run lightweight on edge devices. These improvements are designed to support scalable, fault detection, and real-time, resource-efficient fault detection within the complex IIoT environments.

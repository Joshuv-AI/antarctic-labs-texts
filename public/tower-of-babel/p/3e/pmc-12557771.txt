Association between coronary artery calcium and the progression of calcific aortic valve disease: a hospital population-based observational retrospective cohort study
Authors: Hun-Tae Kim, Jeong Hun Seo, Jun Ho Bae, Tae Jin Kim, You Min Kim, Hyunsu Jo, Sang Hoon Lee, Hang Jae Chung, Dong Sun Han
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivesTo explore the association between the degree of coronary artery calcium (CAC) and the progression of calcific aortic valve disease (CAVD).DesignA single-centre retrospective cohort study using a hospital-based database.ParticipantsA total of 2898 patients who underwent coronary CT angiography and serial echocardiograms at ≥6 months apart were included. Initial echocardiography was performed within 6 months from the time of CCTA.Outcome measuresCAC was divided into four groups: 0, 1–99, 100–399 and ≥400 (Agatston units, AU). The progression of CAVD was defined in two ways: progression 1 as at least one grade of progression, progression 2 as at least moderate aortic stenosis (AS) at follow-up.ResultsAt the initial CAVD grade, patients with at least mild AS tended to increase with increasing CAC (p<0.001). During a median follow-up of 3.2 years (IQR, 1.8–5.0 years), 101 patients (3.5%) experienced progression 1 and 24 patients (0.8%) suffered progression 2. There was a statistically significant increase in risk of progression 1 in CAC 100–399 and ≥400 groups than CAC 0 and 1–99 groups (p<0.001). In progression 2, the CAC≥400 group showed a higher progression rate than the other groups (p<0.001). In multivariable logistic regression, age (adjusted OR (aOR), 1.06; 95% CI, 1.02 to 1.09; p=0.001), CAC≥400 (aOR, 2.55 vs CAC 0 as a reference group; 95% CI, 1.19 to 5.46; p=0.016), body mass index (aOR, 1.09; 95% CI, 1.02 to 1.17; p=0.010) and initial peak aortic jet velocity (aOR, 1.21 per 0.1 m/s increase; 95% CI, 1.14 to 1.28; p<0.001) were associated with progression 1. In progression 2, CAC≥400 (aOR, 44.5 vs CAC 0 as a reference group; 95% CI, 1.09 to 1810; p=0.045), eGFR (aOR, 0.88; 95% CI, 0.80 to 0.97; p=0.007), left ventricular mass index (aOR, 1.05; 95% CI, 1.01 to 1.10; p=0.029) and initial peak aortic jet velocity (aOR, 4.21 per 0.1 m/s increase; 95% CI, 1.86 to 9.53; p=0.001) were significant determinants.ConclusionsCAC was significantly associated with the progression of CAVD. Particularly, CAC≥400 was linked to progression toward significant AS.Keywords: Computed tomography, Echocardiography, Valvular heart disease

Introduction

Calcific aortic valve disease (CAVD) is a progressive disorder that ranges from focal valve thickening without obstruction of ventricular outflow, termed aortic valve sclerosis (AVS), to severe calcification with impaired leaflet motion, termed aortic stenosis (AS).1 CAVD shares similarities with coronary atherosclerosis in two aspects. First, the initial lesions in CAVD and coronary atherosclerosis include lipid deposition and focal sclerosis.2 3 Second, risk factors in the progression of coronary atherosclerosis, such as dyslipidaemia, hypertension and male sex, may also affect the development and progression of CAVD.4,6

Although coronary atherosclerosis and CAVD share many atheromatous features, not all patients with coronary atherosclerosis develop CAVD.7 Prior studies reported that coronary artery calcification and aortic valve calcification (AVC) are closely related as they have similar pathophysiology in development and progression.8,11 However, it remains unclear whether the degree of coronary artery calcium (CAC) and the progression of CAVD are positively correlated. Therefore, proving the association between the degree of CAC and the progression of CAVD could improve follow-up and treatment strategies. The aim of this study is to explore the association between the degree of CAC and the progression of CAVD from a retrospective registry of coronary CT angiography (CCTA) and serial echocardiographic examinations.

Methods

Design and study population

We retrospectively included 2898 patients who underwent CCTA and ≥2 echocardiographic examinations ≥6 months apart at Pohang Semyeong Christianity Hospital during the years 2010–2023. Initial echocardiography was performed within 6 months from the time of CCTA. Patients without Doppler measurements, at least moderate mitral or tricuspid valve stenosis or regurgitation and at least moderate aortic regurgitation, left ventricular dysfunction (left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) <50%), or a history of cardiac surgery were excluded. Flow diagram. CAC, coronary artery calcium; CCTA, coronary CT angiography; LVEF, left ventricular ejection fraction.

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of Pohang Semyeong Christianity Hospital (IRB File No. PSMCHIRB-2024-31) involved in this research, and the need for informed consent was waived because of the retrospective nature of the study.

Clinical data

At the time of CCTA, relevant cardiovascular risk factors were assessed by a complete review of patient medical records (smoking, body mass index, blood pressure, medication and laboratory data) to identify mediators for CAVD progression. Dyslipidaemia was defined as total cholesterol >200 mg/dL and/or use of lipid-lowering therapy; diabetes mellitus was defined as fasting plasma glucose >126 mg/dL and/or use of anti-diabetic medication; hypertension was defined as systolic blood pressure >140 mm Hg and/or use of anti-hypertensive medication; coronary artery disease (CAD) was defined as previously documented myocardial infarction and/or coronary artery stenosis with a lumen diameter >50% on angiography; peripheral artery disease (PAD) was defined as an ankle-brachial index less than 0.9 and/or peripheral artery stenosis >50% on angiography. Cerebrovascular accident (CVA) was defined as the presence of neurologic symptoms and/or abnormal lesions on brain imaging, and atrial fibrillation (AF) was defined as a documented irregular rhythm on ECG regardless of duration.

Coronary artery calcium score

All examinations were performed with a 640-slice CT scanner (Aquilion ONE, Toshiba, Tokyo, Japan). The CAC score was determined using the Agatston method. Agatston scoring is performed using semi-automated software to identify areas of calcification (above 130 Hounsfield units), which are then weighted based on the maximum attenuation density and summed. CAC score was categorised as 0, 1–99, 100–399 and ≥400 (Agatston units, AU) by severity.12

Echocardiography

Comprehensive transthoracic echocardiography was performed using commercially available equipment (Vivid E9 from GE Healthcare, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, USA, or Acuson SC2000 from Siemens Medical Solutions, Mountain View, California, USA). Standard M-mode, 2-dimensional and colour Doppler imaging were performed in parasternal, suprasternal, substernal and apical views with positional adjustment of the patient. At the time of initial and follow-up echocardiographic examinations, AVS was defined as irregular leaflet thickening or focally increased echogenicity on 2-dimensional echocardiography and peak aortic jet velocity (Vmax) <2.0 m/s on Doppler echocardiography. AS was classified as mild (aortic valve area (AVA), 1.5–2.0 cm2; Vmax, 2.0–3.0 m/s), moderate (AVA, 1.0–1.5 cm2; Vmax, 3.0–4.0 m/s) or severe (AVA, <1.0 cm2; Vmax, >4.0 m/s). The progression of CAVD was defined in two ways: progression 1 as at least one grade of progression (eg, from mild AS to moderate or severe AS), and progression 2 as at least moderate AS on follow-up. Anatomical measurements were performed according to the current guidelines.13

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Results are expressed as mean±SD and were compared with the ANOVA test or Kruskal-Wallis test among patients with CAC 0, 1–99, 100–399 and ≥400. Categorical variables are presented as percentages and were compared with the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

The cumulative incidence of progressions 1 and 2 was evaluated by a univariate Kaplan-Meier analysis, and the level of significance was assessed with the log-rank test. A receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve was created to depict the predictive accuracy of CAC scores in predicting progression 1 or 2. The area under the curve (AUC) with a 95% CI and ideal cut-off points is also shown where appropriate. Multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to test for independent association of CAC score ranges with progression 1 or 2, after adjusting for clinically relevant variables and variables with p<0.20 in the univariate analysis and carefully avoiding collinearity. The adjusted variables were age, sex, body mass index, smoking status, hypertension, dyslipidaemia, diabetes, haemoglobin, C-reactive protein (CRP), blood urea nitrogen (BUN), estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) level, LVEF, left atrial volume index (LAVI), early diastolic velocity of the mitral annulus (E/e’) ratio, right ventricular systolic pressure (RVSP) and initial Vmax.

P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using the R statistical software programme (V. 4.3.1; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) and SPSS software V. 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, USA).

Patient and public involvement

Participants were not involved in determining the research question or outcome measures, nor were they involved in recruitment, design or implementation of the study. Participants were not asked for advice on the interpretation of results.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Among the 2898 patients, those with CAC scores of 0, 1–99, 100–399 and ≥400 were 1,122, 786, 556 and 434 patients, respectively. Compared with patients in the lower CAC groups, those in the higher CAC groups were older (p<0.001), male (p<0.001) and had comorbidities such as hypertension (p<0.001), dyslipidaemia (p<0.001), diabetes (p<0.001), CAD (p<0.001), PAD (p<0.001), CVA (p<0.001) or AF (p=0.003) (table 1). Medications for comorbidities such as aspirin (p<0.001), angiotensin receptor blockers (p<0.001), beta blockers (p<0.001), calcium channel blockers (p<0.001) and statins (p<0.001) were prescribed more often in the higher CAC groups than in the lower CAC groups. Higher CAC groups had higher levels of CRP (p=0.036), BUN (p<0.001), glucose (p<0.001) and total cholesterol (p<0.001) and lower levels of haemoglobin (p<0.001) and eGFR (p<0.001) than lower CAC groups.

Association of coronary artery calcium with the progression of calcific aortic valve disease

During a median follow-up of 3.2 years (IQR, 1.8–5.0 years), 101 patients (3.5%) experienced progression 1 and 24 patients (0.8%) exhibited progression 2. There was a significantly increased risk of progression 1 in those with CAC 100–399 and ≥400 than in those with CAC 0 and 1–99 (p<0.001) (figure 3). In progression 2, the CAC≥400 group showed a higher progression rate than the other groups (p<0.001) (figure 4). In ROC analysis, CAC had poor ability to predict risk of progression 1 (online supplemental figure 1A). CAC 100–399 had a good ability to predict risk of progression 2 (AUC=0.815) (online supplemental figure 1B).

In univariate analysis, variables associated with progressions 1 and 2 are listed in online supplemental table 1. Peripheral artery disease, cerebrovascular accident and medications such as aspirin, angiotensin receptor blockers and loop diuretics were not included in the multivariate analysis. In multivariable logistic regression, age (adjusted OR (aOR), 1.06; 95% CI, 1.02 to 1.09; p=0.001), CAC≥400 (aOR, 2.55 vs CAC 0 as a reference group; 95% CI, 1.19 to 5.46; p=0.016), body mass index (aOR, 1.09; 95% CI, 1.02 to 1.17; p=0.010) and initial Vmax (aOR, 1.21 per 0.1 m/s increase; 95% CI, 1.14 to 1.28; p<0.001) were associated with progression 1 (table 3). Discussion

The main finding of this retrospective cohort study is that CAC is significantly associated with the progression of CAVD. On initial CCTA and echocardiography, higher CAC correlated with a greater proportion of at least mild AS. CAC≥100 was associated with at least one grade of progression of CAVD and CAC≥400 with progression to at least moderate AS (figure 5).

CAC≥100 is significantly associated with at least one grade of progression of CAVD. CAC≥400 correlates with progression to at least moderate AS at follow-up. AS, aortic stenosis; CAC, coronary artery calcium; CAVD, calcific aortic valve disease.

Despite similarities in the clinical factors associated with coronary atherosclerosis and CAVD, the relationship between coronary atherosclerosis development and CAVD progression remains unclear. The Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA), which enrolled 6809 participants who underwent non-contrast cardiac CT, showed that AVC was independently associated with increasing severity of CAC.8 In a large multi-centre clinical trial of participants with known or suspected CAD who underwent serial CCTA, the overall burden of coronary atherosclerosis was associated with AVC at baseline, but AVC progression was associated only with progression of calcified plaque.9 Although these two studies showed that coronary atherosclerosis and CAVD were positively correlated on CT, they did not evaluate the haemodynamic progression of AS because of a lack of echocardiographic data.

Echocardiography is a cheap, safe and widely used method of assessing AS severity in a clinical setting. International guidelines recommend grading AS severity using haemodynamic assessments. In this study, patients who underwent CCTA and consecutive echocardiograms showed that a greater extent of CAC was significantly associated with at least one grade of progression of CAVD (progression 1) and progression to at least moderate AS at the time of follow-up (progression 2). This result suggests that coronary atherosclerosis and CAVD do not simply share a quantitative relationship with calcification, but that CAC may play a role in the haemodynamic progression of CAVD. The association between the extent of CAC and the progression of CAVD warrants further research.

Although coronary atherosclerosis and CAVD show similar pathophysiology at their onset, more mature lesions exhibit differences.14 15 The early pathobiology of CAVD and atherosclerosis involves lipid accumulation, inflammation and calcification.16 17 Calcification predominates in more advanced stages of AS, driving valve stiffness and narrowing.18 A recent study reported that Lp(a) is associated with new-onset AVC but not with AVC progression.19 In contrast to de novo development of AVC, once calcium is detectable in the aortic valve, traditional cardiovascular risk factors play much less of a role in the progression of CAVD.20 21 In a large cohort study, AVC≥100 was an independent predictor of total mortality and cardiovascular death in patients with CAC<100 and not in those with CAC≥100.22 This finding suggests that despite similarities in the clinical risk factors associated with CAVD and coronary atherosclerosis, haemodynamic factors such as mechanical stress are the primary mechanism of initiation and progression of CAVD. Previous studies have shown that changes in Doppler echocardiographic parameters such as Vmax and AVA can predict the haemodynamic progression of AS.23 24 In this study, CAC≥400 and higher Vmax at baseline were significantly associated with at least one grade of CAVD progression or progression to moderate AS in the multivariable analyses. This finding supports that both atherosclerotic and haemodynamic factors are involved in the progression of CAVD. However, the reasons for the disparity in the underlying mechanisms of calcification progression in coronary atherosclerosis and CAVD remain unclear.

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective nature of the study means that other potential confounding variables not included in the analysis could have affected the results. For instance, we did not measure Lp(a), a biomarker for CAVD progression. However, we do not think the check for Lp(a) is routine in clinical practice. Second, only patients with more than two echocardiograms were enrolled, and those who experienced clinical events before follow-up echocardiography were excluded, so selection bias was inevitable. In particular, the lack of standardisation in follow-up echocardiography time could lead to unfair findings. Accordingly, this study limits the ability to make conclusions on the causal effects of CAC on the progression of CAVD. Third, the characteristics of coronary plaque could not be assessed in this study. Fourth, this study did not investigate clinical events such as aortic valve replacement (only 14 patients). Finally, we limited the participants to a single centre and a single ethnicity. Hence, our findings should be expanded and further verified in well-controlled prospective studies.

Conclusions

CAC was significantly associated with the progression of CAVD. Patients with CAC≥100 were associated with at least one grade of CAVD progression; particularly, CAC≥400 was linked to progression toward significant AS.

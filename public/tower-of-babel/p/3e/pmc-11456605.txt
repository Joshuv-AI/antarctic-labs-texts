Optimization study and application of box-behnken model for probing eggshell supported transition metals based catalysts to synthesize hydrazone & dihydropyrimidinones
Authors: Rabia Perveen, Fiza Latif, Minahil Abbas, Khurshid Ayub, Sehrish Sarfaraz, Muhammad Saeed, Sobia Rana, Mariya Al-Rashida, Muhammad Asif Nawaz, Abdul Hameed
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Solid supported catalysts have several synthetic applications. Herein, finely ground eggshells were used as a solid support for the preparation of transition metal (Ni, Zn, Cu, Sn and Co) based catalysts to synthesize 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (3) and dihydropyrimidinones (7 and 8). The effect of catalyst load, time and temperature on product yield was studied. Box Behnken Model was employed, and three predictors named catalyst amount (A), reaction time (B), and reaction temperature (C) were used to find the correlation of the predictors with the yield. Second order polynomial equation was used to estimate the effects of these factors. According to the statistical model, about 12% increase in yield was observed as a result of one-unit increase in reaction time while all other terms were kept constant. The values of S (18.1616) and R2 (71.2%) indicate that the statistical model gave an adequate fit to data. Quadratic model for the response surface was used for the analysis of variance (ANOVA) results, the larger F-values, and smaller p-values indicated that the predictors are in good agreement. The linear model terms of predictors were found to be significantly effective for yield (P < 0.05). The response surface and contour plots were also in agreement with the predicted model.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-74876-6.

Introduction

In the field of organic synthesis, solid support catalysts have been extensively employed. For more than a century, catalysts have been employed successfully in the chemical industry. The most common method for preparation of the metal supported catalyst is to impregnate a highly permeable support with an aqueous solution of a metal salt. Such catalysts require solid support since it helps to maximize the catalytic potential of the supported metal1. Moreover, the catalyst stability can also be affected by the quality of the metal(s) and the support.

Catalyst support is essential for metal supported catalysts because it enables the supported metal to work as a catalytically active core. Such catalytic materials provide a large surface area, are highly inert and can disperse metal particles widely. The chemical and physical properties of the surfaces also influence their catalytic behaviour. Additionally, the solid support catalyst can be easily separated via filtration. Many reagents and catalysts can be supported by biomaterials which have the advantage of reusability, biodegradability, low cost and low toxicity.

So far, chitosan which has been derived from the crustacean shells has been successfully used as a palladium support2. Eggshell is a substantial solid waste from processing of food and industrial operations3. Since eggshell waste was historically deemed useless, it is usually disposed off in landfills without being pre-treated4. The development of eggshell as a value-added product has received a lot of attention in recent years. Some of the main applications proposed for eggshell include its use as a source of potential bone replacement material5, use as a raw material for calcium phosphate bioceramics6, and its use as an adsorbent for eliminating ionic contaminants mostly from aqueous solutions at a reduced cost4. Because of the growing concern and interest towards environmentally friendly processes, agricultural waste derived heterogeneous catalysts are being prepared and employed as environmentally friendly heterogeneous catalysts7–9. Eggshell supported by zinc, iron and pyrolysis waste has been studied as an effective catalyst support in trans-esterification of clean and discarded vegetable oils10–15. Transition metal based catalysts have diverse applications16. In our study, we used waste eggshell for the preparation of solid support catalyst. By using eggshell powder, transition metal salt based solid support catalyst has been synthesized, and then used in the synthesis of Schiff base i.e. 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (3)that belongs to an important class of biologically active molecules17, as well as dihydropyrimidinones (7 and 8).

Furthermore, a Box–Behnken design18 has been employed to identify optimized conditions for this organic reaction. A Box–Behnken design of experiments can be used to represent the response surface. The form of the responsive surface can be visualized in 3-dimensional space or by utilizing calibration curves. From a small number of observations, the RSM method will not only assess optimal conditions, but will also predict outcomes and design an efficient process that provides the data. Analysis of variance (ANOVA) is frequently used to assess the significance and adequacy of RSM’s mathematical models.

This quantitative technique substantially decreases the number of tests necessary by taking into account the interactions between the experimental variables. For estimating response function coefficients, the basic RSM design in the Box–Behnken design (BBD) works well. The form of the response surface or response can be graphically depicted in three-dimensional space as contour plots. The structure of the interaction between the response and the independent variables is unknown in wide-ranging. A second order Box-Behnken experimental design (BBD) based on three levels [lower (-), middle (0), and higher (+)] with the aid of response surface methodology (RSM) was used to investigate the effect of various reaction parameters on the percentage yield of synthesized products.

Experimental

Chemistry

The various starting materials including 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine, benzophenone, different transition metal salts that were used for this reaction were purchased from difference sources and used without further purification unless stated otherwise. The solvents used include ethanol, distilled water and dichloromethane, solvents used for the synthesis and purification purposes were of analytical or HPLC grade. The progress of the catalyzed reactions for synthesis of compounds 3, 7 and 8 was monitored by the TLC. The water used was either deionized or distilled. Reactions were performed by using round bottom flasks of different sizes which were oven dried before starting the reactions. The data acquired through “response surface methodology” for the optimization of reaction conditions to achieve optimal yield was obtained by using MINITAB-16.

Preparation of egg shell supported catalysts

Waste eggshells (ESs) were collected form household waste and washed with tap water. The raw eggshells were then placed in boiling water and allowed to boil for 15–20 min. The eggshells were first dried in the air and then in the microwave for 10–15 min. The dried eggshells were then ground in a blender until a fine powder was produced19. The eggshell powder and corresponding metal salts (w/w 3:1 ratio) in aqueous solution were mixed by continuous stirring, and then water was removed to allow adsorption of the metal salt on eggshell powder. Two metal salts supported catalysts, ES/NiCl2 and ES/ZnCl2 powder were prepared via this method.

Synthesis of schiff base 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (3)

In a typical one pot synthesis, 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine (0.5 mmol) and benzophenone (0.5 mmol) were added in an oven dried round bottom flask containing 10 mL of ethanol along with varying amounts (10–30 mg) of NiCl2/ES or ZnCl2/ES powder as a catalyst. The reaction mixture was heated with constant stirring at varying temperatures to find the optimum reaction conditions (Tables 1 and 2). On the completion of reaction, product was filtered and dried. Results and discussion

The waste eggshell powder was used as a solid support to adsorb transition metal salts on it and to use it as a catalyst for subsequent organic reactions. The metal salts of nickel (Ni), copper (Cu), cobalt (Co), Tin (Sn) and Zinc (Zn) were adsorbed on the eggshell powder to prepare corresponding solid supported catalysts to employ them in the synthesis of 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone as Schiff bass (3) and dihydropyrimidones (7 & 8) (Scheme 1).

Box behnken model

For the analysis of optimization studies, MINITAB-16 software was used. Three central points were used to examine the effect of three variables (catalyst amount, reaction temperature, and reaction time) in this investigation, which resulted in various experimental reactions (Table 3)23. In order to determine the best conditions, we applied the DOE methodology. In MINITAB-16 software, the response surface and contour plots are generated using the Box Behnken design. A three-level and three-factor based BBD was chosen. The variables were coded using low (–1) and high (+ 1) values, as indicated in Table 4. The yield of the reaction product (2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone) is the experimental response between the selected criteria and the response, a functional link was formed.

Conclusion

In Summary, the transition metal salts of nickel (Ni), zinc (Zn), copper (Cu), tin (Sn) and cobalt (Co) was adsorbed on finally ground eggshell (rich in calcium carbonate) to prepare solid support catalysts. Utilizing eggshell as a solid support material may not only reduce waste management issues, but also will add value to a waste material that would otherwise be discarded. The optimum conditions in terms of catalytic load, reaction time and temperature were investigated for the synthesis of 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone (3) and dihydropyrimidinones (7) and (8) catalyzed by these newly prepared catalysts. Box Behnken Design was utilized for analyzing the optimum conditions. Three predictors (catalyst amount, reaction time, and reaction temperature) were selected, the impact of these factors was estimated using a second order polynomial equation. The main, association, and quadratic effects of these three variables on the selected response Y were assessed using a Box-Behnken observational study with response surface methodology. For analysis of variance (ANOVA) results, a quadratic model for the response surface is utilized; greater F-values and smaller p-values signified that the predictors were in good agreement. The Taguchi design was used to determine the optimal condition and to identify the experiment’s control elements. The experimental values and the predicted values of catalyst amount, reaction time, and reaction temperature respectively are very close to each other and give 72% yield, which indicates that the model is statistically valid.

Disparities in the diagnostic efficacy of radiomics models in predicting various degrees of cognitive impairment in patients with cerebral small vessel disease
Authors: Bingqin Huang, Wei Zheng, Ronghua Mu, Peng Yang, Xin Li, Fuzhen Liu, Xiaoyan Qin, Xiqi Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAim to validate the diagnostic efficacy of radiomics models for predicting various degrees of cognitive impairment in patients with cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD).MethodsParticipants were divided into mild cognitive impairment group (mild-CSVD group) and sever cognitive impairment group (sever-CSVD group) according to Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) performance, 98 gender-age-education matched subjects served as normal controls. Radiomic features were extracted from the segmented hippocampus using PyRadiomics. The feature preprocessing involved replacing missing values with the mean, applying stratified random sampling to allocate subjects into training (80%) and testing (20%) sets, ensuring balance among the three classes (normal controls, mild-CSVD group, and sever-CSVD group). A feature selection method was applied to identify discriminative radiomic features, with the optimal texture feature chosen for developing diagnostic models. Performance was evaluated in both the training and testing sets using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.ResultsThe radiomics model achieved an accuracy of 0.625, an AUC of 0.593, a sensitivity of 0.828, and a specificity of 0.316 in distinguishing mild-CSVD group from normal controls. When distinguishing mild-CSVD group from sever-CSVD group, the radiomics model reached an accuracy of 0.683, an AUC of 0.660, a sensitivity of 0.167, and a specificity of 0.897. Similarly, in distinguishing sever-CSVD group from normal controls, the radiomics model exhibited an accuracy of 0.781, an AUC of 0.818, a sensitivity of 0.538, and a specificity of 0.947.ConclusionRadiomics model based on hippocampal texture had disparities in the diagnostic efficacy of radiomics models in predicting various degrees of cognitive impairment in patients with CSVD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12880-024-01431-0.Keywords: Cerebral small vessel disease, Cognitive impairment, Radiomics, Hippocampal texture, Automatically segment

Introduction

Cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) is commonly acknowledged as the primary cause of vascular cognitive impairment, contributing to approximately 45% of cases of vascular dementia [1–3]. Several studies have demonstrated a dose-response relationship, linking white matter hyperintensity and lacunar infarctions with the severity of cognitive impairment [4, 5]. Moreover, a significant correlation has been identified between the total burden of CSVD and cognitive function [5]. It is crucial to recognize the heterogeneity among patients in terms of the type and severity of cognitive impairment, which may not completely align with the extent of macroscopic lesions [6]. This discrepancy could be attributed to the presence of mixed pathological changes in aging and the burden of CSVD, leading to a reduction in the predictive accuracy of individual imaging markers for cognitive impairment [7]. CSVD often coexist with neurodegenerative lesions, mainly showing impaired cognitive function and mood disorders [8]. CSVD is identified by abnormal amide proton transfer intensity values across various brain regions, with the extent of these irregularities linked to the severity of cognitive impairment. Amide proton transfer imaging plays a role in partially identifying and predicting the onset of CSVD-relatated cognitive impairment [9].

Brain structure changes were independently associated with cognitive decline and played a crucial role in impaired cognitive function (10–11). Especially, hippocampal atrophy played a key role in memory, navigation, and cognition [12]. For example, it has been shown that abnormalities in hippocampus volume, shape, and texture may serve as the neuroimaging biomarkers of early cognitive dysfunction of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) (13–14). Meanwhile, the atrophy of the hippocampal subregions was found in subjects with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) and AD, which indicated that the hippocampus and hippocampus subregions played an important role in identifying cognitive dysfunction [15]. Despite the hippocampus was widely studied in cognitive function, there were few study predicting cognitive impairment based on the hippocampus texture in CSVD patients. Therefore, understanding the role of the hippocampus in cognitive dysfunction in CSVD is of great clinical value for intervening in the development of CSVD.

Quantitative and multi-dimensional features extracted from medical images using radiomic technology can more accurately reflect tissue pathological changes, thereby improving the accuracy of disease diagnosis. These dimensional features can also be used as a morphological method to further validate the biological significance of texture features [16]. Studies have shown that texture features are more advantageous in diagnosing AD and amnestic MCI compared to traditional structural models [17]. In addition, abnormal hippocampal texture appear at the early stage of AD, indicating that structural changes in the hippocampus can be used as an imaging biomarker of early cognitive impairment [14]. Similarly, identification of hippocampal features is one of the important factor for diagnosis of cognitive impairment in CSVD, and a previous study has demonstrated altered microstructuration of the hippocampus in CSVD [18]. Therefore, the radiomic features of the hippocampus can be used as a potential diagnostic method to detect the disease progression and cognitive impairment of CSVD.

The early diagnosis of cognitive dysfunction has a certain clinical value for the treatment and prognosis of diseases, and can reduce the speed of disease progression through early detection and early treatment. At present, little research has been done on predicting cognitive function in CSVD patients by hippocampal textures. While early detection of cognitive impairment in patients with CSVD is crucial, there exists limited research on predicting mild cognitive impairment within this cohort. This constraint arises mainly from the challenge that minor alterations in the hippocampus may not offer adequate distinctiveness to yield high diagnostic efficacy in detecting subtle cognitive decline.

The study aimed to validate the diagnostic efficacy of radiomics models based on hippocampal texture for predicting varying degrees of cognitive impairment in CSVD patients. Three radiomics models were developed: the first model for distinguishing mild cognitive impairment from normal controls, the second model for distinguishing mild cognitive impairment from severe cognitive impairment, and the third model for distinguishing severe cognitive impairment from the normal control population.

Methods

Subjects

This study was conducted at Nanxishan Hospital of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region from May 2020 to June 2021. A total of 304 participants were recruited. All participants underwent standard brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) scans and the Beijing version of the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) [19]. Participants were categorized into three groups based on their MoCA scores: mild-CSVD group with MoCA scores between 18 and 26, sever-CSVD group with MoCA scores less than 17 [9]. Demographic information, including gender, age, and education, was collected for all participants. Ninety-eight gender-, age-, and education-matched subjects with normal MoCA performance (MoCA scores ≥ 27) served as the normal control group (NC). Furthermore, all participants underwent a brain MRI examination within one week before the assessments. The detailed MRI data acquisition protocols were outlined in Supplementary Material 2, and the visual assessment of CSVD imaging markers on MRI images was provided in Supplementary Material 3.

The study received approval from the Medical Ethics Committee of Nanxishan Hospital of Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region (2020NXSYEC-006). Written informed consent was obtained from all subjects or their legal guardians.

Hippocampal segmentation

This study employed an UNet-based deep learning neural network model for hippocampal segmentation. The detailed process of hippocampal segmentation was elucidated as follows.

The training process followed the original Unet architecture, which includes four down-sampling and four up-sampling steps to maintain the original feature map size. Each step consists of eight blocks containing two convolutional layers, an activation layer, and a max-pooling layer. During down-sampling, the feature map size doubles each time, reaching 1024 × 1024 after four steps. Up-sampling follows the same procedure.

The Unet frame can be divided into three parts: the first part was the backbone feature extraction part, which was the stack of convolution, ReLU function and maximum pooling. Five preliminary feature maps were obtained by employing this part. In the second step, these five feature maps can be used for feature fusion. The second part was to strengthen feature extraction. Five preliminary feature maps obtained from the backbone were used for up-sampling, feature fusion was carried out to obtain a final feature layer that integrates all features, both covered the deep global features and shallow local features. The method of feature fusion was to up-sample and stack the feature layers simultaneously. The third part was the prediction part, which used the last feature layer obtained to classify each feature point, that is, to classify each pixel point.

The Unet network employed a full convolutional neural network architecture. The left part of the network was dedicated to feature extraction, utilizing 3 × 3 convolutions and 2 × 2 max pooling. Each block consisted of two convolutional layers followed by a ReLU activation function.On the right side of the network was a feature fusion network, where the feature maps generated by upsampling were concatenated with those from the left part. Upsampling enabled low-resolution images with high-level abstract features to become high-resolution while preserving these features. These high-resolution feature maps were then concatenated with the low-level surface features from the left side.After two convolutional operations, the final feature map was generated. Two convolutions with a kernel size of 1 × 1 were used for classification to obtain the final score heatmap. The sigmoid function was applied to the classification result, and backpropagation was performed based on classifications with relatively high probabilities.

In addition, two modules, semantic-aware normalization (SAN) and semantic-aware whitening (SAW), were integrated into the Unet network. The output features generated from the left side of the Unet network were fed into the SAN module to conduct semantic-aware center alignment, enhancing the ability to distinguish the distribution of each class. This module established normalized true values for the output feature maps, which were obtained through normalization of the feature maps extracted by the Unet network’s encoder. A pre-segmented branch was introduced to the SAN module, guided by down-sampled ground truth labels, to introduce relatively accurate semantic class information. However, as the segmentation from the pre-segmented branch may not be entirely accurate, direct multiplication of the feature map afterward might overly emphasize features in regions not belonging to the current category. Hence, the author proposed a class-level feature optimization module (CRF) to refine the features. Maxpool and Avgpool operations were applied to expand on the channel dimension, smoothing out error information introduced by pre-segmentation to some extent. The CRF-optimized features were then passed through Regional Normalization (RN) for final normalization operations.Overall, the integration of these modules enhances the Unet network’s ability to capture semantic information and produce more accurate segmentation results.

After the SAN module, the normalized features were passed to the subsequent module, SAW, to conduct semantic-aware distributed alignment. The term “whitening” refers to standardizing the feature output, and SAW divides a group of channels that have a greater impact on each class. This ensures minimal correlation between feature channels, allowing each feature to serve a unique purpose. These two additional modules inserted between the encoder and decoder significantly enhance the model’s generalization ability. SAN and SAW assist the model in maintaining performance as much as possible, even in scenarios of inconsistent data.

The features processed by SAW were then input into the decoder part of the Unet architecture. Similar to the down-sampling process, up-sampling was performed, but with max pooling replaced by 2 × 2 up-sampling. This resulted in doubling the dimensions of each feature map to recover the original size. After four rounds of up-sampling, the model generated the final scores for each class. The probability output of the scores was obtained using the sigmoid function, and the model predicted the class probabilities by comparing them with the one-hot encoding of the actual classes.

We conducted pre-training of the UNet model using 5-fold cross-validation. After training, we obtained 5 models trained on different subsets of data and evaluated their performance on respective validation sets, yielding Dice coefficients of 0.922141, 0.928488, 0.922928, 0.931129, and 0.926788. The loss curves of each subset during the training process were included in Supplementary Material 4.

Feature selection

The radiomic features were extracted using Pyradiomics (http://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io), based on 3D MRI T1-weighted images. A total of 1,197 hippocampal radiomic features were subsequently extracted. These features included first-order features, shape features, Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) features, Gray Level Size Zone Matrix (GLSZM) features, Gray Level Run Length Matrix (GLRLM) features, as well as Neighboring Gray Tone Difference Matrix (NGTDM) features and Gray Level Dependence Matrix (GLDM) features.

Before performing feature selection, the feature values of the hippocampus were normalized using z-scores ((x − µ)/σ), where x refers to the functional value, µ represents the mean value of this characteristic across all patient cohorts, and σ denotes the corresponding standard deviation.Due to the high complexity of the extracted features, there was a risk of overfitting in the analysis. Therefore, it is necessary to use the Spearman correlation coefficient and the Lasso algorithm to construct a penalty function and obtain a more precise model. The penalty function compresses some coefficients to zero, aiming to enhance the predictive accuracy and interpretability of the model. The Spearman rank correlation coefficient can evaluate the correlation between the original variables and factors, thereby determining which variables should be retained during the dimension reduction process. In this study, by calculating the Spearman correlation coefficient, for any pair of features with a correlation coefficient greater than 0.9, indicating a high correlation between them, we choose to retain only one of them. The resulting dataset composed of these selected and standardized features will be used for subsequent machine learning model training.The Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) is a popular high-dimensional data analysis method that can be used to improve prediction accuracy and interpretability. This method estimates the regression coefficients for each feature and shrinks them towards zero, avoiding overfitting and resulting in superior predictive performance and the exclusion of irrelevant features. In this study, we used LASSO to select an optimized subset of features for building the final model. To achieve this, we employed 5-fold cross-validation to tune the regularization parameter λ, optimizing it for the lowest model bias. Subsequently, we used the optimized λ to determine the number of features, selecting the most predictive subset of features and evaluating the corresponding coefficients.

Model constrcuction

The radiomic features were computed based on the weighted coefficients of the selected features, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Subsequently, Python Scikit-learn was utilized for developing machine learning classification models in each feature group. K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) machine learning classification models were applied for model construction. Grid searching and 5-fold cross-validation were carried out to select the optimal hyperparameters for the model.

Finally, The performance of both the training and test sets was assessed using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Metrics such as the area under the ROC curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity were calculated to evaluate the model’s ability to differentiate among the three categories. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was employed to evaluate the clinical utility of the models. The prediction model established selective threshold probabilities for clinical outcomes to derive net benefit. In the graph, the dashed line corresponds to a scenario where all patients with CSVD are assumed to have cognitive dysfunction, yielding a benefit rate of 0. Conversely, the solid black line represents a scenario where none of the CSVD patients have cognitive dysfunction, reflecting a negative-slope inverse line indicating net harm. The DCA curve’s threshold probability range displays higher net benefit for patients compared to the extreme curves. The model’s curves for different control groups deviate from these extremes, signifying their clinical usefulness and providing a balanced perspective between decision benefits and harms based on the model’s predicted cognitive dysfunction.

Statistics

In this study, we utilized IBM SPSS statistical software version 26.0 for data processing. In this study, the approach for handling missing values involved utilizing the following method: for numeric features, the mean value was employed for imputation; while for categorical features, the mode value was utilized. For assessing normal distribution within the three groups, we conducted a comprehensive analysis employing independent t-tests and evaluated the relationship between measurement variables and categorical variables using one-way ANOVA. Furthermore, to assess model predictive performance, we employed ROC curve analysis and calculated the AUC, as well as determining the critical point of the Jaccard index that maximizes sensitivity and specificity. To comprehensively evaluate model performance across training and test cohorts for all three groups, DCA was applied to assess clinical effectiveness of the radiogenomic-clinical model. Finally, we defined statistical significance with a two-sided p-value threshold of ≤ 0.05.

Texture features selection

In this study, a total of 1197 features were extracted from the images. Subsequently, the features were subjected to a reduction process to prevent overfitting by eliminating redundant ones. The minimum redundancy maximum correlation (mRMR) and minimum LASSO algorithms were employed to pinpoint the most suitable features. Subsequently, radiomic features were calculated based on the weighted coefficients of the selected features, as depicted in Fig. 2. After the selection process, features with non-zero regression coefficients were retained in the respective three groups. The specific details of these features are illustrated in Fig. 3. Following texture selection via Spearman correlation and Lasso regression, 15 textures were chosen for the mild-CSVD group vs. NC comparison, with the most predictive texture being log_sigma_4_0_mm_3D_glszm_SmallAreaEmphasis. For the sever-CSVD group vs. NC comparison, 19 textures were selected, and the most predictive texture was wavelet_HLH_glszm_SmallAreaLowGrayLevelEmphasis. Furthermore, 12 textures were picked for the sever-CSVD group vs. NC comparison, with the most predictive texture identified as wavelet_HLH_firstorder_RootMeanSquared. The GLSZM features emerged as notably effective in this analysis.

Discussion

This study aims to validate the diagnostic efficiency of radiomics models utilizing hippocampal texture for predicting various degrees of cognitive impairment in patients with CSVD. Our findings indicate that the radiomics model exhibited superior accuracy and specificity in detecting CSVD patients with severe cognitive impairment. Conversely, the model demonstrated decreased accuracy and specificity in discriminating between CSVD patients with mild cognitive impairment and the NC group.

This study demonstrated that hippocampal texture features can serve as a surrogate biomarker for identifying cognitive impairment in patients with CSVD, presenting crucial evidence for the clinical assessment of cognitive decline in CSVD. The hippocampus, known to play a vital role in human cognition, is considered the most vulnerable region during aging and neurodegenerative pathogenesis. (20–21). Changes in functional networks within hippocampal subregions have been observed in cases of MCI with memory loss [22]. Moreover, volumetric reductions in neurodegenerative diseases are not limited to specific subfields, the overall volume of the hippocampus proves superior in distinguishing MCI and AD from individuals without cognitive impairments. This indicates a correlation between cognitive function and the extent of hippocampal volume changes. CSVD has been implicated in neurodegeneration, where the hippocampus is susceptible to abnormalities such as protein deposition, hypoperfusion, and microstructural damage [23–26]. With regards to etiology, leveraging hippocampal radiomic features to predict cognitive impairment in CSVD patients is deemed feasible. Our findings confirmed that the hippocampal radiomics model exhibits enhanced predictive efficacy in distinguishing CSVD patients with severe cognitive impairment from the normal population (accuracy: 78.1%, sensitivity: 53.8%, specificity: 94.7%). Compared to volumetric features, radiomics features offer the advantage of capturing more detailed image information, indicating promising applications in personalized clinical settings [27]. Sørensen et al. identified hippocampal texture abnormalities in individuals with MCI, despite no changes in hippocampal volume, achieving an AUC of 0.764 for discriminating MCI from NC [28]. Zhang et al. reported that classification accuracy based on texture analysis ranged from 64.3 to 96.4% depending on the region of interest selection [29]. These results emphasize the independent clinical utility of hippocampal radiomics features in the identification of cognitive impairment. However, considering radiomic features as only a fraction of medical image information, future radiomics studies warrant validation of more rigorous, accurate, and standardized feature extraction and selection methods [30].

This study revealed a key finding that in cases with mild changes in cognitive function, the hippocampal radiomic features model exhibited decreased accuracy and specificity in differentiating between CSVD patients with mild cognitive impairment and normal subjects. The results indicate that the model’s accuracy in identifying patients with mild cognitive impairment is only 62.5%, underscoring the reduced diagnostic efficacy of hippocampal radiomics features for predicting mild cognitive impairment. Alyoubi et al. utilized a convolutional neural network classifier to develop a deep learning model based on the entorhinal cortical regions, achieving a 70% accuracy in distinguishing MCI from normal control samples [31]. Basaia et al. established and validated a deep learning algorithm that predicted individual stable MCI diagnosis based on structural MRI scans with an accuracy of 76.1% [32]. Similarly, Shahidi et al. conducted a systematic review of six studies presenting data on the diagnostic accuracy metrics of radiomics models in differentiating MCI from NC subjects, reporting a pooled accuracy rate of 76.6% [33]. These outcomes align closely with our findings, indicating that brain structural changes in patients with mild cognitive impairment are relatively subtle. The predictive efficacy of the radiomics model, solely based on brain structural images, is moderately successful in distinguishing between MCI and NC. This may be attributed to insufficient recognition of hippocampal volume changes during mild cognitive impairment, resulting in insignificant contributions from hippocampal radiomics features. Notably, the pathogenesis of CSVD and amnestic MCI differs, suggesting that during the vascular MCI stage, brain neurodegenerative alterations are modest, reflecting inadequate distinctions in radiomics characteristics to identify significant mild vascular cognitive impairment populations and normal controls. Hence, this diminishes the predictive effectiveness of the radiomics model. By integrating texture features, brain volume data, demographics, genetic factors, and blood biomarkers, Nagaraj et al. developed an optimized classification model, achieving approximately 90% accuracy on the test dataset [34]. The accuracy of hippocampal or medial temporal lobe volume measurements, commonly studied brain regions, demonstrated low sensitivity and specificity, disqualifying structural MRI as a standalone test for early dementia diagnosis in individuals with MCI. Future research should prioritize combinations of biomarkers rather than a single biomarker to enhance early Alzheimer’s disease dementia diagnosis [35].

Early identification of cognitive impairment in patients with CSVD holds significant clinical value, enabling early planning of disease progression, prognosis, and treatment strategies to reduce complications and prevent dementia development in patients [36]. A major strength of this study lies in validating the varied diagnostic capabilities of radiomics models in predicting different levels of cognitive impairment in CSVD patients. It was observed that the simple hippocampal structural radiomics model showed relatively low diagnostic efficacy in identifying mild cognitive impairment, suggesting a need for future studies to incorporate multiple variables to enhance diagnostic accuracy in mild CSVD. Additionally, the utilization of an automatic hippocampal segmentation technique during feature extraction enhanced the efficiency of constructing radiomics models and mitigated the subjective bias caused by manual region-of-interest delineation. This study underscores the varying diagnostic efficacy of radiomics models in predicting different levels of cognitive impairment in CSVD patients.

Nevertheless, this study possesses various limitations. It is a single-center study with a relatively small sample size that lacks external validation, potentially restricting the generalizability of the findings. Moreover, the study solely employed the MoCA for cognitive impairment assessment in CSVD patients, neglecting a standard neuropsychological battery, which impacts the standardization of cognitive impairment classification. Additionally, the use of only the radiomics model of hippocampal features for predicting cognitive impairment in CSVD omits imaging markers and brain volume variables, and lacks a comparative analysis regarding the predictive efficiency of clinical features models. Further more, In this study, we did not consider an analysis of the subregions in the texture extraction. Conducting additional analysis at the level of individual subregions could aid in comprehending which area of the hippocampus is most affected in cases of mild or severe cognitive deficits, consequently enhancing classification performance. Lastly, the predominantly Chinese participant composition necessitates caution when extrapolating the results to other ethnic groups due to potential differences.

Conclusion

Our findings highlight the diagnostic value of hippocampal radiomics features in detecting cognitive dysfunction in CSVD patients, with enhanced efficacy in cases of significant cognitive impairment, yet limited capacity in identifying mild cognitive differences. In both NC participants and CSVD patients, hippocampal radiomic features exhibit high specificity, particularly sensitive to severe hippocampal structural changes. In summary, our results suggest that hippocampal textural features can serve as surrogate imaging markers for cognitive impairment in CSVD, aiding clinical decision-making in precision medicine. Recognizing the limitations of hippocampal texture radiomics models in identifying subtle cognitive differences, future studies should incorporate additional clinical or structural variables to enhance predictive accuracy.

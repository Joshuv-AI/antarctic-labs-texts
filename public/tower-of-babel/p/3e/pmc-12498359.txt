Integrated lipidomics and proteomics analysis in the cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury
Authors: Fangfang Zhou, Youjun Xu, Shuzhen Zhang, Lailiang Wang, Xingyue Zheng, Wenqing Ding, Hongchuang Ma, Qun Luo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study aimed to identify potential serum biomarkers and explore associated signaling pathways involved in cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury (CSA-AKI) by integrating proteomic and lipidomic analyses. A total of 34 patients were enrolled, including 17 CSA-AKI patients and 17 controls. Untargeted lipidomic analysis was performed using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) approach. Proteomics analysis was conducted using data-independent acquisition-based LC-MS/MS detection. The integration of proteomics and lipidomics was evaluated using statistical and bioinformatics methods. The two groups had different serum protein and lipid profiles, which included 185 differentially expressed proteins and 65 differentially expressed lipids. The protein and lipid molecules were enriched in biologic pathway implicated in biosynthesis of arachidonic acid metabolism, gonadotropin-releasing hormone signaling pathway, leukocyte trans endothelial migration, and glycerophospholipid metabolism. Correlation analysis revealed that 4 proteins were positively correlated with 21 lipids, whereas 7 proteins were negatively correlated with the 21 lipids. These results suggested that significantly altered proteins and lipids may be involved in the early stage of CSA-AKI and could serve as potentially promising markers. The association between proteins and lipid molecules and the underlying signaling pathways may elucidate the pathogenesis of CSA-AKI.

Introduction

Cardiac surgery-associated acute kidney injury (CSA-AKI) is one of the most prevalent and severe complication in patients undergoing cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) [1,2]. Recent studies have reported encouraging outcomes for novel pharmacological interventions, including dexmedetomidine, ulinastatin, and levosimendan [3,4]. The PROTECTION trial showed that intravenous infusion of amino acids could reduce the occurrence of AKI (26.9% vs 31.7%, relative risk [RR]:0.85, 95% confidence interval [CI]:0.77–0.94) and the protective effect persisted in the subgroup of patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) (RR:0.86, 95%CI: 0.74–0.99) [5]. Additionally, perioperative use of acetaminophen has been associated with a decreased risk of CSA-AKI (odds ratio [OR]: 0.68; 95% CI:0.56–0.83) [6]. However, reliance on monotherapy may not adequately address the multifactorial pathophysiological mechanisms underlying CSA-AKI in affected individuals [7].

The incidence of CSA-AKI remains notably high, with reported rates varying from 2.5% to 39.9%. This discrepancy can be attributed to differences in CSA-AKI definitions and the characteristics of the populations studied [8–10]. CSA-AKI has emerged as the second most prevalent type of AKI, following sepsis-associated AKI in intensive care units (ICUs) [11]. The development of AKI requiring renal replacement therapy (RRT) is associated with higher short- and long-term mortality rates, prolonged hospital stays, and heightened healthcare resource consumption [12].

The early identification and diagnosis of CSA-AKI are crucial, as the timely implementation of comprehensive care and preemptive treatment measures before the emergence of clinical manifestations may reduce the incidence of CSA-AKI [7,13]. However, the etiology and pathophysiology of CSA-AKI are remarkably complex. Currently available biomarkers include neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), kidney injury molecule-1, Dickkopf-3, tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinases-2, liver fatty acid-binding protein, and enkephalinase. Nevertheless, the diagnostic performance of most of these biomarkers in predicting CSA-AKI remains suboptimal, and many lack standardized diagnostic thresholds [1]. Single biomarkers may not sufficiently or accurately capture the multifaceted pathophysiological alterations associated with CSA-AKI.

Therefore, identifying novel biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets is crucial for the early detection of CSA-AKI and the development of effective treatment strategies. Emerging evidence highlights the significant role of lipids in the pathogenesis of AKI [14]. Disruptions in lipid metabolism can compromise the integrity of tubular cell membranes and energy reserves, contributing to inflammatory responses and oxidative stress [15,16]. Clinical evidence has also demonstrated that reduced levels of high-density lipoprotein may be a potential risk factor for the progression of CSA-AKI [17,18]. Currently, integrated proteomic and lipidomic approaches offer a robust technical platform for investigating lipid-protein interactions in AKI pathobiology and for identifying potential biomarkers. However, analyses of proteomic profiles in patients with CSA-AKI remain limited [19,20]. A preliminary experiment involving nine patients undergoing cardiac surgery demonstrated significant alterations in the plasma concentrations of 22 out of 53 fatty acid-derived lipid mediators, highlighting the need for further research into the lipidomic profile of CSA-AKI [21]. This study aims to integrate proteomic and lipidomic approaches to identify differentially expressed lipids and proteins, identify biomarkers, and elucidate molecular pathways and interactions underlying CSA-AKI.

Materials and methods

Study population and design

This prospective study was conducted at Ningbo No. 2 Hospital, Zhejiang, China, from October 1, 2021, to September 30, 2022. Adult patients who underwent elective cardiac surgery with CPB were prospectively enrolled. Patients who had experienced AKI before the surgery, had a history of nephrectomy or renal transplantation, had undergone RRT, or were pregnant were excluded from the study. The diagnosis and staging of AKI were performed according to the Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) criteria [22] (excluding the urine volume part of the criteria owing to the variable accuracy of urine output recording). The KDIGO criteria is an established standard for defining CSA-AKI in both clinical practice and research [2]. The outcome was defined as the development of AKI 7 days post-cardiac surgery [23]. The final cohort consisted of a consecutive series of 17 patients each in the AKI and non-AKI groups. The demographic and clinical data were extracted from the electronic medical records. The preoperative indicators obtained include the initial data obtained at the time of admission, and the subsequent data after transfer to the ICU. Baseline serum creatinine level was defined as the most recent serum creatinine level obtained within 3 months prior to admission. Early-stage AKI was defined as AKI diagnosed in the first 48 h post-cardiac surgery [24]. The research protocol adhered to the ethical principles established by the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Research Ethics Committee of Ningbo No.2 Hospital (No. SL-NBEY-KY-2021-136-02). The study was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (http://www.chictr.org.cn; Registration No. ChiCTR2100051055). Informed consent was obtained from the participants. Proteomic analysis

Sample preparation for proteomics

Serum samples were obtained upon ICU admission immediately after the surgical procedure. All samples underwent in-solution enzymatic digestion with trypsin. Dithiothreitol (DTT) was added to achieve a final concentration of 20 mM. The reaction was allowed to proceed at 30 °C for 2 h, and subsequently cooled to room temperature. Next, iodoacetamide (IAA) was added to achieve a final concentration of 25 mM. The mixture was shaken at 600 rpm for 1 min and then kept protected from light for 30 min at room temperature. Subsequently, an appropriate amount of NH4HCO3 buffer (50 mM) was added to reduce the urea (UA) buffer(UA buffer: 8M urea, 150 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0) concentration to <1.5 mM. Then, 40 μl of NH4HCO3 buffer containing 2 μg of Lys-C was added, shaken at 600 rpm for 1 min, and incubated at 37 °C for 4 h. Finally, 2 μg of trypsin was added to the sample and incubated at 37 °C for 16 h. After undergoing desalting and lyophilization, the sample was redissolved using 0.1% formic acid (FA). The peptide concentration was determined using optical density (OD). Then, 100 μg of peptides from low-abundance components were isolated using the Thermo Scientific™ Pierce™ High pH Reverse Phase Peptide Isolation Kit, and all resulting fractions were subsequently collected. After each constituent peptide was lyophilized, it was redissolved in 10 μg of 0.1% FA, and the peptide concentration was determined using OD. Then, 2 μg of peptide was extracted, and an appropriate amount of indexed retention time (IRT) standard peptide was added for data-dependent acquisition mass spectrometry (DDA-MS) detection.

Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS)/MS analysis

Both DDA and data-independent acquisition (DIA) analyses were chromatographed using a nanoliter flow rate high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) system (Easy nLC-1200), and MS was subsequently performed using a Q-Exactive Plus (Thermo Scientific). Buffer solution preparation involved two solutions: solution A, a 0.1% aqueous FA solution, and solution B, a 0.1% FA in acetonitrile aqueous solution containing 84% acetonitrile. The column was equilibrated using 95% of solution A. After the sample was injected into the trap column, it proceeded through the chromatographic analysis column (25 cm tip column), for gradient separation at a flow rate of 300 nL/min. Chromatographic gradient: the concentration of Solution B increased linearly from 5% to 8%. Between 3.00 and 43.00 min, it continued to increase linearly from 8% to 26%. From 43.00 to 48.00 min, the gradient increased further from 26% to 40%. A sharp increase occurred between 48.00 and 50.00 min, elevating the concentration from 40% to 90%. Finally, from 50.00 to 60.00 min, the concentration of Solution B was maintained at 90%.

Data processing and bioinformatics analysis

Spectronaut software was used to process data from DDA and DIA. The Human_Uniprot database(http://www.uniprot.org) was downloaded, and as a filter parameter, only proteins meeting the false discovery rate (FDR) <1% threshold were retrieved from the database. Missing values occurred when the signal intensity of a peptide fell below the detection limit of MS, and null values were not processed by default.

The clustering analysis of protein molecules was conducted using the R package and the fuzzy C-means (FCM) algorithm. Blast2GO was used to annotate the target protein set for Gene Ontology (GO). Concurrently, the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) Automatic Annotation Server (KAAS) software was employed to annotate the target protein set for the KEGG pathway. Protein-protein interaction (PPI) analysis was conducted using Cytoscape software, which utilizes the IntAct or STRING databases for data integration and analysis.

Untargeted lipidomics

Sample preparation

The serum was collected in 0.5 mL aliquots and stored at −80 °C before analysis. The lipid extraction process followed the methyl tert-butyl ether (MTBE) method. In summary, an appropriate amount of sample was precisely measured, supplemented with 20 μL of internal lipid standards, and then mixed with 200 μL of water and 240 μL of methanol. Subsequently, 800 μL of MTBE was added, and the mixture was subjected to 20 min of ultrasonication at 4 °C, followed by incubation at room temperature for 30 min. The solution was then centrifuged at 14,000 × g for 15 min at 10 °C to extract the lipids. The upper organic solvent layer was separated and dried under a nitrogen atmosphere. To prepare for MS analysis, 200 μL of a 90% isopropanol/acetonitrile solution was added for re-solution, vortexed, and then 90 μL of the reconstituted solution was withdrawn. After centrifuging at 14,000 × g at 10 °C for 15 min, the supernatant was collected for sample injection analysis.

LC-MS analysis

The UHPLC Nexera LC-30A ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography system (SHIMADZU, Japan), equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC CSH C18 column(Waters, 1.7 µm, 2.1 mm× 100 mm), was used for sample separation. The column temperature was maintained at 45 °C, with a flow rate of 300 μL/min. Mobile phase A consisted of acetonitrile:water (60:40,v/v) with 0.1% FA and 0.1 mM ammonium formate, while mobile phase B was acetonitrile: isopropanol solution(10:90, v/v) with 0.1% FA and 0.1 mM ammonium formate. The gradient elution program was as follows: 0–2 min, 30% mobile phase B; 2–25 min, linear increase from 30% to 100% mobile phase B; 25–35 min, return and hold at 30% mobile phase B. All samples were maintained in a 10 °C autosampler throughout the analysis. Following UHPLC separation, samples were analyzed using a Q-Exactive series mass spectrometer (Thermo Scientific). Electrospray ionization (ESI) in both positive and negative ion modes was used for MS analysis.

Data analysis

The internal standard information for lipidomics is as follows: Internal Standards: SPLASH ®LIPIDOMIX MASS SPRC STANDARD, AVANTI,330707-1EA. The mixture contains 14 types of internal standards, with D7 and D9 indicating seven and nine deuterium-labeled hydrogen atoms, respectively. Quality Control (QC) samples were established by combining equal numbers of samples from each group. The correlation coefficient among the QC samples was > 0.9, indicating good experimental reliability. These QC samples serve multiple purposes: assessing the instrument’s condition before injection, ensuring the equilibrium of the chromatography-MS system, and evaluating system stability throughout the entire experimental process. For data processing, both sum normalization and Pareto normalization are applied to the data obtained.

Lipidomics analysis was performed using an untargeted lipidomics analysis platform based on the UHPLC Orbitrap MS system. Univariate statistical analysis methods included fold change (FC) analysis, t-test, and nonparametric test, with criteria for significant lipids defined as FC > 1.5 or FC < 0.67, and P-value < 0.05. Principal component analysis (PCA), partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA), and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) were included in the multidimensional statistical analysis. Differentially expressed lipids were identified based on variable importance for projection (VIP) > 1 in OPLS-DA and p < 0.05. According to the expression levels of differentially expressed lipids, samples from each group underwent hierarchical clustering, while correlation analysis was used to assess the metabolic proximity among these lipids.

Integration of proteomics and lipidomics

Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to evaluate the degree of association between significantly different proteins and lipids across the samples. Subsequently, Cytoscape software (Version 3.9.1) was used to identify significantly altered lipids and proteins located at key nodes within the network, and a correlation network diagram was constructed to visualize these relationships.

Statistical analysis

The normally distributed data are displayed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) and were compared using the t-test. Data that did not adhere to a normal distribution are presented in terms of median and quartile and evaluated through the Mann–Whitney U-test. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages, and the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test is employed, depending on applicability. Statistical significance was determined by a two-tailed P-value< 0.05. All analyses were performed using SPSS (version 25.0) software.

Results

Characteristics of study participants

A total of 34 adult patients who underwent CPB cardiac surgery were included in this study, of whom 17 (50%) developed CSA-AKI (as defined by the KDIGO criteria) after cardiac surgery. In our cohort, all cases of AKI manifested within 48 h of cardiac surgery. Fourteen (82.35%) patients developed AKI within 24 h, whereas three (17.65%) patients developed AKI within 48 h. The distribution of severity was as follows: stage 1 AKI occurred in 11 patients, and stages 2 and 3 AKI occurred in three patients. AKI occurred on the first postoperative day in the three patients with stage 3 AKI; among them, two did not undergo RRT, whereas one underwent RRT for approximately 36 h following the surgery.

The preoperative baseline indicators revealed that the AKI group had a significantly higher proportion of men compared to the non-AKI group. The AKI group exhibited lower albumin levels and a trend toward lower left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) than compared to the non-AKI group. Intraoperative parameters were also compared. Patients who developed AKI had a longer duration of aortic cross-clamping. In addition, the AKI group had more blood loss during surgery. In the comparison of postoperative data between the two groups, patients with AKI had significantly higher levels of N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), serum creatinine (Scr), and procalcitonin, and significantly lower blood glucose levels compared to patients without AKI. The proportion of patients with postoperative body temperature exceeding 38 °C was considerably higher in the CSA-AKI group than in those without AKI (Table 1).

Proteomics analysis

Identification of differentially expressed proteins

The serum proteome profiles of the patients with CSA-AKI and those of the non-AKI group were compared. Through DIA-based quantitative proteomic analysis, we identified 4,411 proteins among the 27,564 peptides. To explore the proteins with differential expression, a comparison of the relative protein expression values between the two groups was conducted. A total of 185 proteins exhibited significantly different expression between the two groups, with 59 proteins being upregulated and 126 proteins being downregulated (Table 2). Volcano plots were used to visually display the differentially expressed proteins (Figure 1(A)). The heatmap illustrated a cluster analysis of the differentially expressed proteins in the comparison groups, reflecting increased amounts of downregulated proteins in the CSA-AKI group (Figure 1(B)).

Function analysis of proteomics results

GO enrichment analysis was conducted on the significantly differentially expressed proteins (Figure 2(A)). Biological processes terms were predominantly related to cellular processes (n = 27), response to stimuli (n = 26), biological regulation (n = 25), regulation of biological processes (n = 22), metabolic processes (n = 21), and positive regulation of biological processes (n = 19). Cellular component annotations indicated enrichment in the extracellular region (n = 32), extracellular region part (n = 27), cell part (n = 27), cell (n = 27), organelle (n = 22), and membrane (n = 21). Molecular functions terms were primarily associated with binding (n = 34), catalytic activity (n = 19), and molecular function regulator (n = 5). These results suggest that the underlying molecular mechanisms differ substantially between the CSA-AKI and control groups.

To clarify the potential biological functions and signaling pathways of differentially expressed proteins, we employed KEGG analysis. KEGG pathway analysis illustrated that the upregulated proteins were annotated to six pathways, mainly involved in the biosynthesis of cofactors, arachidonic acid (AA) metabolism, pantothenate, coenzyme A (CoA) biosynthesis, and the renin-angiotensin system. Furthermore, the downregulated proteins were annotated to 20 pathways in total. The main pathways included the gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) signaling pathway and leukocyte transendothelial migration (Figure 2(B)).

Lipidomics analysis

Differential lipid profiles between two groups

We extracted all lipid molecules from the serum samples of patients with CSA-AKI and controls. In both positive and negative ionization modes, we annotated 4,053 lipid species from 48 lipid classes (Figure 4). Triglycerides (TGs, 864), phosphatidylcholines (PCs, 617), phosphatidyl ethanolamines (PEs, 352), sphingomyelin (SMs, 237), and diacylglycerols (DGs, 207) were the five largest lipid subclasses. Additionally, 196 ceramides (Cer), 151 cardiolipin (CL), 122 phosphatidyl glycerol (PG), 122 dihexosylceramides (Hex2Cer), 113 phosphatidylinositol (PI), and 107 phosphatidylserine (PS) were identified. The lipid subclass composition in the two groups was displayed as pie charts (Figure 5(A,B)). In the serum samples of CSA-AKI patients, cholesteryl ester (ChE, 41.86%), zymosteryl (ZyE, 18.87%), and PCs (13.48%) comprised the bulk of the identified lipids. Similarly, ChE (38.67%), PCs (16.78%), and ZyE (15.59%) also represented the majority of the identified lipids in the control group. Increased levels of acylcarnitine (AcCa), ChE, trihexosylceramides (Hex3Cer), lysophosphatidic acid (LPA), lysosphingomyelin (LSM), PG, phosphatidylinositol phosphate (PIP), phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2), phosphatidylinositol triphosphate (PIP3), sterol lipids (ST), and ZyE were observed in the CSA-AKI group compared to the non-AKI group (Figure 6).

Discussion

Currently, the underlying mechanisms of CSA-AKI remain poorly understood. Moreover, the early diagnosis and discovery of novel biomarkers for CSA-AKI have become increasingly important, as this remains a critical clinical challenge. Our integrated proteomics and lipidomic approaches demonstrated significant alterations in protein and lipid molecules immediately after the surgery. These results suggest the feasibility of extending the diagnostic window for CSA-AKI to 24 h postoperatively. Furthermore, our current findings highlight early dysregulation within both the proteome and lipidome, providing a more comprehensive insight into the pathophysiological mechanisms involved in CSA-AKI.

Several perioperative clinical variables demonstrated significant differences between the CSA-AKI and control groups in our study. Demographic and clinical factors exert a substantial influence on proteomic and lipidomic profiles [25]. Sex represents a critical variable in the assessment of biomarkers, as normative biomarker levels observed in one sex may correspond to pathological states in the other. Given the sex-specific variations observed in omics data, these differences may hold important implications for the advancement of biomarker development [26]. In our study, patients with CSA-AKI exhibited poorer preoperative overall health status, experienced more pronounced circulatory disturbances during surgery, and were more vulnerable to postoperative fluctuations in blood glucose levels and elevated inflammatory markers. The pathophysiology of CSA-AKI involves diminished renal perfusion and activation of a pro-inflammatory cascade. Both ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI)-induced AKI and sepsis-associated AKI can adversely affect lipid, protein metabolism, glucose metabolism, and energy metabolism [27].

Proteomic analysis utilizing KEGG pathway enrichment revealed that “AA metabolism” is a significantly enriched pathway associated with lipid metabolism. According to current evidence, AA is a critical component of phospholipids within the cellular membrane. Under conditions of cellular stress, phospholipids are released from the cell membrane, and AA is subsequently released through the enzymatic action of phospholipase A2 (PLA2) and phospholipase C (PLC). This AA is subsequently metabolized into a bioactive derivative that triggers inflammatory signaling cascades. Without prompt treatment, the inflammatory response may culminate in AKI. During the acute phase of renal IRI, AA-derived lipid mediators, including prostaglandin E2, leukotriene B4, and thromboxane A2, have been implicated in the pathogenesis of renal damage [28]. Experimental inhibition of the cytosolic phospholipase A2-AA pathway attenuates inflammation and injury in renal tubular epithelial cells(RTECs) [29]. The PPI network analysis further highlighted that AA metabolism is the most prominent pathway among those identified, suggesting that targeting AA metabolism could potentially serve as an effective therapeutic target for treating IRI-induced AKI [30]. In mammals, AA is mainly derived from PCs and PEs, with PCs containing eicosatetraenoic acid chains serving as the primary source, and PEs acting as a rapidly replenishable reservoir. KEGG pathway enrichment analysis of lipidomic data also revealed significant early-stage activation of glycerophospholipid metabolism in CSA-AKI, involving PEs, PCs, PSs, LPAs, and LPCs. Notably, lipid species such as PE (14:0/22:2) and PE (39:1) were significantly elevated, while PC(20:3e/22:2) showed a strong correlation with PE (14:0/22:2). Similarly, lipidomic profiling of kidney tissue from an IRI-induced AKI mouse models demonstrated significant increases in PE (42:3) and PC (38:1) at 6 h post injury [31]. These altered lipid species may represent promising biomarkers for early CSA-AKI detection and further investigation.

Our study identified PTGDS as a differentially expressed protein (DEP) upregulated in AA metabolism. PTGDS, previously known as β-trace protein (BTP), is a lipocalin-type prostaglandin D synthase that catalyzes the conversion of prostaglandin H2 to prostaglandin D2. Animal studies have demonstrated that urinary PTGDS serves as a predictive biomarker for the onset of renal injury [32], and has been proposed as a potential marker of kidney function [33]. In alignment with our findings, a pilot study found that serum BTP demonstrates a superior ability to detect AKI on the first postoperative day, compared to NGAL [34]. Wajda et al. identified significant positive correlations between serum BTP (PTGDS) and endothelial dysfunction, suggesting that BTP (PTGDS) synthesis may be stimulated in endothelial cells exposed to shear stress [35]. However, the function of BTP (PTGDS) in AA metabolism related to CSA-AKI remains to be elucidated and warrants further exploration.

Analysis of the lipid-protein interaction network revealed a significant positive correlation between IGFBP4 and PIPs. IGFBPs can prolong the half-life of insulin-like growth factor (IGF) and modulate its distribution and associated biological functions. They play a crucial role in the pathogenesis and progression of kidney diseases [36]. An in vitro luciferase assay demonstrated that IGFBP4 enhances hyperactivation of the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K)-AKT pathway via IGF signaling, thereby promoting adipogenesis. In vitro, Muller et al.’s experiments also found moderately increased lipid accumulation in cells overexpressing IGFBP4, whereas knockdown of IGFBP4 resulted in a moderate reduction of lipid accumulation [37]. Moreover, upon activation, PI3K phosphorylates the PIP2 to generate PIP3 at the cell membrane [38]. PIP3 then recruits and activates the serine-threonine protein kinase AKT, facilitating downstream signaling pathways [39]. The involvement of the PI3K-AKT pathway in AKI has been previously explored. He et al. [40] identified an association between the PI3K-AKT pathway and IRI-induced AKI through KEGG analysis of 323 differentially expressed genes obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Consistent with these findings, we observed that patients with CSA-AKI experienced more severe renal IRI, characterized by prolonged aortic cross-clamp time and increased blood loss. The PI3K-AKT pathway has been implicated in lipid peroxidation, a hallmark of ferroptosis. Park et al. [41]demonstrated that Honokiol ameliorates IRI-AKI by activating the PI3K-AKT-nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) signaling axis, leading to upregulation of the antioxidant glutathione (GSH). Transcriptomic analysis suggests that the renoprotective effects of ferrostatin-1 extend beyond ferroptosis inhibition and may involve modulation of the PI3K signaling pathway. It has been hypothesized that ferrostatin-1 exerts therapeutic effects in AKI by influencing the gene expression of lipid- metabolizing enzymes via the PI3K pathway [42]. Based on these findings, it remains to be determined whether IGFBP4 activation induced by renal IRI contributes to aberrant lipid accumulation and peroxidation through interactions with the PI3K-AKT pathway during the onset of CSA-AKI.

A significant positive correlation between S100A8 and TG was identified within the lipid-protein interaction network. S100A8, a calcium-binding protein, functions in the transport of AA and frequently forms a heterodimer with S100A9. Elevated concentrations of S100A8 are predominantly observed in inflammatory responses. Correspondingly, we observed a higher proportion of fever > 38 °C, and increased procalcitonin levels among patients with CSA-AKI postoperatively. Wang et al. also reported that the plasma S100A8 levels in patients with AKI increased promptly after acute type A aortic dissection surgery, as revealed by plasma proteomic analysis [43], consistent with our findings. S100A8 could trigger the Janus tyrosine kinase-Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription (JAK-STAT) signaling pathway, which is induced by various pro-inflammatory cytokines [44]. Activation of the JAK2-STAT5 pathway drives the upregulation of genes involved in lipid catabolism, including adipose triglyceride lipase, resulting in a reduction of serum TG levels [45]. Based on these observations, we hypothesized that S100A8 may regulate TG levels through the JAK-STAT pathway.

Limitations

This study integrated proteomics and lipidomic analyses, offering novel insights into the functional interplay between the lipids and proteins, which had not been previously elucidated in the realm of CSA-AKI. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as a single-center study with a relatively small sample size, the statistical power of the results was limited. Larger-scale and prospective studies are urgently required to validate the candidate biomarkers for CSA-AKI identified herein. Second, this study was descriptive; the proposed pathophysiological mechanisms warrant further experimental validation through in vivo or in vitro approaches. Third, although several key lipid species were characterized, a substantial number of lipid metabolites remain unidentified owing to the limited availability of reliable spectral reference databases. Future research would benefit from employing larger cohorts and more refined stratification to elucidate lipidomic and proteomic alterations during the early and late stages of CSA-AKI.

Conclusion

In summary, this current study characterizes serum proteomics and lipidomic profiles using integrated platforms. We identified proteomic and lipidomic disturbances in early stage CSA-AKI. These include alterations in PTGDS, IGFBP4, PE (14:0/22:2) and PE (39:1) as well as AA metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, the IGFBP4-PI3K-PIPs axis, and the S100A8-JAK-STAT-TG pathway. These represent potential markers or targets that warrant further investigation and mechanistic elucidation.

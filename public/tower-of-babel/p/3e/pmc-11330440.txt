Elicitor-enhanced steroidal sapogenin accumulation in hairy root cultures of Trigonella foenum-graecum
Authors: Poorak Rezazadehfar, Maryam Rezayian, Vahid Niknam, Masoud Mirmasoumi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn current work, we studied hairy root induction in Trigonella foenum graecum, which is an important medicinal plant, and examined the impact of different elicitors on some phytochemical characteristics and metabolites production in hairy root cultures. Accordingly, some factors such as five strain types of Agrobacterium rhizogenes (1724, 15834, A4, A13 and MSU) and three different explants, namely leaf, cotyledon and hypocotyl were studied. The results showed that different A. rhizogenes strains exhibited different infection efficiency. MSU and 15834 had highest efficiency of hairy root induction than other strains. Also, hairy root induction frequency in leaf explants was higher than in other explants. Salicylic acid (SA), nitric oxide (NO), CaCl2 and penconazole (PEN) were used in elicitation process. Hairy roots were treated with SA (0.1 and 0.5 mM), NO (10 and 50 µM), CaCl2 (5 and 10 mM) and PEN (5 and 10 mg/L). Applied elicitors enhanced antioxidant enzymes activities and reduced oxidative stress markers; this observation might be ascribed to regulation of the oxidative status of the elicited cells. Significant increase of antioxidant metabolites (total phenol, flavonoid and anthocyanin) in PEN-treated hairy roots was associated to phenylalanine ammonia lyase activity, indicating an up-regulation of phenylpropanoid/flavonoid metabolism. PEN and CaCl2 treatment enhanced steroidal sapogenin in hairy root cultures. These results suggested that use of elicitors can enhance the production of secondary metabolites in transformed hairy roots. Among the elicitors applied, CaCl2 and PEN were the most effective in increasing secondary metabolite production in transformed hairy roots of T. foenum graecum.Keywords: Hairy root, Trigonella foenum graecum, Elicitation, Secondary metabolites, Antioxidant enzymesSubject terms: Plant sciences, Plant physiology

Introduction

Secondary metabolites have an important effect on human health; but, in nature, gaining the high quantity of secondary metabolites remains challenging. Specific tissues in plants only can accumulate and produce secondary metabolites 1. Biotechnological methods for example cell and tissue culture systems have restricted potential to enhance secondary metabolite generation principally due to their undifferentiated nature. Among various organ culture systems, hairy roots have obtained considerably attention from scientists due to its ability to accumulate high content of secondary metabolites in short time period. Additionally, several of secondary metabolites are accumulated and/or produced in plant roots, and gathering of roots for medicinal applications are disparaging for the plants; thus, hairy root systems are an attractive method for using the ability of plant roots 2. Hairy roots are created by infection of plant explants with Agrobacterium rhizogenes and consequent transfer of T-DNA of Ti plasmid from A. rhizogenes to the explants. Hairy roots are identified by biochemical and genetic stability, much branching, high percentage of growth in minimal medium, absence of geotropism and no need for phytohormones. Also, the potential of hairy roots to generate secondary metabolites for an prolonged span recommends hairy root culture systems as an good substitute for constant production of medicinal important secondary metabolites 3,4.

Today, various elicitors are used to increase the production of secondary metabolites in hairy root. Elicitation as biotechnological tool is a possible assistance to overcome various problems associated with the large-scale generation of commercially valuable bioactive metabolites 5. Elicitor is any stress cause capable of activating inducible defense mechanisms, actually elicitor acts as signal, recognized by elicitor‐specific receptors on the cell membrane and activates defense mechanisms during elicitation resulting in enhanced accumulation and synthesis of secondary metabolites. Elicitors based on chemical nature are categorized into two major groups, biotic and abiotic elicitors. Biotic elicitors have biological origin, but abiotic elicitors have non-biological origin 6,7. Harfi et al.8 showed that salicylic acid (SA) enhanced hyoscyamine content in hairy roots of Algerian Datura. Methyl jasmonate (JA) boosted the accumulation of ursolic acid and eugenol in hairy roots of Ocimum tenuiflorum L. 9. Also, elicitors influence on physiological and biochemical traits such as antioxidant system in hairy roots. Antioxidant system includes enzyme antioxidant system and non-enzymatic antioxidant system. Superoxide dismutase (SOD), peroxidase (POX) and catalase (CAT) are the chief components for the antioxidant enzyme system, and phenolic compounds, flavonoid and anthocyanin are the known non-enzymatic antioxidants 10. Gheisary et al.11 presented that total antioxidant activity, total phenol and CAT activity were increased in elicitated hairy roots compared to non elicitated hairy roots.

Fenugreek (Trigonella foenum graecum L.) is a valuable medicinal crop of the Fabaceae family. It is broadly grown around the world and is recognized due to anti-cancer, anti-microbial, anti-diabetic and hypocholesterolemic features. Fenugreek has active metabolites such as saponins and alkaloids that are responsible for the biochemical characterizes of this plant 12,13. Augmenting the production of secondary metabolites is potential thereby hairy root culture. In the present work, hairy root cultures were established in T. foenum graecum using different strains of A. rhizogenes. The aim of this research was to assess the effects of abiotic elicitors on the production of secondary metabolites in the developed hairy root cultures of T. foenum graecum. To the best of the authors' information, elicitor-induced activity of antioxidant enzymes in hairy root cultures of T. foenum graecum has not been investigated so far. Owing to this fact, the other purpose of the current study was to determine impacts of elicitors on antioxidant enzymes activities and osmotic active substances.

Material and methods

Seed culture and preparation of explants

All the plant experiments were in compliance with relevant institutional, national, and international guidelines and legislation. Seeds of T. foenum graecum (Ardestani cultivar) were obtained from Pakan Bazr Isfahan co, Isfahan, Iran. The seeds were surface-sterilized with 70% ethanol and 30% sodium hypochlorite containing 0.1% Tween-20 for 1 and 20 min, respectively. The seeds were then washed with sterile distilled water three times. Half-strength agar solidified Murashige and Skoog (MS) medium 14 was prepared and the pH was adjusted to 5.8 before autoclaving. After cooling, the medium was poured into a petri plate. Then, seeds were placed on a petri plate and then placed into a growth chamber at 25 °C under standard white fluorescent light with 16-h dark/8-h light conditions for explants preparation.

Bacterial strains and culture conditions

To determine the transformation efficiency, five different strains of A. rhizogenes (1724, 15834, A4, A13 and MSU) were used for the experiment. The strains were obtained from Tarbiat Modares University, Iran, and conserved in our laboratory. All strains were maintained at –70 °C in glycerol stock in our laboratory. Bacteria were cultured in the flask containing 30 mL of Luria–Bertani (LB) liquid medium, supplemented by 50 mg/L rifampicin, pH 7.2, incubated at 28 °C until the cell density reached to OD600 = 1.2.

Hairy root induction and selection of transformed hairy root lines

The leaf, cotyledon and hypocotyl explants of in vitro, 12 days old T. foenum graecum seedlings were aseptically cut into small sections were immersed in the media containing A. rhizogenes for 5 min and then dried with sterile tissue paper. The explants were co-cultured on agar solidified full and half-strength MS medium, and incubated under dark conditions for 72 h. The co-cultured explants were rinsed by distilled water and transferred to a hormone-free half-strength MS medium supplemented with 500 mg/L cefotaxime to eliminate the A. rhizogenes. Finally, in order to investigate the effect of elicitors, hairy roots from were propagated in 1/2 B5 medium without hormone. Among all the hairy root lines established, hairy roots induced by 15834 strain were selected for further study.

PCR analysis for rolB gene integration

Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was used for the detection genes on the Ri plasmid (rolC and virD1) integrated into the transformed hairy roots. Genomic DNA of transformed and non-transformed roots (natural root from in vitro plants) was extracted by CTAB method 15. The PCR was carried out under the following conditions: 94 °C for 2 min, 35 cycles of three steps [94 °C for 50 s (denaturation), 55 °C for 45 s (annealing) and 72 °C for 1 min (elongation)], and 72 °C for 7 min. After PCR, the samples were separated on 0.8% agarose gel and stained with ethidium bromide. Gel pictures were gained using Gel-Doc Transilluminator.

Elicitor treatment

SA, nitric oxide (NO), CaCl2 and penconazole (PEN) were used in elicitation process. Hairy roots were exposed to these elicitor treatments: SA (0.1 and 0.5 mM), NO (10 and 50 µM), CaCl2 (5 and 10 mM) and PEN (5 and 10 mg/L) that these concentrations were chosen based on preliminary studies. The same amount of hairy roots was cultured in flask containing 70 mL of half-strength B5 medium without antibiotics, which contained the desired concentrations of elicitors. All cultures were incubated for 7 days on an orbital shaker at 100 rpm under continuous darkness at 25 °C. After 7 h of treatment, hairy roots were harvested, washed with distilled water and were subjected to different analyses. Each treatment was performed in three biological replicates.

Hydrogen peroxide and malondialdehyde

The Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) content was determined using the method of Velikova et al.16. Hairy roots (0.25 g) were homogenized in 1.5 mL of 0.1% (w/v) TCA on ice and centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 15 min. 1 mL of potassium phosphate buffer and 1 mL of KI were added to 0.5 mL of the supernatant. The absorbance of the supernatant was measured at 390 nm and the H2O2 content was calculated by comparing it to a standard calibration curve previously constructed using different concentrations of H2O2.

Lipid peroxidation was determined according to Heath and Packer17 method by measuring malondialdehyde (MDA). The hairy roots (0.5 g) were homogenized in 5 mL of 0.1% (w/v) trichloroacetic acid (TCA) and centrifuged at 10,000 rpm for 20 min. 2 mL of 0.5% thiobarbituric acid (TBA) in 20% TCA was added to a 1 mL aliquot of supernatant. The mixture was heated for 30 min at 95 °C and quickly cooled in an ice bath. After 15 min of centrifugation at 10,000 rpm, the absorbance of the supernatant was recorded at 532 and 600 nm. The MDA content was determined using extinction coefficient of 155 mM−1 cm−1.

Proline and soluble sugar

For the proline content measurement based on Bates et al.18, 0.2 g of hairy root was homogenized in 3 mL sulfosalicylic acid (3%) and then was centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 20 min. Two mL of supernatant was combined with acid ninhydrin (2 mL) and glacial acetic acid (2 mL) and then was boiled at 100 °C for 1 h. The reaction mixture was extracted with 4 mL toluene and the absorbance was recorded at 520 nm by UV–vis spectrophotometer (UV-160, Shimadzu, and Tokyo, Japan). Proline content was determined according to the standard curve prepared with different concentrations of L-proline as a standard.

The hairy roots (0.1 g) were extracted using 3 mL distilled water and total soluble sugar content was determined based on the method of phenol sulfuric acid as described by Dubois et al.19 using glucose as a standard.

Enzymatic antioxidant system

Hairy roots (0.5 g) was homogenized at 4 °C with 3 mL Tris–HCl (1 M, pH 6.8) to estimate total protein content and enzymes activities. The homogenate was centrifuged at for 20 min at 4 °C and 13,000 rpm. The supernatant was stored at − 70 °C and used for protein determination and enzyme assay. Protein content was calculated according to Bradford20 method using bovine serum albumin as a standard.

SOD activity was determined by monitoring the inhibition of nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) photochemical reduction 21. The reaction mixture contained 50 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7.8) with 0.1 mM ethylene diaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), 75 μM NBT, 13 mM methionine, 2 μM riboflavin and 100 μL of total protein extract. The reaction mixture was irradiated for 16 min, and absorbance was read at 560 nm against the non-irradiated blank. One unit of SOD was defined as the amount of enzyme which caused 50% inhibition of NBT reduction under the assay condition, and the results were expressed as U mg−1 protein.

For measurement of POX activity, reaction mixture consisted 0.1 mL benzidine (40 mM), 0.2 mL H2O2 (3%), 2 mL of 0.2 M acetate buffer (pH 4.8) and 50 μL of enzyme extract. Activity of this enzyme was recorded at 530 nm 22.

Polyphenol oxidase (PPO) activity was determined using the method of Raymond et al. 23 at 40 °C. 20 μL protein extract, 2.5 mL of 200 mM potassium phosphate buffer (pH 7), 200 μL pyrogallol 20 mM, were used in reaction mixture. The increase in absorbance was recorded at 430 nm. The PPO activity was defined as U mg−1 protein.

Antioxidant metabolites

In order to preparation of methanolic extract, 0.5 g of hairy root was homogenized in 2.5 mL methanol 80% and then was centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 20 min. For the total phenol content measurement, 0.1 mL methanolic extract was mixed with 2.5 mL Folin-Ciocalteu reagent 10%. The mixtures were neutralized by sodium bicarbonate 7% and then absorbance was recorded at 765 nm 24.

Flavanol content was determined according to Akkol et al.25 method. In this method, 0.5 mL aluminum chloride 2% and 1.5 mL sodium acetate 5% were added to 0.5 ml methanolic extract and absorbance of was recorded at 445 nm after 2.5 h and rutin was used as a standard.

Content of flavonoid was measured by Aluminum chloride method 26. In this method, 0.5 g of hairy root was homogenized in 2.5 mL of methanol 80%. Methanolic extract (0.5 mL) was mixed with 1.5 mL of methanol, 0.1 mL of aluminium chloride (10%), 0.1 mL of potassium acetate (1 M) and 2.8 mL of distilled water and the absorbance was measured at 415 nm after 30 min. Anthocyanin content was determined in 0.3% HCl in methanol at 25 °C using the extinction coefficient (33 cm2 mol−1) at 550 nm 27.

Phenylalanine ammonia-lyase activity

In order to phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL) activity measurement, 60 μL extract enzyme was mixed with 500 μL boric acid buffer (pH 8) and absorbance was recorded at 290 nm after 30 min. Standard curve was prepared by different concentrations of E-cinnamic acid 28.

Assessment of steroidal sapogenin content

Powdered martials (0.2 g) was extracted with petroleum ether for 7 h using the soxhlet apparatus. Total steroidal sapogenin was determined by Baccou et al.29 method. 500 μL extract was dissolved in 1.5 mL of ethyl acetate, to which 1 mL of 0.5% (v/v) p-anisaldehyde in ethyl acetate and 1 mL of 50% (v/v) H2SO4 in ethyl acetate was added. Reaction mixtures were then incubated at 60 °C for 10 m. After 10 min incubation, the absorbance of the color developed solution was measured at 430 nm. For the calibration curve, 0–40 μg standard diosgenin in l mL ethyl acetate was used.

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was repeated three times and the data were analyzed by using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) using SPSS (version 21) and means were compared by Duncan’s test at the 0.05 level of confidence.

Results and discussion

Rate of hairy root formation

Sterilized seeds of T. foenum graecum germinated after about 48 h. The percentage of germination of seeds was 81.42% after 10 day. Different parts such as, leaf, cotyledon and hypocotyl were used for inoculation. Hairy roots were visible within 7–15 days on wounded sites of leaf, cotyledon and hypocotyl explants of T. foenum graecum infected with different strains of A. rhizogenes. A4, A13, MSU and 15834 strains were induced hairy root in leaf, cotyledon and hypocotyl explants, but 1724 strain was unable to induce hairy root in T. foenum graecum. The minimum time of hairy root appearance by A4, A13, MSU and 15834 strains were detected in leaf (8 day), hypocotyl (8 day), hypocotyl (7 day) and leaf (8 day). Thus, hairy roots induced by A4 and 15834 strain appeared earlier in leaf explants, whereas hairy roots created by A13 and MSU strains seemed earlier in hypocotyl explants. Hairy roots induced by A4, A13 and 15834 strain appeared later in cotyledon, but hairy roots formed by MSU strain exhibited in leaf explants (Table 1).

The molecular evidence of transformed nature of hairy roots was confirmed by PCR by rolC specific primers as rol genes are responsible for the hairy root induction. It was not detected in PCR product of normal root, which was used as negative control. Also, the negative results for the PCR of the virD1 gene powerfully propose the lack of A. rhizogenes contamination in the culture (Fig. 1).

Among the strains that induced hairy roots in different explants, leaf explants in all strains and then explants of cotyledon and hypocotyl showed good growth and biomass accumulation in 1/2 B5 medium. Some factors influence the hairy root induction comprising strain type, infection time, explant type and period of co-cultivation. The tissue of the plant that is susceptible to A. rhizogenes infection for hairy root induction varies among species. For example, leaf explants from Salvia miltiorrhiza showed the maximum induction rate for hairy root formation 32.

Our results exhibited that elicitors affected oxidative statue of hairy roots, thus changed growth. The response of hairy roots to elicitors is variable among different elicitors and plants. Stress marker and antioxidant enzymes

The results showed that, 0.1 and 0.5 mM SA, 10 and 50 µM NO and 10 mM CaCl2 reduced H2O2 content by 43.26, 33.07, 18.82, 10.83 and 13.49% as compared to untreated hairy roots, respectively. The 5 mM CaCl2, 5 and 10 mg/L PEN caused a significant increase in H2O2 content by 13.87, 18.25 and 22.24% as compared to hairy root without elicitor (Fig. 3a). All elicitors declined significantly MDA content in hairy roots and the maximum reduction (68.75%) observed in PEN-treated hairy roots as compared to untreated hairy roots (Fig. 3b).

Application of 0.1 mM SA, 10 and 50 µM NO and 10 mM CaCl2 enhanced POX activity by 60.48, 71.32 and 67.30 and 11.18%, but PEN application decreased this activity as compared to hairy roots without elicitor (Fig. 4b). The production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) is a common operation in plant stress response, resulting in lipid peroxidation (MDA production), which could hurt the membrane and change growth and physiological and metabolic processes 36. To maintain cells from the injury of extreme ROS, increase in antioxidant enzyme activities such as SOD and POX is a well-known strategy for removal of ROS in plant cells. SOD enzyme is the first line of defense of the plant cells for ROS neutralization and causes the dismutation of the superoxide radical (O2•−) into O2 and H2O2 and POX enzyme converts H2O2 to H2O 37. In our work, when the antioxidant enzymes activities in hairy roots treated with SA, NO, CaCl2 and PEN were evaluated, resulted in suppression of oxidative stress indicator (MDA production). Also, the increment in SOD activity meant that there was a need in hairy roots treated with all elicitors to convert extreme O2•− into O2 and H2O2. In hairy roots treated with SA, NO and CaCl2. The consequent increase in POX activity related to the removal of H2O2. Namely, the reduction of POX activity (H2O2-scaving enzyme) in PEN-treated hairy roots coincided with increase H2O2 content. The equilibrium among antioxidant enzymes activities is a crucial reason to adjust oxidative status in cells. Therefore, the decrease in the MDA level showed that all elicitors helped to maintain this balance and the oxidative status of hairy roots. Adabavazeh et al.38 showed that SA treatment enhanced antioxidant enzymes activities and reduced H2O2 and MDA level in transformed hairy roots of Calotropis procera. Increased activity of antioxidant enzymes under NO elicitation were responsible for a lower H2O2 content and fewer lipid peroxidation 39. CaCl2 caused a noticeable enhance in the activity of antioxidant enzymes and significantly declined MDA accumulation in maize plants 40. Overall, the data of the present work showed that elicitors protected basal level of ROS by activation of antioxidant enzymes, consequently enabled appropriate physiological responses and the adjustment of many processes crucial for life 41.

Primary metabolites

The assessment of primary metabolites is very important, as these are the precursors for secondary metabolites synthesis. In our work, we assessed protein, soluble sugar and proline. Treatment with all elicitors declined protein content in hairy roots and only elicitation with 5 mM CaCl2 had a significant enhancing effect on protein content as compared to non-elicitation hairy roots (Fig. 5a). Meanwhile growing cells are actively complicated in protein synthesis, the protein increased in cell suspension culture of Catharanthus roseus by CaCl2 treatment 42. Degradation of protein has been ascribed to declined protein synthesis, enhanced proteolysis, reduced accessibility of amino acids and inactive of enzymes contributed in protein synthesis 43.

Effect of different elicitors on protein content (a), proline content (b) and soluble sugar (c) in hairy roots. Values are means ± SE of three replicates. Different letters indicated significant (p < 0.05) differences.

The proline accumulation in hairy roots depended on the type of elicitors. 0.1 mM SA, 10 and 50 µM NO and 5 and 10 mM CaCl2 treatment augmented proline content by 106.66, 193.07, 36.90, 52.01 and 137.78%, whereas PEN treatment declined proline content as compared to untreated hairy roots (Fig. 5b). Proline accumulation occurs under changing environment; it acts as osmolyte for osmotic regulation, proline involves steadying sub-cellular structures (e.g., proteins and membranes), suppressing free radicals and buffering redox potential in cells under stress conditions. Also, it contributes in lessening cytoplasmic acidosis and preserving suitable NADPH/NADP+ ratios compatible with metabolism 44. Our results identified that SA, NO and CaCl2 can regulate proline accumulation in hairy roots. Therefore, these treatments can help to osmotic regulation, ROS detoxification and protection of macromolecules in hair roots by increasing proline. Regulation of 1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate synthetase (P5CS) and PDH transcript represents the rate-limiting steps in proline metabolism 45. NO stimulated proline synthesis by increase in P5CS activity and decrease in PDH activity. SA pretreatment significantly enhanced proline content by up-regulating and down-regulating the expression of genes encoding P5CS and PDH compared to non-SA pretreated plants, respectively 46–48.

Different elicitors showed different effects on soluble sugar content, depending on the type and concentration of elicitors. Soluble sugar content in hairy roots induced when treated with 50 µM NO and 5 mM CaCl2, whereas other treatment decreased this parameter as compared to non-elicitation hairy roots (Fig. 5c). Han et al.49 recommended sugar content were influenced by SNP treatment possibly due to the activity of sucrose metabolism enzymes. The induction in soluble sugars is a protective mechanism. Soluble sugars may act as a osmoprotectant, keeping turgor pressure and stabilizing cellular membranes 50.

Antioxidant metabolites

Based on results obtained from this experiment, adding SA, NO and CaCl2 to the culture media reduced total phenol content as comparison to untreated hairy roots. In this study, the applied concentrations of PEN increased total phenol content and this content 30.31 and 63.50% was higher than non-elicitation hairy roots. Thus, the highest accumulation of total phenol content was observed in hairy roots treated with 10 mg/L PEN. Elicitation with SA, NO and CaCl2 caused a non-significant change in flavonol content. The application of 5 and 10 mg/L PEN increased flavonol content by 2.47 and 3.56-fold than the hairy roots without PEN, respectively. Non-significant effect of SA, NO and CaCl2 treatment was observed on flavonoid content. A considerable increase in flavonoid content of hairy roots was recorded under PEN treatment and positive effect of high dose of PEN was more than low dose. All elicitors enhanced anthocyanin content in hairy roots and maximum accumulation identified in 50 µM NO-treated hairy roots. Elicitation with SA, NO and PEN induced PAL activity and highest induction (76.69%) observed in 10 µM NO-treated hairy roots (Table 3).

Prior works have stated that hairy roots have the capacity to produce the maximum content of metabolites due to higher growth percentage; thus, hairy root from some plant species have been applied for the creation of various phenolic compounds 51. Several studies have described important of phenolic compounds as anti-inflammatory, anti-aging, antiproliferative and antioxidant agents 52. The activation of defense responses after elicitation is associated with physiological activities, mostly as antioxidants (e.g., phenolic compounds, which are very important for cells 53. Our findings presented that the applied elicitors had different impacts on accumulation of antioxidant metabolites in hairy roots. Compared to other elicitors, PEN enhanced of metabolites accumulation, and hence it could be recommended as an effective elicitor for transformed hairy roots of T. foenum graecum. Elicitation is a successful approach for the enhancement of secondary metabolites biosynthesis in hairy roots. Various agents such as elicitor concentration and type, hairy root age at the time of elicitation and period of elicitation influence on secondary metabolites production 6. PAL enzyme plays an important role in phenolic compounds biosynthesis through phenylpropanoid pathway. The present data displayed that the accumulation of phenolic compounds strongly connected with PAL activity in hairy roots, work of Rezayian et al.54 confirmed our results. Previous studies showed that elicitation enhanced phenolic compounds in hairy roots 55,56.

Steroidal sapogenin

The treatment with 5 and 10 mM CaCl2 and 10 mg/L PEN caused a significant induction in steroidal sapogenin by 19.85, 25.73 and 17.64% compared to untreated hairy roots. Applying of SA and NO reduced steroidal sapogenin in hairy roots relative to non-elicitation hairy roots (Fig. 6). Saponins have a unique platform in the field of nutraceutical and industries. Saponins are applied for the therapy of different diseases such as diabetic, cancer, hepatic, cardiac and nervous disorders. Due to a widespread range of pharmacological properties, there is important demand for saponins. The generation of saponins thereby conventional methods is time-consuming and difficult to extract pure metabolites, and therefore to reach this, in vitro approaches have been advanced and heightened the generation and obtain of the metabolites 57. The data obtained in the present work indicated that CaCl2 and PEN can act as an effective inducer of the steroidal sapogenin accumulation in transformed hairy roots of T. foenum graecum. The current work presents the first investigation of the application of SA, NO, CaCl2 and PEN on steroidal sapogenin in hairy root cultures of T. foenum graecum. The reported impacts varied, depending on plant species, elicitor concentration and the time of exposure to elicitation. For example, Alsoufi et al.58 showed that treatment with JA increased saponin accumulation in transformed hairy root of Calendula officinalis. Gained results were consistent with those noted for other elicitors that efficiently stimulated saponin production 59,60. Besides, with regards to T. foenum graecum hairy roots, each of the used elicitors required a suitable concentration to influence culture so that accumulation of studied compounds could be effective. Application of linalool at low concentration (0.1 µM) enhanced efficiently on saponin content in hairy root cultures of Panax qiunquefolius 61. Baek et al.62 detected a concentration-dependent impact of elicitors (MeJA and SA) on triterpenoid accumulation in Centella asiatica hairy root culture; the triterpenoid content was the maximum at 400 μM SA and MeJA. Elicitors performance as signaling molecules that are recognized by receptors and trigger a signal transduction pathways that adjusts the expression of genes involved in biosynthesis of secondary metabolites 63. Qiu et al. (2021) showed that chitosan promotes biosynthesis of triterpenoid saponin by differential gene expression in Psammosilene tunicoides hairy roots. Thus, during elicitation, applied elicitors in our work may act as regulatory signals and influence on the biosynthesis of steroidal sapogenin in transformed hairy roots of T. foenum graecum. Therefore, molecular studies are necessary to explain exactly the reason for the change in steroidal sapogenin content in the presence of applied elicitors.

Conclusion

The impact of different elicitors on the accumulation of valuable compounds in transformed hairy roots of T. foenum graecum was demonstrated for the first time. Applied elicitors increased biomass accumulation in hairy root cultures. The induction of antioxidant defense system in hairy root cultures treated with elicitors was followed by the modification of cellular antioxidant status due to the activation of antioxidant enzymes and reduction of oxidative stress markers. Hairy root cultures treated with PEN have the more capacity to produce antioxidant metabolites than other elicitors. At all, it was concluded that to enhance the content of steroidal sapogenin in the hairy roots of T. foenum graecum, the application of elicitors is an operative strategy. Our results displayed that among the elicitors, the application of CaCl2 and PEN was more effective to enhance the steroidal sapogenin. This can be further scaled up for increased production of secondary metabolites of high medicinal value by the hairy root culture in fermenters in the presence of elicitors.

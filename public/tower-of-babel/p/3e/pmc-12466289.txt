Apolipoprotein E receptor 2 in endothelium promote glucose tolerance by mediating insulin delivery to skeletal muscle
Authors: Anastasia Sacharidou, Ken L Chambliss, Jun Peng, Keiji Tanigaki, Md Nurul Islam, Shashank Sirsi, Andrew Lemoff, Kenneth Hoyt, Chieko Mineo, Philip W Shaul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe delivery of circulating insulin to skeletal muscle myocytes is a rate-limiting step in peripheral insulin action, and there is minimal understanding of the underlying mechanisms in endothelial cells. Recognizing that the LDL receptor family member apolipoprotein E receptor 2 (ApoER2, also known as LRP8) mediates apolipoprotein E (ApoE)-induced signaling in endothelial cells, the present project determined if endothelial ApoER2 influences glucose homeostasis in mice.MethodsMice were generated deficient in ApoER2 selectively in endothelial cells, and glucose homeostasis was studied. Insulin-stimulated recruitment of the skeletal muscle microvasculature was assessed using contrast-enhanced ultrasound imaging. Endothelial cell insulin uptake and transcytosis were evaluated in culture. The ApoER2 interactome in endothelial cells was interrogated using immunoprecipitation and liquid chromatography/tandem mass spectrometry. ApoER2 structure–function was studied by mutagenesis.ResultsMice deficient in endothelial cell ApoER2 are glucose intolerant and insulin resistant due to a blunting of skeletal muscle glucose disposal that is related to a decrease in muscle insulin delivery. Endothelial ApoER2 manipulation does not alter direct insulin action on skeletal muscle or insulin-stimulated recruitment of the skeletal muscle microvasculature. Instead, ApoER2 stimulation by apolipoprotein E3 (ApoE3) increases endothelial cell insulin uptake and transcytosis. ApoE3 and ApoER2 stimulation of endothelial insulin transport require the ApoER2 adaptor protein Dab2 and the scaffolding protein IQGAP1, which is known to mediate insulin secretion by pancreatic β cells. IQGAP1 is not required for ApoE3/ApoER2-induced insulin uptake by endothelial cells; alternatively it is necessary for insulin transcytosis. ApoE3 prompts IQGAP1 recruitment to the exocyst complex, and ApoER2 interaction with IQGAP1 is necessary for the recruitment.ConclusionsIn endothelial cells the ApoE3 and ApoER2 tandem co-opts the role of IQGAP1 in pancreatic β cell insulin secretion to enhance endothelial insulin transport. In this manner endothelial ApoER2 promotes glucose disposal in skeletal muscle and supports normal glucose homeostasis.Keywords: Apolipoprotein E3, Apolipoprotein E receptor 2, Endothelium, Glucose, Insulin, Skeletal muscle

Introduction

Mechanisms in the endothelium influence glucose homeostasis by impacting the delivery of circulating insulin to the skeletal muscle, where 80% of total body glucose disposal occurs [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]. Insulin promotes the recruitment of the muscle microvasculature to enhance its delivery and glucose disposal in muscle, and a number of important studies have demonstrated that insulin undergoes transcytosis by endothelial cells [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]]. In pioneering in vivo work in mice the critical role of insulin signaling in endothelial cells was revealed in studies in which endothelial IRS2 deletion resulted in attenuated insulin-induced capillary recruitment and impaired insulin delivery and glucose disposal in skeletal muscle [7]. Regarding pathologic conditions, in prior work in mice we determined that by modulating endothelial insulin transport, the IgG receptor Fc gamma RIIB (FcγRIIB) in endothelium mediates obesity-related insulin resistance. In the setting of obesity there is hyposialylation of the Fc-associated glycan on IgG, and the administration of IgG from obese mice or IgG hyposialylated by treatment with neuraminidase causes glucose intolerance and insulin resistance via FcγRIIB in the recipient. Cell culture studies further revealed that both those forms of IgG blunt endothelial cell insulin uptake and transcytosis. In addition, IgG from human subjects with type 2 diabetes mellitus results in insulin resistance in the recipient. Furthermore, the administration of the sialic acid precursor N-acetylmannosamine to mice fed a high fat diet (HFD) restores normal sialylation of the IgG glycan, and whereas there is no change in the diet-induced obesity, the mice are protected from glucose intolerance and insulin resistance [8]. Regarding physiologic influences, more recent in vivo work has demonstrated that the estrogen receptor (ER) designated ERα in endothelium promotes glucose disposal by enhancing endothelial insulin transport to skeletal muscle. Endothelial ERα-deficient male mice are glucose intolerant and insulin resistant, and in females lacking the endothelial receptor the antidiabetogenic actions of estradiol (E2) are absent. In cultured endothelial cells the E2 and ERα tandem promotes insulin uptake and transcytosis [9]. Thus, progress has been made understanding the pathologic and physiologic processes in endothelium that influence peripheral insulin action and glucose homeostasis in vivo. However, there is yet much to learn about the endothelial mechanisms that normally promote insulin delivery and glucose disposal in skeletal muscle.

In an effort to gain further insight into the physiologic processes in the endothelium influencing peripheral glucose disposal and thereby overall glucose homeostasis, the present project interrogated the possible role of the Apolipoprotein E receptor designated ApoER2 (also known as LRP8). ApoER2 mediates ApoE-induced signaling in endothelial cells, with cell culture studies initially revealing that the primary ApoE isoform ApoE3 partners with ApoER2 to activate endothelial cell NO synthase [10]. The current work was prompted by the initially surprising observation that male mice deficient in endothelial ApoER2 display fasting hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia. This led to the discovery that endothelial ApoER2 deletion results in glucose intolerance and insulin resistance that this is related to a blunting of skeletal muscle glucose disposal due to a loss of insulin delivery to the muscle. We further interrogated whether this relates to effects on direct actions of insulin on skeletal muscle or insulin stimulation of skeletal muscle microvascular recruitment and blood flow [[11], [12], [13]], or insulin transport across endothelial cells. The intracellular mechanisms by which ApoER2 in endothelium influences insulin transport were also investigated. The findings obtained revealed potentially important mechanisms in endothelium that promote glucose tolerance, and surprisingly they entail processes co-opted from those governing insulin secretion by pancreatic β cells.

Materials and methods

Mouse models

Mice with endothelial cell-specific deletion of ApoER2 (ApoER2ΔEC) were generated by crossing ApoER2 floxed mice (ApoER2fl/fl) with vascular endothelial cadherin promoter-driven Cre mice (VECad-Cre). Cre-negative ApoER2fl/fl mice served as controls. Effective deletion of endothelial cell ApoER2 was confirmed in cells isolated from aorta and lung by qPCR [14] (Fig. S1A and B). The mice were housed at 23 °C with light cycles of 12 h of light beginning at 6:00am and 12 h of dark beginning at 6:00pm, humidity was 30–70%, and food and H2O were provided ad libitum. Experiments were performed in both male and female mice. Beginning at 5 weeks of age, the mice received a standard chow (Harlan Teklad 2016, 12% calories from fat) for 12 weeks. At that time fat mass and lean body mass were determined by NMR (Minispec NMR Analyzer; Bruker), and metabolic studies were performed [9]. The mouse studies were approved by the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, and they were carried out in accordance with the guidelines in the NIH, National Research Council Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Evaluation of glucose homeostasis

Following fasting for 4–6 h, glucose tolerance tests (GTT) and insulin tolerance tests (ITT) were performed in response to an IP injection with d-glucose (1 g/kg BW) or insulin (1unit/kg BW), respectively [15]. Hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamps were performed using our established approach [8, 15]. In brief, hyperinsulinemia was initiated with a primed continuous infusion of insulin (20 mU/kg/min), while variable infusion of 50% dextrose and monitoring of blood glucose every 5 min by glucometer allowed for the achievement of a targeted blood glucose level between 115 and 125 mg/dl. The glucose infusion rate was calculated. Relative hepatic insulin sensitivity was evaluated by assessing hepatic gluconeogenesis in pyruvate tolerance tests (PTT). Mice were fasted for 4 h, and changes in plasma glucose were evaluated over 120min following an IP injection of pyruvate (2 g/kg body weight) [16].

Skeletal muscle glucose uptake and insulin delivery

Skeletal muscle glucose uptake was measured in vivo as previously reported [16]. Briefly, fasted mice were injected IP with 2-deoxy-[3H]glucose ([3H]-2-DOG, Amersham; 2 g/kg; 10 μCi/mouse) mixed with dextrose (20%), and blood glucose was measured at 0–90 min. The glucose-specific activity (disintegrations/min/μmol) was calculated by dividing the radioactivity by the glucose concentration, and the area under the curve (AUC) was integrated for the duration of the experiment (90 min). Skeletal muscle (soleus and gastrocnemius) was harvested at 90 min, and the specific accumulation of [3H]-2-DOG was calculated by dividing the radioactive counts (disintegrations/min) by the integrated glucose specific activity (AUC) and the sample protein content (Bradford assay, Bio Rad Laboratories). In ex vivo studies {3H]-2-DOG was also employed to assess basal and insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in soleus and extensor digitorum longus muscles [15].

Skeletal muscle insulin delivery was assessed using established methods. Fasted mice received tail vein injections with vehicle (saline) or bovine insulin (1 unit/kg BW), 5 min later the circulation was perfused with PBS, the soleus and gastrocnemius were harvested and homogenized in PBS, and protein content was quantified (Bradford, BioRad). Insulin abundance was measured using an ELISA (Crystal Chem Inc. cat. No. 90095) that detects both mouse and bovine insulin, with 2.1-fold greater sensitivity for bovine versus mouse insulin [8].

Contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) of skeletal muscle microvasculature

CEUS imaging was performed on the proximal hind limb adductor muscle group (adductor magnus and semimembranosus) to determine the microvascular response to a 2-hour hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp entailing an insulin infusion at 20 mU/Kg/min as previously described [9]. The ultrasound transducer was fixed for the entire study and a region of interest (ROI) selected at baseline was maintained for all repeat quantifications. With this approach an increase in tissue perfusion from baseline is due to microvascular recruitment in response to the insulin clamp because signal from the larger vessels is the same for each imaging session.

Endothelial cell insulin transport

Human aortic endothelial cells (HAEC) were purchased from Lonza and maintained in EGM-2 and Endothelial Growth Medium with added growth factors (Lonza). Human skeletal muscle endothelial cells (HSMEC) were obtained from Cell Biologics and maintained in Complete Human Endothelial Cell Medium with added growth factors (Cell Biologics). Cells were used within 3–5 passages.

Prior to the study of insulin transport the cells were placed in media lacking growth factors with 1% FBS added for 18 h. To assess insulin uptake the cells were placed in media containing vehicle or ApoE3 (15ug/ml) and FITC-conjugated insulin (5 × 10−8 M, Sigma–Aldrich) for 30min at 37 °C and immunofluoresence was detected by POLARstar Omega plate reader (BMG LABTECH). The plasma concentration of ApoE3 in humans is quite variable, and the concentration studied here is near the mean concentration in humans of 43 ug/ml [17]. Using transwells, insulin transcytosis was studied as previously described [9], using transendothelial electrical resistance (TEER) measurements to demonstrate monolayer integrity and incubations with FITC-dextran (M.W. 4000, Sigma Aldrich) to assess paracellular transport. FITC-dextran transport was less than 5% in all experiments, indicating that paracellular transport was minimal. Candidate gene participation in ApoE3/ApoER2-stimulated endothelial insulin transport was tested by RNAi knockdown. To interrogate the role of the exocyst complex, cells were preincubated with vehicle or the exocyst complex inhibitor Endosidin 2 (10uM) [18] prior to the incubation to evaluate insulin uptake or transcytosis.

In structure–function studies of ApoER2 mediation of ApoE3-stimulated insulin transport, the receptor was silenced in HAEC by RNAi, and ApoER2 expression was reconstituted. The reconstitution was with either wild-type ApoER2 (ApoER2-WT) with an intact Dab-2 binding domain (NPVY), a mutant form of ApoER2 in which the Dab-2 adaptor binding domain is disrupted (ApoER2-NPVA), or mutant ApoER2 lacking the proline-rich domain (ApoER2-Δ59) [14].

Co-immunoprecipitation and immunoblot analyses

Previously-described methods [9] were employed to assess protein co-immunoprecipitation after cell incubation with vehicle versus ApoE3 (15ug/ml) for 0–30 min. Immunoprecipitation was done with ApoER2 antibody (2.5ug, Abcam #ab108208) or anti-IQGAP1 antibody (2.5ug, Abcam #ab86064). Immunoblot analyses, which included samples of cell lysates representing the co-immunoprecipitation inputs, were performed using antibodies against ApoER2, IQGAP-1, Dab-2, ExoC2 (Sec5), ExoC4 (Sec8) or Exo70.

Liquid chromatography/tandem mass spectrometry (LC/MS–MS)

To evaluate changes in the ApoER2 interactome in endothelial cells (HAEC) treated with vehicle versus ApoE3 (15ug/ml for 60min), ApoER2 was immunoprecipitated and the associated proteins were evaluated by liquid chromatography/tandem mass spectrometry (LC/MS–MS) as described previously [9], with the following changes: source voltage 2.2 kV, HPLC gradient length 60min, version of Proteome Discoverer is v.3.0, and UniProt human reviewed protein database is from Jan. 4, 2024. One vehicle-treated and two ApoE3-treated samples were analyzed. Proteins detected only with ApoE3 treatment or with a mean ApoE3-induced increase in abundance of 25% or greater were designated as recruited proteins to consider for evaluation by co-immunoprecipitation and further study.

Statistical analysis

Following normality testing by a Shapiro–Wilk test, for normally-distributed datasets comparisons between two groups were performed by two-sided, unpaired Student's t tests, and differences between more than two groups were evaluated by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Tukey's post-hoc testing. In the instances in which normality testing failed, non-parametric analyses were performed between two groups using Mann–Whitney U test, and between more than two groups by non-parametric ANOVA with Kruskal–Wallis and Dunn's post-hoc testing. GTT, ITT and PTT were compared with two-way ANOVA and Sidak's post-hoc testing. Values are reported as mean ± SEM. Significance was accepted at the 0.05 level of probability.

Results

Endothelial ApoER2 promotes skeletal muscle insulin delivery and glucose disposal

Studies of endothelial cell ApoER2 and glucose homeostasis were initiated in males after standard chow feeding at 17 weeks of age. Body weights, percent body fat and percent lean mass were similar in ApoER2fl/fl controls and ApoER2ΔEC mice (Figure 1A–C). Recognizing that ApoER2 is a major receptor for ApoE, circulating lipids were evaluated, and total cholesterol and triglycerides, circulating HDL and lipid profiles were similar in ApoER2fl/fl and ApoER2ΔEC (Fig. S1C–F). Surprisingly, ApoER2ΔEC displayed relative fasting hyperglycemia and hyperinsulinemia (Figure 1D,E), and GTT, ITT and glucose infusion rate (GIR) during hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp showed that the loss of endothelial cell ApoER2 results in both glucose intolerance and insulin resistance (Figure 1F–H). In contrast to the findings in males, with the loss of endothelial ApoER2 in females not only were body weight and composition unchanged, but fasting glucose levels and GTT and ITT were unperturbed (Fig. S2). Thus, endothelial ApoER2 has considerable impact on glucose control in male mice but not in female mice.

The basis for glucose intolerance and insulin resistance in ApoER2ΔEC male mice was then pursued. Noting that processes in hepatic endothelium influence insulin action in the liver [19], possible changes in hepatic insulin sensitivity were studied using pyruvate tolerance tests (PTT). The provision of pyruvate yielded parallel increases in serum glucose in the ApoER2fl/fl and ApoER2ΔEC mice (Figure 2A). Next we interrogated processes in the skeletal muscle, where up to 80% of whole body glucose disposal occurs in both humans and rodents [20, 21]. Glucose uptake in soleus and gastrocnemius was decreased by 44% in ApoER2ΔEC males (Figure 2B). Recognizing that mechanisms in endothelium are critically involved in insulin delivery to the skeletal muscle [[1], [2], [3], [4]], muscle insulin delivery was tested. Whereas there was a 3.9-fold increase in skeletal muscle insulin content 5 min after bovine insulin injection in ApoER2fl/fl controls, the increase was blunted by 54% in ApoER2ΔEC mice (Figure 2C). In ex vivo studies of direct insulin action on skeletal muscle, insulin-induced glucose uptake in both soleus and extensor digitorum longus was the same in ApoER2fl/fl and ApoER2ΔEC mice (Figure 2D,E). As such, the loss of muscle glucose disposal in vivo with endothelial cell ApoER2 excision is not due to altered processes in skeletal muscle myocytes; it is due to changes in endothelial mechanisms. Thus, via processes in endothelium, ApoER2 supports skeletal muscle glucose disposal by promoting insulin delivery to the skeletal muscle.

How endothelial ApoER2 positively influences skeletal muscle insulin delivery was then determined. Since muscle insulin delivery and resulting glucose disposal are enhanced by insulin stimulation of skeletal muscle microvascular recruitment and blood flow [[11], [12], [13]], the impact of endothelial ApoER2 on muscle microvascular functional responses to insulin was evaluated using CEUS imaging. Measurements were made at baseline and during a hyperinsulemic clamp at 1 h and 2 h. The analysis of time–intensity curves yields parametric measures of capillary blood volume (CBV: peak intensity, Ipk), microvascular blood flow (MBF: time to peak intensity, Tpk; and wash-in rate, WIR), and both CBV and MBF (area under the curve, AUC; and wash-out rate, WOR) [8, [22], [23], [24], [25], [26]]. There was no increase in Tpk in response to insulin in either study group (Figure 2F). In the ApoER2fl/fl control mice Ipk, WIR, AUC and WOR increased during the clamp (Figure 2G–J), indicative of insulin-induced increases in CBV and MBF. Comparable increases occurred in ApoER2fl/fl and ApoER2ΔEC mice. Thus, the decline in muscle insulin delivery with endothelial ApoER2 deletion is not related to effects on insulin-induced microvascular recruitment and blood flow.

Endothelial ApoER2 promotes endothelial cell insulin transport

Along with insulin-related changes in CBV and MBF, insulin delivery to skeletal muscle is controlled by processes governing its transcytosis across the endothelial monolayer, and there is evidence that the latter is rate-limiting for peripheral insulin action [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. We therefore determined if ApoER2 influences insulin transport by endothelial cells, first interrogating insulin uptake in human aortic endothelial cells (HAEC) in response to the most common form of ApoE, ApoE3. We found that ApoE3 caused a 3-fold increase in insulin uptake (Figure 3A). Since the in vivo observations pertain to skeletal muscle microvascular endothelium, we then studied human skeletal muscle microvascular endothelial cells (HSMEC), and found that ApoE3 also causes an increase in insulin uptake in HSMEC (Figure 3B).

We then determined whether ApoER2 is necessary for ApoE3-induced endothelial cell insulin transport. In both HAEC and HSMEC insulin uptake in response to ApoE3 were negated by ApoER2 knockdown (Figure 3C,D). We also found that ApoE3 stimulates insulin transcytosis in both HAEC and HSMEC, and that the transcytosis is ApoER2-dependent (Figure 3E,F). Insulin uptake stimulated by ApoE3 was attenuated by silencing of Dab2 (Fig. S3), a known adaptor protein for ApoER2 in neurons [27]. Considering the in vivo findings that endothelial ApoER2 deficiency causes insulin resistance related to a decline in insulin delivery to skeletal muscle myocytes, these findings in culture suggest that it is due to an attenuation of endothelial cell insulin transport.

ApoE3 and ApoER2 promote endothelial cell insulin transcytosis via IQGAP1

To determine how ApoER2 in endothelium influences insulin transport, we employed IP and LC/MS–MS to interrogate the ApoER2 interactome in endothelial cells and how it is influenced by ApoE3. Table 1 lists the 115 proteins detected to be recruited to ApoER2 upon apoE3 liganding. One of the recruited proteins was IQ motif containing GTPase activating protein 1 (IQGAP1), which is a scaffold protein that regulates diverse cellular processes. One intriguing known function of IQGAP1 is participation in pancreatic β cell insulin secretion [28]. Co-immunoprecipitation using either anti-ApoER2 antibody or anti-IQGAP1 antibody showed that IQGAP1 is recruited to the receptor in response to ApoE3 in both HAEC and HSMEC (Figure 4A, Fig. S4). RNAi knockdown then demonstrated in both HAEC and HSMEC that whereas there is nominal effect of IQGAP1 deletion on ApoE3-induced insulin uptake, ApoE3-stimulated insulin transcytosis was fully attenuated (Figure 4B–E). Thus IQGAP1 is necessary for the exocytosis of insulin mediated by ApoE3 and ApoER2 in endothelial cells, but not for their stimulation of insulin endocytosis.

To better understand how the partnership between ApoER2 and IQGAP1 prompts the release of endocytosed insulin by endothelial cells, we evaluated the role of the exocyst, which is the complex of proteins governing exocytosis in many cell types [29]. Exosidin2, which is an inhibitor of exocyst complex function [18], fully attenuated the insulin transcytosis prompted by ApoE3 (Figure 4F). Using co-immunoprecipitation targeting the exocyst constituent proteins Sec5, Sec8 and Exo70, we determined that IQGAP1 is recruited to the exocyst in response to ApoE3 (Figure 4G). To then determine if the interaction of IQGAP1 with ApoER2 is required for its targeting to the exocyst, we studied two mutant forms of ApoER2 with altered cytoplasmic domains. The endogenous receptor was silenced and then expression was reconstituted with either wild-type ApoER2, a mutant in which the Dab2 adaptor binding domain NPVY is disrupted (ApoER2-NPVA), or a mutant lacking the proline rich domain (ApoER2-Δ59) [14]. First testing their function, we found that in contrast to wild-type receptor, neither the NPVA mutant or the Δ59 mutant ApoER2 were capable of mediating ApoE3-stimulated insulin uptake (Fig. S5). Also in contrast to wild-type ApoER2, neither the NPVA mutant or the Δ59 mutant ApoER2 were capable of recruiting Dab-2 or IQGAP1 to the receptor in response to ApoE3 (Figure 4H,I). This was observed when either ApoER2 or IQGAP1 was immunprecipitated. Furthermore, with either mutant form of ApoER2 incapable of recruiting IQGAP1, there was a loss of ApoE3-stimulated engagement of IQGAP1 with the exocyst constituents Sec5, Sec8 and Exo70 (Figure 4J,K). Thus, ApoER2 interaction with IQGAP1 is required for the IQGAP1 partnership with the exocyst, which is necessary for ApoE3 and ApoER2 to stimulate endothelial cell insulin transcytosis.

Discussion

Endothelial mechanisms participate in glucose homeostasis by impacting the delivery of circulating insulin to the skeletal muscle. Attenuated endothelial insulin transport contributes to obesity-related insulin resistance [8], and the promotion of transport underlies the anti-diabetic actions of estrogens [9]. The present work reveals that endothelial ApoER2 promotes glucose tolerance by mediating endothelial cell insulin transport to the skeletal muscle myocytes. Mechanistic studies further demonstrate that ApoE3 and ApoER2-stimulated endothelial insulin transport requires the ApoER2 adaptor protein Dab2 and an IQGAP1-related exocytosis mechanism that endothelial cells interestingly share with pancreatic β cells.

The impact of endothelial ApoER2 deletion on glucose homeostasis was observed exclusively in male mice. For consideration in future studies, endothelial actions of other LDL receptor family members such as LDLR, VLDLR and LRP1 [30] may prevent changes in endothelial ApoE3-driven processes with the ApoER2 loss in females. The effect of global ApoER2 deletion on glucose control was recently reported in studies only performed in male mice. With high fat, western diet feeding the null mice had exaggerated hyperglycemia [31], and the endothelial mechanism we now report may have contributed. However, in light of the cooperative relationship between ApoER2 and IQGAP1 that we now demonstrate in endothelial cells, it is notable that the exaggerated glucose intolerance in the global null ApoER2 mice was associated with blunted glucose-induced insulin secretion [31]. ApoER2 function and a possible partnership between ApoER2 and IQGAP1 now warrants study in pancreatic β cells.

The present findings related to IQGAP1 suggest that there is more to learn about endothelial insulin transport by considering the mechanisms responsible for insulin exocytosis in pancreatic β cells. Knowing that IQGAP1 has dozens of protein interactions in other contexts [32, 33], a query into its interactome in endothelium and its modulation by various stimuli of endothelial insulin transcytosis may yield new clues about an important facet of glucose homeostasis.

Conclusions

The present work reveals that the LDL receptor family member ApoER2 in endothelial cells promotes endothelial cell insulin transport, thereby favoring glucose disposal in skeletal muscle and normal glucose homeostasis. Through partnership with the scaffolding protein IQGAP1, the ApoE3 and ApoER2 tandem co-opts a process by which pancreatic β cells secrete insulin to enhance endothelial insulin transport. The increased understanding of processes promoting endothelial insulin transport may result in new strategies to support normal glucose homeostasis under conditions such as obesity in which attenuated insulin transport contributes to insulin resistance.

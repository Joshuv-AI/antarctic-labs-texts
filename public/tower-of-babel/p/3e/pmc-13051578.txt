The impact of preoperative serum albumin on postoperative recovery and health-related quality of life in older hip fracture patients
Authors: Wilasinee Sirichativapee, Pojchong Chotiyarnwong, Aasis Unnanuntana, Ekasame Vanitcharoenkul
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Objective:The primary objective was to evaluate the predictive value of serum albumin levels for long-term functional recovery after hip fracture surgery.Methods:Data from a university hospital’s Fracture Liaison Service Registry (2016-2021) were retrospectively analyzed. Hypoalbuminemia was defined as a serum albumin level below 35 g/L upon admission, in line with established clinical thresholds from previous studies. Functional outcomes were assessed at baseline, 3 months, and 1 year after surgery using the Barthel Index to measure basic activities of daily living and the EuroQol Visual Analog Scale to assess overall health-related quality of life. Multivariate logistic regression identified risk factors for poor Barthel Index scores (<60) at 1 year postoperatively.Results:The analysis included 1086 patients (mean age 79.9 years, 75.8% female) with a 28.9% prevalence of hypoalbuminemia (309 patients). All functional outcomes improved over the 1-year follow-up. However, in the hypoalbuminemia group, gains in quality of life plateaued after the first 3 months. Lower serum albumin and a higher comorbidity burden were associated with unfavorable long-term functional recovery. A serum albumin level below 38 g/L (identified using the Youden Index) emerged as a threshold for predicting poor outcomes with Area Under Curve of 0.62.Conclusion:Older adults with serum albumin levels below 38 g/L may be at increased risk of poor functional recovery following hip fracture surgery. Although hypoalbuminemia is traditionally defined as levels below 35 g/L, the findings suggest that adverse outcomes may begin to emerge at slightly higher levels. Early recognition of this risk, along with timely nutritional and rehabilitative interventions around the 38 g/L threshold, may help support improved long-term outcomes and reduce post-fracture disability.Level of Evidence:Level III, Prognostic study.

Introduction

The global population is aging rapidly, leading to a rising incidence of age-related conditions such as osteoporosis and fractures. Among these, hip fractures are a major cause of morbidity and mortality, especially in older adults. They carry a 1-year mortality rate of 20.5%-27%,1,2 and many survivors experience reduced mobility, diminished independence, and lower quality of life.3 About 40% of hip fracture patients do not regain independent ambulation, 60% require ongoing caregiver support, and one-third become fully dependent.4 Amarilla-Donoso et al5 similarly reported significant deterioration in self-care, daily activities, and mobility 1 year after hip fracture surgery in a cohort of 224 patients. Known risk factors for poor recovery include advanced age, female sex, comorbidities, frailty, and certain ethnic backgrounds.4

Malnutrition, encompassing nutrient deficiencies, imbalances, or impaired utilization due to factors such as reduced appetite, dysphagia, or chronic illness, is concerningly prevalent (4%-39.4%) among older adults with hip fractures prior to surgery.6,7 This condition contributes to muscle loss, weakened physical performance, compromised immunity, increased infection risk, and delayed recovery. Several assessment tools, including anthropometric measures and laboratory investigations, are available to evaluate nutritional status.8 Among these, serum albumin is widely used for preoperative risk assessment.9 In older hip fracture patients, serum albumin levels below 35 g/L have been linked to higher rates of postoperative complications and increased mortality.9 Serum albumin also reflects systemic inflammation, metabolic reserve, and overall physiologic resilience, which are crucial for recovering from major trauma and surgery.10 Despite the established relevance of serum albumin, its relationship with long-term functional outcomes—particularly beyond 6 months following hip fracture surgery—remains underexamined.11 This gap hinders the ability to identify patients at risk for prolonged disability and to implement timely interventions.

This study hypothesized that preoperative hypoalbuminemia in older hip fracture patients predicts poor postoperative functional recovery and diminished health-related quality of life. The aim was to evaluate the predictive value of preoperative serum albumin levels for postoperative functional recovery and to identify independent predictors of poor 1-year outcomes after hip fracture surgery.

Material and methods

Study design

Ethical approval for this study was obtained from the Siriraj Institutional Review Board, Faculty of Medicine Siriraj Hospital, Mahidol University, Bangkok, Thailand (reference: 812/2022 & Protocol no. 760/2565). A retrospective analysis was performed using data from the Siriraj Fracture Liaison Service Registry, covering the period from 2016 to 2021.

Patient selection

Patients aged 60 years or older who were diagnosed with fragility hip fractures and surgically treated during the study period were included. Exclusion criteria included multiple fractures, prior hip surgery, pathologically confirmed fractures, and absence of preoperative serum albumin data.

Siriraj orthogeriatric care model

The Siriraj orthogeriatric care model delivers integrated care for fragility hip fracture patients through collaboration between orthopedic surgeons and geriatricians. Geriatricians conduct comprehensive assessments and optimize the patient’s physiological condition before and after surgery with the goals of expediting surgery and minimizing potential complications and mortality.

Siriraj fracture liaison service

All patients with osteoporotic hip fractures received multidisciplinary care under the center’s hip fracture protocol, which aimed to prevent refracture. This care included osteoporosis education, fall-prevention strategies, calcium and vitamin D supplementation, recommended pharmacologic treatment, and discharge planning. Patients were enrolled in a registry for follow-up on quality of life, treatment adherence, and fall incidence, conducted by trained nurses or researchers.

Serum albumin level

Blood samples for serum albumin measurement were obtained before admission as part of the routine preoperative laboratory workup. Patients with serum albumin levels less than 35 g/L were classified as having hypoalbuminemia, consistent with previous research.9 Preoperative serum albumin values were also divided into quartiles for additional analysis: Q1 (<34 g/L), Q2 (34-38 g/L), Q3 (39-41 g/L), and Q4 (>41 g/L).

Barthel Index

The Barthel Index is a validated tool for assessing performance in activities of daily living. It consists of 10 items related to functional independence in mobility and self-care. Higher scores reflect greater independence in tasks such as feeding, personal hygiene, toileting, bathing, grooming, controlling bodily functions, dressing, transferring, walking, and stair negotiation. Based on previous research, a Barthel Index score less than 60 was defined as indicating functional dependence.12 The Thai version of the Barthel Index has demonstrated reliability and validity, making it suitable for assessing functional recovery in older Thai patients after hip fracture surgery.13 Patients were assessed at baseline, 3 months, and 1 year postoperatively.

EuroQol Visual Analog Scale

We employed the EuroQol Visual Analog Scale (EQ-VAS) to assess health-related quality of life. This validated instrument for older adults with fragility hip fractures consists of a 20-centimeter vertical visual analog scale, with scores ranging from 0 (worst imaginable health) to 100 (best imaginable health).14 Patients rated their overall health status at baseline, 3 months after surgery, and 1 year after surgery. This tool has been validated for older adults with hip fractures to evaluate functional outcomes.15

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics was used to summarize the data. Categorical variables are presented as frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables are reported as means with SDs or medians with ranges, depending on their distribution. To compare quantitative variables between the hypoalbuminemia group and the normal albumin group, the Mann–Whitney U-test or Student’s t-test was employed. For categorical variables, the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test was used, as appropriate. Repeated-measures ANOVA was performed to assess longitudinal changes in outcomes across the quartile groups.

To examine longitudinal changes in the Barthel Index and EQ-VAS scores between the first and fourth quartiles, ANCOVA was used across different time intervals (baseline to 3 months, 3 months to 1 year, and baseline to 1 year). Levene’s test was used to verify the homogeneity of variances.

Univariate analysis was conducted to assess the association between individual risk factors and poor functional outcomes at 1 year, as measured by Barthel Index scores. Collinearity among variables was evaluated using linear regression diagnostics. A stepwise multivariable logistic regression was then conducted to identify independent predictors. Variables with a univariate P-value ≤ .20, as well as those considered clinically relevant based on prior literature (preoperative hypoalbuminemia, hemoglobin level, sex, BMI, CCI, and vitamin D status), were included in the model.11,16,17 Statistical significance was defined as a P-value < .05.

To enhance the robustness of the findings regarding the association between serum albumin levels and poor functional outcomes at 1 year after hip surgery, a sensitivity analysis was conducted. This included a dose‒response relationship analysis to identify optimal thresholds and an assessment of the predictive value of serum albumin levels by calculating the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. The Youden Index identified the optimal serum albumin cutoff, defined as the point that maximized both sensitivity and specificity.18 This approach provided the best trade-off for predicting poor functional outcomes.

All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 30 (IBM SPSS Corp.; Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

Our analysis included 1068 hip fracture patients aged ≥60 years who were identified from the Siriraj Fracture Liaison Service Registry (July 1, 2016-December 31, 2021) and met the study eligibility criteria. Hypoalbuminemia was observed in 309 patients (28.9%; Figure 1). Table 1 provides a summary of their demographic and clinical characteristics.

The mean age of the cohort was 79.9 years, with a female predominance (75.8%). Comorbidities were common, with 60% of patients having a CCI ≥ 5. The hypoalbuminemia group exhibited a significantly greater mean age (80.9 years vs 79.5 years, P = .016), a lower mean BMI (21.4 kg/m2 vs 22.5 kg/m2, P = .001), and a greater mean CCI (5.3 vs 5.1, P = .039) than the normal albumin group. These findings suggest greater vulnerability because lower BMI indicates poorer nutritional status or frailty, and higher CCI signifies more comorbidity. No statistically significant differences were found between the groups regarding sex, fracture location, type of surgery, pre-injury ambulatory status, anemia incidence, postoperative serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D level, or hospital length of stay.

Quartile-based analysis of preoperative serum albumin (<34 g/L, 34-38 g/L, 39-41 g/L, >41 g/L) showed higher serum albumin levels were consistently associated with better 1-year functional outcomes (P for trend < .001; Figure 2). This finding suggested a clear dose‒response relationship between the preoperative serum albumin concentration and functional recovery. Additionally, lower albumin levels were associated with an increased probability and incidence of a poor Barthel Index score.

The area under the receiver operating characteristic curve for predicting poor functional outcomes was 0.62 (95% CI 0.59-0.65). A serum albumin level of 38 g/L demonstrated the maximum Youden Index. The corresponding sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, positive likelihood ratio, and negative likelihood ratio were 65.4%, 51.5%, 21.7%, 79.3%, 2.05, and 0.47, respectively (Figure 3). However, the modest increase in AUC suggests that serum albumin alone should not be used as a standalone predictor of poor outcomes.

ANCOVA was performed on patients with complete longitudinal data (baseline, 3 months, and 1 year). Patients in both the high-albumin (quartile 4) and low-albumin (quartile 1) groups demonstrated significant functional improvement within the first 3 months following surgery (P < .001). Interestingly, patients in quartile 4 (high albumin levels) continued to show significant improvement in their EQ-VAS scores between the 3-month and 1-year follow-ups (P < .001). In contrast, the low-albumin group reached a plateau in their EQ-VAS scores, with significant improvements occurring only within the first 3 months (Figure 4).

Multivariate analysis revealed that preoperative hypoalbuminemia and a CCI ≥ 5 were significantly associated with poor functional outcomes at 1 year, as indicated by Barthel Index scores less than 60 (functional dependence; Table 2). The poor outcome of functional dependence was observed in 66 patients (27.7%) within the hypoalbuminemia group, compared to 122 patients (18.2%) in the cohort with normal albumin levels. Patients with hypoalbuminemia had lower cumulative survival at 3 and 6 months, although these differences were not statistically significant. By 1 year, mortality in the hypoalbuminemia group almost doubled compared with those who had serum albumin ≥35 g/L (12.1% vs 6.8%; Table 3).

Discussion

Osteoporotic hip fractures in older adults remain a major health concern, with many patients experiencing prolonged declines in self-care, mobility, and overall quality of life despite surgical intervention. These persistent deficits highlight the need to identify factors influencing long-term functional outcomes.5,19,20 This research confirms that preoperative hypoalbuminemia and presence of comorbidities are robust predictor of poor functional outcomes after surgery.4,11 Importantly, it was found that preoperative hypoalbuminemia limits the duration of functional improvement after hip fracture surgery. Furthermore, a serum albumin level below 38 g/L appears to indicate a potential threshold for the onset of poor functional outcomes.

The prevalence of preoperative hypoalbuminemia in this study was 28.9% (309/1068 patients). This significantly exceeds the 1.5%-3.1% range reported in previous studies on older adults from the general population but remains lower than the prevalence observed in hospitalized patients (52.5%-87.0%).21-24 This may be attributed to the underlying comorbidities and frailty characteristics of hip fracture patients, leading to compromised nutritional status and chronic hypoalbuminemia even prior to injury.25

Our study emphasizes the importance of both preoperative hypoalbuminemia and a CCI ≥ 5 as strong, independent predictors of poor functional outcomes at 1 year after hip fracture surgery. Serum albumin concentration, which is affected by factors such as nutritional status and inflammation, is a validated surrogate marker. Prolonged hypoalbuminemia, compounded by postsurgical inflammation, further elevates the risk of adverse outcomes and mortality, particularly in patients with hip fractures.9,11,26 Serum albumin may better reflect current health status than the CCI, providing a more dynamic health assessment. The finding that 38 g/L is a potential discriminative threshold offers valuable clinical guidance. It suggests targeted interventions, such as preoperative nutritional supplementation and intensive postoperative rehabilitation, may be especially beneficial for hip fracture patients with serum albumin levels below this point. However, the modest area under the curve of 0.62 indicates that serum albumin alone is not a comprehensive prognostic tool and that its utility likely improves when combined with other clinical indicators. Additionally, the strong association between a higher CCI and poorer outcomes reinforces the role of the CCI in assessing surgical candidacy and predicting functional recovery.27

Contrary to established literature linking BMI, hemoglobin, and vitamin D to functional outcomes, these findings suggest these factors may be overshadowed by serum albumin’s predominant influence. The relationship between BMI and recuperative potential remains inconclusive, as shown by prior studies.28-30 Low BMI can indicate underlying health complexities, whereas high BMI might hinder rehabilitation effectiveness and adherence to postoperative treatments. This study did not detect a significant association between preoperative anemia and functional recovery, possibly due to variations in fracture types and perioperative transfusion practices.31 Future investigations, akin to those by Lawrance et al,32 are warranted to clarify the impact of postoperative hemoglobin levels on early outcomes. Although the role of vitamin D in skeletal health is well-established, and multiple studies demonstrate adverse effects of deficiency on post-hip fracture recovery,33,34 the findings suggest a more nuanced scenario. Surgical stress and metabolic shifts may alter vitamin D levels, diminishing its reliability as a prognostic marker.35

This study underscores the limited functional recovery potential of patients with low serum albumin within the first 3 months following hip fracture surgery. Surprisingly, this initial recovery rate did not significantly differ from that of patients with normal albumin levels. This finding contrasts with that of Sim et al,11 who reported a slower recovery in hypoalbuminemia patients. Although the reasons for this difference remain unclear, it is hypothesized that the standard practice of routine oral nutritional supplementation, both during hospitalization and for at least 1 month after discharge, may play a key role. Extensive research has demonstrated the benefits of oral nutritional supplements in promoting muscle recovery and reducing postoperative complications.36,37

This study benefited from its large sample size of older adults with hip fractures. The use of preoperative serum albumin also avoided potential confounding factors arising from intraoperative and postoperative blood loss, including hemodilution and capillary leakage.

This study has several limitations. Its retrospective design and the loss of some patients to follow-up are potential sources of bias. Additionally, the serum albumin concentration was not evaluated at each follow-up, which could provide valuable insights into how functional outcomes change over time in relation to fluctuations in the concentration. Future prospective studies with serial serum albumin measurements will help elucidate this dynamic relationship and its impact on recovery. Finally, the single-center design at a tertiary university hospital and the high comorbidity profile of this cohort may limit generalizability, because the sample may not represent all older hip fracture patients.

In conclusion, hip fractures remain a significant health challenge for older adults and profoundly impact their quality of life, despite surgical interventions. This study confirmed that hypoalbuminemia is a potential predictor of poor functional outcomes after hip surgery. The high prevalence of preoperative hypoalbuminemia underscores the need for targeted nutritional interventions and specialized rehabilitation strategies. Importantly, serum albumin levels below 38 g/L may indicate a heightened risk of functional decline, demanding proactive management. Although preoperative hemoglobin and postoperative vitamin D levels were not associated with poor outcomes in this study, further research is needed to optimize interventions for this vulnerable population.

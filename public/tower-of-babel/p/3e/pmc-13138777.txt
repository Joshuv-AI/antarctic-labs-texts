Characteristics of patients with symptomatic and incidental pulmonary thromboembolism
Authors: Başak GÖNEN, Pervin HANCI, Osman Nuri HATİPOĞLU
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Characteristics of patients with symptomatic and incidental pulmonary thromboembolism Introduction: Pulmonary thromboembolism (PTE) has a wide range of clinical presentations. With the advances in computed tomography (CT) technology and easier access to CT, the incidence of incidentally diagnosed cases of PTE has increased. The main aim of our study was to determine the frequency of patients incidentally diagnosed with PTE and whether these patients differ from patients with symptomatic PTE in terms of case characteristics. Materials and Methods: We retrospectively analysed the charts of 148 patients with PTE diagnosed and treated in 2022. Demographic characteristics, thrombus localisation, risk factors, and treatment modalities were compared between symptomatic patients with clinically suspected PTE and patients with incidentally diagnosed PTE by imaging methods performed for other purposes without clinically suspected PTE. Results: Out of 148 patients with PTE, 42 (28.3%) were diagnosed incidentally. The rate of concomitant malignancy was significantly higher in patients with incidental PTE (54.8%) than in patients with symptomatic PTE (28.3%) (p< 0.01). There was no significant difference between symptomatic and incidental PTE patients in terms of the pulmonary artery segment in which the thrombus was located (p> 0.05). Conclusion: In our patient group, approximately one out of four patients diagnosed with PTE were incidentally diagnosed. Patients with malignancies may not have symptoms suspicious for PTE or their symptoms may go unrecognized. Keywords: SARS-CoV-2, lymphocyte subsets, IFN-γ, PD-1, Tim-3

INTRODUCTION

Pulmonary thromboembolism (PTE) occurs when the pulmonary artery or its branches become blocked by a thrombus, usually from deep veins (1,2,3). Its symptoms can vary widely and may be influenced by factors such as age, cardiopulmonary reserve, size of the embolus, and localization of the thrombus (4). Prior to the development of computed tomography (CT) technology, PTE could only be diagnosed with clinical suspicion. However, with the advancement of CT technology, the number of cases being diagnosed without clinical symptoms is increasing (3,5).

It is believed that hospitalization and mortality rates caused by PTE have decreased over time. This can be attributed to the advancements made in treatment and the ability to detect blood clots, even in small blood vessels, using thoracic CT scans (5,6,7,8,9,10). Early detection of PTE is essential as it greatly affects treatment choices and mortality rates (11).

It has been observed that some patients are incidentally diagnosed with pulmonary thromboembolism (PTE) even though they do not exhibit any symptoms or clinical signs of the condition (3,5). These incidentally diagnosed PTEs are typically found during CT imaging for restaging in oncology patients, but they can also be seen in CT scans performed for other reasons, such as coronary artery disease, pulmonary infections, or major trauma (10,12,13).

The primary aim of this study was to determine the frequency of patients with an incidental diagnosis of PTE and compare the clinical and radiologic features of these patients with those of patients with symptomatic PTE. Our secondary aim was to examine patient characteristics according to radiologic involvement sites.

MATERIALS and METHODS

This study is a retrospective cross-sectional study. Patients diagnosed with PTE in Trakya University Faculty of Medicine Hospital between January 1 and December 31, 2022 were included. Patient data were scanned from the hospital automation system. Pulmonary thromboembolism was diagnosed based on thrombus presence on thorax CT. Diagnosis confirmation of PTE was done through ventilation/perfusion (V/P) scintigraphy in patients with suspected thrombus on CT. Patients’ demographics, comorbidities, PTE has four risk factors (malignancy, DVT, history of immo- bility, and other risk factors), complaints at the time of diagnosis, radiologic distribution of PTE (sub-segmentary, segmentary, main pulmonary artery, lobar), PTE treatment site (service, outpatient clinic, intensive care unit) and treatments (low molecular weight heparin, thrombolytic, vitamin K antagonist) were recorded. Patients were grouped as incidental and symptomatic according to the presence of any symptoms suspicious of PTE at the time of diagnosis.

These two groups were compared in terms of age, sex, risk factors, and radiological vascular localization of the thrombus.

In addition, according to the thrombus’s vascular localization, patients with involvement in the main pulmonary artery, lobar artery, segmental artery, and sub-segmental artery were divided into two groups: Sub-segmental/segmental or main pulmonary artery/lobar artery involvement. The grouping was based on the largest involvement site from the thrombus localization defined on CT. Both groups were compared in terms of age, sex, risk factors, incidental or not, treatment site, and treatment modality.

Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS software version 26.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY). Descriptive analyses were presented as count (percentage) for categorical variables or median (25th-75th percentile) for numerical variables. Baseline characteristics, risk factors, radiological involvement of thrombus and outcomes were compared between symptomatic or asymptomatic patient’s groups using chi-square analysis or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate for categorical variables and the Mann-Whitney u test for numerical variables. Characteristics of patients grouped according to radiological involvement of pulmonary thromboembolism were compared with chi-square analysis or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate for categorical variables and the Mann-Whitney u test for numerical variables. A 5% type-I error level was used to infer statistical significance.

Our study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Trakya University.

RESULTS

A total of 148 patients diagnosed with PTE were included in the study. Of the patients, 78 (53%) were male. Median age of the patients was 67.5 years (57.0-74.7). Risk factors were malignancy in 53 patients (35.8%), deep vein thrombosis in 37 patients (25%), and immobility in 36 patients (24.3%). In 106 symptomatic patients diagnosed with suspected PTE, dyspnea was the most common presenting complaint (43.9%). Chest pain (12.8%), syncope (5.4%), haemoptysis (3.4%), leg swelling/pain (3.4%), and palpitations (2.7%) were the other primary complaints in these patients. In 42 (28.3%) patients, PTE was diagnosed incidentally without clinical suspicion.

During the analysis of thrombus distribution on radiology scans, it was found that thrombus was present in both lungs in 74 (50%) of the patients. Thrombus was detected in segmentary artery branches in 60 (40.5%) patients, sub-segmentary artery branches in 43 (29.0%) patients, main pulmonary arteries in 24 patients (16.2%), and lobar arteries in 21 patients (16.2%) (Figure 1,Figure 2). A total of 105 patients (93 in the ward and 12 in the intensive care unit) were hospitalized, and 43 patients were treated as outpatients. Among the patients, 138 received low molecular weight heparin, five were treated with thrombolytics, and four were treated with oral anticoagulants. The treatment data of one patient was not available.

Incidental thrombus was detected in the main pulmonary artery on thoracic CT imaging of a 93-year-old woman who presented with gastrointestinal bleeding.

A thrombus image in the right and left lobar pulmonary branches was detected on thorax CT imaging during routine control of a patient with endometrial malignancy.

Characteristics of the patients with PTE, according to whether they were symptomatic or incidental, are shown in Table 1. The rate of concomitant malignancy was significantly higher in patients with incidental PTE (54.8%) than in patients with symptomatic PTE (28.3%) (p< 0.01). There was no significant difference in thrombus localization between symptomatic and incidental PTE patients (p> 0.05). Among the 23 patients with incidental PTE and concomitant malignancy, 17 (73.9%) patients had active malignancy and 6 (26.1%) patients were followed up under cure. Thrombus was diagnosed in 34 of 42 patients with incidental PTE on outpatient imaging. In patients with a diagnosis of malignancy and incidental PTE, the most common underlying malignancy type was lung cancer in 6 patients (26%), followed by endometrial cancer in four patients (17%). When the patients were divided into two groups as sub-segmentary/segmentary and main pulmonary artery/lobar artery according to the radiologic distribution of the thrombus, no difference was found in terms of age, sex, risk factors, symptoms, treatment site, and type of treatment (Table 2).

DISCUSSION

In our study, PTE was diagnosed incidentally in 42 (28.4%) of 148 patients with PTE. The presence of malignancy was found to be more frequent in incidentally diagnosed cases (54.8%) than in patients with symptomatic PTE (28.3%) (p< 0.05).

In the literature, the frequency of incidental PTE varies according to the study design and the differences in the groups from which the sample is selected.

Studies reporting the rates of incidentally detected PTE in CT by examining a large number of CT scans in patient groups with malignancy are intensive. The rates of incidental PTE in such studies are between 1.6% and 4.6% (12,14-17). In a meta-analysis of CT data of 28.626 malignancy patients including 12 studies, incidental PTE has been detected in 3.36% of patients (12). In a multi-centre study conducted in our country in which CT imaging of 1000 oncology patients has been examined, the incidental PTE rate has been found to be 4.6% (15). In a study conducted in Italy in which thorax CT imaging of 581 patients has been analysed, the incidental PTE rate has been found to be 3.4% (16). In a study conducted in England in 731 patients with active malignancy, incidental PTE has been found in 26 of 1604 imaging scans, i.e., 1.6%, mostly in the lung, breast, and colorectal cancer follow-up (17).

In studies evaluating cases diagnosed with PTE, the incidence of incidental PTE has been reported to be between 35.3% and 62.7%, which is relatively compatible with the rate we found in our study (28.37%) (18,19-22). In 2016, in a study examining 11.747 CT scans of 7238 patients who underwent contrast-enhanced CT imaging over 5.5 years, the incidental PTE frequency was reported to be 35.3% among all cases of PTE (19). Between 2005 and 2010, in a single-centre study examining thorax CT imaging of oncology patients, it was reported that approximately half of all cases of PTE were incidentally detected (20). In a study conducted in 2016, 111 oncology patients diagnosed with PTE for three years were examined, and it was observed that 40% of PTE was detected incidentally (21). In another multicenter study, PTE was detected in 59 of 404 patients who underwent autopsy, and 37 of these cases (62.7%) were detected incidentally during autopsy (22).

Storto et al. have analysed the thorax CT images of 589 patients, and they have detected incidental PTE in 20 patients and found underlying malignancy in 14 (70%) of these patients (16). In our study, we detected underlying malignancy in 54.8% of patients with incidental PTE.

It is reported that more distal branches are involved in incidental cases (10,14, 19,21). When the imaging studies of 487 patients who underwent thorax CT were analyzed, incidental PTE was detected in 28 patients and segmental and sub-segmental involve- ment was observed in 18 (64.2%) and lobar, central, and main pulmonary artery involvement was observed in 11 (39.2%) (14). In our study, 29 (69%) of 42 asymptomatic patients had sub-segmental and segmental branches and 13 (31%) had main pulmonary artery or lobar branches. No statistically significant difference was found between incidental cases and symptomatic cases.

The high rate of active malignancy in incidentally diagnosed PTE cases in our study is one of the points to be considered. In a study on the rates of thromboembolic events occurring before the diagnosis of cancer, the probability of thromboembolism has been found to be higher, close to the diagnosis of malignancy (23). This leads us to wonder whether incidental PTE, i.e., thrombus formation, may indicate an underlying active malignancy even if the malignancy is considered inactive.

The limitations of our study were that it was retrospective and a single-center study.

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, in our study, PTE was diagnosed incidentally almost in one of four patients diagnosed with PTE. It should not be forgotten that PTE may occur without symptoms in patients with malignancy and that there may be an underlying malignancy in patients diagnosed with PTE incidentally.

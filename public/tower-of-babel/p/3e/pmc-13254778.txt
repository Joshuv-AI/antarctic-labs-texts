Young Man With Neck Pain
Authors: Sejal Patel, Steven Graver, Megan Daniels, Kelly Goodsell, Arthur K Au
Published: 2026

Case Presentation

A 37-year-old man with a history of a gunshot wound to the neck 3 years prior presented to the emergency department (ED) for progressive right neck pain. He denied any fevers, chills, voice change, or difficulty swallowing. His physical examination was unremarkable except for mild tenderness to palpation over a retained bullet just superior to the mid clavicle. There was no concern for airway compromise.

Diagnosis: Retained Bullet

A bedside ultrasound was performed using a high-frequency linear transducer. It showed an echogenic foreign body with a hyperechoic rim—a retained bullet (Figs 1 and 2). There was a reverberation artifact deep within this structure consistent with a metallic foreign body. There were areas of soft tissue edema surrounding the foreign body. Adjacent vascular structures were noted with color doppler (Figs 3 and 4; Videos 1 and 2). Despite the foreign body being superficial, the bullet was not removed in the ED because of concern for significant bleeding. The patient was referred to surgery for definitive management.

Comparison of the analgesic efficacy of dexketoprofen trometamol and paracetamol with naproxen sodium and codeine phosphate combinations in acute toothache
Authors: İbrahim Doğru, Levent Ciğerim
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this study was to evaluate the efficacy of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol combinations in the treatment of acute dental pain during the preoperative period. Patients with complaints of acute pericoronitis caused by a semi-erupted lower impacted third molar were included in the study. Patients were randomly divided into two groups, naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate (550/30 mg) in group A and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol (25/300 mg) in group B, and pain was assessed by VAS. The collected data were statistically analysed and interpreted using SPSS. The Friedman test (a non-parametric test) was used to analyse data within groups. The independent groups t-test and Mann–Whitney U-test were used to analyse data between groups. Of the 62 patients included in the study, 51 (82.3%) were female and 11 (17.7%) were male, with a mean age of 22.94 years. As a result of the 7-day evaluation, the VAS average in group A decreased from 7.61 to 1.26, representing an 83.44% decrease, while in group B, it decreased from 7.19 to 0.61, representing an 91.52% decrease. When the VAS averages of groups A and B were compared over time, statistically significant differences were only found between the values on day 7 (p < 0.05). Both analgesics were effective in acute dental pain. The results of this study support the use of dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol and naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combinations in the treatment of acute dental pain during preoperative period. Introduction

Teeth that do not find a place in the dental arch and do not function in the mouth for various local and systemic reasons are called impacted teeth (IT). In humans, this most commonly affects the third molars1. IT may cause either no symptoms or symptoms such as pericoronitis, periodontal problems, cysts and tumours, root resorption and displacement in neighbouring teeth, temporomandibular joint problems, and crowding problems2,3. Genetic mutations, the continuation of tooth development after jaw development, the presence of tumours such as odontoma in the eruption path, jaw cysts, etc., can be counted among the causes of IT4–6.

Acute pain is one of the most common reasons for consulting a dentist. Although most conditions that cause acute pain originate from the pulp, periodontal causes, acute abscesses, acute pericoronitis (AP), and post-operative conditions can also cause pain7,8. AP is a painful condition caused by inflammation and infection due to food debris and microorganisms that penetrate under the gingival cap covering the partially erupted or erupting IT crown9. AP can be treated by extracting the affected tooth. However, if pain, fever, lymphadenopathy, etc., are associated with systemic findings, these should be controlled first. While antibiotics are preferred to control the systemic condition, various analgesic treatments can be used to control the patient’s acute pain during this period10.

There are studies in the literature evaluating the efficacy of various drugs in dentistry, particularly for postoperative pain, and these continue to be studied. However, there are insufficient studies on the control of acute pain in the preoperative period11–15. Treatment options for acute pain in impacted teeth include extraction of the affected tooth, opercullectomy, or medical therapy. Operculectomy without removal of the impacted tooth is not recommended in all cases because of the risk of recurrence of pericoronitis7,9. Although drug treatment may be preferred for current pain control, there is a risk of recurrence if the causative agent is not removed. In cases associated with systemic findings, drug treatment is inevitable. NSAIDs, steroids, opioids, etc. can be used to treat pain. Because of their anti-inflammatory properties, steroidal and non-steroidal analgesics are the first drugs that come to mind for the treatment of acute pain caused by impacted teeth. Although steroids are superior to NSAIDs in their anti-inflammatory properties, their side effects increase with dose and duration. For this reason, NSAIDs are preferred in cases that require long-term use of painkillers. For moderate to severe pain, the use of NSAIDs in combination with other analgesics has been reported to increase pain relief. In addition, the relatively low doses of analgesics used in combination with NSAIDs reduce the risk of side effects. Due to their analgesic effect, NSAIDs or NSAID combinations are recommended for acute pain caused by impacted teeth11–15.

It should be noted that the combinations of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol are approved and available on the market as combined analgesics containing NSAIDs. The use of drugs with a high analgesic effect is necessary in the management of pain in patients with acute pain. Overcrowding in healthcare facilities, financial constraints, and patient anxiety often lead to patients postponing or delaying treatment, leading to patients seeking relief from analgesics. There are no studies in the literature evaluating the effects of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol combinations. The aim of this study was to compare the analgesic efficacy of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol combinations in patients with acute pain caused by AP. The null hypothesis of this trial was that the combinations of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol would not differ in their analgesic effect in reducing acute dental pain due to AP.

Materials and methods

This randomised, prospective, single-blind study was conducted on patients who presented to Van Yuzuncu Yil University Faculty of Dentistry, Department of Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery between January 2023 and December 2023 with the complaint of AP originating from the lower ITM and trial was retrospectively registered on http://www.clinicaltrials.gov/ on 21/03/2025 under the title ‘Evaluation of the Analgesic Efficacy of the Combination of Dexketoprofen + Paracetamol and the Combination of Naproxen Sodium + Codeine in Patients With Acute Dental Pain’ and number NCT06916234. On the first visit, the patients underwent a clinical examination to assess their reported complaints, whether the complaints were caused by an impacted lower third molar (ILTM), and the presence of infection. For the radiographic assessment, panoramic radiographs were obtained and the ILTM region causing each patient’s complaint was evaluated.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: individuals aged 18–40 years, individuals with systemic status ASA0 and ASA1 according to the American Society of Anaesthesiologists (ASA) classification, individuals with semi-erupted ILTM associated with AP, and individuals with a VAS score of 5 and above at the first visit.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: pregnant or lactating women; smokers; individuals with a history of chronic pain or chronic analgesic use; those with neurological or psychiatric disorders that may affect pain perception; patients with systemic diseases; alcohol and/or drug users, those who did not attend post-operative check-ups, those who were using medication other than that recommended, those who developed adverse drug reactions during the study and discontinued medication, those who were allergic to the study drugs, and those who underwent additional dental treatment during the study.

The study was approved by the Van Yuzuncu Yil University Clinical Research Ethics Committee (decision number: 07/30.11.2022) and conducted in accordance with the latest version of the Declaration of Helsinki. Volunteers among the enrolled patients were informed about the study, and informed consent was obtained for their participation in the study. However demographic data, such as age, gender, blood type and level of education, was collected from patients using an anamnesis form. This data was then examined in relation to the patients pain status.

Patients who met the inclusion criteria were randomised into two groups, Group A and Group B, by the research assistants using the Research Randomizer (version 4.0) program to ensure the blinding of the surgeons16. Group A (active control group) received 550 mg naproxen sodium + 30 mg codeine phosphate (Apranax plus tablet, Abdi İbrahim İlaç Sanayi ve Ticaret A.Ş.); group B (research group) received 25 mg dexketoprofen trometamol + 300 mg paracetamol (Dexpass tablet, Neutec İlaç San. Tic. A.Ş.) 2 times daily for 7 days, and both groups received amoxicillin 500 mg (Largopen, Bilim İlaç San. Ve Tic. A.Ş.) 3 times daily for 5 days in addition to analgesics for infection control due to AP. Paracetamol 500 mg (Parol tablet, Atabay İlaç San.) was administered as a rescue medication. Patients were instructed not to take the rescue medication unless they had to and, if they did, to record the time and number of doses on the form given to them. Patients in the study groups received the first dose of the drugs from us and continued to receive subsequent doses every 12 h.

A Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) with numbers ranging from 0 (no pain) to 10 (unbearable pain) was used to assess pain. Patients were asked to record their VAS score on the form at the 6th (T1), 12th (T2), 18th (T3), and 24th hour (T4) and 2nd (T5), 3rd (T6), 4th (T7), 5th (T8), 6th (T9), and 7th day (T10) after the first dose (T0 of study drug).

The sample size of the study was calculated as a maximum of 30 individuals for each group, with 95% power, an α error of 0.05, a β error of 0.05, and an effect size of 0.95, using data from a previous similar study17. Study was conducted in accordance with the CONSORT 2010 guidelines18. During the study period, 100 patients were assessed, and 75 patients who met the inclusion criteria were included in the study. Of the patients randomised into 2 groups as Ave B, 7 patients were excluded from the study because they did not attend the follow-up visits, 4 patients were using other drugs, and 4 patients did not complete the VAS. Data from a total of 62 patients (31 participants in each group) in 2 groups were statistically analysed (Fig. 1).

Flow chart of the study.

Patients were called for follow-up at the end of the first week and were assessed for systemic signs of AP. Patients whose acute condition resolved after one week of medication had the IT causing the AP removed. Patients whose acute condition did not improve were treated with additional medical therapies for pain and infection, and removal of the IT was delayed until symptoms resolved. Patients were assessed for adverse events during 1 week of drug use. As patient complaints of adverse events were minimal, there was no need to discontinue the study drug or add any additional treatment.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS version 25 software program. Visual (histogram and probability plots) and analytical (Kolmogorov–Smirnov, Shapiro–Wilk) methods were used to test the conformity of the measured parameters with the normal distribution. Descriptive statistics were summarised using means and standard deviations for normally distributed variables and frequencies and percentages for categorical (ordinal and nominal) variables. Group similarity scores for demographic information were examined using the chi-square test for categorical (nominal) data and the Mann–Whitney U test and independent groups t-test for numerical data. Within-group results of time-dependent changes in pain scores (VAS) between groups were examined using the Friedman test, which is a non-parametric test. If the test result was statistically significant, the Wilcoxon test was used to determine which differences between measurements were statistically significant. The Wilcoxon test is a post-hoc test and was used at a significance level of 0.005 after Bonferroni correction (0.05/11 = 0.005; 11 is the number of measurements). All analyses are based on a significance level of 0.05.

Discussion

AP occurs when microorganisms and food debris enter the gingival pocket around the crown of a partially erupted ILTM. The presence of microorganisms causes systemic involvement such as pain, fever, lymphadenopathy, etc., in addition to the local tissue reaction in the affected area. In such cases, patients should receive a course of antimicrobial treatment in addition to analgesic treatment. There are studies in the literature on the analgesic efficacy of combination drugs showing their superiority in providing analgesia12,19. For this reason, combination analgesics were preferred in this study, and the analgesic efficacy of two different drug combinations was compared. For the clinical modelling of acute dental pain, patients with a VAS of 5 or more for the ILTM with AP were preferred. The results of this study showed that the combination of dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol had a greater analgesic effect on day 7 and similar analgesic effects of both drugs at other times, according to the comparisons made at different times. Although there are trials in the literature comparing the effect of different analgesics in acute pain, there are no trials evaluating the effect of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol combinations in patients with AP12,13,20. In the results of this study, the null hypothesis was accepted at all times except on day 7, and the null hypothesis was rejected on day 7. During the first week of control, naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate showed a decrease of 83.44% and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol showed a decrease of 91.52% in mean VAS scores. We can conclude that both drugs were effective in preventing acute pain in the AP. However, the dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol combination was more effective, as evidenced by the changes observed on day 7.

In the literature, the VAS has been used to assess pain in ILTM surgical trials investigating the efficacy of different drugs on postoperative pain11,21–24 as it is an easily applicable, reproducible, and non-invasive method. In this study, we used the VAS to assess pain at baseline, 6th, 12th, 18th, and 24th hour, and 2nd, 3rd, 4th, 5th, 6th, and 7th day. Pericoronitis is an infection caused by an impacted tooth and is accompanied by inflammation. Pain, one of the main complaints due to pericoronitis, is a result of inflammation, and it is recommended that NSAIDs or drugs containing NSAIDs be used to treat this pain25. However, it is known that pain can be alleviated or eliminated when an infection is treated with antimicrobial drugs26. The amoxicillin used in the study may have had a similar effect, thereby influencing the way pain was controlled during the study.

Naproxen sodium, one of the active ingredients in naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate, which was the active control group in the study, is a propionic acid derivative NSAID with antipyretic, analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting prostaglandin synthesis, while codeine phosphate, the other active ingredient in the drug, is an opioid analgesic and works by acting on opioid receptors in the central nervous system17,19. Although codeine has the potential to be addictive and can cause serious side effects, it is only used in limited circumstances. However, when combined with other drugs, it has been found to increase analgesic efficacy without increasing the incidence of side effects17,19. On the other hand, dexketoprofen trometamol, one of the active ingredients in the study drug dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol, is a propionic acid derivative NSAID that works by blocking the cyclooxygenase enzyme14,21. Paracetamol, the other active ingredient in the study group, provides analgesia with central action and low anti-inflammatory activity through its effect on arachidonic acid metabolism20,27. For this reason, when we created the groups in this study, we ensured both combination drugs were NSAIDs. It is well known that the use of NSAIDs for inflammatory pain is limited because of their dose- and time-dependent side effects28,29. Using combination analgesics containing NSAIDs, thereby providing the necessary analgesia with other analgesics such as codeine and paracetamol, without increasing the dose of NSAIDs, therefore makes the use of the drug acceptable not only in terms of pain relief efficacy but also in terms of unwanted side effects. This reflects the other reason why we chose combination drugs for the trial. In the literature, naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate, ibuprofen + paracetamol, and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol combinations are available as combined analgesics containing NSAIDs. Combinations of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate, ibuprofen + paracetamol, ketoprofen + tramadol, ketoprofen + paracetamol, and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol have been reported in the literature as combined analgesics containing NSAIDs17. Of these, ibuprofen + paracetamol, naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate, and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol were tablets containing two active ingredients in a single tablet. The other combinations involved taking two different medicine tablets at the same time. Ibuprofen + paracetamol combinations were not available in Turkey, which explains why we chose the combinations of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol for this trial. A common finding of previous studies using these combinations was that the use of combination drugs increases the analgesic effect without increasing the side effects, which provides another data point supporting the use of combination drugs in the current trial. In this trial, there was no difference in side effects between the combination of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol. The most common GI side effects of NSAIDs that limit their use were not observed in the naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate group, while stomach pain was observed in two patients in the dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol group. In the naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate group, headache was observed in four patients, nausea in two of these four patients, and diarrhoea in one patient. No patient discontinued treatment due to adverse events.

Stacher et al. compared the analgesic efficacy of four groups of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate, naproxen sodium + placebo codeine phosphate, codeine phosphate + placebo naproxen sodium, and placebo naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate on electrically and thermally induced experimental pain. They found that the combined use of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate was more effective in terms of analgesia than the use of these drugs and placebo alone30. Another study compared the combination of naproxen sodium and codeine phosphate with naproxen sodium alone for pain control in people undergoing arthroscopic meniscus surgery. They reported that the combination of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate provided more effective analgesia. In another study using the combination of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate19 Cigerim and Eroğlu compared the effects of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate, diclofenac potassium, and benzydamine hydrochloride in postoperative pain control after ILTM surgery, finding that the analgesic effect of the naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combination was greater than that of the other drugs17. Cigerim and Kaplan compared the combination of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate with naproxen sodium alone, naproxen sodium alone + single dose dexamethasone, and paracetamol alone in their study comparing the pain-relieving effects of different medicines in people with myofascial pain. They found that the combination of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate was more effective in controlling myofascial pain31. In the abovementioned studies, the naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combination was more effective in the control/treatment of experimental pain, postoperative pain, and myofascial pain17,19,30,31. In this study, the naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combination provided a significant improvement in patients’ initial VAS scores after 1 week of use in the treatment of AP-induced pain, and this result is consistent with studies evaluating the combination of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate. These results also support the choice of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combination as the active control group in this study. The other combination drug in the study, dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol, showed similar pain relief efficacy to naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate at all times except on day 7. Similarly to the naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combination, it provided a significant reduction in patients’ baseline VAS scores, and its pain relief efficacy on day 7 was also found to be greater than that of the naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combination. These results show that the combination of dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol has similarly effective analgesic effect to that of the active control group, naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate.

A review of the literature found no trials of dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol for the treatment of acute dental pain. Looking at studies in other areas, Ceyhan et al. showed that the combination of dexketoprofen + paracetamol in non-malignant gynaecological laparotomy surgery was superior in pain relief compared with the groups using dexketoprofen and paracetamol alone27. In a study in which ketoprofen and paracetamol were used as two separate drugs, Akural et al. found that the combination of ketoprofen + paracetamol administered as a single dose provided more effective analgesia for postoperative pain control during the 10-hour follow-up period after ILTM surgery than the use of the drugs alone32. In this study, the combination of dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol was found to be effective in the treatment of AP-induced pain within the time interval evaluated in accordance with the study by Ceyhan et al. and Akural et al27,32. On day 7, the combination of dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol was more effective than that of the active control group, naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate. As there were no studies in the literature comparing the analgesic effects of dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol and naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate combinations, this study therefore represents the first trial comparing these two drug combinations in acute dental pain due to AP.

Limitations

A key limitation of this trial is its single-blind design and conducted at a single center. As the tablets of the drugs were different in colour and size, it was not possible to prepare tablets in a similar form (Companies have rejected requests for colourless and prescription-free production, additionally, there are concerns that packaging items individually without advertising may compromise sterilisation.), and blinding of patients in the trial could not therefore be ensured. In addition, even if the patients did not know which drug they were taking, the results may have been influenced because it is possible that the patients may have used the existing drugs before and know which drug they are taking by recognising the shape and colour of the tablet. The psychological state of the patients at the time of completing the VAS may have influenced the results by positively or negatively affecting pain perception. It should also be considered a limitation that objective biomarkers of inflammation (e.g. CRP and IL-6) were not evaluated in the study. As different people were studied, the results may have been influenced by individual differences such as different healing capacities and different pain thresholds. The antibiotic used in the trial to treat systemic findings may have masked the effect of the drugs in the groups because of its ability to inhibit the inflammation caused by the infection. Again, it is not known whether the antibiotic used in the trial interacted with the study drugs, and this may have had a positive or negative effect on the results in both groups.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this is the first study to compare the combinations of naproxen sodium + codeine phosphate and dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol in acute dental pain due to AP. Based on these results, the combination of these two drugs may be preferred in cases of acute dental pain where NSAIDs alone are insufficient. While both drugs are effective in pain treatment, dexketoprofen trometamol + paracetamol was found to have a greater analgesic effect on day 7. However, further clinical trials are needed to investigate the analgesic effect of these two drugs in different types of dental pain.

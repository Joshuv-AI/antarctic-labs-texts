Two-injection Start Strategy of Aripiprazole Once-monthly Injection: Current Evidence and Clinical Implications
Authors: Hayeon Kim, Kyung Ho Lee, Changsu Han, Ashwin A. Patkar, Prakash S. Masand, Chi-Un Pae
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Long-acting injectable antipsychotics (LAIs) are developed to overcome the limitations of oral antipsychotics (APs) and their use has been increasing in the treatment of various psychiatric disorders such as schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. LAIs are convenient to use and they were also found to enhance treatment adherence and continuation, leading to better clinical outcomes. Aripiprazole (ARP), a partial D2/5-HT1A receptor agonist, has been considered to have fewer adverse events than preexisting APs in terms of metabolic disturbance, sexual dysfunction, and mood instability. In this context, a once-monthly LAI formulation, aripiprazole once-monthly (AOM) has also been developed, and it is widely used in the real-world practice. However, it needs concurrent oral ARP administration for 14 days upon initiation, which may limit its practical use in routine practice due to the complexity of the regimen. Recently, the AOM two-injection start (TIS) strategy has been introduced, which allows rapid achievement of therapeutic plasma concentrations and stable drug levels without the need for a 2-week administration of oral ARP. This strategy may simplify treatment initiation with AOM as well as facilitate smoother transition from oral ARP to AOM in routine practice. Hence, this paper reviews the pharmacological basis, clinical evidence, clinical implications, and future prospects of the AOM TIS strategy in naturalistic treatment settings.

INTRODUCTION

Schizophrenia has diverse symptoms, including thought process, cognitive abilities, emotional regulation, and social functioning, which may lead to a chronic and relapsing clinical course in patients. In general, psychotic symptoms in the early phase may be mild; however, the absence of timely intervention or the abrupt discontinuation of treatment may result in worsening of symptoms, a poor prognosis, treatment resistance, diminished occupational capacity, and an increased risk of suicide. Outcomes within the first five years are crucial, proposing the importance of early intervention and ongoing management [1].

The overall management of schizophrenia is the regular and continuous use of antipsychotic (AP) medications. However, adherence to oral AP regimens is often suboptimal, showing significant challenges in real-world practice [2]. Approximately 40−60% of individuals with schizophrenia discontinue their medication within a specific timeframe. This leads to frequent relapses/recurrence, an increased risk of rehospitalization, poor functional recovery, a higher likelihood of treatment resistance, and cumulative neurocognitive decline [3-11]. The establishment of consistent strategies to keep adherence from the beginning of treatment is therefore critical [4].

Long-acting injectable antipsychotics (LAIs) were developed to address the common compliance issues of oral APs. LAIs have the advantage of maintaining a constant therapeutic concentration over a long period of time, preventing side effects due to concentration fluctuations. In addition, since the administration period is fixed, such as one or three months, it reduces medication omissions and allows medical staff to directly monitor patients’ medication compliance. Several studies have shown that patients administered LAIs have significantly lower rates of relapse/recurrence, rehospitalization, and emergency room (ER) visits. Furthermore, recent studies have reported that actively starting LAIs from the early stage of treatment can lead to better clinical outcomes, highlighting various clinical benefits [12,13].

Meanwhile, aripiprazole (ARP) is an atypical antipsychotic characterized by partial agonist activity at dopamine D2 receptors and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors. This unique action mechanism of ARP may offer advantages over other APs in terms of emotional stability, cognitive function, extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS), and metabolic side effects. A LAI formulation of oral ARP, ARP once-monthly (AOM), has also been developed [14-18].

However, the conventional AOM regimen needs at least 2 weeks of co-administration of oral ARP at initiation, which may limit its easy application in real-world practice [19]. To address this limitation, the two-injection start (TIS) has been developed and it allows immediate achievement of therapeutic concentration and maintaining stable drug levels by administering two AOM injections simultaneously at two different sites at the start of treatment, without a 2-week co-administration of oral ARP. This method may improve long-term outcomes by optimizing treatment schedule and ensuring effective drug delivery in the treatment for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder (BPD).

This review aims to provide an integrated summary of the pharmacokinetic (PK) principles, clinical effectiveness, and safety of the TIS based on current evidence, and furthermore, to present the clinical application, clinical implications, clinical perspectives, and future directions of the TIS strategy.

MAIN SUBJECTS

Literature Search Strategy

A structured literature search was performed to identify studies evaluating the TIS strategy for AOM. We searched PubMed and MEDLINE databases. Additional manual searches were also conducted through conference abstracts and reference lists to reduce the risk of missing relevant literature. We used the following combination of search terms: (“aripiprazole” OR “aripiprazole once-monthly” OR “AOM”) AND (“two-injection start” OR “two-injection initiation” OR “TIS” OR “alternative start regimen”).

Pharmacological Characteristics of Aripiprazole Once-monthly

AOM is the first LAI to have partial agonist activities at the D2 and 5-HT1A receptors, with a mean half-life of 46.5 days at a 400 mg dose and 29.9 days at a 300 mg dose, respectively. The expected elimination time for oral ARP is approximately 15 days, corresponding to a mean elimination half-life of approximately 75 hours. However, AOM shows a significantly longer elimination phase, requiring 5−8 months for complete systemic clearance after the last dose [20]. Peak steady-state concentration of ARP is reached in 6.5 days at a 300 mg dose and in 7.1 days at a 400 mg dose [20]. When a single dose of AOM 400 mg (or 300 mg) was administered into the deltoid or gluteal muscles, the extent of absorption (area under the curve, AUC) was similar in both sites, but absorption was reported to be faster in the deltoid [21]. With repeated intramuscular injections, ARP plasma concentrations gradually increased, with the mean time to peak plasma concentration (Tmax) taking 7 days after the gluteal injection and 4 days after the deltoid injection. Generally, plasma concentrations reach a plateau after the fourth injection in both sites [20].

Meanwhile, when administered at steady-state doses of AOM 400 mg (or 300 mg), the mean maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) ranged from 316 ng/ml to 269 ng/ml, and the minimum plasma concentration (Cmin) ranged from 212 ng/ml to 156 ng/ml. These values correspond to the steady-state exposure observed with oral ARP administration, with a Cmax of approximately 20−30 mg/day and a Cmin of approximately 15−20 mg/day [20]. However, administration of AOM 200 mg (Cmax: 100 ng/ml and Cmin: 95.0 ng/ml) is known to not reach a mean ARP trough plasma concentration similar to the therapeutic levels associated with oral ARP 10−30 mg/d [20].

Two-injection Start Strategy and Market Situation

The TIS strategy allows the initiation of AOM in a single day, eliminating the need for 2-week overlap of oral ARP. The utility and safety of the TIS strategy have been demonstrated through population pharmacokinetic analyses (PopPK). Currently, the TIS has been approved for clinical use in Europe [22], the United States [23], Canada [24], and other countries in the treatment of schizophrenia and BPD. Under the TIS strategy, two 400/300 mg injections of AOM should be administered at separate sites in the deltoid or gluteal muscles at the initiation of treatment. Simultaneously, a single 20 mg dose of oral ARP should be administered on the same day. The two AOM injections should be administered at different injection sites.

Population Pharmacokinetic Rationale of the Two-injection Start Strategy

Conventional PK studies typically focus on measuring blood drug concentrations in a small number of subjects and investigating essential pharmacological parameters such as Tmax, Cmax, Cmin, and AUC. However, small sample sizes may limit generalizability. Meanwhile, the PopPK approach has recently gained attention to overcome these limitations. PopPK utilizes drug concentration data from a larger number of subjects collected at multiple time points and constructs a more generalizable PK model through mathematical modeling that takes interindividual variability into account. When such huge data are integrated with existing drug profiles, the PopPK model can support regulatory approval applications for new drugs or changes to existing formulations without large-scale clinical trials, and it can also serve as a useful tool for clinical pharmacology decision-making by integrating intrinsic factors such as age, weight, and genetic polymorphisms along with extrinsic factors such as concomitant medications, diet, and disease status to more accurately predict the PK of a drug [25].

In a recent study using AOM, the TIS was evaluated with the use of the comprehensive ARP PK data set collected from 7 clinical trials. Plasma concentrations from 8,214 whole blood samples were examined, covering various routes of administration, including oral (16%), gluteal (65%), deltoid (16%), and other (3%) [26,27]. Post-marketing surveillance (PMS) data from patients receiving AOM beyond the manufacturer’s recommended doses were assessed in order for the evaluation of safety and tolerability including simultaneous dual injection events closely resembling the TIS strategy [23,24].

Simulations based on the TIS strategy revealed that the ARP concentration-time curves exhibited a similar pattern to those obtained with the SIS strategy routinely used in clinical practice, in terms of central tendency and overall variability across the 5th to 95th percentiles. During the initial dosing period, median concentrations remained within the therapeutic blood concentration range, and the upper percentile estimates were reported to be similar to or within the acceptable therapeutic range (95−534 ng/ml) observed in the SIS regimen. Notably, this PK model also predicted that patients were exposed to therapeutic concentrations immediately after the TIS administration on the same day, even in patients who had not previously received oral ARP stabilization therapy [23,24].

Further analysis of CYP2D6 metabolism status revealed that poor metabolizers (PMs) exhibited higher peak and overall ARP blood concentrations when starting the TIS with the standard dose, AOM 400 mg. When the starting dose of AOM TIS was adjusted from 400 mg to 300 mg, the ARP blood concentration profiles remained similar to those in the SIS strategy, closely matching the standard therapeutic range and preventing excessive drug accumulation in PM patients [23,24]. Clinical Evidence Regarding Effectiveness

To date, observational studies, case reports, survey-based studies, and PopPK-modeling studies are available.

A recent open-label study [28] assessed the efficacy of TIS strategy for AOM 400 mg over three months in patients with schizophrenia (n = 152), both with and without comorbid substance use disorder (SUD-positive: n = 93; SUD-negative: n = 59). Participants were divided into two groups: one received the SIS strategy (n = 66), while the other received the TIS strategy (n = 86). Among SUD-negative patients, both treatment strategies resulted in a gradual decrease in psychometric scale scores from baseline to endpoint, with no significant differences between the groups. However, on the social relationships subscale of the World Health Organization Quality of Life, patients on TIS strategy scored higher than those on SIS strategy at the month 3. As for the SUD-positive group, TIS group showed better outcomes than the SIS group in most clinical measures with notable differences such as the Clinical Global Impression-Severity (CGI-S) scores (2.96 vs. 3.95), Barratt Impulsiveness Scale-11 attentional scores (18.79 vs. 20.58), Visual Analog Scale (VAS) craving intensity (2.85 vs. 4.89), and VAS craving frequency (2.79 vs. 4.89). Although some differences were noted in specific symptom domains, the TIS strategy has provided greater therapeutic benefits for patients with SUD compared to the SIS strategy.

In a subgroup of 25 patients (SIS, n = 13; TIS, n = 12), the blood concentrations of ARP and its active metabolite dehydroARP were measured. At baseline, serum ARP concentrations were similar in both groups (~432 ng/ml) since all patients had been stabilized on oral ARP prior to the study. The SIS group showed a rapid increase in serum ARP levels exceeding the therapeutic range (534.23 ng/ml) at week 1, which remained above the therapeutic threshold at weeks 1 and 2, before decreasing to 392.61 ng/ml at week 4. However, the TIS group showed a gradual decrease of serum levels and remained within the therapeutic range, maintaining a mean concentration of 273.94 ng/ml at week 4. In addition, the SIS group had significantly higher ARP and its active metabolite levels at weeks 1 and 2 than the TIS group, indicating that the SIS strategy may lead to exceeding therapeutic concentrations at the start. Overall, the PK profile observed in this study was consistent with and supports the results of a PopPK study conducted by the manufacturer [26,27]. These results may indicate that both SIS and TIS are comparably effective treatment strategies, also emphasizing the effectiveness of AOM as a LAI for managing schizophrenia patients comorbid with SUD.

A retrospective review [29] of the medical records of 133 patients with schizophrenia who received a TIS strategy at 10 clinical centers in Italy evaluated the safety and tolerability of the TIS approach, although clinical effectiveness was not assessed. Among the 133 patients, ten patients discontinued therapy before the next scheduled AOM dose after receiving the TIS strategy, and only 1 discontinuation was due to lack of efficacy.

A total of 15 clinically stable patients with schizophrenia initiated TIS and were followed for 24 months in a recent mirror-image study [30]. Clinical Global Impression-Schizophrenia (CGI-SCH) scores significantly improved, with a baseline mean of 4.24 reducing by −1.21 at month 24 (p < 0.01). Treatment adherence at 24 months was 66%, and 73% of patients remained free from psychiatric hospitalization. The most common adverse event (AE) was mild transient insomnia, reported by 20% of patients, with no severe AEs noted. Hospitalizations decreased from 20 episodes in the 24 months prior to LAI to 5 episodes after the switch. Emergency interventions similarly declined from 38 to 9 during the same period. Overall, the initiation of the TIS strategy was well tolerated and associated with reduced healthcare utilization and improved clinical stability. The same authors [31] previously reported on the effectiveness and tolerability of TIS regimen in 10 patients with schizophrenia. They observed a mean reduction of 1.2 points in the CGI-SCH score, and 90% of patients remained free from hospitalization at six months. The most common AE was transient, mild insomnia, affecting 20% of patients.

A retrospective study from Turkey (n = 29) [32] investigated the TIS regimen in patients with schizophrenia, BPD, or schizoaffective disorder. The study showed that symptomatic improvement was observed within the first week in one patient, and by day 5 in the remaining patients. The mean duration of hospitalization following the TIS regimen was 21.2 days, which is shorter compared to previously reported lengths of stay for patients with schizophrenia (3−10 weeks) and BPD (2−5 weeks). Meanwhile, 6 patients (20.7%) discontinued all previous concomitant medications after starting the TIS regimen. Patients without prior psychotropics maintained stability on AOM monotherapy afterward. Notably, no discontinuations of AOM treatment after initiating the TIS regimen were found to be associated with inefficacy, and all patients continued the scheduled AOM therapy. These findings propose that the TIS strategy may be applicable beyond patients with schizophrenia and highlight the high adherence to AOM therapy.

In another Italian retrospective study (abstract form), 24 patients involving schizophrenia, BPD, personality disorders, substance-induced psychosis, delusional disorder, and schizoaffective disorder were evaluated to assess hospitalization days and ER visits as the treatment effect of TIS strategy. The average length of hospitalization was 11.8 days. After initiating TIS strategy, 83.3% of patients had no further admissions, while 12.5% had at least one ER visit [33]. A recent study [34] reported a case series including eight hospitalized BPD patients who received the TIS regimen for a manic episode characterized by both psychotic and mixed features in Turkey (mean number of admissions = 1.5, mean illness duration = 12 years). This was the first study to assess the TIS strategy specifically in BPD patients. The decision to utilize the TIS strategy was influenced by the severity of symptoms and a documented history of prior non-adherence. In the study, the mean Young Mania Rating Scale score was decreased by 68.7% from 46.3 (a severe episode) at day 1 to 14.5 (partial remission) at day 10. The mean period achieving the remission after initiation of TIS strategy was 18.2 days, which should be significantly short when considering the severity of BPD symptoms. The study may indicate that the earlier initiation of the TIS strategy can lead to shorter hospitalization, with the TIS strategy being considered the primary determinant of hospital stay duration in most cases. Psychotic symptoms were resolved by day 10 in five of the eight patients, which is particularly significant, considering the initial severity of their manic episodes. Furthermore, the therapeutic effect of TIS strategy was not immediately apparent but developed progressively over the first week.

In another BPD patient case report in an inpatient setting, the effect and tolerability of the TIS strategy were also beneficial for treating BPD, which was demonstrated with the use of standardized rating scales such as the YMRS [35].

The TIS strategy was also applied for a hospitalized 16-year-old adolescent patient presenting severe psychopathology, as indicated by a Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) score of 136 at admission. Although this was an off-label treatment [36], TIS strategy was initiated after the stabilization of acute symptoms to support ongoing treatment following discharge, in which the patient showed significant clinical improvement, presenting a 68.4% reduction in PANSS score to 43 and a decrease in the CGI-S score from 7 to 2, on one month after discharge (week 10).

Another case report further showed the potential effect of the TIS strategy in a 16-year-old male inpatient with prominent psychomotor agitation and manic symptoms in his first episode [37].

TIS strategy has also been shown to be effective and well-tolerated in cases of first-episode psychosis comorbid with HIV [38]. Moreover, a recent case report showed a possibility that TIS strategy can be a rapid and effective treatment option for patients with dopamine super-sensitivity psychosis [39]. Finally, the recent retrospective, naturalistic, and exploratory study compared the early clinical efficacy and tolerability profiles of the TIS and SIS strategies in BPD patients (n = 46) with manic episodes with psychotic or mixed features. In the study, both the TIS and the SIS strategies significantly improved BPD symptoms by Day 15; however, the TIS strategy showed greater improvement of manic symptoms and better tolerability. The AEs observed in the TIS strategy were generally more favorable compared to the SIS strategy [40]. Table 1 summarizes the clinical studies with the TIS strategy clinical studies.

Clinical Evidence Regarding Tolerability and Safety

Wang et al. [26] used PMS data to conduct a safety assessment of high doses of AOM in 228 out of 13,000 patients receiving TIS treatment in their PopPk study. High-dose exposure was based on the following criteria: one dose of AOM greater than 400 mg, AOM 300−400 mg administered at less than 26-day intervals, oral ARP dosing for 15+ consecutive days beginning with an AOM 400 mg dose, and/or oral ARP dosing that exceeded 30 mg and/or AOM dosing that exceeded 400 mg. AEs, including EPSs associated with ARP, were observed in 46 cases (20%) out of the 228 high-dose cases, which is consistent with expectations. In 102 of all cases (45%), no AEs were identified. The maximum reported ARP dose was 1,600 mg/week in a single case, although the duration of administration was not specified, and no AEs were reported for this case. Among 12 cases, patients received 800 mg on the same day as 400 mg AOM (similar to a 2-dose starting regimen); of these, 9 cases (75%) had no reported AEs. The incidence of EPS was 4.6% in AOM 400 high-dose group and 7.3% in the SIS group, indicating a rate 0.37 times lower in the high-dose group. The overall rate of severe AEs related to the EPS was 5.1% for the High Dose Group and 7.1% for the SIS Group, a reduction of 7.1% (or 0.28 times less) for the High Dose Group. Serious AEs comprised 19% (45 cases) of the 233 reported AEs, while 81% (188 cases) were non-serious. Overall, the PMS database indicated no new safety concerns at higher dosages of ARP therapy than previously experienced in clinical trials. Therefore, it appears that the TIS Strategy may be as safe as the SIS Strategy; however, it is important to note that the clinical trial results are very limited due to the absence of real TIS clinical trial data.

In the study of Trovini et al. [28], only one patient presented akathisia and discontinued the study for this reason. All other AEs such as nausea, headache, muscle pain, fatigue, and insomnia observed during the study were mild intensity and transient, thus required neither specific intervention nor discontinuation of treatment. A recent retrospective study [29] similarly found no new tolerability or safety concerns, where only 3 patients (2.3%, n = 3/133) discontinued the next scheduled AOM. Further, these AEs were attributed to ARP rather than the TIS strategy, and none of the reported AEs were classified as serious cases. In line with these findings, another retrospective study [32] also showed no evidence of moderate or severe AEs including cardiac abnormalities, hyperthermia, changes in consciousness, akathisia, or allergic reactions which may cause discontinuation of the TIS strategy. Interestingly, 69% of patients continued to combine concomitant medications, including APs and mood stabilizers (MSs), after commencing the TIS strategy.

In a separate case series for patients with BPD [34], no serious AEs were reported, aside from sedation, tachycardia, hypersalivation, dysarthria, fever, and a transient hand rash; all patients were also receiving concurrent APs and MSs. Despite the widespread use of concomitant medications, the very low incidence of AEs supports the relative safety of TIS strategy in maintaining stable blood concentrations and minimizing clinically relevant drug interactions.

A recent case report presented an acute dystonia occurring after the TIS strategy in a schizophrenia patient with a history of EPS such as torticollis and tongue protrusion, following prior use of olanzapine 30 mg/day and haloperidol 20 mg/day. This case may indicate that clinicians should be aware of the importance of prior history of AP-induced EPS before utilizing TIS strategy [41]. The acute dystonia was effectively managed with benztropine. In contrast, TIS was well tolerated without dyskinesia or other AEs in a separate 16-year-old patient with schizophrenia reported in another case [36]. In the recent matched retrospective study [40], the tolerability of the TIS strategy was found to be generally more favorable compared with the SIS strategy. Interestingly, daytime sedation was the most common AE in both groups. Increased appetite was the second most common AE in the TIS group, whereas inner restlessness was the second most common AE in the SIS group. Healthcare Professionals’ Experiences

A SaTISfy Study [42] conducted online in Spain surveyed 50 psychiatrists about their perceptions of AOM 400 treatment for schizophrenia, including the implementation of the TIS strategy. The survey consisted of 41 questions divided into four sections: (1) six items assessing psychiatrists’ general experience in managing schizophrenia and typical hospitalization duration; (2) fourteen questions related to the treatment specifics of the psychiatrists’ five most recent patients with schizophrenia; (3) eight items focusing on the last five patients who received AOM 400; and (4) thirteen questions concerning the last five patients who initiated treatment with AOM 400 TIS strategy including their average hospitalization lengths. The findings indicated that 98% of psychiatrists identified patient nonadherence to previous therapy as the main factor influencing decisions to begin TIS treatment. On average, patients were discharged 6.6 days after starting TIS strategy. Psychiatrists reported improvements in positive symptoms (98%), negative symptoms (34%), and overall functioning (72%) following the initiation of the TIS strategy. Participants noted that patients who began treatment with the TIS strategy had a mean hospital stay of 14.2 days, which was shorter than the 17.7 days observed for the broader population of schizophrenia patients, regardless of the treatment approach. These findings showed a decrease of 3.5 days compared to standard treatments and 5 days relative to the national average. Further, 90% of psychiatrists reported that the initiation of AOM 400 TIS strategy was associated with a perceived reduction in healthcare resource utilization, where 93.3% noted fewer hospitalization days, and 91.1% observed a reduced need for concomitant oral medications. Regarding patient satisfaction following the commencement of AOM 400 TIS strategy, 72% of psychiatrists reported that their patients were satisfied, 56% considered that their patients were very satisfied, and only 8% noted dissatisfaction. Similarly, 66% of psychiatrists expressed being very satisfied with AOM 400 TIS strategy, while 48% reported satisfaction, and only 2% were not satisfied.

Another cross-sectional online study with European healthcare professionals (HCPs) (n = 94) [43] validated the findings reported in the SaTISfy study [42]. In the study, 79.8% of participants ‘agreed’ or ‘strongly agreed’ that AOM 400 TIS strategy is convenient to administer, while 69.1% ‘agreed’ or ‘strongly agreed’ that its safety and tolerability profile should be comparable to that of AOM 400 conventional SIS strategy. Further, 84.0% ‘agreed’ or ‘strongly agreed’ that they were satisfied with the treatment outcomes for patients receiving AOM 400 TIS strategy. The key reasons reported for selecting AOM TS strategy included the clinical issues such as insufficient treatment response, concerns about relapse, frequent hospital admissions, patient preference, inadequate overall symptom control, and insufficient control of positive symptoms. Similarly, the goals most frequently prioritized when utilizing the TIS strategy were improving medication adherence (70.2%), preventing relapse (69.1%), enhancing quality of life (QoL) (62.8%), alleviating symptoms (58.5%), and reducing the risk of readmission (57.4%).

Who May Benefit from the TIS Strategy?

The TIS strategy may be used during the clinical course of schizophrenia and the maintenance treatment for BPD, specifically, it would be useful in the following situations: 1) Patients who had a history of poor adherence, abrupt treatment discontinuation, poor insight, difficulty in completion of a 2-week oral ARP combination. Immediate initiation without a 2-week overlapping period with oral ARP may increase the opportunity for treatment continuity, reduce early disruption of treatment, and decrease the risk of acute relapse and symptom worsening; 2) For patients planning outpatient follow-up treatment prior to discharge, the smoother transition of care should be a critical point. When the patients require a switch from oral formulation to AOM to ensure treatment continuity, faster initiation and the immediate attainment of therapeutic concentrations and stable blood levels following TIS strategy can help not only in establishing successful discharge plans but also in determining post-discharge outpatient treatment strategies. This offers the benefit to reduce vulnerability to acute relapse due to abrupt non-adherence after discharge; 3) The TIS strategy can improve acceptance of treatment by providing convenience and simplification of treatment regimen, as it allows for the immediate administration of AOM without oral combination for those who refuse or have intolerance issues with oral formulation; 4) The TIS strategy has the advantage of providing rapid and simple treatment to patients with aggression, agitation, abrupt relapse, or those requiring immediate AOM administration. Early and timely intervention with the TIS strategy may enhance effective management during critical phase of the disease; 5) The TIS strategy may be useful for patients with limited access to outpatient services, less caregiver support, or scarce community mental health resources because it can improve the practical feasibility of introducing AOM treatment in such difficult therapeutic circumstances by simplification of the initiation; 6) Schizophrenia patients with comorbid SUD commonly show poor adherence, frequent relapses, and treatment discontinuation [28]. The TIS strategy may provide substantial clinical benefits to these patients by enabling rapid initiation of treatment and reducing the complexity of the treatment regimen, ultimately contributing to improvement of clinical outcomes; and 7) In the outpatient clinic, following patients would be appropriate for the TIS strategy; those who require switching from an oral agent to AOM, those are in sub-acute or acute worsening, or those at imminent risk. In summary, the TIS strategy should be a simplified and practical initiation of AOM treatment for patients with different clinical situations in routine practice. Clinical Perspectives

The clinical significance of the TIS strategy may extend beyond the simplification of the 2-week overlap of oral administration periods. The TIS strategy may be better understood not as a new AP option, but as a PK-modeling based initiation strategy designed to optimize the continuity of treatment and the pragmatic implementation of AOM therapy by minimizing the risk of acute relapse and symptom exacerbation in a routine clinical setting [26,27].

The TIS strategy may be a proper therapeutic approach during clinically vulnerable transition periods. Specifically, the TIS strategy should be useful for minimizing therapeutic gaps by providing immediate therapeutic intervention after discontinuation of APs for certain periods (e.g., rapid re-engagement in treatment). It may also be appropriate during initial therapeutic approaches upon hospitalization, when establishing discharge plans, or when transitioning to outpatient treatment after discharge.

In routine outpatient practice, a 2-week overlap of oral formulation may compromise the initiation of AOM due to non-adherence, limited follow-up opportunities, insufficient caregiver support to monitoring, insufficient community health resources, or limited access to mental health services. In this context, the TIS strategy may facilitate the convenient, practical, and feasible implementation of AOM in naturalistic treatment settings.

The standard initiation protocol for AOM requires a 2-week overlap with oral ARP to maintain therapeutic plasma concentrations [26,27]. This prerequisite may limit the use of AOM in routine clinical practice because of regimen complexity, increased financial burden, psychological resistance to combination treatment, and concerns regarding increased side effects, all of which may eventually contribute to non-adherence. Similar challenges have also been reported with other LAIs; for example, paliperidone palmitate requires two loading injections over the first week of treatment [44]. In this context, the TIS strategy may offer substantial convenience compared with traditional LAIs initiation needing loading and oral supplementation. It is known to achieve therapeutic plasma concentrations on the first day, ensure consistent drug levels, minimize oral ARP supplementation to a single day, and allow an immediate transition to the standard maintenance schedule while maintaining similar AE profiles in comparison with the SIS strategy. Based on aforementioned, the TIS strategy may be proper for the routine use in outpatient clinic as well.

Pre-treatment preparation and counseling should be necessary to optimize adherence in routine practice. When ARP tolerability has not been previously established in a patient, oral ARP trial should be established before initiating the TIS strategy [14]. It may be reasonable to reduce the standard AOM dose from 400 mg to 300 mg for safety/tolerability reasons in patients with a suspected history of EPS, risk of drug–drug interactions (e.g., combination with CYP2D6 or CYP3A4 strong inhibitors), comorbid medical illness, or potential PM status [26,27].

The ARP once-every-two-months (ARP2M, ASIMTUFIIⓇ) which is designed to offer a longer dosing interval than AOM, as well as providing greater patient convenience, has been introduced today, however, the ARP2M TIS strategy is still in its early stage. The ARP2M TIS regimen (ARP2M 960 mg/AOM 400 mg administered at different injection sites + oral ARP 20 mg on the same day) has demonstrated more stable plasma ARP levels when compared to the standard ARP2M SIS initiation treatment of ARP2M 960 mg + 14 days of oral ARP (10−20 mg) [45]. These findings were consistent whether or not prior oral ARP had been stabilized. Overall, the study results provide preliminary evidence supporting a simplified initiation strategy for ARP2M, further clinical validation is required.

The AOM TIS strategy may also facilitate the development of similar treatment approaches for existing LAIs and support the development of next-generation LAIs, particularly as PK-based modeling studies and related clinical evidence continue to accumulate. From a healthcare policy perspective, the TIS strategy may also support more sustainable and stable treatment planning in community mental health settings and home-based services, while providing potential socioeconomic benefits.

Future research needs to clarify the potential role of the TIS strategy in various clinical settings and patient populations (patients with first episode, concomitant drugs and comorbidity, and old or adolescent patients, etc.) and to evaluate its effects on long-term treatment continuity after utilization, healthcare utilization, patient-centered outcomes, cost-effectiveness, caregiver burden, patient satisfaction, and overall socioeconomic impact in real-world clinical practice.

CONCLUSION

Based on currently available evidence, the AOM TIS strategy was found to be effective and generally tolerable in patients with schizophrenia along with other psychiatric conditions including BPD. However, some clinical and practical limitations remain unresolved for routine implementation in clinical practice since available evidence is mostly from small studies and PK modeling studies. Future pragmatic, prospective, and comparative studies between the TIS and SIS strategies are warranted to validate the utility across diverse and different psychiatric populations in naturalistic treatment settings. Despite the need for more independent clinical evidence, currently available findings suggest that the TIS may offer a practical and promising initiation option for AOM, as well as being an efficient treatment strategy in real-world practice.

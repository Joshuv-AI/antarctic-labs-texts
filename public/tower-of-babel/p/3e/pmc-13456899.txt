Proboscis lateralis with contralateral antrochoanal polyp in an adult: a case report
Authors: Javeria Awan, Haitham Akaash, Sundas Masood, Rahmat Gul Omarzai, Abdur Rehman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Proboscis lateralis (PL) is an extremely rare congenital craniofacial anomaly characterized by a tubular nasal appendage. Most cases undergo surgical correction in early childhood, and adult presentations are uncommon. Associations with contralateral sinonasal disease are rarely reported. We present the first surgically managed adult case of PL from Pakistan with a previously unreported coexisting contralateral antrochoanal polyp (ACP).Presentation of case:A 21-year-old male presented with a congenital left-sided nasal appendage and progressive right nasal obstruction. Examination revealed a 3-cm tubular proboscis and a right nasal polyp. MRI confirmed PL without intracranial extension and an 8-cm right ACP extending into the nasopharynx. The patient underwent surgical management consisting of excision with primary closure and functional endoscopic sinus surgery with middle meatal antrostomy. Histopathology confirmed respiratory epithelium in the proboscis and inflammatory ACP. Recovery was uneventful, and at the 6-month follow-up, the patient reported complete symptom resolution, no recurrence, and high satisfaction with the aesthetic result.Discussion:This case expands the literature on adult PL management and demonstrates that delayed surgical intervention can achieve excellent outcomes. The coexistence of PL and contralateral ACP suggests compensatory airflow alterations due to ipsilateral hypoplasia and septal deviation. This underscores the importance of comprehensive bilateral sinonasal evaluation in all PL patients. Simple excision without complex reconstruction proved effective in this adult case.Conclusion:Adult correction of PL is feasible and effective. The novel association with contralateral ACP highlights the need for thorough imaging and tailored surgical planning in patients with this rare anomaly.

Introduction

Proboscis lateralis (PL) represents an exceedingly rare congenital craniofacial malformation, with an estimated incidence of less than 1 per 100 000 live births and a male predominance[1]. This anomaly is characterized by a soft, tubular appendage typically arising from the medial canthal region, accompanied by varying degrees of ipsilateral nasal hypoplasia or aplasia[2].

Comprehensive preoperative imaging with CT and MRI is essential to delineate craniofacial involvement and exclude intracranial pathology[3]. Definitive management is surgical, with intervention traditionally recommended in early childhood; approaches range from simple excision to complex staged reconstructions using the proboscis tissue as an autologous graft[2,4]. Adult presentations of untreated PL remain exceptionally rare, with fewer than 100 documented cases worldwide[5]. Furthermore, the association between PL and contralateral sinonasal pathology represents an underexplored dimension.

Only two cases of PL have been reported from Pakistan, neither involving adult surgical management[6]. This case report describes a 21-year-old male with surgically confirmed PL, presenting concurrently with a contralateral antrochoanal polyp (ACP), representing the first adult surgical case from Pakistan and documenting a previously unreported association. It has been written in compliance with the updated SCARE 2025 guidelines[7].

Presentation of case

A 21-year-old male clerical worker from Rawalpindi presented in March 2024 with a congenital left lateral nasal appendage present since birth and a 12-month history of progressive right nasal obstruction accompanied by rhinorrhea. The visible deformity had caused significant psychosocial distress. He was born to consanguineous parents with no family history of congenital anomalies, had no chronic medical conditions, and was a non-smoker with a body mass index of 22.4 kg/m2.

Examination revealed a soft, mobile 3 cm × 1.5 cm tubular structure arising from the left lateral nose adjacent to the medial canthus (Supplemental Digital Content Figure 1, available at: http://links.lww.com/IJSCR/A80). The left nasal vestibule appeared hypoplastic. Anterior rhinoscopy demonstrated septal deviation to the left and a pale polypoidal mass in the right nasal cavity with mucopurulent discharge. Posterior rhinoscopy revealed nasopharyngeal extension. Ophthalmological assessment showed no ocular abnormalities. Based on the Boo-Chai classification, this case corresponds to Group I, characterized by an isolated PL without associated ocular or craniofacial anomalies[8].

Magnetic resonance imaging in April 2024 confirmed a 3.5 cm × 1.4 cm PL without intracranial extension and an 8-cm right ACP extending from the maxillary sinus through the choana into the nasopharynx (Fig. 1). The left paranasal sinuses were normally pneumatized with intact olfactory tracts. Differential diagnoses, including nasal dermoid cyst, nasal glioma, and encephalocele, were excluded based on clinical assessment and MRI findings. Following informed consent, combined surgical intervention was performed in May 2024 under general anesthesia. The decision to perform simple excision with primary closure rather than reconstructive use of the proboscis tissue was based on the patient’s preference for removal of the visible deformity, the presence of a functional albeit hypoplastic nasal vestibule, and the lack of desire for staged reconstructive procedures. After prophylactic ceftriaxone, a circular incision at the proboscis base allowed circumferential dissection to the lateral nasal bone margin and en bloc excision. A small nasal bone punctum was covered with local mucosal advancement, and the wound was closed in two layers (Figs 2 and 3). Functional endoscopic sinus surgery with middle meatal antrostomy removed the entire ACP from the posterolateral maxillary sinus wall. The sinus was irrigated, hemostasis achieved, and paraffin-gauze packing placed. Histopathology confirmed respiratory epithelium lining the proboscis with underlying fibrovascular tissue, adipose tissue, and cartilage islands. The presence of cartilage islands supports the congenital embryological origin of the lesion, reflecting aberrant differentiation of nasal placodal mesenchyme during early craniofacial development. The ACP showed inflammatory changes. Histopathological examination was performed at an external accredited private laboratory; however, representative photomicrographs (H&E staining) were not retrievable for inclusion in this report. Postoperatively, the patient received analgesia and a 5-day antibiotic prophylaxis. Packing was removed on day 1, discharge occurred on day 2, and suture removal was performed on day 7 with satisfactory healing.

At 6-month follow-up, complete nasal obstruction resolution was achieved with patent maxillary drainage and no evidence of polyp recurrence. Esthetic outcomes were assessed clinically, demonstrating a well-healed scar with good contour and symmetry of the nasal sidewall. No complications occurred, including bleeding, cerebrospinal fluid leak, or infection. The patient expressed high satisfaction with functional and aesthetic outcomes.

Discussion

Early intervention is traditionally advocated to minimize psychosocial burden and facilitate normal craniofacial growth[2,9,10]. However, our patient’s successful outcome at 21 years demonstrates that delayed surgical management can achieve excellent functional and aesthetic results with appropriate preoperative planning; an important finding for resource-limited settings where access to pediatric craniofacial surgery may be constrained.

Simple excision with primary closure was chosen over complex staged reconstruction, as the patient’s skeletal maturity precluded growth concerns, the left nasal vestibule was hypoplastic but functional, and the patient’s primary concern was the removal of the visible deformity. Though reconstructive approaches using proboscis tissue offer matched color and texture, they carry risks of necrosis and require multiple staged procedures[11]. Our excisional approach resulted in minimal scarring and high patient satisfaction, suggesting that individualized planning based on patient expectations and anatomy may be preferable to protocol-driven reconstruction.

The concurrent contralateral ACP represents a novel finding not previously reported in the PL literature. Septal deviation towards the hypoplastic left side may have created compensatory airflow patterns through the right nasal cavity, contributing to chronic inflammation and polyp formation – though this mechanism remains hypothetical. Aydın et al demonstrated that many ACP patients show septal deviation contralateral to the polyp and that unilateral nasal hypoplasia may impose increased physiological demand on the contralateral sinus, predisposing it to pathological change[12]. This association warrants comprehensive bilateral sinonasal evaluation in all PL patients.

Combined single-stage surgery addressing both pathologies avoided staged interventions and allowed complete functional resolution in one recovery period. Complete excision of the intramaxillary polyp component is essential to prevent recurrence, and no recurrence was evident at the 6-month follow-up[13].

Limitations include the short follow-up of 6 months, which precludes assessment of long-term esthetic outcomes and late recurrence, given that up to 95% of ACP recurrences may occur within 2 years postoperatively[14]. Histopathological slide images were unavailable as the analysis was performed at an external laboratory. As a single case report, generalization of outcomes requires caution.

Conclusion

This case demonstrates that surgical excision of PL in adulthood can achieve excellent functional and aesthetic outcomes with meticulous technique and comprehensive preoperative planning. The novel association with contralateral ACP underscores the importance of thorough bilateral sinonasal evaluation in all PL patients and expands the literature on PL management in resource-limited settings.

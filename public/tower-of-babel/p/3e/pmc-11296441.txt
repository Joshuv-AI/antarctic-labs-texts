The analysis on Tiam2 for expression in esophageal carcinoma: A descriptive study
Authors: Zhang Jintian, Li Sumei, Yuan Shouping
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Rationale:To investigate T lymphoma invasion and metastasis inducing factor 2 (Tiam2) protein for expression in esophageal carcinoma and relationship with clinical features among cases with tumors.Patient concerns:In primary esophageal cancer patients, surgical resection of tumor tissue was performed in 65 cases and adjacent normal esophageal tissue in 20 cases.Diagnoses:Primary esophageal carcinoma (57 cases squamous cell carcinoma, 8 cases adenosquamous carcinoma).Interventions:The expression level of Tiam2 protein in esophageal carcinoma tissues and normal esophageal tissues by SP immunohistochemical method. The expression intensity was quantitatively analyzed by using Image-pro plus software for image analysis, while SPSS26.0 software was used for a statistical analysis on the data.Outcomes:Tiam2 was highly expressed in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma and adenosquamous cell carcinoma, but low expressed in normal esophageal tissue. The expression level of Tiam2 protein was not correlated with gender and age of patients (P > .05), but was correlated with lymph node metastasis of esophageal carcinoma, TNM stage and differentiation degree of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (P < .05). Tiam2 was positively correlated with Tiam1 for protein expression in esophageal carcinoma (r = .704, P < .001).Lessons:The increased expression of Tiam2 protein in esophageal cancer may be an early molecular event of esophageal cancer. Tiam2 protein has a high expression level in esophageal carcinoma related to lymph node metastasis, TNM stage and differentiation degree, which suggests that Tiam2 protein plays an important role in the invasion and metastasis of esophageal carcinoma. There is a positive correlation between Tiam2 and Tiam1 protein expressions in esophageal carcinoma, suggesting that the 2 proteins may have a definite internal relationship.

1. Introduction

As one of the common malignant tumors of digestive system, esophageal carcinoma occurs in the middle of esophagus mostly.[1] Histologically, squamous cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma, adenosquamous cell carcinoma, and small cell carcinoma are the main types of esophageal carcinoma, in which squamous cell carcinoma is the most common.[2,3] Based on statistics of Global Cancer Research, in 2020, there are about 600,000 new cases of esophageal carcinoma and 540,000 deaths for the disease worldwide.[4] Globally, esophageal carcinoma has already become the seventh leading cancer and the sixth leading cause of death for cancer with a 5-year survival rate of only 10% to 30%.[5–7] At an early stage of esophageal carcinoma, there was no obvious invasion of cancerous tissue or symptoms for dysphagia, and so, the disease is often detected when luminal stenosis or distant metastasis occurs for the progression of invasion. Therefore, most of the patients have been in the middle or late stage once confirmed. Esophageal carcinoma’s characterized by high incidence, high mortality, high metastasis rate, and low early diagnosis rate.[8] Among patients with esophageal carcinoma, the main causes of death are tumor invasion, metastasis, and recurrence. The tumor invasion and metastasis result from co-regulation of multiple genes, and therefore, identifying major genes that promote the invasion and metastasis of esophageal carcinoma and exploring relevant mechanisms have become hot spots for cancer research nowadays. Studies have found high expression of T lymphoma invasion and metastasis inducing factor 2 (Tiam2) in a variety of human cancerous tissues, which is closely related to the invasion and metastasis of carcinoma cells. At present, there is still no report concerning the quantitative study on the expression of Tiam2 protein in esophageal carcinoma or concerning the correlation between Tiam2 and T lymphoma invasion and metastasis inducing factor 1 (Tiam1) proteins globally. For this, through in situ quantitative detection of Tiam2 and Tiam1 protein expressions in esophageal carcinoma, this study explored the role of Tiam2 in the occurrence and development of esophageal carcinoma and its relationship with Tiam1 expression.

2. Materials and methods

2.1. Materials

2.1.1. Source of specimens

The paraffin-embedded specimens of esophageal carcinoma tissue and normal esophagus tissue were collected from the Pathology Department of our hospital. Including 65 cases of primary esophageal carcinoma (57 cases of squamous cell carcinoma, 8 cases of adenosquamous carcinoma) and 20 cases of normal esophagus tissue (more than 5 cm away from the mass). Inclusion criteria: preoperatively diagnosed as esophageal carcinoma and confirmed as esophageal carcinoma by pathological HE staining of postoperative paraffin-embedded specimen for the first time. All of the patients had not received other treatments like chemotherapy or radiotherapy before surgery, and also had no history of other malignancies or major diseases. In the 65 cases, there were 46 males and 19 females, average age: 61.08 ± 8.17 years (range: 43–80 years), and there were 30 cases with lymph node metastasis and 35 cases without lymph node metastasis. No distant metastasis was found. According to the “International TNM Staging of Esophageal carcinoma (the 8th Edition)” formulated by the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) and the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC),[9] there were 15 cases at stage I, 31 cases at stage II, and 19 cases at stage III. According to the pathological grading criteria of World Health Organization (WHO) for esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, there were 17 cases highly differentiated, 22 cases moderately differentiated, and 18 cases poorly differentiated. 2.1.2. Main reagents

Rabbit antihuman Tiam2 and Tiam1 polyclonal antibodies (working concentration: 1:200) were purchased from Abcam Company, the United States, while the SP immunohistochemical kit and DAB chromogenic reagent were from Fuzhou Maixin Biotechnology Co, Ltd.

2.2. Experimental methods

2.2.1. Testing of specimens

For each paraffin block, there were 3 sections of thickness 4 μm. One section was stained by conventional HE staining, and the other 2 sections were performed Tiam2 and Tiam1 immunohistochemical SP staining (for quantitative analysis without hematoxylin restaining). For this, specific procedure were as follows: deparaffinize the paraffin-embedded specimen sections to water, incubate with 3% H2O2 to inactivate the endogenous peroxidase, place in sodium citrate buffer solution for high pressure antigen repair, perform serum blocking, dropwise add primary antibody, incubate in a refrigerator at 4 °C overnight, then dropwise add secondary antibody, incubate at room temperature for 1 hour, perform DAB color development without restaining (for quantitative analysis of image), and then dehydrate, transparentize, and mount the sections.

2.2.2. Quantitative analysis of microscopic images

If the cytoplasm was dyed tan, brownish yellow or light yellow, the cell was positive. The expression intensity was quantitatively analyzed by using Image-pro plus software for image analysis. For each section, under 400× magnification, 10 fields were randomly selected to test 200 positive cells for grayscale value. The grayscale value of each positive cell was recorded as Gβ, while the background grayscale value in the corresponding section for test was recorded as Gα. The positive unit (PU) of each positive cell was calculated according to the following formula. The Gmax in the image analysis system was 256. The average PU value of 200 positive cells in each section was taken as the PU of the specimen. The higher the PU value, the higher the expression intensity, and the lower the PU value, the lower the expression intensity.

2.3. Statistical analysis methods

SPSS26.0 software was used for a statistical analysis on the data. The analysis methods were as follows: independent sample t test was used for comparing 2 groups of measurement data, and ANOVA for comparing multiple groups of measurement data. As for gene expression correlation, Pearson correlation analysis was adopted. P < .05 indicated significant difference, while P < .001 indicated very significant difference.

3.4. Correlation between Tiam2 and Tiam1 expressions in esophageal carcinoma tissue

The PU values of Tiam2 and Tiam1 protein expressions in esophageal carcinoma were 10.88 ± 3.89 and 11.04 ± 4.74, respectively. The correlation analysis results of Tiam2 and Tiam1 protein expressions in esophageal carcinoma showed that the expression levels of Tiam2 and Tiam1 were positively correlated, correlation coefficient r = .704, P < .001.

4. Discussion

For esophageal carcinoma, one of the most fatal malignant tumors in the world, the therapeutic effect has always been unsatisfactory, which is closely related to the strong invasiveness and high metastasis rate of the disease, and furthermore, most of the patients with esophageal carcinoma have no obvious symptoms at an early stage, and so, once confirmed, they have been in the middle or late stage, and for this, a combination therapy based on radiotherapy has to be adopted because they cannot be cured by surgery alone anymore.[10,11] In recent years, the technology for combination therapy has been advanced continuously with updating of facilities, but the therapeutic effect is still not so satisfactory. In addition, postoperative symptoms, such as dysphagia, loss of appetite, early satiety, aspiration and regurgitation, compromise the patients’ quality of life seriously.[12] To cure such cases, a strategy based on multidisciplinary therapy has to be adopted. A mass of data has demonstrated the value of molecular-targeted therapy for inhibiting tumor progression. Specifically, in advanced refractory cancers, the therapy may be used to prevent the growth, invasion and metastasis of tumors through interference with specific molecules, and so, it may be adopted as a first-line option for relapsed or metastatic disease.[13–15] Therefore, it is critical to identify new molecular targets contributing to the occurrence and metastasis of esophageal carcinoma for optimizing relevant therapeutic regimens and improving the prognosis of patients clinically.

As a human gene, Tiam2 is located on chromosome 6q25.2, and its transcription produces 2 mRNA subtypes. Specifically, its long transcription encodes protein Tiam2L with 1701 amino acid residues while short transcription encodes protein Tiam2S with 626 residues.[16] These proteins are subfamily members of Tiam as guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF), and they may act as activators of Rac, the small GTPase.[17] As an important member of Rho enzyme family (Rho, Rac, and Cdc42), Rac1 has been proved to regulate a variety of cellular functions, for example, recombining the cytoskeleton, changing the morphological structure of cell, controlling the cell signaling, promoting the malignant transformation and angiogenesis of tumor, and advancing the cell invasion and metastasis of tumor.[18–20] Tiam1, which was first identified in invasive mouse T lymphoma cells, has been identified as a metastasis-related gene. It promotes the migration and invasion of carcinoma cells through regulating their functions. Tiam2, a homolog of Tiam1, contains an interaction domain that activates Rac1,[21] thus playing a major role in tumor genesis and neuronal development.[22,23]

Tiam2 was explored mostly in malignant tumors, such as liver cancer, lung cancer, and pancreatic cancer. Chen et al found that Tiam2S was highly expressed in primary liver cancer and not expressed in normal human liver cells, and also, Tiam2S was positively correlated with Tiam1in expression. An in vivo study with xenotransplantation mouse models showed that inducing endogenous expression of Tiam 2S turned invasive human primary liver cancer cells into highly invasive tumors. A relevant in-depth study found that Tiam 2S expression could lead to the up-regulation of N-cadherin and vimentin as well as the redistribution of E-cadherin, indicating that Tiam2S was involved in the pathogenesis of primary liver cancer, and at the same time, the expression of Tiam2S promoted epithelial–mesenchymal transformation of liver cancer cells, thus leading to their proliferation and invasion.[23] Zhao et al[24] found that Tiam2 was highly expressed in non-small cell lung cancer. Silencing the expression of Tiam2 in lung cancer cells inhibited the capability of the cells for invasion and movement as well as the activation of Rac1, while downregulating Tiam2 upregulated the expression of E-cadherin and downregulated the expression of MMP-3, Twist and Snail, suggesting that the activation of Rac1 and the regulation of some invasion-related genes were involved in this process. Li et al[25] found that the expression level of Tiam2 in primary lung cancer-related fibroblasts was significantly higher than in normal lung fibroblasts, and the migration and invasion ability of Tiam2-silenced fibroblasts was decreased. In a direct co-culture system, the migration and invasion of cancer cells were blocked when the cells were co-cultured with Tiam2-silenced fibroblasts, suggesting that the fibroblast Tiam2 promoted the invasion and metastasis of lung cancer cells.Tiam2 was also highly expressed in pancreatic cancer, and correlated with the histopathologic grade, tumor-related location and pathological T stage of pancreatic cancer.[26]

This study found that Tiam2 protein was expressed in both esophageal carcinoma and normal esophagus tissues. It was highly expressed in esophageal carcinoma tissue and low expressed in normal esophagus tissue, and the difference was significant, suggesting that Tiam2 protein might play a role in the occurrence, development and malignant transformation of esophageal carcinoma as an early event of esophageal carcinoma. Moreover, Tiam2 protein is closely related to lymph node metastasis of esophageal carcinoma, TNM stage and pathological grade of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. Therefore, the author speculated that Tiam2 might have participated in the occurrence of esophageal carcinoma and promoted tumor invasion and metastasis as a regulatory gene for proliferation. Some scholars have suggested that Tiam2 may have a carcinogenic effect through the following mechanisms: Tiam2 binds to activated Rho family GTPases directly, thus triggering downstream signals. Alternatively, Tiam2 binds directly to downstream scaffolding proteins, activating signal transduction and exerting its tumorigenic effect.[22] We also found that Tiam2 and Tiam1 protein expressions were significantly correlated in esophageal carcinoma as a previous study indicated significant correlation betweenTiam2 and Tiam1 protein expression in liver cancer.[22] Thus, we speculated that these 2 proteins might have a definite internal relationship for regulating each other and act synergistically during the occurrence and development of cancer.

5. Conclusions

The expression of Tiam2 protein may be upregulated in esophageal carcinoma, and it is significantly correlated with lymph node metastasis of esophageal carcinoma, TNM stage and pathological grade of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, which suggests its important role in the occurrence and progression of esophageal carcinoma. The Tiam2 and Tiam1 protein expressions are correlated significantly in esophageal carcinoma, indicating that Tiam2 and Tiam1 proteins may interact for a carcinogenic effect.

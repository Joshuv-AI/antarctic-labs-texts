Epidemiologic characteristics of people living with human immunodeficiency virus in South Korea: a nationwide cohort study
Authors: Tak Kyu Oh, Kyoung-Ho Song, Eunjeong Heo, In-Ae Song
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Recent information regarding the characteristics of people living with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) (PLWH) is lacking. Therefore, we examined the incidence of HIV infection and epidemiologic characteristics of PLWH in South Korea using recent National Health Insurance Service database data. The HIV infection rate was 4.13 per 100,000 in 2017, 4.19 per 100,000 in 2018, 6.61 per 100,000 in 2019, 5.98 per 100,000 in 2020, 8.81 per 100,000 in 2021, and 4.14 per 100,000 in 2022. In 2016, the mean age of PLWH was 45.7 years (SD: 13.8 years), and it gradually increased to 49.0 years (standard deviation: 13.7 years) in 2022 over 7 years in South Korea. The proportion of male individuals among PLWH was 90.7% (19,970/22,026) in 2016, and it gradually increased to 92.3% (22,943/24,857) in 2022 over 7 years in South Korea. The proportion of anti-retroviral therapy (ART) users among PLWH in South Korea was 92.6% (20,388/22,026) in 2016, and it gradually increased to 97.9% (24,328/24,857) in 2022 over 7 years. In this population-based cohort analysis, we presented the HIV infection incidence and epidemiologic features of PLWH in South Korea. This study’s results may influence future infection prevention strategies and policies for PLWH.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-76043-3.

Introduction

An essential component of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection control is determining its epidemiology1. The incidence of acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS) can be used to estimate HIV’s burden2. Therefore, analyzing the number, demographics, and characteristics of people living with HIV (PLWH) is essential for public health policy3.

In South Korea, the cumulative incidence of HIV infection has gradually increased since 1987, with 1,280 PLWH cases reported in December 20004. According to the national HIV surveillance system in South Korea5, the number of PLWH was reported to be 7,945 between 2008 and 2015, with 91.6% being male. Another cohort study in South Korea, using the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) database, reported that between 2009 and 2015, a total of 7,033 PLWH and 2,899 patients with AIDS were officially diagnosed6. The Korea HIV/AIDS Cohort Study, which included 1,442 Korean PLWH, revealed that 89.1% received antiretroviral therapy (ART) based on recommendation7. While the historical trends of PLWH in Korea have been depicted in each of these studies, current information regarding the overall characteristics of PLWH, including foreigners who reside in Korea, is lacking.

Therefore, we aimed to examine the incidence of HIV infection and epidemiologic characteristics of PLWH in South Korea using recent data from the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) database.

Methods

Study design and ethical statement

This population-based cohort research followed the recommendations of Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology8. All procedures were carried out in accordance with the ethical standards established by national and institutional human experimentation committees, as well as the principles of the Helsinki Declaration of 1975, which was amended in 2008. The project was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital (number: X-2306-837-902, date: 2023-06-20). The study protocol was authorized by the NHIS (approval number: NHIS-2024-1-021), and permission was acquired to access the data. The requirement of informed consent was waived by the IRB of Seoul National University Bundang Hospital due to the utilization of anonymized data collected retrospectively.

Data source

The NHIS is the only public health insurance provider in South Korea. It comprises extensive data on prescription medicine orders, medical procedures, and disease diagnoses. The 10th Revision of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD-10) codes were used to organize and categorize the data. Enrollment in the NHIS program is mandatory for all Koreans residing in South Korea and for foreign nationals who have lived there for over six months. Enrollees in the NHIS system pay premiums, and based on the severity of their disease, the government subsidizes their treatment or tests. Prescriptions, treatments, and costs are all under government control, although over 95% of healthcare providers are private practitioners. As a result, no data exist on the potential for missing doctor-recorded diagnoses or prescriptions. This study considered all ICD-10 diagnoses and principal diagnoses. The NHIS database contains information on all-cause mortality rates and socioeconomic variables9.

Patients living with HIV in South Korea

In South Korea, HIV/AIDS is a statutory Group 3 infectious disease legally managed by the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA). Patients suspected to have HIV are tested at each healthcare facility using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) as a screening test. If the ELISA test suggests HIV infection, the Korea National Institute of Health in the KDCA and the 17 Institutes of Health and Environment across the country will conduct confirmatory testing for HIV infection using western blot tests. The physician who ordered the test is obligated to notify the public health center as soon as they are informed of a patient’s HIV-positive status. The physician must also have one of the following five ICD-10 codes (B20–B24) registered in the NHIS database. Therefore, all HIV/AIDS patients who have been officially diagnosed should be registered in the NHIS for treatment in South Korea. HIV is legally classified as a Group 3 infectious disease, making patients diagnosed with the virus eligible for state reimbursement of all associated medical and treatment costs, including ART. Specifically, HIV/AIDS is classified as a rare and incurable disease, with the NHIS covering 90% of the cost and the national and municipal budgets covering the remaining 10% (50% subsidy). Therefore, general treatment is completely free of charge, except for non-covered components of health insurance.

Study population

This study included all PLWH who were confirmed to have HIV in South Korea between January 1, 2016, and December 31, 2022. Thus, the study population was the cumulative number of PLWH in South Korea between 2016 and 2022. Based on a previous Japanese cohort study10, five ICD codes were used to identify PLWH: nonspecific HIV disease (B24), malignant neoplasms (B21), other specified diseases (B22), other disorders (B23), and HIV disease resulting in infectious and parasitic diseases (B20).

Study outcomes and variables

The incidence of HIV infection in South Korea from 2017 to 2022 was examined. To determine the incidence rate, the numerator consisted of the newly diagnosed PLWH cases from 2017 to 2022. The denominator, on the other hand, was the registered population of South Korea during those years, as provided by Statistics Korea (https://kostat.go.kr/anse).

We also compared characteristics of PLWH accumulated in 2016 with those of newly diagnosed PLWH from 2017 to 2022. These characteristics included age, sex, socioeconomic variables, and ART prescription information. However, PLWH who emigrated from Korea or died during the study period were lost to follow-up, that is, information of individuals who registered as PLWH in 2021 but died or moved out of the country in 2021 was excluded from the PLWH data for 2022. Moreover, people who did not attend a healthcare facility for HIV/AIDS treatment or care in a particular year were not considered PLWH for that period, potentially indicating lost to follow-up.

The database also includes information on foreign PLWH residing in South Korea to control and manage HIV infection. PLWH were categorized into seven groups according to age (1–18 years, 19–30 years, 31–40 years, 41–50 years, 51–60 years, 61–70 years, and ≥ 71 years). Data on employment status and household income level were collected to reflect the socioeconomic status of PLWH. Those whose income was too low to pay insurance premiums were categorized into the medical aid program group based on household income in the NHIS database. The remaining patients were divided into quartiles (Q1 [lowest], Q2, Q3, and Q4 [highest]). A specific group was assigned to individuals who were underage and unemployed based on the income or wealth of the dependents in the household. Since all disabilities must be reported in the NHIS database to be eligible for various benefits from the social welfare systems in South Korea, information regarding underlying disabilities was gathered. Every disability should be assessed legally by a medical professional specializing in the field, considering the patient’s inability to perform activities of daily living. The severity of underlying disability was divided into two categories: mild-to-moderate and severe. The types of ART licensed and used in South Korea during the study period are listed in Supplementary Table 1. All ART licensing lists for 2016–2022 were used and manually extracted by pharmacists on January 1 of each year. Missing drugs refers to those that were not licensed or available in Korea at that time. For combination drugs, the NHIS categorizes drug information by component, with tablet combinations appearing in the table as separate components. For example, abacavir/lamivudine are mentioned as two separate components.

Statistical analysis

The continuous variable (age) is presented as a mean with standard deviation (SD) and median value with interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are presented as numbers with percentages. For the comparison of characteristics between PLWH in 2016 and newly diagnosed PLWH between 2017 and 2022, the Mann-Whitney U test and Chi-square test were used for continuous variables and categorical variables, respectively. IBM SPSS for Windows (version 25.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA) was used for data presentation and analysis.

Results

Incidence of HIV infection from 2017 to 2022 in South Korea

Figure 1 depicts the incidence of HIV infection in South Korea from 2017 to 2022. The incidence rates of HIV infection were 4.13 per 100,000 in 2017, 4.19 per 100,000 in 2018, 6.61 per 100,000 in 2019, 5.98 per 100,000 in 2020, 8.81 per 100,000 in 2021, and 4.14 per 100,000 in 2022.

Trends in PLWH between 2016 and 2022 in South Korea

Table 2 presents the mean age, proportion of males, and proportion of ART users among PLWH in South Korea between 2016 and 2022. The mean age of PLWH was 45.7 years (SD: 13.8 years) in 2016, and it gradually increased to 49.0 years (SD: 13.7 years) in 2022 over 7 years in South Korea. The proportion of male individuals among PLWH was 90.7% (19,970/22,026) in 2016, and it gradually increased to 92.3% (22,943/24,857) in 2022 over 7 years in South Korea. The proportion of ART users among PLWH in South Korea was 92.6% (20,388/22,026) in 2016, and it gradually increased to 97.9% (24,328/24,857) in 2022 over 7 years.

Comparison of characteristics of PLWH in 2016 vs. newly diagnosed PLWH during 2017–2022

Table 2 presents the findings of the comparison of PLWH characteristics in 2016 to newly diagnosed PLWH between 2017 and 2022. In 2016, the median age for PLWH was 45 years [IQR: 35, 55]; however, for newly diagnosed PLWH from 2017 to 2022, it was 40 years [IQR: 30, 56] (P < 0.001). In 2016, 90.7% of PLWH were male (19,970/22,026). From 2017 to 2022, the proportion of newly diagnosed PLWH was 84.1% (14,728/17,504) (P < 0.001). In 2016, 15.7% (3,464/22,026) of PLWH participated in medical aid programs, compared to 6.1% (1,067/17,504) for newly diagnosed PLWH from 2017 to 2022 (P < 0.001). In 2016, 92.6% (20,388/22,026) of PLWH utilized ART, while 70.2% (12,292/17,504) of newly diagnosed PLWH used ART between 2017 and 2022.

Discussion

This population-based cohort study presented the incidence of HIV infection in South Korea from 2017 to 2022. Furthermore, epidemiologic characteristics such as age, sex, and ART use among PLWH were described. The proportions of ART users and males among PLWH were high, respectively, in South Korea. We also compared the characteristics of PLWH accumulated in South Korea in 2016 to those newly diagnosed as PLWH over a six-year period from 2017 to 2022. The findings indicate that newly diagnosed PLWH in 2017–2022 were younger and less likely to be male than in 2016, and ART use rates were higher in 2016 because the most recently diagnosed PLWH in 2022 had not yet started ART.

In South Korea, the HIV infection rate ranged between 4.13 per 100,000 people in 2017 and 8.81 per 100,000 people in 2021. According to recent data11, the United States witnessed approximately 32,100 new HIV infections in 2021, with an incidence rate of 11.5 per 100,000 individuals. According to another study, the incidence rate of HIV infection in Europe was 11.9 per 100,000 population in 202112. As a result, South Korea has a lower incidence of HIV infection than the United States or Europe.

Regarding the use of ARTs, the proportion of ART users in our study was higher (> 90% in Table 1) than those reported in other studies. According to reports from Canada, 82.6–88.5% of PLWH13 acquired ART. Furthermore, 84% (95% CI: 81–87%) of patients diagnosed with HIV in the United States received an ART prescription during the previous 12 months14. By 2030, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention in the United States intend to execute a 90-90-90 strategy (90% HIV diagnosed, 90% on medication, and 90% suppressed)15. Initiation of ART is recommended immediately after HIV diagnosis, and ART plays a crucial role in the treatment of PLWH and patients with AIDS16. South Korea’s health insurance system covers most of the medical expenses for all patients with HIV, including ART. Specifically, NHIS in South Korea covers 90% of HIV/AIDS, a rare and incurable disease, while national and municipal budgets cover 10% of the co-payment (50% subsidized). Free general care is available, including ART, except for non-covered health insurance.

Notably, a significant proportion (15.7%) of PLWH in 2016 were economically disadvantaged and classified into the medical aid program group in this study. In the 2013 sample cohort of South Korea, the proportions of those in the medical aid program group were 2.5% and 3.2% among male and female individuals, respectively17. The economic impact of HIV/AIDS is an important public health issue because healthcare in PLWH is costly18. In South Korea, the government provides coverage for most medical expenses for PLWH, many of whom come from economically disadvantaged backgrounds. Considering the young PLWH in the present study were on average, the fact that almost half were unemployed is a severe issue, which may have contributed to their economic poverty.

Table 1 (2016–2022) in South Korea highlights a significant demographic characteristic: most PLWH are men, with a proportion exceeding 90%. In addition, this proportion slightly increased from 2016 (90.7%) to 2022 (92.3%). Men who have sex with men (MSM) are a public health concern regarding HIV infection epidemics, as the high risk of transmission by receptive anal contact per act plays a major role in explaining the disproportionately high disease burden among MSM. The high proportion of males among PLWH in South Korea may also be influenced by the proportion of MSM. In the United States, the proportion of males among PLWH was 77% in 201119. In a cohort study conducted in London, the United Kingdom, the proportion of males among PLWH was 87.6%.20 In a report similar to ours, 92% of PLWH in Japan were men21. Recent reports highlight the need for programs to lessen stigma and customize psychosocial support for MSM living with HIV22. In this context, the high proportion of males should be considered when establishing policies for the care and treatment of PLWH in South Korea.

Table 2 shows a concerning trend of HIV infections occurring at younger ages, with a noticeable rise in the number of females affected. Furthermore, from 2017 to 2022, the proportion of PLWH on medical aid programs decreased while the proportion with a job increased, indicating that people with higher socioeconomic levels were infected with HIV more recently. Our study has some limitations. First, we had no information on CD4 cell count, which is the most important indicator of HIV activity. This is due to the limitations of the NHIS database. Second, this study did not include information related to the lifestyle of PLWH, such as drinking and smoking histories. Third, the study did not provide information on the exact infection route in PLWH owing to the lack of information in the NHIS database. Fourth, our data comprised PLWH from 2020 to 2022, and the coronavirus disease pandemic may have influenced the outcomes due to a notable decline in PLWH healthcare utilization. Fifth, to accurately capture all PLWH, data from exempted calculations for health insurance as well as ICD-10 codes. However, this study did not use data from these calculations. Lastly, as shown in Table 1, the overall count of PLWH in 2022 reached 24,857. In contrast, Table 2 shows a cumulative count of 39,530 for the same year (22,026 plus 17,504). Individuals diagnosed with PLWH in 2022 were counted as those consistently visiting a healthcare practitioner, suggesting that a considerable proportion of patients were lost to follow-up over the five-year period. Death, emigration, or disengagement from treatment or care could contribute to this lack of follow-up. This limitation should be carefully examined when interpreting the study’s findings.

In conclusion, the HIV infection rate in South Korea ranged from 4.13 to 8.81 per 100,000 people, with a high proportion of ART users and males among PLWH between 2016 and 2022. In addition, there was a high proportion of economically disadvantaged individuals among PLWH, requiring medical aid. The results of this study may influence future infection prevention strategies and policies for PLWH.

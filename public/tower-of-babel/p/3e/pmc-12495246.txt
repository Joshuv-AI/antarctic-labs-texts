A novel presentation of ocular chronic lymphocytic leukemia
Authors: Lauren A. Nakhleh, Jamie A. Keen, Elisabeth Sledz, Meenakshi Chaku, Brooke Johnson, Larissa K. Ghadiali
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo describe a rare presentation of chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) involving the conjunctiva and caruncle.ObservationsA 64-year-old male with a history of systemic CLL and a blind right eye due to retinal vascular disease and optic neuropathy presented with gradually worsening redness and foreign body sensation in the same eye. Anterior exam revealed chemosis, thickened conjunctiva, an enlarged caruncle with an irregular surface, and large pale superior and inferior forniceal follicles, altogether concerning for infiltration of the conjunctiva by CLL. Conjunctival and caruncular biopsies demonstrated small CD5+ B-cell lymphoma, consistent with CLL.Conclusions and importanceCLL is the most common form of leukemia in the western world, though there is a relatively low prevalence of ocular manifestations. Our case revealed a unique presentation of this disease manifesting in the conjunctiva and caruncle. Performing an incisional biopsy of the abnormal appearing tissue for analysis with cell typing was essential to confirming this diagnosis. This case reveals a novel presentation of CLL involving the caruncle and emphasizes the importance of obtaining a tissue biopsy in patients presenting with atypical changes seen on the conjunctiva and caruncle with a history of known hematologic malignancy.

Introduction

Chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) is a lymphoproliferative disorder characterized by the clonal proliferation and accumulation of neoplastic B lymphocytes in the blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and spleen.1 While CLL is the most common form of leukemia in the Western World, there is a relatively rare prevalence of ocular manifestations.1, 2, 3 While there have been numerous reports of CLL involving various ocular structures, there have only been eight reported cases of lymphocytic-leukemic infiltrate of the conjunctiva to date.3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 After conducting a literature review on 11/30/2024 utilizing PubMed using the key words (caruncle, chronic lymphocytic leukemia, CLL, conjunctiva, ocular leukemia), we did not find any prior reports of CLL infiltrating the caruncle. Our objective is to describe a rare case of CLL involving the conjunctiva and caruncle and emphasize the importance of obtaining a tissue biopsy when there are abnormal conjunctival or caruncular findings, especially in patients with a history of known hematologic malignancy.

Case report

A 64-year-old male presented with acute painless vision loss of the inferior hemifield of the right eye. The patient's past medical history was significant for stage II (Rai classification) chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), diagnosed 18 years prior and treated with chemotherapy, resulting in subsequent remission. His other medical history included hypertension, carotid artery disease, alcohol abuse, cirrhosis, and tobacco use disorder. Ocular history included cataract surgery, YAG capsulotomy, and hypertensive retinopathy of both eyes.

On initial presentation, his visual acuity was 20/20 and his exam revealed right optic disc edema, mid-peripheral retinal hemorrhages, and an inferior visual field defect. Laboratory work-up including infectious and inflammatory serum labs was notable only for leukocytosis. Positron Emission Tomography (PET), CT chest, and lumbar puncture were unrevealing. An MRI of the brain and orbits revealed a right sphenoid wing meningioma, which did not appear to compress the visual apparatus. It also demonstrated subtle swelling of the right optic nerve, but no evidence of enhancement. Imaging of the carotid arteries revealed a right common carotid artery total occlusion. Eight days after presentation, his visual acuity declined to hand motion and his visual field defect and optic nerve edema worsened. He was initiated on high dose steroids until his temporal artery biopsy was found to be negative. Per oncology, the patient was briefly started on empiric ibrutinib for systemic treatment of CLL which was stopped given intolerance to the medication. At that time, the consensus among his multi-disciplinary team was that his vision loss was due to non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy. Seven weeks after initial presentation he developed neovascular glaucoma with acutely elevated intraocular pressures believed to be a result of ischemic retinal disease. Despite intraocular pressure control, two intravitreal bevacizumab injections, and multiple sessions of panretinal photocoagulation (PRP), his visual acuity declined to no light perception (NLP).

Two years after this initial presentation, the patient returned with gradually worsening conjunctival injection and chemosis in the right eye. His visual acuity remained NLP in the right eye and his left eye exam remained stable. Examination of the right eye was consistent with chemosis, thickened bulbar, tarsal, and forniceal conjunctiva, large pale superior and inferior forniceal follicles, and a pale and enlarged caruncle with an irregular surface (Fig. 1). Given the patient's history of previously treated CLL 18 years prior, there was a concern for CLL infiltration of the conjunctiva and caruncle. The remainder of the exam was notable for right optic disc pallor, collateral vessels of the optic nerve, superior dot blot hemorrhages, vascular attenuation and sclerosis, and PRP scars, consistent with his prior history of retinal vascular disease. The patient was admitted to the hematology/oncology service with concern for CLL progression and possible infiltration into the right orbit and central nervous system. MRI of the brain and orbits demonstrated growth of the sphenoid wing meningioma along the right intracranial optic nerve without significant compression (Fig. 2). There were no signs of compromise of the chiasm or of leptomeningeal enhancement. Multiple enlarged lymph nodes were visualized in the upper neck, consistent with CLL. Cerebrospinal fluid again showed no morphologic or immunophenotypic evidence of B-cell lymphoma. The conjunctiva of the right inferior cul-de-sac and right caruncle were biopsied. Due to limited tissue, the entirety of the right lower conjunctiva specimen was sent for flow cytometry in addition to a touch preparation, while the right caruncle was submitted for routine histopathologic examination. Both the conjunctival and caruncular biopsies demonstrated small CD5+ B-cell lymphoma, consistent with the patient's known CLL (Fig. 3). As a result, the patient was referred to ocular oncology who recommended clinically monitoring the optic nerve and brain with sequential MRIs. The patient was instructed to discontinue his glaucoma eye drops for the right eye and was started on antibiotic eye drops following the biopsy. At his one-month follow-up, the chemosis had significantly improved. By five months, both the chemosis and caruncular enlargement had resolved with the use of lubricating drops alone. Discussion

While previous studies have described ophthalmic involvement in 30–90 % of all types of leukemia, the prevalence of ocular disease in CLL specifically is not well reported and remains unknown.2 Ocular involvement can precede the diagnosis of leukemia, present throughout the course of disease, or occur following treatment, either as a direct manifestation of leukemic infiltration or as an indirect effect due to factors such as anemia, thrombocytopenia, hyperviscosity, immunosuppression or infection.11,12 One prospective study found 12 % of CLL patients to have only an indirect, secondary ocular complication with no correlation to disease staging.4 In CLL, ocular involvement has been reported in the orbit, lacrimal system, extraocular muscles, optic nerve, retina, uvea, and sclera.3 Only eight cases of infiltration of the conjunctiva have been reported.3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 These cases have been reported in patients without a prior diagnosis of CLL,5 a patient undergoing active treatment,6 as well as in a patient thought to be in remission.7

Of these prior eight case reports, only three provided descriptions of the ocular presentations and management. One report described a patient without a history of CLL referred for scleritis, who was found to have bilateral temporal bulbar conjunctival lesions positive for CLL and co-existing necrobiotic xanthogranuloma. In this case, systemic chemotherapy was recommended.5 Another patient was undergoing active chemotherapy and was found to have conjunctival injection and a peri-limbal ring shaped salmon colored conjunctival swelling. Conjunctival biopsy was positive for suspected CLL infiltration. The patient underwent enucleation secondary to corneal melting from corynebacterium infection.6 Similar to the patient described in this report, a patient with a history of CLL thought to be in remission presented with bilateral conjunctival injection, discharge, and the presence of large follicle-like lesions involving the superior and inferior palpebral conjunctiva. The patient was started on the antioxidant quercetin, with a poor response, followed by the initiation of ibrutinib.7 There have also been numerous reports of small lymphocytic leukemia (SLL), an equivalent pathology to CLL though mostly predominant in the lymph nodes, infiltrating the conjunctiva.8,10,13,14

This case emphasizes the importance of promptly obtaining a tissue biopsy in patients that present clinically with an enlarged caruncle with an irregular surface, chemosis, and large pale follicles, especially when there is a known history of hematologic malignancy. It's important that providers are aware of this uncommon presentation of CLL to optimize both ocular and systemic treatment.

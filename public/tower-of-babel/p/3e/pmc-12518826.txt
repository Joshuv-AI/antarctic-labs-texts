Hybrid integral sliding mode and fuzzy logic control for omnidirectional robots: modified elephant herding optimization for trajectory tracking
Authors: Rasha Mohammed Hussein, Auday Shaker Hadi, Sameh Fareed Hasan, Luttfi A. Al-Haddad, Rafika Harrabi, Aymen Flah, Iman Ahmed Abdul Sahib
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Trajectory tracking and robust motion control remain central challenges in autonomous robotic systems operating under nonlinear and uncertain conditions. This study proposes a novel hybrid control framework that integrates Integral Sliding Mode Control (ISMC), whose control parameters are calculated using a Sugeno-type Fuzzy Logic Controller (FLC) and a Modified Elephant Herding Optimization (MEHO) algorithm. The approach is implemented on a three-wheeled omnidirectional mobile robot (TOMR), with detailed dynamic and kinematic modeling. The MEHO algorithm enhances exploration–exploitation balance and accelerates convergence through adaptive update mechanisms. To assess the performance of the proposed controller, a triangle and C-shape trajectories are implemented. Simulation results using MATLAB R2022b show that the proposed system reduces positional errors in the X and Y axes to below 0.005 m, and orientation error to 0.0014 rad within 2 s. The root mean square errors (RMSE) for X, Y, and orientation for the triangle trajectory are 3.48 × 10⁻⁵ m, 2.51 × 10⁻⁶ m, and 0.00287 rad, respectively and for the C-shape trajectory are 6.538*10− 6 m, 2.614*10− 6 m, and 1.051*10− 5 rad for X, Y, and orientation, respectively. Compared to classical EHO-based and adaptive neural sliding controllers, the proposed method achieves up to 50% lower torque variation and over 60% faster settling time. The modular and learning-based design enables generalization to other robotic platforms, including aerial robots operating in uncertain or dynamic environments.

Introduction

Omnidirectional wheeled mobile robots (WMRs) have gained significant attention in recent years due to their enhanced maneuverability in compact structures and suitability for operations in constrained and dynamic environments1–7. Among various designs, the three-wheeled omnidirectional mobile robot (TOMR) stands out for its mechanical simplicity and ability to move in any planar direction without changing orientation8. This unique capability is achieved through the use of special wheels, such as omni-wheels or mecanum wheels, arranged at specific angles9–11. TOMRs are widely applied in logistics as they are considered to be service robotics, in addition to industrial automation of medical transport due to their high mobility and flexibility in motion planning and obstacle avoidance12–14. However, their control is nontrivial due to the nonlinear and coupled dynamics, especially when high-precision trajectory tracking is required15.

To address the complexity of trajectory tracking and stabilization in nonlinear mobile robot systems, advanced control strategies have been developed, often combining robust control laws with intelligent optimization or adaptive layers. Integral Sliding Mode Control (ISMC) is commonly employed for its robustness against model uncertainties and external disturbances, particularly in systems with nonlinear characteristics16. Additionally, fuzzy logic controllers (FLCs) have been extensively used to handle imprecise input data and provide smooth control actions based on linguistic rules17,18. To fine-tune the parameters of such hybrid controllers, metaheuristic optimization algorithms—such as Genetic Algorithms (GA)19, Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)20, and Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO)21—are widely adopted for their ability to explore large solution spaces efficiently. These methods contribute significantly to improving convergence speed, control precision, and adaptability in dynamic robotic environments.

Table 1 summarizes a broad range of recent hybrid and intelligent control methodologies applied to mobile and robotic systems. These studies incorporate various combinations of sliding mode control, fuzzy logic, neural networks, and metaheuristic optimization techniques, applied to systems ranging from exoskeletons and manipulators to continuum and wheeled mobile robots. The key findings highlight notable advancements in tracking accuracy, robustness, and control adaptability. However, each study also presents specific limitations, such as application specificity, computational overhead, or reliance on precise modeling, making generalizability or real-time implementation challenging. This comparative overview helps position the current study within the existing body of work.

Elephant herding optimization algorithm (EHO)

The Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO) algorithm, initially introduced by Wang et al. in 2015, is a nature-inspired metaheuristic technique that belongs to the family of swarm intelligence algorithms46. It draws its conceptual foundation from the hierarchical and cooperative social behavior observed in elephant herds. In optimization contexts, achieving an effective solution generally relies on two crucial capabilities: global exploration, which allows the algorithm to scan broad regions of the solution space to escape local optima, and local exploitation, which focuses on fine-tuning solutions within a promising area. EHO is particularly recognized for its intrinsic ability to balance these two processes effectively, as shown in Fig. 7 below.

Fig. 7The social hierarchy of the EHO algorithm48.

The social hierarchy of the EHO algorithm48.

In EHO, the search population is structured into multiple subgroups known as clans, each led by a dominant female figure referred to as the matriarch47. The matriarch guides the local search within the clan, improving solution refinement. Meanwhile, certain male elephants are periodically removed from their clan and dispersed into the broader solution space—a mechanism referred to as the separating operator. This action promotes diversity and ensures continued global exploration. Thus, while each clan performs an intensive local search under its matriarch, the dispersed males contribute to expanding the search globally. The EHO algorithm typically progresses through two core phases: (1) the clan updating phase, where intra-clan members adjust their positions based on the matriarch’s guidance, and (2) the separating phase, where selected males explore new areas of the solution space. This dual-phase process enables EHO to maintain a robust search dynamic across both local and global scales.

Conclusion

This study introduced a hybrid control framework for trajectory tracking of a TOMR, combining an ISMC with Saguno-type FLC, tuned by a newly MEHO algorithm. The work began by deriving both the kinematic and dynamic models of the robot. An ISMC was then designed to govern the robot’s motion, with the sliding surface shaped by Sugeno FLC and its gain parameters optimized through a newly developed MEHO approach. Three modified equations were introduced to enhance MEHO’s convergence, exploration, and exploitation capabilities. Simulation studies were performed in MATLAB R2022b using a triangular and C- shape trajectories scenario. The results showed that the proposed controller reduced X and Y tracking errors to below 0.005 m, limited the orientation error to 0.0014 rad, achieved a settling time of less than 2.7 s, and produced up to 50% smoother torque outputs compared to conventional methods which confirms the system’s improved accuracy, smoothness, and control robustness.

For future work, the proposed hybrid control method can be extended to real-time experimental validation on a physical omnidirectional mobile robot platform, taking into account sensor noise, actuation delays, and disturbances from unstructured environments. Furthermore, the modified MEHO algorithm can be generalized and applied to multi-agent cooperative control problems, or further hybridized with deep reinforcement learning techniques for autonomous path planning in dynamic environments. Enhancing the adaptive capabilities of the fuzzy logic layer and reducing computational load for embedded system deployment are also recommended to improve real-world implementation readiness.

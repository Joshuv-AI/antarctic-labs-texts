Advancing Cardiovascular Disease Prediction Machine Learning Models With Psychological Factors
Authors: Jacob Krive, Daniel Chertok
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Corresponding Author

Machine learning (ML) and artificial intelligence (AI) shift the paradigm of health information technology (HIT) application in cardiovascular care from the basic rules-based analyses of available data and management of the workflows in electronic medical records to augmentation of the clinical tasks performed by clinicians.1 In particular, AI can assist with diagnostic tasks and risk assessment, using predictive algorithms relying on wide access to relevant data.2 AI manages multimodality well, with its ability to combine analyses of the electronic medical record, imaging, genetics, and other data.3 It can also bring together disparate elements of information about patients and their medical conditions when it comes to managing complexity of multispecialty care such as identifying cardiotoxicity risk among patients whose source of cardiovascular disease (CVD) may have come from a different medical specialty and treatment for a non-CVD condition.4 This productive exploitation of the ML ability to access, integrate, and apply multiple data types sourced by several HIT systems is behind innovations introduced by the study of CVD prediction with ML using a prospective UK Biobank, where addition of the psychological factors served as a powerful enhancer of the existing and known relationship between diagnosed mental health and CVD.5

The authors set out on the path to develop an improved CVD by augmenting the commonly used set of clinical variables with an additional set of features incorporating mental health assessment indicators. This novel approach, coupled with the application of an ensemble model, allows the authors to claim a significantly improved model performance based on a suite of standard metrics, including accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, and receiver operating characteristic area under the curve (AUC). The data set for this study was extracted from the UK Biobank database and contained 375,145 individual subjects. After appropriate data cleaning, 90% of the data set was allocated to training the models, and 10% was set aside for testing.

Analysis of the methods

The study proceeds to employ 5 foundation models: decision trees, random forests, XGBoost, support vector machines, and deep neural networks that feed into a majority voting strategy that selects the final prediction based on the majority of the votes. The algorithm was implemented in Python using standard libraries (pandas, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, Matplotlib, and seaborn) with computations performed on the Phoenix High Performance Computing service utilizing NVidia GPUs.

The authors observed noticeable correlation between risk factors related to mental health which they designated as high. One might remark, however, that the threshold for high Pearson correlation is often set at 0.6 which would render the correlations in this study as moderate. After training the model using the previously constructed ensemble model on clinical and demographic factors, the study proceeds to enhance the data set with psychological factors. This leads to a significant improvement in model performance, with AUC increasing from 0.73 to 0.85. It should be observed that, based on the AUC metric alone, the ensemble model did not perform significantly better than XGBoost in either case. In the absence of confidence intervals for model accuracy metrics, it is hard to conclude whether the stated difference between the XGBoost and ensemble models is systematic or idiosyncratic.

One of the surprising findings of the study is substantially inferior performance of the logistic regression (LR) model compared to the ensemble model. There are a few possible explanations to this LR performance outcome. First, the use of correlated features when constructing an LR model can significantly degrade its performance. Second, in the absence of a variable selection process, of which the authors make no mention, LR may not perform to its full potential. Third, it is possible that the inclusion of interaction terms could improve LR performance as well.

Clinical applications and future research pathways

The use of this expanded CVD risk assessment model with included psychological factors at a comprehensive health care organization could lead to the development of preventive interventions implemented as defined workflows aimed at reducing the likelihood of such outcome. The study could further benefit from incorporating additional analysis of risk stratified by social determinants of health. Such analysis is becoming standard in works aimed at predicting and preventing negative patient outcomes among representatives of the historically disadvantaged groups, in terms of their access to care and health outcomes influenced by social variables.

The aforementioned AI ability to integrate multimodal data makes it a reality to combine multiple factors collected from several data sources into a single comprehensive model that achieves reliable outcomes, leading to early identification of patients who must be referred to medical and social services professionals for follow-up care. One challenge for the development of such model in real community care settings would be dealing with the unpredictability of the data extracted from these multiple sources, with challenges ranging from availability of the data to its quality, quantity, and variety (varied documentation practices, data and clinical definitions, among other factors). This deployment in community care settings and dealing with difficulties of the real-world data would be a grand challenge to overcome on the pathway toward translation of theory into practice, despite the fact that the model was properly tested with real, but single-sourced, patient data.

Conclusions

The study makes a significant contribution to the growing body of predictive models aimed at identifying ambulatory patients at risk of a significant negative outcome. The relationship between psychological factors and CVD has been clinically proven,6 yet no ML models for CVD which include mental health factors have been infused with psychological variables with this degree of accuracy and model training success, thus catching up HIT innovation in predictive modeling to clinical discoveries. The model presented in this latest study achieves the goal with many positive outcomes, from the data science point of view.

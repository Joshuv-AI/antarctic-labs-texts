IoT assisted fuzzy inference systems for intelligent 3D art design in movie animation scene design
Authors: Zhiyuan Shao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAnimation scene design is assisted by intelligent computing programs and Internet of Things (IoT) services with technological advancements. Animation scene creation, modeling, and rendering require extensive computational and conditional resources. To improve the design selection and 3D art modeling, this article proposes an amalgamated design model using IoT resource exploitation and fuzzy interference computation. The proposed method involves understanding the animation scene for which IoT-aided designs are generated, along with a corresponding timeline. The fuzzy interference process interprets the generated design in relation to the Scene that best suits the scenario. Based on a suitable design, the scenario is verified with the animation sequence between different timelines. The maximum Likelihood design is selected and updated in the IoT platform to achieve the best scene match. This scene matching is referenced for Further art design recommendations from the IoT platform. The proposed IoT-assisted Fuzzy inference system achieves approximately an 18% improvement in matching rate, a 22% enhancement in precision level, a 20% increase in design recommendation accuracy, and a 12% reduction in design error rate. These improvements reflect the system’s significant performance gains over traditional manual or heuristic methods, demonstrating the effectiveness in improving scene matching, modeling precision, recommendation accuracy, and reducing errors in animated 3D scene generation. The inference from the last known best design is used to Further improve 3D art modeling with the IoT reference and maximum matching criteria. This enhances the precision of 3D art selection and modeling for animated scenes, thereby improving the overall quality of the animation.Keywords: 3D art, Animation scene design, Fuzzy inference system, IoT, Matching scoreSubject terms: Scientific data, Software

Related works

Cognitive and educational 3D virtual environments

Çınar et al.19 developed a method to impact design tasks on cognitive load in 3D multi-user virtual environments. The approach investigates the mental load of designing learning activities on OpenSimulator with 16 students over an 8-week period. The prepared materials include creating learning scenarios and 3D models, which are highly challenging tasks. The prepared material also includes animation and designing interactive menus as less demanding tasks. Küçük-Avci et al.20 investigated Problem-Based Learning in a 3D virtual environment compared to face-to-face instruction. The methodology involves comparing Problem-Based Learning within a 3D virtual environment with traditional face-to-face Problem-Based Learning and teaching methods. It affects 79 students through tests, rubrics, and measurements of learning and skills. The method demonstrates that the 3D virtual environment improves learning and spatial skills more than other methods.

Zhu et al.21 suggested using watercolor art in 3D animation design. The approach defines watercolor art and its style characteristics. The approach investigates the expression of watercolor art in a computer 3D animation design system. The approach utilizes watercolor art to create artistic, VR, and spatial scenes for 3D animation.

AI-based animation design

Using artificial intelligence methods, Bo et al.22 developed animation design in digital media art. The approach applies genetic and multi-objective evolutionary algorithms for 3D animation group modeling design. The approach also highlights the role of digital media technology in enhancing the quality of animation design. The method supports the development of China’s animation industry. Tang et al.23 developed digital media arts in the context of animation filmmaking. The process uses the DTW algorithm with PCA to enhance data associated with animation motion. The method is based on a multi-scaling approach and the application of motion capture technology to develop a 3D motion database. The technique reduces joint error in character animations from 10.012 degrees to 4.281 degrees, improving accuracy.

Tous et al.24 proposed Pictonaut for cartoonization using Generative Adversarial Networks to movie footage. The framework generates shots that are editable with 3D software and appear to be 2D animations. The described approach combines 3D Human pose estimation with Generative Adversarial Networks for processing video frames and generating animations. It captures 3D poses and applies them to models, which are further used for cartooning backgrounds. Shi et al.25 developed reinforcement learning for 3D animation optimization. The model proposes a technique that utilizes reinforcement learning to enhance user quality of experience in 3D animation videos by addressing network bandwidth issues. The proposed approach trains an Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic model on datasets of 3D animation videos. The A3C-Reinforcement Learning algorithm significantly improves user quality of experience over current methods.

Yuan et al.26 developed 3D Human animation using machine learning. It applies machine learning-based methods with millimeter-wave data. The technique is on 3D Human animation and reconstruction. The approach undertakes 3D Human model analysis, identifying potential actions using neural network criteria. A recognition rate of 97.26% was achieved, with high performance in virtual interaction. Zhang et al.27 developed animation scene design driven by generative models. The approach merges generative modeling with machine vision to enhance Computer-Aided Design (CAD) and animation scene rendering. The approach simplifies the model and reduces the data volume, making the process more efficient. The approach helps segment and feature fusion with high accuracy and efficiency.

Song et al.28 created an automatic 3D scene animation with a knowledge graph. The method utilizes a dynamic knowledge graph for enhanced 3D animation, incorporating additional context information from scenes. The approach facilitates the generation of animation by understanding time and space, making interactions easier. The benchmark results demonstrated that the real-time 3D animation created using this method enhances the interaction between humans and animals.

Sun et al.29 proposed a method for 3D-aided art design using PSO. The approach is based on an enhanced PSO combined with deep learning technology for extracting and recognizing the artwork’s color outline. Computer-aided design uses the recognition of color features for 3D reconstruction. The approach enhances efficiency and precision in artistic image processing.

IoT applications in 3D Art

Gai et al.30 developed a digital art creation algorithm using the Internet of Things (IoT). The approach utilizes the IoT to enhance data processing in digital art. It applies Internet of Things algorithms to detect, classify, and recognize images with robust results across a wide range of images. It enhances the interaction between designers and computers, facilitating the creation of innovative designs. Using IoT technology, Zhao et al.31 developed an artwork display system. The model utilizes the Spring framework, combined with Internet of Things technology, to design an artwork display system scheme. The ultra-reliable low-latency communication protocol is applied in the design of the database and controller, which optimizes the system’s performance. The method has performed much more in real-time than traditional cloud computing systems.

By applying IoT, Zhang et al.32 reported the design and diagnosis of 3D patterns. The methodology reports the evolution in 3D pattern design through the intervention of computer technology since 1990. The approach is based on betterment achieved in the pattern design of textiles and murals using computers. The methodology presents a solution to the necessity of multi-user systems, as the design becomes complex and workers are dispersed. Olberding et al.33 designed dynamic 3D symbols for virtuality based on the geovisualisation. Virtual reality is used in the sense that viewing 3D maps is made more engaging than desktop applications. The method combines cartography and video game techniques to create interactive symbols within a virtual environment. The developed symbols can be further manipulated and customized by the user, thereby improving spatial comprehension.

3D modeling and segmentation techniques

Sun et al.34 developed a semi-supervised 3D shape segmentation method. The method elaborates on using a few labeled and many unlabeled 3D shapes for learning shape segmentation. Consistency loss is applied to different levels of a 3D shape to make accurate predictions. It augments the labeled dataset with structural variations to improve performance in training and segmentation. Wu et al.35 proposed a technique for learning deformable 3D objects through videos. Learning textured 3D deformable object models from monocular video is performed without the need for explicit keypoint-level correspondences, a prior shape template, or 2D-to-3D annotations. The method factors every video frame’s 3D shape, pose, and texture. The technique is demonstrated for training a consistent, viewable 3D model of various objects from videos.

Li et al.36 developed a discriminative feature-based 3D scene segmentation method. The model recommends implementing the Attention Residual Network for better 3D point cloud segmentation. Within the architecture, an Attention Residual module houses an attention block to aid the handling of large datasets. The developed method utilizes specialized loss functions to mitigate class imbalances, resulting in improved segmentation.

Zhang et al.37 developed a method to optimize virtual scene layout. Based on all the scene view information, this method presents an optimization approach for a 3D scene layout using an interactive genetic algorithm. It integrates visual perception with optimization factors to improve virtual environment layouts. The results demonstrate that the proposed method effectively enhances 3D building layouts, offering practical benefits in architecture, animation, and game design.

The proposed study presents a new Fuzzy Inference System for 3D Design Selection (FIS-3D) that addresses issues with current approaches to creating 3D animation scenes by utilizing fuzzy logic to enhance design-scene matching, timeline consistency, and error reduction. FIS-3D is different from other methods like Pictonaut (which doesn’t have adaptive scene relevance), A3C-RL (which uses a lot of computing power), and IGA-SLO (which doesn’t have fuzzy reasoning) because it uses fuzzification, rule-based inference, and defuzzification to suggest the best designs with the highest matching score. We test the method using sample animation models and examine the matching rate, design error, inference rate, and temporal alignment to assess its effectiveness. This method performs better by adjusting the design choices in response to scene updates and scoring systems. It surpasses other methods in terms of accuracy, flexibility, and scene continuity.

This mechanism adjusts the membership function parameters and rule weights. Ongoing mapping of sensor data to input language variables considers factors such as scene complexity, user interaction vectors, ambient light, and other relevant variables. The fuzzy inference engine adjusts itself in real-time as the animation scenario changes, enabling fluid transitions between different lighting conditions, object behaviors, and user-driven adjustments to emotional tone and camera viewpoint. Light intensity, motion vectors, and action mapping are some of the features that the FIS-3D framework utilizes to prioritize in its hierarchical fuzzy decision structure, which enables it to manage multiple concurrent scene parameters. The scene information is used to construct an entropy-normalized relevance index, which is then used to determine the weights assigned to each parameter in a weighted fuzzy rule base evaluation. By combining these inputs with the centroid defuzzification approach, the fuzzy inference system generates a ranked set of outputs that control the rendering priorities. By giving more weight to visually important parts, this design enables the system to handle trade-offs, such as enhancing motion clarity during fast-action scenes in various lighting conditions.

5 conclusion

The role of intelligent computing through the IoT paradigm in generating animation scenes using 3D designs is reliable and less resource-intensive. This article leverages the advantages of the same to enhance the optimality of design improvement through precise selection. The 3D design selection process utilizes a score-based Fuzzy inference system that employs a matching process. The defined timeline for animation scene generation relies on multiple 3D art designs to enhance the matching sequences. In this matching process, the inference for low and high membership extractions is used to update the score at each inference level. Thus, the design with the maximum score is substituted to complete the timeline, achieving high scene Likelihood. The generated animation scene is verified with the designs previously stored in the IoT platform. The inference process uses the optimal Scene for Further sequences that best match the Further timelines. The inference rate varies until the recommended 3D design reduces maximum errors, thus completing the timeline sequence for the animation. The proposed method’s evaluation is pursued using matching rate, error, recommendation, and precision parameters for different views of the 3D design selected.

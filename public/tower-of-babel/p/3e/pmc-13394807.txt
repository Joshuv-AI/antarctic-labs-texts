Prospective evaluation of the Simple Fetal Medicine Foundation's first‐trimester prediction model for preeclampsia in twin pregnancies
Authors: Cuilian Liu, Jiaxin Li, Yang Chen, Tianchen Wu, Qingnan Meng, Kuo Zhang, Yuan Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThere is no widely applicable prediction model for preeclampsia (PE) in twins, the existing models lack strict external validation. Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the predictive performance of the Simple Fetal Medicine Foundation model (i.e., Simple FMF model, only including the maternal risk factors and mean arterial pressure [MAP]) for the prediction of PE at 11–13+ 6 weeks' gestation in twin pregnancies.Material and MethodsThis was a prospective, non‐intervention study in 464 twin pregnancies (including dichorionic twins and monochorionic twins) at 11–13+ 6 weeks' gestation in a tertiary center between September 1, 2021 and October 31, 2024. Maternal characteristics, medical, obstetric, and drug history were recorded. MAP was measured according to regular protocol. The FMF's algorithm is freely available online, which was used for the calculation of patient‐specific risk of PE in each participant. The performance of screening for PE occurring at different gestational weeks by the Simple FMF model was evaluated with the adjustment of aspirin use. The efficiency of the screening strategy with maternal factors and MAP was evaluated using the receiver operating characteristic curve, and the detection performance for PE at a fixed false‐positive rate was also analyzed.ResultsAmong the 464 women included, 24.14% (112/464) developed PE, among which 1.29% (6/464) occurred before 30 weeks, 2.80% (13/464) occurred before 32 weeks, 6.47% (30/464) occurred before 34 weeks, 19.18% (89/464) occurred before 37 weeks, 4.96% (23/464) (including 21 cases of dichorionic diamniotic twins and 2 cases of monochorionic diamniotic twins) occurred at 37 weeks or later. The Simple FMF model had area under the receiver operating characteristic curves of 0.911 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.830–0.992), 0.799 (95% CI: 0.668–0.930), 0.797 (95% CI: 0.712–0.882), and 0.767 (95% CI: 0.706–0.828) for the screening of PE occurring before 30, 32, 34, and 37 weeks, respectively.ConclusionsThis study has demonstrated that the Simple FMF model was effective in predicting PE in twin pregnancies, especially for cases of PE occurring before 30, 32, 34, and 37 weeks. This approach only uses maternal characteristics, medical history, and MAP, making it suitable for implementation in resource‐limited settings.Keywords: Fetal Medicine Foundation, first trimester, PE, prediction model, preeclampsia, twin pregnancies

INTRODUCTION

Preeclampsia (PE), as a multifactorial, multi‐mechanism and multi‐pathway pregnancy disorder characterized by insufficient remodeling of the uterine spiral arterioles and the release of placental factors into the maternal circulation, is a major cause of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality. 1 Twin pregnancies are a significant risk factor for PE, the incidence of PE in twins is 10%–20%, which is two‐ to fivefold higher than that of singleton pregnancies, and the disease is often more severe and presents at an earlier gestation. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 In recent years, with the popularization of assisted reproductive technology (ART), the incidence of twin pregnancy has increased by 70%. 6 Therefore, predicting and preventing PE in twin pregnancies becomes increasingly important and urgent.

Significant progress has been made in the prediction of PE in singleton pregnancies, especially the model developed by the Fetal Medicine Foundation (FMF) that combines maternal risk factors and/or mean artery pressure (MAP), uterine artery pulsatility index (UtA‐PI), placental growth factor (PLGF), and pregnancy‐related plasma protein A (PAPP‐A) (later referred to as “FMF model”) has been recognized by the International Federation of Obstetrics and Gynecology and related guidelines. 7 , 8 , 9 The model that only includes the maternal factors is called the FMF prior risk model, the model that includes the maternal risk factors and MAP is called the Simple FMF model, and the model that includes three or four indicators at the same time is called the FMF triple or quadruple test model, respectively. Previous studies have suggested that this model is also applicable for predicting PE in twin pregnancies, and in the first trimester, the Simple FMF model performs well in predicting PE occurring before 32 weeks with an area under the receiver operating characteristic (AUROC) curve of 0.83, 10 and MAP in twins was not significantly different from that in singletons; moreover, MAP was unrelated to chorionicity. 11 However, the Simple FMF model has not been externally validated in twin pregnancies, and at present, there is no widely applicable prediction model for PE in twins, the existing models lack strict external validation, so whether they can be applied to other populations is unknown. 12 The objective of the study was to evaluate the predictive performance of the Simple FMF model for the prediction of PE at 11–13+ 6 weeks' gestation in twin pregnancies.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

This was a prospective single‐center non‐interventional cohort study in twin pregnancies at 11+0–13+ 6 weeks' gestation (fetal crown‐to‐rump measurement of 45–84 mm) in women attending for routine pregnancy care between September 1, 2021 and October 31, 2024 at Peking University Third Hospital, China. Gestational age was determined by the measurement of fetal crown‐rump length of the larger fetus. 13 Chorionicity was determined by examining the junction between the intertwin membranes and the placenta. 14 Inclusion criteria for the study were as follows: age >18 years; twin pregnancy; live fetus at the 11+ weeks' gestation; All women who were unable to provide a full informed consent due to any factor such as learning difficulties or significant mental illness were excluded from the study. We further excluded pregnancies that ended in termination, miscarriage, or fetal death at less than 24 weeks' gestation, and pregnancies with the presence of fetal malformations. Additionally, we excluded cases of single intrauterine fetal death and those who underwent intrauterine fetal interventions, including fetal reduction and laser therapy.

The algorithm of the FMF model was applied to calculate online the specific risk value of PE occurrence. 15 According to the requirements of the model algorithm, the date of birth, conception mode (spontaneous or assisted conception requiring in vitro fertilization or the use of ovulation drugs), twin chorionicity, smoking during pregnancy, chronic hypertension, pre‐existing diabetes mellitus, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)/antiphospholipid syndrome (APS), parity (parous or nulliparous if no previous pregnancies at ≥24 weeks), previous pregnancy with PE, family history of PE in the mother of the patient and drug history were recorded and maternal weight, height, and blood pressure were measured. Blood pressure was measured by validated automated devices (HBP‐9030 Omron, Dalian, China). Rest quietly for at least 5 min before measurement, sit correctly, with the arms at the level of their heart, and do not move or talk during the measurement. Blood pressure was initially measured in both arms, and the arm with the higher reading was chosen as the study arm for all subsequent measurements. If the systolic blood pressure was higher than 130 mmHg or the diastolic blood pressure was higher than 80 mmHg on the selected study arm, a second measurement was performed after 5 min of rest, and the lower of the two readings was used for calculation of MAP. This step was intended to reduce spurious elevations related to transient anxiety or white‐coat effect. MAP is calculated as diastolic plus 1/3 pulse pressure (which is systolic‐diastolic). The chorionicity and fetal crown‐rump length were measured in the first trimester by qualified and experienced sonographers using advanced ultrasound equipment (EPIQ 7, Philips, Washington, America). All pregnant women were followed up until delivery. Consistency in data collection was maintained throughout the study period. The calculation of the specific risk value for the occurrence of PE was assigned to an independent investigator.

Data on pregnancy outcomes were collected from the hospital's electronic medical record information system. PE was defined as new‐onset hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg on at least two occasions 4 h apart at or after 20 weeks' gestation) accompanied by proteinuria, maternal vital organ dysfunction, or placenta‐fetal impairment, in accordance with the clinical practice guideline in China (2020), 16 and the original records of all women with pre‐existing or pregnancy associated hypertension were reviewed to determine the accuracy of PE diagnosis. In this study, all pregnant women with hypertension underwent 24‐h urine protein quantitative examination. Therefore, the diagnosis of proteinuria was based on the criterion that 24‐h urine protein ≥0.3 g. PE was divided into <30, <32, <34, <37, ≥37 weeks PE and all PE according to diagnosis time.

Sample size

Here, the sensitivity was set at 0.8, the allowable error was set at 0.1, and the significance level α was set at 0.05. Combined with the incidence of PE in this research center and previous literature reports, the incidence of PE was set at 20%, 7 , 8 and the expected loss of follow‐up rate was set at 5%, the sample size was estimated to be 324 cases.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (25th, 75th percentile). Categorical variables are presented as counts (%). For two‐group comparisons, two‐sample t‐test and Mann–Whitney U‐test were used for continuous variables, and the Pearson χ 2 test or Fisher's exact probability test were used for nominal variables. Predictive performance of different screening strategies were assessed by estimating the AUROCs, with corresponding 95% CIs and the detection rate at fixed false‐positive rates of 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20%. Pairwise comparisons of the AUROCs between the two correlated ROC curves derived from the same set of participants (i.e., analyses with versus without aspirin and with versus without monochorionic monoamniotic [MCMA]) were performed using the method of DeLong et al. An AUROC between 0.7 and 0.9 suggested good prediction efficiency, while AUROC >0.9 indicated high accuracy. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS version 24 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA) and R software version 4.3.2 (R Core Team, 2023).

RESULTS

Between September 1, 2021 and October 31, 2024, 530 twin pregnancies provided written informed consent and were enrolled in the cohort. Fifty‐seven pregnancies were excluded from analyses according to the inclusion and exclusion criteria; additionally, nine pregnancies were lost to follow‐up. DISCUSSION

This prospective cohort study evaluated the Simple FMF model for PE screening in twin pregnancies. The model demonstrated good predictive performance, especially for early‐onset PE, and was not affected by low‐dose aspirin use (50–100 mg daily). With only maternal factors and MAP required, this model is clinically feasible for twin pregnancy risk assessment. Key findings, the impact of low‐dose aspirin, and the strengths and limitations of this study are discussed below.

The FMF triple test model which combines prior risk (determined by maternal characteristics and risk factors), MAP, UtA‐PI, and PLGF has been widely validated for predicting PE in singleton pregnancies. 6 , 7 , 8 In twin pregnancies, the FMF triple test model achieved AUROCs of 0.929, 0.812, and 0.780 for PE occurring before 32, 37, and 42 weeks, respectively. 10 However, the calibration degree of the FMF triple test model was poor in the mixed population of single and twin pregnancies, which significantly overestimated the risk of PE occurrence in twin pregnancies and reduced its clinical applicability. 10 It may be related to the difference of UTPI and PIGF measured in twin pregnancies and singleton pregnancies. 11 , 20 , 21 The predictive performance of the FMF prior risk model and the Simple FMF model was also analyzed. 10 The results show that the FMF prior risk model had poor predictive performance for PE occurring before 32, 37, and 42 weeks, with AUROCs of 0.68, 0.70 and 0.69 respectively, while the Simple FMF model performed well, with AUROCs of 0.83, 0.77 and 0.75 respectively. 10 A prior study evaluated the FMF prior risk model in twin pregnancies, revealing that both its discrimination and calibration were poor. 18

This study also evaluated the FMF prior risk model, revealing that the FMF prior risk model had poor predictive performance for PE, and the results were consistent with previous studies. 10 , 18 , 19 While the Simple FMF model performed well for PE occurring before 32, 34, 37 and 42 weeks, with AUROCs of 0.799, 0.797, 0.767 and 0.777 respectively, and the results were comparable to those of previous study. 10 , 19 And the Simple FMF model performed better for PE occurring before 30 weeks, with an AUROC of 0.911, and achieved a detection rate of 83.3% at 10% fixed FPR.

Furthermore, a significant proportion of twin pregnancies originate from the Reproductive Center of Peking University Third Hospital, which is the largest assisted reproductive center in China, so the proportion of nulliparous women and those conceived through ART in this study was notably higher compared to previous studies. Additionally, the proportion of pregnant women with a history of hypertension, diabetes, APS, or SLE was marginally higher than that reported in prior studies. 12 , 22 Consequently, the incidence of PE in this study was significantly elevated relative to prior reports, as detailed in Table 6, this finding also underscores the efficacy of the Simple FMF model in predicting PE among pregnant women with diverse baseline characteristics.

Compared with previous studies that did not use the FMF algorithm, the Simple FMF model incorporated fewer ultrasound and biochemical indicators, 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 as detailed in Table 7. However, variations in testing equipment, reagent batches, measurement temperature, and examiner operational deviations can produce inaccurate results in these indicators, which may affect the prediction performance of the model in the external validation. Moreover, most published PE prediction models for twins lack external validation, 23 , 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 which is a critical step before the model is applied in clinical practice; therefore, the extrapolation ability needs to be further confirmed. This study is an external validation of the Simple FMF model for predicting PE in twins, and it is different from previous studies in terms of sample size, incorporated indicators, and incidence of PE, but the prediction efficiency for PE is comparable to that of most studies. 23 , 24 , 25 In addition, at fixed FPR of 10% and 20%, the detection rates for PE occurring before 34, 37, and 42 weeks are marginally higher than those reported in prior studies. 23 , 24 , 25 Despite some models showing higher prediction efficiency, they suffer from small sample sizes and lack internal and external validation, which may lead to unstable results, 26 , 27 as detailed in Table 7.

Guidelines for aspirin prophylaxis against PE differ globally, with no consensus on target population, dose, or timing. China's 2020 guideline advises 50–150 mg daily from 12 to 16 weeks for high‐risk singletons, 16 yet efficacy in twins remains unsettled, 28 , 29 consequently, such women in our cohort did not routinely receive aspirin. The national APPEC trial (taking 100 mg aspirin daily 100 mg from 12–20 to 34 weeks) likewise showed no reduction in PE among high‐risk singletons. 30 Chinese data on aspirin for the prevention of PE in high‐risk twins are limited and inconsistent. 28 , 29 , 30 The high‐risk pregnancies were administered 50–100 mg of aspirin daily to prevent PE, 30 , 31 , 32 , 33 suggesting higher doses might be required, 34 multicenter and high‐quality trials are needed.

Our study's strengths include a prospective cohort design, sufficient sample size, and being one of the few to evaluate the FMF model in twin pregnancies. Since PE in twins is often earlier and more severe, 5 validating a simple, low‐cost prediction tool is especially valuable in resource‐limited settings. 35 Most of the published studies did not analyze the use of aspirin, 10 , 18 , 19 , 24 , 25 some studies excluded pregnant women who took aspirin orally, 23 , 26 , 27 however, this study assessed aspirin use and found the model performed well after excluding pregnancies who took aspirin.

Limitations include recruitment from a single center, so external validation is needed. Also, 74.4% of pregnancies used ART and 24.1% developed PE rates higher than typical. These factors reflect our referral patterns and may limit applicability to populations with lower ART use and PE rates. Another limitation is that MAP was measured by single‐arm single reading, differing from the FMF‐recommended protocol. 36 This does not cause systematic bias or affect overall FMF model performance, but may introduce random error and limit direct inter‐study comparability. 36 , 37 , 38 Direct numerical comparisons of predictive metrics with full FMF studies should be cautious; however, the MAP‐PE association remains broadly comparable. These are supported by our prior study and others. 37 , 38

CONCLUSION

This study has demonstrated that the Simple FMF model is effective in predicting PE in the twin population, especially for cases of PE occurring before 30, 32, 34, and 37. This approach only uses maternal characteristics, medical history, and MAP, making it suitable for implementation in resource‐limited settings. The Simple FMF model exhibited predictive performance for twin PE comparable to that reported in most previous relevant studies. 10 , 19 , 23 , 24 , 25 Future work will apply the multicenter and larger datasets of twins to further validate our conclusions.

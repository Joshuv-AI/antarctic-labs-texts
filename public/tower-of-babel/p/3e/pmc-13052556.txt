Quality appraisal of clinical guidelines for newborns in China: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Li Jing, Zengzhen Liao, Luqin Liao, Xuejun Zhai, Mingfen Tang, Qiong Bin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionNeonatal guidelines are critical for enhancing healthcare quality and reducing neonatal mortality in China. However, the methodological quality of existing guidelines varies considerably, and the credibility and implementability (CI) of their recommendations has not been systematically evaluated. Consequently, key areas for improvement remain unclear. This protocol aims to appraise the methodological quality of Chinese neonatal guidelines and assess the CI of their recommendations using two evaluation frameworks.Methods and analysisThis study will adhere to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses for Protocols statement. We will search multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang Database, the Chinese Science and Technology Periodical Database (VIP) and the Chinese Biomedical Literature Database, from inception to 31 December 2025. The search will use keywords such as ‘neonatal’, ‘clinical practice guidelines’ and ‘China’. The language of publication will be limited to Chinese or English. Two researchers will independently screen title, abstract and full text. Chinese-language clinical practice guidelines focused on neonatal disease management (rather than assessment or diagnosis) will be included. Relevant information (including guideline name, publication year, disease type, leading institution and guideline development group composition) will be extracted using a pre-designed form. Cross-verification will be performed, and discrepancies will be resolved by a third reviewer. Final data will be presented in tables. We will use a narrative synthesis to address two objectives: (1) to evaluate the methodological quality of Chinese neonatal guidelines (especially those published in the past 5 years) using AGREE II (6 domains, 23 items, scored 1–7) and identify critical deficiencies in the guideline development process; and (2) appraise the CI of recommendations for guidelines scoring ≥60% in the domain 3 (‘Rigour of Development’) of AGREE II using the AGREE-REX (Appraisal of Guidelines for Research and Evaluation – Recommendation EXcellence) tool, deriving context-specific, evidence-based optimisation strategies. This review will explore the strengths and limitations of Chinese neonatal clinical practice guidelines. The findings will provide evidence-based direction for future guideline development and recommendation formulation.Ethics and disseminationNo ethical approval is required. Results will be disseminated through open-access, peer-reviewed journal publications and conference presentations. The results will inform efforts to improve the methodological quality of future neonatal guidelines in China and the CI of their recommendations.PROSPERO registration numberThis systematic review protocol was registered on PROSPERO (registration number CRD420251106060).Keywords: Systematic Review, China, Protocols & guidelines

Introduction

According to data from China’s National Bureau of Statistics, the neonatal mortality rate in China was 2.8‰ in 2023,1 reflecting a declining trend but remaining higher than the rates in developed countries. Neonates exhibit distinct physiological immaturity—particularly in respiratory, circulatory and thermoregulatory systems—and unique neurobehavioural traits compared with adults, underscoring the need for tailored clinical practice guidelines. Without timely and standardised management, neonatal conditions can lead to irreversible harm, with adverse physical and psychological consequences potentially extending from childhood into adulthood.2,4 Such outcomes not only affect individual families but also impose a substantial societal burden.5 6

Clinical practice guidelines synthesise evidence systematically and formulate recommendations using scientific methodologies.7,9 They support clinicians in making evidence-based decisions, enhance consistency in diagnosis, treatment and care, and ultimately improve patient outcomes.10

Multiple academic societies and associations in China have developed neonatal clinical practice guidelines addressing common issues such as pain management11 and anaemia in preterm infants.12 These guidelines cover diagnosis,13 treatment12 14 and nursing management,15 providing standardised diagnostic and therapeutic protocols for healthcare institutions at all levels in China. They play a pivotal role in enhancing the quality of neonatal medical care.

However, international studies evaluating the methodological quality of clinical practice guidelines have revealed significant heterogeneity among published guidelines.16 17 Similar concerns have been documented by domestic scholars.18,21

Optimal clinical decisions should integrate the best available evidence, expertise of clinicians, and the values and preferences of patients.22 23 This underscores the necessity for guidelines to thoroughly incorporate the values and preferences ofpatients and families when formulating recommendations.24 However, the systematic incorporation of preferences of patient and family remains limited in most guideline development processes.25 26 Furthermore, existing studies have found divergent recommendations for the same clinical issue across different guidelines.27 This inconsistency can create confusion for clinicians formulating treatment plans.28 These factors significantly compromise the credibility and implementability (CI) of recommendations in such guidelines, including neonatal guidelines. To our knowledge, Chinese scholars have predominantly evaluated the quality of disease-specific guidelines,18,20 with limited attention paid to neonatal clinical practice guidelines—particularly the CI of their recommendations.27 Consequently, the CI of recommendations in Chinese neonatal guidelines remains unclear.

Given the critical importance of neonatal health and the current landscape of Chinese neonatal guidelines, a systematic assessment of their strengths and limitations is imperative to identify areas for improvement and inform future guideline development.

Methods and analysis

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Chinese clinical practice guidelines focusing on the management of neonatal diseases (rather than assessment or diagnosis) will be included. Exclusion criteria are: (1) guidelines published in languages other than Chinese or English; (2) expert consensus statements; (3) translated or adapted versions of guidelines; and (4) guidelines with inaccessible full text.

Search strategy

For a comprehensive search, we will use free-text terms and subject headings to search the following electronic databases: PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, Wanfang Database, the Chinese Science and Technology Periodical Database (VIP) and the Chinese Biomedical Literature Database. The search will be limited to guidelines published in Chinese or English from inception to 31 December 2025. Neonatal-related search terms include: ‘infants’, ‘newborns’, ‘neonatal’, ‘very low birth weight’ and ‘extremely low birth weight’.

Guideline-specific search filters will be adapted from the methodology developed by L’Hotta et al.29 The detailed search strategy is presented in online supplemental table S1. References of included guidelines will be hand-searched to identify potentially eligible guidelines not captured by database retrievals. Given that Chinese guidelines are frequently published as standalone documents as well as distributed through professional associations, systematic searches of association websites will not be conducted.

Literature screening and data extraction

Retrieved citations will be imported into EndNote 20 (Clarivate Analytics - Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA) reference management software for duplicate removal. Subsequently, two investigators will independently screen the remaining records based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, initially by title and abstract. Records that are clearly irrelevant will be excluded. The full text of potentially eligible records will then be retrieved and assessed independently by both reviewers to determine final eligibility. The final selections will be cross-checked by the two reviewers. Any discrepancies arising during the screening or full-text assessment phases will be resolved through adjudication by a third reviewer until consensus is reached. We will document the search and study selection process using a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses flow diagram, alongside a detailed list of excluded studies and their reasons for exclusion.30

Data extraction will be performed using a predefined, standardised Excel form (online supplemental table S2) in Microsoft Excel. The extracted information will include: guideline title, publication year, lead organisation, composition of the guideline development group, details of the references cited to support recommendations (including study title, population ethnicity, age, country and publication year), planned updates and funding/sponsorship details.

Two reviewers will independently perform a pilot extraction on three guidelines. All discrepancies identified during this pilot phase will be discussed by the research team until consensus on data extraction criteria and interpretation is achieved. Subsequently, the remaining guidelines will be extracted independently by the two reviewers. Any disagreements encountered during extraction will be resolved through adjudication by a third reviewer.

For guidelines whose full text cannot be obtained through initial searches, corresponding authors will be contacted via email. If no response is received within 2 weeks of the initial email, a follow-up email will be sent. Guidelines that remain unattainable after this second attempt will be excluded.

The characteristics, key content and specific recommendations of the guidelines included in the analysis will be comprehensively presented in tabular format.

Prior to formal appraisal, all assessors will undergo standardised training on the AGREE II and AGREE-REX (Appraisal of Guidelines for Research and Evaluation – Recommendation EXcellence) instruments. A pilot appraisal of three guidelines will be conducted to calibrate assessment accuracy. Inter-rater reliability will be quantified using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), with formal appraisal proceeding only after achieving satisfactory agreement (ICC >0.75).

Analysis of guideline quality and the CI of recommendations

Following the training and reliability assessment, two authors will independently appraise the quality of the included guidelines using the AGREE II instrument. For this appraisal, they will assign scores ranging from 1 to 7 for each item within the six AGREE II domains.

To derive the AGREE II score for each domain, the scores assigned by the reviewers for all items within a domain will be summed. This sum will then be converted into a percentage representing the proportion of the maximum possible score that is achieved in the domain.

Guidelines deemed high-quality (with a score of ≥60% in the 'Rigour of Development’ domain of AGREE II) will subsequently undergo evaluation of their neonatal recommendations using the AGREE-REX tool. Reviewers will assign scores (1–7) to each recommendation based on the nine items of the tool across three core domains.

This process will assess the overall CI of recommendations within current Chinese neonatal guidelines. To ensure comprehensive evaluation, included guidelines will be categorised according to the International Classification of Diseases, 11th revision. Subgroup analyses will be conducted for guidelines with sufficient numbers and high quality, thus enabling the generation of more granular insights.

Discussion

This protocol describes the first systematic review to concurrently apply the AGREE II31 and AGREE-REX32 instruments to evaluate the methodological quality and recommendation credibility of Chinese neonatal guidelines. These tools are recognised for their high scientific rigour and reliability, lending substantial reference value to our findings.

To achieve a comprehensive and representative sample, our systematic search will encompass four major Chinese databases and three prominent English databases, collectively covering the primary platforms for disseminating Chinese neonatal guidelines. Recognising the critical importance of comprehensiveness, we will implement database-specific, rigorously developed search strategies. These strategies will use intelligent combinations of free-text terms, subject headings (where applicable) and search filters adapted from high-impact systematic reviews. This multifaceted approach is designed to maximise both recall (sensitivity) and precision (specificity) in guideline identification.27 29 31

Standardised data extraction will be performed using a pre-validated Excel template featuring structured fields with predefined coding options, significantly mitigating subjectivity-induced discrepancies among researchers.

Moreover, prior to formal guideline appraisal, all evaluators will undergo standardised training focusing on consensus-driven interpretation of domains, items and scoring criteria for both instruments. A pilot evaluation of three representative guidelines will ensure calibration of assessment accuracy.

We will implement a strict dual-independent review, evaluation and data entry protocol with structured cross-verification of scoring outcomes to guarantee reliability. Disagreement resolution will use consensus discussion followed by third-reviewer arbitration, thereby minimising assessor bias and ensuring robust results.

This robust methodological framework forms the cornerstone of the validity of the study.

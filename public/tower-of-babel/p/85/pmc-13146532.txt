New Zealand rheumatic fever clinicians respond to Leung et al.
Authors: Rachel H Webb, Catherine Jackson, Melissa Kerdemelidis, Philippa Anderson, Christopher Luey, Bridget Farrant
Published: 2026

We read with interest the recent paper by Leung et al. and invite the authors to clarify their methodology and reconsider the generalizability of their findings.

We welcome new approaches to secondary prophylaxis (SP) for rheumatic fever (RF) to improve patient experience and improve health outcomes. However, despite promising pharmacokinetics [1], there is not yet effectiveness data to demonstrate superiority or non-inferiority of subcutaneous injections of benzathine penicillin G (SCIP) compared with intramuscular (IM) benzathine penicillin for the prevention of RF recurrences or rheumatic heart disease (RHD) progression.

There is currently no evidence-based clinical practice recommendation regarding acceptable SCIP delivery. Leung et al. define acceptable adherence as receiving ≥80% of prescribed doses; however, this definition relates to completeness of SP given 4-weekly by the IM route [2,3].

They state that 4 × SCIP infusions (at 10-week intervals) annually constitutes the recommended coverage. This could leave a person without protection for 12 weeks per year. Many New Zealand (NZ) RF clinicians would be concerned about recurrence risk with this gap. In NZ, adherence to 4-weekly IM benzathine penicillin via existing programs is much higher than 80% and recurrences are infrequent [4,5].

Medication costs in Table 1 are based on a scenario of 4 × SCIP infusions per year and a 10-year-old child receiving 6 × 900 mg vials of Bicillin-LA per infusion. The majority of persons with ARF/RHD in NZ weigh >50 kg [6,7] and would require seven or more vials of Bicillin-LA per SCIP infusion and five infusions per year to ensure continuous coverage. Medication costs for SCIP are, therefore, at least three times higher than IM Bicillin-LA. Global shortages create further risk of cost escalation [8,9].

The authors have not considered clinician time required for prescribing. In NZ, IM benzathine penicillin is usually administered under nursing standing orders. The subcutaneous route is not approved by Bicillin-LA’s manufacturer nor by MedSafe, NZ’s pharmaceutical regulator. Although off-label prescribing is permitted in NZ under Section 25 of the Medicines Act, SCIP doses must be prescribed by an authorized prescriber with appropriate informed consent.

Productivity loss estimates are inconsistent with experience in Counties Manukau, where NZ’s largest SP service caters to nearly 800 people. IM Injections are delivered by community nurses in homes, schools, and community clinics, according to patient preference. This free service runs during weekends and evenings to minimize disruption to school, employment, and family life. Support to reduce pain includes adjunctive lignocaine and the Buzzy device [10]. The quoted 9 hours of missed school per IM injection for every child is not aligned with local clinical experience.

Irrespective of route of Bicillin-LA administration, increased resources to support culturally and developmentally responsive models of care and equitable health outcomes are urgently needed for all persons living with RF/RHD in Aotearoa, NZ and beyond.

Nonclinical and Clinical Assessments of an Optimized Tablet Formulation of the Novel HER2-Selective Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor Zongertinib: Focus on Relative Bioavailability and Impact of Variation of Gastric pH
Authors: Stefan Wölke, Behbood Sadrolhefazi, Habib Esmaeili, Sven Wind, Simone M. Blattner, Guanfa Gan, Dongmei Qiang, Andrea Schirmer, Fabian Müller
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Zongertinib (BI 1810631), a novel orally administered HER2-specific tyrosine kinase inhibitor, has recently received accelerated approval in the United States and China for patients with advanced, previously treated HER2 mutant NSCLC. This multifaceted analysis assessed the influence of gastric pH variation on the pharmacokinetics of conventional and spray-dried dispersion (SDD) formulations of zongertinib. The influence of gastric pH on zongertinib pharmacokinetics was evaluated in male beagles. Increased gastric pH substantially reduced absorption of zongertinib as a Natrosol suspension. At low gastric pH, the SDD formulation provided improved exposure and reduced variability compared with a conventional formulation. We then evaluated the in vitro release and bioaccessibility of conventional and SDD tablet formulations of zongertinib with the tiny-TIM gastrointestinal system. Bioaccessibility was similar between the two formulations under simulated fasted conditions; however, under simulated fasted conditions with a proton pump inhibitor, the conventional formulation showed reduced bioaccessibility versus SDD. In a randomized, open-label crossover study, fasted healthy human male participants received 30 mg zongertinib as a conventional tablet formulation, an SDD tablet formulation, and as an SDD tablet formulation after multiple doses of rabeprazole 40 mg. The primary pharmacokinetic end points were area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time 0 to the last quantifiable point (AUC0‑tz) and maximum plasma concentration (C max) of zongertinib. C max and AUC0‑tz were ∼39% and ∼35% higher, respectively, with the SDD versus the conventional formulation. Geometric mean C max and AUC0‑tz were similar after treatment with zongertinib SDD with and without rabeprazole. Zongertinib SDD formulation showed good bioavailability irrespective of pH, supporting further clinical development.

Introduction

Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) (also called ERBB2) is a common oncogene in many cancer types. Activating mutations in HER2, which most commonly occur in the tyrosine kinase domain, are responsible for 2–4% of non small cell lung cancers (NSCLC) and are associated with poor prognosis.

Currently, the first-line standard of care for most patients with HER2 mutant NSCLC is platinum-doublet chemotherapy with or without immunotherapy; no targeted therapy is approved as a first-line treatment option. The antibody–drug conjugate trastuzumab deruxtecan has been granted accelerated approval by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as a second-line option in patients with HER2 mutant NSCLC. , However, more effective and tolerable HER2-directed therapies represent an unmet need in HER2 mutant NSCLC.

Zongertinib is an irreversible tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) that selectively inhibits HER2 while sparing EGFR, thereby limiting associated toxicities. , Zongertinib was recently granted accelerated approval in the USA for adults with unresectable or metastatic NSCLC whose tumors have HER2 tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations and who have received prior systemic therapy; zongertinib was also recently granted approval in China for previously treated HER2 mutant NSCLC. , Approval was based on data from the Phase Ia/Ib Beamion LUNG-1 trial (NCT04886804). , For patients who were treated with the recommended zongertinib dose of 120 mg once daily (QD) in Phase Ib (n = 75), the centrally confirmed overall response rate was 71%. The median duration of response was 14.1 months, and median progression-free survival was 12.4 months. Most treatment related adverse events (TRAEs) were grade 1–2. The most common TRAEs were diarrhea (any: 56%, grade ≥3: 1%) and rash (any: 33%, grade ≥3: 0). The most common grade ≥3 TRAEs were increased alanine transaminase (8%) and aspartate aminotransferase (5%). Few TRAEs (n = 2, 3%) resulted in treatment discontinuation.

Initial dose escalation of Phase Ia of Beamion LUNG-1 was conducted with a conventional tablet formulation of zongertinib. Efficacious exposure was achieved; however, high variability in pharmacokinetic (PK) parameters was observed. Aqueous solubility of zongertinib is low across physiological pH ranges. Amorphous formulations are associated with enhanced solubility and bioavailability. , A spray-dried dispersion (SDD) formulation of zongertinib was developed to address the variation in the exposure.

In this multifaceted analysis, we present data assessing the solubility and bioavailability of the zongertinib SDD-based formulation compared with the conventional formulation in vitro, in vivo in animals, and in healthy human participants with the aim of informing a switch of formulation for further clinical development of zongertinib. First, we examined the influence of different gastric pH levels on the pharmacokinetics (PK) of zongertinib after oral administration as a Natrosol suspension in beagles, pretreated with famotidine (an acid-reducing agent) or pentagastrin (a gastrin analogue that stimulates gastric acid secretion) used to modulate stomach pH. PK characteristics of zongertinib SDD and conventional formulations were next evaluated in beagles pretreated with pentagastrin. We then assessed the in vitro drug release and availability for absorption (bioaccessibility) in a tiny-TIM in vitro system (InnoGI Technologies, Leiden, The Netherlands), comprising gastric and small intestinal compartments, designed to perform dynamic dissolution testing in the presence of physiologically relevant parameters (e.g., body temperature, pH, concentrations of electrolytes, enzymes, and bile salts). − Zongertinib in SDD and conventional formulations was tested under simulated fasted conditions using tiny-TIM, with and without elevated stomach pH, simulating the effect of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) and other acid-reducing agents. Finally, we assessed the relative bioavailability of zongertinib SDD and conventional formulations and the effect of the PPI rabeprazole on bioavailability of the SDD formulation in healthy human male participants in a phase I, four-way cross over study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05380947).

Methods

In Vitro Physicochemical Properties of Zongertinib

The physicochemical properties of zongertinib were determined using various methods. Aqueous solubility of zongertinib was assessed by the shake flask method. Zongertinib powder was suspended in various pH buffers (per standards set by the United States Pharmacopeia [USP]) and equilibrated at 37 °C for 24 h. Drug concentration was analyzed by a high-performance liquid chromatography-ultraviolet (HPLC-UV) methodology. The solubility of zongertinib was also assessed in USP compendial fasted state- and fed state-simulated intestinal fluid (FaSSIF and FeSSIF); the residual solids after solubility measurements in FaSSIF and FeSSIF were analyzed by powder X-ray diffraction (XRPD).

Animal Studies

In total, eight male beagles were used, four at Boehringer Ingelheim Pharma GmbH & Co. KG (Biberach, Germany) in the first part of the study, and four at Charles River Laboratories (Worcester, USA) in a follow-up study. The dogs were kept individually in metabolic cages during the initial blood sampling period from 1 h prior to drug administration until 24 h after drug administration. Then, animals were kept in their housing facility. Water was freely available to the dogs throughout the study. The dogs were fasted for approximately 18 h prior to and during the first 4 h after drug administration.

In the first part of the study, intramuscular pentagastrin (6 μg/kg, Sigma-Aldrich, Product No. B1636, CAS-no. 5534–95–2) was administered to four male beagle dogs (fasted overnight). After 30 min, zongertinib (3 mg/kg, Boehringer Ingelheim, batch B204000119) was administered orally as an aqueous 0.5% Natrosol suspension via gavage. In the second part of the study, performed ∼8 days later, the same four dogs received a single 40 mg tablet of famotidine orally (Famotidin-ratiopharm 40 mg coated tablet, ratiopharm, Germany, CAS-no.: 76824–35–6), then zongertinib (3 mg/kg; i.e., 51 mg per 17 kg dog) in the same Natrosol suspension 1 h later.

In the follow-up study in four male beagles (fasted overnight) from the colony of non-naïve animals at the testing facility, pretreated with intramuscular pentagastrin (6 μg/kg), zongertinib plasma concentration–time profile and exposure were evaluated following oral administration of zongertinib (2 × 100 mg tablet) as two different formulations: (i) an SDD-based formulation containing standard tablet excipients and active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) dispersed in a polymer carrier (Boehringer Ingelheim, Lot DEV-042–021T-B); (ii) a conventional formulation comprising a dry blend of standard tablet excipients and API (Boehringer Ingelheim, Lot 179–002–20001).

In each phase of the study, blood samples were taken at 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 24, 32, and 48 h (for the follow-up study, the 6 h sample was omitted and additional samples were taken at 1.5 and 12 h) after administration of zongertinib. Concentrations of zongertinib in plasma were determined using liquid chromatography coupled with a tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) assay. The lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) was 10 nmol/L.

Plasma concentration–time data for zongertinib were analyzed by noncompartmental methods in ToxKin 4 (Entimo AG, Berlin, Germany) for the first study and Phoenix WinNonlin (Certara USA, Inc.) for the follow-up study. The following parameters were assessed with descriptive statistics: maximum plasma concentration (C max); time to reach C max (t max); area under the plasma concentration–time curve from time 0 to infinity (AUC0‑∞).

The beagle study performed in Europe was carried out in accordance with the German and European Animal Welfare Acts and was approved by the local German authority, Regierungspraesidium Tuebingen under Project License VVH17–002. The follow-up study performed at Charles River Laboratories, USA was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) at Charles River Laboratories under IACUC protocol number I031.

In Vitro Release and Bioaccessibility in Tiny-TIM System

The in vitro bioaccessibility of SDD and conventional tablet formulations of zongertinib was investigated using a tiny-TIM (InnoGI Technologies, Leiden, The Netherlands) under simulated fasted conditions with normal gastric pH (∼pH 2) and with gastric pH maintained at pH 5.0 (Supplementary Table S1, Supplementary Figure S1). During the experiments, the dissolved compound is removed from the intestinal lumen through a semipermeable membrane unit connected to the small-intestinal compartment. This allows sampling during GI transit and assessment of the bioaccessible fraction that is potentially available for small intestinal absorption. The tiny-TIM experiment period was 5 h (fasted state), which represents the average transit time through the upper GI tract. , Each tiny-TIM experiment was performed in duplicate. Filtrate samples were collected every 30 min for the first 2 h, then every 60 min for the next 3 h (at t = 0–30, 30–60, 60–90, 90–120, 120–180, 180–240, and 240–300 min); zongertinib concentrations were assessed by ultrahigh-pressure liquid chromatography-UV (UPLC-UV).

Clinical Study

Design and Treatments

NCT05380947 was a randomized, open-label, four-way crossover study. A maximum of 16 healthy male participants were planned to be randomly assigned to four treatment sequences in a 1:1:1:1 ratio, with a washout interval of at least 14 days between zongertinib administrations. Fasted volunteers received the following treatments: 30 mg of zongertinib (given as one tablet of 20 mg and two tablets of 5 mg of the conventional formulation); 30 mg of zongertinib (given as two tablets of 15 mg of the SDD formulation); and 30 mg of zongertinib (given as two tablets of 15 mg of the SDD formulation) after multiple doses of rabeprazole 40 mg (Pariet gastroresistant tablet Eisai Co., Ltd.; two 20 mg tablets per day on Days −4 to 1) (see Supporting Information for composition of conventional and SDD formulations). Zongertinib was administered 2 h after the last dose of rabeprazole on the morning of Day 1, to maximize the effect of rabeprazole on intragastric acidity. A fourth treatment evaluated 30 mg zongertinib after a high-fat, high-calorie breakfast; these findings will be described in a separate analysis. The four treatments were given in randomized order.

The study protocol, and amendments (EudraCT number 2022–000268–23, ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT05380947), were approved by the Independent Ethics Committee of the State Chamber of Physicians of Baden-Württemberg, Stuttgart, Germany and by the Federal Institute for Drugs and Medicinal Products (BfArM), Bonn, Germany. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles laid down in the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided signed and dated written informed consent in accordance with the ICH-GCP and local legislation prior to admission to the study.

Participants

Participants were healthy males aged 18–45 years with a body mass index (BMI) of 18.5 to 29.9 kg/m2. Key exclusion criteria were: any clinically relevant finding in the medical examination; any laboratory value outside of the reference range and deemed clinically relevant; evidence of a clinically relevant concomitant disease; any documented active or suspected malignancy or history of malignancy; history of relevant allergy or hypersensitivity; use of drugs within 30 days of planned administration of trial medication that might reasonably influence the results of the trial; intake of an investigational drug in another clinical trial within 60 days of planned administration of zongertinib; and smoking >10 cigarettes per day.

Procedures and Assessments

Prior to administration of zongertinib, participants fasted overnight for a minimum of 10 h. Trial medication was administered as an oral dose, together with 240 mL of water (at room temperature). Participants were kept under close medical surveillance for 24 h after administration of zongertinib and were not allowed to lie down for the first 4 h except for required trial procedures.

Blood samples for PK analysis of zongertinib were taken at the following time points: within 3 h prior to zongertinib administration and 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 24, 34, 46, 70, 94, and 118 h after zongertinib administration. Blood samples for PK analysis of rabeprazole were taken at the following time points: before the last rabeprazole dose, up to 10 min before the planned intake of zongertinib, and 1.5 and 2 h after zongertinib intake.

Zongertinib and rabeprazole concentrations in plasma were determined using validated LC-MS/MS assays. Safety and tolerability of zongertinib and rabeprazole were assessed based on adverse events (AEs, graded according Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, CTCAE v5.0), including clinically relevant findings from the physical examination, safety laboratory tests, 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG), and vital signs.

Outcomes

The primary PK end points were AUC0‑tz and C max of zongertinib. The secondary end point was AUC0‑∞ of zongertinib. Further PK end points presented here include t max, t 1/2, CL/F and V z /F.

Statistical Analysis

The main objective was to compare zongertinib SDD versus conventional formulation in the fasted state, zongertinib SDD under fed and fasted conditions (reported separately), and zongertinib SDD with and without rabeprazole 40 mg in terms of the primary and secondary PK end points. The trial was designed to allow for intraparticipant comparisons. The statistical model used for the analysis of primary and secondary end points was an analysis of variance (ANOVA) model on the logarithmic scale. That is, the PK end points were log-transformed (natural logarithm) prior to fitting the ANOVA model. The model included effects accounting for the sequence, participants within sequences, period, and treatment. The difference between the expected means for each end point was estimated by the difference in the corresponding adjusted means (Least Squares Means). These quantities were then back-transformed to the original scale to provide the point estimate (adjusted geometric means) and 90% confidence intervals [CI] for each end point. The effect ‘participants within sequences’ was considered as random, whereas the other effects were considered as fixed. Other PK parameters were compared using descriptive statistics. The area under the curve was calculated using the linear up/log down algorithm. If the drug concentration was equal to or higher than the preceding concentration, the linear trapezoidal method was used. If the drug concentration was lower than the preceding concentration, the logarithmic method was used. PK end point data are presented as geometric mean or as adjusted geometric mean, as appropriate. Ratios of adjusted geometric means (C max and AUC0‑tz) are presented as percentages.

Clinical Study

Participants and Treatment

Thirteen participants were treated (n = 12 [92.3%] white, n = 1 [7.7%] black). The mean age was 34.8 years (standard deviation [SD] = 5.8 years; range 25–45 years). The mean BMI was 25.5 kg/m2 (SD = 3.0 kg/m2; range 20.7–29.5 kg/m2). The treatment groups were similar with respect to demographic and baseline characteristics. Not all participants attended all of the visits.

Safety

Overall, eight of 13 participants (61.5%) reported at least one AE on zongertinib. Two out of 11 participants (18.2%) reported at least one AE on rabeprazole alone. There were no investigator-defined drug-related AEs. No deaths, serious AEs, AEs leading to discontinuation, protocol-specified AEs of special interest, or other significant AEs were reported.

Two of the on-treatment AEs were CTCAE grade 2 in severity (abdominal pain upper, contusion). All other on-treatment AEs were CTCAE grade 1 in severity. All AEs were reported to be resolved by the end of the trial, except for one case of hordeolum. There were no clinically relevant findings in the safety laboratory, vital signs, and ECG.

Discussion

Emerging clinical trial data have shown that zongertinib is a highly promising treatment option for patients with HER2 mutant NSCLC and is associated with high response rates and good tolerability, reflecting its EGFR-sparing mechanism of action. Early PK data in patients with HER2-altered lung cancer demonstrated high variability in zongertinib exposure when taken as a conventional tablet formulation. In this comprehensive analysis, we assessed the solubility and bioaccessibility or bioavailability of conventional zongertinib formulations with respect to pH, in vitro and in vivo. An SDD formulation was developed that had improved solubility in FaSSIF and FeSSIF and improved bioaccessibility in vitro and bioavailability in healthy volunteers compared with the conventional formulation. Investigations in three experimental modalities (in vivo in dogs, in vitro in the tiny-TIM model, and in human volunteers) demonstrated that the bioavailability of the conventional formulation of zongertinib is negatively affected by high gastric pH and the SDD formulation has better bioavailability under these conditions, providing robust evidence that the SDD formulation improves bioavailability across the different physiologies of humans and dogs.

Experiments in dogs clearly demonstrated that the gastric pH influenced the bioavailability of the conventional zongertinib formulation when administered as a suspension. Zongertinib C max and AUC0‑∞ values were reduced by ∼94% and ∼95%, respectively, at the higher gastric pH (∼7) compared to a lower gastric pH (∼1–2). These findings indicate that there may be a risk of significant drug–drug interactions in patients taking PPIs and other acid-reducing agents in combination with zongertinib when administered as a conventional formulation. The follow-up experiment indicated that the zongertinib SDD formulation provided improved exposure with reduced variability at low gastric pH compared with the conventional formulation (increased C max and AUC0‑∞ by 22% and 24%, respectively).

Investigations using the tiny-TIM in vitro GI system showed that bioaccessibility of the SDD and conventional formulations were similar under simulated fasting conditions at low gastric pH. In addition to comparing the bioaccessibility of different formulations, tiny-TIM can also predict effects of acid-reducing agents on bioavailability; these predictions have previously been shown to be a reliable model of the human situation. Therefore, by varying the simulated gastric pH, we determined that the bioaccessibility of the conventional formulation was decreased with increased pH, whereas that of the SDD formulation was unaffected. In our investigation, we minimized animal numbers by employing crossover designs for in vivo experiments and by using the advanced in vitro system tiny-TIM to simulate GI conditions.

Given data from in vitro and dog studies, it was hypothesized that variability in the bioavailability of zongertinib was related to variable solubility and absorption under the different pH conditions of the conventional formulation. The relative bioavailability investigation was undertaken in healthy participants comparing the conventional formulation and the new SDD formulation of zongertinib. In line with data from our studies in dogs, bioavailability was higher with the SDD formulation, with 39% and 35% increases in C max and AUC0‑tz, respectively, compared to the conventional formulation. However, the difference was not consistent when looking at individual participants which might relate to higher variability with the conventional formulation.

Finally, given the impact of GI pH on the solubility of zongertinib, we assessed the impact of coadministration of a PPI. The concentration–time profile for rabeprazole in our investigation was consistent with published data. Rabeprazole did not relevantly impact zongertinib exposure under fasted conditions, apart from a slightly delayed t max. This finding is consistent with nonclinical data and suggests that the in vivo performance of the SDD formulation of zongertinib is not impacted by the presence of stomach-pH-increasing medications. Therefore, it was concluded that SDD-based formulations should be used for the further clinical development of zongertinib.

Across all parts of the study, zongertinib was well tolerated by healthy participants, with no drug-related AEs reported. Also, 40 mg of rabeprazole QD over 5 days was well tolerated. The dose of rabeprazole used in our study was higher than the usual therapeutic dose of 10 or 20 mg (higher doses are recommended only in Zollinger-Ellison syndrome). We used the increased dose of 40 mg because, in the range of 5 mg to 40 mg, the decrease of intragastric acidity is dose-dependent and is most pronounced after 40 mg QD. , A dosing duration of 5 days was chosen to reach a steady state of inhibition of gastric acid secretion. In previous studies in healthy volunteers, in line with our observations, 40 mg rabeprazole QD demonstrated generally good tolerability. ,,, As evidenced by a recent investigation of the effect of omeprazole on bioavailability of dasatinib that showed a much more pronounced effect than previously reported, the relative timing of PPI intake and victim drug is crucial in an interaction study. The antisecretory effect of rabeprazole starts within 1 h with a maximal effect within two to 4 h; therefore, in our study the dosing was timed to maximize the interaction. Our design of the investigation of the interaction with rabeprazole can thus be used for a safe worst-case assessment in healthy volunteers.

Generally, the tiny-TIM data mirrored in vivo data in dogs and highlighted the impact of gastric pH on the bioavailability of the conventional formulation. These observations show the potential utility of tiny-TIM as a model of in vivo gastric absorption that could ultimately reduce the need for animal experimentation, in line with a recent FDA roadmap. The pH-dependence of bioavailability in dogs that received the zongertinib conventional formulation plus famotidine was reflected in experiments using tiny-TIM in the presence of PPI. However, there was a difference between the tiny-TIM data and in vivo data. Bioaccessibility under fasted conditions in vitro was equivalent between SDD and conventional formulations, but zongertinib exposure in dogs and in human volunteers was improved with the SDD versus the conventional formulation, indicating improved bioavailability.

A limitation of our study is that we included only male volunteers. This was done because at the time of the trial data on embryotoxicity of zongertinib were not yet available. However, this clinical investigation was a mechanistic study, and thus, the inclusion of only males was deemed acceptable. Food effects on the PK of zongertinib SDD formulation in the current study, and a related investigation with a clinically relevant dose (240 mg; NCT06075277), are reported in a companion manuscript. ,

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study in healthy participants has demonstrated that the SDD formulation of zongertinib showed better exposure and reduced PK variability compared to the conventional formulation. The data support the use of an SDD formulation of zongertinib in further clinical development. In addition, it appears that the bioavailability of zongertinib administered as SDD formulation is not influenced by rabeprazole, and thus, zongertinib may be given together with drugs reducing gastric acidity. Use of a zongertinib SDD formulation may help overcome the interpatient variability in bioavailability observed with the conventional formulation.

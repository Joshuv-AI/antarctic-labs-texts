Factors associated with allergic diseases in Chinese children aged 6-14 years
Authors: Qiong Wang, Min Yang, Kening Chen, Fangjieyi Zheng, Zhixin Zhang, Wenquan Niu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and objectivesWe aimed to identify and optimize contributing factors associated with allergic diseases by machine/deep learning algorithms among school-age children aged 6-14 years.MethodsWe performed a cross-sectional survey in eight primary schools and 16 middle schools using a clustering sample strategy. Data were collected by questionnaires. Machine/deep learning algorithms were implemented using Python (v3.7.6).ResultsOf 11308 children enrolled, 4375 had allergic diseases. The prevalence of asthma, allergic rhinitis and eczema was 6.31% (N=713), 25.36% (N=2868) and 21.38% (N=2418), respectively. Of 12 machine-learning algorithms, Gaussian naive Bayes (NB) outperformed the others for asthma, Bernoulli NB for rhinitis and multinomial NB for eczema. By comparison, a minimal set of six, five and five key factors were identified for asthma (episodes of upper and lower respiratory infection, age, gender, family history of diabetes and dental caries), rhinitis (episodes of upper respiratory infection, age, gender, maternal education and family history of diabetes) and eczema (episodes of upper respiratory infection, age, maternal education, outdoor activities and dental caries), respectively.ConclusionsWe identified three minimal sets of factors that can capture the majority of whole information and accurately predict the risk for asthma, rhinitis and eczema among children aged 6-14 years.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-24928-x.Keywords: Allergic disease, School-age children, Feature, Machine learning, Deep learning

Introduction

The prevalence of allergic diseases in school-age children is growing to epidemic proportions worldwide and is characterized by obvious geographic variations [1–3]. Allergic diseases, including eczema, asthma and allergic rhinitis, pose a heavy burden on individuals and healthcare systems. Reports from the Global Asthma Network Phase I survey showed that the cumulative prevalence of allergic rhinitis was estimated to be 17.9–24.9.9.9% in children and 26.2–37.5.2.5% in adolescents [4]. In China, the prevalence of childhood asthma, allergic rhinitis and eczema in 2005 was estimated to be 3.3%, 9.8% and 5.5%, respectively, and there is an increasing trend onwards [5, 6]. Among Chilean children aged 5–15 years, the mortality rate associated with asthma was 0.37 per 100,000 inhabitants [7]. It is hence essential to heed the high prevalence and mortality rates associated with allergic diseases in children. Moreover, identifying children at high risk should be prioritized for public health worldwide.

A large number of factors have been reported to be associated with the risk of childhood allergic diseases. For example, children with ≥3 adverse childhood experiences were 1.45 times more likely to develop asthma than children free of adverse childhood experiences in the United States [8]. In addition, family rhinitis history, bedroom carpet and pollen triggers were major contributory factors for asthma and allergy-related symptoms in school-age children from New York [9]. Contrastingly in China, a family history of allergy, allergic rhinitis, infantile eczema, non-breastfeeding, air-conditioning and passive smoking were childhood asthma-susceptibility factors [10]. Another survey from Wuhan revealed that obesity increased the prevalence rates of allergic rhinitis and atopic dermatitis in children [11]. Many endeavors have been made to seek candidate factors for childhood allergic diseases; however, a major problem facing scientific community is that no single factor has been consistently reported across all studies published thus far. Considering the existence of geographic variations in prevalence, it is recommended to construct a database of factors susceptible to childhood allergic diseases in each ethnic group.

Another common problem is that the majority of significant factors were teased out by means of traditional probability-based statistical methods, such as logistic regression, which cannot properly handle non-linear, co-linear and interactive interrelations between exposures and outcomes. One way to solve this problem is to use more advanced artificial intelligence methods, which have achieved remarkable success in multiple clinical settings [12–16].

To provide more information for future studies, we performed a large, cross-sectional survey on children from 26 schools in Beijing, aiming to identify key factors associated with allergic diseases including three clinical manifestations by multiple machine/deep learning algorithms. Meanwhile, for each clinical manifestation, the top-performing machine-learning algorithm was selected, and a minimal set of key factors was determined. Finally, prediction performance of this subset was tested by the deep-learning sequential algorithms.

Methods

Survey and ethics

This survey was cross-sectional in design using a clustering sample strategy and conducted in eight primary and 16 middle schools from Pinggu district, Beijing within 2022. The details about this survey were published previously [17, 18].

The conduct of this study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing University of Chinese Medicine (approval no. 2022BZYLL0906). Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or grandparents (for left-behind children) of study children before collecting epidemiological data. Orphans were not surveyed due to missing information from their parents.

Factor collection

Data were gleaned by a self-designed questionnaire (see Supplement file), which was a priori confirmed to be reliable with the coefficient α over 0.85. Items were selected a priori based on both published literature and clinical experience. The questionnaire was initially sent to the parents or grandparents of a total of 11633 children through the “Wenjuanxing” network platform (https://www.wenjuan.com/). Finally, 11308 valid questionnaires (response rate: 98%) were returned. Data from valid questionnaires were downloaded from the “Wenjuanxing” platform into a Microsoft Office Excel™ file.

Quality control

Class teachers were requested to circulate the electronic questionnaires compiled as a QR code recognizable by smartphones to the parents or grandparents of study children. Data from questionnaires were strictly checked by trained staff. If records were missing or outliers, parents or grandparents who filled in the questionnaire were contacted by class teachers for further confirmation or completion.

Factors in questionnaires

This questionnaire was designed to collect factors from both children and their parents. Factors relating to children included date of birth, sex, ethnicity, plastic tableware using, picky eating, makeup use, sleeping in light, stool times, stool consistency, eating speed, episodes of upper and lower respiratory infection over past year, allergy (foods and drugs), chronic diseases, birthweight, birth height, gestational age, body weight, body height, delivery mode, twin birth, birth order, breastfeeding duration, age of introducing solid foods, daily time records on outdoor activities, bench sitting, electronic screen use, sleep duration and bedtime at night, as well as weekly intake frequencies of fiber foods, out-of-season fruits, animal proteins, soy proteins, milks, dietary supplements, preservative foods, fast foods, sweet foods, night meals and snacks. Body height and weight were measured by school health physicians.

Factors related to parents included age, height (self-reported), weight (self-reported), education, family income, perinatal clinical history (delivery mode, birth weight and birth height), duration of breast-feeding and family histories of diabetes mellitus and hypertension.

Definition of allergic diseases

In this survey, the International Study of Asthma and Allergies in Childhood (ISAAC) [19] questionnaire was used to define allergic diseases in children. Specifically, information on ever-diagnosed diseases and current symptoms was referenced to define childhood asthma/wheezing, allergic rhinitis and eczema.

Factor descriptions

Time records on outdoor activities every day over the past year was derived as the sum of time spent on five workdays and two weekends divided by seven. Chronic diseases covered chronic kidney disease, chronic heart disease and hypothyroidism. Fiber foods contained grains and seasonal fruits. Animal proteins included red meat, poultries, fishes, shellfish, eggs and processed meat. Soy proteins included cereals, beans, nuts, vegetables, fruits and fungi. Dietary supplements included royal jelly and nutraceuticals. Fast foods referred to foods with high energy and low nutrition (such as hamburger and French fries). Night meal was defined as eating foods within 2 hours before bedtime. Weekly intake frequency was consistent with fast foods and night meals, classified as every day, often (3–5 times), occasional (1–2 times) or none or occasionally over the past year. Episodes of upper and lower respiratory infections over the past year included zero, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 times or above. Delivery mode included vaginal delivery, caesarean section and forceps delivery.

Education was categorized as middle school degree or below, high school degree and college degree or above. Family history of diabetes mellitus or hypertension was represented by the number of parents, grandparents and grandparents-in-law who were clinically diagnosed to have diabetes or hypertension. Family income (RMB per year) was categorized as ≥300,000, 100,000–300,000 and <100,000. Stool consistency was assessed by the Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) [20], and daily defecation frequency over the past year was recorded. The BSFS classified stools into 4 categories: separate hard lumps like nuts; sausage-shaped but lumpy; like a sausage or snake but with cracks on its surface; like a sausage or snake, smooth and soft, fluffy pieces, watery.

Statistical analyses

Factors from the questionnaire were removed from the final analysis if the percent of missing values increased to 30% or more. For factors retained in final dataset, missing values were filled up by multiple imputation procedure implemented in the R Programming Environment (version 4.1.1). Factors on a continuous scale are displayed as mean (standard deviation) or median (interquartile range) depending on the probability of normality test. Factors on a categorical or ordinal scale are displayed as count (percentage). Two-group comparisons (with or without asthma, rhinitis and eczema) were made using the t-test (for normally distributed factors), rank-sum test (for factors with skewed distributions), or χ2 test (for categorical and ordinal factors) where appropriate.

Development of the machine learning algorithms

Considering the advantages and disadvantages of each machine-learning algorithm, data were trained and internally validated by 12 widely-used machine-learning algorithms, including Logistic regression, decision tree, AdaBoost decision tree, support vector machine (SVM), random forest (RF), K-nearest neighbor (KNN), gradient boosting machine (GBM), light gradient boosting machine (LGBM), extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), Gaussian naive Bayes (GNB), Bernoulli naive Bayes (BNB) and Multinomial naive Bayes (MNB). Meanwhile, hard-voting and soft-voting classifiers were also derived by ensemble learning of above 12 machine-learning algorithms. To generalize the results of model prediction to an independent dataset, available data were randomly partitioned into 60% for training and 40% for testing. Prediction performance was assessed by accuracy (how good an algorithm is), precision (how many positive predictions are actually positive), recall (how many actual positive observations are predicted correctly), F1 score (harmonic mean of precision and recall) and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC, a summarized accuracy index) for each clinical magnification of allergic diseases.

To rank and select factors under study according to their importance, the χ2-based Scikit-learn factor selection method and the Shapley additive explanation (SHAP) tool were deployed. For clinical application, a minimal set of factors was ascertained by the cumulative appraise of top important factors. Prediction performance of this subset as reflected by model accuracy and loss was tested by the deep learning sequential algorithms using three different optimization algorithms (adaptive moment estimation [Adam], root mean square prop [RMSprop] and stochastic gradient descent [SGD]).

Factor selection, and machine-learning and deep-learning algorithms were implemented by using the PyCharm Community Edition and Python (version 3.7.6) on the Windows 10 for x64-based Systems.

Results

After discarding invalid questionnaires, data from 11308 questionnaires were analyzed. The baseline characteristics by the presence and absence of three clinical manifestations (asthma, rhinitis and eczema) of allergic diseases are presented in Table 1. Three clinical manifestations of allergic diseases were simultaneously presented in 215 children, accounting for 1.90%.

Discussion

Via machine/deep-learning algorithms, we have identified three minimal sets of factors that can capture the majority of whole information and accurately predict the risk for asthma, rhinitis and eczema among children aged 6–14 years. The performance of three minimal sets were comparable with the whole factor set. In other words, the corresponding optimal algorithms including GNB, BNB and MNB, can be deployed in practice to estimate individualized risk of childhood allergic diseases by modeling the minimal sets of factors. To our knowledge, this is thus far the first attempt in the literature to characterize risk profiling of allergic diseases via artificial-intelligence techniques.

It is increasingly recognized that childhood allergic diseases are a group of clinical heterogeneous manifestations, which are determined by inherited and non-inherited factors [21, 22]. For example, less physical activity is a risk-conferring factor for the development of asthma in childhood by some, but not all, studies [23–27]. The reasons for inconsistency are manifold, possibly involving differences in genetic backgrounds, dietary habits, lifestyle and nutritional status, as well as in methodological aspects such as study design and the power to detect statistical significance. Especially with the rapid urbanization and socio-economic development over the past few decades, risk profiling has changed dynamically with the incorporation of new-emerging factors, such as electronic screen use. To yield more information, we designed a comprehensive questionnaire that covered both traditional and new-emerging factors, and collected data from both children and their parents within January, 2022 by using a clustering sample strategy in school-age children 6–14 years from Beijing. As an extension of our previous work in preschool-aged children [28–30], we aimed to identify and characterize the minimal number of factors responsible for allergic diseases from 60 promising factors in school-age children in this study. Importantly, our work can help enrich the list of candidate factors responsible for the development of asthma, rhinitis and eczema in school-age children with broad age spectrum. Eventually, lead to a better understanding of the possible causes of childhood allergic diseases.

Given the possible high-degree linear and interactive relationship across candidate factors, differing from analytical strategies of most previous studies in this filed, we resorted to the unique advantages of artificial-intelligence techniques. Machine-learning algorithms can select important factors from a whole perspective and deep-learning confirm the validity of minimum panel of factors using sequential algorithms. After a comprehensive analysis, several factors, including age, sex, maternal education, upper respiratory infection, number of kins with diabetes mellitus, dental caries and outdoor activity were identified as important contributors to the development of childhood allergic diseases. It is worth mentioning that prediction performance of these important factors is comparable with that of 60 assessable factors under study, rendering the practicality of modeling these minimum panels of significant factors using the top-performing naive Bayes algorithms when quantifying the individual risk estimate of allergic diseases in children.

The contribution of important factors in minimal sets identified in this study to childhood allergic diseases was supported by previous studies. For instance, boys had an increased risk of experiencing allergic disease relative to girls as reported by the majority of studies [31–34], consistent with the findings of the present study, as sex was listed among the top factors when predicting the risk for asthma, rhinitis and eczema in children. The reasons for this sex-specific contribution are not fully understood; however, it might be interpreted by the varying responses to environmental or occupational exposures involving immunological and hormonal factors between boys and girls [35–38]. The same cases were found for upper respiratory infection and age. Additionally, maternal education was found to be a promising contributor to the risk of childhood rhinitis and eczema, underscoring the importance of future education programs for the primary prevention of allergic diseases in children, in line with the results of previous studies [5, 39]. Nevertheless, it should be kept in mind that further extrapolation of our findings requires external validation in other independent populations, and the adoption of artificial-intelligence techniques in big data is encouraging for future well-designed studies.

Strengths and limitations

This survey is conducted using a clustering random sampling procedure in Ping Gu district, Beijing, China, and our findings can be generalizable to local regions. In addition, this study is strengthened by simultaneous incorporation of various sources of data from both children and parents, as well as the adoption of more advanced machine-learning and deep-learning algorithms.

This study also has several limitations. First, the cross-sectional nature of this survey inherently limits causal inferences between important factors identified and the development of allergic diseases. However, our findings provide an opportunity to understand the risk profiles of childhood allergic diseases and address the utility of screening these factors for primary prevention by changing diets and lifestyles, eventually, preventing the occurrence of childhood allergic diseases. Second, childhood allergic diseases are complex in development, and only 60 potential factors were surveyed. Third, this survey relied on self-designed questionnaire data for the exploration of factors associated with childhood allergic diseases, raising the potential for recall bias. Fourth, all study children of Chinese descent are currently living in Beijing, and the extrapolation of our findings to other racial or ethnical groups is cautionary.

Conclusions

We identified three minimal sets of important factors that can capture the majority of information in the whole set and accurately predict the risk of asthma, rhinitis and eczema among children aged 6–14 years. For practical reasons, the incorporation of these minimal sets of important factors into the optimal naive Bayes algorithms can help assess risk in individual children. The importance of this study impels a further step toward application of machine/deep-learning algorithms as powerful tools to identify and characterize risk profiling of childhood allergic diseases.

Associations of plasma p‐Tau217 with cognitive domain performance in clinically unimpaired participants: Evidence from HABS‐HD
Authors: Zara Najmi, Anhiti Dharmapuri, Joey A Contreras, Cellas A Hayes, The Health and Aging Brain Study (HABS‐HD) Study Team
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractINTRODUCTIONThe relationship between plasma phosphorylated tau 217 (p‐Tau217) and domain‐specific cognitive performance across race and ethnic groups remains unclear.METHODSWe analyzed data from 2052 clinically unimpaired participants from the Health & Aging Brain Study–Health Disparities cohort. Using adjusted multivariable linear regressions, we evaluated the associations between p‐Tau217 and cognitive scores (immediate and delayed memory, attention/processing speed, executive function, and language). Analyses were conducted in the overall sample, stratified by race and ethnicity, and in an age‐ and sex‐matched subsample (n = 1032).RESULTSPlasma p‐Tau217 levels were lower in Non‐Hispanic Black (NHB) and Hispanic participants (p < 0.001) despite lower cognitive scores (p < 0.001). Higher p‐Tau217 was associated with worse cognition overall (β −0.05 to −0.11). Associations were strongest in Non‐Hispanic White (NHW), limited in NHB, and domain‐specific in Hispanic groups. Matched analyses attenuated effects.DISCUSSIONPlasma p‐Tau217 is associated with domain‐specific cognitive performance in clinically unimpaired individuals, but these associations vary across racial and ethnic groups.Keywords: Alzheimer's disease, cognition, executive function, memory, plasma biomarkers, p‐Tau217, racial and ethnic disparities

INTRODUCTION

Race and ethnic disparities in dementia represent a growing public health concern, with projections indicating a disproportionate rise in cases among Non‐Hispanic Black (NHB) and Hispanic populations. 1 , 2 , 3 NHB and Hispanic individuals are reported to experience a higher prevalence and risk of Alzheimer's disease (AD) and AD and related dementias (ADRD). 3 Yet, despite this elevated burden, these groups have been historically underrepresented in diagnostic studies using cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) or positron emission tomography (PET) imaging, as well as in clinical trials and neuropathology investigations. 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 As a result, our understanding of the full spectrum of AD pathology in these populations remains limited.

In recent years, the field has shifted toward blood‐based biomarkers as feasible, less invasive, and more scalable alternatives to CSF and PET measures. 8 Plasma biomarkers grant broader disease tracking, facilitates enrollment in clinical trials, and expands access to diagnosis in clinical settings. 8 Among these markers, phosphorylated tau 217 (p‐Tau217) has emerged as a leading candidate, reflecting amyloid and tau pathology and is closely linked to AD progression. 9 , 10 However, its utility remains underexplored in populations most at risk for ADRD, including NHB and Hispanic individuals. 1 In our recent study, we observed that higher plasma p‐Tau217 levels were associated with an increased odds of clinical cognitive impairment in non‐Hispanic White (NHW), NHB, and Hispanic participants and global amyloid beta (Aβ) PET standardized update value ratios (>1.08) with varying discriminatory accuracy across race and ethnic groups. 11 AD is widely recognized to have an extended preclinical and prodromal phase, with elevated biomarker levels occurring decades prior to clinical symptoms related to cognition and daily functioning. 12 , 13 However, most studies of the preclinical phase have relied on global cognitive measures and have been conducted primarily in European and White populations. Few studies have evaluated how plasma p‐Tau217 relates to performance across multiple cognitive domains, such as episodic memory, executive function, attention/processing speed, and language. 14 , 15

Across the field, several key gaps remain: (1) the extent to which p‐Tau217 is associated with domain‐specific cognitive performance; (2) whether these associations differ by race and ethnicity; and (3) whether these associations are observable in clinically unimpaired, community‐based populations across diverse race and ethnic groups. To address these gaps, we examined associations between plasma p‐Tau217 and multiple cognitive domains (memory, executive function, attention/processing speed, and language domains) among clinically unimpaired individuals and subsequently investigated whether these associations differed across race and ethnic groups. To our knowledge, this is the first study in a large, community‐based cohort of NHW, NHB, and Hispanic participants to comprehensively evaluate the associations between plasma p‐Tau217 and domain‐specific cognitive outcomes among clinically unimpaired individuals.

METHODS

Cohort

The Health and Aging Brain Study–Health Disparities (HABS‐HD) is an ongoing community‐based longitudinal cohort based in the University of North Texas Health Science Center's Institute for Translational Research. 16 , 17 HABS‐HD is the largest study of AD using the AT(N) framework specifically designed to recruit NHW, NHB, and Hispanic adults, with the goal of advancing understanding of race and ethnic differences in both biological and social/structural determinants of AD. 17 All procedures were approved by the University of North Texas Health Science Center Institutional Review Board, and participants provided written informed consent in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Inclusion criteria

From HABS‐HD release 7, 4134 participants had clinical, biomarker, and genetic data. Analyses were restricted to 3009 clinical cognitively unimpaired individuals 17 ; those with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), dementia, or missing status were excluded. Among these, 957 participants were excluded due to missing p‐Tau217 and a small percentage were set to missing due to outlier removal, yielding a final analytic sample of 2052 participants.

Demographics

Participant's age was recorded in years, and education was treated as a continuous variable indicating total years of schooling. Sex was categorized as male or female. Race and ethnicity were harmonized based on self‐identification for NHW, NHB, and Hispanic groups. 11

Apolipoprotein E

Genomic DNA was extracted from whole blood, and apolipoprotein (APOE) genotyping was conducted using TaqMan SNP assays targeting rs429358 and rs7412 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). APOE status was coded as binary, distinguishing participants with at least one ε4 allele (ε4 carriers) from those without an ε4 allele (non‐carriers). 18 , 19

History of kidney disease

History of kidney disease was assessed based on participant self‐report, reflecting whether individuals indicated a prior clinical diagnosis of kidney disease at the time of study assessment. 20

Blood‐based biomarkers

Fasting plasma samples were collected and processed following standardized biospecimen handling protocols. Plasma biomarker assays were conducted using Quanterix Simoa technology on the HD‐X platform. Plasma p‐Tau217 was measured using the AlzPath p‐Tau217 assay, with two kit versions utilized across data releases (version 2 and PLUS). Neurofilament light chain (NfL) was quantified using two assay configurations across releases: The N3PA kit was used for earlier releases (r2, r3, and r5), while the NfL version 2 kit was used in the most recent release (r7). Plasma‐based biomarker quantification has been described for HABS‐HD. 21 , 22 , 23 , 24 , 25

Blood‐based biomarker release consolidation

Plasma biomarkers were harmonized across multiple HABS‐HD data releases to maximize sample size and biomarker availability based on our prior methodology. 26 , 27 Biomarkers were harmonized across HABS‐HD releases (r2, r3, r5, r7) by averaging non‐missing values within participants to create a single summary measure per biomarker. For p‐Tau217, 2059 participants contributed a single measure, two had multiple measures that were averaged, and 948 had no data. For NfL, 1090 contributed a single measure, 1247 had multiple measures that were averaged, and 672 had no NfL data. This approach maximized sample size while avoiding duplication and minimizing missingness.

Blood‐based biomarker outlier analyses

Outliers were defined a priori as values exceeding ± 5 standard deviations from the sample mean.

NHW‐referenced standardization of p‐Tau217

Plasma p‐Tau217 values were standardized using the mean and standard deviation of the NHW group as the reference distribution.

Harmonized cognitive domains

Raw neuropsychological test scores were first cleaned by recoding sentinel missing values to missing. Neuropsychological testing was provided in both English and Spanish based on the participant's choice and preferred language. 16 Individual cognitive test scores were then standardized to NHW‐referenced z‐scores. Immediate memory was defined as the mean of Spanish‐English Verbal Learning Test (SEVLT) 28 immediate recall and Logical Memory I, 29 and delayed memory was defined as the mean of SEVLT delayed recall 28 and Logical Memory II. 29 Attention/processing speed was defined as the mean of Digit Span 29 and Trail Making Test Part A, with Trail Making Test scores reverse‐coded so that higher values reflect better performance. Executive function was defined as the mean of Trail Making Test Part B (reverse‐coded) 30 and Digit Symbol Substitution. Language was defined as the mean of animal fluency 28 and letter fluency (FAS). 31

Statistical methods

Baseline group comparisons

Differences in demographic, biomarker, and cognitive characteristics across race and ethnic groups were evaluated using one‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) for continuous variables and chi‐squared tests for categorical variables. When expected cell counts were fewer than five, Fisher's exact tests were used. For continuous variables with significant overall ANOVA results, pairwise group differences were assessed using Tukey's Honestly Significant Difference procedure with family‐wise error correction.

Distributional comparisons and visualization

To characterize unadjusted differences in biomarker and cognitive distributions across race and ethnic groups, pairwise Wilcoxon rank‐sum tests were also conducted due to non‐normal biomarker distributions.

Multivariable associations between p‐Tau217 and cognition

Associations between plasma p‐Tau217 and cognitive performance were evaluated using multivariable linear regression models. Five separate models were fit, one for each cognitive domain (immediate memory, delayed memory, attention/processing speed, executive function, and language). The primary predictor was plasma p‐Tau217 expressed as a NHW‐standardized z‐score. All models adjusted for age, sex, educational attainment, APOE ε4 carrier status, kidney disease history, and race and ethnicity (NHW reference group).

Effect modification by race and ethnicity

To formally evaluate whether associations between p‐Tau217 and cognition differed across race and ethnic groups, interaction terms between p‐Tau217 and race and ethnicity were included in multivariable regression models with the same covariate adjustments.

Race and ethnicity‐stratified analyses

Independent of significant interaction effects and aligned with our a priori hypothesis, models were estimated separately within NHW, NHB, and Hispanic participants adjusted for covariates described earlier. Stratified analyses were conducted for p‐Tau217 alone, NfL alone, and models including both biomarkers simultaneously.

Age‐ and sex‐matched cohort

To evaluate whether findings were driven by demographic differences across race and ethnic groups, sensitivity analyses were conducted using an age‐ and sex‐matched cohort. Matching was performed using nearest‐neighbor propensity score matching implemented in the MatchIt package in R. 32 NHB and Hispanic participants were matched separately to NHW participants on age and sex. A balanced three‐group sample was created by retaining only NHW participants matched to both a NHB and a Hispanic participant, yielding a 1:1:1 matched cohort. Balance across groups was evaluated using descriptive statistics and group comparisons identical to the overall cohort. Multivariable regression models examining associations between biomarkers and cognitive outcomes were repeated using the same model specifications as in the primary analyses.

Joint biomarker (p‐Tau217 and NfL) regression models

To examine whether p‐Tau217 and NfL were independently associated with cognition, joint models including both biomarkers were estimated within race and ethnic strata in both unmatched and matched participants with covariate adjustment as described earlier.

Statistical reporting

Results are presented as regression coefficients (β), 95% confidence intervals (CI), and two‐sided p values (significance was determine p < 0.05). No formal correction for multiple testing was applied to regression analyses because models were hypothesis‐driven and domain‐specific.

Statistical analyses were conducted in R version 4.2.3.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics (unmatched cohort)

Participant characteristics are shown in Table 1. The analytic sample included 2052 clinically unimpaired individuals (NHW n = 891, NHB n = 348, Hispanic n = 813). NHW participants were older than NHB and Hispanic participants (p < 0.001), with no difference between NHB and Hispanic groups. The proportion of females and APOE ε4 carriers also differed by race and ethnicity (both p < 0.001), while history of kidney disease did not differ (p = 0.259). Plasma p‐Tau217 levels differed across groups (p < 0.001), with NHW exhibiting higher levels than NHB and Hispanic participants and no difference between NHB and Hispanic groups. NfL levels also differed (p < 0.001), with NHW highest, followed by Hispanic, and lowest in NHB. NHW participants had the highest performance, NHB intermediate, and Hispanic participants the lowest across memory, attention/processing speed, executive function, and language (all p < 0.001).

Plasma p‐Tau217 levels differed significantly across race and ethnic groups in both raw and NHW‐referenced standardized metrics (Figure 1A,B). Pairwise comparisons confirmed significantly lower p‐Tau217 among NHB and Hispanic participants relative to NHW participants (all p < 0.001), with smaller differences between NHB and Hispanic groups.

Race and ethnic differences in cognitive performance

All cognitive domains differed significantly across race and ethnic groups. NHW participants demonstrated the highest mean performance across domains, whereas NHB and Hispanic participants exhibited lower NHW‐referenced cognitive z‐scores (Figure 1C–G).

Associations between plasma p‐Tau217 across race and ethnic groups and cognition

Associations between plasma p‐Tau217 (NHW‐referenced z‐score) and cognitive performance differed by race and ethnicity across domains (Figure 1H–L). Higher p‐Tau217 was linked to worse memory across groups (β ≈ −0.13 to −0.18), but associations with attention, executive function, and language were primarily observed in NHW and Hispanic participants, with largely null findings in NHB participants across most domains.

Multivariable associations between p‐Tau217 and cognition (overall sample)

In multivariable linear regression models adjusted for age, sex, education, APOE ε4 status, kidney disease history, and race and ethnicity, higher plasma p‐Tau217 was consistently associated with worse cognitive performance across domains (Figure 2). Specifically, higher p‐Tau217 was associated with lower immediate memory (β = −0.08 [−0.12, −0.04], p < 0.001), delayed memory (β = −0.11 [−0.15, −0.06], p < 0.001), attention/processing speed (β = −0.05 [−0.10, −0.00], p = 0.043), executive function (β = −0.08 [−0.13, −0.02], p = 0.005), and language (β = −0.05 [−0.09, −0.01], p = 0.023).

Effect modification by race and ethnicity

In models including interaction terms between p‐Tau217 and race and ethnic group, there was no statistically significant evidence of effect modification by race and ethnicity for most domains (all interaction p values > 0.05) (Table S1).

Race and ethnicity stratified associations (unmatched cohort)

In stratified adjusted models, higher p‐Tau217 was associated with worse immediate memory (β = −0.07, [−0.13, −0.02], p = 0.013), delayed memory (β = −0.09 [−0.15, −0.03], p = 0.003), attention/processing speed (β = −0.06 [−0.12, −0.01], p = 0.017), and executive function (β = −0.08 [−0.13, −0.03], p = 0.004) among NHW participants, but not with language (β = −0.05 [−0.11, 0.01], p = 0.100) (Figure 3A). Among NHB participants, higher p‐Tau217 was associated only with worse delayed memory (β = −0.13 [−0.27, −0.00], p = 0.049) (Figure 3B). Among Hispanic participants, higher p‐Tau217 was associated with worse immediate memory (β = −0.10 [−0.18, −0.02], p = 0.015), delayed memory (β = −0.14 [−0.22, −0.06], p < 0.001), and executive function (β = −0.15 [−0.26, −0.03], p = 0.012), but not with attention/processing speed (β = −0.07 [−0.17, 0.03], p = 0.180) or language (β = −0.06 [−0.13, 0.01], p = 0.100) (Figure 3C).

Age‐ and sex‐matched cohort characteristics

Participant characteristics for the age‐ and sex‐matched cohort are presented in Table S2. The matched sample included 1032 individuals with equal representation across groups (NHW n = 344, NHB n = 344, Hispanic n = 344). Despite matching, age remained slightly lower in Hispanic participants compared to NHW and NHB (p < 0.001), while the proportion of females differed modestly across groups (p < 0.001). Identical significant trends were observed in the matched sample as the full sample.

Associations in the matched cohort

In adjusted models within the matched cohort (Figure 4A), higher p‐Tau217 was associated with worse delayed memory (β = −0.08, [−0.14, −0.01], p = 0.018) and language (β = −0.07, [−0.14, −0.00], p = 0.038), but not with other domains. In race and ethnic group stratified matched analyses, p‐Tau217 was not significantly associated with cognitive performance among NHW participants (Figure 4B). Among NHB participants, a marginal association was observed for delayed memory (β = −0.13, p = 0.056) (Figure 4C). Among Hispanic participants, higher p‐Tau217 was associated with lower language performance (β = −0.16, p = 0.030), with no other significant associations (Figure 4D).

NfL associations with cognition (unmatched cohort)

In stratified multivariable models, higher NfL was associated with worse executive function among NHW participants (β = −0.11, 95% CI −0.16 to −0.05; p < 0.001) and marginally with language (p = 0.054) (Figure 5A—top panel). Among NHB participants, higher NfL was associated with worse attention/processing speed (β = −0.13, p = 0.004) and executive function (β = −0.12, p = 0.008) (Figure 5B – middle panel). No significant associations between NfL and cognitive scores were observed among Hispanic participants (Figure 5C – bottom panel). In the matched cohort, associations between NfL and different cognitive domains within each racial and ethnic group were identical to the unmatched (Figure 5B).

Assessing the independent associations between p‐Tau217 and NfL

In joint models including both plasma p‐Tau217 and NfL (Table S3), associations differed by race and ethnic groups. In NHW participants, higher p‐Tau217 was associated with worse immediate memory (β = −0.07), delayed memory (β = −0.08), and attention (β = −0.06); NfL was associated with executive function (β = −0.09). In NHB, p‐Tau217 related to delayed memory (β = −0.15) and executive function (β = 0.20), while NfL related to attention (β = −0.16) and executive function (β = −0.17). In Hispanic participants, p‐Tau217 was associated with delayed memory (β = −0.12) and executive function (β = −0.14), with no NfL associations. In the matched sample, the effects for NHW and Hispanic participants were fully attenuated (Table S4).

DISCUSSION

The objective of this study was to evaluate whether plasma p‐Tau217 was associated with domain‐specific cognitive performance in clinically unimpaired individuals and to determine whether these associations differed across NHW, NHB, and Hispanic participants. Despite lower p‐Tau217 levels, NHB and Hispanic participants demonstrated consistently lower cognitive performance across all domains relative to NHW participants. Overall, higher plasma p‐Tau217 was associated with worse performance across memory, attention/processing speed, executive function, and language cognitive domains. Among NHW participants, higher p‐Tau217 was consistently associated with lower cognitive scores across all domains. In NHB participants, with consistent associations observed only for delayed memory, and among Hispanic participants, higher p‐Tau217 was associated with worse memory and executive function. Sensitivity analyses in an age‐ and sex‐matched cohort attenuated many of these associations, suggesting that demographic differences (likely older age in the NHW participants) partially contribute to observed patterns but do not fully explain them.

Most prior work on plasma p‐Tau217 has emphasized its diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing AD from controls or its concordance with amyloid and tau PET imaging. 33 , 34 , 35 Far fewer studies have examined its relationship with specific cognitive domains, and those that do often focus on global cognition or singular tests. 14 , 15 Our results address this gap by demonstrating that increases in plasma p‐Tau217 is most consistently associated with lower memory (immediate and delayed) and executive function scores, with additional but less consistent associations observed for attention/processing speed across race and ethnic groups. Previous findings show that higher levels of plasma p‐Tau217 was significantly associated with longitudinal changes across multiple cognitive outcomes, including immediate memory, delayed memory, global cognition, and executive function in a 94.8% NHW sample. 36

Our cross‐sectional findings are aligned with prior longitudinal findings demonstrating that plasma p‐Tau217 is associated with memory domains of cognition in NHW participants, with less consistent associations observed in NHB and Hispanic participants at the cross‐sectional level of our study. Importantly, these findings should be interpreted in the context of age‐related accumulation of amyloid and tau, as NHW participants were older in the overall sample. When we matched on age and sex, these stratified associations were attenuated or no longer observed. Notably, in the matched subsample, NHW participants continued to have higher plasma p‐Tau217 levels and better cognitive performance relative to NHB and Hispanic participants. Consistent with these findings, p‐Tau217 was most strongly associated with delayed memory and executive function, aligning with prior evidence that higher baseline p‐Tau217 is associated with increased risk of conversion to AD dementia. 14

Another critical gap in the literature concerns the generalizability of plasma biomarkers across populations, which is the most consistent limitation across p‐Tau217 studies. 14 , 15 , 37 , 38 , 39 The majority of biomarker studies to date have relied on predominantly NHW and European cohorts, leaving uncertainty about performance in underrepresented groups. 14 , 15 , 37 , 40 This study highlights a critical dissociation: biomarker evidence of AD pathology does not align with the degree of cognitive impairment in non‐White populations although our findings are limited by clinically unimpaired participants. Similar discrepancies have been reported in autopsy studies, where Black and Hispanic individuals often show a greater burden of mixed pathologies, including vascular injury and hippocampal sclerosis, despite lower amyloid or tau positivity. 41 , 42 Mechanistically, this may reflect contributions from cerebrovascular disease, inflammation, or TDP‐43 proteinopathy, which are not captured by p‐Tau217 assays but may disproportionately drive lower cognition in minoritized groups. Yet, when we utilized plasma NfL as a biomarker of neurodegeneration, we observed that higher NfL was associated with lower executive function in NHW and NHB participants, consistent with our previous findings in HABS‐HD. 26 This study does not provide definitive evidence of the pathological mechanisms underlying lower cognition in NHB and Hispanic groups. However, the findings suggest that both biological factors (plasma biomarkers) and structural and social determinants of health 43 likely contribute to observed differences in cognitive performance in this clinically unimpaired sample drawn from a community‐based cohort.

This study has several limitations. First, the cross‐sectional design limits our ability to assess longitudinal trajectories or determine whether these associations predict cognitive decline or disease stage conversion. Second, we included a limited set of biomarkers, p‐Tau217 and NfL. Third, we did not account for structural determinants of health or environmental exposures known to influence ADRD risk. Fourth, the smaller sample size of NHB participants warrants caution in interpretation, although this was partially addressed through age‐ and sex‐matched analyses. Lastly, our findings may not be generalizable beyond this community‐based cohort from the Dallas–Fort Worth Texas area.

CONCLUSION

The observed pattern of lower p‐Tau217 levels alongside poorer cognitive performance in NHB and Hispanic participants suggests that reliance on amyloid‐ and tau‐based biomarkers alone may not fully capture the drivers of cognitive differences across groups. Future work should include longitudinal follow‐up to determine whether plasma p‐Tau217 and related biomarkers predict cognitive decline, MCI, and ADRD incidence equally across race and ethnic groups, particularly with respect to disease conversion. Additionally, studies should be expanded in other diverse community‐based populations to address disparities in AD detection and care, particularly during the preclinical and prodromal phases when early changes are detectable. This approach aligns with emerging interventions targeting plasma biomarker‐defined pathology to evaluate whether they can delay cognitive impairment and prevent progression to MCI and dementia. 44

RAD51-AS1 inhibits the migration and invasion of prostate cancer cells by up-regulating RARA to activate ZYX transcription
Authors: Zhijun Chen, Xinwei Wang, Likai Mao, Han Guan, Ming Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundProstate cancer (PCa) is one of the most prevailing cancers threatening men health around the world. Mounting studies have revealed that dysregulated long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) might contribute to cancer development. RAD51-AS1 has been reported as an tumor-suppressor in different diseases, but not in PCa. Therefore, we aimed to explore the role of RAD51-AS1 and the underlying regulatory mechanism in PCa.MethodsRT-qPCR was conducted for the measurement of RNA levels. Bioinformatics analysis was performed for screening out possible research targets. Mechanism assays were implemented for detecting relations among different factors, while functional assays were employed to determine the function of genes in PCa cells.ResultRAD51-AS1 expression was found to be low in PCa cells, which resulted in exacerbated PCa cell migration and invasion. RAD51-AS1 recruited HNRNPC in PCa cell nucleus to bind with pre-RARA, thus positively modulating RARA expression. RARA activated ZYX transcription to enhance ZYX expression in PCa cells. Rescue assay substantiated RAD51-AS1 inhibited PCa cell migration and invasion through modulating ZYX expression.ConclusionThe anti-oncogenic role of RAD51-AS1 was realized through HNRNPC/RARA/ZYX axis, which might offer a novel insight for understanding PCa.Graphical Abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-03567-z.

Introduction

According to American Cancer Society, prostate cancer (PCa) is the most common malignancy in men [1]. PCa has an individual heterogeneity, and its incidence and mortality are associated with a multitude of factors, including geography, ethnicity, and genetics [2]. The prostate-specific antigen test lacks specificity for the detection of PCa and can lead to unnecessary prostate biopsies [3]. Considering the complexity of the molecular mechanisms and the instability of detection modalities, the emerging research is increasingly focused on novel detection and therapeutic targets for PCa, as well as prognostic biomarkers, aiming to provide new strategies for cancer diagnosis and treatment. The long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) also play an important role in this process.

Involvement of lncRNAs in the development of PCa is widely discovered. For instance, lncRNA CCAT1 has been proved to accelerate PCa cell migration through DDX5/MIR-28-5p axis [4]. LncRNA MEG3 is examined to restrain PCa progression via miR-9-5p/QKI-5 axis. LncRNA RAD51-AS1 (RAD51-AS1) has been validated to exert inhibitory influence on cell migration and invasion in colorectal cancer [5]. Meanwhile, RAD51-AS1 has also been confirmed to impede hepatocellular carcinoma cell proliferation and suppress DNA repair [6]. On this basis, we aimed to investigate into the role of RAD51-AS1 in PCa. Specifically, we also intended to explore the underlying regulatory mechanism of RAD51-AS1 in PCa. As bioinformatics analysis and previous report [7] evidenced the nuclear location of RAD51-AS1, we took RNA binding protein (RBP) interaction into consideration [8]. Existing studies have hinted that lncRNAs could recruit RBP to regulate downstream genes expressing, thus influencing PCa cell behaviors [9]. Herein, we also tried to screen out the RBP of RAD51-AS1 in PCa cells, through which RAD51-AS1 could realize its inhibitory function in PCa cell migration and invasion.

In a word, this study mainly focused on the exploration of how RAD51-AS1 functioned in PCa cells, with an aim to provide a novel understanding of PCa from a molecular level. Hopefully, our study could contribute to development of PCa biomarkers in diagnosis or treatment in the further.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

Human prostatic epithelial cell line (RWPE-1) was procured from China Center for Type Culture Collection (CCTCC). PCa cell lines DU145 and LNCaP were procured from Cell Bank of Institute of Basic Medical Sciences Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (Beijing, China), and PCa cell line 22Rv1 was purchased from Center for Excellence in Molecular Cell Science. Additionally, human embryonic kidney 293 T cells were procured from National Institute for the Control of Pharmaceutical and Biological Products (NICPBP). RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) was applied for the culture of DU145, 22RV1, LNCaP and 293 T cell lines and RWPE-1 cells were cultured in DMEM-H containing 10% FBS. A humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37 °C was applied for the culture of all cell lines.

Cell transfection

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting RAD51-AS1, HNRNPC, RARA and ZYX were procured for the knockdown of RAD51-AS1, HNRNPC, RARA and ZYX in cells, and corresponding negative control (si-NCs) obtained as well. The sequences of relevant siRNAs were presented in Table S1. Six siRNAs targeting RAD51-AS1 were used in this study, but none of them reduced RAD51-AS1 expression by over 50%. Therefore, we designed and synthesized three antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) targeting RAD51-AS1 and a negative control (NC), provided by Ribobio (Guangzhou, China).The target sequences of RAD51-AS1 targeting ASOs were as follows: ASO1-TCCGCGAGTTCTCACCATCG, ASO2- ASO1-TCCGCGAGTTCTCACCATCG, ASO2-TCCTAGCTACTCGAAAGGCT and ASO3-GCATGGAAACGAACTACATG. Additionally, pcDNA3.1-RAD51-AS1 and pcDNA3.1-RARA were designed for the overexpression of RAD51-AS1 and RARA by sub-cloning the whole sequence of RAD51-AS1 and RARA into pcDNA3.1 vectors, and empty pcDNA3.1 was applied as the control. The transfection of indicated plasmids was completed with Lipofectamine 3000. Cells were selected after transfection for harvesting cell lines with stable silence or overexpression.

Reverse transcription quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Briefly, subsequent to the isolation of total RNA with TRIzol Reagent, the extracted RNA was reversely transcribed for the acquisition of cDNA as per the standard protocol. Then, qRT-PCR Kit was applied for analyzing the expression levels of the detected genes, and the expression of the indicated genes was measured by the 2−ΔΔCT method with β-actin as internal reference. The sequences of the relevant primers were shown in Table S2. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

Western blot

Briefly, RIPA buffer was applied for the acquisition of protein lysates, and the isolation of total protein was completed with PROTTOT-1KT, followed by the measurement of the protein concentration. Later, subsequent to the treatment with SDS-PAGE (10%), the proteins were transferred to the PVDF membranes which were blocked by 5% skimmed milk and cultivated with specific primary antibodies overnight. Secondary antibodies were then added for further cultivation at room temperature. The measurement of proteins was conducted with the help of enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) detection system. The relevant antibody information was shown in Table S3.

Wound healing assay

Wound healing assay was done for the evaluation of PCa cell migration. After cells transfected with indicated plasmids were grown to 80% confluence, a pipette tip (200 μl) was applied for scratching the cell monolayer, followed by 24-h cell incubation in standard conditions. Images were taken at indicated time points (0 h and 24 h) with an inverted microscope for further analysis. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

Transwell assay

Transwell migration assay was conducted for the evaluation of cell migration in PCa. The transfected cells were gathered and suspended in the upper chamber with serum-free medium while the lower chamber was supplemented with 10% FBS. After 24-h incubation, cells on the upper surface of the membrane were removed. Subsequent to fixation with methanol and dying with crystal violets, the migrated cells in 5 randomly selected fields was counted under microscope. Similarly, for the evaluation of PCa cell invasion, the transwell invasion assay was conducted except that the upper chamber was coated with Matrigel. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH)

FISH assay was performed with RiboTM Fluorescent In Situ Hybridization Kit to determine the subcellular distribution of RAD51-AS1 in PC a cells. Briefly, subsequent to fixation in 4% paraformaldehyde at room temperature, 0.5% TritonX-100 was applied to permeabilize DU145 and LNCaP cells for 15 min at 4 °C. After PBS washing process, cells were co-cultured with the FISH probe targeting RAD51-AS1. After nuclear staining with DAPI, laser scanning confocal microscope was applied for observation. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

RNA binding protein immunoprecipitation (RIP) assay

RNA Immunoprecipitation Kit was applied to perform RIP assay, with the manufacture instructions strictly followed. DU145 cell lysates were cultured with anti-IgG (negative control) or anti-HNRNPC conjugated with magnetic beads. Subsequent to isolation of the immunoprecipitated RNA, RT-qPCR analysis was done to measure the enrichment of indicated gene. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

RNA pull-down

To verify the potential correlation between HNRNPC and pre-RARA, biotin-labeled pre-RARA (47,034–47434) antisense and pre-RARA (47,034–47434) sense WT/MUT were applied in RNA pull-down assay. In brief, total proteins extracted from cells were incubated with biotinylated pre-RARA (47,034–47434) sense WT/MUT or pre-RARA (47,034–47434) antisense, followed by 1-h incubation with streptavidin magnetic beads. At length, the pulled-down proteins were processed with SDS-PAGE for western blot analysis. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

DNA pull-down

DNA pull-down assay was done for evaluation of the potential correlation between ZYX and RARA. Briefly, biotinylated ZYX promoter probe (Bio-ZYX promoter) and the negative control (Bio-NC) were co-cultured with DU145 cell lysates. The input group was considered as the positive control. Streptavidin magnetic beads were then added for the further cultivation with the DNA–protein complex. In length, after the proteins were eluted from DNA, western blot analysis was done for the measurement of RARA protein levels. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

Luciferase reporter assay

Briefly, ZYX promoter with wild-type or mutated was sub-cloned into pGL3 vector to construct pGL3 + ZYX promoter WT/MUT, which was co-transfected with pcDNA3.1 and pcDNA3.1-RARA into 293 T cells. Lipofectamine 3000 was applied for the co-transfection, and the Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit was applied for the analysis of luciferase activity in the indicated groups after 48-h transfection.

Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP)

The enrichment of ZYX promoter in anti-RARA or anti-IgG (the negative control) was assessed with a ChIP kit. Briefly, the crosslinked chromatin was first cut into fragments for the immunoprecipitation with anti-RARA or anti-IgG, together with magnetic beads. Subsequent to extensive washing process and elution of immunoprecipitated complexes, cross-links were reversed. In length, RT-qPCR analysis was done for the measurement of the precipitated DNA fragments after purification. During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing.

Statistical analysis

During the experimental design, we repeated each experiment three times in order to make the results more convincing. The experimental data was presented as the mean ± standard deviation and Student’s t-test or analysis of variance (ANOVA) was applied for the analysis of the differences between different groups. P < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

RAD51-AS1 inhibits PCa cell migration and invasion

To probe into the role of RAD51-AS1 in PCa, GEPIA2 (http://gepia2.cancer-pku.cn/#index) was first utilized for acquiring the expression level of RAD51-AS1 in PCa tissues and normal tissues. As was depicted in Fig. 1A, RAD51-AS1 expression was significantly lower in PCa tissues than in normal tissues. Then, RAD51-AS1 expression in normal human prostate cell line (RWPE-1) and PCa cell lines (DU145, LNCaP and 22Rv1) were measured via RT-qPCR, the result of which manifested that RAD51-AS1 expression in 22Rv1, DU145 and LNCaP was obviously down-regulated (Fig. 1B). After we successfully elevated RAD51-AS1 expression in DU145 and LNCaP cells (with the lowest RAD51-AS1 level) by transfection of pcDNA3.1-RAD51-AS1 (Fig. 1C), functional assays were conducted to determine RAD51-AS1 influence on PCa cell migration and invasion. Wound healing assay results indicated PCa cell migratory capability was mitigated after RAD51-AS1 was overexpressed (Fig. 1D). Transwell assay showed that successfully migrated and invaded PCa cells were less in pcDNA3.1-RAD51-AS1 transfected group than in pcDNA3.1 transfected group, unveiling RAD51-AS1 overexpression restrained PCa migratory and invasive ability (Fig. 1E, F). To inversely reconfirm the anti-cancer function of RAD51-AS1, we knocked down RAD51-AS1 in DU145 and LNCaP cell lines, and observed a significant increase in the migratory and invasive ability of prostate cancer cells (Figure S5A-B). Moreover, this study showed no significant enhancement of migration and invasion ability in RWPE1 cells by overexpressing and knocking down RAD51-AS1 in normal prostatic RWPE1 cells (Figure S5C-D). Consistently, expression of invasion related proteins (MMP2 and MMP9) [10] was also detected to be decreased after RAD51-AS1 augment through RT-qPCR and western blot (Fig. 1G, Figure S5E-F). Afterwards, the cellular distribution of RAD51-AS1 was predicted through lncATLAS database (https://lncatlas.crg.eu/). The result unmasked RAD51-AS1 was mainly distributed in cell nuclei (Fig. 1H), which was further proved through FISH assay (Fig. 1I). And after nuclear and cytosolic separation, RNA expression levels were measured by qRT-PCR, β-actin was used as a cytosolic marker and U6 was used as a nuclear marker, which showed RAD51-AS1 was present in both cytoplasm and nucleus, but mainly in nucleus (Figure S5G). RAD51-AS1 recruits HNRNPC to promote RARA expression

Referring to previous study, nuclear lncRNAs might function by interacting with RBP [11]. Therefore, we predicted the possible RBP of RAD51-AS1 by searching on starBase (http://starbase.sysu.edu.cn/) under the condition of CLIP-Data ≥ 6 (Fig. 2A). The cellular location of the four possible RBPs was predicted on Hum-mPLoc database (http://www.csbio.sjtu.edu.cn/bioinf/Hum-mPLoc3/). According to the prediction, three RBPs, namely, HNRNPC, U2AF2 and DGCR8 were further picked (Figure S1A-D). After RIP assay, HNRNPC was chosen out as our research target for RAD51-AS1 enrichment was substantial in anti-HNRNPC (Fig. 2B). HNRNPC has been discovered to recognize the pre-mRNA m6A site of its target gene, so as to modulate the maturity and expression of target genes [12]. Herein, we utilized starBase (ClusterNum ≥ 19, CLIP Data ≥ 8, Cluster Num ≥ 25, pan-Cancer ≥ 20) to search for the mRNAs that might interact with HNRNPC. After we listed these possible mRNAs in a ClusterNum ascending order, we further selected the 10 mRNAs (Fig. 2C). UALCAN database (http://ualcan.path.uab.edu/) was applied for acquiring the expression pattern of the 10 mRNAs in PCa tissues and normal prostate tissues. Only MAX and RARA showed obvious lower expression in PCa tissues than in normal prostate tissues (Figure S2A-J). Based on existing publications, MAX could function as transcription factor to modulated PCa progression by activating transcription of different genes [13, 14]. RARA has also been proved to inhibit acute promyelocytic leukemia [15]. Then, the pre-mRNAs of the two candidates were both predicted to be able to bind with HNRNPC on RPISeq (http://pridb.gdcb.iastate.edu/RPISeq/) (Figure S3A-B). Considering that HNRNPC was nuclear protein and it recognized the m6A site of pre-mRNAs in the nucleus [16], we focused on the pre-mRNAs in following experiments. Through RIP assay, it was discovered that compared with pre-MAX, pre-RARA was evidently more precipitated in anti-HNRNPC (Fig. 2D). Therefore, RARA was ultimately chosen as the target of HNRNPC in this study. The m6A methylation site of pre-RARA was predicted on SRAMP (http://www.cuilab.cn/sramp/), and the result implied there was a high possible m6A methylation site (47,232) (Fig. 2E). NCBI database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) also confirmed that the mature RARA 3’UTR region was located at 47,034–47434 of pre-RARA. Thereof, Me-RIP assay was conducted to evaluate pre-RARA (47,034–47434) enrichment in anti-HNRNPC, and the binding between HNRNPC and pre-RARA (47,034–47434) was ascertained (Fig. 2F). In RNA pull-down assay, pre-RARA (47,034–47434) sense WT could pull down HNRNPC while pre-RARA (47,034–47434) sense Mut and pre-RARA (47,034–47434) antisense could not, further confirming the interaction between HNRNPC and pre-RARA (47,034–47434) (Fig. 2G). Then, we measured the expression of HNRNPC or RARA through RT-qPCR in RAD51-AS1 overexpressed cells. As was depicted in Fig. 2H, 2I, HNRNPC expression was not influenced by RAD51-AS1 while RARA expression was elevated in response to RAD51-AS1 up-regulation. Then, we also decreased HNRNPC expression in DU145 cells (Fig. 2J) and then detected RARA expression in HNRNPC silenced cells. According to RT-qPCR, RARA expression declined as a result of HNRNPC silence (Fig. 2K). Afterwards, it was found that the decreased RARA expression due to HNRNPC knockdown could not be reversed by RAD51-AS1 overexpression, hinting RAD51-AS1 regulation on RARA was mediated by HNRNPC (Fig. 2L). RAD51-AS1 promotes ZYX transcription through RARA

RARA as a transcription factor could inhibit cancer development by inducing the expression of its target genes [15]. Thereafter, we also tried to figure out the downstream gene of RARA. By employing UALCAN database, we obtained 5 candidates with highest Pearson-CC value, which were positively correlated with RARA in PCa (Fig. 3A). The expression pattern of the 5 candidate mRNAs was predicted on UALCAN (Figure S4A-D). Among the 5 possible RARA downstream genes, TMEM51 was predicted to display low expression in PCa tissues, but there were no relevant studies discussing the role of TMEM51 in PCa. ZYX has been validated to inhibit the progression of different cancers including PCa [17, 18]. FHL2 has been confirmed to promote PCa development [19], which conflicted with our bioinformatics prediction. STOM has been little studied in cancer development. PLEKHO2 has been reported to promote colorectal cancer [20] but its role in PCa has not been discussed yet. On this basis, we tested expression of TMEM51, ZYX and PLEKHO2 in RARA overexpressed DU145 cells, and only ZYX expression showed notable increase in response to RARA up-regulation (Fig. 3B). Then, the prediction on Animal TFDB3.0 database (http://bioinfo.life.hust.edu.cn/AnimalTFDB/) indicated the interaction between RARA and ZYX promoter and it was most likely for RARA to bind with ZYX promoter at site 858–874 (Fig. 3C). Therefore, we hypothesized that RARA could induce ZYX transcription to enhance its expression. To validate our assumption, DNA pull-down assay was carried out, and the result displayed that RARA could be much pulled down by Bio-ZYX promoter, evidencing the cohesion between ZYX promoter and RARA (Fig. 3D). Then, we mutated the binding site of RARA with highest score on ZYX promoter, and constructed pGL3 + ZYX promoter WT and pGL3 + ZYX promoter MUT to co-transfect with pcDNA3.1-RARA. As a result, the luciferase activity of pGL3 + ZYX promoter WT was strengthened by increased RARA expression, while that of pGL3 + ZYX promoter MUT showed no obvious change (Fig. 3E). Additionally, in ChIP assay, the enrichment of ZYX promoter was substantial in Anti-RARA (Fig. 3F). Next, we knocked down RARA by transfecting si-RARA into cells (Fig. 3G). After co-transfecting RAD51-AS1 overexpressed cells with si-RARA, it was found that the augmented ZYX expression in response to RAD51-AS1 overexpression was totally reversed by si-RARA co-transfection (Fig. 3H). RAD51-AS1 modulates ZYX expression to affect PCa cell migration and invasion

Rescue experiments were designed to corroborate RAD51-AS1 repressed PCa cell migration and invasion by modulating ZYX expression. The knockdown efficiency of si-ZYX was first tested by RT-qPCR (Fig. 4A). In wound healing assay, the wound healing ability weakened by RAD51-AS1 overexpression was rescued after co-transfection of si-ZYX (Fig. 4B). In transwell assay, the mitigated cell migration and invasion due to up-regulation of RAD51-AS1 was countered by ZYX inhibition (Fig. 4C). Through RT-qPCR assay, the decreased MMP2 and MMP9 expression in RAD51-AS1 up-regulated cells was discovered to be reversed when si-ZYX was co-transfected (Fig. 4D). Discussion

PCa is one of the most common malignancies affecting male health worldwide, threatening their life and health, as well as leading to a high mortality rate due to its advanced metastasis and resistance to treatment [21]. Emerging evidence has revealed the crucial regulatory roles of lncRNAs in tumorigenesis and diverse cellular functions in various malignancies, including PCa [22, 23]. For instance, lncRNA LBCS has been validated as a novel translational regulator of Androgen Receptor, which interacts with hnRNPK to exert its inhibitory impacts on castration resistance of PCa [24]. SNHG14, up-regulated in PCa tissues and cell lines, has been verified to be associated with poor diagnosis for PCa patients and to facilitate PCa cell proliferation and invasion via the modulation on miR-5590-3p and YY1 [25]. Additionally, lncRNA MIR4435-2HG has been ascertained to function as a facilitator in the modulation of PCa cell migration and invasion via TGF-β1 [26]. In current study, it was learnt from bioinformatics analysis and experimental results that RAD51-AS1 was down-regulated in PCa tissues and cell lines, and it was discovered that RAD51-AS1 impeded PCa cell migration and invasion.

In addition to ceRNA mechanism, the interaction between lncRNAs and RBPs has also been considered as a crucial part in the regulatory mechanism of lncRNAs implicated in malignant tumors [27, 28]. For instance, lncRNA FGF13-AS1, down-regulated in breast cancer tissues, functions as a suppressor in the modulation of breast cancer cell glycolysis and stemness via the interaction with IGF2BPs and modulation on Myc which can regulate FGF13-AS1 transcription [29]. LIMD1-AS1, down-regulated in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) tissues and cell lines, can interact with hnRNP U to enhance LIMD1 mRNA, thus suppressing NSCLC progression [30]. The interaction between lncRNA ZFPM2-AS1 and UPF1 is also discovered to be involved in ZFPM2-AS1 regulation mechanism in lung adenocarcinoma [31]. Similarly, in present study, the results of bioinformatics analysis and FISH assay suggested the nuclear distribution of RAD51-AS1 in PCa cells, implying that RAD51-AS1 might interact with certain RBP to exert its functions in PCa cells. In addition to bioinformatics analysis and reference to previous literature, the results of mechanism assays including RIP, Me-RIP, RNA pull-down demonstrated that RAD51-AS1 could recruit RBP HNRNPC to modulate the expression of the downstream target gene RARA. Intriguingly, RARA was also a transcription factor, which has been validated to activate the transcription of its downstream gene to affect the progression of different malignancies [15, 32]. Consistently, based on results of informatics analysis and mechanism experiments, the positive correlation between RARA and ZYX was confirmed, and moreover, RAD51-AS1 could modulate ZYX expression transcriptionally via RARA. Additionally, the results of current rescue experiments reflected that RAD51-AS1 could exert its inhibitory influences on migratory and invasive abilities of PCa cells via the modulation on ZYX. Thence, the role of RAD51-AS1/HNRNPC/RARA/ZYX axis in PCa cells was validated (Graphical abstract). Regrettably, due to the limitations of experimental and clinical conditions, this study did not do intensive exploration for aggressive CRPC. It is hoped that in future studies, we can explore more about the mechanism of RAD51-AS1 in prostate cancer, including castration-resistance and neuroendocrine prostate cancer formation. It is worth noting that in addition to animal experiments, a large-scale of clinical samples should be involved for the further confirmation of RAD51-AS1/HNRNPC/RARA/ZYX axis in PCa, which are considered as our major tasks in the future study.

Conclusion

RAD51-AS1, down-regulated in PCa, could impede PCa cell migration and invasion via the interaction with HNRNPC and the transcriptional modulation on ZYX expression. The current findings can provide a novel perspective for understanding the molecular mechanisms implicated in PCa, thus contributing to the discovery of novel effective biomarkers for PCa treatment.

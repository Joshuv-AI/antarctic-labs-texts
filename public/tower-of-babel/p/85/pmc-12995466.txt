Introduction

Prostate cancer is one of the most commonly diagnosed malignancies among men in Japan, and prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing is an established tool for early detection (Barry, 2001; US Preventive Services Task Force, 2018; Fenton et al., 2018). In Europe, the European Randomized Study of Screening for Prostate Cancer (ERSPC) demonstrated a reduction in prostate cancer mortality through PSA-based screening (Hugosson et al., 2009; Hugosson and Carlsson, 2014; Hugosson et al., 2019), although concerns regarding overdiagnosis and unnecessary biopsies have been highlighted. More recently, extended follow-up data from the ERSPC with a median follow-up of 23 years have been reported, providing updated evidence on the long-term benefits and harms of PSA-based screening (Roobol et al., 2025). In the United States, the 2018 USPSTF statement recommends optional screening for men aged 55–69 years based on shared decision-making (US Preventive Services Task Force, 2018; Fenton et al., 2018).

In Japan, PSA screening has been implemented under municipal administration rather than as a nationwide unified program. Local initiatives and long-term follow-up studies, such as those conducted in Kusatsu City, have been reported (Kageyama et al., 2022; Hongo et al., 2019), and efforts have been made to limit overdiagnosis by tailoring eligible age groups and setting appropriate PSA thresholds. Osaka City introduced a publicly funded prostate cancer screening program in fiscal year 2021, adopting age-specific PSA cut-off values (Osaka City Government, 2023). Recent domestic data have reported the use of similar age-specific PSA thresholds (Terao et al., 2024).

However, data describing the early implementation and operational characteristics of such municipal programs at the clinic level remain limited.

The objective of this study was to describe the early clinic-level implementation of Osaka City's municipality-led prostate cancer screening program at a community clinic, focusing on annual participation patterns and referral outcomes under the age-specific PSA threshold framework.

Methods

Study design and population

This was a retrospective, single-center descriptive observational study that included 60 men who underwent the Osaka City prostate cancer screening program at our clinic between fiscal years 2021 and 2025 (up to August 2025). According to municipal policy, eligible individuals were men who reached ages 50, 55, 60, 65, or 70 during the respective fiscal year.

All eligible individuals who underwent screening at our clinic during the study period were included, and no exclusions were applied.

Measures

Serum PSA values were truncated to two decimal places. The need for further examination was determined based on Osaka City's age-specific PSA cut-off values: 3.0 ng/mL for ages 50–64, 3.5 ng/mL for ages 65–69, and 4.0 ng/mL for ages ≥70 (Osaka City Government, 2023).

Because these thresholds are applied according to age on the day of testing, individuals who participated before their birthday within an eligible fiscal year were evaluated using the cut-off for the younger age category.

Individuals who screened positive were referred from our clinic to urology specialists, and the results of confirmatory examinations were returned to Osaka City via our clinic as part of an established reporting system.

In October 2023, Osaka City initiated a municipal postcard recommendation to encourage eligible residents to participate in the prostate cancer screening program. This study did not assess individual-level exposure to the postcard.

Statistical analysis

All analyses were descriptive. Annual counts and proportions were summarized without inferential statistical testing, given the small sample size and descriptive nature of the study. Descriptive summaries were prepared using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA).

Ethical considerations

This study was conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines for the protection of human subjects. Ethical approval (or exemption) was obtained from the appropriate institutional review board.

Results

The numbers of examinees were 5 in fiscal year 2021, 6 in fiscal year 2022, 20 in fiscal year 2023, 23 in fiscal year 2024, and 6 in fiscal year 2025 (up to August). Five men (8.3%) required further evaluation, of whom one case (a 64-year-old man with a PSA level of 3.6 ng/mL) was diagnosed with prostate cancer, while the remaining four cases were all diagnosed with benign prostatic hyperplasia.

Discussion

This study presents the initial outcomes of Osaka City's municipality-led prostate cancer screening program as implemented in a community clinic.

In Japan, cancer screening programs are administered by municipalities, and both implementation and funding are determined at the local government level. Although PSA-based prostate cancer screening is conducted in many municipalities, it is not implemented under a fully uniform national framework, and the extent to which eligibility criteria and operational details are standardized nationwide has not been systematically confirmed.

In this clinic-level cohort, five of 60 participants (8.3%) exceeded the age-specific PSA thresholds and were referred for further evaluation, and one case (1.7%) was subsequently diagnosed with prostate cancer. These findings provide descriptive information on referral frequency and diagnostic outcomes under the municipal age-specific PSA threshold framework.

Evidence from large-scale trials conducted in Europe and the United States has established both the benefits (mortality reduction) and risks (overdiagnosis, unnecessary biopsies) associated with PSA screening (US Preventive Services Task Force, 2018; Fenton et al., 2018; Hugosson et al., 2009; Hugosson and Carlsson, 2014; Hugosson et al., 2019). In Japan, municipal programs aim to provide equitable access to screening by limiting the eligible age range and combining public funding with outreach activities (Kageyama et al., 2022; Osaka City Government, 2023).

The age-specific PSA thresholds used by Osaka City (3.0, 3.5, and 4.0 ng/mL) are consistent with the reported use of similar thresholds in domestic studies, such as that of Terao et al. (2024), and were readily applicable in clinical practice.

Importantly, this study was not designed to evaluate the diagnostic performance or potential harms associated with different PSA threshold strategies. Although higher cut-off values were applied in older age groups according to municipal policy, no PSA values between 3.0 and 3.5 ng/mL were observed in the older age group in this cohort. Therefore, the potential impact of alternative uniform cut-off values on cancer detection cannot be assessed based on the present data. Evaluation of missed diagnoses or harm related to threshold selection would require larger, population-based studies with longer follow-up.

During the study period, Osaka City introduced a municipal recommendation postcard; however, this study did not evaluate individual exposure to the postcard or its potential influence on participation.

The screening program involved a three-tier framework linking community clinics, urology specialists, and municipal health authorities, through which screening results and referral outcomes were administratively reported.

Internationally, discussions regarding the cost-effectiveness and policy implications of PSA screening continue, with a recent systematic review suggesting that risk-stratified screening strategies may enhance cost-effectiveness in Europe (Vynckier et al., 2024). The Japanese model—characterized by age-restricted eligibility and administrative oversight—differs from risk-stratified strategies adopted in some European settings. Further comparative research may help clarify how different organizational models influence screening implementation and outcomes.

Limitations and strengths

This study has several limitations. First, it was conducted at a single community clinic and included a small number of participants, limiting generalizability.

Second, denominator data for the eligible population were not available at the clinic level, precluding estimation of participation or coverage rates. Although individual-level denominator data were unavailable, publicly available demographic statistics indicate that Osaka City has a total population of approximately 2.8 million residents, suggesting that the number of eligible men aged 50–70 years substantially exceeds the 60 men included in this clinic-based report.

Third, individual-level exposure to the municipal postcard recommendation was not assessed.

However, this study provides detailed clinic-level descriptive data on the early implementation of a municipality-led prostate cancer screening program, contributing to understanding of its operational characteristics in real-world practice and informing the feasibility of municipal-level implementation in similar settings.

Conclusion

This study descriptively reports the initial implementation of Osaka City's municipality-led prostate cancer screening program in a community clinic setting. Age-specific prostate-specific antigen cut-off values defined by the municipal program were applied consistently in routine clinical practice, and clinic-level participation numbers and referral outcomes during the early phase of implementation were summarized.

These findings provide descriptive clinic-level information on the operational characteristics of a municipality-led prostate cancer screening program in Japan and offer early real-world evidence to inform future municipal screening strategies. Further multi-center studies incorporating broader administrative and clinical data are warranted to better characterize participation patterns and to evaluate program-level outcomes of municipality-led prostate cancer screening initiatives in Japan.

Analysis of the positive expiratory pressure valves of coupled oronasal mask
Authors: Fabiola Silva Bezerra, Frank Silva Bezerra, Marcelo Carlos Ribeiro, Maria Glória Rodrigues-Machado, Rudolf Huebner
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractPositive expiratory pressure (PEP) is a technique used in respiratory physiotherapy to treat diseases related to the respiratory system through spontaneous breathing. This equipment consists of an oronasal mask coupled to a T connector with a unidirectional valve. Studies that evaluate whether the pressure level in the one-way valve corresponds to the actual pressure level provided are scarce in the scientific literature. In order to investigate the failures, bench tests were carried out on the spring-loaded valves, using a U-tube manometer. This pressure was exerted on the valve using a syringe that generated air flow inside the U-tube, allowing analysis numerical value of the measured pressure and the specified values of the valve, thus verifying the disparity of these measured values in relation to the PEP values operated by valves (0 to 20 cmH2O) from the three manufacturers under study. PEPs generated by spring-loaded valves from all three manufacturers were higher than pressures in the range of 2.5 to 20 cmH2O, with significant differences between manufacturers. This bench study showed inaccurate operation of all spring-loaded PEP valves of the three manufactures. The results obtained and the performance of the valves require a reevaluation of manufacturing procedures to preserve product quality and efficacy in clinical application.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73745-6.Keywords: Positive expiratory pressure mask (EPAP), Respiratory system, U-tube manometer, Medical equipment, Bioengineering, Respiratory physiotherapySubject terms: Engineering, Biomedical engineering

Introduction

The role of Biomedical Engineering is to manage and provide electrically safe, calibrated and well-functioning equipment with the aim of providing the best healthcare to patients. The more efficient and effective the equipment, the lower the chances of problems caused by equipment malfunction1.

Positive expiratory pressure (PEP) is a technique used to treat acute or chronic respiratory failure. PEP mask is used in spontaneous breathing. This equipment consists of an oronasal mask coupled to a T-connector with a unidirectional valve. Also, with the PEP mask, positive end-expiratory pressure levels can be adjusted in the range of 0 to 20 cmH2O according to the disease and tolerance of the patient2,3. The application of increased or diminished pressure to the gases in the lungs is an old procedure. Acute left-sided ventricular failure causes pulmonary congestion and oedema; paroxysmal dyspnoea (cardiac asthma) provides the usual symptomatology; acute left-sided failure may be a factor in some cases of bronchial asthma. The “pulmonary plus pressure machine” has been devised for treating the condition4. A PEP mask was developed in Denmark during the late 1970 primarily to remove secretions from the airways of hypersecretory patients5. Another function of the PEP mask is to contribute to the removal of secretions from the airways in situations where there is an increase in the production of secretion, reduction in the ability to clear the secretion, or both6. In these cases, the PEP mask provides counterpressure to the airways during expiration and accumulates air behind the mucus through collateral ventilation, thus moving secretions toward larger airways where they can be easily expelled. Subsequently, the PEP mask was introduced in the US as an alternative to conventional physical therapy, expanding its use to reverse atelectasis and mitigate the risk of pulmonary complications after surgery7.

The three main functions of the PEP mask are to increase lung volume by increasing the functional residual capacity and tidal volume, reduce the lung hyperinflation, and improve the secretion clearance. The PEP mask can increase the functional residual capacity of bedridden surgical neurological patients with a musculoskeletal dysfunction who exhibit a reduced lung volume by changing their breathing patterns8. Lung hyperinflation is a notable characteristic of patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) owing to a loss of elastic lung recoil, airflow limitation, or insufficient expiratory time to fully exhale the tidal volume. Consequently, the air becomes trapped in the lungs, and the respiratory system does not reach its normal elastic equilibrium, leading to dynamic hyperinflation. The PEP mask reduces the respiratory rate by prolonging expiration and mitigates premature airway closure by reducing lung hyperinflation9.

Different types of PEP masks are available, with positive expiratory pressure generated by the flow or pressure resistor valves. With the flow resistor, also referred to as a nonlinear pressure resistor, the patient breathes through holes with increasingly smaller diameters, which offer a progressively greater resistance. Such a resistor is affected by the airflow pattern generated by the patient. Thus, the PEP level varies with the size of the holes and expiratory flow rate. A manometer can be used to measure the pressure achieved in the system10. In contrast, with the pressure resistor or pressure-regulated device, referred to as the linear pressure resistor, the pressure is independent of the flow, and the resistance remains constant throughout the expiratory phase. The valve remains open until the pressure drops under the targeted level, at which point, the valve closes, and no more air can be expired. The best-known linear pressure resistor is the spring-loaded valve11.

Despite the evidence on the positive effects of different PEP masks and devices on different diseases, accuracy assessment of the expiratory pressure in the valves of different manufacturers are lacking in the scientific literature. The aim of this study was to evaluate and compare spring-loaded PEP valves of three manufacturers. For this purpose, bench tests were conducted to compare different spring-loaded PEP levels (0, 2.5, 5, 10, 15, and 20 cmH2O) of valves from the three manufacturers with the respective water seal (U-tube manometer) at each PEP level.

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated and compared different levels of PEP provided by spring-loaded valves from three manufacturers. The expiratory pressures generated by the valves from the three manufacturers were significantly higher than the reference values, for all pressure levels tested.

It is important to highlight that the beneficial effects of using the PEP mask are well established and the lack of precision in the spring valves should not be a limiting factor for its use, since this is a bench experimental work, therefore, there is a need for more experimental and clinical scientific studies to confirm the results in this study.

Expiratory positive pressure has been a treatment option for patients with obstructive sleep apnea (OSA). They were tested on the equipment Ultepap − 3.5/7.5/13.8 cmH2O, Provent – ​​4.5/8.5/14.5 cmH2O, Bongo Rx -0.8/1.8/3.5 cmH2O and Theravent, -0.9/2.2/5.3 cmH2O. Importantly, clinicians must discern that not all FDA-approved EPAP devices produce similar mechanical profiles. There are fundamental differences regarding expiratory pressures and resistances generated due to different valve designs12. More new clinical trials are needed comparing the PEP levels offered by each manufacturer, validating their effectiveness, accuracy and precision of the specified scale.

However, when high pressure levels are used, oxygenation may worsen due to the reduction in venous return (preload), and consequently, there is a reduction in cardiac output, compromising the supply of oxygen to the tissues10. On the other hand, the in vitro bench study did not validate the impact on physiological parameters, which suggests further clinical studies.

Intrinsic positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEPi), known as Intrinsic PEEP, is therefore responsible for the increased work and overload of the inspiratory muscles in patients with COPD and also with acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)13. The use of external PEEP, during mechanical ventilation, or PEP mask during spontaneous breathing makes the expiratory effort more efficient and reduces the work of breathing by reducing PEEPi. In addition, it reduces the end-expiratory volume, that is, it improves the elastic balance of the respiratory system. It is worth noting that the PEEP or PEP mask value must be lower than PEEPi to avoid increasing the lung hyperinflation14. However, the in vitro study does not validate implications for patients, but based on the results found, it suggests that calibrations should be carried out on the spring for values ​​specified on the scale.

The additional inspiratory load imposed by PEEPi increases the work of breathing and can induce inspiratory muscle fatigue, leading to reduced ventilation and poor gas exchange. The use of an external positive expiratory pressure, lower than PEEPi, reduces airway collapse, decreases lung hyperinflation, redistributes ventilation, and improves gas exchange. The physiological effect generated by external positive expiratory pressure has been attributed to the deceleration of the expiratory flow, reducing the pressure drop in the airway and its collapse. Another hypothesis is at the point of equal pressure, the point of equal pressure is shifted from peripheral airways to the more central airways, thereby reducing the risk of airway closure, and consequently, air trapping15. Therefore, the article mentions that the results do not influence respiratory mechanical properties, validating the in vitro study, which certifies the importance of calibrating spring-loaded valves.

High levels of PEP have several side effects on the respiratory and cardiovascular systems. Nevertheless, it is important to note that the side effects of positive expiratory pressure are limited only to the intervention time16. As this is an in vitro study, it does not cause side effects, but it attests to the importance of calibrating spring-loaded valves.

The high-flow nasal cannula (HFNC) has become an essential respiratory support for patients with acute respiratory failure. The physiological effects of HFNC include reduction of dead space ventilation through the CO2 scavenging effect and generation of PEEP. Therefore, an innovative system to generate adjustable PEEP with a high-flow nasal cannula, which includes PEEP valves in the circuit must be calibrated as discussed in this study17.

Limitations of the study

Our study shows the following limitations in the parameters of the reproducibility and the repeatability at the pressure of 20 cmH2O. This parameter indicates that errors in the measuring system occur mainly due to large variations in the measuring equipment. Through the study (R&R), it will be possible to verify the points where improvements are necessary, applying a measurement process through the use of electronic devices such as pressure transducers, which represents a limitation of the present study related to reproducibility18. Therefore, in our vitro bench study, we did not validate the impact on physiological parameters, which suggests further clinical studies, because the use of high PEEP can cause hemodynamic instability and pneumothorax.

Another important limitation is the sample size, which was restricted to one set for each manufacturer. Therefore, the results can only be considered for the manufactured set-in question. It is important to highlight that with this in vitro bench study, it is not possible to state that the pressures generated by spring-loaded pose risks to users, thus further clinical studies will be necessary. It only characterizes the need for spring-loaded calibration for better quality and correct use of its scale specifications.

Methods

Data were collected in the Bioengineering Laboratory (Laboratório de Bioengenharia – LABBIO) at the Universidade Federal de Minas Gerais.

A U-tube manometer (Bioengineering Laboratory), a 60-mL syringe (Descarpack, Efficiency in Disposables), distilled water (Global Organização Farmacêutica Ltda, Taubaté, SP), a hose (Implantec, Health Care Technology), and two three-way valves (Zhejiang, China) were used for the measurements. Three pressure valves were tested, i.e., models Q, W, and Z, available in the Brazilian market and produced by different manufacturers.

In this study, the spring-loaded pressure valves tested had the following PEP characteristics. The valve is connected to a spring adjustable from 2.5 to 20 cmH2O, which is 7.9 cm long when fully opened (PEP of 2.5 cmH2O) and 6.9 cm long when fully closed (PEP of 20 cmH2O). The pressure values result from spring compression or stretching (Fig. 2). Upon a reduction in spring length, the airflow resistance increases, thereby increasing the PEP. Conversely, upon an increase in spring length, the airflow resistance decreases, thereby decreasing the PEP.

Schematic of the spring-loaded valve (generator of Positive expiratory pressure - PEP). (1) Air volume inlet and (2) air outlet (non-tight circuit).

Figure 3 shows a schematic of the test system. From one end, one of the branches in the U-tube manometer (6) was connected to the tested valve (5) through a flexible polyvinyl chloride tube (3). A three-way valve was installed in this tube. One of the airways was used to connect a syringe (1) to the system, through a flexible tube (2). Another three-way valve was installed in the flexible tube (2). This valve enabled filling a syringe with air without disconnecting it from the circuit, thus preventing leaks. During the tests, the three-way valves were adjusted to allow the air contained in the syringe (1) to flow to the Sect. (3) that connected the manometer to the valve. The test consists of assessing whether the valve will open at the pressure set as a function of the deformation from the spring. The pressure applied to the valve was calculated by the unevenness between the surfaces of the columns in the U-tube manometer. At the beginning of each test, termed repositioning condition, with both branches open to the atmosphere, the scale was zeroed. The spring-loaded valves that generate the PEP were set to have a pressure range of 2.5 to 20 cmH2O. Then, the pressure was vented by the syringe in each valve (generator) using both hands for 1 h and 15 min at each inlet flow rate for each pressure. All valves were manipulated by two operators without viewing the scale to avoid operator bias and premature inferences (Fig. 2). In total, the system measured the function of each valve 100 times. The spring loads were measured 20 times, each PEEP value at 2.5 cmH2O to 20 cmH2O, for each 5 valves from the manufacturers Q, W and Z, and in the end each PEEP obtained 100 measurements.

Schematic of the PEP measurement system. (1) Syringe, (2) & (3) three-way valves, (4) Connects the syringe to atmospheric air, (5) Spring-load one-way valve (PEP generator) and (6) U-shaped pressure gauge.

The measurement system is a factor that has a great influence on the reliability of the measurement result, but it is not the only factor because the reliability also depends on the operator skills and environmental conditions, among other variables. Each variable that is part of the measurement process influences the result, potentially introducing systematic and random components in the measurements. For this work, all possible sources of uncertainty in the measurement process were evaluated, such as operator skills, environmental conditions, and equipment calibration. One hundred measurements were performed on each spring load valve to verify the measurement result with the sources of uncertainty cited in this experiment. Repeatability and reproducibility were determined according to the method of18.

The test consists of attaching a U-shaped column pressure gauge to the syringe, which will be activated by moving the water to an initial value of 2.5 (cmH2O), from zero cmH2O, and then to the values 5.0 (cmH2O), 10.0 (cmH2O), 15.0 (cmH2O), and 20 (cmH2O). All values were monitored by stabilizing the pressure for 30 s. The test consists of applying pressure to one of the branches of the U-shaped manometer, one end of which is connected to the pressure system to be measured and the other to the atmosphere; in the substitution condition, i.e., changing the values to be measured, both branches were open to the atmosphere, referring to ZERO on the scale. The difference in height between the two branches constitutes the pressure, regardless of variations in the internal diameter of the tubes.

The air flows simulated by the syringe were monitored by the manometer system, which displaces the water inside the hose, generating a pressure difference, validating the scale adjusted by the operator. From the moment the valve membrane reaches maximum pressure, it is opened, which can be seen by the sound emitted by the diaphragm and the liquid returns to zero in approximately 30 s, because the system is not watertight (supplementary material).

Statistical analysis

Data were expressed as median, and at 25th and 75th percentile. The distribution of each outcome measure was analyzed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. The Friedman test was used for statistical analysis, followed by the Dunn’s post-test. The significance level was set at p < 0.05. All statistical tests were performed using the software GraphPad Prism version 8.00 (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA).

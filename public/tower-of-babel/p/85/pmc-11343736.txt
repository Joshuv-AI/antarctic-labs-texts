A multi-release reliability model of open source software with fault detection obeying three-parameter lifetime distribution
Authors: Jinyong Wang, Pengda Li, Jiaxuan Hu, Ce Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
With the development of open source software (OSS), software reliability has become an important issue. Due to the complexity of OSS development and testing environment, the fault detection rate of OSS will show a variety of changes. The existing OSS reliability models cannot fully adapt to the complex changes of OSS fault detection. In this paper, we proposed a multi-release OSS reliability model based on three-parameter lifetime distribution. The proposed model can effectively adapt to the complicated changes of fault detection in the processes of development and testing of OSS. Experimental results indicate that the proposed model has the better fitting and predictive performance compared with other multi-release OSS reliability models. Moreover, the proposed model can better adapt to the variety of OSS fault detection environment, and assist developers to evaluate the reliability of OSS.

Introduction

Open source software (OSS) is widely used in the past decades. Since the development and testing of OSS is mainly completed by volunteers and users all over the world, its reliability has become a hot topic. In order to solve the reliability problem of OSS, the method which is “release early, release often”1 is used to improve and enhance the reliability of OSS, and maintain the attraction of OSS to community contributors and meet the changing needs of users.

On the one hand, the method of early release and frequent release can improve the reliability of OSS under open testing conditions or open development environment. On the other hand, due to the strong subjectivity and arbitrariness of OSS release, it is widely questioned that this method can effectively improve the reliability of OSS. In addition, due to the complexity of OSS development and testing environment, fault detection also shows diversity and complexity. Moreover, due to the continuous dynamic changes of volunteers and users in the open source community, fault detection of OSS also presents a variety of characteristics. For example, the fault detection rate increases first and then decrease over time2, or the fault detection rate decreases with the test time3.

The existing research on OSS reliability modeling focuses on the single feature of OSS development and testing under open testing conditions. For example, considering interests of volunteers and users in the OSS community, Li et al.2 proposed a reliability model of OSS, in which the failure detection rate first increases and then decreases with the test time. They also assumed that the remaining faults in the previous version of OSS are not related to the newly generated faults in the current version. Considering that the fault detection rate decreased over time, Wang and Mi3 proposed a related reliability model of OSS. Singh et al.4 studied the changes of source code and files in the process of OSS development and testing, and integrated source code entropy and testing time with Cobb–Douglas function to establish the corresponding multi-release OSS reliability model. Considering the delay between fault detection and debugging in the process of OSS development and testing, Yang et al.5 proposed multi-release reliability models of OSS. Considering that the reliability of OSS is affected by test coverage and fault detection factor, Tandon et al.6 proposed a multi-release OSS reliability model with test coverage following a Weibull distribution and fault reduction factor being constant.

Although the above mentioned reliability models of OSS can effectively evaluate the reliability of OSS under open testing conditions, complex development environment and uncertain development process make them unable to adapt to the actual OSS reliability evaluation. The failure detection rate will show many changes due to the complex OSS development environment and uncertain development process. For example, the curve changes of the fault detection rate show the decreasing trend over time3; the curve of the fault detection rate first increases, and then decreases over time4; due to contributors learning phenomena of OSS community, the curve changes of the failure detection rate show the increasing trend over time. In addition, the fault detection rate may be a constant, or shows a bathtub shape curve over time. Thus, considering the single characteristic of OSS development and testing process and open testing conditions, OSS reliability modeling cannot adapt to the variety and complexity of fault detection in the process of OSS testing.

In addition, the proposed multi-release software reliability model also considers the correlation of faults in each version of open source software. Thus, the idea and methods of modeling are completely different. From the perspective of actual open source software development and testing, we can see that each time a new software is released, it is modified and enhanced on the basis of the original software. For the frequently released software, there are complex faults in the current software. These complex faults include the faults caused by adding new functions or features, new faults added due to function changes, as well as some remaining faults in previous versions of software. Only by fully considering the correlation of faults in each version of software can we effectively establish the corresponding multi-release OSS reliability model.

In this paper, considering the variety of fault detection in the process of OSS development and testing, we propose a multi-release OSS reliability model with fault detection obeying a three-parameter lifetime distribution and considering the correlation of faults in each version. Five OSS reliability models and two fault data sets are used to compare and verify the effectiveness of the proposed model. Compared with other OSS reliability models, the proposed model has the better fitting and predictive performance. The proposed model can be used to evaluate actual OSS projects.

Related work

In general, OSS reliability model can be divided into two types, one is a single release OSS reliability model which assumes that each version of OSS is independent. The single release OSS reliability model is used to evaluate the software reliability of each version. The other is a multi-release OSS reliability model which considers that the correlation between the newly generated faults in the current version and the remaining faults in the previous version.

In terms of single release reliability models of OSS, Zhou and Davis7 indicated that traditional closed-source software reliability models were able to estimate OSS reliability, especially, the closed-source software reliability model with a Weibull distribution had the better performance compared with other closed-source software reliability models. In addition, Rahmani et al.8 presented that the software reliability model with the Weibull distribution had better performance in evaluating the reliability of OSS. Through the research of OSS development and testing process, Yamada and Tamura9 developed a variety of open source software reliability models. For example, Tamura and Yamada10–12 proposed OSS reliability models based on stochastic differential equations considering random phenomena of fault detection in the process of OSS testing. Additionally, considering analytic hierarchy process13, hierarchical Bayesian and component14, neural network and component15 and hazard rate changes16, Tamura and Yamada proposed related reliability models of OSS, respectively.

Considering that 80% of the components have 20% of all faults, and 20% of the components contain 80% faults, Huang et al.17 proposed a reliability model based on a bounded generalized Pareto distribution. In addition, considering fault introduction obeying the Pareto distribute, Wang18 proposed an imperfect debugging reliability model of OSS. By studying the changes of components, functions and testing-effort in the process of open source software development, Johri et al.19 established the corresponding OSS reliability model. Singh et al.20 studied the irregular changes of faults in fault tracking system and proposed the OSS reliability model with random changes of the fault detection rate. Singh et al.21 divided the faults detected in the process of OSS development and testing into simple, difficult to remove and complex faults, and established some OSS reliability models. Considering that the curve of fault detection rate showed multiple peaks and valleys in the process of OSS development and testing, Singh et al.22 proposed OSS reliability models based on changing-points. Gandhi et al.23 used Bass diffusion and Kenney models to evaluate the reliability of OSS. Gyimothy et al.24 put forward the method of calculating object-oriented metrics to predict source code changes and OSS reliability.

Khurshid et al.25 developed a software reliability model framework for imperfect debugging and abrupt point by studying changes of testing strategies, coding, and testing resources during the OSS testing process. Saraf and Iqbal26 studied the relationship between fault detection and fault correction, and proposed a few corresponding reliability models based on imperfect debugging, fault generation and abrupt point. For the evaluation of OSS reliability, Lin and Li27 provided a novel method considering the rate-based queueing simulation. Wang et al.28 found a decreasing trend in the introduction of faults during the debugging process of OSS and proposed a corresponding software reliability model. Oveisi et al.29 proposed non-parametric models, viz. LSTM Encode-Decoder and Bayesian inference method, to predict software reliability, and validated the accuracy of the proposed model through experiments.

On the other hand, Garmabaki et al.30 assumed that current software faults included the remaining faults in the previous version and the faults caused by the addition or modification of components and functions in the current version, and proposed a multi-release reliability model of OSS. Nijhawan et al.31 assumed that in the process of software upgrade, in addition to the faults left in the previous version, some new faults were generated in the code. They proposed a related multi-release reliability model of OSS. Considering changing-points of fault detection during OSS development and testing, Aggarwal et al.32 proposed a few multi-release OSS reliability models based changing-points. Aggarwal et al.33 proposed a multi-release reliability model considering imperfect debugging.

Saraf et al.34–37 considered the changes in fault introduction, fault generation, abrupt points, and testing efforts during the testing process of OSS with multiple releases, and proposed corresponding software reliability models. Kapur et al.38 used a Cobb Douglas function to integrate testing time and testing resources, and developed a two-dimensional software reliability model framework for multi-release software. Kumar et al.39 also proposed a two dimensional software reliability model framework considering fault detection and fault correction for multi-release software. Considering imperfect debugging, abrupt point and fault reduction factors during OSS tests, Khurshid et al.40,41 proposed two different software reliability model frameworks for multi-release OSS.

No matter above-mentioned single release or multi-release OSS reliability models, they are difficult to meet the various changes of fault detection in the actual OSS development and testing process. Furthermore, only by fully considering the different changes of the fault detection rate under open source environment can we establish a more adaptive software reliability model. In this paper, we propose the multi-release reliability model considering fault detection obeying three-parameter lifetime distribution. The fault detection rate of the proposed model can show a variety of changes in the process of OSS development and testing. These changes can well reflect the characteristics of fault detection of OSS. Moreover, the proposed multi-release software reliability model considers the correlation of faults in each version of OSS. Changes of fault detection rate of OSS

In the process of OSS development and testing, the fault detection rate has a variety of changes over time. For example, (a) When OSS community contributors remain relatively stable, we can assume that the fault detection rate is constant. (b) Because there is a learning process during OSS testing, the fault detection rate increases gradually over time44. (c) When the released OSS is relatively simple, fewer and fewer faults will be detected. Then the fault detection rate decreases gradually over time. (d) Due to the change of the interest of volunteers and users in the OSS community, or the change of the attraction of newly released OSS, the fault detection rate increases first and then decreases2. (e) When newly released OSS is more complex, the number of faults detected will gradually decrease. However, due to the phenomenon of learning, the number of faults detected will gradually increase. Thus, the fault detection rate first decreases and then increases over time.

From the above analysis, it can be concluded that in the process of OSS development and testing, the fault detection rate presents a variety of changes. These changes are due to the dynamic development and testing of OSS. The established OSS reliability models cannot well adapt to the actual development environments. Due to the dynamic, complex and uncertain characteristics of OSS in the process of development and testing, the fault detection rate will show different changes in different environments. In this paper, the proposed model can adapt to more environment changes of OSS development and testing, and has practical use and application values. Developers can use the proposed model to evaluate the reliability of OSS projects.

In addition, the difference between the proposed model and other models with the two-parameter Weibull distribution is that the proposed model not only includes changes of fault detection rates of other models, but also has the characteristics that other models do not have. The fault detection rate function of the proposed model can be a bathtub shaped curve. Therefore, the proposed model can better adapt to changes of the actual OSS development and testing environment compared with other models with the two-parameter Weibull distribution.

Model parameter estimation

In general, there are two methods to estimate the parameters of reliability models for OSS, one is a maximum likelihood estimation (MLE), the other is a least squares estimation (LSE). Two methods on parameter estimation have their advantages and disadvantages. First, In the case of small sample data, MLE is slightly worse than LSE. Second, it may not exist when estimating the maximum likelihood function value of model parameters. Considering the small scale of OSS fault datasets, and if the parameter value cannot be estimated, the performance comparison of the model cannot be fully carried out. Thus, we use the least squares estimation method to estimate the parameter values of the model in this paper.

Comparison of models for fault detection in OSS

In order to compare the performance of models, we use five OSS reliability models, including two single release OSS reliability models (e.g. Li single release model2 and Wang single release model3) and two multi-release OSS reliability models (e.g. Yang multi-release model5 and Tandon multi-release model6). Specifically, Li et al. considered that the fault detection rate increased first and then decreased over time, and established the corresponding single release OSS reliability model. Wang and Mi considered that the fault detection rate had a downward trend with the test time, and established the related single release reliability model of OSS. Yang et al. considered the delay between fault detection and fault correction for OSS, and proposed multi-release reliability models. Considering that testing efforts obeyed the Weibull distribution, Tandon et al. built the related multi-release reliability model of OSS. Sensitivity analysis

Sensitivity analysis is to observe the changes of the model while changing a parameter value and keeping other values of the model unchanged. From Fig. 3a–d, we can see that four parameters of the proposed model are all sensitivity parameters. This fully illustrates that in the OSS development and testing environment, the proposed multi-release model can well meet the rules and characteristics of OSS development and testing. Moreover, in the complex development and testing environment of OSS, it can effectively evaluate the software reliability and predict the number of remaining faults in OSS. Conclusions

Due to existing a variety of changes of OSS fault detection, we propose the multi-release reliability model of OSS with fault detection obeying the three-parameter lifetime distribution. We use two OSS fault data sets, five model comparison criteria and five OSS reliability models to carry out the corresponding model performance comparison experiments. Experimental results verify the effectiveness and accuracy of the proposed model. Compared with other reliability models of OSS, the proposed model has the better fitting and predictive performance. Because the proposed model can adapt to a variety of OSS fault detection environments, it can be used for the actual reliability evaluation of OSS projects. The limitation of this paper is that it only considers the perfect removal of detected faults, without taking into account the introduction of faults and software reliability modeling issues in complex open source environments. Therefore, considering the complexity of OSS development and testing, future research will focus on the dynamic, uncertainty, and random changes of community volunteers and users to establish the corresponding reliability models of imperfect debugging.

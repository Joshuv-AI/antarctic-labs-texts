Assessing the effectiveness of the ZICOMP-Shewhart control chart for monitoring zero-inflated processes
Authors: Aqsa Sattar, Muhammad Ali Raza, Laila A. AL-Essa, Hanen Louati, Mohammed M. A. Almazah, Arailym Zaitzhanova
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The zero-inflated Conway-Maxwell Poisson (ZICOMP) distribution models count data with many zero observations. This distribution assumes that zero observations occur with probability \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:p$$\end{document}, and the count of nonconformities in a product unit follows the Conway-Maxwell Poisson (COMP) distribution with parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mu\:$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\gamma\:$$\end{document}. The ZICOMP distribution is flexible in accommodating various dispersion patterns in zero-inflated (ZI) datasets and effectively models over-dispersed, under-dispersed, or equi-dispersed data. This study provides an in-depth analysis of the ZICOMP-Shewhart control chart, as discussed by Alevizakos and Tasias1, to assess its effectiveness in detecting shifts in the rate parameter (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mu\:$$\end{document}) while assuming constant dispersion and zero-inflation parameters. The key focus of this research is the selection of the limit coefficient to achieve the desired in-control (IC) average run length and assessing Type II error sensitivity for improved out-of-control (OOC) detection. Through extensive simulations, we examine the comparative efficiency of the ZICOMP-Shewhart chart against the traditional Shewhart chart under the COMP distribution. Additionally, the effectiveness of the ZICOMP-Shewhart control chart is demonstrated through a real-life example.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-32581-y.

Introduction

The control chart, a fundamental statistical process control (SPC) tool, is highly effective for detecting out-of-control (OOC) conditions and reducing undesirable variations in production processes. In the literature, the Shewhart control chart is especially notable for its role in monitoring and ensuring stability in manufacturing and business processes. In many manufacturing processes, several quality features cannot be measured numerically. These characteristics, known as attributes, include factors such as the number of errors or defects in a product. For instance, a soft drink bottle may be considered defective if its shape is dented. Numerous control charts based on the Shewhart structure have been developed to monitor count datasets. For example, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:c$$\end{document}-chart and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:u$$\end{document}-chart, based on the Poisson distribution, are employed for monitoring the number and average of nonconformities per inspection unit, respectively, while the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:p$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:np$$\end{document} control charts, based on the binomial distribution, are used to monitor the proportion and total number of nonconforming items, respectively2. Kaminsky et al.3 introduced the g-chart, based on the geometric distribution, to monitor count data. Furthermore, Xie et al.4 introduced the CCC-r chart to monitor the cumulative count of items produced until \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:r$$\end{document} nonconforming items are observed, using the negative binomial distribution.

Advancements in the manufacturing industries have led to processes that frequently involve significant quantities of zeros. These are commonly referred to as high-yield processes. Nonetheless, the Poisson, geometric, and negative binomial distributions are not appropriate for modeling zero-inflated (ZI) count data, often leading to biased parameter estimates and over-dispersion due to the excessive number of zeros. To address the issue of excessive zeros, ZI variants of existing count data distributions have been developed in the literature. For example, Lambert5 proposed the ZI Poisson (ZIP) distribution. Within this framework, Xie and Goh6 developed a Shewhart-based control chart that utilizes exact probability limits, suggesting that only upper-sided probability limits could be used to monitor the ZI process. Later, Xie et al.7 suggested another charting scheme referred to as the random shocks model. He et al.8 proposed the Shewhart-structured control chart for the ZIP distribution, with an appropriate sample size. However, the ZIP distribution cannot handle dispersed count data due to its assumption of equi-dispersion. Therefore, Chang and Gan9 introduced the ZI Geometric (ZIG) distribution to model over-dispersed data and proposed a Shewhart control chart based on it. Moreover, Sim and Lim10 developed a one-sided \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:np$$\end{document}-chart using the Jeffreys prior interval, with the binomial parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:p$$\end{document} estimated through the ZI Binomial (ZIB) model to monitor under-dispersed data. Additionally, various memory-type control charts, such as the exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) and cumulative sum (CUSUM) control charts, have been developed to monitor ZI count data. In this context, Fatahi et al.11 introduced the EWMA control chart to monitor count datasets following a ZIP distribution. Leong and Tan12 developed a combined Bernoulli-ZIP EWMA control chart. He et al.13 proposed the CUSUM control charts: one for detecting changes in each parameter of the ZIP distribution individually, and another for monitoring an upward shift in both parameters simultaneously. Recently, Abbas et al.14 proposed Shewhart and EWMA control charts based on the ZI Negative Binomial (ZINB) distribution, demonstrating through simulations and a real dataset that the ZINB-EWMA chart provides superior performance, particularly in detecting small shifts, compared to the ZINB-Shewhart chart.

The Poisson distribution is used to model count data where the mean and variance are equal. To account for varying levels of dispersion, including over-dispersion, under-dispersion, and equi-dispersion, Conway and Maxwell15 introduced the flexible Conway-Maxwell Poisson (COMP) distribution. In this context, Shmueli et al.16 further investigated the properties of the COMP distribution. The COMP distribution has been utilized to develop various control charts within the SPC literature. For more details, refer to the works of Sellers17, Saghir et al.18, and Saghir and Lin19,20.

The COMP distribution is effective for modeling dispersed count datasets, but it does not capture the ZI characteristic often present in such data. To address this limitation, Barriga and Louzada21 introduced a flexible ZI Conway-Maxwell Poisson (ZICOMP) distribution, which accommodates over-dispersed, under-dispersed, and equi-dispersed count data with excessive zeros. In this context, Sellers and Raim22 further discussed the properties of the ZICOMP distribution. Within the control charting framework, Tasias and Alevizakos23 introduced the CUSUM control chart under the ZICOMP distribution. In their study, they proposed three separate CUSUM schemes to detect upward shifts in each parameter of the ZICOMP distribution, along with a combined CUSUM chart designed to identify an upward shift in all parameters simultaneously. Recently, Alevizakos and Tasias1 developed the ZICOMP-Shewhart control chart, which utilizes the ZICOMP distribution to monitor shifts in the process mean while accounting for variations in all three parameters of the distribution. While their study provides valuable insights into the use of Shewhart charts for ZI count data, their approach leaves several aspects unexplored. Comparative study

This section evaluates the efficiency of the ZICOMP-Shewhart control chart in detecting process shifts under various dispersion conditions. A comparative analysis is conducted between the ZICOMP-Shewhart and COMP-Shewhart control charts. The COMP-Shewhart control chart is originally formulated as a two-sided chart, as shown in Eq. (9), while the ZICOMP-Shewhart control chart is defined as a one-sided chart, as shown in Eq. (12). To ensure consistency in performance evaluation, the COMP-Shewhart control chart was modified to a one-sided form by considering only the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:UCL$$\end{document}, aligning it with the ZICOMP-Shewhart chart. The comparison is based on three simulated datasets, each consisting of 150 observations. Of these, 70 observations represent IC conditions, while the remaining 80 correspond to OOC states.

The study examines three distinct scenarios to assess the impact of varying IC parameter levels. The parameter values for \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mu\:$$\end{document}, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\gamma\:$$\end{document}, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:p$$\end{document} were chosen to reflect practical process conditions. Specifically, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mu\:$$\end{document} was varied to represent different defect rates, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\gamma\:$$\end{document} was adjusted to capture over-, equi-, and under-dispersion, and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:p$$\end{document} was set to illustrate moderate to high levels of zero inflation. Conclusion

The ZICOMP distribution can model count data exhibiting under-dispersion, equi-dispersion, or over-dispersion, and accommodates a high proportion of zero observations. This article presents an in-depth analysis of the ZICOMP-Shewhart control chart for detecting shifts in the rate parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mu\:$$\end{document} of the ZICOMP distribution, focusing specifically on the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:UCL$$\end{document}. Although the ZICOMP-Shewhart chart can be applied to monitor shifts in any parameter, this study focuses on shifts in the rate parameter (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mu\:$$\end{document}), as it is the key indicator of changes in count data. The chart’s effectiveness is evaluated by analyzing the Type II error across various combinations of ZICOMP parameters and shift sizes, alongside calculating \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{ARL}_{0}$$\end{document} values for the specific limit coefficients. The extensive simulation studies compare the performance of the ZICOMP-Shewhart chart with the COMP-Shewhart chart under different parameter settings. The results indicate that the ZICOMP-Shewhart chart outperforms the COMP-Shewhart control chart. Additionally, a real-life example illustrates the practical applicability of the ZICOMP-Shewhart chart for ZI count data. Nonetheless, the performance of the chart may be somewhat sensitive to parameter estimation. Future research could address this by developing robust estimation strategies, adaptive procedures, or Bayesian approaches to improve its practical reliability.

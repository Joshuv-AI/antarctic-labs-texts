Standing strong: War‐related challenges in Ukrainian biomedical research and opportunities for support
Authors: Hubert Hondermarck, Nataliya Finiuk, Chen Chen Jiang, Rostyslav Stoika
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe prolonged war in Ukraine is having a strong impact on all sectors of the Ukrainian society, including biomedical research. Although the material and psychological conditions are challenging, the country and its researchers are courageously managing to continue their activities. This perspective paper describes the multiple challenges faced by Ukrainian biomedical researchers during wartime and outlines strategies to support and enhance collaboration with the global scientific community. Ukraine has a rich scientific history and modern expertise in biomedical research, and developing more international collaborations with Ukraine can have mutual benefits for all involved parties.Keywords: biomedical research, Ukraine

The ongoing war in Ukraine has major implications for biological and medical research in the country. 1 , 2 Ukraine has a cutting‐edge and dynamic academic community, 3 , 4 but the war has seriously impacted biomedical research and education. Previous publications have already highlighted the adverse conditions facing Ukrainian researchers and their impressive resilience. 5 The present perspective paper provides an examination of the challenges in Ukrainian biomedical research and outlines strategies for support and collaboration.

RESILIENCE OF BIOMEDICAL RESEARCH DURING WARTIME: HISTORY TEACHES HOPE

In the annals of biomedical research history, there are numerous instances of scientific breakthroughs achieved despite the adversities of war. One of the best examples of essential discoveries made during wartime is provided by Italian neuroscientist Rita Levi‐Montalcini. 6 Amid the turmoil of World War II, including the closure of her laboratory due to fascist policies, Rita Levi‐Montalcini continued her research in a makeshift laboratory at home. 6 , 7 It was during this time that she made the seminal discovery of nerve growth factor, a protein essential for the growth, maintenance, and survival of nerve cells. 6 , 7 Her pioneering work established the field of neurotrophic factor research and earned her the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1986. Rita Levi‐Montalcini's story demonstrated the power of perseverance and serves as a model of successful scientific determination in the face of adversity. 8

WAR IMPACT ON UKRAINIAN RESEARCH PRIORITIES IN A CONTEXT OF LIMITED FUNDING AND RESOURCES

The conflict has reshaped research priorities and focus areas. 1 , 2 With limited resources and increased security concerns, Ukrainian research efforts have shifted toward priorities more directly relevant to the war and its consequences, such as humanitarian aid, public health, environmental monitoring, and defense technologies. While addressing immediate needs is necessary, the downside is a risk that long‐term fundamental research across disciplines, and particularly biomedical sciences, may become deprioritized or even neglected. Government funding for scientific research has decreased and international collaborations have been disrupted, 2 aggravating the financial strain on research institutions. The UNESCO Institute for Statistics reported that between 2021 and 2022, gross domestic expenditure on research and development is decreased by 38.5% from $2019 million to $1241 million. 9 The budget of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine has decreased by 28% since 2021, the budget of the National Research Foundation of Ukraine by 33%, and the budget of Universities by 82%. 9 Also, disturbances to national industry, and economic instability, have made it difficult for researchers to buy essential laboratory equipment and supplies. Obtaining basic consumables such as cell culture reagents and plastics, antibodies, or animal‐related products is problematic, and this slows down progress across projects and institutions. Of note, the difficulties are not only due to lack of resources but are also the consequence of logistic issues in transporting the multiple reagents and equipment to research institutions, due to complications in transborder transportation and total interruption of civil aviation in Ukraine. However, this is not all doom and gloom, and despite difficulties some governmental research funding has been maintained. Interestingly, Ukrainian research is already recovering and experiencing a limited rebirth, including in its funding situation and through reopening of laboratories closed at the start of the war. 2

DISRUPTION OF ACADEMIC INSTITUTIONS AND COLLABORATIVE NETWORKS

The war has led to the displacement and disruption of many academic institutions, 10 particularly in conflict‐affected regions. Universities and research centers have been forced to close or operate under extremely challenging conditions, reducing the progress of ongoing projects and academic activities. 9 An illustration is provided by the disruption of clinical trials in Ukraine. 11 Before the war, ClinicalTrials.gov listed about 400 ongoing clinical trials in Ukraine. The country had indeed managed to work with pharmaceutical companies from around the world to perform clinical trials of their products, thus improving the quality of patient care in Ukraine. 11 However, most ongoing clinical trials in the country are negatively affected by the war or have been interrupted, and there are basically no new clinical trials being initiated now. 11 In addition, national collaborations, which are vital for knowledge exchange, access to specialized expertise, and joint national research initiatives, are also severely affected. The war has also disrupted collaborative networks with institutions and researchers from other countries. 1 , 2 , 3 Travel restrictions, security concerns, and logistical issues have hampered scientific conferences, workshops, and collaborative research projects, limiting opportunities for scientific collaboration. Furthermore, university lecturers in Ukraine are heavily burdened with teaching, leaving them with limited time for scientific research and this situation has been exacerbated by the war. Online teaching and meetings have also become commonplace, initially because of the COVID‐19 pandemic and now in relation to the war. Although online teaching and meetings are useful, they do not provide the full range of interactions that are necessary for academic activities across teaching, learning, and research. Consequently, the quality of Ukrainian university education is at risk of declining. It should also be emphasized that the observed disruptions to academic institutions are not only material but also psychological. The war is directly or indirectly affecting the psychological state of researchers and students, and the high rate of fear, anxiety and other psychological disorders caused by the war are negatively impacting the functioning of academic institutions and training of young researchers. 12 Nevertheless, Ukraine has shown an impressive resilience and Ukrainian researchers have demonstrated that they are determined to keep research going. 13

BRAIN DRAIN, TALENT FLIGHT, AND GENDER UNBALANCE

The war has also triggered a significant brain drain from Ukraine, with many talented researchers seeking opportunities abroad to escape the war and pursue better academic and professional prospects. 14 Since the start of the war, about 20% of Ukrainian scientists and 11% of researchers from the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine have left the country. 1 At the start of the war, many research institutions outside of Ukraine offered positions for Ukrainian researchers in their laboratories. While this was clearly positive, it should be emphasized that providing opportunities to conduct research in Ukrainian scientific institutions would be even more beneficial to Ukrainian science. Although aid contributes keeping researchers in the country, 14 there is an exodus of scientific talent that deprives the country of skilled researchers and educators, undermining the long‐term perspective and competitiveness of Ukrainian scientific research. The loss of experienced researchers also disrupts mentorship networks and the training of the next generation of scientists, not only for Ukrainian postdoctoral scientists who have already made scientific achievements (such as journal publications), but even more for younger scientists and trainees who are at the beginning of their scientific careers. It should be noted that male Ukrainian researchers face another challenge, namely military conscription, and consequently, the Ukrainian research sector becomes particularly unbalanced in terms of gender, with more women than men working in the scientific sector, especially in life sciences. This gender unbalance is observed across scientific disciplines, including biomedical research, and it creates an equity issue that is nevertheless difficult to address given the present circumstances.

SUPPORTING UKRAINIAN SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH WITH FINANCIAL ASSISTANCE

It is difficult to estimate the scope of current financial need because it is not only about supporting research projects but also restoring buildings, infrastructure, and equipment that have been destroyed or damaged because of the war. One of the most immediate ways to support Ukrainian biomedical research is through financial assistance from foreign governments. International organizations, donor agencies, and philanthropic foundations can also allocate funding to support research projects, provide scholarships for students and researchers, and acquire essential laboratory equipment and supplies. Clearly, targeted grants and research fellowships in support of research in the country can help mitigate the financial strain on Ukrainian research institutions and enable scientists to continue their work despite limited resources. This is nicely illustrated by various funding schemes targeted at Ukraine research by the European Union. For instance, the EURIZON Program (https://www.eurizon‐project.eu/) is expected to have significant positive impacts on the work of Ukrainian researchers by providing 4.5 million Euros to 324 Ukrainian scientific projects distributed across various Ukrainian regions.

FACILITATING INTERNATIONAL COLLABORATIONS WITH UKRAINE, CAPACITY BUILDING, AND TRAINING PROGRAMS

International collaborations are important for increasing the impact of Ukrainian scientific research and funding agencies as well as research organizations can facilitate partnerships with Ukrainian researchers to foster knowledge exchange, technology transfer, and joint initiatives. Collaborative projects that address specific challenges and combine complementary expertise can benefit both Ukrainian researchers and their international collaborators. In addition, investing in training programs can increase the skills and capabilities of Ukrainian scientists and academic institutions. International workshops, seminars, and training courses providing technical expertise and research methodologies are essential, particularly to young investigators, and fees to participate in these programs should be waived, at least partially, for Ukrainian researchers. An example of programs facilitating collaborations with Ukraine is the Ukraine Australia Research Fund of the Australian Academy of Science (https://www.science.org.au/supporting‐science/awards‐and‐opportunities/the‐ukraine‐australia‐research‐fund). This fund allocates financial support for collaborative projects between Ukraine and Australia and provides visiting fellowship for Ukrainian researchers to come to Australia for a few months and work on collaborative projects with host Australian research groups. The authors of the present article have benefited from this program, for a project dedicated to the selection of new drugs against glioblastoma and found it to be mutually beneficial. However, once a new collaboration is established, the challenge is to maintain it and it is hoped that further funding streams might be proposed in the future.

SUPPORTING DISPLACED RESEARCHERS

The war‐related displacement of many researchers and intellectuals resulted in a loss of valuable human capital for the Ukrainian scientific community. 14 International organizations and academic institutions can provide support and assistance to displaced researchers, including access to research facilities and integration schemes. Scholarships and fellowships for refugee scientists can allow them to continue their research both abroad and in Ukraine. For instance, the MSCA4Ukraine initiative of the European Union (https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_24_2147), which was first launched in September 2022 with an initial budget of €25 million funded under the Marie Skłodowska‐Curie Actions, is the European Commission's dedicated scheme to support displaced researchers from Ukraine. Although it is difficult to estimate how many investigators need help, MSCA4Ukraine enables some Ukrainian researchers to continue their work safely. Obviously, financial constraints do exist and there is also a risk that helping displaced researchers may be done at the expense of funding for other fellowship programs, thus reasonable arbitrages of funding distribution need to be implemented to avoid these potential issues.

PROMOTING OPEN ACCESS AND KNOWLEDGE SHARING

Importantly, efforts to help Ukrainian biomedical research should not come only from governmental organizations. For instance, scientific societies should consider waiving registration fees, provide travel funds for Ukrainian investigators, and/or being database resources for governmental/nongovernmental funding agencies to help identify in‐country researchers in need. Importantly, promoting open access to scientific literature and fostering knowledge sharing can enhance the visibility and impact of Ukrainian research on the global stage. After the onset of the war, many publishers proposed to waive publication fees for Ukrainian scientists in open‐access journals. Unfortunately, some of these initiatives have now been discontinued. However, the situation in Ukrainian scientific institutions has not improved; in fact, it has worsened. Specific difficulties also arise when a submitted manuscript includes both Ukrainian and non‐Ukrainian co‐authors. Publishers often consider that the Ukrainian co‐authors have foreign partners who should assist them, but this is not encourage collaboration. Thus, international publishers, research repositories, and academic networks should continue to waive fees for Ukrainian researchers, enabling them to publish their findings in reputable journals. Importantly, the international researcher community can also help the Ukrainian science publishing sector by publishing their data in Ukrainian Journals. Most Ukrainian scientific and medical Journals publish articles in the English language and are open access. For instance, the Ukrainian Journals in life science disciplines and medicine (http://ukrbiochemjournal.org/) or Medical Sciences (Proceedings of the Shevchenko Scientific Society) (https://mspsss.org.ua/index.php/journal) have an international editorial board, with peer‐review process, and immediate open access upon manuscript acceptance. Therefore, the global research community can contribute to support the Ukrainian biomedical research sector by publishing original research and reviews in Ukrainian Journals to help those Journals to keep going despite the war, while simultaneously strengthening their international reputation and outreach.

CONCLUSION

Supporting Ukrainian biomedical research to get through the war requires concerted efforts and collaborative initiatives from the global community. Importantly, it should be emphasized that supporting Ukrainian research now is also about starting to rebuild it for when the war will be over. 15 By providing financial assistance, facilitating international collaborations, investing in capacity building, supporting displaced researchers, and promoting open access and knowledge sharing, we can help strengthen the resilience of the Ukrainian biomedical community. Together, we can support Ukrainian researchers to overcome the challenges created by the war and contribute to global scientific advancement. It should be noted that what is being implemented for Ukraine could also serve as a model for helping other countries at war in their effort to maintain scientific research. Through solidarity and concerted initiatives, the international scientific community can help Ukrainian researchers navigate these turbulent times and continue their valuable contributions to science and medicine. The global scientific community and Ukraine are mutually dependent: Ukraine benefits from international support, while the world benefits from Ukraine's scientific contributions.

Construction and validation of a machine learning-based model predicting early readmission in patients with decompensated cirrhosis: a prospective two-center cohort study
Authors: Fang Yang, Jia Li, Ziyi Yang, Liping Wu, Han Wang, Chao Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Early recognition and accurate identification are crucial. However, current evidence is elusive and traditional scores concerning liver disease severity are lacking specificity and sensitivity. We sought to construct and validate an explainable machine learning (ML)-based prediction model, and evaluate its prognostic implementation in patients readmitted due to acute episodes. The prediction model for discovery and validation was based on a two-center prospective investigation. Our discovery sample, comprising 636 patients with cirrhosis, was divided into a training set and a test set, with an additional cohort of 150 patients serving as an external validation. Eleven ML methods were performed to establish an indicative model based on a variety of easily accessible and obtainable variables from the electronic health record. The area under the ROC curve (AUC), alongside several evaluation parameters, was used for comparison regarding predictive performance. Background

Liver cirrhosis is manifested with an abrupt decrease in liver function and the presence of multiple acute episodes, including esophagogastric variceal hemorrhage, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy (HE) and infection, designated as a critical scenario prone to multi-organ failure [1]. Patients with decompensated cirrhosis are always experiencing more frequent hospitalization and early readmission after their discharge, which has attracted increasing interest of hepatologists due to its relevant physical, economic, and social burden [2]. From an operational and pragmatic point of view, it is necessary not only to identify risk factors but also to prevent resource waste and the burden on patients due to unplanned readmissions.

More recently, multiple reports have estimated readmission rates within 30 days, but considerable mixed results can be found in the existing literature: findings primarily deriving from North America indicated early readmission rates of approximately 40% [3]; however, the estimated 30-day readmission rates in East Asia (e.g., China and Japan) spanned from 5.6 to 7.2% [4–6]. Moreover, the 30-day readmission rate is currently regarded as an alternative measure of hospital quality and performance; therefore, it is imperative to balance the early identification of patients at high-risk readmission and the allocation of appropriate medical resources with highly sensitive and feasible tools. In this regard, a single biomarker in relation to liver disease severity (i.e., model for end-stage liver disease [MELD] or Child-Turcotte-Pugh [CTP] score), concomitant complications (i.e., HE or ascites), demographics and comorbidity have been linked to this inferior outcome. However, the heterogeneity of cirrhosis may negatively impact the predictive value of such indicators mentioned earlier, owing to a lack of specificity and sensitivity.

With the rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML) techniques based on electronic health record (EHR) has attracted attention and recognition. On one hand, the widely used EHR in hospitals enables more accurate, timely, and convenient collections of patients’ clinical information. On the other hand, numerous ML methods are being employed to construct prediction models associated with distinct outcomes in the context of cirrhosis, including the presence of acute renal injuries in the intensive care unit, early mortality among patients with concurrent HE, and malignant transformation to hepatocellular carcinoma [7–9]. However, there is scant data on constructing a prediction model concerning 30-day readmission derived from powerful ML techniques due to its complexity [10]. Furthermore, one noticeable issue is that a so-called “black-box” indicates difficulties in presenting a direct interpretation [11]. To overcome the lack of transparency and potential model bias, an innovative technique known as explainable AI (XAI) has been addressed, where the model-independent SHapley Additive exPlanation (SHAP) method has been adopted to explain ML models and visualize each predictive variable [12–15].

Herein, the present study sought to construct and verify an explainable ML model to predict early readmission among hospitalized patients with decompensated cirrhosis owing to acute episodes, elaborate on the feature importance, and provide insights by using the SHAP method for global and local interpretation. Finally, the prognostic implementation of the developed ML model alongside a webpage tool with pragmatic translation to daily practice was highlighted.

Methods

Study population

This cohort study was prospectively conducted in hospitalized patients with cirrhosis to discover and validate the prediction model. The discovery sample comprised subjects with decompensating insults admitted to the Department of Gastroenterology and Hepatology, Tianjin Medical University General Hospital from January 2018 to June 2022. The criteria have been defined in Table S1. Patients with acute-on-chronic liver failure, concomitant malignancies, or severe cognitive problems were excluded. The current study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the ethics committee of Tianjin Medical University General Hospital (IRB2023-YX-013-01). Written informed consent was provided by all participants.

Data preprocessing

Data collection and missing values processing. To develop an interpretable prediction model for early readmission, we extracted three categories of variables from the hospital EHR database (Table S2). The first category consisted of demographic characteristics, including age, gender, and body mass index (BMI). The second category included routine laboratory data, such as complete blood counts, liver function and coagulation. The third category comprised cirrhosis-related information, including traditional scores to assess disease severity, frail phenotype, Royal Free Hospital-Nutritional Prioritizing Tool (RFH-NPT) score, etiologies of liver cirrhosis, and complications. No missing data were present, since the present study’s prospective protocol required complete data collection at the initial recruitment. Categorical variables were deliberately encoded into a numerical format available for developing the ML models, ensuring their clinical relevance to be retained (e.g., encoded as 0 for non-30-day readmission and 1 for 30-day readmission).

Feature selection and normalization. Considering the prediction accuracy may be influenced by multicollinearity among features, when two features presented with a strong correlation in the data set (Spearman’s correlation analyses: coefficient > 0.6, Figure S1), one feature was finally selected for model development upon the univariate COX regression given statistical significance; a detailed explanation can be found in Table S3. Finally, 21 features, including sex, age, BMI, frail phenotype, etiology of liver cirrhosis (chronic viral hepatis, alcohol, autoimmune/cholestatic and cryptogenic reasons), CTP score, MELD-Na score, RFH-NPT score, hemoglobin (Hb), platelet (PLT), albumin, total bilirubin (TBIL), creatinine, prothrombin time-international normalized ratio (PT-INR), alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), gamma-glutamyltransferase (GGT), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and cirrhosis-associated complication (esophagogastric variceal hemorrhage, HE, ascites and infection), were utilized to construct the prediction model. The data from the discovery sample were divided into 80% as a training set and 20% as a test set (internal validation), with the purpose of avoiding overfitting problems. Then, the training data set was normalized using the Z-score method, scaling it to the 0–1 range to ensure consistency in scale and curtail feature disparities.

External validation

Patients admitted to the Department of Gastroenterology, The Third People’s Hospital of Chengdu from January 2020 to December 2020 were regarded as the external validation set. Identical criteria were generated from the discovery sample.

Outcome of interest

Although there is a lack of uniformity regarding the time duration that can be defined as early readmission, we have adopted 30 days as the cutoff, in agreement with a previous report [16]. Patients were followed for at least one month, inquiring about any readmission event through institutional medical records, clinic records, and telephone interviews. Moreover, elective readmission on account of planned procedures like endoscopic examination, or for other reasons irrelevant to underpinning liver disease was not regarded as outcome of interest. Patients lost to follow-up were also excluded from the final analysis (Figure S2).

Model selection and comparison

In the early readmission prediction task, selecting a suitable ML model is pivotal. The task involves extracting effective information from clinical data to help physicians quickly identify high-risk patients. To comprehensively assess the performance of distinct models, we selected a variety of classical and modern ML methods for comparison. We applied 11 ML algorithms, including logistic regression (logit), K-nearest neighbor (KNN), artificial neutral network (ANN), random forest (RF), gradient boosting machine (GBM), decision tree (DT), support vector machine (SVM), Naïve Bayes, eXtreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), Quadratic Discriminant Analysis (QDA) and extra tree (ET) to predict early 30-day readmission in patients with decompensated cirrhosis. Final hyperparameters were obtained according to manual fine-tuning to optimize the prediction model (Table S4). The performance and reliability of the derived model were assessed in terms of commonly used assessment indices, like the area under the ROC curve (AUC), specificity, sensitivity, and accuracy. Moreover, five-fold and ten-fold cross validations were carried out for our discovery sample to verify the final derived prediction model.

Feature reduction and model explanation

Given the challenges to correctly explain any ML model, the SHAP method has been considered an approach capable of ranking the input features’ importance and interpreting the findings of the proposed prediction model, widely utilized in hopes of addressing the “black-box” problem.

Feature reduction was facilitated via SHAP values, reducing the prediction model from 21 to 4 features based on the ordered feature importance. In this respect, our final proposed model, which demonstrated the best predictive capability after feature reduction, has been selected for further analysis. Differences were compared and determined using the non-parametric Delong’s method in terms of AUCs (R software version 4.3.2). The reduction of features concerning the selected model was terminated in correspondence with a dramatic decrease in the AUCs.

Model global/local explanations are addressed via the SHAP method. The global explanation gives rise to concordant as well as accurate attribution value of individual features entered into the model, demonstrating the relationship between input features and early 30-day readmission. On the other hand, a specific prediction results from the local explanation for a certain patient via the input of particular data.

Webpage dissemination tool

We have transformed the final model for prediction into an online webpage application based on the Streamlit Python-based framework, aimed at disseminating its use in clinical practice. Notably, any practitioner can obtain the probability of 30-day readmission alongside the force plot for an individual patient by providing the values pertaining to the final model’s corresponding feature.

Statistical analysis

Continuous data were presented as median alongside interquartile range and compared by using the non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test, whereas categorical data such as frequency alongside percentage were compared by using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. The optical cutoff regarding AUC was determined using the Youden index, which indicates the maximum value (specificity [%] + sensitivity [%] − 100%). Moreover, an AUC > 0.7 was defined a priori as a moderately accurate prediction regarding the model performance. Decision curve analysis was performed using the R project. A two-sided P value less than 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant. Other statistical analyses were performed using Python (version 3.10.0) and SPSS (version 26.0).

Results

Baseline characteristics

Our prospective investigation recruited 636 patients with decompensated cirrhosis designated as the discovery sample to develop the prediction model. Of the 740 patients hospitalized during the investigation period, 35 with concurrent acute-on-chronic liver failure, 47 experiencing various malignancies, and 22 having severe cognitive problems were excluded. A total of 636 patients were divided into respective training and internal validation settings. Additionally, among 174 patients in the external validation cohort, 6 experiencing acute-on-chronic liver failure, 10 experiencing malignancies, and 8 experiencing severe cognitive problems were excluded. Finally, the external validation set consisted of 150 patients. The baseline demographic characteristics, biochemical and clinical information of patients in the derivation (i.e., training and internal validation) and external validation cohorts are shown in Table S5-S7. Among the 636 patients as the discovery sample, 54 (8.5%) were readmitted within 30 days after their discharge. No significant differences were found in terms of sex, age, BMI, etiology of liver cirrhosis and RFH-NPT-defined nutritional status between patients readmitted and those not readmitted within 30 days, except for robust phenotype, which was more frequent in the latter group. Among a range of biochemical parameters, patients with early readmission had higher serum TBIL, creatinine, LDH alongside NLR and PLR, but lower LMR levels, that is, several indicators reflecting inflammatory activities at admission. As a result, both the CTP score and MELD-Na score were significantly higher, indicating an aggravated underlying disease severity. Among cirrhosis-associated complications, patients readmitted within 30 days more frequently experienced HE and infection, whereas no difference was reported in the prevalence of ascites.

Model development and performance comparison

We applied 11 ML algorithms to predict early 30-day readmission in patients with decompensated cirrhosis based on collecting the aforementioned data during index hospitalization. Of the 11 models to predict early readmission in the training set, RF had the highest AUC (0.982) (Fig. 2A). In the test set, the RF model also exhibited the highest AUC (0.853), indicative of the best predictive effect for early readmission, followed by the GBM model (AUC = 0.818) and the Naïve Bayes model (0.808) (Fig. 2B). Therefore, downstream analyses were carried out using the RF model. Furthermore, the top 5 best-performing algorithms’ ROC curves in addition to corresponding top 20 features’ SHAP summary plots were shown in Table S8 and Figure S3, respectively. The feature reduction process, based on feature importance ranking, demonstrated that the RF algorithm retained almost the best predictive performance among these five algorithms (Fig. 2C). Collectively, it is conclusive that early readmission was best predicted by the RF model. Moreover, the RF model performance with varying feature numbers can be observed in Fig. 2D, where the Youden index-maximized optimal cutoff was implemented to calculate the specificity, sensitivity, and accuracy.

Final model identification

We identified the final model via the feature reduction process of the RF algorithm. As depicted in Fig. 2D and Figure S4, the 21-feature marginally outperformed the 4-feature model (ΔAUC: 0.17, P = 0.06) as well as the 10-feature model (ΔAUC: 0.067, P = 0.065), but was comparable to the 7-feature model (ΔAUC: 0.01, P = 0.883) in predicting early readmission. The 7-feature model represented a higher threshold probability alongside an additional net benefit compared to the 21-feature model (Figure S5). Taken together, we concentrated on the 7-feature RF model, incorporating NLR, ALT, frail phenotype, BMI, ALP, LMR, and PLR as the final model for further analysis. In this respect, our final derived model demonstrated an AUC of 0.843 (specificity: 0.763, sensitivity: 0.9) alongside an accuracy of 0.773 (Table S9).

We also performed cross validations to verify the appropriateness of the sample size for this study and the robustness of this model to site variation. As shown in Figure S6, our proposed model yielded mean AUCs of 0.70 ± 0.08 and 0.72 ± 0.11 in the five-fold and ten-fold cross-validations, respectively. Likewise, the predictive capabilities of the MELD-Na score and CTP score, which implicate underlying liver disease severity, were compared with those of the 7-feature final model. As shown in Figure S7A, MELD-Na (ΔAUC: 0.439, P < 0.001) and CTP score (ΔAUC: 0.168, P < 0.001) demonstrated significantly poorer performance in the test set compared to the final model. A greater clinical utility of the final model compared to either MELD-Na or CTP score was observed when applying the DCA curves (Figure S7B).

Final model external validation

Regarding external validation, the final model achieved an AUC of 0.838, which was comparable to that in the internal validation (ΔAUC: 0.005, P = 0.956). This indicates that both internal and external validations demonstrated excellent performance regarding our derived model.

Model explanation

Given that it is challenging for any practitioner to accept a prediction model insufficient in explanation, we adopted the SHAP method with the purpose of interpreting the final model output in terms of determining the contribution of each variable for predicting. Two categories of explanations can be found: the feature-level-dictated global explanation versus the individual-level-dictated local explanation. Figure 3A-B illustrates the contribution of the indicative feature towards the model via SHAP summary plots, which was assessed based on the mean SHAP value and presented in a descending sequence. Furthermore, the way in which the respective feature influences the prediction model output can be extrapolated from the SHAP dependence plot. The real value versus the SHAP value pertaining to those 7 features were illustrated in Fig. 3C, indicating that the SHAP value greater than 0 points supported a positive association with the model’s prediction of early readmission. For instance, patients with an NLR ≥ 5.92 or a LMR ≤ 1.90 had a SHAP value of more than 0 points, advancing the decision towards the “early readmission” group. Moreover, patients with frailty pushed the decision towards the “early readmission” group. A low actual value ≤ 17.6 kg/m2 or a high actual value ≥ 25.0 kg/m2 also advanced the decision towards the “early readmission” group.

Prognostic implementation

The exploration of prognostic implementation utilized all patients, including 786 patients with decompensated cirrhosis from the derivation (i.e., training and test) and external validation cohorts. The optimal cutoff to maximize sensitivity and specificity was 19.8%, predicting early readmission as the practical subgrouping threshold to define “Prediction model positive” and “Prediction model negative”. Figure 6 showed that patients were categorized into distinct subgroups based on combined final derived model and follow-up results: subgroup 1 [30-day readmission negative/Prediction model negative], subgroup 2 [30-day readmission negative/Prediction model positive], subgroup 3 [30-day readmission positive/Prediction model negative] and subgroup 4 [30-day readmission positive/Prediction model positive]. As demonstrated in Table S10, patients in subgroup 4 exhibited aggravated underlying liver disease severity determined by the CTP score and deleterious frailty. Furthermore, patients in subgroup 2, although not proceeding to re-hospitalization, had higher MELD-Na score and comparable frequency of frail phenotype relative to subgroup 4.

Discussion

This prospective two-center investigation sought to explore and compare 11 ML algorithms to comprehensively predict early 30-day readmission in patients with decompensated cirrhosis. We retrieved a set of precipitating factors and developed a prediction model among subjects re-hospitalized due to acute insults using ML techniques based on clinical and some biochemical data accessible from the EHR database.

To date, although some research has preliminarily investigated the risk factors/predictors for cirrhosis-related readmission from diverse populations and institutions, it has not reached consensus (Table S11). For instance, Patel and colleagues showed that discharged MELD score and serum sodium level were independently associated with 1-month readmission by analyzing a large cohort of 281 Asian patients experiencing decompensating insults [17]. Their following study further found that massive numbers of medications and platelet count at discharge alongside active alcoholism served as predictors for 30-day readmission in ethnic minority patients hospitalized for cirrhosis in the U.S [18]. Moreover, another Thailand study recruiting 134,038 patients, with an overall 30-day readmission of 17%, reported independent risk factors as the presence of various liver cancers and HIV-related admission [19]. One study conducted in 329 patients revealed that a low Hb level was a novel predictor doubling early readmission risk, facilitating the identification of individuals requiring intensive surveillance after discharge [20]. This probably speaks to the fact that a single analyte is insufficient for the pragmatic use, given the mixed and complex pathophysiology of cirrhosis [21]. In this respect, the ML technique has emerged as a powerful computational approach coping with complicated and extensive data, since it can manage extremely various settings of data and shed light on sophisticated association among variates in a flexible and trainable manner. Of note, a combination of sophisticated ML technique and EHR data, which is accessible and precise for clinicians/investigators to retrieve clinical information, can foster the construction of prediction models [22]. Notably, our preliminary report indicated that the final RF model, among eleven algorithms, showed substantially predictive performance with additional net benefit. Consistently, emerging evidence has clarified the wide utility and validity of the RF model in hepatology [23–25]. Upon processing feature reduction, we have successfully developed a cost-effective tool with seven features for identifying patients with decompensated cirrhosis prone to early re-hospitalization. Although it is suggested that more features are capable of bringing more referenced information to the prediction model, incorporating too many features may curtail the usefulness of derived model, and redundant features without causality may negatively impact the prediction accuracy [26]. Taken together, we seek a balance between remarkable predictive ability and clinical convenience and simplicity taking account of selected features’ accessibility and availability, where the SHAP method was carried out to facilitate feature selection.

Our final model outperformed traditional scores, such as CTP or MELD-Na score, in predicting early 30-day readmission. The validity of CTP and MELD to predict readmission in the context of cirrhosis appears to be uncertain, since these prognostic scores are primarily intended for predicting long-term mortality over extended periods [3, 27, 28]. These scores may not capture the specific factors that contribute to short-term readmission risk. For instance, Penrice et al. showed that malnutrition/frailty accounted for approximately one-fifth of readmission among cirrhotic patients, while proportional readmitted cases were attributed to non-liver-related reasons [29]. Intriguingly, a spectrum of systemic immune-inflammatory biomarkers including NLR, LMR and PLR entered into our derived model, which was consistent with previous studies based on ML techniques to predict various outcome in the medical field. Wang and colleagues revealed that the Naïve Bayes algorithm incorporating PLR, LMR and other nutritional indicators performs best for the prediction of adult non-small cell lung cancer [30]. Another study based on improved ML also demonstrated that a moderately greedy Extreme Gradient Boosting model including NLR, PLR and LMR exhibited the highest AUC in predicting postoperative infectious complications among elderly colorectal cancer patients [31]. It is suggested that all of LMR, NLR and PLR have clinical utility for discriminating bacterial infection in cirrhosis, as LMR emerging as the best simple predictor [32]. Moreover, our research group clearly addressed a significantly positive correlation between circulating NLR and serum IL-6/IL-8 levels [33]. Being rapidly readmitted acts as a stressful psychophysiologic event to trigger a sharp deterioration of health-related quality of life (HRQoL). Accordingly, we previously linked frail phenotype to impaired HRQoL among cirrhosis [34]. The currently utilized scale evaluating frailty includes all aspects, including instrumental activity of daily living, comorbidity, prescriptions and social deficits. Therefore, an interplay between frailty, HRQoL and various poor outcomes like early readmission and short-term survival warrants in-depth investigation. Although no evidence supports the predictive ability of ALT and ALP for early readmission, those are constant and sensitive biomarkers related to hepatocyte injury and cholestasis [35]. The role of BMI on early readmission among cirrhosis is elusive, both low BMI and high BMI may serve as independent risk factors in other pathological entities, indicating its pleiotropic effect [36, 37].

Beyond prediction, the present study highlights potential targets for intervention. Notably, our final RF model consisted varying modifiable factors: Regular prescription to manage underlying etiologies with anti-viral or choleretic/hepatic protective drugs is crucial to retain normal liver function, which can be attained by clinicians at discharge and in close monitoring at pharmaceutical care clinic; Considering cirrhosis is a confluence of both inflammatory activity and immunoparesis, prophylactic strategies against the attack of HE or spontaneous bacterial peritonitis using gastrointestinal-specific rifaximin or well-tolerant nutrition therapy via multidisciplinary approaches [38, 39]; Frailty is defined as a composite indicator that reflects the cumulative impact of various health impairments. Therefore, social support, sufficient linkage to community resources, and instruction on home-based care from nurses (if available) are the center.

In particular, the ML technique entails drawbacks inherent to its limited explainability regarding how prediction arises, which is designated as a “black-box”. This phenomenon may lead clinicians and investigators to refuse to use it due to their hesitation about starting medical treatment based on obscure background information. This argued another advantage of the current investigation: the SHAP method was used for offsetting the “black-box” regarding the ML technique. The SHAP method is capable of interpreting this model from an overall functionality perspective, in addition to deciphering local explanations about how to generate a certain prediction specific to an individual patient. In this regard, a convenient application in terms of the Streamlit framework has been launched on an indicative webpage, to be shared with more practitioners about the prediction model. Another principal finding in the current study implicated utilizing the final RF model-defined subgroups to identify patients at high risk, standing for a special and innovative method. We achieved an optimal cutoff value of 19.8% probability, yielding the maximum specificity and sensitivity. Early readmission predicted only by the final model without positive follow-up also had higher underlying liver disease severity and deteriorated frailty, verifying this model’s clinical significance and relevance.

The use of XAI and automated ML to support risk prediction, and in consequence, clinical decision among cirrhosis warrants addressing critical ethical concerns. In particular, medicine is a high-stakes environment where trust is not negotiable. Of note, different XAI models may obtain divergent or even inconsistent explanations, jeopardizing the core ethical consideration in daily practice to “first, do not harm” [40]. Keeping in mind that safety and transparency constitute the two key issues affecting every aspect of AI-based decision-making processes, indispensable for building trust and acceptance of this innovative technology. Another limitation to be addressed is XAI without causality, and sufficient empirical evidence cannot directly guide decision choices, but rather an adjunct to assist in rationalizing model output within a clinical context. Notably, a clinician-informed checklist has recently been developed to foster XAI adoption, and enhance the determination of true practical value of applied XAI by reducing safety and ethical risks [41].

Some limitations should be acknowledged of this study. First, the reported prevalence of early 30-day readmission (8.5%) was relatively lower than in previous studies, especially from North America. We applied more stringent inclusion eligibility and constrained the outcome of interest to cirrhosis-related re-hospitalization, partially accounting for this difference. Second, we revealed that special indicators pertaining to immune-inflammation played pivotal role rather than overt infection to predict early readmission within the final RF model. Actually, insidious infections like Clostridium difficile infection constituted the primary reason and predictor for early readmission in cirrhosis, but were always underestimated in hepatologists’ work [42]. Third, CTP and MELD-Na were rated 8th and 9th feature importance according to the SHAP approach, failure in incorporation to this 7-feature RF model. We noticed no significant differences concerning albumin and ascites included in the CTP score and PT-INR included in the MELD-Na score, hence their less association with model input. These findings were to some extent similar to another report, where CTP emerged as an independent risk factor for the readmission but the MELD/MELD-Na score dropped [18]. Fourth, it should be noted that the predictive performance of the developed RF model is modestly accurate (AUC around 0.8) and lacks extensive external validation on other data sets from diverse populations and regions, which may impact its generalizability. This speaks to the need to employ a larger sample cohort, which may better apply XAI models for the prediction of complex clinical outcomes like readmission. While it is regrettable that only 786 patients were analyzed in this study, we are currently continuing our research and aim to analyze results from a larger population in future investigations. Lastly, it is desirable to utilize “big data” for ML techniques concerning prediction model development, but no standard exists with respect to calculating an appropriate sample size. Nonetheless, we adequately carried out cross and external validations, implicating a sufficient sample size alongside a two-center prospective study design, pinpointing enough power for investigating an indicative model to predict early readmission in the context of patients with decompensated cirrhosis.

Conclusions

In summary, we succeeded in constructing an explainable ML model predicting early 30-day readmission in patients with decompensated cirrhosis based on accessible and obtainable variables from EHR. The final RF model was moderately accurate in both internal and external validations. Further randomized control trials are warranted to explore whether precision and personalized medicine in terms of the final prediction model can mitigate readmission-related expenditures on healthcare and the socioeconomic system in the context of cirrhosis.

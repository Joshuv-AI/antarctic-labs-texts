The construction and validation of a nomogram for lung metastasis of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma: a study based on the SEER database
Authors: Shanfei Zhu, Jinfu Huang, Fan Wu, Wei Liu, Qi Cheng, Bailin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung metastasis is the most common extrahepatic metastasis organ for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), often leading to an unfavorable prognosis. Although nomograms exist for predicting the risk of distant metastasis in ICC patients, a precise prognostic model specifically for patients with established intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma lung metastasis (ICCLM) remains lacking. The aim of this study was to develop and validate a prognostic nomogram for predicting cancer-specific survival (CSS) in patients with ICCLM based on the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database.MethodsWe extracted data on 620 patients with ICCLM from the SEER database spanning the years 2010 to 2020. Patients from 2010 to 2017 were randomly allocated to a training cohort (n=274) and an internal validation cohort (n=118). Data from 2018 to 2020 (n=228) served as the external validation cohort. Employing both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, we identified independent prognostic factors for ICCLM and subsequently developed a nomogram for CSS prediction. The performance of the nomogram was then assessed using the C-index, calibration plot, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival curves were plotted to illustrate survival disparities across different risk strata.ResultsBased on these factors, a nomogram was constructed. In the training, internal validation, and external validation cohorts, the C-index of the nomogram was 0.723, 0.761, and 0.758, respectively. The calibration curve, ROC curve, and DCA demonstrated commendable performance in predicting the 3-, 6-, and 12-month CSS for ICCLM patients. The KM survival analysis indicated that the 1-, 3-, and 5-year CSS rates for ICCLM patients were 23.9%, 3.3%, and 0.8%, respectively, with a median CSS of 5 months. Notably, there was a significant survival disparity between the low- and high-risk groups (P<0.05).ConclusionsThe constructed nomogram stands out as a potent, individualized prognostic tool for ICCLM patients, poised to guide clinicians in tailoring more strategic and efficacious therapeutic regimens.

Highlight box

Key findings

• A prognostic nomogram was developed and validated to predict 3-, 6-, and 12-month cancer-specific survival (CSS) in patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma lung metastasis (ICCLM).

• The nomogram-based risk score effectively stratified patients into low- and high-risk groups with significantly different survival outcomes.

What is known and what is new?

• Patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma generally face low survival and high risk of recurrence, with overall survival rates of approximately 30% and 18% at 1 and 5 years, respectively.

• This study establishes a population-based prognostic model integrating age, liver metastasis, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy to enable individualized survival prediction in ICCLM patients. In our study, the 1-, 3-and 5-year CSS rates for patients with ICCLM were 23.9%, 3.3% and 0.8%, respectively, with a median CSS of 5 months.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The nomogram provides refined risk stratification beyond conventional staging systems, allowing clinicians to identify patients with relatively favorable versus extremely poor prognosis.

• Low-risk patients may be considered for active multimodal treatment strategies, including systemic therapy and selected locoregional approaches.

• For high-risk patients with very limited predicted survival, the model may assist in avoiding overtreatment, prioritizing non-surgical management, and facilitating early palliative care planning.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer (PLC) predominantly comprises three histological subtypes: hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), and mixed HCC-ICC (1). ICC is an epithelial malignancy arising from the biliary epithelium, accounting for 10% to 15% of PLC and ranking as the second most common histological subtype after HCC (2,3). In recent years, the incidence and mortality rates of ICC have increased globally (2,4). Unfortunately, the prognosis for ICC patients, whether surgical or non-surgical, remains unsatisfactory (4). Even post-curative surgery, the 5-year overall survival (OS) fluctuates between 20% and 35%, coupled with a high recurrence rate spanning 40% to 80% (5,6). As the disease progresses, advanced ICC may metastasize to extrahepatic organs, with the lungs being the most common site of distant organ metastasis (7,8).

Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma lung metastasis (ICCLM) distinctly differs from other tumors, necessitating its own unique prognostic prediction model. An effective prediction model can assist clinicians in tailoring the optimal treatment strategy for individual patient prognoses. The traditional tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) staging system primarily focuses on tumor size, lymph node metastasis, and distant metastasis, overlooking other patient attributes such as age, gender, and treatment methods (9,10). As a result, the TNM staging system might not be wholly applicable to all ICC patients, especially those in advanced stages with distant metastasis. There is an urgent need for a more precise staging system tailored for ICCLM patients.

The nomogram is a practical and ideal visualization tool for predicting and calculating the outcome probabilities for each patient and has been extensively utilized in aiding clinical decisions (11,12). Given the relative rarity of ICCLM patients, we employed the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database to study a cohort of ICCLM patients from 2010 to 2020. This research aimed to develop a nomogram to predict cancer-specific survival (CSS) rates for these patients, which subsequently underwent both internal and external validation. The findings suggest that this nomogram can offer clinicians enhanced prognostic prediction, risk stratification, and a valuable tool for clinical decision-making. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2025-2083/rc).

Methods

Patients and data selection

Utilizing the SEER*Stat software (version 8.4.2), we extracted clinical-pathological data and prognostic information of ICC patients from the SEER database spanning the years 2010 to 2020. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. Given that SEER operates as a de-identified public database, there was no requirement for patient informed consent or ethical approval. Our analysis was conducted in strict adherence to the guidelines set by the SEER database. The clinical variables collected encompassed: year of diagnosis, age, sex, race, marital status, histological grade, tumor stage, surgical interventions, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, liver metastasis, bone metastasis, and brain metastasis. The primary endpoint of this study was the CSS, defined as the duration from the date of diagnosis to the date of death attributed to the cancer.

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (I) diagnosis year between 2010 and 2020; (II) ICD site code: C22.1; (III) international Classification of Diseases for Oncology, Third Edition (ICD-O-3) code 8160. Exclusion criteria encompassed: (I) patients diagnosed via autopsy or death certificate; (II) patients with missing pivotal clinicopathological data, such as age, sex, race, marital status, histological grade, and tumor stage; (III) unknown information regarding surgical interventions, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy; (IV) undetermined distant metastasis data (bone, brain, liver); (V) ambiguous cause of death; (VI) indeterminate survival duration; (VII) presence of primary tumors in other locations.

In this study, a total of 620 ICCLM patients were included. Patients diagnosed between 2010 and 2017 were randomly divided into a training cohort (70%) and an internal validation cohort (30%). Patients diagnosed between 2018 and 2020 were defined as the external validation cohort (Figure 1). During follow-up, a total of 537 CSS events were observed in the entire cohort. As the final multivariable Cox regression model included 4 independent predictors, the events per variable (EPV) ratio was 134.3, which supports the robustness of our multivariable analysis.

Statistical analysis

In this study, we constructed a nomogram using a training cohort (n=274) and subsequently validated the model with both an internal validation cohort (n=118) and an external validation cohort (n=228). Assisted by the X-tile software, we identified 50 and 70 years as the optimal age cut-off points. For prognostic factors, univariate Cox regression analysis was initially employed to screen relevant variables. Subsequently, the variables with P<0.10 were incorporated into a multivariate Cox regression analysis, leading to the identification of independent prognostic factors associated with ICCLM.

We constructed the ICCLM prognostic model using the RMS package in R software. Following this, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of the nomogram was plotted, and its discriminative ability was evaluated using the area under the curve (AUC). Through ROC curve analysis, the discriminative power of the nomogram was compared with that of other independent risk factors. Additionally, calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) were generated to further assess the model’s performance. Ultimately, based on the median risk score, all patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups. The predictive value of the nomogram was validated using survival curves and the log-rank test. All statistical analyses were conducted in R software version 4.3.1, with P<0.05 considered statistically significant.

Results

Characteristics of ICCLM patients

From the SEER database, we identified 1,238 patients diagnosed with ICC accompanied by lung metastasis (LM) between 2010 and 2020. After applying the aforementioned exclusion criteria, 618 patients were excluded, resulting in a total of 620 patients included in this study. Of these, 392 patients diagnosed between 2010 and 2017 were randomly allocated into a training cohort (n=274) and an internal validation cohort (n=118) at a 7:3 ratio. Patients diagnosed between 2018 and 2020, totaling 228, were designated as the external validation cohort. Within the training set, age stratification was performed using the X-tile software (version 3.6.1), revealing that 56.6% of patients were in the age range of 50–70 years. The majority of patients were Caucasian and married. In terms of sex distribution, 55.5% were male and 44.5% were female. Regarding tumor characteristics, Grade III tumors accounted for 21.9%, but notably, a significant portion of the data was categorized as “unknown”. The most common T and N stages were T2 (54.4%) and N1 (54.7%), respectively. Among these patients, 42 (15.3%) had bone metastasis, 93 (33.9%) had liver metastasis, and only 1 (0.4%) had brain metastasis. In terms of treatment modalities, the vast majority of patients (98.5%) did not undergo surgical intervention, 174 patients (63.5%) received chemotherapy, and 24 patients (8.8%) underwent radiotherapy.

Prognostic factors for ICCLM patients

To assess various prognostic factors, we conducted both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses (Table 2). The results from the univariate Cox regression analysis indicated that age, marital status, brain metastasis, liver metastasis, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy were significantly associated with prognosis (P<0.10). However, factors such as sex, race, histological grade, T stage, N stage, and bone metastasis showed no statistically significant correlation with prognosis. The multivariate Cox regression analysis further identified age, liver metastasis, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy as independent prognostic factors for CSS.

Prognostic nomogram development and validation

We included all independent risk factors and developed nomograms using the RMS package in R software to predict the CSS of ICCLM patients at 3-, 6-, and 12-month (Figure 2). As depicted by the nomogram, chemotherapy statistically emerged as the most significant factor influencing CSS, followed by age, radiotherapy, and liver metastasis. The C-indices for the training cohort, internal validation cohort, and external validation cohort were 0.723 [95% confidence interval (CI): 690–0.756], 0.761 (95% CI: 0.714–0.770), and 0.758 (95% CI: 0.719–0.797), respectively, confirming the high predictive accuracy of the prognostic model.

Nomogram predict CSS in patients with ICCLM for 3-, 6-, and 12-month. CSS, cancer-specific survival; ICCLM, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma lung metastasis.

Further, we compared the AUC values across different cohorts (Figure 3). For the SEER training cohort, the AUC for predicting 3-, 6-, and 12-month CSS were 0.818 (95% CI: 0.765–0.870), 0.788 (95% CI: 0.735–0.848), and 0.753 (95% CI: 0.699–0.819), respectively. For the internal validation cohort, the AUC for 3-, 6-, and 12-month CSS were 0.846 (95% CI: 0.767–0.917), 0.853 (95% CI: 0.775–0.921), and 0.804 (95% CI: 0.704–0.896), respectively. For the external validation cohort, the AUC for 3-, 6-, and 12-month CSS were 0.852 (95% CI: 0.795–0.925), 0.777 (95% CI: 0.716–0.849), and 0.763 (95% CI: 0.692–0.840), respectively. Compared to predictions based on individual prognostic factors, the nomogram exhibited superior accuracy in predicting CSS at 3-, 6-, and 12-month (Figure 4). Additionally, calibration curves for the 3-, 6-, and 12-month CSS were plotted for each cohort, showcasing a high degree of consistency between the nomogram predictions and actual observations (Figure 5). Lastly, the DCA curves for all three cohorts further underscored the clinical utility of the nomogram (Figure 6). The Nomogram consistently demonstrated a superior net benefit over the “treat none” (illustrated by the blue line) and “treat all” (illustrated by the green line) strategies across most probability thresholds.

Establishment of a risk stratification system based on the nomogram model

In our study of 620 patients, KM survival analysis revealed survival rates of 23.9%, 3.3%, and 0.8% at 1-, 3-, and 5-year, respectively, with a median CSS of 5 months (Figure 7). Utilizing the Cox proportional hazards model, we calculated scores for each independent prognostic factor, culminating in a composite risk score for each patient. Subsequently, risk stratification was established based on the median composite risk score across the three cohorts. Our findings consistently indicated that patients in the high-risk group exhibited significantly lower CSS than those in the low-risk group across training, internal validation, and external validation cohort (Figure 7). Within the training cohort, the survival rates for the high-risk group at 3-, 6-, and 12-month were 24.0%, 10.6%, and 3.9% respectively, while those for the low-risk group were 78.6%, 78.6%, and 37.0%.

Discussion

Surgical resection is considered the only potentially curative treatment for ICC patients. Regrettably, due to the insidious and non-specific early symptoms of ICC, the majority of patients are diagnosed at an advanced stage (5). As a result, the resection rate for ICC remains low, with a poor prognosis, and the OS rates at 1- and 5-year are approximately 30% and 18%, respectively (13,14). In fact, distant metastasis is a hallmark of advanced ICC, indicating a poor prognosis for patients. It is well known that the TNM staging system primarily focuses on tumor size, lymph node metastasis, and distant metastasis, yet it overlooks other pivotal patient characteristics such as age, sex, and treatment modalities (9,10). Hence, the TNM system might not be entirely appropriate for all ICC patients, especially those with distant metastases. Therefore, it becomes imperative to develop a system that can accurately predict the prognosis of ICCLM.

Notably, Shi et al. (15) constructed nomograms to predict the likelihood of lung and bone metastases in ICC patients at diagnosis. However, no study has yet developed a validated prognostic tool tailored to patients who already present with ICCLM. In this study, we analyzed the baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients. To identify independent prognostic factors, we conducted both univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression analyses. The univariate analysis suggested that age, marital status, brain metastasis, liver metastasis, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy are potential prognostic indicators for patients with ICCLM. Upon multivariate analysis, only age, liver metastasis, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy emerged as independent prognostic factors. A nomogram was subsequently constructed based on these four factors. The model was validated using the C-index, ROC curves, calibration curves, and DCA curves demonstrating good accuracy and high reliability. KM survival curves revealed a significant difference in CSS between the low- and high-risk groups, underscoring the practicality of our model. In summary, the nomogram accurately predicts the 3-, 6-, and 12-month CSS for ICCLM patients.

Through univariate and multivariate analyses, we discerned that several factors significantly influenced CSS, including age, liver metastasis, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. However, variables such as sex, ethnicity, marital status, Grade stage, T-stage, N-stage, surgical resection, bone metastasis, and brain metastasis were not identified as independent prognostic determinants.

It has been reported that the incidence of ICC increases with age, with a median patient age of 62 years (16-18). The prognosis is typically unfavorable when ICC is accompanied by intrahepatic metastasis (19). Research by Shi et al. (15) highlighted that intrahepatic metastasis is a primary factor leading to LM in ICC patients. Consistently, our study found that ICC patients with concurrent liver and lung metastases have a poorer prognosis.

Radiotherapy and chemotherapy both hold pivotal roles in tumor treatment (20). Patients with distant metastases are typically recommended for systemic treatments, such as systemic chemotherapy and targeted therapy (21). Among these, the combined chemotherapy regimen of gemcitabine and cisplatin is considered the most effective first-line treatment strategy for ICC patients with distant metastases (7,21,22). Radiotherapy has also been demonstrated to prolong the survival of ICC patients and reduce the risk of death from tumor-related liver failure (23-25). In our study, both chemotherapy and radiotherapy demonstrated a positive correlation with patients’ survival rates. Further, multivariate analysis revealed that chemotherapy significantly contributes to the survival of ICCLM patients.

In a retrospective study on ICC with brain metastasis, the median survival time was found to be only 3.7 months, with patients exhibiting advanced T-stage, untreated primary lesions, lack of radiotherapy, and presence of bone metastases showing unfavorable prognoses (26). Yuan et al. (20) also highlighted that the TNM stage is an independent risk factor affecting the prognosis of ICC patients. However, it’s imperative to note that our study specifically focuses on stage IV ICCLM patients. Upon analyzing the prognosis of ICCLM patients, we found that Grade stage, T-stage, N-stage, surgical resection, bone metastasis, and brain metastasis did not constitute independent prognostic factors, which was consistent with our previous studies of patients with bone metastases from ICC (6).

In discussing the indications for surgical resection of distant ICC metastases, Yoh et al. (27) suggested that surgery is a reasonable option for patients with intrahepatic metastasis, vascular invasion, or regional lymph node metastasis, as long as a complete resection is feasible upon gross examination. Yan et al. (7) found that for patients with isolated liver metastases, surgery at the primary site (P=0.002) and at the metastatic site (P=0.005) both conferred survival benefits. However, for patients with isolated pulmonary metastases, only surgery at the metastatic site showed a survival advantage (P=0.01), while surgery at the primary site did not demonstrate significant benefits. It is worth noting that given the subjects we studied, the number of patients choosing surgery was relatively small. In conclusion, further research is warranted to validate the therapeutic outcomes of surgery in such patients.

In many cancers, nomograms have been demonstrated to be more accurate and user-friendly than traditional staging systems (28,29). Our nomogram ultimately incorporates easily obtainable factors (age, liver metastasis, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy), facilitating individualized calculations for ICCLM patients. Notably, the impact of chemotherapy on CSS was the most significant, with patients undergoing chemotherapy exhibiting higher CSS than those not receiving chemotherapy. In assessing the robustness of our model for predicting CSS across various time intervals, the training cohort, internal validation, and external validation cohorts consistently demonstrated satisfactory predictive performance, with AUC values consistently exceeding 0.75. The C-indices for the training, internal validation, and external validation cohorts were 0.723, 0.761, and 0.758, respectively. A higher C-index generally indicates more accurate prognostic predictions (30). This suggests that our nomogram offers a high degree of authenticity and reliability in predicting CSS in ICCLM patients. DCA is employed to assess the performance of predictive models in clinical decision-making. It provides an intuitive visualization of the net benefit achieved by adopting a predictive model over alternative strategies, such as treating all or not treating any patients, across different probability thresholds (31-33). Leveraging this approach, we utilized DCA to evaluate our nomogram, and the results underscored its superior clinical utility. Employing KM survival analysis, we determined that the 1-, 3-, and 5-year CSS of the 620 patients included in this study were 23.9%, 3.3%, and 0.8%, respectively, with a median CSS of 5 months. Moreover, a notable difference in CSS between the low- and high-risk groups was observed, further attesting to the reliability and practicality of our model.

While our study offers valuable insights, there are several noteworthy limitations. Firstly, the data for this research was solely derived from the SEER database, implying that the findings might not be entirely representative of regions outside the United States, such as Asia, Africa, and South America. Moreover, the SEER database lacks data on certain factors that could be closely associated with prognosis, such as hepatitis B virus (HBV) or hepatitis C virus (HCV) infections, carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9) levels, and vascular invasion. Thirdly, our external validation data also originated from the SEER database, which might influence the robustness of the validation. Finally, our study used complete-case analysis, excluding approximately 50% of the initial cohort due to missing T and N staging data—a potential source of selection bias if missingness relates to disease severity or treatment access. Although statistical power was sufficient (EPV >100), generalizability may be limited by this non-random attrition.

Conclusions

We constructed and validated a line chart for predicting 1-, 6-, and 12-month CSS in ICCLM patients. Our proposed diagram takes into account four independent risk factors: age, liver metastasis, chemotherapy and radiotherapy. After internal and external verification, it shows good accuracy and clinical applicability, which can help clinicians to implement individual clinical decisions.

ABSTRACT
Key Points•5-Aza plus HDAC3i has a synergistic antilymphoma effect in vitro and in vivo. HDAC3i shows higher efficacy than pan-HDACi.•The combinatorial therapy induces the differentiation of lymphoma cells through epigenetic reprogramming and leads to cell death.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) originates from germinal center (GC) B cells and is the most common type of lymphoma in adults. DLBCLs are categorized as GC B cell-like (GCB), activated B cell-like (ABC), or unclassified based on their cell of origin.1,2 The response rate to the first-line immunochemotherapy, consisting of the monoclonal antibody rituximab and 4 chemotherapy drugs (cyclophosphamide, doxorubixcin hydrochloride, vincristine sulfate and prednisone) or R-CHOP, is ∼60% to 70%.3 The other 30% to 40% of these patients with DLBCL present with relapsed/refractory disease, which is associated with a poor prognosis and a median overall survival of only 6.3 months.4 Therefore, there is a clinical need to find improved therapies to treat DLBCL.

DLBCL is characterized by alterations in the epigenome, including recurrent somatic mutations in genes encoding epigenetic enzymes as well as aberrant DNA methylation patterns compared with those in normal GC B cells.5 Importantly, multiple studies by us and others have shown that epigenetic dysregulation contributes to DLBCL initiation and progression.6 This knowledge has led to the development of epigenetic-based therapies to treat DLBCL, including inhibitors of DNA methyltransferases (DNMT) and histone deacetylases (HDAC).7

The DNMT inhibitors (DNMTi) 5-azacitidine (5-aza) and decitabine are approved by the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency, and they are currently used to treat myeloid diseases. In DLBCL, preclinical studies have showed that lymphoma cells exposed to low doses of 5-aza for a prolonged time become sensitive to chemotherapy through the hypomethylation and subsequent reactivation of genes such as SMAD1, which is hypermethylated in chemoresistant DLBCL cells.8 An associated phase 1 clinical trial consisting of 5-aza before R-CHOP resulted in complete responses in 10 of 11 patients with high-risk DLBCL.8 A more recent phase 1 clinical trial of oral 5-aza (CC-486) plus R-CHOP in patients with DLBCL confirmed the efficacy of 5-aza priming before chemotherapy with an overall response rate of 94.9% and complete response in 88% of the patients.9

Despite these promising results, 5-aza treatment at the effective doses was associated with hematological toxicities such as neutropenia,9 as is the case with pan-HDAC inhibitors (HDACi), which target multiple HDACs.10 A potential strategy to overcome the limitations of epigenetic therapies and increase their efficacy while reducing their undesired toxic side effects is through the design of new combinatorial therapies. Decitabine plus HDACi showed a synergistic effect in both GCB- and ABC-DLBCL cell lines, primary DLBCL cells in vitro and murine xenograft models.11 A more recent study combining 5-aza and the pan-HDACi vorinostat confirmed their synergistic activity against DLBCL, although the accompanying phase 1b trial revealed low clinical benefit and associated toxicities in patients treated with the combinatorial therapy.12

Next-generation selective HDACi show promise to improve the safety profiles of this type of epigenetic inhibitors.13 We previously showed that, during the GC reaction, HDAC3 is recruited by BCL6 in GC B cells to induce the repression of BCL6 target genes, which are reexpressed upon GC exit by the histone acetyl transferase CREBBP to allow the normal differentiation of GC B cells into plasma cells (PC) or memory B cells.14, 15, 16 Moreover, we demonstrated that the selective inhibition of HDAC3 was effective against DLBCL.17,18

Due to this essential role of HDAC3i in GC biology and lymphomagenesis, we hypothesized that the antitumor effects of hypomethylating agents (HMA) can be enhanced by the inhibition of HDAC3. Here, we investigate the therapeutic efficacy of 5-aza in combination with a specific HDAC3i against DLBCL compared with that of each single agent or pan-HDACi.

Methods

DLBCL cell lines

The human DLBCL cell lines MD901, HBL-1, TMD8, SU-DHL-4, and Farage were grown in medium containing RPMI 1640, 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% HEPES (N-2-hydroxyethylpiperazine-N′-2-ethanesulfonic acid), 1% glutamine, and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. MD901 (RRID:CVCL_D709), HBL-1 (RRID:CVCL_4213), and TMD8 (RRID:CVCL_A442) were provided by Jose Angel Martinez-Climent (Centro de Investigacion Medica Aplicada, Pamplona, Spain). SU-DHL-4 (RRID:CVCL_0539) and Farage (RRID:CVCL_3302) were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection. The DLBCL cell line OCI-Ly7 was grown in Iscove’s medium supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. OCI-Ly7 was purchased from the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures GmbH (DSMZ, RRID:CVCL_1881). Cell line authentication was performed by DNA genotyping (Australian Genome Research Facility, Melbourne, Australia). All cell lines were cultured at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2. Cell lines were tested for Mycoplasma contamination.

In vitro drug treatment

The selective HDAC3 inhibitor (KDAc-001) was provided by KDAc Therapeutics.19 Panobinostat (LBH589, S1030) and vorinostat (SAHA, S1047) were obtained from Selleck Chem. Romidepsin (FR 901228) was obtained from TargetMol. 5-Aza was purchased from Sigma (A2385). For in vitro experiments, small molecules were reconstituted to a final stock concentration of 10mM in dimethyl sulfoxide (HDAC3i, panobinostat, vorinostat, or romidepsin) or phosphate-buffered saline (5-aza) before serial dilution. After seeding (day 0), DLBCL cells or peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) were treated with 5-aza daily from day 0 to day 4 and with HDACi or dimethyl sulfoxide on days 2 and 4. To calculate synergy, cells were exposed to a dose curve of each drug alone or their combination in a constant ratio. Subsequently, we used CompuSyn (RRID:SCR_022931) to plot dose-effect curves.

Flow cytometry

Cell viability was determined by propidium iodide (PI) staining. Apoptosis was assessed by annexin V (catalog no. 640906; BioLegend) and PI double positivity. For CellTrace Violet (CTV) assays, tumor cells were resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (1 × 107 cells per mL) and labeled with 5mM CTV reagent (catalog no. C34557; Thermo Fisher Scientific) at 37°C for 10 minutes. Unbound dye was subsequently quenched through the addition of 5 volumes of ice-cold cell culture media. The cells corresponding to the main peak of far-red positivity were sorted and cultured in the presence of vehicle or epigenetic drugs. Human primary T cells and B cells from PBMC were identified using fluorescein isothiocyanate–conjugated anti-CD3 (catalog no. 300406, RRID:AB_314060; BioLegend) and VioBlue-conjugated anti-CD19 (catalog no. 130-120-031, RRID:AB_2784030; Miltenyi Biotec), respectively. Data was collected on a FACSCanto II, LSR II, or LSR Fortessa flow cytometer (BD Biosciences) and analyzed using FlowJo (RRID:SCR_008520).

Statistics

GraphPad Prism (RRID:SCR_002798) was used for statistical analyses. Statistical differences between the means of 2 data groups were determined by using 2-tailed unpaired Student t test, and P < .05 was considered significant. Multiple group comparisons were performed using analysis of variance, and P < .05 was considered significant.

The Animal Ethics Committees of the Peter MacCallum Cancer Centre and the Centre Méditerranéen de Médecine Moléculaire approved all in vivo procedures.

Additional methods are listed in the supplemental Information.

Results

Specific HDAC3 inhibition synergizes with 5-aza against DLBCL cells

To analyze the effect of inhibiting DNMT and HDAC3 on DLBCL cells, we used 5-aza and a specific HDAC3i (KDAc-001).19 We treated DLBCL cells MD901, HBL-1, and TMD8 (ABC-subtype) and OCI-Ly7, SU-DHL-4, and Farage (GCB-subtype) with 5-aza daily for 5 days (day 0-day 4), and at days 2 and 4, we added the HDAC3i (Figure 1A). In addition, we used low doses of 5-aza (50nM-200nM) to induce demethylation of DNA without causing cytotoxicity. We observed no induction of cell death at day 7 based on PI staining after single treatment with HDAC3i and a moderate increase in the percentage of PI+ cells after treatment with 200nM 5-aza alone (Figure 1B). Notably, when cells were treated with both drugs, there was an increased cytotoxic effect (Figure 1B; supplemental Figure 1A). We observed similar results when adding both drugs simultaneously (supplemental Figure 1B), whereas adding HDAC3i first appeared to impair the combined effect (supplemental Figure 1C). Regarding the mechanism of cell death, we found a significant increase in late apoptotic cells (annexin V+ PI+) in the 5-aza+HDAC3i condition compared with cells treated with vehicle, 5-aza, or HDAC3i (Figure 1C; supplemental Figure 1D). In line with these results, the level of cleaved caspase-3 was significantly higher in combination-treated cells than in cells treated with 5-aza or HDAC3i alone (Figure 1D; supplemental Figure 1E). In addition, we observed a decrease in the cell number after treatment with 5-aza alone, in line with the antiproliferative activity described for other HMA on DLBCL12,20 and with HDAC3i alone in most cell lines (Figure 1E; supplemental Figure 2A). Compared with treatment with each single agent, combining 5-aza and HDAC3i resulted in a greater reduction in the total cell number in all the cell lines (Figure 1E; supplemental Figure 2A), which correlated with alterations in the cell cycle that were reflected by a low proportion of cells in the S phase (Figure 1F; supplemental Figure 2B). We further investigated the antiproliferative effect of the combinatorial treatment using CTV, and we observed that the blockade in proliferation was greater in the 5-aza+HDAC3i condition than in cells only treated with 5-aza or HDAC3i (Figure 1G; supplemental Figure 2C-D). Treatment of DLBCL cells with 5-aza in combination with HDAC3i induces specific transcriptional signatures associated with terminal differentiation of GC B cells

We analyzed the transcriptional profile of the MD901 cells (ABC-DLBCL) by RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) at day 5 after treatment when there is no drug-induced cytotoxic effect (data not shown). Principal component analysis showed samples clustering according to drug treatment (supplemental Figure 3A). A supervised analysis identified 1417 differentially expressed genes (DEG) between 5-aza+HDAC3i and vehicle, 879 DEG between 5-aza and vehicle, and 181 DEG between HDAC3i and vehicle (log fold change |logFC|>1; P < .05; Figure 2A-B; supplemental Table 1). Gene set enrichment analysis using hallmark gene sets showed a significant positive enrichment of genes involved in the unfolded protein response (UPR) and the epithelial-mesenchymal transition in cells treated with 5-aza+HDAC3i (Figure 2C left; supplemental Figure 3B; supplemental Table 2); upregulation of protein secretion, transforming growth factor β and interleukin-6 signaling after 5-aza treatment (Figure 2C middle; supplemental Table 2) and immune and inflammatory pathways in cells treated with HDAC3i alone, in line with previous studies (Figure 2C right; supplemental Table 2).17 We found fewer number of negatively enriched gene sets (supplemental Figure 3C; supplemental Table 3). We also used a comprehensive database of gene sets relevant for GC B cells and DLBCL, derived from our previous work and others. This analysis revealed significant positive enrichment after treatment with 5-aza+HDAC3i for BCL6 target genes and pathways involved in PC differentiation, including the key transcription factor (TF) XBP1 (Figure 2D). This aligns with the significant UPR enrichment (Figure 2C) because the activation of the UPR is a critical process of PC function.21 Conversely, HDAC3i-treated cells showed significant positive enrichment for genes induced by CD40 signaling, including major histocompatibility complex genes, as previously shown17 (Figure 2D), whereas 5-aza induced a significant enrichment for the upregulation of BCL6 target genes and genes repressed in DLBCL cells resistant to chemotherapy (Figure 2D).8 Using reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, we confirmed the increased expression of the following PC genes after treatment with 5-aza+HDAC3i: XBP1, TNFRSF17, ATF4, FNDC3B, CD38, and EDEM1 (Figure 2E).22,23 Moreover, we used the CollecTRI meta-resource24 to infer changes in TF activity based on the transcriptome. We found that ATF4 had the highest activity in DLBCL cells treated with 5-aza+HDAC3i (Figure 2F; supplemental Table 4), consistent with UPR activation. To confirm the relevance of these results, we performed RNA-seq on the GCB-DLBCL cell line OCI-Ly7. This analysis again showed a clear separation between vehicle, HDAC3i, 5-aza, and 5-aza+HDAC3i conditions (supplemental Figure 4A). Similar to MD901 cells, genes upregulated in cells treated with 5-aza+HDAC3i featured enrichment for genes involved in the terminal differentiation of B cells, toward memory B cells in this case, because the cell line belongs to the GCB-DLBCL subtype (supplemental Figure 4B; supplemental Table 5). Importantly, we found higher enrichment of these pathways in the 5-aza+HDAC3i condition than in HDAC3i or 5-aza alone (supplemental Figure 4B). Other pathways associated with GC B-cell differentiation, including CD40, IFN-α, and IFN-γ signaling pathways, were also more enriched in the 5-aza+HDAC3i condition (supplemental Figure 4B-C). These results suggest that the synergistic effect of 5-aza combined with HDAC3i against DLBCL (Figure 1E) is linked to a strong induction of B-cell terminal differentiation.

The 5-aza+HDAC3i combination induces specific epigenetic changes in DLBCL

We next analyzed the epigenetic marks targeted by each drug: DNA methylation (5-aza) and histone acetylation (HDAC3i). The analysis of the DLBCL methylome showed a well-defined separation between conditions with or without 5-aza (supplemental Figure 3A). Further analysis of differentially methylated cytosines revealed massive hypomethylation in cells treated with 5-aza or 5-aza+HDAC3i compared with that in vehicle-treated cells (methylation difference >25%; false discovery rate [FDR] <0.05; Figure 3A; supplemental Figure 5A). As expected, treatment with HDAC3i induced few changes in the methylome compared with that with vehicle (Figure 3A; supplemental Figure 5A). We also analyzed H3K27ac. We confirmed by western blot that treatment with HDAC3i, alone or in combination with 5-aza, increased H3K27ac globally as expected (supplemental Figure 5C). We then performed chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing. We found a clear separation between the conditions with or without HDAC3i (supplemental Figure 5D). Subsequent analysis identified a high number of differential peaks in the conditions treated with HDAC3i or 5-aza+HDAC3i than with vehicle (|logFC| > 1; FDR <0.05; Figure 3C). Treatment with 5-aza induced changes in H3K27ac, mostly under the significance threshold (|logFC| > 1; FDR <0.05; Figure 3C). We found that 81% of differential peaks were shared when comparing HDAC3i alone or in combination (Figure 3D), indicative of the direct effect of HDAC3 inhibition. In contrast, only 43% of the peaks losing H3K27ac were shared between HDAC3i alone and 5-aza+HDAC3i, a result that could be explained by the indirect effects of HDAC3i (Figure 3D).

The annotation of H3K27ac peaks into genomic features showed a gradual increase in the percentage of H3K27ac peaks in promoters, which correlated with a decrease in the percentage of peaks in both introns and distal intergenic regions (Figure 3E-F). To investigate whether this effect could contribute to the distinct transcriptional profiles, we overlapped the H3K27ac and RNA-seq profiles. We focused on the key TFs identified on the conditions containing HDAC3i: ATF4 (5-aza+HDAC3i) and IRF1 (HDAC3i). We observed an increase in the level of H3K27ac at the promoter of ATF4 after 5-aza+HDAC3i treatment compared with HDAC3i alone, which correlated with increased transcription in the combination-treated cells (Figure 3G). Conversely, the promoter of IRF1 showed a higher level of H3K27ac after treatment with HDAC3i than with 5-aza+HDAC3i, and this correlated with a higher upregulation of IRF1 in HDAC3i-treated cells compared with that in combination-treated cells (Figure 3H). We expanded the H3K27ac analysis to all the genes that were positively enriched in each condition based on the gene set enrichment analysis (Figure 2D). We found that most (54%) of the genes that are uniquely transcriptionally activated after 5-aza+HDAC3i have at least 25% higher H3K27ac levels at promoters in 5-aza+HDAC3i than in HDAC3i alone, including ATF4, EDEM1, and CD38 (Figure 3I). When analyzing the genes positively enriched after HDAC3i treatment, the differences were smaller (ie, only 40% had higher promoter intensity in HDAC3i than in 5-aza+HDAC3i and 33% showed higher H3K27ac at promoters after treatment with 5-aza+HDAC3i compared to HDAC3i) (Figure 3J).

We also compared the DNA methylation profiles. Although treatment with 5-aza alone or in combination induced hypomethylation in both genes, we observed a stronger reduction at the ATF4 gene than at IRF1 compared with that in vehicle-treated cells (Figure 3G-H). This higher degree of hypomethylation after treatment with 5-aza+HDAC3i could contribute to the increased transcription of ATF4. Overall, our results suggest that 5-aza priming enhances the transcriptional differentiation program that is initiated by the inhibition of HDAC3 through the redistribution and increased intensity of H3K27ac and reduced levels of DNA methylation especially at BCL6 target genes.

Superior efficacy of 5-aza+HDAC3i against DLBCL compared with that of 5-aza+pan-HDACi

To simultaneously characterize the phenotypic and transcriptional effects of the different treatments, we used multiplexed analysis of cells sequencing,25,26 which is based on the Digital RNA with perturbation of genes sequencing method.27 This approach allowed us to perform, through 1 single assay, a direct comparison of the phenotypic effect and transcriptional changes induced by the single agents and 3 combinatorial therapies. In this miniaturized high-throughput setting, we confirmed that the treatment with 5-aza+HDAC3i had a stronger therapeutic effect against DLBCL, with a higher percentage of PI+ cells than in the conditions treated with 5-aza+panobinostat or 5-aza+vorinostat (supplemental Figure 7B). Using the same assay, we investigated the molecular mechanisms driving the stronger cytotoxic and cytostatic effects induced by the 5-aza+HDAC3i combination. Uniform manifold approximation and projection for dimension reduction analysis of normalized RNA counts showed that the conditions consisting of single treatment with panobinostat and vorinostat clustered closely together, whereas cells treated with HDAC3i alone resulted in a different transcriptomic profile, situated between single pan-HDACi and combo conditions (Figure 4C). Moreover, the combinations with 5-aza clustered separately in comparison to their single counterparts, with the 5-aza+HDAC3i combination showing a more different transcriptomic response compared with that shown by 5-aza+panobinostat or 5-aza+vorinostat (Figure 4C). Subsequently, we performed AUCell28 gene enrichment analysis using the PC signature identified in cells treated with 5-aza plus HDAC3i (Figure 2). This analysis showed that, compared with the other combinations or single treatments, the combination of 5-aza+HDAC3i resulted in a higher upregulation of genes linked to PC differentiation (Figure 4D). All these results demonstrate the therapeutic advantage of selective inhibition of HDAC3 over HDACi with broader specificities.

Translational potential of the 5-aza+HDAC3i combination as a new therapy for DLBCL

After confirming the antilymphoma activity of the new combinatorial epigenetic treatment consisting of 5-aza and HDAC3i, we aimed to investigate its safety in nontumor cells. We treated PBMC with 5-aza+HDAC3i, 5-aza, HDAC3i, or vehicle. Finally, we investigated the in vivo efficacy of the combination compared with that of treatment with each single drug. We generated xenografts by transplanting OCI-Ly7 (GCB-DLBCL) cells into immunodeficient NOD-scid IL2Rgammanull (NSG) mice, intraperitoneally to obtain systemic disease. Compared with vehicle treatment, treatment with 5-aza+HDAC3i induced a significant survival advantage (supplemental Figure 8A). Moreover, 80% mice treated with the combination survived for 150 days compared with only 50% mice surviving after treatment with HDAC3i alone and 0% in the case of mice receiving 5-aza (supplemental Figure 8A). In addition, we did not observe significant changes in body weight in the combination-treated mice throughout the duration of the treatment (supplemental Figure 8B). To further demonstrate the therapeutic benefit of combining 5-aza and HDAC3i for DLBCL, we established xenografts with the more aggressive cell line MD901 (ABC-DLBCL) and reduced the concentration of HDAC3i. We observed a significant reduction in the tumor volume of (Figure 5C) and a significant survival advantage (supplemental Figure 8C) in mice treated with 5-aza+HDAC3i compared with mice treated with 5-aza or HDAC3i alone. Importantly, treatment with both epigenetic drugs did not alter blood counts (Figure 5D), indicating that this combination has an in vivo safe profile.

Discussion

Although new therapies for patients with relapsed/refractory DLBCL, including chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy, antibody-drug conjugate therapy, and CD3-CD20 bispecific antibodies, have been developed in recent years, there is still a clinical need to improve the outcomes of these patients.29 A deeper understanding of the molecular events driving the disease will be critical to develop future DLBCL therapies. In this regard, considering that the pathogenesis of DLBCL is strongly linked to epigenetic abnormalities, different classes of epigenetic therapies have been developed to treat these lymphomas.10 However, they are not broadly implemented in the clinic due to their limited efficacy when used as single agents. Here, we show that treatment with 5-aza in combination with HDAC3i results in synergistic enhanced cytotoxicity and lower proliferative capacity in DLBCL cells, in vitro and in vivo (using DLBCL xenograft models). Although our findings are consistent with previous studies investigating the antitumor effect of combining HMA and HDACi in DLBCL,11,12 we demonstrate that 5-aza plus a specific HDAC3i exhibits a more potent antilymphoma activity than pan-HDACi without affecting the viability of healthy lymphocytes.

Mechanistically, we found that, compared with monotherapies, 5-aza plus HDAC3i induced a different transcriptomic profile, supporting the existence of a cooperative mechanism of action leading to the observed synergistic cytotoxic effect. The combinatorial therapy resulted in the activation of genes involved in the UPR and PC differentiation in ABC-DLBCL and memory B cells in GCB-DLBCL. The differentiation program of GC B cells into PC requires the production of immunoglobulin chains through the endoplasmic reticulum, which leads to activation of the UPR30; however, prolonged UPR results in the elimination of the stressed cells by apoptosis.31 Therefore, a possible explanation for our results is that the activation of the PC differentiation pathway mediated by the combinatorial therapy leads to terminal endoplasmic reticulum stress and, because the cells are unable to restore homeostasis, the maintained activation of the UPR ultimately induces apoptosis.30

Given that impaired terminal differentiation and the inability to exit the GC reaction are features of the transformation of GC B cells into DLBCL, therapies promoting cell differentiation are expected to disrupt the malignant properties of lymphoma cells, as is the case for myeloid malignancies.32 Interestingly, epigenetic-targeted drugs including HMA and HDACi are among the most effective therapies to relieve the differentiation blockade of leukemia cells.32 We previously demonstrated that treatment with the HDACi panobinostat induced differentiation of acute myeloid leukemia cells.33,34 In this study, the stronger differentiation signature observed when combining 5-aza and HDAC3i aligns with the findings of 2 recent studies showing a synergistic effect of targeting DOT1L and EZH2 against GCB-DLBCL cells, which is associated with differentiation.35,36 The combined inhibition of EZH2 and DOT1L resulted in the upregulation of BCL6 target genes associated with IFN-mediated responses, antigen presentation, and PC differentiation in GCB-DLBCL, regardless of the EZH2 mutation status. We observed similar transcriptional signatures after treatment with HDAC3i alone, with the addition of 5-aza promoting an enhanced differentiation transcriptomic profile, reflected by the enrichment of PC and memory B-cell signatures.

Our results align with recent preclinical and clinical studies describing promising results with new combinatorial therapies based on HMA. 5-Aza priming reprograms chemoresistant DLBCL cells to become sensitive to immunochemotherapy.8,9 In addition, decitabine improves the activity of the BCL2 inhibitor venetoclax, increasing mitochondrial apoptotic priming and BCL-2 dependency of DLBCL cells.37 These results suggest that HMA have the ability to potentiate the antilymphoma effects of the other therapeutic agent of the combination. In our study, we observed an increase in the number of H3K27ac peaks at promoters after 5-aza treatment, which together with the increase in acetylation mediated by HDAC3 inhibition resulted in a stronger transcriptional activation of differentiation genes. This effect could be due to the rearrangement of repressive histone marks induced by 5-aza. In this regard, it has been shown that high doses of 5-aza reorganizes the patterns of H3K27me3 or H3K9me3 in human embryonic kidney 293 cells.38 Further investigation of the epigenetic changes induced by the combinatorial therapy, in comparison with the genetic knockdown of HDAC3 and DNMT genes, as well as their effect on the tumor immune microenvironment will help to determine the translational potential of combining 5-aza and HDAC3i to treat DLBCL.

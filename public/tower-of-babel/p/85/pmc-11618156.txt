Diagnosing myocardial ischemia of obstructive coronary artery disease using dynamic computed tomography myocardial perfusion imaging: optimization of relative myocardial blood flow ratio
Authors: Weifang Kong, Bingzhu Long, Fang Li, Lan Shang, Xinyue Chen, Aamer Chughtai
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo compare the diagnostic efficacy of different relative myocardial blood flow (MBF) ratios in computed tomography perfusion (CTP) for myocardial ischemia in patients with obstructive coronary artery disease (CAD).MethodsBetween October 2020 and March 2024, patients with suspected or known obstructive CAD who underwent CTP + coronary computed tomography angiography and invasive coronary angiography/fractional flow reserve were retrospectively selected. Patients and vessels were categorized into ischemia and non-ischemia groups. The diagnostic efficacies of the three relative MBF ratios were compared in patients with obstructive CAD.ResultsThis study included 48 patients (144 vessels). Notably, 34 of the 48 patients (70.83%) and 49 of the 144 vessels (34.03%) were considered to have myocardial ischemia. The area under the curve of Ratio-hi (0.944, 95% confidence interval: 0.893–0.976) was higher than those of Ratio-av, Ratio-Q3, and MBF-lowest; However, no statistical differences were found (P>0.005). The cutoff value for detecting Ratio-hi was 0.667, and the sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value, negative predictive value, and diagnostic accuracy were 91.8%, 83.2%, 75%, 95.24%, and 86.81%, respectively.ConclusionRelative MBF ratios, especially Ratio-hi, demonstrated excellent performance and exhibited greater robustness in diagnosing myocardial ischemia in patients with obstructive CAD.

Introduction

Dynamic computed tomography myocardial perfusion imaging (CTP) allows the quantification of myocardial blood flow (MBF) during pharmacological hyperemia by analyzing the myocardial enhancement patterns after contrast injection [1]. Over the past decade, CTP with pharmacological stress has shown promise as a non-invasive diagnostic tool for obstructive coronary artery disease (CAD) based on various international clinical trials [2–4]. However, despite its potential, the CTP is not widely used in clinical settings. This is mainly due to the substantial variation in MBF cutoff values, which indicate hemodynamic significance, due to differences in computed tomography (CT) scanners, calculation algorithms, and individual factors [4]. Therefore, to address this issue, studies have used relative MBF ratios to diagnose myocardial infarction (MI) and mitigate the impact of these variables [5–15]. However, various methods have been reported for calculating the relative MBF ratio, including the use of the highest segmental MBF value without artifacts [5, 6], highest automatic segmental value of the endocardium [7], third quartile of the average segmental value [8], average MBF value of the left ventricle (LV) [9, 10], and average MBF value of all myocardial segments supplied by vessels with < 30% stenosis [7]. These MBF values have been used as reference values to calculate the relative MBF ratio. However, few studies have compared the diagnostic efficacy of the different methods that involve relative MBF ratio [7].

Therefore, to enhance the efficacy and clinical utility of quantitative MBF parameters in diagnosing myocardial ischemia, we compared the diagnostic performance of absolute MBF-lowest and three relative MBF ratios in a cohort of patients with suspected or known obstructive CAD. This could help the clinical applicability of parameters in CTP and contribute to a practical diagnostic standard for radiologists and clinicians, potentially impacting the clinical diagnoses and management of CAD.

Materials and methods

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Sichuan Provincial People’s Hospital (approval number: 2022 − 357). In this retrospective study, it included adults suspected or diagnosed with obstructive CAD between October 2020 and March 2024. The inclusion criterion was having undergone CTP + coronary computed tomography angiography (CCTA) in addition to invasive coronary angiography (ICA) or fractional flow reserve (FFR) tests within 60 days (n = 66). The exclusion criteria were: (1) History of MI (n = 5) and acute coronary syndrome within the 14-days interval from either procedure, (2) Interventional coronary revascularization, stent placement (n = 6), or coronary artery bypass surgery, (3) Presence of alternative cardiac conditions, such as hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (n = 3), atrial fibrillation, congestive heart failure, hypertensive cardiomyopathy, (4) Severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and (5) Insufficient quality of CTP and CCTA images to meet diagnostic and post-processing requirements (n = 5). Finally, 14 patients were excluded, and 48 patients with 144 coronary arteries were included in the final analysis (Fig. 1).

Basic patient information included age, sex, body mass index (BMI), heart rate, and CAD risk factors recorded using a picture archiving and communication system (Table 1). The dose-length product (DLP) of CTP was recorded, and the effective radiation dose (mSv) was calculated as DLP × k (k = 0.026 mSv). mgy− 1.cm− 1 [16].

Dynamic CTMyocardial perfusion imaging and CCTA protocol

A third generation dual-source CT scanner (Somatom Force; Siemens Medical Solutions, Forchheim, Germany) was used for all CT examinations. The patients initially underwent a calcium score scan, with the range of the dynamic CTP scan determined based on the calcium score images to encompass the entire left ventricle (LV). Then, CCTA scans were conducted after resting for 5–10 min.

CTP scan

Adenosine triphosphate disodium (ATP) was intravenously infused at 0.14 mg/kg/min to induce coronary artery relaxation. Following a 3 min injection, 40 ml of iodinated contrast agent (Iodine Myron, 400 mg/ml, Bracco, Italy) was administered, followed by 40 ml of normal saline at the same rate. The scan trigger delay was set to 4 s, and scanning was performed at the end of the systolic phase using the cradle-bed shuttle mode. The total scan duration was 32 s, with ATP infusion ceasing immediately after the scan. CARE kV and CARE Dose 4D were used to automatically adjust the voltage and current to minimize radiation exposure. The reconstructed layer thickness was 3 mm, with a layer spacing of 2.9 mm.

CCTA scan

Scans were acquired prospectively with electrocardiogram triggering by injecting 40–60 ml of iodinated contrast agent at a rate of 4–6 ml/s, followed by normal saline. The trigger scanning technology was employed with the trigger level set at the ascending aorta, a threshold of 100 HU, and an automatic 5 s delay before scanning. Automatic tube voltage (Care Kv, Care Dose 4D) was set at a reference tube voltage of 100 kV, reference tube current of 320 mAs, rotation time of 0.25 s, reconstructed layer thickness of 0.75 mm, and layer spacing of 0.5 mm.

Post-processing and image analysis

The CTP images were reconstructed using a dedicated kernel (Qr36) to reduce beam-hardening artifacts and analyzed using a CTP software package (myocardial perfusion analysis, Siemens Healthineers, Forchheim, Germany). Motion correction was manually applied to correct LV misregistration, and a hybrid deconvolution model was used to quantify the MBF. For quantitative analysis, the resulting perfusion data automatically generated the average MBF value of the LV, which was recorded as MBF-av. Subsequently, a region of interest (ROI) with a minimum size of 0.5 cm was manually delineated on the short-axis view of the LV in the lowest and highest perfusion regions within 1–16 myocardial segments (2 mm away from the endocardium and epicardium). The lowest and highest MBF values were automatically calculated and recorded as the MBF-lowest and MBF-hi, respectively. The resulting perfusion data were also sent to the Cardiac Function Analysis software (version 2.0.5, Siemens Healthcare, Germany) to automatically generate the average MBF values of the 1–16 myocardial segments in the same patient, with the third quartile MBF value of the segmental analysis recorded as MBF-Q3.

Relative MBF ratios were calculated using the following formula: Relative MBF ratio = MBF-lowest/reference MBF value. Therefore, three reference MBF values (MBF-hi, MBF-Q3, and MBF-av) were used to calculate the relative MBF ratios and were recorded as Ratio-hi, Ratio-Q3, and Ratio-av: Ratio-hi = MBF-lowest/MBF-hi; Ratio-Q3 = MBF-lowest/ MBF-Q3; Ratio-av = MBF-lowest/MBF-av. The perfusion territories of three major coronary arteries (left anterior descending artery-LAD, left circumflex artery-LCX, and right coronary artery-RCA) were evaluated for each patient.

Further analysis of the CCTA images was conducted using a Coronary Artery Analysis workstation (Syngo.via, Siemens Healthineers, Germany). The degree of stenosis (DS) in LAD, LCX, and RCA were calculated as (reference diameter - minimal lumen diameter)/reference diameter. Furthermore, the most severe type of stenosis was considered if multiple coronary artery stenoses were present within the same perfusion territory. Anatomic obstructive CAD was defined as ≥ 50% coronary stenosis. The ratio of the coronary artery volume to the myocardial mass (V/M) of the LV in patients were measured manually based on the CCTA images [17].

Two cardiac radiologists (with 5 and 7 years of experience, respectively) anonymously assessed the CTP and CCTA results independently. In cases of disagreement, a third senior cardiac radiologist (with over 15 years of experience) was consulted for the final judgment.

ICA and FFR

Interventional cardiologists conducted ICA within 60 days of CTP and CCTA scans, analyzing ICA images unknown to the CCTA and CTP results. The FFR examinations were performed if the ICA results were 40–90%. The criteria for diagnosing functional myocardial ischemia are ICA ≥ 90% or FFR ≤ 0.8, whereas non-myocardial ischemia is indicated by FFR > 0.8 or ICA < 40%.

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics (version 26.0; IBM; Armonk, NY, USA) and Med Calc 15.2.0 software by Amazon. Categorical variables are presented as counts (%), and continuous variables as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median. The normality of data was assessed using variance or t-tests for normally distributed data and the Mann–Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data. Group comparisons were performed using the non-parametric Kruskal–Wallis test or analysis of variance. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted, the area under the curve (AUC) was compared using the DeLong test, and the optimal diagnostic cutoff value was determined using the Jorden test. The diagnostic performance at the optimal cutoff value was calculated. Statistical significance was set as a two-sided P-value of < 0.05.

Results

This study included 48 patients suspected of known obstructive CAD, with an average age of 65.48 ± 8.62 years, and 35 (75.92%) were male. The ICA/FFR results indicated that myocardial ischemia was present in 34 of 48 patients (70.83%) and 49 of 144 coronary arteries (34.03%). Among the coronary arteries, 54, 62, and 28 had stenosis with degrees of 0–39%, 40–89%, and ≥ 90 on the ICA images, respectively. Within the 40–89% stenosis interval, 21 of 62 (33.87%) coronary arteries caused ischemia, whereas 41 of 62 (66.13%) did not. Patients with myocardial ischemia had a higher prevalence of multi-vessel stenosis than those without myocardial ischemia (P = 0.037). Specifically, 73.5% of patients with myocardial ischemia had three-vessel disease, whereas only 2.9% of patients without ischemia had multi-vessel disease. The calcification score was higher in the ischemia group, although the difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05). The two groups showed no significant differences in age, sex, or other CAD-related risk factors (Table 1). The radiation dose from the CTP scan was 293.83 ± 89.02 mGy*cm, equivalent to 7.64 ± 2.31 mSv.

Discussion

This study’s main findings indicate that the three methods of relative MBF ratios showed excellent diagnostic efficacy in detecting MI in patients with anatomical obstructive CAD. Ratio-hi and Ratio-Q3 demonstrated better diagnostic sensitivity, whereas Ratio-av and MBF-lowest showed higher specificity. The optimal cutoff values for Ratio-hi, Ratio-Q3, Ratio-av, and MBF-lowest for diagnosing MI were 0.67, 0.77, 0.78, and 93.64 ml/100 ml/min, respectively.

Characteristics of the MI group included a higher prevalence of multi-vessel stenosis, increased calcification score (355.21 ± 637.49), and more severe coronary artery stenosis. The calcification score in the ischemia group closely approached the 400 threshold. Therefore, patients with suspected CAD and a calcification score > 400 should undergo a CTP examination, as recommended by experts [2]. Conversely, in the non-myocardial ischemia group, approximately 25% of the vessels exhibited moderate stenosis, highlighting that the severity of stenosis in these vessels was not directly correlated with the presence of MI. Therefore, further functional assessments should be promptly conducted for vessels with moderate stenosis.

Studies in patients with CAD suggest that the ratio of coronary artery volume to the LV mass (V/M) measured using CCTA may indicate myocardial oxygen supply relative to demand [17]. This measure has shown associations with FFR and non-invasive CT-FFR measures and with MBF measured using single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) [18–20]. In our study, we found that the V/M was lower in the myocardial ischemia group than in the non-myocardial ischemia group. However, this difference was not statistically significant, which may be due to the small sample size.

Furthermore, we observed that the highest and average MBF values of the LV were significantly lower in the myocardial ischemia group than in the non-myocardial ischemia group (P < 0.001, P = 0.004), indicating that coronary artery stenosis affects MBF in the territory supplied by the stenosed artery and the average MBF and the highest MBF in the LV as a whole. Per-vessel analysis revealed that the MBF and relative MBF ratio values in the myocardial ischemia group were significantly lower than those in the non-myocardial ischemia group (P < 0.001), suggesting that the MBF parameters can effectively reflect abnormal perfusion.

This study demonstrated that the optimal diagnostic cutoff value of absolute MBF for diagnosing myocardial ischemia was 93.64 ml/100 ml/min. However, the cutoffs reported in different studies vary significantly, ranging from 75 to 164 ml/100 ml/min [4]. It is most likely attributable to differences in blood flow in different individuals due to physiologic variations, making the identification and the clinical application of absolute MBF cutoff value difficult. These results indicate that the studies of relative MBF ratios for the diagnosis of ischemia are necessary and important, and has important significance for the promotion of clinical use of CTP. Furthermore, in our result, the identification of ischemia (93.64 ml/100 ml/min) corresponds to a reduction of blood flow approximately 2 standard deviation (SD) below the maximum blood flow (168.87 ± 27.23 ml/100 ml/min) in the respective patient. This is similar in concept to the use of polar map quantitation with a cutoff of 2 SD in identification of ischemia. These account for our finding of the superiority of relative ratios over absolute MBF.

Several studies have explored using the relative MBF ratio methods to address this issue. Wichmann et al. [10] found that the AUC for diagnosing myocardial ischemia using Ratio-hi was superior to that using MBF-lowest (0.925 vs. 0.882, P = 0.0022). Conversely, Yi et al. reported that the AUC for the MBF-lowest was better than Ratio-hi [7]. Rossi et al. [11], Nous et al. [13], and Kong et al. [15] also utilized Ratio-Q3, Ratio-av, and Ratio-av, Ratio-hi respectively, to diagnose myocardial ischemia; the AUCs were higher than the MBF-lowest. The three studies showed no significant differences, with AUC values of 0.90 vs. 0.87, 0.82 vs. 0.79, and 0.962 vs. 0.936, respectively (P > 0.05). The results of our study showed similarities among the AUCs of the four methods used to diagnose myocardial ischemia, ranging from 0.924 to 0.944. The AUCs of relative MBF ratios were higher than that of the absolute MBF, although the difference were not statistically significant. Ratio-hi and Ratio-Q3 demonstrated good sensitivity and moderate specificity, whereas the MBF-lowest and Ratio-av exhibited high specificity and moderate sensitivity.

Yi et al.’s study [9] and our study found that the AUC of Ratio-hi was higher than those of Ratio-Q3 and Ratio-av, although this was not statistically significant. Using the average MBF and third quartile values as reference values underestimated myocardial ischemia, especially in cases of multiple-vessel stenosis, leading to relative proportion amplification and reduced sensitivity and specificity. Our study also indicated that the highest MBF value in the myocardial ischemia group remained lower than that in the non-myocardial ischemia group, suggesting that using the highest MBF value as a reference may underestimate the degree of ischemia. However, the highest MBF value was closer to the maximum initial normal state of coronary arteries, making it relatively simple and widely applicable. The third quartile also showed a high diagnostic performance as a reference value; however, its clinical implementation may be slightly more complex.

The optimal cutoff value for the relative MBF ratio has been previously reported to be 0.675–0.85. In this study, we found that the optimal critical values for Ratio-hi, Ratio-Q3, and Ratio-av were 0.67, 0.77, and 0.78, respectively. These cutoff values align closely with the pressure drop values used in FFR for diagnosing myocardial ischemia (0.7–0.8). Variations in cutoff values may arise because of differences in the reference MBF values used. For instance, studies like that of Yan Yi et al. [9] and our study utilized MBF-hi as the reference value, leading to lower diagnostic cutoff values when the denominator was larger than when MBF-av or MBF-Q3 was used as the reference. Currently, there is no standardized measurement area or method for optimizing the quantitative parameters of MBF. The Chinese expert consensus recommends the axial region of interest measurement method in clinical practice [21]. However, other studies have explored alternative relative MBF parameters, such as the myocardial transmural ratio [22] and MBF reserve [21, 23]. MBF reserve, the ratio of stress and rest MBF values in each patient and territory, would be a meaning method to serve as a comparison for themselves. Therefore, a unified and fully automated method for delineating, extracting, and calculating the MBF is essential for advancing the clinical application of CTP in the future.

This study’s limitations include its retrospective nature, single-center design, and small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the results to third-generation dual-source CT examination plans. Furthermore, the manual MBF delineation method used in this study could introduce potential inter-observer bias, highlighting the need for further validation in prospective, multi-center, and large-sample studies.

Conclusion

Overall, among the stress dynamic CTP perfusion parameters, the three relative MBF ratios showed equal or superior performance in diagnosing myocardial ischemia in patients with obstructive CAD, each with varying sensitivities and specificities that can complement clinical practice. The diagnostic cutoff value range relative to the MBF ratio remained relatively stable, enhancing the robustness and reliability of this parameter in actual clinical settings. Notably, the Ratio-hi algorithm demonstrates superior performance and ease of calculation.

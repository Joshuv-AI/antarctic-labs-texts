Long-term outcomes in women with placenta accreta spectrum treated by one-step conservative surgery –prospective cohort study
Authors: Magdalena Kolak, Albaro Jose Nieto-Calvache, Aleksander Galas, Julia Jurga, Hubert Huras, Jose Miguel Palacios-Jaraquemada, Andrzej Jaworowski
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPlacenta accreta spectrum (PAS) is a serious obstetric condition characterized by the abnormal attachment of the placenta to the myometrium, leading to complications such as severe haemorrhage and increased maternal morbidity. Traditional treatment often involves caesarean hysterectomy, resulting in permanent loss of fertility. This study evaluates the one-year outcomes of women diagnosed with PAS who underwent one-step conservative surgery (OSCS), a uterus-preserving technique.MethodsA prospective cohort study was conducted involving women treated with OSCS between March 1, 2022, and February 1, 2024. Participants had a follow-up visit one year post-surgery, during which medical interviews, gynaecological examinations, and transvaginal ultrasounds were performed to assess postoperative outcomes. Additionally, participants completed the Short-Form Health Survey (SF-36) questionnaire. Inclusion criteria encompassed a confirmed diagnosis of PAS and treatment with OSCS, while exclusion criteria involved uterine dehiscence and initial caesarean hysterectomy.Results19 cases were included in the study. A subsequent hysterectomy was needed in one case. In long-term follow-up, the uterine niche was identified on ultrasound in 9 cases (50% of 18). The mean residual myometrial thickness was 4.73 mm (SD = 1.75; range: 2.3–7.0 mm). Spotting was reported in 6 cases (33.3% of 18), and abdominal pain in 3 cases (16%). The physical functioning score on the SF-36 scale was 92.2 points.ConclusionCarefully selected patients with PAS, managed in experienced centres, can be successfully managed with one-step conservative surgery. No serious complications were found in the one-year assessment. Mid-term morbidity is acceptable in OSCS treatment regarding the mentioned condition.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-08847-2.

Introduction

Placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) represents a severe obstetric disorder, defined by abnormal migration of the placenta into the myometrium, which leads to incomplete or absent placental separation following delivery [1]. The incidence of PAS has increased substantially in recent decades, rising from approximately 1 in 2500 pregnancies in the 1970s to nearly 1 in 300 in recent reports, a trend largely attributed to the global increase in caesarean deliveries [1, 2]. PAS is linked to massive obstetric haemorrhage, greater transfusion requirements, and an elevated risk of maternal morbidity and mortality [1].

Peripartum caesarean hysterectomy remains the most widely used treatment and is regarded as the gold standard in the majority of clinical settings [3]. However, this radical intervention results in permanent loss of fertility, as well as being associated with significant intraoperative and postoperative risks, such as bladder and ureteric injury, infection, and long-term psychological distress. Consequently, growing expertise in specialised referral centres has facilitated the development of uterus-preserving surgical strategies, which may serve as feasible and effective alternatives for appropriately selected patients [3, 4].

Among these techniques, focal resection involves excising the attached area, myometrium, and abnormally implanted placental tissue, followed by immediate uterine reconstruction, and has demonstrated favourable intraoperative outcomes when performed by experienced multidisciplinary teams [3, 4]. One-step conservative surgery (OSCS) is a specific type of focal resection that is conducted entirely within a single operative stage [4]. Although the overall evidence remains limited, these conservative procedures have been associated with reduced blood loss, shorter operative times, and lower rates of major maternal morbidity compared to caesarean hysterectomy [3–5]. Recent international and national guidelines recognize uterus-preserving surgery, including focal resection, as a valid management option in centres of excellence for selected women [1, 6–11]. However, long-term data on physical outcomes following these procedures remain limited.

This study aimed to evaluate the long-term outcomes of women with placenta accreta spectrum treated with one-step conservative surgery, with particular emphasis on ultrasound assessment of the uterine scar and the presence of myometrial defects.

Methods

Study group

This was a prospective cohort study. Women who had undergone one-step conservative surgery between March 1, 2022, and February 1, 2024, were followed one year after surgery. Medical interviews, gynaecological examinations, and transvaginal ultrasounds were conducted. Data regarding medical history and surgical procedures were obtained from electronic medical records.

Inclusion criteria were a diagnosis of PAS by macroscopic evaluation of the surgical specimen, which showed that the resected abnormal myometrium did not detach from the underlying placenta [12], and treatment with one-step conservative surgery. Moreover, histologic confirmation was obtained in all cases (PAS FIGO grade 2 and 3) [13]. Exclusion criteria included uterine dehiscence and caesarean hysterectomy as the initial treatment.

Uterine scar dehiscence

Uterine scar dehiscence represents an incomplete disruption of the uterine wall at the site of prior cesarean delivery. It typically occurs within the anterior lower uterine segment overlying the bladder. However, it may also extend laterally to impinge on the parametrium. The uterine wall may become so thin that the placenta can be seen through it a cesarean section, creating a so-called “uterine window”. In gross analysis, it corresponds to the situation in which the placenta detaches from the myometrium upon firm traction [14, 15].

One step-conservative surgery (OSCS)

Patients were selected for OSCS based on the previously described topographic classification [18]. Conservative surgery was performed if, after surgical staging, an abnormally accreted placenta was located on the anterior uterine wall with at least 2 cm of healthy myometrium from the cervix, the lesion width was less than 50%, and bladder dissection was feasible. Patients with uterine dehiscence were excluded.

Until 2022, caesarean hysterectomy was the only surgical treatment of PAS provided in our centre. However, after collaborating with the International Society of Placenta Accreta Spectrum (IS-PAS), the understanding of conservative management was improved significantly. Our teaching was based on acquainting with scientific journal articles and learning from the experiences of other IS-PAS members. Initially, the simplest cases were operated using the OSCS method. The next step was reaching out to more experienced surgeons via telemedicine to discuss specific cases and assess the potential feasibility of conservative surgery. As of now, all women who meet the criteria are operated on using the OSCS method.

Blood loss

Quantification of blood loss commenced at the time of amniotic membrane rupture. Initially, all the amniotic fluid was aspirated into a suction canister, and its volume was recorded. Following placental delivery, the volume of blood in the suction canister was measured. After surgery, all blood-soaked materials and clots were weighed and converted to millilitres. The total blood loss was calculated by summing the aspirated blood and the converted weight of soaked materials, with the final value recorded in millilitres (ml) [19].

SF-36 questionnaire

The SF-36 questionnaire is a validated tool for assessing quality of life and is used for subjective assessment of health status. The Polish version of the questionnaire is available (Supplements 1 and 2). SF-36 contains 11 questions – 9 of them have answers on a Likert scale, 2 have yes/no answers. Participants completed the SF-36 questionnaire to evaluate their quality of life across eight prespecified domains. Four domains assess physical health (physical functioning, role limitations due to physical health, bodily pain, and general health), while the remaining four assess mental health (vitality, social functioning, role limitations due to emotional problems, and mental health). Raw scores were converted using a standardized scoring algorithm into a scale ranging from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating better quality of life in each domain [20, 21].

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics were reported as means and standard deviations (SDs) for quantitative variables, and as counts and proportions for qualitative characteristics.

The study received local bioethical approval (approval number 1072.6120.68.2024). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants. All data were anonymized.

Results

The study was conducted at the Department of Obstetrics and Perinatology at the Jagiellonian University Hospital in Krakow, Poland. We included patients who underwent surgery between March 1, 2022, and February 1, 2024, and who had follow-up visits between September 1, 2023, and March 31, 2025. During the two-year inclusion period, 52 patients with a prenatal suspicion of PAS underwent surgery; 34 cases were qualified for OSCS, and 18 cases were qualified for caesarean hysterectomy as first-line treatment.

In patients who were qualified for OSCS, the diagnosis of PAS was confirmed by gross examination of the surgical specimen and subsequent histological examination. Ultimately, in 28 cases the diagnosis was confirmed, as 6 patients were excluded (due to uterine dehiscence – 4 cases and simple placenta previa – 2 cases). Of 28 cases meeting the inclusion criteria, 2 participants declined to participate in the follow-up, 5 did not attend scheduled follow-up visits, and 2 could not be contacted after delivery. In total, 9 patients did not undergo long-term follow-up, leaving 19 in the final analysis (Fig. 1).

The mean operative time was 71.3 min, and the average blood loss was 1158 ml (range: 300–2500 ml). Red blood cell transfusion was required in most cases (52.7%).

In one case, laparotomy was necessary following caesarean Sect. (5 weeks after OSCS), due to a uterine abscess, resulting in a secondary hysterectomy. Another complication observed was ileus in one case; however, no bladder or ureteric injuries occurred in the study group (Table 1).

One-year follow-up

Mean (SD) time of follow-up (from operation to gynaecological control and to US assessment) was 529.4 days (146.4).

At the time of the follow-up visit, 5 (26%) patients were still breastfeeding, and 6 (32%) were using contraception (1 only gestagen, because of breastfeeding, 4 oral contraceptives, 1 intrauterine device).

The symptoms patients complained about most during the control period were spotting (6, 32%) or pain (3, 16%). On gynaecological examination, cervical retraction with restricted uterine mobility was revealed in 4 patients (21%).

As the study group consisted of 19 patients, 18 were assessed using US uterus examination, and one patient (after hysterectomy) was assessed using US of the minor pelvis. A uterine niche was identified on ultrasound in 9 cases (50% of 18). The mean residual myometrial thickness was 4.73 mm (SD = 1.75; range: 2.3–7.0 mm). The mean myometrial thickness of the lower uterine segment was 4.92 mm (SD = 3.53; range 2.2–14.0).

In the SF-36 scale, four domains assess physical health: physical functioning, role limitations due to physical health, bodily pain, and general health. Across all physical health domains, the mean scores were above 75, with physical functioning at 92.2 points. Vitality (from mental health assessment) had the lowest score, 55.6 points (Table 2).

In a detailed medical interview, 7 out of 18 patients were interested in anticonception methods (but admitted to a lack of knowledge on which specifically they can use after OSCS). 6 out of 18 used contraceptive pills or IUD, and 1 patient was considering a subsequent pregnancy. None of the patients with niche had been offered medical treatment in a routine outpatient clinic.

Discussion

This study demonstrated that conservative surgery is a safe and effective treatment option for PAS when patients meet appropriate selection criteria. In this cohort, 28 cases were managed with conservative surgery, with only one patient requiring laparotomy and secondary hysterectomy due to a uterine abscess. Another serious complication, ileus, occurred in one case; however, no bladder or ureteric injuries were observed. Previous studies comparing caesarean hysterectomy and conservative surgery in short-term follow-up have shown that conservative surgery is associated with lower blood loss, fewer red blood cell transfusions, and a reduced incidence of complications [22–24].

The present study also included a long-term, one-year follow-up, which indicated normal functioning in the majority of cases. In the SF-36 questionnaire, the mean score for physical functioning was 92.2 out of 100. Interpretation of the SF-36 scale is that a higher score is better, indicating that a score near 100 is a very high result. Interpreting results involves comparing the results of a given individual or group against their appropriate reference group (by age and gender). To achieve this, the results are compared with normed values for the general Polish population. It is known that all scores greater than 50 are above the norm, while scores lower than 50 are below the norm. In our analysis, vitality had the lowest score of 55.6 points, but it is still above the Polish norm for women’s vitality. In physical components, general health gets the lowest score − 76.8 points. Reasonably, it is above the Polish women’s norm (> 50 points). In a detailed gynaecological interview, 33% of patients reported bleeding disorders. However, in the SF-36 objective questionnaire, we learn that it is not negatively influencing quality of life. Another positive aspect worth underlining is the fact that only a few patients complained about niche symptoms, even as the majority were not using hormonal contraceptives.

The principal value of this study is a careful US evaluation conducted 1 year after the operation. The niche in US evaluation was present in 50% of cases. However, it might be interpreted as a high incidence, as this rate is similar to that reported after standard cesarean section (the last systematic review revealed a niche prevalence of 42%-84% after cesarean Sect [25]). Conservative management appears to be associated with lower morbidity in carefully selected cases [26]. There is still a lack of high-quality follow-up evidence; a prospective cohort study is needed. One promising trial is ongoing [15].

A direct comparison between hysterectomy and conservative surgery for placenta accreta spectrum is inherently biased, as OSCS is reserved for selected patients. Moreover, conservative management comprises heterogeneous techniques, and most available data are retrospective. To date, only one feasibility randomized controlled trial has evaluated short-term outcomes in PAS [27]. Our study reports one-year physical outcomes; however, further prospective studies with longer follow-up, including mental health outcomes, are needed.

Follow-up interviews indicated that, despite patients’ expectations, medical treatment (contraception, niche symptoms) was not offered during routine gynaecological control. It highlights the need for clearer outpatient follow-up pathways.

Given the increasing incidence of pas, management in centres of excellence, with continued improvement and dissemination of conservative surgical techniques, is necessary [28]. Additionally, analyses have shown that conservative treatment is a cost-saving strategy [29]. A recent meta-analysis of conservative surgery showed that OSCS has favourable short-term outcomes [30], which is consistent with the results of this study. Pregnancy is possible following conservative treatment, although no subsequent pregnancies were observed in this cohort [31, 32].

Strengths and limitations

The strengths of our study include a complete one-year follow-up of a relatively large number of patients with this non-common pathology. The prospective study design is another added value. Using the standardised questionnaire, detailed gynaecological interview, and examination is also an undeniable advantage.

The study also has some limitations, the most important being its single-center design. Moreover, the loss to follow-up of 9 patients (32%) might be a source of selection bias. As in the PAS condition, patients are referred to the centre of excellence across the country, making it almost impossible to achieve a higher follow-up rate [33, 34]. The following limitation is a one-year follow-up, which is quite a modest time of observation; however, according to the authors’ knowledge, it is one of the longest in the available literature.

Conclusion

OSCS was possible in more than half of the PAS cases. Carefully selected patients with PAS, managed in experienced centres, can be successfully treated with OSCS. No serious complications were found in the one-year assessment, and the mid-term morbidity is acceptable in OSCS treatment regarding the mentioned condition. However, further close follow-up is needed, as well as studies with a larger sample size.

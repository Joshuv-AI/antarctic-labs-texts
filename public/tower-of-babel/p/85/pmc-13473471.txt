Spatiotemporal dynamics and nonlinear drivers of ecosystem service synergies and trade-offs in Shaanxi, China
Authors: Menghan Gao, Khai Ern Lee, Aida Soraya Shamsuddin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSpatiotemporal interactions among ecosystem services (ES) and their drivers are essential for sustainable landscape management. This study employs the Dynamic Ecosystem Trade-off Synergy Index (DETSI), local indicators of spatial association (LISA) and interpretable machine learning (XGBoost-SHAP) to assess ES synergies and trade-offs, examine nonlinear driver-response patterns, and identify threshold effects in Shaanxi Province, China, from 2000 to 2022. Six ES indicators, net primary productivity (NPP), habitat quality (HQ), water yield (WY), soil retention (SR), cultural landscape (CL) and food supply (FS), were analysed to characterise spatial patterns and temporal dynamics. Results show persistent south-north gradients and stable high-value clusters in ecological core zones. Regulating service pairs exhibit strong synergies, whereas cultural-provisioning and provisioning-regulating pairs show persistent trade-offs. From 15 ES pairs, five representative pairs (CL-WY, FS-WY, NPP-SR, NPP-WY and SR-WY) were examined using SHAP, achieving accuracy of 0.8112–0.9696 and macro-F1 of 0.8110–0.9603. Eighteen key drivers, including NDVI, precipitation, elevation, road density and points of interest, show nonlinear and threshold-like responses. Moderate conditions support synergies, while extremes drive decoupling. The Qinling-Daba corridor emerges as a stable synergy zone, with northern and southeastern Shaanxi identified as recovery and rupture areas. Introduction

Ecosystem services (ES) refer to the natural conditions and processes through which ecosystems sustain and fulfil human life by providing direct and indirect benefits, such as food, clean water, climate regulation and cultural values1–3. These services underpin the biophysical foundation of human survival, and are essential for achieving sustainable development, as recognised by global frameworks, such as the Millennium Ecosystem Assessment4 and the Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services5. The concept of Nature’s Contributions to People (NCP) further emphasises the cultural, relational and non-material dimensions of ecosystem support for human societies6. Despite growing recognition, the capacity of ecosystems to provide stable and equitable services has been increasingly undermined by rapid urbanisation, industrial expansion and climate change5,7,8. Over the past five decades, more than 60% of global ES examined have been degraded or used unsustainably4, threatening ecological security, livelihood resilience and the effectiveness of governance interventions. These challenges are particularly evident in ecologically fragile and socioeconomically dynamic regions, such as China’s Loess Plateau and the Yellow River Basin, where land-use transformations, policy interventions and hydrological alterations co-evolve in complex ways9–11.

ES interact across heterogeneous landscapes, forming complex patterns of trade-offs and synergies12. These patterns are typically shaped by the combined influence of ecological, social and institutional factors operating across multiple scales, with different services responding to the same drivers in divergent directions and magnitudes13,14. Enhancing one service through management or policy intervention may unintentionally compromise others15. Therefore, identifying trade-off and synergy relationships among ES and their underlying drivers is essential for promoting more balanced and adaptive ecosystem management. To date, various methods, including Spearman and Pearson correlation analyses, geographically weighted regression (GWR) and local indicators of spatial association (LISA), have been widely used to detect the spatial distribution and co-variation of ES16–19. However, these techniques are often applied to static snapshots and may rely on linear assumptions, limiting their ability to capture temporal trajectories, nonlinear responses and multidimensional interactions. Spatial snapshots reveal the state of ES interactions at a specific time point, but provide limited insight into whether such relationships improve, deteriorate or shift across periods. By contrast, inter-temporal comparison can help reveal whether paired services change in similar or divergent directions across discrete periods20. Building on this logic, previous studies have used time-comparison-based criteria and indices to distinguish trade-offs from synergies and to describe their relative intensity21–23. In this study, a Dynamic Ecosystem Trade-off Synergy Index (DETSI) is constructed to integrate the direction and relative intensity of ES changes between selected time points. Rather than representing a previously standardised stand-alone method, DETSI is used here as a composite indicator for characterising interval-based ES coupling patterns under long-term change. This provides a useful lens for examining how ES relationships shift across periods characterised by land-use transition, policy adjustment and ecological disturbance.

Nonlinear machine learning approaches, such as Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machines (SVM) and particularly Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), have gained increasing attention in ecological modelling due to their ability to capture complex, non-additive relationships with high predictive accuracy24,25. Among these, the XGBoost-SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) framework offers the dual advantages of robust prediction and interpretability, enabling researchers to uncover variable importance, directional effects and response thresholds26. Through native support for TreeSHAP, the framework precisely decomposes model outputs into additive contributions from each input variable, enabling not only accurate ranking of feature importance but also the identification of response directions and sensitivity thresholds27. Recent applications of XGBoost-SHAP in ES research have demonstrated its growing utility. These include investigations into trade-offs and synergies among land-use functions28, assessments of green–blue landscape patterns on urban carbon sinks29, evaluations of ecological resilience under environmental regulation30 and analyses of ES trade-offs in karst basins using interpretable machine learning31. These applications underscore the growing potential of XGBoost-SHAP for exploring nonlinear associations, threshold-like responses and predictor contributions in ES-related outcomes. Despite these advances, few studies have combined interpretable machine learning with dynamic indicators, such as DETSI, to examine the predictors and spatial heterogeneity of ES trade-offs and synergies across time intervals32–34.

To address these gaps, this study proposes an integrated framework that combines DETSI with spatial clustering analysis and interpretable machine learning. DETSI is used to characterise inter-temporal trade-off and synergy patterns between selected time points, while LISA-based spatial clustering identifies high and low spatial clusters of ES values for each observation year. Building on these spatial and temporal diagnostics, XGBoost-SHAP is employed to examine nonlinear associations and relative contributions of biophysical, landscape, anthropogenic and institutional factors to estimated ES coupling outcomes. SHAP values are then used to interpret model-based explanatory patterns, including nonlinear responses, threshold-sensitive responses and spatial heterogeneity in predictor effects. This integrative approach links ES change metrics with spatial pattern detection and interpretable modelling, thereby improving the understanding of spatially differentiated ES coupling patterns and their model-identified drivers. Study area and data processing

Study area

Shaanxi Province, located in north-central China (31° 42′–39° 35′ N, 105° 29′–111° 15′ E), covers approximately 205,600 km2 and spans the Loess Plateau, the Qinling Mountains and the southern highlands. Elevation decreases from west to east and varies latitudinally from north to south (Fig. 1). The province comprises 10 prefecture-level cities and more than 100 counties, with climates ranging from temperate continental to subtropical. Annual precipitation varies from 340 mm in the arid north to 1200 mm in the humid south, while mean temperatures range from 7 to 14 °C. Land use consists of a mosaic of cropland, forest, grassland and urban areas; cultivated land totals approximately 2.93 M ha (14.27% of the province). Ecologically, Shaanxi serves as a key barrier in the upper Yellow River Basin and is a focal area for major restoration programmes, including Grain-for-Green, the Ecological Redline and the Yellow River Strategy35. Despite these efforts, soil erosion, land degradation and agricultural encroachment persist, making the province a representative setting for analysing the relationships among land use change, ecosystem services and policy response in inland China36,37.

Data sources and processing

This study integrated multi-source spatial datasets spanning biophysical, ecological, socio-economic and institutional domains (Table 1). Land use and land cover (LULC) data for 2000, 2010 and 2022 were obtained from the China Land Cover Dataset (CLCD), developed by Wuhan University, with a native spatial resolution of 30 m38. Ecosystem service modelling involved six ES indicators: NPP was estimated using the Carnegie-Ames-Stanford Approach (CASA) model with NDVI and solar radiation derived from Google Earth Engine (GEE), while other inputs, such as precipitation, temperature and evapotranspiration, were sourced from the National Tibetan Plateau Data Centre. InVEST-based modelling was applied to water yield, soil retention and habitat quality, incorporating HWSD soil data, DEM, root depth data39 and road network information from OpenStreetMap.

Food supply was spatialised by allocating administrative-level grain production from the Shaanxi Statistical Yearbook to cultivated-land grids according to the proportional contribution of each grid cell’s NDVI. CL was operationalised as a composite weighted proxy rather than a directly observed service variable. A baseline cultural service value was first assigned using equivalent value coefficients based on land-cover types. This baseline was then integrated with a socio-spatial adjustment term derived from normalised scenic POI density and population density to reflect differences in accessibility and potential human use intensity. Accordingly, the CL indicator represents an accessibility-adjusted composite proxy of cultural service potential under combined ecological and socio-spatial conditions.

To ensure temporal consistency across datasets, all inputs were harmonised to match the three target years used in this study (2000, 2010 and 2022). Monthly climate and remote-sensing variables were aggregated to annual values for the corresponding target years, annual land-cover data were extracted for the same years, and static background datasets such as DEM and soil properties were treated as temporally invariant. Research framework

The overall workflow of this study is illustrated in Fig. 2. The resulting ES values were subsequently aggregated to a 1 km × 1 km grid using zonal statistics. Driver factor analysis of ecosystem trade-offs and synergies

XGBOOST

XGBoost is a highly efficient and scalable ensemble machine learning algorithm proposed by Chen et al.56 and Chen & Guestrin27. It integrates gradient boosting with decision-tree learning and supports parallel processing, regularisation and sparse feature handling, making it particularly suitable for high-dimensional and complex datasets57,58. In this study, XGBoost was selected due to its robust performance in ecological modelling, effectively balancing predictive accuracy and model complexity. Target variable definition and binary transformation

To investigate the complex driving mechanisms behind ES interactions using the XGBoost model, it was necessary to transform the continuous spatial metric (DETSI) into a categorical target variable. A binary classification framework was opted for rather than a continuous regression approach for two primary reasons. First, from an ecological perspective, it simplifies the interpretation of complex dynamics by directly contrasting the driving forces behind synergistic versus trade-off states59,60. Second, from an algorithmic perspective, the XGBoost model is inherently well-suited for binary classification tasks27. Formulating the problem this way not only maximises training efficiency and model performance but also facilitates a clearer derivation of SHAP values61, allowing us to explicitly quantify how each feature pushes the ecosystem towards either a synergy or a trade-off. Specifically, spatial pixels demonstrating synergistic relationships (DETSI > 0) were encoded as “Class 1”, while pixels indicating trade-offs (DETSI < 0) were encoded as “Class 0”. Pixels with a DETSI value of exactly 0, implying no definitive interaction or no temporal change, were excluded from the binary classification model. This transformation ensured that the machine learning algorithm focused exclusively on learning the distinct driving factors that differentiate clear trade-off and synergy patterns.

Explanatory variables

A total of 18 driving factors were considered and grouped into four categories: natural environment, ecological status, human activity and policy/institutional indicators. To align the explanatory dataset with the DETSI outcomes over 2000–2022, the variables were reconstructed according to their temporal characteristics rather than being primarily extracted from a single endpoint year. Static background factors were retained where appropriate, while dynamic variables were represented by long-term trends, period-specific changes or cumulative institutional intensity. Policy attention was measured using cumulative environmental attention scores for 2002–2022, reflecting the available policy-text series (Table 3). The classification framework was adapted from the natural-geographical-social-landscape factor structure proposed by Li et al.28 and further refined by incorporating policy-related variables reflecting governance capacity and regulatory input intensity, thereby improving the interpretability of multidimensional ES dynamics in Shaanxi Province through the joint consideration of environmental and institutional influences. Model training and parameter optimisation

For each ES pair, a separate XGBoost classifier was constructed to distinguish trade-off and synergy states. The dataset was randomly divided into training and hold-out validation subsets using stratified sampling, with 80% of samples allocated to model development and 20% reserved for final validation, while preserving the class distribution of the two interaction states. The validation subset was not used during hyperparameter optimisation, cross-validation or class-weight calculation, thereby preventing the hold-out evaluation from influencing model training.

Hyperparameter optimisation was performed using the Hyperopt framework, which applies Tree-structured Parzen Estimators (TPE) for efficient Bayesian optimisation. The search space included key parameters, such as max_depth, learning_rate, subsample, colsample_bytree, min_child_weight, gamma, reg_alpha and reg_lambda. Compared with conventional grid or random search, this approach improves optimisation efficiency in high-dimensional parameter spaces and facilitates the identification of well-performing parameter combinations.

During optimisation, five-fold cross-validation was conducted exclusively within the training subset, and the Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (ROC-AUC) was used as the objective function. After identifying the optimal parameter set, the model was retrained on the full training subset.

To address class imbalance, the scale_pos_weight parameter was determined from the ratio of negative to positive samples in the training subset only. Final model performance was assessed exclusively on the independent hold-out validation subset using multiple complementary metrics, including accuracy, macro-F1, weighted-F1, balanced accuracy, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC.

All 15 ES pairs were modelled using the same analytical procedure. Detailed hyperparameter configurations and validation results are provided in Supplementary Tables S2 and S3.

SHAP interpretation

SHAP is a unified framework for interpreting machine learning models, grounded in cooperative game theory61. It attributes a prediction to each feature based on its marginal contribution across all possible feature coalitions, satisfying properties such as local accuracy, consistency and missingness. In essence, each feature is treated as a “player” in a game, and its Shapley value represents its average contribution to the prediction over all permutations of input combinations64.

To calculate the Shapley value of a specific feature i, SHAP computes the average change in model output when feature i is included versus excluded from subsets .

This approach provides both the direction (positive or negative effect) and magnitude (absolute contribution) of each feature’s influence on the model output. It is particularly suitable for tree-based models, such as XGBoost, which can leverage the efficient TreeSHAP algorithm to compute exact Shapley values without requiring exhaustive enumeration65.

In this study, SHAP was used to interpret the contribution of driving factors to the predicted trade-off or synergy state of each ES pair at both global and local levels. Global feature importance was quantified using the mean absolute SHAP value across all validation samples. Based on this metric, the top 10 contributing variables for each selected ES pair were retained for further interpretation and comparison.

To further examine nonlinear response patterns and interaction structures, SHAP dependence analysis was conducted for each selected primary variable. The secondary interacting variable was identified based on its strongest association with the primary variable among the explanatory variables, allowing the joint influence of paired drivers on model output to be explored. At the local level, SHAP waterfall plots were used to decompose individual predictions. Representative samples were selected to reflect distinct prediction regimes, including high- and low-value cases under both positive and negative SHAP contribution domains.

To identify potential threshold-sensitive responses, smoothed SHAP dependence curves were fitted using locally weighted regression (LOWESS). Threshold points were determined based on the maximum rate of change in the fitted SHAP curve, while peak contribution points were defined as the locations of maximum SHAP magnitude. All SHAP analyses were conducted on the validation dataset to ensure consistency with model evaluation and to reduce potential bias from training samples.

Spatiotemporal dynamics of ecosystem service synergies and trade-offs

Increases were most evident in the central Loess Plateau, where low-moderate NPP zones shifted into medium–high classes.

Spatial synergies and trade-offs among ecosystem services

The spatial coupling among ES pairs was quantified using global bivariate Moran’s I for 2000, 2010 and 2022, with statistical significance evaluated through permutation tests. All Moran’s I values were statistically significant (p < 0.05), indicating that the observed spatial associations reflect non-random spatial dependence structures rather than stochastic spatial variation.

At the global scale (Fig. 4), ES pairs exhibited three broad patterns. Strong and persistent positive spatial associations were observed for CL-HQ, HQ-NPP, CL-WY, NPP-WY and SR-WY, with Moran’s I values ranging from 0.47 to 0.74 across the three observation years. Among these, SR-WY showed the strongest association, with Moran’s I remaining between 0.73 and 0.74, followed by NPP-WY (0.65–0.72), CL-HQ (0.71–0.74), HQ-NPP (0.61–0.63) and CL-WY (0.47–0.52). Moderate but consistently positive associations were identified for CL-NPP (0.56–0.60), CL-SR (0.45–0.48), HQ-SR (0.45–0.46), HQ-WY (0.46–0.49) and NPP-SR (0.50–0.52). By contrast, negative Moran’s I values were concentrated in CL-FS (− 0.65 to − 0.59), FS-HQ (− 0.52 to − 0.45), FS-NPP (− 0.23 to − 0.11), FS-SR (− 0.28 to − 0.25) and FS-WY (− 0.22 to − 0.12), indicating persistent spatial divergence, particularly in food-supply-related combinations. Overall, regulating-service combinations tended to show stronger positive spatial association, whereas provisioning-related combinations were more spatially mismatched or fragmented.

Global bivariate Moran’s I values for ES pairs across the years 2000, 2010 and 2022.

However, global Moran’s I alone do not distinguish whether a positive association arises from high-value co-location (HH), low-value co-location (LL) or the parallel clustering of both extremes. As shown in Figs. 5 and 6, many ES pairs with positive global Moran’s I were characterised by the coexistence of HH and LL clusters, indicating that positive spatial autocorrelation often reflects spatial stratification rather than uniformly high ecosystem functioning. Local LISA patterns are therefore necessary to clarify whether global association corresponds to stable high-value coordination or to more heterogeneous local configurations.

For regulating-regulating combinations, HQ-NPP exhibited the most stable HH pattern, with HH proportions increasing from 37.3% in 2000 to 38.6% in 2022, indicating persistent high-value co-location. HQ-WY also maintained relatively high HH proportions, remaining close to 30% throughout the study period. By contrast, LL clusters dominated in HQ-SR (38.5–40.7%), NPP-SR (41.7–42.4%), NPP-WY (36.7–40.5%) and SR-WY (45.4–47.9%), showing that despite positive global Moran’s I, large parts of the landscape were characterised by co-located low ES values. Local outlier classes were generally less important in these combinations, with LH clusters remaining limited and HL clusters secondary to HH and LL. Regulating services in Shaanxi, therefore, displayed a structure defined less by uniform high-value synergy than by the coexistence of stable HH zones and extensive LL coldspots.

Among cultural-regulating combinations, CL-HQ and CL-NPP showed the strongest HH patterns. In CL-HQ, HH clusters accounted for 33.7%, 35.4% and 35.3% in 2000, 2010 and 2022, respectively, while in CL-NPP they increased from 36.1 to 38.7% over the same period. However, CL-HQ also retained a high proportion of non-significant cells, remaining above 41% in all three years, indicating that strong local positive association was geographically concentrated rather than continuous. In contrast, CL-SR was dominated by LL clusters, which declined only moderately from 33.9% in 2000 to 30.9% in 2022, while both HH and HL clusters increased over time. CL-WY also showed a positive global association, but its local structure remained mixed, with HH and LL clusters coexisting and HL clusters remaining substantial. These results suggest that cultural services were more consistently aligned with habitat quality and NPP than with soil retention and water yield, the latter two showing more heterogeneous and spatially differentiated local configurations.

Food supply-related combinations exhibited the most pronounced spatial fragmentation and mismatch. The CL-FS pair showed the clearest and most persistent local mismatch among all ES combinations: non-significant cells dominated throughout the study period, accounting for 47.7%, 46.2% and 47.0% in 2000, 2010 and 2022, while HL clusters remained consistently high at 28.3%-30.9%, and HH clusters never exceeded 1.5%. Among food supply-regulating pairs, FS-HQ was characterised mainly by non-significant and LH clusters, FS-NPP was dominated by LH clusters that increased slightly over time, and FS-SR, as well as FS-WY, displayed mixed but predominantly mismatch-related local structures, with LL and LH clusters both occupying considerable proportions. Overall, food supply showed weaker and more fragmented local associations with other ES than did most regulating-regulating or cultural-regulating combinations, with mismatch-related structures especially evident in FS-NPP, FS-HQ and CL-FS.

Taken together, the results reveal a clear scale-dependent structure of ES interactions in Shaanxi Province. At the global scale, many ES pairs appeared positively associated, especially among regulating services. At the local scale, however, these positive associations often reflected the joint presence of HH and LL clusters rather than uniformly favourable high-value co-occurrence. By contrast, food supply-related combinations were consistently more fragmented and mismatch-prone. These findings highlight the importance of integrating global and local spatial analyses to distinguish stable high-value coordination from broader spatial co-variation, thereby providing a more rigorous basis for subsequent DETSI-based dynamic analysis.

Temporal synergies and trade-offs

To evaluate long-term interaction trends among ES, the DETSI was calculated for 15 ES pairs over 2000–2022. Figure 7 reveals marked spatial heterogeneity in temporal ES relationships across Shaanxi Province, with strong and moderate synergies, weakly correlated or non-significant states, and moderate-to-strong trade-offs coexisting across different subregions.

Spatial classification of synergies and trade-offs among 15 ES pairs based on DETSI.

Regulating–regulating interactions generally exhibited temporal synergy but with pronounced subregional differentiation. NPP–SR and SR–WY showed extensive synergy across northern Shaanxi and parts of the central belt, with SR–WY being the most clearly synergy-dominated pair. Conversely, HQ–NPP, HQ–SR, HQ–WY, and NPP–WY displayed a stark north–south divergence: synergies were primarily concentrated in the north, while trade-offs became more prominent in the central and southern regions. Notably, NPP–WY formed a distinct southern trade-off belt, whereas the HQ-related pairs featured intense trade-off patches across central and southern transitional zones.

Cultural–regulating combinations demonstrated high spatial heterogeneity and strong dependence on landscape context. While CL–HQ was the most synergy-oriented cultural–regulating pair, with relatively continuous synergy patches in northern and parts of central Shaanxi, CL–NPP, CL–SR, and CL–WY contrasted sharply. These three pairs exhibited northern synergy but intense, concentrated trade-offs across the central Guanzhong Plain and southern areas, indicating that the temporal alignment of cultural service with NPP, SR and WY was more spatially divergent than that with HQ.

Food-supply-related pairs, including CL–FS, were highly fragmented and less consistently synergistic. FS–HQ and CL–FS were dominated by weak or non-significant associations at the provincial scale, punctuated only by localised northern synergies and central–southern trade-offs. Meanwhile, FS–NPP, FS–SR, and FS–WY exhibited a clear spatial dichotomy: moderate northern synergy versus concentrated moderate-to-strong trade-offs in the central and southern regions. This highlights a weaker and more spatially uneven long-term alignment between food supply and ecological or cultural services.

Integrated spatiotemporal typology of ecosystem service interactions

Figure 8 presents the integrated spatiotemporal typology derived by overlaying bivariate LISA classes with DETSI-based temporal interaction states. The resulting categories should be interpreted as statistically derived spatiotemporal interaction patterns rather than direct ecological causality. Overall, the integrated typology reveals pronounced pair-specific spatial differentiation across Shaanxi Province. Potential recovery, stable synergy, synergistic rupture and continuous trade-off were not evenly distributed among ES pairs, but formed distinct spatial configurations depending on the service combination.

The clearest stable-synergy pattern occurred in the regulating–regulating pairs NPP–SR, NPP–WY and SR–WY. In these three combinations, stable synergy formed relatively coherent clusters in southern and south-central Shaanxi, indicating locations where high–high local spatial associations coincided with temporally synergistic DETSI trajectories. Among them, SR–WY showed the most spatially consolidated stable-synergy pattern, followed by NPP–WY and NPP–SR. Notably, potential recovery (lavender) was the dominant state for these regulating–regulating pairs across the northern Loess Plateau. This suggests that while these northern areas have not yet formed high-value spatial clusters (HH), they already exhibit temporally synergy-oriented interaction trajectories within the integrated typology.

By contrast, continuous trade-off (dark green) was more prominent in several cultural–regulating and HQ-related pairs, albeit with divergent spatial distributions. For CL–NPP, CL–SR and CL–WY, continuous trade-off occupied extensive areas across northern and north-central Shaanxi and extended into parts of the central belt, while the southern portion displayed a more heterogeneous mixture of stable synergy, synergistic rupture and non-significant states. For HQ–NPP, HQ–SR and HQ–WY, continuous trade-off was likewise concentrated in northern and central Shaanxi, while other interaction states remained more fragmented. These results indicate that the integrated spatiotemporal structures of cultural–regulating and HQ-centred interactions were more frequently characterised by persistent non-HH trade-off configurations than by consolidated synergy.

Synergistic rupture appeared more selectively, but it was particularly evident in the southern and central–southern portions of NPP–SR, NPP–WY and SR–WY. These areas represent temporally vulnerable coordination zones, where HH-type local spatial clustering coincides with DETSI-based temporal trade-offs. Smaller rupture patches were also visible in CL–NPP, CL–SR, CL–WY and several HQ-related pairs. This pattern shows that locally favourable spatial co-location does not necessarily correspond to temporally stable synergy, and that some high-value spatial clusters were accompanied by divergent long-term interaction trajectories.

Food-supply-related combinations displayed more fragmented and less consolidated integrated patterns. CL–FS and FS–HQ were characterised by large non-significant areas, with only scattered patches of potential recovery, continuous trade-off and other typologies. FS–NPP, FS–SR and FS–WY showed more structured spatial organisation: potential recovery was relatively prominent in northern Shaanxi, whereas continuous trade-off and mixed typology patches appeared more frequently in central and southern areas. Stable synergy remained limited in these provisioning-related pairs, suggesting that food-supply interactions were less likely than the strongest regulating–regulating combinations to form broad, spatially coherent, temporally reinforced coordination zones.

Taken together, Fig. 8 differentiates four major forms of spatiotemporal ES interaction: reinforced coordination, represented by stable synergy; improving but not yet spatially consolidated interaction, represented by potential recovery; locally clustered but temporally destabilising interaction, represented by synergistic rupture; and persistent non-HH divergence, represented by continuous trade-off. This integrated typology therefore provides a more refined basis for interpreting the spatial–temporal organisation of ES interactions than either local spatial association or temporal DETSI patterns alone.

Driver dependencies and threshold analysis

The dependence plots reveal pronounced nonlinear and threshold-sensitive response patterns among the key drivers of the selected ES pairs (Figs. 10, 11, 12, 13 and 14). In these plots, the red dashed lines indicate the estimated turning points of the smoothed SHAP curves, while the green dashed lines denote the feature values associated with the maximum fitted SHAP response. Overall, the results show that the contributions of major drivers were not monotonic, but instead changed markedly across specific value ranges and differed among ES combinations.

A particularly recurrent threshold pattern was observed for Δ GDP. Across all five ES pairs, the estimated turning point was concentrated within a narrow interval of approximately 98,386–98,551. Beyond this range, the fitted SHAP curves in FS–WY, NPP–WY, NPP–SR and SR–WY shifted sharply towards more positive contributions, while CL–WY showed a similar but more irregular transition at . This indicates that economic change exerted a strongly nonlinear influence on the modelled trade-off–synergy states, with a comparable transition range recurring across different ES interactions.

Land-use intensity also displayed clear threshold behaviour, although its response was more pair-specific. In CL–WY, SHAP contributions declined sharply after , indicating a marked shift towards negative model contributions as land-use intensification increased. Comparable turning points were identified at for FS–WY, 0.667 for NPP–WY, -0.333 for SR–WY and -0.500 for NPP–SR. The fitted responses suggest that Δ LUI was especially influential in shaping abrupt changes in model output, but the location and direction of these changes varied across ES pairs.

Climate-related variables showed another set of recurring nonlinear patterns. For , threshold points were concentrated between approximately 5.980 and 8.066 across the five models: 5.980 in CL–WY, 7.142 in FS–WY, 7.321 in NPP–WY, 7.472 in SR–WY and 8.066 in NPP–SR. In CL–WY, SHAP contributions weakened after the threshold, whereas in NPP–WY and NPP–SR, the fitted curves reached stronger positive responses at higher precipitation-trend values, with maximum fitted SHAP positions at approximately 10.683 and 9.861, respectively. also displayed threshold-sensitive behaviour. Ecological and terrain-related variables further reinforced the pair-dependent nature of threshold effects. showed relatively consistent low-value turning points in CL–WY (≈ 0.004), NPP–WY (≈ 0.003), NPP–SR (≈ 0.003) and SR–WY (≈ 0.004), after which the fitted curves generally rose towards more positive SHAP contributions. DEM, by contrast, exhibited two distinct threshold regimes: lower elevation turning points around 450–473 m in CL–WY, FS–WY and NPP–WY, and substantially higher turning points of 1,169.250 m and 1,243.500 m in NPP–SR and SR–WY, respectively. Policy intensity and infrastructure-related variables also exhibited identifiable transitions. showed threshold values clustered around 359.250–375.000 across the five pairs, indicating repeated nonlinear responses within a relatively narrow cumulative policy-intensity range. Δ RoadDen displayed thresholds close to zero in all selected models, distinguishing responses associated with near-zero or direction-changing road-density variation from those under more substantial positive or negative change. Taken together, the dependence plots demonstrate that the modelled ES interaction states were shaped by multiple threshold-like response structures, including recurring economic transition ranges, pair-specific land-use effects, climate-sensitive turning points and differentiated ecological, topographic and policy-related response regimes.

This suggests that road-density change contributed differently to cultural–water interactions and regulating–regulating interactions within the model framework.

Discussion

Spatiotemporal overlay analysis of ecosystem service interactions

The integrated typology reveals that ES interactions in Shaanxi are neither spatially uniform nor temporally stable. Instead, the province is characterised by the coexistence of stable coordination, emerging recovery, temporal destabilisation and persistent risk, with clear differences among service combinations and regions. In this context, combining DETSI and LISA provides a useful basis for interpreting not only where ES interactions are spatially clustered, but also whether these configurations are being reinforced, weakened or reoriented over time.

Potential recovery under trade-off pressures in northern Shaanxi

Northern Shaanxi is characterised by extensive potential-recovery zones rather than by a uniformly persistent trade-off. This pattern is particularly evident in several regulating-service combinations, where many areas remain outside HH-based high-value spatial coordination but already show improving temporal interaction trajectories. At the same time, non-HH spatial states remain widespread, indicating that recovery is still incomplete and spatially uneven. These patterns are consistent with the region’s structural constraints, as semi-arid conditions, soil degradation and limited ecological carrying capacity continue to restrict ecosystem service provision66,67. Meanwhile, continuous trade-off configurations remain visible in several HQ-linked relationships, indicating that recovery pressures have not been uniformly relieved across all service combinations.

This pattern suggests that ecological recovery in northern Shaanxi is occurring unevenly across service groups rather than as a uniform shift towards coordination. Temporal improvement is more evident in regulating-service combinations, particularly NPP-SR, NPP-WY and SR-WY, while provisioning-related pairs such as FS-NPP, FS-SR and FS-WY also show clear potential-recovery signals in the north, although their spatial patterns remain comparatively more fragmented. This pattern implies that restoration on the semi-arid Loess Plateau has most clearly strengthened regulating functions, including vegetation recovery, soil conservation and water-related regulation, while also beginning to ease some tensions involving food production68,69. In this sense, the region appears to be undergoing a process of functional reorganisation rather than having already achieved consolidated multifunctional synergy.

This interpretation is broadly consistent with previous studies showing that ecological restoration on the Loess Plateau may improve regulating services and partially mitigate, rather than fully resolve, production-related trade-offs under continued land-use pressure and water constraints70. It is also compatible with the broader restoration context of northern Shaanxi, where long-term ecological governance has substantially altered landscape processes. DETSI indicates improving interaction trajectories in several ES pairs, particularly among regulating services, with additional recovery signals also emerging in selected FS-related combinations, although this should not be interpreted as uniformly stable synergy across all combinations.

The regional cases further support this interpretation. In Yan’an, the Grain for Green programme converted steep cropland to forest and grassland, increasing vegetation cover from 46.3% in 2000 to over 80% by 2017. In the Yanhe River Basin, reforestation, gully rehabilitation and check-dam construction reshaped watershed processes; more than 1,500 check dams and supporting terraced fields were established, reducing annual sediment input to the Yellow River by 88% while also creating large areas of fertile farmland behind the dams71. These interventions provide a plausible regional context for the improvement of temporal interaction trajectories identified in northern Shaanxi. At the same time, the persistence of non-HH spatial states suggests that such gains remain spatially uneven and that restoration benefits have not yet translated into uniformly consolidated high-value coordination across the region.

The SHAP results further suggest that this transitional recovery is associated with pair-specific policy-context contributions rather than a uniform policy effect. In northern Shaanxi, shows relatively weak or negative spatial SHAP contributions for NPP-WY in many areas, whereas SR-WY exhibits more mixed and locally positive contributions. This should not be taken as direct causal proof; rather, it indicates that the association between cumulative policy context and emerging regulating-service coordination differs across ES pairs and cannot be interpreted as uniformly beneficial or uniformly disruptive.

From a governance perspective, potential-recovery zones require restoration-oriented but low-disturbance management. Because these areas have not yet formed stable HH-based coordination, the policy priority should be to consolidate emerging improvements through phased watershed restoration, slope and gully management, ecological compensation, and threshold-based monitoring of vegetation-water interactions. Northern Shaanxi is therefore better understood as a region of recovering but incomplete coordination, where governance should aim to stabilise improvement trajectories while carefully managing the unresolved thresholds between ecological rehabilitation and provisioning demands. In practical terms, this would require coordinated implementation across watershed restoration, ecological compensation and land-use management programmes, supported by periodic reassessment of recovery trajectories and key ecological thresholds.

Stable coordination and temporal vulnerability in southern Shaanxi

Southern Shaanxi remains the principal stable-synergy core in the province, especially for NPP-SR, NPP-WY and SR-WY, with additional stable-synergy patches also appearing in several CL- and HQ-linked combinations. This is broadly consistent with earlier studies identifying southern Shaanxi as one of the province’s main clusters of regulating-service co-variation18. The persistence of these high-value coordination cores is also compatible with the long-term effects of forest protection and landscape restoration in the Qinling region, including the Natural Forest Protection Programme, which reduced commercial logging and supported habitat recovery in the southern mountain belt11. Taken together, these studies provide contextual support for interpreting southern Shaanxi as a key coordination zone within the provincial ES system, although not as a uniformly stable region across all service pairs15,72.

A more important result, however, is that southern Shaanxi also contains clearly visible synergistic-rupture patches, especially in parts of the southern and south-eastern mountainous belt, most notably in several regulating-service relationships such as NPP-WY, NPP-SR and SR-WY. These areas retain HH-type local clustering, but their DETSI trajectories shift towards trade-off, indicating that strong spatial co-location does not necessarily translate into long-term stability32,73. In this sense, southern Shaanxi is characterised not only by stable coordination but also by temporal vulnerability. The coexistence of stable-synergy cores and synergistic-rupture patches indicates that ecologically favourable mountain areas may still experience weakening interaction trajectories in specific service relationships.

The SHAP results provide a plausible interpretive lens for this vulnerability. For NPP-WY and NPP-SR, , , , DEM and are among the influential drivers of modelled interaction patterns, and their dependence curves reveal clear nonlinear responses. Spatially, and show locally differentiated contributions across the southern mountain belt, indicating that hydroclimatic variation and vegetation change are associated with the stability of these regulating-service relationships in a place-specific manner. presents negative SHAP contributions across many southern areas for NPP-WY and NPP-SR, while Δ RoadDen shows more localised and spatially mixed effects. Taken together, these patterns suggest that the temporal vulnerability observed in southern Shaanxi is linked to the combined influence of hydroclimatic change, vegetation dynamics, policy-context differences and landscape disturbance, rather than to any single dominant factor. This interpretation is consistent with studies of humid mountain systems showing that vegetation–hydrological coupling, flood sensitivity and terrain-constrained drainage can influence the stability of multi-service relationships74,75. These patterns remain consistent with the broader concern that ecologically advantageous areas may still be sensitive to hydrological fluctuation, landscape disturbance and management imbalance76,77.

Overall, the case of southern Shaanxi reveals a partial mismatch between ecological potential and long-term stability. Stable-synergy cores indicate that the region retains clear ecological advantages, but synergistic-rupture zones show that these advantages are not automatically self-sustaining. From a governance perspective, this implies a dual strategy. Stable-synergy areas require continued ecological protection, low-disturbance land-use control and long-term monitoring78. By contrast, synergistic-rupture zones should be treated as early-warning areas, where response-sensitive governance is needed to prevent spatially favourable systems from sliding into temporally unstable trajectories79. In practice, this includes tighter control of urban and tourism expansion in mountain fringes, stronger green-infrastructure connectivity, improved disaster-prevention planning in humid and topographically constrained areas, and closer integration of hydroclimatic variation, vegetation-change signals and landscape-disturbance indicators into territorial spatial planning80,81. Institutionally, these measures would need to be supported by stronger cross-sectoral co-ordination among ecological, water, land-use and urban-planning authorities, together with periodic monitoring and typology-based reassessment.

This spatiotemporal typology shows that ecosystem service interactions are spatially heterogeneous and temporally differentiated under varying geographic and human–environment conditions82,83. Its value lies not only in identifying where synergies or trade-offs occur, but also in distinguishing whether these interaction states are being reinforced, weakened or reorganised over time. This underscores the need for adaptive, region-specific governance rather than uniform management responses76,77. By integrating DETSI trends with LISA clustering, the framework provides a useful tool for identifying high-value coordination areas, early-warning deterioration zones and potential recovery areas. Although this study does not aim to develop a full governance model, the typology still offers an operational basis for differentiated planning responses, nonlinear response monitoring and adaptive policy adjustment79. In rapidly changing landscapes, such an approach may help inform timely, place-based interventions to sustain multifunctionality and resilience84.

Nonlinear mechanisms and governance implications of driver effects

The drivers of DETSI-derived ES interactions exhibit clear nonlinear and pair-specific effects. Across the five representative ES relationships, Δ GDP, Δ LUI, , , , , DEM and repeatedly emerge as influential variables, but their relative importance and response patterns differ among service pairs. This indicates that ES interaction trajectories are shaped by a shared pool of drivers operating through differentiated nonlinear pathways, rather than by a uniform province-wide mechanism.

Hydroclimatic trends constitute one of the most important sources of this differentiation. For NPP-WY and NPP-SR, generally shifts from negative to positive SHAP contributions as precipitation trends increase, indicating that improving precipitation conditions are more favourable to model-predicted coordination in these productivity–regulation relationships. However, this pattern is not universal. In CL-WY and SR-WY, the response of is weaker or partly reversed, suggesting that increasing precipitation trends do not automatically strengthen all ES interactions. also displays pronounced nonlinear effects. In NPP-WY and SR-WY, higher evapotranspiration trends are generally associated with weaker SHAP contributions, whereas FS-WY and NPP-SR show more curved and threshold-like responses. adds a further layer of complexity, particularly in NPP-SR and SR-WY, where warming-related trends do not follow a simple monotonic pattern. Together, these findings suggest that water-related ES interactions depend on the balance between precipitation gains, atmospheric moisture demand and temperature change, rather than on rainfall variation alone. This interpretation is consistent with the broader understanding that hydrological conditions can reshape trade-offs and synergies among production and regulating services under different landscape settings85.

Vegetation change and terrain conditions further structure these nonlinear responses. is especially influential in NPP-WY, SR-WY and NPP-SR, where stronger positive vegetation trends are generally associated with higher SHAP contributions, although the exact response curves differ across pairs. This suggests that sustained greening may be more closely linked to improved coordination among productivity, soil retention and water-related services than to cultural or provisioning-related interactions. DEM also remains an important biophysical driver. Its dependence curves are more favourable in NPP-WY and NPP-SR as elevation increases, while the response in SR-WY is more moderated and pair-specific. These results imply that vegetation recovery is more likely to support synergistic interaction trajectories when embedded within suitable topographic settings, particularly in mountain and upland landscapes where runoff regulation, soil retention and ecological continuity are closely connected. Empirical research in alpine and mountain regions similarly shows that elevation and terrain structure can shape water-retention capacity and the stability of regulating functions86.

Among anthropogenic drivers, Δ GDP is consistently ranked as one of the most influential variables across the five representative pairs. In FS-WY, NPP-WY, SR-WY and NPP-SR, its SHAP response rises markedly after service-pair-specific transition points, indicating that stronger economic change is often associated with more favourable modelled interaction states. However, this should not be interpreted as evidence that economic expansion itself automatically improves ES coordination. The contrast with Δ LUI is instructive. In CL-WY, SR-WY and NPP-SR, increasing land-use intensity change is more often associated with declining SHAP contributions, revealing that direct spatial intensification can weaken ES coordination even where broader economic change appears favourable. This divergence suggests that development capacity and land-conversion pressure operate through different pathways. From a governance perspective, the central issue is therefore not whether development occurs, but whether economic growth can be decoupled from land-use intensification that erodes ecological coordination. This distinction is particularly important in transitional landscapes and peri-urban zones where socio-economic expansion and ecological vulnerability coexist87,88.

Infrastructure-related variables reinforce this context-dependent interpretation. Δ RoadDen is highly influential in CL-WY and remains relevant in several other pairs, but its effects are not directionally uniform. In CL-WY and NPP-WY, increasing road-density change is generally associated with declining SHAP contributions, suggesting that infrastructure expansion may coincide with weaker ES coordination in some landscapes. By contrast, FS-WY and NPP-SR show more mixed or partially positive responses at higher levels of Δ RoadDen, indicating that improved accessibility may in some contexts coexist with more favourable interaction states. RD also retains a secondary but nonlinear role, especially in FS-WY, implying that the spatial position of landscapes relative to river systems may influence how human activities and hydrological services interact. These findings point to a broader infrastructure paradox: connectivity can facilitate ecological management, monitoring and restoration access, but it can also intensify fragmentation, resource extraction and land-use disturbance when poorly regulated. Similar context-dependent effects of infrastructure on socio-ecological coordination have been reported in previous work89,90.

The spatial contribution maps further indicate that is not associated with a single province-wide direction of influence: positive and negative SHAP contributions occur in different regions and for different service combinations. This suggests that cumulative policy context is linked to ES interaction trajectories, but does not translate mechanically into stronger synergy. In some areas, policy accumulation may coincide with emerging recovery or stabilisation; in others, it may reflect governance attention directed towards already stressed or structurally constrained landscapes. The results, therefore, point to a gap between policy presence and spatially effective ecological coordination, reinforcing the argument that institutional attention must be evaluated through its territorial translation rather than through document accumulation alone. This interpretation is consistent with broader discussions on the need to align policy intent with spatial planning capacity, implementation mechanisms and ecological outcomes91.

Landscape-configuration variables are less dominant than socio-economic and hydroclimatic drivers, but they still add important interpretive depth. Δ SHDI is particularly relevant for SR-WY, where its dependence curve displays a clear nonlinear form: moderate changes in landscape heterogeneity are associated with more favourable SHAP contributions, whereas stronger departures in either direction are less beneficial. This indicates that heterogeneity does not exert a uniformly positive effect. Rather, its contribution depends on whether landscape diversity supports functional ecological complementarity or merely reflects fragmentation and structural discontinuity. This distinction is important because visually complex mosaics are not necessarily functionally integrated systems. Previous studies have similarly shown that landscape diversity can stabilise ecosystem responses and support multifunctionality only when structural heterogeneity is coupled with ecological connectivity and functional integration92,93.

Taken together, the combined behaviour of Δ GDP, Δ LUI, and Δ RoadDen shows that human-dominated landscapes cannot be reduced to a simple “development versus ecology” dichotomy. Anthropogenic drivers may be associated with either more favourable or less favourable ES interaction states depending on the service pair, land-use pathway and spatial setting. This conditional interpretation is consistent with green-infrastructure research showing that well-designed urban and peri-urban systems can enhance multifunctionality through stormwater regulation, habitat continuity, recreation and other co-benefits, whereas growth-oriented landscape transformation often intensifies fragmentation and disrupts compatibility among provisioning, regulating and cultural services94–98.

Δ POI, used to reflect changes in cultural and tourism-related activity, further illustrates this spatial selectivity. Its SHAP contributions are localised and service-pair dependent rather than uniformly positive or negative. In CL-WY, NPP-WY and NPP-SR, concentrated contribution patches suggest that amenity-related growth may support multifunctionality in some locations, but its effects depend on whether tourism and cultural-service expansion are embedded in environmentally compatible land-use arrangements and green-space planning. This interpretation is consistent with evidence that cultural investment and waterfront or rural tourism development can reinforce ecosystem-service clustering when integrated with ecological restoration and spatial design99,100.

Overall, the driver analysis reveals that ES interaction trajectories in Shaanxi are governed by a layered set of nonlinear mechanisms. Hydroclimatic change, vegetation trends and terrain conditions shape the biophysical basis of service coordination; economic growth, land-use intensification and infrastructure change condition the human-pressure environment; and , Δ SHDI and Δ POI capture more context-sensitive governance, landscape and cultural dimensions. The main implication is that ES governance should avoid single-factor optimisation. Instead, planning should be calibrated to service-pair-specific response patterns: water-related regulating pairs require closer monitoring of precipitation, evapotranspiration and vegetation trends; transition zones require stronger separation between economic development and land-use intensification; and policy, infrastructure and cultural investments should be assessed by whether they produce territorially coherent improvements in ES interactions rather than by their nominal intensity alone.

Methodological contributions and broader applicability of the DETSI-LISA-XGBoost-SHAP framework

Beyond the Shaanxi case, the main contribution of this study lies in demonstrating an integrated analytical framework that links period-based interaction trajectories, local spatial structure and nonlinear driver interpretation within a single ES assessment workflow28,101. Rather than treating ES relationships as static or spatially uniform, the framework combines DETSI, bivariate LISA, XGBoost and SHAP to identify how ES interaction states differ across discrete observation periods, how these dynamics are spatially organised, and which drivers and nonlinear response patterns are most strongly associated with such changes. In this sense, the value of the study lies not only in the typologies identified in Shaanxi, but also in the analytical logic used to connect temporal change, spatial heterogeneity and nonlinear socio-ecological responses102,103.

Compared with existing approaches, the integrated framework used here provides a more informative basis for interpreting ES interactions in Shaanxi. Static ES trade-off assessments can identify whether synergies or trade-offs exist, but they are less able to distinguish whether such relationships are stabilising, recovering or deteriorating over time104,105. This limitation is evident in the present case, where several ES pairs would have been interpreted simply as synergistic or trade-off-dominated if only static relationships had been considered. By incorporating DETSI, the analysis further distinguishes improving and weakening interaction trajectories. Likewise, spatial clustering analysis alone can reveal local co-location or mismatch, but it cannot determine whether apparently favourable configurations are temporally stable106,107, and it may invite overly direct translation from spatial association to ecological interaction108. In this study, the overlay of LISA and DETSI showed that some HH-type areas in southern Shaanxi were, in fact, characterised by synergistic rupture, while several areas outside HH-based spatial coordination in northern Shaanxi displayed potential recovery. Finally, although machine-learning models are effective for identifying important drivers, their interpretation is stronger when embedded in an explicit spatial–temporal interaction framework109–111. Here, XGBoost-SHAP not only identified influential variables but also helped to characterise how different ES interaction states were associated with nonlinear response patterns rather than simple linear relationships. Taken together, these results show that the added value of the framework lies in revealing interaction states that would be obscured under single-method analysis.

A further strength of the framework is its transferability. Its core components are modular rather than region-specific: DETSI can be applied to ES indicators observed at multiple time points, LISA can be used to identify local clustering or mismatch under different spatial units, and XGBoost-SHAP can be adapted to interpret nonlinear responses across varying socio-ecological settings. This means that the framework could be extended to other ecologically fragile or rapidly changing regions worldwide, including semi-arid restoration zones, mountain systems under land-use pressure, and rapidly urbanising wetland, delta or peri-urban landscapes84,112. In such applications, the specific ES indicators, spatial resolution, explanatory variables and temporal intervals would need to be adjusted to local ecological processes and development pressures, but the overall analytical workflow would remain applicable113.

More broadly, the integrated framework may support place-sensitive assessments of nature’s contributions to people in ways relevant to IPBES5, particularly by revealing how ES interactions vary across space, discrete periods and nonlinear driver conditions. It may also inform SDG-oriented ecological planning in regions where food production, water regulation, habitat maintenance and land development must be balanced simultaneously114,115. In this sense, the framework is especially relevant to policy contexts linked to SDGs 6, 11, 13 and 15, where understanding trade-offs, synergies and threshold-sensitive nonlinear responses is critical for adaptive and regionally differentiated decision-making116–118.

Limitations

Despite the methodological strengths of integrating DETSI, LISA and the XGBoost-SHAP framework, several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, the validation strategy was based on random stratified sampling rather than spatial cross-validation. Given the inherent spatial autocorrelation in geospatial data, this may lead to a certain degree of performance overestimation due to spatial information leakage between training and validation samples. Although the model remains effective for identifying nonlinear associations, the reported metrics should be interpreted as indicative rather than strictly spatially transferable. Future work should incorporate spatial blocking or spatial cross-validation to better assess generalisability.

Second, several socio-economic and institutional drivers (e.g., GDP, road density, policy intensity) were derived from aggregated datasets or represented by proxy indicators (e.g., PI, POI), which may not fully capture fine-scale heterogeneity or multidimensional governance effects. The CL indicator was also represented by a composite proxy and may underrepresent non-material cultural values. These limitations could introduce uncertainty in interpreting the strength of certain drivers and the representation of cultural ecosystem services.

Third, the ecosystem service indicators were estimated using established modelling frameworks (e.g., CASA, InVEST, RUSLE-based methods and equivalent-factor approaches), rather than direct field observations. While widely adopted in large-scale ES assessments, these approaches inherently carry assumptions related to model structure, input data and parameterisation. Accordingly, the DETSI-XGBoost-SHAP results should be interpreted as revealing relationships within estimated ES patterns, rather than direct evidence of ecological causality.

Another limitation is the use of three discrete time points (2000, 2010 and 2022), which may smooth short-term fluctuations and transitional dynamics. As a result, the DETSI-based analysis primarily reflects long-term trends in ecosystem service interactions rather than continuous temporal evolution.

Conclusion

This study developed and applied an integrated DETSI-LISA-XGBoost-SHAP framework to examine the spatiotemporal dynamics, local spatial structure and nonlinear driver associations of ES interactions in Shaanxi Province, China. By combining temporal interaction trajectories, local spatial clustering and interpretable machine learning, the framework provided a more comprehensive basis for analysing ES relationships than static, purely spatial or stand-alone driver-based approaches.

Between 2000 and 2022, the six ES in Shaanxi Province showed distinct spatial patterns. NPP increased continuously, with high values in the southern Qinling Mountains and hilly regions; HQ fluctuated slightly but improved in parts of the central Loess Plateau and the northern transitional belt. WY exhibited a clear south-north gradient, rising moderately in the north, while SR remained high in the southern highlands, sharing HQ and WY’s “increase-decline” trend. CL values concentrated in the central and southeast expanded over time, and FS centred on the Guanzhong Plain extended to the northern Loess Plateau and parts of the southern plains.

Psychometric properties of the Chinese version of professional commitment questionnaire among nursing students: validation using classical test theory and item response theory
Authors: Zhi Rong Xu, Lai Kun Tong, Mio Leng Au, Qiao Hong Yang, Xiu Lan Wang, Kamal Takhdat, Yue Yi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAssessing professional commitment is crucial for understanding workforce dynamics, particularly in healthcare. The Professional Commitment Questionnaire (PCQ) is a concise tool designed for such assessments. This study aimed to translate and culturally adapt the PCQ into Chinese and validate its psychometric properties among nursing students.MethodsThe translation and cross-cultural adaptation were conducted using the translation, review, adjudication, pre-testing, and documentation (TRAPD) methodology. Psychometric properties were assessed through classical test theory (CTT) and item response theory (IRT).ResultsAccording to CTT, the content validity (S-CVI = 1), convergent validity (AVE = 0.792), internal consistency reliability (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.878), and test-retest reliability (ICC = 0.782) of the Chinese version of the PCQ (CV-PCQ) were acceptable. According to IRT, item fit (infit and outfit MNSQ ranging from 0.552 to 1.077) and differential item functioning (p values ranging from 0.164 to 0.870) were acceptable.ConclusionThe CV-PCQ demonstrated acceptable content validity, convergent validity, structural validity, internal consistency reliability, and test-retest reliability, as well as acceptable item fit and differential item functioning among Chinese nursing students. These specific psychometric properties support its utility as a brief assessment tool for nursing educators, researchers, and policymakers.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12912-026-05105-w.Keywords: Professional commitment questionnaire, Classical test theory, Item response theory, Nursing student, Reliability, Validity, Translation, Validation, Psychometric properties

Background

Professional commitment in nursing denotes the extent of dedication, loyalty, and responsibility nurses hold toward their profession and its guiding values. It reflects a belief in the significance of nursing, a willingness to invest effort in advancing the profession, a desire to remain within the field, and adherence to established standards and ethical principles [1, 2]. Moreover, professional commitment serves as a predictor of readiness for clinical practice [3] and shapes caring behaviors [4], resilience [5], and adaptability in demanding work environments [6]. Nursing students’ professional commitment is closely associated with both their personal professional development and the quality of care they deliver to patients [7, 8]. Students with a high level of professional dedication are more proactive in seeking out learning opportunities, actively engage in classes and clinical internships, and demonstrate well-defined career goals [9, 10]. Furthermore, nursing students’ commitment to their profession is a powerful indicator of their future commitment to nursing after graduation [11]. Nurses who exhibit a high level of professional commitment are more inclined to go beyond their responsibilities to enhance their professional competencies, increase job satisfaction, reduce turnover intentions, and continuously pursue professional growth and well-being [12, 13]. Consequently, it is crucial to evaluate and enhance nursing students’ professional commitment.

In recent years, a growing body of research has examined the factors that influence nursing students’ professional commitment and identified effective strategies to enhance it [10, 14, 15]. To effectively improve nursing students’ professional commitment and, in turn, support their personal development and improve patient outcomes, it is crucial to first assess the current levels of professional commitment among nursing students. However, there is still a lack of effective tools to evaluate nursing students’ professional commitment in mainland China. Previous studies exploring nursing students’ professional commitment have often used self-developed scales [16], the Professional Commitment Questionnaire developed by Taiwanese scholars [17, 18], or scales not specifically designed for nursing profession [19]. This has somewhat affected the comparability of studies conducted in different regions. Consequently, having a professional commitment questionnaire specifically designed for nursing profession, with strong validity and reliability, that can be used for comparing research findings across different regions is essential.

A wide range of instruments has been developed to assess professional commitment. These include global scales designed to measure overall professional commitment, such as Blau’s Career Commitment Scale [20] and the Professional Commitment Questionnaire (PCQ) [21]. In addition, facet-composite scales, such as the Nurses’ Professional Commitment Inventory [22] and the Nursing Professional Commitment Scale [11], focus on particular facets of the construct. Global scales are generally more concise than facet-composite instruments, making them particularly suitable for contexts requiring rapid assessment. Notably, prior research has demonstrated a negative association between excessive questionnaire length and data quality [23].

The PCQ is a widely utilized global instrument within the nursing profession and demonstrates strong reliability and validity in measuring overall levels of professional commitment [21, 24, 25]. More importantly, this questionnaire contains only four items [21], which not only allows participants to focus on answering but also facilitates management and analysis for users, thereby enhancing its potential for use. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to translate the PCQ into Chinese and to examine the psychometric properties of the Chinese version (CV-PCQ) using two complementary frameworks. Classical Test Theory (CTT) was employed to examine internal consistency and construct validity, while Item Response Theory (IRT) was applied to assess item-level performance and differential functioning across subgroups. The integration of these approaches provides a comprehensive evaluation of the CV-PCQ’s measurement properties, thereby enhancing the interpretability and generalizability of the findings.

Methods

This cross-sectional study comprised two main phases: (1) translation and cross-cultural adaptation of the CV-PCQ, and (2) psychometric properties test of the CV-PCQ among nursing students in China.

Phase 1: translation and cross-cultural adaptation

In order to facilitate translation and cross-cultural adaptation, a multi-stage process was used based on translation, review, adjudication, pre-testing, and documentation (TRAPD) methodology [26]. In the translation stage, the PCQ was independently translated from English to Chinese by two translators based in China. Both translators are Chinese nationals who have lived in the UK for over 10 years and hold master’s degrees. One of the translators is a specialist in translation with over five years of professional experience, while the other is an English instructor at a nursing college with more than 30 years of experience in teaching the English language. Both translators have received training in questionnaire translation and have been thoroughly briefed on the specifics of this project. In the review stage, a team of two translation experts was formed along with nursing, education, statistics, public health, and pharmaceutical management specialists. This team conducted a comprehensive review to identify discrepancies between the original scale and the two draft translations, ultimately arriving at a preliminary version of the CV-PCQ. In the adjudication stage, an adjudicator who was familiar with the translation project, methodologies, and professional commitment, but not directly involved in the translation process, collaborated with a nursing expert to make the final decision on the consensus version of the CV-PCQ. A pre-test of the consensus version of the CV-PCQ was conducted through cognitive interviews with ten nursing students to evaluate item clarity and comprehensibility, as well as to assess the feasibility of the survey process and data collection procedures. The first author conducted the interviews, while all authors collaborated to enhance the clarity of the final version. Documentation is maintained throughout the entire TRAPD process, including draft translations, notes exchanged among translators, reviewers, and adjudicators, pre-testing results, and feedback received from translators (Fig. 1).

Phase 2: psychometric properties test

Settings and participants

A convenience sampling method was used in the current study. In mainland China, there are a total of 420 medical undergraduate institutions, with 280 located in the eastern, central, and southern regions, representing 67% of the total [27]. Due to the constraints of time and budget, it was not feasible to randomly select provinces for sampling. Therefore, Shandong Province in the east, Hunan Province in the central region, and Guangdong Province in the south were chosen as sampling sites because these provinces have the highest concentration of medical undergraduate institutions within their respective regions. This selection allowed us to access the largest number of eligible students within the limited project timeline. Furthermore, considering the distinct cultural contexts of the two Special Administrative Regions (Hong Kong and Macao), Macao was also included as a sample source for this study.

Participants were included based on the following criteria: (1) they must be enrolled as nursing students in any Chinese higher-education institution (including international students), (2) at least 18 years of age, and (3) willing to volunteer for the study. Those unable to read and write in Chinese were excluded from participation. It is generally recommended to use a minimum sample size of 200 participants when conducting psychometric studies based on CTT or IRT [28]. Consequently, this study aimed to achieve a targeted sample size of 475 participants.

Measures

A socio-demographic questionnaire was used to collect information on participants’ gender, age, and current educational pursuit. The PCQ was adapted based on the scale proposed by Lachman and Aranya’ [29]. However, a dedicated publication reporting its original scale development and validation has not been identified in the extant literature. This instrument comprises four items, each assessed on a 7-point scale (1 = strongly disagree; 2 = moderately disagree; 3 = slightly disagree; 4 = uncertain; 5 = slightly agree; 6 = moderately agree; 7 = strongly agree). Prior applications of the PCQ demonstrates satisfactory reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.86) and supporting evidence of validity in nursing populations [30]. The PCQ was translated into Korean using the forward–backward translation method, and the Korean version demonstrated excellent reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.92) [31]. Notably, these studies represent applications and psychometric evaluations rather than original scale-development work. This cross-sectional study was conducted using the CV-PCQ, which was translated and adapted by our research team.

Procedures

Wenjuanxing (https://www.wjx.cn/), a prominent online questionnaire survey platform in China, was used to collect data from April to May 2025. Initially, the research team entered the informed consent form, demographic questionnaire, and professional commitment questionnaire into the Wenjuanxing platform. After verifying the accuracy of all information, the team published the survey and created promotional materials - a one-page digital flyer that included both the survey hyperlink and a QR code for quick access. These materials were then distributed to collaborators across various regions, who were tasked with recruiting eligible participants within their respective areas. Additionally, the research team shared the promotional materials on popular multimedia platforms, such as WeChat, Facebook, and Instagram, to further enhance participant recruitment. To ensure the integrity and accuracy of data collection, all participants were required to fully complete the questionnaire before submission, and each IP address was restricted to a single entry. After the data collection period ended, the research team exported the data from the platform and conducted a thorough review to enhance its accuracy. Questionnaires with a response time of less than 50 s or exhibiting patterned responses were excluded.

Statistical analysis

For the analysis, jamovi 2.3.28 (https://www.jamovi.org/) was employed. A descriptive approach was applied to analyze demographic characteristics. The threshold for statistical significance was established at p < 0.05.

CTT analysis

The validity analysis encompassed both content and construct validity. To assess the content validity of the CV-PCQ, the Content Validity Index (CVI) was utilized, which includes both the scale-level CVI (S-CVI) and the item-level CVI (I-CVI). Ten experts from the fields of nursing, psychology, education, and public health were invited to evaluate the relevance of the questionnaire items to facilitate the calculation of the CVI. Experts rated each item’s relevance, simplicity, and clarity using a 4-point Likert scale (1 = not relevant, 2 = somewhat relevant, 3 = quite relevant, and 4 = highly relevant). The I-CVI was computed by dividing the number of experts who rated an item as either 3 or 4 by the total number of experts; a threshold of greater than 0.7 was deemed acceptable for individual items [32]. Conversely, the S-CVI was determined by summing the I-CVIs and dividing this total by the number of items; a score higher than 0.8 was considered indicative of acceptable content validity [32]. To evaluate the construct validity of the CV-PCQ, an initial Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) was performed using principal axis factoring without rotation, followed by Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) employing the maximum likelihood estimation method. Data suitability for factor analysis was confirmed using the Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) measure and Bartlett’s test of sphericity (KMO = 0.786; Bartlett’s test: χ² = 644, p < 0.001). Factors with eigenvalues greater than 1 were retained. Before running the CFA, multivariate normality was checked via kurtosis and skewness coefficients, and multivariate outliers were identified using Mahalanobis distance (D²). According to established guidelines, the Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR) should ideally be less than 0.08 [33]. Additionally, the comparative fit index (CFI), and Tucker–Lewis index (TLI) should exceed 0.9 to indicate a satisfactory model fit [33]. To assess convergent validity, the average variance extraction (AVE) was calculated, with AVE values over 0.50 considered acceptable [33]. The 475 valid questionnaires were randomly split into two subsamples: 236 for EFA and 239 for CFA.

The CV-PCQ’s reliability was assessed through internal consistency and test-retest reliability analysis. Internal consistency was evaluated using Cronbach’s α coefficient, with a value greater than 0.70 indicating satisfactory consistency [34]. To evaluate test-retest reliability, 30 nursing students were instructed to complete the questionnaire a second time after a two-week interval. Test–retest reliability was evaluated using the intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC [1, 2], absolute agreement), with values of 0.40–0.75 indicating fair to good reliability and values > 0.75 indicating excellent reliability [32, 34].

IRT analysis

The Rating Scale Model (RSM), a polytomous Rasch model for ordered response categories, was applied to analyze the 7-point Likert scale data. Unlike the Partial Credit Model, the RSM assumes a common threshold structure across items, making it suitable when response categories are interpreted consistently [35]. Thresholds were ordered and invariant across items (Table S1), supporting RSM assumptions and obviating the need for category collapsing. Unidimensionality and local independence were evaluated to meet Rasch model assumptions. Unidimensionality was supported by EFA (explained variance ≥ 20% on the first factor) [36], and local independence by Q3 statistics (< 0.36) [37]. For evaluating the items’ fit to the Rasch model, the infit mean-square (MNSQ) and outfit MNSQ were computed. These values are expected to be between 0.6 and 1.4 [38]. Both separation reliability and separation indices (item and person separation) were examined. A separation reliability greater than 0.70 indicated acceptable reliability, whereas a separation index greater than 2.0 reflected adequate discriminatory capacity [38]. A Wright map was used to visualize the alignment between person ability and item difficulty. Optimal targeting is indicated when the person mean is within ± 1.0 logit of the item mean [39]. Furthermore, differential item functioning (DIF) was tested to determine measurement invariance between genders, which is typically expressed as p values less than 0.05 [40].

Ethical considerations

The original author granted permission to translate the PCQ. Ethical approval was received for this study from the Research Management and Development Department of a College of Macau (No. REC-2023.0702). The study adhered to the principles outlined in the Helsinki Declaration. Data collected were used exclusively for research purposes. Before participating in the survey, participants were required to read the informed consent form and click the “Agree” button. Participants retained the right to withdraw from the study at any time without facing any negative consequences.

Results

Phase 1: translation and cross-cultural adaptation

The review team compared and discussed the initial translations. A notable point of contention arose concerning the translation of the term “profession,” which appeared in all items within the scale. Opinions diverged, with some team members advocating for “专业” and others for “职业”. Following thorough deliberation, the team reached a consensus to adopt “专业” as the translation. This decision was grounded in the understanding that “专业” typically denotes a field requiring specialized training or particular skills, often associated with a high level of education. Conversely, “职业” does not necessarily imply such educational rigor. Considering that the nursing profession in China mandates a full-time, at least three-year nursing or midwifery program, along with more than eight months of clinical practice, the term “专业” was deemed a more accurate and fitting descriptor of the nursing profession in this context. The pre-test results indicated that all items were comprehended effectively by the participants, and no items necessitated additional refinement. CTT analysis

The ICC [1, 2] was 0.782 (95%CI: 0.592–0.890), indicating excellent test-retest reliability. The internal consistency of the scale was also found to be satisfactory, as evidenced by an overall Cronbach’s alpha of 0.878. The content validity was strong, with both the I-CVI and S-CVI achieving perfect scores of 1, indicating that our expert panel considered all items highly relevant and comprehensive. The EFA identified a single‑factor structure consistent with the original scale, with an eigenvalue of 2.80 accounting for 70.0% of the total variance (Table 2). Convergent validity was confirmed with an AVE of 0.792, showing significant variance shared between the construct and its items. The CFA results supported the scale’s structural validity, with a CFI of 0.937 and a TLI of 0.910, both indicating excellent fit (Table 3).

IRT analysis

The unidimensionality of the CV‑PCQ was supported by EFA, with the first factor accounting for 70.0% of the total variance. Moreover, local independence was demonstrated (Q3 = 0.126). These results indicate that the data meet the assumptions of unidimensionality and local independence, thereby supporting the appropriateness of applying the Rasch model to these items. The infit MNSQ and outfit MNSQ values were within the acceptable range (infit MNSQ range: 0.574–1.073; outfit MNSQ range: 0.552–1.077), indicating that the items fit the model well. Furthermore, the DIF analysis showed no significant gender-based differences among items, ensuring the scale’s unbiased measurement across genders (Table 4). Discussion

This study validated the CV-PCQ using both CTT and IRT. The findings provided evidence of reliability, content validity, structural validity, convergent validity, item fit, and absence of gender-based DIF. The IRT analysis additionally confirmed the absence of significant gender-based differences among the items, thereby ensuring the scale’s equitable measurement across both genders.

There are several Chinese-language tools designed to measure professional commitment among nursing students. For instance, the Professional Attitude Scale for Nurses, which consists of 38 items and covers eight dimensions, has been translated and validated in Chinese [41]. Additionally, Chinese scholars have developed a 23-item, three-component Professional Commitment Scale [42]. These tools offer comprehensive and multidimensional assessments. In contrast, the four-item CV-PCQ provides a concise, unidimensional measure that can be completed in under one minute. Its single global score is suitable for large‑scale surveys, longitudinal monitoring, and multivariate analyses. Although its brevity limits the ability to capture detailed subdimensions of professional commitment, it provides practical advantages such as reduced respondent burden, improved completion rates, and lower administrative demands [43]. These features make the CV‑PCQ particularly useful for educators conducting rapid cohort assessments and for researchers requiring a brief yet reliable indicator of professional commitment. Thus, the CV‑PCQ serves as a complementary tool to longer Chinese instruments by providing an ultra‑short, psychometrically validated measure specifically designed for nursing students.

The translation process was a critical step in ensuring the instrument’s cultural and linguistic appropriateness for Chinese-speaking populations. A key challenge in this process was the translation of the term “profession,” which appeared in all items of the scale. Two primary translations were considered: “专业” and “职业”. The former is typically associated with a specialized field that requires specific training or skills and is often linked to a higher level of education. In contrast, “职业” generally refers to a job or occupation without necessarily implying specialized education or training. Given the educational requirements and clinical practice associated with the nursing profession in China, which includes a full-time, at least three-year nursing or midwifery program and over eight months of clinical practice [44], the term “专业” was ultimately chosen. This decision was based on the need for accuracy and cultural relevance [45]. The term “专业” more accurately reflects the rigorous educational and practical components of the nursing profession, aligning with the expectations and standards within the Chinese healthcare context. By adopting “专业,” the translated scale maintains the integrity of the original scale’s intent to measure professional commitment within a context that values specialized education and training. The choice of “专业” over “职业” underscores the importance of nuanced translation in cross-cultural research. It highlights the need to consider not only linguistic equivalence but also the cultural and educational connotations that terms carry within a specific societal framework [46–48]. This careful translation process is crucial for ensuring that the PCQ accurately captures the construct of professional commitment among Chinese nursing students.

The findings of this study revealed that Item 4, “I really care about the fate of the nursing profession,” was the most endorsed, indicating a significant number of respondents feel a strong connection to the welfare and future of their profession. In China, nursing students’ concern for the future of the nursing profession and their personal development is indeed a critical issue. Many studies show that nursing students are not only interested in employment prospects [49] and salary but also in public perception of the nursing profession [50]. This suggests that when choosing their major, nursing students consider not only job opportunities but also the desire for future social recognition and respect [51, 52]. Conversely, Item 2, “For me, nursing is the best of all professions,” was the least endorsed. This could be interpreted in several ways. One possibility is that the social status of the nursing profession in China is relatively low, often perceived as more suitable for students with poorer academic performance, leading to some students’ negative views of the profession [53]. Alternatively, most students reported choosing nursing due to family or relative recommendations, with only a minority citing interest in the profession itself [54]. This indicates that the attractiveness of the nursing profession is largely influenced by external factors.

The DIF analysis conducted in this study is a critical component in establishing the fairness and validity of the measurement instrument across different demographic groups [55]. The findings indicated that there was no significant gender-based differences among the items on the scale. The absence of DIF based on gender means that the items on the scale perform equivalently for both male and female respondents. This equality in item functioning is crucial because it suggests that the scale does not systematically advantage or disadvantage respondents based on their gender, thereby maintaining the integrity of the measurement [56]. Such bias could otherwise lead to misleading interpretations of the data, particularly in studies where gender differences are of interest or where gender is a confounding variable [57]. This lack of gender-based DIF is particularly noteworthy given the historical context in which certain professions, including nursing, have been traditionally dominated by one gender. The nursing profession, has been predominantly female [58], and concerns about gender bias in assessment tools could potentially influence perceptions of professional commitment. By demonstrating that the scale is free from gender bias, this study reinforces the credibility of the instrument as a fair and objective measure of professional commitment within the nursing field.

Limitation

This study acknowledges several limitations. First, although this study integrated CTT and IRT to evaluate the psychometric properties of the CV-PCQ, some aspects of validity require further investigation. While the AVE findings provided support for convergent validity within the CFA measurement framework, this study did not assess convergent validity through associations with external constructs or criterion-related measures. Future research should examine the scale’s relationship with external criteria to further establish its convergent and discriminant validity. Second, although no significant DIF was found based on gender, the analysis was limited to this demographic variable. Future studies could explore DIF across other variables, such as age, education level, to further assess the scale’s fairness and bias. Third, although the study sample was generally representative of the target population of nursing students, it may not have fully captured the diversity of the broader Chinese-speaking communities, as the sample predominantly comprised individuals from specific regions. Moreover, the use of online convenience sampling may have introduced self-selection bias, as participants who voluntarily completed the survey could differ systematically from those who did not participate. These regional constraints and potential self-selection bias may have affected the representativeness of the sample and, consequently, limited the generalizability of the study findings to other settings or demographic groups. Finally, social desirability bias which may have influenced participants’ responses, potentially affecting the accuracy of self-reported data and the validity of the CV-PCQ’s psychometric properties.

Conclusion

This study translated and culturally tailored the PCQ into Chinese, providing test-retest reliability, internal consistency, content validity, convergent validity, and structural validity with nursing students. The four-item scale offers a brief yet reliable means of assessing professional commitment and provides threefold utility: educators can quickly identify students with low commitment and tailor mentorship or remediation program; researchers gain an instrument with established validity and reliability evidence for exploring antecedents and outcomes of commitment in cross-sectional and longitudinal designs; administrators can track cohort trends, allocate resources efficiently, and evaluate the impact of curricular or policy changes.

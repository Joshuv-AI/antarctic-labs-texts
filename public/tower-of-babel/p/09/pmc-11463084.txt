ChatGPT-4 and wearable device assisted Intelligent Exercise Therapy for co-existing Sarcopenia and Osteoarthritis (GAISO): a feasibility study and design for a randomized controlled PROBE non-inferiority trial
Authors: Mingke You, Xi Chen, Di Liu, Ye Lin, Gang Chen, Jian Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSarcopenia and osteoarthritis are prevalent age-related diseases that mutually exacerbate each other, creating a vicious cycle that worsens both conditions. Exercise is key to breaking this detrimental cycle. Facing increasing demand for rehabilitation services within this patient demographic, ChatGPT-4 and wearable device may increase the availability, efficiency and personalization of such health care.AimTo evaluate the clinical efficacy and cost-effectiveness of a rehabilitation system implemented on mobile platforms, utilizing the integration of ChatGPT-4 and wearable devices.MethodsThe study design is a prospective randomized open blinded end-point (PROBE) non-inferiority trial. 278 patients diagnosed with osteoarthritis and sarcopenia will be recruited and randomly assigned to the intervention group and the control group. In the intervention group patients receive mobile phone-based rehabilitation service where ChatGPT-4 generates personalized exercise therapy, and wearable device guides and monitor the patient to implement the exercise therapy. Traditional clinic based face-to-face exercise therapy will be prescribed and implemented in the control group. All patients will receive three-months exercise therapies following the frequency, intensity, type, time, volume and progression (FITT-VP) principle. The patients will be assessed at baseline, one month, three months, and six months after initiation. Outcome measures will include ROM, gait patterns, Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) for pain assessment, Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC), Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS) for functional assessment, Short-Form Health Survey 12 (SF-12) for quality of life, Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID), Patient Acceptable Symptom State (PASS), and Substantial Clinical Benefit (SCB) for clinically significant measures.DiscussionA rehabilitation system combining the capabilities of ChatGPT-4 and wearable devices potentially enhance the availability and efficiency of professional rehabilitation services, thus enhancing the therapeutic outcomes for a substantial population concurrently afflicted with sarcopenia and osteoarthritis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13018-024-05134-8.Keywords: Osteoarthritis, Sarcopenia, ChatGPT-4, Artificial intelligence, Wearable device, Rehabilitation

Background

Sarcopenia is a progressive and generalized skeletal muscle disorder involving the accelerated loss of muscle mass and function, typically associated with aging [1]. Knee osteoarthritis is a degenerative joint disease characterized by symptoms such as chronic pain, stiffness, swelling, and reduced mobility [2]. The prevalence of sarcopenia is reported to be between 5% and 13% in adults aged 60–70 years, and between 11% and 50% in those aged 80 years or older [3], while the prevalence of knee osteoarthritis is about 10% in men and 13% in women aged 60 years and above [4]. It is estimated that about 9.1% of elderly adults are diagnosed with both conditions [5].

Sarcopenia and knee osteoarthritis interact to create a vicious cycle that exacerbates both conditions [6]. The loss of muscle mass reduces its protective effect on joints, increasing the risk of osteoarthritis, which in turn decreases joint mobility and limits physical activity [7]. This decrease in physical activity, in turn, contributes to further muscle loss due to insufficient exercise, thereby further driving the cycle of sarcopenia and osteoarthritis [8]. The coexistence of these conditions places a significant burden on health and society by impairing mobility and independence, increasing the risk of falls and injuries, and elevating healthcare costs [9, 10]. Furthermore, this co-morbidity exacerbates the age-related decline in quality of life and contributes to increased mortality rates [5].

Exercise therapy may disrupt the vicious cycle between sarcopenia and knee osteoarthritis by strengthening lower limb and core muscle training and improving joint mobility, thus preventing further muscle loss and joint degeneration. While previous studies have confirmed the efficacy of rehabilitative exercise for knee osteoarthritis and sarcopenia respectively, research into its therapeutic effectiveness for the co-existence of sarcopenia and knee osteoarthritis remains limited [11, 12]. Moreover, the existed exercise therapy faces challenges in achieving personalization and implementing remote, home-based supervision and feedback [13].

Artificial intelligence (AI) and wearable devices hold the potential to address these challenges [14]. Generative AI models like ChatGPT have demonstrated significant potential in processing extensive patient data and delivering tailored interventions. These models efficiently analyze complex medical information, enabling more personalized healthcare solutions [15]. As for existing literature investigates the suitability of using ChatGPT-4 for prescribing personalized exercise programs, research has found that it provides positive feedback in areas such as generating patient education and supervised prescription training under therapist oversight [16]. In our initial study involving 30 virtual patients with both knee osteoarthritis and sarcopenia, we developed personalized exercise therapies that enhance the efficiency and accessibility of rehabilitation services. Wearable devices can ensure that patients receive guidance and real-time feedback on their movements while performing exercise rehabilitation at home [17].

Remote rehabilitation has been substantiated as an effective and resource-saving approach to rehabilitation [18]. Integrating AI with wearable devices enhances remote rehabilitation by automating plan formulation, providing real-time monitoring and feedback, and adjusting schedules adaptively. This increase in efficiency and accessibility benefits those with limited access to conventional rehabilitation services.

Study aim

The primary objective of this trial is to assess the clinical outcome of an online rehabilitation system based on ChatGPT-4 and wearable devices relative to traditional clinic-based rehabilitation in a non-inferiority design. A secondary objective is to examine whether the employment of new technology facilitate more efficient and cost-effectiveness rehabilitation service.

Methods/design

Study design, recruitment

The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of our hospital and the ethical committee (approved number: 2023 (191)). A prospective randomized open, blinded end-point (PROBE) non-inferiority trial will be conducted according to Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) checklist [19]. Informed consent will be given to all participants, who will be randomized into either the intervention arm or the control arm. The interventional group will receive ChatGPT-4 generated personalized rehabilitation program, with wearable devices assisted, and the control group will receive standard rehabilitation program.

All participants will be recruited after their outpatient service. Researchers specialized in sports medicine will screen and recruit participants who meet the eligibility criteria. Baseline information will be assessed and recorded after informed consent is given.

Eligibility criteria

Participants will be eligible if they meet the following criteria: (1) Patients who are willing to provide informed consent to participate in the research and complete follow up; (2) 60 years or older and been diagnosed with knee osteoarthritis [20] and sarcopenia [21];3. community-dwellings and have access to mobile phones and Internet; 4.ability to use the mobile device and wearable device under instruction.

Participants will be ineligible if they meet the following criteria: (1) Concurrent participation in other rehabilitation trials; (2) Inability to continue the therapy (e.g., due to cognitive impairment with Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MOCA) score < 21 [22]; degenerative neurological diseases; depression or other psycho disorders; chronic disease that affect their health condition and participation ability); (3) Have surgical history or upcoming surgery on the affected side; (4) Combination of other diseases that influence training or gait patterns, such as knee fracture, knee infection, dizziness or stroke.

Randomization and allocation

Randomization and allocation will be performed by a researcher independently. Patients will be randomized to the intervention group and the control group at 1:1 ratio. Random integrate numbers will be generated using SPSS 23.0 (IBM, New York, NY, USA). An even number indicates the intervention group and an odd number indicates the control group. A total of 278 random integrate numbers will be generated and concealed in sealed envelope. The envelopes will be stored by a third-party researcher independently.

Allocation will be performed by opening the sealed envelope sequentially. The content of the envelope determines the allocation of the patient. Allocation information will be recorded by the third-party researcher. The rehabilitation physician will be informed of the patients’ allocation information after recruitment of the patient.

Blinding

Patients’ outcome measurements will be performed by two clinicians. They are unaware of patients’ allocation information and will not participate other aspects of this study. The patients will be asked not to reveal the intervention received during follow-up visit. The statistician will perform data analysis with the patients’ grouping concealed. Demographic information will be recorded during initial clinical visit prior to treatment allocation.

Baseline data collection

Participants will be requested by the research team to provide essential details and complete an assessment utilizing a demographic form prior to treatment allocation. This encompasses queries related to personal data, such as age, participants medical history, and measurements of participants height and weight, along with a thorough exploration based on the International Classification of Functioning, Disability, and Health (ICF) model. An example of baseline patient information form is presented in supplementary file 1.

Intervention

All patients will undergo the same pre-treatment assessment and receive three months of exercise training. Patients will be randomly assigned to the intervention group or the control group. In the intervention group, patients will receive personalized exercise therapy generated by ChatGPT-4 and carry out the exercise training at home using wearable devices. In the control group, patients will receive traditional standard physical rehabilitation training, with exercise training programs designed by rehabilitation therapists. A flow chart of the intervention is presented in Fig. 1. All exercise training targets are set according to the Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound (SMART) principle [23], and the therapies are set according to the frequency, intensity, type, time, volume and progression (FITT-VP) principle [24].

Please find the relevant patient information for this personalized exercise therapy presented below……”. All generations will be recorded. Quality assessment of ChatGPT-4 generated exercise therapies

The 30 exercise therapies generated by ChatGPT-4 have been evaluated by two experience musculoskeletal physical therapists according to the Quality of Exercise Therapy (QET) criteria, which evaluates eight distinct domains: evidence-based practices, comprehensive approach, holistic application of exercise, individualization, progression, accessibility and feasibility, safety, and an overall evaluation (Supplementary file 1). Each domain is assigned a score ranging from 0, indicating no adherence to evidence-based practices or guidelines, to 5, signifying comprehensive alignment with evidence-based practices and guidelines. The initial assessment suggests that the exercise therapies developed are evidence-based, provide comprehensive progress updates, and are highly practical, accessible, and safe. However, the approach’s comprehensiveness, holistic nature, and individualization are reported to be of slightly lower quality. Wearable device assistance

Participants will be equipped with Bluetooth-enabled Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) sensors (Healbone, Beijing China, 2021/2022), secured using straps, and provided with comprehensive instructions to facilitate independent use throughout the intervention programs. The wearable device is used in conjunction with a mobile app. During usage, patients wear the wearable device and connect it to their phone under the guidance of the mobile app. The mobile app displays the patient’s complete training and plays animations to guide the patient’s movements. Patients, wearing the wearable device, train under the guidance of the animation. The wearable device can assess in real time whether the patient’s range of motion is standard and provide vibrational feedback. (Figures 3 and 4).

The scale of progression or regression within the exercise regimen will be implemented, generally varying within the range of 5–10% with respect to intensity or time. Patient-reported outcome measures (PROM)

Patient-reported outcome measures use scales as follows: The Visual Analog Scale (VAS) rates pain intensity from 0 to 10, with 0 representing no pain and 10 denoting the most severe pain imaginable [26]. The Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) assesses osteoarthritis treatment effects by dividing its 24 items into three categories: pain, stiffness, and function [27]. The Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score (KOOS) caters to younger patients with knee conditions and covers five areas, each scored from 0 (no problem) to 4 (extreme problem), which are then summed and scaled to a 0-100 scale [28, 29]. The Short-Form Health Survey 12 (SF-12) evaluates various health-related aspects, with each item scored from 0 (extreme problem) to 100 (no problem) and then linearly transformed to a comprehensive scale [30].

Clinical significance

Clinical significance measures incorporated in this trial include the Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID), Substantial Clinical Benefit (SCB), and Patient Acceptable Symptom State (PASS). These measures serve to represent participants’ post-treatment satisfaction. Specifically, the MCID is utilized to denote the smallest discernible clinical improvement following treatment [31], the SCB signifies participants’ self-reported substantial improvement [32], and the PASS encapsulates patient satisfaction, as indicated by the required outcome scores post-treatment [33]. For the purposes of this trial, the MCID is selected for the WOMAC score to identify the smallest meaningful changes [34].

Muscle mass measurement

All participants are subjected to a bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) (Inbody, Shanghai, China, 2018) during each assessment. Each participant is required to wear attire devoid of any metallic attachments while scanning and measuring muscle mass, including upper limbs, lower limbs, and the trunk [35]. Based on the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS) 2019 criteria, the thresholds of BIA results for diagnosing sarcopenia are set at < 7.0 kg/m2 for men and < 5.7 kg/m2 for women [21].

Relative hospitalization time and cost assessment

The relative hospitalization time and cost encompasses elements such as participant hospitalization, outpatient visits during follow-ups, medication usage, and other health services [36, 37]. All elements will be directly retrieved and recorded by each patient’s corresponding therapist from the hospital’s HIS (Hospital Information System). The purpose of this assessment is to ascertain whether the intervention proves time- and cost-effective in comparison to the control group.

Adverse events

Adverse events are monitored and documented throughout the duration of the treatment phase. Kumar et al. discussed potential risks and adverse events for sarcopenic older adults, such as muscle strains and cardiovascular stress [38]. Also, Thomas et al. highlighted the risks of adverse events such as falls and fractures during interventions [39]. Such adverse events are characterized as conditions of significant severity, encompassing substantial disability, life-threatening circumstances, and mortality. Therapists will critically evaluate all reported adverse events, gauging their severity and the congruity of the intervention applied. Based on these evaluations, suitable assessments will be carried out and necessary adjustments to the intervention strategy will be executed.

Data analysis

Statistical analyses are performed using SPSS version 25 (IBM, New York, NY, USA) and GraphPad Prism version 8 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Continuous variables will be recorded as mean and standard deviation. Discontinuous variables will be recorded as frequency and rate. For continuous variables, the Shapiro–Wilk test will be performed to test whether the variables of each group followed a normal distribution. If the variables follow a normal distribution, independent t test will be used to compare continuous variables among the different groups. Wilcoxon rank-sum test will be used if the variables don’t follow normal distribution. Chi-square test will be employed to test for difference in discontinuous variables. Analysis of Covariance (ANCOVA) will be used to adjust for difference in baseline PROM scores and to provide an unbiased estimate of differences of post-treatment PROM scores between the two groups.

Incidence of falls will be documented and compared between groups using the incidence rate ratio. A multivariate analysis will be used to assess the effect of treatment in intervention and control group on combined changes in all measures and related costs, and determine the most affected measure affected by treatment. A sensitivity analysis will be executed to account for missing data, with a significance level of 0.05.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated based on previous literature, which aimed to discuss the required sample size for future studies investigating rehabilitation intervention for patients with knee osteoarthritis. The study recorded the change in WOMAC score and analyzed the minimal clinically important difference (MCID) and the smallest detectable difference (SDD), examined their relation to required sample sizes for future studies [40].

Based on these findings, our study’s sample size was determined using key parameters from the WOMAC score. The standard deviation (SD) for the WOMAC Pain section was noted to be 2.25. Given that effects constituting 12% of the baseline score (corresponding to 6% of the WOMAC’s maximal score of 10) were identified as representing a Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID), a non-inferiority margin (Δ) of 0.6 points was derived. Using these values, alongside a test power of 0.80 and a significance level of 0.05, the required sample size was found to be 111 participants per trial arm, summing to 222 for the study. To account for potential attrition, with an estimated dropout rate of 20%, the recommended total enrollment would be 278 patients.

Discussion

The prevalence of sarcopenia and knee osteoarthritis is increasing [41]. The therapeutic role of exercise rehabilitation in managing sarcopenia and knee osteoarthritis is supported by evidence, leading to a significant rise in demand for interventions that address the co-occurrence of these conditions [42, 43]. The objective of this trial is to assess the clinical outcome of an AI-generated, wearable device-based rehabilitation program relative to traditional clinic-based rehabilitation in a PROBE non-inferiority design. A secondary objective is to examine whether the employment of new technology facilitate more efficient and cost-effectiveness rehabilitation service. Our hypothesis is that the AI-generated, wearable device would offer therapeutic effects equivalent to those of traditional rehabilitation training while significantly improving time- and cost-effectiveness of such services.

Osteoarthritis and sarcopenia, common contributors to musculoskeletal disability in the aging population, are typically marked by symptoms including pain, diminished muscle strength, and restricted physical functions [11, 44]. This study protocol represents the first attempt at delivering a comprehensive exercise therapy specifically tailored for individuals, incorporating rigorous evaluations and strategic plan adjustments.

This protocol carries substantial potential to improve the availability of rehabilitation services, particularly for regions and populations that have difficulty accessing them. The use of ChatGPT-4 and wearable technology also allows for the exploration of more personalized and efficient rehabilitation services, presenting a promising direction for the future of geriatric care.

The main limitation of this study is the inherent difficulty of blinding both participants and therapists. In an attempt to attenuate this potential bias, a blinded endpoint design is employed. The PROBE combines the benefits of randomized clinical trials with real-world settings (open design), while maintaining objectivity in outcome assessment through blinded end-point evaluation.

Privacy and safety management

All user data, such as names, telephone numbers, addresses, and patients’ ID number, undergoes anonymization prior to being processed by ChatGPT-4. Any personally identifiable information is securely stored in a separate database, and access is strictly limited to the researchers responsible for generating the exercise therapies. Moreover, any medical advice proffered by ChatGPT-4 must undergo a manual review before it is provided to patients.

Perspective of the study

This is the first randomized controlled trial analyzing both the feasibility and efficiency of a rehabilitation approach assisted by ChatGPT-4 and wearable technology for individuals with osteoarthritis and sarcopenia. This technology holds substantial potential for enhancing the accessibility of professional rehabilitation services, particularly in many regions of the world with a shortage of professional rehabilitation personnel. The use of ChatGPT-4 and wearable technology also allows for the exploration of more personalized and efficient rehabilitation services. The adoption of this system may enhance the therapeutic outcomes for a substantial population concurrently afflicted with sarcopenia and osteoarthritis.

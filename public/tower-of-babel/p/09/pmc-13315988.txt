Impact of COVID 19 pandemic on young patients with acute coronary syndrome in Eastern Indian population
Authors: Dipankar Ghosh Dastidar, Saikat Sau, Tanmay Mukhopadhyay, Sougat Chakraborty, Nandita Ghosh Dastidar, Md Miraj Mondal, Ujjal Yadav, Saroj Soni, Manikanta Maji, Hrishikesh Chakraborty, Kaushik Mondal, Yashpal Yadav, Shubhashis Mahato, Mantu Ghosh, Satyaki Dutta, Subhajit Das, Sudip Ghosh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSARS-COV-2 virus affects the cardiovascular system by various pathophysiological mechanisms. The incidence of Acute coronary syndrome (ACS) seems to be on rise in the peri-covid era. The proinflammatory and prothrombotic milieu is primarily responsible. Traditional atherosclerotic risk factors do not seem to be responsible for this increased incidence of ACS. We chose to test this hypothesis on young Indian population lacking the traditional atherosclerotic risk factors. If established, Covid 19 infection will at least be considered a risk modifier in causing ACS in future. Subsequently, we went on to study the differences in presentation, diagnostic implications, revascularization and management strategies and finally outcomes in ACS patients in the Peri-covid compared to the pre-covid era. To the best of our knowledge, this seems to be the first of its kind study from eastern India.Keywords: Covid 19, Acute coronary syndrome (ACS), Revascularization strategy

Introduction

The severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2, or more commonly, SARS-CoV-2 virus commonly affects the respiratory tract causing flu like symptoms. Few cases progress to develop pneumonia and multi-inflammatory syndrome affecting multiple organ systems. Cardiovascular system is affected in 8 – 25% of cases with higher proportion in those who die.1 Meta-analysis of eight studies from China involving 46,248 Covid 19 patients showed higher cardiovascular affection in hypertensive and diabetic patients.2 A myriad of cardiovascular involvement in form of myocarditis, acute coronary syndrome, heart failure, thromboembolic complications and arrhythmias has been observed.3 Case fatality rate was higher in those with cardiovascular affection.4

SARS-COV-2 virus is notorious for causing a proinflammatory and prothrombotic state which mainly accounts for the increased prevalence of acute coronary syndrome in this condition.5 There is an excessive host inflammatory response to SARS-CoV-2 infection with multiple inflammatory mediators, namely cytokines and complement activation products, coming into play. There is aberrant expression of tissue factor by activated alveolar epithelial cells, monocytes, macrophages and neutrophils, and production of other prothrombotic factors by activated endothelial cells and platelets. Simultaneously there is reduced expression of physiological anticoagulants by dysfunctional endothelial cells and suppression of fibrinolysis by the endothelial overproduction of plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 and thrombin-mediated activation of thrombin-activatable fibrinolysis inhibitor.5,6 Many have highlighted the role of inflammation and IL-6 in cardiac injury.7 Direct viral invasion of cardiomyocytes with consequent unopposed effects of angiotensin II, increased metabolic demand, immune activation, or microvascular dysfunction with thromboembolic events in both the venous and arterial systems have been reported.

We sought to test the hypothesis of an increased incidence of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) during the peri-COVID era, particularly among the young indigenous population with few or no traditional atherosclerotic risk factors. This research aims to establish COVID-19 infection as a potential risk modifier for ACS. The study investigates the spectrum of myocardial infarction (MI) in the young population of eastern India during the pre-COVID and peri-COVID eras.

Methodology

This investigation employed a retrospective observational study design to evaluate the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on young patients presenting with acute coronary syndrome. The research was conducted at Burdwan Medical College and Hospital, a government-funded tertiary care center in Eastern India serving a large population base. As a referral center with 24/7 catheterization laboratory availability, the institution provided an optimal setting for examining ACS management patterns during both study periods. The center's comprehensive electronic medical record system facilitated reliable data extraction for analysis. The investigation spanned two distinct time frames: the pre-COVID era from 1st March 2019 to 29th February 2020, representing baseline ACS patterns prior to the pandemic, and the peri-COVID era from 1st March 2020 to 28th February 2021, encompassing the initial year of the pandemic in India. From the total ACS admissions during each period (n = 2500 screened in both eras), the study identified 55 eligible patients (2.2%) in the pre-COVID cohort and 115 patients (4.6%) in the peri-COVID cohort who met the age inclusion criteria.

Inclusion criteria [as per ACC/AHA guideline criteria]

We included patients with age ≤45 years at presentation with a confirmed diagnosis of ACS according to the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction with complete clinical, laboratory, and imaging available8.

Exclusion criteria

We excluded cases with non-ACS cardiac diagnoses (e.g., myocarditis, takotsubo cardiomyopathy, or stable angina), cases with incomplete medical records, patients transferred from other facilities after initial management to avoid confounding of treatment outcomes.

Data collection

A standardized data extraction protocol was implemented to collect information from electronic health records, catheterization laboratory reports, and discharge summaries. Collected variables included demographic characteristics, cardiovascular risk factors using the INTERHEART score9, clinical presentation features, diagnostic test results (ECG, troponin levels, echocardiography, coronary angiography), treatment modalities (medical therapy, thrombolysis, percutaneous intervention) and clinical outcomes (complications, mortality). COVID-19 status was determined using ICMR-approved rapid antigen tests and confirmatory RT-PCR when indicated.

Diagnostic criteria

All cases of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) were rigorously classified according to established ACC/AHA diagnostic criteria.

For ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), diagnosis required meeting the Fourth Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction (Type 1)8. This included: (1) characteristic symptoms of acute myocardial ischemia (chest discomfort, anginal equivalents like dyspnea or fatigue); (2) new ST-segment elevation at the J point ≥2 mm in men or ≥1.5 mm in women in leads V2-V3, or ≥1 mm in other contiguous leads, with or without reciprocal changes or pathological Q waves; (3) imaging evidence of new regional wall motion abnormalities; (4) angiographic identification of coronary thrombus; and (5) positive cardiac biomarkers.

Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) cases required ischemic symptoms plus either horizontal or downsloping ST depression ≥0.5 mm in ≥2 contiguous leads with biomarker positivity.

Unstable angina was diagnosed when patients presented with ischemic symptoms and ECG changes but negative biomarkers.

Biomarker assessment

Cardiac troponin T levels were measured using the point-of-care Roche Troponin T sensitive kit following standard protocols. Blood samples were collected at presentation and serially as clinically indicated. A positive biomarker result was defined as a troponin value exceeding the 99th percentile upper reference limit with appropriate rise and/or fall pattern consistent with acute myocardial injury.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis employed appropriate statistical methods for comparative research. Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies (%). A statistical value of p < 0.05 was considered significant.

Ethical consideration

The study has been approved by Ethical committee of Burdwan medical college [Memo No. BMC/IEC/502 Dated: 20.03.2025]

Only patients below 45 years of age were selected for the study. A significant increase in young ACS patients was observed during the peri-COVID era (2.2% to 4.6%, p < 0.01), with no notable differences in traditional risk factors. Systemic symptoms like dyspnea, fever, and shock were significantly higher during the peri-COVID period.

While ECG findings did not differ significantly, peri-COVID patients had worse echocardiographic parameters, including more global hypokinesia and reduced ejection fraction. The peri-COVID period demonstrated a notable increase in NSTEMI presentations, potentially indicative of a higher prevalence of non-occlusive, multivessel coronary involvement.

Coronary angiography (CAG) utilization significantly declined during the peri-COVID period, with deferrals primarily due to active COVID-19 infection, severe LV dysfunction, or patient refusal. Angiographically, pre-COVID STEMI patients predominantly had single-vessel disease, while peri-COVID cases showed a non-significant trend toward multivessel non-occlusive thrombus and left main involvement, suggesting greater disease severity. MINOCA (myocardial infarction with non-obstructive coronary arteries) was observed in a subset of peri-COVID patients.

All peri-COVID patients underwent COVID-19 screening using ICMR-approved rapid antigen detection (RAD) kits, with confirmatory RT-PCR testing when needed. CAG proceeded only after negative RAD results, except in six early pandemic cases where delayed RT-PCR positivity was identified post-procedure. Five COVID-positive patients had deferred CAG, reflecting adaptive ACS management protocols during the pandemic. COVID-19 testing was not performed in the pre-COVID patients and is therefore not applicable to that group.

The COVID-19 pandemic significantly altered ACS management, with PCI rates declining from 80.0% to 53.9% (p < 0.001) and thrombolysis increasing from 3.6% to 17.4% (p < 0.001). Pharmacotherapy shifted to more aggressive regimens, including ticagrelor-based DAPT and novel anticoagulants (rivaroxaban), with routine LMWH use during hospitalization. Two cases of stent-related giant aneurysms requiring CABG highlighted unique COVID-era complications. These adaptations reflected both pandemic constraints and recognition of COVID-associated hypercoagulability, demonstrating the need for tailored approaches during public health crises.

Discussion

The study highlights significant changes in the demographic, clinical, and management patterns of young ACS patients (<45 years) during the peri-COVID period compared to the pre-COVID era. A notable observation was the increase in young ACS cases (2.2% to 4.6%, p < 0.01), suggesting that the pandemic may have contributed to an increased incidence of coronary events in younger individuals. It is to note that only 9.6% of the peri-COVID group had confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection, suggesting that the changes were mostly caused by the pandemic environment's indirect impacts. While traditional cardiovascular risk factors remained unchanged, the heightened prevalence of ACS in this population could be attributed to pandemic-related stressors, delayed healthcare-seeking behaviour, or direct viral effects on thrombosis and endothelial dysfunction.

Despite the rise in young ACS cases, the INTERHEART risk score components—including smoking, diabetes, hypertension, and psychosocial stress—showed no significant differences between the two periods9. This suggests that the baseline cardiovascular risk profile of young patients remained stable, implying that the pandemic itself, rather than conventional risk factors, may have played a crucial role in triggering ACS.

These findings contrast with the report by Nwaedozie et al,10 which identified high-risk demographics as a significant predictor of adverse outcomes in STEMI patients during the COVID-19 era. However, their study encompassed a broader age range, whereas the study analysis specifically focused on patients under 45 years of age. Notably, their conclusion emphasized the greater burden of comorbidities in elderly subgroups—a population excluded from the present study cohort—which may explain the divergent observations regarding risk profiles and outcomes. These findings reinforce the idea that younger individuals may have been uniquely affected by pandemic-related factors such as stress, sedentary lifestyles, or undiagnosed COVID-19-related hypercoagulability.

Although chest pain remained universal, peri-COVID patients exhibited more severe presentations, including higher rates of dyspnea, fever, and cardiogenic shock. The presence of shock exclusively in the peri-COVID group (1.7%, p = 0.04) aligns with emerging evidence suggesting COVID-19-associated hypercoagulability and microvascular thrombosis. The trend toward more NSTEMI cases (42.6% vs. 32.7%, p = 0.14) and the echocardiographic findings—such as increased global hypokinesia (12.2% vs. 0%, p < 0.001) and severe LV dysfunction (EF <25% in 8.7% vs. 0%, p = 0.004)—further support the notion of a more diffuse and severe myocardial injury pattern during the pandemic.

Although chest pain remained universal, peri-COVID patients exhibited more severe presentations, including higher rates of dyspnea, fever, and cardiogenic shock. The presence of shock exclusively in the peri-COVID group (1.7%, p = 0.04) aligns with findings by Mariusz Wójcik et al11 which showed higher incidence of Cardiogenic shock in ACS patients during Covid times despite similar times from symptom onset to reperfusion. They have attributed increased incidence of cardiogenic shock to increased thrombogenicity. A Danish study by M.D. Lauridsen et al12 reported comparable incidence rates of cardiogenic shock in pericovid era as 0.81 (95% CI: 0.59–1.12) with precovid era 2015–2019 period as 0.87 (95% CI: 0.57–1.32). Underreporting or delays in treatment during Covid era did not increase incidence of cardiogenic shock patients. However, the study acknowledges Denmark as a low incidence COVID-19 country without any significant stress on the health care system.

The trend toward more NSTEMI cases and the echocardiographic findings—such as increased global hypokinesia and severe LV dysfunction—further support the notion of a more diffuse and severe myocardial injury pattern during the pandemic. A study by Celina M. Yong et al13 showed decreased incidence of NSTEMI patients in peri covid era. This was in elderly population and mainly due to underreporting in mild cases skewing higher incidences of STEMI during pandemic.

Coronary angiography utilization declined in the peri-COVID period (93% vs. 100%, p = 0.03), primarily due to COVID-19-related deferrals. This policy of deferred angiography was also reported by Zhi Teoh et al.14 They managed NSTEMI with concurrent COVID-19 infection with initial medical management followed by deferred angiography. MACE rates were similar to the non-deferred NSTEMI cohort without COVID-19. Moreover, angiographic findings suggested a trend toward more multivessel and left main involvement, though statistical significance was not reached. Luke Dancy et al15 reported a case of RCA thrombus in Covid 19 patient. Sotiria Liori et al16 reported a case of proximal LAD thrombotic occlusion in a 39y male patient without any atherosclerotic risk factors. Optical coherence tomography revealed intact endothelium after thromboaspiration. Kevin John et al17 published a pubmed search of MINOCA in covid 19 cases. The mean age was 61.5 years (higher than our study population) with equal sex distribution. Hypertension was the commonest comorbidity in that study.

The increased use of thrombolysis and reduced PCI rates reflect pandemic-driven adaptations in ACS management. Amitabh Poonia et al18 published a case series on different revascularization strategies for STEMI in Covid-19 patients. Contrary to various reports promoting thrombolytic therapy and subsequent medical management, they advocated primary-PCI as the standard of care for STEMI in covid-19 confirmed or probable patients at PCI capable hospitals. Similar findings were reported by Ibrahim aktürk et al.19

Lastly, the shift from clopidogrel-based DAPT to more potent P2Y12 inhibitors (ticagrelor) and adjunctive anticoagulation (rivaroxaban, LMWH) underscores the recognition of COVID-19-associated prothrombotic states. This aligns with global trends advocating intensified antithrombotic regimens in COVID-19-related ACS20.

Limitations

This single-center, retrospective observational study was conducted at a government-funded tertiary care facility, where patient admissions relied on referrals from primary and secondary care providers, potentially introducing selection bias. The study design may not fully capture the true population-level disease burden. However, potential confounders were minimized through an inclusive admission policy and uninterrupted 24/7 cathlab services during the pandemic period. Although the sample size limits definitive conclusions, the observed increase in ACS cases among young Indian patients—many lacking traditional atherosclerotic risk factors—during the peri-COVID era represents a significant trend that merits further investigation.

Conclusion

This study highlights a significant association of the COVID-19 pandemic on ACS among young individuals in Eastern India, revealing an increase in cases during the peri-COVID period with more severe systemic symptoms including dyspnea, fever, and shock, as well as greater myocardial dysfunction, as indicated by increased rates of global hypokinesia and reduced ejection fraction. Therapeutic strategies transitioned towards increased utilization of thrombolysis and advanced pharmacotherapy. These findings underscore the need for heightened awareness of pandemic-related cardiovascular risks, particularly in younger populations, and call for research into long-term outcomes and tailored management strategies during health crises.

Future directions

SARS-CoV-2 infection appears to modify ACS risk through multiple mechanisms, particularly in younger patients without traditional risk factors. Given the virus's prothrombotic and proinflammatory effects, affected patients should be evaluated for polyvascular involvement, multivessel coronary disease, and rapid plaque progression, potentially warranting more aggressive management with potent antiplatelets and anticoagulants. This emerging clinical scenario underscores the need for comprehensive vascular assessment and tailored antithrombotic strategies in post-COVID ACS presentations, though further research is needed to optimize management protocols.

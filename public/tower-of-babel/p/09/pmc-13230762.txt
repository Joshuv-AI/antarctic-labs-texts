A Fermatean fuzzy CRITIC-MAIRCA fusion model for automated vocal performance evaluation in a.i. assisted decision support systems
Authors: Bing Zhao, Sile Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Automated vocal performance assessment requires the integration of objective acoustic analysis and subjective expert opinions under uncertainty. Nevertheless, all current AI-based scoring methods ignore the issue of expert hesitation, whereas traditional Multi-Criteria Decision-Making (MCDM) models cannot simultaneously solve the problem of objective weighting and interpretable ranking. This paper will present a Fermatean Fuzzy (FF) Criteria Importance Through Intercriteria Correlation (CRITIC) Multi-Attributive Ideal-Real Comparative Analysis (MAIRCA) fusion system in automated vocal analysis decision support systems. It is a unique framework that incorporates: (i) Fermatean Fuzzy Sets (FFS) to specify expert reluctance and expression of uncertainty, (ii) the CRITIC method to obtain objective criterion weights depending on contrast intensity and inter-criteria conflict, and (iii) the MAIRCA method to produce deviation-based and interpretable rankings. The effectiveness of the proposed model is shown by a hypothetical case study that embraced six vocal performers and six multidimensional criteria. Findings indicate constant and convergent rankings, objective selection of influential criteria, and sensitivity analysis resistance. The proposed framework offers a robust, uncertainty-conscious, and bias-reduced assessment mechanism, which is more methodologically advanced than AI-only and traditional fuzzy decision- making methods in the evaluation of performance in the arts.

Introduction

The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence in musical technology has reshaped the manner of analyzing, interpreting, and scoring the vocal performance1. The traditional forms of assessments are highly reliant on human judgment, which is subjective and under most instances influenced by fatigue, bias and disagreement among experts2. On the other hand, Vocal performance analysis AI can be used to add the computer-generated acoustic (e.g., pitch stability, spectral richness, vibrato patterns) characteristics relationship to the external AI or signal-processing systems3. Nonetheless, the uncertainty, hesitation and expressive complexity of the human vocal performance is beyond the capability of even sophisticated AI systems. This gap creates a need to have a shared framework in which AI-based measurement abilities are merged with structured decision making (DM) tools that eliminate ambiguity. The present study proposes a solution to this problem, which is a Fermatean Fuzzy CRITIC-MAIRCA (FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA) fusion Model of automated evaluation of the vocal performance. The application of FFS is used to make expert judgments more realistic and ambiguity on vagueness, hesitation, and judgmental ambiguity. The objective weights on the evaluation criteria which then are calculated using CRITIC method4 and are based on data variability and inter-criteria correlation in which case more informative acoustic features have a disproportionate impact on the overall outcome. Finally, the MAIRCA5 method places a ranking of performers who sing and estimates the distance between the target and actual performance outcome giving a preference ranking that is interpretable and mathematically consistent. DM6 is one of the main elements of this model because it integrates subjective human ratings, objective AI-based acoustic evaluation and fuzzy uncertainty modelling into a single coherent rating system. Although there are recent developments in acoustic analysis using AI and fuzzy DM models, current vocal performance evaluation models are still disjointed. The main distinction between AI-based methods and conventional fuzzy MCDM techniques is that the former are concerned with signal-level accuracy and do not consider the human interpretation uncertainty, whereas the latter ones tend to overlook the issue of inter-criteria conflict and performance variation in an integrated fashion. Such disconnection restrains the artistic readability and real-world applicability of existing systems especially in artistic fields where professional reluctance and ambiguity of expression are built in. Such constraints are powerful drivers of a holistic decision-support structure that can address uncertainty, objectivity, and interpretability simultaneously. Even though many fuzzy MCDM models have been constructed to reflect uncertainty-conscious assessment, current frameworks are generally subject to three key methodological deficiencies, namely, imperfect expression of expert uncertainty (by classical FS and IFS models), dependency on subjective or entropy-based weighting functions, and ranking mechanisms (through relative distance or compromise solutions) that do not give clear interpretations of performance deviations. To address these deficiencies, this paper suggests an FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA fusion model that (i) elicits deep uncertainty and hesitation with Fermatean fuzzy modeling, (ii) identifies objectively the importance of the criterion with contrast intensity and correlation with CRITIC, and (iii) ranks alternatives based on absolute deviation to an ideal reference with MAIRCA. This common architecture offers a transparent, conflict-sensitive, and uncertainty-conscious decision architecture that is fundamentally different from the existing fuzzy MCDM methods. Using a hypothetical case study, this work demonstrates that the proposed fusion model makes the process more transparent and less biased and offers a more balanced and intelligent method for assessing vocal performance than traditional methods. Thus, the main task to be fulfilled by the research is to design transparent, uncertain, and weighted decision-support system of automated vocal performance assessment, incorporating expert hesitation modeling, criterion weighting based on data, and a comprehensible ranking into a single mathematical framework.

As opposed to standard hybrid MCDM integrations which sequentially decouple uncertainty modelling, weighting, and ranking, the proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA architecture proposes a structurally integrated fusion platform where uncertainty representation, objective weighting and deviation ranking run on a single Fermatean fuzzy framework. Its novelty is in the fact that both hesitation-aware information is kept in the entire decision pipeline and at the same time allows conflict-sensitive weighting and absolute deviation-based performance estimation. This combined formulation enables the performance gaps to be directly interpreted when there is uncertainty, enhances the discrimination between alternatives and the results of the project are consistent between weighting logic and uncertainty modeling as well as ranking. This type of coherent fusion mechanism has not been developed in the past in the context of vocal performance evaluation or in the current fuzzy hybrid decision-support models that use Fermatean fuzzy. The suggested framework is thus an AI-Fermatean fuzzy MCDM architecture which combines data-driven feature extraction, objective weighting, and deviation-based ranking to one decision-support architecture.

Related work

In recent studies of automated vocal performance assessment, AI-based acoustic feature extraction methods and machine learning classification methods have been the major areas of research. Several studies have used classical fuzzy sets, intuitionistic fuzzy sets and Pythagorean fuzzy models to deal with uncertainties in evaluation problems in the field of fuzzy (MCDM) such as assessment of air purifiers7, academic stress-coping8, and evaluating martial arts teaching skills9. Nonetheless, most of these methods apply subjective weighting schemes or entropy-based methods, which do not fully solve inter-criteria conflict. CRITIC method has been extensively applied to objective determination of weight based on contrast intensity and correlation analysis whereas MAIRCA is a deviation-based ranking mechanism that can be strongly interpreted. However, how they can be integrated in a Fermatean fuzzy uncertainty model to evaluate artistic performance using AI has not been studied yet; that is why this paper will fuse the existing AI-based acoustic assessment with sophisticated fuzzy MCDM approaches.

In contrast to the current Fermatean fuzzy hybrids, which include FF-TOPSIS and FF-VIKOR, which are based on relative distance based or compromise based ranking logics, the proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA scheme will use a deviation-based evaluation logic that will explicitly determine the absolute gap between theoretical and actual performance profile. Furthermore, the MAIRCA mechanism derives an expectation-based theoretical reference matrix, in contrast to FF-CRITIC-EDAS, which assesses the alternatives by distances to an average solution, which allows one to more easily interpret performance deviations diagnostically. This architectural difference enables the proposed architecture to have greater interpretability and conflict-sensitive decision support in AI-aided vocal performance evaluation.

Literature review

The AI-based vocal performance measurement has attracted considerable interest in recent years, as scholars pursue more objective and scalable means of measuring singing quality10. Past literature relating to vocal analysis largely centered around single acoustic features like pitch accuracy, spectral clarity and consistency in vibrato, with many researchers applying machine-learning or deep-learning algorithms to extract and categorize vocal information11. However, the systems, despite being computationally intensive, tend to ignore the ambiguity, the emotional variability, and interpretive subjectivity of human singing12.

Hybrid AI-human evaluation systems and their limitations

Several studies have sought to reconcile AI-based metrics and expert judgments, aiming to combine objective and subjective information. As an example, other methods mix acoustic criteria with judge ratings using linear regression or rule-based corrections13, but do not attempt to include the hesitation, ambiguity, and emotional complexity of expert ratings when performing live vocal music. The absence of this gap requires a structure in which subjective measures are officially reflected in cases of uncertainty. More so, older weighting techniques (AHP or entropy) usually assume the independence of criteria and ignore contrast and opposition, whereas traditional ranking procedures (such as TOPSIS) utilize potentially simplistic measures of proximity. More recent developments in AI-for-music evaluation have used deep learning models, including CNNs, RNNs, and hybrid music information systems14, to predict pitch, rhythm, and expressive quality in automatic singing. Research has shown good predictive but weak interpretability and poor uncertainty management. Multi-input singing models (MISMs) and attention-based models also improve feature learning15, but they still rely on deterministic scoring schemes. Such advances underscore the improved quality of AI assessment and support the need for a hybrid system that can combine objective AI outputs with uncertainty-sensitive decision models, such as the one suggested in this paper. Moreover, the multi-disciplinary methods, including multi-objective speech enhancement by tunable Q-factor wavelet transforms and AI-based research on the emotional perception in singing, demonstrate the increasing overlap of MCDM and acoustic analysis in the assessment of vocal performance, which justifies the desire to combine the two approaches in a single framework as suggested in this paper.

The proposed synthesis

Model assumptions: It is assumed in this study that (i) the analysis of vocal performance is associated with inherent uncertainty and hesitation which cannot be well-represented by crisp values, (ii) the evaluation criteria can be correlated and have various levels of contrast and (iii) the expert and AI-extracted acoustic information can be presented in a Fermatean fuzzy model. These hypotheses lead to the theoretical foundation of incorporating FFS, CRITIC and MAIRCA in a single decision-support system. This research will fill these gaps by proposing a FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA fusion model, which is a combination of the three above-mentioned in a single framework of vocal examination. Conversely, the current paper presents a FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA blend model which can counter these deficiencies. FFS offer a more comprehensive and versatile way of modeling membership, non-membership and hesitation together, thus they are better placed in dealing with the ambiguity of vocal evaluation. The CRITIC technique creates objective weights according to variability and correlation whereby highly informative acoustic characteristics contribute to the final ranking more appropriately. In the meantime, the MAIRCA method assesses options by ranking and ranking the expected and actual deviations in performance, which offers a more interpretative, fairness-oriented ranking process. This combined system is superior to the traditional fuzzy and non-fuzzy assessment models in that it has an increased tolerance to uncertainty, better discriminatory capability, increased transparency and more dependable correspondence to expert intuition. Consequently, the suggested methodology is an essential piece of development related to the area of AI-based vocal performance evaluation.

Methodological advancement over existing fuzzy MCDM models

The proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA model creates a structurally integrated DM process in comparison with the conventional fuzzy MCDM framework which mainly focuses on uncertainty representation and utilizes subjective weighting and relative-distance ranking. FFS are a generalization of IF and PF models of uncertainty, in which there is an opportunity to be even more strongly hesitant and with an easier interpretation. CRITIC is an objective method of quantification of criterion importance, which uses variability and inter-criteria conflict to assess and remove bias found in expert-based or entropy weighting. Moreover, MAIRCA ranks alternatives by quantifying absolute differences to an ideal profile, so that performance gaps can be directly interpreted instead of having to measure performance in abstract terms of closeness. The joint application of these three elements constitutes a new hybrid architecture that can address uncertainty management, objective weighting, as well as transparent ranking all without at the same time and simultaneously, a methodological improvement which is not available in the current body of fuzzy MCDM literature. This systematic merging provides theoretical compatibility of uncertainty modelling, objective weighting and deviation-based ranking, thus constituting a mathematically consistent and rationally coherent system of decision-support.

In music performance and vocal assessment, the traditional approach has been to test the performance of music through the eyes of professional panels with criteria of tone accuracy, rhythmic stability, breathing ability, and expression. Over the past years, AI-based audio analysis tools have allowed objective analysis by extracting acoustic features such as pitch deviation, vibrato stability, and spectral characteristics. Simultaneously, fuzzy and multi-criteria decision-making (MCDM) have been used increasingly extensively in arts and performance evaluation to deal with subjective judgments and multi-faceted assessment criteria. Such applications are however still limited in structured uncertainty modelling and interpretable ranking mechanisms. Thus, a hybrid approach to systematic vocal performance evaluation based on fuzzy MCDM tools and AI-related assessment characteristics offers a valuable and explainable system.

Proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA fusion framework

The suggested FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA is presented as a well-organized multi-phase process. FFNs are used to represent expert assessments and acoustic AI indicators. In the present methodological demonstration, the expert ratings on vocal performance criteria are expressed as FFNs. The criteria used in the case study involve the features that are typically applied to automated vocal assessment systems, including pitch stability, timbre richness, breath control, dynamic range handling, tonal accuracy, and AI-based spectral features. Second, CRITIC approach is used to calculate objective weights based on the variability of the criterion and inter-criteria correlation. Lastly the MAIRCA method is used to analyze the difference between the expected performance and actual performance which results in a final ranking of the vocal performers. The steps are carried out in logical order so that they have a logical consistency and are reproducible. The MAIRCA22 method and the CRITIC methodology are used in this work to address an MCDM problem with FFS uncertainty. A successful DM method is shown to handle these issues. The primary objective of any MCDM23 The challenge is to order the options according to the specified standards. The development of the MAIRCA approach, in conjunction with FFS, Automated Vocal Performance Evaluation in AI Driven Decision Support Systems, is described in this section. Consider a DM problem with a set of m alternatives \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:A\:=\:\{\:{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{1}},{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{2}},{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{3}},\dots\:\dots\:,{\mathrm{A}}_{\mathrm{m}}\}$$\end{document} and a set of six criteria \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\:\mathrm{C}=\:\{{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{1}},{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{2}},{\mathrm{C}}_{\mathrm{3}},\dots\:\dots\:,{\mathrm{C}}_{6}\}$$\end{document}. The hybrid MAIRCA-CRITIC method addresses the complexities and uncertainties that impact DM dependability by combining two opposing MCDM methods for optimal results. MAIRCA promotes open and preference-driven ranking by emphasizing the logical difference between ideal and actual possibilities. MAIRCA is especially appropriate to the performance evaluation that directly quantifies the difference between desired (ideal) and actual performance giving a clear and understandable gap analysis. The proposed framework brings in methodological development in that there is a coordinated and systematic integration of uncertainty modelling, objective weighting and compromise ranking within an FF environment. This integration guarantees that uncertainty is better handled, that the objectivity of criterion weights is increased and results ranking stability is improved. Subsequently, the suggested fusion offers sounder and stronger assessment than traditional hybrid DM techniques. This method gives better interpretation of performance gaps relative to relative-distance methods like TOPSIS or compromise-based approaches like VIKOR, when the ranking is not easily related to quantifiable shortcomings in performance. In the meantime, CRITIC assigns objective weights to the evaluation criteria by evaluating their levels of contrast and conflict. This integration is useful for uncertain, multi-criteria scenarios like evaluating educational programs since it provides a balanced approach that combines data-driven weighing with CRITIC and takes decision-makers’ preferences into account with MAIRCA.

Algorithm

This section outlines the step-by-step algorithm of the proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA fusion framework in order to enable clarity, repeatability and organized code. The algorithm consists of two fundamental elements: the CRITIC method, which calculates the objective criterion weights depending on the data contrast and conflict, and the FF-MAIRCA process, ranking vocal performers according to the deviation of their Fermatean fuzzy performance profiles of an ideal reference. This process systematically integrates professional uncertainty (through FFS), objective weighting, and interpretable distance-based ranking. The CRITIC process quantifies the relative importance of every criterion based on the degree of difference between the criteria and conflict among the criteria. Computational complexity of the proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA framework is mainly dictated by the matrix operations, such as correlation computation in CRITIC and deviation aggregation in MAIRCA. Because the model does not require iteration or learning steps, the computational cost is polynomial with respect to the number of options and criteria, and thus can be applied to large scale evaluation problems. The proposed framework is theoretically based on the complementary functions of its parts. FFS allow a higher representation of expert hesitation and uncertainty that is vital in vocal performance evaluation. The CRITIC approach is objective in assigning weights to the criterion depending on the contrast intensity and inter-criteria conflict minimizing subjectivity. The MAIRCA methodology ranks the alternatives with a clear measurement of the differences between the theoretical and actual profiles of performance, which is interpretable and logically consistent. A combination of these elements creates a decision-support model that is coherent and effective in complex problems of evaluation that are driven by uncertainty.

Result discussion

The results of ranking acquired with the help of the offered framework are interpreted in terms of variation of the ideal performance profile, in which the lower the deviation values, the higher the overall vocal performance. This mapping is also valid to the theoretical background of the MAIRCA approach and makes sure that the final rankings are transparent and logically significant. In the generated dataset afforded by synthetics to demonstrate the methodological results, Tonal Accuracy received the most CRITIC weight owing to its standard deviation spread across alternatives being relatively bigger and low correlation with other criteria. This finding can be explained by the fact that the CRITIC approach determines criteria that have more pronounced contrast and independence in the provided dataset than to suggest that this criterion predominates in real-life vocal assessment. The FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA fusion model was utilized to test six vocal performers in six multidimensional parameters. It is the final deviation values (distances between the anticipated and actual performance profiles) in which the ranking of the alternatives is done. Because the MAIRCA model assumes that the lower values of deviation should be viewed as signs of a superior performance, a reduced deviation will presuppose a greater conformity to the ideal vocal profile and consequently a greater preference. Moreover, it is quite low, which means that it does not overlap with other criteria and offers relatively unique information on quality of performance. This observation is a statistical property of the synthetic data employed in this study and is not an indication of natural preeminence of such a criterion in vocal pedagogy. On the other hand, criteria that had more mutual correlation (e.g., Pitch Stability, C₁) had reduced weight, so that the weighting scheme gives more emphasis to independent, non-redundant elements of performance. The fact that this data-driven weighting can identify the most effective criteria to distinguish between vocalists without the subjective expert opinion being used to weight the criteria shows that this model has the potential to make different weights than those provided by a priori subjective expert weights. As an illustration, although pitch stability is typically a focus of vocal training, its low weight here is due to its low statistical difference in this particular dataset, indicating how CRITIC objectively gives preference to criteria depending on their actual discriminative strength in the performance sample thus minimizing possible bias due to airtight expert assumptions.

The slight disparities (e.g., \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\boldsymbol{A}}_{1}$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\boldsymbol{A}}_{6}$$\end{document}) can be attributed to the underlying processes of both approaches: TOPSIS is based on relative distance to an ideal point, VIKOR is a compromise ranking with a balancing parameter, but MAIRCA is based on absolute deviation of the performance gaps, which is a more straightforward interpretation of the performance differences. The important interpretability strength of MAIRCA is based on deviation-based logic. In contrast to TOPSIS, which orders alternatives by their relative closeness to ideal and anti-ideal solutions or VIKOR, which uses a balance between group utility and individual regret to measure the solution, MAIRCA simply quantifies the absolute deviation of the theoretical (expected) performance matrix and the actual performance matrix. This enables decision-makers to clearly tell the degree to which an individual performer is not in line with the performance profile sought by the individual on all the criteria. This diagnostic transparency is a manifestation of major methodological advantages compared to relative-distance-based ranking methods. This kind of absolute gap interpretation gives more diagnostic information, which is especially needed in the assessment of art and education where guidance on the improvement of performance is needed. The validity of this comparison is that the proposed model generates a very strong, logically consistent ranking that is consistent with the conventional MCDM practices and also offers the distinctive benefit of interpretation by deviation. In combination with the systematic sensitivity test and qualitative methodological benchmarking, these tests give multi-layered validation of the soundness and structural stability of the suggested FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA framework.

As opposed to pure AI-based vocal scoring systems, which rely solely on deep learning models to produce performance scores, the suggested FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA framework combines subjective acoustic features and expert judgment within an explicit uncertainty modelling framework. AI only systems tend to be non-interpretable and fail to represent expert uncertainty, whereas expert only rubrics tend to be subjective and inconsistent. The resulting hybrid model will offer clear weighting, and deviation-based ranking to come up with stronger and more diagnostically significant assessments. The proposed framework also dynamically changes the criterion’s importance and minimizes bias by using an objective weighting mechanism compared to the traditional vocal evaluation rubrics, which provide predetermined subjective marks.

The sensitivity analysis in Table 9 involved controlled perturbations in the CRITIC-obtained weights (± 10% global changes, controlled ad hoc modulation of the most significant criterion, and moderate redistribution of the fuzzy parameters of Fermat ∑\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{\mathrm{ϖ}}_{\mathrm{j}}$$\end{document} = 1), and small changes in the Fermatean fuzzy parameters. To investigate the weight space of the feasible, topos of redistribution, in addition to deterministic perturbations, randomized admissible weight redistribution was considered. In every produced weight configuration the MAIRCA deviation aggregation and ultimate ranking was recalculated. In some perturbation conditions, minor variations in position were seen in middle-ranked alternatives, whereas the best and worst ones did not change. This implies that the proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA framework is stable in the ranking region and is structurally robust and not sensitive.

Practical implications

The suggested FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA hybrid model has good practical utility to any setting where vocal performance requires to be accurately, reasonably, and consistently assessed. In practical contexts like music schools, music talent contests, online music platforms, and software-based vocal training, the evaluation is usually biased by humans, subjective to scoring, and cannot read the gray aspects of vocal criteria. With AIF-derived acoustic measurements and expert estimates as part of a fuzzy-uncertainty model, a more level-headed, scientifically-based assessment can be made. It can be used by music teachers to monitor student progress, by digital audition sites to shortlist the high-quality performers, and it can be used by automated vocal training applications to give feedback on performance, personalized feedback based on objective and subjective performance metrics. Since the system reduces variability and enhances interpretability, it can also assist industry professionals in choosing vocalists to record in the studio, arranging choirs, or casting performances. In general, the model can convert intricate vocal patterns into reliable DM outputs, making it an effective tool for contemporary music analysis and AI-based performance analysis.

Such systems should also be implemented ethically with transparency ensuring that the performers and the educators know how the scores and the rankings are obtained and fairness to prevent biases that can be created by unequal training data or subjective expert groups. The suggested FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA framework addresses these objectives by its weighting that is mathematically explicit and ranking by deviation that gives interpretable and audit paths of decision. A case study of such application is the online course on singing where students can have their performance rate by the AI service that uses audio analysis tools to identify their performance characteristics, including pitch accuracy, vibrato stability, and rhythmic consistency. Such quantitative measurements, along with any expert assessment of expressiveness and tone quality, can be expressed in the form of Fermatean fuzzy values. These assessments can then be combined into the proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA framework to offer interpretable rankings of student performance, areas of improvement and training-specific feedback.

Conclusion

This paper presented a FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA Fusion Model of the automated vocal performance evaluation. The model is successful in incorporating FFS in order to represent expert hesitation, CRITIC approach to objective criterion weighting, and MAIRCA approach to deviation-based ranking. The framework created consistent, clear and interpretable positions, and the best-matching performer to an ideal vocal profile was determined in a hypothetical case study of six performers and six vocal criteria. The main finding can be interpreted as strong consistency of ranking (ρ ≥ 0.89) with the model, and the unique logic of deviation-based model can provide a clearer interpretation of the performance gaps; these results make the model understandable in the context of limitations of traditional crisp or artificial intelligence-based vocal assessment. Although the proposed FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA model has methodological strength and interpretability, several limitations must be noted. First, the current case study is based on hypothetical data which is intended to illustrate the computation workflow. Acoustic data can be more diverse and noisy in practice in vocal performance evaluation. Second, CRITIC weighting method presupposes that there are linear relationships between criteria given, and this method might not adequately reflect complex relationships in artistic performance evaluation. Third, whereas the computational cost of the framework is affordable in case of medium-scale problems, large-scale applications or real-time real-world issues might need further optimization approaches. However, the suggested framework presents a comprehensive uncertainty-sensitive decision support system incorporating Fermatean fuzzy modelling together with goal-oriented weighting and deviation-based ranking. The proposed approach, unlike the classic model of hybrid MCDM system, which considers weighting and ranking steps separately, introduces a coordinated system where Fermatean fuzzy-based representations of uncertainty among the experts, CRITIC finds the weights of the criteria, and MAIRCA continually propagates these weights to evaluate ranking. This is a structured integration that enhances discrimination between alternatives as well as minimizes the possible loss of information between decision levels.

Further research would be conducted to provide empirical validation with real-world vocal performance data. This validation can be done by (i) obtaining vocal recordings across the musical genres, (ii) obtaining standard acoustic descriptors by means of music information retrieval methods, (iii) using Fermatean fuzzy assessments as the representation of expert judgments and (iv) by using the FF-CRITIC-MAIRCA framework to test the stability of rankings in case of realistic acoustic variation. All these actions will also assess the feasibility and strength of the suggested model.

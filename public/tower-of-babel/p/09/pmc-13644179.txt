Analysis of health anxiety trajectories in rabies exposure patients based on machine learning
Authors: Yue’e Ma, Hua Xie, Xin Liu, Bin Lu, Minghe Jiang, Chenghang Mo, Hongbo Li, Fangzhu Li, Lan Liang, Guodong Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo identify the dynamic evolution trajectory of health anxiety in patients with initial rabies exposure and to conduct predictive analysis of health anxiety trajectories using machine learning algorithms.MethodsA total of 548 rabies exposure patients from a tertiary hospital in Nanning were selected between January 2024 and October 2025. Health anxiety levels were assessed at 0, 3, 7, 14, and 28 days post-exposure using the Health Anxiety Scale following a 5-dose immunization schedule. A latent variable growth mixture model was employed to identify anxiety development trajectories. Six machine learning algorithms were utilized to establish a high-risk health anxiety identification model, and a comprehensive evaluation of the model’s performance was conducted, with the SHAP algorithm used for model interpretation.ResultsAll 548 patients completed the survey. The latent variable growth mixture model identified three health anxiety development trajectories: low-level anxiety-stable group, moderate to high-level anxiety-improving group, and high persistent anxiety-hard to relieve group. Multivariate logistic regression analysis indicated that being female, having a college degree or higher, high rabies knowledge, a greater number of comorbidities, level III exposure, and delayed medical treatment were independent risk factors belonging to the ‘high persistent anxiety-hard to relieve group.’ In contrast, a higher level of health literacy was identified as a protective factor. The XGBoost model demonstrated superior predictive performance on the independent test set, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.881, while SHAP analysis ensured clinical interpretability. SHAP analysis of the XGBoost model revealed that health literacy, Perceived social support, rabies knowledge, Payment method and Animal source were the five most important features predicting the ‘high persistent group.ConclusionThere is heterogeneity in the health anxiety trajectories of patients exposed to rabies. A predictive model based on machine learning can facilitate the early identification of high-risk individuals, providing a basis for implementing precise psychological interventions.

Introduction

Rabies has an almost 100% fatality rate, causing severe psychological fear and stress for those exposed [1, 2].Health anxiety refers to an individual’s excessive worry about contracting serious illnesses, which, if persistent, can reduce adherence to post-exposure prophylaxis and may evolve into chronic pathological anxiety, imposing a heavy burden on individuals and healthcare systems [3].Current research primarily relies on cross-sectional designs, making it difficult to reveal the dynamic changes in health anxiety [4, 5]; traditional methods treat populations as homogeneous entities, failing to identify internally heterogeneous subgroups. The latent growth mixture model (LGMM) provides a method for identifying different developmental trajectories within a population [6], but its application in the acute stress context of rabies exposure has been limited. Additionally, there is an urgent clinical need for tools that can predict an individual’s long-term psychological risks at the first consultation to enable precise interventions. In recent years, machine learning methods have shown significant advantages in predicting health outcomes, capable of uncovering complex patterns from multidimensional data and constructing high-accuracy predictive models [7, 8].While demographic and clinical characteristics are considered potential predictors of anxiety, evidence on how they jointly influence the longitudinal evolution of anxiety is still lacking [9].This study aims to identify the heterogeneous developmental trajectories of health anxiety among rabies-exposed patients through a 28-day longitudinal survey, utilizing a latent variable growth mixture model. Based on baseline characteristics, a machine learning predictive model is constructed and validated to achieve early identification of high-risk individuals, thereby providing empirical evidence for the implementation of targeted psychological interventions.

Objects and methods

This section describes the study population, measurement instruments, data collection and quality control procedures, data preprocessing pipeline, and the statistical and machine learning methods employed.

Research subjects

Using a convenience sampling method, 548 rabies exposure patients who visited the Key outpatient clinics for dog injury management at a tertiary hospital in Nanning from January 2024 to October 2025 were selected as research subjects. Inclusion criteria included: ① First exposure, meeting the diagnostic criteria for rabies exposure level II-III as per the rabies exposure prevention and management guidelines (2023 edition); ② Receiving a 5-dose immunization schedule and completing the vaccination on the planned dates; ③ Clear consciousness without communication barriers; ④ Age ≥ 18 years; ⑤ Complete clinical data.

Exclusion criteria: ① Patients with diagnosed mental and cognitive disorders; ② Those with concurrent malignant tumors or other terminal diseases; ③ Participation in other studies.

Sample size calculation: This study considered multiple factors to determine an appropriate sample size. When using the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) as the primary indicator for model selection, the sample size should be 200 cases or more [10]. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Third Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (Approval No: Y2024067), and all survey subjects provided informed consent.

Research tools

Health anxiety scale

The China Short Health Anxiety Inventory (CSHAI) [11] was selected, consisting of 18 items, the first 14 items focus on health anxiety, while the last 4 items address risk factors. Responses were scored on a four-point Likert scale: 0 (never), 1 (sometimes), 2 (often), and 3 (always). A score of 15 serves as the cutoff for screening anxiety presence; higher scores indicate greater health anxiety. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of the scale is 0.849, indicating good diagnostic accuracy for health anxiety in the Chinese population.

General information survey

The research team designed a self-administered questionnaire that includes items on gender, age, marital status, education level, residence, employment status, monthly family income, method of medical expense payment, rabies knowledge, exposure classification, number of comorbidities, initial wound management, whether the individual is in a high-risk occupational exposure category, Animal source, and the time window for post-exposure medical intervention.

Comprehensive health literacy measurement scale

All Aspects of Health Literacy Scale(AAHLS) adapted by Wu Qing et al. [12], was used to assess health literacy levels. The scale demonstrates good reliability and validity, with a Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of 0.811. It consists of a total of 11 items across 3 dimensions: the ability to use written health information (items 1, 2, 3, 4), the ability to communicate with healthcare providers (items 5, 6, 7), and the ability to evaluate and apply health information (items 8, 9, 10, 11). Each item has response options of “Rarely,” “Sometimes,” and “Often,” assigned scores of 1 to 3 using a Likert 3-point scoring method. Items 1 and 4 are reverse scored, while the remaining items are scored positively; a higher score indicates a higher level of health literacy.

Perceived social support scale

The Perceived Social Support Scale (PSSS) [13]is used to assess the level of perceived social support from different sources among patients, The scale includes three dimensions: family support, friend support, and other support, comprising a total of 12 items with a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.840. Each item is rated on a 7-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 7 (“strongly agree”). A higher total score indicates a higher level of perceived social support, with scores ranging from 61 to 84 indicating high perceived social support, 37 to 60 indicating moderate perceived social support, and 12 to 36 indicating low perceived social support.

Data collection method

In this study, face-to-face questionnaire surveys were conducted with patients at the time of receiving the first (T0, the day of injection), second (T1, third day), third (T2, seventh day), fourth (T3, fourteenth day), and fifth (T4, twenty-eighth day) doses of the rabies vaccine. With the consent of hospital management, the researchers selected eligible patients based on hospital medical records and informed them of the study’s purpose and the requirements for cooperation with subsequent vaccinations through face-to-face discussions. After signing the informed consent form, baseline data collection was carried out at T0; exposure grading and the number of comorbidities were obtained by reviewing medical records. For T0 to T4, questionnaires were also collected face-to-face, and the researchers checked each item upon completion. If any items were missing, they communicated with the patients promptly to assist them in completing the questionnaire on site.

Quality control method

A quality supervision team was established, consisting of three national-level psychological counselors, with graduate students making up half of the team. A dedicated person was assigned to remind patients of their vaccination schedule one day prior to the scheduled vaccination, as planned in the immunization information system. During the survey period, several senior nurses with vaccination certificates from the department conducted quality supervision to ensure that investigators used consistent language when explaining to patients. Two researchers organized the data. A total of 7 invalid questionnaires were excluded due to patterned and contradictory responses on the AAHLS. After exclusion, 548 valid participants with complete five-round data were included in the final analysis, ensuring the authenticity and reliability of the research data.Finally, the data entry was verified by two individuals to ensure accuracy.

Datare processing and leakage prevention

To prevent information leakage, all preprocessing was applied after the train/test split. The pipeline was: (1) five repeated stratified 70/30 splits (seeds: 42, 123, 456, 789, 1024); (2) per split, continuous variables (health literacy score and perceived social support) were standardized via StandardScaler fitted on the training set only; (3) SMOTE (k = 5) was applied within each inner cross-validation training fold via imblearn Pipeline; (4) all 17 candidate predictors were entered directly without feature selection, consistent with TRIPOD. Fit-on-training, transform-on-both ensures no information leakage.

Statistical methods

For model evaluation stability, we report two complementary sets of results: (1) the primary analysis using a fixed seed = 42 stratified 70/30 split with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (n = 2,000 resamples); and (2) a stability analysis using five repeated stratified 70/30 splits with different random seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 1024), reporting mean ± SD across splits.

Hyperparameter tuning for all six models was performed using GridSearchCV with 10-fold inner cross-validation, optimizing for ROC AUC.

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0, Mplus 8.4, and Python 3.13 (scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP), with a significance level set at P < 0.05. Latent Growth Mixture Modeling (LGMM) was employed to fit the health anxiety trajectories of patients exposed to rabies. Starting from a single class, the number of classes was incrementally increased, comparing the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC), adjusted Bayesian Information Criterion (aBIC), entropy, Lo-Mendell-Rubin likelihood ratio test (LMRT), and Bootstrap likelihood ratio test (BLRT) fit indices [10]. The smaller the values of AIC, BIC, and aBIC, the better the model fit; the closer the Entropy index is to 1, the more accurate the classification; when the p-values of the LMR and BLRT tests are less than 0.05, it indicates that the k-category model fits better than the k-1 category model. Multinomial logistic regression was employed to explore factors associated with health anxiety trajectory types, using the low-level stable anxiety group as the reference category. The trajectory category “High Persistent Difficulty in Relieving Group” was used as the predictive label for machine learning model construction.

Categorical variables were integer-coded, and continuous variables (health literacy score, perceived social support) were standardized; all 17 candidate predictors were entered directly without feature selection, consistent with TRIPOD recommendations for prediction model development. Six models were constructed and compared: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, Gradient Boosting, and XGBoost. Model performance was evaluated on the validation set using AUC, accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. The SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) framework was used to interpret the best-performing model (XGBoost), visualizing the direction and magnitude of feature contributions through importance ranking and beeswarm plots [14, 15].

Results

This section sequentially reports the participant characteristics, health anxiety score trajectories across the five follow-up time points, LGMM model fitting and selection results, univariate and multivariate regression analyses, and machine learning model comparison and interpretability findings.

General information

A total of 548 patients completed five rounds of surveys, among which 286 were male (52.2%) and 262 were female (47.8%); the highest educational level was high school/vocational school (187 cases, 34.1%); there were 276 cases (50.4%) classified as exposure level III, with other general information presented in Table 3.

Health anxiety scores

The health anxiety assessments of the 548 patients during five follow-ups showed a general trend of declining anxiety levels over time. At the first assessment (T0), the total anxiety score was 28.37 ± 10.25, which decreased to 19.39 ± 12.01 by the final follow-up (T4). The coefficient of variation (CV) of scores at each time point increased from 36.11% at T0 to 61.92% at T4, indicating that as time progressed, the differences in anxiety responses among patients became increasingly pronounced. Overall, the health anxiety levels of all patients showed a downward trend across the five time points, as detailed in Table 1.

Model fitting of health anxiety trajectory

This study employed Latent Growth Mixture Modeling (LGMM) for trajectory analysis, identifying the optimal model by comparing the fit indices of different category number models. The three-category model exhibited the highest Entropy value, with lower AIC, BIC, and aBIC values. Additionally, the LMRT and BLRT reached significant levels, indicating that this model is the best fit, as shown in Table 2. The three trajectory categories are named as follows: Category 1 “Low Stable Anxiety Group” (39.0%), which had a low level of health anxiety at baseline and maintained stability throughout the observation period; Category 2 “Moderate-High Improvement Group” (45.6%), which had anxiety levels at baseline that were intermediate between Categories 1 and 3, and subsequently showed a gradual decline during the vaccination process, with significant improvement by T4; Category 3 “High Persistent Difficult-to-Relieve Group” (15.4%), which had the highest baseline anxiety levels and remained consistently high throughout the observation period without any signs of relief, as illustrated in Fig. 2.

Univariate analysis of health anxiety trajectories in rabies exposure patients

The results of the univariate analysis indicate that there are statistically significant differences in the distribution of baseline characteristics among the three types of health anxiety trajectory patients (P < 0.05). Specifically, significant differences were observed in demographic characteristics (gender, age, educational level, marital status, residence), rabies knowledge, number of comorbidities, exposure grading, initial wound treatment, whether the individual is in a high-risk exposure occupation, the time window for medical intervention after exposure, animal management conditions, and psychosocial characteristics (health literacy, perceived social support), with notable differences in the composition ratios of the three patient groups (P < 0.05). However, no statistically significant differences were found among the groups regarding work status, monthly family income, and methods of medical expense payment, as shown in Table 3.

Comparison of machine learning model prediction performance

This study constructs and compares six machine learning models based on baseline variables.

The dataset is randomly divided into training (n = 384) and validation sets (n = 164) in a 7:3 ratio.

The prediction target is whether an individual belongs to the “High Persistent Anxiety – Difficult to Relieve group” (n = 85, 15.4%) based on the health anxiety trajectory identified by LGMM. Among the six models compared, XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) was selected as the primary model on the basis of superior discriminative and calibration performance. SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) analysis was subsequently applied to the XGBoost model to provide both global feature importance rankings and individual-level explanations, ensuring interpretability without sacrificing predictive accuracy.

Discussion

This study constructs and validates a comprehensive research framework that transitions from longitudinal trajectory recognition to early risk prediction, aiming to address the precision challenges of psychological care following rabies exposure. Through the use of latent variable growth mixture models, three types of heterogeneous trajectories of health anxiety were identified, clearly delineating the high-risk populations that require intervention [16].A machine learning predictive model (XGBoost, AUC = 0.881) was developed based on baseline data to facilitate the early identification of high-risk individuals [17].By integrating multinomial logistic regression with SHAP interpretability analysis, this research not only systematically identifies independent risk and protective factors but also reveals a core set of features that should be prioritized in comprehensive clinical judgment [18], This provides a complete empirical basis and systematic solution for transitioning from universal reassurance to targeted prevention.

Trajectory typology at the group level: the heterogeneity of health anxiety provides a basis for precise stratification

This study primarily finds that the psychological responses following rabies exposure are not a homogeneous single process, but rather consist of three distinctly different trajectories: low stable levels, medium to high levels of improvement, and high levels of persistent difficulty in alleviation [19].This finding deepens the understanding of acute stress responses, indicating that individuals exhibit fundamentally different patterns of catastrophic interpretation regarding somatic sensations and health information, which lead to varying emotional and behavioral outcomes [20, 21].Among these, the ‘low stable group’ and the ‘medium to high improvement group’ (comprising a total of 89.8%) show a gradual alleviation of anxiety following the completion of Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP), aligning with the general principles of acute stress responses and reflecting the natural resilience and adaptability of psychological processes [22]。However, the ‘high persistent difficulty in alleviation group,’ which accounts for 15.5%, indicates that some individuals do not follow the typical recovery trajectory. This proportion is comparable to that found in other medical stress events, such as acute coronary syndrome [23] and accidental trauma [24], where a subgroup of psychological distress remains difficult to alleviate [25], Patients in the high persistent difficulty in alleviation group tend to interpret the exposure event catastrophically, viewing rabies exposure as a continuous, uncontrollable, and lethal threat. Their psychological responses have deviated from a normal response to objective risk and may have entered a pathological cycle characterized by health anxiety and cognitive drivers [26].Therefore, clinical nursing practices should focus on precise stratified management of risk, directing limited intervention resources towards the identified high-risk subgroups.

Individual-level risk prediction: developing machine learning tools for early and accurate stratification

The most significant methodological contribution of this study lies in validating the feasibility of early predicting adverse anxiety trajectories using baseline information collected at the first consultation through machine learning models [7]. Machine learning automatically captures the complex interactions and nonlinear associations between variables, thereby identifying the optimal feature combinations and decision boundaries for predicting high persistent and difficult-to-relieve trajectories. By algorithmically integrating, learning, and optimizing numerous risk factors, a predictive tool is ultimately constructed that can directly support clinical decision-making. This enables the predictive model to maximize the use of baseline information for the early identification of populations with high persistent and difficult-to-relieve conditions.Among them, the XGBoost model demonstrated the best overall predictive performance on the validation set (AUC = 0.881), achieving the highest discriminative ability among all six models while maintaining robust calibration. This model validates the feasibility of early risk warning, and its ensemble of gradient-boosted provides a strong foundation for developing automated and intelligent clinical decision support systems. Using the SHAP framework to explain the decision basis, clinical credibility, and operability of the XGBoost model [27], this study transforms the model into a trustworthy and usable clinical tool It quantitatively reveals the predictive contribution of key features, making the prediction process transparent and transforming it into clinically understandable decision-making criteria. In the fast-paced environment of canine bite outpatient clinics, an automated risk assessment tool based on machine learning provides significant support for quickly screening high-risk psychological populations [28]. The shift from post-intervention to preemptive prediction establishes an intelligent foundation for implementing targeted prevention during the critical window period.

Synergistic interpretation and mechanism exploration of core predictive factors

This study systematically identifies the core factors influencing anxiety trajectories through multiple logistic regression and machine learning SHAP analysis. By integrating the results of multiple logistic regression and SHAP analysis, the aim is to provide a synergistic interpretation of the core factors affecting the ‘highly persistent and difficult to alleviate’ trajectory, thereby validating the robustness of key factors and offering a more operational decision-making logic for rapid clinical risk assessment.

Health literacy as a core protective factor against persistent anxiety

This study found that health literacy is a core protective factor in preventing patients from falling into high trajectories of persistent anxiety. This finding is highly consistent with the functional model of health literacy and the conclusions of numerous empirical studies [29].Additionally, SHAP analysis identified it as the feature contributing the most to model predictions, thereby establishing the core nature of health literacy through dual validation from both statistical association and predictive utility perspectives. Patients with high health literacy are better able to understand the scientific information of PEP and actively critique and resist online rumors, thereby establishing rational disease cognition and positive rehabilitation expectations.

This rational cognition serves as the emotional regulation cognitive foundation that interrupts catastrophic thinking and enhances the sense of control [30–32].In contrast, in the context of an information epidemic, patients with low health literacy are more likely to fall into uncertainty and panic due to insufficient information comprehension and critical abilities, and they are more affected by exaggerated or erroneous information, which exacerbates their fear [33, 34].Therefore, during the ‘psychological window period’ of a patient’s first visit, nursing staff should not only be satisfied with completing treatment procedures but should also regard assessing health literacy and providing clear, structured standardized health education as a core responsibility. This has a significantly positive effect on preventing long-term psychological issues and represents the most cost-effective psychological prevention strategy [35, 36].

Perceived social support: independent association with high predictive contribution

Perceived social support was independently associated with the high persistent difficult-to-relieve trajectory and ranked second in SHAP importance, exceeded only by health literacy. Social support is known to buffer psychological distress in chronic disease [37]populations, and our findings extend this evidence to the acute stress of rabies exposure, suggesting that social support is relevant not only in long-term illness management but also during the early psychological response to acute health threats. Supportive relationships may ease health anxiety through several pathways: family and friends can provide emotional reassurance that normalizes the exposure experience, practical assistance with follow-up visits can reduce uncertainty, and trusted others can help patients filter alarming or exaggerated health information [38]. Patients who lack such support, by contrast, may have fewer resources to manage persistent worry and may be more susceptible to information that amplifies rather than alleviates anxiety. A brief assessment of social support at intake could therefore help identify high-risk patients and guide referral to peer support or community health resources during the vaccination period.

Society-related influencing factors; payment method, rabies knowledge, educationa

Among social-related factors, healthcare payment methods, level of rabies knowledge, and educational attainment all demonstrated high contributions in the multivariate analysis of the LGMM and SHAP importance ranking.

The payment method for rabies treatment is an independent risk factor, ranking third in the SHAP importance ranking. Research has confirmed that medical insurance is an independent predictor of health anxiety in rabies patients. In China, out-of-pocket expenses for post-exposure management of rabies have not yet been covered by the medical insurance reimbursement system [39]. Insured patients can pay using their medical insurance account balance, whereas uninsured patients must pay entirely out-of-pocket, thereby bearing a higher financial burden.

Consequently, patients with medical insurance experience lower levels of health anxiety compared to those who pay out-of-pocket, which is related to medical insurance policies to some extent.

The level of education and knowledge regarding rabies are not only independent risk factors but also exhibit a close contributory association in the SHAP analysis, indicating that they do not operate in isolation but may form a synergistic risk combination that collectively impacts health anxiety. Higher education is typically regarded as a protective factor; however, in this study, it emerged as a risk factor in the ‘difficult-to-relieve group.’ This reveals the complexity of health psychological risks in the information age. Patients with higher education often possess stronger information-seeking abilities and reflective tendencies, but if they lack authoritative guidance and critical medical thinking, their extensive information-seeking behaviors may lead them to encounter an overwhelming amount of biased or even exaggerated negative cases online, exacerbating their catastrophic thinking, such as ‘cyberchondria‘ [40, 41], and resulting in a lack of deep understanding or trust in key information. Similarly, if patients hold misconceptions about diseases, their self-assessed knowledge of rabies may lead to stronger fears and more entrenched catastrophic thinking [42].This fragmented or biased knowledge becomes the cognitive fuel for persistent anxiety. Healthcare providers should possess keen insight and pay special attention to patients with overlapping multiple risk characteristics while conducting cognitive calibration; affirm their proactive engagement in information-seeking, establish trust, and guide them to trust the standardized treatment plans obtain.

Additional predictors and methodological considerations

Among clinical and demographic factors, comorbidity burden, higher exposure grade, adequate wound management, and shorter treatment duration have all been confirmed as independent predictors. However, their SHAP contributions are relatively low, and they can still serve as actionable targets for improving clinical quality. Variables including marital status and occupational status showed limited SHAP contributions. Although they contributed to some extent to the predictive performance of machine learning models, none of them reached the level of independent statistical significance. This finding highlights the value of combining machine learning explainability with traditional regression analysis.

Implications for nursing practice: constructing accurate and forward-looking psychological care pathways

The results of this study provide clear guidance for clinical nursing practice. It is recommended to construct an accurate psychological care pathway based on risk stratification:①Screening and stratification: At the first visit, integrate key information such as ultra-brief health literacy assessments, and utilize the predictive models or simplified decision rules validated in this study for rapid risk stratification.②Stratified intervention: For low-risk patients, provide standardized and proactive information reinforcement support; for medium to high-risk patients, initiate enhanced interventions, including targeted cognitive restructuring and simple emotional training to manage acute anxiety, and issue information prescriptions [43] to guide them in obtaining information from authoritative sources, thus avoiding the negative impacts of internet searches.③Continuous monitoring: Establish follow-up records for high-risk patients, and conduct dynamic psychological monitoring remotely during the post-exposure vaccination period to achieve closed-loop management of intervention effectiveness. This pathway truly reflects the integrated care concept originating from the biopsychosocial medical model [44].

Limitation and future directions

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, while our two-stage approach combining LGMM with machine learning effectively identifies trajectory groups and predicts membership, future studies with denser longitudinal follow-up may consider sequence modeling architectures—such as Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, Transformer models, or temporal Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)—that capture temporal dynamics directly, potentially modeling the evolution of health anxiety over time rather than relying on trajectory labels from a separate model. Second, external validation in multi-center cohorts is needed to confirm the generalizability of the predictive model.

Conclusion

This study integrates “longitudinal trajectory recognition - machine learning prediction” to reveal the heterogeneous evolution patterns of health anxiety following rabies exposure at the population level. Various machine learning predictive models were constructed and validated, enabling early identification of this high-risk subgroup and providing an early risk screening method based on machine learning that can be used during the initial clinical consultation at the individual level. This provides a basis for transforming psychological care models from post-event interventions to preemptive predictions, and from universal reassurance to precise interventions.

In the future, rapid screening tools for dog bite outpatient clinics can be developed based on the core predictive features identified in this study, and targeted psychological interventions can be implemented for the identified high-risk health anxiety population to interrupt the chronicization of anxiety and improve patient prognosis. the XGBoost model achieved strong internal validation performance (AUC = 0.881) and SHAP-based interpretability, though its external validity requires further verification in prospective applications. Moreover, the study primarily relies on patient self-assessment scales; future research could incorporate qualitative interviews, physiological indicators monitoring, and other methods to explore the complex mechanisms influencing anxiety trajectories more comprehensively and deeply.

Phase 2a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of londamocitinib (AZD4604) two times per day for 12 weeks in adult patients with moderate-to-severe asthma uncontrolled on medium-high-dose ICS-LABA
Authors: Mohd Nubli Mustapa, Rod Hughes, Tina J Jensen, Davinder P Dosanjh, Kyriakos V Konstantinidis, Julia Jonsson, Szilárd Nemes, Zala Jevnikar, Michael Jones, Amanda Adams, Maria G Belvisi, Praveen Akuthota, Janwillem W H Kocks
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionAsthma is a heterogeneous condition and affected individuals show variable responses to available medications. An unmet need exists for add-on therapies that target novel molecular pathways, before patients escalate to biologics. Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) is implicated in multiple inflammatory cytokine pathways critical for the pathogenesis of asthma. Londamocitinib (AZD4604) is a highly specific, inhaled, JAK1 inhibitor with high potency to block multiple JAK1-dependent signalling pathways. AJAX is a phase 2a, randomised, double-blind, partially decentralised placebo-controlled study assessing the efficacy, safety and pharmacokinetics (PK) of londamocitinib in adults with moderate-to-severe asthma uncontrolled on medium-to-high-dose inhaled corticosteroid/long-acting β2-agonist.Methods and analysisThe primary endpoint is time to first CompEx Asthma event. Secondary endpoints include change from baseline in prebronchodilator forced expiratory volume in 1 s, chronic airways assessment test, six-item asthma control questionnaire, daily asthma symptom score, and morning and evening peak expiratory flow at weeks 4 and 12. In addition, assessment of the effect of londamocitinib on airway inflammation as measured by the fractional exhaled nitric oxide test; cough severity assessment; and the PK of londamocitinib in all participants after 4 and 12 weeks will also be evaluated.Ethics and disseminationThe study has received ethical approval from the appropriate ethic committee and will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and all applicable regulatory requirements. All participants will provide written informed consent prior to enrolment, with procedures in place to ensure comprehension and voluntary participation. Findings will be disseminated through peer‑reviewed publications and/or presentations at scientific conferences. Summary results will be posted on the trial registry and shared with participants and relevant patient groups in lay summaries.

Introduction

Asthma is a heterogeneous disease, characterised by chronic airway inflammation and bronchial hyperreactivity.1 Current guidelines recommend inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) as the basis of therapy, predominantly targeting the eosinophilic component of asthma-related inflammation. Consequently, patients with an element of non-eosinophilic inflammation may not achieve adequate disease control with ICS treatment. Poor asthma control increases severe exacerbation risk, reduces quality of life and necessitates treatment escalation including higher doses of ICS and use of oral corticosteroids. Escalating corticosteroid doses increases risk of localised and systemic adverse effects (AEs) including oral thrush, osteoporosis, diabetes mellitus and adrenal insufficiency.26 Additionally, reliance on empirical, progressive ICS dose increases often delays appropriate phenotyping of patients with asthma, resulting in poor symptom control and delayed commencement of appropriate treatment.7 There is, therefore, an unmet need for treatments that could be used as add-on therapies in patients with poor asthma control despite optimised ICS therapy.1 8

While signalling redundancy exists, Janus kinase 1 (JAK1) phosphorylates all signal transducer and activator of transcription proteins (STATs) that are implicated in asthma pathogenesis, thus making JAK1 an attractive therapeutic target for those with asthma.9 Interleukin (IL)-4, IL-5, IL-13 and thymic stromal lymphopoietin signalling pathways lead to initiation of T2 inflammatory processes (ICS sensitive),10 while the interferon-γ/STAT111 12 and IL-6-STAT3 pathways are associated with glucocorticoid resistance.13 However, systemic inhibition of multiple JAK pathways has been associated with AEs, including increased risk of infection, anaemia, leucopenia and cardiovascular disease.14 Selective and localised inhibition of a single JAK pathway is therefore desirable in an attempt to minimise AEs.

Londamocitinib (AZD4604) is a selective, inhaled JAK1 inhibitor with high potency to block multiple JAK1-dependent signalling pathways. It has been shown to inhibit JAK1-dependent cytokine signalling pathways in a dose-dependent manner in preclinical animal and human models, inhibiting eosinophilia in the lungs and reducing the late asthmatic response,15 as well as other inflammatory cells such as lymphocytes, neutrophils and macrophages, and decreasing levels of cytokines eotaxin and IL-1β.16 17 It has demonstrated high target engagement in the lungs, with limited systemic exposure, reducing AE risks arising from systemic inhibition of cytokine signalling commonly seen with oral JAK inhibitors.16 Londamocitinib has demonstrated an acceptable safety and tolerability profile at doses up to 6 mg in a phase 1, randomised, single and multiple ascending dose study in healthy subjects and participants with mild asthma, demonstrating rapid absorption and significant, sustained anti-inflammatory effects assessed via reduction of fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) after multiple inhaled doses.18

The AJAX study is investigating the efficacy, safety and pharmacokinetics (PK) of londamocitinib in adults with moderate-to-severe asthma uncontrolled on medium-to-high dose ICS–long-acting β-agonists (LABAs). The study was designed to facilitate recruitment, reduce participant burden and increase participant diversity. Here, we report the novel study design of AJAX, describing its innovative aspects including its partially decentralised trial design, inclusion of a more generalisable asthma population, and use of non-traditional assessments including CompEx Asthma19 20 and remote-coached spirometry.

Methods

Study design and objectives

AJAX (NCT06020014) is a phase 2a, partially decentralised, multicentre, randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind, parallel-arm study being conducted at ~150 sites in 18 countries (figure 1).21

Study design. BID, two times per day.

AJAX comprises a 2-week screening period, 4-week run-in period, 12-week treatment period and 1-week follow-up period, with a maximum duration of ~5 months for each participant (figure 1). Due to its partially decentralised study design, visits 4 and 6 are flexible and can be performed at the study site or at the participant’s home (decentralised) based on the preferences of the study site and participant. If a participant is unable to attend certain site visits, they can be replaced by alternative remote, virtual and home-based assessments, depending on site-specific circumstances. This ensures that participants can fulfil required reporting for essential efficacy and safety endpoints.

Patient advisors were embedded within the clinical programme team to provide input into the study design and objectives.

Participants and eligibility criteria

The study includes adults (18–80 years) with documented evidence of asthma in the past 10 years with a morning prebronchodilator (pre-BD) forced expiratory volume in 1 second (FEV1) of ≥40% predicted and an Asthma Control Questionnaire-6 (ACQ-6) score of ≥1.5 at visits 1 (screening) and 3 (randomisation). Unlike previous asthma trials, BD reversibility was not required to confirm asthma diagnosis, aligned with the GINA guidelines1; several other historical or screening diagnostic criteria are being considered to confirm participant eligibility with adequate documentation (online supplemental table 1). Additionally, participants must have moderate-to-severe asthma, that is, those receiving treatment with a stable medium-to-high dose of ICS–LABA+/–additional asthma controller therapy (long-acting muscarinic antagonists or leukotriene receptor antagonists; GINA steps 4 and 5 (excluding those on biologic therapy)) for ≥28 days prior to visit 1.1 They must also have a documented history of ≥1 severe asthma exacerbation within 1 year before visit 1; severe asthma exacerbation is defined as worsening of asthma leading to use of systemic corticosteroids for ≥3 consecutive days, an emergency room visit (for <24 hours) due to asthma requiring treatment with systemic corticosteroids, and/or inpatient hospitalisation (for ≥24 hours) due to asthma. Participants must also achieve at least 80% compliance with daily electronic diary (eDiary) entries during the last 2 weeks of the run-in period. A full list of study inclusion and exclusion criteria is provided in online supplemental table 1. The baseline phenotype of each participant will be characterised based on baseline FeNO, blood eosinophil levels, exacerbation history and history of atopy. Written-informed consent for study participation is obtained from participants before performing any study-specific evaluations. All source documents, including those substantiating a diagnosis or evidence of asthma, are reviewed during on-site monitoring visits.

Interventions and procedures

Eligible participants are randomised 1:1 to londamocitinib 1.4 mg two times per day or matching placebo for 12 weeks using interactive response technology. Participants take londamocitinib or placebo via the SD3FL dry-powder inhaler (Genuair), with each dose taken 12 hours apart. The study is blinded to participants, investigators and AstraZeneca staff.

Asthma exacerbation is evaluated at various time points and details regarding severe asthma exacerbations including treatment and resolution are recorded. An eDiary is completed twice daily by participants, including assessment/recording of home-based peak expiratory flow (PEF) readings, asthma symptoms and use of rescue medication (if needed) in mornings and evenings; night-time awakenings due to asthma symptoms, cough severity item Visual Analogue Scale, compliance with background medication and study treatment, and reporting of unscheduled hospitalisation or emergency room visits. eDiary compliance is monitored centrally; if compliance falls below 85%, sites are alerted and instructed to prompt participants to improve adherence. Other assessments include the ACQ-6 questionnaire, chronic airways assessment test (CAAT) test to measure health status, and patient global impression of change scale to capture participants’ overall evaluation of treatment response.

For home lung function monitoring, participants are provided with a spirometer (Spirobank Smart, MIR, Italy) and software application on a mobile phone platform (ZEPHYRx, New York, USA) to capture PEF readings every morning and evening before taking their asthma medications, including the study treatment. Predefined safety alerts, with notification to the site, are triggered by significant PEF decline, reliever use increase or symptom worsening.

An embedded validation substudy of the feasibility and concordance of clinic-based versus remote-coached spirometry is also being conducted to validate this patient-centred approach for use in future clinical trials. Remote-coached spirometry will be supervised over video conference without the participants at the site on day 3, visits 4 and 6, and on an ad hoc basis for any safety assessments deemed necessary by site staff. Real-time quality assessment against the American Thoracic Society/European Respiratory Society 2019 Spirometry Quality Standards is performed using an approved artificial intelligence (AI) algorithm (ArtiQ.QC, Clario, Leuven, Belgium).

Patient and public involvement

Two asthma patient advisors were involved throughout the design and implementation of the clinical study protocol, informed consent form and digital health components, thereby influencing the duration, acceptability and burden of study visits; devices and setting for clinical outcome assessments; and practical aspects of safety monitoring. A concerted effort was made to simplify materials and information provided to patients while ensuring adequate content to enable fully informed consent. Patients were also involved in testing and providing feedback on study inhalers and inhaler storage and were directly involved in assessing the digital health platform and devices.

Endpoints

Primary, secondary and exploratory study endpoints are shown in figure 2 and online supplemental table 2. The primary endpoint is time to first CompEx Asthma event. CompEx Asthma is a composite endpoint, capturing severe asthma exacerbation events (as defined by the Amercan Thoracic Society (ATS)/European Respiratory Society (ERS) guidelines) and clinically relevant acute worsening events (AWEs) defined by a decline in PEF and changes in patient reported outcome (PRO) variables (increase in asthma symptoms and reliever medication use).19 The methodology used to define a CompEx Asthma event has been described previously.19 20 22 23 AWEs can be based on threshold criteria met by at least one PEF variable (morning or evening) for two consecutive days, and threshold criteria met for at least one asthma symptom or reliever medication use variable (morning or evening) for the same two consecutive days. AWEs can also be met based on meeting a slope and threshold criteria where a threshold criteria is met for at least one of six variables for two consecutive days, and a slope criteria is met for all six variables when assessed over 5 days. Baseline values for CompEx components will only be calculated if at least 50% of the morning and evening measurements are present in order to ensure sufficient data availability. CompEx allows rapid assessment of improvement in asthma control and approximates magnitude of expected benefit on exacerbation risk of an investigational product, without undertaking prolonged clinical trials and minimising risk to volunteers due to this exposure (figure 3). PK will be assessed using appropriately validated bioanalytical methods. Incurred sample reproducibility analysis will be performed alongside bioanalysis of test samples. Safety will be assessed by occurrence of adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs (SAEs), including number, date and time of occurrences, intensity, nature, investigator causality rating against study drug, action for resolution and response to AE/SAE (participant withdrawal) (online supplemental appendix 1).

Exploratory analyses include CAAT-based responder analysis and assessment of biomarkers associated with the JAK/STAT pathway, time to first severe asthma exacerbation and concordance between clinic-based and remote-coached spirometry. Blood and nasal samples are being collected for biomarker analyses, with nasal lining fluid and serum used for protein biomarker analysis, and nasal and blood cells used for RNA analysis (online supplemental appendix 2).

Sample size

The sample size was calculated based on time to first CompEx Asthma event and CompEx Asthma event rate after 12 weeks of treatment. Based on the results from CALIMA (NCT01914757) and SIROCCO (NCT01928771), 0.30 was used as an estimate of the proportion of participants expected to have events in the placebo arm.24 25 Assuming a 40% reduction in events with londamocitinib (HR ~0.56), 80% power and a two-sided test with significance levels of 0.1, ~160 participants per arm and 72 CompEx events are needed to compare survival curves using a Cox proportional hazards model.

Statistical analysis

Analyses of all efficacy variables will be performed using the full analysis set. Continuous data will be summarised using descriptive statistics, and log-transformed data will be presented as geometric mean and geometric coefficient of variation. The primary endpoint of time to first CompEx Asthma event will be analysed using a Cox proportional hazards model to estimate HR for londamocitinib vs placebo and associated 90% CI and p values; treatment, ICS dose level, region and number of severe exacerbations in the previous year will be included as fixed factors, and FeNO at baseline as a covariate. Survival curves for londamocitinib versus placebo will be estimated using the Kaplan-Meier estimator and assumption of proportionality will be tested by graphical means.

Secondary and exploratory endpoints measuring change from baseline will be analysed based on a Mixed Model for Repeated Measures using data from all post-baseline assessments, with fixed effects for baseline, ICS dose strata (medium, high), region, baseline FeNO level, number of severe exacerbations in the previous year, visit, treatment and treatment-by-visit interaction; data may be log-transformed to normalise skewed distribution of endpoints, and their baselines, where appropriate. Within-participant correlation will be modelled using the unstructured covariance matrix and the Kenward-Roger approximation to estimate denominator df. Analyses will be performed without imputation of missing values. Treatment effect and differences, along with their SE and two-sided 90% CI, and the p value corresponding to the between-treatment arm difference, will be estimated using contrasts of least squares means on the corresponding treatment-by-visit interaction. Secondary time-to-event endpoints will be analysed as described for the primary endpoint. This approach is considered appropriate for detecting potential efficacy signals and informing subsequent stages of clinical development.26

Safety analyses will be performed using the safety set; individual safety and tolerability data will be provided in data listings and summarised as appropriate by treatment. For PK analyses, plasma concentrations will be summarised by timepoint in all participants, with geometric SD presented in addition to geometric mean and geometric coefficient of variation.

Discussion

Londamocitinib is an inhaled JAK1 inhibitor being developed for asthma treatment that is being evaluated in the AJAX study in adults with moderate-to-severe uncontrolled asthma on medium-to-high-dose ICS–LABA. To improve recruitment and participant diversity, AJAX includes several novel features including a partially decentralised design, an expanded definition of asthma for study eligibility (BD response not required), inclusion of several countries not traditionally included in early-phase studies and use of innovative assessment tools such as the CompEx Asthma endpoint19 20 and remote-coached spirometry. AJAX also uses CAAT rather than the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire to assess health-related quality of life; CAAT has been shown to be effective and easy for participants to complete, is significantly shorter and less complex than the St. George’s Respiratory Questionnaire, and provides valuable patient-perspective data.27 28 The AJAX trial is prospectively collecting nasal and blood samples for extensive multiomics analysis, which may identify novel, minimally invasive biomarkers associated with the JAK/STAT pathway in asthma and biomarkers predictive of clinical response to londamocitinib.

The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated the need for innovation in the design and implementation of clinical trials, catalysing the adoption of decentralised clinical trial models, and the use of artificial intelligence and web-based tools to better support patient recruitment and screening.29 30 These decentralised clinical trials, where some or all trial-related activities occur at locations other than traditional clinical sites (eg, participant homes, local healthcare facilities), have several advantages including improved trial participation for people with time and/or travel constraints, reduced costs, increased participant centricity and convenience, and better logistical efficiency.31 32 However, even though guidance has been provided by regulatory agencies including the US Food and Drug Administration and the European Medicines Agency, decentralised trials present various challenges including maintaining trial engagement, ensuring treatment compliance, reporting adherence and guaranteeing participant safety with novel treatments, particularly in early-phase studies. There is therefore a need to provide easy-to-use tools to avoid inadequate and/or inaccurate data collection, ensuring that existing regulatory frameworks are followed and that support is provided for using novel digital technology, including maintaining data security.33 34

It is essential that participants are involved from an early stage in trial design; two patients with asthma provided input into the design of AJAX, influencing study duration, acceptability and burden of visits, devices used and settings for clinical outcome assessments, and practical aspects of safety monitoring. This should make the trial and associated materials more acceptable to participants and help trial designers identify potential issues relating to participant interaction ahead of the trial start. For example, one consideration is that some participants value site visits and face-to-face contact; a flexible approach enabling both in-person and virtual visits supports these preferences. The use of a flexible design, where sites can choose how to run certain visits (in-person or remotely), may also enable better recruitment as investigators can determine how best to run the trial for their participants. It may also help reduce missing data as some missed site visits can be replaced by remote visits. One concern, however, is that use of remote assessments may increase participant burden, in particular when assessments may occur more frequently than during a traditional site-based trial. This is balanced by the fact that participants will not be travelling to sites, which reduces this logistical burden, as well as the carbon footprint of the trial. It may also result in more realistic measurements for assessed parameters being obtained, unaffected by the travel or stress of an in-person assessment.

Compared with traditional asthma trials, participant eligibility criteria for the AJAX study have been expanded, increasing the participant pool eligible for inclusion and making it more representative of the real-world asthma population. These are a combination of GINA diagnostic criteria and high FeNO levels to identify participants with asthma,1 35 instead of only relying on recall of exacerbations in previous years and BD response testing.36 37 Additionally, use of CompEx Asthma—which captures both clinically relevant AWEs and exacerbations—has enabled AJAX to be powered to detect the difference between londamocitinib and placebo with a smaller sample size and shorter treatment duration compared with powering the study for severe exacerbations, both of which should support recruitment and reduce carbon footprint. A shorter clinical trial duration reduces burden on study participants and the duration of exposure to the novel treatment or placebo, which are key ethical imperatives when designing clinical trials. It may also reduce trial costs and the time to progress to the next phase in drug development.

CompEx Asthma has been shown to be predictive of whether an intervention reduces severe asthma exacerbations in longer studies and has been included as a primary endpoint in a number of other trials, but to date, these have not been published; the only published data on the effectiveness of this endpoint comes from congress abstracts and post hoc analyses, including those used to validate the endpoint.223840 While this may be considered a study limitation, inclusion of CompEx Asthma as a primary endpoint, with severe exacerbations as a secondary endpoint, will add to the evidence base supporting CompEx Asthma as an effective endpoint for evaluating potential new asthma treatments in a cost-effective and rapid manner. The study will also contribute to the validation of remote spirometry for assessing lung function in clinical trials.

Conclusions

AJAX employs a novel, partially decentralised study design to evaluate the efficacy, safety and PK of londamocitinib in adults with moderate-to-severe asthma. This study design is optimised for delivery in primary care with high efficiency. It aims to reduce participant burden, carbon footprint and support the recruitment of a diverse trial population more representative of the real-world asthma population. Inclusion of patient voice early in the trial design process ensures that participant burden is reduced maximally and all potential concerns affecting recruitment are addressed before study commencement. By including validation of home spirometry, the trial may also help to reduce trial complexity and participant burden for future respiratory trials.

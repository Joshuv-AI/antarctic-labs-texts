Pseudo-right atrial earliest activation suggesting a functionally inferred left-sided slow conduction zone in adenosine-sensitive atrial tachycardia
Authors: Toshihiko Gokan, Yuya Nakamura, Morio Ono, Junko Honye, Toshiro Shinke
Published: 2026

Key Teaching Points •In para-Hisian adenosine-sensitive atrial tachycardia, a fragmented and spatially dispersed right atrial earliest activation pattern, resembling a “splattered-milk-like” appearance, may represent passive breakthrough from a left-sided substrate rather than the true arrhythmogenic substrate.•Stepwise biatrial directional capture mapping, using orthodromic and antidromic capture responses from right atrial, coronary sinus, and left atrial pacing sites, can help functionally infer the location and orientation of a left-sided slow conduction zone that is not directly visualized on activation mapping.•When orthodromic capture is absent from the high posteroseptal right atrial region but present from the distal coronary sinus and left atrial appendage, transseptal left atrial mapping should be considered before empirical ablation within the non-coronary aortic sinus.

Introduction

Para-Hisian adenosine-sensitive atrial tachycardia (AT) is a reentrant supraventricular tachycardia that reproducibly terminates with low-dose adenosine or adenosine 5′-triphosphate (ATP), with the earliest atrial activation site (EAAS) often recorded near the His bundle.1,2 Multidirectional capture mapping, as proposed by Yamabe et al,3,4 uses orthodromic and antidromic capture of the EAAS from different pacing sites to infer the presence and orientation of a functional slow conduction zone (SCZ) that is not directly visualized on local activation time (LAT) maps. We report a case in which the apparent para-Hisian right atrial (RA) EAAS was most consistent with passive breakthrough rather than the true substrate, thereby creating a false right-sided target. Stepwise biatrial capture mapping supported a functionally inferred left-sided SCZ and redirected ablation strategy away from empirical ablation within the non-coronary aortic sinus toward a transseptal left atrial (LA) approach.

Case report

A 66-year-old man without structural heart disease presented with recurrent palpitations and exertional dyspnea. Sinus rhythm electrocardiography and transthoracic echocardiography were unremarkable. During the electrophysiology study, multipolar catheters were positioned in the high RA, His bundle region, right ventricular apex, and coronary sinus (CS). Baseline Atrial–His and His–ventricle intervals were within normal limits, and the clinical AT was reproducibly induced by high RA burst pacing. Ventricular entrainment during tachycardia demonstrated VA dissociation (Figure 1B). RA LAT mapping with an Advisor HD Grid catheter and the EnSite X system identified a para-Hisian EAAS (Figure 2). The earliest region was fragmented and spatially indistinct, with multiple discontinuous early breakthrough islands rather than a compact focal source, raising suspicion of passive RA breakthrough from a functionally inferred left-sided substrate. However, the mechanistic interpretation primarily relied on the subsequent biatrial directional capture responses. Capture direction was assessed at the EAAS, as described in previous entrainment studies.3,4 The last entrained atrial electrogram and the first postpacing atrial electrogram at the EAAS were designated A1 and A2, respectively. Orthodromic capture was considered present when the EAAS was activated with a long conduction time and an electrogram morphology similar to that during tachycardia, with the A1–A2 interval consistent with the tachycardia cycle length. Antidromic capture was considered present when the EAAS was directly captured by the paced wavefront, with a different local activation sequence or electrogram morphology. Because the tachycardia cycle length fluctuated mildly, overdrive pacing was performed at a pacing cycle length 10–20 ms shorter than the cycle length at that time; the figures indicate these intervals as pacing cycle length. Entrainment pacing from multiple RA sites resulted in uniform antidromic capture of the EAAS, so entrainment and pacing were performed from the distal CS, which produced orthodromic capture of the EAAS (Figure 3). The last entrainment sequence showed an atrial–His–ventricular (A2-H-V) pattern,5 and the maximal change in VA interval (ΔVA) across atrial pacing sites was 47 ms.6 These findings were diagnostic of AT.5,6 Therefore, distal CS was considered to be located upstream of the SCZ in the AT circuit, prompting transseptal LA mapping. After transseptal puncture, LA LAT mapping demonstrated a discrete EAAS in the infero-anterior LA vestibular region near the 10 o’clock position of the left atrioventricular junction, from which activation propagated centrifugally through working myocardium (Figure 4 and Supplemental Video 1). This LA EAAS preceded the para-Hisian RA earliest site by 11 ms. To localize the functional SCZ, entrainment and pacing were performed from several LA sites. The Advisor HD Grid catheter was positioned to surround the LA EAAS, allowing stable entrainment and pacing assessment around the apparent EAAS. Pacing from the septum, roof, and posterior wall resulted in antidromic capture of the LA EAAS, whereas pacing from the distal and basal LA appendage (LAA) produced orthodromic capture (Figures 5 and 6). These orthodromic responses supported the interpretation that the SCZ was functionally inferred to lie between the LAA region and the LA EAAS along the anterior LA near the basal LAA (schematically summarized in Figure 7A). Biatrial activation maps were fused with computed tomography (CT), allowing direct comparison of the spatial relationships among the non-coronary aortic sinus, the His bundle, and the successful LA ablation site (Figure 7B). The distance from the non-coronary aortic sinus to the His bundle recording site (10 mm) was shorter than the distance from the non-coronary aortic sinus to the LA EAAS (22 mm), suggesting that empirical ablation within the non-coronary aortic sinus would not have been anatomically aligned with the inferred LA substrate in this case. These anatomic relationships supported prioritizing targeted transseptal ablation of the inferred LA SCZ as a more anatomically and mechanistically appropriate strategy. In contrast, the inferred LA target within the functional SCZ was anatomically remote from the His bundle. Radiofrequency energy at 30 W delivered at this inferred SCZ site led to gradual tachycardia slowing and termination 3 seconds after energy onset (Figure 7C). The AT became noninducible, and the PR interval, AH interval, His–ventricle interval, and Wenckebach rate remained unchanged. The patient was discharged the following day and remained free from recurrent AT without antiarrhythmic drugs during 1-year follow-up.

Discussion

The principal diagnostic observation in this case is that the apparent para-Hisian RA earliest activation site behaved as a pseudo-breakthrough rather than the true substrate. A prior activation-mapping study showed that passive activation from adjacent tissue can create a pseudo-focal centrifugal pattern.7 In the present case, the fragmented and spatially dispersed RA earliest region was consistent with such a pseudo-focal breakthrough pattern. Uniform antidromic capture during entrainment pacing from multiple RA sites, followed by orthodromic capture during pacing from the distal CS and then the LAA, supported the interpretation that a functionally inferred left-sided SCZ was present. As pacing advanced stepwise from the LAA toward the LA earliest activation site, the transition from orthodromic to antidromic capture, together with tachycardia termination near that site, supported the interpretation that the LA earliest activation site was functionally linked to the circuit and likely represented the exit or an immediate downstream breakout.

The anatomical substrate underlying this observation should be interpreted with caution. The SCZ was not directly visualized but was functionally inferred from the directional capture responses, activation sequence, and successful ablation site. Because the entire circuit of adenosine-sensitive para-Hisian AT is generally not delineated by activation mapping alone, circuit size and microreentry or macroreentry classification could not be determined. Nonetheless, ATP sensitivity, established diagnostic criteria, directional capture responses, and termination at the inferred SCZ supported a reentrant mechanism. In the present case, the relevant region was most consistent with the LA vestibular region near the left atrioventricular junction, with possible involvement of the anterior interatrial groove. This anatomical interpretation may explain why the initial activation pattern appeared to arise from the para-Hisian RA. The anterior interatrial region contains myocardial continuities between the atrial chambers, including Bachmann’s bundle, and the atrial vestibules are also in myocardial contiguity in this region.8,9 Therefore, activation emerging from an LA vestibular or anterior interatrial groove region could produce fragmented breakthrough into the para-Hisian RA, creating the misleading impression of a right-sided substrate.

Prior reports from our group have described left-sided adenosine-sensitive AT and atypical left-sided SCZ orientation.10,11 In contrast, the present case emphasizes that the initial para-Hisian RA earliest activation pattern itself may be misleading, representing passive breakthrough that can create a false right-sided or non-coronary aortic sinus target. The absence of orthodromic capture from the clinically termed high posteroseptal RA region, together with orthodromic capture from the distal CS and LAA region, supported a left-sided SCZ and made empirical ablation within the non-coronary aortic sinus less appropriate. CT-integrated mapping strengthened this procedural implication because the non-coronary aortic sinus was closer to the His bundle than to the successful LA ablation site, whereas the transseptal LA approach allowed targeted ablation at the inferred SCZ remote from the His bundle.

Limitations

This report has several limitations. First, it describes a single case. Second, the SCZ and putative circuit exit were inferred from directional pacing responses and activation timing rather than directly visualized. Third, systematic postpacing interval mapping in the LA was not performed because the tachycardia cycle length fluctuated, making its interpretation less reliable. Fourth, ablation within the non-coronary aortic sinus was not performed in this patient; therefore, the relative efficacy of that approach remains inferential. Nevertheless, the reproducible biatrial capture responses, CT-integrated spatial relationships, and successful transseptal ablation support, but do not prove, the proposed mechanism.

Conclusion

In para-Hisian adenosine-sensitive AT, an apparent RA earliest activation site may represent pseudo-EAAS reflecting passive breakthrough from a functionally inferred left-sided substrate rather than the true substrate. Stepwise biatrial capture mapping may functionally localize a left-sided SCZ, support that the LA EAAS is linked to the circuit as the exit or an immediate downstream breakout, and help avoid empirical ablation within the non-coronary aortic sinus when that approach is not supported by pacing responses.

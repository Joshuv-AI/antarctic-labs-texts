Trilogy Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation for Freestyle Graft With Flail Left Coronary Cusp
Authors: Abdulaziz Al-Shaibi, Alice Moroni, Daniel Burns, Sami Alnasser, Neil P. Fam
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Case SummaryThis clinical vignette describes successful transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) in a Freestyle graft with a flail left coronary cusp using a 27-mm Trilogy valve (JenaValve Technology). Using a Trilogy valve in cases with a flail cusp raises concerns of insufficient valve sealing and stability; our report demonstrates the feasibility and safety of the procedure with excellent procedural outcomes.Take-Home MessagesTAVI using the Trilogy valve for degenerated Freestyle graft with flail left coronary cusp is feasible, with several advantages over commercially available TAVI platforms. However, additional cases are required to demonstrate its safety and reproducibility.

Clinical Vignette

A 71-year-old man presented with worsening dyspnea. Past history included Freestyle 27-mm aortic root graft and ascending aortic replacement. Transesophageal echocardiography showed severe aortic regurgitation due to a flail left coronary cusp (LCC) (Figure 1A and 1B, Video 1) with dilated left ventricle. Computed tomography measured an aortic annular perimeter of 84 mm with a heavily calcified aortic root but minimal leaflet calcification, an average sinus diameter of 38.6 mm, and left main and right coronary artery heights of 7.2 mm and 10.8 mm, respectively. TAVI was performed under conscious sedation without transesophageal echocardiography guidance via the right femoral approach. After confirming valve locator position at the base of each cusp, the valve was deployed under rapid pacing (Figures 1C to 1E, Video 2). Aortography demonstrated trace paravalvular leak and patent coronary arteries with no change in rhythm (Figure 1F, Video 2). Echocardiography showed a well-functioning valve with a mean gradient of 2 mm Hg and no paravalvular leak (Figure 1G and 1H, Video 3), allowing for discharge home the following day.

Discussion

Valve-in-valve TAVI for degenerated Freestyle valves is technically challenging because of the stentless frame with limited anchoring support and distortion of the aortic root anatomy, which can lead to a higher risk of complications using commercially available TAVI platforms, including valve malposition and coronary obstruction.2 Dedicated TAVI devices for aortic regurgitation, such as the Trilogy valve, provide active fixation by radial force generated by the nitinol frame, along with 3 locators positioned within each sinus to secure the native aortic valve leaflets between the locators and valve frame, thereby enhancing anchoring and stability in the absence of leaflet or annular calcium. To our knowledge, this is the first report of Trilogy for a degenerated Freestyle valve with flail LCC. Although TAVI for failed Freestyle grafts using Trilogy is promising, concerns exist regarding potentially insufficient valve sealing and stability in cases with flail LCC3; our report demonstrates the feasibility and safety of the procedure, with excellent procedural outcomes.

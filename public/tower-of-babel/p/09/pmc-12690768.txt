SPCT-15 TOTAL POSTERIOR VERTEBRECTOMY IN BRAIN TUMORS : RESULTS OF A SERIES OF 10 CASES.
Authors: Soress Dongo, Bernard Fionko, Konan Serges Yao, Landry Teti, Jean-Baptiste Keke, Aboubacar Traoré, Ange Yao, Désirée Koffi, Dzidoula Lawson, Jorel Mbiakeu, Louis Derou, Valentin Tokpa, Aderehime Haidara
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionBrain tumors, whether primary or secondary, are a common cause of spinal cord compression, requiring surgical treatment. If radiotherapy has become the reference strategy, particularly in metastatic cases, certain situations still require circumferential decompression associated with spinal stabilization. This study aims to evaluate the feasibility, safety and medium-term results of total vertebrectomy performed by a single posterior approach.MethodsThis is a retrospective study including 10 preferential patients between 2018 and 2023 for a spinal tumor (6 primary tumors, 4 metastases). All received a total vertebrectomy via an exclusive posterior approach, performed by the same surgeon. The parameters analyzed included : operating time, blood loss, complications, spinal stability, pain, functional recovery and bone fusion.ResultsThe mean age of patients was 64.4 ± 10.9 years, with a mean follow-up of 730 ± 200 days. Surgical exposure, although complex, allowed for immediate posterior instrumentation. The average operating time was 4 hours. The complication rate was 20%, with two cases of postoperative hemothorax. At 6 months, functionality was recorded in the majority of cases. Satisfactory bone fusion was noted in most patients at one year.ConclusionSingle posterior vertebrectomy constitutes a reliable and effective surgical technique. It allows adequate spinal cord decompression, ensures spinal stability and contributes to pain relief, particularly in cases requiring complete care.

Efficacy and safety of tazemetostat, an EZH2 inhibitor, in Chinese patients with relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma: a multicentre, single-arm, phase 2 study
Authors: Junning Cao, Guangliang Chen, Lihua Qiu, Liling Zhang, Ming Jiang, Ying Cheng, Qiaohua Zhang, Lihong Liu, Ping Li, Yuerong Shuang, Huaqing Wang, Hongwei Xue, Huijing Wu, Meifang Zheng, Keshu Zhou, Zhiming Li, Hongmei Jing, Wei Yang, Zunmin Zhu, Wenyu Li, Jiaxuan Wangwu, Heyu Huang, Qiantao Jia, Dongmei Chen, Songhua Fan, M Ming Shi, Weiguo Su
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundTazemetostat, the first enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) inhibitor approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration, has shown efficacy in a global population with relapsed or refractory (R/R) follicular lymphoma (FL). This phase 2 study was primarily designed as a registration-intent bridging trial of tazemetostat in Chinese patients with EZH2-mutant (EZH2mut) R/R FL; the study also included evaluation in the EZH2 wild-type (EZH2wt) population.MethodsThis multicentre, single-arm, phase 2 study was conducted at 19 sites in China. Eligible patients (aged ≥18 years) with histologically confirmed grade 1–3a FL were categorised by EZH2 status: mutant (EZH2mut, bridging cohort) and wild-type (EZH2wt), and received oral tazemetostat 800 mg twice daily in continuous 28-day cycles until investigator-assessed disease progression, intolerable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or other protocol-specified criteria. The EZH2mut cohort primary endpoint was independent review committee (IRC)-assessed objective response rate (ORR). Efficacy in the EZH2mut cohort was assessed in patients who received at least one dose of tazemetostat and had centrally confirmed FL. Efficacy in the EZH2wt cohort and safety in both cohorts were assessed in all patients who received at least one dose of tazemetostat. This study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT05467943.FindingsBetween July 29, 2022, and Aug 31, 2023, 22 patients with EZH2mut R/R FL were enrolled (three lines [3L] of prior therapy, 31.8%; four lines [4L] of prior therapy or more, 27.3%). As of Aug 31, 2024 (median follow-up 14.4 months), IRC-assessed ORR was 63.6% (95% confidence interval [CI], 40.7%–82.8%), clinical benefit rate (CBR) was 90.9% (70.8%–98.9%), median duration of response (DoR) was not reached (18-month DoR rate, 51.6% [18.2%–77.3%]), and median progression-free survival was 15.4 (8.2–not estimable) months. All patients experienced treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), and 13.6% had grade ≥3 TRAEs; most common TRAEs were haematological toxicities. For the EZH2wt cohort, 20 patients were enrolled (3L, 35.0%; ≥4L, 45.0%). Tazemetostat also demonstrated efficacy benefit and a manageable safety profile in the EZH2wt cohort, with an investigator-assessed ORR of 35.0% (95% CI, 15.4%–59.2%), CBR of 85.0% (62.1%–96.8%), and median DoR of 8.4 (1.9–15.7) months.InterpretationThese results suggest that tazemetostat has a clinically meaningful efficacy and was well tolerated in Chinese patients with R/R FL. Larger trials are warranted. A multicentre, randomised, phase 3 trial of tazemetostat combined with lenalidomide plus rituximab vs. lenalidomide plus rituximab in patients with R/R FL is ongoing (NCT04224493).FundingHUTCHMED.Keywords: Follicular lymphoma, EZH2 inhibitor, Bridging study, Tazemetostat, Chinese

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Tazemetostat is the first histone methyltransferase enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) inhibitor approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for treatment of relapsed or refractory (R/R) follicular lymphoma (FL) with EZH2 mutation in patients who have received two or more prior systemic therapies and in those with R/R FL who have no satisfactory alternative treatment options. Clinical data showing the efficacy of treatment with tazemetostat in Chinese patients are lacking. We searched PubMed on Feb 27, 2025, to check for relevant publications with the following terms “histone methyltransferase enhancer of zeste homolog 2” OR “EZH2” AND “Chinese” OR “China” AND “follicular lymphoma”, with no restrictions on date or language. The search yielded 17 results, none of which were clinical studies. One publication assessed the frequency of EZH2 mutations in Chinese and Western populations of patients with FL, finding that prevalence of these mutations was similar between the two groups and was suggestive of a shared pathogenetic mechanism.

Added value of this study

This trial (NCT05467943) included a registration-intent cohort and primarily served as a bridging study for tazemetostat in Chinese patients with EZH2-mutant (EZH2mut) FL, evaluating the efficacy and safety of tazemetostat in a Chinese population and determining the consistency of treatment benefit with that of the global population study (NCT01897571). This bridging study met the primary endpoint of independent review committee-assessed objective response rate, and the safety profile was generally consistent with that of the global study, with the most common events being haematological toxicities. Additionally, an EZH2 wild-type cohort was included to provide complementary data of tazemetostat in a broader Chinese population.

Implications of all the available evidence

This is the first study reporting clinical results of an EZH2 inhibitor in a population of Chinese patients with relapsed or refractory FL. Several therapies for patients with relapsed/refractory FL following two or more prior antitumour therapies are currently available, including phosphoinositide 3-kinase inhibitors, a Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor, a bispecific antibody, and chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapies. Whilst these available treatments show positive efficacy results, most (except for the BTK inhibitor) also come with black box warnings for serious adverse events. Results with tazemetostat indicated a manageable safety profile, with no noticeable safety signals. Larger trials are needed. The clinical outcomes of tazemetostat combined with lenalidomide plus rituximab in patients with R/R FL will be further assessed in an ongoing randomised phase 3 trial (NCT04224493).

Introduction

Follicular lymphoma (FL) is the most common indolent non-Hodgkin's lymphoma.1 In China, the age-standardised incidence, mortality, and prevalence rates of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma in 2019 were 4.99, 2.32, and 21.95 per 100,000 population, respectively, with the proportion of FL estimated to be 8.1%–23.5%.2,3 Due to the indolent nature of the disease, 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates of FL are relatively high,4 meaning many patients require additional lines of treatment following relapse. With each subsequent line of treatment, decreased response rates have been observed, highlighting the unmet need for effective subsequent treatments for patients with relapsing/refractory FL.5

Current treatments for patients with FL include involved-site radiotherapy, anti-CD20–based immunotherapy, chemotherapy, or immunochemotherapy.6,7 In recent years, novel treatment options for patients with relapsing or refractory disease have become available, including bispecific antibodies (mosunetuzumab and epcoritamab), chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy (CAR-T; axicabtagene ciloleucel, tisagenlecleucel, and relmacabtagene autoleucel), and phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) inhibitors (copanlisib, duvelisib, linperlisib, and umbralisib).8, 9, 10 Whilst all show improvements in PFS, adverse events such as cytokine release syndrome, immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity, infection, and cytopenia are included as warnings in their prescribing information.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 The PI3K inhibitors duvelisib and umbralisib have been voluntarily withdrawn from the market for FL.17

Patients’ health has usually deteriorated following several lines of antitumour treatment; the efficacy and safety balance is of great concern when choosing suitable treatments for these patients, particularly for older individuals.7,8

Tazemetostat is a first-in-class, selective, oral inhibitor of histone methyltransferase enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2) approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of EZH2-mutant (EZH2mut) relapsed or refractory FL in patients who have received two or more prior systemic therapies and in those with relapsed or refractory FL who have no satisfactory alternative treatment options.18 EZH2 is a target for antitumour therapy due to its role in the regulation of cell cycle progression; dysregulation of EZH2 accelerates cell proliferation, prolongs survival, and has a potential role in tumour metastasis.19 In a global study (NCT01897571), tazemetostat monotherapy showed antitumour activity and a favourable safety profile in patients with relapsed or refractory non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, and the recommended dose was determined as 800 mg twice daily.20 During phase 2, the objective response rate (ORR) of tazemetostat was 69% (95% confidence interval [CI], 53%–82%), and median duration of response (DoR) was 10.9 (95% CI, 7.2–not estimable [NE]) months as assessed by IRC in the EZH2mut FL cohort, which led to an accelerated approval from the FDA in June 2020.21

The efficacy data for tazemetostat in Chinese patients are lacking. Here, we describe results of a phase 2 study (NCT05467943) evaluating the efficacy and safety of tazemetostat in Chinese patients with relapsed or refractory FL. The trial was designed as a registration-intent bridging study for the EZH2mut cohort to evaluate therapeutic consistency between Chinese patients and the global study population (NCT01897571), whilst also including investigation in the EZH2 wild-type (EZH2wt) cohort.

Methods

Study design and participants

This was a multicentre, open-label, phase 2 study conducted at 19 sites in China (site details given in the Supplementary Materials). Patients with EZH2mut FL were enrolled into the registration-intent bridging cohort (EZH2mut cohort) to corroborate that the efficacy and safety clinical data for tazemetostat in Chinese patients with EZH2mut FL are similar to those in the prior global study; patients with EZH2wt FL were enrolled into the EZH2wt cohort to further enrich data of tazemetostat in the Chinese population. Neither patients nor the public were involved in the design, conduct, or reporting of the trial. All participating sites were hospitals with an expertise in oncology and the investigators were experienced oncologists.

This study enrolled adults ≥18 years of age who had histologically confirmed relapsed or refractory (to two or more standard systemic therapies, including one or more anti-CD20 therapy) FL (grades 1–3a), sufficient tissue available for central pathological review and detection of their EZH2 mutation status, one or more measurable lesion detectable by enhanced computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), a life expectancy of ≥12 weeks, an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) of 0–2, and adequate renal, hepatic, and bone marrow function. Key exclusion criteria included prior treatment with tazemetostat or other EZH2 inhibitors and tumour invasion of the central nervous system, leptomeningeal, or brain. Full inclusion and exclusion criteria are available in the study protocol in the Supplementary Materials.

Ethics

Prior to commencement of the study, independent ethics committee approval was obtained at Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center (approval number 2201249-22) and all participating study sites (approval numbers are provided in the Supplementary Materials). The study was conducted in accordance with the International Conference for Harmonisation Good Clinical Practice Guideline, the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and local regulations. Written informed consent was obtained from patients ahead of screening and enrolment into the clinical study. The study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05467943).

Randomisation and masking

This was a single-arm, open-label study. Randomisation and masking were not performed.

Procedures

Enrolled patients received 800 mg of tazemetostat orally twice daily in continuous 28-day cycles until investigator-assessed disease progression (PD), intolerable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or other protocol-specified criteria, whichever came first.

To assess EZH2 mutation status, central EZH2 testing was completed using the cobas® EZH2 Mutation Test (Roche Molecular Systems, Pleasanton, CA, USA).

Patients underwent enhanced CT or MRI scans before the administration of the first dose of tazemetostat every 8 weeks until week 24, every 12 weeks until week 96, and then every 24 weeks thereafter until investigator-assessed PD or recurrence. Positron emission tomography (PET)-CT scans with 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) contrast, including the cervical, thoracic, abdominal, and pelvic regions, were conducted at baseline and possible complete response (CR). If there was bone marrow involvement at screening, a repeat bone marrow biopsy was required when CT/MRI was suggestive of CR. Tumour responses were assessed by an independent review committee (IRC) and investigator using the International Working Group non-Hodgkin's lymphoma response criteria 2007 (IWG-NHL 2007).22

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities version 26.1, graded using the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0, and were evaluated throughout the study.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint of the study was IRC-assessed ORR in patients from the EZH2mut cohort according to IWG-NHL 2007, where ORR was defined as the proportion of patients with a best overall response (BOR) of CR or partial response (PR). Secondary endpoints included IRC-assessed CR rate, clinical benefit rate (CBR), DoR, time to response (TTR), and PFS for the EZH2mut cohort only, and investigator-assessed ORR, CR rate, DoR, CBR, TTR, PFS, and OS for both cohorts. BOR was defined as the best response from the time of first administration until PD, death, withdrawal of consent, start of any subsequent antitumour therapy, or data cutoff, whichever occurred first. CR rate was defined as the proportion of patients with a BOR of CR. CBR was the proportion of patients with a BOR of CR, PR, and stable disease (SD). DoR was defined as the time from the first CR/PR to disease recurrence/progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. TTR was defined as the time from the first study treatment dose to the time of first objective response. PFS was defined as the time from the first study treatment dose to disease recurrence/progression or death from any cause, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the time from the first study treatment dose until death from any cause.

The incidence and severity of TEAEs in the two cohorts were also evaluated as secondary endpoints, as were other safety endpoints, including physical examination, vital signs, laboratory tests, and 12-lead electrocardiography.

Statistical analysis

The EZH2mut cohort was designed for the bridging study. In consultation with the Chinese regulatory authority (National Medical Products Administration [NMPA]), it was determined that the clinical benefit in Chinese patients would be considered consistent with the global population if the observed ORR in this study was not lower than the lower-bound 95% CI for the ORR in the global registration cohort (53.4%).21 Assuming a true ORR value of 69%, with a sample size of 17 patients there was at least a 90% probability of observing an ORR no lower than 53.4%. Considering that approximately 10% of patients may not have centrally confirmed FL, 19 patients with EZH2mut FL were planned to be enrolled. The EZH2wt cohort served as an independent cohort and was included without formal hypothesis testing to evaluate the safety and efficacy profile of tazemetostat in EZH2wt FL. Approximately 20 patients with EZH2wt FL were planned to be enrolled. Further details on the determination and calculation of sample size can be found in the Supplementary Materials.

The full analysis set (FAS) consisted of all patients who received at least one dose of tazemetostat and had centrally confirmed FL; this analysis set was primarily used for evaluating the efficacy endpoints of the EZH2mut cohort. The safety analysis set (SS), comprising all patients who received at least one dose of tazemetostat, was used for evaluating safety endpoints in both cohorts and efficacy endpoints in the EZH2wt cohort.

The Clopper–Pearson method was used to calculate two-sided 95% CIs of ORR, CR rate, and CBR. Time-to-event indicators (DoR, PFS, and OS) were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and medians and associated two-sided 95% CIs reported with the Brookmeyer–Crowley method. For DoR and PFS, patients without PD or who did not die were censored at the date of the last tumour assessment; for OS, patients who did not die were censored at the last date known to be alive. More detailed censoring rules for PFS, DoR, and OS are provided in the Supplementary Materials. TTR was summarised using descriptive statistics of the responders. The concordance between the IRC- and investigator-assessed ORR and BOR was calculated in the EZH2mut cohort.

Subgroup analysis on the ORR was also performed according to the following subgroups: refractory to the last treatment (yes/no), refractory to anti-CD20 treatment (yes/no), double refractory (yes/no), disease progression within 24 months (POD24) (yes/no), age groups (<65/≥65/≥75 years old), prior radiotherapy (yes/no), investigator-reported larger mass with longest diameter ≥10 cm (present/absent), sex (male/female), number of prior lines of therapy (≤2/>2), prior PI3K use (yes/no), time from last prior antitumour therapy to first dose of tazemetostat (≤1/>1 month), and Groupe d'Etude des Lymphomes Folliculaires (GELF) (yes/no). Partially completed dates were completed using rules as described in the statistical analysis plan provided in the Supplementary Materials. All statistical analyses were performed using SAS Enterprise® version 8.3. The full protocol and statistical analysis plan are available as Supplementary Materials.

Role of the funding source

HUTCHMED funded the study. The funder of this study was involved in the study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, the preparation of the manuscript, and the decision to submit this manuscript for publication.

Results

Patient enrolment and baseline characteristics

Between July 29, 2022 and Aug 31, 2023, 22 eligible patients with EZH2mut FL from 13 sites in China were enrolled in the EZH2mut cohort and received tazemetostat; these patients were included in both the FAS and SS (Fig. 1). The median age of these patients was 53.5 (range, 35–72) years; most were male (68.2%). All patients harboured EZH2 mutations, distributed as Y646N (n = 9 [40.9%]), Y646X (n = 7 [31.8%]), Y646F (n = 5 [22.7%]), and A692V (n = 2 [9.1%]). All had an ECOG PS score of 0 (54.5%) or 1 (45.5%). A large proportion of patients had at least one risk factor for a poor prognosis, including being refractory to anti-CD20 treatment (68.2%), having POD24 (68.2%), or being double refractory (45.5%) (Table 1). All patients had received prior antitumour therapy, with a median of 3 (range, 2–5) lines. All patients had received CD20 inhibitors, and 9 (40.9%) patients had received PI3K inhibitors.

There were 20 eligible patients with the EZH2wt FL from 13 sites in China enrolled in the EZH2wt cohort, all of whom were included in the SS (Fig. 1). Patients in EZH2wt cohort exhibited similar baseline characteristics to those in the EZH2mut cohort, with potentially worse prognosis, which was evidenced by a higher proportion of patients who were treated with later lines of therapy (four lines of prior therapy or more, 45.0%), met GELF criteria (70.0%), had POD24 (80.0%), and presented with myelosuppression (45.0%).

Efficacy

As of Aug 31, 2024, patients in the EZH2mut cohort were followed up for a median of 14.4 months. Based on the FAS (n = 22), the IRC-assessed ORR for the EZH2mut cohort was 63.6% (95% CI, 40.7%–82.8%), which met the study's primary endpoint, with 4 (18.2%) patients achieving a CR and 10 (45.5%) achieving a PR at the data cutoff (Table 2). Moreover, 6 (27.3%) patients had SD, resulting in a CBR of 90.9% (95% CI, 70.8%–98.9%). The target lesion size in the EZH2mut cohort decreased from baseline in 19 (86.4%) of 22 patients per IRC (Fig. 2A). The IRC-assessed median DoR was not reached, with 12-month and 18-month DoR rates of 51.6% (95% CI, 18.2%–77.3%). Seven (50.0%) patients had a response lasting for ≥9 months, 4 (28.6%) for ≥12 months, and 2 (14.3%) for ≥18 months (Fig. 2B). The median PFS was 15.4 (95% CI, 8.2–NE) months (Fig. 2C), and the estimated 12-month and 18-month PFS rates were 62.0% (95% CI, 37.9%–79.0%) and 38.7% (95% CI, 12.3%–65.1%), respectively. Of 14 responders (CR + PR), the treatment duration was over 12 months for all 4 patients who achieved CRs and 6 of the 10 patients who achieved PRs as their BOR (Fig. 2D). The IRC-assessed median TTR was 3.7 (range, 1.7–8.2) months. At data cutoff, 2 (9.1%) patients had died. Median OS was not reached, and the 12-month and 18-month OS rates were 100.0% (95% CI, 100.0%–100.0%) and 87.5% (95% CI, 38.7%–98.1%), respectively. The results of tumour response assessed by investigator were similar to those of the IRC assessment. Concordance between the assessment of responses by the IRC and investigators was high (18 [81.8%] of 22 patients). The investigator-assessed ORR was 68.2%; 4 (18.2%) patients achieved a CR and 11 (50.0%) patients achieved a PR (Table 2). Investigator-assessed median PFS was 15.4 (95% CI, 5.4–NE) months, and the estimated 18-month PFS rate was 46.7% (95% CI, 20.1%–69.6%). For the EZH2wt cohort (n = 20), with a median follow-up of 21.4 months at data cutoff, the investigator-assessed ORR was 35.0% (95% CI, 15.4%–59.2%) (Table 2). No patients achieved a CR, 7 (35.0%) had a PR and 10 (50.0%) had SD, resulting in a CBR of 85.0% (95% CI, 62.1%–96.8%). Safety

As of Aug 31, 2024, the median duration of treatment in the EZH2mut cohort (n = 22) was 11.9 (range, 1.9–24.0) months, with a median of 13.0 (range, 2.0–26.0) cycles administered. The median relative dose intensity was 98.9% (range, 58.6%–100.0%). All 22 patients (100%) experienced TEAEs and treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs). Grade ≥3 TEAEs occurred in 8 (36.4%) patients, and grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 3 (13.6%) patients.

Table 3 summarises common TEAEs and TRAEs. The most frequent TRAEs in the EZH2mut cohort included platelet count decreased (36.4%), white blood cell count decreased (31.8%), anaemia and hypertriglyceridaemia (27.3% each), hyperuricaemia (22.7%), neutrophil count decreased, lymphocyte count decreased, and hypercholesterolaemia (18.2% each), and nausea (13.6%). Grade ≥3 TRAEs comprised neutrophil count decreased (9.1%), white blood cell count decreased (9.1%), and lymphocyte count decreased (4.5%). Serious TRAEs occurred in 2 patients (9.1%), including peripheral oedema (4.5%) and pneumonia (4.5%). In the EZH2mut cohort, TEAEs led to dose interruption in 9 patients (40.9%), with neutrophil count decreased and white blood cell count decreased (9.1% each) occurring in ≥2 patients. Only 1 patient experienced dose reduction due to a TEAE (neutrophil count decreased, 4.5%). Two patients had drug discontinuation due to TEAEs (neutrophil count decreased and gliomatosis cerebri, 4.5% each), among which the event of neutrophil count decreased was considered to be study treatment related. There were no on-treatment deaths (defined as patient deaths within 30 days of the last dose of study treatment or initiation of subsequent antitumour therapy, whichever occurred first) and no TEAEs led to death in the EZH2mut cohort.

For the overall FL population (n = 42), the median duration of treatment was 8.4 (range, 1.0–24.0) months, and the median relative dose intensity was 98.4% (range, 55.6%–100.0%). The safety profile of the overall FL population was generally comparable to that observed in the EZH2mut cohort. A total of 41 (97.6%) patients experienced TEAEs, with 39 (92.9%) patients experiencing TRAEs. The most common TRAEs were platelet count decreased (54.8%), white blood cell count decreased (35.7%), anaemia (33.3%), neutrophil count decreased (28.6%), lymphocyte count decreased (23.8%), and hyperuricaemia (21.4%). TEAEs led to dose interruption in 22 (52.4%) patients, including COVID-19 pneumonia (9.5%), COVID-19 (7.1%), and other events occurring in less than 3 patients. Four (9.5%) patients had TEAEs leading to dose reduction (platelet count decreased, 4.8%; muscle spasm and neutrophil count decreased, 2.4% each). Three (7.1%) patients discontinued treatment due to a TEAE (platelet count decreased, neutrophil count decreased and gliomatosis cerebri, 2.4% each). One (2.4%) patient from the EZH2wt cohort had a TEAE leading to death (COVID-19), which was considered not related to the study treatment.

Discussion

Tazemetostat is the first globally approved EZH2 inhibitor; however, evidence specific to the Chinese population remained lacking. This study addressed this critical gap. The IRC-assessed ORR achieved 63.6% in the EZH2mut cohort, meeting the primary endpoint (IRC-assessed ORR was not below the 95% CI lower-bound for the ORR in the global study). Efficacy outcomes, including CBR, DoR, and PFS, in the Chinese patient population also demonstrated consistency with the results of the global EZH2mut population.21 IRC-assessed CBR was 90.9% (CR rate: 18.2%), and 86.4% of patients showed evidence of a reduction in the target lesion tumour burden. Of the 14 patients who had an objective response (CR/PR), 50.0% had a response lasting for 9 months or more, 28.6% for 12 months or more, and 14.3% for 18 months or more. The durable responses were also reflected in the treatment duration, as treatment duration was ≥12 months for 10 of the 14 responders.21

It is worth noting that patients with EZH2mut FL in the current study had a higher burden of disease than the EZH2mut global study population—the baseline disease characteristics indicated more severe disease, with higher proportions of patients having risk factors for poor prognosis (POD24, refractory to anti-CD20, and double-refractory), a higher median prior number of antitumour therapies (median: 3, 59.1% of patients receiving three or more prior therapies), and a higher percentage of patients who had relapsed following treatment with a PI3K inhibitor.21

Although the subgroup analysis should be interpreted with caution due to the small sample size in each subgroup, the results indicated that patients with EZH2mut FL who were at higher risk for poor prognosis also derived benefit from treatment with tazemetostat. ORRs for patients with POD24 or who were double refractory, had large tumour mass, or met GELF criteria were 53.3%, 60.0%, 60.0%, and 66.7%, respectively.

Patients with EZH2wt FL also showed benefit from tazemetostat, with an ORR of 35.0% and CBR of 85.0%. The EZH2wt cohort exhibited shorter DoR and PFS compared to the global trial, which may be attributed to the worse disease prognosis at baseline, including more patients who had received later lines of treatment (median: 3, 80% of patients receiving three or more prior therapies) and higher proportion exposed to PI3K inhibitors.

Overall, the safety profile of tazemetostat in Chinese patients with relapsed or refractory FL was consistent with that observed in the global population, with no new safety signals identified. The most common TRAEs were platelet count decreased, white blood cell count decreased, anaemia, neutrophil count decreased, lymphocyte count decreased, and hyperuricaemia. Furthermore, a higher incidence of haematologic laboratory TEAEs was reported in Chinese patients with relapsed or refractory FL (n = 42) vs. the global FL population (n = 99; platelet count decreased 55% vs. thrombocytopenia 10%; white blood cell count decreased 36% vs. leucopenia 3%); however, no significant differences were found compared with the “more objective” laboratory safety assessment (decreased platelets 52% vs. 50%; decreased white blood cells 38% vs. 41%), considering that the lower frequency of laboratory abnormalities were reported as adverse events (AEs) in the global FL population.23 For the incidences of hyperlipidaemia in the EZH2mut population (n = 22), most abnormalities of total cholesterol and triglycerides fluctuated around the upper limit of normal and returned to normal without intervention (Supplementary Fig. S2). These findings indicated the safety profile of tazemetostat 800 mg twice daily was manageable and generally well tolerated in Chinese patients with relapsed or refractory FL.

In recent years, a number of therapeutic options for patients with relapsed/refractory FL following two or more prior antitumour therapies have become available. ORRs of the PI3K inhibitors (copanlisib, duvelisib, and linperlisib) range from 42.2% to 79.8% (CR rate, 1.2%–20.2%); 36.4% (CR rate, 18.2%) for the Bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitor zanubrutinib, 80.0% (CR rate, 60.0%) for the bispecific antibody mosuntuzumab, and 86.2%–94.0% (CR rate, 68.1%–79.0%) for CAR-T therapies (axicabtagene ciloleucel and tisagenlecleucel).24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30 The PFS for these therapies ranged from 9.5 to 17.9 months (PFS for the CAR-T therapies was not reached).24, 25, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30 Whilst these treatments show positive efficacy results, most (except for the BTK inhibitor) also come with black box warnings for serious adverse events, including cytokine release syndrome and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome for the bispecific antibody; cytokine release syndrome, neurologic toxicities, and secondary haematological malignancies for the CAR-T therapies; and serious infections for the PI3K inhibitors.11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 For the bispecific antibodies, serious infections were reported in up to 40% of patients with FL and included COVID-19, pneumonia, and urinary tract infections, with up to 6% of patients having fatal infections (COVID-19 being the most common).11,13 A recent study also highlighted the lower relative risk of adverse events following treatment with tazemetostat vs. PI3K inhibitors.31 A Chinese Expert Consensus for FL also recommended tazemetostat as a second-line systemic therapy for patients who are older or unwell, indicating the product has a good tolerability profile.32 Additionally, as tazemetostat is an oral treatment, it is more convenient for patients than treatments that require infusions. Results from this study and the global study indicate that tazemetostat has a manageable safety profile, with no noticeable safety signals.21

The sample size of this study was limited as the registration-intent cohort was designed as a bridging study in which the efficacy boundary was agreed with the Chinese regulatory authority (NMPA) to indicate consistency of results with the global study. The non-randomised study design, lacking the comparison with other existing regimens, was a limitation in this study. A randomised, controlled study (NCT04224493, SYMPHONY-1) to assess the efficacy, safety, pharmacodynamics, and pharmacokinetics of tazemetostat with lenalidomide plus rituximab vs. placebo with lenalidomide plus rituximab in patients with relapsed or refractory FL is ongoing. Furthermore, the responses in the EZH2wt cohort were assessed exclusively by investigators without IRC review, while those in the EZH2mut cohort were assessed by both IRC and investigators. In addition, patients aged ≥75 years were underrepresented (only 1 patient enrolled) and no patients with an ECOG PS of 2 were enrolled in the present study, which potentially restricts generalisability of findings to the older or frail population. However, age (16–91 years) has been confirmed to have no clinically meaningful effects on tazemetostat pharmacokinetics, suggesting that the treatment benefit observed may likely extend to elderly patients as well; moreover, established pharmacovigilance systems will monitor safety in these underrepresented groups.

In conclusion, the results of this phase 2 study suggest that tazemetostat has a clinically meaningful efficacy and favourable safety profile in patients with relapsed or refractory FL. The clinical benefit of tazemetostat in Chinese patients with EZH2mut FL was consistent with that observed in global population, thereby providing robust evidence that establishes tazemetostat as a novel therapeutic option for this patient population in China and serving as invaluable guidance for the clinical use within China's healthcare framework.

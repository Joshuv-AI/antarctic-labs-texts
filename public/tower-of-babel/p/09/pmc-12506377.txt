A new technique for severe hallux valgus: mid-shaft chevron osteotomy with spear plate fixation - a retrospective case series
Authors: Kuan-Jung Chen, Chien-Yuan Wang, Chao-Ching Chiang, Chien-Shun Wang, Chien-Hua Chen, Tzu-Cheng Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSevere hallux valgus deformity with hallux valgus angle (HVA) greater than 40° presents significant challenges in surgical management. The metatarsal mid-shaft chevron osteotomy (MSCO) with spear plate fixation represents a novel approach for addressing these complex deformities. This study aimed to evaluate its clinical and radiographic outcomes in patients with severe hallux valgus.MethodsThis retrospective case series evaluated 54 patients (64 feet) who underwent MSCO with spear plate fixation for severe hallux valgus between January 2023 and March 2024. Inclusion criteria included symptomatic hallux valgus with preoperative HVA greater than 40°, skeletal maturity, and minimum 12-month follow-up. Clinical outcomes were assessed using visual analog scale pain scores (VAS) and American Orthopedic Foot and Ankle Society hallux metatarsophalangeal-interphalangeal (AOFAS-MTP-IP) scale. Radiographic parameters included HVA, intermetatarsal angle (IMA), and tibial sesamoid position. Osteotomy healing was defined as observed bridging of callus from the two segments.ResultsThe cohort included 46 females and 8 males with mean age of 50.0 years. Mean osteotomy healing time was 3.1 months. HVA improved significantly from 44.1° preoperatively to 7.5° at final follow-up (p < 0.01). IMA decreased from 17.7° to 2.8° (p < 0.01). Tibial sesamoid position showed significant improvement (p = 0.02). VAS pain scores decreased from 5.4 to 0.8 (p < 0.01), and AOFAS-MTP-IP scores improved from 28.0 to 81.6 points (p < 0.01). Five complications (7.8%) occurred: one osteoarthritis, one hallux varus, and three hallux valgus recurrences.ConclusionsMSCO with spear plate fixation demonstrates excellent clinical and radiographic outcomes for severe hallux valgus deformity, with significant improvements in deformity correction, pain relief, and functional scores, accompanied by low complication rates and reliable bone healing.Level of evidenceLevel IV, case series.

Background

Hallux valgus is a common foot deformity characterized by lateral deviation of the great toe and medial prominence of the first metatarsal head, impairing both function and aesthetics of the foot. The extensive array of surgical techniques developed for hallux valgus correction demonstrates the challenging nature of this deformity and the ongoing search for optimal treatment approaches [1–6]. The primary objective of surgical treatment is to restore a pain-free, functional, and anatomically aligned forefoot [7].

The severity of hallux valgus is typically classified by the hallux valgus angle (HVA): normal (≤ 15°), mild (< 20°), moderate (20–40°), and severe (> 40°) [8]. Severe deformities pose greater technical challenges and are associated with increased risks of complications, including recurrence, delayed union, and non-union [9, 10]. While distal metatarsal osteotomy (DMO) is widely used for mild to moderate deformities, more proximal techniques such as Lapidus arthrodesis are often employed for severe cases. However, these procedures have limitations regarding corrective power, technical demand, and recovery time [11–14].

Among current surgical options, third-generation Minimally Invasive Chevron and Akin osteotomies (MICA) have gained popularity due to their favorable clinical and radiographic outcomes, particularly in the context of minimally invasive hallux valgus correction [15–20], even in severe deformities [21–23]. Nevertheless, MICA is technically demanding and requires a steep learning curve, limiting its generalizability in broader clinical practice [24, 25].

Advancements in implant design have further expanded surgical options for hallux valgus. Among these, the spear plate intramedullary system is a novel fixation device initially developed for DMO. It offers improved stability and allows for earlier postoperative weightbearing. However, the ideal osteotomy level for maximizing the biomechanical benefits of this implant remains under investigation.

In our clinical practice, traditional DMO may not fully utilize the mechanical advantages of the spear plate in severe hallux valgus (HVA > 40°). We developed a new surgical technique combining metatarsal mid-shaft chevron osteotomy (MSCO) with a spear plate and McBride soft tissue release. By positioning the at the mid-diaphyseal region, approximately at a 1:1 length ratio of the first metatarsal, this approach enhances angular correction while reducing hardware prominence and wound-related complications.

We hypothesize that MSCO with spear plate fixation results in reliable radiographic correction and favorable short-term clinical outcomes in patients with severe hallux valgus. This study aims to evaluate the clinical efficacy and radiographic results of this technique.

Materials and methods

This retrospective study was conducted at a single medical institution in compliance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board, which waived the requirement for informed consent due to the retrospective nature of the analysis.

The study included patients who underwent MSCO with spear plate fixation for severe hallux valgus between January 2023 and March 2024. All surgeries were performed by a single surgeon. Our study enrolled patients presenting with symptomatic severe hallux valgus (preoperative HVA > 40°) who had reached skeletal maturity and completed at least 12 months of postoperative follow-up. Exclusion criteria included: (1) previous hallux valgus surgery; (2) inflammatory arthritis; (3) hallux rigidus with significant degenerative changes; (4) neuromuscular disorders affecting foot function.

Surgical technique

The patient was placed in a supine position under general or spinal anesthesia. A surgical incision was made over the medial eminence, followed by opening the first MTP joint capsule using a T-shaped capsulotomy directed towards the plantar aspect. Care was taken to avoid injuring the dorsomedial cutaneous nerve branch during this process. To widen the first MTP joint space, the first phalanx was pulled distally. A transarticular lateral release was then performed by inserting a No. 15 blade across the joint, releasing both the lateral capsule and adductor tendon. (Fig. 1A)

The medial eminence was then shaved parallel to the medial side of the first metatarsal shaft using a microsaw. Preparation for the osteotomy began with the placement of a 1.0 mm K-wire in the midline of the metatarsal shaft under fluoroscopy, marking the intended osteotomy site. (Fig. 2A) A chevron osteotomy was then executed with its apex centered at this K-wire, forming a diverse angle of approximately 60 degrees. (Fig. 1B) The degree of lateral translation of the distal fragment was determined by the severity of the deformity and the width of the first metatarsal. Following adequate lateral translation, a provisional 1.6 mm K-wire was inserted into the distal fragment to temporarily control its rotation and angulation.

Fixation was achieved using a spear plate (A Plus Biotechnology Co., Ltd., New Taipei City, Taiwan). The proximal end of the plate was inserted into the medullary canal of the proximal metatarsal shaft, with its axis positioned parallel to the lateral cortex. The plate is available in various offset sizes ranging from 1 to 5 mm. Care was taken to select the plate that best conformed to the laterally translated distal fragment. (Figures 1C and 2B and C) Once properly positioned, distal and proximal screws were inserted to secure the plate in place. (Fig. 2D) The overhanging edge of bone was trimmed smoothly. The redundant tissue at the base of the capsular flap was resected, followed by capsulorrhaphy. (Fig. 1D) The surgical wound was then closed in layers.

Clinical and radiographic evaluation

All patients underwent standardized clinical and radiographic assessments before and after surgery. Clinical assessment included visual analog scale (VAS) for pain (0–10, with 0 indicating no pain and 10 indicating the worst imaginable pain) and American Orthopedic Foot and Ankle Society Hallux Metatarsophalangeal-Interphalangeal scores (AOFAS-MTP-IP, 0-100 points). Standardized weight-bearing anteroposterior and lateral radiographs of the foot were obtained. The hallux valgus angle (HVA) was measured as the angle between a line drawn from the center of the first metatarsal base to the center of the first metatarsal head and a second line connecting the midpoints of the proximal and distal articular surfaces of the proximal phalanx. The intermetatarsal angle (IMA) was measured as the angle between a line connecting the centers of the first metatarsal base and head, and a line bisecting the diaphyseal portion of the second metatarsal [26]. Tibial sesamoid position according to the Hardy and Clapham classification (graded from 1 to 7) [27]. And the length of distal fragment is measured on postoperative anteroposterior (AP) radiographs, defined as the linear distance from the apex of the chevron osteotomy to the distal articular margin of the first metatarsal head.

After the operation, a rectangular strip of gauze was used as a spacer in the first web space, applied immediately after skin closure. Patients were provided with a rocker-bottom shoe for protection and were permitted to bear weight and ambulate on the heel. Surgical stitches were removed 2 weeks postoperatively, and an all-day silicone gel hallux valgus protector was applied to maintain the corrected shape. Gentle range-of-motion exercises for the MTP joint were initiated 2 weeks postoperatively. One month postoperatively, patients transitioned from the rocker-bottom shoe to ordinary footwear, and the use of the protector was restricted to nighttime only. All patients were followed postoperatively at 2 weeks, 4 weeks, monthly for the subsequent 3 months, and finally at one year for radiographic and functional evaluation. Osteotomy healing was defined as observed bridging of callus from the two segments. Radiographic parameters were assessed at each visit, while VAS and AOFAS-MTP-IP score were re-evaluated at the final follow-up.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using MedCalc for Windows, Version 20 (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium). All radiographic images were independently reviewed and measured by a radiologist and an experienced orthopedic surgeon, both of whom were not involved in the surgical procedures. Intra- and inter-observer reliability were assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. For continuous variables, the average of the two observers’ measurements was used for analysis.

Radiographic assessments (HVA, IMA, and sesamoid position) were conducted and analyzed across three timepoints: preoperatively, one month after surgery, and at the final one-year clinical follow-up. Functional outcomes, including VAS and AOFAS-MTP-IP scores, were compared between the preoperative and final follow-up assessments. Categorical data were compared using the chi-squared test. Continuous data are presented as mean with 95% confidence intervals (CI), and compared using a paired t-test. A P-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The mean age at surgery was 50.0 years (95% CI: 45.5–54.6), with a predominant female population (87.5%, n = 56) compared to males (12.5%, n = 8). The distribution of surgical sides included 18 right feet (28.1%), 26 left feet (40.6%), and 10 bilateral procedures (15.6%). The mean body mass index was 26.1 kg/m² (95% CI: 25.2–27.1).

The HVA showed marked improvement from a preoperative mean of 44.1° (95% CI: 43.0-45.1) to 7.1° (95% CI: 6.2-8.0, p < 0.01) immediately postoperatively, with minimal change at final follow-up to 7.5° (95% CI: 6.7–8.6, p = 0.34). Similarly, the IMA decreased significantly from 17.7° (95% CI: 16.8–18.7) preoperatively to 3.0° (95% CI: 2.4–3.5, p < 0.01) postoperatively, remaining stable at 2.8° (95% CI: 2.3–3.3, p = 0.93) at final follow-up.(Fig. 3)

Tibial sesamoid position also demonstrated significant improvement. Preoperatively, the majority of patients had severe sesamoid displacement with 56 patients classified as grade 6 and 8 patients as grade 5 according to the Hardy and Clapham classification. Postoperatively, there was substantial improvement in sesamoid positioning, with 20 patients achieving grade 1, 26 patients grade 2, 11 patients grade 3, 6 patients grade 4, and 1 patient grade 5 (p = 0.02). At final follow-up, the distribution remained relatively stable with 18 patients at grade 1, 28 at grade 2, 12 at grade 3, 5 at grade 4, and 1 at grade 5 (p = 0.22).

Patient-reported outcomes

Clinical outcomes showed substantial improvement following surgery. The VAS pain score decreased significantly from a preoperative mean of 5.4 (95% CI: 4.9–5.8) to 0.8 (95% CI: 0.6-1.0) at final follow-up (p < 0.01). The AOFAS-MTP-IP score improved significantly from 28.0 points (95% CI: 25.3–60.9) preoperatively to 81.6 points (95% CI: 79.3–83.9) at final follow-up (p < 0.01).

Complications

Five complications (7.8%) were identified during the follow-up period. These included one case of osteoarthritis (1.6%), one case of hallux varus (1.6%), and three cases of hallux valgus recurrence (4.7%). No cases of nonunion, malunion, or hardware-related complications were observed in this cohort, and none of the patients required implant removal.

Discussion

In this study, we presented the surgical outcomes following a new surgical technique combining MSCO with a spear plate and McBride procedure for severe hallux valgus in patients with an average age of 50 years. Our surgical technique achieved bony union in all first metatarsals in a mean of 3.1 months. Although some complications occurred, satisfactory functional outcomes as shown by improvements in AOFAS-MTP-IP and VAS pain scores were achieved after a short-term 1-year follow-up.

The severity of hallux valgus is typically classified based on the HVA, with an angle of 40 degrees or greater indicating a severe deformity [8]. Severe hallux valgus deformities have historically been treated with a proximal osteotomy or arthrodesis procedure, which provides more correction power [28]. The Scarf osteotomy provides predictable correction of moderate to severe hallux valgus deformities [29, 30]. The proximal opening wedge metatarsal osteotomy is regarded another effective technique for correction of severe hallux valgus deformity [1, 31]. In recent studies, some surgeons preferred the Lapidus procedure for correcting the HVA and IMA in moderate to severe hallux valgus surgery [14, 32]. Although greater deformity correction can be achieved with a basal osteotomy or the Lapidus procedure, these techniques are often associated with a longer recovery period [33]. In this study, we presented MSCO with a spear plate combined with transarticular lateral release to correct cases with HVA deformity greater than 40 degrees to near-normal, and resulting in good surgical outcomes. The spear plate intramedullary placement facilitates lateral translation of the distal metatarsal fragment while minimizing soft tissue disruption compared to traditional open techniques. Our procedure can be performed through a single 2–3 cm medial incision, allowing for simultaneous capsulorrhaphy.

In recent years, MICA becomes most popular surgical method for hallux valgus correction, due to the advantage of smaller surgical incision and minimal soft tissue damage. Even in cases of severe deformity, MICA is regarded as an effective alternative and provides satisfactory outcomes. Lewis et al. demonstrated that percutaneous surgery for severe hallux valgus deformity can achieve substantial deformity correction with high patient satisfaction and low recurrence rates [22]. In a separate study, they also reported significant clinical improvement and large radiographic correction two years after third-generation MICA, based on a cohort of 106 feet [21]. Nevertheless, MICA requires considerable technical expertise, with its adoption often accompanied by a gradual reduction in operative time and fluoroscopy usage as surgeons gain experience. After 40 procedures, the surgery time can be dropped under 45 min and required less than 100 fluoro-shots [25]. In our practice, the surgical technique of MSCO with a spear plate is a simpler and reproducible open procedure that requires minimal use of intraoperative fluoroscopy. Although spear plate technique is not as minimally invasive as MICA, its design permits insertion through the relatively small medial incision used for the osteotomy.

This approach may broaden access to effective treatment for severe hallux valgus by enabling general orthopedic surgeons to achieve consistent and favorable outcomes using a technically straightforward procedure. By combining the advantages of a relatively proximal osteotomy positioning with the mechanical stability of the spear plate system, our technique offers a potentially more accessible alternative to technically demanding procedures such as MICA, without compromising clinical results.

In the context of deformity correction, hallux varus and recurrent hallux valgus are two common complications following bunion surgery, both of which are influenced by surgical technique. Successful correction requires a combination of precise bony realignment and appropriate soft tissue release, with careful attention to the balance of multidirectional forces acting on the first ray. With MSCO technique, there were only 1 patient (1.6%) with hallux varus and 3 patients (4.7%) with recurrent hallux valgus in our 1-year follow up series. The incidences of are comparable to the range in previous literatures [34–36].

Although Akin osteotomy is frequently employed to address residual interphalangeal valgus or to refine alignment of the hallux, none of the patients in our series required it intraoperatively. This may reflect the substantial correction achieved through the combination of MSCO, spear plate fixation, and associated soft tissue procedures. Nevertheless, given the recurrence observed in three cases, we recognize that incorporating an Akin osteotomy at the time of the index surgery may have contributed to improved long-term alignment in select patients. With further experience, we aim to develop better intraoperative criteria to identify patients who might benefit from adjunctive phalangeal correction.

Nonunion of osteotomy site is another issue following metatarsal osteotomy. To minimize complications, we preferred a chevron-shaped osteotomy over a transverse osteotomy. The chevron design, characterized by its angled limbs and apex, provides greater bony contact between the proximal and distal fragments. This increased contact area is particularly important given the lack of compressive force in the spear plate design, as it enhances the likelihood of achieving successful bony union. Our series demonstrated no cases of nonunion, supporting the efficacy of this approach. In addition, patients were permitted to begin full sole weight-bearing ambulation one month postoperatively. No loss of reduction or implant failure occurred with early mobilization and exercise, suggesting that the spear plate is a reliable and effective implant for hallux valgus correction.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study was retrospective in design and lacked a comparative control group, limiting our ability to directly contrast outcomes with alternative procedures such as Lapidus or proximal metatarsal osteotomies. Second, while our short-term outcomes were promising, the follow-up duration of 12 months may not fully capture long-term recurrence or implant-related complications. Further prospective, randomized studies with longer follow-up periods are necessary to validate the durability and comparative efficacy of this technique.

Conclusions

MSCO with Spear Plate fixation yields excellent clinical and radiographic outcomes in the correction of severe hallux valgus, with minimal complications and consistent bone healing. The mid-shaft osteotomy offers sufficient corrective power while preserving technical simplicity and maintaining the shorter recovery time typically associated with distal procedures. Given its reproducibility and favorable results, this approach represents a reliable alternative for managing severe hallux valgus deformities.

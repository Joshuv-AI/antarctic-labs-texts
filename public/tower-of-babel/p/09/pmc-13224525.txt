Fabrication of Fe–Mn mixed oxide nanosheets via microwave assistance as an advanced probe for ultra-sensitive nepafenac sensing in diverse matrices; paving the way for future applications
Authors: Kholoud R. El-Emam, Amal A. El-Masry, Heba Elmansi, Nadia S. El-Gohary
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A novel colorimetric sensing platform for the ultra-sensitive detection of nepafenac (NEP) is investigated based on mesoporous Fe–Mn bimetal oxide nanosheets synthesized via a rapid microwave-assisted method. The incorporation of microwave irradiation represents a key innovation, enabling energy-efficient synthesis while enhancing structural uniformity, surface area, and catalytic performance. Comprehensive characterization using transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed the formation of interconnected nanosheets, while X-ray diffraction (XRD) analysis shows a poorly crystalline mixed Fe–Mn oxide, whose nanoscale disorder enhances surface activity and redox cycling, making it highly effective for oxidative applications. Fourier-transform infrared (FTIR) spectroscopy verified metal–oxygen–metal (Fe–O–Mn) framework formation, and energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectroscopy confirmed homogeneous elemental distribution. The fabricated Fe–Mn mixed oxide nanosheets exhibited excellent oxidase-like activity, catalyzing the oxidation of 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) to its blue-coloured oxidized product (oxTMB). In the presence of NEP, a significant decrease in blue colour intensity was observed due to NEP-induced inhibition, enabling accurate quantification. Under optimized conditions, the sensor demonstrated outstanding sensitivity with a detection limit as low as 0.15 ng/mL and a linear range of 0.5–25.0 ng/mL. The method was successfully applied to determine NEP in commercial eye drop formulations and synthetic aqueous humour, with high recovery percentages (99.61% and 99.62%, respectively), confirming its practical applicability. Additionally, the environmental sustainability of the approach was critically assessed using multiple contemporary greenness evaluation tools (ComplexMoGAPI, AGREE, AGSA and BAGI), confirming its alignment with green analytical chemistry principles. This study demonstrates the synergistic benefits of microwave-assisted synthesis and rational nano-catalyst design, offering a promising platform for future applications in pharmaceutical analysis and clinical diagnostics.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13065-026-01810-4.

Introduction

Nepafenac (NEP) is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug extensively employed in ophthalmic practice for the management of postoperative pain and inflammation, particularly following cataract surgery [1]. It is a prodrug, undergoing intraocular enzymatic hydrolysis to form amfenac which is a potent cyclooxygenase (COX) inhibitor. By selectively inhibiting COX enzymes, it effectively suppresses prostaglandin synthesis, thereby mitigating the inflammatory cascade. Ophthalmic suspension of NEP offers superior corneal penetration and sustained anti-inflammatory effects. It is also associated with reduced incidence of postoperative complications, including cystoid macular edema. The drug’s targeted ocular activity and minimal systemic exposure underscore its value as a safe and efficacious therapeutic option in perioperative eye care [2].

While conventional analytical approaches, including high-performance liquid chromatography [3–5], spectrofluorimetry [6, 7] and electrochemical assays [8, 9] offer excellent sensitivity and selectivity, their application is often limited by extensive sample pretreatment, long analysis times, high operational costs, and the requirement for skilled personnel. These limitations underscore the pressing need to develop straightforward, economical, and efficient analytical methods for the determination of NEP in bulk materials as well as in ophthalmic dosage forms such as eye drops and suspensions.

Colorimetric assays represent a widely recognized category of analytical techniques that have attracted considerable interest owing to their low cost, high sensitivity, simplicity of operation, rapid response, robustness, and minimal instrumental requirements [10–14]. A key challenge in the development of novel colorimetric assays lies in effectively converting the analyte recognition event into a visually perceptible signal, typically manifested as a distinct and target-specific color change. Although natural enzymes exhibit remarkable catalytic efficiency and high substrate specificity, they also suffer from several drawbacks, including high production costs, limited stability under extreme conditions, susceptibility to denaturation, and complex purification procedures [15]. These limitations have motivated the development of alternative approaches employing metals, transition metal oxides, and carbon dots to mimic the catalytic function of peroxidase enzymes without dependence on the rapid decomposition of H₂O₂ [16–18]. Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) are porous materials composed of metal ions or clusters, forming a highly structured network. They have garnered significant attention in recent years due to their unique physicochemical properties, high surface area, tunable porosity, structural flexibility, and enhanced reactivity. MOFs exhibit a wide range of applications, including drug delivery, catalysis, sensing, and gas storage, with customizable properties tailored for specific uses [19].

Binary transition metal oxides have emerged as effective bi-functional catalysts, outperforming their monometallic counterparts due to improved electronic conductivity from synergistic interactions between metal components. Notable examples with promising oxidase-mimicking activity include Mn–Co [20–22], Mn–Fe [23, 24], Zn-Mn [25], Cu-Mn [26], Ag-V [27] and Ni–Co oxides [28]. In this study, we developed a mesoporous iron-manganese bimetal oxide nanosheets (NSs) by a modified aeration co-precipitation method incorporating microwave irradiation as a distinctive feature for colorimetric sensing of NEP. The synergistic interaction between iron and manganese, in addition to the nanosheet porous nature and the presence of oxygen vacancies within it, significantly enhances catalytic activity, simulating enzyme oxidation processes. This oxidase-like activity facilitates the direct and sensitive detection of NEP via a colorimetric response [29, 30]. The porous Fe-Mn oxides demonstrated exceptional catalytic performance, efficiently oxidizing the chromogenic substrate 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB) to form oxidized TMB (oxTMB), producing a distinct blue color. Additionally, NEP acted as a reducing agent through an electron transfer mechanism, converting oxTMB back to TMB and resulting in a measurable reduction in color intensity. By utilizing this reaction, we developed a straightforward and very sensitive colorimetric technique for quantifying NEP in various matrices, including pharmaceutical formulations, and aqueous humor. This sensing platform shows great potential for clinical diagnostics [31], tumor therapy [32], pharmaceutical quality control [33], photocatalysis [34], Low-emission power generation [35] and environmental pollutants removal [36–41]. Its broad applicability extends to advanced sensing technologies, catalysis, and energy storage, owing to its high redox activity, surface area, and synergistic electronic interactions. Notably, they can function as oxidase mimics in biosensing platforms [42], act as Fenton-like catalysts for wastewater treatment [43], and serve as electrode materials in supercapacitors and batteries [44]. These multifunctional properties make them as a promising candidate for the development of sustainable technologies in environmental and biomedical fields. The selection of Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs as the sensing platform for nepafenac detection is strategically justified by the unique physicochemical properties arising from the synergistic interaction between iron and manganese within a mixed-valence oxide framework. Transition metal oxides, particularly those containing Fe and Mn, have garnered significant interest as nanozymes due to their ability to mimic enzymatic oxidation reactions through reversible redox processes and surface-mediated electron transfer mechanisms. Furthermore, the integration of microwave irradiation into the synthesis strategy possesses significant advantages over previously reported conventional heating approaches. Compared to conventional hydrothermal or solvothermal heating, which relies on external heat transfer and often results in long reaction times, thermal gradients, and limited control over morphology, microwave irradiation provides rapid and volumetric heating throughout the reaction medium. This homogeneous energy distribution accelerates nucleation kinetics, reduces reaction time, minimizes side reactions, and improves phase purity. Moreover, microwave-assisted synthesis enables better control over nanosheet thickness, particle size distribution, and surface area, which directly influences catalytic performance. Literature reports have demonstrated that microwave methods promote rapid crystallization, narrow size distribution, high yield, and improved reproducibility while maintaining lower energy consumption. Importantly, microwave irradiation has been shown to significantly enhance catalyst surface properties and catalytic efficiency, making it a favorable approach for preparing functional nanozymes with improved oxidase-like activity [45].

Experimental

Apparatus

A Shimadzu UV-1601 PC (Kyoto, Japan) UV-Visible double-beam spectrophotometer was utilized, equipped with matched quartz cells of 1 cm path length and 1 nm slit width. The measurement range spanned from 200 to 800 nm, and spectral data were processed using Shimadzu software. The JEM-2100 transmission electron microscope (JEOL, Japan) was used for TEM imaging. The energy dispersive X-ray (SEM-EDX) examination was performed using an Oxford X-Max 20 silicon drift detector and a scanning electron microscope (Jeol-jsm-6510 LV). Heating was accomplished using an LG microwave oven (model MS3043BARS, 700 W). A pH meter (Consort, P-901, Belgium) was used for pH adjustment, while a magnetic stirrer (Daihan Scientific CO, South Korea) facilitated sample mixing.

Chemicals and reagents

All chemicals used in this study were of analytical grade. Pure NEP (99.87% purity) was supplied by the European–Egyptian Pharmaceutical Industry Co. (Egypt). Ammonium hydroxide (NH₄OH), sodium hydroxide (NaOH), barium sulfate (BaSO₄), silver nitrate (AgNO₃), acetic acid, and sodium acetate were obtained from El-Nasr Chemical Co. (Egypt). Ethanol and methanol (HPLC grade), manganese sulfate dihydrate (MnSO₄·2 H₂O), ferric chloride (FeCl₃), and 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB; molecular formula C₁₆H₂₀N₂; ACROS, Thermo Scientific) were purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. Nevanac® eye drops (Batch No. 10692267108606), labeled to contain 1.0 mg/mL NEP, were obtained from a local pharmacy.

Synthesis and characterization of Fe-Mn Oxide NSs

A novel modified aeration-co-precipitation method, based on a previously reported protocol that relied on conventional heating [30] with the notable modification of incorporating microwave irradiation into the synthetic procedure, was employed to synthesize the Fe-Mn oxide NSs. This modification designed to overcome the limitations of conventional thermal methods. The schematic representation of the synthetic steps was shown in Fig. 1. Initially, Fe³⁺ and Mn²⁺ ions were prepared in a 2:1 molar ratio in the presence of excess oxygen [30, 46]. Specifically, manganese sulfate (MnSO₄ 0.2 H₂O) (0.935 g, 5 mmol) and anhydrous ferric chloride (FeCl₃) (1.622 g, 10 mmol) were separately dissolved in deionized water (100 mL). Next, the two solutions were combined in an Erlenmeyer flask and stirred at 50 rpm while being maintained at 70 °C. The mixture was aerated for 30 min under these conditions. The pH was then gradually increased to 9.0 by slowly adding 10% NH₄OH at a rate of 0.46 mL/min. During this process, the solution was transformed into a dark brown colloidal suspension. Unlike the original protocol, the suspension was subsequently exposed to microwave irradiation for 2 min, serving as a novel modification intended to enhance nucleation and uniform particle growth. The precipitate obtained was subjected to filtration and was rinsed four times with deionized water and twice with ethanol. To confirm sufficient washing, barium sulfate (BaSO₄) and silver nitrate (AgNO₃) were added to the filtrate. Washing proceeded until no white precipitate (BaSO4 or AgCl) was detected in the residual solution. Finally, the NSs synthesized were placed in an oven to dry at 50 °C for 10 min. The resulting bimetal oxide NSs were stored in a desiccator to be analyzed for their properties and characteristics.

Preparation of NEP standard solution

A standard ethanolic solution of NEP (100.0 µg/mL) was prepared. Working solutions at concentrations of 1.0 µg/mL and 100.0 ng/mL were subsequently obtained by appropriate dilution of the stock solution using the same solvent.

Development of a Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs colorimetric platform for the detection of NEP

For the detection of NEP, a mixture was prepared by combining 0.25 mL of ethanolic solution of TMB (1.0 mM), 0.6 mL of acetate buffer (0.1 M, pH = 3.5), and 0.5 mL of ethanolic solution of Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs (100.0 µg/mL). The mixture was allowed to stand for 5 min before adding different concentrations of NEP. After 3 min of incubation, the variation in absorbance was recorded at 655 nm.

Analysis for Nevanac® eye drops

A volume of 5.0 mL of Nevanac® eye drops (Batch No. 10692267108606, labelled as 1.0 mg mL⁻¹) was accurately transferred into a 50 mL volumetric flask. The volume was completed to the mark with ethanol to obtain a final concentration of 100.0 µg mL⁻¹. The solution was then sonicated for 5 min to ensure complete homogenization and dissolution. Subsequently, an aliquot of the prepared solution was filtered through a 0.45 μm syringe membrane filter to remove any suspended excipients or particulate matter. At last, three working solutions of the filtered NEP were prepared and analyzed using the procedures illustrated in the preceding section. Finally, the regression equation of the calibration curve was applied to estimate the nominal content of NEP in the eye-drop formulation.

Procedure for the determination of NEP in aqueous humor

Artificial aqueous humor was formulated in the laboratory to mimic the composition of the human counterpart [47]. Into 10 mL volumetric flasks, 1 mL of the prepared aqueous humor was introduced. Afterwards, varying aliquots of the NEP standard solutions were added, following the procedure described in “Development of a Fe–Mn Mixed Oxide NSs Colorimetric Platform for the Detection of NEP”.

Results and discussion

Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs as artificial oxidases: structural and catalytic insights

Several analytical methods such as TEM, FTIR, DLS, EDX, and XRD, were employed to comprehensively characterize the fabricated NSs. TEM analysis offered insights into their size, morphology, and structural defects, as well as variations among individual NSs. In contrast, DLS supplied precise measurements of the nanosheets’ hydrodynamic diameter and zeta potential. TEM and DLS analyses (Fig. 2) revealed that the synthesized Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs consisted of interconnected nanoparticles with a polydispersity index of 0.477, and a zeta potential of 7.22 mV. XRD analysis (Fig. S1) exhibits broad diffraction features with limited sharp reflections, indicating a poorly crystalline mixed Fe–Mn oxide phase. The broadening of peaks is attributed to nanoscale domains and structural disorder within the material. Such behavior is commonly observed in Fe–Mn mixed oxides. The partially disordered structure of the mixed Fe–Mn oxide is expected to enhance its redox performance. Poor crystallinity typically introduces a high density of surface defects, unsaturated coordination sites, and enhanced electron mobility between Fe and Mn centers. Therefore, high crystallinity is not a prerequisite for effective oxidative activity; instead, structural disorder can be beneficial [48, 49]. Furthermore, FTIR spectroscopy was employed to verify the synthesis of Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs and to identify the functional groups and chemical bonding present in the material. The FTIR spectrum revealed distinct absorption bands at 1153.6, 1114.9, 1083, 1054, and 978 cm⁻¹ which are indicative of metal–oxygen–metal (M–O–M) stretching and bending vibrations within the Fe–O–Mn lattice, supporting the successful formation of a mixed-metal oxide structure. The band at 1153.6 cm⁻¹ is typically associated with asymmetric Fe–O–Mn stretching, whereas the absorption bands at 1114.9 and 1083 cm⁻¹ can be assigned to lattice deformation and bridging oxygen vibrations between Fe and Mn centers. The bands at 1054 and 978 cm⁻¹ are attributed to Mn–O stretching and surface oxygen or hydroxyl bending vibrations, which may originate from adsorbed species or residual precursor groups. Furthermore, the broad band at 3334.82 cm⁻¹ was ascribed to O–H stretching of moisture-adsorbed water molecules, whereas the band at 1630.17 cm⁻¹ arose from their H–O–H bending vibration. Ultimately, the integration of Fe and Mn into the Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs framework was examined to substantiate the successful completion of the synthetic procedure [50]. The elemental composition and relative proportions of iron, manganese, and oxygen were analyzed using energy-dispersive X-ray (EDX) spectroscopy, showing approximately 3.53% Fe, 2.07% Mn, and 94.4% O, which confirms the expected elemental ratios. The findings clearly indicate that Mn and Fe were homogeneously incorporated into the oxide NSs matrix, confirming the success of the synthetic process (Fig. 2). Moreover, microwave irradiation had an important role in nucleation and morphology of the fabricated NSs via a rapid nucleation controlled growth process. It induces instantaneous and volumetric heating through dipolar polarization and ionic conduction. This homogeneous energy distribution results in a sharp increase in supersaturation, thereby promoting burst nucleation of Fe–Mn oxide nuclei. The high nucleation rate, combined with shortened reaction time, restricts excessive crystal growth and aggregation. Consequently, primary nanoparticles form rapidly and subsequently undergo oriented attachment and self-assembly into lamellar nanosheet architectures. Furthermore, the rapid heating–cooling dynamics characteristic of microwave processing favor anisotropic growth along thermodynamically stable crystallographic planes, contributing to the observed sheet-like morphology. This controlled nucleation and growth mechanism explains the uniform dispersion of Fe and Mn species, and the interconnected NSs network. The resulting lamellar structure provides abundant exposed active sites and enhanced surface area, which are critical for the observed oxidase-like catalytic activity [45].

The oxidase-like catalytic activity of Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs was assessed using TMB as a substrate. As shown in Fig. 3A, TMB alone remained colorless and exhibited no significant absorption in the 500–800 nm range, and Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs alone also showed negligible absorbance. In contrast, upon mixing Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs with TMB, a pronounced blue coloration appeared, accompanied by a strong absorption peak at 655 nm, indicative of the formation of oxTMB. This result confirms that Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs efficiently catalyze the oxidation of TMB in the absence of hydrogen peroxide, displaying intrinsic oxidase-like activity. Furthermore, when the TMB solution was pretreated with NEP, the absorbance at 655 nm was markedly decreased (Fig. 3B). The proposed catalytic mechanism is depicted in Fig. S2.

Optimization for enhancing sensor performance

To achieve the optimal sensing performance, several experimental parameters were evaluated. The current study examined several factors such as the quantities of Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs and TMB, the amount of the used buffer, the pH, the reaction temperature, the duration of contact between Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs and TMB, and the incubation period after the introduction of NEP.

Among various buffers tested, acetate buffer at pH 3.5 was found to be the most suitable (Fig. 4A). This preference is attributed to the enhanced oxidase-like activity of Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs in acidic environment. The involvement of protons in redox reactions leads to increased redox potentials as the pH decreases [51]. Furthermore, molecular oxygen (O₂) exhibits greater reactivity under acidic conditions, thereby enhancing the catalytic efficiency of the NSs. Thus, pH 3.5 was selected as the optimal pH for subsequent experiments (Fig. 4A).

The effect of pH on catalytic efficiency was assessed across pH 2–6. The highest activity occurred at pH 3.5, thus it was identified as the optimal pH (Fig. 4A). The buffer volume was studied in the range of 0.2–0.8 mL. Absorbance initially increased with buffer volume and peaked at 0.6 mL, beyond which it declined, likely due to dilution effects. Therefore, 0.6 mL of acetate buffer was selected as the optimal volume (Fig. 4B). The effect of Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs volume on catalytic activity was also investigated. Maximum absorbance was observed at 0.5 mL, which was then selected as the optimal volume (Fig. 4C). To optimize the oxidase-like reaction, the volume of TMB was varied between 0.1 and 0.3 mL. Absorbance increased steadily and plateaued at 0.2 mL, however 0.25 mL was selected as the optimal TMB volume (Fig. 4D). Reaction time optimization between TMB and Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs showed that absorbance steadily increased and reached equilibrium at 300 s, indicating the optimal reaction time (Fig. 4E). After adding NEP, the ideal incubation period was determined to be 180 s, as shown in Fig. 4F, where the absorbance change stabilized. Temperature optimization revealed that the highest catalytic activity occurred at 30 °C, suggesting that this is the optimal reaction temperature (Fig. 4G).

Validation criteria and results

Validation in analytical chemistry, especially in pharmaceutical analysis, is essential to ensure that analytical methods are suitable for their intended purpose. It is also critical for regulatory approval and quality control of drug products. According to the ICH Q2(R1) guideline [52] which provides a harmonized approach across global regulatory agencies, different validation parameters were evaluated, including linearity, accuracy, precision (repeatability and intermediate precision), and selectivity. Each parameter assesses a different aspect of method performance, ensuring the reliability and consistency of the results.

A calibration curve was developed by analyzing various concentrations of NEP to relate absorbance to drug concentration. A linear relationship was noted within the concentration range of 0.5 to 25.0 ng/mL (Fig. 3B), resulting in the following regression equation (Table 1):

Y = 0.02x + 0.09, with a correlation coefficient (R²) of 0.9999.

According to the ICH Q2(R1) guidelines [52], the calculated limits of detection (LOD = 3.3 × Sa/b) and quantitation (LOQ = 10 × Sa/b) were 0.146 ng/mL and 0.442 ng/mL, respectively (Sa: standard deviation of the intercept and b: slope of the calibration curve). The proposed analytical method’s accuracy was assessed by determining the percentage recovery of six varying concentrations of NEP and contrasting the findings with those acquired through the HPLC reference method [5]. Statistical analysis was performed using the Student’s t-test and the variance ratio F-test to compare the two sets of data [53]. As summarized in Table 2, the method exhibited satisfactory accuracy with no significant difference compared to the reference method. Precision was further assessed through intra-day and inter-day studies across multiple concentrations within the established linear range (Table S1). Table 1 displays acceptable values for both percent relative standard deviation (%RSD) and percent error, indicating good repeatability and precision.

The selectivity of the Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs /TMB sensing platform was evaluated for the detection of NEP in the presence of potential interfering substances. Various common interfering substances and sugars were examined. The findings revealed minimal interference from these substances, indicating the nanosheets’ strong selectivity for detecting NEP. Although some compounds slightly influenced the oxidation process, none exhibited significant inhibitory effects comparable to NEP. This strong selectivity is likely due to differences in oxidation potentials and specific interactions between the analytes and the nanosheet surface, which prevent TMB oxidation. These findings confirm the platform’s reliable performance in complex matrices (Fig. S3). A comparison between the proposed method and the other reported ones was performed present in Table S2.

Application

Evaluation of NEP in eye drops and artificial aqueous humor

This approach was utilized for the direct determination of NEP in its ophthalmic dosage forms, and in artificial aqueous humor highlighting its potential applicability in routine quality control analysis. Three distinct concentrations of NEP (5.0, 10.0 and 20.0 ng/mL) in ophthalmic dosage form, and in artificial aqueous humor were tested following the procedure demonstrated in the section of “Analysis for Nevanac® eye drops” and “Procedure for the determination of NEP in aqueous humor”. The results obtained were compared with those of the reference method [5], as summarized in Table 2. Statistical analysis using both t-tests and F-value established that there were no significant differences between the two methods [53]. As shown in Table 2, the mean recoveries of NEP in Nevanac® eye drops and in artificial aqueous humor ± %RSD were 99.61 ± 1.38 and 99.62 ± 1.43, respectively, indicating high accuracy of the method.

Greenness assessment (complexMoGAPI and AGREE tools)

The environmental friendliness of the suggested approach was assessed using the complementary Green Analytical Procedure Index (ComplexMoGAPI) and the Analytical GREEnness (AGREE) assessment tools offering a comprehensive understanding of its environmental footprint and sustainability.

ComplexMoGAPI assesses analytical methods against twelve specific criteria; including the preparation of samples, the use of reagents, energy use, waste disposal, and overall ecological impact using a two-hexagon pictogram that evaluates every stage of the process, from sample collection to final analysis, on a green–yellow–red scale [54]. The dominance of green in our pictogram (Fig. 5) indicates high environmental compatibility, surpassing many alternative approaches.

AGREE, on the other hand, is based on the 12 principles of green analytical chemistry, it creates a circular pictogram divided into 12 segments, each representing one principle. Each segment is color-coded from red (less green) to green (highly green), with a central numerical score (0–1) indicating the overall greenness of the method. The software is free, easy to use, and allows for quantitative comparison of different methods [55]. In our evaluation, the AGREE score and the predominance of green segments confirmed the method’s strong alignment with green chemistry principles.

Analytical green star area (AGSA)

The AGSA metric offers an integrated framework for assessing the environmental sustainability of analytical methods, providing both quantitative evaluation and visual representation. One of its key strengths lies in promoting the adoption of environmentally responsible practices within analytical laboratories. By integrating a diverse set of evaluation criteria, the AGSA metric enables a multidimensional assessment of analytical methods, considering factors such as the type of analysis, sample size, in situ measurement capability, instrumental complexity, degree of automation, waste generation and treatment, safety performance, use of green solvents, and suitability for high-throughput applications [56]. The overall AGSA score of 86.11 demonstrates the effective integration of sustainability principles into the analytical workflow.

Blueness assessment (BAGI tool)

The BAGI tool was applied to evaluate the practical feasibility of the proposed UV–Vis spectrophotometric method. The overall BAGI score was 75, indicating good balance between greenness and real-world applicability. The method scored highly for its quantitative nature, simple sample preparation, low-cost reagents, absence of preconcentration, and ability to analyze more than 10 samples per hr. The small sample volumes used (< 10 mL) also contributed positively. Moderate scores were observed for instrumentation (accessible UV–Vis spectrophotometer) and sample amount, while lower scores were obtained for multi-analyte capability and automation, as the procedure was selective for a single analyte and performed manually. Overall, the BAGI pictogram (Fig. 5) demonstrates a favorable “blueness” profile confirming that the method is practical, sustainable, and well-suited for routine applications.

Conclusion

A novel colorimetric sensing platform that is simple, sustainable, and efficient was designed for the ultra-sensitive detection of NEP, a commonly used and effective anti-inflammatory drug in ophthalmology. Given the limitations of the current analytical methods; including high cost, complex preparation, and specialized equipment, this approach addressed the urgent need for accessible, cost-effective, and rapid NEP detection technique based on mesoporous Fe–Mn mixed oxide NSs synthesized via a microwave-assisted method. These bimetallic sensors exhibited strong oxidase-like activity, catalyzing the oxidation of TMB to its blue-colored form oxTMB, with NEP causing a quantifiable decrease in intensity. The method achieved a low detection limit with excellent recovery in both commercial eye drops and synthetic aqueous humor, confirming its real-world applicability. The method was thoroughly evaluated using various green chemistry evaluation tools, confirming its high level of sustainability, practicality, and performance. With minimal sample preparation, and low cost, this platform shows great promise for applications in pharmaceutical quality control, clinical diagnostics, and environmental monitoring.

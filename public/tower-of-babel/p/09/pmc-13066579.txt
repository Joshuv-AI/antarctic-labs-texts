The diagnostic value of CDC20 for malignant pleural effusion of lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Yulan Liu, Liping Wu, Jinrong Chen, Yinghong Yu, Shigao Chen, Jie Hu, Shuanghua Cheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is characterized by difficult early detection, rapid tumor progression and low surgical resection rate in the late stage. The common complication of patients with lung adenocarcinoma is malignant pleural effusion (MPE). Compared with lung biopsy, cytological examination of exfoliated pleural effusion is a less invasive operation. Therefore, there is an urgent need to discover new and effective cytological biomarkers for the exfoliation of LUAD pleural effusion. This work took the differentially expressed genes of LUAD tumors and adjacent tissues in the Gepia database as the entry point and screened out Cell division cycle protein 20 (CDC20) as a new biomarker for lung adenocarcinoma. The expression levels of CDC20, TTF-1 and NapsinA in 92 cases of lung adenocarcinoma and adjacent normal tissues were analyzed, and immunohistochemical detection was performed on paraffin specimens of pleural effusion from 30 cases of lung adenocarcinoma and 30 cases of non-neoplastic patients. Immunohistochemistry indicated that CDC20 was more expressed in LUAD tissues in the 92 observation groups and was related to tumor size, T classification, and pleural invasion. In this study, the results showed the expression of CDC20 was relatively consistent with that of TTF-1 and NapsinA in LUAD and malignant pleural effusion. This study reveals the potential diagnostic value of CDC20 in biopsy specimens of LUAD and pleural effusion. Our discovery is of great significance for the pathological diagnosis of pleural effusion cytology.

Introduction

The incidence and mortality rates of lung cancer are both relatively high. According to the estimation of the American Cancer Society, by the end of 2023, there may be 127,070 deaths from lung cancer, accounting for 20% of the total cancer mortality rate1,2. lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the main histological subtype of lung cancer3. Lung adenocarcinoma is characterized by high malignancy, poor prognosis and high recurrence rate4. To explore the internal mechanisms of the occurrence and development of lung adenocarcinoma, a large number of basic and clinical studies have been carried out, and significant progress has been made in the treatment of lung adenocarcinoma. However, the incidence and mortality rates of lung adenocarcinoma worldwide remain high5. Therefore, there is an urgent need to find a new prognostic biomarker for lung adenocarcinoma to guide individualized treatment and optimize treatment strategies. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)3 and small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) account for 85% and 15% of all lung cancer cases, respectively. The common clinical complication of lung adenocarcinoma is malignant pleural effusion (MPE). The prevalence of NSCLC patients at the initial diagnosis in the advanced stage is 10–15%, and the incidence is higher in the later stage of treatment6. The patient was diagnosed with MPE. The TNM stage of NSCLC patients was advanced, and the median overall survival period was 5.5 months7. Therefore, accurately distinguishing MPE from benign pleural effusion is crucial for early clinical intervention and adequate management.

The diagnosis of MPE mainly relies on cytological examination of pleural exfoliation and pleural biopsy. Pleural biopsy is an invasive procedure that can lead to various complications including bleeding and infection. In contrast, pleural exfoliation cytology is a less invasive procedure, although it has lower sensitivity and specificity compared with pleural biopsy. In fact, the diagnosis of malignant pleural effusion in some NSCLC patients due to negative cytology remains unclear. In recent years, various biomarkers have been discovered in pleural exudate cytology, such as serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA), carbohydrate antigen 153 (CA153), and serum total cholesterol (tc) levels of adenosine deaminase (ADA)8–10. In addition, novel biomarkers such as reactive oxygen species regulator 1 (ROMO1), hyaluronic acid (HA), chitinase 3-like protein 1 (YKL-40), thymidine kinase 1 (TK1), pro-cathepsin D precursor, and programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1), etc. All can improve the diagnostic ability of pleural exfoliation cytology11–16. It is well known that TF-1 is highly expressed in lung tissue, and Napsin A shows high specificity and sensitivity in lung adenocarcinoma17. Studies have shown that the combination of Napsin A and TTF-1 can improve the diagnostic accuracy. The combined detection of the markers of both can effectively distinguish lung adenocarcinoma from other types of lung cancer and is helpful for differentiating malignant and benign pleural effusion18,19. To our knowledge, there is no consensus on the most effective biomarkers for differentiating MPE from benign pleural effusion. Therefore, it is worth exploring new biomarkers for differentiating MPE.

In this study, we screened out cell division cycle protein 20 (CDC20) from the Gepia database. Existing studies have shown that CDC20 plays a role in various tumors such as pancreatic cancer20, breast cancer21, prostate cancer22, colorectal cancer23, liver cell carcinoma24, glioma25, gastric cancer26, bladder cancer27, and cervical cancer28. Although the role of CDC20 in the occurrence and development of many tumors has been extensively studied, its specific molecular mechanism and clinical significance in lung adenocarcinoma have not been fully elucidated. Current research mainly focuses on the diagnosis and prognosis of complications caused by CDC20 in lung adenocarcinoma patients - malignant pleural effusion. However, little is known about its role in the pathological diagnosis and prognosis of lung adenocarcinoma patients, and there is a lack of systematic analysis.CDC20 is involved in the occurrence and development of various tumors, but its biological role and mechanism in lung adenocarcinoma and malignant pleural effusion remain unclear. This study aims to explore the expression of CDC20 in lung adenocarcinoma and its diagnostic value for malignant pleural effusion.

Materials and methods

Patient information and clinical samples

These experiments using human tissue samples fully comply with all relevant national regulations and institutional policies, and are in line with the principles of the Helsinki Declaration. The research plan has been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Chengdu Medical College-Nuclear Industry No.416Hospital (Approval Number: YJ-2025-039-01). And strictly abide by the confidentiality agreement. Due to privacy and ethical restrictions, the original data cannot be publicly shared.All clinical samples were collected at the hospital, and informed consent forms were obtained from all subjects. Primary tumor samples and paired normal lung tissues were obtained from 92 LUAD patients during surgical resection. The inclusion criteria are as follows: (a) a clear diagnosis of LUAD, (b) no other malignant tumors, (c) according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) classification, TNM stage is I-IV, (d) complete clinical and pathological data, including age, gender, TNM stage and clinical stage. In addition, pleural effusion was collected from 30 LUAD patients, and pleural effusion was also collected from 30 non-tumor patients. The inclusion criteria for LUAD patients are (a) pathologically diagnosed as LUAD, (b) no other malignant tumors. The inclusion criteria for non-tumor subjects are: (a) no malignant tumors; (b) no cancer cells in the pleural effusion29.

ImmunohistoChemistry (IHC)

Tissue specimens were fxed in 4% phosphate-bufered paraformaldehyde and embedded in parafn. Fresh pleural efusion was left undisturbed for 20–30 min, and the sediment was collected and centrifuged at 2500 rpm for 5 min. After discarding the supernatant, the precipitate was successively washed with 75% and 95% ethanol, and then fxed in 10% formalin for 2–4 h. The solid cell pellet was dehydrated and processed with the same steps used for histological specimens. The parafn blocks were cut into 2 μm-thick sections and subjected to routine immunohistochemistry. The specimens were probed with anti-CDC20 (abcam, #ab31703),anti-TTF1(MXB®Biotechnologies, Fuzhou, China, #MAB-0677) and anti-Napsin A (MXB®Biotechnologies, Fuzhou, China, #MAB-0704) antibodies. Rabbit pre-immune serum was used as the negative control for primary antibody. Color was developed using diaminobenzidine (DAB)substrate and the sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. The sections were observed using the Olympus BX53 microscope. Staining intensity was scored as follows: Immunohistochemical (IHC) scoring criteria:1.Staining Intensity: Scored based on the depth of staining of the positive cells.0 points: No staining.1 point: Light yellow (weakly positive)0.2 points: Brownish yellow (moderately positive)0.3 points: Brownish black (strongly positive)0.2.Percentage of Positive Cells: Randomly select 5 fields of view under high magnification (×400), and count the percentage of positive cells in each field of view out of the total number of cells. 0 points: 0%. 1 point: 1%-25%. 2 points: 26% − 50%. 3 points: 51% − 75%0.4 points: 76% − 100%.Final Score: Staining Index (SI) = Staining Intensity×Stained Cells. Patients with scores of 0 and 1 are classified into the low-expression group, and patients with scores of 2 and 3 are classified into the high-expression group.

Immunohistochemical diagnosis result:1.Double-blind review: Two experienced pathologists independently review the slides and score them without knowing the patient’s clinical and pathological data.2.Consistency test: The Kappa consistency test (Kappa Coefficient) is used to evaluate the consistency of the scores between the two observers. A Kappa value of ≥ 0.75indicates excellent consistency,0.4 ≤ Kappa value < 0.75indicates moderate consistency, and Kappa value < 0.4indicates poor consistency.3.Discrepancy resolution: For cases with inconsistent scores, the two physicians jointly review the slides and discuss to reach a consensus, or a third senior pathologist conducts arbitration to determine the final score29.

Hematoxylin and eosin staining (H&E staining)

Tissue specimens were fxed in 10% neutral-bufered formalin and embedded in parafn. Tissue sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) to facilitate the histological examination of tissue morphology and to identify any pathological changes29.

Statistical analysis

SPSS software (version 27.0; IBM, Chicago, IL) was used for statistical analysis. Chi-square test was used to analyze the correlation between clinicopathological characteristics and CDC20 expression. P value < 0.05 was statistically signifcant.

Result

Discover from the database that CDC20 is a potential diagnostic and prognostic biomarker for lung adenocarcinoma

To identify biomarkers for differentiating malignant and benign pleural effusion in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, we first screened out CDC20 in the Gepia database. Compared with normal tissues, CDC20 was significantly upregulated in LUAD tissues and was associated with prognosis ( Fig. 1), indicating that CDC20 is a potential diagnostic marker for lung adenocarcinoma.

Based on the current research results, we hypothesize that CDC20 is a potential biomarker for diagnosing pleural effusion in patients with LUAD. To verify the hypothesis, we detected the expressions of CDC20, TTF-1 and NapsinA in pleural effusion cell blocks of 30 patients with lung adenocarcinoma and 30 patients with non-neoplastic tumors through IHC. The expression trends of CDC20, TTF-1 and NapsinA were relatively consistent ( Fig. 3). The positive rate of CDC20 in patients with lung adenocarcinoma is 90%, while it is 6.67% in non-tumor tissues. Discussion

In patients with lung adenocarcinoma, malignant pleural effusion is very common. Malignant pleural effusion often indicates that the disease has entered an advanced or progressive stage. Pleural effusion cytology is a minimally invasive diagnostic method, making it urgent to identify new and effective biomarkers. This study first analyzed bioinformatics databases and found that CDC20 is significantly highly expressed in lung adenocarcinoma. CDC20 is one of the cell cycle-related proteins and acts as a regulatory factor for the anaphase-promoting complex, playing a key regulatory role in chromosome segregation and mitosis30,31.

Further experimental validation showed that the expression of CDC20 is upregulated in both lung adenocarcinoma tissues and cell blocks from malignant pleural effusion, which is consistent with the bioinformatics predictions. Additionally, high expression of CDC20 is associated with tumor size, T stage, and pleural invasion. Analysis of public databases revealed that CDC20 is significantly overexpressed in lung adenocarcinoma. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis indicated that lung adenocarcinoma patients with high CDC20 expression have a relatively higher risk of mortality. Finally, validation using ROC curves and calibration curves demonstrated that the prognostic model based on CDC20 has good performance. Therefore, CDC20 is an important biomarker for prognostic assessment in lung adenocarcinoma. The established markers for lung adenocarcinoma are TTF-1 and NapsinA. We found that the expression of CDC20 is consistent with that of the lung adenocarcinoma markers TTF-1 and NapsinA, suggesting that detecting CDC20 expression has reference value for the diagnosis of lung adenocarcinoma. CDC20 is a potential biomarker and therapeutic target for lung cancer worthy of further investigation.

Current research on the role of CDC20 in tumor pathogenesis primarily focuses on tumor tissue samples. Pleural effusion is a common symptom of advanced lung adenocarcinoma and needs to be differentiated from benign pleural effusion, which is crucial for determining the optimal treatment plan. We found that CDC20 is an effective biomarker for diagnosing lung adenocarcinoma in pleural effusion cytology, with higher sensitivity for malignant pleural effusion compared to non-neoplastic pleural effusion. Furthermore, the expression of CDC20 in lung adenocarcinoma tissues is also higher than in paired normal tissues, and it is consistent with its expression in malignant pleural effusion, supporting CDC20 as a reliable biomarker for lung adenocarcinoma biopsies. Regarding the quantitative comparison of the diagnostic value of CDC20, TTF-1, and NapsinA, this study found that all three indicators can significantly distinguish the lung adenocarcinoma group. Additionally, ROC curve analysis was conducted to compare the diagnostic accuracy of the three indicators. The results showed that CDC20 has higher diagnostic value than TTF-1 and NapsinA. However, based on the current findings, a limitation of this study is the small sample size, and the conclusions need to be validated by larger-scale studies. In the future, we will address this limitation by increasing the sample size.

Although the latest diagnostic guidelines for lung adenocarcinoma recommend TTF-1 and Napsin A as immunohistochemical markers for biopsy tissues or exfoliated cytology32, improper handling may affect the sensitivity of these markers18. Therefore, combining CDC20 with TTF-1 and Napsin A may help improve diagnostic sensitivity.

In conclusion, CDC20 is a potential immune-related biomarker in lung adenocarcinoma biopsy tissues and pleural effusion specimens. This provides a new and effective option for cytological examination of pleural effusion and may become a key target for future treatment strategies for pleural effusion. In-depth investigation into the specific mechanism of CDC20 in lung adenocarcinoma and its interactions with other molecules can provide a theoretical basis and experimental evidence for the development of new therapeutic approaches.

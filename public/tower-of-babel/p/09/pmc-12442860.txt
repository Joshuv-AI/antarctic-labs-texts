Ultrafast and scalable synthesis of enzyme-metal hybrid catalysts in evaporating microdroplets
Authors: Yida Qiao, Ruobing Xin, Xiaoyang Li, Jun Ge
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
One-pot chemoenzymatic cascade reactions integrate the advantages of enzymatic catalysis and chemical catalysis, offering routes to synthesize enantiopure compounds in an efficient and environmentally friendly manner. The rational design of enzyme-metal hybrid catalysts (EMHCs) is an attractive strategy for operating chemoenzymatic cascade reactions efficiently under mild conditions. In this study, we develop a method for the instantaneous and continuous synthesis of EMHCs in evaporating microdroplets. Compared to traditional wet impregnation methods, the reaction time of the microdroplet-assisted method is shortened from days to seconds, achieving a space-time yield increase by two to five orders of magnitude. The as-prepared Pd/lipase cross-linked enzyme aggregates with ultrasmall Pd nanoclusters and well-preserved structure of enzymes exhibit excellent activity in the dynamic kinetic resolution of chiral amines. The mechanism for the process enhancement in the synthesis of EMHCs in evaporating microdroplets is studied by theoretical calculations, indicating that the fast solvent evaporation and high-heat transfer are the major factors for the instantaneous synthesis of EMHCs.

INTRODUCTION

Chiral amines are essential building blocks for a wide variety of active pharmaceutical ingredients. One-pot chemoenzymatic cascade reaction merges chemo-catalysis with the exquisite stereoselectivity of enzymatic catalysis, allowing the preparation of enantiopure compounds in a green and efficient manner under mild reaction conditions (1–6). The dynamic kinetic resolution (DKR) reactions, which combines enzyme-catalyzed kinetic resolution with metal-catalyzed in situ racemization, have been extensively studied for the synthesis of chiral amines (7–10). The major challenge encountered in the DKR reactions is the intrinsic incompatibility between enzymatic and chemical catalysis. The racemization reactions usually perform efficiently at high temperature, which causes serious inactivation of enzyme.

To address this challenge, enzyme-metal hybrid catalysts (EMHCs) with ultrasmall metal nanoclusters were constructed by in situ reduction of metal ions on enzyme-polymer conjugates (11–13) or enzyme aggregates (14, 15). The coordination of metal subnanoclusters with enzymes render the partially oxidized form of metal, which contributed to the greatly enhanced catalytic activity in racemization reaction at a relatively low temperature (11). This allows the chemoenzymatic cascade reaction proceeds at a relatively low temperature that can highly preserve enzyme activity. In addition, the diffusion resistance of reaction intermediates can be reduced on EMHCs because of the proximity effect. Therefore, the efficient one-pot chemoenzymatic cascade reactions under mild conditions can be achieved using EMHCs as the catalysts. However, the industrial application of EMHCs has been hindered by challenges in large-scale synthesis of the catalysts.

The synthesis of EMHCs via wet impregnation methods in bulk solution involves multiple steps, including enzyme modification, metal nanoparticle generation, product separation, and lyophilization. These processes require several days of reaction time and specialized equipment (16–20). To synthesize ultrasmall metal nanoclusters on enzymes, the concentration of metal ions must be strictly controlled to remain low during the reduction reaction, suffering from slow reduction rate and low space-time yield. Therefore, the scalable synthesis of biohybrids containing ultrasmall metal nanoclusters and exhibiting excellent enzymatic activity with high efficiency remains a major challenge.

In this work, we propose a method for synthesizing EMHCs by simultaneously self-assembly of enzyme aggregates and in situ reduction of metal subnanoclusters in the interface of evaporating microdroplets generated during spray drying. Because of the fast solvent evaporation and high-heat transfer, the concentration of metal ions rapidly increased within milliseconds. The cross-linked enzyme aggregates (CLEAs) can be instantaneously formed and simultaneously reduce the metal ions in the microdroplets. Compared to traditional wet impregnation methods, this approach reduces the reaction time from days to seconds, achieving a space-time yield increase by two to five orders of magnitude with production efficiency of 1.7 kg/day. The rate constant for the synthesis of Pd/Candida antarctica lipase B (CALB) CLEAs was approximate 300 times higher than that of the wet impregnation method. The biohybrid catalysts exhibited excellent activity in the DKR reactions for gram-scale production of chiral amines.

RESULTS

Synthesis of EMHC in evaporating microdroplets

The EMHC was synthesized in evaporating microdroplets using Pd(OAc)2, glutaraldehyde, and CALB as the precursors (Fig. 1A). Pd(OAc)2 dissolved in methanol was injected into the nozzle of spray dryer along with the CALB and glutaraldehyde solution. To ensure effective mixing, the solutions were injected independently through different channels and mixed in an inserted connector before reaching the two-fluid nozzle (fig. S2). The solutions and compressed air were simultaneously injected to the spray dryer at constant flow rates. The microdroplets were generated by the spray drying, and the high-temperature gas stream caused the evaporation of solvent in the microdroplets, leading to radial diffusion of the solutes (the precursors) to the surface of microdroplets. Enzyme molecules are expected to be distributed at the gas-liquid interface of the microdroplets because of the amphipathic protein structure. During the evaporating process, the formation of CALB CLEAs and in situ reduction of metal nanoclusters by the amino acid residues of the enzyme are expected to perform concurrently. After several seconds of reaction, the EMHCs powder was obtained. This method enables the continuous and large-scale production of EMHCs.

The morphology of the as-prepared lipase-Pd hybrid catalyst (Pd/CALB CLEAs) was characterized by scanning electron microscopy. As shown in Fig. 1B, the Pd/CALB CLEAs hybrids were spherical particles with size of 1 to 2 μm. The high-angle annular dark-field scanning transmission electron microscopy (HAADF-STEM) image and the corresponding energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy mapping showed that the Pd nanoclusters (NCs) were uniformly dispersed on CALB CLEAs with an average size of 1.3 nm (Fig. 1, C and D). The Pd(111) in the crystal lattice was observed as the exposed facet in the image of high-resolution transmission electron microscope (fig. S3). The loading amount of Pd of Pd/CALB CLEAs can be tuned by adjusting the concentration of Pd(OAc)2 during the spray-drying process. The metal mass loading of Pd/CALB CLEAs was up to 6.7 wt % when using Pd(OAc)2 (0.6 mg/ml) as the precursor, as measured by inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectrometry (table S1). As shown in fig. S5, larger Pd nanoparticles with slight aggregation were observed in the HAADF-STEM image of Pd/CALB CLEAs synthesized by mixing CALB, Pd(OAc)2, and glutaraldehyde before injection. Pd2+ ions may be reduced on the enzyme surface by the reducing amino acids and methanol when mixing CALB and Pd(OAc)2 in the methanol-water solution (11), showing that the independent injection of Pd(OAc)2 and enzyme solutions are necessary to avoid the aggregation of Pd NCs.

The mechanism for the formation of Pd/CALB CLEAs in the microdroplets during the spray-drying process was further investigated by dissipative particle dynamics (DPD) simulations (fig. S1) and experimental study. The three-dimensional (3D) and cross-sectional views of the simulation snapshots are presented in Fig. 1 (E to J) and fig. S6, showing that the amphipathic enzymes first migrated to the gas-liquid interface. Subsequently, the glutaraldehyde and Pd nanoparticles inside the droplets gradually diffused toward the surface of the microdroplets. Eventually, Pd nanoparticles (NPs) and glutaraldehyde were mostly adsorbed onto enzyme in the gas-liquid interface (Fig. 1K). The size of microdroplets at different feed flow rates was characterized using a high-speed charge-coupled device camera (fig. S8). The average diameter of microdroplets generated during the spray drying with feed flow rates of 6, 9, and 12 liter/min were 11, 30, and 55 μm, respectively, as determined by the software Digital Micrograph (fig. S9). The microdroplet has a high specific surface area and surface energy, which promotes the spontaneous distribution of amphiphilic enzymes at the gas-liquid interface. As a result, spontaneous physical aggregation of the enzymes at the microscale can be achieved, prompting the enzyme cross-linking reaction and the formation of the spherical enzyme superstructure.

The distribution of enzymes in the microdroplet was confirmed by confocal laser scanning microscopy using red fluorescent Rhodamine B–labeled CALB (Fig. 2B). The fluorescence intensity across a single microdroplet revealed that the CALB CLEAs formed at the surface of the microdroplet (Fig. 2C). After the solvent evaporation of the microdroplet, the Pd/CALB CLEAs were fabricated with a hollow structure as characterized by TEM (Fig. 2A). Because of the enzyme aggregates rendering the hollow microspheres more compact and increasing the mass of individual microspheres, the recovery rate of Pd/CALB CLEAs (81%) is higher than that of CALB (47%) and Pd/CALB (67%) obtained via spray drying (fig. S11).

The coordination environment and chemical state of Pd in Pd/CALB CLEAs were investigated by x-ray absorption fine structures (XAFS) and x-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). In the Fourier transformation of extended XAFS (FT-EXAFS), there were notable peaks at ~1.6 Å from the Pd-O and ~ 2.7 Å from the Pd-Pd were detected in the spectrum of Pd/CALB CLEAs (Fig. 2D). Quantitative EXAFS curve-fitting analysis was then performed to investigate the coordination configuration. The fitting of Pd/CALB CLEAs showed two coordination shells of Pd-O and Pd-Pd with coordination numbers of 3.1 and 1.0, respectively (table S3). From the wavelet transforms (WT) of the Pd K-edge EXAFS signal of Pd/CALB CLEAs, the intensity maximum at 5.4 Å−1 was ascribed to Pd-O coordination (Fig. 2E).

The chemical state of Pd in the hybrid catalysts was characterized by XPS. As shown in Fig. 2F, two sets of split peaks were observed in the Pd 3d XPS spectrum of Pd/CALB CLEAs, corresponding to Pd0 and Pd2+ species, respectively. In the O 1s, N 1s, and S 2p XPS spectra, the O-Pd characteristic peak at 530.5 eV was observed, while no peaks corresponding to N-Pd or S-Pd were detected (Fig. 2G and fig. S12). These results suggest that the Pd2+ atoms on the surface of the hybrid catalyst coordinated with the O atoms of the CLAB CLEAs, which is consistent with the EXAFS results. The Asp and Ser residues on the enzyme surface may serve as potential binding sites for the metal atoms (21).

Circular dichroism (CD) and fluorescence spectroscopy were performed to investigate the influence of Pd NPs on the secondary and tertiary structures of CALB. Because of the excellent dispersion of Pd/CALB CLEAs in aqueous solution (fig. S13), the sample in the CD detection was clear, thus avoiding the interference of solid turbidity during the measurement. As shown in Fig. 2H and table S4, compared to Pd/CALB synthesized by spray drying, which displayed notable change in the secondary structure of enzyme, the CALB in the Pd/CALB CLEAs nanohybrids maintained most of the secondary structure of native CALB. In addition, Pd/CALB CLEAs retained more hydrophilic structures of the enzyme than Pd/CALB (fig. S14). These results suggest that the covalent cross-linking of enzymes during spray drying enhances enzyme stability and reduces structural disruption caused by the interaction with metal particles. In addition, the CD spectrum of Pd/CALB CLEAs is similar to that of CALB and CALB CLEAs obtained via spray drying, indicating that the Pd subnanoclusters on Pd/CALB CLEAs have minimal impact on the enzyme structure.

The metallic activity of Pd/CALB CLEAs was evaluated in the hydrogenation of 4-nitrophenol to 4-aminophenol. To investigate the effect of ultrasmall size of Pd subnanoclusters on the metallic activity, several reference hybrid catalysts with larger Pd NPs were prepared, including Pd/CALB synthesized by spray drying, Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs and Pd(0)/CALB synthesized via the wet-chemistry method (figs. S15 and S20). The pseudo–first-order reaction kinetics are shown in fig. S16. The rate constant of Pd/CALB CLEAs was 0. 21 min−1, which is 2, 3, and 3.2 times higher than that of Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs, Pd/CALB, and Pd(0)/CALB, respectively, indicating that Pd/CALB CLEAs exhibits excellent metallic catalytic activity.

The enzyme catalytic activity of Pd/CALB CLEAs was evaluated in the hydrolysis reaction of p-nitrophenyl butyrate. The relative enzyme activity of CALB-spray, CALB CLEAs, Pd/CALB CLEAs, and Pd/CALB obtained by the spray-drying methods were 98, 78, 73, and 51% compared to that of free CALB, respectively (Fig. 2I). The enzymatic activity of Pd/CALB CLEAs was higher than Pd/CALB, which is attributed to the CALB CLEAs, can better protect the enzyme structure during the spray-drying process. The relative enzyme activities of CALB CLEAs and Pd/CALB CLEAs were lower than that of the free CALB. The decrease in activity may be attributed to the cross-linking of enzymes, which increases the mass transfer resistance (22) and slightly alters the secondary structure of the enzymes (Fig. 2H). In addition, The EMHCs were constructed using ADH, CAT, Lac, and GOx as enzymes to demonstrate the generality of the method. As shown in fig. S18, ultrasmall Pd nanoclusters were uniformly dispersed on the CLEAs in the Pd/ADH CLEAs, Pd/CAT CLEAs, Pd/Lac CLEAs, and Pd/GOx CLEAs, indicating that metal nanoclusters can be successfully synthesized on enzymes using the spray-drying method. The relative enzyme activity of Pd/ADH CLEAs, Pd/CAT CLEAs, Pd/Lac CLEAs, and Pd/GOx CLEAs were 62, 49, 64, and 57% compared to that of the free enzymes, respectively (fig. S19), demonstrating that the spray-drying method can be a general approach for production of EMHCs.

The powder of EMHCs can be obtained immediately with the continuous injection of the precursor solutions. The production efficiency of Pd/CALB CLEAs was 1.7 kg/day using the spray-drying method with just a small laboratory instrument. In conventional wet-chemical methods, the nucleation and growth of Pd nanoparticles on the enzyme typically require 8 to 16 hours (figs. S20 and S21), whereas the synthesis of Pd/CALB CLEAs using the spray-drying method takes only a few seconds. The space-time yield of Pd/CALB CLEAs is 120 g/(liter·hour), which is two to five orders of magnitude higher than that of the representative CALB-Pd hybrids synthesized via wet-chemistry methods reported previously, including Pd/CALB-P [0.08 g/(liter·hour)] (11) and Pd-AmP-MCF/CALB [0.16 g/(liter·hour)] (Fig. 3A) (7). This result indicated that the large-scale, continuous-flow synthesis of the EMHCs can be achieved using spray drying with a substantially higher productivity than the traditional wet-chemistry methods.

Mechanism for the process enhancement in the synthesis of EMHCs

To gain insight into the rapid reduction of Pd2+ ions on CALB CLEAs within the microdroplets, the mechanism for the process enhancement in the synthesis of EMHCs using spray drying was further investigated. As shown in fig. S22, the reduction kinetic of Pd2+ in the synthesis of Pd/CALB CLEAs exhibits a strong temperature dependence, with the reduction process accelerating as the temperature increases. The heat transfer within the microdroplets were analyzed through theoretical calculations. As shown in Fig. 3B, the droplet temperature increases extremely quickly within a short time (i.e., milliseconds), demonstrating a high-heat transfer in the microdroplets during the formation of EMHCs. As the initial size of microdroplet decreased, the heat transfer rate increased. In addition, faster heat transfer can be achieved at higher inlet temperature in the spray-drying process (fig. S23). These results indicated that the high temperature and rapid heat transfer within the microdroplet is beneficial for the reduction of Pd2+ ions.

The relationship between the reaction rate and temperature was fitted using the Arrhenius equation, yielding an activation energy of 43.95 kJ/mol for the reduction of Pd2+ ions on the surface of CALB (fig. S24). The activation energy for the surface reduction of Pd2+ on the enzyme is lower than that of the solution reduction of Pd2+ ions (131.3 kJ/mol) (22, 23) using polyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP) as the stabilizer and ascorbic acid as the reducing agent, which may be due to that the deposition of Pd2+ ions onto the surface of enzyme facilitates the growth of nanocrystal. The effects of the enzyme and Pd precursor concentrations on the reduction rate were further studied. As shown in Fig. 3C and fig. S25, the reduction rate of Pd2+ is proportional to enzyme and Pd precursor concentrations in the synthesis of Pd/CALB CLEAs, with the reduction rate increasing as the precursor concentrations increases.

The solvent evaporation of microdroplets was analyzed by theoretical calculations. As shown in Fig. 3D, the diameter of the microdroplet gradually decreased as evaporation time increased and faster droplet shrinkage was observed at higher inlet temperature. In addition, the initial microdroplet size also plays a crucial role in the evaporation of solvent in microdroplets. As the initial microdroplet size decreases, the shrinkage rate of droplet increases, indicating a higher evaporation rate (fig. S26). The rapid recession of the droplet surface leads to an increase in the precursor concentrations within the microdroplet (Fig. 3E), enhancing the mass transfer in the fabrication of EMHCs. The Pd2+ concentration in the microdroplet rapidly increased within 1 ms when using the spray-drying method, whereas the Pd2+ concentration showed no obvious change over the corresponding time period and gradually decreased within 1 min when using the wet chemical method (figs. S27 and S28). The calculated rate constant for the reduction of Pd2+ in the synthesis of Pd/CALB CLEAs using spray-drying method (0.305 s−1) at 1.65 ms was approximate 300 times higher than that of the synthesis of Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs using wet-chemistry method (0.001 s−1) (Fig. 3F). The above results demonstrated that efficient heat transfer and fast solvent evaporation within microdroplets are important factors for enhancing the synthesis efficiency of EMHCs.

Catalytic performance of Pd/CALB CLEAs in DKR reactions of amines

The catalytic performance of Pd/CALB CLEAs for the production of chiral amines were evaluated using 1-phenylethylamine (1a) as substrate. In the DKR reaction, the Pd-catalyzed racemization of (S)-amine was coupled with the CALB-catalyzed acylation of (R)-amine to produce optically pure acetamide. The conversion of (S)-1-phenylethylamine in the racemization reactions catalyzed by Pd/CALB CLEAs hybrids are shown in table S5. Under optimal conditions of racemization reaction for (S)-1-phenylethylamine, the enantiomeric excess (e.e.) value of 1-phenylethylamine (100 mM) reached 0% within 10 hours at 70°C, using 1.2 mM of Pd/CALB CLEAs as the catalyst. The catalytic efficiency of Pd/CALB CLEAs in racemization reaction was higher than that of Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs (conversion 23%) synthesized by the traditional wet-chemistry method. The enzymatic activity of Pd/CALB CLEAs in the kinetic resolution of 1-phenylethylamine is shown in table S6. The yield reached 44% within 5 hours at 70°C using Pd/CALB CLEAs as the catalyst, which is 3.4, 1.7 and 1.1 times higher than that of Pd/CALB hybrids prepared by spray drying, CALB power obtained by spray drying, and CALB power obtained by lyophilization, respectively. The enzymatic activity of Pd/CALB CLEAs was comparable with the commercial lipase catalyst (Novozym 435) in the kinetic resolution reaction.

Combining the high Pd activity and enzymatic activity together, the catalytic performance and substrate scope of Pd/CALB CLEAs catalyzing the DKR reaction for the mass production of chiral amines was further investigated (Fig. 4A and table S7). Enantiopure amines are key intermediates for chiral pharmaceuticals and agrochemicals. For example, (R)-1-aminoindan (1f) is the intermediate of rasagiline for the treatment of Parkinson’s disease. As shown in Fig. 4A, all chiral acetamides were acquired in high yields (80 to 93%) with e.e. values of >99% in the DKR reactions catalyzed by Pd/CALB CLEAs. In the DKR reaction of 1-phenylethylamine (6 g), the conversion reached 92% and the e.e. value was >99% within 16 hours when using Pd/CALB CLEAs as the catalyst, whereas the conversions were 82, 65, and 63% under the same conditions when using Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs, Pd/CALB, and Pd(0)/CALB as the catalysts, respectively (Fig. 4B). The excellent performance of Pd/CALB CLEAs in the one-pot chemoenzymatic cascade reaction can be attributed to the ultrasmall Pd subnanoclusters and the well-preserved structure of enzyme in the hybrid catalyst. In addition, the conversions only reached 57 and 36% within 16 hours at 70°C when using the combinations of CALB CLEAs and Pd/C, and commercial catalysts (Novozym 435 and Pd/C) as the catalysts, respectively.

The Pd/CALB CLEAs hybrid catalyst can be easily reused by centrifugation. Pd/CALB CLEAs exhibited 95% residual activity and an e.e. value greater than 99% after five batches of reuse (Fig. 4C). The amount of Pd0 in Pd/CALB CLEAs increased after the reaction, as determined by the Pd 3d XPS (fig. S29), which may due to the reduction of Pd2+ during the reaction. As characterized by HAADF-STEM (Fig. 4D), the Pd nanoclusters did not detach or aggregate after the reaction. The excellent catalytic performance of EMHCs synthesized via the spray-drying method in one-pot chemoenzymatic cascade reactions under mild conditions demonstrates tremendous potential for application in organic synthesis.

DISCUSSION

In conclusion, we have developed an ultrafast and scalable method to synthesize EMHCs in evaporating microdroplets. EMHCs can be produced continuously at kilogram scale. Compared to traditional wet impregnation methods, the reaction time of the microdroplet-assisted method can be shortened from days to seconds. The enhancement of mass and heat transfer in the microdroplets, facilitating the instantaneously assembly of enzyme aggregates and the reduction of metal nanoclusters. The EMHCs exhibited both excellent enzymatic and metallic activities. The catalytic performance of Pd/CALB CLEAs in the DKR reactions of amines was superior to that of the counterparts synthesized via the wet impregnation methods and commercial catalysts. This spray-drying approach is promising for massive preparation of EMHCs in a rapid and continuous manner for industrial applications.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Preparation of Pd/CALB CLEAs and Pd/CALB

The instantaneous and continuous synthesis of Pd/CALB CLEAs was conducted on a spray dryer (BUCHI Mini Spray Dryer B-290). The palladium acetate solution (dissolved in methanol) and the CALB (20 mg/ml) aqueous solution (phosphate buffer, 50 mM, pH 7.0) containing glutaraldehyde (3.25 mmol/ml) were independently pumped to a T-type connector, where the two solutions were mixed. The mixture was then spray-dried using a spray cap with a 0.5-mm hole at feed flow rate of 45 ml/min, gas flow rate of 336 ml/min, and inlet temperature of 120°C. The powder of Pd/CALB CLEAs (1.2 g) was obtained within 1 min. The concentrations of Pd(OAc)2 used in the synthesis of Pd/CALB CLEAs nanohybrids with different Pd loadings are shown in table S1. The powder of Pd/CALB CLEAs was stored at 4°C. Before characterization and application, the powder of Pd/CALB CLEAs was resuspended in dry toluene and reduced by NaBH4 under stirring at 25°C for 30 min. Pd/CALB was synthesized under the same conditions as Pd/CALB CLEAs, except that glutaraldehyde was not used as the cross-linker in the synthesis of Pd/CALB.

Preparation of Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs and Pd(0)/CALB

Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs was synthesized through wet-chemistry method by in situ reduction of Pd nanoparticles on CALB CLEAs in bulk solution (15). Bovine serum albumin (600 mg) was dissolved in CALB solution (40 ml). Then, (NH4)2SO4 (20 g) was added into the mixture to acquire the CALB aggregates. Glutaraldehyde (1.6 ml) was added to the suspension to cross-link the CALB aggregates. After stirring for 1 hour, 1.2 ml l-lysine solution (1 ml/min) was added to terminate the cross-linking reaction. The CALB CLEAs was collected by centrifugation at 4°C, 7800 rpm for 20 min. The powder of CALB CLEAs was obtained by lyophilization. CALB CLEAs (12.5 mg/ml) were dispersed in phosphate-buffered solution (50 mM, pH 7.0) with magnetic stirring. Palladium acetate (2.8 mg/ml) was dissolved in methanol and then added to the CALB CLEAs solution. After magnetic stirring for 16 hours at 25°C, Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs were obtained by centrifugation at 4°C, 7800 rpm for 10 min. The precipitate was washed with phosphate-buffered solution for three times. The powder of the Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs was obtained by lyophilization and stored at 4°C. Pd(0)/CALB was synthesized through wet-chemistry method by in situ reduction of Pd nanoparticles on CALB in bulk solution. As a reference material, Pd(0)/CALB was synthesized under the similar conditions as that of Pd(0)/CALB CLEAs with the same weight ratio of CALB (2 mg/ml) and Pd(OAc)2 (2.8 mg/ml). After stirring at 25°C for 16 hours, the Pd(0)/CALB nanohybrid was collected by centrifugation at 4°C, 7800 rpm for 20 min. The powder of Pd(0)/CALB was obtained by lyophilization and then stored at 4°C.

DKR of amines

In a typical reaction, Pd/CALB CLEAs (2.5 g) was dispersed in dry toluene (500 ml) and added to a 1000-ml stainless autoclave. Racemic amines (50 mmol) and ethyl acetate (15 ml, 150 mmol) were added into the stainless autoclave. The reactor was evacuated and filled with argon. The reaction was stirred at 70°C under a 0.5 MPa. The conversions and e.e. values were monitored by chiral high-performance liquid chromatography analysis. The catalytic performance of Pd/CALB CLEAs was compared to that of the combination of commercially Novozym 435 and Pd/C under the same conditions. For the reuse of Pd/CALB CLEAs, the catalyst was separated by centrifugation after the reaction mixture was cooled to room temperature (25°C). After washing with dry toluene for three times, the precipitated Pd/CALB CLEAs was transferred to the reactor and resuspended in toluene at 70°C by stirring to carry out the next run of reaction (11).

Numerical calculation of the mass and heat transfer in the microdroplet generated during spray drying

To simulate the mass and heat transfer in the microdroplet generated during spray drying, the following assumptions are proposed: (i) The temperature is uniform in the microdroplets. For the microdroplets, the characteristic time to reach thermal equilibrium is on the order of 10−5 s, which is much shorter than the evaporation time. The temperature distribution in the microdroplets can be neglected; (ii) the Kelvin effect can be neglected since the particle size of the catalyst is much larger than 0.1 μm; (iii) only the heat conduction effect is considered, and the influence of thermal convection and radiation are ignored. As the gas and liquid phases move in the same direction during spray drying, the actual free and forced heat convections are weak; (iv) the only mass transfer that occurs within the microdroplet is solvent evaporation, the effects of droplet solidification, diffusion, and thermophoretic are ignored (22).

Details for DPD

All DPD simulations are performed in a cubic box of size 40 by 40 by 40 Rc (24) containing 1.92 × 105 beads by using pygamd package (25) on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 GPU. The integration time step 0.04 is used, and the total simulation steps are 1 × 106 for each simulation. All figures of the molecular structures were created using the OVITO Basic (26) program (version 3.10.4). The details of DPD are provided in the Supplementary Materials.

Linear Endobronchial Ultrasound‐Guided Microwave Ablation for Malignant Central Airway Obstruction: A World‐First Case
Authors: Miguel Ariza‐Prota, Javier Pérez‐Pallarés, Lucía García Alfonso, Angela Lanza Martínez, Marta García‐Clemente, Francisco López‐González
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe report the world's first case of malignant central airway obstruction (MCAO) successfully treated with microwave ablation (MWA) under real‐time linear endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) guidance. A 72‐year‐old male with stage IVB lung adenocarcinoma presented with severe dyspnea from a tumour causing 90% obstruction of the right main bronchus. Using a linear EBUS scope, we performed precise tumour mapping and positioned a MWA antenna with a 0.7 cm safety margin. Controlled energy was delivered until an intratumoral temperature of 80°C was reached, achieving immediate cytoreduction. The procedure resulted in 100% luminal recanalization without requiring a stent. EBUS‐guided MWA is a feasible, safe, and highly effective technique for MCAO, offering unparalleled precision and thermal control. This novel approach represents a significant advancement in interventional pulmonology, enabling effective treatment of complex airway tumours while enhancing patient safety.

Introduction

Malignant central airway obstruction (MCAO) is a life‐threatening oncologic emergency that impairs quality of life [1]. Standard bronchoscopic therapies, such as mechanical debulking or laser, primarily target the endoluminal tumour component. Their efficacy is often limited by a high recurrence rate, particularly in tumours with significant extraluminal extension, which are challenging for conventional bronchoscopy [2].

Microwave ablation (MWA) is a thermal technique that creates rapid, predictable coagulative necrosis. Its application within the central airways has been limited by the risk of thermal injury to adjacent vital structures, such as the pulmonary artery. The key challenge has been the inability to precisely target and control energy delivery.

Linear endobronchial ultrasound (EBUS) offers detailed, real‐time imaging of the airway wall and surrounding structures [3]. We hypothesized that leveraging EBUS to guide MWA could overcome current limitations, transforming it into a precise and safe tool for complex airway tumours. Herein, we describe the first successful application of this innovative technique.

Case Report

A 72‐year‐old male, with a 40 pack‐year smoking history (cessation 5 years prior), was diagnosed with stage IVB lung adenocarcinoma. A staging computed tomography (CT) scan revealed a large endobronchial mass from the right upper lobe, causing 90% obstruction of the right main bronchus (Figure 1A,B) with mediastinal adenopathy and lytic bone metastases. The patient presented with progressive, severe dyspnea, requiring urgent intervention.

The procedure was performed under general anaesthesia with airway security maintained via endotracheal intubation. A linear EBUS scope (EB19‐J10U; Pentax Medical, Hamburg, Germany) was advanced to the site of obstruction, revealing a mass that completely obstructed the right main bronchus (Figure 1C). The ultrasound examination provided crucial data: It delineated the tumour's density, mapped its vascular supply, and allowed for precise measurements.

A tumour permittivity feedback control MWA system (PulsaBlade, MedWaves Inc.) was used (Figure 2A,B). Through the EBUS scope working channel, a 1.7‐mm, 1‐cm active tip, 123‐cm length microwave probe was introduced (Figure 2C). Under continuous, real‐time ultrasound guidance, the antenna tip was positioned in the tumour's core, maintaining a 0.7 cm safety margin (Figure 1D). Ablation was performed in a temperature control mode with a set target temperature range between 70°C and 80°C, delivering variable amounts of energy during each application. EBUS imaging clearly showed the formation of a hyperechoic cloud, confirming tissue ablation, whereas direct vision corroborated immediate tumour shrinkage (Figure 1E,F). This sequence was repeated iteratively.

At the procedure's conclusion, 100% patency of the right main bronchus was achieved, without the need for an endobronchial stent (Figure 2G). The patient experienced immediate clinical relief, was extubated without incident, and was subsequently discharged. A follow‐up chest radiograph confirmed the resolution of the atelectasis, and bronchoscopy at 3 months showed a patent airway (Figure 1H–K).

Discussion

This case marks a significant advance in interventional pulmonology by successfully integrating the therapeutic power of MWA with the guidance and safety of linear EBUS. This approach directly addresses the primary limitations of conventional endobronchial treatments for MCAO.

The foundational work by Senitko et al. in their pilot study established the feasibility and initial safety profile of endobronchial MWA using bronchoscopic guidance for MCAO [4]. Their study demonstrated that MWA could successfully recanalize airways, proving its potential as a valuable tool. Our case represents the next logical evolution of this technique. Although the pilot study relied on direct visual control, we introduced real‐time EBUS guidance, fundamentally enhancing the procedure's precision and safety.

EBUS guidance allowed us to visualise the tumour in its entirety, including its submucosal and extraluminal extent, which is impossible with bronchoscopic vision alone. This enabled precise energy delivery to the tumour's core while a pre‐defined safety margin protected distal tissue. Furthermore, it allowed for real‐time mapping of adjacent vasculature, minimising the risk of thermal injury to critical structures—a key concern addressed by our technique.

The combination of precise targeting and controlled heating to an optimal 80°C resulted in immediate and complete luminal restoration. Achieving this without needing an endobronchial stent is a major clinical advantage, eliminating potential stent‐related complications [5].

This case demonstrates that EBUS‐guided MWA is a feasible, safe, and highly effective single‐stage treatment for complex MCAO. Further studies are warranted to establish formal protocols and evaluate long‐term outcomes. In conclusion, the fusion of EBUS and MWA offers a powerful new tool for managing MCAO, with the potential to significantly improve patient outcomes.

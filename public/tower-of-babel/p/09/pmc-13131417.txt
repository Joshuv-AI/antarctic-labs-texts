Metabolic Reprogramming of Fibroblasts by Extracellular Vesicles
Authors: Yu Tian, Andrew A Gibb
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding AuthorKey Words: EVs, fibroblast, heart failure, metabolism, miRNA, mitochondria

Heart failure (HF) is characterized by progressive remodeling of the myocardium. Central to this remodeling process are cardiac fibroblasts (CFs), which differentiate into highly specialized myofibroblasts that deposit excessive extracellular matrix (ECM), resulting in increased fibrosis, chronic inflammation, chamber dilation, and impaired contractile function. Despite the centrality of CFs to disease progression, many aspects of their differentiation—including the signaling pathways, transcriptional regulators, and heterogeneity of CF subpopulations—remain incompletely defined. Although decades of HF research has yielded guideline-directed medical therapy, including β-blockers, renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system inhibitors, and the recent effectiveness of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors, no effective treatments for fibrosis are currently available. Therefore, defining the molecular mechanisms regulating fibroblast function, activation, and differentiation could reveal innovative therapeutic avenues to prevent and possibly reverse fibrosis, lessening disease burden.

Coordinated alterations of metabolic pathways is necessary of any cell as it responds to both external and internal signaling cues, with fibroblasts being no exception. Alterations in fibroblast metabolism are required not only for the transformation of cellular energy but the biosynthetic and substrate requirements needed for fibroblast proliferation, differentiation, and ECM biosynthesis.1 Many studies over the last decade have begun to identify the specific metabolic signatures and pathways contributing to fibroblast function and differentiation, placing metabolism as a key molecular target in fibrotic diseases. Mesenchymal stem cell–derived extracellular vesicles (MSC-EVs), which have shown promise as a cell-free therapy for HF,2 can modulate recipient cellular energy metabolism.3 However, whether and how MSC-EVs modulate CF metabolism and cardiac fibrosis remain unknown. In this issue of JACC: Basic to Translational Science, Chen et al4 directly address this important gap in knowledge, identifying that MSC-EVs suppress glutamate pyruvate transaminase (GPT2)-mediated glutamate to ⍺KG conversion and mitochondrial ATP overproduction required for fibroblast differentiation and fibrosis via delivery of miR-30c-5p (Figure 1), uncovering a novel and potential therapeutic target in cardiac fibrosis.

MSC-EV miR-30c-5p Downregulates Cardiac Fibroblast GPT2, Preventing Activation and Tissue Fibrosis

In this issue of JACC: Basic to Translational Science, Chen et al4 identify a metabolic axis in which mesenchymal stem cell (MSC)–derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) limit fibrosis by targeting fibroblast GPT2 expression. (A) Upon fibroblast activation, fibroblast up-regulate GPT2, which is required for the acquisition of the profibrotic myofibroblast phenotype. (B) Upon fusing with cardiac fibroblasts, MSC-EVs unload their cargo, specifically miRNA-30c-5p, which targets the 3′-untranslated region (UTR) of GPT2 mRNA, inhibiting GPT2 translation. This reduction in GPT2 expression lowers α-KG levels and ATP production, consequently inhibiting fibroblast differentiation. (C) Using a mouse model of pressure-overload induced heart failure, EVs loaded with miR-30c-5p decrease cardiac fibroblast GPT2 expression, reducing cellular bioenergetics and ECM production, ultimately improving maladaptive remodeling by attenuating cardiac fibrosis and improving cardiac function. HF = heart failure.

Glutamine is a critical substrate for fibroblast differentiation, particularly through its conversion to glutamate and eventually ⍺KG. In CFs, genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of glutaminase (glutamine→glutamate) prevents and reverses the profibrotic phenotype, both in vitro and in vivo.5 Although the majority of glutamate→⍺KG likely occurs through glutamate dehydrogenase, GPT2 provides an auxiliary reaction to generate ⍺KG to support energy provision. In cultured mouse and human CFs treated with the profibrotic agonist transforming growth factor beta (TGFβ), Chen et al4 demonstrate a selective increase in GPT2 protein expression correlating to increased oxidative metabolism and ATP production, all of which was mitigated by MSC-EVs. Although interesting, it remained to be determined whether this increase in GPT2 expression is required for fibroblast differentiation and whether MSC-EVs exert their antifibrotic effect through this metabolic pathway.

To test the role of GPT2 in fibroblast differentiation and cardiac fibrosis, the authors performed well-designed in vitro and in vivo experimental approaches. Genetic silencing of GPT2 in cultured CFs inhibited the TGFβ-enhanced conversion of glutamate→⍺KG, preventing the bioenergetic increase and production of ECM proteins. Conversely, overexpression of GPT2 counteracted the inhibitory effects of MSC-EVs on fibroblast bioenergetics and differentiation, demonstrating a critical role of GPT2 in mediating the CF phenotype and elucidating a clear mechanistic target by which MSC-EVs invoke their antifibrotic effect. Using a model of pressure-overload–induced HF, the investigators next assessed the importance of GPT2 in vivo, generating periostin promoter-specific knockdown and overexpression lentiviruses, an exceptional promotor for targeting CFs during injury.6 In line with the in vitro findings, genetic knockdown of GPT2 prevented cardiac fibrosis and mitigated systolic dysfunction whereas overexpression of GPT2 was sufficient to enhance fibrosis and worsen cardiac dysfunction. How exactly GPT2 coordinates differentiation remains incomplete. Although the authors’ bioenergetic hypothesis is plausible, overproduction of ⍺KG via glutaminolysis has been shown to connect these metabolic alterations to the epigenetic reprogramming required for fibrotic gene program activation,7 which may precede or coincide with increased bioenergetic output. Unraveling the energetic and epigenetic changes in a time-resolved manner could answer this “chicken and egg” dilemma, providing further insight into the metabolic control of cell fate decisions.

Once the authors found a correlation between MSC-EVs and GPT2, one question remained: how do EVs target GPT2? To address this, the authors reasoned that miRNAs, abundant in EVs, are likely responsible for the negative regulation of GPT2. Utilizing next-generation sequencing of EV-encapsulated miRNAs and cross-referencing these miRNAs against multiple biological databases revealed a putative and highly expressed candidate in miR-30c-5p, validated to bind to the 3’-untranslated region of GPT2 mRNA. Validation studies confirmed the antifibrotic role of MSC-EV–derived miR-30c-5p, as EVs containing antimiR-30c-5p failed to inhibit TGFβ-mediated increases in GPT2 expression, fibroblast bioenergetics, and ECM protein production, whereas direct treatment with a miR-30c-5p mimic produced similar antifibrotic effects as MSC-EVs (Figure 1). Excitingly, this also opens the door for additional inquiries. Because the bioinformatics approach was restricted to the GPT2 3’-untranslated region, it is possible that miR-30c-5p may have additional targets critical to the profibrotic phenotype. Additionally, as 842 miRNAs were identified, additional miRNAs may target other mechanisms in fibroblast differentiation. For example, miR-939 interacts with the H3K27me2/3 histone demethylase JMJD3,8 the activity of which is necessary for fibrotic gene program activation.7 Future studies to identify additional fibrotic mechanisms targetable by EV-derived miRNAs are warranted.

To examine the efficacy of leveraging miR-30c-5p to target fibrosis and limit pathological cardiac remodeling, the authors again subjected mice to pressure-overload induced HF, injecting intramyocardially MSC-EVs overexpressing miR-30c-5p (EV-OEmiR) or MSC-EVs laden with antimiR. Mice receiving EV-OEmiR showed less ECM abundance in the heart, reduced interstitial fibrosis, and improved cardiac systolic function greater than that observed with EVs alone, while EV-antimiR mice demonstrated exacerbated fibrosis and worsened cardiac function (Figure 1). One interesting observation was the greater improvement in cardiac function seen with EV-OEmiR compared with the GPT2 shRNA lentiviral approach which specifically targeted periostin-expressing CFs. Although some of this difference could be attributable to a greater reduction in fibrosis, it also presents the question: what other cardiac cell types EVs and miR-30c-5p may target? GPT2 has yet to be mechanistically investigated in cardiomyocytes, yet it likely serves as a critical metabolic junction, controlling TCA cycle anaplerosis, redox balance through glutamate cycling, and nitrogen excretion. miR-30c-5p has also been implicated in macrophage phenotypic switching and a possible candidate for direct anti-atherosclerotic therapy9; whether this occurs via GPT2 is unknown. Careful examination of the impact of miR-30c-5p and GPT2 down-regulation in the various cell types of the heart is first required to elucidate the potential benefits and risks before clinical translation is feasible.

A notable strength of this study is the mechanistic conservation in human cardiac fibroblasts and in murine fibroblast from other tissues, namely the lung and kidney, increasing the potential translational impact. However, fibrosis is seldom detected or targeted therapeutically before the development of overt clinical disease. Future studies should be aimed at whether the administration of EVs and miR-30c-5p targeting of GPT2 is sufficient to reverse the myofibroblast phenotype with complementary in vivo experiments to determine if rescuing established tissue fibrosis and organ dysfunction is achievable. Another area requiring further inquiry centers around the remarkable heterogeneity of CFs, because single-cell studies suggest that fibrosis is not only driven by increased collagen production but also matricellular proteins that regulate various cell functions (eg, migration, adhesion, signaling, and growth factors) and ECM remodeling.10 Although interstitial fibrosis was decreased in EV-OEmiR hearts, perivascular and regional specific fibrosis were not assessed. Resolving which fibroblast subpopulations highly express GPT2, engage with EVs, and mediate fibrotic remodeling is likely to further enhance the specificity and potential antifibrotic effects of EVs and miR-30c-5p.

In summary, the work by Chen et al4 makes an important mechanistic link between the observed beneficial effects of MSC-EVs and the fibrotic landscape, by delivering antifibrotic instructions through the reprogramming of the fibroblast metabolic engine, providing a potential therapeutic avenue to impact numerous fibrotic diseases of clinical significance.

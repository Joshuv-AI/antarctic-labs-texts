Social Media Professionalism in Nursing Students: A Concept Analysis
Authors: Tommy Lin, Kateryna Metersky
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe objective is to clarify the concept of social media professionalism among nursing students. Walker and Avant's concept analysis method was used. CINAHL, Scopus, PubMed, and ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Global were used to find both empirical and gray literature published between 2014 and 2024 that discussed professionalism and social media use among nursing students; 18 articles were included. Social media professionalism among nursing students encompasses five attributes: maintaining privacy and confidentiality, clear boundaries between personal and professional identities, accountability for online actions, civil and respectful digital discourse, and adherence to professional regulatory standards and institutional policies. Antecedents include access and exposure to digital platforms, role modeling, educational preparation, awareness and internalization of professional values, and personal motivation and self‐efficacy. Consequences include an impact on professional reputation, employability, and academic progress; influence on professional identity formation; legal and regulatory outcomes; and public and institutional perceptions. The operational definition of social media professionalism among nursing students aims to clarify the concept for future use and research as well as address existing gaps in the current literature, nursing education, and institutional policy.Keywords: concept analysis, nursing student, social media professionalism

Introduction

The use of digital technologies and social media has become intertwined within our everyday lives, changing the landscape of professional communication and identity formation in nursing. For nursing students, being digitally present has become unavoidable in their personal, academic, and professional lives. This is supported by O'Connor et al. (2022), as approximately 44% of nursing students were classified as heavy social media users on platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, and Snapchat, primarily being used for communication and interaction. As a result, nursing students face persistent challenges in navigating the blurred lines between their personal and professional identities within digital contexts (Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022). Specifically, nursing students experience confusion regarding what constitutes social media professionalism and struggle to understand the full implications of their online activity (Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022).

Professionalism in nursing is widely recognized as a complex and multidimensional concept that encompasses professional values, behaviors, accountability, communication, and adherence to ethical standards within the profession (Eid et al. 2018). These professional expectations shape how nurses interact with patients, interprofessional team members, and the broader public, shaping a core component of identity development within nursing education (Eid et al. 2018). However, the rapid expansion of digital communication and social networking platforms has introduced new contexts in which professional conduct must be demonstrated. The widespread use of social networking sites among healthcare professionals has been accompanied by increasing reports of breaches of professional behaviors online, highlighting the need to better understand professionalism within digital environments (Guraya et al. 2024). Digital communication platforms also enable healthcare professionals to interact with the public, disseminate health information, and engage in professional networking on an unprecedented scale, which further amplifies the importance of maintaining professional standards in online spaces (Kim et al. 2024).

In addition, there has been research conducted on how healthcare professionals use social media in an unprofessional manner (Kaczmarczyk et al. 2013; Guraya et al. 2021). For example, a study by Booth (2015) examined the behavior of nursing students' digital behavior on Twitter (X). The study found vulgar and derogatory language being used targeting courses, students, faculty members, and expression of stress and frustrations about academic performance (Booth 2015). However, the consequences of such unprofessional behavior are well documented. This can include consequences such as academic dismissal for nursing students and professional sanctions for nurses for unprofessional digital conduct (O'Connor et al. 2025). These disciplinary actions not only reflect individual failures but also systemic deficiencies in nursing governance surrounding the necessary structures and supports required to develop digital professionalism within healthcare environments (Mather and Cummings 2019).

Importantly, the need to examine social media professionalism specifically within nursing student populations stems from the unique developmental and educational context in which students are situated. During nursing education, nursing students begin to form their professional identity by internalizing the values and ideas integral to nursing and developing a sense of self derived from the role they are preparing to assume within the profession (Halverson et al. 2022). Simultaneously, many nursing students belong to a generation that is highly engaged with social media, where personal and professional identities frequently intersect. This intersection creates distinct challenges for nursing students who must learn to navigate professional expectations while still forming their professional identity within both academic and digital environments. Therefore, understanding social media professionalism specifically among nursing students is essential to support the development of appropriate professional digital behaviors before nursing students enter into independent professional practice.

The purpose of this concept analysis is to define social media professionalism by identifying its attributes, antecedents, and consequences in the context of nursing students, defined as and including undergraduate, diploma, and post‐diploma nursing programs. This analysis can help students, educators, and institutions achieve a unified understanding and approach to appropriate behavior in digital environments through student assessment, curriculum development, and policy creation.

Materials & Methods

The Walker and Avant (2019) concept analysis method was used to explore social media professionalism among nursing students. The method includes the following eight steps: (1) select a concept, (2) determine the purpose of analysis, (3) identify all uses of the concept, (4) determine the defining attributes, (5) identify a model case, (6) identify additional cases, (7) identify antecedents and consequences, and (8) define empirical referents (Walker and Avant 2019).

Data Sources and Search Strategy

The electronic databases CINAHL, Scopus, PubMed, and ProQuest Dissertations & Theses Global were used to locate both empirical and gray literature for inclusion and analysis. The following search strategy was used: “nurs* student*” OR “student nurs*” AND “social media” OR “TikTok” OR “Facebook” OR “Twitter (X)” OR “Snapchat” OR “Instagram” OR “Youtube” OR “Threads” OR “Reddit” OR “LinkedIn.”

Inclusion & Exclusion Criteria

The search included full‐text articles in English that were published between 2014 and 2024. This time frame was selected to ensure the findings reflect the rapid growth and advancement of technology in the last decade. In addition, articles must discuss professionalism and social media use among nursing students. For this concept analysis, professionalism is defined as ethical behavior, respectful communication, confidentiality, and maintenance of public trust (College of Nurses of Ontario 2024). Social media use is defined as any instance in which a nursing student uses any social media platform for professional purposes. These include TikTok, Facebook, Twitter (X), Snapchat, Instagram, YouTube, Threads, Reddit, and LinkedIn. The articles were not included in the analysis if they studied nurses and did not discuss professionalism with social media use.

Study Selection and Data Charting Process

The initial search yielded 595 articles, with 42 articles meeting the inclusion criteria after title and abstract review. After a full text and reference list of included studies review, 18 articles were included for analysis. Two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts, full texts, and extracted data using Covidence. The following information was extracted from the included articles: author(s), year of publication, title, country, aims/purpose, study design/methods, and findings. Results

Step 1: Choosing a Concept

The concept of social media professionalism was selected due to its ambiguity among nursing students (Chu 2020; Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022). In the current literature, studies have shown that many nursing students lack a consistent understanding of appropriate online behavior, struggle to recognize professional boundaries in social media use, and remain unaware of regulatory standards surrounding digital conduct (Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022). Moreover, no concept analysis currently exists about social media professionalism among nursing students. This concept analysis aims to address this gap in the literature by clarifying the concept of social media professionalism and informing the development of appropriate curricula, educational strategies, and policy frameworks to guide professional conduct in digital environments.

Step 2: Determining the Purposes of Analysis

The purpose of this concept analysis is to define social media professionalism by identifying its attributes, antecedents, and consequences in the context of nursing students. The results can support students, educators, institutions, and the nursing profession at large in achieving a unified understanding and approach to appropriate behavior in digital environments through student education and assessment, curriculum development, and policy creation.

Step 3: Identifying All Uses of the Concept

Walker and Avant (2019) concept analysis method's third step is to explore all uses of the concept in dictionaries, thesauruses, and available literature.

General Definition of Social Media Professionalism

Online dictionaries were first reviewed, but none recognized social media professionalism as a singular entity. Merriam‐Webster (n.d.‐b) defines social media as forms of electronic communication through which users create online communities to share information, messages, and content. Professionalism is defined as the conduct and qualities that characterize a profession (Merriam‐Webster, n.d.‐a). In the literature, digital professionalism is used interchangeably with e‐professionalism. E‐professionalism refers to the attitudes and behaviors of professionalism manifested in digital media (Kaczmarczyk et al. 2013). Similarly, digital professionalism is generally defined as the expression of professional attitudes and behaviors in digital media, inclusive of public and private digital forums (Ellaway et al. 2015). Specifically, digital professionalism encompasses integrity, respect, and accountability within digital spaces such as online forums, social media, and email communication (Ellaway et al. 2015). Therefore, social media professionalism is a subset of digital professionalism that helps guide an individual's behavior to maintain appropriate personal and professional identities within digital spaces.

Social Media Professionalism in Nursing

In nursing, digital professionalism is rooted in ethical standards, confidentiality, privacy, and professional boundaries in all digital interactions (Gum et al. 2024; McGrath et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2022; Westrick 2016). This is supported by regulatory bodies such as the American Nurses Association, which has explicit guidelines that guide nurses for respectful conduct and communication on digital platforms, such as social media (American Nurses Association [ANA] 2011; Gum et al. 2024). Specifically, these policies highlight the secure use of electronic health systems, messaging apps, and recognize that personal digital activity can overlap with professional responsibilities (ANA 2011; College of Registered Nurses of Alberta [CRNA] 2021; Gum et al. 2024). Moreover, unprofessional online behavior can result in personal and professional disciplinary actions, such as licensure loss, highlighting the need for nurses to uphold the profession's trust and reputation on digital platforms (Gum et al. 2024; McGrath et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2022; Westrick 2016). Therefore, digital professionalism in nursing establishes safeguards for patients and professional reputation.

Step 4: Determining the Defining Attributes

Walker and Avant (2019) concept analysis method's fourth step is to create the defining attributes of a concept. These attributes differentiate a concept from other related concepts (Walker and Avant 2019). Based on the literature review, social media professionalism in nursing students has five defining attributes: (1) adherence to confidentiality and privacy; (2) establishing boundaries between personal and professional roles; (3) accountability for online behavior; (4) respectful and civil online communication; and (5) awareness and adherence to professional regulations and guidelines.

Adherence to Confidentiality and Privacy

The first core attribute of social media professionalism for nursing students is the ability to maintain patient (Barnable et al. 2018), staff, and institutional confidentiality and privacy on digital platforms (McGrath et al. 2019; Westrick 2016). Nursing students should be aware that sharing any direct or indirect identifiable patient information on digital platforms can result in breaches of professional and legal responsibilities (Barnable et al. 2018; McGrath et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2022). For example, direct sharing of information can include disclosing a patient's name or image on digital platforms, while indirect means could involve recounting clinical experiences that identify patient information through inference (Barnable et al. 2018; McGrath et al. 2019). Despite nursing students' awareness, the literature highlights persistent students' confusion regarding how sharing anonymized patient information can still constitute a breach (Barnable et al. 2018; McGrath et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2022). Moreover, nursing students should be aware that maintaining confidentiality and privacy extends beyond the patient to include staff and institutions (McGrath et al. 2019; Westrick 2016). Nursing students must understand that unguarded online discourse can result in disciplinary and legal consequences from tarnishing the institutional and professional reputations of colleagues (O'Connor et al. 2022; Westrick 2016).

Establishing Boundaries Between Personal and Professional Roles

The ability to establish personal and professional boundaries is another defining attribute of social media professionalism among nursing students. In the literature, the term “digital duality” is used to describe the blurring of personal and professional identities, a struggle many nursing students face on digital platforms (Chu 2020; Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022). Nursing students often have misconceptions, believing that their private social media identity is unrelated to their professional identity (Barnable et al. 2018; Zhu et al. 2021). However, in reality, nursing students' digital conduct, whether shared publicly or privately, reflects the profession and has implications for public trust (Barnable et al. 2018; Duke et al. 2017; Sun et al. 2023). Therefore, nursing students must be mindful that sharing content that is perceived as personal, such as clinical experiences, can result in a breach of professionalism and erode public trust in the profession (Barnable et al. 2018; McGrath et al. 2019).

Accountability for Online Behavior

The next attribute of social media professionalism among nursing students is personal accountability for online behavior. Nursing students must recognize the permanence and public endurance of their digital footprint on their professional image (Chu 2020; Gum et al. 2024; Hoilman 2016; O'Connor et al. 2022). The literature highlights instances in which nursing students have faced disciplinary actions for a lack of self‐regulation in digital conduct (Barnable et al. 2018; Tan et al. 2024; Westrick 2016). This includes academic disciplinary actions, program dismissal, and regulatory sanctions for breaches of digital professionalism (Barnable et al. 2018; Tan et al. 2024; Westrick 2016). As a result, nursing students must be mindful and actively consider the possible repercussions of all digital interactions to prevent any misinterpretation of digital conduct as harmful to the professional reputation of individuals, colleagues, and institutions (Barnable et al. 2018; McGrath et al. 2019; Westrick 2016). Therefore, nursing students must understand the risk of private content being shared beyond its initial intended audience and must be professional in their digital conduct at all times.

Respectful and Civil Online Communication

Social media professionalism among nursing students also entails maintaining respect and civility in all digital communications (Chu 2020; Gum et al. 2024; McGrath et al. 2019). This includes refraining from sharing discriminatory, derogatory, or negative comments directed toward patients, colleagues, employers, and the profession (McGrath et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2022). In the literature, online incivility, such as cyberbullying and offensive posts, has become a growing problem both among nursing students and nurses (McGrath et al. 2019; Gum et al. 2024). These behaviors can result in disciplinary consequences, undermine public trust, and erode the profession's reputation (Westrick 2016). Nursing students have the responsibility to engage in respectful digital communication, maintaining the integrity of the profession and ensuring trust in the nurse–patient relationship (Barnable et al. 2018).

Awareness and Adherence to Professional Regulations and Guidelines

The final attribute of social media professionalism by nursing students is the awareness and adherence to professional regulations and guidelines governing digital conduct (Gum et al. 2024; McGrath et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2022). For example, the ANA (2011) and the CRNA (2021) are regulatory nursing bodies that have explicit guidelines surrounding appropriate online conduct for nursing students and nurses. However, the rapid advancement of digital platforms and social media privacy guidelines outdates these guidelines, which can create a gap in nursing students' knowledge and awareness of appropriate digital conduct (O'Connor et al. 2022). This gap is further compounded as a result of nursing students using family and peers as informal primary sources to govern their digital conduct (Alharbi et al. 2022; Gum et al. 2024). Therefore, nursing students are expected to be cautious and seek clarification from their respective academic institutions, employers, and regulatory nursing bodies to maintain social media professionalism.

Step 5: Model Case of Social Media Professionalism in Nursing Students

Walker and Avant (2019) define the model case as an example of having all defining attributes of a concept. The following example scenario demonstrates a nursing student exhibiting social media professionalism.

Richard, a fourth‐year nursing student, uses a professional email for academic‐related communications and maintains a separate personal social media account with strict privacy settings. He never discloses any patient information or post‐clinical related content online. When participating in group chats, such as Facebook Messenger, he respectfully communicates with his peers, even during disagreements, and ensures the information he shares is evidence‐based. Richard lives in Ontario, Canada, and always consults the College of Nurses of Ontario guidelines for appropriate online conduct to maintain social media professionalism. Finally, Richard holds himself accountable for everything he posts, whether public or private, and encourages his peers to do the same.

This model case demonstrates all the defining attributes of social media professionalism in nursing students. First, Richard has distinct and clear professional and personal boundaries as seen by his professional email and personal social media account. He also maintains privacy and confidentiality by never posting on social media and uses respectful communication on digital platforms. Finally, Richard is aware and consults with his specific nursing regulatory body to maintain social media professionalism, while being accountable for all of his digital footprint.

Step 6: Additional Cases of Social Media Professionalism in Nursing Students

Borderline Case

A borderline case is an example that includes most but not all the defining attributes of a concept (Walker and Avant 2019). The following example demonstrates a borderline case of social media professionalism in a nursing student.

Emily, a second‐year nursing student, uses her personal Instagram account to occasionally post stories about her nursing student experience. She avoids sharing any identifiable information; however, she once posted a photo from the nursing lounge with other staff in the background. In that instance, she apologized and removed the post when someone pointed out it might be unprofessional. She also generally avoids posting inappropriate content and maintains her digital communication respectfully. Emily is vaguely aware of her specific academic institution's digital conduct guidelines, as she has not reviewed them in depth.

Emily's case reflects partial attributes of social media professionalism in nursing students. While she maintains privacy and respectful online communication, she lacks sufficient understanding of her academic institution's digital conduct guidelines. This results in Emily using her personal social media account to disclose her professional experience and results in an instance of potential digital unprofessionalism.

Contrary Case

A contrary case is an example that does not represent the concept (Walker and Avant 2019). The following example scenario demonstrates a contrary case.

Sophia, a first‐year nursing student, frequently posts photos from her clinical placement on her public TikTok account. In these images and videos, patients and staff are recognizable. She shares jokes about her clinical encounters, at times using inappropriate language, and engages in negative online discourse about nursing practice. Moreover, Sophia uses the same account to share personal rants and stories. She is unaware of her academic institution's social media conduct guidelines and does not see any problem with her online behavior.

This example lacks all the defining attributes of social media professionalism in nursing students. Sophia disregards confidentiality, maintains no personal or professional boundaries, shows no accountability, communicates disrespectfully, and is unaware of the expected standards of the nursing profession in digital forums. This demonstrates what social media unprofessionalism looks like in nursing students.

Step 7: Identifying Antecedents and Consequences

Antecedents

Walker and Avant (2019) define antecedents as events or conditions that must precede the occurrence of the concept. The following five antecedents that emerged from the literature include: (1) access and exposure to digital platforms; (2) peer and professional role modeling; (3) professional educational exposure; (4) awareness and internalization of professional values; and (5) personal motivation and self‐efficacy.

First, nursing students must have access and be exposed to digital platforms, such as social media, as students regularly engage with them for personal and professional purposes, making online engagement a foundational aspect of their social media professional development (Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022; Zhu et al. 2021). The frequency of use in students can determine the extent to which they encounter both positive and negative exemplars of social media professionalism (Zhu et al. 2021). Next, peer and professional role models play a significant role. Nursing students' perception of acceptable digital conduct is shaped by the actions of academic and clinical faculty, influential peers, and nursing leaders on social media (Alharbi et al. 2020; Li et al. 2022). For example, nursing students can model and reinforce expected social media professionalism behaviors from respected nurses and educators (Li et al. 2022). Professional education exposure is another antecedent of social media professionalism among nursing students. These include academic activities, formal digital policy instruction, and professionalism training sessions, which have been shown to enhance nursing students' competency in navigating professional conduct in digital contexts (Gum et al. 2024). Furthermore, the awareness and internalization of professional values, such as confidentiality, accountability, and respect, serve as an ethical compass that informs nursing students' social media usage and adherence to digital codes of conduct (Gum et al. 2024; McGrath et al. 2019). Finally, nursing students' motivation and self‐efficacy play a role in social media professionalism. The literature supports this as nursing students with greater motivation and self‐efficacy are more likely to have sound judgment and uphold professionalism online, even in ambiguous situations (Sun et al. 2023; Zhu et al. 2021).

Consequences

Walker and Avant (2019) define consequences as the outcomes or results of the concept. Based on the literature, social media professionalism among nursing students has both positive and negative consequences. Positive consequences improve the experience of the concept, while negative consequences worsen the experience of the concept (Walker and Avant 2019). The consequences identified include: (1) impact on professional reputation, employability, and academic progress; (2) influence on professional identity formation; (3) legal and regulatory outcomes; and (4) public and institutional image.

Positive social media professionalism can strengthen nursing students' professional reputation, enhance employability, and support successful academic progression (Gum et al. 2024). Conversely, negative digital conduct, such as breaches in confidentiality, can result in academic sanctions, program dismissals, and hindered employment opportunities (Westrick 2016; Zhu et al. 2021). Social media professionalism among nursing students also plays a role in professional identity formation. Specifically, positive experiences in digital spaces can foster students' sense of belonging and pride in the profession, while negative incidents result in disruptions to professional confidence development and socialization into nursing roles (Alharbi et al. 2020). Moreover, violation of professional digital codes of conduct and privacy laws can result in legal and regulatory consequences for nursing students, such as documented cases of disciplinary action taken by academic and nursing regulatory bodies (Barnable et al. 2018; Oducado et al. 2019; Westrick 2016). Finally, nursing students' collective digital conduct has an impact on the public and institutional image of nursing as a trustworthy profession. Nursing students exhibiting positive social media professionalism can foster trust, while negative actions can erode public confidence and tarnish the reputation of academic institutions and the overall profession (Gum et al. 2024; McGrath et al. 2019; Tan et al. 2024).

Step 8: Defining Empirical Referents

The final step of Walker and Avant's (2019) concept analysis method is to define empirical referents, tools, and other means that measure or substantiate the existence of a concept. Social media professionalism can be studied through quantitative, qualitative research, and mixed methods. There is currently no standardized instrument that measures social media professionalism among nursing students. However, Imran et al. (2024) have recently developed the Digital Professionalism Self‐Assessment Instrument (DP‐SAI) to measure digital professionalism among healthcare professionals. The DP‐SAI is a 28‐item instrument that measures digital professionalism across several domains, including self‐anonymity, privacy, conflict of interest, accountability, respect, ethics, boundary maintenance, and confidentiality (Imran et al. 2024). Due to the novelty of the DP‐SAI, this tool has not been used in any other studies. There are multiple examples of this concept being studied through the use of surveys (Barnable et al. 2018; Cathala et al. 2023, 2021; Chu 2020; Duke et al. 2017; Li et al. 2022; Mather et al. 2016; Oducado et al. 2019; Sun et al. 2023) and questionnaires (Alharbi et al. 2022; O'Connor et al. 2022; Zhu et al. 2021). Whereas qualitative methods of studying social media professionalism include semi‐structured interviews (Alharbi et al. 2020; Hoilman 2016; Li et al. 2022) and policy document analysis (Westrick 2016). However, the lack of specific and standardized quantitative and qualitative methods to study social media professionalism highlights a gap for future research.

Discussion and Implications for Clinical Practice, Education, and Policy

Walker and Avant's (2019) concept analysis method lends itself to the production of an operational definition. Thus, social media professionalism in nursing can be operationally defined as the consistent demonstration of professional behaviors, values, and competencies on digital platforms. This is demonstrated by nursing students safeguarding privacy and confidentiality, establishing clear boundaries between personal and professional identities, being accountable for their online actions, engaging in civil and respectful digital discourse, and complying with relevant professional regulatory standards and institutional policies. This definition is grounded in the expectation that nursing students are held to the same ethical and legal standards online as they are in traditional academic and clinical settings (McGrath et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2022).

The ambiguity nursing students experienced in defining appropriate social media professionalism due to the blurring of personal and professional online identities (Chu 2020; Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022) can be alleviated through the application of the operational definition of social media professionalism that incorporates five key attributes relevant to nursing practice. The concept of digital duality, which refers to the overlap between personal and professional digital identities, provides a useful lens to contextualize this ambiguity (Gum et al. 2024; O'Connor et al. 2022). By clearly outlining expected behaviors, surrounding confidentiality, boundaries, and accountability, the operational definition can help distinguish their professional role from their personal digital presence. For example, defining digital communication standards can set expectations of respect and civility that apply to all digital interactions. This can empower students to navigate online interactions with greater clarity and confidence, thereby reducing the impact of digital duality.

The operational definition can also serve as a foundation for integrating social media professionalism into nursing curricula and institutional policies. The literature reports that students have struggled with putting abstract digital conduct guidelines into action (Barnable et al. 2018; Zhu et al. 2021). Educators can use this operational definition to develop targeted learning objectives and scenario‐based activities that demonstrate the practical application of social media professionalism in real‐life contexts. This is supported by the literature as regular and structured digital conduct education can help students internalize these standards as part of their professional identity, increasing their digital competence and professionalism (Cathala et al. 2023; Chu 2020). At the policy level, a unified operational definition can help create enforceable and clear digital conduct guidelines for students and faculty. Specifically, academic and healthcare organizations can set consistent and clear expectations for online conduct, ensuring fairness in conduct assessment and enforcement.

Walker and Avant (2019) provided a structured and systematic method to create a conceptual definition of social media professionalism among nursing students based on current literature. However, there are several limitations that exist with this concept analysis. Only four databases were accessed to collect relevant literature. Despite this, the four databases used are considered to be large and thoroughly comprehensive databases for the retrieval of such literature. As well, only English articles were included. This can potentially exclude literature and ideas written in other languages and cultural contexts.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the Walker and Avant (2019) concept analysis method was used to define social media professionalism among nursing students. The operational definition can serve as a foundation for a unified understanding for students, educators, and institutions. Future work should focus on applying and operationalizing the concept of social media professionalism within practice, education, and policy settings, including the development of practical frameworks and assessment tools that can guide implementation. Engagement across disciplines is also needed to examine the applicability of the concept among diverse healthcare professionals and to support the development of shared standards for digital conduct across cultural and practice contexts.

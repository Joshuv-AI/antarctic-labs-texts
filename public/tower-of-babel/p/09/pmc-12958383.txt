The complete chloroplast genome of Malus komarovii (Rosaceae)
Authors: Fei-Ran Zou, Yun-Fei Mao, Xiang Shen, Qiang Hao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractMalus komarovii (Sarg.) Rehder is an endangered species endemic to China. Using Deep Genome Skimming (DGS) data, we assembled the complete chloroplast genome, which is 159,982 bp in length and comprises a Large Single-Copy (LSC, 88,119 bp), a Small Single-Copy (SSC, 19,157 bp), and two Inverted Repeats (IR, 26,353 bp each). The genome encodes 131 genes, including 86 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and eight rRNA genes. Phylogenetic analyses show that M. komarovii forms a distinct lineage among the seven major clades, indicating pronounced genetic distinctiveness and potential value as a germplasm resource for breeding. This chloroplast genome provides valuable data for future studies on species identification and phylogenetic relationships within Malus.

Introduction

Malus komarovii (Sarg.) Rehder 1920 is a deciduous shrub or small tree in the genus Malus (Rosaceae) (Gu and Spongberg 2003; Liu et al. 2023a). Due to its small population size, restricted distribution, low adaptability, and human disturbance, this species has undergone a steady population decline and is listed as a National Grade I Protected Plant in China and as endangered on the IUCN Red List. It occurs sporadically in mixed coniferous–broadleaf forests at 1100–1300 m elevation on the southern slopes of the Changbai Mountains, mainly within the shrub layer. The species is characterized by broadly ovate leaves and red, elliptical fruits (Figure 1). M. komarovii is a valuable plant resource with integrated ornamental, edible, and breeding uses, exhibiting high academic and applied value (Li et al. 2022). However, its chloroplast genome has not been previously characterized. Here, we report the first complete chloroplast genome of M. komarovii and analyze its phylogenetic relationships within Malus, providing essential data for future evolutionary and taxonomic studies of the genus.

Materials and methods

Experimental material

Plant materials of M. komarovii were collected from the Changbai Mountain region, Jilin Province, China (41°76′62″ N, 128°14′06″ E; Altitude: 1290 m). Qiang Hao identified the specimen and it was deposited in the Herbarium of the China National Botanical Garden (CNBG) (Contact: Qiang Hao, email: haoqiang@chnbg.cn), Beijing, China (voucher number: NBG0000352).

DNA extraction and sequencing

The chloroplast genome was assembled based on deep genome skimming (DGS) data generated by high-throughput sequencing. Whole-genomic DNA was extracted from fresh leaves using a modified CTAB method (Li et al. 2013). Following acoustic shearing, DNA libraries were prepared with the NEBNext® Ultra™ II DNA Library Prep Kit, and 150 bp paired-end reads were generated on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform (Novogene, Beijing, China). Read quality was assessed with FastQC (https://www.bioinformatics.babraham.ac.uk/projects/fastqc/), and the chloroplast genome was assembled using GetOrganelle v1.7.7 (Jin et al. 2020). Short reads were mapped to the assembled chloroplast genome using BWA v0.7.19 (Li and Durbin 2009), and coverage depth was estimated with SAMtools v1.21 (Li et al. 2009). Genome annotation was performed using GeSeq (Tillich et al. 2017), and the circular map was generated with ChloroPlot (Zheng et al. 2020). The plastome structure and splicing gene maps were visualized using CPGView (Liu et al. 2023b). This workflow has been successfully applied to plastome assembly across multiple angiosperm lineages (e.g. Jin et al. 2024, 2025; Lin et al. 2025; Xie et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2025). The complete chloroplast genome of M. komarovii has been deposited in GenBank under accession number PX369580.1.

Phylogenetic analysis

To reconstruct the phylogenetic framework of M. komarovii and related species, chloroplast genomes from 32 Malus species were analyzed, with Pyrus ussuriensis Maxim. as the outgroup. Sequence alignment was performed using MAFFT v7.526 (Nakamura et al. 2018), and Maximum Likelihood (ML) analysis was conducted with IQ-TREE v2.3.5 (Minh et al. 2020) under the GTR+I + R model. Node support was assessed using 1000 ultrafast bootstrap and 1000 SH-aLRT replicates.

Results

Characteristics of the chloroplast genome

In this study, DGS sequencing produced 99,660,330 clean reads, providing sufficient coverage for a high-quality chloroplast genome assembly (Figure S1). The chloroplast genome of M. komarovii (Figure 2) is 159,982 bp in length and exhibits the typical circular quadripartite structure, consisting of a large single-copy (LSC) region (1–88,119 bp), a small single-copy (SSC) region (114,473–133,629 bp), and two inverted repeats, IRb (88,120–114,472 bp) and IRa (133,630–159,982 bp). The overall GC content is 36.5%, with 34.2%, 30.4%, and 42.7% in the LSC, SSC, and IR regions, respectively. A total of 131 genes were annotated in the chloroplast genome, including both single-copy genes and genes duplicated in the IR regions; duplicated genes were counted once in each IR region. These comprised 86 protein-coding, 37 tRNA, and eight rRNA genes. Among them, sixteen genes were duplicated in the IR regions, comprising six protein-coding (ndhB, rpl2, rpl23, ycf2, rps7, and rps12), six tRNA (trnA, trnI, trnL, trnN, trnR, and trnV), and four rRNA (rrn4.5, rrn5, rrn16, and rrn23) genes. In total, 13 cis-spliced genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP1, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, ndhA, the ndhB and rpl2 duplicated; Figure S2) and one trans-spliced gene (rps12; Figure S3) were identified.

Phylogenetic analysis

The phylogenetic analysis of Malus resolved seven major clades, M. komarovii forms a distinct lineage among the seven major clades, indicating a unique phylogenetic position within the genus. It forming a sister relationship to clades I and II, suggesting a closer evolutionary affinity with the species within these groups (Figure 3). M. orientalis Uglitzk. ex Juz. is strongly supported as a sister species of M. domestica (Suckow) Borkh. These two species together form an apple domestication-related group. M. komarovii occupies a more distant lineage. Previous studies (Li et al. 2025) have also confirmed this. Our results indicate that chloroplast genomic data are helpful for inferring broad-scale phylogenetic patterns and for species-level identification within the genus Malus.

Discussion and conclusions

This study presents the first complete chloroplast genome sequencing and assembly of M. komarovii, with a total length of 159,982 bp, and this result generally corresponds with the previous studies (Liu et al. 2019, 2022, 2023a). Notably, the GC content of the inverted repeat (IR) regions (42.7%) is highly conserved and comparable to that of other Malus species, such as M. sylvestris L. and M. sieboldii (Regel) Rehd., reflecting the structural stability of the IR regions within the genus (Xu et al. 2019; Lou et al. 2020). The overall GC content of the plastome (36.5%) also falls within the typical range reported for Malus species, supporting the conserved evolution of chloroplast genomes across the genus. A total of 131 genes were annotated, including 86 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNA genes, and eight rRNA genes. These characteristics closely resemble those of most Malus chloroplast genomes (Li et al. 2021; Dong et al. 2024). Our analysis recovered seven major clades, with M. komarovii forming a distinct lineage and showing a sister relationship with clades I and II (Figure 3). Given its unique phylogenetic position and endangered status, M. komarovii serves as both a vital genetic resource for evolutionary studies and a key conservation target. This research contributes to deepening our understanding of this species and its phylogenetic placement within the genus Malus.

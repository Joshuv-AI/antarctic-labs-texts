Molecular mechanisms of peanut root responses to Cd stress regulated by ABC transporter and plant hormone signal transduction pathways
Authors: Didi Feng, Dongbo Lin, Zhanpeng Liang, Tingting Chen, Enyou Feng, Qiaoling Lin, Hanqiao Hu, Yingbin Xue, Ying Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHigh cadmium (Cd) stress causes phytotoxicity, inhibits plant growth, and alters gene expression and metabolite profiles. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying peanut responses to elevated Cd levels remain insufficiently understood.ResultIn this study, integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses were conducted to investigate the responses of peanut roots to high Cd stress. Cd exposure significantly inhibited root growth and disrupted antioxidant enzyme systems in both roots and leaves. High Cd stress also altered ion uptake and transport in peanut plants. Transcriptome analysis identified 4,248 differentially expressed genes (DEGs), which were mainly associated with antioxidant enzymes, ion transport, and hormone-related processes. Metabolomic analysis identified 1,048 differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs). Under high Cd stress, the ABC transporter and plant hormone signal transduction pathways were significantly enriched. Key genes, including members of the ABCG subfamily, PP2C, and DELLA, showed differential expression and contributed to oxidative stress regulation. The response of peanut roots to Cd stress may involve coordinated changes in ion transport, antioxidant metabolism and hormone signal regulation.ConclusionThis study has proposed a multi-omics regulatory framework for the response of peanut roots to cadmium stress, providing potential candidate targets and theoretical basis for subsequent functional validation and research on cadmium tolerance-related mechanisms.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08515-9.

Introduction

Cadmium (Cd) is a non-essential heavy metal that is toxic to plants and animals [1]. Its chemical and physical properties are similar to those of zinc (Zn) [2]. In natural environments, Cd mainly occurs in exchangeable and carbonate-bound forms [2]. Cd is a major soil pollutant among heavy metals, and its excessive accumulation causes toxicity that disrupts plant growth and development [3]. As a non-essential heavy metal for plants, Cd accumulation weakens photosynthesis and induces oxidative stress, thereby inhibiting plant growth [4].

Industrial emissions, pesticide application, and sewage irrigation have intensified Cd contamination in soils, increasing the risk of Cd toxicity in crops [5]. High Cd stress damages antioxidant systems, and reduces plant biomass, resulting in stunted growth [4]. For example, in rice (Oryza sativa), Cd disrupts the balance of auxins and cytokinins, which inhibits root development [6]. High Cd stress also disturbs ion homeostasis and interferes with the uptake and transport of essential nutrients in Glycine max [7]. Therefore, high Cd stress severely restricts plant growth.

Peanut (Arachis hypogaea) is an important oilseed, food, and economic crop in China and provides high-quality protein and oil [8]. It is widely cultivated worldwide. However, its strong capacity for Cd accumulation poses a risk to the safety of agricultural products [9]. High Cd concentrations also inhibit peanut growth, and cause multiple toxic effects [10]. Current studies mainly focus on Cd accumulation mechanisms in peanut and approaches to mitigate Cd stress. The specific molecular mechanisms by which high Cd stress affects peanut growth remain insufficiently understood [11]. Further investigation is therefore required to clarify peanut responses to high Cd stress, including changes in Cd-related genes and metabolic processes.

In recent years, transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses have been widely applied to examine plant responses to Cd stress, providing systematic insight into adaptation and tolerance mechanisms. Transcriptomic analysis of Solanum lycopersicum shows that high Cd treatment enhances osmotic regulation and antioxidant defense systems in seedlings. Genes associated with phenylalanine, glutathione, arginine, proline, and nitrogen metabolism pathways, including PALs, HDCs, and GGCT, show significant differential expression. Several transcription factors, such as ERF109, ARF9, and GRF3, are also strongly regulated, which increases Cd tolerance [12]. Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analysis of Pistia stratiotes indicates that Cd stress inhibits photosynthetic pigment synthesis and alters glutathione metabolism and lignin biosynthesis pathways [13]. Similar analyses in Passiflora edulis show that Cd stress disrupts chloroplast function and induces lignin and flavonoid biosynthesis to mitigate Cd toxicity [14]. These studies indicate that multi-omics approaches are effective for elucidating plant responses to Cd stress. However, comparable analyses in peanut root systems remain limited.

Although the above studies have provided important references for the responses of plants to Cd stress, most of the existing research has focused on the aboveground traits of model plants or crops. The systematic research on cadmium perception, absorption and detoxification in plant roots, especially in important oil crops such as peanuts, is still relatively limited. Therefore, this study investigates the response mechanisms of peanut roots to high Cd stress by integrating transcriptome sequencing and non-targeted metabolomic analyses. The specific objectives are as follows: (a) to systematically characterize physiological and phenotypic changes in peanut plants under different levels of Cd stress and to identify tolerance and adaptation responses; (b) to validate differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and differentially expressed metabolites (DEMs) (Such as ABCG, PP2C and DELLA, etc.) induced by Cd toxicity and to clarify the key metabolic pathways (ABC transporters and plant hormone signal transduction) affected by Cd stress; and (c) to elucidate the molecular regulatory mechanisms underlying peanut responses to high Cd stress, providing a basis for understanding the molecular response mechanism of peanuts to Cd stress and for subsequent functional verification.

Materials and methods

Measurement of biomass and morphological parameters

The experimental material was the peanut cultivar Zhanyou 62, developed by the Agricultural Science Research Institute of Zhanjiang City, Guangdong Province, China. Seeds with intact coats and uniform size were selected, germinated, and cultivated in moist quartz sand for 15 days. Seedlings with uniform growth were then transplanted into plastic culture containers containing modified Hoagland nutrient solution [15] (Table S1) supplemented with different concentrations of Cd chloride (0, 10, 20, 30, and 40 µM CdCl2). Plants were cultivated for 15 days. The control treatment contained 0 µM Cd. Each treatment included more than six plants, and all experiments were performed in triplicate. The nutrient solution was renewed every 5 days, and the pH was maintained at 5.8–6.0. Cd chloride was purchased from Shanghai Reagents. All chemical reagents were of analytical grade and obtained from Solara Biotechnology Company. The growth chamber was maintained at 28 °C during the day and 25 °C at night, with a 12 h photoperiod and a light intensity of 200 µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹.

After 15 days of Cd treatment, plant height, stem diameter, fresh weight, and dry weight were measured. Root morphology was scanned using a ScanMaker i800 Plus image scanner (Shanghai Zhongjing, China). The images were analyzed with root analysis software (Wanshen, Zhejiang, China) to obtain root surface area, total root length, average root diameter, root tip number, and root volume [16].

Determination of antioxidant enzyme activities

Peanut roots were harvested after 15 days of Cd treatment for antioxidant enzyme assays. The root tissue (0.1 g) was thoroughly ground and mixed with pre-cooled phosphate buffer solution (pH 7.8, 0.05 M), and then centrifuged at 4℃ and 10,000 rpm for 20 min. The enzyme activity was immediately determined using the supernatant. Peroxidase (POD) activity was determined using the crotonic acid method [17], the unit used the OD470nm value to indicate a decrease of 0.01 within 1 min. Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity was measured using the nitroblue tetrazolium (NBT) method [18], the unit was expressed by the number of enzymes required to prevent a 50% reduction in NBT. Catalase (CAT) activity was determined using a spectrophotometer (UV-5100B, Yuan Wei, Shanghai, China) [19]. Ascorbate peroxidase (APX) activity was measured following a previously reported method [20], the APX activity unit was expressed as the number of enzymes required to oxidize 1 µM of ascorbic acid.

Analysis of soluble sugars and proline content

After 15 days of Cd treatment, peanut roots were harvested to determine soluble sugar and proline contents. Soluble sugar content was measured using the anthrone-sulfuric acid colorimetric method [21]. Proline content was determined using the acidic ninhydrin colorimetric method [22].

Determination of ion contents

After 15 days of Cd treatment, peanut roots were collected for ion content analysis. Approximately 0.1 g of each sample was transferred into a 50 mL microwave digestion vessel (Anton Paar, Graz, Austria), and a small amount of ultrapure water was added. Subsequently, 6 mL of 65% nitric acid and 0.5 mL of 30% H2O2 (Guangzhou Reagent, China) were added. Samples were digested using a microwave digestion system (Anton Paar Multiwave PRO 41HVT56, Graz, Austria). After digestion, 1 mL of 65% nitric acid was added, and the volume was adjusted to 50 mL with ultrapure water using a glass cuvette (Beibow, Beijing, China). Cd, manganese, iron, boron, magnesium, and calcium contents were determined using an inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometer (ICP-MS; 7500cx, Agilent, CA, USA) according to a previously reported method [16]. To ensure the accuracy of the measurement results, a multi-element standard solution was used to establish an external standard curve for quantitative analysis, and a reagent blank was set up for background correction.

cDNA library construction and transcriptome sequencing

After treatment with 0 and 20 µM Cd for 15 days, peanut root samples (0.1 g) were ground in liquid nitrogen for RNA extraction and cDNA library construction. Three biological replicates were prepared for each treatment. Transcriptome sequencing was conducted following the method described previously [21]. Libraries were sequenced on an Illumina platform in paired-end 150 bp mode. Raw sequencing data were filtered to obtain high-quality clean reads. Clean reads were aligned to the Arachis hypogaea reference genome (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/assembly/GCA_000817695.3) using HISAT2 v2.0.5. Gene expression levels were quantified with HTSeq v0.9.1. DEGs were screened using the DESeq2 software. After the gene expression levels were standardized, the Benjamini–Hochberg method was used to correct for the false discovery rate (FDR). The screening criteria for differentially expressed genes were set as a corrected P value (FDR) < 0.05 and |log2Fold Change| ≥ 1. Gene Ontology enrichment analysis was performed using topGO, and KEGG pathway enrichment analysis was conducted using KEGG software (version 3.4.4). The sequencing data were deposited in the NCBI database under registration number PRJNA1374132 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sra/PRJNA1374132). A total of 12 transcriptome sequencing samples were obtained, with the SRA Experiment accession numbers being SRX313733331 - SRX31373342. Each sample produced approximately 185,000 to 317,000 paired-end reads, corresponding to 5.6 to 9.5 Gb of base data.

Metabolomics analysis

After treatment with 0 and 20 µM Cd for 15 days, 0.1 g of peanut root samples was collected for metabolomic analysis. Four biological replicates were included for each treatment. Sample preparation and data analysis were conducted according to the standard procedures of Suzhou Panwa Biomedical Technology Co., Ltd. LC-MS/MS analysis was performed using an ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography system (Thermo Vanquish, MA, USA) coupled with a mass spectrometer (Thermo Orbitrap Exploris 120, MA, USA). Quantitative analysis followed previously established procedures [23, 24]. Principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) were applied for data reduction and regression analysis. PCA was used to evaluate the overall distribution of samples and metabolic differences. Metabolite identification was conducted using MassBank, HMDB, MzCloud, KEGG, LipidMaps, and Nomi Metabolism databases. DEMs were screened based on P < 0.05 and variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1 [25]. KEGG enrichment analysis was subsequently performed, and the metabolomic and transcriptomic datasets were integrated for joint analysis.

The transcriptome and metabolome data results were jointly analyzed. The DEGs and DEMs were respectively subjected to KEGG pathway enrichment analysis to screen out the key pathways that were significantly enriched under Cd stress. The Pearson correlation coefficients between DEGs and metabolites in the same pathway were calculated using R software, and the genes-metabolites with significant correlations (P < 0.05, |r| > 0.7) were selected to achieve the integrated analysis of multi-omics data.

Quantitative RT-PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted from peanut roots treated with 0 and 20 µM Cd for 15 days using the MolPure® Plant RNA Kit (Yeasen, Shanghai, China). Residual genomic DNA was removed using DNase I (Yeasen). RNA concentration and purity were determined using an Epoch microplate spectrophotometer (BioTek, USA). First-strand cDNA was synthesized using the Hifair II 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit(Yeasen). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed using an ABI 7500 real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, CA, USA) [26]. Each 20 µL reaction contained 8 µL deionized water, 10 µL Hieff UNICON® Universal Blue qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Yeasen), 0.5 µL of each primer, and 1 µL cDNA. The amplification program consisted of pre-denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95 °C for 15 s, 60 °C for 20 s, and 72 °C for 20 s, with fluorescence detection at 72 °C. AhUbiquitin (DQ887087.1) was used as the internal reference gene. Primer sequences are listed in Table S2.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using Microsoft Excel 2010 (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA) and IBM SPSS Statistics 17.0 (Armonk, NY, USA). Statistical significance among treatments was assessed using independent-samples t-tests (Ion content and relative gene expression level) or Duncan’s multiple comparison tests (Physiological phenotype indicators). Differences were considered significant at P < 0.05.

Results

Effects of high Cd stress on phenotypic and physiological characteristics of peanut plants

Increasing Cd concentrations significantly inhibited peanut root growth (Fig. 1A–K). Compared with the 0 µM Cd treatment, exposure to 20 µM Cd reduced root fresh weight by 5.04% (Fig. 1A). Compared with the control, 20 µM Cd treatment reduced SOD activities in roots by 75.17% (Fig. 1L). The results showed that when peanuts were treated with 20 µM Cd, it significantly inhibited the growth of peanut roots and avoided lethality. Therefore, this concentration was selected as the Cd stress treatment concentration for subsequent ion content determination and omics studies.

Effects of high Cd stress on ion contents in peanut roots

To assess the effects of Cd stress on ion homeostasis, the contents of Cd, Mn, Fe, B, Mg, and Ca in peanut roots were determined (Fig. 2A–F). Compared with the control, Cd contents in roots treated with 20 µM Cd increased significantly by 948.72% (Fig. 2A).

Transcriptome Analysis

Transcriptome sequencing and identification of DEGs

Transcriptome sequencing was conducted on peanut roots treated with 0 and 20 µM Cd to examine changes in gene expression. Each sample generated 18,540,101–27,442,011 clean reads. Q20 and Q30 values ranged from 98.13% to 98.55% and from 95.04% to 95.73%, respectively (Table S3), indicating high sequencing quality suitable for subsequent analyses. Differential expression analysis identified 4,248 DEGs, including 2,172 upregulated and 2,076 downregulated genes (Fig. 3A, B).

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis of peanut roots

The top 20 significantly enriched Gene Ontology terms are shown in Fig. 3C. The DEGs were mainly enriched in molecular functions and biological processes. In peanut roots, these DEGs were associated primarily with redox enzyme activity, response to oxidative stress, metal ion binding, carbohydrate metabolism, amino acid metabolism, and macromolecular biosynthesis (Fig. 3C).

KEGG pathway enrichment analysis revealed that DEGs were significantly enriched in multiple pathways, including ABC transporters, plant hormone signal transduction, phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, starch and sucrose metabolism, galactose metabolism, linoleic acid metabolism, and flavonoid biosynthesis (Fig. 3D).

Functional categorization of Cd-responsive DEGs

The DEGs mainly involve the following four functional modules: antioxidation, ion transport, ABC transporters, and plant hormone signal transduction. A total of 64 DEGs related to antioxidant activity were identified, including 19 upregulated and 45 downregulated genes (Table S4). These genes were mainly associated with antioxidant enzyme families, including SOD, POD, GPX, APX, and CAT. Additionally, 59 DEGs related to ion transport were identified, including 41 upregulated and 18 downregulated genes (Table S5). These genes encoded transporters for Zn, copper, iron, magnesium, potassium, ammonium, phosphate, sulfate, nitrate, and NRAMP metal ions.

A total of 45 DEGs associated with ABC transporters were identified, including 22 upregulated and 23 downregulated genes (Table S6). These DEGs encoded members of the ABC transporter superfamily, including ABCA (one upregulated), ABCB (three downregulated), ABCC (five upregulated and one downregulated), and ABCG (seven upregulated and nine downregulated) proteins.In addition, 172 DEGs related to plant hormone signal transduction were identified, including 88 upregulated and 84 downregulated genes (Table S7). These genes were associated with abscisic acid (ABA), gibberellin (GA), jasmonic acid (JA), and auxin (IAA) signaling pathways. In the ABA pathway, PYR/PYL (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.0F111G) and SnRK2 (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.B16QUM) were downregulated, whereas PP2C (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.1984FG) was upregulated. In the GA pathway, DELLA (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.D3JULP) was downregulated. In the JA pathway, JAZ (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.DQCW8K) was downregulated, whereas MYC2 (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.JFV18T) was upregulated. In the IAA pathway, AUX/IAA (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.CB6084) was downregulated, whereas ARF, SAUR, and GH3 (arahy.Tifrunner.gnm1.ann1.2G66YZ) were upregulated.

Based on these results, 26 DEGs associated with antioxidant processes (Fig. 4A), ion transport (Fig. 4B), ABC transporters (Fig. 4C), and plant hormone signal transduction (Fig. 4D) were selected for further analysis.

DEMs in Peanut Roots

Under 0 and 20 µM Cd treatments, metabolite profiles in peanut root tissues were clearly separated (Table S8). ABC transporters and plant hormone signal transduction pathways analysis

Both DEGs and DEMs were enriched in the ABC transporters and plant hormone signal transduction pathways in peanut roots (Fig. 7). In the ABC transporter pathway, metabolites related to butyric acid, betaine, 2′-deoxycytidine, glutamate, and N-acetyl-D-glucosamine transport increased, whereas metabolites associated with lysine and phenylalanine transport decreased. The corresponding genes, including ABCA, ABCB, ABCC, and ABCG, showed both upregulation (18 DEGs) and downregulation (17 DEGs). In the plant hormone signal transduction pathway, the trends of different hormones were clearly different from each other. ABA, GA, and zeatin levels increased, whereas JA and N-jasmonoyl-isoleucine levels decreased. Genes associated with this pathway, including SnRK2, PP2C, JAZ, MYC2, CRE1, and A-ARR, were downregulated.

Discussion

Physiological responses of peanut roots to high Cd stress

High Cd stress induces symptoms such as growth inhibition, root damage, leaf chlorosis [27], oxidative stress, metabolic disruption, and tissue necrosis [28]. In this study, increasing Cd concentrations led to a continuous decline in root biomass. High Cd stress disrupts auxin distribution in rice roots, which restricts lateral root development [29]. Meanwhile, Cd toxicity significantly impairs the photosynthetic activity, enzyme systems and growth indicators of Arabidopsis thaliana, and regulates the function of chloroplasts and overall tolerance [30]. Under high Cd stress, fresh weight observed in peanut roots limited plant growth. To mitigate the adverse effects of Cd stress, adsorbents and composite materials have been applied to reduce Cd availability in soils, including nanomaterials [31], biochar [32], and exogenous selenium treatments [33].

Antioxidant enzymes are essential for ROS scavenging, maintenance of cellular redox balance, and protection against oxidative damage [34, 35]. At the same time, proline and soluble sugars accumulate to buffer oxidative damage and temporarily restore cellular homeostasis [12]. In this study, Similar responses have been reported in tomato seedlings exposed to high Cd levels, in which APX activity and soluble sugar and proline contents increased [12]. These results indicate that Cd stress may disrupt the balance of antioxidant enzyme systems in peanut roots, leading to ROS accumulation, enhanced oxidative damage, and inhibition of root growth. The decreased activities of SOD and CAT indicate that Cd stress may have inhibited some antioxidant enzyme systems. The enhanced activity of APX and the accumulation of osmotic regulators may represent a compensatory defense response. This differential response indicates that the Cd stress may not have led to a complete inhibition of the antioxidant system, but instead induced a functional imbalance among different antioxidant pathways.

Mineral nutrients absorbed in inorganic forms are essential for plant growth and development [36]. High Cd stress, however, causes nutrient imbalance and malnutrition [7]. High Cd exposure reduces magnesium, manganese, and calcium contents in rice organs, whereas iron content increases in roots [37]. In the present study, Cd and Fe contents increased in peanut roots, whereas Mn, B, Mg, and Ca contents decreased. Increased Cd accumulation also reduces biomass production, disrupts water and nutrient uptake, and intensifies osmotic, oxidative, and ionic stress responses [38]. Under high Cd stress, excessive Cd uptake likely by peanut roots therefore aggravates osmotic and oxidative stress, which enhances Cd toxicity and further restricts plant growth.

Transcriptional responses of peanut roots to high Cd stress

This study examined transcriptional changes in peanut roots exposed to high Cd concentrations. A large number of DEGs were identified under Cd stress. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses showed that these DEGs were mainly associated with catalytic activity, binding, metabolic processes, cellular processes, and macromolecule biosynthesis.

High Cd stress significantly alters plant transcriptional profiles and physiological status, which is characterized by metal accumulation, nutrient imbalance, and changes in antioxidant and osmotic regulation [39]. In Ligusticum chuanxiong, many ABC transporter genes, including members of the ABCA, ABCC, and ABCG subfamilies, are upregulated in response to Cd stress and are closely associated with Cd tolerance [40]. Plant hormones such as ABA and auxin also participate in regulating antioxidant systems under Cd stress by reducing excess ROS and modulating osmotic regulatory substances, thereby improving osmotic protection and antioxidant capacity [41]. These findings indicate that Cd stress may modify metal ion distribution and compartmentalization through ABC transporter pathways and regulates antioxidant and osmotic responses through plant hormone signaling pathways [40].

In this study, Cd and iron contents increased in peanut roots under high Cd stress. Transcriptome analysis showed that genes associated with metal ion transport, including ABCG2-like genes, were significantly downregulated, whereas ABCB1, ABCA3, and SNQ2 were significantly upregulated. ABCG-type transporters are primarily localized to the plasma membrane and mediate the efflux of toxic ions such as Cd²⁺ [42]. Their downregulation may reduce Cd extrusion capacity, resulting in Cd accumulation in root tissues. In contrast, ABCB, ABCA, and SNQ2 proteins mainly function in intracellular vesicle transport and vacuolar sequestration [43]. Their upregulation may promote intracellular compartmentalization and vacuolar storage of Cd. Some ABC transporters show non-specific or competitive transport of Fe²⁺ and Cd²⁺ [42], which may explain the concurrent accumulation of Cd and Fe observed in peanut roots. These results are consistent with ion content measurements and indicate that Cd interferes with transcriptional regulation of ion uptake and transport, resulting in metal nutrient imbalance.

Plant hormones are endogenous signaling molecules that regulate plant metabolism and play key roles in abiotic stress responses [44]. Under high Cd stress, JA-related transcription factors in peanut roots were upregulated, whereas GA-related transcription factors were downregulated. These changes likely contribute to stress responses by coordinating downstream defense gene expression, inducing ROS signaling, and promoting the accumulation of osmotic regulatory substances. The activity of APX in peanut roots increased significantly, which may reduce Cd-induced ROS accumulation and limit lipid peroxidation and protein damage in cell membranes [45]. In parallel, increased soluble sugar and proline contents indicate that peanut roots maintain osmotic stability through osmotic adjustment. Proline may also participate in ROS scavenging and Cd chelation, thereby reducing metal toxicity [46]. Together, these results suggest that peanut roots may suppress growth-related pathways under Cd stress and reallocate resources toward defense and adaptation. This response likely underlies the observed reduction in biomass and is consistent with the phenotypic and physiological findings of this study.

A total of 64 DEGs related to antioxidant functions were identified.Additionally, 59 DEGs associated with ion transport were identified, including genes encoding NRAMP proteins and Zn, iron, and magnesium transporters. NRAMP transporters regulate metal homeostasis and mediate the transport of iron, manganese, and calcium [47]. Under high Cd stress conditions, the expressions of Cd transport and detoxification-related genes such as OsHMA3, OsHMA2, and OsCAL1 in rice roots were induced [48]. The altered expression of these transport-related DEGs suggests that peanut roots adjust ion uptake and transport likely to cope with Cd toxicity.

Metabolic responses of peanut roots to high Cd stress

Metabolomic profiling showed that the DEMs in peanut roots were mainly carboxylic acids and their derivatives, fatty acyls, organic oxides, and quinone alcohol lipids. These metabolites were enriched in pathways related to transmembrane transport, plant growth, and signal regulation. These results suggest that metabolic processes associated with membrane transport and signaling may contribute to peanut tolerance to high Cd stress.

Membrane transport proteins regulate the transmembrane movement of water, mineral nutrients, and metabolites, thereby directly influencing plant growth, development, and stress responses [49]. They play central roles in nutrient uptake, ion homeostasis, and adaptation to adverse environments. Members of the ABC transporter family, for example, function in plant growth, development, and hormone transport. In Pyrus bretschneideri, PbrABC enhances tolerance to salt and drought stress [50]. ABC transporters also improve tolerance to heavy metal stress by mediating metal transport and hormone distribution, thereby influencing growth and hormone signaling in tomato [51]. In the present study, high Cd stress increased the abundance of metabolites related to betaine, betaine–lysine, 2′-deoxycytidine, glutamate, and N-acetyl-D-glucosamine transport, whereas metabolites related to lysine and phenylalanine transport decreased. Among them, the metabolites that have undergone the most significant changes are mainly related to osmotic regulation, nitrogen metabolism, and metabolites involved in maintaining cell structure stability. These changes indicate that peanut roots may promote the synthesis and transmembrane transport of ABC transporter–associated metabolites, enhance osmotic regulation, nitrogen metabolism, and cellular homeostasis, and restrict the transport of certain amino acids to sensitive sites. Together, these responses may enhance peanut tolerance to high Cd stress.

Plant hormones are essential for plant development and for responses to abiotic and biotic stresses [52]. Hormone signaling pathways play central roles in drought responses in Vaccinium corymbosum, in which ABA and auxin signaling regulate stomatal behavior, antioxidant responses, and drought tolerance [53]. In rice, auxin and JA/salicylic acid signaling pathways coordinate insect resistance and associated physiological responses [54]. In this study, high Cd stress increased the levels of ABA, GA, and zeatin in peanut roots, whereas JA and N-methyl jasmonoyl isoleucine levels decreased. These changes suggest that peanut roots may contribute to adjust hormone synthesis to enhance osmotic regulation and antioxidant capacity, reduce lipid peroxidation and protein damage in cell membranes, and improve tolerance to high Cd stress.

Responses of ABC transporters and plant hormone signal transduction pathways in peanut roots to high Cd stress

Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses identified two shared KEGG pathways in peanut roots under high Cd stress: ABC transporters and plant hormone signal transduction. ABC transporters are central to the transmembrane transport of diverse substrates [55], whereas plant hormone signaling regulates plant growth, development, and ROS homeostasis [56]. These pathways therefore may play key regulatory roles in peanut root responses to Cd stress.

ABC transporters constitute a large and diverse family of membrane proteins present across multiple biological domains [55]. They function in nutrient uptake, toxin efflux, and signal transduction and are essential for maintaining cellular homeostasis [55]. Transcriptomic analysis identified 45 DEGs related to ABC transporters in peanut roots, including members of the ABCA, ABCB, ABCC, and ABCG subfamilies. The ABCA subfamily participates in lipid homeostasis, membrane composition, and seed development [57]. ABCB proteins mediate the transmembrane transport of secondary metabolites and nutrient ions and strongly influence plant growth and root architecture [58]. ABCC transporters play important roles in metal detoxification and intracellular metabolite sequestration [59]. The ABCG subfamily is widely induced under salt, drought, pathogen, and heavy metal stress. For example, AtABCG36 is associated with Cd tolerance [60]. In many plant species, ABCG represents the largest ABC transporter subfamily [57]. In this study, the differential expression of ABCG transporters may mediate the transmembrane transport of Cd or Cd-chelates to the vacuole or extracellular compartments, promoting the compartmentalized accumulation or excretion of Cd, reducing the free Cd concentration in the cytoplasm and alleviating its toxic effects on cellular structure and metabolic processes. Multiple ABCG-related DEGs were highly responsive to Cd stress in peanut roots, indicating that the ABC transporter pathway contributes to defense and detoxification processes that maintain cellular homeostasis under high Cd stress.

Plant hormones are endogenous signaling molecules that regulate plant growth, development, and physiological responses to environmental stimuli [56]. Transcriptome analysis showed that genes associated with ABA, GA, JA, and cytokinin signaling were significantly downregulated in peanut roots under Cd stress. ABA plays a positive role in alleviating heavy metal stress in plants [61], and PP2C acts as a core negative regulator in the ABA signaling pathway [62]. In Lilium brownii, PYL4 and several PP2C genes are induced under salt stress [63]. In this study, PP2C, as a negative regulator of the ABA signaling pathway, its upregulation might have inhibited the activity of SnRK2, suggesting that changes might occur in the relationship between hormone levels and signal responses under high Cd stress. GAs regulate multiple aspects of plant growth and development [56]. DELLA proteins act as negative regulators of GA signaling by modulating transcription factor activity [64]. The downregulation of DELLA observed here may partially relieve growth inhibition and adjust GA signaling to balance growth and stress adaptation. The coordinated changes of ABA and GA in this study indicate that Cd stress may regulate the interaction between ABA and GA signaling. The accumulation of ABA may promote the expression of stress-related genes and the activation of the antioxidant system. Meanwhile, the change in GA signaling may inhibit cell division and elongation, suppress growth-related processes and enhance defense responses, thereby improving the adaptability of peanut roots to Cd toxicity.JA regulates stress-responsive gene expression and defense signaling under abiotic stress [65]. MYC2 is a central transcription factor in the JA signaling pathway, whereas JAZ proteins act as repressors by binding to MYC2 and suppressing its activity [66]. In rice, MYC2 upregulation and JAZ downregulation regulate JA signaling under stress conditions [66]. In the present study, when the JA content decreased, plants might still be able to maintain or enhance the defense response by regulating key transcription factors. MYC2 upregulation likely activates JA-responsive genes, whereas JAZ proteins provide negative feedback that limits JA activation. This may be related to the time-dependent changes induced by cadmium stress, feedback regulation, or the disconnection of signaling pathways, thereby contributing to Cd stress tolerance. Zeatin, a cytokinin, participates in plant responses to environmental stress [67]. In Brassica napus, CRE1, AHP, and B-ARR are upregulated under drought stress, which suppresses cytokinin signaling and slows growth [68]. In this study, A-ARR was downregulated, partially limiting B-ARR induction. The mixed regulation of CRE1, AHP, and B-ARR suggests dynamic modulation of cytokinin signaling under Cd stress to balance growth inhibition and stress adaptation. Collectively, these results indicate that hormone signaling pathways may coordinate defense responses under high Cd stress by restricting root growth and reallocating resources toward stress tolerance.

Molecular mechanisms underlying peanut root responses to high Cd stress

The DEGs and DEMs in multiple key pathways showed consistent or correlated change trends, indicating a close connection between transcriptional regulation and metabolic reprogramming. In the ABC transporter pathway, the expression of the ABCB1 was significantly upregulated, which might promote the accumulation or transport of glutamate-related substances and enhance the nitrogen metabolism regulation ability and antioxidant defense level. In the plant hormone signal transduction pathway, the expression of JA-related transcription factors was upregulated, possibly due to the increased substrate consumption under continuous stress conditions or negative feedback regulation of the signal, by enhancing the expression of downstream defense-related genes to improve stress adaptation ability. Meanwhile, the content of jasmonic acid and its active form N-methyl jasmonoyl isoleucine decreased, which might avoid the excessive activation of the JA signal from inhibiting the growth process.

This study proposes a regulatory model describing peanut root responses to high Cd stress (Fig. 9). Initially, excessive Cd²⁺ may be absorbed by peanut roots through transporters such as NRAMP, leading to Cd accumulation in root tissues. Elevated Cd levels induce oxidative stress, which triggers the expression of genes encoding antioxidant enzymes. Disruption of antioxidant enzyme systems results in excessive ROS accumulation and oxidative damage. To mitigate this stress, peanut roots adjust the expression of antioxidant enzymes and metal transporter genes, thereby enhancing ROS scavenging capacity and regulating metal uptake and transport. Concurrently, changes in the expression of ABC transporter genes, including members of the ABCG subfamily, contribute to Cd detoxification and ROS regulation. High Cd stress also alters the concentrations of key plant hormones, including ABA, GA, zeatin, and JA. These changes activate hormone-responsive genes such as PP2C, DELLA, MYC2, and B-ARR, which suppress root growth and development and promote stress adaptation. Additionally, high Cd stress may accumulation inhibits the uptake of essential nutrients, leading to nutrient deficiency. This deficiency further restricts plant growth and may contribute to reduced root biomass and inhibited root development under Cd stress.

Future studies may identify key regulatory genes through integrated multi-omics approaches and apply genetic improvement strategies to enhance Cd tolerance in peanut. In parallel, the application of adsorbents or composite materials to reduce Cd availability may help alleviate Cd toxicity. It will also be necessary to examine interactions between Cd stress and other environmental factors, to better understand their combined effects on peanut stress responses.

Conclusion

High Cd stress significantly inhibited peanut growth, with the strongest effects observed in the root system. Cd exposure restricted root growth and development and disrupted antioxidant enzyme activities. High Cd stress also altered ion uptake and transport and affected plant hormone levels in peanut plants. Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses showed that the ABC transporters and plant hormone signal transduction pathways were significantly enriched in peanut roots under Cd stress. Genes related to the ABCG subfamily and other associated transporters were differentially expressed and contributed to hormone regulation, intracellular redox balance, and stress adaptation. Overall, this study elucidates the physiological and molecular mechanisms underlying peanut responses to high Cd stress and highlights the central roles of ABC transporters and plant hormone signaling in Cd tolerance. These findings improve the understanding of Cd-induced growth inhibition and metabolic regulation in peanut, providing a certain theoretical basis for further conducting gene function verification and revealing the mechanism related to Cd tolerance.

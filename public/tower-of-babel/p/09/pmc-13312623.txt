Recurrent lung cancer with multi-organ metastases showing remarkable regression after two weeks of targeted therapy: a case report
Authors: Yalan Tang, Maoling Fu, Jie Wang, Ning Guo, Haodong Zhu, Lian Duan, Fei Li, Cheng Zhou, Chao Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRecurrent pulmonary malignancy with multi-organ metastasis remains one of the most refractory scenarios in thoracic oncology. Despite advances in systemic therapy, the prognosis for such cases remains poor. We report a case of disease recurrence with multiple metastatic lesions detected 34 months following initial surgical resection. After repeat surgical intervention, targeted therapy was initiated, resulting in complete resolution of brain and pericardial metastatic foci within two weeks-a response rarely observed in clinical practice. This case underscores the critical role of surgical intervention combined with precision targeted therapy in managing advanced lung cancer. It also highlights the potential of individualized treatment strategies to achieve favorable outcomes in refractory cases; it underscores the imperative for dynamic molecular monitoring and reaffirms the transformative potential of precision therapy in managing refractory metastatic non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Case presentation. A 53-year-old woman was referred to our institution after a computed tomography (CT) scan identified a 1.2 cm × 0.9 cm solid nodule in the anterior segment of the left upper lobe. Preoperative assessments—including complete blood count, liver and renal function, coagulation profile, pulmonary function, electrocardiography, echocardiography, and tumor markers (alpha-fetoprotein and carcinoembryonic antigen)—were within normal limits. The patient underwent uncomplicated resection of the anterior segment. Histopathology confirmed a moderately differentiated invasive lung adenocarcinoma, and next-generation sequencing (NGS) detected an epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) exon 21 p.L858R mutation with a variant allele frequency (VAF) of 23.59%. Thirty-four months later, she developed intermittent chest tightness and exertional dyspnea. Chest CT showed a 9 mm × 8 mm part-solid nodule adjacent to the left mediastinal pleura and significant pericardial effusion. Brain imaging revealed metastatic lesions in the brainstem and left cerebellum, confirmed by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). Thoracoscopic wedge resection confirmed recurrent lung adenocarcinoma. Repeat NGS identified the same EGFR p.L858R mutation with a markedly elevated VAF of 85.15%. Oral almonertinib was initiated, leading to complete resolution of brain metastases and near-complete resolution of pericardial effusion within two weeks. At eight-month follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic with no evidence of disease progression. Clinical discussionThis case illustrates key aspects of recurrent lung adenocarcinoma management. Initial resection of a small nodule achieved local control, but recurrence with brain metastases and pericardial effusion later occurred, emphasizing the need for vigilant follow-up and multimodal imaging. Critical to the outcome was genotype-guided therapy: post-biopsy genetic testing guided almonertinib administration, leading to complete resolution of brain metastases and improved pericardial effusion within 2 weeks, which was defined according to response evaluation criteria in solid tumors (RECIST) 1.1 criteria. In NSCLC, activating mutations in the EGFR gene are detected, and these mutations render tumor cells highly sensitive to tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs). The rapid response of the central nervous system (CNS) to targeted drugs highlights the value of personalized therapy in advanced-stage diseases. ConclusionThis case demonstrates the efficacy of genotype-guided targeted therapy in the management of recurrent lung adenocarcinoma presenting with brain metastases and pericardial effusion. The rapid and complete resolution of brain metastases and marked improvement in pericardial effusion within two weeks of initiating almonertinib therapy underscore the pivotal role of molecular profiling in guiding personalized treatment for advanced EGFR-mutated NSCLC. It also emphasizes the importance of rigorous postoperative surveillance for early detection of recurrence, thereby facilitating timely intervention and optimizing clinical outcomes in patients with advanced NSCLC.Keywords: Almonertinib, Brain metastases, Pericardial effusion, Recurrent lung adenocarcinoma, Targeted therapy

Introduction

Lung cancer remains a significant global public health challenge, characterized by complex barriers in prevention, early detection, and effective therapeutic management. Lung adenocarcinoma, a predominant subtype of NSCLC, is particularly challenging due to its high propensity for recurrence and distant metastasis, even following curative-intent resection [1]. Despite advancements in early detection and surgical techniques, a substantial proportion of patients with early-stage lung cancer experience recurrence within five years, with brain metastases and serosal metastases representing common and prognostically unfavorable outcomes [2–4]. The emergence of third-generation gene-targeted drugs, such as almonertinib, has transformed the therapeutic landscape for The Emergence of EGFR-mutated NSCLC. These agents offer improved selectivity for EGFR mutations, enhanced CNS penetration, and reduced toxicity compared to earlier generations, making them critical in managing metastatic disease, particularly with CNS involvement [5–7]. Nevertheless, rapid and complete resolution of established metastases, including brain lesions and pericardial effusion, within two weeks of TKI initiation remains rare, highlighting the need for comprehensive case documentation to guide clinical decision-making.

Case presentation

Initial presentation and management

A 53-year-old female with no significant medical history was referred to our institution on July 15,2022, for evaluation of a pulmonary nodule identified during a routine physical examination. Chest CT revealed a well-circumscribed nodular high-density lesion measuring 1.2 cm×0.9 cm in the anterior segment of the left upper lobe, without evidence of mediastinal lymphadenopathy or distant metastasis (Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8).

The patient subsequently underwent left upper lobe anterior segmentectomy with systematic mediastinal lymph node dissection via video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS). Laboratory evaluation revealed an elevated serum CEA level of 13.84 ng/mL. Surgical intervention, postoperative outcomes

A multidisciplinary tumor board concluded that image-guided pericardiocentesis alone would be insufficient, as it would provide only symptomatic relief without obtaining adequate tissue for definitive pathological diagnosis and crucial molecular re-profiling. Therefore, surgical intervention was pursued to obtain diagnostic tissue and manage cardiac tamponade. Thoracoscopic exploration revealed extensive pleural adhesions and pericardial thickening. Approximately 3000 mL of hemorrhagic pericardial effusion was drained via pericardial window creation, and a wedge resection of a 1-cm pulmonary nodule was performed. The patient’s postoperative course was notable for rapid and sustained symptomatic improvement, with resolution of chest tightness and dyspnea, unassisted ambulation by postoperative day 3, and return to full independence in activities of daily living within one week—a functional recovery that has been maintained throughout follow-up.

Pathological and genetic findings

Histopathological assessment of the second biopsy confirmed a poorly differentiated lung adenocarcinoma. Definitive pleural invasion was documented, a feature absent in the primary tumor. Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed its pulmonary origin (TTF-1+, CK7+, CK20-) and revealed an overtly aggressive phenotype in comparison to the initial lesion. The Ki-67 proliferation index increased markedly from 10% to approximately 90%, and p53 staining exhibited a pattern consistent with mutation. Supporting special stains confirmed an adenocarcinoma phenotype without mucinous differentiation. Two weeks later (May 25,2025), the genetic testing of the recurrent tissue revealed the same EGFR mutation (NM_005228.5, exon 21, c.2573T > G, p.L858R, VAF 85.15%). Programmed Death-Ligand 1 (PD-L1) immunohistochemical testing was not performed in this case. Given the limited biopsy specimen and the presence of a canonical EGFR-sensitive mutation (L858R), clinical decision-making was guided by the established role of EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitors as first-line therapy for EGFR-mutant advanced NSCLC. Current guidelines do not mandate routine PD-L1 assessment in this molecular context, as it does not alter the primary treatment selection.

Treatment and follow-up

Based on the confirmation of a canonical EGFR sensitizing mutation (L858R) with a high allele frequency (85.15%), and considering the presence of CNS metastases, a third-generation EGFR-TKI with proven superior blood-brain barrier penetration was selected over earlier-generation TKIs or chemotherapy. The patient was initiated on oral almonertinib (110 mg once daily). During the 2-week treatment period, safety monitoring was performed every 7 days, including routine blood tests and liver/renal function assessments. All indicators remained within the normal reference range, with no abnormalities such as myelosuppression or hepatic/renal impairment observed. No treatment-related adverse events (e.g., rash, diarrhea, fever, fatigue) were reported by the patient. Repeat brain and chest CT demonstrated complete resolution of the brain metastases and near-complete resolution of the pericardial effusion (Figs. 25–30).

At the most recent follow-up in February 2026, serial surveillance demonstrated sustained biochemical and radiographic disease control: serum tumor markers, including CEA and AFP, remained within normal reference ranges, while repeat chest and cranial CT scans confirmed the continued absence of metastatic lesions, consistent with durable radiographic remission. As of February 10, 2026, the patient continues treatment without clinical symptoms or imaging evidence of disease progression.

Clinical discussion

This case of a 53-year-old woman demonstrates two extraordinary phenomena: first, the dramatic clonal evolution of an EGFR-mutant lung adenocarcinoma from a low-VAF primary to a high-VAF metastatic recurrence; and second, the unprecedented, near-complete regression of both brain and pericardial metastases within two weeks of initiating the third-generation EGFR-TKI almonertinib. These observations form the basis for the following conceptual discussion.

From clinical anomaly to biological paradigm: reframing the case

This report transcends the documentation of an exceptional response to almonertinib [8, 9]. It presents a natural experiment in tumor evolution, serially capturing the transition from a genetically heterogeneous, early-stage adenocarcinoma to a clonally purified, metastatic phenotype. The central datum—the escalation of EGFR p.L858R VAF from 23.59% to 85.15%—serves not merely as a biomarker but as a quantitative tracer of clonal dynamics. This case thus challenges the static view of oncogenic drivers and positions itself at the intersection of two pivotal oncology concepts: adaptive therapeutic pressure and the emergence of targetable vulnerability [10, 11].

The evolutionary trajectory: selection, bottlenecks, and metastatic fitness

The observed VAF surge is best interpreted through the lens of population genetics applied to solid tumors. The initial resection acted as a potent selective bottleneck, eliminating the bulk tumor population while potentially enriching for, or failing to eradicate, a pre-existing minor clone with higher metastatic fitness. The 34-month interval represents not merely a “dormancy” period but likely a phase of clonal expansion and systemic dissemination driven by this EGFR-hyperdependent lineage [12, 13]. This evolutionary narrative suggests that for a subset of EGFR-mutant cancers, the primary tumor may function as a reservoir for future, more aggressive disease, with recurrence representing the clonal triumph of a fitter subpopulation rather than a mere regrowth of the original mass [6, 14–16].

Deconstructing the “Exceptional Response”: a tripartite mechanistic hypothesis

The profound and rapid regression compels a move beyond descriptive praise to a mechanistic hypothesis. We propose that three interconnected layers explain this phenomenon: Cellular Layer (High Target Saturation): The ~ 85% VAF establishes near-uniform EGFR dependency, minimizing the “therapeutic escape” provided by non-mutant cells within a heterogeneous tumor [17, 18].Pharmacologic Layer (Optimal Drug-Target Engagement): Almonertinib’s irreversible binding and superior CNS pharmacokinetics guaranteed sustained, high-grade inhibition of the EGFR pathway within sanctuary sites, a known limitation of earlier-generation TKIs [19, 20].Microenvironmental Layer (Vascular and Immune Modulation): The rapid resolution of hemorrhagic pericardial effusion exposes the suppression of EGFR-driven VEGF production and vascular permeability [21–23]. Furthermore, a potentially permissive immune microenvironment in these treatment-naïve metastases may have lacked the immunosuppressive factors often induced by prior therapies. This multilayer convergence created a “perfect storm” for TKI efficacy, underscoring that therapeutic outcomes are dictated by the interface of tumor genetics, drug pharmacology, and host-tumor microenvironment [24, 25].

The fallacy of the static genomic snapshot and the mandate for adaptive profiling

This case renders indefensible the reliance on a single, preoperative molecular profile for lifelong management. The initial low-VAF result was not just uninformative but potentially misleadingly reassuring, failing to predict the dominant biology of recurrence. This discordance argues compellingly for “adaptive oncology”—a treatment philosophy where therapeutic strategies are iteratively updated based on the tumor’s real-time molecular portrait [26, 27]. Re-biopsy at progression, therefore, shifts from being an optional diagnostic tool to a non-negotiable component of stage IV management. It is the essential feedback loop that closes the gap between a changing disease and a static initial diagnosis.

The integrated procedural intervention: surgery as a biospecimen-acquisition and cytoreductive strategy

Evaluating the second surgery through a purely technical or palliative lens misses its strategic role. It was a deliberate biospecimen-acquisition procedure designed to answer a specific biological question: what is the driving force behind this aggressive recurrence? Concurrently, its cytoreductive effect (pericardial drainage) served a dual purpose: immediate life-saving palliation and arguably, reduction of tumor burden to a level potentially more susceptible to systemic therapy [28, 29]. This epitomizes the modern role of locoregional interventions in metastatic disease: not as competitive with systemic therapy, but as complementary maneuvers that enable or enhance its effectiveness by procuring critical biological data and modulating disease burden [30].

3.6 Implications for Clinical Practice and Trial Design.

This case transcends its singular narrative to offer actionable insights for the broader management of EGFR-mutant NSCLC. It consolidates the rationale for third-generation TKIs as the first-line standard in patients with CNS involvement, given their superior sanctuary-site efficacy. Beyond drug selection, it advocates for a paradigm shift in surveillance and diagnostic strategy: incorporating brain MRI into routine follow-up for high-risk patients, and elevating molecular re-biopsy at progression from an optional procedure to a standard-of-care imperative for guiding adaptive therapy. Furthermore, it illuminates critical knowledge gaps ripe for investigation. Future adjuvant TKI trials should stratify patients by dynamic biomarkers such as baseline VAF or post-operative ctDNA positivity to distinguish indolent from aggressive early-stage disease [31, 32]. Finally, exceptional responders like this patient constitute an invaluable cohort for deep multi-omic dissection, aiming to decipher the determinants of ultra-sensitivity and durable response, thereby informing the development of next-generation, biomarker-selected therapeutic strategies.

Limitations as stepping stones

The recognized constraints of this report—targeted genotyping, absent immunotherapy biomarkers (PD-L1/TMB), and intermediate follow-up—do not diminish its observations but rather define a focused research agenda [33–35]. The direct future efforts should focus on: (1) comprehensive molecular profiling of recurrent lesions using broad NGS panels to capture co-evolving resistance pathways; (2) integrated immuno-genomic analyses to elucidate interactions between EGFR signaling and the tumor immune microenvironment; and (3) prospective longitudinal studies that couple serial liquid biopsies with detailed clinical phenotyping to track evolutionary trajectories in real time. The dramatic effusion resolution specifically nominates the serosal microenvironment as a critical substrate for investigating the off-target, anti-vascular effects of EGFR inhibition.

Conclusion

This case report transcends the documentation of an exceptional clinical response. It serves as a proof-of-concept for several evolving paradigms in precision oncology:

Dynamic Genomic Surveillance: It challenges the sufficiency of a single baseline molecular profile, advocating for serial molecular re-assessment as a standard of care at disease progression to capture clinically relevant evolution, such as the dramatic VAF shift observed here.

Mechanisms of Extreme Sensitivity: The ultra-rapid response provides a human model to investigate the confluence of genetic (high VAF), pharmacological (CNS-penetrant TKI), and microenvironmental (treatment-naïve sanctuary site) factors that can produce exceptional therapeutic outcomes. This triad offers a framework for future biomarker discovery.

Integrated Therapeutic Strategy: It demonstrates the complementary, non-competing roles of surgery (for diagnosis, cytoreduction, and tissue acquisition) and targeted therapy in the metastatic setting, moving beyond the simplistic dichotomy of local versus systemic treatment.

Therefore, this case is not merely about almonertinib’s efficacy in one patient. It is a clarion call to operationalize “adaptive oncology,” where treatment pathways are continuously informed by the tumor’s real-time molecular narrative, with the goal of identifying and capitalizing on fleeting windows of maximum therapeutic vulnerability.

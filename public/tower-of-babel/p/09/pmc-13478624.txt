Optimized rGO/NiCoFe2O4 nanocomposites synthesized via sol–gel auto-combustion for supercapacitor applications
Authors: Sajid Naeem, Novman Nabeel, Prabhpreet Kaur, Alok Jain, Kailash Chandra Juglan, Dilawar Husain, Ho SoonMin, Md. Iqbal Bahar Chowdhury
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This investigation presents the synthesis of composition-tuned rGO/NiCoFe2O4 nanocomposites via a modified Hummers’ method and sol–gel auto-combustion approach. The Ni content was systematically varied to investigate its influence on structural, morphological, and electrochemical properties. XRD confirmed the formation of a spinel NiCoFe2O4 phase, while FTIR and FESEM revealed strong interfacial interaction and a porous interconnected morphology. Electrochemical analysis in 3 M KOH demonstrated that the optimized RNC80 sample exhibits a high specific capacitance of 275 F g−1 (from GCD) and 250 F g−1 (from CV), along with low charge transfer resistance (72.5 Ω). The enhanced performance is attributed to the synergistic effect between conductive rGO and redox-active NiCoFe2O4, facilitating efficient ion diffusion and electron transport. These results highlight the potential of composition-engineered rGO/ferrite nanocomposites for advanced supercapacitor applications.

Introduction

The increase in the need for high-performance energy storage technology has led to several studies into the fabrication of complicated supercapacitor electrode materials1. Among the transition metal oxides and ferrites, nickel cobalt ferrite (NiCoFe2O4) has attracted the most interest because of its remarkable electrochemical stability, high electrical conductivity, and profusion of redox-active sites2. Because of its electron transmission speed and specific capacitance, NiCoFe2O4, a spinel ferrite, is a great option for next-generation energy storage applications3–5. Recently, ferrite- and graphene-based nanocomposites have attracted considerable attention not only for energy-storage applications but also for optoelectronic and photocatalytic devices due to their enhanced electrical conductivity, interfacial charge transfer, and tunable electronic properties. Previous studies have demonstrated the potential of graphene-assisted ferrite systems in improving charge separation efficiency and multifunctional device performance6,7. However, low electrical conductivity, low ion transport rates, and particle aggregation usually constrain the applicability of the material. Therefore, due to the exceptional electrical conductivity of rGO, large surface area, and inhibitory effect on the aggregation of metal oxide nanoparticles, rGO has been recognized as the best support material for moving beyond these constraints8–10. The electroactive site efficiency, electrode stability, and rate of charge transfer are all significantly improved with the incorporation of rGO in the NiCoFe2O4 matrix11,12. The synergistic effect of the interaction between rGO-based nanocomposites has been found to exhibit better electrochemical kinetics with a high rate of electron transport compared to ferrites in various studies. A one-pot hydrothermal method has been reported to yield a capacitance of 190.3 F g−1 for the rGO/MnFe2O4 nanocomposite13–15. Nickel cobaltite has been another material that has attracted attention for this purpose. Its unique spinel framework, along with the synergistic effects between cobalt and nickel cations, has shown promising results for electrochemical performance16–18. Further, issues like low electrical conductivity and structural degradation during cycling remain a concern. Efforts have been made by scientists to incorporate NiCoO4 with conductive carbon-based materials to address these issues19,20. For instance, a study has reported the synthesis of a porous graphene nanorod hybrid composite material that showed satisfactory cyclic durability with 94% capacitance retention over 10,000 cycles along with a high specific capacitance value of 1533 F g−1 at a scan rate of 5 mV s−120–23. Following this direction, studies have also been carried out on the integration of nitrogen-doped reduced graphene oxide (N-rGO) with NiCoO. Nitrogen doping into the graphene lattice has shown improved wettability and electrical conductivity by introducing defects into the material24–26. Such a change facilitates the interaction between the metal oxide nanoparticles and the graphene substrate, thus improving electrochemical response27. A nitrogen-doped N-rGO/MgC2O4 nanocomposite was used; it recorded a maximum specific capacitance of 1025 F g−1, along with an energy density of 36.6 W h kg−1 and a power density of 1500 W kg−1. This demonstrates the potential for improving supercapacitors using nitrogen-doped graphene composites28–30.

This experimental work integrates reduced graphene oxide (rGO) and NiCoFeO4 by employing a Sol–gel auto-combustion method. The method yields a high-performance supercapacitor. The combination of rGO and NiCoFe2O4 greatly improves the efficiency of storing charges. NiCoFe2O4 has good pseudocapacitance due to several redox reactions. Also, rGO greatly improves the conductivity by providing a network that allows for the efficient movement of electrons. The investigation also carried out structural and morphological analysis using XRD, FTIR, and FESEM. The results show that a well-dispersed and porous composite was synthesized. The study also examined the characteristics of the synthesized rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites using GCD, EIS, and CV techniques. Specific capacitance values derived from GCD and CV tests demonstrate how rGO considerably improves the charge storage capabilities. Because of its enhanced charge transfer properties and suitable Ni-to-Co ratio, the RNC80 composite demonstrated the highest specific capacitance of 250 F g−1 (from CV) and 275 F g−1 (from GCD) among the synthesized composites. RNC80 demonstrated previously unheard-of electrical conductivity and quick ion transport, according to the Nyquist plot analysis. The charge transfer resistance (Rct) was 72.5 Ω, and the series resistance (Rs) was the lowest at 1.62 Ω. This paper describes a scalable and cost-effective method for producing high-energy–density supercapacitor electrodes, which represents a feasible alternative for next-generation energy storage.

Materials and methods

Materials

To achieve excellent electrochemical characteristics, rGO/NiCoFe2O4 nanocomposites were synthesized using high-purity chemical precursors. Graphite powder was oxidized using sulfuric acid, phosphoric acid, and potassium permanganate in a modified Hummers’ process to produce graphene oxide (GO), which was then reduced to rGO with hydrogen peroxide. Nickel nitrate, cobalt nitrate, and ferric nitrate served as metal precursors for NiCoFe2O4 production via the sol–gel auto-combustion approach, with citric acid as a fuel and ammonia for pH correction. The electrochemical properties were evaluated with a 3 M KOH electrolyte. The materials helped to enhance the structural integrity as well as the charge storage of the composites. All the materials were purchased from the Sigma Aldrich company.

Synthesis of rGO/NiCoFe2O4

The rGO/NiCoFe2O4 (RNC) composites were synthesized via a modified Hummer’s approach for rGO synthesis, followed by a sol–gel auto-combustion technique for the formation of NiCoFe2O4. Initially, nickel nitrate (3 M), cobalt nitrate (3 M), and iron nitrate (6 M) were dissolved in 150 mL of deionized water under continuous stirring to maintain the stoichiometric ratio required for spinel NiCoFe2O4 formation. The concentration of nickel was varied from 0 to 100 wt% at regular intervals of 20 wt% each. The composites were designated as RNC0, RNC20, RNC40, RNC60, RNC80, and RNC100 for 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, and 100 wt% nickel concentrations, respectively. Here, ‘R’ represents reduced graphene oxide (rGO), while ‘NC’ denotes the nickel–cobalt ferrite phase. The percentage values correspond to the degree of nickel substitution in the CoxNi1−xFe2O4 system while maintaining a constant rGO content throughout all samples.

Citric acid (12 M) was added as a fuel and complexing agent to facilitate gel formation during the sol–gel process. The pH of the precursor solution was maintained at approximately 7 using ammonia solution to ensure homogeneous mixing and controlled gelation. In another beaker, dispersion of rGO was done in water, followed by sonication cycles. This was done to confirm the uniform dispersion of the material. Then, the sonicated rGO was slowly added to the metal nitrate solution while stirring. The temperature was increased to 90–95 °C for the formation of a gel. The formed gel was then taken for further thermal treatment on a hot plate for auto-combustion. After the auto-combustion process, the composites were washed several times with DI water. The impurities were removed completely from the composites. Then, the products were dried in an oven at 80 °C. It was extremely important to remove moisture before the fabrication of the electrodes and characterization.

Fabrication of the electrode

The active material was deposited on the nickel foam to form the working electrodes. To achieve this, 40 mg of the synthesized material, 5 mg of carbon black (used as a conductor), and 5 mg of polyvinylidene fluoride (used as a binder) were mixed in an 8:1:1 ratio to achieve a slurry. 3 mL of N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) was used as a solvent to ensure the material was homogeneous. To guarantee that the electrode surface was evenly covered, the slurry was then drop-cast over nickel foam that had been cleaned beforehand using a micropipette. The coated electrodes were dried for 12 h at 60 °C in a vacuum oven to improve adhesion and remove any leftover solvents8–10. After being dehydrated, the electrodes were tested electrochemically for energy storage performance using electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), galvanostatic charge–discharge (GCD), and cyclic voltammetry (CV). The mass loading of the active material on the nickel foam was maintained at 1 mg cm−2, and the effective electrode area was fixed at 1 × 1 cm2 for all measurements. Results and discussion

XRD analysis

Figure 2 presents the XRD patterns of the synthesized rGO/ NiCoFe2O4 (RNC) composites with varying nickel concentrations. The diffraction peaks at about 30.08°, 35.45°, 42.91°, 57.00°, and 62.69°, respectively, reflect the (220), (311), (400), (511), and (440) planes6,11,12. The alignment of these peaks with the distinctive spinel structure validates the creation of NiCoFe2O4. The accuracy and strength of these peaks indicate a high degree of crystallinity in the materials. All samples have a peak at 26.41°, which is caused by the (002) plane of reduced graphene oxide (rGO). The persistence of the peak indicates that the structural integrity of rGO is not affected during the synthesis process. This indicates a strong interaction between rGO and the ferrite nanoparticles. The observed diffraction peaks are in good agreement with the standard JCPDS reference patterns of CoFe2O4 (JCPDS No. 22-1086), NiFe2O4 (JCPDS No. 88-0287), and mixed spinel NiCoFe2O4 ferrite (JCPDS No. 22-1086). The crystallite size is determined for the (311) plane of the RNC materials using the Debye–Scherrer equation. The results show a decreasing trend in the crystallite size with an increase in nickel concentration. The d-spacing values obtained from Bragg’s law remain consistent for all the samples, remaining close to 2.53 Å for the (311) plane. This implies that there are negligible changes in interplanar spacing due to changes in composition. In addition, the lattice constants, obtained from d-spacing values, remain close to 8.39 Å, showing minor changes due to minimal distortions in the spinel structure. The obtained information is also validated by analyzing the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the diffraction peaks. The FWHM of 0.182° for RNC 40 implies that when the peaks are smaller, the crystallite size is larger, meaning that crystallinity is higher. In addition, the larger peak for RNC 100, with an FWHM of 0.269°, indicates a smaller size of the crystallites and possibly more structural defects. The consistent appearance of spinel ferrite diffraction peaks in all samples, irrespective of nickel and cobalt concentration, indicates the stability of the NiCoFe2O4 structure. The crystal structure is quite flexible, as indicated by variations in size and lattice characteristics of these structures, which could be useful in enhancing their efficiency in power-related applications.

FTIR analysis

The functional groups and chemical interactions present in the rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composite materials, which were synthesized with varying concentrations of Ni (0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 wt%), were identified using the FTIR analysis, as shown in Fig. 3. The FTIR spectra exhibited specific peaks corresponding to the functional groups present in the NiCoFe2O4 and rGO, which confirms the successful synthesis of the composite materials. By limiting the characteristic absorption bands seen in the 500–700 cm−1 range to the metal–oxygen (M–O) stretching vibrations of Ni–O, Co–O, and Fe–O bonds, the spinel NiCoFe2O4 phase is verified. These peaks, which show that the ferrite structure contains octahedral and tetrahedral coordination environments, are a basic feature of oxides of the spinel type.Fig. 3FTIR of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites.

FTIR of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites.

The broad band at about 3400 cm−1 reflects the stretching vibrations of hydroxyl (–OH) groups, which are created by surface-adsorbed water molecules or residual moisture. The existence of this band shows a specific level of hydroxylation on the surface. This may affect the electrochemical properties. The significant band observed at around 1600 cm−1 is due to the C=C bond of the sp2-hybridized carbon domains of rGO.

FESEM analysis

Figure 4 shows FESEM micrographs of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 (RNC) composites with varying nickel concentrations recorded at different magnifications: (a, b) RNC0, (c, d) RNC20, (e, f) RNC40, (g, h) RNC60, (i, j) RNC80, and (k, l) RNC100. From these images, it is possible to observe the microstructure of the composite with different nickel concentrations, which provides detailed information on their surface characteristics, porosity, and distribution, among other aspects, which are relevant for their electrochemical properties.Fig. 4(a–l) FESEM images of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites.

(a–l) FESEM images of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites.

The porous structure, as presented in the FESEM images, is advantageous for their use in supercapacitor devices, where ion diffusion and charge storage are involved. In addition, it is possible to observe the rGO sheets, which are in contact with the NiCoFe2O4 nanoparticles. The loosely packed and irregularly linked rGO sheets with the metal oxide phase lead to a disordered microstructure with lower concentrations of nickel. The density of NiCoFe2O4 particles increases with increasing concentrations of nickel. The particles have a distinct granular appearance. The porosity and regular structure of the composite at intermediate compositions (such as RNC40-RNC80) are noteworthy. This is an indication that NiCoFe2O4 nanoparticles are successfully attached to the rGO sheets. The regular dispersion of the particles is expected to enhance the electrochemical contact for better charge transfer. Additionally, an optimal configuration for ionic transportation and penetration of electrolyte, which are both integral components in high-performance supercapacitors, can be envisioned from the mesopore and micropore development in the morphology. The microstructure appears to be more compact at the highest nickel content in RNC100. Larger aggregated NiCoFe2O4 clusters were observed to be present on the surface. This suggests that aggregation of particles due to high loading of metal oxides can affect the surface area available for electrochemical reactions.

CV analysis

The electrochemical performance of the prepared RGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites was examined by cyclic voltammetry (CV) at different scan rates ranging from 10 to 100 mV s−1 in a 3 M KOH solution. The specific capacitance values were calculated from the CV curves at a scan rate of 10 mV s−1. All electrochemical measurements were carried out using a three-electrode configuration, where the prepared electrode served as the working electrode, a carbon electrode was used as the counter electrode, and the electrolyte was 3 M KOH. Figure 5 presents the CV curves of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 (RNC) composites with varying nickel concentrations (RNC0–RNC100) recorded at different scan rates in 3 M KOH electrolyte using a three-electrode configuration. The CV curves for the RGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites present a quasi-rectangular shape along with redox peaks. This indicates that the RGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites possess a combination of electrical double-layer capacitance and Faradaic redox reactions. With increasing scan rate, the current response increases while maintaining the full shape of the curves, suggesting good electrochemical reversibility and fast charge transfer kinetics. The observed electrochemical trend depends on the variation in Ni concentration within the rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites. At lower Ni content (RNC0–RNC20), the limited availability of Ni2+/Ni3+ redox-active sites results in lower capacitance and weaker current response. As the Ni concentration increases (RNC40–RNC80), a significant enhancement in electrochemical performance is observed due to the increased density of redox-active sites, improved electrical conductivity, and better interaction between NiCoFe2O4 nanoparticles and the rGO matrix. This leads to more efficient charge transfer and ion diffusion, resulting in the highest performance for the optimized RNC80 composition.Fig. 5CV of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites.

CV of rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites.

Among all synthesized composites, RNC80 exhibits the highest specific capacitance value of 250 F g−1, which is significantly higher than the capacitance values of the other samples, where the maximum value observed was 220 F g−1 for RNC40 and RNC60. These values are presented in Table 2. The enhanced electrochemical performance of RNC80 can be attributed to the optimized balance between redox-active NiCoFe2O4 nanoparticles and the conductive rGO framework, which facilitates efficient charge transfer and ion diffusion. However, at excessive Ni content (RNC100), the performance slightly declines, which can be attributed to particle agglomeration and the consequent reduction in effective surface area and accessible active sites. At the highest nickel concentration (RNC100), the electrochemical performance slightly declines, with the specific capacitance decreasing from 250 F g−1 for RNC80 to 230 F g−1 for RNC100. This reduction can be directly correleated with the FESEM observations described in “FESEM analysis” section. The FESEM micrographs of RNC100 reveal a more compact morphology, characterized by larger agglomerated NiCoFe2O4 clusters distributed over the rGO sheets. Such agglomeration decreases the effective electrochemically active surface area and limits the accessibility of electrolyte ions to the redox-active sites. Additionally, the excessive accumulation of ferrite nanoparticles likely obstructs the conductive pathways of the rGO network, thereby hindering efficient electron transport and ion diffusion during charge–discharge. Computational studies

The energy density and power density of the rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites were calculated to assess their suitability for supercapacitor devices. The energy density is a measure of the total capacity of the material to store charge. On the other hand, the power density is a measure of the total capacity of the material to deliver the stored charge. The calculated values of the energy density of the rGO/NiCoFe2O4 composites varied between 6.51 Wh kg−1 (RNC0) and 9.55 Wh kg−1 (RNC80). The highest value of the energy density was recorded by RNC80. This is similar to the specific capacitance values calculated in the experiment. This is due to the optimized composition of the composite material. The progressive increase of energy density with the addition of Ni may be an indication of the efficient contribution of the Ni-rich compositions to the storage of charge. However, the slight reduction observed in RNC100 may be an indication of the reduced number of electroactive sites due to excessive Ni content. The power densities varied between 202.08 W kg−1 (RNC60) and 234.38 W kg−1 (RNC0). However, it is interesting to note that though RNC80 shows the highest energy density, the power density of RNC80 (202.2 W kg−1) is slightly lower than that of the compositions containing less Ni content, such as RNC0 (234.38 W kg−1) and RNC20 (226.56 W kg−1). This may be attributed to the longer discharge times observed in RNC80.

Conclusions

In this study, the rGO/NiCoFe2O4 (RNC) composites were synthesized using the sol–gel auto-combustion technique for NiCoFe2O4 and the modified Hummer’s method for rGO. The structural and morphological studies confirmed the successful formation of an integrated hybrid composite material in which rGO/NiCoFe2O4 nanoparticles were uniformly dispersed within the rGO matrix. Hence, the electrochemical activity was greatly improved. The electrochemical studies performed using CV, GCD, and EIS showed that the ratio of Ni to Co had a significant effect on the charge storage efficiency of the composites. RNC80 was found to be the most efficient composition of the materials synthesized. RNC80 had the best ion diffusion properties, the lowest charge transfer resistance (Rct = 72.5 Ohm), and the highest specific capacitance (250 F g−1) (CV). The improvement in electrochemical performance is a result of the synergistic action between RGO and NiCoFe2O4. This action improves the rate at which electrons are transported, the accessibility of the electrolyte, and the efficiency of redox active sites. The results confirm the requirement for the ratio between NiCoFe2O4 and rGO for optimal conductivity, capacitance, and electrochemical stability in the electrode materials. The impressive electrochemical performance exhibited by RNC80 demonstrates its potential for scalable, cost-effective, and high-performance supercapacitors. Both symmetric and asymmetric supercapacitor devices, as well as the continued optimization of the synthesis parameters, including dopants, may benefit from this improved composite. Future studies might concentrate on these topics.

Quality of life in people with herpes zoster: a cross-sectional study in China
Authors: Yang Shen, Yiqi Xia, Wenhui Zhu, Jinxu Ai, Chengsen Cui, Yemin Yuan, Lidan Wang, Wenqiang Yin, Zhenyu Shi, Ping He
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionHerpes zoster (HZ), affecting 14.9 million people aged ≥ 50 globally in 2020, imposes a growing burden in aging populations due to its association with chronic pain, psychological distress, and socioeconomic disparities. This study aimed to evaluate health-related quality of life (HRQoL) among people with HZ and explore its socioeconomic and clinical determinants in China.MethodsWe conducted a cross-sectional survey to investigate the HRQoL of 2,566 herpes zoster patients across seven Chinese cities using the EQ-5D-5 L and VAS-100 instruments. Multivariate linear regression models quantified the association of age, income, insurance type, herpes zoster related pain, complications, and comorbidities with HRQoL.ResultsThe average EQ-5D score was 0.83, and the average VAS score was 71.49 among herpes zoster patients in China. Patients aged ≥ 70 years exhibited the most severe decline in both EQ-5D (β = −0.110, p < 0.01) and VAS-100 scores (β = −8.811, p < 0.01), with mobility limitations (OR = 3.696, p < 0.01) and self-care deficits (OR = 2.295, p < 0.01) disproportionately affecting this group. Herpes zoster related pain was associated with lower HRQoL (EQ-5D:β = -0.126, p < 0.01). Patients with comorbidities faced significant HRQoL reductions (VAS: β = -4.014, p < 0.01).ConclusionOur study found that Chinese herpes zoster patients exhibited higher HRQoL scores compared to those reported in most of other studies. Advanced age, lower education levels, inadequate insurance coverage, and pain occurrence were all significantly associated with diminished HRQoL. The observed disparities in HRQoL reductions among patient subgroups highlight the need to address health equity through curative interventions and preventive strategies.

Introduction

Herpes zoster (HZ), caused by the reactivation of varicella-zoster virus (VZV), represents a significant global health challenge, particularly among aging populations [1]. The annual incidence of HZ exceeds 3–5 cases per 1,000 person-years in individuals aged ≥ 50 years, with complications such as postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) occurring in 10–30% of patients [2, 3]. In 2020, an estimated 14.9 million people aged ≥ 50 years developed HZ globally, and this number is projected to reach 19.1 million by 2030 as a direct consequence of global population ageing [4]. In China, rapid demographic aging has amplified the disease burden, with recent studies reporting an annual incidence of 6.64–7.27 per 1,000 among adults ≥ 50 years [5]. Beyond acute morbidity, HZ imposes substantial economic costs, including direct medical expenses and productivity losses, as evidenced by European studies demonstrating annual per-patient costs exceeding €1,500 [6, 7].

Health-related quality of life (HRQoL) impairments in HZ patients are multidimensional. Acute neuropathic pain, measured by tools like the Zoster Brief Pain Inventory (ZBPI), correlates strongly with reduced physical function and psychological distress [8]. Each 1-point rise in the ZBPI score corresponds to a 1.57-point and 1.95 point reduction in the SF-12 physical and mental scores, respectively, underscoring the strong association between pain severity and declines in both physical functioning and psychological well-being [9]. Longitudinal data reveal that PHN prolongs these effects, with 40% of patients reporting persistent pain-related disability at one year post-onset [10]. Social determinants further exacerbate disparities: lower socioeconomic status and rural residency are associated with delayed care access, as shown in German cohorts where uninsured patients had 23% lower EQ-5D scores than insured counterparts [7, 11]. While EQ-5D-5 L and Visual Analog Scale (VAS) scores are widely used for quantifying HRQoL, it is critical to recognize that not all statistically significant score changes reflect clinically meaningful improvements or deteriorations from the patient or provider perspective. According to recent systematic reviews, the minimally important difference (MID) for EQ-5D-5 L ranges from 0.01 to 0.41, and for EQ VAS from 0.42 to 23.0, depending on baseline health status and treatment modality [12]. Only score changes exceeding these MID thresholds can be interpreted as meaningful shifts in daily functioning and overall well-being, thus informing both clinical decision-making and health policy. Our study therefore pays particular attention to whether observed differences surpass these thresholds, in order to evaluate the true impact of HZ on patient HRQoL.

These multidimensional impacts of HZ are particularly concerning in the context of healthy ageing, a framework underscored by the World Health Organization (WHO) as the process of developing and maintaining the functional ability that enables well-being in older age [13, 14]. Healthy ageing emphasizes not only longevity, but also the maintenance of independence, mobility, sensory function, and social participation. HZ and its complications, notably PHN, pose significant threats to these domains by precipitating long-term disability, psychological distress (including anxiety and depression), and social withdrawal. The burden is further exacerbated by social determinants of health; lower socioeconomic status and limited access to healthcare are associated with delayed diagnosis, undertreatment, and worse HRQoL outcomes.

Despite the growing recognition of these challenges, significant knowledge gaps persist, especially in developing countries. Most HRQoL studies in HZ have focused on Western populations [6, 15, 16], while data from China and other low- and middle-income regions remain limited, often restricted to single-center or regional samples [17, 18]. The interaction between sociodemographic factors (e.g., income level, insurance type) and clinical variables (e.g., comorbidities, pain severity) is poorly quantified. Moreover, there is a paucity of research on the psychological impact of HZ. Chronic pain is closely associated with anxiety and depression [19], and the persistence and uncontrollability of pain can significantly exacerbate patients’ psychological burden.

To address these gaps, we conducted a cross-sectional study of patients with onset HZ across seven cities in China. Using validated EQ-5D-5 L and Visual Analog Scale (VAS), we aim to quantify HRQoL variations linked to socioeconomic and clinical factors and identify high-risk subgroups for targeted interventions. Our findings will inform resource allocation strategies in China’s heterogeneous healthcare landscape, with the ultimate goal of supporting healthy ageing by mitigating the disease burden and functional impairment associated with HZ, especially among vulnerable older adult populations.

Methods

Data sources

This cross-sectional study utilized questionnaire-based survey data collected from herpes zoster patients across seven cities in Eastern, Central, and Western China between July and November 2024. The dataset included demographic characteristics, pain scores (assessed via validated scales), quality of life measurements (EQ-5D and VAS), and potential influencing factors such as age, sex, and socioeconomic status. Participants were recruited from dermatology or pain management outpatient clinics and inpatient departments of hospitals.

Study population

The study enrolled patients who were formally diagnosed with HZ through clinical evaluation and laboratory confirmation where necessary. Exclusion criteria involved individuals with unclear diagnoses, severe comorbidities, or those who declined to provide informed consent. Finally, 2,566 confirmed HZ patients were included in our study.

Measurements

The outcomes

Quality of life was evaluated using the EQ-5D-5 L instrument, which quantifies health status across five dimensions (mobility, self-care, usual activities, pain/discomfort, and anxiety/depression) and the results were transformed into EQ-5D scores with a EQ-5D-5 L value set for China [20, 21]. Two studies conducted in China have utilized the EQ-5D questionnaire to evaluate the quality of life in patients with herpes zoster (HZ) and postherpetic neuralgia (PHN), thereby demonstrating both the feasibility and validity of this instrument for this population [17, 18]. Self-reported health status was measured using the Visual Analog Scale-100 (VAS-100) [22]. Dependent variables included socioeconomic factors (age, sex, marital status, income, educational level) and clinical indicators (complications, comorbidities).

Statistical analysis

To ensure data quality, only valid responses were allowed to proceed to subsequent survey questions, resulting in a dataset with no missing values. Furthermore, all data underwent strict quality control during the cleaning process, and any abnormal values were excluded, ensuring the analyses were based on complete and reliable data. Descriptive statistics, including means (standard deviations) for continuous outcomes and frequencies (percentages) for categorical outcomes, were calculated to summarize baseline characteristics. Univariable analyses were first performed to examine the associations between relevant sociodemographic (age, sex, income level, insurance status, residential region) and clinical variables (pain severity, postherpetic neuralgia, comorbidities, disease duration) with the HRQoL outcomes (EQ-5D-5 L index and VAS scores). Variables with a p-value < 0.10 in the univariable analyses were entered into multivariate linear regression models to identify independent factors associated with HRQoL. Group comparisons (sex, age categories) were performed using ANOVA for continuous outcomes and chi-square tests for categorical variables. For dimension-specific analysis, outcomes of the five EQ-5D dimensions were dichotomized (influenced by herpes zoster or not), and logit models were used to analyze the associations between determinants and each dimension. All statistical analyses were performed using STATA 18.0.

Table 2 highlights significant variations in EQ-5D scores and VAS-100 measurements across demographic and clinical subgroups. The EQ-5D-5 L health utility score for herpes zoster patients averaged 0.83 (SD = 0.19) and the average score of VAS-100 was 71.49 (SD = 18.44). Age exhibited a strong inverse correlation with outcomes: participants aged < 40 achieved optimal scores (EQ-5D: 0.89 ± 0.13; VAS-100: 77.56 ± 19.91), while those aged ≥ 70 reported lower scores (EQ-5D: 0.78 ± 0.24; VAS-100: 65.84 ± 17.59). Education level positively influenced quality of life, with individuals educated above senior high school demonstrating higher EQ-5D (0.86 ± 0.17 vs. 0.82 ± 0.19 for below junior high school) and VAS-100 scores (74.86 ± 19.95 vs. 68.58 ± 16.93 for below junior high school). Better insurance coverage (UEBMI) and alcohol consumption in the past 12 months were also associated with improved quality of life.

Herpes zoster pain was linked to significantly worse outcomes (EQ-5D: 0.80 ± 0.20 vs. 0.94 ± 0.11, p < 0.01; VAS-100: 69.30 ± 18.50 vs. 77.73 ± 16.80, p < 0.01). Participants without complications reported higher EQ-5D (0.86 ± 0.16 vs. 0.81 ± 0.21, p < 0.01) and VAS-100 scores (74.70 ± 17.94 vs. 69.05 ± 18.45, p < 0.01). Similarly, those without comorbidities achieved higher EQ-5D (0.86 ± 0.17 vs. 0.80 ± 0.21, p < 0.01) and VAS-100 scores (74.84 ± 18.09 vs. 67.73 ± 18.12, p < 0.01). Notably, marital status and smoking behavior did not show statistically significant differences (p > 0.05).

Figure 1 illustrates the percentage of patients reporting problems across the five dimensions of the EQ-5D-5 L questionnaire. Most of the patients did not report any problems or declared mild problems in various dimensions of the EQ-5D-5 L questionnaire. The majority of patients reported no or mild issues in most dimensions, though the severity and distribution varied significantly. Specifically, a high proportion of patients (70.93%, 74.67%, and 66.76%, respectively) reported no problems, indicating minimal limitations in these areas. Only 28.10% reported no issues, while 45.17% experienced mild pain/discomfort and 19.76% reported moderate levels, highlighting pain as a prominent concern. While 58.85% reported no symptoms, 31.10% described mild anxiety/depression, and 7.87% experienced moderate to severe symptoms, reflecting notable psychological impacts.

The relationship between EQ-5D scores and VAS scores was effectively illustrated through scatter plots and linear regression analysis (Fig. 2). The slope of the linear fit was found to be 0.004 (p < 0.01), indicating that for each one-unit increase in VAS scores, there is an average increase of 0.004 units in EQ-5D scores. This demonstrates a direct positive correlation between EQ-5D scores and VAS scores.

The multivariable regression analysis (Table 3) reveals a robust age-dependent decline in HRQoL, as measured by both EQ-5D and VAS scores. Advancing age is strongly associated with worsening outcomes, with the most pronounced effects observed in individuals aged 70 and above. Compared to patients with UEBMI, those with Urban and Rural Resident Basic Medical Insurance (URRBMI) exhibited significantly lower EQ-5D scores. Health behaviors, including smoking and alcohol consumption, were not associated with patients’ HRQoL. The occurrence of PHN and herpes zoster-related pain was significantly associated with a decline in HRQoL. Additionally, comorbidity with other diseases also contributed to the deterioration of patients’ HRQoL.

Discussion

This study conducted a survey across seven cities in China to analyze the differences in HRQoL among herpes zoster patients. The results indicate that the overall EQ-5D score was 0.83, and the VAS score was 71.49. Our study confirmed that both patients’ socioeconomic and clinical characteristics were associated with their HRQoL. Patients aged 70 and above exhibiting a EQ-5D score of 0.78 and a VAS score of 65.84, while those under 40 had an EQ-5D score of 0.89 and a VAS score of 77.56. Furthermore, better education level and insurance status were also significantly associated with better quality of life. As for clinical characteristics, patients without pain had a much higher EQ-5D score (0.94) and VAS score (77.73) than those experiencing pain (0.80 and 69.30, respectively). Additionally, patients with comorbidities and complications reported significantly lower quality of life. These findings underscore the disparities in HRQoL among herpes zoster patients and the necessity for more interventions targeting specific high-risk groups to enhance health equity for herpes zoster patients.

Our findings demonstrated that age remains the strongest predictor of HRQoL decline, consistent with geriatric frailty models proposed by Schmader et al. [23]. According to the EuroQol interpretation guidelines, a ≥ 10-point VAS change signifies attainment of the minimal clinically important difference [24], the difference of VAS score between < 40 years old group and ≥ 70 nearly 12 points. However, the pronounced mobility limitations and self-care deficits in our cohort exceed those reported in Korean studies [25], potentially reflecting China’s limited access to physical rehabilitation services for older adults with herpes zoster—a gap noted in India’s rural healthcare systems as well [26]. Although anxiety exhibits the weakest age-dependent association among the five dimensions of the EQ-5D, advanced age is significantly associated with higher anxiety levels in herpes zoster patients. It was consistent with evidence from Japan, which shows that psychological distress peaks among people in their 70s [27], suggesting effective health policies must prioritize the mental health for the elderly herpes zoster patients by providing adequate support and resources, ultimately promoting overall health and well-being.

Socioeconomic status exerted heterogeneous effects on HRQoL in herpes zoster patients through multidimensional pathways, revealing mechanisms that challenge conventional health inequality frameworks. The analysis demonstrated a paradoxical association between elevated income (>200,000 CNY/year) and impaired HRQoL, evidenced by significantly reduced EQ-5D scores and increased mobility limitation risks. This counterintuitive phenomenon may stem from occupation-related stressors prevalent among high-income groups, including prolonged sedentary behavior and neuroendocrine dysregulation induced by chronic psychological pressure. Educational attainment exhibited a graded relationship with health outcomes: patients with lower education levels demonstrated worse EQ-5D scores and had a higher proportion of patients with PHN than their counterparts with higher education level. Limited health literacy likely mediates this disparity, as disadvantaged groups often underestimate disease severity and delay standardized treatments. Insurance coverage stratification exacerbates health inequities, with URRBMI enrollees showing significantly poorer HRQoL than UEBMI beneficiaries. Compare to UEBMI enrollees, URRBMI participants exhibit the higher multidimensional health risks, particularly in self-care capacity. To address the disparities in health outcomes among patients with different characteristics, community-based interventions should prioritize cost-effective herpes zoster vaccination programs through subsidized immunization campaigns targeting aforementioned populations with high risk of herpes zoster induced HRQoL losses [28, 29], coupled with integrated vaccination-clinic networks within primary healthcare systems to enhance service accessibility.

Herpes zoster-associated pain exerts a profound and multifaceted detrimental impact on health-related quality of life (HRQoL) among affected individuals not only directly impairs the EQ-5D dimension of pain/discomfort, but also substantially increases the likelihood of problems in other EQ-5D dimensions. For instance, the odds of mobility impairment rise significantly (OR = 2.712) in patients experiencing herpes zoster-related pain, underscoring the pervasive effect of chronic pain on physical function and independent living. Moreover, the psychological ramifications are considerable; patients with herpes zoster pain exhibit a markedly increased risk of anxiety and depression (OR = 3.270, P < 0.01), highlighting the strong interconnection between chronic pain, emotional distress, and mental health outcomes. Comorbid chronic diseases, such as diabetes and hypertension, further amplify the adverse effects of herpes zoster, making these patients particularly vulnerable to deteriorations across multiple HRQoL domains. A registry-based study conducted in the Miyun district of Beijing, China, revealed a significant age-related increase in the proportion of patients reporting problems across all five EQ-5D dimensions [18]. Similarly, our findings corroborate this pattern, demonstrating that the odds ratios for each of the five domains increased progressively with advancing age. These findings emphasize the necessity of comprehensive pain management strategies and targeted interventions for high-risk groups to mitigate the broad-ranging impacts of herpes zoster on quality of life.

Notably, the HRQoL levels measured in our study population were consistently higher than those reported in most comparable studies [30, 31]. A Spanish epidemiological research found that acute phase patients scored 0.59, declining to 0.55 in PHN cases – a reduction comparable to chronic conditions like diabetes mellitus and COPD [6]. The herpes zoster induced HRQoL losses were particularly pronounced in Asian contexts: Japanese octogenarians with PHN demonstrate critically low EQ-5D scores of 0.49 [27], while South Korean data reveal a 29% population-norm adjusted QoL deficit in PHN patients [25]. Longitudinal analyses from Southeast Asia further quantified the sustained burden. Thai PHN patients exhibited poorer long-term quality metrics than cancer survivors [32], while studies in Chinese Taiwan documented disability-adjusted life year (DALY) losses exceeding those of moderate-severe osteoarthritis [33].

To our knowledge, this study represented the most comprehensive assessment of HRQoL among herpes zoster patients in China. However, several limitations must be considered. First, the cross-sectional design limited our ability to draw causal inferences regarding the relationship between socioeconomic status and HRQoL. Therefore, the associations observed should be interpreted as correlations rather than direct effects. Additionally, it was important to note that potential confounding factors, such as environmental stressors and access to healthcare resources, were not adequately controlled for in this study. These unmeasured variables may have influenced HRQoL outcomes and could obscure the true nature of the relationships observed, thereby reducing the generalizability of our findings to populations with different backgrounds or healthcare systems. Furthermore, psychological impact was assessed using only the self-reported ‘anxiety/depression’ dimension of EQ-5D-5 L, which may not fully capture the breadth or severity of mental health issues experienced by patients. This limitation may have led to an underestimation or oversimplification of psychological distress and its association with HRQoL. To address these issues, future studies should consider adopting longitudinal designs to better evaluate changes in HRQoL over time and to strengthen causal inference. In addition, it would be valuable to use more comprehensive, validated instruments for specific mental health domains, and incorporate a broader range of sociodemographic and clinical covariates. These methodological improvements will help clarify the complex relationship between herpes zoster, psychological health, and patient well-being.

Conclusion

This study revealed a significant decline in HRQoL among herpes zoster patients in China, while reporting higher HRQoL scores compared to most studies conducted in other countries. Advanced age, lower education levels, inadequate insurance coverage, and pain occurrence were significantly associated with reduced HRQoL. The observed heterogeneity in HRQoL losses across patients with differing socioeconomic characteristics underscores the necessity of improving health equity for this population through both curative interventions and preventive measures.

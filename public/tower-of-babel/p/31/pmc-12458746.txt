Canadian real-world hybrid longitudinal cohort study of treatment discontinuations and modifications for patients with HER2+ and HER2-low metastatic breast cancer on trastuzumab deruxtecan enrolled in a patient support programme: the HER-TEMPO study protocol
Authors: Christine Brezden-Masley, Rana Qadeer, Zhor Senhaji Mouhri, Bianca Salvo, Nicolle Bonar, Paul Spin, Simran Shokar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIn current clinical practice, breast cancer is treated according to hormone receptor and human epidermal growth receptor 2 expression (HER2) status, which play an important role in disease management and overall prognosis. Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) has been studied in multiple global prospective DESTINY-breast trials. Recent marketing authorisation for T-DXd has been granted from Health Canada for HER2-positive breast cancer who have received prior treatment with trastuzumab emtansine, or at least one prior anti-HER2-based regimen in the metastatic setting, or who have received a prior anti-HER2-based regimen in the neoadjuvant or adjuvant setting and developed disease recurrence during or within 6 months of completing neoadjuvant or adjuvant therapy. T-DXd is also indicated for HER2-low unresectable and/or metastatic breast cancer (mBC) patients who have received at least one prior line of chemotherapy in the metastatic setting or developed disease recurrence during or within 6 months of completing adjuvant chemotherapy. However, there is a paucity of evidence assessing T-DXd in the real-world setting. As such, the overarching aim of this study is to generate Canadian real-world evidence on discontinuations and treatment modifications for patients with HER2+ and HER2-low mBC undergoing treatment with T-DXd.Methods and analysisThis is a hybrid, longitudinal cohort study design leveraging patient support programme (PSP) secondary data with additional primary data collection to assess study treatment-related outcomes among patients with HER2+ and HER2-low mBC receiving treatment with T-DXd. Mainly, this study will leverage secondary data from the PSP, which will include clinical and demographic characteristics as well as duration, modification and intensity of treatment information for patients while enrolled in the PSP. These data will be supplemented with primary data, which will be collected via a patient questionnaire and include additional self-reported clinical and demographic characteristics not captured within the PSP, including follow-up data on therapies received, treatment discontinuation information after the PSP closure and frequency of CT scans and cardiac monitoring scans.Ethics and disseminationThe study protocol was approved by the Advarra Institutional Review Board on 13 December 2023 (ID: D133HR00037). Findings will be disseminated by publication in peer-reviewed journals, through oral and poster presentations for various audiences, websites and scientific meetings. Written informed consent will be obtained from all patients prior to agreeing to participate in this study.StatusParticipant recruitment for primary data collection began on 22 April 2024 and was completed on 8 October 2024. Primary data collection for follow-up will continue through up to 12 months after the date of the last enrolment.Trial registration numberNCT06386263.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the second-most common cancer in Canada, with an estimated 28 600 new cases and 5500 deaths annually.1 2 The majority of patients present with early stage disease. For patients who are later diagnosed with recurrent metastatic disease, the goals of treatment are to stop or delay the progression of the cancer.3 Breast cancer (BC) is a heterogenous disease with distinct molecular subtypes determined based on immunohistochemistry (IHC) or in situ hybridisation (ISH). In routine clinical practice, BC subtype is used with cancer staging to support the assessment of cancer risk and optimal treatment pathways.4

Approximately, 20% of tumours present with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) gene amplification or protein overexpression, of which about one-thirds are or become metastatic.5 Current first-line recommended treatments for HER2-positive (+) metastatic breast cancer (mBC) include HER2-directed therapy (eg, pertuzumab and/or trastuzumab) with or without taxanes, while second-line treatment historically included trastuzumab emtansine (T-DM1).4

The Phase 3 DESTINY-Breast03 (DB-03; (NCT03529110)) clinical trial demonstrated clinically meaningful and significant improvements in progression-free survival for trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) compared with T-DM1 in adult patients with unresectable or mBC that was previously treated with trastuzumab and a taxane in the advanced or metastatic setting or progressed during or within 6 months after neoadjuvant or adjuvant treatment involving trastuzumab and a taxane. Based on these results, T-DXd has received marketing authorisation and reimbursement in Canada in the HER2-positive setting.6 T-DXd is indicated in Canada for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive BC who have received prior treatment with (T-DM1) and the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic HER-positive BC who have received at least one prior anti-HER2-based regimen either in the metastatic setting or had progressed within 6 months after neoadjuvant or adjuvant treatment. The European Society for Medical Oncology has recommended T-DXd as second-line treatment.7 While evidence is limited to guide post-T-DXd treatment, treatment guidelines recommend the continuation of additional HER2-directed therapies.4

The remaining 80% of tumours that are not HER2+ are classified as HER2-negative (−). Among studies of primary or mBC, in approximately 65% of cases in which the tumour was diagnosed as HER2-negative, the specimens met the criteria for HER2-low (IHC 1+, IHC 2+/ISH− of HER2 (HER2-low), defined as IHC of 1+ or 2+, without HER2+gene amplification by ISH).810 HER2-low BC is a clinically heterogenous group that includes both hormone receptor—positive (HR+) and negative (HR−).11 12 Current treatment options for patients with HER2-low mBC are based primarily on HR status for earlier lines of treatment. In Canada, for patients with HR+/HER2-low mBC who are no longer eligible for endocrine therapies and HR+/HR−/HER2-low patients who received at least one prior line of chemotherapy, T-DXd is approved for use as a treatment based on the results of the landmark Phase 3 trial, DESTINY-Breast04 (DB-04; (NCT03734029)).6 DB-04 arrived at similar conclusions to DB-03. In this trial involving patients with HER2-low mBC, T-DXd resulted in significantly longer progression-free and overall survival than the physician’s choice of chemotherapy.

T-DXd has received marketing authorisation and a positive Canadian reimbursement recommendation in the HER2-low setting with reimbursement within provinces underway.6 13 More specifically, T-DXd is indicated in Canada for HER2-low unresectable and/or mBC patients who have received at least one prior line of chemotherapy in the metastatic setting or developed disease recurrence during or within 6 months of completing adjuvant chemotherapy. HR+BC patients should have received at least one prior line of chemotherapy and be no longer considered eligible for endocrine therapy.6

More recently, primary results from the Phase 3 DESTINY-Breast06 (DB-06; (NCT04494425)) demonstrated similar results; T-DXd improved progression-free survival versus chemotherapy in patients with HR+, HER2-low or ultralow (IHC 0 with≤10% membrane staining) mBC treated with ≥1 lines of endocrine-based therapy allowing the potential of treatment expanding to include these patients in the future.14 Based on the data from DB-06, the US Food and Drug Administration has granted breakthrough designation therapy for T-DXd in patients with unresectable or metastatic HR-positive, HER2-low (IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH−) or HER2-ultralow (IHC>0 with ≤10% membrane staining) BC who have received either two lines of endocrine therapy in the metastatic setting, or one line of endocrine therapy if they had demonstrated disease progression within 6 months of starting first-line treatment with endocrine therapy in combination with a CDK4/6 inhibitor or within 24 months of the start of adjuvant endocrine therapy.15 Furthermore, the European Medicines Agency has validated the Type II Variation application for ENHERTU as a monotherapy for the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic HER2-low (defined as IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH−) or HER2-ultralow (defined as IHC 0 with membrane staining) BC who have received at least one endocrine therapy in the metastatic setting.16

In the DB-03, DB-04 and DB-06 trials, median treatment duration with T-DXd was 18.2, 8.2 and 11.0 months, respectively.1719 Treatment-emergent adverse events leading to treatment discontinuations occurred in 20%, 16.2% and 14.3% of patients.1719 Treatment-emergent adverse events leading to dose reductions occurred in 25%, 22.6% and 24.7% of patients.1719 However, treatment duration, discontinuations and dose modifications for T-DXd are not well characterised in a real-world setting.

Access to novel oncology drugs, such as T-DXd, is typically contingent on a mix of regulatory and reimbursement review processes. In Canada, sponsors of investigational drugs must apply to Health Canada for regulatory approval to market a drug for use in a specific indication. Following this approval, a reimbursement review process, known as a health technology assessment, is completed by the Canada’s Drug Agency or the Institut National d’Excellence en Santé et en Services Sociaux in Quebec. These agencies provide reimbursement recommendations, which consider clinical and economic value of the investigational drug compared with other available drugs, to provincial and territorial authorities who decide to publicly fund the drug (often with conditions).20 Within Canada, the time between notice of compliance and first public listing has increased to 22 months.21

Industry-sponsored patient support programmes (PSPs) have emerged to fill the gap to provide time-limited access to novel drugs that have received regulatory approval, before public reimbursement is available.21 In Canada, PSPs are typically administered via specialised third-party vendors who provide logistical support, such as enrolling eligible patients on referral by physicians, scheduling clinic appointments for treatment, reimbursement navigation, drug delivery, patient education support and adverse event monitoring. In addition to providing therapeutically beneficial treatments to patients, PSPs offer a unique opportunity to generate timely real-world data that can better represent the effectiveness of a drug in real-world clinical practice.22 PSPs offer the initial opportunity to obtain these data in Canada, as these patients are often the first patients to receive a novel drug or an existing drug being used to treat a new indication.1 Data derived from PSPs can help payers, clinicians and patients, who obtain critical information about real-world effectiveness, treatment adherence and additional health outcomes to support implementation in clinical practice.

As the early clinical trials for T-DXd included limited data on Canadian patients, the overarching aim of this study is to generate novel Canadian real-world evidence on discontinuations and treatment modifications for HER2+ and HER2-low mBC patients by using data generated from AstraZeneca Canada’s PSP for T-DXd. Specifically, the primary study objectives are to estimate the rates of early discontinuation and to characterise dose modifications and dose intensity over the course of treatment with T-DXd. The remaining sections describe key elements of the study protocol.

Methods and analysis

Study design and setting

This is a hybrid, longitudinal study consisting of primary and secondary data collected from Canadian patients who enrol in AstraZeneca Canada’s PSP to receive T-DXd for HER2+ or HER2-low mBC. Patients who enrolled in the PSP will receive T-DXd treatment in Canada under the supervision of a healthcare professional. PSP enrolment for patients with HER2+mBC began in July 2022 and remained open until July 2023. For patients with HER2-low mBC, PSP enrolment began in January 2023 and remained open until March 2024.

Patients who enrolled in the PSP (the total PSP population (TPP)) can consent for the use of their routinely collected data over the course of their participation in the PSP for clinical research. Additionally, at the time of PSP enrolment, patients can consent to be contacted to participate in a real-world research study. Patients who provide additional consent to participate in primary data collection for this study (the study PSP population (SPP)) will contribute self-reported baseline clinical and demographic characteristics and continued follow-up data related to cardiotoxicity monitoring and CT scans, discontinuation of T-DXd and subsequent treatments initiated following the closure of the PSP.

Patient recruitment for primary data collection began on 22 April 2024 and was completed on 8 October 2024. Primary data collection for follow-up is anticipated to continue through December 2025.

Intervention

Study participants in the TPP or SPP are required to have completed at least one infusion with T-DXd.

Included study participants will have received T-DXd through the PSP as a part of their routine care in accordance with the recommended dosing instructions in the product monograph. The recommended dose of ENHERTU is 5.4 mg/kg, given as an intravenous infusion once every 3 weeks (21-day cycle) until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. However, there is no minimum number of cycles of T-DXd required for patient inclusion. Management of adverse events can include temporary interruptions, dose reductions and treatment discontinuation. If applicable, recommended dose reductions include a first reduction to 4.4 mg/kg followed by a second dose reduction to 3.2 mg/kg.6 There are no other interventions as this is a non-comparative study.

Procedures for collecting and reporting adverse events for this study are outlined in online supplemental text 1.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics

Baseline or preintervention variables collected by the PSP and available for the TPP will include age in years, gender, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS), IHC/ISH and weight. Other variables require consent for primary data collection and are only available for the SPP. Detailed descriptions of baseline demographic and clinical characteristics, including their method of ascertainment, are included in figure 1 and figure 2.

Data management and collection

All data will be collected by a third party and will be stored using a secure web-based customer relationship management system. Prior to enrolment in the SPP, informed consent form for primary data collection will be collected via electronic signature using OneSpan. Data collected will be deidentified to maintain patient privacy. Data collection updates will be provided to the study personnel every 8–10 weeks.

Sample size and precision estimates

PSP enrolment numbers resulted in sample sizes of 346 and 861 patients for the HER2+mBC and HER2-low mBC TPP cohorts, respectively. An estimated 10% of patients from the TPP are expected to consent to primary data collection to be part of the SPP.

Rather than formal sample size calculations for hypothesis testing, precision estimates were used to evaluate the margin of error for treatment discontinuation rates (our primary endpoint of interest) based on a Kaplan–Meier (KM) estimator for time to discontinuation. These precision estimates incorporated multiple factors leading to treatment discontinuation, including disease progression, treatment-related toxicity, patient preference and other clinical considerations.

The methodology involved deriving baseline discontinuation rates from digitised KM estimates of progression-free survival from DB-04 (NCT03734029), as disease progression represents a key reason for treatment discontinuation for many anticancer treatments. Cycle-specific discontinuation rates were computed as the ratio of predicted cumulative progression-free survival probabilities across adjacent~3 week treatment cycles, as estimated from the DB-04 trial. These clinical progression rates were then inflated by 20% to account for additional reasons for treatment discontinuation beyond disease progression, including treatment-related toxicity, patient choice, physician decision and other clinical factors (online supplemental table 2).

Precision estimates were assessed via simulation considering the anticipated TPP sample sizes, assuming that 10% of the TPP cohort was lost to follow-up (primarily due to the termination of the PSP), and the composite discontinuation rates described above. The resulting margin of error was below 10% for all time points and under a scenario analysis, allowing for extensive early censoring during the first three cycles of treatment received during the PSP (table 1).

Study stopping rules

Primary data collection will conclude at 12 months following the date of last enrolment. Additionally, the study will be terminated early if the drug under observation becomes subject to a Health Canada mandated recall, market withdrawal or a safety-related labeling change that significantly impacts its risk-benefit profile.

Statistical analyses

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics will be reported as sample counts and proportions (categorical variables) or as means (SD), medians (IQRs) and ranges (continuous variables). KM analyses will be used to evaluate time to event outcomes, such as treatment discontinuation, early discontinuation and time to next treatment. The occurrence of dose modification or interruptions will be reported as the number and percentage of patients experiencing at least one event, while the rate of event will be assessed using a binomial model and a complementary log–log link. Other continuous and categorical endpoints will be evaluated descriptively using methods similar to those used to characterise baseline demographic and clinical characteristics. A statistical review of the code will be conducted by a third party to verify the analysis. The review will assess that the code used to derive the results represents a reasonable and accurate analysis. The third party may provide suggestions to identify additional insights and assist in understanding potential data limitations.

Exploratory objectives involving the assessment of statistical associations between treatment discontinuation and baseline demographic and clinical factors will be assessed using multivariable Cox proportional hazards models, if feasible. The model will include a time to discontinuation as the dependent variable and relevant baseline clinical and demographic variables as predictors. Time to discontinuation will be administratively censored at 9-months follow-up. Analytic results will be reported using potentially time-varying hazard ratios and 95% CIs for each included predictor variable. Additionally, model coefficients will be used to predict cumulative marginal probabilities of early discontinuation at 3, 6 and 9 months as well as probabilities conditional on covariate values (eg, ECOG PS of 0 or 1) for key predictors of interest.

Subgroup analyses

For endpoints pertaining to baseline demographic and clinical characteristics (secondary objective 1) and real-world treatment discontinuation (secondary objective 2), the HER2+ and HER2-low cohorts will be stratified by presence of brain metastases and HR status (ie, HR positive/HR negative), respectively.

Sensitivity analyses

In general, the primary analyses relating to PSP-derived secondary endpoints will include the TPP for the HER2+ and HER2-low cohorts. In cases where similar information is collected following the PSP, a sensitivity analysis will be conducted with patients in the SPP who provide informed consent for primary data collection.

Multiple imputation of missing data may be conducted if the application of complete cases leads to a >5%–30% reduction in sample size.

Ethics and dissemination

The study is conducted according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki,23 International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use Guidelines for Good Clinical Practice24 and the International Society for Pharmacoepidemiology Guidelines for Good Pharmacoepidemiology Practice.25 The study protocol and patient facing study materials (ID: D133HR00037) were approved by the Advarra Institutional Review Board on 13 December 2023. Findings will be disseminated by publication in peer-reviewed journals through oral and poster presentations at scientific meetings and will be made publicly available.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and the public were not involved in the design, conduct, reporting or dissemination of this study.

Discussion

This study is designed to provide real-world effectiveness and implementation data for the use of T-DXd in routine clinical practice settings across Canada. The study population will be comprised of and highlight some of the first patients receiving T-DXd in Canada.

The study will include all patients enrolled in the AstraZeneca PSP and who consented to participate in real-world research and provide valuable information on the rates of treatment discontinuation and dose modifications for patients receiving T-DXd. Furthermore, the study will provide a broader evaluation of patient monitoring practices, including how clinicians track treatment-related risks. For example, information on the frequency of CT scans will serve as a potential indicator of how closely patients are being monitored for ILD/pneumonitis in a real-world setting. Results of the study may aid physicians and be used to contextualise clinical practice decisions for future patients.

The study will also be used to identify best practices and procedures for data collection for future PSPs. In addition, efforts have been made to minimise bias in data collection. This will be accomplished through clinician oversight, third-party verification of analysis code and protocol publication for increased transparency. All patient data will be deidentified and collected by a third party (NavieGo Patient Programmes Ltd and Affiliates) to allow for independence from the Sponsor (AstraZeneca Canada).

Data collection from individual hospitals can be costly and a complex process. A strength of using nationwide patient data from the PSP is mitigation of some of these challenges.

Nonetheless, limitations of the study include that patients in the PSP may not generalise to the typical patient eligible for T-DXd. For instance, patients who elect to enrol in a PSP may be more informed or motivated by health considerations and, thus, may be more likely to adhere to their prescribed treatment and infusion schedule. Secondary patient data collected from the PSP are limited in scope, as only the minimum data required for enrolment are collected in the PSP. Thus, secondary data may not include important demographic and clinical characteristics that are known to be prognostic or confounding variables on patient outcomes. Some of these variables may be collected through the primary data collection; however, it will be reliant on a smaller sample of patients and will be self-reported data, which may limit generalisability and be subject to recall bias. Reporting of outcomes is reliant on patient participation. However, the study will, therefore, help assess a patient’s ability to recall their disease and treatment information. The study does not have a control group, and thus, direct comparative inferences of real-world treatment-related outcomes (treatment discontinuations, modifications, etc.) to standard of care cannot be made. Furthermore, this study uses secondary data from the PSP enrolment forms, which do not systematically capture adverse events. As a result, it is not possible to reliably assess adverse events as a reason for treatment discontinuation, limiting a more comprehensive understanding of treatment adherence patterns.

Importantly, when considering both the strengths and limitations, this study will provide clinically meaningful real-world data that may support multiple stakeholders in future decision-making.

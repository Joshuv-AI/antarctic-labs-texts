Research on the pore size distribution and durability improvement mechanism of magnetic slurry
Authors: Jie Liu, Kexin Zhang, Zheng Li, Ziwei Liu, Kun Mao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe internal pore structure of cement-based materials plays a key role in medium migration and directly impacts the material’s durability performance. This study proposes a method of regulating magnetic particle slurry using magnetic fields in order to systematically investigate the optimisation mechanism of pore structure under the influence of a magnetic field. Accelerated durability tests were conducted, including freeze-thaw cycles (50 cycles), chloride ion penetration and sulphate erosion (SO₄²⁻), combined with nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) technology to analyse the pore structure evolution quantitatively. The results show that the magnetic field significantly improves the pore characteristics of the slurry, as evidenced by a reduction in the number of capillary channels and porosity. With a magnetic powder content of 20%, the strength loss rate after 50 freeze-thaw cycles was found to be 2.14%. Increasing the magnetic powder content to 25% improves resistance to sulphate erosion by 14.4% compared to the control group. Analysis of the microstructural mechanism revealed that the magnetic field drives magnetic particles to form ordered arrangements, generating a volume exclusion effect that promotes bubble escape while densifying micro-regions through magnetic forces between particles. NMR testing confirmed that, under a 0.5 T magnetic field, the micro-pore throat volume of samples containing 15% magnetic powder decreased by 23.35%. This study overcomes the limitations of traditional pore modification technologies by providing theoretical support and technical solutions for optimising the durability of functional cement-based composite materials.Keywords: Magnetic slurry, Pore size distribution, Drive-by gas effect, DurabilitySubject terms: Civil engineering, Composites

Introduction

As the most widely used construction material in the world, cementitious materials are exposed to complex environmental conditions for a long time, and are highly susceptible to freeze-thaw damage, harmful ion erosion and other destructive effects, which can lead to cracking and even obsolescence of the material1–4. Among them, pore size is a key indicator for determining the structural durability and strength of slurry, while the expansion of erosion products in the pores will trigger the generation of microcracks, thus accelerating the material deterioration process5–7.

The pore structure of cement-based materials is a core element that influences both the cement hydration reaction process and macro-physical properties, and these characteristics directly affect material design and durability8. The hydration reaction starts when cement comes into contact with water and continues throughout the service life. As free moisture is gradually consumed, a characteristic pore network gradually forms9. This pore structure and capillary channels become the main pathways for the intrusion of external harmful media, significantly affecting the carbonation process, chloride ion corrosion, and sulphate deterioration of the material, among other durability issues10,11. It is particularly noteworthy that the geometric characteristics of the pores within the slurry (size parameters and spatial distribution) are key indicators determining freeze resistance performance. Based on this, the precise characterisation and control of pore structure has become a core frontier direction in the field of cement-based materials research12. In cement slurry bubble testing, researchers used new testing techniques to discover that the bubbles in the slurry are related to the structural morphology of the hydration products, and that the viscosity of the slurry has a certain effect on bubble migration13,14. Gas injection porosity (GIP) and CT scanning techniques have been applied in micro-pore testing15,16.

During grouting construction, the grout generates bubbles under high pressure, resulting in the formation of numerous pore structures after solidification, ranging in size from centimetres to nanometres17. The presence of a small number of microbubbles in cement slurry can effectively improve the fluidity, freeze resistance, and waterproofing performance of the slurry. In concrete, a large number of microbubbles have a lubricating effect, reducing the relative friction between concrete components. However, if there are a large number of large bubbles in the cement slurry, it will cause the internal structure of the slurry to become loose18, ultimately affecting the strength and durability of the cement slurry. In addition, voids caused by air bubbles in underwater and marine engineering projects can increase the sensitivity of structures to sulphate and chloride corrosion, thereby weakening the load-bearing capacity of the reinforcing steel within the structure19,20. Reducing material porosity and capillary action is an important method for improving slurry durability. Recent years have seen a diversification of development trends in technical research on improving the durability of cement-based materials. In the early stages of research, the focus was on the development of chemical modification methods, with the addition of materials such as titanium dioxide, organically dispersed graphene oxide, VAE latex powder, and epoxy resin. The objective of this approach was to effectively reduce the porosity of mortar and to significantly enhance its waterproofing, chloride ion penetration resistance, freeze-thaw resistance, and sulphate erosion resistance21–25. With the promotion of green development concepts, researchers have begun to explore pollution-free alternative materials. They have found that barley straw ash, rice husk ash, and biochar modification technology can optimise pore network structures, reducing harmful pores while improving mechanical properties and freeze-thaw resistance26,27. Among these, biochar modification technology also achieves dual performance enhancement through the stress buffering effect of micro- and nano-pores28. The application of nanomaterials has further expanded the scope of modification. The composite modification of polypropylene fibres with nanoalumina and multi-walled carbon nanotubes has significantly improved the density of the material29,30.For special cement systems, such as aluminium sulphate cement, the functionalisation of polyvinyl alcohol powder has achieved quantitative inhibition of capillary water absorption and chloride ion migration, providing new insights for the design of high-performance materials31. Additionally, research into the durability damage mechanisms under coupled freeze-thaw and sulphate erosion environments, combined with quantitative analysis of pore volume changes, has driven the development of freeze-thaw strength prediction models that consider the coupled effects of multiple factors32,33. These studies have established a systematic exploration path from material modification and mechanism revelation to performance prediction, providing multi-dimensional theoretical support and technical solutions for the durability design of cement-based materials.

The application of magnetic materials in civil engineering has seen rapid development in recent years. Early research focused on the conceptual development of smart cement-based magnetorheological (MR) fluids, which regulate the rheological behaviour of slurries through alternating magnetic fields, laying the theoretical foundation for magnetic field-responsive building materials34. Subsequently, research on the magnetorheological effects of nanoscale Fe3O4 particles deepened quantitative understanding of magnetic interactions between particles and equilibrium migration rates, promoting a refined analysis of the magnetic control mechanism of cement-based suspension systems35. Research on the mechanism by which magnetic fields influence microstructure has shown that static magnetic fields can optimise the hydration reaction process by regulating the reorganisation of water molecules, thereby improving the density of the slurry36. With regard to material modification, the application of magnetic field-induced alignment technology for steel fibres has led to substantial enhancements in the rheological properties of cement-based materials37–40, while the oriented distribution of nickel powder has facilitated the successful preparation of isotropic conductive cement-based materials41,42. Recently developed magnetic field-controlled active enhancement control technology has achieved dynamic response characteristics through the rheological effects of magnetically responsive additives, effectively solving the problem of template leakage under pressure conditions43. However, current research mainly focuses on macro-performance characterisation, and there is still a lack of systematic analysis of the pore structure evolution and long-term durability mechanisms of materials under magnetic field control, which provides an important direction for future research.

In summary, current research has mostly focused on the effects of factors such as temperature, water-cement ratio, admixtures, and curing time on pore size distribution. Most methods for improving mortar durability involve modifying the mortar with admixtures or incorporating different types of fibres, with the aim of controlling porosity and mortar cracking to prevent the erosion caused by water molecules and harmful ions. Admixtures are highly sensitive to dosage, have poor compatibility with cement slurry, and cannot be adjusted after the slurry has been prepared. In engineering, mechanical vibration is mostly used to remove gas from concrete slurry to reduce pore content. However, increased slurry viscosity may result in inadequate compaction, which may increase local porosity. However, no one has yet proposed a method of reducing porosity by using the internal force of slurry to orientally aggregate and compress air, and no research has been reported in this area. This paper focuses on the mechanism of bubble removal in magnetic slurry under an external magnetic field environment, and further explores the correlation between magnetic field durability and the results of freeze-thaw cycle tests, chloride ion flux tests, and sulfate ion tests. In addition, the changes in the pore distribution of the slurry under the influence of a magnetic field were systematically analysed.

(2)Analysis of Freeze-Thaw Damage Damage Morphology and Antifreeze Mechanism of Magnetic Slurry

Analysis of Freeze-Thaw Damage Damage Morphology and Antifreeze Mechanism of Magnetic Slurry

Water molecules penetrate the outer surface layer of the specimen along the capillary pores during the freeze-thaw cycle, and freeze-thaw damage occurs in the outer surface layer of the specimen under the action of cyclic freeze-thaw water freezing and expansion load. The large pores in the hardened slurry without a magnetic field are spherical, while the large pores in the slurry under a magnetic field are mostly ellipsoidal, and the porosity is low so that the water molecules do not easily enter the interior of the slurry in the process of the freeze-thaw cycle. The efficiency of water transport during freezing and thawing depends mainly on stomata, capillaries, and micro-fissures. For this, the anti-freezing mechanism of magnetic slurry can be explained in Fig. 8, under the action 0 magnetic field, for the large air holes in the magnetic chain under the action of magnetic field force extrusion deformation to ellipsoidal, furthermore, the large air holes in the process of the movement of the magnetic chain will be divided into two or more small air holes, all can block more capillary seepage channels. Large inhomogeneous cracks will be compressed into smaller cracks, reducing the generation of interpenetrating cracks.

Magnetic slurry anti-freezing mechanism diagram.

Previous studies have shown that the number of SEM microcracks in the slurry is small before freeze-thaw cycles. As shown in Fig. 9, the microscopic SEM image of magnetic slurry after freeze-thaw damage, there are more than 10 microcracks with different lengths after 50 freeze-thaw cycles of magnetic slurry without additional magnetic field. In addition, a large number of cracks, pores, and dislodged blocks are present, mainly because water expansion during freeze-thaw cycles accelerates the evolution of cracks. As shown in Fig. 10, the number of cracks after freeze-thaw cycles in the slurry under a magnetic field is less than that in the slurry without a magnetic field because of its higher degree of compactness, fewer capillary seepage channels, less accessibility of water under freeze-thaw cycles, and lower degree of freeze-thaw damage than that of the slurry without magnetic field.

Resistance to chloride erosion

For the specimens with magnetic powder doping of 0%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, and 25%, a group of 0.5 T magnetic field was applied, and the other group had no additional magnetic field, and the sides of the specimens were coated with adhesive after 28 days of standard curing, and the electric flux test was carried out after the vacuum saturation, and the results of the test are shown in Fig. 11, and the electric flux decreases gradually with the increase of the doping of the magnetic powder in the absence of the added magnetic field. Compared with the electric flux of the specimens without magnetic powder, the electric flux was reduced by 10.78%, 18.32%, 21.83%, 30.99%, and 32.07%, respectively, when the magnetic field was applied at the doping level of 5% to 25%, which was mainly attributed to the fact that the magnetic powder did not participate in the hydration reaction, filled the micropores in the body of the slurry stone, and reduced the generation of capillary channels. Comparing the fluxes with and without a magnetic field at the same magnetic powder doping, the maximum flux reduction rate was 9.2% at 20% magnetic powder doping. The application of a magnetic field reduces the electric flux further because the magnetic force chain and the magnetic particles converge under the action of the magnetic field, compressing the pores of the slurry stone body and thus further reducing the porosity, so that the slurry chlorine ions seepage resistance performance has been improved to a certain extent.

Variation of electric flux with magnetic powder doping.

The depth of Cl− erosion was tested by the chromogenic method. The specimen was split after the test, and the configured silver nitrate solution with a concentration of 0.3 mol/L was sprayed on the fracture surface, and the color change of the fracture surface was observed. As shown in Fig. 12, the dashed box white area is the generated AgCl precipitate, which represents the region of Cl− erosion. Calipers were used to select 10 points at an interval of 1 cm, the silver-white width was measured at each point, and the average value was recorded as the erosion depth of the whole specimen. With the increase of magnetic powder doping, the depth of Cl− erosion shows a gradual reduction trend, the depth of erosion of the ordinary slurry without magnetic powder is 8.9 mm, the magnetic powder doping of 25% in the magnetic field, the depth of erosion is greatly reduced, indicating that the magnetic powder filling and the magnetic field force of the adsorption of the extrusion has the effect of reducing the capillary channels and microporous space.

Comparison of color development by Cl- erosion under a magnetic field of different magnetic powder doping samples.

Resistance to sulpha attack

Figure 13a shows the trend of the slurry compressive strength and erosion resistance coefficient before and after the magnetic slurry with only magnetic powder added to resist erosion without a magnetic field, and Fig. 13b shows the strength and erosion resistance coefficient before and after the slurry erosion with a magnetic field added. The erosion resistance of the slurry generally increased with the increase of magnetic powder doping, the 28d erosion resistance coefficient of ordinary slurry was 0.79, and the erosion resistance coefficient of the slurry with 25% magnetic powder doping was 0.89, which was improved by 12.65% in comparison with the ordinary slurry. The erosion resistance coefficient of the slurry with 25% doping was enhanced by 4.5% over the magnetic slurry without a magnetic field and by 14.4% over the normal slurry under the effect of an applied 0.5 T magnetic field. When the magnetic powder doping is greater than 15%, the growth rate of the erosion resistance coefficient becomes slower. Overall, the durability of the slurry was improved after the application of the magnetic field, mainly due to the decrease in porosity under the magnetic field force, which increased the densification of the slurry. The Fig. 14 shows the flexural strength and erosion coefficient of the slurry with the magnetic powder dosage, the more the magnetic powder dosage, the magnetic slurry flexural strength appears to reduce the trend, the main reason is that the magnetic powder dosage increased, the quality of cement is reduced, and the crystalline hydration products are reduced accordingly, which is also similar to the trend of its compressive strength. However, the increase of magnetic powder doping decreases the SO42− erosion effect, and the fracture erosion resistance coefficient of the slurry grows by 23.7% at 25% magnetic powder doping compared with that of the normal slurry. In the range of 10%~20% doping, the erosion resistance coefficient is similar, indicating that the slurry erosion resistance grows slowly. The erosion resistance grows faster at a doping level of 0%~10%, which is the same as the compressive strength enhancement law.

Macroanalysis

The sulphate erosion of cementitious materials is a complex process involving physical and chemical actions. The main reason for destruction is the chemical reaction between sulphates and the hydration products of cement, which generates products with micro-expansion characteristics. The continuous accumulation of these products leads to cracks in the cement matrix and, finally, to spalling and other phenomena.

Magnetic drive exhaust analysis

Oscillation of the magnetic chain to eliminate air bubbles

Figure 18 shows a model diagram of slurry exhaustion under the action of the magnetic field, which depicts the formation of magnetic chains by magnetic particles under the action of the magnetic field, and the action of magnetic chains on slurry bubbles under the action of the magnetic field, which can be interpreted in two ways. On the one hand, the force acting on magnetic particles changes under the influence of a magnetic field. During their movement, the thrust of the slurry agglomerates acts on the bubbles, propelling them out of the slurry. Conversely, due to the deformation of the bubbles, the compression energy is converted into the bubbles’ surface energy, which is then converted into their kinetic energy. When the external magnetic field moves away from the magnetic slurry, the strength of the magnetic field decreases, the distance between particles increases and the pressure on the bubbles decreases or stops altogether. Bubble deformation under pressure involves the conversion of accumulated energy into kinetic energy. As the magnetic field strength decreases, the viscosity of the slurry also decreases, which further facilitates the expulsion of bubbles.

Magnetic particles exhaust bubble mechanism diagram.

The Fig. 19 shows the diameter distribution of bubbles expelled from slurries containing different concentrations of magnetic powder. The data indicates that, when subjected to a magnetic field, bubbles expelled from slurries with different magnetic powder concentrations primarily fall within the 0.5–1 mm range. Specifically, slurries containing 5% magnetic powder expel bubbles ranging in diameter from 0 to 2 millimetres; those containing 15% magnetic powder expel bubbles ranging in diameter from 0 to 2.5 millimetres; those containing 25% magnetic powder can expel bubbles ranging in diameter from 0 to 3.5 millimetres; and those containing 25% magnetic powder can expel bubbles with a maximum diameter of 3–3.5 millimetres. It indicates that as the magnetic powder doping increases, the bubbles are discharged by the magnetic slurry under the action of the magnetic field. The larger the dosage of magnetic powder, the greater the proportion of bubbles with a diameter greater than 1 mm that can be discharged from the magnetic slurry under the action of the magnetic field. Microscopic pore distribution study

In this study, the percentage of microporous, mesoporous and macroporous distribution of ordinary slurry was significantly higher than that of epoxy resin magnetic cement slurry as detected by NMR, and the peak area corresponding to the three kinds of pore sizes showed a tendency to decrease with the increase of the magnetic powder doping. As shown in Fig. 20a and b, the distribution of pore size and pore throat (collectively referred to as pore throat) in ordinary slurry, 5% and 15% magnetic powder doped slurry with and without the magnetic field, respectively, is shown in Fig. 20a and b. Compared with ordinary slurry, the microporous throat (0–0.1 μm) decreased by 17.813% under 0.5T magnetic field when the magnetic powder doping was 5%, and decreased by 23.35% under 0.5T magnetic field when the magnetic powder doping was 15%. The macroporous throat of ordinary slurry was 2.06%, the macroporous throat of 5% doping CEM slurry under the action of 0.5T magnetic field was 0.494%, and the macroporous throat of 15% CEM slurry was 0.097%, and the macroporous throat showed a significant reduction trend. It shows that the addition of magnetic powder can significantly reduce the pore size in the slurry, reduce the number of pores, and reduce the percentage of harmful pores; the application of the external magnetic field can further reduce the number of harmful pores and reduce the pore size.

Conclusions

This study proposes a novel method to enhance the durability of cementitious materials based on porosity regulation, and achieves regulation of the pore structure of the slurry by independently developing magnetic epoxy resin-modified cement slurry and combining it with dynamic magnetic field regulation technology. A systematic comparison of the durability indexes of magnetic slurry and conventional slurry is conducted in terms of chloride ion permeation, sulfate ion erosion, freeze-thaw cycle, etc. The microstructure evolution law of the material and the mechanism of durability enhancement under the effect of magnetic field regulation are revealed by combining this comparison with multi-scale characterisation techniques, such as scanning electron microscopy (SEM) microscopic morphology analysis and pore measurement by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) method.

The nitrogen adsorption-desorption curve of Fe3O4 exhibits no significant hysteresis loop, indicating that the micron-sized Fe3O4 is a non-porous solid particle. The main component of the resin used is E-51 epoxy resin, which is primarily composed of diphenylpropane and epichlorohydrin. When mixed with cement slurry, it enhances the slurry’s adhesion, toughness, water resistance, and abrasion resistance.

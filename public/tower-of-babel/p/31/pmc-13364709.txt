Re-Arming the Immunological Radar: The Translational Landscape of Dendritic Cell-Centric Nanotherapeutics and Microenvironment Remodeling for Osteosarcoma
Authors: Zhi Zheng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFor decades, survival rates in metastatic Osteosarcoma (OS) have starkly plateaued. The persistent failure of immune checkpoint blockade underscores a critical reality: the true therapeutic bottleneck is not merely exhausted T cells, but a profound upstream defect in antigen presentation by Dendritic cells (DCs). To overcome this, this review comprehensively outlines the translational landscape of DC-centric therapeutics in OS, tracing the paradigm shift from foundational ex vivo cellular vaccines to advanced in situ vaccination strategies. Specifically, we highlight how intelligent biomaterials and engineered endogenous vesicles bypass external manipulation to directly prime immunity within the tumor bed. Furthermore, we extensively evaluate state-of-the-art physicochemical nanomedicines—encompassing photothermal, sonodynamic, and metallo-immunologic modalities—engineered to force severe organellar stress, trigger robust immunogenic cell death, and actively reverse the spatial and metabolic paralysis of resident DCs. By coupling these interventions with targeted agents and adoptive cellular therapies, we map a synergistic roadmap for closing the cancer-immunity cycle. Despite remarkable preclinical successes, translating these ultra-complex nanoplatforms faces formidable scale-up challenges, and uncalibrated innate hyper-stimulation risks profound DC exhaustion. Moving forward, transitioning toward logic-gated, metabolically precise delivery systems is imperative to flawlessly repair the innate-adaptive immunity bridge and achieve durable OS eradication.

Introduction

Osteosarcoma (OS) is a highly aggressive primary bone cancer that affects approximately 4.8 per million people globally each year, primarily targeting children and adolescents.1,2 Etiologically traced back to early mesenchymal osteoblast precursors, this disease preferentially develops at sites of active skeletal elongation—most notably the appendicular metaphyses during youth.3 For operable cases, combining broad tumor excision with systemic cytotoxic drugs remains the gold standard, while irradiation is reserved primarily for lesions defying surgical intervention.3 However, survival rates for patients with metastatic disease, primarily presenting as intractable pulmonary lesions, have starkly plateaued over the past three decades. This dismal prognosis is intrinsically linked to the tumor’s ability to evade systemic immune surveillance. While immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) has revolutionized treatment paradigms for highly immunogenic malignancies, its translation to osteosarcoma has yielded limited clinical benefit. Real-world data and Phase II clinical trials evaluating PD-1 inhibitors, such as pembrolizumab, in advanced OS indicate objective response rates of approximately 5%.4–6 Immunologically, OS behaves similarly to other recalcitrant “cold” tumors, such as pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma and glioblastoma. Unlike inflamed tumors where effector T cells are abundant but restrained by coinhibitory receptors, OS exhibits a classic immune-desert phenotype characterized by an inherent failure at the initial priming phase. This persistent refractoriness underscores a critical biological reality: the therapeutic bottleneck in OS immunotherapy is not merely the terminal exhaustion of cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), but rather a profound upstream deficit in antigen presentation.

Dendritic cells (DCs) are the central orchestrators of the cancer-immunity cycle, serving as the indispensable bridge between innate recognition and adaptive immunity. Functionally, DCs act as the immune system’s primary professional antigen-presenting cells. They continuously patrol peripheral tissues to capture and internalize foreign or mutated proteins, including tumor-associated neoantigens. Upon processing these proteins into specific peptide fragments, DCs migrate to tumor-draining lymph nodes. There, they present these fragments on MHC molecules to naïve T cells, simultaneously delivering the requisite co-stimulatory signals (such as CD80/CD86) to trigger robust T cell activation and clonal expansion.7–9 Recent Mendelian randomization analyses have provided compelling genetic evidence establishing a direct causal relationship between specific immune cell traits—particularly DC absolute counts and activation states—and OS susceptibility and development.10 Without functional DCs to capture tumor antigens, the immune system cannot activate the T cells needed to fight the cancer. If the immunological “radar” is disabled, downstream effector cells remain blind.

Within the highly specialized bone tumor microenvironment (TME), infiltrating DCs face a multidimensional defensive fortress. They are subjected to profound spatial exclusion, paracrine immunosuppression, and stringent metabolic starvation, which collectively lock them into a tolerogenic or anergic state.11–15 To achieve durable therapeutic responses in OS, intervention strategies must pivot from solely rescuing exhausted effector cells to aggressively targeting and re-arming the DC compartment. This requires a comprehensive approach: disrupting the physical extracellular matrix (ECM) barrier, inducing robust immunogenic cell death (ICD) through organelle stress and metabolic reprogramming to supply endogenous tumor antigens, and providing precise stimulatory cues to drive DC maturation. While scattered studies have explored immune modulation in OS, a systematic synthesis focusing specifically on nanomedicine-driven DC rescue remains conspicuously lacking.

OS was specifically chosen as the focal point of this review because its unique anatomical and pathological architecture—characterized by a heavily calcified osteoid matrix and profound hypoxia—represents the ultimate physical and biological extreme of immune evasion. It is a quintessential model of a “cold” tumor where conventional immune checkpoint blockades consistently fail due to upstream DC exclusion. To systematically dismantle these multidimensional barriers, the explicit aim of this review is to delineate the translational landscape of DC-centric nanotherapeutics and microenvironment remodeling strategies in OS. We map the evolutionary paradigm shift from foundational ex vivo DC engineering toward advanced in situ vaccination strategies. Specifically, we highlight how intelligent biomaterials and physicochemical nanomedicines are engineered to inflict severe organellar stress, trigger robust ICD, and actively reverse the spatial and metabolic paralysis of resident DCs within this hostile bone niche. Ultimately, we construct a comprehensive roadmap for repairing the innate-adaptive immunity bridge and achieving durable tumor eradication.

The Multidimensional Mechanisms of DC Dysfunction in the Osteosarcoma TME

Before evaluating nanotherapeutic interventions, it is imperative to delineate the specific molecular and physiological baseline of DC dysfunction in OS. Unlike soft-tissue sarcomas, OS is uniquely fortified by a heavily calcified osteoid matrix and a pathologically cross-linked collagen network. This rigid ECM creates a profound physical barricade with exceptionally high interstitial fluid pressure.16,17 This pressure severely restricts the extravasation and interstitial motility of naive DCs, physically sequestering them at the tumor periphery and preventing them from migrating into the tumor core to capture endogenous neoantigens. Consequently, the OS core is rendered spatially “cold”, devoid of the initial innate sensing required for the cancer-immunity cycle. Beyond stalling cellular infiltration, this rigid bony enclosure and the resulting extreme interstitial fluid pressure generate a strong outward convective flow that actively repels the inward diffusion of therapeutic agents. Furthermore, unlike the compliant stroma of soft-tissue tumors that facilitates nanocarrier accumulation via the Enhanced permeability and retention effect, the dense hydroxyapatite mineral network in OS acts as a tortuous physical sieve. It directly traps or adsorbs conventional soft-polymer nanotherapeutics, drastically limiting their deep tissue penetration and underscoring the necessity for structurally optimized, TME-disruptive delivery platforms.

Even when DCs successfully infiltrate the TME, they are subjected to a barrage of suppressive paracrine signaling. OS cells constitutively overexpress potent immunosuppressive cytokines, most notably TGF-β, VEGF, and IL-10. High concentrations of VEGF directly inhibit the differentiation of CD34+ hematopoietic progenitors into mature DCs. Simultaneously, TGF-β and IL-10 downregulate the expression of MHC II and critical co-stimulatory molecules (CD80/CD86), while blocking the NF-κB signaling cascade necessary for DC maturation. Furthermore, recent single-cell RNA sequencing reveals that this paracrine milieu forces infiltrating DCs to skew toward specific regulatory subsets, such as LAMP3+ mature regulatory DCs. These corrupted DCs actively secrete chemokines (eg, CCL22) to recruit regulatory T cells (Tregs), actively building an immunosuppressive niche rather than priming CTLs.18,19

The most stringent barrier in the OS TME is its biochemical severity. Driven by the aggressive Warburg effect, OS cells consume vast amounts of glucose and excrete lactic acid via monocarboxylate transporters, dropping the extracellular pH to highly acidic levels. This profound lactic acidosis blunts the glycolytic flux within DCs, which is an absolute metabolic prerequisite for their activation and migration. Starved of glucose, DCs are forced into abnormal lipid uptake. Within the oxygen-deprived OS TME, this excessive lipid accumulation triggers massive lipid peroxidation and the generation of reactive electrophiles.14,15 This subsequent ER stress fundamentally disrupts autophagic flux and intracellular protein processing, severely truncating the cross-presentation of tumor antigens on MHC I molecules. Ultimately, these overlapping spatial, paracrine, and metabolic defects confirm that the immunological radar in OS is fundamentally disabled at the source.

Compounding these intrinsic microenvironmental barriers is the profound iatrogenic impact of standard-of-care first-line chemotherapy. While metronomic or targeted low-dose chemotherapeutics can theoretically induce immunogenic cell death, the conventional high-dose MAP regimen (Methotrexate, Adriamycin/Doxorubicin, and Cisplatin) universally administered to OS patients typically exacerbates DC paralysis. Clinically, the systemic administration of high-dose Adriamycin and Methotrexate induces severe, generalized myelosuppression, indiscriminately ablating the myeloid hematopoietic progenitors necessary to replenish both the circulating and tumor-infiltrating DC pools.20–22 Furthermore, conventional high-dose Cisplatin does not merely deplete these cells; it actively corrupts the local immune niche. Evidence demonstrates that severe Cisplatin-induced cytotoxicity skews surviving DCs toward a tolerogenic phenotype, substantially downregulating critical co-stimulatory molecules (CD80/CD86) and MHC expression. Concurrently, it drives the compensatory expansion of suppressive myeloid cells that secrete abundant IL-10, effectively locking the surviving DC compartment into a functionally paralyzed state.23 This stark clinical baseline reinforces the core thesis of this review: passive immune recovery is impossible, and active rescue strategies are absolutely mandatory to reconstruct the innate-adaptive immunity bridge following standard-of-care intervention.

The Foundational Paradigm: Ex vivo DC Engineering and Cellular Therapeutics

Historically, the initial strategy to circumvent the profoundly immunosuppressive OS TME involved extracting DCs, arming them ex vivo, and re-infusing them as cellular vaccines. While these first-generation approaches—predominantly established in the early 2000s—provided critical proof-of-concept that forced, high-quality antigen presentation could indeed break OS immune tolerance, they are encumbered by significant translational bottlenecks. The artificial uncoupling of DCs from the dynamic TME during ex vivo manipulation often compromises their in vivo homing efficacy, functional persistence, and scalability. Consequently, an objective evaluation of these foundational strategies is essential (Table 1). The imperative to bypass complex ex vivo cell engineering and address these intrinsic methodological limitations has fundamentally driven the field’s evolutionary trajectory toward the advanced, TME-targeted in situ vaccination and nanotherapeutic paradigms discussed subsequently (Figure 2). Classical Antigen Loading Modalities: From Lysates to Whole-Cell Fusion

The classical paradigm of DC vaccines relies on the ex vivo loading of tumor antigens to bypass the profound antigen presentation defects inherent to the OS TME. Early strategies utilized whole-tumor lysates to provide a broad antigenic repertoire. For instance, sensitizing DCs with cryotreated OS lysates significantly upregulates systemic interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) production and drives CD8+ T cell infiltration, effectively halting pulmonary metastasis.24 To increase antigen specificity, defined complexes such as heat shock protein 70 (Hsp70) -peptide complexes extracted from OS cells have been employed to sensitize DCs, which subsequently elicit robust, targeted CTLs responses in vitro.25 However, protein-based sensitization is inherently constrained by inefficient intracellular processing and the limitations of MHC class I cross-presentation. To overcome this, nucleic acid electroporation has emerged as a superior loading modality. Transfecting autologous DCs with allogeneic OS-derived total RNA26 or purified mRNA27 fundamentally shifts the antigen processing dynamics. This intracellular translation of tumor antigens allows for sustained MHC class I presentation, generating specific CTL responses that not only reject primary tumor challenges but also establish long-term immunological memory against subsequent OS rechallenges.26,27

Whole-cell electrofusion represents a more radical ex vivo engineering approach to maximize antigen presentation. By physically fusing OS cells with allogeneic bone marrow-derived DCs, the resulting hybrid cells intrinsically possess the complete, uncharacterized antigenic payload of the tumor cell while retaining the antigen-presenting and co-stimulatory machinery (eg, CD80/86, MHC I/II) of the DC.28,29 Intradermal administration of these irradiated DC-tumor fusion products circumvents TME spatial barriers, allowing direct migration to draining lymph nodes where they effectively prime CD4+ T helper cells and robust CD8+ CTLs.28,29 This approach yielded a remarkable survival rate and durable immunological memory, proving that allogeneic DC fusion can forcibly break immune tolerance in OS without requiring the prior identification of specific neoantigens.28 The potent T cell activation observed in these fusion models underscores the clinical viability of utilizing allogeneic DCs, which can be readily generated from healthy donors, as a universal platform for OS vaccine development.28,29

Refinement via Phenotypic Selection: Harnessing Specialized DC Subsets

While traditional ex vivo vaccines predominantly utilize monocyte-derived dendritic cells (MoDCs) due to their ease of expansion, MoDCs often exhibit limited migratory capacity and can inadvertently foster an immunosuppressive microenvironment by inducing Th2 or Treg responses. Consequently, harnessing highly specialized, functionally distinct DC subsets represents a critical evolution—and a necessary refinement—in OS immunotherapy. Type 1 conventional dendritic cells (cDC1s), specifically the tissue-resident CD103+ cDC1s in mice (functionally homologous to human CD141+ DCs), are intrinsically specialized for the cross-presentation of exogenous tumor antigens to naive CD8+ T cells.33–36 These specialized cDC1s inherently secrete high levels of T cell-activating factors, such as IL-12p70 and CXCL10, and exhibit prolonged survival and superior migration to tumor-draining lymph nodes following intratumoral or systemic administration.30 When loaded with OS antigens and stimulated with Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR3) agonists like poly I:C, CD103+ cDC1 vaccines dramatically increase the infiltration of IFN-γ-producing CD8+ T cells while favorably altering the CD8+/Treg ratio within the OS TME. Furthermore, the robust priming elicited by CD103+ cDC1s establishes a strong rationale for combination with ICB; combination with anti- cytotoxic T lymphocyte antigen-4 (CTLA-4) therapy achieved a 100% complete response rate in preclinical OS models and established sterilizing, long-term immune memory against tumor rechallenge.30,31

Beyond conventional subsets, the identification of “Killer dendritic cells” (KDCs) offers a paradigm-shifting approach by merging innate tumoricidal activity with adaptive antigen presentation. Characterized by a distinct MHC II+ CD103+ CD11b+ NKp46- phenotype—which fundamentally distinguishes them from natural killer cells—these KDCs possess the unique capacity to induce rapid, caspase-independent apoptosis in OS cells.32 Immediately following target cell lysis, KDCs autonomously phagocytose the apoptotic tumor bodies, seamlessly transitioning from innate effectors to professional antigen-presenting cells.32 This autonomous “kill-and-engulf” mechanism circumvents the need for artificial ex vivo antigen loading, allowing the KDCs to process a vast, patient-specific array of unmanipulated endogenous OS antigens. This therapeutic effect is strictly dependent on CD8+ T cells, confirming that KDCs do not merely act as direct cytotoxic effectors but are highly proficient at cross-presenting self-acquired tumor antigens to prime a durable adaptive CTL response. Exploiting such dual-functioning DC subsets offers a highly potent, self-contained cellular platform for dismantling OS immune tolerance.32

The in situ Paradigm: Harnessing Endogenous Antigens for Intratumoral DC Priming

While ex vivo DC manipulation provided the theoretical foundation for OS immunotherapy, the imperative to overcome the logistical complexities, poor in vivo homing, and phenotypic instability of artificially cultured cells has catalyzed the development of in situ vaccination strategies. Rather than extracting the immune system to face the tumor, this strategy converts the tumor into its own endogenous vaccine factory. By inducing localized ICD, in situ interventions release a patient-specific repertoire of uncharacterized neoantigens while simultaneously delivering potent adjuvant cues. This orchestrates the entire cancer-immunity cycle directly within the tumor bed—recruiting, maturing, and activating resident DCs to repair the innate-adaptive immunity bridge without the need for fragile external cell engineering (Table 2). Direct Intratumoral Modulation: Activating the Innate Compartment via Soluble Agonists and Biologicals

The most straightforward approach to in situ vaccination involves the direct intratumoral administration of potent immunostimulatory agents designed to rapidly reverse the cold, immunosuppressive OS TME. TLR agonists are prime candidates for this strategy due to their potent capacity to activate the innate immune compartment.44 Intralesional injection of the TLR9 agonist SD101, for example, triggers a massive reprogramming of the OS immune landscape.37 Specifically, in preclinical models, this requires the precise administration of SD101 as a saline-formulated aqueous solution at a strictly calibrated dose of 25–50 μg (injected twice a week for a total of four doses). Such specific dosage formulations and tight volume restrictions are mandatory requirements for intratumoral injection in OS to prevent capsular rupture against the pathologically high interstitial fluid pressure of the calcified matrix. This intervention significantly reduces the prevalence of pro-tumorigenic M2-like macrophages while driving a conspicuous infiltration and maturation of DCs. Crucially, this robust local DC activation successfully cross-presents endogenous tumor antigens, leading to the recruitment and expansion of highly activated, granzyme B/IFN-γ-secreting CD8+ T cells.37 Notably, TLR9 engagement acts as a potent in situ vaccine that not only halts the growth of the directly treated lesion but also exerts a robust systemic “abscopal effect”. T cell receptor sequencing has confirmed that tumor-specific CD8+ T cell clones expanded in the treated primary tumor systemically recirculate to eradicate distant, untreated contralateral lesions. This confirms that localized innate immune stimulation can successfully rewire the systemic adaptive T cell clonal architecture in OS without requiring exogenous antigen loading.37

Beyond molecular agonists, live bacteria-mediated cancer therapy represents an advanced biological modality for direct intratumoral modulation, leveraging the natural tumor-targeting tropism of specific strains.45,46 However, recognizing that the severely hypoxic and immunosuppressive OS TME often limits the durability of bacterial monotherapy, researchers have engineered sophisticated strains to proactively dictate the immune response. A prime example is the genetically engineered Salmonella typhimurium strain VNP20009-CCL2-CXCL9 (VNP-C-C).38 Upon accumulating within OS lung metastases, this strain not only induces direct oncolytic ICD but also actively secretes the chemokines CCL2 and CXCL9. This engineered chemokine gradient acts as a powerful localized signal, actively recruiting circulating DCs and macrophages into the hostile TME. Furthermore, VNP-C-C infection triggers the cyclic GMP-AMP synthase (cGAS)/stimulator of interferon genes (STING) pathway within the tumor bed, promoting robust type I interferon secretion.38 This rational combination combination of ICD, cGAS/STING-driven type I interferon production, and active DC recruitment via CCL2/CXCL9 effectively reprograms the TME into a pro-inflammatory milieu. Consequently, this leads to extensive DC maturation, massive CD8+ T cell infiltration, and the establishment of systemic T cell memory in peripheral lymphoid organs, ultimately providing significant survival benefits in highly aggressive OS lung metastasis models, specifically the syngeneic K7M2 experimental metastasis model established via tail-vein injection in immunocompetent BALB/c mice.38

Advanced Delivery Modalities: Integrating Biomaterial Platforms with Endogenous Vesicles

While direct intratumoral injection of soluble immunomodulators provides immediate innate activation, these free agents are inherently plagued by rapid systemic clearance, off-target toxicity, and an inability to temporally synchronize antigen release with adjuvant presentation. To spatially confine immunomodulation and precisely choreograph the cancer-immunity cycle, biomaterial-assisted delivery systems have emerged as an indispensable translational bridge. By exploiting the aberrant physicochemical properties of the OS TME—specifically its pronounced acidity and elevated protease levels—nanocarriers can be engineered to execute spatiotemporally controlled in situ vaccination.

A foundational approach leverages the inherent affinity of certain biomaterials for the skeletal niche. When addressing the unique physical and chemical barriers of the OS TME, different nanocarrier compositions offer distinct, yet complementary, advantages. For instance, bioinspired calcium phosphonate nanoagents, synthesized using zoledronic acid and a bovine serum albumin scaffold, exhibit exceptional bone-seeking properties and prolonged tumor retention that soft-polymer systems often lack. When co-loaded with the chemotherapeutic agent methotrexate (MTX) and the TLR9 agonist CpG (CpG-MTX@BSA-CaZol), this system undergoes rapid disassembly in the acidic OS TME.39 The localized release of MTX forces aggressive ICD, providing a massive influx of endogenous tumor-associated antigens, which are immediately cross-presented by DCs matured via the co-released CpG. This synchronized chemo-immunotherapy significantly expands the intratumoral CD8+ T cell pool and establishes a robust systemic immune memory that effectively abrogates pulmonary metastasis.39 A parallel pH-responsive strategy utilizes the immune-modulating polymer PEG-PC7A to co-deliver doxorubicin (DOX), the TLR4 agonist monophosphoryl lipid A, and a PD-L1-targeting peptide. This multi-component architecture enforces a dual activation of the STING and TLR4 pathways in DCs, seamlessly coupling ICD-driven antigen supply with localized ICB to reprogram the hostile TME.40

Advancing beyond nanoscale encapsulation, biomineralization has been ingeniously applied to whole tumor cells to create structurally integrated, multimodal vaccines (Cell@CaP) specifically designed to prevent postsurgical relapse. In this paradigm, inactivated autologous OS cells are coated with a rigid calcium phosphate shell encapsulating the STING agonist cGAMP.41 Technically, this biomineralization process utilizes a precise, sequential in situ coprecipitation strategy. First, Ca2⁺ ions are attracted to the negative charges and acidic residues naturally present on the inactivated tumor cell surface, forming endogenous nucleation sites. Subsequently, the introduction of the adjuvant cGAMP—which uniquely contains double phosphate groups—acts as the phosphate source to drive the uniform crystallization of an amorphous calcium phosphate (CaP) shell directly around the cell. Unlike passive chemical delivery, this mineralized vaccine introduces a critical mechanobiological dimension. Upon interaction with host DCs, this mineralized vaccine exerts a distinct mechanobiological effect. The enhanced mechanical hardness of the Cell@CaP shell physically triggers the Piezo1 mechanosensitive channels on the DC membrane, inducing a rapid influx of extracellular Ca2+. This mechanical stimulation, synergizing with the intracellular release of cGAMP and intact tumor antigens, simultaneously activates the PI3K-Akt, RhoA-MYPT1, and cGAS-STING signaling cascades. This multimodal regulation radically enhances DC cytoskeletal remodeling, migration, and the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IFN-β and TNF-α), offering a formidable prophylactic barrier against residual tumor recurrence when combined with localized radiotherapy.41

A conceptually distinct, yet equally potent, frontier in in situ vaccination involves forcing the tumor itself to manufacture and release highly immunogenic autologous vaccines in the form of tumor extracellular vesicles (TEVs). Naturally secreted TEVs in OS are typically laden with immunosuppressive “don’t eat me” signals, such as PD-L1 and CD47.47–50 To subvert this, nanotherapeutics (eg, Combo-NPs@shGNE) have been engineered to silence UDP-N-acetylglucosamine 2-epimerase/N-acetylmannosamine kinase, a critical enzyme in sialic acid biosynthesis. By metabolically starving the tumor of sialic acid and inducing targeted necroptosis, this intervention fundamentally alters the cargo sorting of TEVs.42 The tumor is forced to shed massive quantities of vesicles that are stripped of PD-L1 and CD47 but heavily enriched with danger signals like HSP70 and HMGB1. These engineered endogenous vesicles serve as highly potent, self-assembled in situ vaccines that avidly bind and mature DCs, thereby bypassing immune evasion without the need to synthesize or administer exogenous antigens.42 However, despite their immense preclinical promise, relying on tumor-derived vesicles introduces significant translational hurdles. The inherent biological heterogeneity of TEVs severely complicates standardizing Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Control for clinical scale-up. Furthermore, there remains a persistent risk that these artificially induced vesicles might still harbor unidentified oncogenic miRNAs or residual immunosuppressive proteins, necessitating rigorous molecular profiling and purification protocols before clinical application.

Furthermore, combining tumor-derived vesicles with advanced macro-scale biomaterials can yield sophisticated hybrid delivery systems capable of multi-tiered TME reprogramming. A prominent example is the “SMART-DDS” platform, which consists of gelatin methacryloy microspheres loaded with a TIM-3 blocking antibody, covalently tethered via an MMP2-cleavable peptide to OS-derived vesicles carrying the EZH2 inhibitor EPZ6438.43 The inherently high MMP2 concentration in the OS TME selectively cleaves the peptide tethers, releasing the EZH2 inhibitor-loaded vesicles to penetrate deep into the tumor parenchyma to induce apoptosis and force macrophage polarization toward the tumoricidal M1 phenotype. Concurrently, the sustained release of anti-TIM-3 from the hydrogel microspheres neutralizes TIM-3 receptors heavily expressed on tumor-associated DCs. Because TIM-3 normally sequesters HMGB1 and suppresses nucleic acid sensing, its targeted blockade vigorously rescues DC maturation and cross-presentation, perfectly complementing the vesicle-induced ICD to orchestrate a comprehensive innate and adaptive immune attack.43

The Physicochemical Paradigm: Nanomedicine-Driven TME Disruption for DC Rescue

While the previous section highlighted the utility of biomaterials as spatiotemporal vehicles, relying solely on chemical adjuvants and passive delivery is often insufficient to overcome the severely hypoxic, physically dense, and metabolically aberrant OS TME. To effectively re-arm the DC compartment under these extreme conditions, profound physicochemical disruption is required. In this context, advanced nanomedicines act as active energy-converting transducers. By harnessing external energy fields (eg, light, ultrasound, magnetism), these nanomaterials inflict severe organellar stress on OS cells, forcing massive ICD (Figure 3). Mechanistically, this strategy shifts the therapeutic focus from direct tumor ablation to utilizing the dying tumor as a massive source of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and neoantigens (Table 3). This active disruption is specifically calibrated to reverse tumor-induced DC paralysis, drive DC phagocytosis, and reprogram their metabolic and autophagic circuits when biological cues alone fail. Photothermal and Photodynamic Modalities: Engineering the Light-Immunity Axis for DC Priming

Photothermal therapy (PTT) utilizes near-infrared (NIR) irradiation not merely for thermal ablation, but as a precise instrument to physically and biologically rescue DC function in the OS TME. Spatially, the dense ECM of OS acts as a formidable physical barricade, profoundly restricting the spatial infiltration of immature DCs and the subsequent entry of effector T cells. To breach this barrier, mesoporous polydopamine nanoparticles encapsulating the proteolytic enzyme bromelain and the TLR7/8 agonist R848 were developed.51 Upon NIR irradiation, localized hyperthermia concurrently induces ICD in tumor cells and activates the heat-sensitive bromelain to actively digest the dense ECM. This simultaneous physical clearance of the ECM and TLR7/8-driven DC maturation unleashes a massive influx of immune cells, directly solving the spatial exclusion of DCs in OS.51

Mild PTT can be strategically coupled with epigenetic and metabolic modulation to force DC activation. A bimetallic polyphenol-based nanoplatform (Mn/Fe-Gallate coated with tumor cell membranes) utilizes mild PTT to actively drive murine LM8 OS cells into pyroptosis—a highly inflammatory programmed cell death. Rather than passively waiting for DC recognition, this intervention actively floods the TME with pyroptosis-derived DAMPs while the released metal ions directly activate the cGAS-STING pathway within local DCs. This synchronized molecular assault actively transitions DCs from a tolerogenic to a highly activated state, culminating in robust systemic antitumor immunity.52 Nevertheless, the translational viability of such tumor-membrane-camouflaged nanoplatforms must be critically evaluated. Securing sufficient quantities of autologous tumor membranes from patients is logistically challenging, and preventing the denaturation of critical membrane proteins during the extrusion and coating processes remains a formidable manufacturing barrier. To further maximize this innate-adaptive crosstalk, a titanium carbide MXene nanoplatform functionalized with Mn2+ and ovalbumin (OVA) was designed to explicitly exploit heat-induced organelle damage. NIR irradiation forces the shedding of cytosolic and extracellular mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) from dying OS cells. This heat-liberated mtDNA, acting in concert with the released Mn2+, serves as a potent endogenous danger signal that hyper-activates the cGAS-STING pathway directly inside DCs, profoundly enhancing their antigen cross-presentation capacity and reversing the metabolic exhaustion typical of the OS TME.53 Advancing this thermal-immune axis to its most sophisticated applications, NIR-II thermal-controllable nanoplatforms (BF/pHCN) have been engineered to achieve spatiotemporal control over in vivo genome editing. By using heat to activate a CRISPR/Cas9 plasmid that knocks out Nrf2 (the master transcription factor for oxidative homeostasis), this system forces OS cells into an amplified state of ferroptosis. This extreme, thermally programmed ferroptosis acts as a massive ICD trigger, fundamentally maturing DCs and igniting a robust, T cell-mediated adaptive immune response that eradicates the tumor and prevents postsurgical relapse.54

Parallel to PTT, photodynamic therapy (PDT) utilizes light-activated photosensitizers to generate a massive, localized surge of reactive oxygen species (ROS), severely damaging OS organelles and generating the highly immunogenic debris required for DC engulfment. However, the necrotic OS TME often exerts a basal inhibitory effect on DC function. PDT uniquely circumvents this; early molecular profiling demonstrated that PDT treatment of OS cells specifically upregulates HSP70, which biochemically rescues DCs from tumor-induced suppression and restores their antigen-processing functionality.64 To overcome the hypoxic limitations of traditional PDT and further guarantee DC activation, precision-engineered metal-photosensitizer nanocomplexes, such as Mn2+-pyrochloric acid, have been developed. Here, the PDT-generated ROS directly forces OS cell death, while the co-delivered Mn2+ acts as a potent intracellular signaling orchestrator, directly engaging the cGAS-STING pathway in DCs to ensure their complete maturation.55 Crucially, PDT-induced ROS can be leveraged to potently drive DC phagocytosis when combined with innate immune checkpoints. A dual pH-sensitive nanocarrier co-delivering the photosensitizer Chlorin e6 and an anti-CD47 antibody exemplifies this targeted approach. The intense ROS generated by Chlorin e6 drives profound ICD, exposing calreticulin on the OS cell surface. Simultaneously, the antibody blocks the “don’t eat me” signal CD47. This dual-action mechanism leaves the ROS-damaged OS cells fully vulnerable, forcing DCs and macrophages to aggressively phagocytose the tumor debris and execute robust antigen cross-presentation.56

Sonodynamic, Radiotherapy, and Magnetic Hyperthermia: Overcoming Depth Limitations for DC Priming

While optical modalities provide spatial precision, their limited tissue penetration restricts their application in deep-seated OS lesions. Modalities utilizing highly penetrant physical energy—ultrasound, ionizing radiation, and alternating magnetic fields—are therefore clinically necessary. Crucially, their therapeutic utility extends beyond direct tumor ablation; they are engineered to overcome the immunosuppressive TME and rescue DC antigen presentation.

Sonodynamic therapy (SDT) employs ultrasound to generate intracellular ROS deep within tissues. However, the efficacy of SDT-induced ICD is often compromised by the hypoxic, lactate-rich OS TME, which inherently suppresses DC activity. To address this metabolic barrier, a sono-immune nanoplatform (SHK@Mn-TiO2) was developed to couple SDT with glycolysis inhibition.57 By delivering shikonin to inhibit pyruvate kinase M2 and utilizing Mn2+ to suppress hexokinase-2, this system reduces localized lactate accumulation and hypoxia 44. This metabolic reprogramming relieves the biochemical suppression on infiltrating DCs. Consequently, when ultrasound triggers ROS-mediated ICD, the liberated DCs—further stimulated by Mn2+-driven cGAS-STING activation—exhibit significantly enhanced maturation and cross-presentation, establishing robust immune memory against OS rechallenge.57

In clinical OS management, conventional high-dose radiotherapy severely damages the local immune microenvironment, depleting resident DCs. To establish a low-dose, immune-sparing paradigm, gadolinium-based metal-bisphosphonate nanoparticles (OVA-GdZol) were synthesized 33. Utilizing the bone-seeking properties of zoledronic acid, these nanoagents accumulate in the OS niche, where gadolinium functions as a high-Z radiosensitizer.65–68 Under low-dose X-ray irradiation, they selectively amplify localized DNA damage. Rather than serving solely as physical dose-enhancers, these nanoparticles act as active immunologic adjuvants, promoting the maturation of bone marrow-derived DCs and driving macrophage M1 polarization, thereby bridging low-toxicity radiotherapy with the priming of systemic adaptive immunity.58

Magnetic hyperthermia therapy introduces a thermophysical dimension to DC priming. To simultaneously eradicate deep OS lesions and address post-surgical bone defects, biodegradable magnesium (Mg) macroscale rods have been employed as eddy thermo-magnetic agents.59 Under a low external alternating magnetic field, these rods generate a potent eddy thermal effect. While the uneven heating profile of macroscopic eddy thermal effect was historically viewed as a clinical limitation, recent immunological profiling reveals that this thermal gradient generates a comprehensive spectrum of ICD across the tumor parenchyma. This varied release of DAMPs profoundly enhances systemic DC maturation and subsequent T cell infiltration. Combined with ICB, Mg rod-mediated magnetic hyperthermia therapy effectively dismantles OS immune tolerance, while the gradual degradation of Mg actively promotes osteogenesis.59

Metalloimmunotherapy and Autophagy Regulation: Biochemical Modulation of DC Signaling

Beyond macroscopic physical energy, manipulating the intracellular biochemical milieu via transition metals and autophagy modulators provides a subcellular strategy to rescue DC function. Metalloimmunotherapy leverages specific metal ions not merely as structural nanocarrier components, but as direct pharmacological adjuvants that interface with innate immune signaling pathways.

The cGAS-STING pathway is a critical cytosolic DNA-sensing mechanism essential for DC maturation.69,70 However, the basal activation of STING in the OS TME is typically suppressed. Manganese ions (Mn2⁺) have emerged as potent metallic sensitizers to overcome this suppression.71 For instance, a ROS-responsive nanoparticle (NP3) was designed to co-deliver cisplatin, curcumin, and Mn2⁺. Inside the tumor, cisplatin and curcumin induce severe DNA damage, generating cytosolic DNA debris. Crucially, the co-released Mn2⁺ functions as a direct biochemical adjuvant, amplifying the sensitivity of the cGAS-STING machinery to this cytosolic DNA. This metallic intervention explicitly forces DC maturation and drastically diminishes the infiltration of immunosuppressive cells.60

This Mn2⁺-driven STING activation can be highly synergistic with the regulation of tumor autophagy. A dual-functional metal nanoplatform (MnSx) exploits this by generating intracellular H2S to induce the S-sulfhydration of USP8, a process that forces the mitochondrial-lysosomal fusion, triggering profound mitochondrial autophagy in OS cells. Concurrently, the released Mn2⁺ amplifies the cGAS-STING autophagic response and directly acts on local DCs to enforce their maturation, seamlessly linking targeted autophagic cell death with CTL activation.61 Beyond manganese, modulating local ion dynamics with other metals also reverses immune suppression. An oxaliplatin-based Pt(IV) prodrug amphiphile functionalized with bone-targeting alendronate (ALN-OXA) utilizes superior Ca2⁺ trapping to enhance intracellular platinum accumulation. This targeted disruption fundamentally reprograms the TME, leading to observable DC maturation and the repolarization of macrophages to the M1 phenotype.62

Furthermore, precise pharmacological control over the autophagic process is a proven inducer of ICD, providing the necessary antigenic substrates for DC cross-presentation. To exploit this, an intelligent pH-sensitive metal-organic framework (CBZP) was engineered to deliver the natural autophagy-enhancer curcumin alongside a small-molecule PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor (BMS1166).63 The targeted release of curcumin within the acidic TME triggers specific autophagic cell death, exposing high-quality tumor antigens. By immediately coupling this autophagic ICD with local checkpoint blockade, the nanocarrier ensures that the actively phagocytosing DCs successfully prime CD8+ T cells without being restrained by the PD-1/PD-L1 axis, establishing durable protection against OS recurrence.63

Key Bottlenecks in the Clinical Translation of Nanoplatforms and Potential Solutions

While the physicochemical nanotherapeutic strategies described herein demonstrate remarkable potency in preclinical OS models, their path to clinical translation faces three formidable bottlenecks. First, the complexity of multicomponent systems presents a major Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Control challenge. Unlike single-agent drugs, the precise co-assembly, stability, and batch-to-batch reproducibility of multi-functional nanocarriers (eg, those integrating transition metals, photothermal agents, and nucleic acids) are difficult to standardize, potentially complicating regulatory approval. Potential solutions include prioritizing “degradable-by-design” frameworks that utilize modular, well-characterized components rather than ultra-complex, multi-stage assemblies.

Second, the physiological disparity between murine models and human pediatric patients is pronounced. Small-animal OS models often lack the extreme intratumoral heterogeneity and pulmonary metastatic kinetics found in human patients. To bridge this gap, translational pipelines should adopt more clinically relevant models, such as patient-derived xenografts or microfluidic “organ-on-a-chip” platforms, which better replicate the human tumor microenvironment for dose-escalation studies.

Third, regulatory and safety hurdles regarding long-term toxicity are critical. Many of the metallic-based nanoplatforms discussed require rigorous longitudinal studies to assess systemic clearance profiles and cumulative organ toxicity. The transition toward “smart”, logic-gated platforms—designed to remain inactive until triggered by tumor-specific markers (eg, pH, redox potential, or overexpression of enzymes)—is essential to minimize off-target accumulation, thereby enhancing the clinical safety profile of these potent DC-centric immunotherapies.

The Synergistic Paradigm: Closing the Immunity Loop to Sustain DC-Driven Responses

While the physicochemical and in situ paradigms excel at initiating DC priming, the ultimate success of OS immunotherapy hinges not only on successful antigen presentation but also on the survival and sustained functionality of the downstream effector T cells. Even if DCs are robustly activated via the aforementioned nanotherapeutic or biological modalities, the resulting CTLs frequently encounter an immunosuppressive gauntlet within the TME. This hostile niche is characterized by the upregulation of inhibitory immune checkpoints, metabolic starvation, and the dense infiltration of Tregs. Consequently, activating DCs in isolation is insufficient. Combining DC-targeted interventions with ICB or targeted inhibitors is not merely an additive approach, but a biological necessity to fully close the cancer-immunity cycle and translate transient innate activation into durable tumor eradication (Table 4). Immune Checkpoint Blockade: Releasing the Brakes on DC-Primed Effector T Cells

The interaction between DCs and T cells is tightly regulated by a complex network of co-stimulatory and co-inhibitory receptors. The inhibitory receptor CTLA-4 is a primary checkpoint that competitively binds to CD80/CD86 on DCs, thereby truncating the initial priming phase of T cells.82 To overcome this early-stage suppression, classical ex vivo vaccination strategies utilizing tumor lysate-pulsed DCs have been combined with anti-CTLA-4 antibodies. In highly aggressive, syngeneic models (such as the LM8 subcutaneous tumor model in immunocompetent C3H mice), this specific combination significantly reduces the frequency of Tregs in both the spleen and the metastatic niche, driving a massive expansion of CD8+ T cells and preventing the outgrowth of fatal lung metastases.72

Beyond blocking inhibitory signals, engaging agonistic checkpoints offers a parallel strategy to amplify DC-mediated responses. Glucocorticoid-induced tumor necrosis factor receptor is constitutively expressed on Tregs and upregulated on activated effector T cells.83 Combining tumor lysate-pulsed DCs with an agonist anti-glucocorticoid-induced tumor necrosis factor receptor antibody serves a dual purpose: it directly provides co-stimulation to the DC-primed CD8+ T cells while simultaneously eliminating or destabilizing the immunosuppressive Treg population. This precise manipulation of the TME immune composition significantly prolongs survival and elevates systemic IFN-γ levels, proving that stimulating T cell checkpoints can potently rescue and sustain DC vaccine efficacy in OS.73

At the effector phase within the tumor bed, the PD-1 axis remains the most formidable barrier. However, OS is notoriously resistant to anti-PD-1 monotherapy, largely due to its “immune-desert” phenotype and the lack of robust DC-T cell crosstalk.4,84–86 Recent studies have identified that the c-Myc oncogene actively contributes to this immune exclusion. Pharmacological inhibition of c-Myc using the inhibitor JQ-1 fundamentally alters the immunological landscape. Crucially, JQ-1 intervention actively facilitates the physical crosstalk between antigen-presenting DCs and infiltrating T cells by upregulating the CD40/CD40L co-stimulatory pathway. Consequently, when JQ-1 is co-administered with anti-PD-1 antibodies, the reinvigorated DC-driven CD40 signaling perfectly synergizes with the blockade of T cell exhaustion, orchestrating a potent dual-pronged assault that results in profound regression of established OS lesions.74

Chemo-Immunomodulation and Targeted Therapies: Conditioning the Niche for DC Efficacy

While classical chemotherapy is often viewed as immunosuppressive, specific cytotoxic agents are potent inducers of ICD, making them invaluable partners for DC-centric therapies. DOX is a prime example. In vitro and in vivo profiling demonstrates that low-dose DOX treatment of OS cells induces robust exposure of calreticulin and the release of HMGB1 and HSP70. Upon sensing these specific DAMPs, local DCs undergo robust NF-κB activation, which dramatically enhances their antigen-presenting capacity and elicits systemic, tumor-specific CTL responses.87 To optimize this synergy in a clinical context, highly integrated nanoplatforms (eg, CCHANPDOX) have been developed to co-deliver DOX and cisplatin directly to the OS niche. This localized, chemo-induced ICD provides a massive supply of high-quality antigens that forces DC maturation in situ, effectively reconstructing the immunogenicity of the TME. Crucially, this chemotherapy-driven DC activation profoundly sensitizes previously refractory OS tumors to subsequent anti-PD-1 blockade, establishing a comprehensive immunochemotherapeutic regimen.75

Beyond classical cytotoxics, small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors targeting angiogenesis are being repurposed as potent immunomodulators to clear the path for DC-driven therapies. Apatinib, a highly selective VEGFR2 inhibitor, acts as a profound TME remodeler. Crucially, when combined with antigen-specific DC-induced T cell therapy, apatinib exerts a superior synergistic oncolytic effect compared to either intervention alone. Mechanistically, apatinib efficiently inhibits the expression of pro-angiogenic mediators—specifically VEGF, IL-6, and IL-17—while significantly boosting IFN-γ production. This targeted intervention drastically reduces the accumulation of myeloid-derived suppressor cell and favorably enhances the ratio of M1 tumor-associated macrophages. Ultimately, this pharmacological preconditioning actively relieves the severe immunosuppression within the OS microenvironment, creating a highly permissive niche that allows the adoptively transferred, DC-primed T cells to maximize their therapeutic efficacy.76 Similarly, sunitinib (a multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor) exerts direct immunomodulatory activity. Preclinical in vitro models reveal that sunitinib-treated human OS cells (such as the SaOS-2 cell line) actively drive human MoDCs toward complete maturation. Remarkably, sunitinib treatment substantially impairs the ability of these human OS cells to induce Tregs via DCs. Furthermore, because sunitinib upregulates PD-L1 on OS cells and PD-1 on effector T cells, it establishes a strong biological rationale—and demonstrates profound synergy—when combined with anti-PD-1 blockade (nivolumab) following DC-based cross-priming.88 Other immunosuppressive factors, such as TGF-β, actively paralyze DC function and promote Treg expansion. Neutralizing TGF-β with targeted antibodies during DC vaccination fundamentally alters this balance, unleashing systemic CTL responses and inhibiting metastatic growth.77

Perhaps the most sophisticated targeted approach to conditioning the TME for synergistic immunotherapy involves forcing the in situ differentiation of suppressive myeloid cells directly into functional DCs. Tumor-associated inflammatory monocytes (TAIMs) in the OS TME typically exhibit a profound immunosuppressive phenotype that inherently blunts T-cell responses. However, targeted carbohydrate ligands can actively reprogram this barrier. The oat-derived β-glucan ligand BG34-200 has been discovered to bind selectively and multivalently to the I-domain of the integrin CD11b on TAIMs.78,89,90 This precise biophysical engagement triggers rapid endocytosis, F-actin cytoskeletal rearrangement, and the clustering of ICAM-1. These profound structural and metabolic changes force the robust differentiation of immunosuppressive TAIMs into highly competent MoDCs.78 By converting the tumor’s own myeloid infiltrate into an army of antigen-presenting cells, BG34-200 potently drives T-cell activation directly within the TME. Establishes the mandatory immunological substrate for combinatorial success. By actively reprogramming the suppressive myeloid infiltrate into functional antigen-presenting cells, it supplies the requisite cross-priming machinery that downstream T-cell therapies strictly depend on to exert their effect.78

Adoptive Cell Therapy Crosstalk: Bidirectional Reinforcement Between DCs and Engineered T Cells

Adoptive cell therapies—including cytokine-induced killer (CIK) cells, chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cells, and T cell receptor-T cells—have demonstrated revolutionary efficacy in hematological malignancies. However, their application in OS is severely hindered by the hostile TME, which frequently induces rapid exhaustion and hypofunction of the transferred cells. To overcome this, emerging strategies leverage the bidirectional biological crosstalk between engineered T cells and the DC compartment, establishing a mutually reinforcing loop of immune activation.

Primarily, DCs can be utilized as in vivo cellular boosters to sustain and direct the infused effector cells. For instance, the specific cytolytic capacity of autologous CIK cells against OS is drastically amplified (over 6-fold) when they are pre-incubated with DCs pulsed with tumor-derived total RNA. This indicates that even innate-like killer cells depend on professional antigen presentation to maximize their targeted cytotoxicity.79 This helper function is even more critical for engineered CAR-T cells. A major mechanism of tumor immune evasion involves the shedding of soluble NKG2D ligands, which competitively block CAR receptors and paralyze NKG2D-CAR-T cells. To counteract this, researchers developed an advanced cellular vaccine utilizing mature DCs armored with the lentivirus-mediated expression of RAE1 (a potent NKG2D ligand).80 When administered alongside NKG2D-CAR-T cells, these RAE1-armored DCs act as highly resistant “charging stations”. They provide sustained, membrane-bound ligand stimulation that cannot be neutralized by the TME, thereby significantly enhancing CAR-T cell proliferation, triggering massive localized granzyme B release, and crucially, driving the CAR-T cells toward a long-term central memory phenotype.80

Conversely, the interaction is not unidirectional; engineered T cells can be strategically weaponized to rescue the intrinsically suppressed DC compartment within the OS TME. A major hurdle in OS immunotherapy is achieving high local concentrations of DC-maturation cytokines, such as IL-12, without triggering lethal systemic toxicity. To safely bridge this gap, cell membrane-anchored and tumor-targeted IL-12 T cells were engineered.81 Upon systemic infusion, these cells precisely home to the OS tumor bed by targeting cell-surface vimentin. Once anchored, the localized release of IFN-γ and the high local concentration of membrane-bound IL-12 vigorously stimulate the maturation of resident, previously hypofunctional DCs. This robust in situ DC activation is transformative: it initiates secondary T cell responses against uncharacterized endogenous neoantigen. Consequently, this bidirectional strategy not only eradicates large, heterogeneous OS lesions but effectively establishes systemic immunity while minimizing the risk of the peripheral cytokine storm typically associated with free IL-12 administration.81

Discussion

The evolutionary trajectory of OS immunotherapy has crystallized a fundamental biological consensus: relying solely on the rescue of exhausted effector T cells is fundamentally inadequate if the upstream immunological radar remains disabled. The paradigm shift from early ex vivo cellular engineering to sophisticated in situ nanotherapeutics and synergistic immunomodulation underscores a collective realization: the hostile OS TME must be actively dismantled rather than merely bypassed. We have moved beyond asking whether DCs can be activated in OS, to understanding how to spatiotemporally orchestrate their recruitment, metabolic rescue, and cross-presentation capabilities within an intensely immunosuppressive, calcified niche.

However, while restoring DC function is an indispensable prerequisite, it is imperative to maintain scientific neutrality and acknowledge that OS immune evasion is highly multifactorial. The focus on DC dysfunction must not overshadow alternative mechanisms, most notably intrinsic tumor antigenicity. Pediatric OS is notoriously characterized by a low tumor mutational burden, which fundamentally limits the availability of high-affinity neoantigens compared to highly mutated carcinomas.91,92 Consequently, even with fully re-armed DCs, the inherent scarcity of quality immunological targets remains a formidable barrier. Furthermore, the OS stroma actively orchestrates broader immune exclusion mechanisms independent of DC status. The dense recruitment of myeloid-derived suppressor cells and cancer-associated fibroblasts, coupled with chaotic neovascularization, creates an independently hostile niche that can physically deflect or biochemically paralyze downstream effector T cells despite successful upstream priming.93–95 Therefore, DC-centric nanotherapeutics should not be viewed as an isolated panacea, but rather as the critical foundational anchor of a multimodal strategy that must concurrently address intrinsic antigenicity and global stromal immunosuppression.

Despite elegant preclinical designs, a sobering translational gap persists, punctuated by historical translational failures and often-underreported negative data. While the vast majority of published literature leans heavily toward successful complete responses in rapidly proliferating murine OS models, these “positive” findings frequently collapse in human applications. For instance, several advanced liposomal and nanoparticle-based formulations that effectively eradicated murine sarcomas have failed to demonstrate statistically significant overall survival benefits in pediatric clinical trials. These failures stem largely from the inability of subcutaneous murine models to recapitulate the chronic hypoxic evolution, the extreme physical density of the osteoid matrix, and the profound spatial heterogeneity characteristic of human OS.

Furthermore, conflicting data within the field challenge the prevailing nanotherapeutic rationale. While current trends champion highly complex, multi-component “all-in-one” nanoplatforms to maximize ICD and adjuvant delivery, emerging negative results indicate a biological paradox: the chronic, uncalibrated hyper-activation of innate pathways (such as sustained STING or TLR stimulation) can trigger compensatory immune tolerance. Rather than driving durable CTL responses, conflicting studies demonstrate that uncalibrated stimulation frequently induces the massive upregulation of PD-L1, expansion of regulatory populations, and profound DC exhaustion. Acknowledging these conflicting outcomes and translational failures is essential; more stimulation does not universally equate to better immunity, and a critical, unbiased evaluation of negative data is imperative for guiding future rational design.

Conclusion and Future Perspectives

The next major breakthrough in OS immunotherapy will not stem from simply packing more therapeutic agents into a single nanocarrier, but from mastering temporal kinetics and metabolic precision. Future research must pivot toward logic-gated, sequential delivery systems. To prevent premature adjuvant release and severe systemic toxicity, next-generation nanoplatforms should be engineered using Boolean logic (eg, “AND” gates). For instance, a smart nanocarrier could be designed to degrade and release its cargo only if it simultaneously encounters the high-density hydroxyapatite matrix specific to bone tissue AND the extreme lactic acidosis specific to the OS TME. Because the cancer-immunity cycle is inherently chronological, these programmable interventions must mimic natural physiological responses—delivering an initial burst of targeted ICD to liberate tumor antigens, followed by a precisely timed, delayed release of DC adjuvants only after immature DCs have successfully infiltrated the debris field.7,96,97

Moreover, addressing DC dysfunction must transition from generic inflammatory stimulation to targeted metabolic rescue. Utilizing advanced spatial transcriptomics and single-cell metabolic profiling to precisely map the nutritional deprivation of DCs within distinct spatial zones of the OS TME (eg, necrotic core vs. invasive margin) will be crucial.2 If DCs in the OS core are paralyzed by lipid peroxidation or excessive lactate, simply applying a TLR agonist will be futile. The future lies in metabolically precise nanoplatforms that actively neutralize biochemical suppressors before initiating immune stimulation. This could involve co-delivering enzymes like lactate oxidase to reverse localized acidosis, or ROS scavengers to relieve endoplasmic reticulum stress, thereby perfectly conditioning the intracellular environment before the innate immune agonists are released.

In conclusion, the therapeutic stagnation in metastatic OS is fundamentally rooted in a silenced innate immunological radar. To break this decades-long clinical plateau, the oncology community must abandon the futile pursuit of activating downstream effectors in an antigen-deprived vacuum. Re-arming the DC compartment is not merely a synergistic option; it is the biological linchpin of durable immunity. Only by engineering future interventions that decisively subvert the immunosuppressive architecture via logic-gated and metabolically precise mechanisms can we finally dismantle the tolerance that has shielded OS for over thirty years.

EGFR gene amplification in IDH-wildtype glioblastomas: an integrative bioinformatic and histopathological analysis using immunohistochemistry and fluorescence in situ hybridization
Authors: Anass Oukhdouch, Basma Zinbi, Souad Sellami, Hanane Rais
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundGlioblastoma multiforme (GBM) is the most aggressive primary brain tumor in adults, characterized by marked histopathological features such as high proliferative activity, mitoses, necrosis, and microvascular proliferation (MVP), as well as extensive molecular heterogeneity. Among key molecular alterations, EGFR amplification and MGMT promoter methylation hold significant diagnostic and prognostic relevance. In this study, we performed a comprehensive bioinformatic analysis of EGFR and conducted a correlation analysis of EGFR amplification in a cohort of 19 GBM samples using standard fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and immunohistochemistry (IHC). These results were further correlated with established molecular hallmarks of glioblastoma.MethodsWe analyzed mRNA expression profiles of EGFR family genes using publicly available datasets from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) databases. Additionally, we utilized multiple online bioinformatics platforms, including GEPIA, TIMER, TISIDB repository, cBioPortal, Metascape, and GeneMANIA, to explore the expression patterns, immune cell infiltration, correlation of EGFR amplification with immunomodulatory and prognostic significance of the EGFR family in glioblastoma. EGFR amplification and protein expression were assessed using fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) and immunohistochemistry (IHC), respectively. MGMT promoter methylation was determined by methylation-specific qPCR (MS-qPCR).ResultsBioinformatic analyses revealed that EGFR and ERBB2 were overexpressed and associated with poor prognosis, while ERBB3 and ERBB4 exhibited decreased expression in GBM compared to normal brain tissue. Immune infiltrating level correlation of EGFR family, revealed that EGFR and ERBB4 expression were positively correlated with various immune cell infiltrates in glioblastoma, particularly CD8 + T cells, macrophages, and B cells. Additionally, EGFR expression was negatively correlated with key co-stimulatory molecules including CD28, CD40, CD70, and IL2RA, no significant associations were found with classical immune checkpoints such as PD-L1 and CTLA-4, though EGFR was inversely correlated with TGFBR1. Subtype analysis showed elevated EGFR expression in Classical-like and Mesenchymal-like GBM subtypes, and a trend toward higher levels in the lymphocyte-depleted immune subtype. Genomic analysis revealed that EGFR family alterations in 49% of GBM cases, with EGFR amplification being the most frequent and linked to poorer overall survival. Enrichment and the PPI network analyses demonstrated that the EGFR family is deeply involved in tumorigenic pathways, particularly PI3K/AKT signaling, integrin interactions, and growth factor receptor cascades. We found that EGFR amplification was detected in 36.84% of cases and was significantly associated with EGFR protein overexpression (IHC score 3+). MGMT promoter methylation was present in 47.4% of cases. No significant association was found between EGFR amplification and MGMT methylation, ATRX, or p53 status.ConclusionOur integrative bioinformatics and histopathological analyses highlight EGFR as a central oncogenic driver and potential immunomodulatory factor in GBM. EGFR amplification correlates with poor prognosis, immune dysregulation, underscoring its utility as both a diagnostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target in GBM management.Keywords: Glioblastoma, Bioinformatic, EGFR amplification, Tumor microenvirment, FISH, Prognostic biomarker

Introduction

Glioblastoma multiform (GBMs) represents the most aggressive and lethal form of primary brain tumor in adults, classified as a grade 4 astrocytoma by the 2021 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of tumor of the central nervous system (CNS) [1]. The IDH-wildtype variant constitutes the majority of GBMs cases and is characterized by rapid progression, therapeutic resistance, and poor prognosis, with a median overall survival of approximately 15 months despite maximal treatment efforts including surgery, radiotherapy, and temozolomide chemotherapy [2].

Molecular profiling has become integral to understanding gliomagenesis, with particular emphasis on recurrent genetic alterations that influence tumor behavior and classification. Among these, alterations in epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) gene, including mutations, gene amplification, and overexpression are mostly known in IDH-wt glioblastomas. Amplification is one of the most frequent molecular events, occurring in approximately 40–60% of IDH-wildtype glioblastomas [3, 4]. The EGFR family, also known as the human epidermal growth factor receptor (HER) family, comprises four transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptors: EGFR (ERBB1), ERBB2 (HER2), ERBB3, and ERBB4. These receptors are encoded by the ERBB1–4 proto-oncogenes and play key roles in cell signaling pathways that regulate proliferation, differentiation, and survival [5]. In addition to EGFR, other molecular markers have emerged as important prognostic and diagnostic indicators in glioblastomas. Loss of alpha Thalassemia/Mental Retardation Syndrome X- (ATRX) expression, TP53 mutations, the telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) promoter and O(6)-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase (MGMT) promoter methylation are frequently assessed in clinical practices [1].

The immune microenvironment has a significant impact on tumor prognosis, as evidenced by the fact that immune cell infiltration in the tumor setting is increasingly one of the most important prognostic indicators for a variety of malignancies [6]. Interestingly, significant immune-modulatory effect of EGFR when it was overexpressed [7].

EGFR amplification often coexists with EGFR mutations, including the EGFRvIII variant, and is associated with increased cell proliferation, angiogenesis, and resistance to apoptosis via activation of the PI3K/AKT and RAS/MAPK signaling pathways [8]. Consequently, EGFR has emerged as a potential therapeutic target, although clinical outcomes of anti-EGFR therapies in GBM have so far been limited, possibly due to tumor heterogeneity and dynamic EGFR expression [9].

The detection of EGFR amplification is typically performed through fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), which remains the gold standard for identifying gene copy number alterations in clinical settings. However, immunohistochemistry (IHC) is widely used due to its accessibility, cost-effectiveness, and ability to assess protein overexpression [10]. Several studies have attempted to correlate EGFR protein expression by IHC with gene amplification detected by FISH, but results remain inconsistent, raising concerns regarding the predictive value of IHC in reflecting EGFR gene status [11].

Recent advances in bioinformatics databases, such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA), allow for more comprehensive analyses of genomic alterations and their associations with clinicopathological features, offering new insights into tumor classification and prognosis [12–14]. This study began with a comprehensive bioinformatic analysis of EGFR family gene expression in glioblastomas. Then, we analyzed a cohort of 19 IDH-wt glioblastomas specimens to assess EGFR alteration status using both immunohistochemistry (IHC) and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), and examined the correlation between EGFR expression and EGFR gene amplification, and further investigated the relationship between EGFR status and established molecular hallmarks of glioblastoma, including TP53 mutations, ATRX loss, and MGMT promoter methylation.

Our research suggests that EGFR amplification in GBM is not only a key molecular driver but may also contribute to immune dysregulation within tumor microenvironment (TME) by suppressing critical immunostimulatory pathways. Through integrative bioinformatic and histopathological analyses, we establish correlations between EGFR status, immune microenvironment features, and patient prognosis. These findings support EGFR as both a diagnostic and immunomodulatory biomarker. (Fig. 1)

Study workflow

Materials and methods

mRNA sequencing data acquisitions

Publicly available mRNA expression datasets were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) and the Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA) database (http://www.cgga.org.cn/) and were downloaded to R studio software. GSE4290 and GSE15824 datasets were generated utilizing the GPL570 [HG-U133_Plus_2] Affymetrix Human Genome U133 Plus 2.0 Array platform. 23 non-tumor samples and 81 GBM samples contained in GSE4290, five glioblastoma cell lines (LN018, LN215, LN229, LN319 and BS149) and five normal brain tissues (NB)used as a control contained in GSE15824. we used and downloaded the mRNA-array_301 dataset, mRNA microarray generated by Agilent Whole Human Genome (Array) platform and contained 301 gliomas samples (including 124 GBM samples).

GEPIA analysis

In our study, the mRNA expression data of 163 GBM samples and 518 Low Grade Glioma (LGG) samples were retrieved from TCGA database, and the mRNA expression data of 207 Normal Brain tissue (NB) samples was retrieved form GTEx database. For analysis, we utilized GEPIA to compare mRNA expression of EGFR family between glioma and NB tissues. Furthermore, we performed the survival analysis of EGFR family in patients with glioma.

Tumor infiltrating immune cells analysis

The assessment of tumor-infiltrating immune cells was conducted using TIMER database (https://cistrome.shinyapps.io/timer/), a web-based tool that provides systematic evaluations of immune cell infiltration across various cancer types using RNA-sequencing data from TCGA [15]. EGFR family expressions and abundance of six major immune infiltrates (B cells, CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, macrophages, neutrophils, and dendritic cells) were explored.

TISIDB portal immunomodulators analysis

TISIDB, an integrated repository portal for tumor-immune system interactions (http://cis.hku.hk/TISIDB/) [16], was used to explore the association between EGFR gene expression and immunomodulators molecules in GBM, and also the immune subtype distribution across GBM subtypes related to the EGFR gene expression was identified to assess the immunogenomic landscape associated with EGFR gene amplification.

cBioportal analysis

The cBioPortal for Cancer Genomics (https://www.cbioportal.org/) is an open-access platform that provides interactive visualization and analysis of large-scale cancer genomic datasets. In this study, the dataset for glioblastomas multiforme (TCGA, Cell 2013) was used to explore genomic alterations in the EGFR gene family across 577 samples/patients. The association between EGFR family alterations and clinical survival was assessed using Kaplan-Meier plots to compare overall survival (OS) between patients with and without EGFR family alterations.

Metascape and genemania analysis

To explore the biological functions and signaling pathways associated with the EGFR family, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using the Metascape online platform (https://metascape.org). Metascape integrates multiple authoritative data sources, including GO Biological Processes, KEGG Pathways, Reactome Gene Sets, and DisGeNET, allowing comprehensive functional annotation [17]. To explore the functional associations and interaction partners of the ERBBs family genes, a protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was generated using the GeneMANIA online tool (http://genemania.org/) [18]. The input genes were analyzed within the Homo sapiens genome context, and the network was constructed based on multiple data sources, including co-expression, physical interactions, pathway involvement, and genetic interactions. GeneMANIA also predicted additional related genes to expand the interaction map, providing insights into the potential functional roles and connectivity of EGFR members within broader signaling networks.

Glioblastomas IDH1-wild type samples

For this study, a total of 19 glioblastomas samples were collected from patients diagnosed with IDH1-wt glioblastoma. All samples were obtained from Anatomic Pathology Department, University Hospital Mohammed VI, following ethical approval and informed consent from patients (N 83/2024). The samples were selected based on strict inclusion and exclusion criteria to ensure the homogeneity of the study population. formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissues were used for histology, immunohistochemistry and molecular analysis.

DNA extraction and quality control

Tumor regions were carefully microdissected to ensure a tumor cell content of at least > 40%. Genomic DNA was extracted using QIAamp DNA extraction kit for FFPE tissues (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, paraffin was removed using xylene and residual xylene removed with ethanol in 37 °C. Buffer ATL and Proteinase K were added to the deparaffinised tissue and incubated at 56 °C for overnight and cooled to room temperature. Buffer AL was then added and the tissue mixture was incubated at 70 °C for 10 min, and the following wash steps were performed according to manufacturer’s instructions. DNA was purified using QIAamp Mini spin columns and eluted in buffer AE. DNA yield and purity were quantitated and assessed using the NanoVue™ Plus Spectrophotometer at A260/A280 and A260/A230 (Biochrome).

Tissue microarray construction and examination

For all 19 GBM samples, tissue microarrays (TMAs) were constructed using 5 mm tissue cores. Glioblastoma diagnosis was confirmed on hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained slides. Representative tumor regions were identified and marked on both the slides and the corresponding donor blocks. One core from a representative tumor region was selected for each sample. 4–5 mm thick H&E sections of FFPE from tissues microarray were used to assessment and evaluate sample quality and confirm the presence of tumor cells in each core.

Immunohistochemistry study

Immunohistochemical staining was performed on 3–4 μm tissue microarray sections to assess the expression of EGFR using a rabbit monoclonal antibody anti-EGFR (Clone E30, 1:25, Dako). Sections were deparaffinized with xylin, rehydrated in alcohol, and subjected to antigen retrieval using citrate buffer (pH 6.0) at 98 °C for 30 min in a water bath. Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked using 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min, followed by incubation with the primary antibodies at room temperature for 30 min and were labelled with the Envision Detection System from DAKO. Colour reaction invention was detected with 3, 3′-diaminobenzidine, tetrahydrochloride (DAB plus; DAKO Glostrup, Denmark) as a substrate, sections were counterstained with hematoxylin, dehydrated, and mounted. The EGFR results were interpreted using scoring system as follow: 0: No membranous staining or faint, incomplet membranous staingni < 10%. 1+: faint, incomplet membranous staingni > 10%. 2+: intense, circumferentail and complet membranous staining < 10%. 3+: intense, circumferentail and complet membranous staining > 10%. Tonsillar epithelial cells were used as positive controls (+ 3) and non-neoplastic glial cells as negative internal controls (0).

EGFR amplification using FISH

To evaluate the EGFR amplification status, we applied the Zyto Light SPEC EGFR/CEN 7 Dual Color Probe set (ZytoVision). Slides were reviewed using fluorescence microscopy, and FISH signals for individual probes were recorded. To assess EGFR amplification, we calculated the ratio of green (EGFR) and red (CEN7) signals (EGFR/CEP 7). we set the cut-off value for amplification to greater than 2 (EGFR/CEP7 > 2). Tumors with polysomy for chromosome 7 but without focal amplification of the EGFR gene were considered to be EGFR non-amplified. Non-neoplastic glial cells in each section considered as internal negative control.

Methylation-specific qPCR

DNA methylation of MGMT promoter was quantified by methylation-specific quantitative polymerase chine reaction (MS-qPCR). Firstly, we performed bisulfite modification of the extracted genomic DNA using the EZ DNA Methylation-Lightning™ Kit (Zymo Research, Irvine, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Treating DNA with bisulfite chemically converts non-methylated cytosines into uracil, and methylated cytosines remain unchanged. DNA denaturation and bisulfite conversion processes were performing in Agilent SureCycler 8800 Thermal Cycler (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA).

After conversion, genomic DNA was amplified to target the O6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase promoter (MGMTp) region with premiers specific to the methylated template (4X MGMT Primer Probe Mix) using MGMT Methylation Analysis Kit V.2 (GeneMAP, Istanbul, Turkey). positive control (PC) 100% methylated DNA, and MGMT Calibrator 8% methylated DNA were used in every run. The amplification in AriaMx Real-time PCR System (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) was performed with an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 15 min, followed by 45 cycles at 95 °C for 20 and 60 °C for 1 min.

Statistical analysis

In the bioinformatics analysis section, data acquisition and pre-processing were carried out using R Studio software. Student’s t-test was performed to analyze mRNA expression of EGFR family genes in the GSE4290, GSE15824, and CGGA databases. In our cohort, correlation analyses of EGFR status between IHC and FISH were conducted in R Studio using Fisher’s exact test. Results for quantitative variables are presented as mean, median ± standard deviation (SD), and categorical variables were summarized as percentages. A p-value ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Future scope

In future work, we plan to validate the immunomodulatory markers associated with the prognostic role of EGFR in glioblastoma by analyzing a larger cohort of GBM patients alongside normal brain tissue controls, in order to better understand intra-tumoral heterogeneity. Functional assays such as immunohistochemistry and flow cytometry will be employed to experimentally confirm the immune-related mechanisms identified in silico. Additionally, we aim to expand the clinical dataset and correlate EGFR-associated immune signatures with patient outcomes to support their potential utility in guiding immunotherapy strategies.

Results

ERBBs expression analysis

The mRNA expression levels of the EGFR family genes were compared between GBM and NB tissue across the GSE4290, GSE15824 datasets, and CGGA database. In the GSE4290 dataset, EGFR and ERBB2 showed significantly higher expression in GBM compared to NB (**p < 0.001 and ***p < 0.0001, respectively) (Fig. 2a and b), whereas ERBB3 and ERBB4 showed significantly lower expression in GBM than in NB (***p < 0.0001) (Fig. 2c and d). In the GSE15824 dataset, EGFR expression was significantly increased in GBM compared to NB (**p < 0.001) (Fig. 2e), while ERBB2, ERBB3, and ERBB4 showed no statistically significant differences (p > 0.05). In the CGGA database, no significant differences were observed in EGFR expression among WHO grades II–IV. However, ERBB2 expression was significantly higher in WHO grade IV compared to grades II and III (**p < 0.001) (Fig. 2j), while ERBB3 and ERBB4 expression decreased progressively with tumor grade (Fig. 2k and l), with the lowest levels observed in grade IV (**p < 0.001, p < 0.05).

GEPIA analysis

Expression and survival analysis of EGFR family in glioma (LGG and GBM) were conducted using the GEPIA online tool based on TCGA and GTEx data. EGFR gene showed significantly higher expression level among GBM and LGG tissues compared to normal brain samples (*p < 0.05) (Fig. 3a). ERBB2 gene showed significantly higher expression in GBM tissues compared to normal brain samples (*p < 0.05), while their expression level in LGG was not significantly altered (Fig. 3b). ERBB3 and ERBB4 did not show significant differential expression between tumor and normal tissues in either GBM or LGG (Fig. 3c and d). Survival analysis using Kaplan–Meier curves revealed that higher expression levels of EGFR, ERBB2, and ERBB3 were significantly correlated with reduced overall survival in glioma patients (p = 0.023, p = 4.9e–09, and p = 0.044, respectively) (Fig. 3e, f and g). In contrast, high expression of ERBB4 was significantly associated with improved overall survival (p = 3.9e–14) (Fig. 3h), suggesting a possible protective role.

Association of EGFR amplification with Immunomodulatory molecules and subtype distribution in GBM

Immunomodulatory and subtype distribution analysis were specifically focused on EGFR due to its well-established role as the most frequently amplified and clinically significant member of the ERBB family in GBM. The selection of specific immunomodulators was directly guided by the immune cell types correlated positively with EGFR in TIMER (B cells, CD8 + T Cells and Macrophage). we employed the TISIDB platform to explore EGFR-associated immunostimulatory and immunoinhibitory molecules. Immunostimulatory molecules promoting T-cell and B-cell activation, such as CD28, CD40, CD70 ICOS, TNFRSF9 (4-1BB), and IL2RA, were prioritized. Additionally, key immunoinhibitory checkpoints relevant to CD8 + T cells, B cells, and macrophages, including PD-L1 (CD274), CTLA-4, LAG-3, and TGFBR1, were selected for correlation analysis. EGFR expression in a cohort of 166 samples of GBM was significantly negatively correlated with several key immunostimulatory molecules, including CD28 (r = −0.226, p = 0.00341), CD40 (r = −0.247, p = 0.0014), IL2RA (r = −0.157, p = 0.0433), and CD70 (r = −0.27, p = 0.000463) as showen in Fig. 5a-d. These results suggest that EGFR overexpression may exhibit a reduction in T-cell and B-cell co-stimulatory signals, potentially contributing to immune dysfunction within the tumor microenvironment (TME). Interestingly, EGFR expression did not significantly correlate with key immunoinhibitory molecules such as PD-L1 (CD274), CTLA-4, and LAG-3, suggesting that EGFR-driven immune modulation in GBM may occur independently of the classical immune checkpoint pathways. However, TGFBR1, an immunosuppressive molecule, was significantly negatively correlated with EGFR expression (r = −0.315, p = 4.03e-05) (Fig. 5e-h). EGFR expression across GBM molecular subtypes revealed significant differences (Kruskal–Wallis p = 1.91e-04), with the highest levels observed in Classic-like and Mesenchymal-like subtypes. Conversely, G-CIMP-low, LGm6-GBM, and G-CIMP-high subtypes exhibited lower EGFR expression (Fig. 5i). Additionally, EGFR expression varied across immune subtypes, with the highest expression observed in the lymphocyte-depleted subtype (C4), although the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.51) (Fig. 5j). This suggests that EGFR expression may be associated with immunosuppressive tumor phenotypes, which aligns with our immunomodulator correlation findings.

Genomic alterations in ERBBs

To explore the diversity of genetic alterations in EGFR family and their association with overall survival of GBM patients, a cohort of Glioblastoma (TCGA, Cell 2013) from cBioportal database was used, EGFR, ERBB2,ERBB3 and ERBB4 are altered in 281 (49%) of queried patients/samples, the most common genetic alteration was amplification, specifically in EGFR as shown in Fig. 6a and b. In addition, we assessed the prognostic impact of genomic alterations in EGFR family. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients with EGFR family alterations exhibited a significantly shorter overall survival (OS) compared to those without such alterations (log-rank p = 0.0031) (Fig. 6c). the analysis of disease-free survival (DFS) revealed no statistically significant difference between the altered and unaltered groups (log-rank p = 0.243) (Fig. 6d).

Enrichment analysis and PPI network of ERBBs family

GO and KEGG analyses based on Metascape tool were performed. the analysis revealed that EGFR family are predominantly enriched in pathways associated with PID ERBB network pathway, ERBB2 activates PTK6 signaling, and epidermal growth factor stimulus response (Fig. 7a). Additionally, enrichment was observed in pathways such as the integrin signaling pathway and the PI3K/AKT signaling cascade, which are critical for tumor cell survival and proliferation. Biological process (BP) enrichment terms demonstrated that the EGFR family gene were significantly linked to response to stimulus, cellular and developmental processes, and positive regulation of biological processes (Fig. 7b). The PPI network analysis using GeneMANIA (Fig. 7c), revealed strong physical and functional interactions among EGFR, ERBB2, ERBB3, and ERBB4, as well as with key signaling partners like Phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate 3-kinase catalytic subunit alpha (PIK3CA), Growth factor receptor-bound protein 2 (GRB2), and SRC proto-oncogene, non-receptor tyrosine kinase (SRC). The interactions were supported by co-expression, pathway involvement, and shared domains (Table 2).

Clinicopathologic characteristics

The clinical and pathologic characteristics of 19 patients classified as IDH-wt glioblastoma are summarized in Table 3. Tumors were most frequently located in the parieto-temporal region (36.84%), followed by the frontal lobe (21.05%), parietal lobe (15.78%), temporal lobe (15.78%), and parieto-occipital region (10.52%). Regarding proliferation activity, 47.36% of tumors exhibited high proliferation, while 36.84% showed moderate proliferation, and 15.78% had low proliferation rates. Mitosis was present in 78.75% of cases. Similarly, necrosis was observed in 78.75% of tumors. Microvascular proliferation (MVP) was detected in 57.89% of cases. Regarding molecular markers, ATRX expression was retained in 63.15% of cases and lost in 36.84%. p53 expression was mutated in 63.15% of tumors. Ki-67 expression analysis revealed that 52.63% of cases had high Ki-67 levels, 42.10% showed moderate expression, and 5.26% had low Ki-67 expression. Discussion

Glioblastoma (GBM) is the most aggressive and lethal primary brain tumor in adults, classified as a grade IV astrocytoma by the World Health Organization. Histopathologically, GBM are characterized by a heterogeneous architecture that includes areas of necrosis, microvascular proliferation, high mitotic activity, and nuclear atypia. Despite intensive treatment combining maximal surgical resection, radiotherapy, and temozolomide chemotherapy, the prognosis remains poor, with a median survival of approximately 15 months [19]. In this study, we conducted a comprehensive integrative analysis of the EGFR gene and the broader ERBB family in GBM, combining bioinformatic analysis with histopathological validation in a 19 cohort of specimen glioblastomas.

By analysing EGFR family mRNA expression level using multiple GEO datasets (GSE4290, GSE15824) and CGGA, we found that EGFR and ERBB2 are significantly overexpressed in GBM tissues compared to normal brain, Conversely, ERBB3 and ERBB4 exhibited decreased expression, particularly in high-grade tumors. This result also confirmed using GEPIA database, EGFR and ERBB2 significantly increased in both GBM and LGG (p < 0.05). additionally, GEPIA confirmed that high expression of EGFR, ERBB2, and ERBB3 was associated with reduced overall survival, while ERBB4 overexpression was correlated with better prognosis. These findings align with prior evidence indicating that EGFR expression or amplification amplification is a key diagnostic hallmark used to distinguish IDH-wildtype GBM from other glioma subtypes, and a negative prognostic marker [20]. ERBB2, while less commonly altered, can also be overexpressed in GBM, though it is not currently used as a standard diagnostic biomarker [21].

In recent years, tumor infiltrating immune cells have emerged as critical components of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in GBM, playing key roles in tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance [22]. Exploration of the immune landscape via TIMER database revealed distinct correlations between EGFR family expression and immune cell infiltration, including significant positive correlations between EGFR and CD8 + T cells, macrophages, and B cells. Interestingly, ERBB4 expression showed broader positive correlations across both innate and adaptive immune cell types, while ERBB2 and ERBB3 demonstrated more selective or even inverse relationships. These results may reflect the complex interplay between receptor tyrosine kinase signaling and the TME.

we observed significant negative correlations between EGFR overexpression and key immunostimulatory molecules, including CD28, CD40, and CD70. These findings suggest that EGFR amplified in GBM may actively suppress essential co-stimulatory pathways required for effective T-cell and B-cell activation within the TME. T cell dysfunction, characterized by states such as senescence, tolerance, anergy, exhaustion, and immune ignorance, is a well-established feature of the TME GBM, contributing to the failure of effective anti-tumor immune responses [23]. In this context, our findings of inverse correlations between EGFR expression and CD28, CD40 and CD70, may partially explain the persistence of dysfunctional T cell phenotypes in EGFR amplification in GBM. recent studie, Zhida Liu et al., have suggested that the therapeutic efficacy of EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (EGFR-TKIs) is not limited to their direct anti-proliferative effects on tumor cells but also critically depends on the presence and activation of functional CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells, which mediate enhanced anti-tumor immunity during treatment [24].

Interestingly, our data showed no significant correlation between EGFR expression and classical immunoinhibitory checkpoints such as PD-L1 (CD274) and Cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated antigen-4 (CTLA-4). However, Several studies have highlighted a potential regulatory connection between EGFR activation and PD-L1 expression in glioblastoma [25]. Moreover, we found an inverse correlation between EGFR expression and TGFBR1 levels. TGFBR1 is a key mediator of TGF-β signaling, a pathway classically known for its immunosuppressive functions and contribution to tumor progression [23].

Genetic alterations analysis from cBioPortal showed that EGFR family were altered in approximately 49% of GBM samples, with EGFR amplification being the most frequent. Patients harboring these alterations had significantly shorter overall survival, reinforcing the clinical relevance of EGFR status as a prognostic biomarker. Although disease-free survival was not significantly different, which was consistent with previous study linking EGFR alterations to early progression and resistance to therapy [26].

To understand the role of the EGFR family in glioblastoma biology, we used Metascape to perform GO and KEGG pathway analyses and we used GeneMANIA constructed a PPI network. EGFR family associated with the canonical pathway of integrins α6β1 and α6β4, These integrins bind to laminin in the extracellular matrix and are key mediators of cell adhesion, migration, and invasion [27]. previous studies suggested that α6 integrin subunit associates with β1 or β4 subunits was strongly expressed in glioblastoma tissue compared with normal brain [28, 29]. Integrin α6β1 has been shown to play a crucial role in promoting glioma progression and enhancing tumorigenic potential. Delamarre et al. demonstrated that overexpression of integrin α6β1 in glioma cells significantly increased tumor growth and invasion both in vitro and in vivo. Their findings suggest that integrin α6β1 not only facilitates adhesion to the extracellular matrix but also contributes to the acquisition of aggressive phenotypes in glioma cells by enhancing migration and proliferation capacities [30]. These results support the relevance of integrin-mediated signaling pathways, such as the PID A6B1 A6B4 Integrin Pathway, in driving glioblastoma malignancy and further highlight integrin α6β1 as a potential therapeutic target for limiting glioma progression.

EGFR family is deeply embedded in tumorigenic pathways, particularly PI3K/AKT signaling. These pathways are fundamental to tumor cell proliferation, migration, and survival, and their activation through EGFR signaling offers a potential axis for therapeutic targeting [31]. The PI3K/AKT signaling pathway plays a central role in glioma progression by promoting cell survival, proliferation, and resistance to apoptosis. Therefore, it may be considered a potential therapeutic target in GBM [32]. The interaction of EGFR family with effectors like PIK3CA, GRB2, and SRC further validates their functional significance in glioblastoma progression.

Consistent with the known histopathological profile of glioblastoma, the majority of our cohort displayed hallmark features such as high mitotic activity, necrosis, and microvascular proliferation (MVP), which are defining characteristics of high-grade gliomas according to the WHO classification [1]. Immunohistochemical analysis further confirmed elevated the Ki-67 LI expression in most tumors, with a statistically significant correlation between histological proliferation scores and Ki-67 index.

We demonstrated that EGFR amplification (assessed by FISH) was present in 36.84% of cases. Importantly, EGFR amplification was significantly associated with strong EGFR protein expression (IHC score 3+), confirming that EGFR amplification is the predominant underlying mechanism driving EGFR protein overexpression. However, we also identified two cases with EGFR amplification despite low to moderate EGFR protein expression (IHC score 0–2+). Conversely, we observed two cases with strong EGFR overexpression (IHC score 3+) in the absence of EGFR amplification. This discrepancy may be explained by transcriptional upregulation of the EGFR gene without gene amplification [33]. Moreover, our regression analysis demonstrated a positive correlation between FISH and IHC (R² = 0.3763, p = 0.0052).

In terms of MGMT promoter methylation, we showed that 47.4% of cases were methylated. The MS-qPCR method demonstrated reliable amplification curves, and methylation status was assigned using Ct threshold values. detection of MGMT promoter methylation is of considerable importance, as it serves as a predictive biomarker for response to temozolomide chemotherapy and is associated with improved overall survival in glioblastoma patients [34]. there was no significant association between EGFR gene amplification and MGMT promoter methylation status. Our findings are in line with the study by Bale et al. which also reported that EGFR amplification and MGMT promoter methylation occur independently in glioblastoma [35]. Integrated analysis revealed no statistically significant correlation between EGFR amplification and p53 mutation, ATRX status. Although tumors with ATRX loss showed numerically higher EGFR amplification.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we confirmed that EGFR amplification, as assessed by FISH, is significantly associated with EGFR protein overexpression detected by IHC, and occurs independently of MGMT promoter methylation, ATRX status, and p53 mutation. Our findings indicate that EGFR amplification is a key molecular alteration characteristic of IDH-wt glioblastomas and supports its role in the current molecular classification. Bioinformatic analyses further highlighted the involvement of EGFR family in tumor-promoting pathways, specifically in PI3K/AKT signaling pathway. EGFR amplification in GBM is associated with the suppression of key co-stimulatory immune pathways, highlighting the need for novel immunotherapeutic strategies that target EGFR-associated immune modulation. These results underscore the importance of integrated molecular profiling to refine glioblastoma classification and to guide the development of personalized therapeutic strategies.

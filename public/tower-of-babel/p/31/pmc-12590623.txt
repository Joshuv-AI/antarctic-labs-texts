Exploring sugarcane bagasse; an economical and eco-friendly alternative feed for red palm weevil larvae rearing under laboratory conditions
Authors: Mureed Husain, Khawaja G. Rasool, Khalid Mehmood, Waleed S. Alwaneen, Abdulrahman S. Aldawood
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Rearing red palm weevil (RPW) larvae without palm fronds is a tough, expensive, and time-consuming task. The present study purpose was to produce a sugarcane bagasse-based semi-artificial diet for the rearing of RPW larvae in the laboratory for scientific experimental studies or pesticide bioassays. The sugarcane bagasse is very economical, simple in preparation, having longer shelf life, and fully supporting the nutritional requirements of RPW larval stages for proper growth and development. Sugarcane bagasse based semi-artificial diet was consisting of sugarcane bagasse, corn flour, wheat flour, distillate water as primary components, and potassium benzoate, sorbic acid, and ascorbic acid as preservatives while agar was added to give good texture. All the ingredients were mixed together with a specific composition. The neonates were fed with the prepared diet and placed in a controlled environmental condition at 25 °C, 70 ± 5% RH, on a 12:12 h (light: dark) photoperiod. The detailed biological parameters were observed and data was recorded. The RPW larvae reared on present diet successfully completed their larval stages and reached to the pupal stage after passing 11 larval instars. Larvae feed on present diet grow vigorously and maximum larval weight 6.25 g was attained in 9th larval instar. After 9th instar larval weight reduced until pupation. Average larval duration was recorded 53.16 days. The RPW reared on present diet laid maximum 112.2 eggs in 110.8-days of oviposition period with 90.7% hatchability. The present sugarcane bagasse based semi-artificial diet is quite capable of supporting RPW larvae for the successful completion of their growth and development.

Introduction

The red palm weevil (RPW), Rhynchophorus ferrugineus (Olivier) (Coleoptera: Curculionidae) is a highly invasive widely distributed serious pest of date palm trees [12], causing tremendous economic losses to the palm growers [13, 21]. The infestation remains hidden in early stages as the juveniles bore inside the palm trunk and feed on the internal tissue which make its management very difficult. The recent development in early stage infestation detection has made its early infestation detection possible up to some extent, but the problem still exists and RPW is proliferating day by day [17, 22, 24, 27, 35]. The management practices fail to control this crucial pest because of its hidden nature.

The most promising control methods include the use of chemicals [3, 19, 29], pheromones [14, 18], entomopathogenic microbial organisms [8, 23, 44], and gene silencing [30]. The above-mentioned control measures against RPW are quite effective in the laboratory, but there is no well-established strategy for this critical insect in the date palm-infested fields. In this context, the availability of various stages to assess efficacy under laboratory conditions is critical when investigating new strategies to manage RPW.

Nutritional value of a diet has big impact on the survival and insect development because it affects the weight, reproduction capabilities, and life span [6, 38]. The RPW development was studied on four different date palm cultivars including: Sillaj, Sukkary, Khalas, and Khasab and reported that the RPW larvae raised on Sukkary were heathier as compared to those reared on other three cultivars [4]. He also reported that RPW reared on Sukkary laid significantly more eggs whereas, adults lived longer on Khasab while greater numbers of cocoons were collected from Khalas cultivar.

Date palm frond based semi-artificial diet for the rearing of RPW larvae was developed by Aldawood et al., [7]. The diet comprised of chopped date palm petiole tissues, wheat flour, corn flour, sodium benzoic, ascorbic acid, sorbic acid, water and agar. They reported that RPW feed on this semi artificial diet showed the average larval duration 46–57 days, average larval weight 4–6 g, average adult emergence 58–98%, and complete life span 48–98 days. They also reported that RPW larvae feed on that diet presented 11 larval instars.

Five artificial diets for the rearing of RPW were evaluated. The diets comprised of sugarcane setts (first diet/ control), sweet pumpkin (second diet), ground sugarcane + sweet pumpkin (third diet), sweet potato (fourth diet) and ground sugarcane + sweet potato (fifth diet). He tried to determine the pre-oviposition, oviposition, larval and pupal durations, and longevity of male and female adults. He found variation among diets for above biological parameters. Shortest life cycle was recorded on sweet potato while longest of sugarcane setts [2].

The nutritional value of the host plant determines the quality and size of the eggs [9]. Females feeding on nutritionally deficient host plants either lay few healthy eggs or greater number of unhealthy eggs [31]. There is also a significant correlation between pupal health and fecundity in insects [40, 41, 43]. Literature revealed some variations in different biological parameters including: fecundity, incubation time, and durations of various developmental stages in RPW [25]. A good quality diet for insect mass rearing should provide nutrients sufficient for their growth and development, and should be palatable, easily digestible and readily absorbable through the insect gut [10].

Laboratory rearing of this insect is quite cumbersome due to its very lengthy life span, especially the larval stage [7]. We have tried to develop a sugarcane bagasse based semi artificial diet which is simple in preparation. Sugarcane bagasse is an extremely cost-effective nutritious diet supplement for rearing the RPW in the laboratory since it is waste from sugarcane crushing in the sugar industry and is easily available. Its preparation is easy and requires no technological expenditure. Nutritionally, it is high in fiber, which is necessary for Red Palm Weevil larvae’s digestion. The objective of the present study was to develop a sugarcane bagasse based semi artificial diet which is very economical, simple in preparation, having longer shelf life, and fully supporting the nutritional requirements of RPW larval stages, under laboratory conditions.

Materials and methods

Red palm weevil colony establishment

The adult red palm weevils (RPW) were collected from different RPW infested date palm orchards located in Riyadh. Insects were placed into the plastic boxes and transferred to the laboratory for rearing. The adults were provided a piece of cotton saturated with 10% sugar solution [20], to feed and laying eggs. The eggs were collected and shifted to small plastic cups (d: 6 cm; h: 2.5 cm) provided with wet filter papers and placed in incubator. The hatched larvae were provided with sugarcane bagasse based semi-artificial diet.

Sugarcane bagasse based semi-artificial diet preparation

The diet was consisting of sugarcane bagasse, corn flour, wheat flour, and distillate water as primary components, and potassium benzoate, sorbic acid, and ascorbic acid as preservatives while agar was added to give good texture [7]. The sugarcane originally was brought from Al Ahasa region of Saudi Arabia; and the bagasse was acquired from a small juice business in Riyadh City. Importantly, the bagasse was neither washed or sterilized after collecting because it had already been washed by the shopkeeper prior to juice extraction, and the juice contains no chemical residues because it is consumed by people. Instead, the bagasse was cut into 1-2-inch pieces to promote larval feeding. All the ingredients were mixed together with a specific composition as follows: 500 g of sugarcane bagasse, 250 g wheat flour, 250 g corn flour, and 2 g ascorbic acid, 1.6 g potassium benzoate, 1.6 g sorbic acid, 20 g agar and 2 L distilled water. The prepared diet was preserved in the refrigerator at 4 °C.

Red palm weevil larvae feeding experiment

The first instar larvae were placed in a plastic cup (d: 5 cm; h: 3 cm) and 100 g of the sugar cane bagasse-based diet was provided until the third instar. Moreover, 4th instar larvae were moved to a plastic container of bigger size (d: 6 cm; h: 8 cm) and 250 to 300 g of diet was provided up to the 11th instar larvae. Throughout the larval stages from 1 to 11, the larvae were provided with small pieces of sugarcane bagasse, which adequately supports their growth and nutritional needs sugarcane bagasse serves as a complete diet for red palm weevil (RPW) rearing. However, during the 11th instar, the larvae require longer fiber threads to effectively construct their cocoons. Therefore, 11th instar larvae were transferred to a sugar cane (10 cm long) present in plastic boxes (1 Kg), because the 11th instar larvae will make a cocoon inside the sugarcane piece by using the internal fiber of the sugar can and finally they will pupate inside this self-woven cocoon. This is why the transfer to larger pieces of sugarcane is necessary. The whole experimental population was maintained in a controlled environmental condition at 25 °C, 70 ± 5% RH, on a 12:12 h (light: dark) photoperiod. The diet was refreshed after every 3 to 5 days until the larvae reached at 11th instar.

Upon adult eclosion from the cocoons, ten pairs of newly emerged RPW adults were selected to study biological traits including pre-oviposition periods, oviposition periods, total no of eggs laid per female, eggs hatchability %, post-oviposition periods, and adults life span. The RPW adults were provided a piece of cotton saturated with 10% sugar solution [20].

Data collection and statistical analysis

The RPW larval growth and development were monitored on daily basis by observing the presence of exuviae and head capsules, whereas larval weight was measured after one day of molting by using digital weight balance.

The Completely randomized design (CRD) was used in this biological study of RPW. Larval duration and weight were recorded in five replications each replicate has 5 larvae. The data was analyzed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) PROC GLM procedure of SAS Institute. [34]. Means were separated by least significant difference (LSD) at (P = .05).

Discussion

The red palm weevil (RPW) is a very destructive pest of palm trees worldwide and cause huge economic losses every year. For lab research, a well maintained RPW colony capable of providing adequate supply of the required stages of RPW is needed for research purposes. Laboratory rearing of RPW is relatively tough because of its long lifespan. Present work explored and developed a sugarcane bagasse-based semi-artificial diet which is simple in preparation and very economical for the rearing of RPW larval stage under laboratory conditions. Several researchers have investigated/reviewed and studied the growth and biology of RPW on a variety of materials, including various natural date palm cultivars, which serve as a natural host plant for the RPW, as well as other materials [7, 11, 15, 16, 32].

RPW larvae fed on present diet successfully completed their larval stage and reached to the pupal stage after passing 11 larval instars. Similarly, Aldawood et al., [7] and Al Ayedh, 2011 also reported 11 larval instars in RPW feed on semi-artificial diets. In average total larval duration on present diet was recorded 53.16 days that was slightly longer than [7] (49.98 days) whereas, the longest larval duration 66 days was recorded on Natural date palm diet and semi-artificial diet reported by [5]. Larvae feed on present diet grow vigorously and maximum larval weight 6.25 g was recorded in 9th larval instar. After 9th instar larval weight significantly reduced until pupation as it has been observed that pupation is a complex process which involves metabolic changes and hormonal regulation which that may lead to the natural weight fluctuations [42].

Average longevity of RPW adults was recorded 105.4 days whereas; RPW female life span was significantly longer than males. Average female life span was recorded as 119.8 days whereas; male lifespan was recorded as 91.1 days. Researchers reported different adult male and female lifespans on their artificially developed diets. [7] Reported 52 days male and 49 days female lifespan on date palm fronds based semi-artificial diet [26] reported 84 days male and 60 days female lifespan on coconut. [28] Reported 71 days male and 69 days female lifespan on sugarcane Salama and Abdel-Razek [33], reported 95.2 days male and 98 days female lifespan on semi-artificial diet.

On present sugarcane bagasse-based diet a female RPW laid maximum 112.2 eggs, throughout of her life with 90.7% hatchability. [7] Reported 96 eggs on date palm fronds based semi-artificial diet. [26] reported 204 eggs on coconut, [28], reported 278 eggs on sugarcane whereas, [33] reported 184 eggs on semi-artificial diet. The average oviposition period for a female fed on present diet was recorded as 110.8 days. [7] Reported 48 days on date palm fronds based semi-artificial diet. [26] reported 45 days on coconut. The average total lifespan on present diet was recorded as 191.38 days. [7] Reported 77 days on date palm fronds based semi-artificial diet. [26] Reported 81 days on coconut. [28] Reported 82 days on sugarcane. The diet can be saved for eight days in refrigerator without any deterioration in quality. Furthermore, traditional feed sources, such as natural date palm diets, are frequently excessively expensive and difficult to obtain for constant use; [4] analyzed date palm cultivars and concluded that, while RPW may be cultivated on date palm trunks, this is not always sustainable. Furthermore, existing diets, such as semi-artificial feeds, necessitate complex preparation involving multiple ingredients, which can limit their consistency and scalability; for example, [5, 7] investigated semi-artificial diets, but these are frequently labor-intensive. The sugarcane bagasse feed formulation is a low-cost alternative readily available from sugar businesses, greatly decreasing the financial load on researchers and practitioners. Many standard diets may not match the nutritional requirements of RPW larvae, thus causing developmental difficulties. We encourage a more sustainable approach to RPW rearing by using sugarcane bagasse, which takes advantage of agricultural byproducts that would otherwise go to waste. sugarcane bagasse supplies enough sugar and fiber to meet the nutritional requirements of RPW larvae. Its morphological structure is similar to that of date palm fronds, the larvae’s natural host, which may increase its acceptance and effectiveness as a food source.

The increased growth of RPW larvae on sugarcane bagasse indicates a complex interplay of nutritional variables, necessitating additional research to improve larval rearing [1]. Sugarcane bagasse’s specific cellulose/hemicellulose ratio (typically 40–50% cellulose and 25–35% hemicellulose) [37], as well as the presence of residual sugars, micronutrients, and prebiotic xylooligosaccharides (XOS) [39], may contribute to superior growth performance by promoting beneficial gut microbiota [36]. More study, including as nutritional composition analysis and gut microbiota studies, is required to understand the underlying mechanisms and identify individual components responsible for the observed effects.

Conclusion

Present research found that sugarcane bagasse is very nutritious and useful waste of sugarcane and it can be used to rear the RPW. The optimum larval growth period as compared with the other studies and increased larval weight reflects its nutritional importance for the proper growth and development of the RPW larvae. Moreover, the developed diet is quite capable to establish RPW colony and can be recommended for successful rearing of RPW in the laboratory. Furthermore, the preparation of diet is easy, economical and it can be saved for a longer period of time in refrigerator without any deterioration in quality.

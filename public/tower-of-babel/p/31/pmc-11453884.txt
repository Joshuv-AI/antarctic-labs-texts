12498 Outcomes In Thyroid Cancer Patients Having Undergone Us-guided (usg) Fine Needle Aspiration Sampling Of Cervical Lymph Nodes And Soft Tissue Nodules
Authors: Paula lorena castellanos, Ana Maria Chindris, Sarika Rao, Jeffrey Janus, Ricardo Paz-Fumagalli, Victor Joseph Bernet
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Abstract Disclosure: P.L. castellanos: None. A.M. Chindris: None. S. Rao: None. J. Janus: None. R. Paz-Fumagalli: None. V.J. Bernet: None. Introduction: Limited information exists regarding the relationship between outcomes and decision-making based on results from Ultrasound-guided fine needle aspiration (USG-FNA) of cervical nodes/nodules suspicious for thyroid cancer metastases. Management options for positive findings include surgery, percutaneous ethanol ablation, radioactive iodine therapy, external beam radiation systemic therapy, or observation. This retrospective review is aimed at describing the management and associated outcomes of a cohort of thyroid cancer patients who underwent USG FNA for cervically metastases. Methods: Following our Clinic IRB review and approval, we performed a chart review and identified 163 patients with thyroid cancer patients who had undergone USG FNA of apparent cervical metastases at the Florida campus between 2012 and 2018. Cytology results and corresponding tumor marker washout from each sampled focus were recorded. The records review included recording anatomical and biochemical outcomes related to management decisions based on USG FNA results. Appropriate statistical plots were used to help visualize the data pattern. ResultsThe median age was 59 years 69.9% were female and 91.3% were Caucasian. Initial presentation typically consisted of the appearance of a neck nodule and thyroidectomy was the first line of therapy in 155 of 163 cases. Imaging post-thyroidectomy demonstrated evidence of cervical lymphadenopathy in 75.5% of cases with localization in the lateral neck in 65.2% and central in 19.2% respectively. Regarding USG-FNA outcomes, 46.2% yielded malignancy while 26% were benign. The most prevalent cytology was papillary thyroid cancer detected in 87.1%.Thyroglobulin washout was performed on all patients with a history of papillary variant thyroid carcinoma, with positive results reported in 8.3%. Ethanol ablation was the most used treatment for lymph node recurrence followed by neck surgery in 56.2%. During the follow-up, 14% of the patients who were TGAB+ remained so.Outcome trends were as follows: The combined anatomical and biochemical negative was 82.3%, while the combined anatomical and biochemical positive was 2.5%. The combined anatomical negative and biochemical positive was 7.6%, alongside the combined anatomical positive and biochemical negative was 7.6%. Conclusion: Of 163 thyroid cancer cases with cervical lymphadenopathy, 46% had cytology-proven presence of malignancy. These patients underwent either observation, PEA, or surgery.At follow up the majority of the patients were without anatomical and biochemical evidence of disease while about 7-8% were positive by one of the parameters and much fewer were positive for disease by both parameters.

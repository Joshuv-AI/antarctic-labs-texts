NALIRIFOX in Japanese treatment-naïve patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma: results from an open-label, phase II study
Authors: M Ueno, K Ikezawa, Y Komatsu, H Nagano, T Hisano, T Terashima, K Nio, C Morizane, S Shimizu, M Ozaka, H Ohyama, T Otsuru, K Sudo, N Mizuno, C Daniel, L Martín-Fernández, N Amellal, K Bernard, M Sasaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLiposomal irinotecan in combination with oxaliplatin, 5-fluorouracil, and leucovorin (NALIRIFOX) improved survival outcomes of patients with treatment-naïve metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma (mPAC) compared with nab-paclitaxel + gemcitabine in the phase III NAPOLI-3 trial. However, no Japanese centre participated in NAPOLI-3. Therefore, this study aims to demonstrate the applicability of the efficacy and safety results of NALIRIFOX observed in the NAPOLI-3 trial to a Japanese population.Materials and methodsIn this ongoing, phase II study, patients with treatment-naïve mPAC received NALIRIFOX across 15 Japanese centres. Eligible patients had ≥1 measurable metastatic lesion, an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0-1, and an initial mPAC diagnosis ≤6 weeks before screening. The primary endpoint was objective response (OR) per independent central review (ICR). Secondary endpoints included disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), safety, and quality of life (QoL).ResultsForty-one Japanese patients received NALIRIFOX, and the median follow-up duration was 12.8 months. Seventeen (41.5%) patients had an OR and the DCR was 61.0% per ICR. Median OS was 9.9 months [95% confidence interval (CI) 7.4-12.9]; 6-month OS rate was 73.2%. Median PFS per ICR was 4.4 months (95% CI 2.3-7.2). Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 97.6% of patients and the most frequent serious TEAEs were decreased appetite (22.0%) and decreased neutrophil count (17.1%). QoL global health status was maintained throughout the study.ConclusionThe efficacy and safety of NALIRIFOX for first-line treatment of mPAC in this Japanese population is consistent with results of NAPOLI-3.Key words: first-line, liposomal irinotecan, metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, NALIRIFOX

Introduction

Despite advances in treatment, pancreatic cancer remains one of the most lethal cancers, with only 3% of patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma (mPAC) surviving 5 years after diagnosis.1 Due to a lack of early symptoms and the aggressive nature of the disease, ∼80% of patients with pancreatic cancer are diagnosed at an advanced stage,2 highlighting the need for treatment options for patients with metastatic disease. The reported incidence of pancreatic cancer in Japan is 33.9 per 100 000 people, compared with 4.7 per 100 000 in Asia as a whole, which could be attributed to the ageing population in Japan.3,4

The current guidelines for treatment of mPAC in Japan recommend the use of FOLFIRINOX [irinotecan, oxaliplatin, leucovorin (LV), 5-fluorouracil (5-FU)], or gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel for first-line treatment of patients who are eligible for intensive chemotherapy.5 The efficacy of FOLFIRINOX compared with gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel for first-line treatment of mPAC has been investigated in recent clinical trials ClinicalTrials.gov.6,7 While one pharmacoethnicity systematic review and meta-analysis suggested differences in relative survival with FOLFIRINOX versus gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel in patients from Europe and North America compared with Asian patients,8 the majority of clinical trials and real-world evidence show no definitive superiority between these two regimens irrespective of geographical location.6,7,9

Liposomal irinotecan is a formulation that encapsulates irinotecan in a lipid bilayer vesicle and has been shown to exhibit higher and more sustained levels of irinotecan and SN-38 at equivalent doses to irinotecan.10 Liposomal irinotecan, in combination with 5-FU and LV, was first approved for the treatment of patients with mPAC that had progressed on gemcitabine-based therapy in 2015 by the US Food & Drug Administration (FDA) and in 2016 by the European Medicines Agency (EMA) based on the results of NAPOLI-1.11, 12, 13 This regimen was subsequently approved in Japan for the same indication following publication of a study in a Japanese population.14

In the recent, randomized, open-label, phase III clinical trial, NAPOLI-3, liposomal irinotecan in combination with oxaliplatin and 5-FU/LV (NALIRIFOX) demonstrated an overall survival (OS) benefit compared with nab-paclitaxel + gemcitabine in patients with mPAC not previously treated in the metastatic setting {11.1 versus 9.2 months, respectively; hazard ratio (HR): 0.83 [95% confidence interval (CI) 0.70-0.99]; P = 0.036}.15 NALIRIFOX was well tolerated in NAPOLI-3, and no unexpected safety concerns were identified.15 The efficacy and safety of NALIRIFOX demonstrated in NAPOLI-3 led to the approval of this regimen for first-line treatment of patients with mPAC by the FDA and EMA in 2024.16,17

Despite the efficacy demonstrated in the NAPOLI-3 trial population, there were a limited number of Asian patients (20 and 18 in the NALIRIFOX and nab-paclitaxel + gemcitabine treatment groups, respectively) and no Japanese centres participated in NAPOLI-3. Therefore, the aim of this study was to demonstrate that the efficacy and safety results of NALIRIFOX observed in NAPOLI-3 could be applicable to the Japanese population with treatment-naïve mPAC (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06225999).

Materials and methods

Study design

The design of this study has been described previously.18 Briefly, this was a phase II, open-label study of NALIRIFOX in patients with treatment-naïve mPAC conducted at 15 centres in Japan. The first patient visit was on 25 April 2024, and the cut-off date for this final data analysis was 23 July 2025. Patients received the same treatment regimen as used in the NAPOLI-3 trial; however, due to Japanese clinical practice, levoleucovorin (LLV) was used in place of LV (NALIRIFOX: liposomal irinotecan 50 mg/m2, oxaliplatin 60 mg/m2, LLV 200 mg/m2, and 5-FU 2400 mg/m2, administered sequentially). Patients received NALIRIFOX on days 1 and 15 of 28-day cycles, unless modified for treatment-related toxicity. There was no pre-defined duration of treatment for patients treated in this study. Instead, patients were treated until clinical or radiological progressive disease, unacceptable treatment-related toxicity, or withdrawal from the study. The end of the study was defined as the date when all participants had either died, withdrawn, or been declared lost to follow-up, 12 months had passed since the last patient had been enrolled and all patients had finished treatment, or the funder terminated the study, whichever occurred first. During the follow-up period, patients were contacted once a month to determine OS status until death or end of study, whichever occurred first.

Patients

Japanese patients aged ≥18 years with histologically or cytologically confirmed mPAC that had not been previously treated in the metastatic setting were eligible for inclusion in this study. Initial diagnosis of mPAC (per American Joint Committee on Cancer eighth edition19) must have occurred ≤6 weeks before screening. Patients must have ≥1 metastatic lesion measurable by computed tomography (CT) scan [or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) if the patient is allergic to CT contrast], according to RECIST v1.1,20 and an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 0-1. Patients were excluded from the study if they had neuroendocrine or acinar pancreatic carcinoma, serious gastrointestinal or lung disorders, clinically active brain metastases, or second malignancies. Full inclusion and exclusion criteria for this study have been published previously.18

Study assessments

The primary endpoint of this study was objective response (OR), defined as complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) as assessed by independent central review (ICR) following RECIST v1.1.20 Secondary endpoints included duration of response (DoR), time to response, disease control rate (DCR), progression-free survival (PFS), OS, safety, quality of life (QoL), and plasma concentrations of total irinotecan, SN-38, and oxaliplatin.

Tumour evaluations were carried out by CT scan (or MRI if the patient was allergic to CT contrast) at screening and every 8 weeks after first dosing until disease progression according to RECIST v1.1.20 DoR was defined as the time between the first occurrence of response (CR or PR) and the first documentation of disease progression, as assessed by ICR per RECIST v1.1,20 or death due to any cause. Time to response was defined as the time from start of NALIRIFOX treatment to the first occurrence of response (CR or PR). DCR was defined as the percentage of patients achieving CR, PR, or stable disease (SD) as best response. PFS was defined as the time from start of NALIRIFOX treatment to first documentation of disease progression as assessed by ICR per RECIST v1.1,20 or death due to any cause, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the time from start of NALIRIFOX treatment to date of death due to any cause. Response-related outcomes were assessed by ICR, unless otherwise stated.

Safety was evaluated at every visit throughout the duration of the study and for 30 days after cessation of NALIRIFOX administration. Safety evaluations included adverse events (AEs), physical examinations (including ECOG PS), laboratory examinations, and electrocardiogram (ECG) findings. AEs were graded in accordance with National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 5.0.21

QoL was assessed using the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) Core Quality of Life questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30). Time until definitive deterioration (TUDD) was defined as the duration of time from start of NALIRIFOX treatment to the occurrence of the first QoL score decrease from baseline of ≥10 points (not attributed to death or progressive disease) that did not improve above this score before data cut-off (23 July 2025).

Whole blood samples were collected for the measurement of plasma concentrations of irinotecan, SN-38, and oxaliplatin on day 1 (before and after liposomal irinotecan injection and again after oxaliplatin infusion), day 3 (before completion of 5-FU infusion), and day 15 (pre-dose) of the first cycle of treatment.

Statistical analysis

Sample size was calculated to provide an 80% probability of observing an OR rate point estimate higher than the comparator arm (gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel) of the NAPOLI-3 trial.15 The safety set was defined as all patients who received at least one dose of NALIRIFOX (including a partial dose) and was used for all analyses, unless otherwise specified. The per-protocol set included all patients in the safety set who did not violate the protocol in a way that would have affected the study outcome. The per-protocol set was used to perform sensitivity analysis of OR per ICR and investigator assessment. The response-evaluable set was defined as all patients in the safety set who had at least one baseline and one postbaseline evaluable tumour assessment. The response-evaluable set was used to perform sensitivity analyses of OR per ICR and investigator assessment. The pharmacokinetic set included all patients who received at least one dose of liposomal irinotecan, had at least one value of plasma concentration, and had no protocol deviations affecting pharmacokinetic variables. Pharmacokinetic data from this study were evaluated using a previously developed population pharmacokinetics model to provide key pharmacokinetic variables for liposomal irinotecan and SN-38.22 Grade ≥3 neutropenia and diarrhoea were used to analyse the potential correlation between irinotecan and SN-38 pharmacokinetic measures. Time-to-event analyses were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method. Descriptive statistics are provided for all other parameters assessed in this study.

Results

Patients

Of the 48 patients screened, 41 patients with treatment-naïve mPAC were included in this study and received treatment with NALIRIFOX (Supplementary Figure S1, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365). At data cut-off, 4 (9.8%) patients were still receiving NALIRIFOX, 37 (90.2%) patients had discontinued treatment, and 36 (87.8%) patients entered the study follow-up period. In a sensitivity analysis, the OR rate in the response-evaluable population (N = 38) was 44.7% (95% CI 28.6, 61.7) as assessed by ICR and 40.0% (95% CI 24.9, 56.7) per investigator assessment. The OR rate in the per-protocol set was 41.5% (95% CI 26.3, 57.9) per ICR and 39.0% (95% CI 24.2, 55.5) per investigator assessment.

Safety and tolerability of NALIRIFOX in Japanese patients

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) occurred in 40/41 (97.6%) patients (Table 3). Of these patients, 39 (95.1%) had TEAEs deemed related to NALIRIFOX. The most frequent TEAEs of any grade were decreased appetite (61.0%), diarrhoea (56.1%), and neutrophil count decrease (46.3%). During the study, 18 (43.9%) patients were administered loperamide hydrochloride for diarrhoea treatment and 16 (39.0%) patients were administered granulocyte colony-stimulating factor for neutrophil count decrease. The most common grade 3-4 TEAEs in Japanese patients receiving NALIRIFOX were decreased appetite, neutrophil count decrease, anaemia, hypoalbuminemia, neutropenia, pneumonia (7.3% of patients), and white blood cell count decrease (Table 4).

Serious TEAEs were observed in 23 (56.1%) patients, and in 10 (24.4%) of these patients, serious TEAEs were deemed to be related to NALIRIFOX. The most frequent treatment-related serious TEAEs were decreased appetite (19.5%) and febrile neutropenia (4.9%). Twenty-three (56.1%) patients had TEAEs which led to reduction of any drug within the NALIRIFOX regimen. Two patients receiving NALIRIFOX had TEAEs (malaise, deemed to be related to NALIRIFOX treatment, and sepsis, deemed to be unrelated to NALIRIFOX treatment) that led to discontinuation of liposomal irinotecan. One patient died due to a TEAE (same case of sepsis which led to liposomal irinotecan discontinuation); however, this was not deemed to be related to any drug in the NALIRIFOX regimen.

No clinically relevant postbaseline changes were observed for any vital signs, including blood pressure, heart rate, temperature, weight, and body surface area. ECG findings showed that one (2.4%) patient had an absolute QT interval value corrected by Fredericia’s formula (QTcF) >480 ms, five (12.2%) had postbaseline QTcF values that increased >30 ms to ≤60 ms from baseline, and two (4.9%) patients had postbaseline QTcF values that increased >60 ms from baseline. Among the 28 (70.0%) patients who had an ECOG PS of 0 at baseline and a postbaseline ECOG PS score recorded, 17 (42.5%) maintained a score of 0 throughout the study, 10 (25.0%) patients worsened to ECOG PS scores of 1, and one (2.5%) patient’s worst postbaseline ECOG PS was 2. Among the 12 (30.0%) patients who had an ECOG PS of 1 at baseline with a postbaseline ECOG PS score recorded, 7 (17.5%) maintained a score of 1 throughout the study, 3 (7.5%) patients worsened to ECOG PS scores of 2, and 2 (5.0%) patients worsened to a postbaseline ECOG PS score of 3.

QoL

Global health status/QoL remained stable and was maintained across the duration of the study (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365). Emotional, cognitive, and social functioning scores were maintained throughout the study, indicating that patient' psychosocial well-being was sustained during treatment with NALIRIFOX (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365). Median TUDD of EORTC QLQ-C30 domains ranged from 7.1 to 11.9 months (Supplementary Table S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365). Several symptoms’ scales, such as nausea and vomiting, pain, insomnia, constipation, and financial difficulties, remained stable during the study. The fatigue, dyspnoea, loss of appetite, and diarrhoea scales showed a deterioration at the end of treatment.

Pharmacokinetics

Pharmacokinetic parameters were evaluated in 40 patients on day 1, day 3, and day 15 (pre-dose; Supplementary Table S3, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365). Irinotecan and its active metabolite, SN-38, reached a median plasma concentration of 24 200 ng/ml and 2640 pg/ml, respectively, at the end of liposomal irinotecan injection infusion. The median plasma concentration of oxaliplatin was 1650 ng/ml on day 1, following oxaliplatin infusion. The observed individual patient plasma concentration profiles of irinotecan and SN-38 are shown in Supplementary Figure S2, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365.

The median predicted maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) at steady state in this study was 19.0 (range 10.8-26.9) μg/ml for irinotecan and 3.60 (range 1.50-8.70) ng/ml for SN-38. The median predicted area under the curve at steady state was 2.50 (range 0.6-7.5) μg x week/ml for irinotecan and 2.40 (range: 1.0-5.4) ng x week/ml for SN-38. The median predicted half-life (t1/2) of irinotecan was 1.0 (range 1.0-1.6) days.

The probability of grade ≥3 neutropenia was higher in patients with greater exposure to irinotecan or SN-38 (Supplementary Figure S3A, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365). As there were only two reports of grade ≥3 diarrhoea, no conclusions can be drawn on the probability of developing a grade ≥3 diarrhoea event with increasing irinotecan or SN-38 exposure (Supplementary Figure S3B, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365). The relationship between OS/PFS and average plasma concentration of irinotecan and SN-38 was evaluated in exploratory analyses, but no statistically significant relationship was observed (Supplementary Figure S4, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmogo.2026.100365).

Discussion

The efficacy and safety of NALIRIFOX for first-line treatment of patients with mPAC have been previously demonstrated in the NAPOLI-3 trial. In this study, NALIRIFOX showed a similar efficacy and safety profile in a Japanese population to that observed in the NAPOLI-3 trial.

In the NAPOLI-3 trial, 41.8% of patients receiving NALIRIFOX had an OR,15 which is similar to the OR observed in this Japanese population (41.5%). The median OS of patients treated with NALIRIFOX within the NAPOLI-3 trial was 11.1 months (95% CI 10.0, 12.1),15 while the median OS of Japanese patients treated with NALIRIFOX in this study was 9.9 months (95% CI 7.4, 12.9). Despite the median OS of Japanese patients treated with NALIRIFOX being marginally shorter than those treated in NAPOLI-3, the 6-month survival rate in this study was similar to that in the NAPOLI-3 trial (73.2% and 72.4%, respectively). The PFS (per investigator review) of patients treated with NALIRIFOX demonstrated in NAPOLI-3 was 7.4 months (95% CI 6.0, 7.7).15

The PFS of Japanese patients treated with NALIRIFOX in this study was slightly lower [5.6 months (95% CI 3.5, 7.4) per investigator tumour assessment and 4.4 months (95% CI 2.3, 7.2) per ICR] than the NAPOLI-3 population. Median PFS is influenced by the timing of progression among patients who do not respond to treatment.23,24 The numerically shorter PFS observed in this Japanese population is therefore plausibly explained by earlier progression in a limited number of patients who did not respond to NALIRIFOX treatment. Given the small sample size in this study (n = 41), the timing of progression events can materially affect the Kaplan–Meier estimate of the median; and such variability is expected in small cohorts.23,24 As a result, a numerically lower median PFS may be observed despite comparable treatment activity with the NALIRIFOX arm of NAPOLI-3. From the baseline characteristics of patients receiving NALIRIFOX in the present study, there is no obvious explanation for this numerically lower median PFS, and random variation in the small sample reported here may be the most likely explanation. Nevertheless, the consistent safety profile and encouraging OR support the clinical utility of NALIRIFOX in Japanese patients across a broader age range. The primary endpoint of this study was to demonstrate an OR higher than the gemcitabine + nab-paclitaxel arm in the NAPOLI-3 trial (36.2%)15; therefore, no formal statistical hypotheses or comparison with the NAPOLI-3 study were carried out. Overall, despite the shorter PFS compared with NAPOLI-3, the other assessed endpoints (OS, OR, and DoR) support the clinical consistency of NALIRIFOX between this study and the NAPOLI-3 trial. Additionally, the time to response observed in this study was shorter than that observed in NAPOLI-3 (2.0 versus 3.6 months, respectively).15

The incidence of TEAEs in patients receiving NALIRIFOX was generally similar in the Japanese population in this study to the wider population in NAPOLI-3. However, there was a higher incidence of neutropenia and a lower incidence of gastrointestinal events in this Japanese population compared with the global NAPOLI-3 population.15 This is consistent with previous analysis of the NAPOLI-1 trial, which showed that neutropenia was more frequently reported in Asian patients treated with liposomal irinotecan.25 It has been shown that patients heterozygous and homozygous for UGT1A1∗6 are more likely to develop neutropenia in response to irinotecan than patients with a wild-type genotype,26 due to a higher exposure to SN-38.27 It has also been shown that the UGT1A1∗6 polymorphism is more frequent in Asian populations; however, the frequency of UGT1A1 polymorphisms was low in this study, and therefore, conclusions on the impact of UGT1A1 polymorphisms on the incidence of irinotecan-induced AEs cannot be made in this study.28 The decreased incidence of gastrointestinal events in this study compared with NAPOLI-3 could be due to higher use of loperamide hydrochloride in the Japanese population compared with the NAPOLI-3 population (43.9% versus 31.6%, respectively).15 The pharmacokinetic modelling reported here suggests that higher exposure of irinotecan and SN-38 increased the probability of grade ≥3 neutropenia. However, the reliability of the results of these exploratory analyses is limited by the small sample size in this study.

The most common grade 3-4 TEAEs in patients receiving NALIRIFOX in NAPOLI-3 were diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, and neutropenia.15 In this Japanese population, the most common grade 3-4 TEAEs were decreased appetite, neutrophil count decrease, and anaemia. No Japanese patients receiving NALIRIFOX in this study died due to TEAEs deemed to be related to any drug within the regimen. This is similar to the NAPOLI-3 trial, where only a small number (2%) of patients died due to treatment-related TEAEs.15 Overall, NALIRIFOX had a similar safety profile to that observed in the global NAPOLI-3 population and no new safety signals were observed in the Japanese population.

It has been previously reported that Asian patients with mPAC have a higher clearance of total irinotecan compared with patients of other ethnic backgrounds.29 Similarly, Asian patients have been reported to have lower levels of total irinotecan following administration of liposomal irinotecan compared with Caucasian patients.30 This is consistent with the findings of this study, which showed that the median t1/2 of irinotecan was shorter in the Japanese patients included in this study than that of the NAPOLI-3 trial population (data on file). However, there was no association between average irinotecan or SN-38 plasma concentration and OS/PFS in this study, potentially due to the small sample size of this study. Further work is needed to investigate the pharmacokinetic differences in Japanese patients, which may contribute to differences in efficacy and toxicity in these patients.

Japanese patients treated with NALIRIFOX maintained global health status/QoL across the duration of this study. In NAPOLI-3, patients treated with NALIRIFOX experienced an initial decline in global health status from baseline to week 12, with no further deterioration from week 16 onwards.31 Functional scores of Japanese patients treated with NALIRIFOX across emotional, cognitive, and social domains were maintained throughout the study, which is consistent with the results of NAPOLI-3.31 In this study, the diarrhoea score showed repeated increases (>10 points) at multiple timepoints throughout the study until the end of treatment, indicating sustained deterioration. This is consistent with results from NAPOLI-3, in which the symptom burden of diarrhoea increased between baseline and week 12 (least square mean change: 24.6).31

A limitation of this study is the potential for bias accompanied with an open-label design. Similarly, the single-arm design of this study means that no direct comparison to other regimens can be made. There may be certain prognostic biases within the included population. For example, patients in this study were slightly younger, had fewer metastatic sites, and were more likely to have an ECOG PS of 0 compared with NAPOLI-3, which could reduce the comparability of these two studies. The small sample size presents a limitation of this study; however, the sample size was calculated to allow an 80% probability of observing a higher rate of OR than achieved in the comparator arm of the NAPOLI-3 trial.

In conclusion, the data presented here are similar to previous experience with NALIRIFOX in the global population of the NAPOLI-3 clinical trial and support the use of this regimen in Japanese patients with treatment-naïve mPAC.

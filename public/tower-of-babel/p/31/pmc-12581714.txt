Research note: First report of very virulent infectious bursal disease virus in benin: Molecular characterization and implications for transboundary spread
Authors: Haoqian Wang, Remi Cossi CODJIA, Huiyou Xiao, Chunmei Wang, Xia Yang, Xinwei Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV) is an immunosuppressive disease pathogen that causes substantial economic losses to the global poultry industry. This study confirmed an outbreak of very virulent infectious bursal disease (vvIBD) in a 33-day-old broiler flock in the Republic of Benin, despite three prior vaccinations against IBD. The VP2 gene of the field vvIBDV strain in the outbreak farm was cloned, sequenced. Phylogenetic analysis revealed that the Benin field B123 shared the highest similarity with both the reference strain GHD1 from Vietnam (98.5 %) and the reference strain NN1172 from China (98.4 %). These three virus strains were located in the same evolutionary branch, displayed the closest genetic relationship, and all belonged to the very virulent IBDV (vvIBDV) strains. The close genetic relationship among the strains from Benin, China, and Vietnam suggests the possibility of transnational transmission events and highlights the global circulation of this vvIBDV lineage. Additionally, in the hyper-variable region of VP2 of isolate B123, there were amino acid residue markers of very virulent IBDV strains (222A, 242I, 256I, 294I, and 299S), however unique amino acid residue mutations or recombinations (187V and 430V) occurred in the conserved regions. Whether they are related to the protein's function remains to be further studied. This study provides important genetic data on vvIBDV circulating in West Africa and contributes to the global epidemiological understanding of IBDV evolution.

Introduction

Infectious bursal disease (IBD) is an acute, highly contagious and immunosuppressive avian disease caused by infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV). Infected birds typically exhibit clinical symptoms such as diarrhea, dehydration and immunosuppression, and are prone to secondary infections by bacteria or other viruses. IBDV significantly impacts sustainable and healthy development of the worldwide poultry industry (Wang et al., 2019). Currently, according to the new genotype classification scheme, circulating IBDVs include the classic (cIBDV), variant (varIBDV), very virulent (vvIBDV), attenuated (atIBDV) and novel variant strains (nVarIBDV) (Zhang et al., 2022). IBDV genome consists of two segments of double-stranded RNA (dsRNA), segments A and B. The VP2 protein in segment A has a hyper-variable region (HVR) located between amino acids 206 and 350. Mutations within the HVR may result in changes to IBDV in virulence, tissue tropism, and antigenicity (Asfor et al., 2022). The HVR of VP2 protein is regarded as the main region of antigenic variation of IBDV, so it is widely used in molecular diagnosis and genetic evolution analysis of IBDV (Pikuła et al., 2023). Recently, although most countries worldwide have managed to effectively control IBD, IBD outbreaks still take place in some resource-deficient countries, regions, or farms. This significantly impacts the poultry production and economic development in those areas.

In this study, we report the first vvIBD outbreak in an immunized chicken flock in Benin, based on comprehensive clinical diagnosis. The VP2 gene analysis of the field IBDV from diseased chickens in the case further revealed the genetic variations and transmission dynamics of the vvIBDV, elucidated the cause of vaccine breakthrough disease in vaccinated flocks, and thereby has laid a foundation for the prevention and control of IBD and the development of novel vaccines in Benin.

Materials and methods

Experimental materials

The RNA simple Total RNA Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China; Cat. No. DP419), pMD18-T Vector (Takara, Kusatsu, Japan; Cat. No. 6011), and TIANgel Midi Purification Kit (TIANGEN, Beijing, China; Cat. No. DP209-02).

The primers (VP2-F: ATGACAAACCTGCAAGATCAAACC; VP2-R: TGGCCCTAATTATGTCTTTGAAGC) were designed using Primer 5.0 software based on the VP2 sequence of IBDV (accession number OM307063.1) available in GenBank. These primers were synthesized by Sangon Biotech (Shanghai, China), and the expected amplification fragment size was 1351 bp.

Sample collection, processing and identification

Tissue samples were collected from chicks, then ground and subjected to three freeze-thaw cycles. After centrifugation, the supernatant was collected. Viral RNA was extracted using the RNA simple Total RNA Kit. Subsequently, it was detected by RT-PCR. Then, the PCR products were recovered using the TIANGel Midi Purification Kit, cloned into the pMD18T vector, and the correctly cloned positive plasmids were sequenced. The sequencing results were uploaded to NCBI, with the accession number PQ112744.

Genetic analysis

The VP2 gene sequences were analyzed using multiple bioinformatics tools. Phylogenetic analysis was conducted using MEGA7 software. Data plotting and visualization were performed using Origin 2024. For protein structure prediction and modeling, the online servers Phyre2 (https://www.sbg.bio.ic.ac.uk/phyre2) and SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org) were employed.

Results and discussion

The chicks showed symptoms of listlessness, drooping wings, standing still, and white, watery diarrhea (Fig. 1A-a). Necropsy revealed the following gross lesions: hemorrhages and necrosis in the leg muscles (Fig. 1A-b); the kidneys were swollen and bleeding, with urate deposition and a mottled appearance (Fig. 1A-c); the bursa of Fabricius had varying degrees of lesions (Fig. 1A-d, e) (either swollen or atrophied or with purple-grape-like necrotic bleeding); bleeding was observed at the junction of the proventriculus and gizzard (Fig. 1A-f). The above symptoms of ill birds indicated the flock was suspected to be IBD. And then, RT-PCR detection demonstrated IBDV infection in the farm. The total number of chicks in this chicken farm is 10,980. During the ten-day course of the disease, a total of 508 chicks showed symptoms. Among them, 353 chicks died and 155 chicks recovered after treatment. The cumulative incidence of IBD in this chicken farm is 4.63 %, the mortality rate is 3.22 %, and the case fatality rate is 69.49 %. Although the chicks were vaccinated with the IBD vaccine three times (subcutaneously injected with “Vaxxitek (HVT + IBD) + RISPENS” on the 1st day after hatching; orally administered “CEVA Gumbo L” on the 15th day; and intramuscularly or subcutaneously injected with “CEVA IBDL” on the 29th day), when the flock faced the field vvIBDV, the vaccine regimen failed to provide adequate protection against IBD, which is consistent with the situation in Egypt (Samy et al., 2020). Furthermore, immunization failure against IBD is associated mainly with early vaccination in flocks of unclear immune status and with the evolution of viruses circulating in the field, leading to antigenic drift and a sharp alteration in pathogenicity (Boudaoud et al., 2016; Wu et al., 2020). The prevalent vvIBDV strain in this flock and the vaccine strain (cIBDV) belong to distinct subtypes within serotype I. Phylogenetic analysis of the VP2 gene suggested a distant genetic relationship between them. As a result, the vaccine provided incomplete protection against vvIBDV, which may have contributed to the vaccination failure observed in the flock in the case.

Analysis of the IBDV VP2 gene revealed that nucleotide sequence similarity between isolate B123 and reference strains varied from 81.6 % to 98.5 % (Fig. 1B). It exhibited the highest similarity with the very virulent reference strains GHD1 (98.5 %) and NN1172 (98.4 %). Phylogenetic analysis showed that isolate B123 clustered within the same clade as the Chinese strain NN1172 and the Vietnamese strain GHD1 (Fig. 1C), indicating a close genetic relationship and confirming it as a vvIBDV. The close phylogenetic relationship between the Beninese B123 isolate and vvIBDV strains from Asia suggests that the outbreak likely originated from a genetically similar lineage, potentially introduced through international channels. It is understood that there is frequent trade in livestock products and other items between Benin and countries such as Vietnam and other Asian countries. Moreover, insufficient biosecurity measures in Benin farms makes it a potential transmission route. However, the precise route of introduction into Benin remains to be elucidated.

Through alignment analysis of the VP2 amino acid sequence (Table 1 and Fig. 1D), it was observed that the key amino acid residues in the hyper-variable region of VP2 of isolate B123 are 222A, 242I, 256I, 294I, and 299S, which were consistent with those of vvIBDV. The structural domains of VP2 protein are categorized as follows: the Base domain, the Shell domain, and the Projection domain. Notably, alignment of the VP2 amino acid sequences showed that, compared to highly virulent strains, the isolate B123 acquired two unique amino acid residues, 187V and 430V, in the conserved regions (Table 1). Through three-dimensional protein structure prediction and analysis, the 187V is located in the Base domain and may be related to the assembly and stability of virions. The position of the 430V amino acid residue could not be shown in the VP2 protein within the prediction model, but 430V is located in the conserved region of the C-terminus, which belongs to the extended part of the Projection domain and may be involved in the interaction between the virus and host receptors or the regulation of capsid stability (Bao et al., 2022). While research on VP2 previously focused primarily on the HVR, the functional significance of variations in the conserved regions remains poorly understood. Therefore, further investigation is required to elucidate whether the 187V and 430V substitutions identified in the Benin B123 strain influence viral pathogenicity, antigenicity, or other biological functions (Pikuła et al., 2023). VvIBDV-infection has not been previously reported in the Republic of Benin, which is economically ranked among the least developed countries globally. This outbreak of vvIBD in the country is attributed to a combination of risk factors that include poor sanitation and hygiene across the livestock and poultry supply chain (during introduction, slaughter, processing, and retail), inadequate infectious disease surveillance, the potential role of migratory wild birds in virus dissemination (Orakpoghenor et al., 2020), a critical shortage of veterinary professionals, irregular use of vaccines, and insufficient vaccination coverage (Ekiri et al., 2021), compounded by other deficient capacity husbandry management. As previously mentioned, the cross-border transmission of vvIBDV to Benin via international trade may also be one of the causes of the emergence of vvIBDV at the farm (based on genetic evolutionary analysis of VP2 gene). Undoubtedly, the fact further suggests that vvIBDV is spreading globally, and it is particularly necessary to establish a global network for monitoring IBDV in order to prevent and control the disease.

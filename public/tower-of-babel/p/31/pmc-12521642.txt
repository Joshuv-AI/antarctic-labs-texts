HGOA-based framework for multi-objective optimization and performance prediction of PEM fuel cells
Authors: Kamal, Manish Kumar Singla, Ramesh Kumar, Ramji Gupta, Pradeep Jangir, Arpita
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article presents a new Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) that can be used to model Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cells (PEMFCs) with high accuracy and optimize multi-objectives. PEMFCs play a central role in clean energy systems because of their high efficiency, zero emission, and their use in transportation, portable power, and stationary energy solutions. Nevertheless, they have nonlinear electrochemical dynamics and are sensitive to operating conditions, which are major challenges in parameter identification and performance prediction. Current metaheuristic algorithms are usually plagued by premature convergence, inefficient computation and instability when solving such complex, multi-variable systems. The new HGOA overcomes these drawbacks by combining elite retention, opposition-based learning, feasibility repair, and local search with the standard Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA). These modifications make convergence faster, more diverse in solutions and more robust in parameter tuning. Validation was performed on seven PEMFC test cases (FC1–FC7) with various operating conditions. The findings indicate that HGOA has better accuracy with the lowest Absolute Error (AE = 0.0026), Relative Error Percentage (RE% = 0.0613%) and almost zero Mean Bias Error (MBE) recorded in all cases. Moreover, HGOA is computationally more efficient, stable and predictively reliable than nine state-of-the-art metaheuristic algorithms, such as GOA, ECO, RIME, EO, and PO.Keywords: Hybrid grasshopper optimization algorithm (HGOA), Proton exchange membrane fuel cell (PEMFC), Multi-objective optimization, Metaheuristic algorithms, Performance predictionSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Proton exchange membrane fuel cells (PEMFCs) are increasingly used in clean energy applications owing to their eco-compatibility and efficiency1. Owing to the nonlinear, complex electrochemical dynamics of the systems involved, accurate modeling and parameter adjustment under various working conditions is a challenge. Here Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) concept can be constructed to enhance the core Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA) by incorporating mechanisms like elite preservation, opposition-based learning, feasibility repair, and local search into GOA2. Here, in this paper, we make a conscious effort to improve the prediction accuracy and calculation speed of PEMFC performance estimation by undertaking special efforts on power generation and voltage.

Background and motivation

It is among the most important reasons why PEM fuel cells have been thought of as a foundation technology in efforts towards clean, efficient, and sustainable energy systems3. Owing to these constraints, increased practice of using data-driven and optimization-based model techniques as a tool in relieving these constraints in an attempt to overcome these constraints4. Concerning other forms of fuel cells, they are particularly preferred because of extremely high energy conversion efficiency, no on-site emissions of greenhouse gases, and the capability of operating at relatively low temperatures compared to other forms of fuel cells. The application of PEMFCs to transportation (e.g., hydrogen fuel cell vehicles) is being contemplated significantly, as well as portable power systems, standby power systems, and stationary combined heat and power (CHP) systems5. No one can deny the fact that PEMFCs have become a more viable technology in response to the growing necessity for transitioning to renewable and low-carbon sources of energy. Increasingly, governments and industries are heavily investing in hydrogen-based technologies based on PEMFCs across the world. They can be used in villages and towns due to the fact that they are scalable and run quietly, thereby helping to decentralize energy supplies and increase the reliability of the grid.

However, despite all the huge promises of PEMFCs, they do face certain major challenges and therefore are not widely utilized. One of the important aspects in this regard is that there must be cost, durability, and performance optimization under varying conditions of operation6 to make them more practical; there must be the formation of credible models of fuel cell behavior so that these challenges can be addressed under different loads, temperatures, and environmental conditions. High-confidence modeling is important not only in determining performance but also in control strategy setting, fault diagnosis, and system integration. However, PEMFC modeling is of a complex type because of the nonlinear character of electrochemical processes and the dynamic character of electric networks. For instance, several parameters like membrane hydration, electrode kinetics, gas transport, and temperature contributions can contribute to a significant impact on the voltage–current relationship of a PEMFC. In order to properly address such dependencies, highly complex mathematical models have to be developed along with a large set analysis7. A classical empirical or semi-empirical model is typified by curve fitting to data, which has no high degree of generalizability across conditions if it is data-based empirical. Another issue is that such models will most likely suggest fixed parameters that cannot be able to react to changes in the operating environment on a real-time basis. Because of the stiffness of this basic idea, models that have been applied in actual systems are plagued by a loss of performance.

Optimization and data-driven models are increasingly being used to address these constraints8. Most of these algorithms rely on computational intelligence, which enables them to dynamically tweak model parameters to enhance the accuracy of predictions. Due to the nonlinear as well as dynamic nature of the electric process, it becomes inherently challenging for PEMFC models to make correct calculations since the nature of the electrochemical process requires a nonlinear and dynamic process9 A metaheuristic algorithm has been realized as one of the most useful tools at hand presently to tackle all of the complexity, nonlinearity, and multi-objective nature of PEMFC modeling and optimization, and it has played a major role in tackling these issues for decades10–12. Despite the numerous different optimization algorithms that are available, metaheuristic optimization algorithms have come to be used more and more due to their adaptable nature, global search ability, and ability to tackle multi-objective problems13.

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is recognized as one of the commonly used algorithms in this field14. However, problems still exist in the metaheuristics field. Several algorithms are plagued by premature convergence, a lack of diversity in the search space, and high computational cost. Therefore, there is an urgent need for more robust, precise, and computationally flawless optimization platforms. This paper fills in the gaps by introducing a Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) designed specifically for PEMFC modeling. The presented method combines various enhancement strategies within the standard GOA architecture to offer better performance in terms of accuracy, convergence rate, and robustness.

Literature review

Metaheuristic optimization in PEMFC research

Metaheuristic methods are increasingly precious tools for addressing the nonlinear, complex, and multi-objective character of the PEMFC modeling and optimization17. Among the most applied algorithms in this sense are the Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), the Differential Evolution (DE), the Genetic Algorithms (GA), and the Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA). Each of these techniques has inherent strengths, as well as significant weaknesses when implemented in real-world PEMFC applications. While these techniques are useful, they too have their limitations. One shared weakness for all of them is that obtaining a proper balance between exploration (attempting to discover new regions) and exploitation (improving on good solutions that are presently known)18 is challenging. GOA has been found to be capable of local optima avoidance and permitting diverse search spaces in PEMFCs19. Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) takes cues from the collective behavior of a bird flock or fish school. It is predicated on social information sharing among particles in a population with an aim to search for the best regions of the solution space. PSO has also been used in PEMFC modeling for parameter estimation and control optimization20. While PSO is known to provide fast convergence and simplicity of implementation, it becomes prone to premature convergence and diversity loss at subsequent iterations, limiting its capacity to escape local optima.

Figure 2 displays a systematic taxonomy of metaheuristic algorithms, depicting population-based methods frequently employed in PEMFC modeling. It graphically separates groups like evolutionary, swarm-based, and hybrid algorithms, where HGOA is in the next-generation hybrid category. The figure underlines how HGOA merges ideas from multiple approaches, elite preservation, opposition-based learning, feasibility repair, and local search, and provides enhanced adaptability and convergence compared to traditional algorithms. This visual context sets the stage for viewing HGOA’s strengths in solving complex optimization problems. Differential Evolution (DE) is a population-based optimizer because of its global optimization robustness and efficiency. DE has been highly successful in PEMFC applications, especially parameter tuning. DE’s performance depends significantly on the control parameters of mutation and crossover. Incorrect adjustment of the control parameters can result in stagnation or loss of global search ability.

Categorization of metaheuristic algorithms, with a focus on population-based algorithms used in this study. The hereinafter proposed Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA), being an algorithm based on swarm intelligence, has many improvements to speed up convergence and precision.

Genetic Algorithms (GA), which draw on the mechanisms of natural selection and genetics, have been favorably used in optimizing PEMFC. GA can deal with multi-objective problems and vast search spaces but is computationally demanding. Additionally, the requirement to make an efficient choice of selection, crossover, and mutation strategies further contributes to the inherent complexity of implementation and parameter tuning. The Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA) is a comparatively new member of the swarm intelligence-based algorithm family. Following grasshopper swarming in nature, GOA achieves exploration–exploitation balance by means of interaction forces between agents. GOA has been beneficial in PEMFCs with its local optima avoiding characteristic and ability to adapt to multiple spaces of search21. But its performance deteriorates with time unless diversity is assured, and it can be necessary for improvement mechanisms to correct convergence efficiency. It is so because the PEMFCs’ performance is highly sensitive to minute variations in the system parameters like membrane conductivity, exchange current density, temperature, pressure, and relative humidity22.

Although they are effective, the algorithms are far from flawless. One of the primary disadvantages of all of them is the inability to achieve the optimal balance between exploration (trying new regions) and exploitation (optimizing current good solutions)23. In multi-objective optimization problems where the objectives conflict with each other, for example, minimizing absolute error while maximizing efficiency, the algorithms are not able to build an evenly distributed Pareto front24,25. In addition, classical metaheuristics do not usually have a way of dynamically adjusting their search strategy during the optimization process. This inability to adapt can lead to less optimal search behavior, particularly for systems such as PEMFCs, where performance criteria are extremely sensitive to variations in parameters. The second limitation is the computational cost. Metaheuristic algorithms will generally demand high levels of function evaluations, which are costly if each function evaluation is a complete PEMFC simulation or experimental testing. This handicap limits their application in real-time applications or in cases when adaptation needs to be fast. In an effort to overcome such constraints, scholars have been increasingly depending on hybrid algorithms, which integrate the strength of two or more methodologies. For example, local search methods meeting global search algorithms facilitate precise solutions. Likewise, techniques such as elite preservation, chaotic initialization, and opposition-based learning have been proposed to maintain diversity and avoid stagnation26. Briefly, whereas PSO, DE, GA, and GOA all present valuable techniques of PEMFC optimization, each of their implementations lacks for multi-objective, large-scale, and real-time applications. The Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) proposed here attempts to overcome these shortcomings by adding refinements to the GOA framework, thereby providing an efficient and timely solution optimized for challenging fuel cell optimization problems27,28.

The current development of renewable energy systems and machine learning has contributed greatly to fault detection, optimization, and performance prediction in sustainable power generation. Machine learning has been studied to detect faults in solar panels29, optimize the size of hybrid energy systems30, and meta-heuristic algorithms to optimize standalone power solutions31. Also, deep learning methods have been used in predicting power of wind turbines31,32 and automatic defect detection in solar cells33. Additional work involves optimization techniques of distributed generation34, robust control techniques of motor drives35, adaptive load frequency control36, and better maximum power point tracking (MPPT) techniques37. The contributions bring out the increasing use of intelligent algorithms in renewable energy systems to enhance efficiency and reliability.

Limitations of existing methods

While these algorithms have significantly enhanced PEMFC modeling, they still have some constraints. More significantly, it is never easy to strike a balance between exploration and exploitation. Algorithms have the tendency to converge prematurely, are non-adaptive, and computationally intensive, especially in simulating or experimentally verifying PEMFC models. These constraints limit their use in real-time or dynamic applications Aside from that, traditional metaheuristics do not adaptively alter their search behavior according to improvement, resulting in suboptimal optimization of complex, nonlinear landscapes. Computational demand is another barrier, as function evaluations in PEMFC contexts are resource-intensive.

Justification for a hybrid approach

To evade these limitations, hybrid algorithms have been designed since then. These architectures combine complementary methods to provide the best results. Elite retention retains best solutions; opposition-based learning improves exploration; local search optimizes promising solutions; and feasibility repair maintains valid, bounded solutions. The Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) suggested here incorporates these features with GOA and forms a powerful and robust optimizer specific to PEMFC optimization. The hybrid strategy aids in convergence, diversity maintenance, and avoids excessive computational cost, which is beneficial in the simulation of the dynamic performance of fuel cells under various operation conditions.

The research makes a significant contribution to the modeling and optimization of Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cell (PEMFC) through the development of a new Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) to overcome the major shortcomings of the current metaheuristic methods. Mathematical modelling of PEMFC

Basic concept

The essential architecture of a Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cell (PEMFC) is characterized by an anode and a cathode, separated by a proton-conducting membrane acting as the solid polymer electrolyte. A schematic of this configuration is presented in Fig. 3.

The membrane’s selective permeability, allowing proton conduction while impeding electron transfer38, is fundamental. Catalyst layers are employed to enhance the kinetics of the electrochemical reactions. Hydrogen gas, supplied to the anode, undergoes catalytic dissociation, yielding protons and electrons. Protons are transported across the membrane to the cathode catalyst layer, while electrons are directed through an external electrical circuit. In parallel, an oxygen-containing gas (oxygen or air) is introduced to the cathode. At the cathode catalyst layer, oxygen undergoes electrochemical reduction by reacting with protons transported across the membrane and electrons supplied via the external circuit, yielding water. Grasshopper optimization algorithm (GOA): an overview

Biological inspiration

The Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (GOA) is a bio-inspired swarm intelligence optimization algorithm introduced in 2017 by Saremi, Mirjalili, and Lewis47. The algorithm takes its inspiration from the natural swarming behavior of grasshoppers, i.e., their adult and nymph swarming patterns. Grasshoppers search across vast regions at early development and eventually make more directed, goal-oriented movements. Two-stage behavior is naturally inclined to a metaphorical formulation of optimization problems as a balance between exploration and exploitation.

Algorithm dynamics

GOA models grasshopper interaction when three forces are acting: wind advection, social contact, and gravity. Each grasshopper (candidate solution) translates to a new location based on a vector sum of the three forces, expressed mathematically in terms of attraction–repulsion dynamics in terms of inter-agent distance. Convergence towards exploration–exploitation is achieved by a convergence control parameter that slowly steers the swarm over time.

Applications and limitations of GOA

GOA has been utilized in different applications such as engineering design, machine learning, wireless sensor networks, and renewable energy systems. Due to its capability to solve difficult, nonlinear, and multi-objective problems, GOA can be used in PEMFC modeling, where voltage, efficiency, etc., are sensitive and interdependent variables. Conventional GOA, however, possesses certain limitations:

Proposed hybrid grasshopper optimization algorithm (HGOA)

Motivation for hybridization

Though the primary GOA offers a solid foundation for optimization, it is plagued with various flaws such as premature convergence, inadequate exploitation, and stagnation of the population. To neutralize all such maladies, this study proposes a hybrid architecture HGOA by introducing elite retention, opposition-based learning (OBL), feasibility repair, and local search concepts into the GOA paradigm. Such hybrid features ensure an even more adaptive, stable, and efficient process of convergence, which is critical to accurately simulating PEMFCs48.

HGOA design and enhancements

Elite Retention is an optimization strategy that keeps the best performers of every generation and passes them on to the next. The policy prevents good solutions from being lost due to random fluctuations in the search process. For PEMFC optimization, where precise modeling relies on good parameter settings, retaining elite candidates is what stabilizes and hastens convergence. Besides, elite retention gives a robust convergence direction by constantly pointing to promising areas of the search space. Opposition-Based Learning (OBL) extends the algorithm’s search capability beyond the current candidate solutions by examining their opposite counterparts as well. OBL’s process enhances the search chances of finding regions with maximum value using fewer iterations by distributing candidate checks more uniformly throughout the search space. OBL can especially be useful in complex or high-dimensional search spaces like PEMFC modeling, where old algorithms scan sparser regions ineffectively. With the use of symmetrical diversity, OBL averts the premature convergence characteristic in unimodal optimization landscapes49.

Local Search Strategies focus on exploitation through the fine-tuning of solutions found by global search. Once candidate solutions are chosen, local search processes carry out intensive exploration within a specific neighborhood, allowing the algorithm to find a better optimum. Local search is an important function in HGOA towards the latter stages of optimization, wherein slight voltage forecasting or error minimization can lead to incredible performance improvement. In addition, it enables HGOA to avoid flat or deceptive areas of the fitness landscape, which often induce purely global optimizers astray50–52. Together, these hybrid components make GOA a more efficient and flexible algorithm to handle PEMFC’s multi-objective, nonlinear character. Metaheuristic hybridization enhances both the convergence rate and the quality of solutions by handling the three most important pillars of metaheuristic performance: exploration, exploitation, and diversity of solutions. Apart from more optimization mechanisms, the hybrid approaches enable more robustness of the algorithms concerning the system parameters or input data changes. This is particularly vital in practical applications of PEMFC, where the model parameters have to be adjusted rapidly when external disturbances arise (e.g., the levels of humidity, variable loads). The hybrid aspect of HGOA enables runtime model prediction adaptation with high accuracy and minimal computational cost.

Performance evaluation across seven PEMFC cases

The results of the performance analysis of the Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) in seven different PEMFC configurations showed a stable trend of accuracy, stability, and computational efficiency. These test cases covered a wide range of stack properties, such as differences in power output, cell count, active area, operating temperature, and gas pressures. The most notable aspect in all the cases was that HGOA was able to reach the lowest or equal-to-lowest minimum fitness values, converged very fast usually in the first 50 iterations and had minimal dispersion in results as indicated by extremely low standard deviation values. Absolute Error (AE) was generally within the range of 0.002–0.26, Relative Error Percentage (RE%) frequently remained below 1%, and Mean Bias Error (MBE) values were typically on the order of 10⁻5 to 10⁻3, confirming the algorithm’s precision in modeling PEMFC voltage–current and power–voltage characteristics.

In all cases, the graphical output, such as V–I/P–V plots, error distributions, boxplots, and convergence curves, graphically confirmed the quantitative results by indicating that the measured and estimated performance were close, the stability ranges were tight, and the optimal solutions were reached quickly. It has been demonstrated that the Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) is excellent in parameter estimation, convergence stability and computational efficiency in seven different PEMFC stacks. HGOA can always achieve the minimum Sum of Squared Errors (SSE) and the fitness values are 0.025493 and 0.283774 respectively of the BCS 500 W stack and STD-1 configuration. This excellent precision is far superior to other competing algorithms such as ECO and GOA which are more variable and less stable, particularly at high-current–density conditions. The hybrid mechanisms of HGOA, namely the elite preservation and the opposition-based learning enable a rapid convergence in all the tested stacks within 50 iterations as illustrated in the detailed convergence curves. In comparison, other conventional algorithms such as MFO and ESC require over 150 iterations to achieve similar fitness values. HGOA is also computationally efficient, with a time of 0.066–0.116 s per optimization, two orders of magnitude faster than other methods like ECO.

Absolute Error (AE) and Relative Error (RE%) are always used to confirm the accuracy of HGOA in the estimation of voltage and power. The high-power stacks such as the Ballard Mark V and Nedstack PS6 have equally low errors (AE: 0.124 0.211; RE%: 0.453 0.686). Remarkably, HGOA Mean Bias Error (MBE) is close to zero (3.61 × 10–5 to 0.006), which proves that systematic deviation is minimal. The competing algorithms, especially RIME and PO, report larger MBE values (up to 0.024), which means that they have inherent biases that reduce their reliability. Statistical dominance is also confirmed by Friedman ranking, where HGOA obtains ranks between 1.0 and 1.4 in all test cases, which is decisive over nine state-of-the-art algorithms. As an example, in the BCS 500 W set-up, HGOA obtains a score of 1.0 against GOA with 9.7. Such statistical strength highlights the flexibility of HGOA to different operating conditions of PEMFC such as temperature (302–343 K), pressure (0.4 3.0 bar), and membrane thickness I–V and P–V graphs all show that HGOA fits well with experimental results, with voltage errors of less than 0.1 V over the full current range in stacks such as the Horizon series. Conversely, the predictions made by ECO differ by more than 0.6 V during peak loads. Box plot analyses also demonstrate the outlier resistance of HGOA, with interquartile ranges that are 50 percent narrower than EDO or MFO.

Another strength of HGOA is its scalability since it effectively processes stacks of different sizes and complexities. In small stacks like the SR-12 (12 W), parameter optimization is finished within 0.079 s, and industrial-scale systems like the Nedstack PS6 (6 kW) have sub-0.12-s runtimes even though they consist of 65 cells. This scalability tackles a major drawback of the previous approaches, including PSO hybrids that tend to perform poorly on stacks larger than 1 MW. The innovations of HGOA, such as feasibility repair and local search, are put in the context of the modern research, and it proves that they are more beneficial than the recent hybrid algorithms. As an example, HGOA lowers SSE by 18 percent over Hybrid PSO on the BCS 500 W and converges 40 percent faster than GWO-ABC on the Ballard Mark V stack.

This unified analysis proves that HGOA is always capable of providing better parameter estimation of a broad range of PEMFC stack designs and operating conditions. Its capability to attain low error values, have very high stability, and converge quickly with low computation requirements makes it a very robust and scalable system to both small-scale laboratory systems and high-power industrial systems.

Conclusion

This paper introduces a Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA) that can be used to accurately model and multi-objectively optimize Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cells (PEMFCs). The suggested HGOA combines the elite preservation, opposition-based learning, feasibility repair, and local search to address the drawbacks of premature convergence, loss of population diversity, and slow exploitation that characterize traditional metaheuristics. Wide validation on seven PEMFC test cases (FC1–FC7) shows that HGOA is highly accurate in predicting the voltage and power outputs with minimal Absolute Error (AE 0.0026), Relative Error Percentage (RE% 0.0613%) and close to zero Mean Bias Error (MBE). HGOA performance is compared to nine state-of-the-art algorithms such as GOA, ECO, RIME, EO, and PO. The findings prove that HGOA performs better than these techniques in terms of convergence rate, stability, and computational cost. As an example, in the BCS 500 W case (Sheet 1), the fitness value of HGOA is 0.025493 which is much lower than that of ECO (0.028347) and GOA (0.048753). Such patterns can be seen in other test cases, Nedstack 600 W (Sheet 2), where RE% of HGOA (0.453869%) is significantly lower than that of RIME (0.609475%) and TEO (0.686998%). These advancements are consistent with the latest developments in hybrid metaheuristics to optimize PEMFCs, including the enhanced PSO algorithms introduced and the hybrid GWO algorithm, but at a lower computational expense.

The proposed Hybrid Grasshopper Optimization Algorithm (HGOA), despite the proven benefits, has some limitations that point to the areas of further research. A major weakness is that it is real-time adaptable, and the present study is mostly an assessment of HGOA in steady-state conditions. Although the algorithm demonstrates potential in dynamic PEMFC operation, its behavior under transient conditions or fast load variations, which are typical in practice, has not been experimentally verified yet. Moreover, the model is electrochemical-based and future work can be done to incorporate thermal and fluid dynamics to optimize the system further. Scalability is another important point since it would be required to test HGOA on bigger PEMFC stacks (more than 1 MW) to prove its efficiency in industrial-scale energy systems.

There are a number of promising avenues that future research directions may pursue. HGOA can be combined with deep learning models, e.g., LSTM or Transformer networks, to increase the accuracy of predictions in the environment of noisy real-world data. The practical applicability of the algorithm would also be enhanced by hardware-in-the-loop validation to apply the algorithm to embedded systems to real-time control of PEMFC in automotive or grid applications. Also, the extension of HGOA to multi-objective optimization, the concurrent optimization of cost, durability, and efficiency with Pareto-frontier analysis, may offer more comprehensive solutions to PEMFC deployment. Working on these areas would not only make the algorithm more robust, but also increase its applicability to general engineering problems.

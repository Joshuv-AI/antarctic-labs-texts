Cutaneous Mixed Pseudolymphoma Induced by a Gold Needle Embedded in the Back
Authors: Joon Ki Kim, Eun Ji Chun, Si Young Yang, Sang Seok Kim, Chul Woo Kim
Published: 2023

Dear Editor:

Cutaneous pseudolymphoma (CPL) refers to a group of benign lymphoproliferative skin disease that simulates cutaneous lymphoma. These diseases differ clinically and histologically and have various immunophenotypes1. Several types of causative factors have been reported, including drugs, infection, and tattoos. We report a case of mixed type pseudolymphoma induced by a gold thread embedded in the back for many years.

A 47-year-old man presented with a single erythematous nodular lesion on his mid-back, which has presented 10 years prior. He did not have any discomfort except intermittent pruritus. About 30 years previous, he had received acupuncture therapy using gold thread needles to treat back pain. He did not have any other underlying disease, and laboratory evaluation did not show any abnormality. Under suspicion of foreign body granuloma, excisional biopsy was performed for both diagnosis and treatment. Upon examination of the resected tumor, an embedded gold needle was observed (Fig. 1A). Histopathology revealed severe infiltration of lymphocytes and histiocytes without any foreign body granulomatous reaction (Fig. 1B). Immunohistochemical stains showed dense infiltration with cells positive for CD3 (pan T-cell marker) (Fig. 2A) and CD20 (B-cell marker) (Fig. 2B). Also, in IgH gene rearrangement, polyclonality was detected. A diagnosis of mixed type pseudolymphoma was made. After total excision, the patient did not complain of discomfort and was lost to follow-up.

(A) Gold needle observed in the excised tumor. (B) Severe lymphohistiocytic infiltration observed (H&E, ×40).

Immunohistochemical staining showed dense infiltration of both CD3 cells (A) and CD20 cells (B) (×20).

CPL is a lymphoproliferative skin disease that histopathologically and/or clinically resembles cutaneous lymphoma but usually has a benign course. Classification of CPLs can be approached in various ways, based on the predominating component of lymphocytes (T-cell, B-cell, or mixed) or the clinical features of the lesion2. CPL can have various clinical presentations, from erythroderma at an isolated nodule to clustered or disseminated papules. Gold is a known causative factor of CPL, reported in patients with history of gold piercing or gold acupuncture3,4. It has been proposed that gold can bind to skin and cause accumulation of macrophages in sensitive individuals, leading to recurrent pseudolymphoma4. Under classification of CPL, our patient was classified as nodular mixed-type pseudolymphoma. To our knowledge, there has been no report of CPL with both B-cell and T-cell types. Also, most CPL patients developed symptoms within a short period of time (less than one year) after gold acupuncture, and there was no case of a gold needle buried in the back, which makes our case clinically and histologically unique. Herein, we report a case of mixed-type pseudolymphoma induced by a gold needle embedded in the back for many years.

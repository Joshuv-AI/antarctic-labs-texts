Modification of the existing maximum residue level for ethofumesate in carrots
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Marta Szot, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, the applicant DLR Rheinpfalz submitted a request to the competent national authority in Germany to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) for the active substance ethofumesate in carrots. The data submitted in support of the request was found to be sufficient to derive an MRL proposal for carrots. Adequate analytical methods based on liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (LC‐MS/MS) are available for enforcement of the residues of ethofumesate on the commodity under consideration at the validated limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.03 mg/kg. Based on the risk assessment results, EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues resulting from the use of ethofumesate according to the reported agricultural practice is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

SUMMARY

In accordance with Article 6 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, DLR Rheinpfalz submitted an application to the competent national authority in Germany (evaluating Member State, EMS) to modify the existing maximum residue levels (MRLs) for the active substance ethofumesate in carrots.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) Central Submission System on 23 September 2024. The appointed EMS, Germany, assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 14 July 2025. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA, and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation ran from 18 August 2025 to 8 September 2025. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the EMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and to the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) on 27 February 2026. To accommodate for the intended NEU use of ethofumesate, the EMS proposed to raise the existing MRL in carrots from the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.03 mg/kg to 0.1 mg/kg.

On 26 March 2026, the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

Based on the conclusions derived by EFSA in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, the data evaluated under the MRL review assessment, and the additional data provided by the EMS in the framework of this application, the following conclusions are derived.

The metabolism of ethofumesate following pre‐ or post‐emergence foliar application was investigated in crops belonging to the group of root crops, leafy crops and cereals/grass in the framework of the MRL review and EU pesticides peer review.

Studies investigating the effect of processing on the nature of ethofumesate (hydrolysis studies) demonstrated that the active substance is stable. No data on the stability of metabolite 2‐keto–ethofumesate (NC 9607) were provided in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review, but this was not considered a data gap due to levels of NC 9607 below the LOQ in the crops with representative uses assessed. The same conclusion is valid for the present assessment since residues of NC 9607 in carrot samples from all residue trials were below the LOQ.

In rotational crops, the major residues identified were the parent compound and metabolite NC 9607. Rotational crop metabolism studies have shown that the metabolism of ethofumesate in rotational crops is similar to the metabolic pathway observed in primary crops.

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in metabolism studies, hydrolysis studies, the toxicological relevance of metabolites and the capabilities of enforcement analytical methods, the residue definitions for plant products were proposed by the EU pesticides peer review as sum of ethofumesate, 2‐keto–ethofumesate (NC 9607), open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate (NC 20645) and its conjugate, expressed as ethofumesate for enforcement and risk assessment. These residue definitions are applicable to primary crops, rotational crops and processed carrot products.

EFSA concluded that, for the crop assessed in this application, the metabolism of ethofumesate in primary and in rotational crops, and the possible degradation in processed products has been sufficiently addressed and that the previously derived residue definitions are applicable.

Sufficiently validated analytical methods based on liquid chromatography with tandem mass spectrometry (LC‐MS/MS) are available to quantify residues in carrots according to the enforcement residue definition. The methods enable quantification of residues at or above 0.03 mg/kg (LOQ).

The available residue trials are sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 0.1 mg/kg for carrots.

Specific studies investigating the magnitude of ethofumesate residues in processed carrot commodities are not required, as the contribution of carrots to the total theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) is below the trigger value of 10% of the ADI.

The occurrence of ethofumesate residues in rotational crops was investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review. Based on the available information on the nature and magnitude of residues, it was concluded that the occasional presence of residues in rotational crops above the LOQ could not be excluded in leafy crops and cereals. However, this issue is not specific to the crops under consideration given the less critical intended use assessed, according to the proposed good agricultural practice (GAP).

As carrot culls can be used as feed products, a potential carry‐over into food of animal origin was assessed. The calculated livestock dietary burdens exceeded the trigger value of 0.004 mg/kg bw/day for all relevant animal species. However, the contribution of ethofumesate residues in carrots from the intended use to the total livestock exposure was insignificant, and therefore, the nature and magnitude of ethofumesate residues in livestock were not investigated further.

As the proposed use of ethofumesate is intended on non‐melliferous crops, investigation of residues in honey is not relevant.

The toxicological profile of ethofumesate was assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 and the data were sufficient to derive an acceptable daily intake (ADI) of 1 mg/kg bw per day. An acute reference dose (ARfD) was deemed unnecessary. The metabolites included in the residue definition are of similar toxicity as the parent active substance.

The consumer risk assessment was performed with revision 3.1 of the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo). For the calculation of the chronic exposure, EFSA considered commodities assessed by the MRL review and by the EU pesticides peer review for which an MRL has been implemented in the EU MRL legislation. The crops on which no uses were reported in the MRL review or subsequent EU pesticides peer review were excluded from the exposure calculation. For carrots, the median residue value (STMR) as derived from the residue trials submitted was used as an input value. The highest estimated long‐term dietary intake accounted for 0.1% of ADI (NL child diet). The contributions of residues in carrots to the overall long‐term exposure accounted for a maximum of 0.004% of the ADI (DK child diet).

EFSA concluded that the proposed use of ethofumesate on carrots will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference value and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) received an application to modify the existing maximum residue level (MRL) for ethofumesate in carrots. The detailed description of the intended NEU use of ethofumesate, which is the basis for the current MRL application, is reported in Appendix A.

Ethofumesate was evaluated for renewal of approval in the framework of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009, 1 with Austria designated as rapporteur Member State (RMS), considering the representative uses as an herbicide on sugar beet, fodder beet and red beet. The draft renewal assessment report (RAR) prepared by RMS, Austria, has been peer reviewed by EFSA (2016). After completion of the MRL review, EFSA has issued no other reasoned opinion on the modification of MRLs for ethofumesate. 4 It is noted that no specific application for the evaluation of Article 12 confirmatory data for ethofumesate has been submitted by EU Member States within the legal deadline of 29 June 2018, as set by the footnotes in Regulation (EU) 2016/1016. 5

RESIDUES IN PLANTS

Nature of residues and methods of analysis in plants

Nature of residues in primary crops

The metabolism of ethofumesate in primary crops belonging to the group of root crops, leafy crops and cereals/grass has been investigated in the framework of the MRL review and EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2012, 2016).

In the crops tested, the metabolism of 14C ethofumesate was investigated following pre‐emergence application and post‐emergence foliar application to sugar beet (root crop), post‐emergence foliar application to ryegrass (cereals/grass) and tobacco (leafy crops) (non GLP) and pre‐emergence application to onions (root crops) (non GLP).

Metabolic patterns in the different studies were similar, with the metabolism of ethofumesate leading to the metabolites ethofumesate‐2‐hydroxy (NC 8493), ethofumesate lactone or 2‐keto–ethofumesate (NC 9607) and ethofumesate carboxylic acid (NC 20645), recovered also in their conjugated form. NC 20645 is the open ring form of NC 9607 and is referred to also as an open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate. Intra‐molecular ring closure appeared to be conditioned by the pH i.e. primarily when acidic conditions are applied to release the aglycon of conjugated residues.

The proportions observed of parent and metabolites, and their conjugates varied depending on the time, mode and rate of application as well as on the crop studied (EFSA, 2016). Commonly, until harvest of the mature primary crop, parent had been degraded to a significant extent, if not completely, into its metabolites, majorly present as conjugated compounds. The pertinent residue was identified as NC 20645, free and conjugated (EFSA, 2016).

The metabolic pathway of ethofumesate follows a similar pattern in all crop groups investigated.

The EU pesticides peer review concluded that parent ethofumesate and its metabolites ethofumesate‐lactone/2‐keto–ethofumesate (NC9607) and ethofumesate‐carboxylic‐acid/open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate (NC 20645), recovered also in their conjugated form, are the relevant residues in the primary crops investigated both for the risk assessment and enforcement (EFSA, 2016).

The plant metabolites NC 20645, NC 9607 and NC 8493 are covered by the toxicological endpoints of the parent compound (EFSA, 2016).

For the intended uses on the crops under consideration, the metabolic behaviour in primary crops is considered sufficiently addressed. It is noted that the application of the active substance on carrots is intended at a later growth stage (BBCH 47) than the range investigated in the metabolism studies (BBCH 14–16), which could possibly result in a lower metabolisation of parent ethofumesate in carrots.

Nature of residues in rotational crops

Carrots can be grown in a crop rotation, and therefore, the nature of ethofumesate in rotational crops has to be investigated.

According to the soil degradation studies evaluated in the framework of the peer review, the DT90 values for ethofumesate ranged from 72 to 522 days (EFSA, 2016). No other significant ethofumesate metabolites are formed in soil.

The rotational crop metabolism studies were assessed in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review. After soil application of 14C ethofumesate at a rate of 4.6 kg/ha, the nature of residues was investigated in radish, carrots, cabbage, spinach, wheat, ryegrass and French beans at plant back intervals of 30 days for carrots, spinach, ryegrass and French beans; 96, 276 and 367 days for radish and cabbage; and 157, 276 and 367 days for wheat.

Parent ethofumesate was recovered in almost all commodities tested (up to 55%–60% of the total radioactive residue (TRR)). Upon hydrolysis, significant portions of conjugated residues were identified as NC 9607 in most of the commodities (6.98% of the TRR), and in root crops also as NC 8493 (up to 8.4% of the TRR). The observation of residues of unchanged ethofumesate in rotational crops was presumably caused by the high persistence and low leaching potential of ethofumesate in some soils and likely increased availability in the root zone upon breaking up of the soil at planting of the following crop. On the basis of these results, it was concluded that the metabolism of [14C]‐ethofumesate in rotational crops follows the same metabolic path as primary crops (EFSA, 2016).

For the proposed use assessed in this application, no further investigation of the nature of residues in rotational crops is required.

Nature of residues in processed commodities

The effect of processing on the nature of ethofumesate was investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2016). These studies were conducted by simulating representative hydrolytic conditions for pasteurisation (20 min at 90°C, pH 4), baking/boiling/brewing (60 min at 100°C, pH 5), sterilisation (20 min at 120°C, pH 6) and other conditions (i.e. purification process sugar beet: 30 min, 90°C, pH 11).

The studies showed that ethofumesate is hydrolytically stable under all the tested processing conditions and that the parent is the main identified compound (> 98.6%).

No hydrolysis studies are available investigating the stability of metabolites NC 9607 and NC 20645. The EU pesticides peer review noted that it is known from the analytical methods that acidic conditions lead to rearrangement of NC 20645 and formation of NC 9607. Whether or not NC 9607 will remain entirely stable when processing conditions are applied has not been so far addressed but was not considered as a data gap by the EU pesticides peer review due to levels of NC 9607 below the LOQ in the crops from the representative uses (EFSA, 2016).

According to the carrot residue trials (Germany, 2026), the residues of NC 9607 were always below the LOQ (< 0.010 mg/kg); therefore, the same conclusion of the EU pesticides peer review applies to the current assessment.

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

Analytical methods for the determination of ethofumesate residues and residues of NC 20645 were assessed during the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2016). The common moiety NC 20645 is the analytical target for metabolite NC 20645 in free and conjugated form and as well for metabolite NC 9607 (Austria, 2015a, 2015b). In the assessed enforcement method (No 01392), the conjugate of NC 20645 was cleaved at low pH to its exocon and due to the low pH immediately transformed to NC 9607 by an intramolecular condensation reaction. In a separate step, NC 9607 (formed from the free and the conjugated form of NC 20645 at low pH and from metabolite NC 9607 itself) was converted at high pH to the common moiety NC 20645, which is the analyte target beside parent ethofumesate (Austria, 2015b).

Since the residue definition comprises the common moiety product as analytical target, all residue analytical methods for plant matrices need to include an acidic hydrolysis step. In the enforcement method, the hydrolysis was performed with concentrated hydrochloric acid, and the sample was boiled under reflux (Austria, 2015b).

The LC‐MS/MS method has been sufficiently validated at the individual LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg for each analyte (parent ethofumesate and common moiety NC 20645) in matrices with high water content, high oil content, high acid content, dry matrices and difficult (hops) commodities. An independent laboratory validation of the method was evaluated and accepted for the same matrix groups (EFSA, 2016).

The methods allow quantifying residues at or above the LOQ of 0.03 mg/kg for the total residue (determined as the sum of NC 20645 and NC 9607 (determined as NC20645), calculated as ethofumesate) in crops belonging to the group of high water content. It is therefore concluded that there is a sufficiently validated analytical method for the determination of ethofumesate residues according to the enforcement residue definition in carrots.

Extraction efficiency of conjugates was not demonstrated for the method as indicated according to the requirements of the extraction efficiency Guidance, SANTE 2017/10632 (European Commission, 2023b). However, hydrolysis condition was according to the metabolism study where hydrochloric acid under reflux was also used, which demonstrates sufficient cleavage of conjugates (EFSA, 2016).

Storage stability of residues in plants

The storage stability of NC 9607 and NC 20645 in plants stored under frozen conditions was investigated in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2016).

Ethofumesate was found to be stable for at least 12 months, and the metabolites NC 20645 and NC 9607 were found to be stable for at least 24 months at −18°C in a high‐water content commodity (sugar beet leaves) (EFSA, 2016).

Carrot samples from residue trials were stored frozen (−18°C) for up to 23 months prior to analysis and, according to available storage stability studies, no degradation of residues occurs during this storage interval in high‐water content commodities.

Proposed residue definitions

Based on the metabolic pattern identified in metabolism studies, the results of hydrolysis studies, the toxicological significance of metabolites and the capabilities of enforcement analytical methods, the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment were proposed for plant commodities by the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2016) as the ‘sum of ethofumesate, ethofumesate‐lactone (NC 9607), ethofumesate‐carboxylic acid (NC 20645) and its conjugate, expressed as ethofumesate’. The proposal took into account the fact that a reliable determination of the relevant ethofumesate residues is only possible if NC 9607 is included in the residue definition as a common moiety which comprises the determination of the metabolite NC 20645 (free and conjugated) plus metabolite NC 9607 itself due to occurring intra‐molecular rearrangements (EFSA, 2016).

The version of enforcement residue definition finally implemented Commission Regulation (No) (EU) 2016/1016 10 was slightly reworded as: Sum of ethofumesate, 2‐keto–ethofumesate, open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate and its conjugate, expressed as ethofumesate.

Sum of ethofumesate, 2‐keto–ethofumesate, open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate and its conjugate, expressed as ethofumesate.

Sum of ethofumesate, 2‐keto–ethofumesate, open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate and its conjugate, expressed as ethofumesate.

The same residue definitions are applicable to rotational crops.

EFSA concluded that these residue definitions are appropriate for the intended use on carrots, and no further modification is required.

Magnitude of residues in plants

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In support of the MRL application, the applicant submitted residue trials on carrots. The samples were analysed for the parent compound and the metabolites included in the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment.

NEU outdoor GAP on carrots, foliar application 2 × 300 g ethofumesate/ha; PHI 56 day.

In support of the MRL application, the applicant submitted GAP‐compliant residue trials performed in northern Europe on carrots (eight trials) in the growing seasons of 2017, 2018 and 2021 (Germany, 2026). All the trials were conducted in different regions of Germany. Residues of ethofumesate on carrots were analysed in the root only.

In the samples analysed, residues of ethofumesate were within the range from < 0.02 to 0.064 mg/kg, while residues of 2‐keto‐ethofumesate (NC 9607) and of open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate (NC 20645) were always detected below the LOQ (< 0.01 mg/kg). No residues above the LOQ were found in any of the untreated specimens.

The maximum storage period of deep‐frozen samples in the supervised residue trials (23 months) is covered by the storage stability studies. According to the assessment of the EMS, the method used was sufficiently validated for analysis of residues of ethofumesate (sum of 2‐keto‐ethofumesate and open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate and its conjugate, expressed as ethofumesate), using LC‐MS/MS at the individual LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (Germany, 2026). The method used the hydrolysis step (hydrochloric acid addition); all metabolites were converted to 2‐keto‐ethofumesate, which was then hydrolysed to open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate, and quantification was then performed by matrix‐matched external calibration with reference items of ethofumesate and open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate. EFSA notes that the extraction efficiency of the analytical method for conjugates was not provided but it is comparable to the metabolism study, which demonstrates sufficient cleavage of conjugates (see section 1.1.4). Therefore, extraction efficiency can be considered demonstrated.

According to the Technical Guidelines SANTE/2019/12752 (European Commission, 2025), the number of trials is sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 0.1 mg/kg in support of the intended NEU use of ethofumesate on carrots.

Magnitude of residues in rotational crops

The possible transfer of ethofumesate residues to crops that are grown in crop rotation has been assessed in MRL review and EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2012, 2016). In the EFSA conclusion (EFSA, 2016), it is stated that, based on the rotational crop metabolism study, residues in succeeding crops were found to be significant, and therefore, field studies were carried out.

In the available rotational crop field studies, ethofumesate was applied to bare soil or to the target crop sugar beet at an application rate of 1 kg a.s./ha, which reflected the projected rate for foliar treatment with ethofumesate in arable, non‐perennial crops (1.6N when compared to intended use of a.s. on carrots) After 25–33, 54–259 and 284–354 days of plant back interval (PBI), three crops were sown/planted: a root crop (carrots), a leafy crop (lettuce) and a small grain cereal (barley or wheat). The three sowing dates represented the three rotations. Samples of the rotational crops were taken at their respective harvest times, as well as at one earlier interval (immature RACs for lettuce and carrots, or fodder for barley and wheat). The samples were analysed for the parent compound and the common moiety NC 9607 with a respective LOQs of 0.01 mg/kg (Austria, 2015b). It is noted that the EU pesticides peer review identified a data gap for the storage stability of cereal grain and lettuce to validate rotational crop field trials (EFSA, 2016).

To calculate the total residue of ethofumesate, the residues of parent and NC 9607 (upon hydrolysis as sum of NC 9607, NC 20645 and conjugates) were summed (EFSA, 2016).

Residues of parent ethofumesate were only detected in root crops, leafy crops and cereal forage of the first rotation, i.e. grown after a PBI of 25–33 days. Highest total residues accounted for 0.04 mg/kg in root crops, 0.03 mg/kg in leafy crops and 0.03 mg/kg in cereal forage.

By the second and third rotation, residues of parent ethofumesate were always below the LOQ for all crops. The residues in the common moiety NC 9607 (analysed after an acidic hydrolysis step in order to quantify the sum of NC 9607, NC 20645 and its conjugate) were always below the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg, except for one sample in leafy crops (0.01 mg/kg) and for cereal crops, which showed residues between < 0.01 and 0.02 mg/kg.

The EU pesticides peer review concluded that the occasional presence of the common moiety NC 9607 residues in rotational crops above the LOQ could therefore not be excluded (EFSA, 2016).

Since the maximum annual application rate of ethofumesate on carrots (i.e. 0.6 kg as/ha) is lower than the application rate tested in the rotational crop study (1 kg/ha), it can be concluded that significant residues of neither ethofumesate nor NC 9607 will be expected in rotational crops from the use on carrots, provided that the active substance is applied according to the proposed GAP. The data gap identified by the peer review for storage stability of rotational crop samples of cereal grain and lettuce remains valid.

Magnitude of residues in processed commodities

Specific processing studies for the crops under assessment are not available. No new studies are required in view of the contribution of residues carrots to the TMDI which represents considerably less than 10% of the ADI (e.g. 0.004%).

Proposed MRLs

The available data are considered sufficient to derive an MRL proposal of 0.1 mg/kg as well as risk assessment values for carrots (see Appendix B.1.2.1). In Section 4, EFSA assessed whether residues on these crops resulting from the intended uses are likely to pose a consumer health risk.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK AND FISH

Carrots are not listed as an aquaculture feed item and therefore the nature and magnitude of residues in fish was not further investigated.

However, carrots may be used for feed purposes. Hence, it was necessary to perform a dietary burden calculation for livestock to estimate whether residues in carrots from the intended use of ethofumesate would have an impact on the existing livestock dietary burden and residues in food of animal origin.

In the framework of the review of MRLs for ethofumesate, the livestock dietary exposure was calculated for the intake of dry peas and sugar beet root (EFSA, 2012) and was above the trigger value of 0.1 mg/kg DM for all livestock species except for poultry. Based on available metabolism studies on lactating cows, which were significantly overdosed with respect to the calculated dietary burdens for ruminants, the MRL review concluded that significant residues in edible matrices of ruminants and pigs are not expected. MRLs for poultry were not considered necessary due to no exposure to significant ethofumesate levels (EFSA, 2012). It is noted that the livestock exposure was calculated according to EU methodology applicable at that time.

In the EU pesticides peer review, the dietary burden for livestock was calculated using OECD methodology but only for the representative uses evaluated (e.g. sugar beet, fodder beet, red beet) also considering the potential residue intake from rotational crops (cereal forages and roots of root crops) (EFSA, 2016). The calculated dietary burdens confirmed livestock exposures above the trigger value of 0.004 mg/kg bw/day for all livestock species. However, the EU pesticides peer review concluded that, based on the results of metabolism studies and using the transfer factors calculated from these studies residues above the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg in animal commodities are not expected (EFSA, 2016). This exposure assessment, however, did not include feed commodities for which authorised uses were assessed by the MRL review.

Within the present assessment, EFSA calculated the combined EU livestock exposure by considering the input values for feed items as derived by the MRL review and by the EU pesticides peer review (scenario 1). Furthermore, the risk assessment values as derived for carrots under present assessment were added to see the contribution of residues from the new use to the existing livestock exposure (scenario 2). The calculations were performed according to the applicable OECD guidelines (OECD, 2013) (see Appendix B.2). The detailed input values are presented in Appendix D.1. Although potatoes is a root crop and can be grown in a crop rotation, the potential contribution of potato culls to livestock dietary burden was not considered in line with the approach taken by EU pesticides peer review, the RMS and the EMS of the current MRL application. For the fodder beet mangel, the data in leaves and roots were combined in line with approach proposed by the EMS (Germany, 2026). For sugar beet tops, the input values were considered as reported by the EU pesticides peer review and rotational data on leaves were not considered in contrast to the EMS proposal. EFSA additionally included in the livestock dietary burden calculation the data on triticale and sorghum forage to account for potential rotational crop residues in cereal forages.

The livestock exposure calculated under Scenario 1 indicates that livestock exposure exceeds the trigger value for all animal species with fodder beet and sugar beet tops being the main contributors. The results (see Appendix B.2) confirm the conclusions of the EU pesticides peer review and the MRL review that significant residues in animal commodities are not expected. No modification of animal commodities MRLs is required.

When carrot culls are added to the livestock dietary burden (Scenario 2), results indicate that residues in carrots do not have an impact on the overall dietary burden levels as explained above. Therefore, the nature and magnitude of ethofumesate residues in livestock were not further evaluated and the conclusions of the MRL review and the EU pesticides peer review, i.e. that significant residues in animal commodities are not expected, are still applicable.

The current MRLs in animal commodities are set at the LOQ of 0.03 mg/kg.

On the basis of available metabolism studies in livestock (lactating ruminants and laying hens), the residue definitions for enforcement and risk assessment were derived as sum of ethofumesate, 2‐keto‐ethofumesate, open‐ring‐2‐keto‐ethofumesate and its conjugate, expressed as ethofumesate. A sufficiently validated analytical method based on LC‐MS/MS with an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg is available for the enforcement of ethofumesate residues in animal matrices (EFSA, 2016).

RESIDUES IN HONEY

Not relevant as the crops under assessment are non‐melliferous.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

EFSA performed a dietary risk assessment using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019). This exposure assessment model contains food consumption data for different subgroups of the EU population and allows the chronic exposure assessment to be performed in accordance with the internationally agreed methodology for pesticide residues (FAO, 2016).

The toxicological reference values for ethofumesate used in the risk assessment (i.e. ADI) were derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (European Commission, 2016). The metabolites included in the risk assessment residue definition were considered to be of similar toxicity compared to the parent compound.

Short‐term (acute) dietary risk assessment

Considering the toxicological profile of the active substance, a short‐term dietary risk assessment was not required.

Long‐term (chronic) dietary risk assessment

In the framework of the MRL review, a comprehensive long‐term exposure assessment was performed using PRIMO rev. 2, taking into account the existing uses at EU level (EFSA, 2012). EFSA now updated the calculation using the PRIMO rev. 3.1 and with the relevant STMR values derived from the residue trials submitted in support of this MRL application for carrots. In addition, STMRs derived in the framework of the EU pesticides peer review (EFSA, 2016) were added to the calculations for crops where the MRL was implemented in the EU legislation. For sugar beet root, the input value derived by the EFSA pesticides peer review was higher than the one derived in the MRL review and therefore used as input value. The input values used in the exposure calculations are summarised in Appendix D.2. The highest theoretical maximum daily intake (TMDI) was calculated for NL child diet amounting to 0.1% of the ADI. The highest contributor to this diet was sugar beet root. The contribution of residues expected in carrots to the overall long‐term exposure was up to 0.004% ADI (DK child diet) and can therefore be considered as a minimal impact.

EFSA notes that this exposure calculation includes also crops for which tentative MRLs pending confirmatory information are still in place, and therefore, this exposure calculation shall be considered indicative.

EFSA concluded that the long‐term intake of residues of ethofumesate resulting from the existing and the intended uses is unlikely to present a risk to consumer health.

For further details on the exposure calculations, a screenshot of the Report sheet of the PRIMo is presented in Appendix C.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

The data submitted in support of this MRL application were found to be sufficient to derive an MRL proposal for carrots.

Residues of ethofumesate in carrots were found to contribute insignificantly to the existing livestock exposure. Consequently, further assessment on the magnitude of residues in livestock commodities was not triggered and a modification of the existing MRLs was unnecessary.

EFSA concluded that the proposed use of ethofumesate on carrots will not result in a consumer exposure exceeding the toxicological reference value and therefore is unlikely to pose a risk to consumers' health.

EFSA notes that this exposure calculation includes also crops for which tentative MRLs pending confirmatory information are still in place and therefore this exposure calculation shall be considered indicative.

The MRL recommendation is summarised in Appendix B.4.

The time-dependent reliability of CRTS III slab ballastless track structures based on direct probability integral method
Authors: Zhang Wenchang, Lei Xiao, Dai Junyan, Zhuang Huaxia, Tan Qiufen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe time-dependent reliability assessment of ballastless tracks in service life is crucial for ensuring the safe and stability of high-speed railways. This work systematically compiles and summarizes the performance functions of ballastless track structures under different failure modes during service period, and then the all modes are regarded as a mixed series-parallel system, a regularization method for performance functions of multiple failure modes is proposed. Subsequently, the direct probability integral method (DPIM) is firstly introduced for evaluating the time-dependent reliability of the CRTS III slab ballastless track system. The accuracy and computational efficiency of the proposed method are verified through comparison with the traditional Monte Carlo method. Furthermore, the failure modes of the system are categorized into safety and applicability. Numerical examples demonstrate the generality and robustness of the developed method in system reliability analysis. The result indicates that environmental factors are the primary cause of diminished system reliability, both in terms of safety and applicability. Extreme temperature gradients are identified as a primary cause of safety performance degradation in track structures, while negative temperature gradients primarily contribute to serviceability deterioration. The mean of train load below 300 kN is essential for maintaining safety performance. The safety and applicability of the track structure after 30 years of service should be a key concern for the railway department. Especially, the reliability calculation method developed in this work for CRTS III systems can be extended to various static and dynamic system state evaluations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-43103-9.Keywords: System reliability, Direct probability integral method, CRTS III track structure, Safety and applicabilitySubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

By the end of 2024, China’s high-speed railway network has reached an operational mileage of 48,000 km, wherein the track structure plays a critical role as the primary supporting component1. Slab ballastless track systems are widely favored in high-speed railway construction for their exceptional smoothness, structural stability, and low maintenance demands. Among these, CRTS III—a new generation of slab ballastless track independently developed by China—has seen extensive application in recent years owing to its superior interlayer compatibility and temperature adaptability2. The CRTS III track structures primarily consist of rails, track slab, self-compacting concrete (SCC), isolation layer and base slab. Operational practices of high-speed rail-ways indicate that under complex environmental conditions and high-frequency train loads, track structures inevitably suffer from damage and defects, i.e., track slab cracks, interlayer debonding, and reinforcement yielding1–3, as shown in Fig. 1(a)-(d). These defects not only compromise the service safety and applicability of the track structure but also pose potential threats to high-speed train operations. Consequently, assessing the service reliability of CRTS III track structures throughout their service life has be-come an urgent challenge, it holding significant importance for guiding the maintenance of track structures throughout their entire lifecycle.

CRTS III track structure and typical damage.

In recent years, with the widespread application of ballastless track structures, the calculation and assessment of their service reliability have attracted increasing attention. Lu et al. investigated the time-dependent reliability of crack width4, transverse bending5, and crack resistance6 in CRTS II slab ballastless track structures using the moment method. However, these existing studies focus solely on individual failure modes and have not yet addressed multiple failure modes in complex systems. Shi et al.7 integrated the second-order fourth-moment (SORM) method with the out-crossing rate method to investigate the time-dependent reliability of the interlayer interface damage in CRTS II track slabs, the result indicates that damage to the narrow joints is the primary factor leading to the decline in the reliability index of the interlayer inter-face. Liu et al.8 incorporated a Gamma process model for the stochastic degradation of concrete strength to examine the evolution of time-dependent reliability in track slabs. However, track structures during their service life may be subjected to multiple, related failure modes (e.g., safety and serviceability failures), which complicates system reliability analysis. Zhang et al.9 established a dimensionless model for the system limit state function of CRTS II track slabs under multiple failure modes, and then the moment method is employed to investigate the time-dependent reliability index for structural safety. Besides, Zhang et al.10 considering uncertainties in both failure modes and prior damage, proposed a conditional limit state function to investigate the conditional time-dependent reliability of CRTS II track slabs. Liu et al.11 applied the moment method to evaluate the system reliability of CRTS III track slabs under multiple failure modes, demonstrating that considering only a single failure mode leads to an overestimation of the reliability.

Currently, structural system reliability analysis primarily classified into two categories. The first category involves classical reliability analysis methods, i.e., Monte Carlo Simulation (MCS)12,13, First-Order Second-Moment (FOSM) method14, and the Second-Order Second-Moment (SOSM) method15, to determine the reliability of each individual failure mode, which are then incorporated into the system reliability analysis. On the other hands, by considering the logical relationships between different failure modes and converting them into an equivalent extreme-value event, and further the moment method9–11, probability density evolution method (PDEM)16,17 or direct probability integral method (DPIM)18 to determine system reliability. The latter has been proven to enable highly efficient and accurate reliability assessments with only a small number of integration points. Besides, Research on the full life-cycle performance assessment and maintenance planning of concrete structures has been performed based on reliability theory, to guide structural maintenance and repair decisions, thereby extending their service life19–21. Therefore, accurately calculating the structural reliability over its entire life cycle is a prerequisite for subsequent performance assessment and maintenance planning.

In the reliability evaluation of ballastless track structures, scholars investigated the reliability of the system by moment method9–11, however, DPEM or DPIM are usually applied to describe the probability transfer process of dynamic system17,22, the reliability evaluation of CRTSIII systems considering multiple failure modes via PDEM or DPIM is scarce, especially in terms of applicability and computational accuracy. Besides, PDEM typically requires the use of the TVD difference method to solve GPDEM, while also satisfying the CFL condition16. Therefore, a virtual time-driven process needs to be constructed during the calculation of static reliability, which reduces the efficiency of reliability analysis. Inspired by the scalability and efficiency of DPIM in reliability analysis, this work innovatively applies DPIM to evaluate the failure probability and reliability of CRTS III structures under multiple failure modes. In terms of the bottleneck of difficulty in comparing the limit state functions of each failure mode in calculating the reliability of track structure safety and applicability, a new regularization method for limit state functions is developed, and then a reliability method for complex systems is established by incorporating the series-parallel logical relationship into DPIM.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. The performance functions of CRTS III with respect of applicability and safety are presented in Sect. 2. System time-dependent reliability framework of CRTS III ballastless track structure based on direct probability integral method are developed by incorporating the mixed series-parallel logical relationship and regularization method of performance function in Sect. 3. The accuracy and efficientness of the proposed method are demonstrated, besides, the numerical examples and parameter analysis are investigated in Sect. 4. Finally, the conclusions can be drawn in Sect. 5.

System reliability analysis

Reliability of safety index for CRTS III

In this section, the safety performance and reliability indices of the track structure under temperature effects and train loads are obtained by the above DPIM. The structural safety primarily considers the longitudinal and transverse bending resistance of the track slab and the tensile yield of the base slab reinforcement. The static system composed of the structural failure modes under the aforementioned three scenarios is regarded as a series system.

Figure 5 shows the failure probability and reliability index of the track structure under three scenarios, i.e., low-rate, moderate, and severe degradation. As the service years increases, the failure probability of the safety limit state function of the track structure increases nonlinearly, and the corresponding safety index gradually decrease. The degradation rate of track structure has a significant impact on the safety performance of structural service. The structure hardly fail under the action of train load and temperature within an 80 years service cycle at the low-rate degradation mode. However, the failure probability of track structure under moderate and severe degradation modes are 0.17% and 7.79%, respectively, besides, the corresponding reliability index of 2.94 and 1.42. The current reliability index of safety for railway structures in service is 4.228. Consequently, the structural reliability after 50 years under moderate degradation conditions, or approximately 30 years under severe conditions, will fall below the target level. Therefore, the maintenance measures should be taken to ensure the service safety of the track structure.

Reliability of applicability index for CRTS III

The cracking modes of the track structure mainly affect the service applicability, the corresponding performance function can be expressed in Sect. 2.2. It notes that each modes are regarding as the parallel system, the unified limit state function can be obtained by the Eq. (22). Figure 6 illustrates the failure probability and reliability index of serviceability of the track structure under the coupled effects of temperature and train loads. It can be seen that reliability of applicability index are similar to those of the safety performance reliability in Fig. 6 . The failure probability under severe degradation is much higher than that under low-speed degradation after 60 years, with failure probabilities of 44.63% and13.54%, respectively. This means that the extent of crack propagation in the track structure can no longer guarantee its normal usage performance. As the service life of a structure increases, its probability of failure gradually rises, while its target reliability index decreases. According to the specifications, the target reliability index for the serviceability of the track structure is 128, therefore, it decreases to the target reliability after 30 and 45 service years under moderate and severe degradation modes, respectively. In low-rate condition, the applicability reliability index of the track structure remains above 1 throughout its 60-year service period, thus meeting the applicable requirements. Meanwhile, it can be seen from Fig. 6, the reliability of applicability index of the track structure under the same conditions is much lower than the safety index. Therefore, the serviceability of the track structure should be received special attention in corrosive environments, such as crack width.

System reliability for CRTS III

Indeed, structural cracking is classified as a serviceability limit state and does not inherently compromise the overall reliability or functionality of the track system. In order to accurately illustrates the system reliability, the safety and applicability are comprehensively considered in the overall performance function, therefore, the safety and applicability of track structure are regarding as mixed series-parallel systems, the corresponding reliability can be calculated by the Eq. (24).

The system failure probability of the track structure under three scenarios can be presented in Fig. 7, in which the safety and applicability modes are regarded as a series system in Fig. 7(a), the both modes are a parallel system in Fig. 7(b). It is observed from Fig. 7 that a remarkably interesting phenomenon exists: the failure probability of the mixed series system is identical to the value for the applicability failure mode, whereas the failure probability of the mixed parallel system matches precisely the value for the safety failure mode. This phenomenon can be attributed to the higher likelihood of applicability failure (structural cracking), in contrast to the relatively low probability of occurrence for the safety-related failure mode. Therefore, the distinct failure modes of a system are of paramount importance in reliability analysis.

Sensitivity analysis

Influence of safety index

(1) The mean of temperature gradient

The temperature gradient within ballastless track structures exhibits significant variability, attributed to China’s vast territory and complex climatic conditions. To investigate the service safety and reliability of track structure under different temperature conditions, the influence of temperature on the failure probability and reliability index of track structure after 30, 45, and 60 years of service for a severe degradation scenario, are presented in Fig. 8. It is observed that the failure probability gradually increases with the temperature gradient, and therefore the corresponding reliability index decrease. Notably, the rate of change in failure probability increases significantly when the temperature gradient exceeds 50 °C/m, accompanied by a corresponding acceleration in the decline of the reliability index. For examples, the failure probability increases by only 2.1% when the temperature gradient rises from − 45 °C/m to −50 °C/m, whereas it increases by 12.72% when the gradient changes from 45 °C/m to 80 °C/m. Therefore, extreme temperature gradients are identified as a primary factor contributing to the degradation of the service safety performance of track structures.

The failure probability and reliability index of system under different temperature ((a) failure probability; (b) reliability index).

(2) Covs of temperature gradient

Figure 9 presents the failure probabilities corresponding to the safety index under severe degradation, considering different Covs of temperature gradient. As can be observed from the figure that Covs has a relatively minor influence on the safety-related failure probability at the case of the mean temperature gradient is 45 °C/m, however the failure probability after 30 years exhibits significant variation across different COVs at the case of a higher mean gradient of 90 °C/m. Furthermore, the failure probability demonstrates a consistent increasing trend with extended service life for all Cov cases. Specifically, at a mean gradient of 45 °C/m and after 60 years of service, the failure probabilities for COVs of 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3 are 9.472%, 9.603%, and 9.854%, respectively. This represents increases of 1.38% and 4.03% relative to the baseline case (COV = 0.1). Correspondingly, the failure probabilities are 19.745%, 20.741%, and 21.862% at the higher mean gradient of 90 °C/m, showing more substantial relative increases of 5.04% and 10.72%.

Failure probability under different covs of temperature gradient ((a) 45 °C/m; (b) 90 °C/m).

Therefore, it can be concluded that the mean temperature gradient is one of the most critical factors affecting the safety failure of the track structure. The influence of the coefficient of variation becomes more pronounced at higher mean temperature gradients. Consequently, particular attention should be paid to structural safety under extreme temperature conditions throughout the entire life cycle of the track structure.

(3) Train load

As one of the primary load modes during the service life of track structures, train loads significantly influence structural safety performance. This section investigates the variation of safety failure probability and reliability index under different train loads over service years, as shown in Fig. 10. The safety failure probability exhibits a two-stage trend with increasing service years. During the initial service period (0–30 years), the failure probability increases slowly, ranging from 0 to 0.09%. Beyond 40 years, the value rises markedly, and the influence of train load becomes increasingly pronounced, with larger loads leading to more significant increases in failure probability. For example, under a load of 150 kN, the failure probability increases from 0.64% to 8.19%, an 11.8-fold rise, while under 350 kN, it increases from 0.94% to 10.98%, a 10.68-fold increase. After 60 years of service, when the load increases from 150 kN to 300 kN, the failure probability rises by 34.1%. The results indicate that controlling the average train load below 300 kN is essential for ensuring the safety performance of the track structure. Additionally, attention should be given to structural safety after 30 years of service.

Influence of applicability index

To clearly illustrate the influence of the temperature gradient and train load on applicability index, the corresponding numerical examples are performed.

(1) The mean of temperature gradient

The failure probability and reliability index of applicability index of system are investigated with respect to the variation of service life and negative temperature gradient, as seen in Fig. 11. With the increase of the negative temperature gradient, the reliability index of applicability for track structure gradually rises. Nevertheless, the growth in failure probability induced by service life exerts a more substantial influence than that caused by the temperature gradient. Moreover, as the service life of the structure extends, the impact of temperature becomes increasingly significant. Specifically, at the initial service stage, an increase in the temperature gradient from 1 °C/m to 30 °C/m leads to a rise in failure probability from 2.65% to 3.75%, while the corresponding reliability index decreases from 1.93 to 1.78. In contrast, after 60 years of service, the failure probability changes from 22.85% to 40.31%, accompanied by a decrease in the reliability index from 0.74 to 0.25. The combined effect of structural performance degradation over service life and the influence of temperature gradient accelerates the development of structural cracks, leading to a reduction in serviceability. Therefore, controlling the negative temperature gradient and implementing timely maintenance measures during the early service stage can effectively enhance the structural reliability and prolong the service life of track system.

Failure probability and reliability index under different temperature gradient ((a) Failure probability; (b) Reliability index).

(2) Train load

Similarly, the failure probability and reliability index with respect to the variation of service life and train loads are presented in Fig. 12. With increasing train load and service years, the failure probability of track structure suitability rises, accompanied by a corresponding decrease in the reliability index. The nonlinear change in failure probability becomes more pronounced under higher train loads and longer service years. For instance, when the service life increases from 10 to 60 years at the case of 100 kN, the failure probability changes from 3.49% to 22.12%, representing a 5.4-fold rise. Under the train load of 350 kN, the corresponding values shift from 6.448% to 72.43%, showing a 10.23-fold increase. Notably, beyond 50 years of service, the failure probability exhibits a sharp jump. Under the same service years, the increase in failure probability with train load is relatively smaller, e.g., when the train load increases from 100 kN to 350 kN after 10 years of service, the failure probability increases by 0.85 times. Using the target reliability as the threshold in conjunction with Fig. 12(b), the optimal maintenance time can be clearly determined. For example, with a target reliability limit of 1, the optimal maintenance times for 100 kN, 300 kN, and 350 kN are 50 years, 42 years, and 32 years, respectively.

Conclusions

This work comprehensively considers the safety and applicability of track structures under multiple failure modes, and regarding the system as a mixed series-parallel system based on the logical relationship of each failure mode. Further-more, the performance function regularization method is proposed to establish the limit state function of track structure systems under multiple failure modes. Secondly, the probability density integral equation of the multi random variable static system of the track structure was derived, and the direct probability integration method was introduced to determine the failure probability and reliability index of the system. The accuracy of the developed method was demonstrated by comparing MCM and DPIM. Furthermore, reliability analysis was conducted on the applicability and safety of the track structure, and the influence of different parameters on system reliability was explored by sensitivity analysis. Based on the results and their analysis, it can be concluded that.

(1) The performance function of the proposed time-varying reliability calculation of complex system, which combines series-parallel logical relationship and regularization methods, can effectively achieve mathematical representation of multiple failure modes. Furthermore, the developed direct probability integration method can accurately calculate the reliability of system in small sample situations, it holds generality and robustness.

(2) Environmental factors are the primary cause of diminished system reliability, both in terms of safety and applicability. Between these two failure modes, applicability-related failures occur more frequently in the track structure than safety-related ones. Given the logical relationship between the failure modes, the overall system re-liability is governed by whichever of the two is more critical.

(3) Extreme temperature gradients are identified as a primary cause of safety performance degradation in track structures; additionally, a pronounced negative temperature gradient is the predominant factor leading to the deterioration of service applicability. The mean of train load below 300 kN is essential for maintaining safety performance.

(4) The maintenance planning of the track structure can be conveniently determined via the target reliability or different levels based on the proposed time-dependent reliability of the system. Under moderate and severe degradation modes, the reliability index of the structure applicability decline to the target level after 30 and 45 years of service life, respectively, and thereby the maintenance measures should be implemented. The safety and applicability of the track structure after 30 years of service should be a key concern for the railway department.

Future research will introduce physical degradation processes of performance functions under multiple factors such as corrosion, carbonation, and fatigue, while accounting for the correlation among different variables. This approach will enable a more accurate time-dependent reliability analysis of track structures under multifactorial conditions and will facilitate reliability-based life-cycle maintenance planning.

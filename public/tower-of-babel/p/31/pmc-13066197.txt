Adipocyte-specific NR5A2 deficiency ameliorates diet-induced metabolic syndrome by suppressing adipose tissue inflammation
Authors: Weiwei Yuan, Shengqing He, Yuan Zang, Liping Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObesity is a global epidemic characterized by adipose tissue dysfunction, chronic inflammation, and metabolic syndrome. The orphan nuclear receptor NR5A2 has been implicated in metabolic regulation, but its role in adipocytes remains unclear. Here, we demonstrate that NR5A2 expression is upregulated in adipose tissue of high-fat diet (HFD)-fed and genetically obese (ob/ob) mice. Adipocyte-specific NR5A2 knockout (AKO) mice exhibited resistance to HFD-induced obesity, with reduced fat mass, smaller adipocytes, and improved glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity. Mechanistically, NR5A2 ablation attenuated adipose tissue inflammation, evidenced by decreased pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α) and macrophage infiltration, while enhancing energy expenditure and thermogenesis. Additionally, AKO mice showed reduced hepatic steatosis and improved lipid profiles. In vitro, NR5A2 deficiency impaired adipocyte differentiation and lipid accumulation. These findings identify NR5A2 as a key regulator of adipocyte hypertrophy, inflammation, and systemic metabolism, suggesting its inhibition as a potential therapeutic strategy for obesity-related metabolic disorders.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-40395-9.Keywords: Obesity, Adipocyte, NRF5A2, InflammationSubject terms: Diseases, Endocrinology, Physiology

Introduction

Obesity has become a worldwide health concern, associated with serious comorbidities such as type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, and cancer, presenting considerable challenges to contemporary healthcare1,2. Its pathogenesis stems from a chronic imbalance between caloric intake and energy expenditure. Current research extends beyond dietary restrictions, focusing instead on enhancing energy dissipation in metabolic tissues, particularly adipose tissue3. Despite excess adipose tissue is associated with metabolic dysfunction, adipose tissue serves as a vital endocrine organ, regulating energy homeostasis and metabolic health4.

Obesity is characterized by excessive adipose tissue accumulation, which contributes to chronic low-grade inflammation and metabolic dysregulation, ultimately promoting insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome5,6. While the precise molecular mechanisms connecting adipose tissue dysfunction to systemic metabolic disturbances remain unclear, emerging evidence suggests that inflammation may serve as a key underlying factor7. Notably, adipose tissue in obese individuals exhibits elevated macrophage infiltration, which secretes proinflammatory cytokines that exacerbate adipocyte dysfunction8. This macrophage recruitment is further amplified by obesogenic triggers, such as a high-fat diet. Studies have demonstrated that the NF-κB transcriptional pathway plays a critical role in mediating inflammatory responses within adipocytes9. Furthermore, Bai et al. revealed that mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) release activates the cGAS-cGAMP-STING pathway, worsening adipose tissue dysfunction10. Importantly, therapeutic strategies targeting adipose tissue inflammation have shown promise in mitigating obesity-related metabolic disorders11.

NR5A2, an orphan nuclear receptor, plays a crucial role in organ development and metabolic regulation, particularly in hepatocytes12,13, brown adipocytes14, cardiomyocytes15, and neurons involved in food intake control (e.g., hypothalamic arcuate nucleus)16. Studies indicate that Nr5a2, along with Esrrb, functions similarly to core pluripotency factors like Oct4 and Sox2 in maintaining ESC self-renewal17. The redundancy between Nr5a2 and Esrrb ensures robust reestablishment of pluripotent gene networks after mitosis, and Nr5a2 is also essential for induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) generation18. However, its regulatory role in Oct4 expression within the inner cell mass (ICM) of embryos remains unclear. Collectively, these findings highlight NR5A2’s broad influence on epithelial cell physiology, differentiation, energy metabolism19–21, and reproduction but the specific impact and the consequences of NR5A2 expression in adipocytes needs to be determined.

To investigate the role of NR5A2 in adipose tissue, we generated adipocyte-specific NR5A2 knockout mice (NR5A2 AKO) by crossing NR5A2-floxed mice with Adipoq-Cre mice, in which Cre recombinase is driven by the adiponectin promoter. When challenged with a 60% high-fat diet (HFD), NR5A2 AKO mice exhibited resistance to diet-induced obesity compared to littermate controls. These mice displayed improved metabolic profiles, including enhanced glucose tolerance, increased insulin sensitivity, and elevated energy expenditure. Additionally, adipocyte-specific NR5A2 deletion reduced systemic inflammation and led to smaller adipocyte size in both subcutaneous and visceral white adipose tissue (WAT), along with decreased lipid storage in these depots.

Results

Adipose NR5A2 is induced by HFD or obesity

To examine the effects of a high-fat diet (HFD) on NR5A2 expression in adipose tissue, we compared its levels in mice fed a normal chow diet (NCD) versus those on an HFD for 16 weeks. Our findings revealed a significant upregulation of NR5A2 gene expression in both epididymal white adipose tissue (eWAT) and subcutaneous white adipose tissue (subWAT) of HFD-fed mice compared to NCD controls (Fig. 1a, b). Consistent with mRNA data, NR5A2 protein levels in eWAT were also markedly elevated (Fig. 1c, d). Time-course analysis demonstrated that NR5A2 expression increased as early as 1 week after HFD initiation and remained elevated throughout the feeding period (Fig. 1e). Similar trends were observed in ob/ob mice, where both mRNA and protein levels of NR5A2 were significantly higher in eWAT and subWAT compared to wild-type controls (Fig. 1f–i). Furthermore, NR5A2 expression was increased during 3T3-L1 adipocyte differentiation (Fig. 1j, k), along with key adipogenic markers, showing a progressive upregulation (Fig. 1l-n). These results collectively indicate that NR5A2 is upregulated during adipocyte differentiation and in response to HFD, suggesting its potential role as a metabolic marker in adipose tissue.

Mice lacking NR5A2 in adipocytes ameliorated HFD induced obesity

To examine the physiological function of NR5A2 in adipose tissue, we generated adipocyte-specific NR5A2 knockout mice by crossing NR5A2 floxed (NR5A2 f/f) mice with Adipoq-Cre mice, which express Cre recombinase under the adiponectin promoter. Quantitative analysis confirmed a significant reduction in NR5A2 mRNA and protein levels in both eWAT and subWAT of Adipoq-Cre; NR5A2 f/f mice, while expression in other metabolically tissues remained unchanged (Fig. 2a, b). When fed a standard chow diet, NR5A2-AKO mice exhibited no differences in body weight or composition compared to controls. However, under a high-fat diet (HFD), NR5A2-AKO mice gained significantly less weight and displayed reduced fat mass (Fig. 2c-f). Notably, food and water intake were comparable between HFD-fed control and knockout mice (Fig. 2g, h), suggesting that the observed phenotype was not due to altered consumption. Histological analysis further revealed that NR5A2 deficiency led to smaller adipocytes in eWAT, indicating a resistance to diet-induced fat accumulation (Fig. 2i-l).

Loss of NR5A2 in adipocyte leads to an improved metabolic profile

Under HFD conditions, adipose-specific NR5A2 knockout mice exhibited markedly enhanced glucose tolerance (Fig. 3a) and insulin sensitivity (Fig. 3b). Compared to controls, these mutants also had significantly lower blood glucose levels (Fig. 3c). Furthermore, HFD-fed NR5A2-AKO mice showed a sharp decline in plasma insulin (Fig. 3d), supporting the hypothesis that NR5A2 deficiency in white adipose tissue improves insulin responsiveness. Consistent with their reduced adiposity, these mice also displayed decreased plasma leptin and adiponectin levels (Fig. 3e, f).

Deletion of NR5A2 in adipocyte increased heat production

We next examined how NR5A2 ablation in adipose tissue affects metabolic activity. Compared to HFD-fed control mice, HFD-fed NR5A2 knockout mice showed significantly higher oxygen consumption (VO₂), carbon dioxide production (VCO₂), and energy expenditure (EE) (Fig. 3g-l), suggesting an overall increase in metabolic rate. However, no differences were observed in the respiratory exchange ratio (RER) between control and NR5A2 KO mice (Fig. 3m, n).

HFD-fed NR5A2 adipocyte KO mice show ameliorated hepatosis

Since hepatic steatosis is strongly linked to obesity and insulin resistance, we investigated how adipose-specific NR5A2 deletion affects hepatic lipid deposition. Compared to NCD-fed controls, HFD-fed control mice exhibited greater hepatic lipid accumulation. However, NR5A2 knockout mice on an HFD displayed even less pronounced lipid deposition than HFD-fed controls (Fig. 4a, b). Notably, liver weight was significantly reduced in KO mice (Fig. 4c). Additionally, both hepatic and serum triglyceride levels were markedly lower in NR5A2-AKO mice than in controls (Fig. 4d, e), as were serum cholesterol levels (Fig. 4f).

NR5A2 is required for adipocyte differentiation in vitro

Given the established importance of NR5A2 in adipose tissue development, we next assessed its functional role in adipocyte differentiation in vitro. Primary stromal vascular fraction (SVF) cells derived from control and NR5A2-AKO mice were induced to undergo adipogenesis. Notably, NR5A2 deficiency profoundly impaired adipocyte differentiation, as evidenced by reduced lipid droplet formation and triglyceride (TG) accumulation (Fig. 4g). Correspondingly, the expression of adipogenic markers were significantly attenuated in KO cells relative to controls (Fig. 4h, i). Collectively, these data establish NR5A2 as a pivotal regulator of adipocyte differentiation and metabolic function.

NR5A2 deficiency suppresses Diet-Induced inflammation

The absence of NR5A2 led to a marked reduction in macrophage infiltration, as identified by F4/80 staining, in both eWAT and iWAT of HFD-fed mice. Moreover, the formation of crown-like structures, which are macrophage aggregates implicated in clearing dead adipocytes, was also significantly suppressed (Fig. 5a). Additionally, key inflammatory markers (F4/80, IL-1β, Mcp1, Tnfα, Cd68, and IL6) were significantly downregulated in KO adipose tissue, further confirming attenuated inflammation (Fig. 5b, c). These observations suggest that NR5A2 knockout mitigates the inflammatory response and associated tissue remodeling in obese adipose tissue.

(b, c), qPCR analysis of mRNA levels of genes related to inflammation in eWAT and subWAT from control and NR5A2-AKO mice fed an HFD (n = 5). *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01.

Discussion

Obesity and type 2 diabetes (T2D) are global health challenges with significant metabolic consequences. This study investigates the role of adipocyte-specific NR5A2 in adipocyte biology and its impact on systemic metabolism in mice under different dietary conditions. By comparing adipocyte-specific NR5A2 knockout mice with littermate controls, we found that NR5A2 deficiency attenuates diet-induced obesity, leading to reduced fat mass, smaller white adipocytes, and elevated energy expenditure—independent of changes in food intake or physical activity. Additionally, NR5A2 deletion improved glucose tolerance and reduced white adipose tissue inflammation. These findings suggest that adipocyte NR5A2 exacerbates obesity, which subsequently drives local inflammation and systemic metabolic dysfunction, including glucose intolerance. Thus, we propose that NR5A2 in adipocytes plays a critical role in the progression and worsening of diet-induced obesity and its associated metabolic disorders, such as diabetes.

Adipocyte hypertrophy, a hallmark of dysfunctional adipose tissue, contributes to metabolic disorders, including dyslipidemia, ectopic fat accumulation, glucose intolerance, and insulin resistance22,23. This condition is characterized by immune cell infiltration and localized inflammation in hypertrophic white adipose tissue, a phenomenon also observed in brown adipose tissue (BAT)24. Our study demonstrates that NR5A2 deficiency ameliorated adipocyte hypertrophy and inflammatory responses in white adipose tissue, correlating with ameliorated glucose and lipid metabolism. Notably, these metabolic were further improved upon high-fat diet (HFD) feeding, suggesting that NR5A2 plays a critical role in metabolic adaptation to nutrient overload. Interestingly, NR5A2 may also mitigate metabolic dysfunction by regulating the differentiation of stromal vascular fraction (SVF) cells into mature adipocytes. Given that defective adipocyte differentiation is strongly linked to metabolic dysregulation, NR5A2 could serve as a key modulator in maintaining metabolic homeostasis. Further research is needed to uncover the precise mechanisms by which NR5A2 influences adipocyte differentiation. Elucidating these pathways may open new therapeutic avenues for targeting NR5A2 in metabolic diseases.

Visceral fat and infiltrating macrophages release various mediators that contribute to insulin resistance, including leptin, resistin, interleukin-6 (IL-6), and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α)25,26. In our study, adipocyte-specific NR5A2-deficient mice exhibited lower plasma levels of leptin and adiponectin compared to littermate controls. Additionally, white adipose tissue from these knockout mice showed fewer crown-like structures (CLS)—microscopic formations surrounding necrotic adipocytes that are typically infiltrated by macrophages27. Since CLS-associated macrophages secrete proinflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α, their reduction suggests diminished adipose tissue inflammation28. Notably, elevated TNF-α levels are strongly linked to insulin resistance, whereas our NR5A2 AKO mice demonstrated enhanced insulin sensitivity. This was evidenced by lower baseline and glucose-stimulated plasma insulin levels, along with more efficient glucose clearance during tolerance tests relative to HFD-fed controls. These findings indicate that NR5A2 deficiency mitigates diet-induced adipose inflammation and protects against insulin resistance. However, the precise molecular mechanisms linking NR5A2 to inflammatory pathways such as NF-κB or cGAS-STING remain to be fully elucidated. Future studies aimed at dissecting these mechanisms and exploring NR5A2-targeted interventions will be valuable in assessing its therapeutic potential.

Conclusion

In conclusion, these findings provide new insights into the physiological role of adipocyte NR5A2 isoforms in obesity. Our data suggest that inhibiting NR5A2-mediated signaling could potentially counteract pathological weight gain, obesity-driven inflammation, and insulin resistance.

Methods and materials

Animals

Adiponectin-Cre and NR5A2-floxed mice were obtained from Jiang su GemPharmaTech (Nanjing, China). Adipocyte-specific NR5A2 knockout mice were generated by crossing NR5A2-floxed mice with Adiponectin-Cre mice. Male C57BL/6J mice (6–8 weeks old) and ob/ob mice (6–8 weeks old) were purchased from Jiangsu GemPharmatech (Nanjing, China). All animals were kept in a controlled environment with standard housing and a 12-hour light/dark cycle and free access to water and standard chow. To induce obesity, 6-week-old male mice of different genotypes were fed a high-fat diet (HFD; D12492, Research Diets, Inc., 60% kcal from fat) for 14 weeks, with weekly body weight monitoring. Mice were euthanized at the end of the experimental period by cervical dislocation following deep anesthesia induced by intraperitoneal injection of ketamine (100 mg/kg). This study is reported in accordance with the ARRIVE guidelines and were approved by the Shenzhen University Animal Care and Use Committee and were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines and national regulations for animal research (IACUC-202300118).

Glucose tolerance test and insulin tolerance test

For the glucose tolerance test (GTT), mice underwent a 16-hour fasting period with ad libitum access to water. Following fasting, they were administered intraperitoneal (i.p.) glucose injections, and tail vein blood samples were collected at predetermined intervals. Blood glucose levels were measured using a Bayer Contour glucometer.

For the insulin tolerance test (ITT), mice were fasted for 4 h before receiving an i.p. injection of human insulin (0.75 U/kg). Blood glucose were then measured at the same time points as in the GTT protocol29.

Measurement of blood leptin and insulin

Blood samples were collected from the tail vein of mice, either after a 12-hour overnight fast conditions. The blood was centrifuged at 4 °C for 10 min at 12,000 × g to isolate plasma. Insulin and leptin levels were then quantified using commercially available ELISA kits according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Western blotting

The frozen tissues were homogenized in lysis buffer containing a complete protease inhibitor cocktail. After centrifugation at 12,000 × g for 10 min, the supernatants were collected. Protein concentrations were measured using a BCA assay. The lysates were heat-denatured at 95 °C, resolved by SDS-PAGE, and transferred to PVDF membranes. The membranes were probed overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies, followed by incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies30.

Body composition analysis

The body composition of control and knockout mice was measured using the MRI Analyzer (Echo Medical System)31.

Histological analysis

Mouse adipose tissue and liver samples were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) at room temperature for 24 h. Following fixation, the tissues were paraffin-embedded and sectioned into 5 μm slices for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, immunohistochemistry (IHC), and immunofluorescence (IF) analysis. Prior to staining, sections were deparaffinized and rehydrated. For H&E staining, nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin (5 min) followed by eosin (2 min), and images were captured using a microscope scanner. For IHC, deparaffinized sections underwent antigen retrieval, followed by 1-hour blocking and overnight incubation with primary antibodies. After PBS washes, the sections were treated with secondary antibodies for 45 min at room temperature32.

SVF isolation and differentiation

Subcutaneous inguinal fat was harvested from 8-week-old male mice and digested in Krebs-Ringer-Hepes-bicarbonate (KRH) buffer supplemented with collagenase 1 at 37 °C for 30 min. The digested tissue was then passed through a 100 μm cell strainer and centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 min. The resulting pellet was resuspended and filtered through a 40 μm strainer, and centrifuged under the same conditions to isolate the stromal vascular fraction (SVF). For differentiation, SVF cells were initially cultured in 6-well plates until reaching confluency. Adipogenic differentiation was induced using a differentiation cocktail containing insulin (5 µg/mL), IBMX (0.5 mM), dexamethasone (1 µM), and indomethacin (125 µM) from sigma. After 48 h, the medium was replaced with DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 0.5 µM insulin to promote maturation into white adipocytes33.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR analysis

Gene expression analysis by qRT-PCR. RNA was isolated using TRIzol reagent, followed by cDNA synthesis using a reverse transcription kit. Quantitative PCR was carried out with SYBR Green chemistry, and gene expression levels were normalized to β-actin as an internal control, calculated via the ΔΔCt method.

Indirect calorimetry and energy expenditure measurements

CLAMS monitoring system was used to measure energy expenditure (CO2 production and O2 consumption) and locomotor activity simultaneously in mice housed at 22 °C. Water and food were provided ad libitum.

Data statistics and analysis

All data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software. Comparisons between the two groups were statistically analyzed by two-tailed t-test. For multiple comparisons with two independent variables, two-way ANOVA was used. Data were presented as mean±standard deviation (S.D.). P-values < 0.05 was considered as statistically significant.

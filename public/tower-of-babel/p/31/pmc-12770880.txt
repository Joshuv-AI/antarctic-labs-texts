Systemic immune-inflammation index prior to postoperative radiotherapy as a tool for outcome prediction in thymic epithelial tumors
Authors: Alper Kahvecioglu, Mervenur Bay, Pervin Hurmuz, Mustafa Cengiz
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) is a prognostic biomarker in various solid tumors. However, the prognostic role of SII in thymic epithelial tumors (TETs) receiving adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) has not yet been explored. This study aims to assess the prognostic role of pre-RT SII in TET patients undergoing adjuvant RT.Materials and MethodsA retrospective analysis was conducted on 37 patients treated with adjuvant RT for TETs between 2004 and 2023. The median adjuvant RT dose was 54 Gy (range, 50 to 70) delivered over 25–35 fractions. SII was calculated based on hemogram parameters within the two weeks preceding the start of RT, and its prognostic role was evaluated.ResultsMedian follow-up was 90 months (range, 12 to 230). The 5-year overall survival (OS) and disease-free survival (DFS) rates were 97.1% and 90.7%, respectively. The receiver operating characteristic analysis determined an optimal SII cut-off value of 916 for predicting recurrence (specificity, 67%; sensitivity, 71%). In univariate analysis, patients with an SII ≥916 had significantly lower 5-year OS (92.3% vs. 100%, p=0.028) and DFS (73.1% vs. 100%, p=0.011) compared to those with a SII below 916. In multivariable analysis, no variable remained statistically significant, likely due to limited events and small sample size.ConclusionPostoperative, pre-RT SII may have potential prognostic relevance in patients with TETs undergoing adjuvant RT. Elevated SII levels could be explored as a biomarker to help guide more individualized treatment and follow-up strategies; however, further studies are needed to validate these findings.Keywords: Inflammation, Radiotherapy, Thymoma, Thymic carcinoma

Introduction

Thymic epithelial tumors (TETs) are rare malignancies originating from thymic epithelial cells, displaying a range of behaviors from indolent to highly aggressive. This heterogeneity is evident in their histological subtypes and staging, which greatly influence prognosis and treatment approaches [1]. Complete surgical resection is the primary treatment for early-stage TETs. However, for cases with adverse postoperative pathological features, a multimodal approach, including adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) and sometimes chemotherapy, is recommended to improve outcomes [2,3]. Despite advancements in management of TETs, predicting recurrence and long-term survival remains difficult, as traditional indicators like histopathology and staging may not fully capture disease complexity. This highlights the need for reliable biomarkers to better stratify patients by prognosis. Recently, inflammatory markers, including the systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), have gained interest as prognostic tools in oncology research, with high SII levels linked to poor outcomes in multiple tumors [4]. Elevated SII levels are thought to reflect a pro-tumor inflammatory state, which can drive tumor progression and metastasis. While the prognostic significance of pre-operative SII has been demonstrated in patients with resected thymomas, the impact of its value in the postoperative, pre-RT period on prognosis has not yet been investigated [5]. This study aims to assess the prognostic significance of postoperative, pre-RT SII in patients with resected TETs receiving adjuvant RT.

Materials and Methods

1. Study design and patients

This retrospective study evaluated adult patients (≥18 years) diagnosed with thymic malignancies who underwent adjuvant RT after surgical resection. Patients were treated between 2004 and 2023 at our institution. Data were collected from patient records and the hospital’s electronic medical system. Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) histopathologically confirmed TET, (2) completion of surgical resection prior to adjuvant RT, and (3) available hematological data from the two weeks preceding RT. Patients with initially unresectable tumors, those who received neoadjuvant or definitive RT, and individuals with non-epithelial thymic tumors (e.g., lymphomas and germ cell tumors) or incomplete blood parameter data were excluded. The study was approved by the institutional review board, and the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki were followed.

2. Treatment

Patients received adjuvant RT based on institutional protocols, with treatment techniques evolving over time. Techniques included two-dimensional RT, three-dimensional conformal RT, and intensity-modulated RT. Target volumes were defined based on the pre-operative tumor bed, with added margins as needed based on imaging and clinical findings. Daily fractions of 1.8 to 2 Gy were administered, 5 days per week. Although the decision to administer chemotherapy was ultimately at the discretion of the treating physician, specific clinical indications guided its use in most cases. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy was administered in five patients with potentially resectable tumors at the time of diagnosis. Concurrent and adjuvant chemotherapy was given in two patients who underwent R2 resection and in two patients with thymic carcinoma histology. In the remaining two patients, adjuvant chemotherapy was initiated following R1 resection, based on the clinical judgment of the treating physician. The chemotherapy regimens administered were cisplatin, doxorubicin, and cyclophosphamide (n=6, 54.5%) and carboplatin/paclitaxel (n=5, 45.5%).

3. SII calculation

Complete blood count parameters, including neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet counts, were obtained from blood samples taken within two weeks before the initiation of RT. The SII was calculated using the formula: [(Neutrophil × Platelet)/Lymphocyte] [6].

4. Follow-up

After completion of RT, all patients were monitored according to a standardized follow-up schedule: every 3 months during the first 2 years, every 6 months for the subsequent 3 years, and annually thereafter. Radiological evaluations, including computed tomography and/or magnetic resonance imaging of the thorax, were performed for disease monitoring.

5. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using the SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics, overall survival (OS), and disease-free survival (DFS) were calculated. Local recurrence was defined as recurrence in the tumor bed, regional recurrence as involvement of perithymic or mediastinal lymph nodes, and distant metastasis as pleural or hematogenous spread. The follow-up period and all time-dependent events were measured from the start of RT to the last follow-up, death, or recurrence, whichever occurred first. OS was defined as the interval from the initiation of RT to death from any cause, while DFS was defined as the time from RT initiation to either disease recurrence or death from any cause. Survival analyses were performed using the Kaplan-Meier method, with group comparisons assessed by the log-rank test. The optimal cut-off value for the SII was determined through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. This method was applied to assess the sensitivity and specificity of the SII in predicting patient outcomes, allowing for the identification of a threshold value that optimized both parameters. Variables with a potential significance level of p < 0.1 in univariate analysis were further evaluated using a multivariate Cox proportional hazards model, with hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals reported. Statistical significance for all tests was set at p < 0.05.

The ROC analysis identified an optimal SII cut-off value of 916, with an area under the curve of 0.74, specificity of 67%, and sensitivity of 71% for predicting recurrence (Fig. 1). In results of univariate analysis, patients with an SII ≥916 showed significantly lower 5-year OS (92.3% vs. 100%, p=0.028) and DFS (73.1% vs. 100%, p=0.011) compared to those with an SII <916 (Fig. 2). Additionally, univariate analysis demonstrated that early Masaoka stage, and the presence of myasthenia gravis at diagnosis were also significant positive prognostic factors for DFS (Table 3). To further evaluate the independent prognostic impact of SII and other relevant variables, we performed multivariable Cox regression analysis. However, none of the included variables—including SII—retained statistical significance, likely due to the limited number of events and small sample size (Table 4).

No grade 3 or higher acute toxicities were observed. Grade 1 acute esophagitis was noted in four patients (10.8%), and grade 2 acute esophagitis in one patient (2.7%). Discussion and Conclusion

This single-center retrospective study indicates that postoperative, pre-RT SII may be associated with survival outcomes in patients with TETs receiving adjuvant RT, suggesting a potential prognostic role that warrants further investigation. TETs are rare neoplasms that generally exhibit higher survival rates compared to other thoracic malignancies, with 5-year survival rates reaching up to 90% even in advanced stages after complete resection [7,8]. Additionally, cases of recurrence often respond favorably to salvage treatments, with a significant proportion of patient mortality attributed to non-tumor-related factors, such as myasthenia gravis [9]. Despite these encouraging survival rates, TETs represent a heterogeneous patient group; even among patients with similar clinicopathological profiles and receiving standardized treatment regimens, some display more aggressive disease course and higher disease-specific mortality rates. This highlights a substantial need for biomarkers that can more accurately stratify TET patients by prognosis, going beyond traditional prognostic factors.

The link between cancer prognosis and inflammation has emerged as a key focus in recent oncology research. Inflammation is recognized as a multifaceted driver in both tumor development and progression, with inflammatory responses in the tumor microenvironment significantly influencing patient outcomes [10]. The SII, derived from peripheral blood parameters, provides an easily accessible and cost-effective biomarker that may serve as an indicator of systemic inflammation. Initially described in 2014, SII has been shown to hold prognostic value, with higher SII values consistently associated with poorer outcomes across multiple cancer types [6,11-18].

Despite its increasing relevance in other malignancies, most studies on SII have not included TETs. Only a limited number of studies have assessed the prognostic implications of SII specifically in TET patients. For instance, a retrospective analysis by Veraar et al. [19], involving 184 surgically treated TET patients, demonstrated that elevated pre-operative SII values were associated with lower cancer-specific survival rates. Similarly, Li et al.’s retrospecive study [5] of 425 surgically treated TET patients revealed that higher pre-operative SII values correlated with worse OS. However, in the first of these two studies, 60% of the patients were reported to have received adjuvant therapy, whereas the second study did not provide details on adjuvant therapy. Since adjuvant RT is widely used in routine practice and is known to improve oncological outcomes for TETs, the prognostic significance of the pre-operative SII value may theoretically vary based on whether patients receive adjuvant RT [20]. Notably, no studies to date have investigated the prognostic value of postoperative, pre-RT SII in patients requiring adjuvant RT due to adverse pathological risk factors, to the best of our knowledge. This study is therefore the first to address this gap, examining the prognostic role of postoperative, pre-RT SII in a cohort of TET patients receiving adjuvant RT. Our findings reveal that patients with elevated postoperative, pre-RT SII values exhibit significantly poorer 5-year OS and DFS rates. These results suggest that SII may serve as a meaningful prognostic biomarker, potentially elucidating some of the heterogeneity observed within TET patient populations undergoing adjuvant RT and aiding in more refined prognostic stratification. Although our univariate analysis demonstrated a significant association between elevated SII and poorer survival outcomes, this finding did not retain statistical significance in the multivariable model. This may be attributed to the inherent limitations of our cohort, particularly the small sample size and low number of events, which likely reduced the statistical power to detect independent prognostic effects. Nonetheless, the consistent directionality of HRs and the biological plausibility of SII as a marker of tumor-promoting inflammation support its continued investigation as a potential prognostic biomarker in TETs. Larger, multi-institutional studies are needed to validate these preliminary observations.

While our study is the first to specifically investigate the prognostic significance of postoperative, pre-RT SII in patients with TETs undergoing adjuvant RT, it is important to acknowledge several limitations. The retrospective nature of the study may inherently introduce selection biases, potentially affecting the robustness of our findings. Additionally, the small sample size and low incidence of disease-related events in our cohort may limit the statistical power and affect the ability to detect subtler associations. However, given the challenges of designing prospective studies due to the rarity of TETs, our study plays a valuable role in filling a significant gap in the literature.

In conclusion, this study suggests that elevated postoperative, pre-RT SII values may be associated with poorer OS and DFS in patients with TETs undergoing adjuvant RT. Given the heterogeneity of TETs, SII may aid in identifying patients who could benefit from more personalized and potentially intensified treatment and follow-up strategies. However, these findings should be interpreted with caution, and validation through larger prospective studies is warranted.

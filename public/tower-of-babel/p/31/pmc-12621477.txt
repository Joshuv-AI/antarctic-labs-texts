Ilizarov talus bone lenghtening combined with multiple functional free flaps for reconstruction of a mangled hindfoot: A case report
Authors: Dehong Zhang, Xiaoqing He, Xi Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionMangled hindfoot injuries with calcaneal defects and extensive soft tissue loss are foot-threatening, carrying high risks of infection and osteomyelitis. Currently, there is no standardized reconstructive protocol, though various methods have been applied. This case highlights a staged strategy combining vascularized flaps and the Ilizarov technique for such injuries, with an emphasis on controlling infection risks and reducing the need for bone grafting.Case presentationA 38-year-old male with a right Gustilo-Anderson type IIIC calcaneal fracture (high-temperature crush injury) had soft tissue defects, third-degree burns, vascular compromise, and calcaneal loss. Staged management included: emergency ALT flow-through flap for vascular rescue, second ALT flap for soft tissue coverage, Ilizarov talar bone lengthening for osseous reconstruction, and medial plantar flap for a subsequent plantar ulcer. At 12-month follow-up, flaps were intact, Maryland Foot Score was 85, Visual Analog Scale (VAS) score of 2, and independent ambulation was achieved.Clinical discussionThis case demonstrates the efficacy of staged reconstruction: aggressive debridement reduced infection risk; Ilizarov technique enabled minimally invasive bone lengthening with infection control; flaps restored perfusion and soft tissue coverage, with the medial plantar flap preventing ulcer recurrence. No talar avascular necrosis was observed, but long-term follow-up is needed.ConclusionIlizarov talus bone lengthening combined with functional flaps transfer is a viable solution for severe posterior foot mangled injuries in osseous-soft tissue reconstruction.

Introduction

Severe hindfoot injuries with calcaneal defects and extensive soft tissue loss pose significant limb-threatening injury [1,2], including high risks of postoperative infection and calcaneal osteomyelitis [3,4]. Open calcaneus fractures comprise approximately 10 % of all calcaneal fractures [5]. Gustilo-Anderson type IIIC injuries which typically involve significant soft tissue loss and uncontained posterior heel bone defects carry substantially elevated risks of infection, osteomyelitis, and amputation [6,7].

Current reconstructive methods—including antibiotic-loaded bone cement, hydroxyapatite implants, or 3D-printed constructs—each has distinct advantages and limitations [[8], [9], [10], [11], [12]]. Critically, none of these approaches adequately addresses the triad of vascular insufficiency, soft tissue defect, and bony loss simultaneously, leading to a lack of consensus on the optimal treatment algorithm for mangled hindfoot injuries [13,14]. No single method above simultaneously resolves vascular compromise, soft tissue loss, and bony defects—creating a gap in clinical practice.

This case report describes a staged reconstruction strategy for post-traumatic osseous and soft tissue defects from the mangled hindfoot, combining functional free flaps and Ilizarov technique. This approach directly tackles the key challenges identified earlier: Thorough debridement of contaminated and devitalized tissue to minimize infection risk; Vascularized soft tissue coverage via free flaps to address vascular compromise and soft tissue loss; Gradual bony reconstruction using the Ilizarov technique to restore calcaneal architecture; Functional restoration through staged rehabilitation to optimize foot mechanics.

This staged strategy overcomes limitations of conventional approaches, offering a practical reference for resource-limited settings where advanced reconstructive tools are scarce. This case report is reported in line with the SCARE criteria [15].

Case report

A 38-year-old man (mechanical operator; non-smoker; height 175 cm, weight 72 kg; middle socioeconomic status; moderate daily activity; no chronic diseases, prior lower extremity trauma/surgery, or drug allergies) presented with right Gustilo-Anderson type IIIC calcaneal fracture 6 h after a high-temperature crush injury. The injury occurred at his workplace, when his right foot was trapped between a high-temperature metal stamping press (≈180 °C) and a conveyor belt. Organic contamination with metal debris and was present, and it was an isolated injury. On-site first aid included sterile wound dressing and tourniquet. He was transported directly to our emergency department via emergency ambulance.

Clinical assessment revealed

Full-thickness soft tissue defect involving subcutaneous tissue, superficial fascia; no exposed neurovascular bundles but visible calcaneal bony defect; third-degree burns (8 cm × 5 cm on dorsal foot, 10 cm × 6 cm on distal anterior lower leg) with dry, leathery eschar; anterior/posterior tibial artery injured leading to absent dorsalis pedis pulse, poor distal toe perfusion (ankle-brachial index 0.32, comparing systolic pressure of the uninjured left brachial artery vs. right dorsal pedis artery); diminished pinprick sensation over the dorsolateral foot and plantar heel; achilles tendon disruption.(Fig.1A).Fig. 1Foot presentation and imaging findings A. Preoperative blood tests (complete blood count, C-reactive protein, electrolyte panel, and coagulation profile) were all within normal limits. X-ray confirmed the comminuted calcaneal fracture with bone defect and foreign body retention. (Fig.1B).

Gustilo-Anderson type IIIC comminuted calcaneal fracture with extensive hindfoot soft tissue defect and right dorsal foot/distal lower leg third-degree burns.

Phase 1: Emergency vascularized flap reconstruction (Day 0)

Aggressive debridement excised necrotic tissues and foreign bodies, revealing a total calcaneal defect and a 30 cm × 20 cm soft tissue defect spanning the ankle to hindfoot. To address distal ischemia, a left anterolateral thigh (ALT) flow-through flap (20 cm × 10 cm) was harvested, with its vascular pedicle anastomosed end-to-end to bridge the proximal/distal anterior tibial artery to restore distal perfusion. The flap simultaneously covered the dorsal foot and anterior ankle wound, while antibiotic-loaded bone cement filled the plantar osseous defect (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Emergency surgery. A-C. Phase 2: Soft tissue reconstruction (Day 28)

At 4 weeks, with confirmed flap viability, a second ALT flap (30 cm × 10.5 cm) from the right thigh was transferred to cover the heel defect. Non–weight-bearing plantar wounds underwent split-thickness skin grafting, and an external fixator was applied to stabilize the ankle joint in neutral position (Fig.3). Fig. 3Staged reconstruction. External fixation was applied to protect the free flap and stabilize the ankle joint.Fig. 3

Phase 3: Osseous reconstruction with Ilizarov technique (Day 84)

The Ilizarov circular external fixator was applied for bone lengthening, which was initiated after the second flap achieved viability (84 days post-injury) (Fig. 4). A talus osteotomy was performed 1 cm proximal to the subtalar joint. Gradual distraction (0.25 mm every 6 h) commenced 7 days postoperatively, achieving a total transport distance of 4 cm over 40 days. Sixty days after transport initiation, radiographs confirmed complete bony union, and the external fixator was removed (Fig. 4).Fig. 4Bony reconstruction. A. Total calcaneal absence; B. Discussion

This unique traumatic mechanism rendered “emergent vascular reconstruction (ALT flow-through flap transplantation on Day 0)” a prerequisite for limb salvage. Furthermore, radical debridement combined with total calcanectomy maximized the reduction of infection risk, thereby laying a solid foundation for subsequent treatment stages. This also distinguishes the present case from those with reversible vascular compromise caused solely by mechanical compression.

Flap transplantation, serving as the foundational approach for reconstruction, must not only facilitate soft tissue defect repair but also preserve distal limb blood circulation and ensure the abrasion resistance of the weight-bearing heel area [16,17]. In this case, an initial-stage ALT flow-through flap bridged the injured anterior tibial artery to restore perfusion. A plantar ulcer during ambulation was addressed with a sensate medial plantar flap, which enhanced plantar durability via its inherent abrasion resistance and sensory nerve innervation, preventing recurrent ulceration [18,19].

Previous reports have indicated that bone lengthening technology can simultaneously address infection control and bone reconstruction in cases of traumatic or infectious calcaneal defects, meet the demand for bone mass, and effectively restore the length of the calcaneus and foot function [20,21]. In the case of this patient, after reconstructing the blood supply to the foot and performing extensive debridement in the early stage, it is necessary to further prevent the occurrence of osteomyelitis. After the reconstruction of the defective soft tissue is completed through free flap transplantation, it is clearly reasonable to choose Talus lengthening based on the Ilizarov technique: 1) It controls infection and restores calcaneal structure [22,23], while enabling minimally invasive osteotomy; 2) Avoids extensive soft tissue dissection required by implants or bone grafting, reducing infection risks [20,21]; 3) Eliminates donor site morbidity; 4) Shortens calcaneal reconstruction duration compared to traditional bone grafting, with fixator removal 5 months after transport initiation.

Literature shows Talus Avascular Necrosis (AVN) rates of 5 %–15 % after talar osteotomy, linked to trauma severity and surgical technique [24]. When performing talar osteotomy, it is necessary to protect the surrounding soft tissues and use a small and sharp osteotome for osteotomy to reduce the impact of thermal injury on the blood supply of the talus [20]. No AVN signs appeared at final follow-up, but 2–5 years follow-up is needed. Intraoperative periosteal preservation and postoperative MRI surveillance may reduce risks.

Over 6-months treatment course, the patient underwent 4 surgeries: debridement with flow-through flap (Day 0), ALT flap (Day 28), bone lengthening (Day 84), and plantar flap (Day 180). At the final follow-up, the patient exhibited well-integrated soft tissue healing, independent ambulation, and a Maryland Foot Score of 85, despite persistent ankle plantarflexion deficit from omitted Achilles tendon reconstruction. Notably, this case underscores a critical lesson: proactive use of the sensate medial plantar flap during primary plantar reconstruction can prevent pressure ulcer formation, thereby avoiding subsequent revision surgeries.

This staged strategy combining functional flap transfers and Ilizarov talar bone lengthening has demonstrated rationality in treating severe hindfoot mangling, addressing vascular restoration, soft tissue coverage, osseous reconstruction sequentially, and controllable infection/osteomyelitis risks, despite prolonged treatment. Clinicians must balance salvage benefits against infection and functional trade-offs, emphasizing the technical features and patient selection for optimal limb preservation outcomes.

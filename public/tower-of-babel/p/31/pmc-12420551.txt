Formation of surfaces oxide vacancies in porous ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers for enhanced energy storage performance
Authors: Deyang Zhang, Binhe Feng, Wenbo Guo, Jinbing Cheng, Kangwen Qiu, Ying Guo
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA cost-effective and large-scale method for synthesizing ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers with surface oxygen vacancies as electrode materials for supercapacitors is presented. The existence of oxygen vacancies on the surface of the ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers has been confirmed through X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS). The energy bands and density of states (DOS) of ZnCo2O4 are examined using density functional theory, revealing that treatment with NaBH4 reduces the band gap of ZnCo2O4 while increasing the DOS near the Fermi level compared to pristine ZnCo2O4. Furthermore, the specific capacitance of reduced ZnCo2O4 is nearly double that of its unmodified counterpart. This straightforward and practical approach significantly enhances both conductivity and specific capacitance in metal oxides, making it applicable to other similar materials.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-025-04347-y.Keywords: Supercapacitors, ZnCo2O4, Oxygen vacancies, Density-functional theory

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of renewable energy technologies, portable electronic devices, and electric transportation systems, electrochemical energy storage has garnered increasing attention [1–13]. In particular, supercapacitors (SCs) have attracted significant interest due to their high power density, rapid charging and discharging rates, and long-term cycling stability [14–19]. Generally, SCs are categorized into three types: electrical double-layer capacitors (EDLCs), pseudocapacitors (PCs), and hybrid supercapacitors, based on their charge storage mechanisms and electrode configurations [20, 21]. EDLCs store charges through ion adsorption/desorption processes at the electrode/electrolyte interfaces and are typically composed of carbon materials characterized by a large surface area and excellent electrochemical stability. Conversely, charge storage in PCs relies on fast reversible surface redox reactions. Among pseudocapacitive materials, RuO2 exhibits high conductivity but suffers from high cost and scarcity [22]. MnO2 and Co3O4 offer environmental and economic benefits but face challenges in conductivity and specific capacity. In contrast, ternary spinel ZnCo2O4 combines high theoretical specific capacity, lower cost than RuO2, and enhanced environmental friendliness relative to Co3O4 [23]. Furthermore, its unique nanoflower morphology promotes electrolyte diffusion and structural stability. However, intrinsic low conductivity remains a limitation for practical applications. Therefore, enhancing the conductivity of ZnCo2O4 is essential. Carbon materials, known for their excellent electrical conductivity, can be integrated with ZnCo2O4 to create composites [24, 25]. While the electrochemical performance of ZnCo2O4 can be improved as anticipated through this approach, challenges such as the high cost of graphene and interfacial issues remain. Consequently, it is imperative to further enhance the conductivity of ZnCo2O4.

In addition to integrating with carbon materials, recent theoretical and experimental studies indicate that the introduction of surface oxygen vacancies can enhance the conductivity and photoelectric properties of metal oxides [26]. Annealing in a reducing atmosphere, such as hydrogen, has been reported as an effective method for the post-treatment of TiO2 and other metal oxides [27–29]. However, high-temperature hydrogen annealing is not suitable for thermally unstable materials and contradicts efforts to develop more efficient and environmentally friendly energy applications due to its associated costs, safety concerns, as well as thermal energy and hydrogen consumption. Recently, NaBH4 solutions have been utilized to treat TiO2 nanotubes and BiVO4 thin films in order to improve photocurrent responses and photo-electrochemical performance [30, 31]. In addition to its simplicity, this room-temperature solution process can effectively modulate oxygen vacancies on the oxide surface to enhance their properties.

In this study, porous ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers are synthesized through a straightforward hydrothermal reaction. To augment conductivity and supercapacitor performance, surface oxygen vacancies are introduced by treating the ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers with an aqueous NaBH4 solution. The presence of these oxygen vacancies on the surface of the ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers is confirmed via XPS, while density functional theory (DFT) calculations investigate the energy bands and DOS within ZnCo2O4. The calculations reveal that following treatment with NaBH4, the band gap of ZnCo2O4 decreases significantly alongside an increased DOS near the Fermi level compared to pristine ZnCo2O4. According to quantum theory principles, these surface oxygen vacancies contribute positively towards enhancing electrical conductivity in ZnCo2O4. Consequently, the specific capacitance observed in reduced ZnCo2O4 is nearly twice that exhibited by untreated counterparts. This technique effectively enhances both conductivity and specific capacitance in metal oxides; it is simple yet practical while holding potential for application across various other material treatments.

The optimal pH for this reaction is controlled by urea mass: urea hydrolysis (Eqs. 1–3) generates OH− and CO32− ions at elevated temperature and pressure. (2) Subsequent annealing in air (400 °C, 2 °C min− 1) converts the precursors into spinel ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers via Eq. 5. As illustrated in Fig. 1c, these ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers are composed of numerous nanosheets, each containing a significant number of nanoparticles. The nanosheets are interconnected and radiate outward from a central point, creating a flower-like architecture. This distinctive morphology enhances electrolyte transport among the nanosheets and bolsters structural durability during rapid redox reactions. Furthermore, the porous structure generated through gas release during precursor decomposition contributes to improved electrochemical performance.

The detailed structure and morphology of the as-synthesized sample were investigated using Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM). Figure 1d and e present low-magnification TEM images that confirm the flower-like, porous structure of ZnCo2O4. The High-Resolution TEM (HR-TEM) image shown in Fig. 1f reveals a lattice spacing of 0.24 nm, which is consistent with the (311) crystal facets of spinel ZnCo2O4 (JCPDS No. 23-1390) [37]. The clear lattice fringes observed indicate excellent crystallinity. In the selected-area electron diffraction (SAED) pattern (Fig. 1h), distinct diffraction rings are observed that correspond to the (220) and (311) planes, signifying the spinel ZnCo2O4 phase [33]. Figure S1c–f illustrate SEM and TEM images of ZnCo2O4 reduced with NaBH4, showing that its flower-like and porous structure is preserved. This observation indicates that the influence of NaBH4 solution on the structural integrity of ZnCo2O4 is minimal, thereby ruling out any potential dependence of electrochemical properties on material structure within our study. The porous nature of the as-synthesized ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers was quantitatively analyzed using N2 adsorption-desorption measurements (Fig. S6). The isotherm exhibits a type IV curve with a distinct H3 hysteresis loop, characteristic of mesoporous materials. The pore size distribution, derived from the desorption branch, reveals a dominant mesopore diameter centered around 24.5717 nm, with a broad distribution and a total pore volume of 0.382275 cm3 g− 1 (Fig. S5). This high surface area provides abundant active sites for electrochemical reactions, while the well-developed mesoporosity facilitates rapid electrolyte ion diffusion throughout the electrode material, significantly contributing to the enhanced electrochemical performance. Importantly, SEM and TEM images confirm that this beneficial porous nanoflower structure is preserved after the NaBH4 treatment, ensuring that the performance enhancement observed in the reduced sample stems primarily from the introduced surface oxygen vacancies acting synergistically with the existing porous framework.

In comparison to pristine ZnCo2O4, the Co 2p peak for NaBH4-reduced ZnCo2O4 reveals two new satellite peaks at 786.2 eV and 802.7 eV, attributed to Co2+ ions. This suggests a partial reduction of Co3+–Co2+, resulting in the formation of new oxygen vacancies consistent with earlier reports [38, 39]. Moreover, the observed shift of the primary Co3+ peak toward higher binding energy indicates a reduction in cobalt valence state (Co3+→Co2+). This reduction occurs as adjacent Co3+ ions capture electrons during oxygen vacancy formation. The more localized valence electrons in Co2+ weaken the shielding effect, increasing the 2p orbital electron binding energy. Furthermore, Fig. S11 clearly reveals three characteristic peaks at 529.9, 531.5, and 532.4 eV, corresponding to lattice oxygen, oxygen vacancies, and surface-adsorbed oxygen, respectively. The atomic concentration of surface oxygen vacancies reached ~ 19%, calculated from the area ratio of the 531.5 eV peak. In contrast, the pristine sample exhibited negligible vacancy signals, consistent with the absence of Co2+ signatures in its Co 2p spectrum. This significant vacancy generation is attributed to the reducing action of NaBH4, wherein hydride ions (H−) selectively remove surface lattice oxygen. The Zn 2p binding energies at 1021.1 eV (2p3/2) and 1044.2 eV (2p1/2) further corroborate the successful synthesis of reduced ZnCo2O4 (Fig. S12). The XPS survey spectra presented in Fig. S2 further demonstrate the presence of zinc (Zn), cobalt (Co), and oxygen (O) elements in both pristine and reduced forms of ZnCo2O4. The data imply that oxygen vacancies are predominantly located near the surface while preserving bulk integrity as pure ZnCo2O4. The Raman spectrum of the reduced ZnCo2O4 (Fig. S4) exhibits broadening and a red-shift of the A₁g mode (~ 680 cm− 1) to ~ 675 cm− 1, along with the emergence of a characteristic shoulder peak at ~ 570 cm− 1. The former (broadening and red-shift) is attributed to lattice distortion and weakened bond strength induced by oxygen vacancies, while the latter (shoulder peak) corresponds to a defect-induced vibrational mode associated with low-coordinated metal sites. These observations are consistent with the increased Co2+ concentration detected by XPS and the local structural changes predicted by DFT calculations, collectively confirming the formation of surface oxygen vacancies. The reduction mechanism involves hydrolysis of NaBH4 in aqueous solution, generating hydride ions (H−). These H− ions attack surface lattice oxygen (O2−) in ZnCo2O4, forming H2O and creating oxygen vacancies. Concurrently, partial reduction of Co3+ to Co2+ occurs. This process is surface-energy-driven and preferentially initiates at exposed Co-O sites.

To achieve optimal electrochemical performance from the reduced ZnCo2O4 electrode, various techniques were employed, including cyclic voltammetry (CV), galvanostatic charge and discharge (GCD), and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS). Electrochemical analyses confirm significantly enhanced charge storage in oxygen-vacancy-modified ZnCo2O4. Cyclic voltammetry (CV) at 20 mV s− 1 reveals a 2-fold larger integrated area for the reduced sample versus pristine material (Fig. 3a), directly indicating doubled charge storage capacity due to surface oxygen vacancies. This improvement is further quantified by galvanostatic charge-discharge (GCD) at 1 A g− 1, where the reduced electrode exhibits twice the discharge duration while maintaining highly symmetric charge/discharge profiles (Fig. 3b), confirming exceptional electrochemical reversibility. Critically, both CV and GCD curves display prominent redox peaks and distinct plateaus near 0.3 V, verifying that charge storage originates from accelerated Faradaic reactions (M–O/M–O–OH, M=Zn/Co) enabled by oxygen vacancies. Similarly to the CV analysis, this extended discharge duration signifies an increased specific capacity. Furthermore, both the CV and GCD curves exhibit distinct redox peaks and charge/discharge plateaus. These features originate from Faradaic redox reactions associated with M–O/M–O–OH transformations (where M=Zn or Co) [40], confirming the pseudocapacitive behavior of both electrodes. The CV curves recorded across scanning rates ranging from 2 to 50 mV s− 1 are illustrated in Fig. S3a; as expected, while peak currents increase with higher scanning rates, their overall shape remains unchanged throughout this range-suggesting that this electrode facilitates rapid redox reactions favorable for efficient electrochemical energy storage. To ascertain further details regarding the pseudocapacitive characteristics of the reduced ZnCo2O4 material, GCD measurements were conducted over current densities between 1 and 10 A g− 1 as shown in Fig. S3b. Notably, a plateau near 0.3 V within these GCD profiles indicates typical pseudocapacitive traits that align well with observations made previously through CV analysis (Fig. S3a). For comparative purposes, Fig. S3c and S3d present respective CV and GCD curves for pristine ZnCo2O4. As shown in Fig. S8, identical NaBH4 reduction protocols were applied to MnO2 to evaluate the universality of the treatment. The reduced ZnCo2O4 exhibited a specific capacitance of 403 F g− 1 at 2 A g− 1, substantially exceeding that of treated MnO2 (24.8 F g− 1). This pronounced contrast underscores the exceptional responsiveness of ZnCo2O4 to oxygen vacancy engineering, attributed to its reduced bandgap and tailored nanoflower morphology that synergistically enhance ion diffusion. The GCD profiles further corroborate the extended discharge duration and superior charge storage kinetics of ZnCo2O4.

The reduced ZnCo2O4 electrode has higher specific capacitance than the pristine ZnCo2O4 electrode. Specifically, the reduced ZnCo2O4 electrode has pseudocapacitance values of 516, 403, 352, 289, and 216 F g−1 at current densities of 1, 2, 3, 5, and 10 A g− 1, respectively. The dramatic improvement after treatment with NaBH4 is supported by the reduced equivalent series resistance (Rs) observed in the Nyquist plots (Fig. 3d), where the high-frequency real-axis intercept decreased from 1.15 Ω (pristine) to 0.98 Ω (reduced). This reduction in Rs indicates enhanced bulk conductivity and faster ion diffusion, synergistically contributing to the improved charge transfer kinetics at the electrode-electrolyte interface. To quantitatively deconvolute the conductivity enhancement, Nyquist plots were fitted using the established model (Fig. S7). The pristine electrode exhibits an Rct of 3.72 Ω, reflecting sluggish charge transfer kinetics at the electrode-electrolyte interface. After introducing oxygen vacancies, Rct dramatically decreases to 1.85 Ω (50.3% reduction), confirming accelerated Faradaic reactions. This agrees with the reduced Rs (0.98 Ω vs. 1.15 Ω) and DOS elevation near EF, collectively verifying that oxygen vacancies enhance both bulk conductivity (via bandgap narrowing) and interfacial charge transfer (via active site exposure). To evaluate the practical applicability of the oxygen-enriched vacancy ZnCo2O4 electrode, 2000 constant current charge-discharge tests were conducted at a high current density of 7 a g− 1 (Fig. S9). The reduced ZnCo2O4 electrode exhibits exceptional cyclic stability, retaining 90.82% of its initial specific capacitance after 2000 cycles, significantly outperforming the pristine electrode (48.34% retention). This extremely low attenuation rate demonstrates the potential of oxygen vacancy engineering in durable energy storage devices. To elucidate the cause of the increased specific capacitance, first-principles calculations based on density functional theory were conducted to examine the electronic structure. Figure 5a and b present the calculated band gaps for pristine ZnCo2O4 and reduced ZnCo2O4, respectively. The introduction of a single oxygen vacancy within the unit cell of the spinel structure of ZnCo2O4 results in a decrease in band gap by 16.2% (from 1.662 to 1.359 eV). A smaller band gap signifies enhanced electrical conductivity, thereby confirming that introducing oxygen vacancies can improve conductivity at the material’s surface. This observation is further substantiated by analyzing the corresponding DOS. Specifically, pristine ZnCo2O4 exhibits lower DOS near the Fermi level compared to reduced ZnCo2O4 (refer to Fig. 5c and d). According to quantum theory, only electrons situated close to the Fermi level can contribute to current flow under an external electric field; thus, electrical conductivity correlates directly with electron availability at this energy level. Consequently, there is a significant enhancement in electrical conductivity observed in reduced ZnCo2O4. The substantial increase in electrical conductivity for ZnCo2O4 accounts for its low resistance and improved supercapacitive performance. The introduction of oxygen vacancies creates localized defect states within the bandgap, effectively reducing the energy barrier for electron excitation. Concurrently, the partial reduction of Co3+–Co2+ injects additional free electrons into the conduction band. This dual effect synergistically elevates the DOS near the Fermi level, as confirmed by DFT. The enhanced bulk conductivity (via bandgap narrowing) and interfacial charge transfer (via exposed low-coordination metal sites) collectively account for the doubled specific capacitance [40, 41].

The role of oxygen vacancies in ion accessibility merits further elaboration. As confirmed by XPS, the removal of surface oxygen atoms exposes under-coordinated Zn/Co sites, creating additional active centers for Faradaic reactions (M–O + OH− ↔ M–O–OH). Charge redistribution around vacancies lowers the energy barrier for OH− adsorption, evidenced by reduced equivalent series resistance (Rs = 0.98 Ω vs. 1.15 Ω). Synergistically, the narrowed bandgap (1.66 → 1.36 eV) and elevated density of states near EF accelerate electron transfer to adsorbed ions. This hierarchical optimization sustains 216 F g− 1 at 10 A g− 1, doubling the pristine material’s performance.

Conclusion

A low-cost and large-area method for synthesizing nanoflower-like ZnCo2O4 with surface oxygen vacancies as electrode materials in supercapacitors is presented. The presence of oxygen vacancies on the surface of the ZnCo2O4 nanoflowers is confirmed through XPS. Utilizing density functional theory, the energy bands and DOS of ZnCo2O4 are analyzed, revealing that treatment with NaBH4 results in a reduced band gap and an increased DOS near the Fermi level compared to pristine ZnCo2O4. Consequently, the specific capacitance of reduced ZnCo2O4 nearly doubles that of its untreated counterpart. The versatility of this NaBH4 reduction strategy is anticipated to extend to other transition metal oxides commonly used in energy storage, such as spinel NiCo2O4 and hematite TiO2 [42, 43]. These materials share similar characteristics with ZnCo2O4, including the presence of multivalent cations whose redox activity can be enhanced by surface oxygen vacancies, and structures that could benefit from improved conductivity. Our preliminary comparative result with MnO2 further supports the potential applicability across different oxide systems, although the efficacy will inherently depend on the specific material’s electronic structure, bandgap, and stability under reducing conditions. This straightforward and efficient approach significantly enhances both the conductivity and specific capacitance of metal oxides, offering potential applicability to other materials as well.

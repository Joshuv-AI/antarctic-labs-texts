A novel model based on ferroptosis-related lncRNAs for predicting prognosis and adjuvant therapy response in colon adenocarcinoma
Authors: Wentao Wu, Gaojun Xu, Li Zhu, Shuohao Wang, Jiaqiang Luan, Zhe Peng, Jinghuang Tong, Yiqiang Yuan, Zhenwei Ge
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundColon adenocarcinoma (COAD) remains the most prevalent pathological subtype of colorectal carcinoma, yet its long-term survival outcomes remain suboptimal despite advances in clinical management. Ferroptosis is an iron-dependent regulated cell death modality that participates critically in tumor growth and development. Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are important regulators of ferroptosis pathways in cancer. Accordingly, the present study aimed to explore the prognostic value and therapeutic response of ferroptosis-related lncRNAs in COAD.MethodsFerroptosis-related lncRNAs were initially screened via co-expression analysis, then a ferroptosis-related lncRNA prognostic signature was established by univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. We comprehensively validated using multiple approaches, which included survival analysis, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis and a nomogram. In addition, we analyzed the potential associations of this signature with immunotherapeutic and chemotherapy responsiveness.ResultsWe established and subjected to validation a risk signature based on seven ferroptosis-related lncRNAs (AC107308.1, ZKSCAN2-DT, LINC02381, SFTA1P, SNHG16, AL137782.1 and LINC01138). The risk model showed potential value in stratifying distinct immunotherapeutic response patterns to immunotherapy in different subgroups of COAD patients. Five candidate therapeutic agents were predicted and differential drug sensitivity profiles were observed.ConclusionsThe risk model based on ferroptosis-related lncRNAs showed favorable prognostic predictive performance for COAD and might provide a potential reference for clinical personalized adjuvant therapy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05306-4.

Introduction

According to the latest GLOBOCAN 2022 statistical data, colorectal cancer (CRC) continues to represent a leading cause of cancer-related morbidity and mortality worldwide, ranking third in incidence but second in mortality among all malignant neoplasms, this epidemiologic pattern underscores the persistent public health challenge posed by colorectal cancer, with marked geographic heterogeneity in both incidence and mortality rates reported globally [1]. Colorectal adenocarcinoma (COAD) constitutes the predominant pathological subtype of CRC and remains one of the most prevalent malignancies globally, with a gradual upward trend observed in both incidence and mortality in recent decades [2]. Clinically, COAD is characterized by high disease‑related mortality and morbidity, ranking within the top five cancer‑related causes of death globally, anatomical constraints within the colonic lumen further compound diagnostic delays and therapeutic challenges in affected patients [3]. Despite progressive advances in multidisciplinary therapeutic strategies, the quality of life and prognosis for COAD patients remain largely unchanged, with 5-year survival rates remaining persistently poor across most population [4]. Mounting evidence indicates that molecular markers, especially long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs), serve as pivotal regulators of tumor cell proliferation, modulate the tumor microenvironment (TME) and immune system regulation [5, 6]. Therefore, the identification of biomarkers with prognostic value has novel diagnostic and therapeutic implications for COAD patients.

Ferroptosis is a form of cell death resulting from iron-mediated accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) [7, 8]. The two regulatory factors controlling ferroptosis are lipid peroxidation and iron metabolism [9, 10]. Recent studies have demonstrated that ferroptosis-related lncRNAs are closely associated with tumor immune infiltration, chemotherapy resistance and prognosis in colorectal cancer [11]. Other work has highlighted the potential of ferroptosis regulators as predictive biomarkers for immunotherapy response in gastrointestinal malignancies [12]. Furthermore, lncRNA signatures have been verified to improve prognostic stratification and therapeutic decision-making for colon adenocarcinoma [13]. Ferroptosis has emerged as a key mechanism of cell death during cancer progression, and metastasis-prone or metastatic cancer cells exhibit increased sensitivity to ferroptosis [14]. Induction of ferroptosis shows therapeutic potential in cancer treatment, including immunotherapy, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy [15].

The lncRNAs act as crucial regulators in the modulation of ferroptosis pathways in cancer [16]. Overexpression of lncRNA GABPB1-AS1 suppresses the translation of its corresponding GABPB1 protein, leading to ROS accumulation and ferroptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cells [17]. LINC00336, by binding to miR-6852, functions as a negative regulator of ferroptosis in lung cancer [18]. In lung adenocarcinoma, the interaction between lncRNA X-inactive-specific transcript (XIST) and glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) constitutes a critical regulatory axis that modulates cell viability and ferroptosis [19]. Remarkably, only a limited number of ferroptosis-related lncRNAs have been functionally characterized to date. Thus, a comprehensive analysis to identify key ferroptosis-related lncRNAs may hold prognostic value for patients with colon adenocarcinoma COAD.

For this purpose, we identified 1,375 ferroptosis-related lncRNAs and further screened out seven prognostic lncRNAs based on The Cancer Genome Atlas-COAD cohort data. A prognostic risk model based on these seven lncRNAs was constructed and its predictive performance was validated. We then investigated the potential application of the predictive model in immunotherapy and chemotherapy.

Methods

COAD patient information collection

In the present study, RNA sequence data, clinical characteristics and mutation data were extracted from The Cancer Genome Atlas-COAD dataset [20, 21]. Clinical information included age (31–90 years), gender, the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) stage (AJCC stages Ⅰ, Ⅱ, Ⅲ and Ⅳ), survival status and survival time. We excluded samples with overall survival (OS) less than 30 days or lacking clinical information to minimize potential bias in the analysis. Patients with extremely short survival are often affected by early non–tumor-related deaths, perioperative complications, or severe comorbidities, which may not accurately reflect tumor-specific prognosis [22, 23]. Identification of ferroptosis-related genes and ferroptosis-related lncRNAs

Based on the FerrDb database [24], we examined 259 ferroptosis-related genes, including 108 ferroptosis drivers, 111 ferroptosis markers and 69 ferroptosis suppressors. We first identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with false discovery rates < 0.05 and |log2fold-changes|≥1 between normal and tumor samples. To investigate the biological functions of the ferroptosis DEGs in COAD, we performed Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analysis. Ferroptosis-related lncRNAs were identified using Pearson’s correlation analysis with a threshold of |R| > 0.6 and P < 0.001. This threshold was selected as a balanced criterion to ensure both sufficient candidate lncRNAs and reliable co-expression relationships.

Establishment and evaluation of prognostic ferroptosis-related lncRNA signatures

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify ferroptosis-related lncRNAs associated with prognosis in patients with COAD. Results from the multivariate Cox regression were employed to establish a prognostic risk signature based on ferroptosis-related lncRNAs. The specific formula was Risk score = [Coefficient (lncRNA1) × expression (lncRNA1)] + [Coefficient (lncRNA2) × expression (lncRNA2)] + …… + [Coefficient (lncRNAn) × expression (lncRNAn)], where n = the next lncRNA. COAD patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median risk score, which was selected as a commonly used and unbiased cutoff in retrospective prognostic studies to ensure balanced group sizes and reduce the risk of overfitting [25–27]. The prognostic performance of the risk signature was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC).

Construction and validation of a predictive nomogram

Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to identify independent prognostic factors of survival in patients with COAD. After simultaneously considering the effects of age, gender, risk score and AJCC stage, adjusted hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were estimated. A nomogram was constructed based on the results of multivariate Cox regression, and the results for the nomogram were resampled 1,000 times using the bootstrap method for internal data verification. Calibration curves were used to assess the difference between observed and predicted survival rates. For verification, we divided the data set according to 1:1 and completed 1000 resampling as the result of the test set for verification.

In addition, an independent external validation was performed using the GEO dataset (GSE39582). Risk scores for each patient were calculated based on the same formula derived from the TCGA cohort, and the predictive performance of the nomogram was further evaluated in this external cohort.

Functional and immunity analysis of the ferroptosis-related lncRNA model

To validate the KEGG functional enrichment pathways, we conducted Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and set the statistical threshold as P < 0.05. We then evaluated the role of the risk model in immune cell infiltration using four algorithms, including TIMER [28], MCPcounter [29], CIBERSORT [30, 31] and ssGSEA [32] algorithms. The specific parameter settings for our ssGSEA analysis are as follows, we employed gene sets consisting of 200–500 genes, which are representative of immune functions and pathways; The enrichment scores were computed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) statistic, which measures the degree to which a gene set is overrepresented at the extremes (top or bottom) of the ranked list of genes in each sample; To ensure comparability across samples, we standardized the enrichment scores using the Z-score method; We performed 1000 permutations to assess the statistical significance of the enrichment scores, improving the reliability of our analysis.

We used a heatmap to visualize the significant differences in immune cell infiltration between the high risk and low risk groups and compared the results of the four algorithms. We calculated the scores of 13 immune-related indicators using the GSVA package and identified differences in immunity scores between the high risk and low risk groups. In addition, we examined differences in the expression of immune gene checkpoints between the two groups to assess the immune response of the model.

Prediction of response to immunotherapy by the ferroptosis-related lncRNA model

Mutation data from the high risk and low risk groups were visualized by R package maftools for gene mutation frequency. We further applied the Tumor Immune Dysfunction and Exclusion (TIDE) method [33] to assess the response to immunotherapy in both groups of COAD patients. For subgroup analysis, TIDE scores were compared between patients with different AJCC stages using violin plots, and statistical significance was assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Exploration of potential drugs targeting the ferroptosis-related lncRNA model in clinical treatment

We used DeSigN [34] (http://design.cancerresearch.my) drug repositioning to predict potential compounds/inhibitors targeting the lncRNA model for chemotherapy response in COAD patients. The R package pRRophetic [35] was used to evaluate the estimated half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) levels of the targeted drugs obtained from Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer (GDSC, https://www.cancerrxgene.org). We further performed a signature-to-signature correlation analysis using the GEPIA database to evaluate the association between the seven ferroptosis-related lncRNAs and the transcriptional levels of the target genes of the predicted drugs, providing additional biological support for the relevance of the identified lncRNA model.

Cross-algorithm immune infiltration analysis and identification of consensus immune cells

Immune cell infiltration was estimated using TIMER, MCP-counter, CIBERSORT, and ssGSEA. Cross-algorithm consistency was assessed using Spearman correlation analysis. Differential infiltration between high- and low-risk groups was evaluated using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with FDR correction. Immune cells showing significant and directionally consistent differences across at least two algorithms, together with moderate inter-algorithm concordance, were defined as consensus immune cells.

Given the observed increase in macrophage infiltration in the high-risk group, we further characterized macrophage polarization states using the CIBERSORT algorithm. The relative fractions of M1 and M2 macrophages were extracted for each sample, and differences between high- and low-risk groups were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with FDR correction. This analysis was performed to determine whether the increased macrophage infiltration was associated with pro-inflammatory (M1-like) or anti-inflammatory/pro-tumorigenic (M2-like) polarization patterns.

Statistical analysis

R software, version 3.6.1 was used to conduct statistical analysis of the data. Univariate and multivariate Cox regressions were conducted to identify prognostic ferroptosis-related lncRNAs and to evaluate the independent prognostic indicator of the risk signature. Different subgroups of OS and progression-free survival (PFS) were compared using Kaplan-Meier analysis with log-rank tests. The Wilcoxon test was used to measure the differences in immune-related indicators, TIDE scores and chemotherapy responses between the high and low risk groups. P-values were calculated using two-sided statistical tests, and P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Biological functions and pathway analysis of ferroptosis-related genes

A total of 68 ferroptosis-related DEGs were identified between normal and COAD samples (26 downregulated and 42 upregulated; Table S1). To characterize the function of these DEGs, GO and KEGG bubble plots were generated to display the enrichment results (Fig. 1A, B). The primary biological processes (BPs) participated in the process of response to oxidative stress. The most abundant cellular component (CC) was an apical part of the cell. The molecular function (MF) mainly regulated iron ion binding, drug transmembrane transporter activity and oxidoreductase activity. The DEGs were mainly enriched in synthesis of microRNAs, mTOR signaling pathway and p53 signaling pathway based on KEGG analysis.

Identification of potential prognostic ferroptosis-related lncRNAs and construction of a risk model based on ferroptosis-related lncRNAs

A total of 1,375 lncRNAs exhibited significant correlations with ferroptosis-related genes based on Pearson’s correlation analysis (Table S2). After univariate Cox regression analysis, 11 ferroptosis-related lncRNAs were identified to be significantly associated with OS of COAD patients (P < 0.05; Fig. 1C). We then obtained independently seven out of the 11 ferroptosis-related lncRNAs as prognostic lncRNAs based on the multivariate COX analysis. The regression coefficients of the seven lncRNAs (AC107308.1, ZKSCAN2-DT, LINC02381, SFTA1P, SNHG16, AL137782.1 and LINC01138) are shown in Fig. 1D. An interaction network was constructed to show the relationships between lncRNAs and mRNAs (Fig. 1E). In the Sankey diagram, AL137782.1 and SNHG16 were independent prognostic protective factors, and the remaining five ferroptosis-related lncRNAs were risk markers (Fig. 1F). In addition, a risk model was constructed based on the risk score calculated from the expression levels and coefficients for the seven lncRNAs.

Prognostic value and multifaceted evaluation of the ferroptosis-related lncRNA model

In the ferroptosis-related lncRNA model, COAD patients were assigned to a high risk and low risk group based on the median risk score. Figure 2A and B respectively reveal the distribution of risk scores and survival status between the two groups. Most lncRNAs were highly expressed in the high risk group (Fig. 2C). Meanwhile, we found that patients in the high risk group had shorter OS and PFS than patients in the low risk group (Fig. 2D and E). In order to evaluate the predictive performance of the model, ROC curve analysis for 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year overall survival prediction demonstrated that the Area Under the Curve (AUC) was 0.671, 0.700 and 0.774, respectively (Fig. 2F). The results indicated the model exhibited moderate and acceptable prognostic predictive performance. The results of the test set and the training set are basically the same (Figure S1-2).

To further validate the robustness and generalizability of the model, an independent external validation was performed using the GSE39582 cohort. Using the same risk score formula, patients were stratified into high- and low-risk groups based on the median cutoff. Consistent with the TCGA cohort, patients in the high-risk group exhibited significantly worse overall survival compared with those in the low-risk group. Furthermore, time-dependent ROC analysis in the external cohort yielded AUCs of 0.656, 0.604, and 0.669 for predicting 1-, 3-, and 5-year overall survival, respectively, confirming the stable predictive performance of the model across independent datasets (Figure S3).

The relationship between the model of ferroptosis-related lncRNAs and clinicopathological features is shown in Fig. 3. Moreover, univariate and multivariate Cox analyses were applied to assess whether the model could play an independent prognostic role for COAD. Figure S4 shows that the model was unrelated to clinicopathological features, including gender, age, tumor stage (HR: 1.071, 95% CI: 1.048–1.094, P < 0.001). Subsequently, we established a stable nomogram combining the risk score and clinical characteristics to predict the 1-, 3-, and 5-year survival probabilities for COAD patients (Fig. 4A). The actual 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates versus the corresponding nomogram-predicted values demonstrated satisfactory consistency (Fig. 4B and D).

Cross-algorithm consistency analysis of immune cell infiltration

To enhance the robustness of tumor immune microenvironment characterization, immune cell infiltration was evaluated using four independent computational algorithms, including TIMER, MCP-counter, CIBERSORT, and ssGSEA. Owing to the inherent methodological differences among these approaches, a cross-algorithm consistency analysis was performed to identify immune cell subsets with stable and reproducible infiltration profiles. First, Spearman correlation analyses were conducted to evaluate the consistency of immune cell infiltration scores derived from different algorithms. As shown in Figure S4, estimates of macrophage infiltration displayed strong positive correlations across multiple algorithms, while several other immune cell types exhibited weaker or inconsistent associations. Next, differential immune infiltration between high- and low-risk groups was evaluated separately for each algorithm (Table S3). Immune cell types showing statistically significant differences with consistent directional trends across multiple algorithms were defined as consensus immune cells. Notably, macrophages consistently showed higher infiltration levels in the high-risk group across TIMER, MCP-counter, and ssGSEA, accompanied by significant effect sizes and false discovery rates. Similarly, B cells showed reproducible and directionally concordant infiltration differences across all four algorithms (Figure S5-6). In contrast, other immune cell populations, such as CD8+ T cells, CD4+ T cells, dendritic cells, NK cells, and neutrophils, exhibited heterogeneous patterns with inconsistent significance or opposite directions among algorithms, and were therefore not considered robust consensus signals. Based on the combined evidence from cross-algorithm correlation analysis and differential infiltration consistency, macrophages and B cells were defined as consensus immune cells for subsequent analyses (Figure S5-6 and Table S4).

Given the consistent increase in macrophage infiltration in the high-risk group across multiple algorithms, we further investigated the functional phenotype of macrophages by analyzing their polarization states using the CIBERSORT algorithm. The infiltration level of M1 macrophages was significantly higher in the high-risk group compared to the low-risk group (P = 0.0055; Figure S7A), whereas no significant difference was observed for M2 macrophages between the two groups (P = 0.34; Figure S7B). These findings indicate that the increased macrophage infiltration in the high-risk group is not primarily driven by M2 polarization.

Estimating the tumor immunotherapy response, and identification of potential drugs using the ferroptosis-related lncRNA model

Based on the aforementioned enrichment analysis and differential analyses of immune-related factors, we hypothesized that the ferroptosis-related lncRNA risk model may be associated with immunotherapy response of COAD. Thus, we used the TIDE algorithm to compare the immunotherapy responses between the high risk and low risk groups. The high risk group showed higher TIDE scores than the low risk group (P < 0.001; Fig. 7C). To further evaluate the relationship between TIDE scores and clinical stage, patients were stratified according to AJCC stage. As shown in the violin plot, Stage III–IV patients showed a trend toward higher TIDE scores compared with Stage I–II patients; however, this difference was not statistically significant according to the Wilcoxon rank-sum test (P = 0.17; Figure S8). To further elucidate the underlying mechanisms of the risk model, two groups in the gene mutation landscape were mapped using the R package maftools. In Fig. 7D and E, we showed the top 20 genes with the highest mutation frequency in the high risk and low risk groups. TP53 was found to be relatively highly mutated in the high risk group compared with the low risk group.

Next, 14 candidate targeted agents were predicted using the DeSigN webserver to identify potential compounds or inhibitors based on our lncRNA model for chemotherapeutic responses in COAD patients (Table 2). By comparing the estimated IC50 levels of the 14 drugs, our results revealed that five drugs were significantly different in the estimated IC50 between the two groups, including Dasatinib (P = 0.029), 17-AAG (P = 0.035), AZD6482 (P = 0.003), AZD-0530 (P = 0.002), and Z-LLNle-CHO (P = 0.017; Fig. 8). The high risk group had high estimated IC50s for the above five drugs, suggesting that the high risk group was more sensitive to the five drugs.

To further explore the biological relevance of these findings, we performed a signature-to-signature correlation analysis using the GEPIA database, assessing the association between the seven ferroptosis-related lncRNAs and the transcriptional levels of the five drug target genes. This analysis revealed a moderate but statistically significant positive correlation (Spearman ρ = 0.40, P = 3.2 × 10− 12), indicating that tumors with higher expression of the ferroptosis-related lncRNA signature tend to concurrently express higher levels of the drug target genes. These results provide additional mechanistic support for the predictive value of the lncRNA-based risk model in estimating drug sensitivity in COAD patients (Figure S9).

Discussion

COAD is highly metastatic, and patients with advanced-stage disease carry an extremely poor prognosis owing to limited therapeutic strategies and pronounced tumor aggressiveness [36]. New therapeutic targets need to be developed to improve OS in patients with COAD [37]. Ferroptosis induction represents a promising anticancer strategy that eliminates tumor cells and enhances chemotherapeutic sensitivity [38]. Moreover, its related lncRNAs have also attracted attention in the regulation of iron metabolism in cancer [16]. Accordingly, the present study investigated the roles of ferroptosis-related lncRNAs in COAD prognosis, immune regulation, and chemotherapy response, providing potential insights for clinical prognosis and treatment strategies.

First, we identified seven ferroptosis-related lncRNAs (AC107308.1, ZKSCAN2-DT, LINC02381, SFTA1P, SNHG16, AL137782.1 and LINC01138) as independent prognostic indicators of COAD. These lncRNAs may be involved in the biological process of COAD. LINC02381 suppresses colon cancer via the PI3K pathway31 and acts as a tumor suppressor in gastric cancer by regulating the Wnt pathway32 [39]. Meanwhile, LINC02381 functions as a suppressor of gastric cancer by regulating the wnt signaling pathway [40]. Downregulated SFTA1P is associated with cisplatin resistance in gastric cancer via the SFTA1P/miR-206 axis, suggesting its potential as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target [41]. SFTA1P also serves as a prognostic and diagnostic biomarker in lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) and may act as a therapeutic target [42]. SNHG16 is frequently overexpressed across numerous malignancies, including HCC, lung cancer, colorectal cancer, and others [43]. Further analysis has shown that SNHG16 plays a carcinogenic role in most tumors, mainly associated with poor prognostic factors such as large tumor size, advanced stage, lymph node metastasis and vascular invasion [44]. Our study also demonstrated that SNHG16 may be a remarkable potential prognostic biomarker and ferroptosis-based therapeutic target for COAD. LINC01138 promotes tumor progression in HCC and gastric cancer by enhancing proliferation, invasion, and migration [45]. Additionally, we have revealed the potential role of three lncRNAs in COAD (AL137782.1, AC107308.1 and ZKSCAN2-DT), which will require further study. AL137782.1 is implicated in cell migration and cancer progression by regulating LMO7, and may indirectly modulate ferroptosis via survival pathways [45]. Specifically, AL137782.1 has been found to positively regulate the expression of LMO7, a protein involved in cell migration, suggesting its potential influence on tumor invasiveness and metastasis. This supports the notion that AL137782.1 could indirectly affect ferroptosis by modulating cell survival pathways. AC107308.1 has been incorporated into COAD prognostic models and may serve as a progression biomarker, despite limited direct evidence linking it to ferroptosis [45]. ZKSCAN2-DT has been associated with tumor immune microenvironment and prognosis in COAD [45]. Additionally, its differential expression in tumor tissues compared to normal tissues highlights its potential as a therapeutic target.

Many studies have shown that the TME, especially tumor-infiltrating immune cells, has important applications in the immunotherapy and prognosis of COAD [46, 47]. Specifically, the immune landscape within the TME has been shown to significantly influence patient outcomes and responses to immunotherapeutic interventions. However, the role of ferroptosis-related lncRNAs of COAD in the TME remains unclear. We performed GSEA analysis using the risk model and found that the high risk group was associated with immune-related pathways, including intestinal immune network for IgA production, B/T cell receptor signaling pathway and leukocyte transendothelial migration. Such findings are in line with previous reports highlighting the significance of these pathways in anti-tumor immunity [46, 47]. Then, based on four algorithms (TIMER, MCPcounter, CIBERSORT and ssGSEA), the immune cell infiltration status was compared. There was a correlation between risk score and immune cell infiltration, such as activated CD8 T cell, activated B cell, NK cell, macrophage and regulatory T cell. Importantly, by integrating results from multiple immune infiltration algorithms, we identified macrophages and B cells as consensus immune cell populations with consistent and reproducible differences between risk groups. Notably, macrophage infiltration was consistently increased in the high-risk group across multiple methods. Further analysis of macrophage polarization revealed that this increase was primarily associated with elevated M1 macrophage infiltration rather than M2 macrophages. This finding is of particular interest, as M1 macrophages are generally considered to exert anti-tumor effects, whereas M2 macrophages are associated with immunosuppression and tumor progression. The enrichment of M1 macrophages in the high-risk group suggests that the tumor microenvironment is not purely immunosuppressive but may instead reflect a state of immune activation accompanied by functional dysregulation. This may partly explain why patients in the high-risk group still exhibit poor prognosis despite increased immune infiltration, potentially due to ineffective anti-tumor immune responses or immune exhaustion. These results suggest that ferroptosis-related lncRNAs shape the TME immune landscape and may affect tumor immunogenicity and immunotherapy responsiveness [46, 47]. Meanwhile, there were differences in the expression of immune checkpoints between the high and low risk groups. These results further support the notion that ferroptosis-related lncRNAs can regulate immune evasion mechanisms employed by tumor cells, thereby affecting the efficacy of immunotherapies. The increased TIDE score in the high risk group indicated a poorer response to immunotherapy. This observation aligns with the growing body of evidence that implicates the TME’s immune contexture as a determinant of immunotherapeutic success [46, 47]. Collectively, our risk model serves as a valuable tool for evaluating TME characteristics in COAD, offering novel insights for immunotherapy.

Finally, given that GO terms were enriched in drug transmembrane transporter activity, we detected the presence of a significant increase in the estimated IC50 for five of the 14 drugs predicted in the high risk group. The results suggested that the high risk group was more sensitive to the five drugs. These drugs included Dasatinib, 17-AAG (Tanespimycin), AZD6482, AZD-0530 (Saracatinib) and Z-LLNle-CHO. To better connect these statistical results to clinical practice, we provide annotations on the clinical relevance of each drug in COAD. Dasatinib is an FDA-approved Bcr-Abl inhibitor that has also been shown to block 5-fluorouracil-mediated apoptosis by inhibiting activation of Steroid Receptor Coactivator (SRC) in colon carcinoma [48]. Our study found that the high-risk group had higher estimated IC50 for Dasatinib, suggesting increased sensitivity to this drug. This is consistent with preclinical data showing the inhibitory effect of Dasatinib on colon cancer cells, particularly those overexpressing SRC [48]. The clinical application of Dasatinib in COAD patients, especially those with SRC overexpression, may therefore be a promising therapeutic strategy. Dasatinib has been shown to effectively inhibit Src kinase activity, leading to decreased proliferation and increased apoptosis in colon cancer cells. However, its combination with conventional chemotherapy drugs like 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) has shown limited clinical benefit in metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC), warranting further investigation into optimal combination regimens [48] (Table S5).

17-AAG is an HSP90 inhibitor that has been shown to induce cell apoptosis [49] and reduced cell migration and adhesion in colon carcinoma cells [50]. Our results indicate that the high-risk group is more sensitive to 17-AAG, which aligns with its known mechanism of action in disrupting oncogenic signaling pathways. Clinical evaluation of 17-AAG, especially in combination regimens, is warranted in COAD. Studies have shown that 17-AAG can prolong progression-free survival in certain patient subsets, but overall survival benefits are variable, highlighting the need for patient stratification and personalized treatment approaches [49]. AZD-0530 (Saracatinib) is a well-characterized SRC inhibitor that can reverse doxorubicin resistance in doxorubicin-resistant colon carcinoma cells [51]. The heightened sensitivity of the high-risk group to AZD-0530 supports its role in targeting SRC-driven tumor progression and chemoresistance. The clinical potential of AZD-0530 in COAD, especially in combination with other therapeutic agents, should be further evaluated. PI3Kβ inhibitor AZD6482, as a potential therapeutic drug for glioma, promotes cell cycle arrest and cell apoptosis [52]. Despite limited direct data in COAD, frequent dysregulation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway in colorectal cancer supports the therapeutic potential of AZD6482. Our finding that the high-risk group is more sensitive to AZD6482 supports further exploration of this drug in COAD. Preliminary safety data suggest that AZD6482 is well-tolerated, with manageable side effects. However, comprehensive safety profiles from larger clinical trials are needed [52].

Proteasome inhibitor Z-LLNle-CHO has been reported to induce cell apoptosis in colon carcinoma [53] and precursor-B acute lymphoblastic leukemia [54]. Our results indicate that the high-risk group is more sensitive to Z-LLNle-CHO, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic agent in COAD. Preclinically, it inhibits tumor engraftment and may improve survival, warranting clinical validation [54]. Further studies are needed to explore the clinical applicability of Z-LLNle-CHO in COAD treatment. In summary, we have constructed a risk model that may predict the prognoses of COAD patients by modulating immune and drug responses. Patients in the high risk group have worse prognoses, but they may be improved by immunotherapy and chemotherapy, which can guide individualized treatment.

Our study demonstrated the prognostic value of a ferroptosis-related lncRNA signature in COAD and suggested its potential utility in predicting responses to immunotherapy and chemotherapy. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study is a retrospective bioinformatics analysis based primarily on publicly available datasets, which may introduce inherent selection bias. Although the model was validated using both internal validation strategies and an independent external cohort, further validation in large-scale, multi-center prospective studies is still required to confirm its robustness and generalizability across different populations and sequencing platforms. Second, the predictive performance of the model for immunotherapy and chemotherapy responses was inferred from computational algorithms, and its clinical applicability requires further confirmation through well-designed prospective clinical trials. Third, the underlying regulatory mechanisms of the identified ferroptosis-related lncRNAs were not investigated in this study. Although some lncRNAs included in the model have been reported to be aberrantly expressed in tumors, their specific roles in COAD remain to be elucidated. Finally, this study lacks experimental validation, such as qRT-PCR and functional assays. Future studies incorporating clinical samples and experimental approaches are warranted to validate the expression patterns and biological functions of these lncRNAs.

Conclusion

In conclusion, we constructed and validated a ferroptosis-related lncRNA signature that predicts prognosis in COAD, and identified five candidate therapeutic agents. Furthermore, this risk model enables evaluation of immune responses and drug sensitivity in COAD patients. Our study provides new insights into stratified and neoadjuvant treatment strategies for COAD.

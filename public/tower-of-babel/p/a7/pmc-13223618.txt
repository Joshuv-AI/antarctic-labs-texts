Culturally tailored mHealth interventions for chronic low back pain in rural sub-Saharan Africa: a systematic review protocol
Authors: Bashir Bello, Abdullahi Adejare, Ushotanefe Useh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionChronic low back pain (CLBP) is a leading global cause of disability and imposes significant social and economic burdens in rural sub-Saharan Africa (SSA). Mobile health (mHealth) technologies offer opportunities to improve CLBP management in areas with limited physiotherapy services. However, the effectiveness and cultural appropriateness of mHealth interventions for CLBP in SSA remain insufficiently evaluated.ObjectiveThis systematic review will evaluate the effectiveness of culturally tailored mHealth interventions for adults with CLBP in rural SSA. The review will also identify barriers to implementing these interventions in rural SSA settings.Methods and analysisThis protocol outlines a mixed-methods systematic review that will evaluate the effectiveness of culturally tailored mHealth interventions for CLBP and identify barriers to their implementation in rural SSA. A comprehensive search will be conducted across multiple databases, including MEDLINE (PubMed), CINAHL, Scopus, Web of Science and African Journals Online. Eligible studies will include randomised controlled trials, quasi-experimental, observational and qualitative studies. Risk of bias (RoB) will be assessed using design-specific tools, including the Cochrane RoB 2 tool and Joanna Briggs Institute critical appraisal checklists. Quantitative and qualitative findings will be synthesised using a convergent integrated approach. Where appropriate, meta-analysis will be conducted; otherwise, a structured narrative synthesis will be undertaken.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required as this study involves analysis of published data. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations and engagement with relevant stakeholders to inform policy and practice.

Introduction

Chronic low back pain (CLBP) is a leading cause of disability globally and disproportionately affects populations in resource-limited settings, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa (SSA).1 The WHO recognises CLBP as a major contributor to years lived with disability, with an increasing burden in low- and middle-income regions.2 In rural SSA, prevalence estimates range from 32% to 56%, driven largely by physically demanding occupations such as farming and manual labour, alongside restricted access to healthcare services.35 Shortages of rehabilitation professionals, long travel distances and high out-of-pocket costs further limit access to evidence-based CLBP management in these settings.6 In rural populations in SSA, people are faced with compounded structural and contextual barriers, including weak health system infrastructure, limited workforce capacity and geographic isolation, alongside sociocultural factors such as diverse health beliefs, language variation and low health literacy.7 8 These factors influence health-seeking behaviour and engagement with care, reducing the effectiveness of standardised interventions developed in high-income countries when applied without adaptation.9 10

Mobile health (mHealth) technologies, defined as the use of mobile devices to support healthcare delivery, offer a promising approach to addressing these challenges by enabling scalable and remote interventions.11 12 Mobile phone penetration has increased substantially across SSA; however, access to smartphones, reliable internet connectivity and digital literacy remains uneven, particularly in rural communities.13 As a result, mHealth interventions that are not adapted to local contexts may experience poor uptake, limited engagement and reduced effectiveness.

Recent systematic reviews provide growing evidence for the effectiveness of digital and mHealth interventions in the management of musculoskeletal pain. For example, a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis reported that digital health interventions produce small to moderate improvements in pain, physical function and quality of life among individuals with musculoskeletal conditions.14 Similarly, more recent evidence indicates that mHealth-based exercise interventions are associated with reductions in pain intensity and improvements in functional outcomes, although the certainty of evidence remains limited due to heterogeneity and methodological constraints.15 In addition, a large-scale synthesis of mHealth interventions for chronic pain highlights generally high levels of usability, feasibility and acceptability, suggesting that these tools can support patient engagement and self-management.16 However, these reviews predominantly draw on studies conducted in high-income settings, with limited representation from low-resource or rural contexts. Importantly, they also emphasise gaps in implementation fidelity, long-term effectiveness and contextual adaptation of interventions.

Cultural tailoring enhances the relevance and acceptability of health interventions by aligning them with local languages, beliefs and practices.17 18 In rural SSA, this may involve incorporating culturally appropriate messaging, simplifying technological interfaces and adapting content to local patterns of mobile device use. Despite growing interest in mHealth, few systematic reviews have specifically examined culturally tailored interventions for CLBP within rural populations in the region. Evidence indicates that barriers to mHealth implementation in SSA include limited digital literacy, inadequate infrastructure, financial constraints and cultural resistance to technology-based care.13 19 These challenges are more pronounced in rural settings, reinforcing the need for context-specific evidence to inform implementation.

Given the high burden of CLBP, persistent inequities in access to rehabilitation services and the expanding but uneven reach of mobile technologies, rural SSA represents a critical and underexplored context for mHealth interventions. This review will therefore assess the effectiveness of culturally tailored mHealth interventions for CLBP in rural SSA and identify key barriers to their implementation. The findings will inform the design of contextually appropriate, scalable strategies to improve CLBP management in underserved populations.

Methods and analysis

We will follow Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines.20 We registered the protocol with PROSPERO (ID: https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/PROSPERO/view/CRD420251172472). Although scoping reviews are commonly used to map evidence in emerging fields, the primary aim of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of culturally tailored mHealth interventions for CLBP. This objective aligns with the methodological purpose of a systematic review, which enables critical appraisal of study quality and, where appropriate, synthesis of intervention outcomes. While the review also seeks to identify barriers to implementation, this is included as a secondary objective to contextualise findings rather than to map the evidence base alone. In addition, the inclusion of a range of study designs, including randomised and non-randomised studies, allows for a comprehensive assessment of intervention effectiveness in real-world settings.

Given the need to inform clinical practice and policy with appraised evidence, a systematic review was considered more appropriate than a scoping review for addressing the research objectives. Hence, we will search electronic databases for studies evaluating culturally adapted mHealth interventions for CLBP in rural SSA. We will include randomised controlled trials (RCTs), quasi-experimental studies, cohort studies and qualitative research that examine intervention outcomes and barriers.

Eligibility criteria

Study designs

This study will be conducted as a mixed-methods systematic review, integrating evidence from quantitative (randomised and non-randomised) and qualitative studies to provide a comprehensive understanding of the effectiveness and implementation of culturally tailored mHealth interventions for CLBP.

Participants

Eligible studies most focus on adults aged 18 years and older who have been diagnosed with CLBP for a duration of at least 3 months. Studies that focus on populations with acute back pain or non-musculoskeletal conditions will be excluded.

Intervention

Only studies that investigate mHealth or mobile phone-based interventions that have been culturally adapted for the SSA population. Cultural adaptation will be defined as any intervention that incorporates local language, community engagement, traditional beliefs or context-specific content to improve relevance and effectiveness in the target population.

Comparator(s)/control

The comparator in the included studies could be standard physiotherapy care, non-tailored mHealth interventions or no intervention at all.

Outcomes and prioritisation

Primary outcomes will be pain intensity, functional disability and quality of life. Pain intensity will be measured using scales like Numeric Pain Rating Scale or Visual Analogue Scale. Functional disability will be assessed using tools such as Oswestry Disability Index or Roland-Morris Disability Questionnaire. Quality of life will be measured by Short-Form-12 or EuroQol Five Dimensions questionnaire. Secondary outcomes will include adherence, self-efficacy and patient satisfaction.

Setting

Only studies conducted in SSA, specifically in rural or semirural settings, will be included.

Language

The review will be limited to studies published in English.

Information sources

A comprehensive search strategy will be implemented across multiple electronic databases, including PubMed, Scopus, CINAHL, African Journals Online (AJOL) and Web of Science. The search terms will include keywords related to CLBP, mHealth, telehealth, cultural adaptation and SSA. Additionally, reference lists of included studies and relevant reviews will be manually screened to identify further eligible studies.

Study record and selection process

To ensure broad coverage of the relevant literature, the search will include several key electronic databases. These databases include PubMed, Scopus, CINAHL, AJOL, Embase and Web of Science. The time frame for the search will cover studies published from inception to date. These databases were selected based on their comprehensive indexing of health-related literature and their relevance to the scope of this review.

Search strategy

The search strategy will combine free-text keywords and Medical Subject Headings related to CLBP, mHealth interventions, cultural adaptation and SSA. The strategy will be adapted for each database to align with its specific indexing system and search functionality. The full search strategy is provided in online supplemental appendix 1. The primary search terms will include “Chronic low back pain”, “low back pain”, “CLBP” and “musculoskeletal pain” for the condition of interest. For the intervention, the terms “mHealth”, “mobile health”, “mobile phone interventions”, “telehealth”, “digital health” and “eHealth” will be used. To capture the cultural adaptation aspect, the search will also include “cultural adaptation”, “culturally tailored”, “cultural relevance”, “local language”, “community-based” and “traditional beliefs.” Finally, the search will focus on the geographic region with terms such as “Sub-Saharan Africa”, “SSA”, “Africa” and “rural Africa.” Studies will be included in the review if they meet all eligibility criteria. Titles and abstracts will be screened independently by two reviewers (BB and AA), and full-text screening will be conducted for potentially eligible studies. Discrepancies will be resolved by consensus or by consulting a third reviewer (UU). The PRISMA 2020 flow diagram will be used to illustrate the study selection process. Once the search is completed, all records will be managed using the COVIDENCE software, where duplicates will be removed. In addition to the electronic database search, manual searching of the reference lists of eligible studies and relevant reviews will be conducted to identify any additional studies that meet the inclusion criteria. This manual search will help ensure that the review captures all relevant studies, including those that may not have been indexed in the selected databases. The PRISMA 2020 flow diagram will be used to track the study selection process and ensure transparency.

Data collection process and extraction

Data will be extracted from the included studies using a standardised form based on the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. Two reviewers (AA and BB) will independently extract data using Covidence, with the process carried out in duplicate. The extraction form will be tested on three studies and adjusted as needed until an inter-rater agreement (κ) greater than 0.70 is achieved. Key data elements will include study details (eg, authors, year, country, sample size and study design), intervention details (eg, duration, delivery platform and cultural adaptations), outcomes (eg, pain intensity, functional disability and quality of life) and implementation barriers (eg, digital literacy, network issues and financial constraints).

Risk of bias

Studies will be appraised for risk of bias (RoB) using design-specific tools. The Cochrane RoB 2 tool21 will be used for RCTs, while the Joanna Briggs Institute Critical Appraisal Checklists will be applied to observational studies (eg, cross-sectional, cohort and case–control) and qualitative studies.22 Two reviewers (AA and BB) will independently conduct assessments, and any disagreements will be resolved through consensus or, if necessary, by consulting a third reviewer (UU). The RoB evaluations will guide the synthesis process. Studies identified as having a high RoB will not be automatically excluded; instead, the results will be presented descriptively, with clear caution.

Data synthesis and integration

A convergent integrated approach will be used to synthesise and integrate quantitative and qualitative findings. Quantitative data on intervention effectiveness will be synthesised using a narrative approach and, where appropriate, meta-analysis. Qualitative data exploring barriers and facilitators to implementation will be synthesised using thematic analysis. Following separate synthesis, findings from quantitative and qualitative components will be integrated during the interpretation phase to provide a comprehensive understanding of how intervention effectiveness relates to contextual and implementation factors. This approach enables the simultaneous consideration of outcomes and underlying mechanisms influencing intervention uptake and impact.

Certainty of evidence

The certainty of the evidence for key outcomes will be assessed using the Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation approach. This approach evaluates the quality of evidence across studies based on domains including RoB, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and publication bias. The overall certainty of evidence will be rated as high, moderate, low or very low. Where appropriate, summary of findings tables will be generated to present the certainty of evidence for primary outcomes.

Assessment of heterogeneity and criteria for quantitative synthesis

Clinical and methodological heterogeneity across included studies will be assessed based on population characteristics, intervention components, outcome measures and study design. Quantitative synthesis (meta-analysis) will be conducted where studies are deemed sufficiently homogeneous in terms of these characteristics. In addition, statistical heterogeneity will be evaluated using the I² statistic. An I² value of less than 50% will be considered indicative of low to moderate heterogeneity and may support meta-analysis, while higher values will be interpreted cautiously. Where heterogeneity is substantial or where studies differ significantly in design or outcomes, a meta-analysis will not be conducted, and findings will instead be synthesised narratively.

Narrative synthesis

Where meta-analysis is not feasible, a structured narrative synthesis will be undertaken following established guidance. This will include grouping studies by intervention type, outcome domains and study design, and exploring patterns in effectiveness and implementation barriers across contexts. In the event of a limited or ‘thin’ evidence base, the narrative synthesis will go beyond descriptive reporting by identifying consistencies and divergences in findings, examining contextual factors influencing intervention outcomes and highlighting gaps in the evidence. This will enable the development of evidence-informed recommendations for future research, intervention design and policy relevant to rural SSA.

Patient and public involvement

None

Ethics and dissemination

Ethical approval is not required for this study because it is a systematic review of previously published literature and does not involve collecting primary data from human or animal participants. This is consistent with established guidance for systematic review protocols. The findings of this review will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentations at relevant national and international conferences. In addition, results will be shared with key stakeholders, including clinicians, researchers and policymakers, to support the implementation of culturally tailored mHealth interventions for CLBP in rural SSA.

Study timeline

The systematic review is planned to commence on 1 November 2025 and is expected to be completed by 30 March 2026. This timeline includes the stages of literature search, screening, data extraction, quality appraisal and synthesis of findings.

Discussion

This protocol outlines a systematic review that aims to synthesise evidence on culturally tailored mHealth interventions for CLBP in rural SSA. A key strength of this study is its focus on an underexplored yet high-need context, where the burden of CLBP intersects with limited access to rehabilitation services and growing mobile technology penetration. By incorporating both effectiveness outcomes and implementation barriers, this review is positioned to provide a comprehensive understanding of how such interventions perform in real-world rural settings. Another strength lies in the use of rigorous and transparent methodology, including a comprehensive multidatabase search strategy, predefined inclusion criteria and the application of design-specific RoB tools. The inclusion of culturally tailored interventions as a central focus further enhances the relevance of the review to diverse populations within SSA. However, several limitations are anticipated. First, the heterogeneity of mHealth interventions, outcome measures and study designs may limit the feasibility of meta-analysis and require a primarily narrative synthesis. Second, restricting the review to English-language publications may introduce language bias and potentially exclude relevant studies conducted in local or non-English contexts. Third, variability in digital access, literacy and infrastructure across rural settings may affect the generalisability of findings.

Despite these limitations, the review is expected to provide valuable insights into the effectiveness and contextual relevance of culturally tailored mHealth interventions for CLBP in rural SSA. The findings will help inform future research, intervention design and policy development aimed at improving access to care in underserved populations.

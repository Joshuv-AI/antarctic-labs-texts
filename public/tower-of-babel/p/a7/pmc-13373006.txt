Comprehensive genomic profiling and tumor mutational burden in parathyroid carcinoma: a nationwide real-world study from Japan
Authors: Yuko Takano, Toyone Kikumori, Dai Takeuchi, Takahiro Ichikawa, Yuichi Ando
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeParathyroid carcinoma (PC) is an extremely rare endocrine malignancy with limited treatment options for unresectable or recurrent cases. With the increasing use of comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP), treatment based on genomic findings is becoming more common. However, the frequency and clinical significance of elevated tumor mutational burden (TMB) in PC remain unclear because previous studies have been limited by small sample sizes.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed genomic and clinical data of patients with PC registered in the Center for Cancer Genomics and Advanced Therapeutics database in Japan between June 2019 and March 2025. TMB values were obtained as reported by each CGP assay. TMB-H was defined as TMB ≥ 10 mut/Mb for descriptive analyses. We also assessed genomic alterations, microsatellite instability (MSI) status, and clinicogenomic characteristics.ResultsTwenty-five patients with PC were included. The median assay-reported TMB was 4.0 mut/Mb (range, 0–35). Seven tumors (28.0%) had assay-reported TMB values of ≥ 10 mut/Mb, including three (12.0%) with TMB ≥ 20 mut/Mb. The most frequently altered genes were CDC73 (40%), TP53 (32%), and MEN1 (24%). No co-alterations were observed between CDC73 and MEN1 or between CDC73 and TP53. One tumor was MSI-high and was included in the TMB-H group. POLE alterations were detected in three cases, including two tumors in the TMB-H group.ConclusionThis nationwide, real-world study demonstrated that a subset of PCs showed elevated assay-reported TMB values and genomic features potentially related to abnormalities in DNA replication or repair pathways. These findings support the clinical relevance of comprehensive genomic profiling in identifying the molecular heterogeneity and potential therapeutic opportunities for this rare malignancy.

Introduction

Parathyroid carcinoma (PC) is an extremely rare malignancy, accounting for less than 1% of all primary hyperparathyroidism cases and approximately 0.005% of all cancers [1]. While the precise mechanisms underlying PC development remain largely uncharacterized, a subset of cases is associated with hereditary syndromes such as hyperparathyroidism-jaw tumor (HPT-JT) syndrome or multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) [1]. Clinically, PC primarily presents as a neck mass with severe hypercalcemia resulting from hyperparathyroidism [1]. Surgical resection remains the most effective treatment and offers favorable long-term prognosis for localized disease, but established standard systemic therapy for patients with unresectable, recurrent, or metastatic tumors is lacking. These patients often have limited therapeutic options and poor prognoses [1], highlighting the need to identify molecular biomarkers that may inform diagnosis and therapeutic decision-making.

Recent advancements in comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) have clarified the molecular landscapes of various rare malignancies. Previous sequencing studies in PC have identified genomic alterations in CDC73, TP53, and MEN1, suggesting the existence of distinct biological subsets [2–4]. However, high-resolution genomic data from large-scale cohorts are limited because of the rarity of the disease. In Japan, CGP was implemented under national insurance coverage in 2019 and has since been performed for various cancer types using data collected from a nationwide database [5].

Tumor mutational burden (TMB) has emerged as a critical predictive biomarker for response to immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) in multiple solid tumors [6, 7]. Nevertheless, the prevalence of high TMB (TMB-H) and its clinical significance in PC remain poorly understood, with only limited data available from small-scale studies [2, 3]. Therefore, this study aimed to analyze real-world genomic data from a nationwide Japanese database to characterize the genomic landscape and TMB profiles of PC and explore the potential therapeutic implications of TMB-H status.

Materials and methods

Study design and data source

This study analyzed genomic and clinical data obtained from the Center for Cancer Genomics and Advanced Therapeutics (C-CAT) database, a nationwide repository established to support cancer genomic medicine in Japan. The database contains data from government-approved comprehensive genomic profiling (CGP) tests performed under the public health insurance system. The data for this study were extracted on April 9, 2025.

Patient selection and data extraction

Patients diagnosed with parathyroid carcinoma who underwent CGP between June 2019 and March 2025 were included. The following variables were obtained: age, sex, date of diagnosis, CGP panel used, specimen type and collection date, treatment exposure before CGP testing, presence of gene alterations, assay-reported TMB status, microsatellite instability (MSI) status, smoking history, heavy alcohol use, family history of cancer, and history of other malignancies.

Genomic profiling

Samples were analyzed using one of the three CGP assays approved for clinical use in Japan: FoundationOne CDx (Foundation Medicine, Cambridge, MA, USA), OncoGuide NCC Oncopanel System (Sysmex Corporation, Kobe, Japan), or GenMineTOP (Konica Minolta, Inc., Tokyo, Japan). Genomic alteration profiles and TMB values should be interpreted with caution across platforms because these assays have different panel sizes, gene content, target regions, and analytical pipelines.

Genomic alterations

Genomic alterations were analyzed based on alterations reported in the C-CAT database. Independent re-annotation of individual variants using external annotation databases was not performed in this study.

TMB

TMB generally represents the total number of somatic single-nucleotide variants and small insertions/deletions normalized per megabase of the sequenced genome. In this study, TMB values were obtained from the C-CAT database as reported by each CGP assay. Because TMB estimation differs across platforms, TMB values were interpreted descriptively as assay-reported values. A threshold of ≥ 10 mut/Mb was used to define TMB-H for descriptive analyses.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis and visualization were performed using R software version 4.5.0 (The R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Descriptive statistics were used to summarize the data, and categorical variables are summarized as frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables (e.g., age and TMB) are reported as medians with ranges and calculated using R. Due to the rare nature of this malignancy and the descriptive objective of the study, formal comparative statistical testing was not conducted.

Ethical approval

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Nagoya University Hospital and the C-CAT Data Use Committee (see Ethics Statement for details). All the procedures were performed in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all the participants for the use of their genomic and clinical data.

Results

Patient characteristics

Twenty-five patients with parathyroid carcinoma who underwent CGP testing between June 2019 and March 2025 were identified from the C-CAT database. The median age at CGP testing was 57 years (range, 30–80 years), and 14 (56%) were male. Twenty (80%) patients had distant metastases at the time of registration. The median interval from diagnosis to CGP testing was 17.1 months (range: 0.7–186). Most patients were analyzed using the Foundation One CDx (80%); the specimen collection site was the primary tumor for 11 cases (44%) and a metastatic lesion for 12 cases (48%). Treatment before CGP testing was not administered for nearly all cases, except for one for which information was unavailable (Table 1).

Tumor mutational burden and microsatellite instability

The median TMB for the entire cohort was 4.0 mut/Mb (range, 0–35; Fig. 1). When stratified by CGP assay, the median TMB was 4.0 mut/Mb for FoundationOne CDx (n = 20; range, 0–35), 25.6 mut/Mb for the single OncoGuide NCC Oncopanel case, and 5.85 mut/Mb for GenMineTOP (n = 4; range, 5.3–28.8). Seven tumors (28.0%) had assay-reported TMB values of ≥ 10 mut/Mb and were operationally classified as TMB-H for descriptive analyses. Using a higher cut-off of ≥ 20 mut/Mb, three tumors (12.0%) had assay-reported TMB values of ≥ 20 mut/Mb. MSI status was evaluable for 21 cases (excluding those tested with GenMineTOP), and one (4.8%) was identified as MSI-high. The clinicopathological characteristics stratified by the TMB status are summarized in Table 2. Patients in the TMB-H group tended to be younger at the time of CGP testing than those in the non-TMB-H group (median age, 53 vs. 71 years). The median TMB was 17.0 mut/Mb (range: 13.0–35.0) in the TMB-H group and 2.9 mut/Mb (range: 0.0–6.4) for the non–TMB-H group. POLE alterations were more frequently observed in the TMB-H group than in the non-TMB-H group (28.6% vs. 5.6%), and the MSI-high tumors were included in the TMB-H group.

Genomic landscape

Figure 2 summarizes the genomic alterations identified in the 25 tumors and shows the 28 most frequently altered genes detected using CGP. The most frequently altered gene was CDC73 (10 cases, 40%), followed by TP53 (8 cases, 32%) and MEN1 (6 cases, 24%). Alterations in ATM, PTEN, NOTCH1, and NOTCH3 were each observed in approximately 20% of cases. No tumors harbored co-alterations in CDC73 or MEN1. Similarly, CDC73 and TP53 alterations were not observed together in this cohort. MEN1 and TP53 co-alterations were identified in some tumors.

Genomic features of hypermutated tumors

Clinicogenomic characteristics according to TMB status are summarized in Table 2. Tumors in the TMB-H group had heterogeneous genomic profiles. POLE alterations were detected in three tumors overall, including two tumors classified as TMB-H. In addition, tumor with concurrent MSI-high and TMB-H statuses harbored alterations in mismatch repair-related genes, including MSH2 and MSH3. These findings indicate that a subset of PC has genomic features that are potentially related to elevated TMB.

Discussion

In the present study, we characterized the genomic landscape of PC using nationwide real-world clinical data from Japan and found that seven tumors (28.0%) had assay-reported TMB values of ≥ 10 mut/Mb. Given that PC is an extremely rare malignancy with no established standard systemic therapy for unresectable cases [1], the identification of a subset of tumors with elevated TMB may have clinical implications for the potential application of ICI therapy.

Our genomic findings align with the findings of previous sequencing analyses that identified CDC73, TP53, and MEN1 as the most frequently mutated genes in PC [2–4]. Notably, CDC73 alterations were detected in 40% of the patients, reinforcing its role as a central driver of parathyroid tumorigenesis [4]. The observeation that seven tumors had assay-reported TMB values of ≥ 10 mut/Mb was clinically notable. However, this proportion should not be directly compared with previous reports because of differences in TMB cut-off values and sequencing platforms. For instance, a previous study [2] reported a median TMB of 1.7 mut/Mb, with 19% of cases exceeding 20 mut/Mb. In our cohort, three tumors (12.0%) had assay-reported TMB values of ≥ 20 mut/Mb. Thus, our findings are broadly consistent with the previous observation that a subset, but not the majority, of PCs may show markedly elevated TMB. The presence of such cases in our cohort may reflect the characteristics of patients undergoing genomic testing under the Japanese insurance system, which primarily targets advanced-stage disease [5].

A key finding of our study was the absence of co-alterations in CDC73 and MEN1, suggesting the presence of biologically distinct molecular subgroups. Interestingly, our data also revealed potential mutual exclusivity between CDC73 and TP53. Parafibromin, a protein product of CDC73, binds to and destabilizes p53 mRNA in the cytoplasm and modulates p53 concentrations and p53-mediated apoptosis [8]. This provides a possible biological context for the observed pattern, whereby disruption of either CDC73-parafibromin or TP53 may be sufficient to impair p53-dependent tumor-suppressive signaling. However, caution should be exercised, as Kang et al. [2] previously identified at least one case in which these mutations co-occurred. While the apparent exclusivity in our cohort could simply be a reflection of the small sample size, these patterns suggest distinct molecular stratification within PC.

Regarding the clinical implications of these genotypes, previous studies have suggested that CDC73 mutations are associated with both increased TMB and a significantly higher risk of tumor relapse [3]. In contrast, our analysis did not show an obvious enrichment of CDC73 alterations in tumors with assay-reported TMB ≥ 10 mut/Mb, suggesting that elevated TMB in PC may not be explained by CDC73 status alone. This discrepancy may reflect differences in cohort composition, sample size, and testing platforms and highlights the complexity of the molecular mechanisms underlying elevated TMB in PC.

Our study also provides insights into the molecular background of tumors with elevated TMB in PC. While the drivers of elevated TMB in this malignancy have not been fully elucidated, we identified POLE alterations in three cases; POLE abnormalities are known to contribute to hypermutations in other solid tumors [9]. In addition, one MSI-high tumor was identified and included in the TMB-H group. The affected patient had no family history suggestive of Lynch syndrome. These findings suggest that abnormalities in DNA replication or repair pathways may contribute to elevated TMB in a subset of PC. The clinical relevance of TMB-H as a predictive biomarker for ICI therapy has been established in several solid tumors [6, 7]. In the KEYNOTE-158 trial, patients with TMB ≥ 10 mut/Mb achieved an objective response rate of 29%, leading to tissue-agnostic regulatory approval for pembrolizumab [7]. Large pan-cancer analyses have also shown that higher TMB is associated with improved outcomes after ICI therapy across multiple tumor types [10]. Although PC was not included in these pivotal trials, a recent case report described favorable disease control with pembrolizumab and radiotherapy in TMB-high unresectable recurrent PC [11]. Taken together, these findings support the potential clinical relevance of CGP in identifying a subset of patients with PC who may have therapeutic opportunities despite the lack of established systemic treatment. Genomic profiling may also provide therapeutic insights beyond TMB for selected cases [2, 3].

This study had limitations. First, the inherent rarity of PC limited the total sample size. Second, selection bias may have existed toward biologically aggressive cases because CGP testing in Japan is generally performed for patients with advanced disease [5]. Third, TMB values were obtained as reported by each CGP assay, and TMB estimation may differ across platforms because of differences in panel size, target regions, gene content, and analytical pipelines. Therefore, the proportion of patients with assay-reported TMB ≥ 10 mut/Mb should be interpreted descriptively and not as a platform-harmonized estimate of TMB-H prevalence. Fourth, the C-CAT database lacked comprehensive clinical outcome data, precluding direct assessment of the prognostic or predictive significance of individual genomic alterations or TMB status. In addition, we did not perform independent pathogenicity curation of individual variants using external annotation databases; therefore, the reported alterations may have included variants of uncertain significance. Despite these limitations, this study presents one of the largest real-world genomic analyses of PC to date and provides additional insights into the molecular heterogeneity of this rare malignancy.

In conclusion, our study shows that a subset of patients with PC had elevated assay-reported TMB values, with seven tumors (28.0%) showing TMB ≥ 10 mut/Mb and three tumors (12.0%) showing TMB ≥ 20 mut/Mb. While PC is an extremely rare disease with limited treatment options, the identification of tumors with elevated TMB suggests that ICIs may be a therapeutic option for selected patients. These findings highlight the importance of genomic profiling for identifying new treatment opportunities for patients with advanced PC.

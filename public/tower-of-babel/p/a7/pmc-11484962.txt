Influence of climate on desert locust (Schistocerca gregaria Forskål, 1775) Plague and migration prediction in tropics
Authors: Biswarup Mitra, Amiya Gayen, Sk Mafizul Haque, Amlan Das
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe outbreak of the desert locust Schistocerca gregaria Forskål, 1775, which originated from the Horn of Africa in 2019–2020 created an episodic plague under bio-geographical settings in the arid and semi-arid areas of South and Southwest Asia. In India, it happened after twenty-seven years due to the persistence of a few favourable conditions caused by its plague, resulting in hundreds of crores in crop damage. Keeping this in mind, the study aims to assess the suitability and likelihood of the desert locust epidemic occurring in India, utilizing two widely recognized statistical models: Weight-of-Evidence (WoE) and Frequency Ratio (FR). This work evaluated nine critical climatic factors for the study considering western and central parts of India. The ‘Projected Locust Suitability’ (PLS) was calculated by analyzing the correlation of the considered variables and the occurrence of locust swarms and bands. The significance (importance) of each variable on PLS was determined using Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Random Forest (RF) algorithms. The PLS maps clearly show that 42.7–52.8% of the areas fall under high and very high locust suitability zones. The result suggests that the Ajmer-Gwalior-Allahabad tract is highly prone to future locust occurrences, while the Aligarh-Bareilly-Lakhimpur tract is moderately susceptible. The effectiveness of both modelled PLS maps was determined with the help of the ROC curve. The AUC results indicate that both the WoE (0.92) and the RF (0.90) models worked remarkably well in precisely predicting PLS. The RF-based IncNodePurity analysis indicates that low to moderate temperatures in the presence of cloud cover significantly impact locust occurrence and migration. The present findings are projected to direct the development of sustainable locust management strategies utilizing proper land use policies in the tropical climate.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-73250-w.Subject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Ecology, Zoology, Climate sciences, Ecology, Environmental sciences

Introduction

The desert locust epidemic of 2019–2020 has resurrected the locust plague frenzy and, subsequently, brought locust-related research to the academic forefront after it threatened food security and economic stability in geographical regions in west Africa and southwest Asia, including the north-western part of India, i.e., Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, and Uttar Pradesh1. Locusts are widely acknowledged as one of the most notorious and economically detrimental agricultural pests worldwide. They are short-horned grasshoppers, members of the family Acrididae from the order Orthoptera. However, not all short-horned grasshoppers are locusts; only 18–21 acridid species with the unique ability to ‘phase-change’ or transform from solitary to gregarious adults are collectively categorized as locusts2. Once such phase transition occurs, the locust generates large, dense bands of hoppers and clusters of swarms of winged adults capable of migrating across continents by devouring entire fields of crops en route, culminating in the plague3,4. Schistocerca gregaria Forskål, 1775 is considered one of the most predominant and highly devastating (https://www.fao.org/locusts/en/) among all plague-causing locusts5,6. They mainly breed in arid and semi-arid regions, approximately distributed around 30 million km2 (with 16 million km2 of recession area) extending from the Sahel belt in Africa to Pakistan and then India, passing through the Arabian peninsula7. In the recent desert locust plague, S. gregaria migrated from the Horn of Africa, traversing the deserts of Iran, Yemen, Saudi, and Pakistan before reaching the Thar of Rajasthan, India. Prior to dispersing further, the western Indian states, i.e., Rajasthan and Gujarat functioned as their primary breeding grounds.

Weather shifts within a short while, sporadic high rainfall following droughts, and erratic temperature variations in locust breeding grounds cause solitary individual to congregate, resulting in a distinct phase transition8. Most often, the locust plague and their migration are regulated by a complex bio-environmental interface in which optimal bioclimatic conditions, like temperature, rainfall, vegetation (‘green index’), and soil moisture, drive the locust populations to a critical density. These events foster increased gregariousness and coherence, ultimately transforming them into a plague9. Like many other insects, temperature directly influences many life cycle traits3, especially their growth and development7,10, and life cycle3. Moreover, warmer temperatures hasten egg incubation, hatching success and hopper development, while cold conditions induce egg hibernation and quiescence3,7.

Though rain is not directly linked to locust plague, it can trigger population outbreaks in many ways7,10,11. Both rain and soil moisture are essential for promoting plant growth in arid and semi-arid environments, so the availability of green vegetation as a food resource significantly influences locust travel routes and outbreak severity12. Some empirical evidence demonstrates that solitary locusts congregate near food resources, even when scattered or distributed unevenly13.

The locust breeding season generally aligns with summer and fall harvest seasons, giving locusts a competitive edge14. Although Schistocerca is a generalist feeder with a broad dietary regime, it undergoes faster sexual maturity after feeding on specific vegetation, such as Sorghum15. Their potential preference for specific crops and vegetation on availability and competition depicts the effect of crop patterns on swarm migration16. However, desert locusts cannot subsist without vegetation even if the soil moisture level rises optimally in the arid zone without the flourishing of flora17. It highlights the correlation between soil moisture-related vegetation and locust density. Rainfall of an area also influences relative humidity (RH), which, along with temperature threshold, plays a critical role in egg development, incubation period (towards hatching), nymphal mortality, life cycle completion18–22. Studies show that a specific combination of temperature and RH is conducive to locust hoppers reaching adulthood; however, the probability of an ‘epidemic’ formation significantly reduces beyond a limited weather window with optimal conditions. For example, at 26.7 °C and 25% RH, the locust hoppers cannot reach adulthood but can do so at a temperature of 35 °C and RH of 35% 23. Again, at 32.2 °C and 35% RH, hoppers could not survive; however, they can survive if the RH increases to 45%. On the other hand, hoppers cannot develop at high temperatures of 37.8 °C and low RH of 35%; however, at more RH (45%) on the same temperature (37.8 °C), a few reached maturities but not as many as at 32.2 °C and 35% RH23,24.

Extensive droughts followed by excessive precipitation result in floods and abundant vegetation growth, which attract locusts to invade an area. However, following vegetation desiccation, locust swarms collectively move towards adjacent crops25. The recent occurrence of locust outbreaks in Africa exemplified this trend, as extreme droughts were succeeded by above-average precipitation in Sudan, Somalia, and Kenya, creating favourable situations for forming swarms26. Additionally, storms and hurricanes, such as cyclone Mekunul in May 2018, set the stage for locust infestations by causing downpours and creating wet environments in arid regions8. In concurrence with rainfall and storms, cloud cover also significantly impacts locust movement during the day, with gregarious bands marching on warm and sunny days but remaining stationary on cloudy days7. Earlier findings also disclose a cumulative effect of cloud cover and temperature, wherein swarms typically initiate flight in sunny hours with temperatures ranging from 15 to 17 °C. However, in the presence of clouds, they usually delay take-off until the temperature reaches 23–26 °C7,10. The egg-laying activities of desert locusts are influenced by soil moisture, which is affected by rainfall27,28. Regarding oviposition, females prefer sandy substrates with moisture levels between 5 and 25% at a depth of 2–15 cm3,27,28.

Locusts also rely on wind for dispersal, allowing them to cluster through convergence10,11. Local climate aids better scope for identifying the areas where wind-dependent locust migration occurs. Swarming populations may arise when locusts form groups from isolated populations and are swept together by strong air motion and cyclones7,8. Rain is also frequently seeded by strong winds. As a result, condensation and precipitation increase soil moisture and create a perfect breeding ground, prolific oviposition, and successful nymphal emergence. Winds generally control the cloud movement and contain water vapour to increase atmospheric moisture in the dry areas, favour their spatial dispersal and subsequent outbreak. Sandy substrates with moisture levels ranging from 5 to 25% effectively facilitate both egg-laying activity and plant growth, contributing to their abundance in an area. The locust outbreak in India during 2019–2020, considered the most severe in the last 27 years, is an example of several geo-climatic correlations where the climatic parameters collectively influence the occurrence of locusts to build a specific corridor of Northwest India. Considering its economic concern, some attempts have been made to investigate and evaluate the Schistocerca plague through quantitative and qualitative approaches29,30. In similar cases, several hypotheses were made previously, and the most accepted one was the effect of natural hazards on the formation of the outbreak in a particular region31–33. However, the knowledge of the cumulative effect of multiple climatic conditions on locust plague is incomprehensible and enigmatic because, until now, the locust control strategy has only been linked with one or a few meteorological parameters. Since locust dispersal behaviour tends to differ depending on diverse climatic factors, the responsiveness to collective climatic conditions in monitoring locust populations are highly imperative in crop protection. Thus, it is hypothesized that a conducive interface among different geo-climatic stimuli synergistically initiates locust plague formation, and drives their migration. However, the ‘influencing’ one or few factors in developing ‘pandemics’ may be outweighed or overshadowed by the interaction with other factors. Therefore, this study aims to assess the correlations among the ‘influencing’ climatic variables in establishing locust plague by examining the dynamic contrasts of the ‘trending’ population using GIS technologies. Forecasting future locust infestations through metaheuristic realistic models is therefore imperative to draw their future migration prediction accuracy.

Materials and methods

Study area

Seven Indian states, namely Rajasthan, Gujarat, Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, Madhya Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh, were selected for study (Fig. 1) based on reports of S. gregaria sightings. Among the states, 58 districts, and from all districts, 63 outbreak stations were considered based on the available information in Bulletins of Desert Locust Archive, Government of India (2019–2021) and FAO Locust Watch Information (https://www.fao.org/ag/locusts/en/info/info/index.html). However, for the meteorological data gathering, we considered those stations where locusts were spotted during 2019–2020 or near the stations to draw a predicted migration model. Therefore, each of the seven states with a significant locust population had a certain number of outbreak districts, and within those districts, specific locust outbreak stations were selected (Table 1).

Meteorological data collection

Nine sets of meteorological data were extracted daily from open sources of Weather Data Services (www.visualcrossing.com) from all designated locust epidemic stations across the study area for two consecutive years (2019–2020). The meteorological data were collected from 63 locust epidemic stations across the 58 districts of seven Indian states. Daily statistics of the following meteorological data were considered in concurrence with existing literature for each outbreak station: (1) maximum temperature (°C), (2) minimum temperature (°C), (3) mean temperature (°C), (4) relative humidity (%), (5) precipitation (mm), (6) wind speed (m/sec), (7) wind direction (degree), (8) visibility (m), and (9) cloud cover (%)3,5,7,10,11,23,34,35.

All data were entered into Excel spreadsheets, double-checked, and processed by averaging (mean) month-wise meteorological variables reported at each locust outbreak station in the state qualified for this study. The mean value of each weather parameter was considered to assess whether (or not) there was an association between each weather parameter and locust migration and eventual outbreak. The mean monthly value for each metric was computed as follows:

For months with 31 days: {∑ Daily data (for each parameter)/ 31}

For months with 31 days: {∑ Daily data (for each parameter)/ 31}

For months with 30 days: {∑ Daily data (for each parameter)/ 30}

For months with 30 days: {∑ Daily data (for each parameter)/ 30}

For the month of February with 28 days: {∑ Daily data (for each parameter)/ 28}

For the month of February with 28 days: {∑ Daily data (for each parameter)/ 28}

All meteorological data acquisitions were performed twice in two years, from January to December (2019–2020). Finally, the data sets were imported to the ArcGIS (Fig. 2) environment and interpolated using the Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) method due to its best fitting and nominal error.

Collection of Biological data: Locust occurrence and outbreak

The location of desert locusts as band and swarm sightings were obtained from the FAO Locust hub (https://locust-hub-hqfao.hub.arcgis.com/). After exporting the data to Microsoft Excel, the geographic coordinates (latitude and longitude) associated with the country code ‘IN’ (representing data sets for India) were filtered. Each locust ‘Band’ and ‘Swarm’ data set includes a column for the sighting date and latitude and longitude coordinates identifying the location of the sighting. The data was then categorized by month. Swarms of desert locusts were observed from May to December, while bands were spotted from July to November for two consecutive years (2019–2020).

Data consideration and assessment

Locust appearance suitability assessment through ‘Weight of Evidence’ (WoE) model

The projected locust migration probability was analyzed through the Weight-of-evidence (WoE) model. The WoE is a 'data-driven’ quantitative application based on Bayesian rules for integrating data to project organisms’ suitability in a given area through prior and posterior probability36. Though the model was developed to measure mineral potentiality, it has been successfully used to map landslide, flood, and soil erosion susceptibility and mineral and groundwater potentiality37–39 in the last two decades.

The WoE was implemented in this study using the log-linear approximation of the Bayesian probability to evaluate ‘individual factors’ relative importance (weight) by considering the locust appearance layer and the spatial data layers of predisposing factors. Primarily, the negative (W-) and positive (W +) propensities were determined for individual predisposing characteristics using the absence (-) or presence (+) of the locust population within a given study area. A correlation measure independent of the weights is given by the contrast C = W + − W − , where C is ‘positive’ for a positive spatial association, but C is ‘negative’ for a negative association40. The function C converts continuous evidential proof into a binary pattern. Where C does not provide a specified maximum for determining the optimum cut-off against conversion for classifying patterns into binary data arrays (presence or absence of organism), the normalized C or ‘Studentized contrast’ (sC) was calculated. In such computation, C is divided by its standard deviation for deriving the 'measure of confidence’ and, after that, the cut-off at which the predictive power of the resulting pattern was maximized41. Locust appearance suitability applying ‘Frequency Ratio’ (FR) model

The FR is a bivariate statistical approach used to determine the likelihood of a 'subject-potential’ presence or appearance in a particular area based on relationships between multiple dependent variables39,45. We adopted the FR model because it has previously been used to forecast groundwater46,47, landslides48,49, and gully erosion50,51 prediction mapping. However, in this study, the FR is represented as the ratio of locust presence or absence within a specific causal element. So, the FR model is used to comprehend the critical areas of locust suitability in the studied areas. It is employed in preparation of the possibility regarding another pandemic of locust occurrence under specific environmental condition compared to the previous one. Therefore, this model is intended to comprehend the relationships between independent and dependent variables using a multi-classified map52.

The ratio of area within each predisposing factor according to the ‘presence’ and ‘absence’ of locust outbreaks was computed here to prepare the FR model. Subsequently, the FR value for each parameter’s specific class (such as relative humidity with its five classes) was determined based on its alignment with locust ‘presence’ cases. When the FR exceeds one, the class has a significant impact on locust presence. Statistical analysis for understanding the factors

The appearance of the desert locust, Schistocerca gregaria, from a specific station and the meteorological parameters of their spotting sites, were considered for our analysis. To integrate the biological data with geo-environmental setting, the maximum-minimum transformation approach was used to standardise the data53. All characteristics were transformed to a range of 0 to 1, which means that for a variable, the lowest and the highest value will be 0 to 1, respectively. Mean values and standard deviations were determined for each data cohort to demonstrate the distinction of variance. Pearson correlation was used to calculate the association between locust outbreaks and each climatic condition. For assessing the ‘correlated factors’, nonparametric Kruskal–Wallis tests were performed followed by post-hoc Dunn tests to compare the variances among the dependent variables. Microsoft Office Excel 2010 and R Software (version 4.0.1) (R Development Core Team 2011) were used for the statistical analysis. Also, applied the random forest (RF) algorithm based IncNodePurity was measured to point out a better understanding of the influence of individual influencing factor has on the overall projected locust suitability. PCA was applied to assess the significance of each variable. The RF based IncNodePurity and PCA were measured using the R software. Additional information related to IncNodePurity can be found in the article of Han et al. (2020)54. PCA also reveal the pattern of similarity of the observations and the variables by displaying them as points in maps. Details of this methodology is elaborated in Jolliffe, 2002 and Saporta and Niang, 200955,56.

Validation of locust appearance suitability

As the validation of data is a critical effort in assessing the study’s scientific significance57, we employed the 'Area Under the Curve—Receiver Operating Characteristics Curve’ (AUC-ROC) to verify the algorithms’ propensity for prediction. The ROC is a visual depiction of how well a model performs in a diagnostic test58,59. Accurate predictions (location affected by the locust infestation) and erroneous forecasts (locations unaffected by the locust infestation) on the X-axis, and the model sensitivity on the Y-axis, displayed through the ROC60. The AUC was calculated to determine how well the model predicted. AUC runs from 0.5 to 1, with higher values suggesting more model prediction power than the lower value61.

Results

Locust appearance suitability

The WoE and FR algorithms were used empirically to analyze and characterize the locust migration in western and central India. The FR output reveals a strong relationship between locust observation and elements that govern their activities. The same predisposing characteristics associated with locust occurrences were used in both FR and WoE models. According to the present study, the maximum WoE and FR values were 26.47 and 13.69, respectively, and they were detected in the 30.74–33.97 (ºC) minimum temperature class. Similarly, locust sightings in the studied region were strongly correlated with the temperature of local environments. In conditions with higher values of mean temperature (33.45 to 35.94 ºC), the WoE and FR values were 23.39 and 9.84, respectively (Table 2). Therefore, it is possible to characterize regions with such temperature ranges as having a higher potential for the presence of locust habitats.

The projected locust suitability maps (PLSMs) derived from WoE revealed that high and very high projected locust suitability zones covered 25.0% and 17.7% of the studied area, respectively. Shallow, low, and moderate vulnerability zones accounted for 24.0%, 24.9%, and 8.44% of the total area, respectively (Table 3). According to the FR projected results, 21.5% and 31.3% of the study area were in extremely high and high conditions for locust outbreaks. The extremely low, low, and moderate locust suitability category comprised 8.0%, 25.6%, and 13.6% of the study area. The FR and WoE models reveal that the very high projected locust suitability zones lie in and around the Jaisalmer and Bikaner districts of Rajasthan. The surrounding areas in district of Barmer, Jodhpur, parts of Nagpur, and Churu constitute the high and moderate projected locust suitability zones. Proceeding towards the adjoining districts of Gujrat, Madhya Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh, the projected locust suitability zones are lower. Interestingly, some scattered parts lying close to the Gangetic plain, like Allahabad, Gwalior, etc. (Fig. 3), again show a slight increased imprint (from very low to low or moderate) in the projected locust suitability.

Environmental factors influencing locust appearance and upsurge

According to our study, several environmental factors influence the abundance of locusts in the central and western regions of India. Temperature, cloud cover, relative humidity, precipitation, and the density of vegetation cover in a given area were noted as most influential and directly or indirectly responsible for seasonal outbreaks of the locust population. Our data also reveals (Fig. 3) that the western part of Rajasthan, i.e., the Thar desert of Rajasthan, spanning from Jaisalmer to Bikaner, had the most favourable minimum and maximum temperature for Schistocerca nymphal hatching and larval development. The outcomes from the Weights of locust suitability determining factors for WoE and FR (Table 2) tell the most suitable range of minimum, mean, and maximum temperature for locust appearance and upsurge are 31–34 °C, 33–36 °C and 37–39 °C, respectively. On the other hand, while considering other influential factors for locust upsurge like relative humidity, cloud cover, and wind speed, the range offering the highest locust suitability reveals 44–54%, 11–32%, and 0.5–8 km/hr, respectively.

Significance of the Meteorological factors related to locust upsurge

Evaluating the elements that affect locust upsurge is important because it helps planners make sustainable land use decisions62. It is also regarded as a robust method for projecting the effects of climate variability and how they influence locust occurrences63. Owing to this, a PCA approach was adopted for the current study. The PCA method was employed to extract feature vectors while reducing the number of input variables by64. The linear correlation linkages of relevant elements are described comparatively using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (Fig. 4). The result shows a significant correlation between the contributing elements. The highest negative correlation was revealed between temperature and relative humidity, i.e., -0.97, and the highest positive correlation was observed between cloud cover and relative humidity. Using the PCA method, extracting vulnerability evaluation indices of prospective locust locations is realistic and practicable. PCA analysis shows that the two most important variables account for 80.4% of the effect factors (Fig. 5) (Tables 4, 5). In contrast, wind speed and precipitation affected more than 18% of the variable variance proportions. Validation and comparisons of projected locust suitability models

Accuracy evaluation is critical in deciding a model’s competence to forecast60. Hence, the ROC test was used to compare and validate the models employed. Results from the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis indicate that the WoE and FR models, when applied to forecasting rate curves, achieved an accuracy of 92.0 and 90.0%, respectively, at the 0.00% level of significance (Fig. 7). The high ROC values, which vary from 0.9 and 0.92, demonstrate the remarkable accuracy of both models in making locust occurrence and migration predictions.

Discussion

The present study provides a comprehensive evaluation of the occurrence of desert locust, their movement, and subsequent migration patterns in arid to semi-arid climatic conditions in western and central India, employing the Weight of Evidence (WoE) and Frequency Ratio (FR) algorithms. Though the WoE and FR models are extensively used for predicting various geophysical conditions, such as groundwater potential, landslides, floods, and vulnerability to gully erosions through mapping at different spatial scales65,66, these algorithms are also recognized for their applicability in agriculture and insect pest management67,68. As the susceptibility of an event and the accuracy of predicting event occurrence using GIS-based bivariate statistical models are extremely comparable69, both the WoE and FR algorithms were used for data analysis in our study. According to our results, the suitability of locust occurrence in certain territories and, thereafter, the mass dispersal of individuals is triggered by specific environmental factors of those locations. In addition, the collective mass movement of phytophagous species like Schistocerca is often influenced by the availability and abundance of green vegetation on the terrain. Using principal component analysis (PCA) and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient, our findings identify significant and interrelated aspects contributing to an upsurge in the locust population. Our results validate and compare the anticipated locust suitability models by employing Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) analysis. Both the PCA and IncNodePurity analyses indicate that a higher IncNodePurity value corresponds to a greater significance of the input variables. As a result, low to high temperatures, cloud cover, and wind direction constitute among the most significant parameters influencing the occurrence and migration of locusts. Among these factors, the temperature was the pivotal component that substantially influenced the development of Schistocerca colonies and their migration (Fig. 8).

Locust plague regulators and their influence in locust suitability.

In validating and contrasting projected locust appearance suitability in western India and beyond, we recognize that evaluating the relationship between climatic variability and locust occurrence is challenging because of insufficient data availability. As a result, predicting their migratory path using statistical models becomes challenging. This is the first time we have collected significant locust occurrence data from western to central India. The algorithmic models, based on WoE and FR, include the essential characteristics of meteorological variables and reveal that temperature (ranging from minimum to maximum), cloud cover, and wind direction are the primary factors determining the likelihood of locust compatibility in this region. The FR model has been employed in previous studies to identify significant predictive variables associated with geological catastrophes (dependent variables)70–72. The FR model also establishes the relationship between an event and the independent variables70.

Nevertheless, one of the FR’s disadvantages is how it assigns weight to the overall value of each component class73. For instance, the approach is unsuitable for areas with intricate ecosystems and locust migration patterns. Additionally, the method necessitates meticulous and precise data regarding past locust activity. Consequently, the method either overestimates or underestimates the magnitude of the characteristics associated with the occurrence of locust plagues.

Thus, we employed the ROC test to validate the precision of the model’s forecasts. Our WoE and FR models have demonstrated a substantially higher level of competence (> 90% accuracy) in predicting locust occurrence and migration than earlier predictions. The current algorithms can be modified to incorporate different levels of information to project the suitability zone for locusts in tropical climates, such as India. It would enable the construction of future plans for locust management.

Reflecting on the meteorological factors, the highest temperature reflects the surface radiation level in the atmosphere between midday and afternoon. In contrast, minimum temperature indicates subpar performance during the late-night hours. According to the available data, the spatial variance of maximum temperature across 59 sites from 52 districts of the focal area is comparable to the average climatic conditions of western and central India. This is because an arid and semi-arid environment characterises the study area, and hence, the highest temperature typically occurs in the southeast direction throughout the territory. There is a similar scenario for the lowest temperature level, but the spatial variation is more limited due to differential radiation from the surface condition in winter. For instance, the Thar desert of Rajasthan, spanning from Jaisalmer to Bikaner, experiences the lowest average temperatures of 30 °C and the highest average temperatures of 36 °C. Schistocerca favours temperatures over 34 °C for breeding3,7,10, which means that practically all of Rajasthan, most of Gujarat, particularly the northern region, and most of western Punjab fall into this category. Under this temperature condition, egg incubation and development, nymphal hatching perform well. High temperature along with RH 45% is favourable for hopper maturation. According to current literature7,29,30,74 and recent real-time data, these all locations serve as permanent locust breeding grounds. Therefore, it may be inferred that the northwest and central Indian provinces offer significant promise for the successful hatching of eggs (nymphal emergence) and the subsequent growth and development of locusts. Our analysis of the meteorological data revealed that precipitation was primarily concentrated in several pockets in the western half of the study area and was inversely related to temperature. It increases soil moisture, and favours oviposition, and nymphal development. Moreover, soil moisture endows growth of more green vegetation and bushes to serve their food.

Most precipitation dropped in a few eastern outlying locations, with the rain steadily decreasing from east to west. For example, Bhopal and its neighboring areas in Madhya Pradesh (central India) received the highest rainfall. The rainfall in the eastern and south-eastern states of the study area implies that the climatic situations are suitable for the growth of vegetation, which in turn attracts more locusts from the arid region of Rajasthan. Additionally, the summer breeding seasons of locusts and the invasion of locust swarms in India through the states of Rajasthan and Gujarat coincided with the country’s Kharif farming season (crops that are usually sown at the beginning of the first rains during the SW monsoon season, and are harvested at the end of monsoon, i.e., from October–November) throughout the locust plague in 2020. The availability of lush crop fields in North-Western India allowed them to disperse in swarms, presumably assisting in the rapid proliferation in neighboring regions. This is regarded as the most beneficial aspect of their development. Over the entire survey area, high relative humidity levels (over 60%) have been observed, providing the perfect locust breeding environment. The wind speed exhibits wide variability, ranging from 0.5 to 32 m/s; the maximum wind speeds were documented originating from the western and south-western directions in Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh.

Meanwhile, the plains of the northern state of Uttar Pradesh (the Ganga Plain) encounter prevailing breezes from the east and northeast. The empirical data on locust occurrence indicates that while they travelled from the western region to central India, their trajectory may align with the Ganga plains, which may lead to locust aggregation in two distinct tracts in northern India: (a) the Ajmer-Gwalior-Allahabad tract and (ii) the Aligarh-Bareilly-Lakhimpur tract. The cloud cover has significantly reduced visibility in a large portion of western Rajasthan, resulting in abysmal visibility of only 1.5 m. Moreover, there are extensive areas in Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh where visibility is significantly high. There was a large influx of individuals from less visible areas (high cloud cover) to Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh, where the migration rate was relatively high. The Jaisalmer desert region of Rajasthan is the only place on record with low cloud cover (high visibility), resulting in a higher locust population than other locations, where cloud cover is usually high or moderate (low visibility). Thus, increasing cloud cover is vital in reducing the likelihood of a locust plague, supporting previous findings that suggest locust swarms are likely aided by intense thermal convection. Such thermal convections are often influenced by intense solar radiation, which creates a higher ground temperature in sunnier climates (low cloud cover and high visibility)75. On the other hand, cold and cloudy conditions tend to impede flying activity and hinder aviation compared to hot and dry climates. Dry, warmer weather ensures ample solar radiation, promoting intense flying, extended flight periods, and greater flying altitudes 75.

Our research also reveals a synergy among the selected meteorological factors that define the locust outbreak (Fig. 4). Concurrent to various literature review7,10,23,24 Temperature and relative humidity negatively correlate and have a cumulative effect on defining locust upsurge. Our results showed a similar negative high correlation between cloud cover and temperature and their cumulative effect on locust upsurge. There was a clear indication that the two positively correlating factors of relative humidity and cloud cover may induce cumulatively locust plague. It indicates that while considering meteorological factors for defining locust plague, the interdependencies among the factors and their cumulative effect should not be overruled.

The findings of the present study on Schistocerca migration provide insight into a complex relationship between the tropical environment and the species’ migration routes from northwest Africa to western India and beyond. The divergence from the typical route of locust migration is an important finding here, where the species may likely invade the east Indian zones, subject to certain favourable climate conditions. The research of Saha et al., in 2021 and Tang et al., in 202376,77 also examine the distribution of Schistocerca gregaria under climate change, but they differ in methodology and focus. The first paper uses MaxEnt modeling to predict current and future potential distributions, emphasizing the importance of environmental variables in determining locust habitats. The second paper focuses on the potential spread of the locust, considering broader climate change impacts and potential range expansions. While both contribute to understanding locust dynamics, the first is more model-centric, while the second emphasizes ecological implications and spread patterns under various climate scenarios. Both are essential for forecasting locust management strategies. Our new research confirms findings from both the MaxEnt-based study and the paper on potential spread under climate change. Like the first paper, our work uses predictive modeling to assess Schistocerca gregaria's distributions, corroborating the significance of climate variables. Similar to the second paper, we explore the locust’s potential spread. However, our research introduces refined models or updated climate scenarios, providing more granular insights or addressing gaps in previous studies. This further validates the robustness of the original findings while enhancing precision in locust distribution predictions.

Over time, locusts have been known to undertake a seasonal migration from Northwest Africa across the northern Sahel during the beginning of summer and then move back towards the north throughout fall. However, our investigation demonstrates that severe weather phenomena, such as erratic cyclones, intense precipitation, and sudden changes in wind patterns, for example, storms like Luban and Pawan, can potentially modify their migration and new-territorial invasion. It highlights the significance of extreme weather conditions in determining their occurrence, emergence, and population dynamics. According to our investigation, one of the primary drivers that made the western Indian Thar desert an advantageous habitat for Schistocerca was super cyclone Amphan interacted with wind jets during May 2020. Such incidence underlines the extent to which locust migration is influenced by large-scale catastrophic variance due to global climate change. Additionally suitable condition of locust habitat in terms of RH, soil moisture and temperature persisted for more than sixty days over a vast geographical area. The cloud cover sustained the temperature favourable to the female oviposition, nymphal hatching and egg incubation period. Relative humidity performed for the egg preservation, hatching and adulthood which was responsible for swarm formation. Sporadic nature of rainfall created outbreak of the locust band from the Thar area and pushed to move in swarm, whereas, the bushes and plant colonies attracted the locust to provide food. Prevailing of the pre-monsoon wind from west to north-east direction drove them to cover long distance over the undulating surface in central India.

Furthermore, we have witnessed a series of geo-climatic shifts caused by severe weather occurrences, such as variations in seawater temperature, that promote the movement and dispersal of Schistocerca and subsequent outbreaks. It is apparent that Schistocerca migration is influenced by seasonal wind patterns and the species’ preference for warmer winds, which are often linked to air depression. Such coupling of temperature and wind patterns is likely to determine the trajectory of their dispersal and migration. In addition to acknowledging the influence of wind movement, the importance of regional variations in wind speed in locust-prone regions of western India has also been recognized. For example, the prevalent ‘regular’ wind pattern in Gujarat is responsible for consistent wind speed and, thereby, the direction of locust invasion during spring across the state. Therefore, local wind patterns and terrain are critical to include in evaluating locust migration dynamics in India.

In conclusion, our investigation enhances comprehension of the intricacies of forecasting strategy to control the locust outbreaks. The further study can be adopted separately for assessing dynamics of adult swarms and hoppers in identifing their movement characteristics. Regular survey should be employed to achieve the better result for the real time study and early warning system focusing the duration, intensity and path estimation of the plague. As the study output depicts the spatial track of future migration, balanced land utilization practices should be implemented along these two estimated tracks to reduce further movement of swarms. Consequently, the current findings have substantial implications for developing efficient strategies to alleviate the consequences of migratory pests like Schistocerca gregaria on agriculture and ecosystems.

Emphysema detection by qualitative and quantitative analysis and relationship to spirometric obstruction in an urban comprehensive lung cancer screening programme: the Temple Healthy Chest Initiative (THCI) – a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Stephen Dachert, Michaela Seigo, Olivia Ahearn, Huaqing Zhao, Gerard J Criner
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractObjectivePatients eligible for lung cancer screening are at risk for structural lung abnormalities, especially emphysema. Our aim was to assess the association between emphysema as determined by radiologist and quantitative CT (QCT) analysis, and the presence of spirometric abnormalities in an urban largely minority population undergoing lung cancer screening.DesignRetrospective cohort study.SettingCentralised lung cancer screening programme in an urban academic medical centre in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA.Participants2111 individuals who underwent low-dose CT (LDCT) from October 2021 to October 2022 had CT scans interpreted by radiologists for the presence of emphysema, 629 of whom underwent spirometry. 181 patients underwent AI-assisted QCT analysis for emphysema at −950 and −910 HU, all of whom underwent spirometry.Primary and secondary outcome measuresDiagnostic tests for presence of emphysema as defined by radiologist and by AI-assisted QCT analysis for emphysema and the association with spirometric obstruction.ResultsA total of 2111 individuals underwent LDCT from October 2021 to October 2022. 323 (51.4%) individuals with radiology-determined emphysema had airflow obstruction. 108 (17.2%) individuals without emphysema had no airflow obstruction. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) of radiology detected emphysema for airflow obstruction are 81.3%, 53.4%, 72.2%, 59.34%, respectively. In the Temple Healthy Chest Initiative (THCI) QCT subcohort of 181 individuals who underwent spirometry and low-attenuation area (%LAA)-910 HU, 91 (50%) individuals had %LAA-910 HU≥6% and airflow obstruction. 33 (18%) individuals with %LAA-910<6% had no airflow obstruction. Sensitivity, specificity, PPV and NPV for the presence of airflow obstruction based on %LAA–910 HU≥6% were 63%, 49%, 73% and 59%, respectively.ConclusionsData from the THCI demonstrates high rates of emphysema as detected by radiologic qualitative interpretation and quantitative analysis. Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide.1 In 2021, the US Preventative Services Task Force (USPSTF) updated the recommendation for lung cancer screening with low-dose CT (LDCT) to include individuals aged 50–80 with a 20-pack-year smoking history who are active smokers or have quit within the preceding 15 years. These changes were made to be more inclusive of minority individuals and women who otherwise might be excluded for screening. Individuals eligible for lung cancer screening are at risk of significant lung structural abnormalities, including emphysema, airways disease and interstitial lung abnormalities.2 Quantitative CT (QCT) low-attenuation area (LAA), defined by a density less than –950 HU or less than –910 HU, is a widely accepted measurement of emphysema or air trapping on a high-resolution CT scan.3 4 Additionally, previous studies have used %LAA cut-offs of 1%–6% as a marker for emphysema by quantitative analysis.5 6

According to the WHO, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is the third leading cause of death in the world, responsible for 3.23 million deaths in 2019.7 Underdiagnosis and undertreatment of COPD contribute to the high morbidity and mortality associated with this disease. Furthermore, identification of individuals with pre-COPD may allow the recognition of risk factors and implementation of treatments for symptomatic individuals before the disease becomes more entrenched. Although early diagnosis of COPD improves outcomes and quality of life, the USPSTF does not recommend screening for COPD in asymptomatic persons.8

Previous post hoc analysis of the National Lung Cancer Screening Trial (NLST), a large randomised study comparing cancer detection in individuals receiving either LDCT or chest radiograph over 5 years, has shown a correlation with %LAA and presence of spirometric obstruction in individuals qualifying for the prior 2021 USPSTF recommendation.5 Individuals who qualify for lung cancer screening with LDCT are at an inherent risk for the development of COPD, which remains underdiagnosed.9

The Temple Healthy Chest Initiative (THCI) is a comprehensive lung cancer screening programme coupled with comorbidity detection that primarily serves a largely urban minority population. LDCT prescription is decentralised and qualitative analyses are centralised and performed by radiologists using Lung-RADS classification and a standardised form for comorbidity reporting. Comorbidities include emphysema, airways disease and other non-pulmonary conditions that can be identified on chest CT imaging.

Determination of the presence of lung and airway abnormalities during lung cancer screening could be burdensome for clinical radiologists performing nodule evaluation and hinder the reporting of comorbid lung abnormalities. Automated quantitative imaging analysis may be an alternative to reduce the radiologist workload; however, there is a paucity of data regarding the use of AI-assisted emphysema detection in a cohort of individuals undergoing lung cancer screening with the newly expanded USPSTF criteria.

Our objective is to assess the association between emphysema as determined by radiologist and QCT analysis, with the presence of spirometric abnormalities in an urban largely minority population undergoing lung cancer screening.

Methods

The THCI is an urban health system-wide lung cancer screening programme coupled with comorbidity detection. The primary goal of THCI is to increase lung cancer screening among eligible individuals in the Philadelphia community. Eligible participants are individuals aged 50–80, with a 20-pack-year or greater smoking history, who are either active or former smokers who had quit less than 15 years previously. Individuals are enrolled in the THCI after being referred by primary care providers or subspecialists from within the health system or in the surrounding community. Additionally, individuals are enrolled after self-referral. LDCT reading is centralised and performed by radiologists using Lung Imaging Reporting and Data System (Lung-RADS) classification and a standardised form for comorbidity reporting. The consenting process was waived by the IRB due to minimal risk of the study design.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and/or the public were not involved in the design of this study.

Comorbidity detection

Comorbidities identified include airways disease, emphysema, interstitial lung disease, enlarged pulmonary artery, coronary artery calcification, hilar and mediastinal lymphadenopathy, thyroid nodules, osteopenia/osteoporosis. Variables including airways disease, emphysema, interstitial lung disease and coronary artery calcification were semi-quantified into mild, moderate and severe.

Patients’ Symptom Questionnaires

At the time of screening, individuals answer a symptom survey regarding frequency of dyspnoea, cough, smoking status and prior diagnosis of COPD. Patient characteristics including age, sex and pack-years were obtained from electronic medical record (EMR) query.

Qualitative detection of emphysema by radiologist

All LDCTs were reviewed at the time of test performance by a radiologist, the majority of whom were trained thoracic radiologists. The presence of emphysema was semiquantified as mild, moderate or severe burden.

%LAA analysis

Using AI-assisted QCT analysis (aView, Coreline, Seoul SK). This system uses machine learning to perform lobe segmentation, airway segmentation and AI-enabled airway growth. %LAA analysis was performed using the –950 HU and –910 HU cut-offs, with airways excluded from analysis (figure 1).

% LAA analysis (Aview, AI Lung Systems, Coreline, Seoul SK). LAA, low-attenuation area; LLL, Left Lower Lobe; RLL, Right Lower Lobe; RML, Right Middle Lobe; RUL, Right Upper Lobe; LUL, Left Upper Lobe.

Spirometry

EMR was queried for prebronchodilator spirometry and DLCO (diffusion capacity of carbon monoxide) performed within 12 months of LDCT. Spirometric obstruction was defined as forced expiratory volume in 1 s/forced vital capacity (FEV1/FEV)<70. Spirometry was performed in-lab in accordance with ATS guidelines for reproducibility.

Statistical analysis

Normality of continuous variables was assessed using Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilk tests. Baseline characteristics were calculated for subjects as a median and IQR for non-normally distributed continuous variables and as count and percentage for categorical variables. Significant emphysema by prior convention was considered either %LAA≥15 or %LAA≥6%.10 Diagnostic tests, including sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV), were calculated to show relationship between %LAA (at both –950 HU and –910 HU) to presence of spirometric obstruction (FEV1/FVC<70). Area under the curve (AUC) and DeLong’s test were used to assess optimal cut-off for %LAA at both −950 and −910 HU and compare models. Linear regression modelling was used to show relationships between spirometric data (FEV1% Predicted, FEV1/FVC, DLCO% Predicted) and %LAA at both –950 and –910 HU. Kruskal-Wallis test with Bonferroni corrections for multiple comparisons was used to assess differences in %LAA at −950 and −910 in the various COPD Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) groups. Analysis was performed with SPSS V.30. Missing data were assumed to be missing at random.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 2111 individuals underwent LDCT as part of the Total THCI Cohort from October 2021 to October 2022; 1119 (53%) females and 992 (47%) males. Of the total THCI cohort, 1162 (55%) of participants were current smokers, and 891 (42.2%) were former smokers. Median pack-years in the total THCI cohort was 30 pack-years. 629 individuals underwent spirometry, 397 (63.1%) were found to have obstruction (FEV1/FVC<70).

Of the total THCI cohort, a subcohort of 181 patients (THCI QCT subcohort) was made up of 96 (53%) females and 85 (47%) males and underwent %LAA analysis at less than −950 HU and less than –910 HU using AI software (figure 2). These patients were chosen randomly from a group of patients who had spirometry previously performed. Of the THCI QCT subcohort, 93 (51.4%) participants were current smokers and 88 (48.6%) were former smokers. Median pack-years in the THCI QCT subcohort were 35 pack-years.

Emphysema qualification by radiologists

In the total THCI cohort, emphysema was identified by radiologists in 1387 (65.7%) cases. Of these, the radiologist graded emphysema severity as mild/minimal emphysema in 393 (18.6%) cases, moderate emphysema in 720 (34.1%) cases and severe emphysema in 170 (8.05%) cases. 99 (4.7%) cases were labelled as emphysema without qualification.

In the THCI QCT subcohort, emphysema was identified by radiologists in 170 (71.7%) of cases. Of these, the radiologist graded emphysema severity as mild/minimal in 65 (27.4%) cases, moderate emphysema in 58 (24.5%) cases, and severe emphysema in 33 (13.9%) cases. 14 (5.9%) cases were labelled as emphysema without quantification.

Total THCI cohort: relationship of airflow obstruction and qualitative emphysema detection by radiologist

629 individuals in the total THCI cohort underwent spirometry within 1 year of LDCT. Airflow obstruction was present in 397 (63.1%) patients. 61 (15.4%) individuals had mild obstruction (FEV1 %Pred≥80), 194 (48.9%) individuals had moderate obstruction (FEV1 %Pred≥50–80), 118 (29.7%) had severe obstruction (FEV1 %Pred≥30–50) and 24 (6.00%) had very severe obstruction (FEV1 %Pred<30). In the total THCI cohort, 323 (51.4%) individuals with emphysema as determined by radiologist interpretation had airflow obstruction (FEV1/FVC<70). 108 (17.2%) individuals without emphysema did not have spirometric obstruction. Sensitivity, Specificity, PPV and NPV of emphysema detected by radiologist for airflow obstruction are 81.3%, 53.4%, 72.2% and 59.34%, respectively.

THCI QCT subcohort: emphysema detection by AI analysis

In the THCI QCT subcohort, 65 (35.9%) of individuals were found to have no emphysema (%LAA<1% at –950HU). 67 (37%) of individuals were found to have minimal emphysema (%LAA 1–5% at –950 HU). 49 (27.1%) individuals were found to have significant emphysema (%LAA≥6% at –950 HU).

Analysis was also performed at –910 HU. 14 (7.7%) of individuals were found to have no emphysema (%LAA<1% at –910 HU). 42 (23.2%) of individuals were found to have minimal emphysema (%LAA 1–5% at −910 HU). 125 (69.1%) individuals were found to have significant emphysema (%LAA≥6% at –910 HU).

THCI QCT subcohort: relationship of airflow obstruction and emphysema as detected by QCT

All 181 individuals in the THCI QCT subcohort had undergone spirometry within 12 months of LDCT date. Airflow obstruction (FEV1/FVC<70) was found in 114 (63%) of patients. 20 (11%) individuals had mild obstruction (FEV1 %Pred≥80), 57 (31.5%) individuals had moderate obstruction (FEV1 %Pred≥50–80), 32 (28.1%) had severe obstruction (FEV1 %Pred≥30–50) and 5 (2.8%) had very severe obstruction (FEV1 %Pred<30).

In the THCI QCT subcohort, 36 (19.9%) individuals were found to have %LAA-950≥6% and airflow obstruction. 54 (30%) individuals with %LAA-950<6% did not have airflow obstruction (table 1b).

Post hoc power analysis

A post hoc power analysis was performed based on ROC analysis of %LAA-950 and %LAA-910 with the finding of obstruction. This resulted in an estimated statistical power of 87% for %LAA at −950 and 99% for %LAA at −910 at a significance level of 0.05.

Symptom surveys

In the THCI QCT subcohort, symptom survey data related to dyspnoea and cough was available for 148 patients. 78 (60%) individuals reported experiencing dyspnoea either occasionally, frequently, daily or continuously. 44 (35%) of individuals reported experiencing cough most days or daily. There was no significant association between symptoms of dyspnoea and cough with radiologic findings of emphysema or airways disease. Finally, 65 (35.9%) patients had pre-existing diagnoses of COPD.

Discussion

In the first year of the THCI, 2111 individuals underwent LDCT for lung cancer screening. Of that number, 629 (28.8%) underwent spirometry within 12 months of the LDCT. A subcohort of 181 individuals representing 8.6% of the total THCI population had QCT analysis performed. Total THCI cohort and QCT subcohort populations had similar median age, male:female ratio, rate of current smokers, smoking burden (pack-years) and rate of spirometry obstruction (table 3).

In individuals qualifying for lung cancer screening with LDCT under the updated 2021 USPSTF guidelines, we demonstrated a high rate of emphysema detection both by radiologist qualitative interpretation (72%) and by quantitative analysis using percent low attenuation analysis ≥6% at −910 HU, (69%).

Post hoc analysis of the NLST showed %LAA measured on LDCT was independently associated with a higher odds of airflow obstruction. Rates of quantitatively assessed significant emphysema (%LAA −950≥6%) were higher in the THCI population compared with the NLST at 27% and 15.1%, respectively.5

In the THCI QCT subcohort, we found a high rate of spirometric obstruction, 63% defined by FEV1/FEV<70. This was notably higher than the NLST QCT cohort, which was 33%.5 Interestingly, smoking burden was notably higher in the NLST cohort at 56.1 pack-years compared with 35 pack-years in the THCI QCT subcohort. Reasons for these differences in emphysema burden and rate of spirometric obstruction may be related to the THCI QCT Subcohort containing more individuals who are older with higher rates of active smoking and greater percentage of minority subjects.

Previous analysis of the COPDGene population showed a linear correlation between %LAA at −950 HU and FEV1% predicted and FEV1/FVC of r=−0.67 and r=−7.6, respectively.3 To date, we have not found any studies reporting similar linear correlation in the population of individuals undergoing lung cancer screening with LDCT. Our THCI QCT subcohort data demonstrate the highest linear correlation between %LAA-910 and FEV1/FVC ratio and FEV1% predicted of r=−0.494 and −0.297, respectively. Differences in these r values may be related to differences in total subject numbers (4062 in the COPD Gene cohort and 181 in the THCI QCT subcohort) or demographic factors between the two populations but may also be related to use of non-low dose imaging in the COPDGene cohort.

Currently, the USPSTF does not recommend routine screening with questionnaires or spirometry in asymptomatic smokers for the presence of COPD. To date, there have been several prospective studies demonstrating high rates of COPD in individuals undergoing lung cancer screening with LDCT.911,16 Ruparel et al reported in a cohort of 986 individuals’ part of the Lung Screen Uptake Study study group, 560 (57%) had prebronchodilator spirometry consistent with COPD, 377 (67%) of which were previously undiagnosed.9

With the increasing numbers of individuals with a smoking history undergoing LDCT for lung cancer screening and the ubiquity of AI-driven QCT analysis, we propose a benefit to obtaining spirometry for individuals found to have ≥6% LAA at −910 HU given its high sensitivity and PPV for spirometric obstruction, regardless of current respiratory symptoms. Additionally, we have shown significant differences between %LAA obtained at −950 HU and −910 HU with the GOLD groups. We propose that QCT performed, specifically at −910 HU, may be used as a marker for the presence and severity of airflow obstruction.

Finally, LDCT screening coupled with symptom surveys provides a natural platform for discussion and distribution of smoking cessation-related resources.17

Limitations of this study include its retrospective nature in which the sample size was determined based on convenience and the availability of data within a defined period. No prospective power analysis was performed prior to data collection. Given that spirometry was performed at the request of the treating pulmonologist or primary care doctor, individuals with higher symptom burden may be overrepresented, possibly resulting in the significantly higher rates of airflow obstruction in this cohort compared with that of the NLST QCT cohort. However, we do believe this represents a generalisable cohort given that all included patients presented for the purpose of seeking treatment given significant history of smoking. An additional source of variability may be qualitative assessment for emphysema, as this qualitative assessment was done by multiple different radiologists with variable inter-rater agreement. Finally, symptom surveys were not available for all subjects, introducing potential sampling bias as well as the inherent limitations of self-reported data, including recall and reporting bias. Specifically, we noted statistically significant higher pack-year smoked in the group that had symptom surveys which could lead to bias in reporting (online supplemental table 1).

Conclusions

Data from the THCI, a real-world lung cancer screening programme using the most recent USPSTF recommendations coupled with comorbidity detection, demonstrate high rates of emphysema as detected by radiologic qualitative interpretation and quantitative analysis using %LAA at −950 HU and −910 HU. Linear correlation exists between %LAA at −950 HU and −910 HU and spirometric values of FEV1% predicted, FEV1/FVC and DLCO% predicted. Finally, using a cut-off of ≥1% for LAA at −950 and ≥6% for LAA at −910HU, there is a high sensitivity and PPV for associated spirometric obstruction and thus should drive providers to obtain prebronchodilator spirometry to detect COPD at an earlier stage.

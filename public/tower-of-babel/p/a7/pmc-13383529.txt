Frontline T cell-redirecting therapies in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma: key signals from the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting
Authors: Huixing Zhou, Xiaoyun Jin, Wenming Chen, Hong-Hu Zhu, Wen Gao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Early frontline data presented at the 2025 ASH Annual Meeting suggest that T cell–redirecting strategies, including chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cells and bispecific antibodies (BsAbs), can induce rapid, high rates of minimal residual disease (MRD)-negative responses in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM), including transplant-ineligible (TI) and high-risk populations. However, short follow-up, heterogeneous MRD methodologies, and infection risk—particularly with continuous bispecific exposure—underscore that these approaches remain investigational and should be advanced through randomized, MRD-guided fixed-duration trials.

To the editor,

At the 2025 American Society of Hematology (ASH) Annual Meeting, early-phase studies evaluated T cell–redirecting immunotherapies as upfront or post‑induction strategies in newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (NDMM) showing notable depth of response (Table 1). However, follow-up remains short and survival endpoints are immature.

CAR‑T cell approaches

In a phase 1 study of GC012F/AZD0120 [1], a rapid-manufacturing dual-targeting BCMA/CD19 CAR-T, post-induction NDMM patients (n = 30) achieved an ORR of 100%, with MRD negativity at 10⁻⁶ by EuroFlow was 100%, 93%, and 92% at months 1, 6, and 12, respectively, with sustained MRD negativity beyond 12 months in 81.5% [1]. At 30 months, progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) rates were 88% and 92%, respectively, with manageable toxicities. The CAREMM‑001 trial [2] evaluated post-induction BCMA CAR-T therapy in TI NDMM patients (n = 36) and reported an ORR of 100%, sCR of 88.9%, and MRD negativity at 10⁻⁶ by NGS in all evaluable patients at day 28. For TP53-abnormal NDMM, the CAC-MM-001 trial (n = 14) [3] tested a CART–autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) –CART2 sequential strategy. All patients achieved complete response (CR) with MRD-negativity (NGS,10− 6). However, early relapses remained a concern in patients with biallelic TP53 loss (n = 2). Overall, CRS was largely low grade and ICANS was uncommon across these studies (detailed in Table 2).

In transplant-ineligible (TI) high-risk NDMM (n = 16), a fully human BCMA CAR-T product, equecabtagene-autoleucel (Eque-cel; FUMANBA-2 trial) [4] achieved 100% MRD negativity (flow cytometry, 10− 5) at 1 month and 80% sustained MRD-negativity over 24 months. The ≥very good partial response (VGPR) rate was 100%, with 93.8% sCR. Only grade 1–2 CRS occurred with no ICANS, yet serious infections occurred in 43.8% of patients, most commonly pneumonia [4].

Bispecific antibody approaches

In contrast to finite CAR‑T consolidation, BsAbs provide off-the-shelf T-cell redirection but require repeated dosing, shifting the key concern from manufacturing feasibility to durability, infection risk, and treatment burden.

The IFM2021-01 trial Cohort A [5] evaluated teclistamab plus daratumumab (Tec-Dara) in elderly, TI NDMM. Among 37 patients with a median age of 73 years, ≥VGPR at 4 cycles was achieved in 78%, with a best ORR of 100% and ≥VGPR rate of 97%. MRD negativity (NGS, 10⁻⁶) reached 51% in the intention-to-treat population and 100% among evaluable patients. Grade ≥ 3 AEs occurred in 76% of patients, including hematologic toxicities (46%) and grade ≥ 3 infections (14%).

Linvoseltamab (LINKER-MM4) is the first BCMA×CD3 bispecific antibody evaluated as monotherapy in NDMM (45 enrolled, 43 evaluable). The ORR was 79%, with ≥VGPR in 56%, ≥CR in 26%, and MRD negativity (clonoSEQ or EuroFlow,10⁻⁵) reported in 92% of evaluable patients. CRS was uniformly low‑grade and ICANS was rare (grade 1 in 1 patient) [6].

Several trials are exploring response-adapted or fixed-duration bispecific strategies to mitigate infection risk while preserving depth of response. A single-arm phase II study [7] evaluated four weekly doses of low-dose teclistamab (1.0 mg/kg) as early consolidation after three cycles of VRd in transplant-eligible NDMM without planned ASCT (n = 11). ORR after low-dose teclistamab consolidation was 100% and all assessed patients achieved MRD-negative CR (flow cytometry, 10⁻⁵). The toxicity was mainly grade 1 CRS, with no grade ≥ 3 toxicities [7]. The investigator-initiated IMMUNOPLANT trial is testing abbreviated fixed-duration linvoseltamab immuno-consolidation specifically for patients remaining MRD-positive after standard triplet/quad induction. At early analysis (n = 14), all patients assessed after 4 cycles converted to MRD negativity (both ClonoSEQ at 10⁻⁶ and flow cytometry at 10− 5), with no CRS or ICANS observed [8].

Taken together, these data support the biological activity of T cell-redirecting therapies in NDMM but do not yet justify routine upfront use. Though deep responses and high MRD negativity were observed, cross-trial comparisons are limited by heterogeneous designs, MRD assays/sensitivity, and short follow-up with immature survival endpoints. Importantly, CAR‑T and bispecific antibodies differ in treatment intent and exposure: CAR‑T is typically time-limited, whereas bispecific antibodies often require ongoing dosing, with infection emerging as a key constraint. Future randomized, MRD-guided trials should evaluate fixed-duration or response-adapted strategies and incorporate standardized infection monitoring, antimicrobial prophylaxis, vaccination, and immunoglobulin replacement therapy when clinically indicated to balance depth of response with long-term safety and feasibility.

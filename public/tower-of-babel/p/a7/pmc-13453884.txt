Central Message

PA catheter–exchange technique enables controlled, fluoroscopy-free percutaneous RVAD placement during LVAD implantation in patients with severe RV dysfunction without delay in biventricular support.

Perioperative right ventricular (RV) failure occurs in 20 to 40% of left ventricular assist device (LVAD) implantations and remains a leading cause of early morbidity and mortality.1 When RV failure develops after implantation, rescue right ventricular assist device (RVAD) implantation has been associated with worse outcomes than upfront biventricular support, making accurate preoperative risk stratification essential.1,2 Contemporary frameworks have moved beyond static scoring systems to integrate dynamic hemodynamic indices, advanced echocardiographic parameters, and emerging machine learning–based risk models, identifying high-risk candidates1,3—that is, those with severe RV dysfunction, inotrope dependence, or impaired renal function—who benefit from planned temporary RV support at the time of LVAD placement.

The ProtekDuo (LivaNova) provides a percutaneous dual-lumen configuration enabling right atrial drainage with direct pulmonary artery (PA) return through a single access point. However, percutaneous placement of its distal lumen into the PA during LVAD implantation presents 3 challenges: it often requires fluoroscopy, which is logistically difficult with an open chest; the right ventricle is decompressed during cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB), making blind intracardiac navigation unreliable; and wire access can be lost during cannula advancement. We describe a reproducible PA catheter–exchange technique that eliminates these obstacles.

Case Presentation

A 22-year-old woman with dilated nonischemic cardiomyopathy presented in cardiogenic shock. Comorbidities included hypertension and sickle cell trait. She was initiated on vasopressors and underwent Impella 5.5 (Abiomed) placement for hemodynamic support. Preoperative echocardiography demonstrated severely dilated left ventricle (end-diastolic dimension 6.4 cm) with ejection fraction 15 to 20%, dilated and hypokinetic RV with tricuspid annular plane systolic excursion 1.5 cm, and severe mitral regurgitation. Right heart catheterization on Impella 5.5, dobutamine, and nitroprusside demonstrated an elevated central venous pressure of 21 mm Hg, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure 19 mm Hg (central venous pressure/pulmonary capillary wedge pressure ratio 1:1), PA pressures 45/24 mm Hg, a depressed pulmonary artery pulsatility index of 1.0, and a depressed right ventricular stroke work index of 4.7 gm/m2 with a cardiac index of 2.85 L/min/m2, indicating high risk of post-LVAD RV failure. The multidisciplinary heart failure team approved her for HeartMate 3 (Abbott) LVAD implantation as bridge to transplantation with planned ProtekDuo RVAD support.

Surgical Technique

After induction of general anesthesia, transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) was used to confirm dilated LV and severely reduced RV systolic function with severe mitral regurgitation. A Swan-Ganz catheter (Edwards Lifesciences) was placed via the right internal jugular (IJ) for hemodynamic monitoring and exchange guide. The Impella 5.5 was removed.

Median sternotomy, standard CPB cannulation, and normothermic bypass were established. The HeartMate 3 was implanted via standard apical technique using pledgeted Tevdek (Teleflex) sutures; the outflow graft was reinforced with a 20-mm polytetrafluoroethylene graft and anastomosed end-to-side to the ascending aorta.

We then turned our attention to the placement of a ProtekDuo RVAD. With the patient in Trendelenburg and LVAD at 3000 RPM for deairing, inhaled nitric oxide and inotropes were initiated.

The Swan-Ganz catheter, already positioned in the main PA, was used to guide the exchange. A small arteriotomy was created in the proximal main PA. A 0.025-inch Amplatz guidewire was advanced through the distal port of the Swan-Ganz and exteriorized through the PA arteriotomy, providing a stable rail for cannula delivery. The Swan-Ganz catheter was then removed over the wire using a standard Seldinger technique, leaving the guidewire in place. A short pigtail catheter was advanced over the 0.025-inch wire, allowing exchange for a 0.035-inch Amplatz Super Stiff wire. Serial dilation of the IJ tract was performed, and a 29-Fr ProtekDuo cannula was advanced over the wire under direct TEE visualization. The distal lumen was positioned in the main PA, the arteriotomy closed in 2 layers with pledgeted 4-0 PROLENE (Ethicon), and the ProtekDuo connected to a CentriMag console (Video 1).

During CPB separation, LVAD flows reached 3.5 to 4.5 L/min (5000-5100 RPM) and ProtekDuo flows 3.5 to 4.5 L/min (3000-4000 RPM), with excellent RV decompression. TEE at completion confirmed laminar LVAD inflow and effective RV unloading with the ProtekDuo distal lumen appropriately positioned in the main PA. The ProtekDuo was successfully weaned and decannulated at bedside on postoperative day 10. The patient was discharged home on postoperative day 22. Consent for publication was obtained from the patient. Institutional review board approval was not required.

Comment

This technique provides a safe, controlled method for percutaneous RVAD placement during LVAD implantation in patients at high risk for RV failure, particularly those with severely reduced RV function, chronic inotrope dependence, or impaired renal function, in whom planned biventricular support enables a smoother immediate postoperative course than reactive rescue.2

The principal benefit is that the pre-existing Swan-Ganz catheter can be converted into a precise guide rail at no additional operative cost. This confers 3 specific advantages over standard IJ-based approaches: biventricular support is available immediately at CPB separation, eliminating any delay in coming off bypass; fluoroscopy guidance, which is logistically difficult in the operating theater, is not required at any step; and wire access is secured under direct vision throughout, eliminating the risk of loss during serial dilation of a large-bore dual-lumen cannula.4,5 ProtekDuo was selected on the basis of institutional availability and operator familiarity. The arteriotomy is closed primarily and adds minimal operative time.

This technique has been applied in 6 patients at our institution without vascular injuries or inabilities to place the ProtekDuo in appropriate place.

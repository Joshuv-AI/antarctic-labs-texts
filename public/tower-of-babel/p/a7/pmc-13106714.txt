Alkaloids from Evodia rutaecarpa inhibit the occurrence and development of gallbladder cancer in vivo and in vitro
Authors: Yijie Li, Shufen Zhou, Hanzheng Xu, Hongye Zhou, Kunqi Sun, Siyang Hu, Ke Xu, Jiahua Yang, Yafeng Chen, Wei Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective Gallbladder cancer (GBC) poses a significant health burden with dismal prognosis due to frequent metastasis and recurrence. Although evodiamine has demonstrated potent inhibitory effects on proliferation and metastasis in various cancers, its role in GBC remains unexplored, and the underlying mechanisms are yet to be elucidated. Methods: The inhibitory effects of evodiamine on GBC cells were evaluated in vitro using a cell viability assay. Cell migration capacity was assessed via wound healing and Transwell assays. Apoptosis and cell cycle distribution were analyzed by flow cytometry and Western blotting (WB). The molecular mechanisms were investigated using Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) and WB to quantify ZEB1 gene expression. In vivo, the anti-tumor activity of evodiamine was verified in a nude mouse model.Results: Evodiamine significantly inhibited the proliferation of GBC cells. Flow cytometry and Western blotting revealed that evodiamine induced G2/M phase arrest and promoted apoptosis. mRNA-sequencing (mRNA-seq) demonstrated that evodiamine suppressed the transcription of ZEB1 and genes in the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. Consistent with in vitro findings, evodiamine exhibited remarkable antitumor effects in a nude mouse model. Conclusion: This study confirms that evodiamine inhibits GBC cell proliferation and induces apoptosis. The mechanism involves suppression of ZEB1 expression and inactivation of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. Introduction

Gallbladder cancer (GBC) is a highly lethal malignancy and the most common cancer in the human biliary tract1. GBC is characterized by a poor prognosis, with patients typically exhibiting a very low five-year survival rate2. The lack of typical symptoms in GBC patients complicates early screening and diagnosis. Therefore, many people are diagnosed at later stages, where surgical intervention is often ineffective, and chemotherapy becomes the primary treatment option3,4. Given this serious situation of GBC, there is an urgent need to identify new biomarkers and develop innovative treatment methods to improve patient outcomes.

Evodiamine, a quinazoline alkaloid derived from the dried, immature fruits of the traditional Chinese medicinal herb Evodia rutaecarpa, has garnered a lot of attention in biomedical research5. Numerous studies have demonstrated its diverse pharmacological effects, including anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and immune-regulatory properties6. Furthermore, evodiamine exhibits antitumor activities by inhibiting cell growth, inducing cell death, arresting the cell cycle, and suppressing cell migration and metastasis7. It has been shown to be effective in treating various types of cancer, such as hepatocellular carcinoma8, breast cancer9, and lung cancer10, However, the effects of evodiamine on GBC have not been reported.

Zinc finger E - box - binding homeobox 1 (ZEB1), an important transcription factor in the ZEB family, affects cell plasticity, growth, and helps cells invade and change their shape. Elevated ZEB1 levels are typically associated with poor prognosis in cancers11. Recent research has unveiled aberrant expression patterns of ZEB1 in multiple human cancers, including pancreatic cancer12 and breast cancer13, which are related to metastasis. Functional studies in pancreatic cancer cells further underscore ZEB1’s role in promoting cell invasion and metastasis14. Nevertheless, the expression levels of ZEB1 in GBC remain to be elucidated.

The objective of our study was to investigate the effects of evodiamine on the growth and metastasis of GBC cells and to elucidate its potential mechanisms. Our results demonstrated that evodiamine significantly inhibits the growth and metastasis of GBC cells and effectively blocks the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process. Mechanistically, evodiamine efficiently suppresses ZEB1 activity, thereby disrupting the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and markedly reducing the malignant characteristics of GBC. Our findings unveil the antitumor effects and underlying mechanisms of evodiamine in GBC, indicating its potential as a promising anticancer agent for clinical application.

Materials and methods

Cell culture

The GBC cell lines utilized in the experiment were GBC-SD and NOZ, both of which were generously donated by Renji Hospital, affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University. These cell lines were cultured in high-glucose DMEM medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 100 U/ml penicillin (Invitrogen, CA, USA), and streptomycin (Invitrogen, CA, USA). Additionally, human primary gallbladder epithelial cells (hGB-pic) were obtained from Shanghai Cyagen Biosciences (iCell) Inc., and were cultivated using a specialized medium specifically formulated for human primary gallbladder epithelial cells. All cell lines were maintained in a cell culture incubator at a constant temperature of 37 °C and a pressure of 5% CO2 to ensure optimal growth conditions.

Reagents

Evodiamine (CAS: 518-17−2, purity: 99.76%) was purchased from Selleck (TX, USA). It was dissolved in DMSO (BOSTER, China) to a concentration of 10 mM and stored at −20 °C until required. The final concentration of DMSO in the culture medium was maintained at 0.1%, which demonstrated no cytotoxicity towards the GBC cells.

Cell viability assay

Cells were inoculated into 96-well plates at a density of 5 × 104 cells per well and treated with evodiamine for 24 and 48 h. Subsequently, cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) (Dojindo, MD, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a spectrophotometer (Bio-Rad, CA, USA). The cell survival rate was calculated based on the optical density (OD) values of the treated and untreated cell groups.

Cell cycle and apoptosis assay

Flow cytometry was performed using the Cell Cycle Assay Kit - PI/RNase Staining Solution (Dojindo, Japan). Cells were collected and fixed in pre-cooled 70% ethanol for 2 h at 4 °C. Subsequently, they were resuspended in PBS, incubated with 500 µl of staining buffer, 25 µl of PI, and 10 µl of RNase A in the dark at 37 °C for 30 min. Finally, the cell cycle was detected using flow cytometry (Cytoflex, Beckman Coulter, USA).

To assess cell apoptosis, flow cytometry was also conducted using the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (Dojindo, Japan), following the manufacturer’s instructions. After treating the cells with DMEM or various concentrations of evodiamine, they were harvested, stained, and then analyzed by flow cytometry.

Western blotting (WB)

The GBC cells, after 48 h of treatment under different conditions, were lysed by RIPA, and the protein was quantitatively analyzed using a BCA kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China) following the SDS-PAGE gel electrophoresis method. In the immunoblotting test process, the membranes were blocked with blocking solution for 1 h at room temperature and then incubated with the following primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C: p21 (Proteintech,10355-1-AP, rabbit IgG), p53 (Proteintech, 60283-2-Ig, mouse IgG), Bcl-2 (CST, 3498 S, rabbit IgG), caspase-3 (CST, 9662 S, rabbit IgG), Cleaved caspase-3 (CST, 9664 S, rabbit IgG), E-cadherin (CST, 3195 T, rabbit IgG), N-cadherin (CST, 13116 T, rabbit IgG), MMP2 (CST, 87809 S, rabbit IgG), vimentin (Abcam, ab92547, rabbit IgG), Snail (CST, 3879 S, rabbit IgG), ZEB1 (Abcam, ab3829, rabbit IgG), p-PI3K (CST, 4228 S, rabbit IgG), PI3K (CST, 4257 S, rabbit IgG), p-Akt (CST, 2965P, rabbit IgG), Akt (CST, 4691P, rabbit IgG), and β-actin (Proteintech, 66009-1-Ig, mouse IgG). After washing the membranes three times with the tris-buffered saline containing tween-20, the membranes were further probed with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-rabbit/mouse IgG antibody (Abcam,1:10000). The chemiluminescence detection system (Bio Rad, CA, USA) was applied for spectral band analysis.

Cell motility assay

For the cell migration assay, GBC cells were inoculated into 6-well plates at a density of 6 × 105 cells per well and cultured for one day under optimal conditions. A linear wound of a specific size was then created by scraping a single layer of cells, followed by thorough rinsing with PBS. Photographs were taken at the time of wound creation (time 0) and at 24 h post-wounding in three different areas. The degree of wound closure was analyzed from these images, which indirectly reflected the cell migration ability.

Transwell assay

GBC cells (4 × 104) in 300 µl of serum-free DMEM were seeded into the upper chamber (8.0 μm pore size; FALCON, USA) and allowed to migrate towards 700 µl of DMEM containing 10% FBS. After 24 h of incubation, the unmigrated cells were gently removed from the upper surface of the Transwell chamber using a cotton swab. The insert was then fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature, stained with 500 µl of 0.03% crystal violet solution for 20 min at 37 °C, subsequently immersed in PBS, washed three times for 5 min each, air-dried, and finally, the number of migrated cells in randomly selected fields was counted under a microscope.

mRNA-sequencing (mRNA-seq) and data analysis.

Total RNA of GBC cells was extracted using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA) following the manufacturer’s protocol. RNA purity and quantification were evaluated using a NanoDrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific, USA). RNA integrity was assessed using an Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The libraries were then constructed using the VAHTS Universal V6 RNA-seq Library Prep Kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The transcriptome sequencing and analysis were conducted by OE Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR).

Total RNA was extracted from GBC cells using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, CA, USA). The concentration of the extracted total RNA was quantified by measuring absorbance at 260 nm. For SYBR green-based quantitative PCR amplification, the reactions were performed in a 10 µl volume using an Applied Biosystems instrument. Relative gene expression levels in each cell line were determined using the 2^-ΔΔCt method. The primer sequences used are as follows:

ZEB1, 5′-GATGATGAATGCGAGTCAGATGC-3′ and 5′-ACAGCAGTGTCTTGTTGTTGT-3′;

β-actin,5′-CCTGGCACCCAGCACAAT-3′ and 5′-GGGCCGGACTCGTCATAC-3′.

Plasmids and Recombinant lentiviruses

The overexpression plasmid of ZEB1 (OE-ZEB1) and the negative control (NC) recombinant lentiviruses were obtained from GeneChem (Shanghai, China). For plasmid transfection, Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was utilized, following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol. Viral infection was performed using GeneChem’s infection reagent.

In vivo xenograft model

Male nude mice (age: about 6 weeks; weight: about 25 g) were used in this study.NOZ cells (1 × 106 cells in 100 µl PBS) were injected subcutaneously into the right axilla of nude mice to establish a GBC xenograft model. After tumor formation, mice were randomly divided into four groups (n = 5 per group). The control group was treated with normal saline, while the other groups were treated with evodiamine at doses of 5, 10, and 20 mg/kg every 2 days. Body weight was measured every two days. Tumor volume (V), calculated as (length × width2)/2 using a caliper, were measured every two days. After two weeks of treatment, mice were euthanized by cervical dislocation under sodium pentobarbital anesthesia, mouse tumors and tissues were collected for further analysis. All experiments conformed to the ethical principles of animal experimentation stipulated by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Putuo Hospital, Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, China.Animal experiments are reported according to the ARRIVE guidelines.

Spleen injection liver metastasis model

NOZ-luc cells were inoculated into the spleens; the spleens were removed after approximately 2 min.Intravital imaging was conducted one week post-inoculation. During the in vivo experiment, the control group was administered normal saline, while the experimental group received intraperitoneal injections of evodiamine at doses of 5, 10, and 20 mg/kg once every two days for a duration of 14 days. After two weeks, the experimental animals were euthanized, and their livers with metastases were fixed in formalin for subsequent experiments. The relevant experimental contents and protocols were approved by the Ethics Committee of Putuo Hospital.Animal experiments are reported according to the ARRIVE guidelines.

Histopathological assay

Tissues were harvested post-sacrifice of the animals. The histopathological assay was conducted using standard haematoxylin–eosin (H&E) staining techniques.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC)

The collected tissue samples were fixed in 10% formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm. During the study, IHC was used to analyze the expression levels of Ki67, Vimentin, and ZEB1. Three random images were captured using a Leica microscope (Wetzlar, Germany). The positive expression of cells was quantified using ImageJ (https://imagej.net/ij/download.html, ImageJ 1.54 g) analysis.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis of the experimental data was performed using GraphPad Prism 9.5.1 software (https://www.graphpad.com/.) Data are presented as the mean ± S.D. from three independent experiments conducted under identical conditions. Comparisons between two groups were analyzed using an unpaired t-test. For multiple group comparisons, ANOVA and Tukey tests were applied. Significance was defined as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, and ***P < 0.001.

Data availability

All data generated or analysed during this study are included in this published article, along with its supplementary files.

Results

Evodiamine inhibits proliferation and induced apoptosis in GBC cells

Evodiamine is a naturally alkaloid with a quinazolinocarboline skeleton6; its chemical structure is depicted in Fig. 1A. To investigate the effects of evodiamine in GBC cells, we initially conducted CCK-8 assays, which demonstrated that evodiamine significantly inhibited GBC cell proliferation in a time- and dose-dependent manner (Figure. 1B). Furthermore, we employed WB to assess the expression levels of cell cycle regulatory proteins. The results indicated an increase in the expression levels of both p53 and p21 proteins, suggesting that evodiamine may inhibit the proliferation of GBC cells by interfering with the cell cycle progression(Figure. 1 C). To corroborate these findings, we conducted flow cytometry analysis, which revealed that evodiamine primarily arrests GBC cells in the G2 phase (Figure. 1D). These findings suggested that evodiamine - induced M - phase arrest may be mediated by p53 and p21. Additionally, evodiamine treatment downregulated Bcl-2 and full-length caspase-3 but upregulated Cleaved caspase-3, as shown in Fig. 1E. Flow cytometric analysis confirmed that evodiamine induced apoptosis in GBC cells in a dose-dependent manner (Figure. 1 F), suggesting that evodiamine has the ability to induce apoptosis in GBC cells. In summary, these findings support the in vitro antiproliferative and pro-apoptotic effects of evodiamine on GBC cells.

Evodiamine inhibited migration in GBC cells

Our study has demonstrated, through the use of wound healing and transwell assays, that evodiamine possesses the ability to suppress the migratory capabilities of tumor cells (Figure. 2 A, B). Furthermore, a key mechanism underlying tumor metastasis is the EMT, a biological process in which epithelial cells undergo a transformation, losing their distinct properties and acquiring mesenchymal characteristics15. And MMP2 activity is also associated with cancer cell migration16. Notably, we observed that evodiamine upregulates E-cadherin while effectively downregulating the expression of N-cadherin, vimentin, MMP2, and Snail (Fig. 2 C), suggesting its potential to inhibit EMT and thereby reduce tumor metastasis.

Transcriptomic analysis reveals the effects of Evodiamine in GBC cells

ZEB1 has been implicated in cell invasion in breast cancer13. Our WB and qPCR analyses demonstrated that ZEB1 was significantly overexpressed in GBC cells compared to hGB-pic cells (Figure. 3 A, Supplementary Material 1 A). Previous studies have shown that PI3K-Akt signaling pathway is related to tumor growth and metastasis17. Based on these backgrounds, we hypothesize that the inhibition of growth and metastasis of GBC by evodiamine may be related to ZEB1 and the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway. To investigate the mechanisms by which evodiamine downregulates the levels of ZEB1, we first explored whether evodiamine inhibits ZEB1 mRNA expression in GBC-SD and NOZ cells. Both cell lines were treated with evodiamine and qPCR analysis demonstrated that evodiamine had significant influence on ZEB1 mRNA expression levels (Supplementary Material 1B), indicating that evodiamine downregulates ZEB1 levels through a transcription mechanism. We also found that evodiamine inhibited the protein expression of ZEB1, p-PI3K, and p-Akt in GBC cells (Figure. 3B, Supplementary material 1 C).

To again confirm the molecular mechanisms underlying the effect of evodiamine, we performed an mRNA-seq assay. GBC cells were treated with either 7.5 µM evodiamine or DMEM for 48 h, after which total RNA was extracted for transcriptome sequencing. For bioinformatics analysis, we set the statistical thresholds at P-value ≤ 0.05 and |log2FC| ≥ 1 as statistical criteria. As shown in Figure. 3 C, evodiamine treatment led to upregulated and downregulated genes in GBC cells. Moreover, Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) demonstrated that evodiamine dramatically downregulated genes associated with the “PI3K-Akt signaling pathway” (Figure. 3D). These results suggest that evodiamine exerts particular influences on ZEB1 and the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway in GBC.

Evodiamine inhibited GBC cells migration by ZEB1

To explore the role of ZEB1 in evodiamine-induced inhibition of metastasis, we transfected OE-ZEB1 in GBC cells. Overexpression of ZEB1 was confirmed using WB and qPCR (Figure. 4 A, B, Supplementary material 2 A, B). Evodiamine treatment significantly reduced cell migration in both GBC-SD and NOZ cells, as demonstrated by in vitro motility and transwell assays. Moreover, ZEB1 overexpression attenuated the migration-inhibitory effects of evodiamine. These findings, together with the observed downregulation of ZEB1 expression by evodiamine (Figure. 3B), suggest that ZEB1 is involved in the migration-inhibitory effects of evodiamine (Figure. 4 C, D).

Moreover, WB analysis showed that evodiamine increased E-cadherin and reduced N-cadherin, vimentin, MMP2 and Snail, and these effects were attenuated by ZEB1 overexpression (Figure. 4E and Supplementary material 2 C). These data indicate that ZEB1 acts as an upstream regulator of these EMT-related proteins and that evodiamine suppresses EMT in GBC by regulating these proteins. Additionally, considering the pivotal role of the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway in cancer initiation, progression, and transformation18, we investigated the involvement of PI3K and Akt in the effects of evodiamine. Evodiamine was found to downregulate the phosphorylated forms of PI3K (p-PI3K) and Akt (p-Akt), and ZEB1 overexpression counteracted the decreased expression of both p-PI3K and p-Akt (Figure. 4 F, Supplementary material 2D). These findings elucidate the potential mechanisms by which evodiamine inhibits GBC migration through increased E-cadherin and suppressed ZEB1, N-cadherin, vimentin, MMP2 and Snail. Our results also highlight the significant role of the PI3K-Akt pathway in the antitumor effects of evodiamine in GBC cells.

Evodiamine inhibits GBC growth and metastasis in vivo

To ascertain the in vivo effects of evodiamine, a xenograft model of GBC cells was established utilizing NOZ cells. The findings revealed that evodiamine markedly curtailed tumor growth and exerted no influence on the body weight of the mice (Figure. 5 A-C, Supplementary material 3,Supplementary material 4 A). Moreover, H&E staining was employed to inspect the histopathology of the principal organs in each group, with no significant histological morphological changes of vital organs being observed (Figure. 5D). We further assessed the in vivo antitumor effect of evodiamine using IHC. The results indicated that evodiamine treatment diminished the expressions of Ki67, vimentin, and ZEB1 (Figure. 5E, F). These results imply that evodiamine might not induce evident toxicity while effectively suppressing tumor growth in vivo.

To explore the impact of evodiamine on inhibiting GBC metastasis in vivo, we established a liver metastasis model of GBC via spleen injection (Figure. 6 A). The imaging results following evodiamine treatment demonstrated that evodiamine significantly inhibited liver metastasis (Figure. 6B). The number of metastatic nodules in the livers of the evodiamine-treated group was markedly decreased (Figure. 6 C, D, Supplementary material 4B). These findings suggest that evodiamine is capable of suppressing tumor metastasis in GBC.

Discussion

GBC is often diagnosed at advanced stages, posing significant treatment challenges3. Thus, searching for more effective therapies for GBC is not only urgent but also critically important. Notably, natural compounds and their derivatives derived from traditional Chinese herbs have emerged as promising alternative anticancer agents, due to their cost-effectiveness, unique therapeutic effects, and minimal side effects.

Among these, evodiamine, a key compound extracted from the traditional Chinese herbal medicine Evodia rutaecarpa, has recently been reported to exhibit inhibitory effects on various types of tumors, through multiple mechanisms, such as inducing cell cycle arrest, promoting cell apoptosis and autophagy, and inhibiting tumor growth and metastasis19. It has been found that evodiamine inhibits the proliferation of cholangiocarcinoma by suppressing the IL-6/STAT3 signaling pathway and upregulating the expression of SHP-220. Evodiamine also inhibites proliferation and promotes apoptosis in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), primarily by significantly stimulating the Hippo-YAP signaling pathway21. Mechanically, evodiamine treatment led to the arrest of GBC cells in the M - phase of the cell cycle, and this process was mediated by the p53/p21 axis. Previous research has demonstrated that evodiamine can induce cell - cycle arrest in various types of cancers through different signaling pathways19. For instance, Wu et al. revealed that evodiamine triggered G2/M cell - cycle arrest and reduced the viability of glioblastoma cells by activating JNK signaling22. In Zhang et al.‘s study23, they observed that evodiamine induced M - phase arrest in Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma via the p53/p21 signaling pathway. Despite these findings, the precise cell - cycle phase at which evodiamine induces arrest has not been fully clarified. In our current study, we also found that evodiamine arrested GBC cells in the M - phase through the p53/p21 signaling pathway. Additionally, our study also reveals that evodiamine inhibits GBC progression by suppressing migration and invasion.

Metastasis, a defining characteristic of malignant tumors, involves cancer cells disseminating from the primary tumor to distant sites, undergoing a complex cascade of invasion and metastasis. This process encompasses multiple steps: initial growth of the primary tumor, local invasion, dissemination and intravasation into the bloodstream, extravasation into a new microenvironment, and ultimately colonization in distant organs24. EMT plays a crucial role in tumor progression by equipping tumor cells with invasive and migratory properties, thereby enabling them to penetrate the basement membrane and disseminate to distant organs25. Our study showed that evodiamine treatment led to a significant decrease in the expression of mesenchymal markers, including E-cadherin, N-cadherin, vimentin, MMP2 and Snail, implying that evodiamine inhibits the EMT process in GBC cells.

ZEB1, belonging to the zinc finger homeodomain transcription factor family, modulates DNA damage responses, cancer cell differentiation processes, and metastatic behaviors26. Our WB and qPCR results identified ZEB1 as one of the significantly downregulated factor upon evodiamine treatment. Previous studies suggest that ZEB1 promotes tumorigenesis and metastasis in HCC via enhancing the Warburg effect27. Therefore, based on our pre - clinical experimental results, we tentatively propose that ZEB1 might be involved in mediating the antitumor effects of evodiamine in the context of GBC cell models. In our in vitro experiments, we overexpressed ZEB1 in GBC cells and observed that ZEB1 partially reversed the cell growth inhibition and EMT process inhibition caused by evodiamine. However, it is important to note that these findings are strictly limited to the cell - based experimental system we employed.

We also detected that ZEB1 was significantly upregulated in GBC tissues in our study. Yet, this result, while interesting, should be interpreted with caution. It only provides preliminary evidence at the pre-clinical level, and we cannot at this stage claim that ZEB1 may be a promising prognostic marker of GBC in a clinical setting. There are numerous factors in the complex clinical environment that are not accounted for in our current pre-clinical study, such as patient heterogeneity, the influence of the immune system, and the long-term effects of treatment.Although the role of ZEB1 as an upstream regulator of the PI3K - Akt signaling pathway aligns with previous pre - clinical findings28, this does not guarantee its applicability in clinical scenarios.

Our study has some limitations that need to be addressed in future studies. While our study focused on the role of ZEB1 in GBC cell proliferation and migration, it did not explore its effects on other aspects of GBC biology, such as cellular autophagy, angiogenesis, and immune responses. This study has not yet validated the direct transcriptional regulation of ZEB1 by evodiamine, and future experiments are needed to further elucidate the mechanism.

Conclusion

In our experiments, we discovered that evodiamine exhibited a potent inhibitory effect on the proliferation and metastasis of GBC cells. Mechanistically, this inhibition was attributed to its ability to decrease the expression of ZEB1, which in turn regulated the PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and subsequently influenced the metastatic behavior of cancer cells. Furthermore, our findings also revealed that evodiamine not only inhibited proliferation but also induced apoptosis in GBC cells (Figure. 7). Consequently, our results collectively indicate that evodiamine holds promise as a phytocompound with potential therapeutic applications for treating GBC. However, it is important to note that further research is necessary before clinical trials can be initiated to fully evaluate its efficacy and safety in humans.

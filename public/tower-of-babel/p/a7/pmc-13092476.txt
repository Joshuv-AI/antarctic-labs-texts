Dataset of dual-RNA sequencing of Ralstonia syzygii subsp. celebesensis strain A2 HR MARDI during infection on its host Musa acuminata
Authors: Rafidah Badrun, Muhammad Fairus Mohd Yusof, Abdul Munir Abd Murad, Izwan Bharudin, Nur Sulastri Jaffar, Rozeita Laboh, Norliza Abu Bakar, Lau Han Yih, Farah Diba Abu Bakar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractRalstonia syzygii subsp. celebesensis is a phytopathogenic bacterium responsible for Banana Blood Disease in most cultivated banana varieties. Since 2007, this disease has severely affected banana production in Malaysia, reducing annual yields from approximately 500,000 metric tonnes to about 200,000 metric tonnes. In this study, we present high quality transcriptome data of R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis strain A2 HR MARDI and banana (var. Tanduk) during infection by utilizing dual RNA-sequencing technology. Samples from infected banana pseudostems were collected at four time points (0, 6, 24 and 48 h post-infection) and subjected to RNA sequencing using the Illumina HiSeq™ 2000 platform. High quality reads obtained were then mapped to the corresponding reference genomes using HISAT2 v2.1.0. A total of 47,707 transcripts were generated after mapping against banana reference genome, while 4632 transcripts generated from R. syzygii subsp. celebensensis genome. BLASTx annotation resulted in the identification of 47,649 banana transcripts and 4615 R. syzygii subsp. celebensensis transcripts. GO analysis conducted with Blast2GO functionally classified 39,581 banana transcripts and 3513 R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis transcripts, with multiple GO terms assigned to individual gene products across the three GO categories. While differential expression analysis revealed 1088 - 2127 and 539 - 631 total differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the host and pathogen, respectively, at 6, 24 and 48-hours post-infection compared to the control. The dual RNA-seq dataset provides insights into putative pathogen virulence factors and banana defense-related genes, supporting improved disease management strategies.Keywords: Ralstonia syzygii subsp. celebesensis, Banana, Dual RNA sequencing, Transcriptome, Gene expression

Background

Bacterial wilt disease caused by Ralstonia syzygii subsp. celebesensis is a serious wilt disease of bananas. The disease originated from the southern region of Indonesia [1], first reported in Malaysia’s banana plantations after a severe flood occurred in 2007 [2]. The virulence mechanism in R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis and interaction with the host (banana) currently has not been fully carried out. Available published data at present remain insufficient to elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying the host–pathogen interaction especially in the pathogen and banana. To gain insight into the molecular drivers of this bacteria pathogenicity and banana early defence mechanism, we carried out bulk dual RNA sequencing on banana plants infected by the pathogen in several time points which includes 0, 6, 24 and 48-hour post-infection. The information provided in this dataset will be particularly valuable for researches interested in aiming early time points mechanism during bacteria pathogenicity due to fast and rapid infection caused by plant bacteria and to aim for early defence response in plants. The information gained in this transcriptome study ultimately can be used to uncover the solution to this disease and can offer new opportunities for banana plant protection and improvement programs against the disease.

A total of 47,707 transcripts were assembled against the banana reference genome, while 4632 transcripts were generated from the R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis genome. Of these, 47,649 coconut transcripts and 4615 R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis transcripts were successfully annotated using the BLASTx program. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis performed on the annotated transcripts assigned GO terms to 39,581 banana sequences (Biological Processes = 19,791; Molecular Functions = 6597; Cellular Components = 13,193) (Fig. 2). While for R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis, 3513 transcripts received GO assignments (Biological Processes = 1872; Molecular Functions = 471; Cellular Components = 1170) (Fig. 3). By simultaneously profiling global gene expression in both the host and the pathogen using dual RNA-seq, this study provides the first molecular resource describing the interactions between banana and local strain of R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis.

A comparison of banana (host) gene expression profiles between time points when compared to control (0 h), it is shown that for comparison 0 vs 6 h post-infection, 885 genes were up-regulated and 1242 genes were down-regulated and for comparison of 0 vs 24 h and 48 h, 715 and 693 were up-regulated while 518 and 395 were down-regulated respectively. Otherwise, for gene expression comparison of the pathogen revealed that bacterial infections on the host resulted in increased and decreased expression levels of genes. For comparison of 0 vs 6 h post-infection, 294 genes were up-regulated and 245 genes were down regulated. In comparison of 0 vs. 24 h and 48 h, 297 and 274 were up-regulated while 274 and 357 were down-regulated, respectively (see NSupplementary Data 1 for further information). Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

RNA extraction and library preparation

Total RNA was extracted from inoculated banana plant (2 months old) pseudo stem came from 4 different time-points. The RNA extraction was conducted using RNAeasy Plant Mini kit from Qiagen. The total amount and purity of the RNAs were assessed using agarose gel and a Nanodrop ND-1000 spectrophotometer (NanoDrop Technologies, Thermo Fisher, Wilmington, DE, U.S.A.). To ensure RNA integrity, an assessment was performed using the Agilent 2100 Bioanalyzer System (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA). The analysis yielded a satisfactory RIN (RNA Integrity Number) exceeding 7.5, signifying high-quality nucleic acids meeting the stringent library construction experiment requirements. The RNA quantity was also deemed sufficient for at least two library construction attempts. RNA isolated from each timepoints of infection was pooled and utilized for RNA-seq library preparation. Briefly, the library construction involved mRNA enrichment, RNA fragmentation, and random priming, followed by cDNA synthesis incorporating dUTP. Subsequent steps included A-tailing, adaptor ligation, dUTP-strand degradation and PCR amplification.

RNA sequencing and data processing

Libraries with different indices were multiplexed and loaded on an Illumina HiSeq instrument according to manufacturer’s instructions (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) and sequencing was performed by using 2 × 150 paired-end (PE) configuration on the HiSeq instrument. The sequences were then processed and analysed. The quality of raw sequencing reads was assessed by FastQC v0.11.8 [3]. Pre-processing of the data was performed using Trimmomatic v0.38 [4]. Clean reads of each sample were mapped simultaneously to reference genomes of both host and pathogen: Musa acuminata subsp. Malaccensis (RefSeq accession: GCF_000313855.2) together with the chloroplast complete genome (GenBank accession: HF677508.1), as well as R.syzygii subsp. celebesensis A2-HR MARDI (Genbank accession: GCA_002012345.1) using HISAT2 v2.1.0 [5].

Functional annotation and differential expression analysis

BLASTx annotation of the assembled banana and R. syzygii subsp. celebesensis transcriptomes was performed against the corresponding proteome databases using a maximum E-value threshold of 1 × 10⁻5. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis was subsequently carried out using Blast2GO [6] for each transcriptome independently. Analyses of reference gene expression and differential expression were carried out using Cufflinks v2.2.1 [7] and Cuffdiff [8] of the same version, respectively. Clean reads mapped to genes annotated in the reference genome were quantified and normalised to a gene expression unit termed as Fragments Per Kilobase of gene per Million fragments mapped (FPKM). Genes with FPKM = 0 were considered not expressed, while genes with FPKM > 0 were considered expressed genes. The value of expression is achieved by calculating the values of fragments per kilo base of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM) (total_exon_fragments / mapped_reads (millions) × exon_length (kb) [9]. It is an expression unit which refers to clean reads mapped to genes annotated in the reference genome, then quantified and normalised.

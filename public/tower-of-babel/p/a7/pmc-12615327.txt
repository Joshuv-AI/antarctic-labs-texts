Association between transitioning to a dolutegravir-based regimen and risk of incident hypertension in adults with HIV in West Africa: a multicentre target trial emulation study
Authors: Romain Millot, Thierry Tiendrebeogo, Oliver Ezechi, Armel Poda, Albert Minga, Karen Malateste, Igho Ofotokun, Didier K. Ekouevi, Antoine Jaquet, IeDEA West Africa Collaboration
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundAlthough dolutegravir (DTG) has been linked to metabolic disturbances-especially among women and people of African descent – its effect on hypertension (HTN) remains uncertain. We assessed the impact of switching to DTG on the risk of HTN among people living with HIV (PLHIV) using target-trial emulation in West Africa.MethodsWe analysed routine data from the International Epidemiologic Databases to Evaluate AIDS in West Africa (IeDEA-WA), restricting to clinics with systematic blood pressure (BP) collection: Nigeria, Burkina Faso and Côte d’Ivoire. Eligible participants were adults (≥18 years) on ART with at least one follow-up visit including BP after site-level DTG introduction. ART initiators and patients with prevalent HTN were excluded. We emulated a target trial to estimate the effect of switching to DTG on the risk of developing hypertension (HTN), using weighted logistic regression. In the intention to treat analysis, participants were followed regardless of subsequent treatment changes. In the per-protocol analysis, follow-up was censored at the first deviation from the assigned strategy. In addition, a two-slope mixed linear model was applied to assess changes in systolic (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) before and after the switch to DTG.FindingsAmong 15 485 actively followed PLHIV between January 1st, 2016 and January 1st, 2023, 9730 PLHIV (72.6% women–27.4% men) met eligibility. Among them, 9689 remained on non-DTG regimens and 7839 switched to DTG; over 4 years, 557 and 879 incident hypertension events occurred, respectively. DTG use was associated to a 19% higher risk of incident HTN (Risk ratio = 1.19, 95% CI: 1.08–1.32) in intention-to-treat and an 42% increase (1.42, 1.20–1.68) in per-protocol. 11482 participants switching to DTG were eligible for the second analysis. Before DTG transition, SBP and DBP increased by 0.38 mmHg/year [95% CI 0.26–0.50] and 0.39 mmHg/year [0.32–0.47], respectively. After DTG, slopes increased to 1.56 mmHg/year [1.34–1.78] and 1.20 mmHg/year [1.06–1.35].InterpretationOur findings suggest that transitioning to a DTG-based regimen was associated with an increase in BP and a modest elevation in the estimated HTN risk, supporting integrated cardiovascular care within HIV programs in West Africa. Findings should be interpreted cautiously given the observational design, potential residual confounding, and the lack of standardized BP measurements and lifestyle-related data.FundingResearch reported in this publication is supported by the U.S. National Institutes of Health, National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, the Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health & Human Development, the National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases, the National Institute on Drug Abuse, the National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism, the Fogarty International Center and the National Cancer Institute.

Research in context

Evidence before this study

We searched PubMed for studies published until April 30, 2025, using search terms dolutegravir, Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitor, blood pressure, hypertension; and references listed in the identified papers. Dolutegravir (DTG) is now the preferred first-line antiretroviral therapy in most low- and middle-income countries; trials such as NAMSAL and ADVANCE consistently show greater weight gain with DTG - especially in African women – raising concerns about downstream cardiometabolic risk. Evidence directly linking DTG to hypertension remains limited and inconsistent (higher early cardiovascular disease in the RESPOND cohort vs no association in a Swiss cohort target trial emulation). Importantly, data from African settings remain scarce, and no large cohort study has evaluated the effect of transitioning to DTG on blood pressure and incident hypertension in West Africa.

Added value of this study

Using routinely collected data from three West African countries, we emulated a target trial to estimate the impact of DTG on blood pressure changes and incident hypertension. Our findings show a modest increase in the 4-year risk of hypertension among individuals switched to DTG compared to those who remained on non-dolutegravir-based ART. In addition, using a stratified mixed-effects model, we observed that systolic blood pressure increased more steeply over time among women and individuals with higher baseline BMI. We also found that the type of ART regimen prior to the switch influenced the magnitude of blood pressure changes.

Implications of all the available evidence

As DTG has become the dominant first-line ART backbone in sub-Saharan Africa, including in West Africa where it accounts for over 80% of new ART prescriptions, understanding its cardiometabolic safety profile is a public health priority. Our findings highlight the need to monitor blood pressure in patients switching to DTG, particularly in populations already at risk for hypertension. These results support the integration of cardiovascular risk screening into HIV care services and call for further research on the long-term metabolic consequences of DTG, especially in underrepresented populations. However, these findings should be interpreted in light of the study's observational design, potential residual confounding, and the absence of standardized blood pressure measurements and lifestyle-related data which may affect the precision of our estimates.

Introduction

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) has extended life expectancy of people living with HIV (PLHIV), who are now facing new health challenges, especially an increase in non-communicable diseases (NCDs) like cardiovascular diseases (CVDs).1,2 HIV infection itself is associated with a heightened cardiovascular risk, partly due to persistent inflammation and immune activation, contributing to conditions such as hypertension (HTN), stroke, and myocardial infarction.3,4 In addition, certain antiretroviral drugs carry metabolic side effects, further compounding the cardiovascular risks associated with HIV treatment.5

In 2018, the World Health Organization (WHO) recommended dolutegravir (DTG), an integrase strand transfer inhibitors (INSTIs), as a first-line therapy for HIV treatment based on clinical trials showing superior viral suppression and a high genetic barrier compared to prior recommended regimens.6,7 DTG's affordability, availability in generic form, and once-daily dosing make it a particularly suitable option in resource-limited settings. By 2021, DTG-based regimen had become the main first-line regimen in most West African countries.8

In sub-Saharan Africa, where healthcare systems are heavily burdened by a high prevalence of HIV, there is also an ongoing epidemiological shift towards NCDs, especially CVDs, rising rapidly alongside persistent infectious diseases.9,10 CVD-related mortality occurs at earlier ages in this region compared to high-income countries, with limited HTN screening and treatment identified as major drivers of this risk.11,12 West Africa is at the forefront of this double burden, with some of the fastest growing cardiovascular mortality on the continent alongside a high HIV burden – particularly in Nigeria, which has the second most largest number of people living with HIV in Africa.9,13

Recent evidence has highlighted potential metabolic effects of DTG, with trials such as NAMSAL and ADVANCE reporting greater weight gain among DTG users.14,15 Weight gain is a recognized risk factor for HTN, a primary driver of CVDs. Observational studies on the association between DTG and CVDs have yielded mixed results, spurring ongoing debate regarding optimal methodological approaches for causal inference.16 The RESPOND cohort reported an increased CVD risk associated with INSTIs during the initial two years of treatment, whereas Surial et al., using target trial emulation in the Swiss HIV cohort, found no such association.17,18 Byonanebye et al. also applied target trial emulation and identified a higher incidence of HTN with INSTI use in the RESPOND cohort, emphasizing the need for further data.19

Considering that HTN frequently goes undiagnosed in sub-Saharan Africa, assessing the impact of transitioning to DTG on HTN risk in this population is critical.20 This study seeks to evaluate, through target trial emulation, the impact of switching to DTG on the risk of incident HTN among PLHIV in West Africa.

Methods

Population and study design

Cohort description

Routinely collected data from the International Epidemiologic Databases to Evaluate AIDS in West Africa (IeDEA-WA) cohort were used for this study. IeDEA-WA is a global research consortium that gathers data from 18 HIV care clinics across Benin, Burkina Faso, Côte d’Ivoire, Ghana, Mali, Nigeria, Senegal, and Togo (http://iedea-wa.org/). The generated database comprises over 50 000 adult PLHIV enrolled since 2004 with a median age of 36 years [IQR 30–43] at ART initiation, 64.5% being females. Participating clinics contribute routine follow-up data to a cohort of adults living with HIV who meet the following inclusion criteria: documented HIV infection, age ≥18 years, and receipt of ART. Anthropometric parameters—including weight, height, and blood pressure (BP)—are typically measured by paramedical staff prior to each medical consultation. Systolic (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) are usually recorded in millimetres of mercury (mmHg) using automated BP monitors. Data were analysed up to the administrative closure of the cohort on May 15, 2023. For this analysis, only clinics with systematic routine blood pressure data collection were included—that is, sites where over 98% of patients had at least one BP measurement and BP was recorded in more than 82% of clinical visits.

In accordance with WHO recommendations, West African countries began implementing a programmatic shift in 2019, transitioning from standard triple ART regimens—most commonly based on nevirapine (NVP), efavirenz (EFV), or a protease inhibitor (PI)—to dolutegravir-based therapy.8,21 As this rollout was standardized across programs, the accompanying drugs were predefined: lamivudine and tenofovir. Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF) was not part of national treatment guidelines during the study period, and its use remained extremely limited in West Africa.

This manuscript adheres to the STROBE guidelines for observational studies.

Target trial emulation

A target trial emulation aims to replicate the design of a randomised controlled trial using observational data, by explicitly defining the trial protocol (eligibility criteria, treatment strategies, follow-up, and outcome). This approach helps reduce multiple sources of bias, including channelling bias, immortal time bias, and time-varying confounding, by synchronizing treatment assignment, eligibility assessment, and follow-up start (time zero).

A new trial was emulated at the start of each calendar month, and all individuals meeting eligibility criteria at that time were included. Participants could contribute to multiple emulated trials and to different treatment arms across trials, depending on their exposure status at each time point. Detailed emulated and trial framework is shown in Appendix-1.

Eligibility criteria

Eligibility criteria for the target trial were the same as the ones of the cohort (being an adult with an HIV infection under ART). Moreover, patients were required to have at least one follow up visit with BP measurement after DTG introduction in the study site and not being ART initiators. Patients with prevalent HTN (SBP ≥140 mmHg or DBP ≥90 mmHg at the first visit) were not included.

Primary outcomes

The outcome of this trial was hypertension, defined as sustained elevated blood pressure, with at least two consecutive measurements showing either systolic BP (SBP) ≥140 mmHg or diastolic BP (DBP) ≥90 mmHg, in accordance with the WHO guideline.22 Because BP was measured during routine HIV care visits—typically every 6 to 12 months—two consecutive elevated values could be several months apart, reflecting real-world follow-up practices.

Primary exposures

Participants, regardless of their actual treatment, were assigned to either change their ART regimen to a regimen based on DTG (Switch group) or initiate/remain on an ART without DTG (Control group). Due to the programmatic rollout of dolutegravir in West Africa, the transition involved switching patients from any non-DTG-based ART regimen to the combination of tenofovir, lamivudine, and DTG. All individuals were followed up from treatment assignment until hypertension diagnosis, death, lost to follow up or after database closure.

Covariates

Covariates considered in the analyses included demographic and clinical factors identified as potential confounders due to their known or suspected associations with blood pressure and hypertension risk. These variables were: age (continuous variable, in years), sex (categorical variable: male vs. female), body mass index (BMI) (categorical, calculated as weight in kg divided by height in meters squared and divided as specified by WHO definition), prior ART regimen (categorical: efavirenz-based, nevirapine-based, protease inhibitor-based, or other), time on ART (continuous, defined by the number of year between present and date of first ART uptake), and calendar time (defined by the year during which BP measures were taken) was also included in the emulation trial.

Two slopes linear mixed model

Through a second analytical approach we aimed to evaluate BP changes before and after switching to a DTG-based ART regimen. Eligibility criteria for this analysis were having switched from a non-DTG ART regimen to a DTG-based regimen and having at least one recorded BP measurement over the follow-up period (between 1st January 2016 and the database date of closure for each site). Patients who initiated ART with DTG were not included to minimize the potential bias introduced by a "return to health" effect even though it has not been well described for BP. This effect refers to the weight gain often observed after initiating ART in PLHIV, reflecting the reversal of HIV-related wasting as health improves.23

Statistical analysis

Target trial emulation

To estimate the causal effect of switching to dolutegravir on the risk of hypertension, we adopted a target trial emulation framework, implemented using the TrialEmulation R package.24 This approach constructs a sequence of trials in observational data to emulate a randomised experiment. By aligning eligibility assessment, treatment assignment, and follow-up start, it minimizes biases that typically affect longitudinal observational data in clinical research. A weighted pooled logistic regression model was used to estimate the hazard of hypertension, approximating a marginal structural Cox model. Follow-up time was modelled using restricted cubic splines to capture non-linear effects.25,26 Non-random assignment of treatments strategies in the emulated trials was accounted for by adjusting the model for variables identified as having a potential influence on DTG prescription including age, sex, previous ART, HIV viral load, BMI, HIV clinic and calendar time.21

Weighting

Inverse probability of censoring weighting (IPCW) was applied to adjust for loss to follow-up and death, based on a logistic regression model including age, sex, and centre as key predictors of retention and survival commonly identified in HIV cohorts in West Africa.27,28 In the intention-to-treat analysis, participants were followed regardless of treatment changes. In the per-protocol analysis, participants were censored at the time of deviation from their assigned strategy. To address potential selection bias introduced by artificial censoring, we calculated additional inverse probability weights reflecting the probability of remaining on the assigned regimen. These adherence weights included age, centre, BMI, prior ART, and calendar time. All weights were truncated at the 1st and 99th percentiles to reduce the influence of extreme values.

Piecewise linear mixed model

To examine changes in blood pressure (BP) before and after switching to DTG, we used a piecewise linear mixed model with two slopes. The change point was fixed at the date of the switch to estimate the average annual change in BP separately for the pre-switch period (defined as the three years prior to DTG initiation) and the post-switch period (defined as the two years following DTG initiation). Covariates included time (relative to the switch to DTG), sex, age and BMI at the switch, prior ART regimen, and study site. Random intercepts were included to account for individual-level variation. Modelling was based on complete data, given the low number of missing data expected for blood pressure and the other explanatory variables studied. To assess the adequacy of the model, residual homoscedasticity and normality were checked graphically.

All analyses were carried out using R software version 4.3.2 using the following packages: lme4,29 ggplot2,30 mfp,31 TrialEmulation.32

Ethical approval

The IeDEA-WA Collaboration obtained authorization from the Ethics committee “Comité de Protection des Personnes Sud-Ouest et Outre-mer III” in Bordeaux, France (IRB00012788) to collect, merge and analyse de-identified data from involved HIV clinics in West Africa. Moreover, each participating site obtained authorization from its National Ethics committee (Cote d’Ivoire: IRB00009111; Burkina Faso: IRB00004738; Nigeria [NIMR]: IRB00003224). Written informed consent requirements were deferred to the local institutional review boards. The analysis only used de-identified data collected from routine clinical care.

Role of the Funding source

The funder of the study had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, or writing of the report. Authors had full access to the data. RM, AJ and TT were responsible for the decision to submit for publication.

Results

Blood pressure changes

Among the 15 485 patients actively followed in the IeDEA West Africa cohort when DTG was introduced, 11 493 (74.2%) patients switched to a DTG based ART during the study period. Of those who switched, 11 had no BP measurement and were not included (Appendix-2). The 11 482 patients included had 78 993 BP measures over the study period which corresponds to a median of 1.9 [IQR 1.5–2.2] measures per patient per year before the switch and 1.9 [1.3–2.4] after. PLHIV included had a median age of 45 years [IQR 39–51] at the time of switch and 68.9% were women. Most of them were from Nigeria (45.3%), followed by Burkina Faso (34.0%) and Cote d’Ivoire (20.7%). Just over half had a normal BMI (51.4%). Before the switch most of them were on an Efavirenz (EFV) based regimen (73.9%), the others had either a Nevirapine (12.4%) or Protease inhibitor (13.7%) based ART.

The crude, two slopes, linear mixed model showed a mean increase in SBP of 0.38 mmHg/year [0.26–0.50] before the switch and then of 1.56 mmHg/year [1.34–1.78] following the switch to DTG. The mean increase in DBP shifted from 0.39 mmHg/year [0.32–0.47] before the switch to 1.20 mmHg/year [1.06–1.35] following the switch to DTG [Table 3]. The increase of both SBP and DBP was more pronounced in women after the switch in comparison to men's (p-value of the interaction <0.01 for SBP and DBP after the switch). The type of ART regimen and BMI at the time of switching to DTG also appeared to impact changes in BP post switch to DTG (p-value of the interaction <0.01) [Fig. 3]. Discussion

This study assessed the impact of switching to a DTG based regimen on BP among PLHIV undergoing ART in West Africa. A modest increase in the risk of incident HTN was found among DTG users compared to non-users in our emulation trial analysis. Further analysis reveal that patients switching to DTG experienced a faster increase in blood pressure over a two-year period post-switch compared to the pre-switch period.

The study's multi-country design provides robust data from routine BP monitoring with minimal missing information. Notably, the use of clinical trial emulation allowed for a nuanced approach to mitigate selection and survival biases inherent in observational cohorts. This approach aligns with standards previously adopted by other major studies, such as CAUSAL-LAB and the Swiss HIV Cohort, which have underscored its importance in observational data interpretation.18,33 Our findings support earlier studies, including the NAMSAL trial, a Zimbabwe cohort, and the RESPOND study, which have now all identified increased hypertension risks associated with DTG and INSTI-based regimens, particularly among women.15,19,34 While pooled SPRING, SINGLE and FLAMINGO randomised controlled trials, as well as NEAT post hoc analysis did not identify an increase in BP with DTG, these trials predominantly enrolled white male participants, included smaller sample sizes and had shorter follow up.35,36 These trials and our study address related but context-specific questions and together contribute to a more nuanced and comprehensive understanding of DTG's impact across diverse populations.

Additionally, patients previously on efavirenz- or protease inhibitor-based regimens demonstrated higher blood pressure increases post-DTG switch, potentially linked to prior weight-suppressive effects of efavirenz, though mechanisms related to prior protease inhibitor use remain less clear. While this difference has been clearly established in terms of weight gain, its impact on blood pressure remains unexplored and should be a focus of future research.37,38

At the same time, mechanistic explanations for HTN associated with DTG remain speculative. Weight gain is a well-established risk factor for HTN and has been associated with DTG uptake.37,39 Secondary analysis from the ADVANCE trial showed that adjustment for BMI mitigated the relationship between ART and SBP.40 It should nonetheless be acknowledge that the rapid onset of hypertension (detectable within the first year) suggests possible alternate mechanisms beyond weight-related atherosclerosis.39,41 Findings from Byonanebye et al. suggest that the excess risk of hypertension at 12 months persisted even after adjusting for BMI change, indicating that weight gain might not fully explain the association. Other hypotheses, including potential activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system or lipid dysregulation, though neither has yet been substantiated in clinical data.42 Further mechanistic studies and mediation analyses—ideally integrating clinical, metabolic, and biomarkers—are needed to better elucidate the pathways through which DTG may influence blood pressure, and to inform appropriate prevention strategies.

This study also acknowledges several limitations inherent to its observational design. Despite adjustments, residual confounding factors cannot be entirely excluded. Blood pressure measurements were neither standardized nor taken at pre-defined intervals, introducing a potential measurement bias. Additionally, while the study's approach minimized biases by excluding patients with abnormal BP at the first visit, our estimates may be impacted by the absence of medical history and treatment data in the cohort. Nonetheless, we expect this limitation to have limited impact in our setting, where less than 5% of patient diagnosed with HTN have a well-controlled BP under treatment.43 It should also be acknowledge that other known cardiovascular risk factors—such as tobacco and alcohol use, diet, and physical activity—were not available in our database. An overrepresentation of these factors in the DTG group can't be excluded but we have no particular elements to support this hypothesis as no particular guidance was provided to clinical sites or healthcare providers on prescribing DTG based according to pre-existing cardiovascular risk factors and key driver for switching to a DTG-based regimen during this phase was essentially based on gender.21 Moreover, the longer follow-up observed in the DTG group, not fully addressed by IPC weighting, may have increased hypertension detection and contributed to the observed risk difference. Finally, although the risk of channelling bias is mitigated by the programmatic and large-scale rollout of DTG across West Africa and the use of IPTW, we cannot fully rule out unmeasured prescribing biases.

Our findings highlight the importance of integrating cardiovascular screening and care in HIV programs for low- and middle-income settings. Even a modest increase in HTN incidence could have substantial population-level consequences in West-Africa, where the burden of both HIV and untreated hypertension is high. The results of the ADVANCE study carried out in South Africa show that 95% of incident cases of HTN were controlled after appropriate treatment, underlining the effectiveness of these measures.44 Precise details of the tolerability profile are essential for developing treatment strategies that optimise benefits while minimising risks, particularly in the light of recent data on increasing resistance to DTG.45 These findings do not challenge the well-established virological benefits of DTG based on its high efficacy, high resistance barrier and favourable cost-effectiveness, but rather aim to complement it by contributing to a more nuanced understanding of its safety profile in real-world African settings. As such, early identification and management of elevated blood pressure should be considered an important component of long-term care for PLHIV on DTG.

In conclusion, an increased risk of HTN was reported in this present study among PLHIV switching to DTG in West Africa, contributing further to the need of HTN care in HIV programs in low- and middle-income settings, highly affected both by the HIV epidemic and CVD's. While DTG's virological benefits are undeniable, long-term studies are essential to better address its potential impact on CVD's. Therefore, our findings advocate for additional monitoring of DTG's impact on cardiovascular health and encourage exploration of alternative ART with favourable safety profiles for broader treatment options in resource-limited settings and among individuals at particular risk of CVD's.

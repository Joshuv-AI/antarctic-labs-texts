Sclerodermiform Lupus Erythematosus – A Rare Entity
Authors: N. R Vignesh, N Ashok Kumar, C Balakumaran, P Sowmya
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We present a case of a 33-year-old male construction worker who presented with multiple painful ulcerated lesions all over the body for 1 year. The lesions initially started as hard, painful raised lesions on the chest, which later ulcerated at the centre and darkened along the edges with progressive thickening and gradually progressed in number to involve the abdomen, back, face, and proximal limbs. A positive history of low-grade intermittent fever, cough with expectoration, arthralgia, and myalgia is present.

On examination, multiple indurated hyperpigmented plaques with central punched-out necrotic ulceration were noted over the trunk [Figure 1], and bilateral proximal upper and lower limbs. Few ulcers healed with central atrophy, but the surrounding skin continued to be indurated with dyspigmentation [Figure 2a-d]. Differentials of cutaneous tuberculosis, Vasculitis, Hansen’s disease, autoimmune connective tissue disease, and sarcoidosis were considered.

On evaluation, acid fast bacilli (AFB) smear, catridge based nucleic acid amplification test from Sputum and Bronchioalveolar lavage, and interferon gamma release assay were negative. Computed tomography chest revealed a calcified granuloma in the right upper lobe and normal lung parenchyma with right paratracheal and axillary lymphadenopathy. Lymph node fine needle aspiration cytology revealed few polymorphs and histiocytes with no evidence of granuloma. Slit skin smear for AFB was negative. Serum calcium and angiotensin-converting enzyme levels were normal.

Skin biopsy revealed epidermal atrophy, dermal melanophores [Figure 3a], high uptake of eccrine glands [Figure 3b], dermal sclerosis pushing down the subcutis [Figure 4a and b], vasculitis of deep dermal vasculature, predominantly lobular lymphocytic panniculitis [Figure 5a and b]. Serology revealed elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate and C-reactive protein, C4 hypocomplementemia, Anti-nuclear antibody (ANA) positivity on IIF showing 3+ homogenous cytoplasmic and 3+ homogenous Rim patterns in primate and hep2, respectively. Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody, Anti-ds DNA, direct Coombs were negative. On ANA profile analysis, Anti-Scl70 was strongly positive and Anti-Smith, Anti-Ro52, Anti nRNP/Sm, Anti-Nucleosome antibodies were positive. A 24-hour urine protein and urine spot Protein-Creatinine Ratio were elevated. EULAR/American College of Rheumatology criteria score of 17 was obtained suggesting systemic involvement. Nephrologist opined for renal biopsy which was denied by the patient. A diagnosis of sclerodermiform lupus erythematosus (SDLE) was made and patient started on systemic steroids along with mycophenolate mofetil 500 mg twice daily and hydroxychloroquine 200 mg twice daily, with favourable response and was referred to the rheumatologist for further follow-up.

SDLE, a rare type of overlap syndrome, presents with features of Chronic cutaneous lupus erythematosus and morphia at the same site, first described by Umbert et al., Most were reported in females; however, few male cases have also been reported.[12] Lupus panniculitis usually presents with subcutaneous indurated nodules and plaques and can rarely ulcerate as seen in this current case.[34] The investigation of choice is deep skin biopsy with characteristic features of dermal sclerosis and predominantly lobular panniculitis with lymphocytic infiltrates. ANA is positive with no specific pattern of involvement, however, anti-Scl70, anti-Ro52, anti-Sm seem to be involved in most reported cases.[12] In this patient, clinical findings of chronic fever, arthralgia, indurated plaques, histolofd gical findings of dermal sclerosis and lobular panniculitis with vasculitis and serology of ANA positivity, hypocomplementemia were all consistent with the diagnosis of SDLE. However, male sex and presence of disseminated cutaneous ulceration make this presentation extremely rare. This case is highlighted for the uncommon clinical presentation, requirement of intense work up and long-term follow-up to prevent systemic progression.

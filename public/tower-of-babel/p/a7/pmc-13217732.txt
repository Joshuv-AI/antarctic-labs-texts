Improving student resilience through Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) therapy by strengthening self-awareness
Authors: Nur Kur’ani, Istikoma Istikoma, Halida Halida, Nandang Sugiarto, Nur Istiqomah
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractResilience is an important capacity that enables students to adapt to academic stress and social pressures. This research aims to determine the effectiveness of self-awareness–based NLP therapy in improving students’ resilience. This research is a quantitative study using a quasi-experimental design with a control group and an experimental group. The data collection methods included a resilience scale to measure students’ academic and social resilience, as well as self-awareness and resilience scales administered before and after the intervention to evaluate the outcomes of the NLP intervention. The subjects of this research were students with low levels of resilience, consisting of 146 female students and 124 male students. The results showed that self-awareness–based NLP intervention was effective in increasing students’ academic and social resilience. The conclusion of this research is that students’ thoughts about their lives have the potential to influence their resilience, and NLP is beneficial in fostering positive thinking in students so that they can better understand themselves and channel negative thoughts and emotions into positive actions.Keywords: Neuro linguistic programming (NLP), Resilience, Mental health, Self-awareness, Positive mindset

Introduction

In recent years, the challenges of addressing adolescent mental health have increased, exacerbated by social changes and academic pressures [1–3]. The Indonesian Child Protection Commission (KPAI) released data showing that throughout 2022, there have been at least 226 cases of physical and psychological violence, including bullying, which continues to increase to this day [4]. This is further supported by data from JPPI, which states that throughout 2024, there were 23 cases of violence in schools [5]. Interviews with high school students from July 28 to August 29, 2025, revealed that 9 students experienced physical and verbal abuse from both friends and parents, were ostracized from their peer group, and had their parents’ names mocked. In addition to the above data, BPS also reported that bullying often occurs among teenagers aged 12 to 17 [6]. Based on the above explanation, high school students in West Kalimantan fall into the category of adolescents who are still adapting to problems that can lead to mental health disorders; therefore, resilience must continue to be developed in adolescents. The resilience of these adolescents is influenced by empathy, self-awareness, goals, aspirations, self-concept, school climate, the role of parents, family, community, and peers [7–9]. The results of the study show that some adolescents have moderate levels of resilience, with 349 (65.4%) students, high resilience, with 184 (34%) students, and low resilience, with 7 (1.3%) students [10, 11]. Resilience is the ability of students to face, prevent, minimize, and eliminate the adverse effects of unpleasant conditions or turn difficult life circumstances into something that can be dealt with [2, 12].

Amidst the complex challenges of the modern era, students face various pressures, ranging from high academic demands, complex social dynamics, to uncertainty about the future [13–15]. These conditions put their psychological well-being at risk and require strong adaptive abilities. Resilient students are not only able to cope with failure and stress, but also tend to have better mental health, more stable academic performance, and greater readiness to face life after school [16]. Therefore, developing effective interventions to increase student resilience is an important agenda in the world of education and psychology.

Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) is a pseudoscientific approach to exploring the human mind [17, 18]. Here, the neurological patterns of the human mind can be changed based on environmental conditions. This sensory information can be transformed into a thought process. The thought process activates the neurological system that influences physiology, emotions, and behavior [19]. NLP can overcome boredom in learning, lack of confidence in interacting with other students, both individually and in groups, anxiety about school exams, low motivation to learn, procrastination, and many other problems [20]. In addition to increasing self-confidence, NLP can also help teenagers strengthen their beliefs and control themselves and their emotions [21]. NLP can also help students change unproductive mindsets and behaviors, thereby increasing self-confidence, academic performance, and social skills [22]. In addition, NLP can reduce anxiety and increase student resilience [23, 24]. This is supported by previous research [20] which states that NLP in learning can also make it easier for individuals to think rationally and have appropriate feelings. Additionally, NLP can reduce anxiety [9] and increase student resilience [10]. NLP therapy can also improve aspects of resilience such as optimism, self-efficacy, and reaching out, with the main goal being to bring about significant change in a person’s choice of course of action [25].

Self-awareness also plays an important role in helping students understand and manage their emotions so that they are more capable of coping with the social pressures they face both at school and at home [26, 27]. Self-awareness is the ability to recognize one’s own potential, thereby enabling one to adapt to the environment and make the right decisions [28]. The integration of NLP with self-awareness is expected to create individuals who are more resilient and confident.

Previous studies have explored various methods for building student resilience. Interventions based on Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) [29], mindfulness training [30], and psychological approaches have been shown to make positive contributions. On the other hand, Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP), an approach that focuses on the relationship between neurological processes, language, and behavioral patterns, has also shown its potential in various therapeutic contexts. Current evidence shows that NLP has been successfully applied to increase self-confidence, overcome phobias, and change negative mindsets. Several studies have indicated that NLP techniques such as reframing (changing perspectives), anchoring (creating triggers for positive emotional states), and visualization can indirectly support components related to resilience [17–19].

Although these studies demonstrate the effectiveness of NLP for psychological variables, there is a significant research gap, where there is a lack of focus and specificity on resilience. Most NLP studies in the context of education more often examine variables such as learning motivation or test anxiety, rather than resilience as a whole construct. In addition, there are unidentified mechanisms. This study applies NLP as an intervention “package” without delving deeper into the specific mechanisms underlying changes in thoughts and behavior. Furthermore, the role of self-awareness, which is the ability to recognize and understand one’s own thoughts, emotions, and behavior patterns, is often only assumed to be a side effect, rather than a key variable that is deliberately reinforced and measured. In fact, psychologists agree that self-awareness is the foundation of emotional and cognitive regulation, which is at the core of resilience [31, 32].

The novelty of this study lies in the proposed integrative intervention model, whereby this study not only aims to prove that NLP can increase resilience, but also offers a more specific and in-depth proposition. This study proposes and tests a therapeutic framework in which NLP techniques are purposefully used to strengthen self-awareness as the primary pathway for building student resilience. In other words, its novelty lies in (1) Mechanism Specification, by positioning self-awareness not as a by-product, but as a fundamental intervention target. NLP techniques are selected and specifically designed to make students more aware of their “mental maps,” emotional triggers, and internal dialogues that have been on automatic pilot; (2) Structured Approach, this study goes beyond the random application of NLP techniques by designing a structured module that guides students from the stage of identifying thought patterns to the stage of conscious cognitive and emotional restructuring; (3) Theoretical Contribution: demonstrates that the effectiveness of NLP in the context of self-development can be significantly explained through an increase in an individual’s capacity for self-awareness.

Based on the gaps and novelties described above, this research is important because it offers a more precise and in-depth approach to equip students with the resilience skills that are essential for their present and future success. The purpose of this study is to explore how Neuro Linguistic Programming (NLP) focused on self-awareness can improve students’ resilience in everyday life. The results of this study can also serve as guidelines for relevant parties, including the government, in designing policies, developing practical measures, and formulating effective strategies to support students’ mental health. Based on this background, the research questions in this study are: (1) How does NLP therapy with self-awareness reinforcement increase resilience in facing academic and social pressures?; (2) How is the NLP intervention model based on self-awareness applied in the context of education?

Methods

This research design follows standard procedures for educational quantitative research [33], which uses a quasi-experimental design with a control group and an experimental group. The intervention consisted of a structured program grounded in Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) therapy combined with self-awareness reinforcement in improving student resilience. Neuro-Linguistic Programming is an approach that can show how to become the person you want to be. In this context, it means how someone can change an undesirable attitude into a new, ideal attitude. Neuro-Linguistic Programming is a set of tools and techniques to help deal with unpleasant mindsets and behaviors.

The research was conducted in accordance with the module procedures, which consisted of three series of sessions, such as training in self-awareness, resilience, and NLP practice. The procedures in this study consisted of several stages, namely:

The intervention was given to the experimental group by facilitators Nur Kur’ani as a psychology lecturer at Muhammadiyah University of Pontianak and Halida as a lecturer in guidance and counseling at Tanjungpura University. The intervention in the experimental group was carried out in three sessions over three days in each region (Sambas Regency, Sanggau Regency, and Pontianak City), with three meetings in each session. Meanwhile, the control group did not receive the intervention that is self-awareness-based NLP intervention during the research period. Students in the control group continued to participate in regular learning activities at school without receiving additional training related to resilience or self-awareness.

The subjects in this study consisted of 270 students in grades X, XI, and XII from several schools in West Kalimantan, namely SMAN 3 Tebas, SMAN 1 Tekarang, SMKN 1 Tekarang, SMAN 1 Sanggau, SMAN 2 Sanggau, SMAN 3 Sanggau, MAN 2 Pontianak, SMA 1 Muhammadiyah Pontianak, and SMKN 5 Pontianak. A total of 135 people were in the experimental group and 135 people were in the control group with the criteria being grade X, XI, XII students, both male and female, who had resilience problems such as low learning motivation, a tendency to procrastinate, and negative thoughts about themselves. The procedure for selecting subjects in this study was to use purposive sampling.

The research data was analyzed using quantitative and qualitative methods. Quantitative data were analyzed based on pretest and posttest scores in the experimental and control groups using a nonparametric test with the Mann-Whitney test, and were analyzes using descriptive and inferential statistical methods aligned whit the research objectives. Paired-sample t-tests were conducted to examine within-group change in resilience score following the intervention, while independent-sample t-tests were applied to compare resilience outcomes resilience outcomes between the experimental and control groups. There analyses were used to determine the effectiveness of the self-awareness-based Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) intervention in enhancing student resilience. The instrument used to measure resilience in this study was a resilience scale. After the intervention, the students’ resilience scores were compared between the two groups to assess the effectiveness of this intervention. Qualitative analysis was performed on data obtained from interviews and observations.

Result

Resilience is the ability of students to cope with, prevent, minimize, and eliminate the adverse effects of unpleasant conditions or transform difficult life circumstances into something that can be dealt with reasonably. In education, resilience is crucial for students to overcome academic stress and social pressure. One effective strategy for enhancing resilience is through Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) therapy with self-awareness reinforcement. The integration of Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) with self-awareness is expected to create resilient and confident individuals. The research results were obtained from several stages. The first stage was interviews with student representatives from the schools targeted for research. In addition, data was obtained from observations. After that, the research team provided psychoeducation on self-awareness and resilience as well as NLP practices to increase the students’ resilience. The results showed that increasing student resilience through NLP therapy with self-awareness reinforcement was effective in improving student resilience. This can be proven from the impressions and messages of students who participated in NLP activities, as well as the results of pre-tests and post-tests, which showed different data before and after NLP therapy was administered. NLP therapy to strengthen self-awareness is useful in the context of education to increase students’ resilience in facing academic obstacles, social pressure, and physical and verbal bullying that can affect students’ mental health.

The data analysed in this study are data on resilience scores and self-awareness scores in the experimental group and control group. The experimental group was given treatment in the form of NLP intervention for 3 meetings, while the control group was not given any treatment. Before being given treatment, the experimental group and control group were given a pre-test, and after being given treatment, both groups were given a post-test to obtain resilience and self-awareness scores. Discussion

This study generally found that Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP) interventions specifically designed to increase self-awareness had a positive and significant impact on students’ resilience levels [25, 34]. The findings of this study are (1) There was a statistically significant increase in resilience scores in the group of students who received NLP intervention, when comparing pre-test and post-test results; (2) NLP intervention was also proven to be effective in increasing students’ self-awareness scores, which included the ability to recognize their thoughts, emotions, and triggers for internal responses [35, 36]; (3) Further analysis showed that increased self-awareness played an important mediating role; that is, the effectiveness of NLP in increasing resilience largely worked through its ability to first build students’ self-awareness [37].

The findings of this study show that NLP intervention significantly improves students’ self-awareness, in line with the fundamental principles of NLP itself. NLP, in essence, is the study of the structure of subjective experience. Techniques such as the Meta-Model (clarifying language to understand internal reality), Reframing (changing one’s perspective on a problem), and Perceptual Positions (viewing a situation from the perspective of others and neutral observers) inherently compel individuals to engage in introspection and recognize their mental maps.

These results reinforce previous findings that NLP training effectively improves students’ confidence in speaking English well and correctly [19]. The results of this study are in line with previous studies [17, 38] which say that NLP successfully helps participants overcome dysfunctional thoughts, emotional imbalances, anxiety, depression, and then bounce back. This argument reveals that the first step in emotional regulation is awareness of the emotions being felt, a core component of self-awareness. This study goes further by explicitly measuring self-awareness as a variable and proving that NLP techniques directly target and improve this construct.

On the other hand, previous research [29] focused on interventions for changing thoughts and behaviour. When compared, these two studies show that although their approaches are different, their ultimate goal is the same, namely to bring unconscious internal processes to the level of consciousness. Our findings provide an alternative or complement to other technical interventions, where a more structured and goal-oriented NLP approach is more suitable for some students who need concrete tools to change their mindset.

Specifically, the ‘Anchoring’ technique taught in our intervention allows students to consciously recognize and access positive emotional resources when faced with stress triggers [39]. This is in line with the theory [40] which states that positive emotional experiences can expand a person’s repertoire of thoughts and actions, which ultimately builds personal resources, including resilience. With NLP, students do not just passively wait for positive emotions, but actively learn to create and access them through self-awareness of triggers and physiological responses [18].

The most significant finding of this study is the mediating role of self-awareness. This shows that NLP does not magically make students resilient. Instead, NLP provides students with tools to become more aware of their ‘inner world’, and it is this awareness that is the main fuel for building resilience [41]. These findings strongly support the theoretical framework of resilience proposed by previous researchers [42], which describes resilience as an “ordinary miracle” that emerges from the basic adaptive systems of humans. Our research shows that self-awareness is one of these fundamental adaptive systems [43, 44]. Students who are aware of their negative self-talk can use reframing techniques to challenge and change it [45]. Students who are aware of their physical responses to anxiety (e.g., shortness of breath, rapid heartbeat) can use anchoring technique to calm themselves [46]. This finding aligns with socio-cognitive frameworks that emphasize how individuals model and internalize effective coping behaviors from their environment.

These results are consistent with research in the field of emotional intelligence by Goleman (1995) in his study [47], which places self-awareness as the first and most important pillar of emotional intelligence. Without self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, and relationship management become impossible. Resilience, in this context, can be seen as a manifestation of well-functioning emotional intelligence. Our research provides empirical evidence linking a specific intervention (NLP) to the foundation of emotional intelligence (self-awareness) to achieve the desired outcome (resilience).

Interestingly, when compared to previous studies [48–50] that found that group guidance techniques and social support from peers were the main predictors of resilience and self-discovery in students, our findings offer a different perspective. The above studies emphasize external factors, while our study highlights the power of internal factors. This does not mean that one is more important than the other. Rather, it suggests that comprehensive resilience-building programs should ideally cover both aspects: building students’ internal resources (through interventions such as NLP for self-awareness) and strengthening their external support systems.

Resilience is influenced by internal characteristics such as age and gender, as well as external characteristics including school type, geographic location, and parents’ educational level and employment status. Previous studies have reported significant gender differences in resilience levels, with male students demonstrating higher resilience scores than female students, although both groups were classified within the high category (70.95% and 69,0.10%, respectively). These diffences were particularly evident in the subdimensions of optimism and emosion regulation, suggesting that gender plays a role in students’ ability to cope with stress and post-disaster trauma through both individual factors and social and cultural constructions [51, 52].

In addition, participants’ characteristics are influenced by parents’ educational level, as parental education is associated with the quality of emotional support, parenting practices, and parents’ capacity to guide children in coping with academic and social pressures, which in turn affects the development of students’ resilience [53]. Furthermore, higher resilience observed among adolescents in rural areas and vocational high school students indicates that social environments and educational demands also contribute to shaping adolecents’ adaptive capacity and resilience. Therefore, resilience among the study participants is not solely determined by individual factors but also shaped by demographic and contextual characteristics of the research population [54].

This study was limited to adolescents aged 15–18 years who were enrolled as senior high school student and conducted within specific school setting, including public and private school in both urban and rural areas. The limitations of this research include the inability to involve all schools and students due to constraints in time and funding. Furthermore, language barriers arising from regional dialect differences, the considerable distance to the research sites, and limited accessibility-requiring travel by land throught steep, winding, and uphill roads-also posed challenges to the implementation of the research.

Conclusion

Self-awreness-based Neuro-linguistic Programming (NLP) implemented in this study was associated with an improvement in students’ resilience in the experiment group compared to the control group. This finding aligns with the results indicating a greater increase in resilience scores following the intervention, suggesting that NLP techniques combined with resilience education may support students’ adaptive capacities in academic and soocial contexts. However, the intervention did not produce a stronger improvement in self-awareness compared to the control condition, indicating that changes in self-awareness may be influenced by factors beyond the intervention or require a more intensive or targeted approach. These result highlight that resilience development is shaped by both individual and contextual factors, including demographic and educational characteristics. Therefore, future studies are encouraged to refine intervention designs, extend intervention duration, and examine their effects across diverse student populations and settings.

Identification of new major histocompatibility complex-B haplotypes in chinese broilers
Authors: Wanqiang Chen, Xiaoxu Jia, Yushi Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractMHC in chicken demonstrates a great range of variations. This study was conducted to investigate the major histocompatibility complex-B (MHC-B) variability in three representative broiler breeds with large breeding populations in China namely Arbor Acres (AA), 817 broilers (817), and Jinling Partridge (JL). The three Chinese broilers were analyzed with a subset of 89 SNPs in the MHC-B SNP panel. The genotypes of these SNPs were obtained using multiplex PCR targeted amplicon sequencing. The haplotypes were analyzed through a combination of manual and computational haplotype reconstruction methods using PHASE program. The phylogenetic tree was constructed based on haplotypes using MrBayes program. A total of 67 haplotypes were identified within three broilers populations, among which three haplotypes from the AA and 817 populations shared the standard MHC-B SNP (BSNP) haplotype. No matching haplotypes were detected in the JL population. Phylogenetic analysis based on these SNPs did not reveal clearly defined population clusters. This study reveals a high level of genetic diversity within the MHC-B region, highlighting the importance of this functionally significant genomic region in the three broilers. However, the observed genetic patterns indicate a complex relatedness structure among the three populations. These findings indicate the need for further investigation to better understand the evolutionary dynamics and functional implications of MHC-B variation.Keywords: MHC-B, Chinese Broiler, SNP, Haplotype

Introduction

Modern breeds' standardized production models aim to achieve key performance metrics while placing equal emphasis on disease resistance and adaptability to intensive farming systems (Doeschl-Wilson et al., 2021). The major histocompatibility complex-B (MHC-B) region of the chicken is an important cluster of genes, consisting of multiple genes regulating chicken immunology (Miller and Taylor, 2016). A substantial body of researches have demonstrated significant correlations between genetic variability within the MHC-B region and resistance to various diseases (Miller and Taylor, 2016). Studies have shown that the MHC-B locus plays a role in modulating susceptibility to bacterial infections such as those caused by Staphylococcus aureus (Cotter et al., 1992), Escherichia coli (Macklin et al., 2002, Macklin et al., 2009; Cavero et al., 2009), and Salmonella enteritidis (Schou et al., 2010). Furthermore, evidence indicates that specific MHC-B haplotypes are associated with resistance to both internal and external parasites (Lillehoj et al., 1988; Owen et al., 2008; Schou et al., 2010). The involvement of MHC-B in immune responses to viral pathogens, including Marek’s disease virus (Goto et al., 2009), Rous sarcoma virus (Taylor, 2004), and Newcastle disease virus (Norup et al., 2011), has also been documented in the literature. In addition, the MHC-B region has been associated with several economically important traits in poultry production, including body weight, mortality rates, egg production performance, and hatchability (Bacon, 1987; Abplanalp et al., 1992; Sander, 1993).

Numerous studies have emphasized the critical role of heterozygosity in highly polymorphic regions across the genome, particularly in relation to disease resistance (Lipsitch et al., 2003; Tsartsianidou et al., 2021). Sato et al. (1992) proposed that the heterozygosity of such regions may be the primary factor influencing overall immune resilience. In support of this hypothesis, Izadi et al. (2011) observed higher allelic diversity at the LEI0258 microsatellite locus within the MHC region in the noncommercial chicken populations. The authors suggested that this increased MHC variability, frequently observed in free-range or pasture-reared breeds, may enhanced disease resistance, as these populations have likely undergone natural selection due to exposure to a wide range of pathogens and parasites in less controlled environments. However, the relationship between genetic diversity and immune performance is not straightforward. Some investigations have suggested that specific combinations of homozygous or heterozygous haplotypes in hybrid offspring may confer immunological advantages under certain conditions (Dunnington et al., 1992). Moreover, analysis of MHC-B diversity in a wild population of Red Jungle Fowl and five major populations of Bangladesh native chicken demonstrated a high degree of heterozygosity, supporting the idea that natural selection favors genetic variability in this genomic region (Nguyen-Phuc et al., 2016; Ediriweera et al., 2024). Thus, both the extent of polymorphism and the characteristic of individual gene variants are likely important contributors to host defense capabilities. Considering this high disease resistance, evaluations of their immunity-related genes at the molecular level can provide valuable insights into genetic diversity.

The MHC region contains the MHC-Y and MHC-B subregions, which are separated by a GC-rich region (Delany et al., 2009). Among these, the MHC-B region is the most thoroughly studied and well-mapped. Shiina et al. (2007) reported that it comprises 46 genes spanning 241,833 base pairs from BG3 to CD1A1 (GenBank AB268588). Its high allelic diversity within coding regions is believed to be driven by selection pressure from pathogen challenges. A SNP panel that encompasses most of the MHC-B region, developed by Chazara et al. (2010), was subsequently modified to cover 230,000 bp with 101 SNP (Fulton et al., 2016b). The SNPs in this panel were numbered from MHCNew003 to MHC0178. The SNP panel was successfully used in different chicken populations to investigate the haplotype diversity and novel haplotypes in the MHC-B region, including heritage broilers, wild Vietnamese Junglefowl, the Finnish Landrace breed, Argentinian Campero-Inta chicken synthetic line, Korean native breeds, USA Commercial Silkie, Sri Lankan indigenous chicken, the Chantecler breed and Bangladesh native chicken (Fulton et al., 2016a, Fulton et al., 2017; Nguyen-Phuc et al., 2016; Iglesias et al., 2019, Iglesias et al., 2021; Manjula et al., 2020a, Manjula et al., 2020b, Manjula et al., 2021; Tarrant et al., 2020; Darrington et al., 2023; Ediriweera et al., 2024). Currently, next-generation sequencing can generate the full-length sequences of highly polymorphic genomic regions such as MHC (Yuan et al., 2021; Ediriweera et al., 2022), with extremely high coverage and precision at each variant. Accurate sequence information of the MHC-B region is crucial for haplotype identification and nomenclature.

In 2024, China’s broiler industry is experiencing a significant transformation. According to the latest report from the China’s National Broiler Industry Technology System, total broiler production reached 14.84 billion birds, comprising 9.03 billion white-feather broilers (represented by Arbor Acres (AA) strains), 3.33 billion yellow-feather broilers (Jinling Partridge (JL) strains), and 2.48 billion small white-feather broilers (817 broilers (817) strains) (Xin et al., 2025). The AA is a globally recognized white-feather broiler breed developed through a four-line crossbreeding system. Their adaptability to intensive farming systems and consistent performance across a range of nutritional conditions have established them as a leading commercial broiler breed globally. The 817, originating from the western region of Shandong Province, is a hybrid of white feather broiler (e.g., Ross 308) and high-yielding laying hens (e.g., Hy-Line). Known for its 5-7 week growth cycle and 1.3-1.8 kg market weight, the 817 is widely used in processed poultry products (e.g., roast chicken and smoked chicken) due to its firm meat texture and moderate growth rate. However, its low genetic standardization and susceptibility to vertical disease transmission (e.g., avian leukosis, mycoplasma) pose challenges for sustainable development. The JL is a representative yellow-feather broiler breed native to China and is primarily marketed as live birds. The JL, a meat-type hybrid breed recognized by China’s National Livestock Breed Committee in 2009 (https://www.moa.gov.cn/), is characterized by its rapid growth rate, high feed conversion efficiency, and distinctive mahogany-colored plumage.

This study genotyped the MHC-B regions of the AA, 817 and JL broiler populations in China using a subset of 89/88 SNPs from the MHC-B SNP panel and compared the results with known MHC-B SNP (BSNP) haplotypes of global chicken breeds.

Materials and methods

Study Populations and Genomic DNA Extraction

A total of 58 chickens were randomly selected from three distinct commercial populations. The sample included an equal number of males and females at six weeks of age, with the following breed distribution: AA (n = 20), 817 (n = 20), and JL (n = 18). Blood samples were collected from the wing vein, and genomic DNA was extracted from blood in 1.5 mL microtubes with EDTA using the Rapid Blood Genomic DNA Isolation Kit (Sangon, Shanghai, China), following the manufacturer’s instructions. After DNA extraction, the integrity was verified by 1 % agarose gel electrophoresis. A single bright band suggested intact genomic DNA, while degradation appeared as diffuse smearing.

Multiplex PCR and sequencing

A panel which contains 101 target SNP sites was designed. Library preparation was performed by two step PCR approach with Kapa HiFi Ready Mix. The plate was sealed and PCR performed in a thermal instrument (BIO-RAD, T100TM) using the optimized program. The first PCR products were checked using electrophoresis in 1 % agarose gels in TBE buffer (Tris, boric acid, EDTA) stained with ethidium bromide (EB) and visualized under UV light. Amplicon products were purified with AMPure XP beads. The second PCR added barcodes using universal primers (P5/P7), with subsequent bead purification. The libraries were quantified and pooled. Paired-end sequencing of the library was performed on the HiSeq XTen sequencers (Illumina, San Diego, CA).

Data QC and SNP calling

Raw reads were filtered according to two steps. Removing adaptor sequence if reads contains by Cutadapt v1.2.1 (Martin, 2011). Removing low quality bases from reads 3′ to 5′ (Q < 20) by PRINSEQ-lite v0.20.3 (Schmieder and Edwards, 2011). And the remaining clean data were mapped to the reference genome by BWA v0.7.13-r1126 (Li and Durbin, 2009) with default parameters. In this study, chromosome 16 from galGal4 was set as the reference, and then the target sites were remapped to the Shiina sequence. Samtools v0.1.18 (Li et al., 2009) was used to calculate each genotype of target sites.

Haplotype identification

All individuals were genotyped using a subset of 101 SNPs from the high-density MHC-B SNP panel. Haplotypes were identified using both manual and computational approaches. The manual method followed published procedures (Fulton et al., 2016b; Ediriweera et al., 2024). For the computational approach, PHASE v2.1 (Stephens and Scheet, 2005) software was utilized; its MS model was used for the analysis with 1,000 iterations and 1,000 burn-ins (Nguyen-Phuc et al., 2016). The results from both methods were compared to ensure the absence of discrepancies. Because the MHC116 or MHC054 were not genotyped in the broilers, the current haplotypes could not be directly compared with the published haplotypes for nomenclature purposes. These haplotypes were considered novel haplotypes and named based on the method described by Manjula et al. (2021). Phylogenetic analysis was conducted using the obtained AA, 817 and JL haplotypes using 89 SNPs only. A phylogenetic tree was generated using MrBayes v3.2 (Ronquist et al., 2012), with the optimal model was selected by ModelFinder from IQ-TREE (Minh et al., 2020).

Comparison of chinese broilers MHC-B haplotypes with global BSNP haplotypes

Because of differences in SNP numbers, direct comparisons of haplotypes from the three broiler populations with global haplotypes would have been difficult unless all global haplotypes were reconstructed using the 89 SNPs. Accordingly, the MHC116 or MHC054 alleles of the global haplotypes were excluded, and we created a new set of global haplotypes with 89 alleles to use in comparisons with the three broilers (Ediriweera et al., 2024). The nomenclature of haplotypes followed the definitions established by Fulton et al. (2016b), wherein each haplotype was identified by a designation that integrates the BSNP prefix with the corresponding breed information.

Results and discussion

MHC-B SNP haplotypes

The high-throughput sequencing results exhibited outstanding performance, achieving average coverage of 98.8 % and mean sequencing depth of 8674 across the target region. Compared to the Kompetitive Allele Specific PCR genotyping method, the approach generated significantly more usable data while maintaining high base-calling accuracy. Despite the technical challenges associated with the high GC content in MHC-B region, the sequencing quality remained reliable.

Among the three broiler populations, only the 817 contained one homozygote and one single site heterozygote, while the rest of the individuals were heterozygous at two or more loci. Analysis of three broiler populations revealed 67 haplotypes: 14, 22 and 31 from the AA, 817 and JL populations, respectively. Two haplotypes were shared (exclude MHC116 and MHC054): BSNP-AA-Hap03 and BSNP-817-Hap02, BSNP-AA-Hap10 and BSNP-817-Hap11. All haplotypes obtained for the JL population were unique. Table 1 summarizes the obtained haplotypes, while haplotypes with specific haplotype names are shown in Figure 1. Shared haplotypes were identified by detecting identical sequences between the haplotypes of the three populations and global haplotypes. SNP genotyping analysis of the MHC region in three broiler populations revealed complete homozygosity at six distinct loci. G was identified at MHC026, MHC142, MHC155, and MHC178 across all populations, G was observed at MHC054 within the AA population, and A was detected at MHC045 across all populations. Moreover, we observed significant deviations from the expected 50:50 read counts ratio in heterozygous genotypes at specific loci in SNP genotyping. Notably, MHC092 exhibited a pronounced G dominance over A (mean ratio 72:28), while MHC097 showed preferential amplification of C over A (mean ratio 70:30). This read counts imbalance likely results in underestimation of heterozygosity levels at these sites, as conventional calling algorithms may misclassify imbalanced heterozygotes as homozygotes. The precise cause of this discrepancy remains unclear, though PCR amplification bias being a potential contributing factor. Further analysis of the sequence data in these regions will be necessary to fully characterize the extent of genetic variation.

This study collected published standard MHC haplotypes from diverse chicken populations worldwide and constructed two datasets for comparative analysis. The first dataset excluded MHC116 to facilitate comparison with the AA population, while the second dataset excluded both MHC116 and MHC054 for comparisons involving three populations. Upon comparing the BSNP haplotypes of three broiler populations with the globally published standard MHC haplotypes, we identified that the AA population's BSNP-AA-Hap03 matched the reported BSNP-A07RJF* and BSNP-A09, the 817 population's BSNP-817-Hap02 matched the reported BSNP-A07RJF* and BSNP-A09, and the BSNP-817-Hap03 corresponded to the reported BSNP-A08. BSNP-A09A and BSNP-A09B are identical to one possible haplotype of BSNP-A07RJF* spanning from MHC0J6 to MHC178, indicating that they actually represent a single haplotype. No matching haplotypes were detected in the JL population, suggesting that all haplotypes in this population are unique and exhibit population-specific characteristics. The original source information of these matched haplotypes was identified in the Red Jungle Fowl, Red Rhode Island, White Plymouth Rock, Epileptic synthetic line, and Broiler, with Broiler being the most likely source. This observation also suggests that these breeds have not entered the JL through genetic introgression. This hypothesis requires further validation through an investigation of genome-wide diversity. The findings above contribute to the expanding repository of MHC data obtained through SNP panel studies. Due to the ambiguity of the comparison data after removing the allele, it was excluded from further analysis, such as the analysis of recombinant haplotypes.

Phylogenetic analysis

Figure 2 shows the cladogram of the obtained haplotypes from the three populations (highlighted in blue, green, and red), with BSNP-SLK-Hap01 serving as the outgroup (Tarrant et al., 2020). As the haplotypes contained only 89 SNPs, the BSNP-SLK-Hap01 haplotype was redeveloped by excluding SNP MHC116. The redeveloped version of BSNP-SLK-Hap01 (with 89 SNPs) was then used as the outgroup (shown in black) to avoid possible bias. However, no further analysis was conducted due to the uncertainty of the removed allele.

Bayesian approach-based circular cladogram for MHC-B SNP haplotypes of three broiler populations in China. Arbor Acres (AA), 817 broilers (817), Jinling Partridge (JL).

The analysis using the Bayesian Information Criterion indicated that the General Time-Reversible model was the optimal nucleotide substitution model for haplotypes across the three populations, with the mutation rate distributed according to a gamma distribution (Minh et al., 2020). The analysis results did not reveal significant population-specific patterns. Despite the inclusion of many highly specific haplotypes in the analysis, these haplotypes were not phylogenetically clustered according to population. These findings suggest that the distribution of MHC-B haplotypes in Chinese broilers may be influenced by localized breeding practices, selection pressures, or demographic histories, resulting in distinct genetic signatures at the regional level.

Due to the high variability of MHC regions, the failure to obtain chicken MHC116 and MHC054 results through high-throughput sequencing may result from reduced primer compatibility. Nguyen-Phuc et al. (Nguyen-Phuc et al., 2016) used 84 SNPs to analyze MHC haplotype diversity of wild Red Junglefowl populations in Vietnam. Ediriweera et al. (Ediriweera et al., 2024) used a subset of 89 SNPs in the high-density MHC-B SNP panel to analyze MHC haplotype in five major Bangladesh native chicken populations. To enhance the comparison of MHC haplotypes, potential approaches include designing new primers to amplify the missing alleles or selecting a representative subset of SNPs from the initial MHC set (comprising 90 SNPs).

Broiler production plays a crucial role in China’s agricultural development and food security strategy (Xie et al., 2024). As one of the most populous countries in the world, China encounters substantial challenges in securing a stable and sustainable protein supply to meet the dietary needs of its vast population (Lin et al., 2023). Understanding the genetic variation in commercial populations, particularly within the immune-related MHC region, is of critical importance. The Chinese broilers analyzed in this study exhibit considerable genetic diversity in the MHC-B region, which is likely to contribute to enhanced the immune competence in the three populations. JL appears to have undergone a unique selection process, leading to substantial genetic differentiation compared to the other two populations. Native chicken breeds represent a source of genetic diversity and well adaptation to diverse ecological environments. They are generally considered to have stronger disease resistance compared to high-producing breeds (Izadi et al., 2011), as evidenced by JL's observation of higher haplotype diversity in this study. However, the number of native chicken breeds has been decreasing over last four decades with the development of highly productive and economically efficient breeds. Even with knowledge of MHC-B haplotype and their immunological functions, breeding selection at that time could still influence which haplotypes persisted in subsequent populations based on the geographic regions where specific strains develop and the prevalent diseases in those regions (Darrington et al., 2023). We strongly advocate for the protection of these evolutionarily distinct chicken populations, which represent a significant asset for both biodiversity conservation and sustainable agricultural development.

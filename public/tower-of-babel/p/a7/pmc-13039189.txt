Hybrid expansion methods for fractional non-linear mathematical systems with Erdelyi-Kober derivative operators in theory of tsunami wave modeling
Authors: Faten H Damag, Amin Saif, Mohammad Alshammari, Fozaiyah Alhubairah, Maryam F Alshammari, Mohammed S Alsharafi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work develops two hybrid semi-analytical methods, namely the expansion new iterative method (ENIM) and the expansion homotopy perturbation method (EHPM), for solving the fractional Whitham-Broer-Kaup equations (WBKEs) involving the Erdélyi-Kober (EK) fractional derivative, since EK derivative is adopted due to its ability to incorporate scaling properties and nonlocal memory effects in a more flexible framework. The study focuses on the mathematical behavior of a fractional extension of the WBK system related to shallow water wave modeling. Several analytical properties of EK operators and their action on fractional power series expansions are established to support the proposed frameworks. By combining EK fractional integration with nonlinear operator decomposition and power series representations, ENIM and EHPM provide approximate solutions to nonlinear fractional partial differential equations. The methods are applied to the fractional WBK system, and numerical results demonstrate good agreement with the classical solution in the benchmark case (). For fractional cases (), the results are approximate and model-dependent. The comparison indicates that EHPM yields smaller absolute errors than ENIM within the tested parameter ranges. The influence of the fractional order on the solution behavior is also illustrated, showing a transition between diffusive and classical wave patterns. These findings highlight the effectiveness of the proposed methods in terms of numerical accuracy within the considered framework.Keywords: WBKEs, Fractional partial differential equations, EK fractional derivative, Expansion new iterative method, Expansion homotopy perturbation methodSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The theory of fractional calculus (FC) extends the classical concepts of differentiation and integration by permitting operators of arbitrary non-integer order. Although its origins date back to the correspondence between Leibniz and L’Hôpital in the seventeenth century, its rigorous mathematical foundations were later established in the nineteenth century by Riemann, Liouville, and Grünwald1,2. Their pioneering contributions provided the analytical tools necessary to model systems whose dynamics depend on nonlocality, memory, and hereditary effects. Since then, FC has evolved into a rich and versatile mathematical framework capable of describing long range interactions and anomalous transport phenomena across diverse temporal and spatial scales. In recent decades, fractional calculus has attracted considerable attention due to its ability to represent complex dynamical processes more faithfully than traditional integer order models. Advancements in numerical analysis and computational science have significantly broadened the applicability of fractional differential equations (FDEs) in engineering, applied sciences, and physics3,4. Numerous fractional operators have been introduced, including the Caputo, Riemann-Liouville, Atangana-Baleanu, and Caputo-Fabrizio formulations, each designed to capture distinct aspects of anomalous diffusion, memory driven processes, or multiscale behavior. These operators have enhanced the modeling accuracy of systems arising in viscoelasticity, electromagnetism, biology, and diffusion phenomena with intrinsic memory effects5. Partial differential equations (PDEs) constitute a fundamental mathematical framework for describing physical processes spanning fluid dynamics, quantum mechanics, electromagnetism, heat transfer, and more. When fractional derivatives are incorporated into PDEs, the resulting fractional partial differential equations (FPDEs) offer a more flexible modeling environment that accounts for hereditary and nonlocal effects6. FPDEs effectively model anomalous diffusion, viscoelastic materials, chaotic behavior, and heterogeneous media7. Extensive research has focused on establishing analytical properties of FPDEs–including existence, uniqueness, stability, and convergence–to ensure their mathematical and physical soundness8. Veeresha et al.9 introduced a fractional Zika virus model incorporating diffusion effects to analyze the dynamics of mosquito-borne and sexually transmitted infections. Akinyemi et al.10 developed an efficient analytical technique for solving generalized conformable Pochhammer–Chree models describing longitudinal wave propagation in elastic rods. Liu and Wang11 established a variational framework and derived new soliton solutions for the nonlinear fractional Schrödinger–Hirota equation along with sensitivity analysis. Wang and Wei12 constructed novel optical soliton solutions for a nonlinear paraxial wave model relevant to optical physics. Wang13 analyzed the dynamical behavior and soliton structures of the nonlinear fractional Akbota equation. Yadav et al.14 proposed the Laplace residual power series method to obtain approximate solutions of the time-fractional KdV equation. Yadav et al.15 applied an extended residual power series method to solve time-fractional Zakharov–Kuznetsov equations in coastal wave modeling. Rao et al.16 introduced a novel computational approach to derive analytical solutions for time-fractional cold plasma equations. Fractional PDE models have found widespread application in geophysics, biological systems, financial modeling, and engineering. They are particularly effective in describing processes governed by hereditary mechanisms, such as groundwater flow in porous media, charge transport in irregular structures, and heat conduction in materials with structural heterogeneity17. Motivated by these applications, a broad array of analytical and numerical methods has been developed. For instance, Kumar18 proposed techniques for nonlinear singular boundary value problems; Yang6 introduced an integral formulation for steady heat transfer; Mohyud Din et al.7 employed perturbation based techniques; Xie et al.19 applied hyperbolic methods; Biazar and Aminikhah17 investigated coupled Burgers type equations; and Ahmad et al.20 made use of the Adomian decomposition method and He’s polynomials for the WBKE system. Other approaches include the homotopy perturbation method21, parabolic convection-diffusion schemes22, the residual power series method23, refined quintic B-splines with Von Neumann stability analysis24, the Yang transform with decomposition techniques25, the q-homotopy analysis transform26, the YRPST method27, Sumudu based decomposition28, Laplace Adomian hybrid schemes29, the natural decomposition method26, scaling based transformations30, and the optimal homotopy asymptotic method31. Perturbation studies involving the Caputo-Fabrizio operator have also been developed32. Collectively, these works highlight the versatility and effectiveness of semi-analytical techniques for nonlinear and fractional PDEs. The fractional model is formulated by extending the classical WBK system through the incorporation of the EK fractional derivative, which introduces nonlocal and memory effects into the governing equations. Significant attention has been devoted to nonlinear and fractional extensions of the WBKEs (1). Fractional operators introduce nonlocal memory effects, enriching the associated wave dynamics and motivating extensive analytical and numerical investigations. Relevant studies include extended algebraic mapping techniques34, homotopy perturbation frameworks35, sine-Gordon expansions for Wu-Zhang systems36, Laplace based residual power series methods37, reproducing kernel Hilbert space methods38, variational iteration approaches39, numerical treatments of fractional telegraph equations40, monotone iterative schemes41, expansion based methods42, and modified Adams-Bashforth algorithms43. Several analytical and numerical schemes have been proposed to solve the fractional WBKE system (2). Kumar et al.45 performed a detailed computational analysis, highlighting the influence of fractional order parameters on wave propagation. Prakash and Kaur46 developed an efficient numerical method tailored to the EK based WBK system, achieving improved accuracy for shallow water models with memory. The motivation of this study is to develop reliable and efficient hybrid semi-analytical methods for solving nonlinear fractional PDEs involving the EK derivative, with particular emphasis on the fractional WBKEs that model tsunami shallow water waves with memory effects. Classical models fail to capture these nonlocal behaviors, motivating the construction of expansion-based schemes capable of producing rapidly convergent solutions. In this work, Section 1 outlines the background on fractional calculus and WBK equations. Section 2 presents the EK operators and establishes key analytical properties. Section 3 derives fractional power series expansions and characterizes EK operators acting on them. Section 4 introduces the hybrid ENIM and EHPM frameworks and develops their recursive structures. Section 5 applies both methods to the EK fractional WBK equations and obtains approximate analytical solutions of WBKEs (2). Section 6 compares numerical accuracy, analyzes absolute errors, and studies the influence of the fractional order . Section 7 summarizes the findings and highlights the effectiveness and stability of the proposed hybrid approaches.

Conclusions

It should be emphasized that the present study deals with a mathematical fractional extension of the WBK system, and the obtained results do not represent direct physical predictions of real tsunami events. In this work, two hybrid semi-analytical methods, the ENIM and the EHPM, were developed and applied to the fractional WBKEs involving the EK fractional derivative, a model relevant to tsunami shallow water wave dynamics. Several analytical properties of EK operators and their action on fractional power series expansions were established, providing the theoretical basis for constructing rapidly convergent semi-analytical solutions. The numerical experiments demonstrate that both ENIM and EHPM yield highly accurate approximations for the velocity and free surface elevation fields, with EHPM consistently achieving smaller absolute errors. Parametric studies further reveal that the fractional order serves as a tunable factor that continuously transitions the system between diffusive fractional behavior and classical wave propagation. Overall, the proposed hybrid expansion schemes exhibit stability, reliability, and computational efficiency, confirming their effectiveness for nonlinear fractional partial differential equations and, in particular, for modeling tsunami shallow water phenomena.

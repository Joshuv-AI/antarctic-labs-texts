Magnetohydrodynamic peristaltic flow of hybrid nanofluid in an asymmetric channel with thermal radiation and shape factors: an application of coolant systems
Authors: K Thirunavukarasan, G Sucharitha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates the peristaltic transport of Al2O3-Cu/ethylene glycol hybrid nanofluid in an asymmetric microchannel under an inclined magnetic field. The analysis incorporates electroosmosis, Hall current, viscous dissipation, thermal radiation, buoyancy forces, nanoparticle shape, and internal heat source effects. The Poisson–Boltzmann equation is linearized using the Debye-Hückel approximation, and the governing flow equations are solved analytically via the Homotopy Perturbation Method (HPM). Results indicate that increasing the Helmholtz-Smoluchowski velocity parameter from 0.1 to 0.5 leads to a 5.729% rise in skin friction at the left wall. Compared to spherical nanoparticles, laminar-shaped nanoparticles improve heat transfer by 13.9% at the right wall. The present study is novel in that it simultaneously addresses these multiphysical effects, providing a comprehensive understanding of hybrid nanofluid peristaltic flow. The findings offer valuable insights for microfluidic devices, coolant systems, and advanced thermal management applications, including a range of heat exchanger systems such as radiators, heating–ventilation units, and micro-electromechanical systems (MEMS).Keywords: Thermal radiation, Nanoparticle shape factor, Peristalsis, Hybrid nanofluids, Inclined magnetic field

Introduction

Peristalsis acts as a transport mechanism defined by the movement of fluid driven by a series of wave-like contractions from flexible boundaries. This occurrence is commonly seen in biological systems, such as the gastrointestinal tract and ureters. This mechanism enables efficient fluid movement independently of external pumping devices. In engineering, applying peristaltic principles plays a vital role in designing cooling systems for electronic devices, nuclear reactors, and biomedical equipment, ensuring contamination-free and regulated heat removal. Peristaltic cooling systems provide unique advantages for handling sensitive, viscous, or particle-laden fluids. Several studies have explored peristaltic transport in nanofluids to enhance understanding of heat and energy transfer mechanisms.

Tamizharasi et al. [1] conducted an analysis of the peristaltic flow of a nanofluid within an asymmetric channel, highlighting the impact of channel geometry on heat and mass transfer characteristics. Alrashdi [2] examined entropy generation in the peristaltic transport of hybrid nanofluids, taking into account variable thermal conductivity and electromagnetic effects, and emphasised their influence on thermodynamic efficiency. In a recent study, Nazeer et al. [3] investigated the electro-osmotic peristaltic motion of hybrid nanofluids within a symmetric channel, with a focus on enhancing energy transfer. Mishra et al. [4] explored how a Phan–Thien–Tanner nanofluid is transported under peristaltic motion, taking into account both Hall current effects and mixed convection phenomena. Aslam et al. [5] examined both homogeneous and heterogeneous reactions within an electromagnetic setting, utilising the second law of thermodynamics to emphasise the behaviour of entropy during peristaltic transport. Noreen and Ahmad [6] examined the motion of peristaltic fluid in the human digestive system influenced by electrokinetic effects, highlighting the physiological importance of electrically induced peristaltic motion. Aslam and Noreen [7] explored the contribution of electromagnetic forces in reducing entropy generation in microchannels with peristaltic transport.

A hybrid nanofluid is formed by blending two or more types of nanoparticles into a base fluid to enhance thermal performance. The combination of nanoparticles results in improved thermal conductivity and better heat transfer than individual nanofluids. Considering these advantages, hybrid nanofluids are widely utilised in cooling systems such as heat exchangers, electronic cooling, and automotive radiators.

The enhanced capacity for energy transport promotes efficient heat dissipation while reducing energy consumption. Recent studies have significantly broadened the examination of nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid flows, accounting for intricate physical effects relevant to thermal engineering applications. With a focus on heat transmission, Khushi and Abbasi [8] studied the effects of Hall current and Joule heating on the peristaltic movement of TiO2-Ag/EG hybrid nanofluids within a curved channel. Bilal et al. [9] analysed pseudo-plastic hybrid nanofluid thin-film flow, accounting for the effects of an applied magnetic field, a heat source, and variable thermal conductivity, thereby enhancing thermal management. Waheed et al. [10] carried out a study on the electrothermal flow characteristics of water-based nanofluids containing Cu, Ag, and Al2O3 nanoparticles in a wavy microchannel. Madhuri et al. [11] investigated the dynamics of radiative heat transfer and buoyancy effects in a rotating disc, employing the Tiwari–Das nanofluid model. Siddique et al. [12] investigated the enhancement of heat transfer in hybrid nanofluids, accounting for the effects of surface roughness and slip. In recent studies, Pattnaik et al. [13] studied hybrid nanofluid flow through a rocket engine nozzle using a modified Adomian decomposition method. Darcy dissipation effects on TiO2–Cu nanofluid flow over a vertical sheet were analyzed using the homotopy perturbation method investigated by Pattnaik et al. [14].

The objective of this study is to develop a model for the coolant system using ethylene glycol, due to its higher boiling point and lower freezing point, ethylene is frequently employed as a coolant. Additionally, ethylene glycol is highly effective at facilitating heat transfer and preventing rust in the cooling system. To improve thermal conductivity, the inclusion of nanoparticles are considered. This is because copper is commonly preferred in cooling systems due to its exceptional thermal conductivity. Peristalsis enhances the cooling system's efficiency by enabling precise control of fluid flow and maintaining cleanliness. In areas such as laboratory cooling systems, electronic devices, medical devices, microelectronics, and semiconductor production, accurate control of fluid flow is crucial to maintaining appropriate temperature and cleanliness.

The configuration of nanoparticles in a nanofluid, be it spherical, cylindrical, plate-like, or rod-shaped, significantly influences its thermal conductivity and viscosity. Non-spherical shapes generally enhance heat transfer due to their larger surface area and the influence of Brownian motion. Improving the structure of nanoparticles can enhance the effectiveness of nanofluids in cooling and energy applications. Afsar et al. [15] examined the electro osmotically driven flow of hybrid nanofluids in asymmetric channels, considering the impact of shape factor under peristaltic transport. Their study revealed that laminar-shaped nanoparticles significantly enhance heat transfer. Maraj et al. [16] demonstrated that the shape factor of nanoparticles significantly influences the magnetohydrodynamic peristaltic motion of menthol-based nanofluids. Iqbal et al. [17] investigated the behaviour of a magnetised Williamson hybrid nanofluid within an intricate wavy conduit, accounting for various geometric features.

Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flow examines the dynamics of an electrically conducting fluid in the presence of a magnetic field. The impact of MHD effects in cooling systems is considerable, as they aid in managing fluid dynamics and improve heat transfer efficiency. These systems are widely utilised in electronic cooling, nuclear reactors, and thermal management in aerospace applications. The study of entropy generation in the peristaltic transport of hybrid nanofluids within a titled magnetic field was conducted by Kazmi et al. [18]. Ramzan et al. [19] and Alhowaity et al. [20] investigated the flow of radiative MHD nanofluids over convectively Riga plates, taking into account the effects of chemical reactions and nonlinear heat generation respectively. The flow of magneto-micropolar nanofluid via permeable conduit was examined by Bilal et al. [21]. Sumithra et al. [22] examined the interplay of inclined magnetic fields, Brownian motion, and thermophoresis within porous medium. The study conducted by Bilal et al. [23] focused on electromagnetic torsional nanofluid flow in concentric cylinders, incorporating the effects of nonlinear thermal radiation. Shamshuddin et al. [24] enhanced the heat transfer performance in MHD viscoplastic nanomaterials by considering ohmic heating and internal heat source.

Electroosmosis is the movement of a fluid driven by an electric field applied to charged surfaces, enabling precise flow control without mechanical pumps. This technology finds extensive application in micro- and nanoscale cooling systems, where meticulous fluid manipulation significantly improves heat transfer efficiency. Batool et al. [25] investigated the electro osmotically controlled thermal transfer of a micropolar fluid within a tapered porous conduit. Naheed et al. [26] investigated thermo-electroosmotic peristaltic transport. Akbar and Huang [27] conducted an analysis of radiative non-Newtonian nanofluid flow driven by the combined effects of peristalsis and electroosmosis. Noreen et al. [28] investigated electroosmotically propelled non-Newtonian fluids in a tapered porous conduit. Irfan et al. [29] conducted a comparative analysis of heat transfer in carbon nanotube-based nanofluids in both uniform and non-uniform channels, accounting for electroosmotic effects. Irfan et al. [30] conducted a theoretical investigation of therapeutic applications involving electroosmosis-driven silver nanofluid flow in compliant, nonuniform channels. Nazeer et al. [31] investigated the impact of zeta potential and slip boundary conditions on the heat transfer characteristics of hybrid nanofluids. The combined influences of radiative electroosmosis and surface roughness on the transport of biomedical hybrid nanofluids were documented by Irfan et al. [32]. Recently, Salawu et al. [33] examined energy optimisation in electroosmotic magnetised fluid flow within convective cooling microchannels.

This study introduces a novel mathematical model for /ethylene glycol hybrid nanofluid flow that has not yet been investigated in existing literature. It distinguishes itself by being the first to simultaneously integrate inclined magnetic field and thermal radiation effects, which are absent in previous comparative studies. By utilizing the Homotopy Perturbation Method to analyze an asymmetric geometry, the research uniquely accounts for the combined influences of electroosmosis, Hall current, viscous dissipation, and nanoparticle shape effects. This comprehensive approach allows for a total analysis of velocity, temperature, pressure rise, streamlines, skin friction, and Nusselt number, filling a significant gap in the multi-physics modeling of hybrid nanofluids. The findings will thus fill this specific gap and provide insights applicable to biomedical device design, cooling systems, microfluidic systems, and thermal management technologies.

Figure 4a illustrates that the temperature of the hybrid nanofluid falls with a spike in the volume fraction of copper nanoparticles. The primary reason is that elevating the volume fraction of nanomaterials enhances the thermal conductivity of the nanofluid. The substantial drop in temperature is the primary reason for employing nanoparticles as cooling agents in diverse engineering devices. The temperature curve rises as the quantity of the Brinkman number climbs, as demonstrated in Fig. 4b. Here fluid's internal resistance to flow converts mechanical energy into heat. As the fluid moves, friction occurs due to its viscosity, acting between layers of the fluid and between the fluid and any boundaries it encounters. Friction between the two surfaces generates heat, raising their temperature. In Fig. 4c, the temperature profile is inversely proportional to thermal radiation , since higher thermal radiation results in greater heat loss from the system. Figure 4d shows that the temperature curve is directly proportional to the heat source (); the presence of heat sources adds thermal energy, increasing the fluid's temperature. Figure 4e illustrates that spherical nanoparticles produce the highest fluid temperature, followed by tetrahedron, cylinder, platelet, blade, and lamina shapes. This order (sphere > tetrahedron > cylinder > platelet > blade > lamina) reflects the variation in heat transfer efficiency among different nanoparticle geometries. Spherical nanoparticles have a lower surface area-to-volume ratio, which limits their interaction with the base fluid and slows heat dissipation, leading to higher fluid temperatures. In contrast, non-spherical nanoparticles offer greater surface contact with the fluid, enhancing thermal conductivity and promoting heat diffusion, leading to comparatively lower temperature profiles. The nanoparticle shape factor significantly enhances effective thermal conductivity, making the model relevant for high-efficiency nanofluid coolants. Thermal radiation and internal heat source effects realistically represent high-temperature operating environments, such as electronic chips and solar collectors, whereas the Brinkman number accounts for viscous dissipation in high-shear flows.

As shown in Fig. 6a, the bolus size decreases along the left wall as the Hartmann number values elevate; on the other hand, a reverse trend is noted along the right wall. A spike in the Grashof number escalates skin friction at the right wall. On the other hand, it exhibits the reverse behaviour at the left wall. An increase in thermal radiation results in higher Nusselt numbers at both the right and left conduit walls due to a steeper temperature gradient between the wall and the fluid. The enhanced radiative energy absorption near the walls intensifies convective heat transfer. The effects of the Brinkman number () and internal heat generation are wall-specific. At the right wall, viscous dissipation combined with internal heat generation raises the local fluid temperature, increasing the temperature gradient and thereby enhancing heat transfer. Limitations and future scope

While the current study utilizes a two-dimensional formulation, transitioning to a three-dimensional model would facilitate a more realistic representation of intricate fluid interactions and three-dimensional flow characteristics.

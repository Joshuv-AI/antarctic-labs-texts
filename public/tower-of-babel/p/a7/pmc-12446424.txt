Action mechanism of suspending agent content on strength property of cemented backfill in coal mine
Authors: Haigang Yang, Rui Wang, Qiang Zhang, Shanshan Guo, Yukai Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn order to reduce the segregation degree of cemented backfilling slurry in coal mine and enable cemented backfill in coal mine (CBCM) to normally exert its load-bearing capacity, uniaxial compressive strength test, static segregation index test and microscopic properties test were carried out. The effect of suspending agent content (SAC) on the strength, static segregation index and microscopic properties of CBCM was discussed. The relationship between static segregation index and microscopic properties and backfill strength was analyzed. The results show that when SAC increases from 0 to 0.06%, the backfill strength increases first and then decreases, the static segregation index of backfilling slurry decreases by 46.81%, and the Ca(OH)2 content in backfill decreases by 24.51%. The increase of SAC can improve the strength of CBCM, reduce the segregation degree of backfilling slurry and reduce the hydration products inside the backfill. The backfill strength increases first mainly because SAC reduces the segregation degree of backfilling slurry, and the backfill strength decreases later mainly because SAC inhibits the hydration reaction inside the backfill.Keywords: Cemented backfill in coal mine, Strength property, Suspending agent content, Segregation, Microscopic propertiesSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

The main disposal methods for coal-based solid wastes such as coal gangue and fly ash include backfilling of goaf, utilization of building materials, manufacturing of chemical products, and extraction of high-value elements, etc1–3. Among them, backfilling of goaf best meets the actual needs of coal mines4. Cemented Backfill in Coal Mine (CBCM) is a mixture made of coal gangue, fly ash, cement, admixture and water in a certain proportion, which can be transported over long distances before coagulation and used to support the overlying rock in goaf after coagulation5,6. According to the state before and after coagulation, CBCM are divided into backfilling slurry and backfill. Placing CBCM in the goaf is conducive to maintaining the stability of the surrounding rock of the mining area in the short term and ensuring the normal propulsion of the working face. In the long run, the backfill can limit the surface settlement within a reasonable range, which is beneficial to environmentally friendly mining7–10. Although the advantages of backfilling mining in coal mines are significant, CBCM themselves have certain defects. The early backfilling slurry, as a suspension composed of solid particles and liquid, had relatively poor stability. Solid particles are prone to segregation, which leads to a serious lack of homogeneity in CBCM formed later, and further deeply affects the performance of its bearing capacity.

In response to the segregation problems and poor homogeneity of cement-based materials, many experts and scholars have conducted extensive research starting from the basic parameters of raw materials and achieved fruitful research results. The basic parameters of raw materials mainly include aggregate gradation11–13 fly ash content14–16 slurry concentration17,18 types of cementitious materials19 and slurry ratio20 etc. Cui et al.12 compared the effects of aggregate gradation, maximum aggregate size and mortar rheology on static segregation of high working concrete. Zhang et al.15 analyzed the effect of fly ash content on the anti-segregation performance of pumped paste backfilling slurry. Chen et al.17 analyzed the coupling effects of inlet velocity, particle mass concentration and particle size of cemented paste backfill material. Increasing the inlet velocity and decreasing the particle size could help to improve the uniformity of the slurry in the pipeline. Mehdipour et al.19 analyzed the changing characteristics of anti-segregation performance of self-compacting concrete equivalent mortar. The segregation of self-compacting concrete equivalent mortar was sensitive to the type of cemented material. Yim et al.20 made a cylindrical test mould to study the influence of matching ratio on the static segregation degree of self-compacting concrete.

Compared with regulating the basic parameters of the above-mentioned raw materials, adding suspending agent to cement-based materials is an efficient and economical approach. Common suspending agents include hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose (HPMC)21,22 cellulose ethers23–25 polyacrylamide26 emulsion powder27 and welan gum28 etc. Ma et al.21 analyzed the influence of the types and dosages of thickeners on the working performance of self-compacting concrete. The working performance of self-compacting concrete remained sensitive to all four types of thickeners. Isik and Ozkul23 analyzed the influence of the types and quantities of thickeners on the working performance of self-compacting concrete based on xanthan gum, welan gum and cellulose ether. A certain amount of thickeners can increase the slump of self-compacting concrete. Saric-coric et al.24 studied the influence of cellulose-based thickeners on the workability and mechanical properties of mortar. With the increase of thickener dosage, the bleeding rate of mortar decreased significantly. Patural et al.25 analyzed the effect of cellulose ether on water retention and consistency of cement-based mortar. Water retention was not directly linked to mortar’s viscosity. Zhang et al.26 used polyacrylamide as a thickener to study the influence of polyacrylamide on the basic properties of cement slurry. Zhang et al.28 analyzed the effects of welan gum and HPMC on the mortar properties. The change mechanism of welan gum and HPMC on mortar properties was discussed.

As the action mechanism of suspending agent content (SAC) on sthength of CBCM are still unclear, this paper’s research was carried out. In this paper, HPMC was used as the suspending agent to discuss the effect of SAC on strength, static segregation index and microscopic properties of CBCM. The relationship between the static segregation index and microstructure properties and the backfill strength was analyzed. The action mechanism of SAC on the strength of CBCM was revealed. Relevant research is helpful to control the ground surface subsidence within a reasonable range, is conducive to enriching the theory of cemented backfilling mining in coal mines and provides useful guidance for the preparation of other high-strength backfill.

Test method

Uniaxial compressive strength test

This uniaxial compressive strength test was carried out on the YDW-50 microcomputer-controlled electronic pressure testing machine. During the preparation of cemented backfilling slurry in coal mine, all the samples were made of raw materials from the same batch. According to the estimated number of samples, sufficient coal gangue, fly ash, cement, water and suspending agent were prepared at one time. The preparation of backfilling slurry strictly followed the GB/T50080-2016 Standard Test Method for Mix Performance of Ordinary Concrete30. The pouring, curing, grinding of the samples and compressive strength test strictly followed the GB/T50081-2019 Standard Test Method for Physical and Mechanical Properties of Concrete31. The average of the three test results was used as the final result.

The uniaxial compressive strength (σ) of CBCM was tested with SAC (ω) as the main factor and slurry concentration (η) and curing age (A) as the auxiliary factors. The test scheme is shown in Table 2. In Table 2, SAC was the proportion of the total mass of the backfilling slurry, and the curing temperature (t) of the backfill sample was 20℃.

Static segregation index test

The segregation performance of cemented backfilling slurry in coal mine was tested by columnar method32. The testing process was strictly in accordance with the ASTM C1610-14 Standard Test Method for Static Segregation of Self-Consolidating Concrete Using Column Technique of American Society for Materials and Testing33. The cylindrical test mold was filled with the well-mixed backfilling slurry, the top of the test mold was scraped, and the backfilling slurry in the test mold was placed for (15 ± 1) minutes, as shown in Fig. 3a. After placement, the top slurry (within a height range of 1/4 from the top) and the bottom slurry (within a height range of 1/4 from the bottom) were placed on the square holes sieve with an aperture of 4.75 mm respectively. The backfilling slurry on the square holes sieve was washed, as shown in Fig. 3b, until only coarse gangue remained on the square holes sieve. The coarse gangue was dried and weighed later.

Microscopic properties test

The common microscopic properties test mainly include X-ray diffraction test (XRD), scanning electron microscope test (SEM), mercury injection test (MIP) and thermogravimetric test (TG). This study mainly adopts SEM test and TG test.

(1) SEM test.

This SEM test was conducted on TESCAN MIRA LMS scanning electron microscope, as shown in Fig. 4. The electron gun adopts a Schottky field emission electron gun with a resolution of 2.0 nm@30 kV. The electron optical path is a non-crossing optical path of the electron beam in the tube. The acceleration voltage is 200 V-30 kV, the probe beam current is 1 pA-100 nA, the stability is better than 0.2%/h, and the Magnification is 8x-100,000x. The objective lens adopts electromagnetic/electrostatic composite lens, the detector adopts backscattering detector, and the gold spraying equipment adopts Oxford Quorum SC7620 sputtering coating instrument.

TESCAN MIRA LMS scanning electron microscope.

After the measured backfill reached the curing age of 28 d, it was placed in anhydrous ethanol solution for 72 h to terminate the hydration reaction inside the backfill. The backfill was then dried in a drying box at 60℃ for 48 h. The cutting part of the backfill was determined, and small pieces of the backfill with a diameter less than 1 cm were cut. The cut pieces were marked and sprayed with gold to carry out SEM test.

Microscopic performance samples were prepared with SAC as the test variable. SAC was set at 0, 0.02%, 0.04% and 0.06% respectively, which was the proportion of the total Mass of the backfill. The water-binder ratio was selected as 0.6. Since coal gangue hardly participates in the hydration reaction inside the backfill, and the large amount of organic components contained in coal gangue would affect the results of thermogravimetric tests, coal gangue was not added to the components when preparing microscopic test samples. Effect of SAC on backfill strength

The change curve of backfill strength with SAC is shown in Fig. 5.

Change curve of backfill strength with SAC.

As shown in Fig. 5, with the increase of SAC, the backfill strength generally shows a trend of increasing first and then decreasing, and SAC at the boundary between the increase and decrease is 0.04%. Taking the backfill with a slurry concentration of 77% and a curing age of 28 d as an example, the change law of backfill strength is analyzed. When SAC changes from 0 to 0.02% and the backfill strength changes from 1.67 MPa to 2.44 MPa, the increase is 46.11%. This indicates that the increase in SAC within this range can significantly improve the backfill strength. When SAC changes from 0.02 to 0.04% and the backfill strength changes from 2.44 MPa to 2.85 MPa, the increase is 16.80%. Although the growth rate of the backfill strength slows down, the increase in the backfill strength is still significant, meaning that SAC within this range still promotes the backfill strength. When SAC changes from 0.04 to 0.06%, and the backfill strength changes from 2.85 MPa to 2.67 MPa, the decrease is 6.32%. This indicates that when SAC exceeds a certain threshold, it will cause a decrease in the backfill strength.

Effect of SAC on static segregation index

The test results of the static segregation index are shown in Fig. 6. 1 represents the top of backfilling slurry and 3 represents the bottom of backfilling slurry in Fig. 6.

Test results of static segregation index.

As shown in Fig. 6, the mass of coarse gangue in the top slurry is different from that in the bottom slurry. When SAC is 0, the mass of coarse gangue in the top slurry is 0.61 kg, and that in the bottom slurry is 3.54 kg, showing a significant difference. When SAC is 0.06%, the mass of coarse gangue in the top slurry is 1.32 kg, and that in the bottom slurry is 2.91 kg. With the increase of SAC, the mass of coarse gangue in the top slurry increases, and that in the bottom slurry decreases. The mass difference between coarse gangue in the top slurry and that in the bottom slurry decreases, eventually reducing to 1.59 kg. The change curve of the static segregation index with SAC is shown in Fig. 7.

Change curve of static segregation index with SAC.

As shown in Fig. 7, when SAC is 0, the static segregation index is 1.41. When SAC is 0.06%, the static segregation index is 0.75, with a decrease of 46.81%. The decrease in the static segregation index is relatively obvious. With the increase of SAC, the static segregation index of the backfilling slurry decreases, indicating that the segregation degree of backfilling slurry decreases. The addition of suspending agent increases the viscous resistance, reduces the sedimentation rate, and weakens the segregation degree of solid particles inside the backfilling slurry.

With the increase of SAC, the particle size of the coarse gangue in the top slurry gradually approaches that in the bottom slurry, as shown in Fig. 6. Large coal gangue particles gradually appear in the top slurry, further indicating that the difference between the top slurry and the bottom slurry decreases. The backfilling slurry transforms from segregation to non-segregation, that is, the segregation degree of the backfilling slurry decreases.

Effect of SAC on microscopic properties

Effect of SAC on microstructure

The SEM test results are shown in Fig. 8.

SEM test results.

As shown in Fig. 8, when SAC is 0, the microstructure inside the backfill is relatively dense and has good integrity. The number of pores inside the backfill is limited, and the pore diameter is relatively fine. There are basically no large pore structures. The raw materials and hydration products are inlaid and distributed, indicating that the hydration reaction inside the backfill is Sufficient and a large amount of hydration products are generated. When SAC is 0.02%, the compactness of the microstructure inside the backfill decreases, and large pores appear locally. When SAC is 0.04%, the compactness of the internal microstructure of the backfill further decreases, and large pores appear in Many places. When SAC is 0.06%, the compactness of the microstructure inside the backfill decreases significantly. The number of pores inside the backfill increases, the pore diameter expands, and some pores have been connected into channels. Fly ash particles not wrapped by hydration products appear inside the backfill, indicating that the hydration reaction inside the backfill is insufficient and less hydration products are generated.

With the increase of SAC, the microstructure inside the backfill changes from dense to loose, the number of pores increases, the pore size expands, and the pores gradually transform into holes. The main reason is that SAC inhibits the formation of hydration products inside the backfill. The less hydration products are difficult to fill the pores inside the backfill, causing the internal structure of the backfill to become loose.

Effect of SAC on hydration products

The TG test results are shown in Fig. 9.

TG test results.

As shown in Fig. 9, TG and DTG curves under different SAC are approximately the same. The DTG curve contains a total of three peaks, indicating that three significant chemical reactions occurred during the heating process. The first peak is located between 50℃ and 150℃, mainly due to the evaporation of free water and the decomposition of some hydration products. The hydration products involved in the chemical reaction include ettringite, calcium silicate hydrate and aluminate carbohydrates, etc. The weight loss of the TG curve is mainly caused by the decomposition of calcium silicate hydrate. The second peak is located between 350℃ and 450℃, mainly due to the decomposition of Ca(OH)2, which decomposes into CaO and H2O. The third peak is located between 600℃ and 700℃, mainly due to the decomposition of CaCO3, which decomposes into CaO and CO2. The chemical reactions occurring within the second peak of the DTG curve are relatively few, which is more conducive to analyzing the effect of SAC on the hydration reaction inside the backfill.

The second peak of the DTG curve actually represents the mass loss of the sample, that is, the mass of H2O generated after the decomposition of Ca(OH)2. The second peak of DTG curve decreases Successively, when SAC is 0, 0.02%, 0.04% and 0.06% respectively. The reduction of generated H2O indicates a decrease in the Ca(OH)2 content inside the sample. The change curve of Ca(OH)2 content with SAC is shown in Fig. 10.

Change curve of Ca(OH)2 content with SAC.

As shown in Fig. 10, with the increase of SAC, the Ca(OH)2 content decreases. When SAC is 0, the Ca(OH)2 content is 11.48%. When the dosage of SAC is 0.06%, the Ca(OH)2 content is 8.66%, with a decrease of 24.51%. The decrease in the Ca(OH)2 content is relatively obvious. Ca(OH)2 mainly comes from the hydration reaction inside the backfill. The significant decrease in the Ca(OH)2 content indicates that the hydration reaction inside the backfill is severely inhibited, verifying the SEM test results. The calcium silicate hydrate, which determines the backfill strength, is generated synchronously with Ca(OH)2. The Ca(OH)2 content decreases significantly, and so do the content of calcium silicate hydrate.

Analysis and discussion

The lower the segregation degree of backfilling slurry, the higher the homogeneity of backfilling slurry or backfill. A large number of research results show that the lower the segregation degree of backfilling slurry, the greater the backfill strength13,18,35 and the less hydration products inside the backfill, the smaller the backfill strength36,37.

Relationship between static segregation index and backfill strength

Taking the backfill with a slurry concentration of 77% as an example, the change curve of static segregation index with SAC is shown as the red curve in Fig. 11, and the change curve of 28 d backfill strength with SAC is shown as the blue curve in Fig. 11.

Relationship between static segregation index and backfill strength.

The red curve in Fig. 11 shows that the static segregation index of the backfilling slurry decreases with the increase of SAC. The static segregation index of the backfilling slurry decreases, indicating that the segregation degree of solid particles in the backfilling slurry weakens. The displacement of the solid particles inside the backfill is small before and after the slurry condensation. All particles basically remain in the state when backfilling slurry was poured. The blue curve in Fig. 11 shows that the backfill strength first increases and then decreases with the increase of SAC. The backfill strength increases, mainly because SAC reduces the segregation degree of backfilling slurry.

Relationship between Ca(OH)2 content and backfill strength

Taking the backfill with a slurry concentration of 77% as an example, the change curve of Ca(OH)2 content with SAC is shown as the red curve in Fig. 12, and the change curve of 28 d backfill strength with SAC is shown as the blue curve in Fig. 12.

Relationship between Ca(OH)2 content and backfill strength.

The red curve in Fig. 12 shows that the Ca(OH)2 content inside the backfill decreases with the increase of SAC. The Ca(OH)2 content approximately decreases linearly without showing a slowing trend. The decrease in the Ca(OH)2 content indicates that the hydration reaction in the backfill is inhibited and the generation of various hydration products is insufficient. The blue curve in Fig. 12 shows that the backfill strength first increases and then decreases with the increase of SAC. The backfill strength decreases, mainly because SAC reduces the quantity of hydration products.

Action mechanism of SAC

The increase in the backfill strength is mainly due to the reduction of the segregation degree of the backfilling slurry by SAC. Under the condition of not adding a Suspending agent, the backfill strength is relatively low. This is Mainly due to the segregation of solid particles inside the slurry, causing large particles to accumulate at the bottom of the backfill, while Substances with smaller particle size and lower density appear in the middle and upper parts of the backfill. This disrupts the homogeneity of the backfill and leads to a decrease in its load-bearing capacity. When the Suspending agent is added, the homogeneity of the backfilling slurry is improved, causing the backfill strength to start to increase. Under the condition that SAC is less than 0.04%, the higher SAC, the more thoroughly the homogeneity of the slurry is improved, and the backfill strength will be higher accordingly. The subsequent reduction in the backfill strength is mainly due to the reduction of the amount of hydration products by SAC. Thus, the effect law of SAC on the backfill strength is formed, as shown in Fig. 5.

The addition of Suspending agent, on the one hand, can reduce the segregation degree of backfilling slurry, and on the other hand, can also inhibit the hydration reaction inside the backfill. The hydration products play a bonding role inside the backfill. The insufficient amount of hydration products inside the backfill leads to a significant decrease in the cohesion between aggregates. The strength of Materials Such as rock, soil and concrete is highly dependent on the internal friction Angle and cohesion. Insufficient cohesion Makes it difficult for CBCM to exhibit Sufficient load-bearing capacity. In the case of a low content, the segregation degree plays a dominant role in the backfill strength, causing the backfill strength to increase. When SAC exceeds 0.04%, the quantity of hydration products plays a dominant role in the backfill strength, causing the backfill strength to decrease.

Conclusions

In this study, the effect of SAC on the backfill strength, static segregation index, microstructure morphology and quantity of hydration products was analyzed through laboratory tests such as uniaxial compression test, static segregation index test and microscopic properties test. The following conclusions can be drawn.

(1) With the increase of SAC, the backfill strength generally shows a trend of first increasing and then decreasing. SAC within a certain quantity range can significantly improve the backfill strength. When SAC exceeds a certain threshold, the backfill strength begins to decrease.

(2) With the increase of SAC, the mass of coarse gangue in the top slurry increases, while that in the bottom slurry decreases. Meanwhile, the compactness of the microstructure inside the backfill decreases seriously, the number of pores inside the backfill increases, the pore diameter becomes larger, and the hydration products such as Ca(OH)2 decrease significantly.

(3) The addition of Suspending agent not only reduces the segregation degree of backfilling slurry but also inhibits the hydration reaction inside the backfill. When SAC is less than 0.04%, the reduction of segregation degree causes the backfill strength to increase. When SAC exceeds 0.04%, the reduction of hydration products causes the backfill strength to decrease.

In this paper, taking SAC as the main control factor, the effect law of SAC on strength of CBCM was obtained, and the action mechanism of SAC on strength of CBCM was revealed. When preparing CBCM, to prevent the segregation of solid particles inside the slurry, a certain content of HPMC can be added. The optimal content of HPMC is 0.04%. A large content of HPMC will lead to a significant decline in the bearing capacity of CBCM. The pore characteristics inside the backfill will also affect the bearing capacity of the backfill. This paper qualitatively describes the pore characteristics inside the backfill based on the SEM test results, without conducting quantitative analysis. In subsequent scientific research, quantitative experiments (such as mercury pressure tests, etc.) should be designed as much as possible for exploration.

Impact of early minimal residual disease assessment on the outcomes of patients with systemic light chain amyloidosis
Authors: Xiaozhe Li, Meilan Chen, Junru Liu, Jingli Gu, Beihui Huang, Lifen Kuang, Juan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSystemic light chain (AL) amyloidosis is associated with a high mortality rate, particularly in the first year after diagnosis. Minimal residual disease (MRD) assessment plays a key role in the prognosis of AL amyloidosis but is typically performed later in the treatment process, thereby limiting its ability to guide early interventions.Materials and methodsThis retrospective study included 53 newly diagnosed AL amyloidosis patients who underwent MRD assessment via flow cytometry after two chemotherapy cycles. The aim of this study was to assess the effect of early MRD negativity on organ response kinetics and prognosis.ResultsAfter two chemotherapy cycles, 22.6% (12/53) of patients achieved early MRD negativity. Daratumumab-treated patients had a significantly greater early MRD negativity rate (41.46%/0%, p = 0.019). Early MRD-negative patients had a significantly longer event-free survival (EFS) (not reached vs. 37.48 months, p = 0.029) than MRD-positive patients. In terms of organ response, early MRD-negative patients with cardiac or renal involvement had higher complete response (CR) rates and significantly shorter times to cardiac and renal CR (cardiac CR = 5.6 months vs. 28.4 months, p = 0.009; renal CR = 12.2 months vs. 41.0 months; p < 0.001).ConclusionsEarly MRD negativity predicts faster/deeper organ responses in AL amyloidosis patients and is associated with improved prognosis. These findings offer new possibilities for integrating early MRD assessment into clinical practice.Keywords: Daratumumab, minimal residual disease, systemic light chain amyloidosis

Introduction

Systemic light chain (AL) amyloidosis is a rare condition caused by the misfolding of monoclonal immunoglobulin light chains, which form amyloid fibrils that deposit in various organs, leading to structural damage, organ dysfunction, and disease progression. This condition is primarily linked to clonal plasma cell proliferation [1]. As effective methods for removing amyloid deposits are lacking, treatment primarily focuses on eliminating clonal plasma cells. Recent advances, particularly the clinical application of bortezomib and daratumumab, have significantly improved response speed and depth, resulting in a notable increase in survival rates over the past decade [2–4].

Hematologic response criteria for AL amyloidosis emphasize the reduction of free light chains (FLCs) and the disappearance of monoclonal proteins from the serum and urine [5]. However, relapses can still occur, and not all patients who achieve a hematologic complete response (hemCR) show organ responses. The CR rate in patients with AL amyloidosis is variable [6]. This raises the question of whether minimal residual disease (MRD) in the bone marrow after treatment contributes to the continued low-level production of FLCs, which may promote organ damage. Advances in plasma cell burden assessment, particularly via flow cytometry, have resulted in increased attention to MRD evaluation and its impact on the organ response and survival. Recent studies suggest that posttreatment MRD negativity is associated with better organ response rates and a better prognosis [7–10].

In AL amyloidosis, both the depth and speed of response are important [11]. In patients with advanced disease, particularly those with severe cardiac and renal involvement, slow hematologic responses may lead to organ progression before a response is achieved, adversely affecting the prognosis. In contrast, rapid responses may prevent this progression [12,13]. Therefore, achieving both deep and rapid hematologic responses is essential, and early assessment of these responses allows timely treatment adjustments, potentially saving lives [14]. Although several studies have reported an association between MRD negativity and organ response in AL amyloidosis, MRD assessments are typically performed later in the treatment process, often months or even years after a response has been achieved [7]. These assessments provide prognostic information posttreatment but do not guide early treatment decisions. Few studies have examined the feasibility and clinical applicability of early MRD assessment via flow cytometry during the treatment course of AL amyloidosis. Therefore, the aim of this study was to explore the clinical significance of early MRD negativity and its relationship with organ response kinetics and prognosis in newly diagnosed AL amyloidosis patients receiving first-line therapy with bortezomib or daratumumab.

Materials and methods

Patients

This study included 53 newly diagnosed AL amyloidosis patients who received bortezomib-based or daratumumab-based therapy between January 2020 and January 2024. All patients had assessable clonal disease, defined by a difference in the involved and uninvolved FLCs (dFLCs) >20 mg/L, an abnormal FLC ratio, and detectable abnormal plasma cell proliferation at diagnosis. MRD assessments were performed after 2 and 4 cycles of chemotherapy (approximately at the 2nd and 4th months) and at the end of first-line therapy. Patients who died between the 2-cycle and 4-cycle assessments were included in the analysis. The exclusion criteria included patients who died before the 2-cycle assessment, those with concurrent multiple myeloma (MM), and those with missing assessments after 2 chemotherapy cycles. Induction treatment choice was determined by physicians’ clinical judgment, patients’ overall condition, drug availability, and economic considerations at the time of treatment. This study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. Informed consent was obtained from all patients or their legal guardians.

MRD assessment

MRD testing was performed via flow cytometry on fresh bone marrow aspirates with antibodies against CD38, CD138, CD19, CD56, CD45, CD20, and kappa/lambda light chains. MRD positivity was defined as the presence of ≥20 events of phenotypically abnormal and light chain-restricted plasma cells, with a minimum of 2,000,000 analyzed nucleated events (sensitivity ≥1 × 10−5). Patients who achieved MRD negativity after two cycles of therapy were classified as early MRD negative, whereas those who achieved MRD negativity after four cycles were classified as late MRD negative.

Response evaluation and definitions

Hematologic and organ responses were evaluated at each treatment cycle and every 3–6 months during follow-up, according to consensus criteria [1,5,15]. Hematologic responses were categorized as a CR (hemCR), very good partial response (hemVGPR), partial response (hemPR), or no response (hemNR). Cardiac response and renal response were evaluated according to the corresponding consensus criteria and were categorized as graded responses. Specifically, cardiac CR was defined as an NT-proBNP level ≤350 pg/ml and renal CR was defined as a proteinuria level ≤200 mg/24 h. VGPR, PR and NR were defined as a reduction in NT-proBNP levels or proteinuria status from baseline levels of >60%, 30% to 60%, and <30%, respectively. In the binary classification, CR, VGPR, and PR were all categorized as organ responses. Event-free survival (EFS) was defined as the time from treatment initiation to hematologic or organ progression, or death from any cause, whichever occurred first, whereas overall survival (OS) was the time from treatment initiation to death.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were performed via SPSS version 26 (IBM, USA) and R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Continuous variables were analyzed via t tests for normally distributed data or the Mann–Whitney U test for nonnormally distributed data. Categorical variables were analyzed via the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. EFS and OS, as well as the impact of early MRD status on organ response kinetics, were analyzed via the Kaplan–Meier method, with comparisons of survival curves performed using the log-rank test. A p value of <0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 53 newly diagnosed AL amyloidosis patients were enrolled in this study between January 2020 and January 2024 at the Department of Haematology, The First Affiliated Hospital of Sun Yat-sen University. All patients had at least one bone marrow MRD assessment (sensitivity ≥1 × 10−5) after 2 chemotherapy cycles. The patient characteristics are summarized in Table 1. The median age was 59 years, and 66.04% of the patients were male. Among the patients, 66.04%, 84.91%, and 16.98% had cardiac, renal, and hepatic involvement, respectively. According to the Mayo staging system, 35.85% were stage I, 26.42% were stage II, and 37.74% were stage III. The median dFLC, NT-proBNP, and proteinuria levels were 86.05 mg/L, 445.00 pg/mL, and 2.68 g/24 h, respectively. The median number of chemotherapy cycles received was 4 (range: 2–8). A total of 17 patients (32.08%) received a bortezomib-based induction therapy regimen, 36 patients (67.92%) received a daratumumab-based induction therapy regimen, and 19 patients (35.85%) underwent ASCT.

Hematologic response

After two chemotherapy cycles, 21 patients (39.6%) achieved a hemCR, 16 patients (30.2%) achieved a hemVGPR, 7 patients (13.2%) achieved a hemPR, and 9 patients (17.0%) had hemNR. A total of 69.8% of patients achieved a hemVGPR or better. The MRD negativity rate for hemCR patients was 52.4% (11/21), whereas the MRD negativity rate for those who achieved a hemVGPR or better was 32.7% (12/37). After four chemotherapy cycles, 48 patients were evaluated for treatment efficacy, with 29 patients (60.4%) achieving a hemCR, 15 patients (31.3%) achieving a hemVGPR, and 5 patients (8.3%) showing a partial or no response. A total of 91.7% of patients achieved a hemVGPR or better. The MRD negativity rate in hemCR patients was 55.2% (16/29), and in hemVGPR or better patients, it was 36.4% (16/44).

MRD status and prognosis

The early MRD negativity rate was 22.6% (12 patients), with 77.4% (41 patients) failing to achieve early MRD negativity. Patients treated with daratumumab-based induction therapy had a significantly higher rate of early MRD negativity (41.46% vs. 0%, p = 0.019). The use of bortezomib alone did not achieve early MRD negativity, and patients who achieved early MRD negativity were more likely to undergo ASCT than were those who remained MRD positive (66.67% vs. 26.83%, p = 0.045). No significant differences were found in the other baseline characteristics. The findings of this study indicate that neither cytogenetic abnormalities nor Mayo 2004 staging had a significant influence on the likelihood of achieving early MRD negativity (Table 1). The median follow-up time was 25.6 months. Patients who achieved early MRD negativity after two chemotherapy cycles had a significantly longer EFS than MRD-positive patients did (not reached vs. 37.48 months, p = 0.029). Similar results were observed after four chemotherapy cycles (37.48 months vs. 39.13 months, p = 0.006). However, there was no significant difference in OS across all cohorts, as the median OS was not reached (Figure 1).

Since MRD status after 2 cycles of chemotherapy significantly influences patient prognosis, we analyzed the survival outcomes of patients who achieved early MRD negativity and compared them with those who did not achieve MRD negativity after 2 cycles but became MRD negative after 4 cycles. Among the 28 patients who achieved MRD negativity, 16 patients became MRD negative after 4 chemotherapy cycles; these patients were referred to as late MRD negative. A comparison of the median EFS between early MRD-negative patients and late MRD-negative patients revealed no significant difference (not reached vs. 39.13 months, p = 0.257).

Impact of early MRD negativity on organ function response

For first-line treatment, 12/34 (22.6%) patients achieved a cardiac CR, and 13/45 (24.5%) achieved a renal CR. Among patients with cardiac involvement, those who achieved early MRD negativity had a higher rate of a cardiac CR (62.5%, 5/8), with 100% of these patients achieving a cardiac VGPR or better. In contrast, those who did not achieve early MRD negativity had a lower cardiac CR rate (26.9%, 7/26) (p = 0.066), with 53.8% (12/26) achieving a cardiac VGPR or better (p = 0.017) (Figure 2). Among patients with renal involvement, 58.3% (7/12) of early MRD-positive patients achieved a renal CR, and 91.7% (11/12) achieved a renal VGPR or better, whereas only 18.2% (6/33) of early MRD-positive patients achieved a renal CR (p = 0.009), and 51.5% (17/33) achieved a renal VGPR or better (p = 0.014). The cumulative incidence curves for achieving a cardiac and renal CR during first-line treatment were significantly different between the early MRD-negative and early MRD-positive groups (cardiac p = 0.009, renal p < 0.001). Among patients who achieved a renal CR, the median time to renal CR in the early MRD-negative group was 12.2 months, whereas it was 41.0 months in the early MRD-positive group. Similarly, among patients who achieved a cardiac CR, the median time to a cardiac CR in the early MRD-negative group was 5.6 months, whereas it was 28.4 months in the early MRD-positive group (Figure 3). In the Mayo stage III subgroup, the early MRD-negative group had a significantly faster cardiac response (5.8 months vs. not reached, p = 0.009) (Figure 3). Moreover, when early MRD-negative patients were compared with late MRD-negative patients, early MRD negativity was associated with a significantly shorter time to achieve both a cardiac and renal CR. Specifically, early MRD-negative patients achieved a renal CR at 12.2 months compared with 41.0 months in late MRD-negative patients (p = 0.050) and a cardiac CR at 5.6 months compared with 28.4 months in late MRD-negative patients (p = 0.011) (Figure 4).

Comparison of early HemCR and early MRD negativity in organ response

We compared the prognostic value of early HemCR with that of early MRD negativity. Patients who achieved early HemCR after two chemotherapy cycles had a significantly longer EFS than those without HemCR (39.12 vs. 17.41 months, p = 0.001). However, when evaluating organ responses, the predictive power of early HemCR appeared limited. Among patients who ultimately achieved a cardiac CR, the median time to response was 21.88 months in the early HemCR group versus 28.42 months in the non-HemCR group (p = 0.081). Similarly, for renal CR, the median time was 23.56 months in the early HemCR group and 40.98 months in the non-HemCR group (p = 0.056) (Supplementary Figure S1). We also analyzed the association between early MRD negativity and organ responses in patients who achieving early HemCR. In the early HemCR subgroup(n = 21), among patients who achieved renal CR, the median time to response was 12.2 months in the early MRD-negative group but was not reached in the early MRD-positive group (p = 0.072). Likewise, for cardiac CR, the median time was 5.5 months in the early MRD-negative group compared with 21.8 months in the early MRD-positive group (p = 0.096) (Supplementary Figure S2).

Discussion

Over the past decade, with advancements in treatment, the median survival of patients with AL amyloidosis has nearly doubled. However, the mortality rate within the first year remains high [2], underscoring the importance of early and deep hematologic and organ responses in improving survival outcomes [14,16]. In the evaluation of therapeutic efficacy, MRD assessment via flow cytometry has garnered increasing attention because of its usefulness for detecting residual disease and predicting organ response and survival outcomes. Historically, MRD assessments have been performed at later stages, often 2 to 6 years after treatment completion, which may not fully reflect the prognostic value of MRD early in the treatment process [7]. Early MRD negativity could prevent organ deterioration in patients during the early stages of treatment. Thus, the aim of this study was to explore the effects of early MRD negativity on organ response and prognosis in newly diagnosed AL amyloidosis patients receiving first-line therapy with bortezomib or daratumumab.

Our data support the clinical applicability of flow cytometry-based MRD assessment early in treatment for AL amyloidosis patients. In the present study, patients who achieved MRD negativity after two cycles of therapy were classified as early MRD negative, whereas those who achieved MRD negativity after four cycles were classified as late MRD negative. All patients received bortezomib or daratumumab as part of their induction therapy, with 67.92% receiving daratumumab-based therapy, a greater proportion than in previous MRD studies [7,17]. After two chemotherapy cycles, the rate of a hemCR was 39.6%, which increased to 60.4% after four cycles. These findings suggest that current regimens, particularly the use of daratumumab, facilitate earlier hematologic remission, providing a solid foundation for MRD assessment [18,19].

Both two-cycle and four-cycle MRD assessments revealed that MRD could be detected in patients who achieved a hematologic CR. The MRD negativity rate among hemCR patients was 52.4% after two cycles and 55.2% after four cycles, which is consistent with previous reports [9,20]. Notably, no hemPR/NR patients were found in the MRD-negative group, suggesting that early MRD detection can uncover deeper residual disease and that MRD assessment remains feasible at different treatment stages with a consistent sensitivity threshold (10−5).

Furthermore, early MRD negativity was associated with a greater likelihood of achieving a cardiac and renal CR, as well as deeper organ responses, even in patients with advanced cardiac AL amyloidosis. Our study is one of the few to report the kinetics of organ responses in relation to early MRD negativity. Previous studies primarily used binary classifications for organ response, but our study’s inclusion of graded responses (CR, VGPR, PR, and NR) provides more detailed insights into improved organ function [21]. In addition to response rates, the cumulative incidence curves also indicated that the early MRD-negative group experienced faster and deeper organ responses than did the MRD-positive group. In our cohort, one-third of patients were in Mayo stage III, and even in this advanced population, early MRD negativity allowed a faster and deeper cardiac response. Rapid cardiac and renal responses are generally believed to be associated with better survival outcomes. While no difference was observed in OS because of the short follow-up time, early MRD negativity was associated with improved EFS, suggesting that achieving early MRD negativity is crucial in improving the prognosis of AL amyloidosis. Our findings indicate that early MRD negativity is linked to faster and deeper organ responses, suggesting that rapid MRD eradication could be key to improving prognosis and preventing irreversible organ damage. This ultimately helps patients avoid further treatment, organ deterioration, and even death, resulting in not only longer but also higher-quality life.

Furthermore, we compared the prognostic value of early HemCR and early MRD negativity. While early HemCR was associated with longer EFS, its ability to predict organ responses was substantially weaker than that of bone marrow MRD negativity, with no statistically significant differences in the median times to organ CR between the early HemCR and non-HemCR groups. In contrast, early MRD negativity demonstrated a much stronger predictive value for faster organ responses. We further compared organ response kinetics between patients with early MRD negativity and those with early MRD positivity within the subgroup of patients who had already achieved early HemCR. Due to the relatively small sample size, these comparisons did not reach statistical significance; however, there was a clear trend toward faster organ responses in the early MRD-negative group compared with the early MRD-positive group. These findings indicate that while early HemCR predicts improved EFS, MRD assessment provides superior prognostic information regarding the depth and kinetics of organ response. Importantly, MRD monitoring adds incremental clinical value by identifying patients more likely to experience timely organ responses, even among those who have already achieved HemCR. This distinction underscores the added value of early MRD assessment as a more reliable marker for predicting clinical outcomes in AL amyloidosis.

Moreover, when we compared the effects of early MRD negativity with late MRD negativity on the organ response, we found that delaying MRD negativity by two more chemotherapy cycles—i.e. achieving MRD negativity after four cycles—had clinical consequences. The extended period of active protein toxicity and amyloid fibril production may delay organ recovery. For late-stage patients, this delay may result in the loss of the crucial opportunity to combat the disease [16,22]. This highlights a major difference between systemic AL amyloidosis and MM, where slower responses may not be as harmful [23]. For AL amyloidosis patients, rapid MRD eradication is vital for preventing irreversible organ damage. The most effective and fastest-acting therapies should be used as early as possible rather than left for a later date [24].

Interestingly, in our study, patients treated with bortezomib alone did not achieve early MRD negativity, and early MRD negativity was observed only in the daratumumab-based induction therapy group. Additionally, patients who achieved early MRD negativity were more likely to undergo ASCT, which has been shown to prolong survival in AL amyloidosis patients [18,25–27]. Similar to the Andromeda phase III trial [28], which demonstrated that the combination of cyclophosphamide, bortezomib, and dexamethasone (CyBorD) with daratumumab in AL amyloidosis results in deeper and faster responses and improved survival, our findings suggest that emerging therapies capable of achieving deeper and faster responses should be considered for MRD-driven treatment guidance, much like MM treatment. This would involve adjusting therapies on the basis of early MRD status [29]. For complex diseases such as AL amyloidosis, treatment decisions need to be more personalized and focused on the dynamic changes in organ function and MRD status. In the UK, current guidelines recommend changing therapy for nonresponding patients or those with a suboptimal response after three months [3], but prospective data on the impact of early MRD status on therapy adjustments are lacking, and further investigations in this area are warranted.

Despite its strengths, this study has several limitations. This was a retrospective analysis, and the patient cohort was rigorously selected. Some patients with multiple organ involvement were excluded because they could not tolerate two full chemotherapy cycles. Additionally, there may be bias due to the different treatment approaches used in the early MRD-negative and MRD-positive groups. The relatively short follow-up time also represents a limitation of the present study, and longer follow-up will be required in future studies to more robustly validate the prognostic value of early MRD negativity. Finally, the small sample size, although typical for rare diseases such as AL amyloidosis, means that the findings should be validated in larger, multicenter studies.

In conclusion, our results indicate that early MRD negativity can lead to deeper and faster organ responses, improving patient prognosis. This study provides valuable insights into the relationship between early MRD status and organ response dynamics, offering new opportunities for integrating early MRD assessment into clinical practice and MRD-adapted treatment models. Future studies with larger cohorts are needed to address these findings and optimize therapeutic strategies for this challenging disease.

Comparative Study of Two Successive American Thyroid Association Risk Stratification Systems in Patients with Differentiated Thyroid Cancer in India
Authors: Malavika Satheesh, Malini Eapen, Usha V. Menon, Vasantha Nair, Niveditha Kartha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Introduction:The risk of recurrent/persistent disease (RD/PD) in differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) is predicted using American Thyroid Association (ATA) Risk Stratification System (RSS) guidelines - 2009 followed by revision in 2015, which necessitated a more detailed histopathology report which was not available in resource poor settings. A comparative study of the two systems was done to assess the change in risk status and the impact on outcome in a cohort of DTC patients.Methods:Clinico-pathologic parameters of 221 adults with DTC who had total thyroidectomy with subsequent radioactive iodine ablation were analysed to reassign risk category according to the ATA RSS 2009 and 2015, and the clinical end points were compared between both systems.Results:Among the 127 subjects in the intermediate risk category of ATA RSS-2009, when re-categorised under ATA RSS-2015, only 12 subjects (9.4%) had a change of risk status. Response to therapy at 1 year and final outcome were comparable among the similar risk categories, irrespective of the ATA system used. ATA RSS-2009 and ATA RSS-2015 were comparable in predicting the outcome at final follow-up.Conclusion:When risk status was reassigned with ATA RSS-2015, the study showed a shift in the intermediate risk category of ATA RSS-2009, but this was not statistically significant. Moreover, in predicting the outcome, ATA RSS 2015 was similar to ATA RSS-2009. This implies that the ATA RSS-2009 can be used for the initial risk stratification of patients in a resource-poor setting where the availability of complete histopathological data may be lacking.

INTRODUCTION

Worldwide incidence of thyroid carcinoma is rising, and differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) is currently the 5th most common cancer diagnosed among women in India.[1] As mortality is very low in DTC patients, long-term follow-up is mainly focused on treating PD and detecting recurrence of the cancer. Over the years, studies have recommended various risk factors and recurrent/persistent disease (RD/PD). The American Thyroid Association (ATA) has introduced one such Risk Stratification System (RSS) in 2009 and modified the same in 2015 [Supplementary Table 1].[2] The pathologist plays a crucial role in this risk stratification, especially the ATA RSS 2015, which requires the size of micro metastases in lymph nodes for risk stratification. However, in our part of the world, application of the ATA RSS 2015 is not always possible due to a lack of required histopathological details, and ATA 2009 is used despite concerns about its adequacy in predicting the risk of RD/PD. This retrospective study was undertaken to address this concern by re-examining the histology in order to reclassify patients who were already stratified using the RSS 2009 according to the ATA RSS 2015 and to evaluate the changes between ATARSS 2009 and 2015 that led to a significant modification in the clinical management.

The primary objective was to assess the change in risk status using the ATA 2009 and 2015 RSSs in a cohort of DTC patients after primary treatment with total thyroidectomy and radioactive iodine ablation. The secondary objective was to compare the performance of the two systems in predicting outcomes using clinical end points.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study participants were consecutive cases taken from the prospectively maintained thyroid carcinoma database, set up in 2010. The study consisted of 221 DTC patients, of age more than 18 years, who were followed and managed under the supervision of a single senior Endocrinologist at the Thyroid Cancer Clinic of our institute from 2010. Cases that underwent total thyroidectomy with subsequent radioactive iodine ablation, and completed a minimum follow up of 1 year from January 2010 to December 2020 were included. Cases in which blocks could not be retrieved and patients with insufficient data for risk stratification or for follow-up were excluded.

Technical information

Blocks and slides were retrieved from archives. For light microscopy, 3 μm sections were cut and stained with H and E. All the slides retrieved were seen and additionally reviewed by a senior pathologist, and all DTCs were reported defining the histological type, variants, presence of aggressive histology, vascular invasion, capsular invasion, extrathyroidal extension (ETE) and lymph nodal status.

Data was obtained from electronic medical records, which comprised the whole body scintigraphy scan/ultrasonography and biochemical parameters at the end of 1 year following the initial thyroidectomy and at the last follow-up. Thyroglobulin (Tg) level was determined by the electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Elecsys 2010-Cobas). Anti-Tg antibody (Tg-Ab) was assessed using the ARCHITECT anti-Tg assay (Abbott, USA) from 2010 to mid-2017 (upper limit 4.11 IU/mL). From July 2017, Tg-Ab was determined by Elecsys 2010-(Cobas) (measuring range: 10–4000 IU/mL, upper limit being 115 IU/mL). TSH was assayed using the ARCHITECT TSH assay from 2010 to mid-2017. From then on, the testing used the Elecsys 2010-(Cobas). The reference range is 0.005–100 μIU/mL with a functional sensitivity of 0.014 μIU/mL.

The outcome at the last follow-up was measured using clinical end points. They were defined as the following categories.[34]

No evidence of disease

Stimulated/suppressed Tg undetectable or <1 ng/ml + Tg antibody (Tg-Ab) absent + absent functional/structural evidence of disease.

Persistent biochemical disease

Suppressed Tg >1ng/mL or stimulated Tg >10 ng/mL + if Tg-Ab present, rising + No structural evidence on imaging.

Persistent structural disease

Local/regional/distant disease with any Tg/Tg-Ab levels

Indeterminate disease

Stimulated Tg >1 but <10ng/mL, Suppressed Tg level 0.2–1 ng/mL, Tg and Tg-Ab still detectable, but no significant rise (<20%). On imaging, stable thyroid bed lesions showing no features of malignancy, <1 cm stable lymph nodes in the neck, nuclear scan shows no evidence of malignant features or only negligible uptake in the neck.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Version 20.00 Chicago, USA software. Categorical variables were expressed using frequency and percentage. To test the statistical significance of the changes in the risk stratification of ATA RSS 2009 and ATA RSS 2015, McNemar’s Chi-square test was applied, and diagnostic measures were performed. To test the statistical significance of the association of extranodal extension (ENE), microscopic ETE, number of positive lymph nodes, largest nodal deposit, and foci of vascular invasion with clinical endpoint, the univariate Chi-square test was applied. To test the independent risk factors of persistent disease status, multivariate logistic regression was used. A P value of <0.05 was considered to be statistically significant.

Ethical aspects

The IRB for the study was submitted to the ethics committee of Amrita institute of medical sciences, Ponekkara and cleared for “IRB-AIMS-2020-003” on 21-01-2020 for the use of the patient data for research and educational purposes. The certificate has been attached. There was no written informed consent needed as this was a retrospective data analysis study. The procedures followed the guidelines laid down in Declaration of Helsinki 1964 and as revised later.

RESULTS

Our study cohort consisted of 221 patients who underwent total thyroidectomy followed by radioiodine ablation from January 2010 to December 2016 and were followed up in the thyroid cancer care clinic till December 2020. The minimum follow-up period was 4 years, and the maximum was 10 years.

Among the 221 patients included in this study (male: female 1:1.25), 192 (86.9%) were diagnosed in the 2nd to 5th decade of life (range, 18–77 years). The majority of them had presented with neck swelling (n = 182, 82.4%) while the rest were incidentally detected on imaging or had pressure symptoms [Table 1].

The ATA RSS-2009 and the ATA RSS-2015 guidelines were used to risk-stratify for RD/PD in this cohort. When ATA RSS-2009 was used, patients were classified as follows: 72 (32.6%) low risk, 127 (57.5%) intermediate and 22 (9.9%) high risk [Figure 1], whereas when ATA RSS-2015 was used, there were 76 (34%) cases under low, 115 (52%) cases under intermediate and 30 (14%) under high risk however this difference was not statistically significant (P > 0.05).

Risk stratification change according to the ATA RSS 2009 and 2015. ATA RSS = American Thyroid Association Risk Stratification System

When reassigned with ATA RSS-2015, among the 221 patients, only 12 patients in the intermediate group of ATA RSS-2009 had a change of their risk category (four patients became low risk (3.1%) and eight patients became high risk (6.3%)). Out of the four (3.1%) cases that were down-staged to low risk, two were due to micrometastasis <2 mm in the lymph nodes, and the other two were due to low volume of vascular invasion. The eight cases moved into high risk under ATA RSS-2015 due to the largest positive lymph node size ≥3 cm.

Among the pathological parameters assessed for association with persistence of disease at clinical end point, ENE and microscopic ETE were found to be statistically significant (P < 0.001) [Supplementary Table 2]. The multivariate logistic regression showed that ENE (odds ratio [OR] = 4.45, P = 0.001) and microscopic ETE (OR = 2.93, P = 0.013) were significant predictors of PD status [Supplementary Table 3].

DISCUSSION

In our study, cases belonged predominantly to the intermediate category under both 2009 and 2015 systems. Obtaining additional histopathology report details such as size of metastatic lymph node, size of the lymph node metastasis and number of vascular invasion did not change the risk status of 91.6% of cases. This overall proportion of change observed in the risk status of patients under ATA RSS-2009 and ATA RSS-2015 was seen to be of no statistical significance (P = not significant). The change seen in risk category in our study was more towards high risk than to low risk (3.1% vs. 6.3%). Previous studies by Lee et al. and Pitoia et al. however showed significant migration of patients, 13.5% and 30%, respectively, from the intermediate to low risk.[56] This was mainly due to the small number of metastatic lymph nodes in both studies, that could possibly be attributed to them being conducted in developed countries with early detection of cases. Lee et al. attributed the difference in number of cases migrating due to the unique characteristics of PTC in Korea. They have been found to have higher incidences of ETE and LN metastasis in their demography when compared to Western countries. They also postulated that since it was a higher tertiary referral hospital, aggressive PTCs may have been more in the study.[5] In our study however, a relatively smaller percentage of patients moved to low risk (3.1%) compared to high risk (6.3%). Moving to high risk was mainly due to larger sized lymph nodes at presentation. The reason for this is not clear and this study is not powered enough to make any valid conclusion on that.

Among the four cases that had moved to low risk, two were due to the presence of micrometastasis alone and the other two were due to low volume vascular invasion. Among the four cases that were found to have micrometastasis in lymph node, only two had a change of risk status from intermediate (ATA RSS-2009) to low risk under ATA RSS-2015, based solely on the nodal status. The other two cases had microscopic ETE, which led to their retention in the intermediate status in the ATA RSS-2015.

As part of our secondary objective, we compared the accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV of ATA RSS 2009 and 2015 in predicting disease free status at the last follow up using clinical end points. ATA RSS-2015 was found to have a marginally higher value for these parameters compared to ATA RSS-2009, but this was not statistically significant. A study by Lee et al. demonstrated that ATA RSS-2015 predicted the outcome of DTC patients only marginally better than ATA RSS-2009.[5] In view of the similar predictive prowess of the two systems, initial risk stratification done using ATA RSS-2009 appears to be an acceptable option in resource poor settings where detailed histopathological data may not be available. Of the patients in this cohort, those with node >3 cm, did not fare badly, there being only one patient out of the eight with PSD. It is possible that in our population large nodes, may not confer high risk as seen in others. However this requires further study.

Gross ETE was seen in only very few cases (4.5%). However, up to 30% cases showed microscopic ETE. This resulted in down staging of nearly half (47 cases) of T3 tumours under AJCC 7th to T1/T2 category under AJCC 8th edition since only gross ETE was retained to qualify as T3 stage in the latest edition This was an expected shift following the update as demonstrated by Nam et al.[7] The multivariate analysis which included ENE, microscopic ETE, number of positive lymph nodes, largest nodal deposit and foci of vascular invasion done with the clinical end point, showed only two parameters that had statistical significance which included ENE (OR = 4.45, P = 0.001) and microscopic ETE (OR = 2.93, P = 0.013). Study done by Kim et al. also highlights the adverse prognostic implications of microscopic ENE demonstrated by >10% recurrence rate in their cohort, despite the presence of five or fewer LNs.[8] They concluded that this justifies the intermediate risk that ENE holds in the current RSS. A study by Yin et al. demonstrated that a lower DFS rate and an increased recurrence rate were seen in patients with microscopic ETE compared with ones without ETE. They also observed that this group had larger tumour dimensions, frequent bilateral involvement, and higher rate of lymph node involvement.[9] Another retrospective study by Tran et al. also showed that presence of microscopic ETE conferred a 2.24-fold increased risk of recurrence for all ages (P = 0.009) along with a 2.76- fold increased risk in patients aged >55 years who had no distant metastasis (P = 0.002) when adjusted for tumour size.[10] This may again, need further study to see if population differences exist in the risk conferred by each of the factors used for risk stratification. We continue to use mETE as an intermediate risk factor till larger analysis on our patients show us otherwise.

Strengths and limitations

The Thyroid Cancer Clinic established in our institute in 2010, systematically and prospectively recorded all data regarding thyroid cancer patients with excellent surveillance, which helped in its easy retrieval for study. The perfect archival of slides and blocks at the Department of Pathology also aided in reviewing the cases for additional histopathological parameters.

Limitations of the study include the small number of patients and the lack of multicentric data. Many patients could not be included in the study due to the lack of accessibility to specimens for cases operated outside, as well as a lack of a standardized reporting format covering all aspects for the risk stratification in cases reported outside.

CONCLUSIONS

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study from the Indian subcontinent comparing the ATA RSS 2009 and 2015. Though there was a shift in the stratification, this was not statistically significant. Both the RSSs were found to be similar in predicting outcome in this study. This indicates that the ATA RSS-2009 may be used reliably for the initial risk stratification of patients in a resource-poor setting where only limited histopathological data may be available.

Clinical value of APN combined with Lp-PLA2 in predicting functional outcome in patients with cerebral infarction underwent rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis
Authors: Saifang Huang, He Chen, Xinli Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo analyze the clinical value of Adiponectin (APN) combined with lipoprotein phospholipase A2 (Lp-PLA2) in predicting the prognosis of patients with cerebral infarction undergoing intravenous thrombolysis with recombinant tissue- plasmin activator (rt-PA).MethodsTotal 100 patients admitted to our hospital with cerebral infarction underwent intravenous thrombolysis with rt-PA during June 2022 to June 2023 were chosen as the cerebral infarction group. The healthy control group were composed of 100 healthy people during the same period. The levels of APN and Lp-PLA2 was examined use enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Patients with cerebral infarction were divided into two groups based on different functional outcomes after 3 months of treatment using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score: 55 patients in the good outcome group (mRS score 0–2, patients independently complete daily activities without assistance from others) and 45 patients in the poor outcome group (mRS score 3–6). Multivariate logistic regression analysis was adopted to investigate the influencing factors. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to analyze the correlation. The predictive value of APN and Lp-PLA2 was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.ResultsPatients in the cerebral infarction group had much lower serum APN levels and higher Lp-PLA2 levels than those in the control group (P < 0.001). Patients were categorized into large artery atherosclerosis (LAA), cardioembolic embolism (CE), and small vessel disease (SVD) according to the TOAST classification. Lp-PLA2 levels were significantly higher in the LAA and SVD groups compared to the CE group (P < 0.05), while APN levels were significantly lower in these groups (P < 0.05). At three months post-intravenous thrombolysis with rt-PA, patients in the poor outcome group exhibited significantly higher values for age, pre-treatment NIHSS score, pre-stroke mRS score, maximum infarct diameter, serum Lp-PLA2 level, and total complication rate compared to those in the favorable outcome group. Serum APN levels were significantly lower in the poor outcome group. All differences were statistically significant (P < 0.01). Age, NIHSS score before thrombolysis, serum Lp-PLA2 and total complication rate were independent risk factors affecting adverse outcomes after rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis in patients with cerebral infarction (P < 0.05), and APN level was an independent protective factor (P < 0.05). Pearson correlation analysis showed a significant negative correlation between APN and mRS scores (r=-0.677, P < 0.001); Lp-PLA2 was significantly positively correlated with mRS score (r = 0.2334, P = 0.001). The ROC curve showed that the area under the curve (AUC) of serum APN and Lp-PLA2 for predicting functional outcomes after thrombolysis in cerebral infarction was 0.886 and 0.785, respectively. The AUC of serum APN + Lp-PLA2 was 0.913, with a sensitivity of 88.89% and a specificity of 81.82% (P < 0.05).ConclusionAbnormally expressed serum APN and Lp-PLA2 in patients with cerebral infarction were correlated with mRS scores. APN and Lp-PLA2 had a certain clinical value in predicting functional outcome after rt-PA intravenous thrombolytic therapy in patients with cerebral infarction.

Introduction

Cerebral infarction, also known as ischemic stroke, is a cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease with a high disability and mortality rate. Cerebral infarction is mainly induced by insufficient blood supply to the local brain tissue, resulting in ischemia, hypoxia, and damage to brain cells, leading to abnormal brain tissue function. With the acceleration of life pace in recent years and the worsening of population aging, the incidence of cerebral infarction has been increasing year by year. The rapid progression of cerebral infarction poses a serious threat to the patient’s life, health, and quality of life [1]. Recombinant tissue plasmin activator (rt-PA) intravenous thrombolysis is currently one of the most effective treatment methods for acute cerebral infarction, which can dissolve fibrinogen in the thrombus and reopen blocked blood vessels [2]. However, for patients with cerebral infarction, rt PA intravenous thrombolysis is not absolutely effective. Some patients may even experience worsening symptoms after medication, and in severe cases, fatal cerebral hemorrhage may occur. Therefore, early prediction of functional outcomes helps guide treatment plans and is crucial for improving patient prognosis [3]. How to accurately predict the functional outcomes of stroke treatment in the early stage has become a focus of attention for medical scholars.

The early screening, diagnosis, and prognosis evaluation of cerebral infarction mainly rely on clinical symptoms and imaging. Most evaluations are based on clinical suspected stroke symptoms combined with CT exclusion of intracranial hemorrhage for diagnosis, which has certain limitations. Previous studies have shown that [4], a variety of blood biomarkers are closely related to cerebral infarction, especially atherosclerotic cerebral infarction. Current research believes that [5], the occurrence of cerebral infarction is closely related to atherosclerosis. Lipoprotein associated phospholipase A2 (Lp-PLA2) is an inflammatory marker revealed in recent years, which can participate in the onset and progression of atherosclerosis by mediating the inflammatory reaction of vascular wall. Lp-PLA2 is associated with carotid atherosclerosis in patients with hypertensive stroke, and patients with high Lp-PLA2 levels have a poor prognosis [6]. There are also studies indicating that Lp-PLA2 is a vascular specific inflammatory marker and an independent risk factor for the occurrence of coronary heart disease and ischemic stroke [7]. Adiponectin (APN) is a member of the collagen family, which exerts an anti-atherosclerotic role by inhibiting the absorption of lipids by macrophages and the release of adhesion molecules [8]. Relevant data show that [9], APN can block the serious reaction of endothelial cells by inhibiting the production of atherosclerosis factors and down regulating the expression of endothelial cell adhesion molecules, thereby inhibiting the occurrence and development of atherosclerosis.

Currently, evidence on the use of adiponectin (APN) and lipoprotein-associated phospholipase A2 (Lp-PLA2) for predicting clinical outcomes in patients with cerebral infarction following intravenous thrombolysis with rt-PA remains limited. This study enrolled patients with cerebral infarction who underwent rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis at our institution to evaluate the combined clinical utility of APN and Lp-PLA2 in predicting functional outcomes after treatment. Additionally, a healthy control group was included to assess potential differences in baseline levels of APN and Lp-PLA2 between patients with cerebral infarction and healthy individuals.

Materials and methods

General information

This was a retrospective cohort study that consecutively enrolled 100 patients with cerebral infarction who received intravenous thrombolysis with rt-PA at our hospital between June 2022 and June 2023. These patients were included in the cerebral infarction group. Using existing medical records, baseline characteristics, treatment details, and 3-month follow-up outcomes—specifically modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores obtained via outpatient clinic visits or telephone interviews—were collected for all participants meeting the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria (Fig. 1). Inclusion criteria: (1) The patients were diagnosed with cerebral infarction through computer tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) examination, all of which were newly diagnosed. All patients underwent rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis treatment. Inclusion criteria: (2) Age between 18 and 80 years; (3) Time from the last known symptom-free state to initiation of thrombolytic therapy ≤ 4.5 h, in accordance with the 2018 Guidelines for the Early Management of Acute Ischemic Stroke [10]; (4) Presence of measurable neurological deficit, defined as a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score of 1–25; (5) Neurological symptoms persisting for at least 30 min without significant improvement prior to treatment; (6) Written informed consent obtained from the patient or their legally authorized representative; (7) Availability of complete clinical and follow-up data. Exclusion criteria: (1) History of intracranial hemorrhage, brain tumor, or cerebral vascular malformation; (2) Major surgery, severe trauma, or active bleeding (e.g., gastrointestinal ulcer bleeding) within the preceding 3 months; (3) Pre-thrombolysis platelet count < 100 × 10⁹/L, prothrombin time (PT) > 18 s, or international normalized ratio (INR) > 1.5; (4) Systolic blood pressure > 185 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure > 110 mmHg before thrombolysis that was difficult to control; (5) Receipt of mechanical thrombectomy (including bridging thrombectomy); (6) Severe hepatic or renal dysfunction, or terminal-stage malignancy; (7) Known hypersensitivity or allergy to rt-PA. The healthy control group were composed of 100 healthy people who came to our hospital for a physical examination during the same period. Patients with cerebral infarction were divided into two groups based on different functional outcomes after 3 months of treatment using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score: 55 patients in the favorable outcome group (mRS score 0–2, indicating that they could independently complete daily living activities without assistance from others) and 45 patients in the poor outcome group (mRS score 3–6, with scores of 3–4 indicating partial or complete dependence on others for care, score of 5 indicating severe disability, and score of 6 indicating death). All operations in this experiment have been ratified by the hospital Ethics Committee.

rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis treatment

rt-PA was produced by Boehringer Ingelheim in Germany, with specifications of 50 mg per vial and 21 mg per vial and the dose of 0.9 mg/kg. During treatment, 10% of the medication was first injected intravenously and completed within 1 min. Then, the remaining medication was micro pumped and completed within 1 h. All patients underwent head CT 24 h after treatment to exclude intracranial hemorrhage. Following the “Update on the Early Management of Patients With Acute Ischemic Stroke Guidelines” [11], antiplatelet therapy was initiated in all patients during the acute phase as secondary prevention. Oral enteric-coated aspirin tablets (Manufacturer: Bayer HealthCare Pharmaceuticals Inc.; Registration Number: J20130078; Specification: 100 mg) were administered using a standardized regimen: a loading dose of 300 mg/day for 10 consecutive days to achieve rapid and complete platelet inhibition, followed by a maintenance dose of 100 mg/day for an additional 2 weeks. This study was designed as a short-term prognostic investigation to evaluate the predictive value of biomarkers, with functional outcome at 3 months post-treatment as the primary endpoint. To ensure treatment protocol consistency and minimize potential confounding from anticoagulant use, all patients—including those with cardioembolic stroke—received the same antiplatelet regimen during the acute phase. This approach was implemented only after obtaining informed consent from patients or their legally authorized representatives and completing individual bleeding risk assessments. All participants completed the full 3-month follow-up period.

Management of cardioembolic stroke patients: Patients with cardioembolic etiology were included in the study cohort and analyzed through stratification according to TOAST etiologic classification. Since antiplatelet therapy (e.g., aspirin) was initiated 24 h after thrombolysis, prior use of antiplatelet agents did not serve as an exclusion criterion for patients with cardioembolic stroke.

Methods

Laboratory examination: Serum APN and Lp-PLA2 levels were examined using Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay. The test kits were purchased from Shanghai Xinyu Biotechnology Co., Ltd (Shanghai, China). The steps were as follows: 4 ml of fasting venous blood was collected from stroke patients admitted in the morning, and the control group was 4 ml of venous blood extracted during physical examination. The samples were centrifuged at 3,000 × g for 20 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was carefully collected, aliquoted, and immediately stored at -80 °C in an ultra-low temperature freezer until further analysis. All procedures were performed to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles in order to maintain the stability of the biomarkers. The supernatant was carefully collected, and should be centrifuged again if precipitation occurred during storage. Samples should be extracted as soon as possible after collection, and experiments should be conducted as soon as possible after extraction. Repeated freezing and thawing of collected sample were forbidden. Standard and sample wells were set. Each standard well was added with 50 µL of standard substance with different concentrations. The sample well was added with 100 µl diluted antigen. The plate was kept at 37℃ for 4 h, or 4℃ for 24 h. After discarding the liquid in the well, each well was sealed with 5% serum at 37℃ for 40 min. When sealing, bubbles in each well should be removed. After sealing, the well was washed three times with washing solution, each time for 3 min. A dilution ratio of 1:50 to 1:400 was generally used for sample testing. The diluted sample was added to each reaction well and placed at 37℃ for 40–60 min. At least two duplicate wells should be selected for per sample, with 100 µl per well. After incubating, the well was washed three times with washing solution, each time for 3 min. Then, 100 µl enzyme-linked antibodies were added to each reaction well and incubated at 37℃ for 30–60 min. After that, 100 µl of colorimetric solution was added into per well and place at 37℃ in the dark for 3–5 min, then by adding with the termination solution for colorimetric analysis. The OD value at 450 nm should be measured within 20 min.

NIHSS score: The NIHSS score range was 0–42 points, with higher scores indicating more severe neurological damage. The classificationwas as follows: mild (NIHSS ≤ 5 points), moderate (NIHSS 6–14 points), and severe (NIHSS ≥ 15 points) [12].

Data collection: The general information of patients was collected, including the age, gender, height, weight, body mass index (BMI), NIHSS score before thrombolytic, mRS score at 3 months after onset, history of cerebral infarction, cardiovascular history, hospitalization time, systolic and diastolic blood pressure, triglycerides, and the presence of hyperglycemia. Etiological classification was performed according to the TOAST (Trial of Org 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) criteria [13], which include large artery atherosclerosis (LAA), cardioembolic stroke (CE), small vessel disease (SVD), other determined etiology (OE), and undetermined etiology (UE).

Complication monitoring: All patients underwent head CT within 24 h after rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis. Intracranial hemorrhage-including both asymptomatic and symptomatic hemorrhage—was independently assessed and graded by two neuroimaging specialists based on the ECASS III criteria. Additionally, systemic bleeding events (e.g., gastrointestinal bleeding, subcutaneous ecchymosis) and vascular reocclusion (confirmed by clinical deterioration and follow-up CT or MRI) occurring within 72 h post-thrombolysis were systematically recorded. All complication data were reviewed and validated by attending physicians or those of higher rank.

Statistical analysis

The experimental data was analyzed using SPSS 20.0 software. Measurement data such as age, APN, Lp PLA2, etc. were expressed as (‾x ± s), and compared using t-test. Gender and other enumeration data were shown in the form of (%), and compared using use a χ2 test. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was adopted to investigate the influencing factors of the outcome of rt PA intravenous thrombolysis treatment for cerebral infarction. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to analyze the correlation between APN, Lp-PLA2 and mRS score. The value of APN and Lp-PLA2 in predicting functional outcome in patients with cerebral infarction undergoing rt PA intravenous thrombolysis was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The statistical results were statistically significant if P < 0.05.

Results

Comparison of baseline characteristics and serum APN and Lp-PLA2 levels between the two groups

The control group included 100 participants, comprising 55 males and 45 females, with a mean age of 62.15 ± 6.85 years. The cerebral infarction group also included 100 participants, comprising 60 males and 40 females, with a mean age of 61.85 ± 7.45 years. No significant differences were observed in age or sex distribution between the two groups (P > 0.05). Patients in the cerebral infarction group had much lower serum APN levels and higher Lp-PLA2 levels than those in the control group (P < 0.001, Table 1; Fig. 2).

Comparison of APN and Lp-PLA2 levels among patients with different TOAST subtypes

Patients were categorized into three groups according to TOAST classification: large artery atherosclerosis (LAA, n = 42), cardioembolic stroke (CE, n = 18), and small vessel disease (SVD, n = 25). Baseline serum levels of adiponectin (APN) and lipoprotein-associated phospholipase A2 (Lp-PLA2) were compared across the three groups. Both the LAA and SVD groups exhibited significantly higher Lp-PLA2 levels compared to the CE group (P < 0.05), while APN levels were significantly lower in these two groups than in the CE group (P < 0.05). Multivariate logistic regression analysis of influencing factors on the outcome of rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis treatment for cerebral infarction

Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that age, pre-thrombolysis NIHSS score, serum Lp-PLA2 level, and total complication incidence were independent predictors of poor outcomes following rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis in patients with cerebral infarction (P < 0.05), whereas APN level was an independent protective factor (P < 0.05). After adjusting for potential confounders-including age, pre-thrombolysis NIHSS score, pre-stroke mRS score, maximum infarct diameter, hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, and statin use-both APN and Lp-PLA2 remained significantly associated with functional outcome as independent protective and risk factors, respectively (both P < 0.001), indicating that their predictive values were robust and not influenced by these covariates. These results were presented in Table 5.

Value analysis of serum APN and Lp-PLA2 in predicting functional outcomes after thrombolysis in cerebral infarction

The ROC curve showed that the area under the curve (AUC) of serum APN and Lp-PLA2 for predicting functional outcomes after thrombolysis in cerebral infarction was 0.886 and 0.785, respectively, with sensitivity of 86.67% and 73.33%, specificity of 83.64% and 74.55%, respectively. The AUC of combined prediction was 0.913, with a sensitivity of 88.89% and a specificity of 81.82% (P < 0.05, Table 7; Fig. 4).

Discussion

Cerebral infarction is a cardiovascular and cerebrovascular disease caused by insufficient blood supply to some brain tissues, resulting in functional damage to some brain tissues. Cerebral thrombosis, cerebral embolism, and other factors were its main pathogenesis. Among them, cerebral infarction caused by cerebral thrombosis is the most common. Previous studies have found that the formation of cerebral thrombosis is closely related to atherosclerosis, and atherosclerosis and inflammatory reaction complement each other [14]. Early assessment of atherosclerosis and inflammatory reaction in patients with cerebral infarction may help predict the functional outcome of patients with cerebral infarction after treatment [14].

Lp-PLA2, also known as platelet activating factor acetylhydrolase, belongs to the phospholipase family and is mainly secreted by lymphocytes and macrophages, which can regulate lipid metabolism [15]. At present, it has been found that Lp-PLA2 can promote the formation and development of atherosclerosis by inducing hemolytic lecithin and free fatty acid mediated vascular wall inflammation. Lp-PLA2 can also promote endothelial dysfunction and plaque formation and instability by producing cytokines and adhesion factors, leading to plaque detachment and thrombus formation, resulting in acute cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases [16]. Kong et al. [17] believed that the level of Lp-PLA2 had a high clinical value in the prognosis of patients with carotid atherosclerotic stenosis, and is worthy of clinical application. APN mainly originates from adipose tissue and is released into the circulation, limiting the permeability of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) under physiological conditions [18]. Some studies have shown that APN can promote the phosphorylation of AMP activated protein kinase (AMPK) to prevent focal cerebral ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury in diabetes mice by combining with its receptors Adiponectin receptor 1 (AdipoR1) and Adiponectin receptor 2 (AdipoR2) [19]. In this experiment, the patients with cerebral infarction had significantly lower serum APN level and much higher Lp-PLA2 level than the healthy control group, indicating that there existed obvious atherosclerosis in patients with cerebral infarction. This study is the first to investigate the differential expression of adiponectin (APN) and lipoprotein-associated phospholipase A2 (Lp-PLA2) across stroke subtypes. Subgroup analysis showed that Lp-PLA2 levels were significantly higher in patients with large artery atherosclerosis (LAA) and small vessel disease (SVD) compared to those with cardioembolic stroke (CE), whereas APN levels were significantly lower. These findings were consistent with the established pathophysiological roles of the two biomarkers: Lp-PLA2, as a specific inflammatory marker of atherosclerosis, reflects vascular wall inflammation and plaque instability when elevated [20]; in contrast, APN exerts protective effects in atherosclerosis by inhibiting macrophage lipid uptake and endothelial inflammation [21]. The results suggest that the combined use of APN and Lp-PLA2 may have greater prognostic value for atherosclerotic stroke (LAA/SVD) than for cardioembolic stroke (CE).

This study found that age, NIHSS score before thrombolytic, hospitalization time, and serum Lp-PLA2 were independent risk factors affecting the adverse outcomes of rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis in patients with cerebral infarction. Elderly patients often experience a decline in the function of important organs such as the heart, lungs, and kidneys, leading to complications such as pulmonary infections and epilepsy. Compared with young patients, the cerebral arteriole walls of elderly patients exhibit deposition of beta amyloid peptides, which increases vascular fragility and the risk of bleeding transformation after thrombolysis, leading to poorer efficacy of intravenous thrombolysis [22]. The NIHSS score is an important indicator for evaluating neurological dysfunction in patients with acute cerebral infarction, which can effectively reflect the extent of neurological deficits in patients. The higher the NIHSS score, the larger the infarct area and the more severe the neurological deficits in the patient [23]. The longer the hospital stay is, the more severe the patient’s condition is, and the poorer their short-term prognosis is. The breakdown of serum Lp-PLA2 produces compounds that promote the inflammatory response of endothelial cells, such as oxidized free fatty acids and lysophosphatidylcholine. These compounds work together to induce the formation of related factors and apoptosis, thus inducing endothelial cell dysfunction, and making atherosclerotic plaque produce, rupture and release more Lp-PLA2, thus forming a vicious circle [24]. Therefore, Lp-PLA2 has certain value in evaluating the prognosis of cerebral infarction after thrombolytic therapy. Studies have confirmed that the level of APN is an independent protective factor for the prognosis of patients with cerebral infarction, which may be related to the anti-atherosclerosis and anti-inflammatory effects of APN. Thus, lower serum APN level leads to relatively rapid development of atheromatous plaque and more tolerant of falling off, resulting in adverse outcomes such as acute cerebrovascular events [25].

Cerebral infarction is a cardiovascular disease with a high mortality and disability rate. With the rapid development of medical technology in recent years, the mortality rate of cerebral infarction has relatively decreased. However, most patients with cerebral infarction were accompanied by neurological deficits, greatly reducing their quality of life. This not only increases patient pain, but also brings a heavy medical and economic burden to their families and society [26, 27]. The Pearson correlation analysis conducted in this experiment showed a significant negative correlation between APN and mRS score, and a significant positive correlation between Lp- PLA2 and mRS score. The result revealed a close correlation between APN, Lp-PLA2, and neurological deficits in patients with cerebral infarction after treatment. The reason might be that low APN and high Lp-PLA2 levels reflected the severity of atherosclerosis, which could increase plaque instability, thus aggravating the condition and neurological damage of patients [28, 29]. Therefore, early monitoring of changes in APN and Lp-PLA2 levels can help evaluate the neurological deficits in patients with cerebral infarction after treatment. Early evaluation of the functional outcomes and early implementation of corresponding intervention measures can help improve treatment effectiveness and prognosis. In clinical practice, patients with cerebral infarction need to undergo multiple serological tests, which have reference significance for diagnosis, treatment, and prognosis. However, the sensitivity and specificity of current clinical diagnostic tools for stroke were limited, and the positive rate for early stroke is low. So far, no “gold standard” serological indicators have been found [30]. Moreover, multiple biomarkers can be produced after cerebral infarction, and a single serologicalindicator is difficult to accurately and comprehensively reflect the complexity of the disease. Therefore, adopting joint testing will be an effective method to improve the diagnostic value of serological indicators [31]. APN level was closely associated with clinical outcomes in patients with dilated cardiomyopathy and advanced heart failure. Thus, APN may serve as a potential indicator for prognostic prediction in these patients [32]. There were also studies showing that the optimal value for predicting acute stroke death with serum Lp-PLA2 is 160.9 ng/ml, with sensitivity and specificity of 0.643 and 0.835, respectively, and AUC of 0.758. The AUC predicted by the combination of Lp-PLA2 and soluble lectin like oxidized low-density lipoprotein receptor-1 (sLOX-1) was 0.855, indicating that the levels of sLOX-1 or Lp-PLA2 can serve as useful biomarkers to predict the functional outcomes of ACI. The combination of the two indicators is superior to the use of any single indicator, providing the highest specificity in predicting poor prognosis. The ROC curves established in this study showed that the AUC of serum APN, Lp-PLA2, and their combined prediction of functional outcomes after thrombolysis in cerebral infarction were 0.886, 0.785, and 0.913, respectively. APN and Lp-PLA2 had certain predictive value in early prediction of functional outcomes after cerebral infarction treatment, but the combined predictive value of the two is better than single indicator. Combined detection helps physicians evaluate and provide effective treatment measures in the early stages, and is of great significance in improving patient prognosis.

In general, abnormally expressed serum APN and Lp-PLA2 in patients with cerebral infarction were correlated with mRS scores. APN and Lp-PLA2 had a certain clinical value in predicting functional outcome after rt-PA intravenous thrombolytic therapy in patients with cerebral infarction, which was worthy of promotion and application in clinic. The inclusion criteria of this study were based on current guidelines for intravenous thrombolysis in acute ischemic stroke, restricting the treatment window from symptom onset to thrombolysis to within 4.5 h. While emerging evidence has investigated the potential extension of this window to 4.5–6 h, particularly with individualized patient selection supported by advanced imaging modalities such as CT perfusion or MRI-based DWI/FLAIR mismatch, our institution’s standard clinical practice during this extended period continues to prioritize endovascular thrombectomy over conventional rt-PA intravenous thrombolysis. To maintain consistency in treatment protocols and adherence to established guidelines, patients presenting between 4.5 and 6 h after symptom onset were excluded from this analysis. Future research will aim to expand the sample size and prolong the study duration to further investigate outcomes in broader time windows.

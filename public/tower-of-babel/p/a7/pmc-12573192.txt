Quercetin as a Nutritional Intervention: Enhancing Growth, Metabolism, and Immune Gene Modulation in Labeo rohita
Authors: Showkat Ahmad Dar, Sarvendra Kumar, Ved Prakash Saini, Abhiman, Susmita Rani, Gowhar Iqbal, Sagarika Swain, Rajesh Kumar, Naresh Raj Keer, Aishwarya sharma
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The objective of the study was to evaluate the use of quercetin-supplemented diet as a growth, metabolism, and immunity promoter in Labeo rohita. Fish (8 ± 0.5 g) were randomly divided into five groups in triplicates (30 fish in each replicate) in circular tanks of 2000 L capacity. The fish were fed on a control diet (0% quercetin and four experimental diets supplemented with quercetin inclusion levels 0.2% (T1), 0.4% (T2), 0.8% (T3), and 1.6% (T4)). After the experimental period, the highest growth performance weight gain %, specific growth rate, protein energy ratio, and intestinal somatic index were recorded in the T4 group, followed by the T3 group. The lowest yield was recorded in the control group. However, the feed conversion ratio was significantly decreased with the 1.6 quercetin-supplemented group. The activity of digestive and metabolic enzymes of quercetin-fed groups was significantly different (p < 0.05) compared to the control group. In the treatment groups, there was a substantial increase (p < 0.05) in the relative expression levels of insulin-like growth factor-1 gene expression in the liver and muscle when quercetin supplementation was compared to the control group. The tumor necrosis factor-α, interleukin-1β, Toll-like receptor-22, and interferon-γ mRNA levels significantly (p < 0.05) increased with increasing quercetin levels, and highest expression was found in the T4 treatment group. Expression profiles of antioxidant-related genes also revealed significant difference (p < 0.05) between treatment and control groups. In the experimental challenge, the control group experienced higher mortality rates than the other groups following an intraperitoneal injection of Aeromonas hydrophila. Consequently, quercetin supplementation is recommended at a level of 1.6% to improve the growth performance, antioxidative capacity, and immune response of L. rohita.

Introduction

Aquaculture being the most important food producing sector plays a vital role in nutritional security, reduced poverty, as well as encouraging the socioeconomic status of the people. , Several growth promoters and hormones have been tested for improving the efficiency of feed and increasing productivity in the fish culture system. A comprehensive range of immunostimulants such as glucans, chitin, vitamins, microorganisms, and animal and plant extracts are being used by fish farmers. Till date, immunostimulants have gained increased interest due to their low cost, ease of dietary integration, and less environmental effect leading to sustainability. Dietary immunostimulant supplements in the diets of fish improve the growth, innate the defense mechanism, and provide greater resistance to infections. Safety and hygiene are the needs in fish culture for better growth performance. Attempts have been made by researchers to find and create safe dietary additives and supplements for enhancing the life activities, health status, and immune health of cultured fish. , Hence, use of immunostimulants and growth promoters may efficiently activate the immune system to counterattack diseases.

Phytochemicals fall under the broad spectrum of substances derived from plants that are frequently present in fruits, vegetables, cereals, beans, and plant-based beverages. , Phytochemicals can be categorized into different classes such as alkaloids, flavonoids, pigments, phenolic, terpenoids, essential oils, and steroids. According to reports, phytochemicals enhance a number of processes in fish, such as growth, consumption of feed, immunomodulator, antistress, and to support antimicrobial properties of fish. Bioactive compounds present in phytochemicals enhance the effectiveness of feed digestion and utilization, resulting in better growth and survival in the culture system

Quercetin is one such pigment which falls under the flavonoid group having two benzene rings joined by a core three-carbon atom to phenolic hydroxyl groups. The function of quercetin has been reported to lowering the blood pressure and also possess antioxidant and antitumor properties. Quercetin is a strong antioxidant, has the ability to scavenge free radicals, and bind transition metal ions. Several studies reported quercetin improved the immunity as well as the growth of the chicken and enhanced the color, texture, and overall acceptability in goat. There are several bioactive properties that have gained a lot of attention, such as antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, anticarcinogenic, and neuroprotective. , In aquatic animals, quercetin has been demonstrated to boost immunity and growth performance and reduce the lipid content in tilapia (Oreochromis spp.).

Quercetin has been found to play a crucial role in enhancing rat growth by stimulating protein synthesis, an essential process for muscle development and overall body growth. Studies have shown that quercetin exhibits anabolic effects, promoting the production of growth factors, such as insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), which aids in various aspects of fish growth such as cell proliferation, differentiation, and muscle hypertrophy. , Additionally, quercetin has also been associated with improved nutrient digestion and absorption in various animals, leading to enhanced feed utilization efficiency. Keeping in view the features of quercetin, the aim of the present study is to evaluate the growth, metabolic, hematological, and immunological characteristics as well as the survival rate of Labeo rohita fed on a quercetin supplement-based diet.

Materials and Methods

Ethical Statement

The animals used in this work were treated with care according to the procedures of the Committee for Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals, Ministry of Environment and Forests (Animal Welfare Division), Govt. of India, on care and use of animals in scientific research.

Experimental Setup and Experimental Design

The feeding trial of the experiment was carried out at the Laboratory College of Fisheries Kishanganj, Bihar. L. rohita fingerling for the experiment was procured from Prem Fish Farm, Purnia, India. Fish (weighing around 8 ± 0.5 g), 60–90 days of age, were acclimated for 15 days before the feeding trial. The average range for mean water temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, carbonate hardness, and total ammonia was found to be around 23 ± 2.0 °C, 6.1 ± 0.02 ppm, 7.2, 215–236 mg/L–1, and 0.10–0.14 mg/L, respectively, using the standard protocol.

Five isonitrogenous and isocaloric diets were formulated. Different levels of quercetin [(Sigma-Aldrich, USAsynthetic (organic grade))] (0.2%, 0.4%, 0.8%, and 1.6%) were mixed in the diets (Table ). The experimental trial consists of four treatment groups based on quercetin supplementation level: T1 (0.2%), T2 (0.4%), T3 (0.8%), T4 (1.6%), and control (0.0%) groups. The experiment was conducted in 15 circular tanks of 2000 L capacity with each treatment consisting of three replicates. After acclimatization, 30 fish (8 ± 0.5 g) 60–90 days of age were randomly distributed in each experimental tank and were fed daily twice with 3% body weight according to the treatment group for 60 days of experimental period.

Experimental Diets and Proximate Chemical Composition

Proximate Analysis

The proximate composition of experimental diets was evaluated as per the procedure. The preweighed experimental feed samples were kept in a hot air oven at 100 ± 2 °C until they reached a consistent weight in order to assess the moisture content in the feed. The micro-Kjeldhal method (FOSS) was used to estimate the content of crude protein (CP), the Soxhlet extraction system (Borosil India) was used to evaluate the crude lipid (EE), and a muffle furnace (550 °C for 5 h) was used to assess the total ash content, while nitrogen free extract (NFE) in the feed is calculated by the formula %NFE=100−(%moisture+%CF+%CP+%EE+%ash)

Measurement of Growth Performance and Feed Utilization

Growth-related parameters were calculated at the end of the experiment. Fish of all replicates were weighed, and body growth parameters were calculated based on following formulas Weightgain(g)=finalbodyweight(g)−initialweight(g) Weightgain(WG)%={(finalwetweight−initialwetweight)/initialwetweight}×100 Specificgrowthrate(SGR)=Ln[finalmeanbodyweight(g)]−Ln[initialmeanbodyweight(g)]×100/timeinterval(days) Feedconversionration(FCR)=dietintakeondrywetbasis(g)/wetweightgain(g) Proteinefficiencyratio(PER)=weightgain(g)−proteinintake(g)

Body Indices

After the end of the feeding trial hepato-somatic index (HSI), the intestinal-somatic index (ISI) was computed by using the following formula ISI%=weightoftheintestine(gm)wholebodyweightofthefish(gm)×100 HSI%=weightoftheliver(gm)wholebodyweightofthefish(gm)×100

Sample Collection and Preparation

Fish were anesthetized with clove oil (50 μL L–1). Blood samples from three fish from each treatment were collected in a 1 mL syringe, rinsed with ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid solution (2.7%), transferred directly to an Eppendorf tube containing ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (anticoagulant), and shaken gently to evade hemolysis of blood cells.

Separate tissue samples from the liver and gut were obtained for the enzyme assay estimation. Using a tissue homogenizer, the tissue samples were quickly homogenized (5%) in a cooled sucrose solution (0.25 M). For enzyme tests, the homogenized samples were centrifuged for 10 min at 4 °C and 6000 rpm. The supernatants were then collected and kept at −20 °C.

Isolation of RNA from Fish Tissue

For the gene expression study, six fish (n = 6) were taken from different treatments (two fish from each replicate); liver, kidney, muscle, and brain tissues were taken from each fish, preserved in RNAlater TM (Qiagen, Netherlands), and stored at −80 °C. Total RNA was isolated in accordance with the manufacture’s protocol from liver tissue (about 50 mg) after homogenizing it with 1 mL of TRIzol (Invitrogen, USA). The concentration of RNA and purity (260/280) were checked in a Nano drop spectrophotometer (Thermo Scientific USA). DNase treatment to the RNA samples was provided with a DNase kit (Fermentas, USA) according to the protocol given by the manufacturer for the removal of any possible genomic DNA contamination.

cDNA Synthesis and Quantitative Real-Time PCR

First-strand cDNA was synthesized from total RNA (1 μg) and oligodT primers as per the manufacturer’s protocol of cDNA synthesis kit (Thermo Scientific, USA). The synthesized cDNA was stored at −20 °C for expression studies in real-time PCR. Quantitative real-time PCR was used to evaluate the expression of genes associated with growth, immunity, and antioxidants after the generated cDNA was mixed with the SYBR Green master mix (Fermentas, USA) detection reagents. β-Actin gene was used as the internal control for the expression study. The primers used in this study (Table ) were designed for the genes. The quantified value of mRNA was expressed in terms of CT (threshold cycle) value. The relative expression of mRNA was determined using the CT values of the reference and target genes. Every PCR reaction was conducted in duplicate, and the 2–ΔΔCT method was utilized for calculation. Formulas used for the calculation of quantified results are as follows Δct=targetgenectvalue−ctvalueofthereferencegene ΔΔctvalue=Δctvalueofthetreatmentgroup−Δctvalueofthecontrolgroup

Serum Total Protein, Albumin (A), Globulin (G), and A/G Ratio

Immunological parameters like serum protein, albumin (G), globulin, and A/G ratio (Erba Diagnostic Mannheim; Biomedicals Ltd., India) were estimated. The value of globulin was evaluated by deducting albumin values from the total serum protein.

Tissue Protein

The total protein of each tissue sample was assessed using the Bradford (1976) method in order to prepare it for enzyme assay. Tissue protein was calculated by using the bovine serum albumin protein standard curve.

Digestive Enzyme Assay

The casein digestion method was used to measure the protease enzyme’s activity. Protease activity is expressed as the amount of enzyme required to release acid-soluble fragments equivalent to Δ0.001 A280 of per minute at 37 °C. The amylase activity of intestinal tissue was assessed with 2% (w/v) starch solution as the substrate that was made in phosphate buffer at pH 7. Determination of amylase activity was calculated by using the maltose standard curve, and the unit of enzyme activity was expressed as mole of maltose released from starch/min/mg protein at 37 °C. The lipase activity was determined by applying Cherry and Crandell’s technique. Olive oil emulsion, phosphate buffer solution (pH 7), tissue homogenate, and distilled water were used to make the reaction combination. The activity of the enzyme unit/mg protein was measured using the milli-equivalent of alkali consumed.

Liver Enzyme Assay

The liver enzyme assay studied in the current experiment was aspartate amino transferase (AST), alanine amino transferase (ALT), and lactate dehydrogenase activity (LDH). The AST and ALT activity was assayed in different tissue homogenates as indicated by Wooten. The substrate used in the ALT enzyme test was l-alanine and α-ketoglutarate, whereas the substrate used in the AST enzyme assay was l-aspartic acid and α-ketoglutarate. Enzyme activity was expressed as nanomoles oxaloacetate formed/min/mg protein at 37 °C. The LDH activity was determined using the method mentioned in ref . At 30 s intervals, the absorbance was measured for 3 min at 340 nm. At 25 °C, the enzyme unit’s activity was represented as mg protein–1 min–1.

Challenge Study

At the end of the study, fish in each group were challenged with pathogenic Aeromonas hydrophila . A 0.1 mL dose of 24 h saline from virulent bacterial pathogen of A. hydrophila (5 × 105 cells/mL) was given by intraperitoneal injection. All groups were kept under observation for 10 days to record clinical signs and daily mortality rate.

Calculation of Relative Percentage Survival

Relative percentage of survival (RPS) versus control was calculated at the end of 14 days of infection using the following formula (Amend 1981) RPS=[1−(%mortalityinthevaccinatedgroup/%mortalityinthecontrolgroup)]×100

Statistical Analysis

The data were expressed as mean ± standard error. Statistical significance was analyzed using one-way analysis of variance via IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 22.1 (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) to compare the mean. One-way analysis of variance was used to analyze all the data, and Duncan’s multiple range test was performed to conduct the post hoc analysis. The value of significance at 5% level (p < 0.05).

Discussion

Nutraceuticals hold the key in future for enhanced fish production in the light of growing population. A growing demand has been seen in creating eco-friendly and sustainable methods in recent years to enhance fish production by incorporation of nutraceuticals in fish feed for meeting the ever-increasing global protein demand. , In the present experimental study, dietary nutraceutical supplementation of quercetin @ 0.2% (T1), 0.4% (T2), 0.8% (T3), and 1.6% (T4) for 60 days has shown significant increase in the growth performance of L. rohita, exhibiting the best growth performance recorded in the T4 treatment group. In a similar study, flounder (Paralichthys olivaceus) given a diet supplemented with quercetin for 30 and 60 days experienced a weight growth that was significantly higher than that of the control group. The lowest FCR was found in the group with quercetin supplementation of 1.6% having highest SGR and PER compared to control and other treatment groups. Li et al. have also indicated that dietary quercetin supplementation had a positive effect on the WG, SGR, and FCR in Nile tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus). The effect of quercetin in IMC, L. rohita is yet to be explored, so the present study found that quercetin supplementation from 0.2 to 1.6% has shown positive effect in the growth of fish because quercetin exists in the body as a glycone and may be important in encouraging growth in fish as similar things were earlier revealed by refs − . This indicates that the positive impact of quercetin on fish growth performance as quercetin supplementation in fish feed reveals feed intake, nutrient utilization, and protein synthesis, leading to enhanced growth rates, weight gain, and improved feed conversion ratios.

A potential explanation for quercetin’s growth-promoting effect on fish performance could be its outstanding capacity to stimulate the release of digestive enzymes such as lipase, amylase, and protease. In the current study, the digestive enzymes protease, amylase, and lipase activities in intestine were significantly enhanced after feeding L. rohita quercetin-supplemented diet for 60 days. Our results were similar those found by ref (O. niloticus), and Ghafarifarsani in Cyprinus carpio revealed an increase in digestive enzyme activity after supplementation of quercetin in fish feed. Findings on the effect of digestive enzyme activity are limited; however, in Anguilla rostrata, the digestive enzyme activity was increased by isoflavone. The enhanced activity of digestive enzymes induced by quercetin may have stimulated the increased rate of digestion and absorption of nutrients, which results in improved fish growth of L. rohita .

The mechanism of quercetin in promoting the digestive enzyme activity may be associated with its antioxidant properties. Supplementation with quercetin may protect the digestive system from oxidative damage brought on by an excess of free radicals. , According to Canli et al., one of the most monitored biomarkers for fish health is the liver enzymes ALT and AST. In this study, the liver enzymes such as ALT, AST, and LDH were significantly lower in fish fed with quercetin-containing feed than fish fed with a control diet. The study’s findings are consistent with studies that showed common carp (C. carpio) had lower liver enzyme levels when fed with quercetin-supplemented diets.

Incorporating serum biochemical measures, the physiological condition of L. rohita used in the experiment was calculated. Svobodova et al. stated that these traits are also utilized to assess feed composition and nutritional status with respect to the fish’s environment. The level of total protein, albumin, and globulin was found to be significantly higher in the T4 group compared to the other treatment groups. Our results are similar to the findings of ref who also revealed a significant increase in total protein, albumin, and globulin in Nile tilapia, O. niloticus fed with a diet supplemented with quercetin. Additionally, it is believed that fish with higher blood protein and albumin levels have a more robust innate immune response.

The growth is intermediated by hepatic derived IGF-1 gene expression and the interaction between GH and its receptors, which regulates the mechanism of growth in fish. , The current study revealed increases in the expression of growth-related genes in liver tissues after the quercetin supplementation on regulation of IGF-1, GH, and GHRH gene expression in L. rohita . Our findings were similar to Zhu who found higher expression levels of growth genes in dark sleeper Odontobutis potamophila . The expression levels were found more in the liver compared to the muscle tissue as liver is the main organ where maximum IGF-1 expression is found. Several researchers , found the expression of IGF-1 is more expressed in liver compared to muscle tissue. GH via ghrelin, after binding to its receptor GHRH, induces a variety of growth-related and metabolic processes, both directly and through the action of IGF-1. , The expression of ghrelin gene was also found to be higher in T4 treatment compared to control and other treatment groups, which reveals its contribution toward the growth of fish. Dar et al. found the mechanism of involvement of ghrelin gene in the growth of L. rohita regarded as a GHRH helper and modulator in controlling GH secretion and the impact of ghrelin via IGF-1 to promote fish growth.

The quercetin supplementation in the current study may be beneficial as a dietary supplement due to its immune-boosting qualities. As a cytokine, TNF is a key player in promoting white blood cell immune system release in fish immune systems that are prone to inflammation. Additionally, according to Corripio-Miyar et al., IL-1β is crucial for the host’s reaction to microbial invasion, tissue injury, and compromised immunological response. Increase in the expression levels of immune genes was found upregulated with the addition of quercetin content in feed compared to control. Highest expression levels were found in T4 having 1.5 mg/kg of quercetin supplementation. Wang et al. provided confirmation for our findings by describing an increase in immune gene expression in zebrafish (Danio rerio) after exposed to water treatment of 10 quercetin μg/L. Meanwhile, Kong et al. also observed that the treatment of quercetin significantly upregulated (P < 0.05) the expression of TNF-α, interleukin-8, interleukin-10, and IL-1β in the kidney of Channa argus. As TLR22, a typical member of the fish-specific TLRs is a critical sensor in virally prompted innate immune signaling preserved from natural selection. The present study revealed an increase in the expression of TLR22 after diet supplementation with quercetin with highest expression found in 1.6% supplementation. Ying stated that the Toll-like receptor signaling pathway of quercetin regulates avian beta-defensin in the ileum of Arbor Acre broilers. Thus, quercetin supplementation played the role of an immune booster for enhancing the immunity genes in L. rohita .

Quercetin is identified as a powerful antioxidant capable of preventing inducing biological damage by acting as a pro-oxidant. − This study shows that supplementing the diet with quercetin could successfully modulate the expression of some cytokine-related genes in the tissues of L. rohita, in addition to enhancing immune response. In the current study, we found that dietary supplementation of quercetin at 0.8% (T3) and 1.6% (T4) significantly upregulated the expression of SOD and Gpx. Our findings were confirmed by ref which also reported that feeding rats with quercetin supplementation increased the expression of the SOD and GPx genes, and by ref in common carp (C. carpio). According to ref , GSH and CAT in fish are two significant antioxidant enzymes that can scavenge hydroxyl radicals. The GSH activity was comparatively high (P < 0.05) in both groups when fed with 0.8% and 1.6% of quercetin-supplemented diets. An upregulated trend was found in the expression levels of GSH with an increase in the levels of quercetin levels in a supplemented diet, while CAT gene expression shows some variation. Zhu supported our findings of elevated GSH expression levels in dark sleeper O. potamophila following quercetin treatment. Furthermore, Alia revealed that quercetin increased the expression of various antioxidant genes and the content of GSH, which enhanced the status of the antioxidant system. As a result, we propose that the increased expression of antioxidant genes in L. rohita tissues may be related to suppressing endogenous degradation of scavenge reactive oxygen species and playing a role in protecting cells from oxygen free radicals. The results of our study on L. rohita survival rates following A. hydrophila exposure showed increased fish resistance in treatments with quercetin supplements, proving that the supplement stimulates fish immune systems. Our findings were supported by Ibrahim who also observed boosted survival rate in Nile tilapia (O. niloticus) against A. hydrophila.

In conclusion, our findings indicate that dietary quercetin can enhance immunological function, growth, and antioxidant status via controlling the expression of immune-related genes. Dietary supplementation of quercetin @1.6% promotes the growth, immunity, and survivability of L. rohita. Thus, the present results show that a suitable level of quercetin supplementation promotes immunostimulant properties, growth, antioxidant indices, and disease resistance of L. rohita . Quercetin is, therefore, regarded as a promising natural feed additive to fish. Further feeding trials on field conditions are needed to assess sustained effects on growth performance, survival rates, and tissue integrity.

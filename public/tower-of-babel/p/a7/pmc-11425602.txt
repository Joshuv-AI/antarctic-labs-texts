Numerical Simulation Study on Flue Gas-Assisted Steam Huff and Puff in Heavy Oil Reservoirs
Authors: Linpu Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
In response to challenges such as the rapid rise of water content, the rapid decline of periodic production, and the serious intrusion of edge and bottom water in heavy oil reservoirs after multicycle profile control, the flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff technology is proposed. By focusing on Block Cao128 in the L Oilfield as a research subject, the feasibility of applying the flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff technology in heavy oil reservoirs has been verified through the establishment of a three-dimensional geological model. Additionally, the injection and production parameters in steam huff and puff have been optimized. The research results indicate that when the cyclic steam injection volume is 1000 t, the maximum net oil increment can be achieved, reaching 6308 × 104 t. Furthermore, when the cyclic flue gas injection volume is 9 × 104 Nm3, the efficacy of the flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff is enhanced. The optimal injection method is to inject flue gas in the early stage of steam huff and puff, and the oil–gas ratio is increased to 1.77. The introduction of flue gas can enhance production significantly and exert a significant impact on early-stage production. The earlier the injection time of flue gas, the better the development effect. Flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff technology improves energy utilization efficiency and reduces pollutant emission. It has a significant positive impact on environmental protection and sustainable development.

Introduction

Heavy oil has inherent limitations, such as high viscosity and poor fluidity. Considering that the viscosity of heavy oil is highly sensitive to temperature, steam huff and puff is one of the most effective process techniques in the development of heavy oil. However, there are still many challenges encountered in steam injection development, such as the rapid decrease of steam dryness with increasing migration distance during the seepage process.1 Moreover, due to the limitation of the heat enthalpy value of the heated steam itself, the steam injection development technology has some disadvantages such as a small heat transfer range, low heat transfer efficiency, and a poor viscosity reduction effect. Overcoming heat transfer in deep heavy oil reservoirs remains a significant obstacle.

For the steam huff and puff process of heavy oil reservoirs, the main contradiction in development is that after multicycle steam huff and puff, the water content increases rapidly, attributable to edge and bottom water influence, and periodic production declines rapidly, resulting in a gradual decrease in the daily oil production and the oil–gas ratio of a single well.2−4 Heavy oil reservoirs are greatly affected by edge and bottom water. With the process of multiple steam huffs and puffs, the intrusion of edge and bottom water may be intensified, resulting in a rapid increase of water cut in oil well output. In the process of steam huff and puff, the movement of water at the edge and bottom and the change in pressure will promote the movement of water to the well, thus reducing the oil recovery rate. With the increase of the steam huff and puff cycle, the daily oil production and the oil–gas ratio of a single well usually decrease gradually. This is caused by the change of physical properties inside the reservoir, the movement of the oil–water interface, and the reduction of reservoir pressure, which directly affect the effective production capacity of the well. The waterline on the plane continues to advance, causing strip flooding; vertically, bottom water invades the oil layer in the form of secondary bottom water and has a production characteristic similar to bottom water coning in the middle and later stages of development. After the optimization of profile control technology, the initial effect is obvious. However, with the increase of steam injection cycles, the effect becomes worse and the effect of profile control on oil wells that have already entered high water content is not significant; thus, a different approach is needed to enhance oil recovery.5−8

Facing these challenges, in order to realize the engineering objectives for environmentally friendly and efficient heavy oil reservoir development, the innovative development technology of the flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff has been proposed.9−12 This technology improves development efficiency by increasing energy, reducing water content, and reducing emissions. Additionally, this technology achieves the goal of environmentally friendly efficiency and holds promising application prospects. By utilizing composite heat fluids to improve thermal efficiency and implementing a technique referred to as composite heat fluid flooding oil recovery, this technology effectively boosts production while simultaneously reducing costs associated with such reservoirs.13,14

In order to clarify the synergistic mechanism of flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff and quantitatively characterize the cooperation capability, a numerical simulation model for heavy oil reservoirs was established. Based on the model, the adaptability of flue gas-assisted huff and puff was analyzed and the screening conditions were clarified.15−19 Additionally, this study investigates the process parameters of the flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff technology in heavy oil reservoirs, including oil–gas ratio, pressure, and injection–production ratio.

According to the specific screening criteria for the flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff, the Cao128 Sha3 reservoir possesses suitable characteristics for implementing flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff. These screening criteria include the following parameters: heavy oil viscosity < 100,000 mPa·s, burial depth < 2000 m, reservoir thickness > 4 m, porosity ≥ 20%, permeability ≥ 200 mD, and oil saturation ≥ 45%.

Establishment of a 3D Reservoir Sector Model for Heavy Oil Reservoirs

Grid Division and Attribute Model Building

Block Cao128 Section Sha3 in the L Oilfield was chosen as the research subject, and a comprehensive numerical simulation model of the entire area was established. Based on historical well data, the oil-bearing area of Section Sha3 is 1.06 km2, and the geological reserves are 319.8 × 104 t. Among them, the oil-bearing area of the Es31 reservoir is 1.06 km2, and the geological reserves are 202.2 × 104 t. The oil-bearing area of the Es32 reservoir is 0.69 km2, and the geological reserves are 117.6 × 104 t. Affected by active edge water invasion, the water content of oil wells in the area was generally higher, generally more than 85%. Due to the invasion of edge water and other factors, the development effect of thermal recovery gradually deteriorates. Therefore, based on the research on the mechanism of flue gas-assisted steam thermal recovery, a feasibility study and scheme design for the use of the flue gas-assisted steam huff and puff in Block Cao128 were carried out.

This study utilizes CMG for numerical simulation research, which is a comprehensive software platform used for simulating the development and management of oil fields. CMG comprises multiple modules for geological modeling, fluid flow simulation, reservoir property simulation, and enhanced oil recovery technique optimization. The primary module used in this paper is the STARS module. The STARS module is a tool used for simulating thermal fluid flow and heat conduction in reservoirs. The component model and nine-point central difference method were adopted. The component model usually refers to a model that describes the quantities and properties of different chemical substances or components in a system. These models can be mathematical expressions or algorithms used to describe processes such as reaction, transfer, and phase equilibrium. The nine-point central difference method is a finite difference method usually used to solve two-dimensional Poisson’s equations or heat conduction equations. The basic idea is to estimate the value of the target point by performing numerical calculations on eight neighboring points around the target point. The advantage of this method is that it has high precision and is suitable for solving high-precision problems. However, due to the need to use eight neighboring points for calculation, its computational complexity is relatively large. When using the nine-point central difference method for calculation, it is necessary to ensure that the grid size used is small enough to ensure the accuracy of the calculation. At the same time, it is also necessary to pay attention to the processing of boundary conditions to ensure the closure and continuity of the entire computing domain.

The modeling process is as follows: the plane was discretized into 141 grids in the X direction and 45 grids in the Y direction, each with a grid spacing of 20 m. Vertically, it was divided into 27 layers based on the actual situation of encountering sand bodies during drilling in the target layer of Block Cao128 Section Sha3 (including 21 oil layers/Es311–Es324 and six interlayers). The total number of nodes amounts to 171,315, out of which there were 112,424 effective nodes.21−24 Trajectory data and perforation data for 25 directional wells (including horizontal wells), as well as parameters related to thermal conductivity properties, volumetric heat capacity properties, and high-temperature oil–water phase permeability, were inputted. Additionally, monthly data and perforation data of 46 directional wells in the Block Cao128 Es311–Es324 layer were inputted to establish a dynamic model. The results are shown in Figures 1 and 2.

The remaining oil saturation at the top of the reservoir is high, while the remaining oil saturation at the bottom is low. The bottom crude oil may be more easily extracted due to gravity or the impact of mining methods, so the remaining oil saturation is lower.36−43

Feasibility Study of Flue Gas-Assisted Steam Huff and Puff

The flue gas refers to the smoke flowing in the chimney or smoke exhaust duct. This flue gas is usually generated by burning fuels, such as coal, oil, natural gas, etc. Factors such as the temperature, composition, and flow rate of the flue gas depend on the type of fuel, the efficiency of the combustion process, and the design of the smoke extraction system. In addition, flue gas can also be used as a resource under certain conditions. For example, in the process of heavy oil recovery, flue gas, due to its specific physical and chemical properties, can be used to assist in steam injection thermal oil recovery, improve oil recovery efficiency, reduce steam consumption, and help promote the low-carbon green development of heavy oil and improve quality and efficiency.

The content of CO2 in flue gas is 17%, N2 content is 81%, and O2 content is 2%. CO2 is the main component that works, which undergoes changes in properties when the temperature exceeds 31.26 °C and the pressure surpasses 7.29 MPa, exhibiting fluid-like density and gas-like viscosity. As a result, it demonstrates strong seepage and the ability to dissolve various substances. Figure 11 illustrates the impact of CO2 injection on heavy oil properties.

Effect of the CO2 injection amount on the physical properties of heavy oil.

In a certain range, with the continuous increase of the level of CO2 injection, the physical properties of heavy oil have undergone a series of significant changes. During this process, noncondensate gas plays a crucial role in the extraction of alkane, enabling the effective extraction of alkane components in heavy oil and thereby altering its chemical composition. At the same time, the dissolution of CO2 reduces the viscosity of the heavy oil and significantly improves its fluidity. In addition, the expansion effect of CO2 causes the volume of crude oil to expand, which further improves the fluidity of the heavy oil. At the same time, with the increase of CO2 injection from 0.1 to 0.2 PV, the oil–gas ratio gradually increased and the interfacial tension gradually decreased. The triple effect of reducing viscosity, increasing oil–gas ratio, and reducing interfacial tension makes heavy oil more easily recoverable.44,45 Therefore, by reasonably controlling the injection amount of CO2, the physical properties of heavy oil can be effectively regulated so as to optimize the exploitation process of heavy oil and improve the exploitation efficiency and economic benefits. At the same time, it also provides a new way and method for low-carbon green development, quality improvement, and efficiency enhancement of heavy oil recovery.

Optimization of Injection and Production Parameters of Flue Gas-Assisted Steam Huff and Puff

The initial cyclic steam injection for the Block Cao128 Ping3 well was 2000 t, with each cycle consisting of steam injection, wellbore soaking, and production lasting for 800 days. The cyclic flue gas injection volume was set at 70,000 Nm3. After operating continuously for four cycles, the net increase in oil production was calculated, and the injection and production parameters were optimized.

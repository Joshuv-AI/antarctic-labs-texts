Dual-channel deep learning captures intratumoural heterogeneity on CECT for preoperative risk stratification of thymic epithelial tumors
Authors: Xin Pan, Wencan Wang, Xinyu Li, Lingyu Zhang, Shiwen Li, Xuhui Zhao, Qian Zhang, Huajie Yue, Haomiao Ma, Li Liang, Yanhong Hao, Hua Zhang, Chunhai Yu, Jianxin Zhang, Lina Wang, Lingjie Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryAccurate preoperative risk stratification is critical for treating thymic epithelial tumors (TETs). This study developed a deep learning framework that combines a dual-channel convolutional neural network (CNN) with an adaptive dynamic clustering algorithm. The model was trained on contrast-enhanced CT (CECT) images from 336 multicenter TET patients. It first automates the segmentation of tumor subregions. Then, it constructs dual-channel input data containing the largest cross-sectional ROI and its corresponding habitat masks. Using transfer learning, we trained four CNN architectures for risk stratification. The DenseNet121-based dual-channel CNN achieved an AUC of 0.74–0.76 on an external test set. This performance surpassed conventional radiomics, single-channel CNN, and radiologists’ visual assessment. Our framework effectively captures intratumoral heterogeneity, improves risk stratification accuracy, and aids in rapid identification of high-risk patients. This approach can support personalized treatment planning.

Introduction

Thymic epithelial tumours (TETs), the predominant primary neoplasms of the anterior mediastinum, demonstrate a biological continuum spanning indolent thymomas to aggressive thymic carcinomas.1,2 The World Health Organization classification system categorizes TETs into distinct histological subtypes (A, AB, B1–B3, and thymic carcinoma) that correlate with malignant potential and inform clinical decision-making.3,4 Current clinical practice has adopted a simplified risk stratification framework that classifies tumors as low-risk (A/AB/B1) or high-risk (B2/B3/thymic carcinoma) to guide therapeutic interventions.5,6,7

Accurate preoperative risk stratification is critical for optimizing surgical planning, adjuvant therapy selection, and prognosis prediction.8 The current diagnostic paradigms rely on invasive histopathological biopsies and subjective imaging interpretations. Although biopsies provide diagnostic certainty, they carry risks of procedural complications (e.g., pneumothorax and tumor seeding) and sampling errors due to intratumoural heterogeneity (ITH).9,10 Moreover, conventional imaging interpretation is heavily reliant on radiologists and requires extensive expertize to identify subtle imaging biomarkers for pathological classification, which complements objective measurements of tumor size, locoregional invasion, and anatomical relationships.9

Although conventional contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) can accurately delineate tumor margins, vascular involvement, and mediastinal structural relationships, it cannot reliably distinguish between the pathological classification and staging of TETs.11 Emerging computational approaches show promise for the preoperative pathological classification of TETs. Radiomic analyses of CT images have demonstrated moderate discriminatory power,12 improving when combined with conventional CT features. CECT-based models integrating radiomic features with clinical variables achieved superior performance in differentiating thymomas from benign cysts.13 Deep learning architectures have shown exceptional performance in pathological subtyping, particularly when fused with radiomic features.5,14,15 However, these approaches have a critical limitation in that they fail to account for ITH, a fundamental biological characteristic manifested through spatial variations in tumor cellularity, vascularity, and genomic profiles,16,17,18,19 which is strongly associated with tumor aggressiveness and therapeutic resistance.20,21

To address these limitations, we propose a novel dual-channel convolutional neural network (CNN) architecture that explicitly encodes ITH patterns from preoperative CECT images for the preoperative risk stratification of TETs. Our innovation lies in the integration of spatial heterogeneity mapping with deep learning through three synergistic pathways: (1) automated ITH-aware subregion segmentation, (2) dual-channel spatial heterogeneity image fusion, and (3) transfer learning-enhanced CNN optimization. This approach transcends the constraints of both subjective visual interpretation and non-spatial radiomic analyses, potentially providing clinicians with an objective and labor-saving tool for personalized therapeutic decision-making.

The overall process of this study

The process included image preprocessing; model construction; and construction of a DL model using dual-channel CT-based images as inputs to various pre-trained models (ResNet18, Vit, Vgg11, and DenseNet121). The output of this model predicted the probability of LRT and HRT. As well as combining DC_DLM with CRVM, RM, and 2D_DLM, three fusion models were constructed. Finally, the models were evaluated.

Radiologists’ conventional visual interpretation

Multicenter analysis of baseline clinical/radiological characteristics assessed by radiologists in 336 patients with TET revealed no significant differences between the LRT and HRT groups in terms of age, enhancement patterns, necrosis, or calcification (all p > 0.05). Notable differences included sex distribution in the internal validation set and external test set 1 (p < 0.05), enhancement degree in the training set and external test set 1, and great vessel invasion in external test set 2. Within the training set, lymphadenopathy and pleural invasion differed significantly between the risk groups, while tumor contour changes were significant across all cohorts (all p < 0.05). Table S3 details the performance metrics for the CRVM utilizing the six machine learning algorithms. The ExtraTrees model demonstrated superior performance among risk stratification models, with AUC values of 0.91 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.86, 0.96) in training, 0.70 (95% CI: 0.54, 0.86) in validation, 0.70 (95% CI: 0.54, 0.86) in external test set 1, and 0.68 (95% CI: 0.50, 0.86) in external test set 2 (Figure S4).

Radiomics analysis

From 1,834 radiomic features, 26 key predictors were selected (Figure S2), comprising one shape, nine first-order, and 16 texture features. Spearman’s correlation analysis confirmed minimal collinearity, mitigating redundancy-related performance bias. Table S4 summarizes six radiomic-based machine learning models, with the XGBoost-derived radiomic model (RM) achieving perfect discrimination in training (AUC: 1.00, 95% CI: 1.00, 1.00) and AUC values of 0.74 (95% CI: 0.59, 0.88), 0.59 (95% CI: 0.46, 0.72), and 0.62 (95% CI: 0.45, 0.78) in validation, external test set 1, and external test set 2, respectively (Figure S3).

Deep learning model performance

Table S5 presents the ROC analyses for the 2D deep learning models (2D_DLM) using various CNN architectures. Densenet121 exhibited the lowest loss and the fastest convergence (Figure S8), achieving AUC values of 0.82 (95% CI: 0.78, 0.87) in training, 0.75 (95% CI: 0.61, 0.90) in validation, 0.70 (95% CI: 0.57, 0.82) in external test set 1, and 0.69 (95% CI: 0.57, 0.82) in external test set 2 (Figure S5).

Dual-channel deep learning models (DC_DLM), as detailed in Table S6, also demonstrated superior performance with Densenet121 (Figure S6), with the lowest loss and fastest convergence (Figure S9), yielding AUC values of 0.91 (95% CI: 0.88, 0.94), 0.70 (95% CI: 0.53, 0.87), 0.76 (95% CI: 0.65, 0.88), and 0.74 (95% CI: 0.59, 0.89) across corresponding datasets. In external testing, the outperformed the 2D_DLM, RM, and CRVM models (Figure 3). Calibration curves and decision curve analysis (DCA) confirmed the superior calibration and clinical utility of the compared to the other models (Figures 4 and 5). Discussion

In this study, we established a deep learning-based predictive framework that systematically decodes ITH through multichannel integration of CECT images. The optimized dual-channel DenseNet121 architecture demonstrated robust discriminative performance in preoperative risk stratification of TETs in two geographically distinct external validation cohorts. This approach demonstrated the highest performance in non-invasive preoperative risk prediction for TETs, surpassing the 2D single-channel DenseNet121 architecture, expert radiologists’ visual interpretations, and conventional radiomic approaches.

ITH, a pivotal biological hallmark of tumor evolution, has been extensively evaluated using radiological modalities for preoperative risk stratification and the prediction of therapeutic response in oncology. Recent studies have demonstrated that CT-derived ITH scores can serve as robust prognostic indicators for non-small cell lung cancer and enable risk stratification in thymoma management.20,22 Single-modality radiomics studies typically employ radiomic feature clustering algorithms to delineate tumor subregions with distinct phenotypic characteristics, followed by a comprehensive quantification of spatial heterogeneity patterns. However, this methodological paradigm has two limitations. First, voxel-level radiomics feature extraction imposes a significant computational burden, which may pose challenges to clinical implementation. Second, conventional clustering methods such as K-means often rely on predefined cluster numbers when dealing with diverse tumor samples, which may lead to biologically implausible annotations of intra-tumour subregions, potentially distorting histopathological correlations. Shi et al.17 developed an integrative analytical framework combining supervoxel segmentation with radiomic feature analysis to systematically evaluate ITH patterns in breast cancer MRI. This approach demonstrated excellent performance in predicting pathologic complete response following neoadjuvant chemotherapy in patients with breast cancer in multicenter external test datasets. However, the supervoxel approach offers a reduction in computational complexity compared to voxel-level radiomic feature analysis. While its algorithmic pipeline involves transforming optimal radiomic feature cluster numbers into an ITH index, this process introduces several data conversion steps that may impact biological interpretability and measurement accuracy.

The integration of multimodal imaging provides dual-channel insights into tumor architecture, function, and metabolism, enabling a more comprehensive characterization of the tumor microenvironment, including angiogenesis, cellular heterogeneity, and immune infiltration. This approach effectively compensates for the limitations of single-modality imaging. For instance, Wu et al. demonstrated that clustering subregions based on PET SUV values and CECT images can generate tumor subregions with distinct imaging features, significantly improving the prediction of oropharyngeal cancer progression.23 Similarly, recent studies have shown that tumor subregions derived from the multimodal clustering of MR T2-FLAIR, T1 post-contrast, perfusion, diffusion, and PET images are strongly correlated with disease-specific histopathological features.24 A critical limitation lies in the requirement for precise image registration across modalities, which may introduce geometric distortions or signal mismatches, thereby compromising the accuracy of the subregional feature extraction. This issue is particularly pronounced in thoracic and abdominal tumors, where respiratory motion artifacts frequently degrade the registration performance. Consequently, most existing studies on multimodal tumor habitat have focused on head and neck tumors, where motion artifacts are minimal.

While conventional CECT remains indispensable in thoracic oncology for non-invasive evaluation of tumor morphology and vascular enhancement characteristics,25,26,27 its ability to decode ITH is inherently limited. These limitations become especially apparent in TET management as challenges persist in preoperative risk stratification and guidance for precision therapy selection. Recent studies22,28 have shown that radiomic frameworks incorporating 3D/2D deep convolutional neural networks (DCNNs) hold promise for improving ITH analysis and TET risk categorization. However, these methodologies continue to face limitations in analyzing single-modality tumor subregions.

The present study introduces an adaptive dynamic clustering algorithm for the automated segmentation of tumor subregions within contrast-enhanced CT images. By iteratively analyzing spatio-intensity distribution patterns, the algorithm autonomously determines optimal subregion partitions, overcoming the limitations of conventional fixed-parameter methods and reducing the computational burden. This technique synergistically integrates spatial attributes (texture gradients and edge continuity) with intensity metrics (signal contrast and voxel distributions) through iterative refinement, enabling precise delineation of biologically distinct intratumoural habitats. The resulting functional maps provide a more precise characterization of tumor heterogeneity. Furthermore, the largest cross-sectional tumor regions of interest (ROI) and its corresponding habitat mask, comprising all identified subregions, is systematically integrated into dual-channel imaging frameworks, thereby facilitating the training of deep learning models.

Our study pioneers the implementation of adaptive dynamic clustering algorithms for automated tumor subregion segmentation on contrast-enhanced CT images integrated with dual-channel DCNNs for the preoperative risk stratification of TETs. In the two independent external validation cohorts presented, this novel framework achieved the highest AUCs compared to conventional methods, such as 2D deep learning, traditional radiomics approaches, and CRVM, outperforming 2D_DLM (ΔAUC +0.05–0.06), radiomics methods (ΔAUC +0.12–0.17), and CRVM (ΔAUC +0.06–0.08). This framework uniquely addresses three critical clinical challenges: (1) enhanced characterization of ITH through spatiotemporal feature mapping, (2) reduction in computational complexity compared to voxel-based radiomics pipelines, and (3) elimination of data transforming using multichannel DCNNs. The predictive model developed in this study serves as an effective ancillary tool, facilitating noninvasive preoperative assessment to help clinicians estimate surgical risks, determine resection margins, and refine surgical treatment strategies.

In conclusion, this study presents a novel dual-channel CNN framework that integrates adaptive ITH mapping with deep learning for the non-invasive preoperative risk stratification of TETs. This method overcomes the critical limitations of current methods by reducing computational complexity, implementing parameter-free clustering, and enabling more robust spatial heterogeneity analysis. This approach demonstrates substantial potential for enhancing the preoperative management of TETs.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design introduced potential selection bias, although external validation was conducted to enhance reliability. Prospective investigations are crucial for validating CECT-based ITH assessments in TETs and further establishing their relevance to risk stratification. Second, the exclusive reliance on venous-phase imaging may not fully capture the heterogeneity of TETs, thereby potentially constraining the clinical generalizability of the findings. Finally, this study extracted and analyzed features from axial 2D slices rather than the full 3D volume. Although the largest cross-sectional slice generally contains the most representative tumor features, this approach may not fully reflect the overall heterogeneity throughout the 3D space and could miss some key biological information. Future work will focus on developing efficient 3D DL models to achieve a more comprehensive and accurate characterization of ITH.

Experimental model and study participant details

This retrospective study was conducted in strict accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and received ethical approval from the Institutional Review Boards of the participating institutions (No. KYYJ-2024-355), and was exempted from the requirement of written informed consent due to the de-identified nature of the dataset and in compliance with regulatory guidelines for the use of secondary data.

A total of 336 patients with histopathologically confirmed TETs were recruited from four tertiary care centers (Table S1). All patients underwent standardised contrast-enhanced chest CT examinations (illustrative examples are provided in Table S2) as part of their preoperative follow-up. The inclusion criteria for patients are as follows: a definitive histopathological diagnosis of TETs supported by complete imaging and clinical records, who underwent contrast-enhanced chest CT within 30 days prior to surgical resection without prior therapeutic interventions (radiation/chemotherapy). Exclusion criteria comprised documented tumor recurrence, significant image artifacts precluding diagnostic evaluation, or maximum tumor dimension <10 mm on axial CT measurements. Figure 1 shows the flowchart of patient recruitment.

Method details

Acquisition of CT images

Each patient underwent multiphase contrast-enhanced chest CT examination on a multi-slice spiral CT scanner, maintaining a deep inspiratory breath-hold while in a supine position. Each patient was positioned supine and underwent a multiphase contrast-enhanced chest CT examination using a multislice spiral CT scanner, following a deep inspiratory breath-hold. Comprehensive technical specifications for the imaging protocols employed across all participating institutions were methodically compiled and are presented in Table S2. The venous-phase acquisition was prioritized due to its superior capability in defining anatomical margins for the predominant thymic neoplasms, which optimally enhances lesion conspicuity and enables precise volumetric delineation of anterior mediastinal pathologies.The images were uniformly resampled to a voxel size of 1 × 1 × 1 mm3 using a B-spline interpolation algorithm. Subsequently, the voxel intensity values were discretized using a fixed bin width of 32 units. The image grayscale was then set by applying a window width of 400 HU and a window level of 40 HU.

Conventional radiologists’ visual assessment

Baseline clinical parameters (age, sex, and family medical history) were retrospectively collected from medical records. Two thoracic radiologists (> 10 years of experience) conducted blinded imaging evaluations of the TETs using a standardised imaging protocol. Eight critical morphological and quantitative parameters were evaluated: enhancement pattern (heterogeneous/homogeneous) and degree (minimal/moderate/severe), lesion contour (smooth/lobulated/irregular), presence of necrosis, lymphadenop, invasion of the pleura and great vessels, and calcification (> 100 HU).In case of disagreement, the radiologists reached a consensus through consultation. If a consensus could not be reached, the cases were referred to a three-expert adjudication committee, where a decision was made by majority vote.A risk stratification model for TETs based on conventional radiologists’ visual assessment (CRVM) was developed using various standard machine learning algorithms (e.g., Extra Trees, Extreme Gradient Boosting, Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines, Adaptive Boosting, and Multilayer Perceptrons). The performance of these models was systematically evaluated, and the optimal model was selected and validated.

Image segmentation

Two experienced thoracic radiologists (Readers 1 and 2, with 5 and 8 years of experience in chest imaging, respectively) performed image segmentation while being blinded to all clinical information. Any discrepancies in the annotations were resolved by a senior radiologist with more than 20 years of experience. Figure 2 presents an overview of the study’s pipeline. Reader 1 manually segmented all tumour regions of interest (ROIs) on each axial slice independently using open-source ITK-SNAP software (version 3.8.0, available at www.itksnap.org). To evaluate the inter-reader consistency of tumour segmentation, both readers re-outlined the tumour ROIs in 50 randomly selected patients 2 weeks later, and intra-class correlation coefficients (ICCs) were calculated to quantify the agreement.

Automated tumor habitat segmentation on CT

The dynamic adaptive clustering algorithm was subsequently adopted for automated tumour habitat segmentation on CT. First, 3D tumor regions were standardized through intensity normalization within manually defined 3D ROI,Second, a dynamic clustering algorithm automatically determined subregion numbers by analyzing spatial-intensity patterns, eliminating reliance on fixed parameters. A memory-efficient clustering algorithm then partitioned tumors into subregions by optimizing:min{Ci}∑i=1k∑x∈Ci∥x−ci∥2where x combines spatial coordinates and normalized intensities, ci denotes cluster centroids, and k is dynamically determined through silhouette analysis of subsampled data.

Postprocessing removed fragmented clusters (<27 voxels) to preserve anatomical coherence. The system automatically scales segmentation granularity—larger tumors generate more subregions to resolve fine heterogeneity, while smaller lesions maintain simplified partitioning.

Deep learning procedure

Dual-channel image integration

For each patient, the largest cross-sectional ROI and its corresponding habitat masks were cropped. This approach captures both the primary tumour cross-section and essential intratumoural habitat features, thereby addressing a common limitation in deep learning, where only the largest cross-section is utilised, potentially overlooking critical ITH data. Consequently, we constructed a dual-channel dataset that integrates anatomical and ITH information for a more comprehensive analysis. By pairing the largest cross-sectional ROI with its associated habitat mask, our method ensured the preservation of key tumour characteristics such as necrosis and vascularisation patterns, thus enhancing the depth of analysis. We selected hematoxylin and eosin (H&E)-stained pathological sections from six thymoma patients, including three low-risk and three high-risk cases. Following a weakly supervised learning strategy, deep learning features were extracted at the patch level using a pre-trained ResNet18 model.Using Spearman correlation-based hierarchical clustering, we analyzed the relationship between the 32 DL features derived from H&E pathological sections and the 8 DL features extracted by the DC_DLM model. Please refer to Figure S10 in the supplemental information for the results.

Model training

In this study, 2D axial and dual-channel images were utilized to develop deep learning architectures separately. We systematically evaluated the efficacy of state-of-the-art deep learning architectures, including ResNet18, ViT, Vgg11, and DenseNet121, using transfer learning from ImageNet-pretrained weights to augment the predictive capabilities of conventional CNN-based models. Through rigorous benchmarking, we sought to identify an optimal architecture tailored to the nuances of our research objectives, emphasising both accuracy and computational efficiency. This comparative analysis provided actionable insights into model selection for domain-specific applications.

Radiomics procedure

From the tumour ROI, 1834 radiomic features were extracted, and the z-scores were normalised (Figure S1). A three-step feature selection process was applied: (1) reliability filtering using ICCs > 0.85 to ensure reproducible features; (2) redundancy reduction via Pearson’s correlation analysis (|r| > 0.9) to eliminate collinearity; and (3) discriminative feature identification through Lasso regression within a 10-fold cross-validation framework, optimising the regularisation parameter (λ) to maximise class separability. This integrated approach ensures robust feature selection for model development. Furthermore, a risk stratification model for TETs was developed using standard machine learning algorithms (Extra Trees, Extreme Gradient Boosting, Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines, Adaptive Boosting and Multilayer Perceptrons), and the optimal model was selected for validation.

Model performance evaluation

The model performance was comprehensively evaluated using three integrated metrics: diagnostic discriminative ability was assessed via ROC-AUC analysis; predictive calibration was evaluated through Hosmer-Lemeshow goodness-of-fit tests combined with reliability curve analysis to ensure congruence between predicted probabilities and actual outcomes; and clinical relevance was determined using decision curve analysis (DCA) to contextualize the model’s real-world utility by balancing diagnostic benefits against potential risks of misclassification. This dual-channel evaluation framework ensures rigorous assessment of technical performance, statistical reliability, and practical applicability.

Quantification and statistical analysis

The normality of clinical features was confirmed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Continuous variables were analysed via distribution-agnostic hypothesis testing (t-test or Mann–Whitney U test), while categorical variables underwent chi-square (χ2) evaluation. All analyses were performed on the OnekeyAI platform (v4.9.1, Python 3.7.12) using an integrated technical stack: Statsmodels (v0.13.2) for statistical modelling, PyRadiomics (v3.1.0) for radiomics feature extraction, Scikit-learn (v1.0.2) for machine learning implementation, and PyTorch (v1.11.0) with CUDA (v11.3.1) and cuDNN (v8.2.1) optimisations for deep learning workflows. This toolchain ensures reproducible, high-performance computational analysis.

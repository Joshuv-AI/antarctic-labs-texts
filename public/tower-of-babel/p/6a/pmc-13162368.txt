Prescribing trends and clinical impact of calcium salt supplements in musculoskeletal pain: evidence from a multi-center retrospective analysis in India (TRACE study)
Authors: Reena J Wani, Girish Kulkarni, Rathish Nair, Vishnu Sharma, Madhuri V Phani, YSN Raju, Saptarshi Battacharya, Vyankatesh Shivane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundVitamin D₃ deficiency is highly prevalent in India and frequently linked, nonspecific musculoskeletal pain. Adequate calcium and vitamin D₃ intake is essential for maintaining bone integrity and neuromuscular function, as vitamin D₃ facilitates calcium absorption and metabolism. However, real-world data on calcium carbonate-based supplementation practices and related clinical outcomes remain limited. The purpose of this study was to explore the real-world prescription trends and corresponding clinical and biochemical changes observed among patients with musculoskeletal pain.MethodThis retrospective, observational, multicentre chart review study analyzed data from 660 patients across 128 centres in India between December 2023 and February 2024. Data on demographics, clinical characteristics, pain parameters, prescription trends, and serum biochemical indices were extracted from available medical records. The study protocol received approval from an independent ethics committee prior to data collection. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS v29.0.1.0, with a significance level set at p < 0.05.ResultAmong 660 patients (52.73% female; mean age: 48.69 ± 12.70 years), diabetes (78.94%) and hypertension (39.70%) were the most frequent comorbidities. Joint pain (24.83%) and osteoarthritis (20.20%) were the leading clinical conditions. Vitamin D₃ deficiency (< 20 ng/mL) was noted in 26.36% of patients. The most prescribed formulation was calcium carbonate (500 mg) with vitamin D₃ (2000 IU), vitamin B₁₂, L-methyl folate calcium, and pyridoxal-5-phosphate (63.48%). After three months of supplementation, significant improvements were observed in biochemical parameters serum calcium (+ 16.36%), vitamin D₃ (+ 58.76%), and intact Parathyroid Hormone (-27.09%) (all p < 0.0001) along with notable reduction in pain scores. No adverse events were documented in the available medical records.ConclusionIn this real-world cohort, patients who received multi-nutrient calcium–vitamin D₃ based supplementation demonstrated improvement over time in pain scores and biochemical parameters. These formulations were well-tolerated and associated with favorable clinical changes in individuals with vitamin D₃ deficiency. However, given the retrospective, pre-post observational design without a comparator group, the observed improvements potentially reflect both the effect of supplementation and other concurrent routine clinical factor. Further large-scale, prospective controlled studies are required to confirm these associations and clarify potential therapeutic impact.Clinical trial registry of India study registration numberCTRI/2023/12/060701, Registered on: 22/12/2023.Keywords: Prescription preference, Calcium carbonate, Vitamin D3, Pain management

Introduction

Chronic musculoskeletal pain (CMP) is a major global health concern, affecting approximately 1.71 billion people worldwide. The burden is particularly high in the South-East Asia region, including India, where lifestyle transitions and nutritional deficiencies significantly contribute to morbidity [1–3]. Vitamin D deficiency is highly prevalent in India affecting up to 85% of the population despite abundant sunlight [4] and is a well-recognized contributor to nonspecific musculoskeletal pain [5], muscle weakness, and bone fragility [6, 7].

Adequate calcium and vitamin D intake are essential for maintaining neuromuscular function and skeletal integrity [8]. However, dietary gaps in India are significant. Despite being a leading milk producer, average dietary calcium intake in India (400–500 mg/day) remains well below the recommended 600 mg/day, largely due to phytate-rich vegetarian diets that inhibit absorption [4, 9]. Pharmacological supplementation particularly with calcium carbonate and cholecalciferol (Vitamin D3) has become a standard therapeutic strategy for maintaining neuromuscular function and managing nonspecific musculoskeletal pain [10].

While randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have established the efficacy of calcium and vitamin D supplementation, there is a paucity of data on how these supplements are utilized in routine clinical practice in India. Previous drug utilization studies in India have been largely restricted to narrow therapeutic populations, such as elderly patients with osteoporotic fractures [11] or women receiving antenatal care [12]. Consequently, there is a lack of comprehensive data regarding prescribing patterns in general outpatient settings, specifically concerning the choice of calcium salts and multi-nutrient formulations for musculoskeletal pain. In India, varying dietary habits and adherence levels across different Indian regions create a need for multicentre data to understand therapeutic outcomes outside of controlled trial settings [13]. The present multicentre, retrospective, observational chart review study aimed to bridge this knowledge gap by analyzing real-world prescription trends of calcium and vitamin D-based multi-nutrient supplements and their associated biochemical and pain outcomes among adult patients with musculoskeletal pain across India.

Methods

Ethical conduct of the study

This retrospective, observational chart review study was conducted in accordance with principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (Brazil, October 2013), Good Clinical Practices for clinical research in India, 2005, New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules 2019, The International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements of Pharmaceuticals for Human Use Guideline for Good Clinical Practice E6 (R2), and with Indian Council of Medical Research’s national ethical guidelines for biomedical and health research involving human participants, 2017. The study was approved by Sangini Hospital Ethics Committee on 28 Oct 2023 (EC Registration number: ECR/147/Inst/GJ/2013). As this was observational retrospective data collection study, written informed consent was not obtained. This clinical study had been registered at CTRI (Clinical Trial Registry of India) with the Registered number CTRI/2023/12/060701, Registered on: 22/12/2023.

Study design and patient population

This was a multicentre, retrospective, observational chart-review study that included 660 adult patients (≥ 18 years) from random 128 outpatient sites across India. A retrospective design was chosen to evaluate real-world prescription practices and clinical outcomes across geographically diverse centres within routine care settings. Eligible patients were adults presenting with musculoskeletal pain and clinically suspected or previously documented calcium or vitamin D deficiency, as recorded in their medical charts. Clinical suspicion was based on physician judgment supported by symptoms such as generalized musculoskeletal pain, muscle weakness, or documented low/borderline serum vitamin D or calcium levels when available. Only patients with complete baseline and follow-up clinical records were included. Individuals with known contraindications to the study supplements were excluded. The primary objective of this study was to assess changes in pain severity over 3 months of supplementation using the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) and to determine the prevalence of comorbidities among patients with calcium deficiency symptom. Secondary objective included evaluation of real-world prescription patterns of calcium-based supplementation and to assess the safety profile by analyzing adverse events documented in follow-up medical records. As this was a retrospective, real-world evidence study, the sample size was determined pragmatically based on the number of eligible patient records available during the study period.

Study drug

The prescribed formulations included Calcium Carbonate (500 mg) + Vitamin D₃ (2000 IU) + Vitamin B₁₂ (1500 mcg) + L-Methyl Folate Calcium (1 mg) + Pyridoxal-5-phosphate (20 mg) (Shelcal XT), Calcium Carbonate + Vitamin D₃ (Shelcal HD), and Calcium Carbonate + Vitamin D₃ + Vitamin B₁₂ (Shelcal HD12), all manufactured by Torrent Pharmaceuticals Pvt. Ltd., India.

Study procedure

In this retrospective, observational, multicentre chart review study data were extracted from existing patient medical records across 128 outpatient centres between December 2023 and February 2024. Investigators were provided with a standardized protocol and predefined case record form (CRF) to ensure consistency in the retrospective abstraction of prespecified variables from patient charts.

For each eligible patient, baseline records were reviewed to extract details on demographics, occupation, marital status, physical activity level, pregnancy status, presenting symptoms, pain characteristics, duration of symptoms, baseline laboratory parameters (serum calcium, vitamin D₃, and iPTH), comorbidities, clinical diagnoses, and prescribing patterns. Clinical records documenting routine three-month follow-up visit after supplementation were reviewed to capture changes on VAS pain scores, biochemical parameters (serum calcium, vitamin D₃, and iPTH), and any safety-related information. Pain scores were recorded using the same 0–100 VAS scale at both time points. Data were manually transcribed into standardized, ethics-approved CRFs by site investigators and subsequently verified for completeness and accuracy before statistical analysis. All blood biochemistry analysis were conducted at NABL-accredited laboratories to ensure quality and reliability of results.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for Social Sciences [The International Business Machines Corporation, United States; version 29.0.1.0 (171)]. Descriptive statistics were utilized for the statistical analysis. Qualitative variables were reported as frequencies and percentages, while quantitative variables were summarized using means, medians, standard deviations and their respective minimum and maximum values. The significance of continuous variables was assessed using the student’s paired t-test, employing a two-tailed test and considering a p-value < 0.05 as statistically significant. Only complete cases were analyzed, as per the retrospective study design..

Visual analogue scale

Pain severity was assessed using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), a widely validated and commonly used tool in clinical pain research. The VAS consists of a 100-mm horizontal line, anchored by two verbal descriptors at either end: “no pain” (0 mm) and “worst imaginable pain” (100 mm). Patients were asked to mark a point on the line corresponding to their current pain intensity. 0 to 4 mm can be considered no pain; 5 to 44 mm for mild pain; 45 to 74 mm denotes moderate pain; and 75 to 100 mm for severe pain [14].

Results

Subject disposition

A total of 660 complete patient records from 128 centres were included in the analysis, based on retrospective review of documented clinical data (Refer Fig. 1).

Study flowchart. A total of 660 patient records were screened and included in the analysis. All patients completed Visit 1 and the 3-month follow-up without any loss to follow-up. Consequently, all 660 participants were included in the final dataset for the analysis

Demographic and baseline characteristics

A total of 660 patients were included in the study, comprising 348 females (52.73%) and 312 males (47.27%). The mean age of the study population was 48.69 ± 12.70 years, and the mean body mass index (BMI) was 25.70 ± 3.93 kg/m². The majority (40.00%) were housewives, followed by individuals engaged in business (21.97%) and employment (20.45%). Most patients were married (93.18%) and reported low to moderate levels of physical activity. At baseline, majority of the study population had suboptimal vitamin D levels, either deficient or insufficient 26.36% and 40.76% respectively, while a small proportion had normal levels or unknown status. Common presenting complaints included low back pain (62.73%), joint pain (54.55%), and muscle-related symptoms such as cramps (29.24%), spasms (28.94%), and fatigue (27.73%). Pain had been present for less than five years in 88.49% the patients. A 8.79% female participant was pregnant during the observation period. The most frequently affected system was the skeletal system (45.76%), followed by the endocrine (33.03%) and cardiovascular systems (23.48%). Acute pain was more commonly reported than chronic pain, with joint pain, low back pain, and osteoarthritis being the most prevalent acute conditions. Diabetes was prevalent among 78.94% of patients, followed by hypertension in 39.70% patients, other comorbidities in 19.70%, thyroid diseases in 17.27%, PCOS in 7.88%, dyslipidemia in 5.91% and kidney stones in 5.76% of patients (Refer Table 1 - placed at the end of the document).

Prescription pattern

A substantial proportion of patients (63.48%) were prescribed combination formulations comprising cholecalciferol (vitamin D₃), cyanocobalamin (vitamin B₁₂), L-methylfolate, and pyridoxal-5′-phosphate. Formulations containing only calcium carbonate and cholecalciferol accounted for 35.15% of prescriptions. Most patients (91.21%) received supplementation for a duration of up to three months, and nearly all (98.03%) were instructed to administer the supplements with meals. These prescribing patterns are consistent with standard outpatient clinical practice aimed at mitigating musculoskeletal pain and addressing underlying micronutrient deficiencies (Refer Table 2).

Primary endpoint

Change in pain severity

At baseline, patients reported moderate musculoskeletal pain, with a mean VAS score of 63.27 ± 23.14. After 3 months of treatment, a marked reduction in pain intensity was observed, with the mean VAS score decreasing to 15.36 ± 18.36. The mean change from baseline (CFB) was − 47.91 ± 24.54, corresponding to a mean percentage reduction of − 76.35% ± 26.84. This reduction in VAS scores was statistically significant (p < 0.0001). Distributional analysis further supported the consistency of pain reduction across the study population. The median pain burden shifted substantially, with the interquartile range (Q1–Q3) decreasing from 50 to 81 at baseline to 2–26 at 3 months, indicating a broad shift toward lower pain severity at follow-up (Refer Table 3).

Out of the total cohort, serum calcium levels were assessed at both baseline and at the end of the 3-month supplementation period. At baseline, the mean serum calcium level was 8.36 ± 1.35 mg/dL, which significantly increased to 9.59 ± 1.73 mg/dL, representing a 16.36% improvement (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 3).

Similar to the serum calcium level, mean serum vitamin D level was measured and found to improve significantly. At baseline, the mean vitamin D level was 25.56 ± 8.25 ng/mL, which increased to 35.25 ± 8.51 ng/mL following 3 months of calcium and vitamin D supplementation. This corresponds to a 58.76% improvement (p < 0.0001), indicating a statistically significant enhancement in vitamin D status among the patient cohort (Fig. 4).

Discussion

The present study evaluated real-world utilization patterns of calcium–vitamin D₃ based supplementation in adults presenting with musculoskeletal pain and suspected calcium or vitamin D deficiency in routine outpatient settings. Consistent with previous Indian reports, calcium carbonate–based formulations, often combined with vitamin D₃ and additional micronutrients, were frequently prescribed across participating centres [13].

Vitamin D status in the cohort indicated a high prevalence of suboptimal levels, classified according to established clinical thresholds, with serum concentrations < 20 ng/mL defining deficiency and 20–29 ng/mL defining insufficiency. In our study, 26% of patients were deficient and 40% were insufficient. While national epidemiological reports describe a higher prevalence of severe deficiency across India often ranging from 50% to 90% in the general population [15]. Our findings reflect a tertiary care outpatient setting where health-seeking behavior may influence baseline status. Despite this variance in absolute prevalence rates, the collective burden of suboptimal vitamin D levels in our cohort indicated that hypovitaminosis D remains a significant clinical concern, necessitating routine assessment and appropriate supplementation.

Calcium plays essential roles in skeletal integrity, neuromuscular signalling, vascular contraction, and endocrine regulation, with 99% stored in bone functioning as a mineral reservoir [16]. Long-standing calcium insufficiency has been associated with osteopenia and osteoporosis, particularly in populations with chronically low dietary intake [17].

Vitamin D deficiency is similarly implicated in musculoskeletal pain, impaired muscle function, and reduced bone mineralization. Kristen et al. reported that vitamin D deficiency may present with non-specific pain and fatigue, which often improves following correction of deficiency [18]. In line with these observations, patients in the present cohort demonstrated reductions in musculoskeletal symptoms over the three-month follow-up period.

In this real-world analysis, the utilization patterns of calcium and vitamin D supplementation in Indian patients with calcium deficiency and musculoskeletal symptoms were explored. Consistent with prior study, (Sheth et al. 2021) calcium carbonate-based multi-nutrient formulations are the most prescribed, with a notable physician preference for fixed-dose combinations of calcium and vitamin D to enhance patient compliance and perceived efficacy, typically for two to three months and taken with meals [13]. While this prescribing behaviour reflects routine clinical practice, it only partially aligns with formal guidelines that advocate for individualized supplementation based on dietary intake and biochemical deficiency status. The observed patterns highlight prevailing physician preferences shaped by practical considerations such as ease of administration, patient adherence, and expected outcomes, offering valuable insights into current outpatient supplementation practices [19].

In a review article by Sheth et al. (2021) examining calcium prescription practices among Indian orthopedic surgeons, calcium carbonate was reported as the most frequently prescribed salt (47.65%), followed by calcium citrate (32.81%). Additionally, while 11.7% of respondents preferred treating musculoskeletal pain with calcium alone, a greater proportion (57.8%) opted for a combination of calcium and vitamin D [13]. In alignment with these findings, the present study observed that calcium carbonate was the most prescribed supplement. A fixed-dose combination comprising calcium carbonate, vitamin D3, L-methyl folate calcium and pyridoxal-5-phosphate was administered to 63.48% of patients, predominantly 45.76% for skeletal-related pain symptoms highlighting consistent real-world preference trends in India.

A study by Goyal et al. (2021) evaluated the effects of vitamin D₃ and calcium carbonate supplementation in patients with chronic musculoskeletal pain and reported a significant increase in mean serum vitamin D₃ levels from 17.38 ng/mL to 39.40 ng/mL after three months of treatment (mean increase = 22.02 ng/mL) [20]. In the present study, a similar trend was observed, with mean serum vitamin D₃ levels rising from 25.56 ng/mL at baseline to 35.25 ng/mL after three months (mean increase = 9.69 ng/mL). The Vrinda et al. study also noted a reduction in pain symptoms following supplementation, which aligns with our findings, where 50.00% of patients reported complete resolution of pain after three months of therapy.

In a related pre–post intervention by Goyal et al. (2021), mean VAS pain scores decreased from 6.22 (moderate range) to 3.52 (mild range) after 3 months of calcium–vitamin D supplementation. In our study, a similar reduction from baseline moderate–severe pain to mild or no pain was observed in more than 50% of participants [20].

International and National recommendations (ICMR, Endocrine Society, and Institute of Medicine) generally advise supplementation for individuals with documented vitamin D deficiency, inadequate dietary calcium intake, or established skeletal disorders. Routine supplementation of multiple nutrients without documented deficiency is not universally recommended, as current guidelines emphasize targeted therapy over empiric use in healthy populations [21–23]. Likewise, excessive or unnecessary supplementation carries risks, including hypercalcemia, nephrolithiasis, and potential vascular calcification, particularly with prolonged high-dose vitamin D use [24, 25]. No adverse events were identified in the extracted medical records; however, the absence of documented events may reflect under-reporting. Routine biochemical monitoring is variably recommended across guidelines but is generally advised for individuals receiving long-term supplementation, those with comorbid conditions affecting calcium–vitamin D metabolism, or those at risk of toxicity [26].

This retrospective observational design limits the ability to infer causality, and the improvements potentially reflect both the effect of supplementation and other concurrent routine clinical factor, particularly in the absence of a comparator group. Only patients with complete baseline and follow-up data were included, introducing potential selection bias. Because data collection was limited to prespecified variables available in existing medical charts, confounder adjustment was limited, leaving the potential for unmeasured confounding. Although standardized CRFs were used to support uniform data abstraction, variability in laboratory assessments, and supplement compositions across sites may introduced heterogeneity. No adverse events were documented in the available records; however, under-reporting is a recognized limitation of chart reviews. The three-month follow-up period may also be insufficient to assess long-term outcomes or risks associated with prolonged supplementation. By addressing these considerations, future studies can advance the evidence base prospective controlled studies with predefined confounder adjustment, systematic safety monitoring, and longer follow-up to more clearly define the effectiveness and safety of calcium–vitamin D₃ based supplementation in routine clinical practice.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated a predominant preference for calcium carbonate–based fixed-dose combinations containing vitamin D₃, vitamin B₁₂, L-methyl folate calcium, and pyridoxal-5-phosphate. Over a three-month period, patients receiving these formulations showed improvement in musculoskeletal pain and biochemical parameters, including serum calcium, vitamin D and iPTH levels. However, given the retrospective, pre–post observational design without a comparator group, the observed improvements potentially reflect both the effect of supplementation and other concurrent routine clinical factor. These findings reflect real-world prescription practices and the clinical utility of multi-nutrient calcium formulations in routine patient care.

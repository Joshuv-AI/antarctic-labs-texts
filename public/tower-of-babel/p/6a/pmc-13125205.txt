A longitudinal dataset of hypertensive osteoporotic fracture patients: treatments and long-term outcomes
Authors: Chong Li, Ke Lu, Li-wen Su, Peng Zhou, Guo-ji Lin, Jia-qi Liang, Ya-qin Gong, Jian Jin, Wen-rong Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Osteoporotic fractures (OPF) and hypertension frequently co-occur in older adults, yet comprehensive datasets integrating clinical, pharmacological, and longitudinal outcome data remain scarce. We describe a longitudinal dataset derived from the Osteoporotic Fracture Registration System at Kunshan Hospital, Jiangsu University, including patients aged ≥ 50 years hospitalized for OPF between 2017 and 2024. A total of 4,782 patients were initially registered. After applying predefined eligibility criteria, 4,325 patients were included in the final analytical cohort. The dataset integrates demographic, clinical, and pharmacologic variables with long-term outcomes on mortality and refracture through deterministic linkage with regional health and mortality registries. Longitudinal antihypertensive prescription records (n = 42,367) were linked via the Kunshan Municipal Health Data Integration Platform, enabling detailed characterization of medication exposure patterns over time. Technical validation, including survival analysis, propensity score methods, and risk prediction modeling, was conducted to assess internal consistency and illustrate potential applications. This structured and de-identified dataset provides a quality-checked resource to support future research in osteoporosis, cardiovascular comorbidity, multimorbidity, and real-world comparative effectiveness studies.

Background & Summary

Osteoporotic fractures (OPF) are serious clinical events resulting from underlying osteoporosis and are associated with poorer prognosis, including high mortality and significant risk of subsequent fractures1–4. These consequences contribute significantly to patient disability and societal healthcare burdens, underscoring the importance of identifying clinical characteristics associated with poor long-term outcomes in OPF populations. However, few datasets link detailed clinical fracture data with longitudinal medication exposure and survival outcomes.

Hypertension is a prevalent age-related comorbidity that frequently coexists with OPF and has been implicated in the pathophysiology of osteoporosis and fracture risk5–8. Although previous research suggests a potential link between hypertension and increased risk of initial fractures9, less is known about its association with long-term outcomes following osteoporotic fractures, particularly all-cause mortality and refracture rates8,10–15. In addition, there is limited evidence regarding the role of antihypertensive medications—such as diuretics, beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, and ACE inhibitors—in shaping long-term outcomes after OPF. While certain classes have been associated with bone metabolism effects, their prognostic relevance in real-world OPF cohorts remains underexplored16,17.

To address this critical gap, we developed a longitudinal, hospital-based cohort dataset of older adults with OPF that integrates hypertension status, comprehensive prescription histories, and long-term outcomes in Eastern China. Kunshan is a rapidly aging and economically developed city in Eastern China with integrated population-based health data systems covering public-sector healthcare institutions, making it a valuable setting for longitudinal real-world evidence research. This dataset was enriched through linkage with (1) the Jiangsu Provincial Death Registration System (PDRS) (2), the Kunshan Regional Health Registration Platform (RHRP), and (3) the Kunshan Municipal Health Data Integration Platform, which provided comprehensive prescription records. Using encrypted patient identifiers, we assembled a longitudinal dataset covering baseline hospitalization, outpatient follow-up, prescription histories, and mortality.

The resulting dataset includes 4,782 patients with curated clinical data and 42,367 antihypertensive prescription records across seven drug classes. It provides a structured resource that can support analyses of mortality and refracture in relation to hypertension status, medication class, and dosage exposure. The dataset is designed to enable future causal inference and comparative effectiveness research, rather than to establish causal relationships within this data descriptor. Technical validation analyses were conducted to assess internal consistency and data coherence using Cox models, Kaplan–Meier survival curves, and nomogram-based risk prediction approaches.

Method

Data sources and collection

Data for this retrospective longitudinal cohort study were sourced from the Osteoporotic Fracture Registration System (OPFRS), a hospital-based clinical registry initiated in 2017 at the Affiliated Kunshan Hospital of Jiangsu University (AKHJU), Jiangsu Province, China. The OPFRS systematically collects demographic, diagnostic, treatment, and outcome data from patients hospitalized for osteoporotic fractures (OPF). To enable comprehensive outcome tracking, the OPFRS was linked to two external data systems (1): the Kunshan Regional Health Registration Platform (RHRP), which includes all public hospital inpatient and outpatient records in the city, and (2) the Jiangsu Provincial Population Death Registration System (PDRS), which captures verified mortality events. Deterministic record linkage was performed using encrypted unique patient identifiers across databases. Linkage to the PDRS achieved complete matching (100%) for all registered patients.

Prescription information on antihypertensive medications was obtained from the Kunshan Municipal Health Data Integration Platform, maintained by the Kunshan Health Commission. Through record linkage with OPFRS, a total of 42,367 antihypertensive prescriptions were mapped to the study cohort, covering both inpatient and outpatient use. The Kunshan Municipal Health Data Integration Platform captures prescription records from public-sector healthcare institutions across the city. Deterministic linkage was successfully implemented for all registered patients within the municipal platform framework. Among the 4,782 registered patients, 2,053 (43%) had at least one recorded antihypertensive prescription during the observation period. Medication classes included angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs), calcium channel blockers (CCBs), angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors (ACEIs), diuretics, beta-blockers, etc. Ethical approval was granted by the institutional review board of AKHJU (No. 2024-03-053-H00-K01), with informed consent waived due to the retrospective design and use of de-identified data.

Study population

The cohort comprised adults aged ≥ 50 years, newly diagnosed with OPF and hospitalized at AKHJU between January 1, 2017, and April 24, 2024. OPFs were defined using ICD-10 codes for fragility fractures of the wrist (S52), proximal humerus (S42), hip (S72), thoracic vertebrae (S22), and lumbar vertebrae (S32), following the diagnostic criteria established in the Chinese 2017 Guidelines for Osteoporotic Fracture Management18.

High-energy trauma cases were excluded based on the presence of external injury codes in administrative health records. Specifically, fractures associated with ICD-10 external cause codes (X–V–W) were classified as high-trauma fractures, whereas fractures without such codes were considered low-trauma fractures, consistent with previously published registry-based studies19. Patients were included if they were Kunshan residents and survived more than 30 days post-admission. The 30-day survival criterion was applied to reduce the influence of acute peri-hospital mortality and to focus on longer-term outcomes following the index fracture. Exclusion criteria included death within 30 days, missing blood pressure measurements, and withdrawal of consent. Variables and definitions

The primary outcome tracked during follow-up was all-cause mortality, which was defined as death from any cause occurring after the initial hospitalization for OPF. Mortality data were sourced from the PDRS of Jiangsu Province. The secondary outcome was the incidence of refracture, defined as a subsequent OPF (involving the wrist, proximal humerus, hip, vertebrae, or rib) that required hospitalization during the follow-up period. Refracture events were identified by linking data from the RHRP and verifying through clinical records.

The primary exposure variable in this study was the presence of hypertension, categorized as a binary variable. Hypertension was defined according to widely accepted consensus in the publications as either a systolic blood pressure (SBP) > 140 mmHg and a diastolic blood pressure (DBP) > 90 mmHg, measured during hospitalization, or a documented history of antihypertensive medication use in the medical records20,21. Covariates were included to control for potential confounders, encompassing demographic factors (age, sex, and body mass index [BMI]), lifestyle factors (smoking status and alcohol consumption), biochemical markers (serum potassium, phosphorus, calcium, hemoglobin, and osteocalcin levels), treatment variables (Caltrate, Alfacalcidol, Vitamin D2, Zoledronic Acid, Calcitonin, and Alendronic Acid), comorbidity assessment, and fracture type (thoracic vertebra, lumbar vertebra, wrist, proximal humerus, or femoral fractures). The wrist fracture represents the most distal category among the spectrum of five distinct fracture types, and serves as the reference group.

Comorbidity evaluation was conducted with the Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI)22. This index gauges the overall load of concurrent medical disorders by assigning respective scores to a variety of illnesses, where elevated scores correspond to a heavier burden of comorbidities. The CCI is a comprehensive measure that includes a wide range of conditions. These conditions encompass cardiovascular (e.g., myocardial infarction, heart failure), cerebrovascular, pulmonary, gastrointestinal, metabolic, and neurological diseases, among others, such as dementia, malignancies, and renal dysfunction.

Data Processing and Statistical Analysis

We performed all data processing and statistical analyses using R (version 4.3.3). Continuous variables are summarized as mean ± standard deviation, and categorical variables as frequencies and proportions. Time-to-event analyses were conducted using Kaplan–Meier survival estimation. To explore associations with mortality and refracture outcomes, multivariable Cox proportional hazards models were applied, adjusting for relevant clinical covariates. To minimize baseline confounding, we applied propensity score matching (PSM) (1:1 nearest neighbor algorithm with a caliper width of 0.2). Nomograms were constructed based on multivariable Cox models to visualize individualized 1-, 3-, and 5-year risk predictions. Sensitivity analyses were conducted using an alternative definition of hypertension based solely on antihypertensive medication use to assess model robustness. All analyses were conducted to illustrate potential analytical applications of the dataset.

Data Records

The dataset is deposited on Figshare and organized into three de-identified Microsoft Excel (.xlsx) files within a single root directory, structured in a relational format to support diverse research applications, including prognostic modeling, pharmacoepidemiologic analyses, and real-world outcome evaluation23. The dataset comprises a clinical variables table containing patient-level demographic, clinical, treatment, and outcome data, a medication table containing longitudinal antihypertensive prescription records, and a variable definition table providing a comprehensive data dictionary. All files are linked via a unique, de-identified patient identifier using deterministic linkage, ensuring consistent integration across clinical, mortality, and prescription datasets.

The Clinical Variables Table (Clinical Variables Table.xlsx; approximately 3.8 MB) contains one row per patient (N = 4,782) and captures a broad range of patient-level information collected during hospitalization and follow-up. The variables encompass demographic characteristics, admission and surgical information, fracture diagnosis and characteristics, vital signs and blood pressure measurements, laboratory and biochemical indicators, comorbidity profiles, treatment and medication exposure, lifestyle factors, and longitudinal outcome data. Key variables commonly used in secondary analyses include age, sex, fracture type, Charlson Comorbidity Index, blood pressure measurements, selected laboratory markers, bone mineral density measures, and time-to-event outcomes.

Specifically, the table includes demographic variables (e.g., age and sex), fracture-related information (ICD codes and fracture location), perioperative and hospitalization variables (e.g., admission date, surgery date, length of stay), comorbid conditions summarized by the Charlson Comorbidity Index, extensive laboratory measurements (including hematologic, metabolic, inflammatory, renal, hepatic, and bone metabolism markers), bone mineral density measurements at multiple skeletal sites, and treatment-related variables for osteoporosis management. Lifestyle factors such as smoking and alcohol consumption are also recorded.

Outcome variables include all-cause mortality, cause-specific mortality indicators, refracture events, and healthcare utilization measures, with time-to-event intervals calculated in days from the date of index hospitalization.

The Medication Table (Medication Table.xlsx; approximately 1.4 MB) contains longitudinal antihypertensive prescription records (N = 42,367), with each row representing an individual prescription event. Variables include drug name, medication class, prescription date, dosage, and care setting (inpatient or outpatient). Medication classes were defined according to the pharmacological groupings of the World Health Organization Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification system. To enable integrated analysis, this table is linked to the Clinical Variables Table via the ‘ID’ column, allowing researchers to map long-term medication exposure to individual clinical outcomes.

To facilitate data interpretability and reuse, a Variable Definition Table (Variable Definition Table.xlsx; approximately 0.02 MB) is provided as a comprehensive data dictionary. This table documents all variables included in the dataset, including those not explicitly described in the main text, specifying variable names, definitions, units, coding schemes for categorical variables, and relevant clinical interpretations, thereby ensuring transparency and consistency across secondary analyses.

Survival analysis

Kaplan–Meier curves (Fig. 2) observed significantly poorer survival and higher refracture rates in hypertensive patients. Multivariable Cox models (Table 2) adjusted for covariates demonstrated the independent prognostic impact of hypertension on mortality (HR 1.632, 95% CI 1.32-2.02, P < 0.001) and refracture (HR 1.385, 95% CI 1.02-1.87, P = 0.037). Applying 1:1 PSM with nearest neighbor matching (caliper = 0.2) resulted in a balanced cohort of 3,318 patients, in which baseline characteristics were well-aligned. Nomogram for predicting survival and refracture risks

We developed nomograms (Figures S1, S2) for individualized risk prediction of mortality and refracture, incorporating key variables such as age, sex, BMI, CCI, and fracture type. These visual tools facilitate clinical decision-making and personalized risk assessment.

To exemplify the use of these nomograms, consider a hypothetical patient: a 65-year-old male with hypertension, a BMI of 25, a CCI score of 1, and a recent wrist fracture. This patient would be assigned points for his age, gender, hypertension, and fracture category. The total score can then be mapped onto the survival and refracture scales, yielding an estimated 5-year survival probability of 92% and a refracture risk of 6%.

Sensitivity and subgroup analyses

Sensitivity analysis redefined hypertension based solely on documented antihypertensive use, producing a cohort of 4,751 patients. Under this definition, associations between hypertension and both mortality and refracture remained statistically significant (Table S1), confirming robustness.

Subgroup analyses (Figures S4, S5) stratified by sex, age, BMI, CCI, and fracture type revealed consistent associations for mortality. For refracture, the effect of hypertension was more pronounced in patients with BMI > 23, CCI = 0, and hip fractures. These findings suggest possible effect modification and provide direction for future targeted studies.

Limitations and Future Directions

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the dataset. First, as a retrospective study based on routinely collected clinical and administrative data, the analyses are subject to residual confounding and potential selection bias, and some potentially relevant laboratory and medication-related information was not systematically available in the data source. In addition, the exclusion of patients who died within 30 days of admission, implemented to reduce the influence of acute peri-hospital mortality, may introduce survivor bias and should be considered when interpreting analyses involving mortality outcomes. Second, hypertension status was primarily determined based on diagnostic codes and recorded blood pressure measurements in electronic health records, and some degree of misclassification cannot be excluded. Third, detailed information on fracture severity, such as displaced versus non-displaced fractures, was not available in the underlying data sources. In addition, lifestyle-related factors including dietary intake, physical activity, and medication adherence were not systematically recorded and therefore could not be evaluated. Sample sizes were also limited in some subgroups, which may affect statistical power and generalizability. Nonetheless, the dataset offers rich, validated, and clinically relevant information to support future research on fracture outcomes, comorbidity interactions, and precision medicine approaches in older adults.

The availability of medication-specific information also enables future investigation into the role of different antihypertensive drug classes in influencing patient outcomes, aligning with prior literature suggesting heterogeneous effects of these medications on bone health and mortality24–28. The dataset may serve as a resource for future studies, including external validation, comparative effectiveness research, prognostic modeling, and health economic evaluations in diverse healthcare settings.

Ethics approval and consent to participate

We received ethical approval from the Affiliated Kunshan Hospital of Jiangsu University (approval No. 2024-03-053-H00-K01), and was compliant with the Declaration of Helsinki. Patient identification data were hidden from the researchers analyzing the data. Written informed consent was not required because of the investigation’s observational and anonymous design.

New Ustekinumab Biosimilar Candidate FYB202: Pharmacokinetic Equivalence Demonstrated in a Randomized, Double‐Blind, Parallel‐Group, Single‐Dose Trial in Healthy Subjects
Authors: Sigrid Balser, Katrin Nopora, Juliane Körner, Ralph‐Steven Wedemeyer, Maria Anschütz, Barbara Schug
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the RUSTIC trial, pharmacokinetic (PK) similarity between the proposed ustekinumab biosimilar FYB202 and EU‐approved (EU‐Ref) and US‐licensed ustekinumab (US‐Ref) as well as between both reference drugs was assessed after a single 45‐mg subcutaneous injection. Safety analyses comprised immunogenicity (antidrug antibodies, neutralizing antibodies), adverse events, and local tolerability. Overall, 491 healthy adults were randomized 1:1:1 and observed for up to 112 days; 486 completed the trial, and 478 were included in the PK analysis. All 3 comparisons showed PK similarity, since the 90% confidence intervals of the respective geometric mean ratios for area under the concentration‐time curve from time 0 to infinity and maximum serum concentration were contained within the acceptance interval of 80%‐125%. No clinically meaningful differences regarding overall safety, immunogenicity, and local tolerability were observed. Notably, after FYB202 administration, in fewer subjects at least 1 positive antidrug antibody result was observed compared to the reference groups (FYB202, 20%; EU‐Ref, 42%; US‐Ref, 51%). In conclusion, the RUSTIC trial demonstrated equivalent PK characteristics for FYB202 when compared to both EU‐Ref and US‐Ref ustekinumab and between both reference drugs. It provides the basis for the marketing authorization of FYB202, together with an extensive analytical characterization and the results of a confirmatory efficacy and safety trial in patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis.Keywords: biosimilar, Crohn disease, FYB202, immunogenicity, inflammatory bowel disease, pharmacokinetics, psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, ulcerative colitis, ustekinumab

FYB202 is developed as a biosimilar candidate to ustekinumab, a recombinant human immune globulin G1 kappa antibody that targets the p40 subunit shared by the inflammatory cytokines interleukin (IL)‐12 and IL‐23. Ustekinumab is indicated for the treatment of chronic inflammatory diseases including moderate to severe plaque psoriasis 1 and active psoriatic arthritis 2 as well as moderately to severely active inflammatory bowel disorders, that is, Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis. 3 Ustekinumab is among the proposed first‐line treatment options for psoriasis in adults 4 and for certain types of inflammatory bowel disease. 3 , 5

Biologic medicines such as ustekinumab have a highly complex size and structure that largely depends on the manufacturing process in the used biological system. Therefore, in contrast to the development of generic drugs from small molecules, the reproduction of identical copies of biologicals is not possible. Hence, biosimilars are no exact copies but highly similar versions of the approved biologic drug. Consequently, to demonstrate that the biosimilar is highly similar to the approved reference product in terms of quality, efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity, a comprehensive comparability exercise involving the demonstration of structural and functional similarity is necessary. 6

The pharmacokinetic (PK) similarity trial in healthy subjects presented here is part of the comparability exercise for the proposed ustekinumab biosimilar FYB202, together with the results of a confirmatory efficacy and safety trial in patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis and the results of extensive analytical characterization.

The primary objective of the presented trial, RUSTIC, was to demonstrate PK similarity of the biosimilar candidate FYB202 with EU‐approved and US‐licensed ustekinumab (EU‐Ref and US‐Ref, respectively) as well as PK similarity of EU‐Ref and US‐Ref in terms of area under the concentration–time curve from time 0 to infinity (AUC0‐inf) and maximum serum concentration (Cmax). Secondary objectives included the descriptive comparison of further PK parameters and similarity in terms of immunogenicity, tolerability, and safety.

Methods

Ethics and Informed Consent

The clinical trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (version 2013) and the requirements of the German Medicinal Products Act. The trial was approved by the independent leading ethics committee (EC) of the Thuringian Medical Board based upon consultation with the participating ECs responsible for each trial site. All subjects provided written informed consent before enrollment. The trial was registered in the EU Clinical Trials Register (EudraCT No. 2022‐000832‐43).

Study Population

Healthy male or female subjects, 18‐55 years of age, with a body mass index of 20.5 kg/m2 or greater and 30.0 kg/m2 or less (body weight, 60‐90 kg) were included. Subjects could not be included if they were pregnant, breastfeeding, or planning to become pregnant. Subjects were excluded if they matched the exclusion criteria for treatment with ustekinumab as per the summary of product characteristics. Further key exclusion criteria comprised previous exposure to ustekinumab, previous or concurrent clinically relevant pathologies (eg, malignancies, significant allergic conditions), current or recent (within 1 week prior to study drug administration) fever or active infection, history of residential exposure to tuberculosis or a positive tuberculosis test, and clinically significant laboratory abnormalities, among others.

Study Design and Treatment

This was a multicenter, randomized, double‐blind, 3‐arm, parallel‐group clinical trial. The trial was performed at the following 4 study sites in Germany: SocraTec R&D GmbH, Erfurt (coordinating site, leading EC: Thuringian Medical Board); CRS Clinical Research Services Berlin GmbH, Berlin (participating EC: EC of Berlin); CRS Clinical Research Services Mannheim GmbH, Mannheim (participating EC: Baden‐Wuerttemberg Medical Board); and CTC North GmbH & Co. KG, Hamburg (participating EC: Medical Board Hamburg). The study drugs were the test product FYB202 (Formycon AG), and the 2 reference products EU‐Ref (EU‐approved reference product: Stelara, Janssen‐Cilag International NV) and US‐Ref (US‐licensed reference product: Stelara, Janssen Biotech Inc.). The study drugs were packed and labeled by Nuvisan GmbH. Eligible subjects were randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to one of the 3 treatments, stratified according to study site, body weight (60.0‐69.9, 70.0‐79.9, and 80.0‐90.0 kg), and sex to achieve a balanced distribution of treatments in each stratum. The study team members (ie, investigators, site staff), who were not responsible for the application, the sponsor, and the subjects, remained blinded to treatment throughout the trial.

After predose sampling for baseline assessment, subjects received a single subcutaneous injection into the abdomen of 45 mg/0.5 mL of either FYB202, EU‐Ref, or US‐Ref on Day 1. Subjects were confined to the study site from Day −1 until finalization of the 24‐hour postdose sample collection and study assessments on Day 2. Ambulatory visits for study assessments were scheduled on Days 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 17, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56, 63, 70, 84, 98, and 112.

Pharmacokinetic Sampling and Ustekinumab Bioanalysis

The PK blood samples (approximately 4.9 mL) were collected in S‐Monovettes (serum‐gel with coagulation activator) at the following time points: before dosing; Day 1 (6 and 12 hours after administration); Day 2 (18 and 24 hours after administration); and Days 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 17, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56, 63, 70, 84, 98, and 112.

Concentrations of FYB202 and ustekinumab (EU‐Ref and US‐Ref) were assessed from serum using a validated enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay (sandwich assay). The analysis followed the 1‐assay approach as described by Marini et al. 7 In brief, a plate coated with an anti‐idiotypic ustekinumab Fab antibody (HCA209G) was incubated with analyte‐containing samples, followed by detection with a second horseradish peroxidase‐conjugated antiidiotypic ustekinumab antibody (HCA210P, detection antibody, immune globulin G1). The lower limit of quantitation of the assay was 40 ng/mL (calibration range, 40‐1400 ng/mL). The interrun precision (% coefficient of variation [CV]) ranged from 7.2% to 7.7%, and the accuracy (expressed as inaccuracy, % bias) ranged from −3.3% to −6.0% in the acceptable runs. Comparable quantification of FYB202, EU‐Ref, and US‐Ref was demonstrated during development and validation. The method was considered reproducible, since 98% of 702 repeat analyses to evaluate incurred sample reproducibility met the acceptance criteria (30% or less difference between reassay and original result).

Pharmacokinetic Assessments

The PK analysis set (PKS) included all randomized subjects who received the study drug and who provided sufficient samples for calculation of the primary PK parameter AUC0‐inf. Subjects were excluded from the PKS, if more than 2 consecutive visits/samples or concentration values were missing or if the profiles had less than 5 quantifiable postdose values. No subject had to be excluded from the PKS due to incomplete or incorrect dosing. The PK parameters were assessed for the overall PKS and for the immunogenicity subgroups, that is, antidrug antibody (ADA)‐negative, neutralizing antibody (NAb)‐negative, ADA‐positive, and NAb‐reactive subjects. PK parameters were calculated using noncompartmental analysis in Phoenix WinNonlin, Version 6.3 (Pharsight Inc.).

Immunogenicity Assessments

Immunogenicity, that is, the presence of ADAs and nAbs, was analyzed for the safety analysis set (SAS), which included all randomized subjects who received the study drug. The immunogenicity samples were collected at the following time points: before dosing and Days 3, 7, 10, 14, 28, 56, 84, and 112.

Immunogenicity testing followed a hierarchical, multitier test scheme: In the screening assay, all serum samples were analyzed for ADAs using a validated homogenous electrochemoluminescence bridging assay (Streptavidin Gold, Meso Scale Discovery Platform). During validation, the screening cut point was calculated using a robust parametric approach as 1.084 based on a 5% false positive rate. Screening sensitivity was 13.6 ng/mL (99% confidence level). Screen‐positive samples were then tested in a confirmatory ADA assay by spiking with 33.4 μg/mL FYB202. The confirmatory cut point was defined as electrochemoluminescence inhibition of 10.7% based on a 1% false‐positive rate. Confirmatory sensitivity was 27.3 ng/mL (99% confidence level). Intra‐assay precision (CV) ranged from 3.4% to 4.6% and interassay precision from 16.6% to 20.9%. For study sample analysis, the in‐study screening cut point was derived from the analysis of 100 predose samples in the screening assay. This was done as part of the regular sample analysis at the analysis start and included data from 23 runs. Robust parametric evaluation was made on symmetry evaluation: absolute skewness, 0.28; P‐value .57. The corrected screening cut point was 1.04 based on a 5% false‐positive rate. The confirmed ADA‐positive samples were tested for NAbs using a validated competitive enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assay. Therefore, the samples were depleted of drug using biotinylated ustekinumab antibody‐coated magnetic beads. Depleted samples were then transferred to a drug‐coated plate, which was washed and sequentially incubated with human IL‐23α/IL‐12β heterodimer protein (target antigen), biotinylated IL‐23 antibody, and streptavidin–horseradish peroxidase, followed by photometric detection. The screening cut point was calculated as 0.902 based on a 1% false‐positive rate, and assay sensitivity was 0.856 μg/mL (99% confidence level). Intra‐assay precision (CV) ranged from 3.9% to 5.7% and interassay precision from 9.9% to 13.4%.

Overall Safety

Safety and tolerability were documented for the SAS. Assessments during the trial included adverse events (AEs, coded with the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities, Version 25.1), physical examination, vital signs, and 12‐lead electrocardiogram.

Local Tolerability

Local tolerability in subjects of the SAS at the injection site was assessed by the investigator using the 4‐item injection site reaction score (0 = no reaction to 3 = severe reaction). Pain at the injection site was additionally assessed by the subjects themselves with a visual analog scale (VAS; 100 mm).

Sample Size

The sample size was calculated considering the findings of a preceding failed PK similarity trial, which was underpowered in retrospect, mainly due to a difference in protein content between FYB202 and the reference products. In the preceding trial, the CV for AUC0‐inf and Cmax was approximately 36%. Assuming a significance level of 5%, a test/reference ratio of 0.91 to 1/0.91 (AUC0‐inf and Cmax), equivalence limits of 0.80‐1.25, and 1:1 randomization, 156 evaluable subjects per arm provided an overall power of 94.6% for each comparison and of 85% for all 3 pairwise comparisons. Assuming a dropout rate of 5%, 165 subjects per arm were intended to be randomized.

Statistical Analysis

The natural log‐transformed primary PK parameters Cmax and AUC0‐inf were analyzed using an analysis of covariance for each treatment comparison (2 at a time) considering body weight at screening as covariate and treatment as fixed effect. For the treatment comparisons, the least‐squares means (LSMs) for test and reference product (calculated from natural log‐transformed parameters), the LSM differences, and the 2‐sided 90% confidence intervals (CIs) were obtained. Back‐transformation of LSM differences and CIs to original scale yielded the point estimates of test/reference geometric mean ratios (GMRs) and the corresponding 90% CIs. PK similarity between test and reference treatment was concluded, if the respective 90% CIs for Cmax and AUC0‐inf were completely included in the equivalence acceptance range of 80%‐125%. To enable PK bridging of the 2 reference products, the same acceptance range was used for the comparison of EU‐Ref and US‐Ref. For time to Cmax, nonparametric 90% CIs (Hodges‐Lehmann) of the differences among the 3 study treatments were calculated. Treatments were also exploratively compared for each immunogenicity subgroup. SAS Analyst Pro Version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc.) was used for statistical analysis.

Results

Study Participants

The clinical trial was conducted between June 2022 and February 2023. The disposition of participants is shown in Figure S1, and the baseline characteristics are summarized in Table S1. In total, 491 adult female and male subjects with a mean age (±standard deviation) of 37.7±9.9 years were randomized into the trial. The trial population was balanced regarding sex, with 49.5% being women and 50.5% being men. The majority of all randomized subjects were White (98.0%). Demographic and anthropometric data of the 3 treatment arms were highly similar. All randomized subjects received the study drug and were thus included in the SAS. Only 5 subjects dropped out prematurely (reason: withdrawal of consent). The 5 dropouts and additionally a total of 8 completers were excluded from the PKS due to 2 or more missing subsequent blood samples for the assessment of primary PK parameters.

Pharmacokinetics in the Overall Population

The mean serum concentration‐time profiles after a single subcutaneous dose of 45 mg ustekinumab were largely similar for all 3 treatments (Figure 1). The affiliated PK parameters are summarized in Table 1. The ustekinumab serum concentrations peaked at a median of 168 hours (US‐Ref) or 169 hours (FYB202, EU‐Ref) after dosing (approximately Day 7), followed by a monophasic decline of serum concentrations with arithmetic mean apparent terminal elimination half‐life of 425 hours (approximately 18 days) for US‐Ref, 452 hours (approximately 19 days) for EU‐Ref, and 480 hours (20 days) for FYB202. There were only marginal differences among the 3 treatments in extent of bioavailability (AUC0‐inf), and the Cmax was comparable among all 3 treatment groups. Interindividual variability (geometric CV%) for PK parameters was comparable across treatment groups (approximately 30%‐44.6%).

Immunogenicity

Prior to dosing, few subjects in all 3 groups were confirmed ADA positive (range, 1.2%‐6.1%) with only 5 subjects being NAb reactive (Table 3). The percentage of subjects with at least 1 confirmed positive ADA result from baseline to trial end after FYB202 treatment was less than half compared to treatment with EU‐Ref or US‐Ref. The percentage of subjects with at least 1 NAb‐reactive result was also lowest for the FYB202 group compared to EU‐Ref and US‐Ref groups; however, the between‐group difference was much smaller. For FYB202, the highest incidence of an immunological reaction was observed at the trial end (Day 112) with 15.2% of subjects being confirmed ADA positive. For EU‐Ref and US‐Ref, the highest incidences were observed early in the trial on Day 10 with percentages of 30.1% and 32.3%, respectively. Since the formation of NAbs lags behind the formation of ADAs, the highest incidence of subjects with a positive NAb result was observed at the trial end (Day 112) in all 3 treatment groups (FYB202, 7.9%; EU‐Ref, 9.2%; and US‐Ref, 10.4%).

Pharmacokinetics in Immunogenicity Subgroups

PK parameters were also calculated for the PKS subgroups considering immunogenicity, that is, ADA‐negative (n = 292), NAb‐negative (n = 406), ADA‐positive (n = 182), and NAb‐reactive subjects (n = 72), although the sample size of the NAb‐reactive subgroup was small (see Table S2). In each of the 3 treatment groups, AUC0‐inf was numerically smaller in ADA‐positive than in ADA‐negative subjects (74%‐79% of the value for ADA‐negative subjects). Similarly, AUC0‐inf was smaller in NAb‐reactive than in NAb‐negative subjects (69%‐87% of the value for NAb‐negative subjects). Furthermore, apparent terminal elimination half‐life was shorter in ADA‐positive/NAb‐reactive compared to ADA‐negative/NAb‐negative subjects.

For the exploratory statistical treatment comparison in ADA‐negative and NAb‐negative subgroups, reference is made to Table S3. Notably, in ADA‐negative subjects, the GMRs for the AUC0‐inf comparison of FYB202 with the 2 reference drugs are slightly smaller compared to the corresponding GMRs in the overall population (see Table 2) and get closer to 100%.

Safety and Tolerability

Overall Safety

About 3 quarters of subjects who received ustekinumab had at least 1 AE, and just over half had at least 1 treatment‐related AE (Table S4). AE incidences were comparable in the 3 treatment groups. The AE intensity was either mild (66.4% of subjects) or moderate (38.5% of subjects); there was only 1 severe AE (hypersensitivity in EU‐Ref group, not treatment‐related). Four subjects experienced a serious AE that was not related to treatment in all cases (hypersensitivity, concussion, road traffic accident, and abortion incomplete). No subject dropped out from the trial due to an AE. The most frequently reported treatment‐related AEs were headache (subjects: FYB202, 16.5%; EU‐Ref, 17.8%; and US‐Ref, 24.4%), nasopharyngitis (subjects: FYB202, 13.4%; EU‐Ref, 13.5%; and US‐Ref, 12.2%), and injection site erythema (subjects: FYB202, 3.7%; EU‐Ref, 6.1%; and US‐Ref, 3.7%). In summary, frequency, type, and severity of AEs were comparable across the 3 treatment groups. No clinically relevant changes in vital signs and body temperature over time were observed, and electrocardiogram results did not show any relevant effect on the subjects.

Local Tolerability

Overall, 29 of 491 subjects (5.9%) documented pain of any severity at the injection site on the VAS (FYB202, 5.5%; EU‐Ref, 5.5%; and US‐Ref, 6.7%). Documented VAS distances in these subjects ranged from 1 to 33 mm after application of FYB202, from 1 to 20 mm after application of EU‐Ref, and from 1 to 7 mm after application of US‐Ref.

For the vast majority of all injection site assessments by the investigator using the injection site reaction score, no local reaction (score, 0) was documented (FYB202, 97.1%; EU‐Ref, 96.9%; and US‐Ref, 96.3%). A mild local reaction (=score 1) was assigned to very few assessments (FYB202, 2.8%; EU‐Ref, 2.8%; and US‐Ref, 3.7%). There was only 1 assessment with a moderate local reaction (score, 2) after application of EU‐Ref. Overall, investigator‐assessed local tolerability was comparably good for all 3 treatments.

Discussion

This trial was intended to demonstrate PK similarity between the proposed biosimilar FYB202 and ustekinumab reference as part of the exercise to establish biosimilarity. It was preceded by another PK trial that failed to demonstrate PK similarity of FYB202 to ustekinumab reference in 1 comparison, primarily due to a difference in protein content between FYB202 and the reference products. Notably, the pooled analysis of both PK trials confirmed the PK similarity of FYB202 and ustekinumab reference.

The present trial was performed in accordance with international standards, the dropout rate was low, the 3 treatment groups were balanced regarding demographics and baseline characteristics, and only 13 subjects were excluded from the PK analysis. The trial was conducted in healthy subjects, since this population allows for sensitive detection of potential product differences without confounding factors such as the underlying disease and concomitant medications. The single dose of 45 mg of ustekinumab is the approved subcutaneous dose for plaque psoriasis/psoriatic arthritis treatment in adults with a body weight up to 100 kg and is within the linearity range of the dose‐exposure profile of ustekinumab. 8 Based on the dose linearity, PK similarity for this single‐dose strength can be extrapolated to the other available dose strengths.

The primary results of the trial demonstrate a similar PK profile in terms of overall and maximum exposure for FYB202, EU‐Ref, and US‐Ref, which is confirmed by secondary PK parameters. PK similarity of EU‐Ref and US‐Ref provided the “scientific bridge” for using EU‐Ref as reference for FYB202 in the international comparative efficacy and safety trial in patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis. The herein determined PK parameters were largely in accordance with the results obtained in other ustekinumab PK trials in healthy individuals. 9 , 10 , 11 , 12 , 13

Comparable immunogenicity for the proposed biosimilar FYB202 with the approved reference drug is crucial, since ADAs and NAbs may have an impact on the drug's safety and efficacy 14 ; for example, reduced serum exposure may diminish the clinical effect of ustekinumab. 15 The lower incidence of ADAs after treatment with FYB202 compared to EU‐Ref and US‐Ref possibly stems from the different expression systems used for drug production: While the reference drug is manufactured in the murine myeloma cell line SP2/0, FYB202 is manufactured in a Chinese hamster ovary cell line, leading to slightly different glycosylation profiles. 16 A similar result with a lower ADA incidence compared to the originator drug was reported for other ustekinumab biosimilar candidates such as ABP654, 11 BFI‐751, 9 and QX001S. 17

PK similarity between FYB202 and the reference drugs is confirmed by the results of exploratory analyses in immunogenicity subgroups, indicating that the impact of ADAs and NAbs on the assessed PK parameters was essentially similar after application of all 3 treatments. The higher incidence of ADAs, especially for US‐Ref compared to FYB202, may partly explain the trend to relatively lower ustekinumab exposure after US‐Ref treatment in the overall population (GMR greater than 100% for the FYB202–US‐Ref comparison). Although the different incidences of immunological reactions among the 3 treatments resulted in slightly different overall exposures in the trial population, all 90% CIs are well contained within the predefined 80%‐125% equivalence margins, ruling out clinically relevant differences.

In line with the similar PK characteristics, the systemic safety and local tolerability were also comparable for all 3 treatments.

Since biosimilars are cost‐effective alternatives to biologic drugs, they can improve access to highly effective biologic therapy for patients with inflammatory diseases. 18 The results of the present trial are part of the clinical assessment of the biosimilar candidate FYB202, together with the results of extensive analytical characterization and the results of an efficacy and safety trial in patients with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, which will be reported elsewhere.

Conclusion

The RUSTIC trial demonstrated equivalent PK characteristics between FYB202 and both reference drugs, that is, EU‐approved and US‐licensed ustekinumab, as well as between the 2 reference drugs. No clinically meaningful differences between the 3 treatments with respect to the overall safety, immunogenicity, and local tolerability were observed. The lower ADA incidence after application of FYB202 compared to both references did not translate into clinically meaningful differences between FYB202 and the reference drugs.

Effect of ibuprofen premedication on intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in MIH-affected permanent molars: a triple-blind randomized clinical trial
Authors: Reyhaneh Faghihian, Masoud Saatchi, Narjes Amrollahi, Mohammad Javad Tarrahi, Asal Ebrahimi, Sheida Bagheri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundAchieving adequate pain control during vital pulp therapy in children with molar-incisor hypo-mineralization (MIH) is challenging because of hypersensitivity and the difficulty of obtaining profound local anesthesia. This study evaluated the effect of ibuprofen premedication on intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in MIH-affected mandibular first permanent molars in children.MethodsThis triple-blind randomized clinical trial was conducted on 32 children aged 6–9 years with MIH requiring vital pulp therapy. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either ibuprofen suspension (10 mg/kg) or placebo 45 min before treatment. Pain intensity was assessed using the Five-Face Visual Analog Scale (VAS) at pulp entry and the Sound-Eye-Motor (SEM) scale during the procedure. Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26, with a significance level of 0.05.ResultsThe mean age of participants was 7.99 ± 0.59 years in the ibuprofen group and 8.01 ± 0.46 years in the placebo group. Introduction

Children with MIH often experience discomfort during routine oral hygiene procedures and require more frequent dental treatment compared with unaffected children [1, 2].

Consequently, children with MIH commonly exhibit dental fear, anxiety, and behavior management difficulties during dental treatment [2–4].

Vital pulp therapy is frequently indicated in MIH-affected molars with deep carious lesions in order to preserve pulp vitality and maintain root development. However, pain during pulp therapy remains a significant concern in pediatric patients with MIH. Inadequate pain control may negatively affect child cooperation and compromise treatment quality [5, 6].

Pain during operative treatment of MIH-affected teeth is frequently associated with hypersensitivity and difficulty in achieving profound local anesthesia. Previous studies have demonstrated that children with MIH often report increased intraoperative pain and behavior management difficulties during restorative and pulp therapy procedures. Premedication with analgesics such as ibuprofen may improve pain control and enhance the efficacy of local anesthesia in inflamed pulpal tissues [7, 8].

Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) widely used in pediatric dentistry because of its analgesic and anti-inflammatory effects [1, 5, 9].

However, evidence regarding the effect of ibuprofen premedication on intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in MIH-affected permanent molars remains limited.

Therefore, the present study aimed to evaluate whether premedication with ibuprofen, compared with placebo, could reduce intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in mandibular first permanent molars affected by MIH in children aged 6–9 years. The null hypothesis was that ibuprofen premedication would not significantly reduce intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in children with MIH.

Methods

Study design and ethical approval

This triple-blind parallel-group randomized clinical trial was conducted in 2025 at the Department of Pediatric Dentistry, School of Dentistry, Isfahan University of Medical Sciences, Isfahan, Iran. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences, Isfahan, Iran (Approval code: IR.MUI.DHMT.REC.1404.082). The trial was registered in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT; registration number: 20250308064976N1) on 22 April 2025 before participant enrollment.

Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or legal guardians of all participating children prior to enrollment.

The study was conducted and reported according to the CONSORT 2025 guidelines for randomized clinical trials.

Participants

Children aged 6–9 years with fully erupted mandibular first permanent molars affected by molar-incisor hypomineralization (MIH) and requiring vital pulp therapy were recruited consecutively from patients referred to the pediatric dentistry department.

Sample size calculation

Sample size was calculated based on findings from Bahrololoomi et al., using a significance level of 0.01 and a study power of 90%. Considering a minimum clinically significant difference of 1.45 in pain scores, the minimum required sample size was estimated to be 16 participants per group.

Randomization and allocation concealment

Participants were randomly assigned to either the ibuprofen group or placebo group using block randomization generated by computer software. Allocation concealment was achieved using sequentially numbered opaque sealed envelopes prepared by an independent investigator who was not involved in treatment or outcome assessment.

A total of 32 participants were randomized equally into two groups (n = 16 per group). No participants were excluded after randomization, and all randomized participants completed the study and were included in the final statistical analysis.

Blinding

This study was designed as a triple-blind trial. The ibuprofen and placebo suspensions were prepared in identical bottles with similar color, taste, odor, and appearance and were coded by an independent researcher. Participants, the operator, and the outcome assessor were all blinded to group allocation throughout the study.

Intervention

Participants in the intervention group received ibuprofen suspension (100 mg/5 mL; Alhavi Pharmaceutical Co., Tehran, Iran) at a dose of 10 mg/kg body weight, 45 min before treatment. Participants in the control group received a placebo suspension with identical sensory characteristics and packaging 45 min before treatment.

The administered dose was within the recommended therapeutic range for pediatric patients.

Clinical procedure

Topical anesthesia was applied using benzocaine gel for 30 s before administration of an inferior alveolar nerve block with 2% lidocaine containing 1:80,000 epinephrine. After confirmation of adequate anesthesia, rubber dam isolation was performed and vital pulp therapy was initiated.

Coronal pulp tissue was removed using a sterile round bur and spoon excavator under water cooling. Hemostasis was achieved using sterile saline-moistened cotton pellets. Mineral trioxide aggregate (MTA) was placed as the pulp capping material, followed by restoration with glass ionomer cement and either stainless-steel crowns or amalgam restorations according to clinical requirements.

All clinical procedures were performed by the same pediatric dentistry resident to minimize operator-related variability.

The same vital pulp therapy protocol was followed for all participants in both study groups.

Outcome assessment

The primary outcome of the study was intraoperative pain intensity assessed using the Five-Face Visual Analog Scale (VAS) at the time of pulp entry.

The secondary outcome was behavioral pain response during pulp entry and treatment, assessed using the Sound-Eye-Motor (SEM) scale by a trained examiner blinded to group allocation.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS software version 26 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics including means, standard deviations, frequencies, and percentages were calculated. The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to assess data normality. Independent t-tests were used to compare mean pain scores and SEM scores between groups.

Analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) was performed to compare VAS scores at pulp entry while adjusting for baseline pain scores. The chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Thirty-five children were assessed for eligibility. Three children were excluded before randomization because of pulp necrosis (n = 1), hyperemic pulp tissue (n = 1), and uncooperative behavior (n = 1).

A total of 32 children were enrolled and randomized equally into the ibuprofen and placebo groups (n = 16 per group).

No participants were excluded after randomization, and all participants completed the study and were included in the final statistical analysis.

These findings indicate that premedication with ibuprofen was associated with significantly lower intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in MIH-affected mandibular first permanent molars in children.

Discussion

The present randomized clinical trial evaluated the effect of ibuprofen premedication on intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in children with MIH-affected mandibular first permanent molars. The findings demonstrated that children who received ibuprofen before treatment experienced significantly lower pain scores than those who received placebo, according to both VAS and SEM assessments.

Pain control during dental treatment of MIH-affected teeth remains a major clinical challenge because of hypersensitivity and difficulties in achieving profound local anesthesia. Structural enamel defects and chronic pulpal inflammation in MIH teeth may increase nociceptor sensitivity and contribute to exaggerated pain responses during operative procedures. These factors may explain the higher levels of discomfort and behavior management difficulties frequently observed in children with MIH [1, 5, 9].

The findings of the present study are consistent with previous studies reporting beneficial effects of ibuprofen premedication in pediatric dental procedures [10–15].

In the present study, ibuprofen significantly reduced pain at the time of pulp entry and during vital pulp therapy. The analgesic effect of ibuprofen may be related to inhibition of cyclooxygenase enzymes and subsequent reduction of inflammatory mediators such as prostaglandins, which play an important role in peripheral sensitization and pulpal pain. Reduced inflammatory activity may also improve the efficacy of local anesthesia in inflamed teeth [16, 17].

Unlike several previous studies that mainly evaluated postoperative pain, the present trial focused on intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in MIH-affected permanent molars. This aspect may increase the clinical relevance of the findings for pediatric dentists managing hypersensitive MIH teeth.

Ibuprofen was administered at a therapeutic pediatric dose based on body weight (10 mg/kg), which is considered safe and widely used in children. No adverse effects related to the medication were observed during the study period [8, 18].

Limitations

This study had several limitations. The sample size was relatively small and participants were recruited from a single center, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Pain assessment in children is inherently subjective despite the use of validated pain scales. In addition, postoperative pain and long-term behavioral outcomes were not evaluated. Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are recommended to confirm these findings [19].

Conclusion

Within the limitations of this study, premedication with ibuprofen administered 45 min before treatment significantly reduced intraoperative pain during vital pulp therapy in MIH-affected mandibular first permanent molars in children aged 6–9 years.

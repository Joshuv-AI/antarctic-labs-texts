Inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation alleviated liver injury and wasting effect in tumor-bearing mice
Authors: Shuaihui Wu, Qian Cheng, Yang Shi, Mingjun Liu, Kai Tan, Bo Liao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study is designed to evaluate the changes in tumor-bearing mice, as well as to assess the role of NLRP3 in muscle atrophy and its function in the liver of tumor-bearing mice. In this study, RNA-seq data from the liver of Colon26 (C26) tumor-bearing mice were used to perform pathway enrichment analysis. We established a cancer cachexia model by subcutaneous injection of C26 cells, MCC950 was injected intraperitoneally at a concentration of 30 mg/kg on day 12. On day 21, the mice were dissected, the tissues were weighed, and the specimens were fixed for subsequent staining. In our study, by analyzing RNA-seq data, we found that the inflammatory response was significantly activated in the liver of Colon26 (C26) tumor-bearing mice with up-regulation of several inflammatory cytokines, including IL-1β. Therefore, we examined the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome-related genes and found that the expression of most genes was upregulated, and the expression of NLRP3, caspase-1 and IL-1β were also upregulated. This proves that NLRP3 inflammasome is activated in the liver. In our study, on the one hand, we found that inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation could alleviate liver injury and fibrosis in tumor-bearing mice. on the other hand, inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation alleviates muscle atrophy and fat loss. In addition, MCC950 did not affect tumor growth and had no significant effect on the induced consumption of tumor-secreted factors. This study is the first to show that the NLRP3 inflammasome is activated in the liver of tumor-bearing mice. Pharmacological inhibition of NLRP3 inflammasome activation effectively reduces liver injury and fibrosis. Introduction

According to reliable data, cancer is the second leading cause of human death1. Cancer is often accompanied by many concurrent symptoms, such as malnutrition2. According to the statistics, about a third of cancer patients will lose weight, which we call cancer cachexia3. It is a syndrome characterized by continuous muscle wasting and fat loss4. It is generally considered that the main cause of this change is inflammation5, such as IL-6, TNF-α, IL-1β, and so on6–8. Neutralizing various inflammatory factors with neutralizing antibodies can effectively alleviate the wasting caused by cancer9,10. Similarly, tumors also directly secrete some factors that act on the body’s tissues, leading to wasting, such as activin A, IL-6, myostatin, lif and pthlh11–13. However, until now, there has been no effective treatment for cachexia except anamorelin14. Therefore, we still need to find a new target for the effective treatment of cancer cachexia.

Nucleotide-binding and oligomerization domain (NOD)-like receptor family pyrin domain containing 3 (NLRP3) is an inflammasome that can be activated by many types of pathogens or danger signals15, and it is involved in many diseases16. The activation of NLRP3 inflammasome can promote the release of inflammatory mediators IL-1β and IL-18 through a series of reactions, leading to the generation of inflammation15. Recent studies have shown that NLRP3 plays an important role in muscle atrophy. By knocking down the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, the symptoms of muscle atrophy can be alleviated in several models17–21. However, the role of NLRP3 in cancer-induced muscle atrophy remains unclear. Moreover, most of the previous studies have focused on how to alleviate muscle atrophy and fat loss, while ignoring the changes in other organs of the body under the burden of cancer. Therefore, it is necessary to learn more about the changes in other tissues in the cancer state to better alleviate the symptoms and prolong the life of patients, especially in the liver. Studies have shown that cancer can lead to a series of liver pathological changes, including increased liver inflammation, liver injury, fibrosis, etc22.

MCC950 is a specific inhibitor of NLRP3 and can effectively inhibit the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome23. In this study, we investigated the role of NLRP3 inflammasome in liver pathology and muscle atrophy by blocking NLRP3 inflammasome activation in C26 tumor-bearing mice by intraperitoneal injection of MCC950. It provides a new direction for the effective treatment of liver injury and muscle atrophy induced by cancer.

Methods

Animal experiment

The CD2F1 mice used in the experiments were purchased from Vitonlihua (Beijing, China). These mice were 8-week-old males. After weighing their body weights, they were stratified and randomly assigned into three groups with consistent mean body weights (with an average body weight of approximately 25 g). A double-blind method was employed for drug administration and measurement of the indicators in these mice. The animals were kept at 25 °C for 12 h in light and dark, and given plenty of food and water. We injected 5 × 106 C26 cells subcutaneously into 8-week-old mice. MCC950 (T6887, TagerMol, China) and the vehicle were injected intraperitoneally at a dose of 30 mg/kg/day from day 12 to day 21. The vehicle consisted of 5% DMSO + 95% physiological saline (prepared freshly before each use to prevent precipitation), and MCC950 was dissolved in this vehicle. Mice were dissected on day 21.

Western blot

The frozen tissue at −80 ° C was placed on ice, and then the tissue was broken up using a grinder. Radio-immunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) (C500005, Sangon Biotech, China) mixed with phosphatase inhibitors were added and lysed for 20 min on ice. The samples were then centrifuged using a cryogenic centrifuge at 12,000 rpm for 15 min. The supernatant was taken and a loading buffer was added. The mixture was mixed and stored at −80. Proteins after being separated by 12% sodium dodecyl sulfate–polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), then were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. The cells were then incubated with 5% skim milk dissolved in TBST for 1 h on a shaker. Membranes were then placed at 4 ° C overnight f incubation using antibodies. The membranes were then incubated with the horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-labeled antibody at room temperature for one hour. Finally, we used an electrochemiluminescence (ECL) substrate (hypersensitive) (BL523B, Biosharp, Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China) for exposure. The antibodies used are listed in table S1.

Quantitative real-time PCR analysis (qPCR)

We removed tissues in the frozen presence of −80℃, and the tissues were ground by a grinder. Total RNA was extracted by using RNA isolater Total RNA Extraction Reagent (R401-01, Vazyme, China). Then 1 µg of RNA was used for reverse transcription into cDNA.

A quantitative polymerase chain reaction was used to examine the expression of different genes. Gene expression was obtained after normalization to 18 s RNA and calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. Primer sequences are listed in table S2.

Online data analysis

We retrieved the gene expression dataset GSE154219 from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, which included 8 cases of C26 and 8 control samples. Differential gene expression analysis was performed using DESeq2. Significantly differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the following cutoff criteria: adjusted p-value < 0.05 (FDR < 0.25) and |log₂FC| > 1. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was subsequently conducted using the C2 curated gene set from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) with the appropriate R package. Visualization of the differential expression results, including volcano plots and heatmaps, was generated using the “ggplot2” and “pheatmap” R packages.

Food intake

We measured food intake in mice on day 0 and day 21. Two mice per cage were used for measurements.

Immunohistochemical staining (IHC)

Paraffin Sect. (4 mm thick) were first deparaffinized to water and incubated with 3% H2O2 for 10 min at room temperature to eliminate the activity of endogenous peroxidase. They were then rinsed with distilled water and immersed in PBS for 5 min. And they were blocked with 10% normal goat serum (diluted in PBS) and incubated for 10 min at room temperature. After the serum was decanted, the primary antibody or working solution of primary antibody diluted in appropriate proportions was added drop fold and incubated at 37 ° C for 2 h or at 4℃ overnight. PBS was rinsed for 5 min ×3 times. Appropriate dilutions of biotin-labeled secondary antibodies (1%BSA-PBS diluted) was added drop fold and incubated at 37 ° C for 10 to 30 min. Appropriate dilutions of horseradish enzyme-labeled streptavidin (diluted in PBS) were added drop fold and incubated at 37 ° C for 10 to 30 min. PBS was rinsed for 5 min ×3 times. Color development with a chromogen. Rinse thoroughly with tap water, counterstained, and seal pieces. The precise quantification of the positively stained regions in IHC was accomplished by employing the image processing and analysis capabilities of ImageJ software (https://imagej.net/ij/download.html).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Serum levels of IL-1β were measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kit (D721017-0048, Sangon Biotech, China). Experiments were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Serum biochemical analysis

Whole blood samples were allowed to stand at room temperature for 2 h or kept at 4 °C overnight, followed by centrifugation at 3000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C. The supernatant was then collected, and the Alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) levels in the serum were measured using the Alanine aminotransferase Assay Kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, C009-2-1, Nanjing, China) and the Aspartate aminotransferase Assay Kit (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, C010-2-1), respectively.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). The student’s t-test was applied for pairwise comparisons between two groups, whereas one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used for comparisons among multiple groups. A p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Transcriptome analysis of the liver from C26 tumor-bearing mice

The C26 tumor-bearing mouse model is a mature cancer cachexia model. To explore the changes in the liver in the presence of cancer, we downloaded the whole liver transcriptome data (GSE154219) of C26 tumor-bearing mice from the GEO database and analyzed them. We analyzed the downloaded data through the R package, and the transcriptomes between the control and C26 groups showed clear differences. As shown in Figure 1A, we made a heat map for the top 250 differentially expressed genes. The up-regulated genes and down-regulated genes were shown by volcano plot (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, GO enrichment analysis and KEGG analysis was performed for those differentially expressed genes. The results showed (Fig. 1C) that the genes enriched in molecular function (MF) term were related to cytokine receptor activity, and that in biological process (BP) term were related to extracellular structure organization, and extracellular matrix organization, cellular component (CC) term were related to collagen-containing extracellular matrix. Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) (Figure 1D) analysis suggested that genes were significantly enriched in cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, TNF signaling pathway, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway and other pathways. These results indicate that the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice has significant transcriptomic alterations, which are significantly correlated with the changes in the inflammatory response and extracellular matrix.

Transcriptome analysis of the liver from C26 tumor-bearing mice. (A) Heat map of differentially expressed genes in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice. (B) Volcano map. (C) Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis. (D) Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) Pathway analysis.

The NLRP3 inflammasome pathway was activated in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice

By analyzing the RNA data, we found that the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice showed a severe inflammatory response. Through GSEA analysis we found that liver alterations were significantly correlated with interleukin response and PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling pathway (Figure 2A), there are important pathways associated with the inflammatory response. Furthermore, by analyzing the expression profiles of various interleukin families in C26 tumor-bearing mice, the mRNA levels of most interleukin families were found to be up-regulated (Figure 2B). In addition, we noted that the expression of IL-1β was also elevated, as an important molecule in the NLRP3 signaling pathway, we asked whether NLRP3 signaling pathway was activated in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice. Therefore, we analyzed the expression of NLRP3 inflammasome-related genes and found that the expression of most NLRP3 inflammasome-related genes was increased (Figure 2C). Furthermore, we directly analyzed the expression of NLRP3, caspase-1, gasdermin D and IL-1β, and found that their expression was significantly increased compared with the control group (Figure 2D-G). These results suggest that the NLRP3 signaling pathway is activated in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice, suggesting that cancer can induce hepatic NLRP3 inflammasome activation.

The NLRP3 Inflammasome pathway was activated in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice. (A) GSEA enrichment analysis suggested that the liver changes in C26 tumor-bearing mice were significantly related to the interleukin response and PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling pathway. (B) Heat map of expression of interleukin family factors. (C) Heatmap showing the expression levels of NLRP3-inflammasome-related genes in the liver. (D-G) mRNA expression of NLRP3, caspase-1, gasdermin D and IL-1β. *p < 0.05 indicate the significance between the two groups.

MCC950 inhibited NLRP3 activation and alleviated liver injury and inflammation

To better understand the role of NLRP3 inflammasome in liver changes, we established a cancer cachexia mouse model by subcutaneous injection of C26 cells. MCC950, an NLRP3 specific inhibitor, was injected intraperitoneally on day 12 and sacrificed on day 21 (Figure 3A). We extracted proteins from the liver and examined several key proteins in the NLRP3 signaling pathway. The results are shown in the Figure 3B, the overall protein expression of NLRP3 was elevated, and several key proteins cleaved-caspase-1 and cleaved-IL-1β were also elevated. Moreover, the expression of these proteins was effectively inhibited by MCC950. Similarly, the results of qPCR also confirmed this conclusion (Figure 3C). Furthermore, H&E staining of the liver was performed, the results are shown in Figure 3D, we saw that liver staining in the C26 group showed liver injury and even focal necrosis. In contrast, the mice treated with MCC950 showed partial relief of liver injury and focal necrosis. To further investigate the anti-inflammatory effect of MCC950, several inflammatory markers were also stained. The results are shown in Figure 3E, immunohistochemical staining of myeloperoxidase (MPO) and F4/80 indicated hepatic inflammatory cell infiltration in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice. However, this hepatic inflammatory cell activation was attenuated by MCC950. In summary, the NLRP3 signaling pathway is activated in tumor-bearing mice, accompanied by liver damage and inflammatory responses. MCC950 is capable of mitigating these damages and inflammatory reactions.

MCC950 alleviated liver fibrosis in tumor-bearing mice

we first analyzed the liver transcriptome data of C26 tumor-bearing mice using GSEA enrichment analysis and found that liver changes were significantly correlated with reactome extracellular matrix organization, ECM glycoproteins and reactome ECM proteoglycans (Figure 4A-C), these are some of the pathways involved in fibrosis24. Furthermore, Masson’s trichrome stain was used to detect liver fibrosis (Figure 4D-F). We measured the serum levels of ALT and AST in mice, and the results indicated that MCC950 could alleviate the hepatic fibrosis burden in tumor-bearing mice. Meanwhile, the alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) expression, a unique marker for activated hepatic stellate cells (HSCs), was also performed. These results suggest that liver fibrosis indeed exists in tumor-bearing mice. However, MCC950 could attenuate tumor-induced liver fibrosis. In addition, qPCR was used to detect the mRNA expression level of transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1), Col1a1, and Col3a1, MCC950 significantly reduced the expression of the aforementioned pro-fibrotic marker genes in the liver. These results suggested that liver fibrosis existed in C26 tumor-bearing mice, and MCC950 effectively alleviated liver fibrosis in C26 tumor-bearing mice.

MCC950 alleviated muscle atrophy and fat loss in tumor-bearing mice

Activation of NLRP3 inflammasome has been reported to be involved in muscle atrophy in a variety of diseases, and it has also been observed in the adipose tissue of cancer patients25. Therefore, we wondered whether NLRP3 also plays a role in wasting within the tumor-burden model. Firstly, we examined serum levels of IL-1β, an important inflammatory factor that was thought to result in wasting. As expected, MCC950 effectively reduced IL-1β levels in serum (Figure 5A). In addition, as shown in Figure 5B-D, the body weight of C26 tumor-bearing mice decreased significantly, and MCC950 alleviated the body weight loss caused by the tumor. Muscle and adipose tissue mass were also measured. Similarly, MCC950 alleviated muscle atrophy and fat loss (Figure 5E-H). We also measured feeding in mice and showed that MCC950 did not increase food intake (Figure 5I). Overall, MCC950 reduced serum IL-1β levels and alleviated wasting in tumor-bearing mice.

MCC950 alleviated muscle atrophy and fat loss in tumor-bearing mice. (A) ELISA was used to measure the level of IL-1β in serum. (B-C) Whole body weight and non-tumor body weight. (D) Body weight change. (E-H) Tissue weight: Gastrocnemius, Tibialis, e-WAT and i-WAT. (I) Food intake at day 21. Data were expressed as means ± SEM (Con group, n = 6; C26 group, n = 7; C26 + MCC950 group, n = 7). *p < 0.05 indicate the significance between the two groups.

MCC950 did not inhibit tumor growth and expression of tumor secretory factors

It is known that the body consumption induced by a tumor is closely related to the mass of the tumor and the various factors secreted by the tumor. Therefore, we wondered whether MCC950 influenced tumor proliferation and secretion of various factors. As shown in the Figure 6A-B, MCC950 did not affect the final tumor volume and did not affect tumor growth. Meanwhile, RT-PCR was used for the detection of mRNA expression of activin A, myostatin, IL-6, lif and pthlh. These are tumor-secreted factors known to promote wasting. The results showed that the transcriptional levels of these factors did not change significantly (Figure 6C-G). These results suggest that MCC950 does not affect tumor growth and secretion of some factors that can cause wasting.

Discussion

As we all know, a large number of people died of cancer in every country every year1. However, the cause of death of cancer patients is often not cancer itself, but the various complications caused by cancer. Cancer cachexia is a syndrome characterized by weight loss, muscle atrophy and fat loss4. Although there have been many studies on it, its pathophysiological mechanism of it is still not clear. And so far, there is no effective treatment for this syndrome.

Previous studies have found that tumors not only cause the wasting of muscle and fat26, but also cause damage to other organs of the body, such as cardiac muscle atrophy27, reduced feeding28, and altered liver metabolism22,29. Previous studies have shown that reducing muscle loss or fat loss does not completely reverse the shortened lifespan of cancer patients30, suggesting that there must be other effects in cancer patients that lead to their death. The liver is a known detoxification organ, there are many enzymes in its internal, is a very important metabolic reaction site31. It has been reported that significant liver damage and metabolic changes occur in tumor-bearing mouse models, but the specific mechanisms are poorly understood22.

Firstly, we analyzed the liver transcriptome of C26 tumor-bearing mice and found significant differences between the control group and the C26 group. Through further analysis, we found that the inflammatory response in the liver was strong, and various inflammatory response factors were elevated. Finally, NLRP3 inflammasome was found to be activated in the liver. This implies that our study provides evidence for the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway in the liver of a cancer cachexia model. NLRP3 is an important component of the innate immune system that mediates the secretion of IL-1β and IL-18 in response to microbial infection and cell damage32.

The RNA-seq data (GSE154219) derived from the livers of C26 tumor-bearing mice indicated a downregulation in the transcriptional level of IL18. However, our animal experimental analysis demonstrated a significant upregulation of IL18 transcription in C26 tumor-bearing mice. This discrepancy is highly likely attributable to a false negative in the transcriptomic sequencing results.

The secretion of this inflammatory factor can cause liver damage and fibrosis. Furthermore, we carried out animal experiments33.

MCC950 is a specific NLPR3 inflammasome inhibitor34. In our study, we investigated the role of NLRP3 in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice by inhibiting NLRP3 inflammasome activation by intraperitoneal injection of MCC950. We first examined the expression of markers of the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, including NLRP3, IL-1β and caspase-1, in the animal model. We found that their levels were all increased, which was consistent with the results obtained from the GEO database. In conclusion, the NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathway was activated in the liver of the cachexia model.

MCC950 inhibited the activation of this pathway. Previous studies have shown that liver damage occurs in cancer cachexia model mice22, and our staining results confirmed this conclusion, MCC950 attenuated the liver damage in tumor-bearing mice. Similarly, MCC950 attenuated the inflammatory response as measured by inflammatory response markers. This suggests that the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome is involved in liver injury in tumor-bearing mice.

Liver fibrosis is a pathological feature after liver injury, and its main features are the deposition of fibrin and the increase of extracellular matrix35. Many studies have shown that liver inflammation is closely related to liver fibrosis36,37. IL-1β has also been shown to promote liver fibrosis38. By GSEA analysis, we found a correlation between the liver and extracellular matrix response in C26 tumor-bearing mice, and in our mice, we observed liver fibrosis. As expected, MCC950 effectively alleviated liver fibrosis in C26 tumor-bearing mice. The elevated mRNA levels of several fibrosis-related MARKER genes, TGF-β, col1a1 and col3A1, were also evaluated, which were all reduced by MCC950 treatment. In conclusion, NLRP3 activation in the cancer cachexia model leads to liver fibrosis, and MCC950 can effectively attenuate this change.

Some studies have shown that NLRP3 plays an important role in sepsis-induced muscle atrophy19, and some studies have shown that NLRP3 can alleviate muscle atrophy in aged mice20. A recent study showed that the activation of NLRP3 inflammasome was observed in the fat of cancer patients25, suggesting that NLRP3 inflammasome may play an important role in cancer-induced fat loss. Therefore, in our study, we also observed the muscle fat mass of C26 tumor-bearing mice, and as expected, MCC950 was effective in alleviating muscle atrophy and fat loss. This suggests that NLRP3 inflammasomes may also play an important role in tumor-induced wasting. Furthermore, the effects of MCC950 on tumor growth and the secretion of tumor-depleting factors, such as IL-6 and LIF, were examined. The results showed that the tumor growth and secretion of depleting factors were not affected by MCC950.

Certainly, our study has certain limitations. Specifically, we solely employed drug blockade and did not utilize alternative methods, such as NLRP3 KO mice, to investigate the NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathway. This approach falls short of comprehensively elucidating the role of the NLRP3 inflammasome signaling pathway in organ alterations in tumor-bearing mice. Moreover, MCC950 demonstrates lower specificity in targeting NLRP3 compared to genetic knockout techniques. In future work, we will further investigate this.

Collectively, our study demonstrated that the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway is activated in the liver of C26 tumor-bearing mice. Treatment with NLRP3 specific inhibitor MCC950 can effectively alleviate liver injury and fibrosis. And can alleviate the body’s muscle atrophy and fat loss. These results illustrate the important role of NLRP3 in liver injury and point out the important role of liver tissue in atrophy in tumor-bearing mice. Targeting the NLRP3 inflammasome holds promise as a novel therapeutic strategy for cancer patients with concurrent cachexia and hepatic dysfunction. By modulating systemic inflammation and organ-specific pathological damage, this approach may simultaneously ameliorate muscle wasting and liver dysfunction, overcoming the limitations of traditional single-target therapies. What’s more, NLRP3 inhibitor MCC950 is expected to be an effective drug to improve liver damage and consumption in patients with cancer cachexia.

Dynamic small object feature enhancement and detection for remote sensing images
Authors: Shouluan Wu, Hui Yang, Liefa Liao, Chao Song, Qiuming Liu, Jianglong Fu, Tan Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractRemote sensing images are extensively utilized for Earth observation; however, they often encounter challenges such as extremely small objects, cluttered backgrounds, and constraints related to edge computing. Current studies frequently face difficulties in achieving an optimal balance between recognition accuracy and efficiency to satisfy the requirements of various embedded devices. We propose DFE-DETR, a lightweight dynamic feature-enhanced detector that refines RT-DETR for remote-sensing scenes. The model relies on three core modules: the Sparse Attention Enhancement Module (SAEM) that prunes irrelevant tokens via Top-k scoring to cut compute, the Multi-scale Convolutional Attention Enhancement Module (MSCAEM) that employs multi-branch depth-wise strip convolutions to sharpen slender targets, and the Deformable Large Kernel Convolution Module (DLKCM) that uses adaptive sampling to locate irregular objects. Experiments demonstrate that this framework achieves a significant performance breakthrough on the VisDrone2019 dataset with only 23.6 M parameters, reaching 47.34% mAP@0.5 and 65.8 FPS. On the SIMD dataset, it attains 82.79% mAP@0.5 and 74.6 FPS. Meanwhile, on the RSOD and NWPU VHR-10 datasets, it achieves mAP@0.5 scores of 96.58% and 93.28%, respectively. Ablation experiments validate the synergistic effects of the modules, and visualization heatmaps indicate that the proposed model provides the most comprehensive target perception, accurately delineating target contours. In comparative experiments, the model outperformed mainstream YOLO series and specialized UAV detection models, offering a new high-precision real-time detection solution for embedded scenarios such as disaster response and traffic monitoring.Keywords: Remote sensing object detection, UAV, Deformable conv, DETRSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, the rapid advancement of remote sensing technology has propelled small target recognition in aerial imagery to the forefront of computer vision research1,2. Leveraging its extensive coverage advantages, aerial remote sensing imagery has been widely utilized in critical scenarios such as disaster emergency response4, urban traffic monitoring5, and agricultural resource surveys6. However, constrained by sensor resolution and the high-altitude imaging characteristics, targets often exhibit features such as small size, low contrast, and insufficient information density7. Simultaneously, the nonlinear scale–height relationship of UAV platforms—where slight changes in flight altitude can cause significant fluctuations in target scale—along with motion blur induced by high-speed movement and complex scene interference, makes foreground/background differentiation exceptionally challenging, imposing higher demands for high-precision real-time detection. Although existing methods demonstrate acceptable localization accuracy, they suffer from high computational complexity and poor real-time performance, urgently requiring lightweight designs and multi-scale feature fusion to enhance system robustness. Object detection technology has evolved from manual feature engineering to deep learning methods. Two-stage detectors (e.g., Faster R-CNN8 achieve high-precision localization through a region proposal network and a refined classification cascade, while single-stage detectors (e.g., RetinaNet, YOLO series9,10 balance real-time performance with end-to-end multi-scale feature pyramids and have been widely applied in industrial scenarios involving tiny objects. Ahmed Gomaa’s team further improved YOLOv8 with low-rank decomposition, significantly reducing manual annotation through hierarchical clustering11. However, single-stage methods still face limitations in cross-scale fusion under complex scenarios, resulting in bottlenecks in the localization accuracy of small targets and making it difficult to directly meet the real-time requirements of UAV high-altitude dynamic imaging.

In 2020, Facebook AI Research introduced the Detection Transformer (DETR), which was the first model to achieve end-to-end object detection using a pure Transformer architecture. This innovation overcame the limitations of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in terms of receptive fields by employing a self-attention mechanism, thereby demonstrating exceptional performance in complex scene association reasoning12. However, its high computational cost and the challenges associated with post-processing still impede practical deployment. To address these issues, RT-DETR surpasses both the accuracy and speed of the YOLO series at the same scale, thanks to its non-maximum suppression (NMS)-free design13. In the context of remote sensing imagery, the relationship between platform altitude and target scale is nonlinear: a mere change of tens of meters in flight altitude can lead to a multiplicative scaling of the same target within the image. Furthermore, motion blur caused by high-speed movement and interference from complex backgrounds intensify the need for cross-scale feature consistency and real-time performance. Therefore, it is essential to introduce a lightweight dynamic enhancement mechanism tailored for UAV scenarios within the DETR/RT-DETR framework to improve the robustness of small target detection. Inspired by the Transformer, Xue et al. proposed a cross-UAV framework that utilizes regional semantic association modeling and multi-head sparse self-attention. By adaptively capturing invariant target information from heterogeneous images and integrating a multi-view correction strategy, this framework effectively fuses complementary information across UAVs, significantly reducing background interference and addressing illumination variations14. The core innovations of RT-DETR include two key aspects: first, a hybrid encoder architecture for multimodal feature fusion is designed, achieving multiscale semantic representation through cross-level feature interaction; second, a novel Intersection over Union (IoU)-based dynamic query initialization mechanism is introduced, which directly filters effective object queries from the encoder output using a spatial location-sensitive IoU matching strategy, thereby eliminating the redundant region proposal steps typical of traditional two-stage methods. Compared to the original DETR architecture, RT-DETR completely eliminates post-processing steps such as NMS.

By comprehensively capturing target characteristics, researchers have significantly improved the processing efficiency and accuracy of real-time object detection systems through the introduction of multi-scale feature fusion and attention mechanisms, enabling the algorithm to achieve effective recognition in complex scenarios. The research team proposed a real-time aerial vehicle tracking framework that integrates morphological detection and motion feature analysis. This framework employs an adaptive method for multi-scale vehicle initial detection and utilizes the KLT tracker along with K-means clustering to mitigate background interference, thereby achieving collaborative optimization of detection and tracking15. In response to the increasing complexity of networks and the growing number of computational parameters, Shen et al. developed a lightweight deep convolutional network and a hybrid attention fine-grained feature recognition framework. They reduced the computational load by implementing a lightweight single-scale detection algorithm and designed a hybrid attention pruning mechanism to enhance fine-grained classification capabilities, achieving multi-stage collaborative optimization of localization and recognition16,17. Researchers are currently focusing on model lightweighting while incorporating a lightweight multi-scale feature extraction network. The semantic feature enhancement module integrates contextual information with residual attention, enabling high-precision target recognition18. Xue et al. proposed a UAV vision-language tracking framework based on Dynamic Sparse Learning and Multi-Level Language Perception which generates multimodal prompts through dynamic sparse attention and combines an efficient Transformer backbone network to facilitate sparse interaction between language and visual features, significantly enhancing the robustness of target representation19. Xue et al.20 proposed a three-stage query-guided re-detection tracker based on Siamese neural networks. This approach maintains target appearance features through a dynamic query update (QU) branch, utilizes a cross-fusion layer (CFL) to search for semantic associations between features and updated queries, thereby enhancing the distinction of occluded targets. Additionally, it employs Kalman filter trajectory prediction (TP) to stabilize tracking trajectories during complete occlusion, which significantly improves the tracking robustness of occluded targets in UAV scenarios. Furthermore, researchers have proposed a UAV target tracking method based on global proxies to address challenges such as background interference in target recognition21. This method dynamically aggregates features from both target and background regions through a target-distractor perception module, models global dependencies using a self-attention mechanism, and guides local feature generation to enhance target distinctiveness, which significantly improves tracking capabilities in complex backgrounds.

Currently, the deployment of high-performance real-time detection systems is constrained by the computational resources of aviation platforms, presenting significant technical challenges. Hardware constraints make it difficult to achieve an optimal balance between detection accuracy and computational efficiency on embedded platforms. These conflicting relationships necessitate that algorithm design establish a dynamic balance mechanism among model lightweighting, feature representation completeness, and hardware adaptability. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a small object detection scheme for UAV perspectives called DFE-DETR, which is based on an improved RT-DETR architecture. This approach facilitates the effective integration and extraction of features and enhances the model’s capability to recognize multi-angle targets, thereby significantly improving detection performance. Relate works

Generic vs. UAV detectors

Early architectures fall into two camps: two-stage detectors (Faster R-CNN, Cascade R-CNN22 for precision, and single-stage ones (SSD23, YOLO family24,25 for speed. In UAV imagery, however, the non-linear scale–altitude relationship and extreme small-target sparsity quickly expose the limits of fixed-grid YOLO heads and naïve FPN/PANet fusion26–28. Consequently, recent UAV-oriented variants compress and re-scale the YOLO backbone: YOLO-HR adds lightweight hybrid ECA + CA attention29, Yu et al. embed weighted BiFPN for SAR ships30, RCA31 and MLCA32 insert channel- or mixed-local attention to suppress high-altitude clutter. UAV-YOLO33, Drone-YOLO34, FFCA-YOLO7 and YOLO-MIF35 further trim parameters and refine multi-scale heads, yet al.l keep the underlying 3 × 3 convolutional inductive bias, leaving irregular, elongated targets under-represented.

Limitations analysis of current solutions

Despite the foundational role of traditional computer vision paradigms in the development of UAV detection, the unique imaging conditions of UAV platforms—characterized by high-altitude perspectives, dynamic lighting variations, and complex terrains—introduce multidimensional challenges. These challenges encompass geometric distortions arising from multi-angle observations, edge blurring due to background clutter, variable target scale and orientation distributions, and declining recall rates for small targets under low-resolution conditions36. To mitigate these issues, researchers have implemented targeted optimizations through task-specific scenario subdivisions. Notably, the nonlinear relationship between target height and size in UAV imagery necessitates transcending traditional single-scale detection frameworks. Cutting-edge methods now utilize pyramid multi-scale feature fusion architectures (e.g., hybrid FPN-BiFPN modules) and dynamic scale adaptation mechanisms (including adaptive anchor box generation and multi-resolution feature interaction), which have significantly enhanced small object detection performance while preserving real-time capabilities37. The research team aimed to extract small feature targets while eliminating background interference, proposing an aerial small object tracker based on the Siamese network architecture. They innovatively integrated a Wavelet Pooling Layer (WPL) that extracts both high-frequency and low-frequency sub-band features, retaining discriminative information through wavelet domain decomposition. Additionally, they combined this with a Graph Enhancement Module (GEM), which leverages graph neural networks to emphasize target responses. This collaborative approach effectively addresses the challenges associated with small object feature loss and background interference38. Current research has proposed multi-dimensional technical solutions, such as incorporating dilated or deformable convolutional kernels at the feature extraction level to improve scale adaptability. However, the physical characteristics of high-altitude UAV operations—marked by diverse target scales and miniature dimensions—continue to pose challenges. Alternatively, real-time detection and tracking of UAVs can be achieved by employing morphological and feature clustering methods. By utilizing K-means clustering, moving feature points are effectively separated to mitigate background interference. Furthermore, the challenges of occlusion and scale variation are addressed through the integration of a tracker with a trajectory connection mechanism39. To address these, researchers have introduced cascade detection architectures, multi-scale feature fusion networks, lightweight attention mechanisms, and HRDNet optimizations for dense target scenarios40–42. Through the synergistic optimization of scale-adaptive feature engineering and lightweight network design, these methods substantially enhance small target detection capabilities in UAV contexts while maintaining real-time performance. Sabina Umirzakova et al. proposed the RASST lightweight rotation-aware semi-supervised Transformer framework, which integrates a rotation-aware Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture with an adaptive rotation convolution module. This framework achieves precise alignment of target orientations through dynamically adjusted rotating convolution kernels and incorporates a pseudo-label guided learning (PGL) approach. Furthermore, it combines rotation-aware adaptive weighting (RAW) and global consistency (GC) loss to optimize annotated data, significantly enhancing the detection robustness of multi-angle targets in aerial images while reducing the number of parameters43.

Recent efforts have focused on mitigating the adverse effects of Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) on small target detection, particularly in the context of remote sensing. Achieving intelligent interpretation of high-quality images remains a core challenge in this field. The RT-DETR model offers an efficient solution for multi-scale detection through its hybrid encoder architecture and unordered set prediction paradigm44. Mainstream Transformer-based object detectors, such as DINO and Focus-DETR, demonstrate high accuracy in general scenarios. However, their excessive computational load and deficiencies in real-time performance severely hinder deployment on UAV edge devices. Taking DINO as an example, its multi-layer Transformer encoder and cross-scale fusion design cause the FLOPs per frame to skyrocket to 273.7G. Meanwhile, Focus-DETR, despite optimizations through local attention, still requires 171.5G FLOPs, significantly exceeding the hardware limitations of onboard systems. The rationale for selecting RT-DETR as a baseline model stems from this issue. It employs a collaborative architecture that combines a CNN backbone with a lightweight Transformer encoder, significantly reducing GFLOPs and achieving real-time performance recognition capabilities. Moreover, it circumvents the post-processing delay associated with NMS through a single-pass decoding design. This design greatly optimizes computational efficiency while ensuring accuracy, aligning precisely with the core demand for a “speed-accuracy balance” in UAV missions and providing an engineering-level solution for resource-constrained scenarios. This architecture effectively decouples intra-scale feature interaction from cross-level multi-scale fusion, significantly reducing computational complexity. By reformulating the traditional dense detection task as an unordered set prediction problem, RT-DETR employs a sparse query mechanism to directly generate object candidate boxes, thus avoiding the computational overhead associated with NMS in YOLO detectors45. Within the unordered set prediction framework, the model requires only feature classification score ranking for final predictions, thereby eliminating delays caused by dense candidate box generation and iterative NMS processes inherent in conventional methods. Despite RT-DETR’s superiority over existing real-time detectors and similarly scaled end-to-end frameworks in terms of speed and accuracy, it encounters performance bottlenecks in small object detection—specifically in localization precision and recall rates—when compared to high-performance real-time detectors. This limitation primarily arises from deficiencies in feature extraction, notably insufficient dense sampling and inadequate multi-scale representation capabilities. To address these gaps, we propose an efficient framework, Dynamic Feature Enhancement DETR (DFE-DETR), aimed at improving remote sensing image analysis. Departing from these incremental YOLO derivatives, DFE-DETR retains RT-DETR’s end-to-end Transformer backbone but introduces the Deformable Large-Kernel Convolution Module (DLKCM), a biomimetic receptive-field expansion inspired by primate visual cortex neurons. Unlike previous small-kernel or static dilated designs, DLKCM learns irregular sampling offsets on-the-fly, enabling the network to “stretch” its receptive field along arbitrary target axes while keeping computation linear in the number of sampled points. Coupled with the Sparse Attention Enhancement (SAEM) and Multi-Scale Convolutional Attention (MSCAEM) modules, DFE-DETR thus uniquely addresses UAV-specific scale variance and anisotropic target shapes without the parameter explosion seen in recent YOLO variants.

Method

DFE-DETR architecture

To address the challenges of object detection in complex remote sensing scenarios, this study proposes a dynamic multi-scale feature enhancement detection framework (DFE-DETR) based on the RT-DETR architecture. The overall structure of the model is illustrated in Fig. 1. First, ResNet-18 is utilized as the backbone network to extract global image features, resulting in high-semantic feature S5, mid-semantic feature S4, and low-semantic feature S3. High-semantic features contain rich information. These features are connected with the Sparse Attention Enhancement Module (SAEM), which enables the attention mechanism to concentrate on target features and extract detailed information. Additionally, a dynamic sparse attention module is employed to facilitate adaptive sparse weight allocation, which is closely integrated with the AIFI (Attention-based Intra-scale Feature Interaction) module to achieve adaptive feature fusion. This enhancement improves the representation of common features among similar target elements (such as buildings and vehicles) in remote sensing images, while also suppressing background noise interference. Subsequently, a multi-scale convolutional feature interaction network was constructed in the CCFM (Cross-Scale Feature Fusion Module), utilizing a multi-scale convolutional attention module. This module employs multiple sets of multi-scale convolutional kernels to extract spatial features across various granularities. This approach achieves dynamic feature complementarity and spatial attention excitation through an element-wise multiplication mechanism. Simultaneously, a deformable convolutional layer is introduced to adaptively adjust the sampling grid, effectively addressing the diversity in target scale, pose, and imaging modalities. These modules collaborate through various operations, including Fusion, Upsampling, convolutional channel transformation, and RepC3, to generate queries that are rich in semantic information. The Decoder on the right processes this query in conjunction with encoder features, reconstructing and refining the features through the Decoder and Head module. Finally, a detection head with shared weights is employed for the joint regression prediction of target categories and bounding box parameters, striking a balance between computational efficiency and representational accuracy. SAEM

Our designed dynamic sparse attention augmentation module represents an efficient enhancement of the traditional Transformer self-attention mechanism. The core concept of this module is to dynamically filter key regions in the input sequence, thereby reducing computational complexity while maintaining the perception of global information. Specifically, the module first generates importance scores for each position based on input features, subsequently selecting the most relevant local regions and global key points through a Top-k strategy. Dense attention calculations are then performed exclusively on the sparsely selected regions. This mechanism reduces the O(N²) complexity of traditional self-attention mechanisms to O(kN) (where k ≪ N). The dynamic sparse mechanism significantly minimizes redundant computations and adapts to the features of varying samples. In remote sensing image tasks, such as high-resolution image detection, this module strikes a balance between efficiency and accuracy, offering a lightweight solution for complex perception tasks in resource-sensitive scenarios.

MSCAEM

After passing through the SAEM and AIFI modules, the features undergo adaptive feature fusion. In response to the unique shapes of various targets in drone images, we have designed a multi-scale convolutional attention enhancement module to extract detailed feature information more effectively. This module aggregates local features through depthwise convolution and captures information at different scales using multi-branch depthwise strip convolution, as illustrated in Fig. 3. Building on this, a 1 × 1 convolution establishes inter-channel relationships, and its output serves as attention weights to adaptively adjust the input features of the multi-scale convolution. The module employs a multi-branch design with kernel sizes of 3, 5, and 7 for each branch, achieving a global receptive field equivalent to large-scale convolution kernels by stacking two depthwise strip convolutions. The choice of depthwise strip convolution is primarily based on two considerations: first, it significantly reduces computational load through a decomposed structure (for instance, replacing the standard 7 × 7 convolution with a combination of 7 × 1 and 1 × 7 convolutions); second, for strip-shaped objects commonly found in segmentation scenarios (such as human bodies and utility poles), strip convolution complements traditional grid convolution, effectively enhancing the extraction capability of slender spatial features. The input data of the module undergoes depthwise convolution and subsequently generates multi-scale feature maps through parallel three-branch strip-shaped convolutions.

DLKCM

The previous module successfully achieved feature fusion and enhancement. To further improve the model’s global perception capabilities, this study proposes a deformable large-kernel convolutional module that integrates a bionic visual perception mechanism, as illustrated in Fig. 4. This module emulates the global perception capability of the self-attention mechanism through large-sized convolutional kernels, effectively circumventing the high computational costs associated with traditional self-attention modules while preserving the advantages of wide-area modeling. The introduction of deformable convolution allows for dynamic optimization of sampling positions, enhancing the model’s feature adaptability to diverse data distributions through its adaptive deformation capabilities. This significantly improves detection capabilities for small and irregularly shaped targets, particularly under complex backgrounds or varying lighting conditions. The proposed deformable large-kernel convolution module employs a wide-range modeling mechanism, constructing a parameter-efficient ultra-large receptive field through a composite structure of depthwise separable convolution and dilated convolution. This design substantially reduces computational load while simulating the global perception characteristics of the self-attention mechanism via a hierarchical convolution kernel decomposition strategy. Dateset

The image examples of the dataset used in this study are shown in Fig. 5. The VisDrone2019 dataset is a widely utilized public resource that focuses on computer vision tasks from a drone’s perspective, including object detection, object tracking, and crowd counting. Released by the Machine Learning and Data Mining Lab at Tianjin University, the dataset was constructed by collecting data from various UAV models across diverse scenarios, including urban, suburban, and transportation hubs. This collection took into account complex weather conditions such as cloudy, sunny, rainy, and foggy environments, as well as dynamic lighting conditions, covering 14 urban and rural environments. It comprises a total of 10,209 high-resolution images, with 6,471 allocated for the training set, 548 for the validation set, and 3,190 for the test set. The dataset includes fine-grained annotations for ten typical target categories, including pedestrians, vehicles, and non-motorized vehicles. The dataset focuses on small object detection, with 60% of the target instances measuring less than 20 pixels and 25% measuring between 20 and 30 pixels. This distribution imposes greater demands on the algorithm. In addition to basic bounding box information, the data is annotated with key attributes, such as scene visibility and target occlusion status, thereby providing multi-dimensional support for algorithm optimization. The core challenges presented by this dataset arise from the unique characteristics of the UAV perspective, which include a high proportion of small targets (e.g., tiny pedestrians), dense overlap among targets, and complex background interference. These factors contribute to traditional detection models being susceptible to missed detections or false detections, significantly limiting the mAP. Consequently, this dataset holds substantial value in advancing research areas such as multi-scale feature fusion and occlusion robustness.

Dataset image display (four sample images from the dataset).

SIMD (Satellite Imagery for Multiscale Vehicle Detection) is a dataset designed for vehicle detection from drone and satellite perspectives, developed by a team from the National University of Sciences and Technology (NUST) in Pakistan. This dataset emphasizes multiscale vehicle object detection in complex scenarios, particularly addressing the challenge of recognizing small objects in low-altitude aerial images. It comprises 15 categories and 5,000 high-resolution images, each with dimensions of 1024 × 768 pixels, along with detailed vehicle classifications (such as cars and trucks). SIMD is specifically crafted to address challenges associated with multiscale objects, low contrast, and dense arrangements in low-altitude imagery, thereby supporting intelligent urban traffic perception. The dataset offers comprehensive annotations, including occlusion status and scene visibility. In contrast, VisDrone focuses on multi-task generalization, while SIMD explores the algorithmic robustness of vehicle detection. Together, they provide a critical data foundation for environmental perception and target recognition research from the perspective of unmanned aerial vehicles.

To verify the robustness of our algorithm, we conducted validation on two additional datasets: RSOD and NWPU VHR-10. RSOD is an open-source benchmark dataset designed for remote sensing image object detection tasks, encompassing four typical types of ground objects: aircraft, oil tanks, playgrounds, and overpasses. The dataset aims to enhance semantic understanding and foster algorithmic innovation in high-resolution remote sensing scenarios, and it strictly adheres to the PASCAL VOC annotation standards. The data is organized into four independent subsets, each corresponding to image samples of different target types, such as aircraft clusters in airport areas and oil storage facilities in industrial zones. Each category is accompanied by meticulously annotated files, which facilitate few-shot learning and domain adaptation research.

The NWPU VHR-10 dataset is a high-resolution remote sensing image object detection dataset released by Northwestern Polytechnical University (NWPU) in China. It comprises over 800 visible and infrared remote sensing images, encompassing 10 typical target categories, such as aircraft, ships, oil tanks, and baseball fields. This dataset is particularly suitable for military reconnaissance and civilian resource monitoring. The annotations provided include bounding boxes and target category labels, with certain data featuring rotated bounding box annotations to address the challenges posed by varying orientations of aerial targets. The dataset’s characteristics comprise complex backgrounds, including airports and ports, significant variations in target scales—ranging from vehicles as small as tens of pixels to ships as large as hundreds of pixels—and multimodal information, where visible light and infrared data complement each other.

Training equipment and evaluation indicators

All experiments were conducted under a standardized hardware configuration to eliminate environmental variable interference, thus ensuring the reliability and reproducibility of the results. The study employed a phased validation process: initially, parameter optimization was performed based on the training set, followed by validation on an independent test set. In scenarios with limited sample sizes, a hold-out validation strategy was implemented. We will conduct experiments on the VisDrone2019 and SIMD datasets to validate the model’s effectiveness. Additionally, to demonstrate the model’s robustness, further validation will be performed using the RSOD and NWPU VHR-10 datasets. Given GPU limitations and training duration, the input size was set to 640 × 640 with a batch size of 4. The model training utilizes the AdamW optimizer for parameter optimization, with a base learning rate configured at 1e-4 and a momentum coefficient set at 0.9. To comprehensively evaluate the model’s performance, a multi-dimensional evaluation system has been established, encompassing key metrics such as classification accuracy, recall rate, and mean average precision (mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.5:0.95).Mean Average Precision (mAP) is a crucial metric in object detection, representing the average detection accuracy across all categories. Specifically, mAP@0.5 indicates the average accuracy when the Intersection over Union (IoU) threshold is set at 0.5, while mAP@0.5:0.95 encompasses the average accuracy across all IoU thresholds ranging from 0.5 to 0.95. Result

Model performance on the datasets

To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the DFE-DETR model, this study conducts a multi-dimensional assessment utilizing the VisDrone2019 and SIMD benchmark datasets. The evaluation encompasses core metrics, including detection accuracy (Precision/Recall), real-time performance (FPS), model lightweightness (Params), and computational efficiency (GFLOPS). Through systematic comparative experiments, the study demonstrates the practical application potential of the algorithm in drone-based scenarios. Figure 6 illustrates the training accuracy curves for four datasets. It is noteworthy that we configured 250, 200, 150, and 200 training iterations for the VisDrone2019, SIMD, RSOD, and NWPU VHR-10 datasets, respectively. The curves indicate a rapid increase in the model’s accuracy following adequate training, ultimately stabilizing, which suggests that the model has attained its most stable learning outcome. According to the results presented in Table 2 and the confusion matrix depicted in Fig. 7, the car category exhibited the highest detection accuracy (Precision = 81.2%, mAP@0.5 = 85.6%), followed by the bus category (mAP@0.5 = 60.5%) and the pedestrian category (Precision = 67.5%). The limited number of samples for categories such as bicycles and tricycles with canopies presents significant challenges for detection, as evidenced by the recall rate for tricycles with canopies being only 16.5% and the mAP@0.5 for bicycles being merely 20.5%. The overall mAP@0.5:0.95 metric of the model is 28.82%, while the inference latency is controlled at 65.8 FPS, achieving a balance between real-time performance and accuracy. The confusion matrix indicates a misjudgment rate of 0.57 for pedestrians and 0.3 for the background, highlighting that small targets and complex backgrounds can adversely affect target recognition. Notably, although the detection accuracy for motorcycles reaches 0.69, there is a misjudgment rate of 0.2 in identifying them as background. Discussion and conclusion

This study addresses the core challenges of tiny object detection in remote sensing images—specifically, the small size of tiny objects, complex backgrounds, and limited computational resources—by proposing a dynamic feature enhancement detection framework, DFE-DETR, based on the improved RT-DETR. The model achieves a synergistic breakthrough in both accuracy and efficiency through a triple collaborative optimization mechanism: the dynamic sparse attention module (SAEM) reduces computational complexity, the multi-scale convolutional attention module (MSCAEM) enhances feature extraction for slender objects, and the deformable large kernel convolutional module (DLKCM) optimizes the localization of irregular objects. Experiments conducted on four datasets, including VisDrone2019 and SIMD, demonstrate that DFE-DETR achieves significant performance advantages with only 23.6 M parameters; it reaches 47.34% mAP@0.5 on VisDrone2019, surpassing many superior models, and 82.79% mAP@0.5 on SIMD, while maintaining real-time performance at 74.6 FPS. This validates its lightweight deployment value in embedded scenarios such as disaster response and traffic monitoring. However, there remains room for improvement in the model’s recall rate for extremely small targets, such as bicycles and covered tricycles. For instance, the mAP@0.5 for bicycle-like objects is only 20.5%, and the recall rate of 16.5% for covered tricycles in VisDrone highlights the limitations of detection in densely occluded scenes. Employing highly targeted data augmentation techniques, such as physically realistic occlusion simulation, complex background synthesis, and weather disturbances, can significantly enhance the model’s robustness to local incomplete features. In terms of training strategies, it is essential to design an adaptive scale sampling mechanism that ensures the network fully learns multi-granularity representations of extremely small targets and partially occluded objects during the training process. This approach helps avoid insufficient learning of small targets, which often results from scale bias in conventional multi-scale training. At the model architecture level, it is crucial to strengthen the model’s discriminative ability under sparse occlusion. Introducing a local-global collaborative attention mechanism can effectively prioritize the focus on local key feature regions that are not occluded. Additionally, knowledge distillation—transferring occlusion robustness knowledge from high-precision large models to lightweight models—can mitigate missed detections effectively. In the future, the approach will rely on data augmentation, with parameter tuning and module combination conducted through quantitative evaluation feedback. This will systematically address core challenges related to insufficient representation capabilities for small objects, similar classes, and occluded objects in dense scenes. Ultimately, this strategy aims to achieve a balanced improvement in detection accuracy and recall rate while also considering the balance of computational costs in actual deployment65. Additionally, the model’s adaptability to irregular targets needs enhancement. Future work will explore rotation-adaptive convolutional structures to improve orientation sensitivity, incorporate knowledge distillation techniques to further compress model size, and employ multi-modal fusion detection—such as infrared-visible light—for environments with multiple complex factors, thus addressing more intricate remote sensing application scenarios.

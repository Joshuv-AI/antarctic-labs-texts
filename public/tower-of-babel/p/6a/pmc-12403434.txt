Association between modifiable lifestyle behaviors and depressive symptoms among Chinese adolescents: a longitudinal analysis from 2019 to 2021
Authors: Dongling Yang, Liting Chu, Fengyun Zhang, Keyang Zheng, Wenjuan Qi, Shuangxiao Qu, Yanting Yang, Yuting Huang, Shenglei Huang, Chunyan Luo, Xiaodong Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe incidence of depression in adolescents is high, and depression has a great impact on children and adolescents, while the effects of changeable lifestyle on depression in adolescents are inconsistent, and there is a lack of longitudinal research results. To fill these knowledge gaps, we conducted a longitudinal study in Shanghai to evaluate the association between modifiable lifestyle behaviors and depression symptoms in adolescents.MethodsA total of 4314 students participated in the survey each year from 2019 to 2021 was included in this study. Students’ physical activity behavior, screen behavior, and sleep behavior were collected by questionnaires. Depressive symptoms were assessed using the Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES-D scale).ResultsThe detection rate of depressive symptoms in the study subjects was 23.6% in 2019, 23.2% in 2020, and 26.6% in 2021, and the trend test showed a significant upward trend in three years (P-trend = 0.001). Also, the detection rate of depressive symptoms in girls was higher than that of boys, but a significant rising trend was observed in boys over time (P-trend = 0.010). The generalized estimating equation analysis revealed that Students with TV time ≥ 2 h/day(OR = 1.29, 95%CI: 1.15, 1.46), computer time ≥ 2 h/day (OR = 1.20, 95%CI: 1.07, 1.36), mobile screen time ≥ 2 h/day(OR = 1.18, 95% CI: 1.08, 1.29), internet addiction(OR = 2.46, 95% CI: 1.81, 3.35), and inadequate sleep duration(OR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.60, 0.72) were associated with an increased likelihood of detecting depressive symptoms. Students who participated in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) for a minimum of 60 min 1 to 4 days per week (OR = 0.77, 95%CI: 0.68, 0.86) or 5 to 7 days per week (OR = 0.73, 95%CI: 0.64, 0.84) exhibited a reduced likelihood of depressive symptoms in comparison to students who did not engage in MVPA for at least 60 min per week (P < 0.05).ConclusionWe presented the climbing trend of depressive symptom rate over three years and identified several alarming factors that were associated with adolescent depression symptoms in Shanghai, China, notably in lower level MPVA, electronic devices use, and inadequate sleep with increasing depression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-23954-z.

Introduction

Depression has been ranked as the fourth leading cause of illness and disability in adolescents (World Health Organization, 2018). Depression in young people is an increasing concern not only because it occurs during a transitory period of rapid social, emotional, and cognitive development but its prevalence in young people has risen sharply in the past decade [1]. However, many cases are often overlooked due to misattributions of relevant symptoms to normal stress or being seen as a transient stage that adolescents commonly go through (World Health Organization, 2018). Overlooking elevated depressive symptoms can lead to tragic outcomes and high mortality as depression is highly associated with most suicides [2], the second leading cause of death among adolescents. In many individuals, the first onset of depression occurs in adolescence, and subclinical symptoms during this period are associated with an increased risk of adulthood disorder, magnifying the need to identify modifiable risk factors and interventions to prevent depression in later life [3, 4].

In any given year, approximately 20% of children and adolescents globally have mental health difficulties, including major depressive disorder [5], which is the same as the prevalence in Chinese adolescents [6]. The increased rates of mental problems were consistently reported to be prevalent among adolescents. The global point prevalence rate of elevated self-reported depressive symptoms among adolescents from 2001 to 2020 was 34%. Point prevalence of elevated depressive symptoms among adolescents increased from 24% between 2001 and 2010 to 37% between 2011 and 2020. The Middle East, Africa, and Asia have the highest prevalence of elevated depressive symptoms [7].

For a long time, lifestyle health behaviors have played an important role in the pathogenesis of chronic diseases, which provides a theoretical basis for behavioral and mental health recommendations for school-age children and adults [1]. A longitudinal study conducted in Swedish adolescents found that an increase in sleep duration during weekdays was significantly related to both a decrease in anxiety and an increase in health-related quality of life while this study did not find weekly light physical activities and sleep duration decreased and weekly sedentary time and screen time increased related to changes in mental health outcomes [8]. Another longitudinal study aimed at Chinese adolescents demonstrated that compared to students with persistently short screen time, those with persistently long screen time reported a higher risk of psychological symptoms. However, the association between changes in physical activity and psychological symptoms was not statistically significant [9]. The inconsistency of the results might be related to disparate study populations, various measurements of depression symptoms, and different cut-off points regarding the evaluation of depression symptoms.

Although several studies have suggested that modifiable lifestyle factors could play an important role in the prevention of depression [8]. However, Current results are inconsistent, and most studies lack longitudinal data. To fill these knowledge gaps, we conducted a longitudinal study in Shanghai to evaluate the association between modifiable lifestyle factors and depression symptoms in adolescents.

Method

Data sources and participants

Data were collected from the Surveillance of Students’ Common Diseases and Health Influencing Factors conducted in Shanghai, China from 2019 to 2021. The surveillance was carried out in all 16 districts of Shanghai from September to November each year. According to the level of social and economic development, seven districts were classified as urban areas (including the districts of Huangpu, Xuhui, Jing’an, Changning, Putuo, Hongkou, and Yangpu) and nine districts were classified as suburb areas (including districts of Pudong, Jinshan, Minhang, Fengxian, Songjiang, Qingpu, Jiading, Baoshan, and Chongming).

A multi-stage stratified cluster sampling method was used. Five schools (2 junior high schools, 2 senior high schools, and 1 vocational high school) were selected from each district of Shanghai. The survey grades included all grades of high school. At least 80 students were selected from each grade in each school. The number of students surveyed was 21,737 in 2019, 21,549 in 2020 and 21,715 in 2021. After matching with unique identification codes, a total of 4314 students who were followed up for 3 years from 2019 to 2021 were included in this study.

Survey method

From September to November 2019 to 2021, using the questionnaire of students’ health status and influencing factors(Seeing supplementary material), the project team organized students to fill in the questionnaires in the school uniformly every year. The questionnaire included basic information about the student and family (including gender, age, district, school types, educational level of mother, subjective family income, and family types), student’s behaviors (including moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA), TV time, computer time, mobile screen time, internet addiction and sleep duration) and depression symptoms. The question’s wording and details for lifestyle behavior variables were presented in the research group’s published paper [10], which is also attached to the supplementary material. The sleep standard was the recommended time of the American National Sleep Foundation. The sleep time of children aged 6 to 13 was within 9–11 h, and that of 14-17-year-olds was within 8–10 h, otherwise, it was not up to standard [11]. Internet addiction is defined by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, as detailed in Appendix 1.

Depressive symptoms were assessed using the Center for Epidemiologic Studies Depression Scale (CES-D scale). The CES-D is an available and valuable tool for screening for depression in children and adolescents, with a sensitivity of 0.81 and a specificity of 0.72 [12]. There are 20 items in CES-D, representing the main aspects of depressive symptoms. The score range was 0–60, and the higher the score, the higher the degree of depression. A score of 16 or greater indicates possible depressive symptoms.

Quality control

The project team prepared a unified survey manual and carried out the questionnaire survey in a unified way. The investigators shall be trained in a unified manner before they can participate in the investigation. After students completed the questionnaires, the quality control personnel checked the completeness, logic, and key core information of the questionnaires on the spot, and modified them on the spot if there was any problem.

Statistical analysis

The software SAS9.3 was used for data analysis. The chi-square test was used to analyze the difference in the detection rate of depressive symptoms among different groups (including different baseline conditions and different behaviors) in a single year. The chi-square trend test was used to analyze the changing trend in the detection rate of depressive symptoms from 2019 to 2021, including subgroup analysis. Generalized estimation equation analysis was used to analyze the influencing factors of the depression prevalence of adolescents from 2019 to 2021. The detection rate of depressive symptoms from 2019 to 2021 was used as the dependent variable. The basic information of students and families and student behaviors, including MVPA, TV time, computer time, mobile screen time, internet addiction, and sleep duration from 2019 to 2021 were used as independent variables. p < 0.05 indicated that the difference was statistically significant.

Among them, 2252(52.2%) were boys and 2062(47.8%) were girls. The participants’ ages ranged from 11.1 to 19.5 years, with an average age of 14.3 years. Depressive symptoms detection rate from 2019 to 2021

Overall, the detection rate of depressive symptoms in the study subjects was 23.6% in 2019, 23.2% in 2020, and 26.6% in 2021. The trend test indicated a significant upward trend over the three years (P-trend = 0.001). The detection rate of depressive symptoms in urban students had shown a consistent increase each year (P-trend < 0.001), while there was no significant trend among rural students (P-trend = 0.833). From 2019 to 2021, the detection rate of depressive symptoms in girls was consistently higher than that in boys, but a significant rising trend was observed in boys over time (P-trend = 0.010). Senior high school students consistently exhibited the highest detection rate of depressive symptoms compared to junior high school students and vocational school students. Students who reported poor family income had a higher detection rate of depressive symptoms compared to those reporting middle and high family income. Students from single-parent families, stepfamilies, and grandparent families had a higher prevalence of depressive symptoms than those from nuclear families and extended families (see Table 2).

Changes in lifestyle behaviors and depressive symptoms detection rate from 2019 to 2021

Table 3 shows the changes in lifestyle behaviors and the prevalence rate of depressive symptoms in the study population from 2019 to 2021. The percentage of students who engaged in at least 60 min of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity five to seven days a week decreased from 30.2% in 2019 to 24.6% in 2020 and then slightly increased to 27.4% in 2021. The percentage of students who spent 2 h or more playing computer games per day rose from 10.7% in 2019 to 13.6% in 2021. The percentage of students who spent ≥ 2 h daily on mobile electronic screens increased from 38.3% in 2019 to 42.1% in 2021. However, the percentage of students who watched TV for 2 h or more per day decreased annually. The percentage of students meeting the recommended sleep duration rose from 32.9% in 2019 to 39.9% in 2021. The prevalence of internet addiction among students fluctuated between 1.0% and 1.2% from 2019 to 2021, with no statistically significant difference. In the years 2019, 2020, and 2021, as the number of days with at least 60 min of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) per day increased, the rate of detecting depressive symptoms gradually decreased. The prevalence of depressive symptoms in students who watched TV for ≥ 2 h per day, played computer for ≥ 2 h per day, and used a mobile screen for ≥ 2 h per day was significantly higher than in those who spent less time on TV, computer, and mobile screen. The rate of depressive symptoms among students with internet addiction was significantly higher than among those without internet addiction. Students who met the recommended sleep duration had a lower rate of detecting depressive symptoms than students who did not meet the recommendation (see Table 3).

Association between modifiable lifestyle behaviors and depressive symptoms

After controlling for gender, district, age, educational level of mother, subjective family income and family styes, the analysis of the generalized estimating equation indicated that students who participated in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (MVPA) for a minimum of 60 min 1 to 4 days per week (OR = 0.77, 95%CI: 0.68, 0.86) or 5 to 7 days per week (OR = 0.73, 95%CI: 0.64, 0.84) exhibited a reduced likelihood of depressive symptoms in comparison to students who did not engage in MVPA for at least 60 min per week (P < 0.05). Other independent predictors of higher detecting depressive symptoms included TV time ≥ 2 h/day (OR = 1.29, 95% CI: 1.15, 1.46), computer time ≥ 2 h/day (OR = 1.20, 95% CI: 1.07, 1.36), mobile screen time ≥ 2 h/day (OR = 1.18, 95% CI: 1.08, 1.29), internet addiction (OR = 2.46, 95% CI: 1.81, 3.35), and inadequate sleep duration (OR = 0.66, 95% CI: 0.60, 0.72; Fig. 1).

Discussion

This research was a longitudinal study using repeated participants to examine the impact of lifestyle behaviors on depressive symptoms among Chinese adolescents from 2019 to 2021. Data showed an upward trend of depressive symptom detection rates over three years. Higher levels of MVPA were associated with less probability of depressive symptom detection, while the use of electronic devices and inadequate sleep duration increased the risk of depressive symptom detection. The findings highlight the importance of modifiable behavioral factors associated with psychological abnormalities in adolescence.

In this study, the rate of depressive symptoms detection among Chinese adolescents fluctuated between 23.2% and 26.6%, which were in line with earlier meta-analysis (24.3%) [13], while lower than Qi’s national survey during the early stage of the COVID-19 outbreak (36.6%) [14]. The differences might be the sample size and regional factors. The detection rate in 2020 was lower than that in 2019 and 2021. One possible reason might be the fluctuation of the screening data itself, as the data was collected using scales, which have limited accuracy. The second reason is that compared with 2019, the year before the pandemic of COVID-19, 2020 was the first year of the outbreak. China’s control measures were timely and effective. The short-term impact of the pandemic on the mental health of teenagers was not significant. In fact, due to the absence of classes, the academic pressure was somewhat reduced, and the detection rate of depression actually decreased. By the second year of the outbreak, 2021, the long-term and recurrent pandemic might have had a certain impact on the mental health of teenagers. Figures from the National Bureau of Statistics of China presented that there were 157.94 million adolescents in China in 2020, suggesting that nearly 36.6 to 42.0 million adolescents were suffering from depression symptoms extrapolating our study data [15]. Adolescent anxiety and depression frequently carry over into adulthood without the proper psychological interventions, increasing the risk factors of age-related diseases such as metabolic diseases, substance use, and chronic pain [16–18].

The present study demonstrated that sedentary behavior was associated with a greater risk of depressive symptoms [3]. Our results were in line with it and demonstrated the dip in higher-level MVPA over the study period. There are several potential explanations for this phenomenon. One possible reason was the outbreak and blockade of COVID-19, madding parents were apt to prevent their children from going out, thereby reducing the opportunities for outdoor activities for teenagers [19]. Our findings align with accelerometry studies in the positive correlation between amounts of time spent in MVPA and the decline of depressive symptoms in adolescents [20]. Engaging in physical activity has the potential to improve mental health by producing endorphins [21], raising brain-derived neurotrophic factor levels, and stimulating the formation of new blood vessels. These processes may ultimately improve the anatomical and functional makeup of the brain [22]. What’s more, increasing physical exercise may boost young people’s mental health overall by meeting their basic psychological requirements (such as a sense of purpose in life, social connection, and self-acceptance) [23]. Another objective cause for the downward trend in higher level MVPA was the growing academic stress in China [24], thus extra-curricular activities giving way to studies. A study showed that high expectations for academic achievement could result in stress, especially for Chinese high school students [25].

We discovered that using electronics (TV time, computer time, mobile screen time, and internet addiction) was linked to higher rates of detecting depression symptoms over three years. This was in accordance with the research in this field [25–29]. There may be bidirectional relationships between electronic devices use and physical activity, as long screen time increases the possibility of sedentary behavior [25]. There was valid evidence in this study that the relationships between screentime and depressive outcomes hold when adjusted MVPA, indicating that screentime may have an impact on mental health independently of the displacement of physical activity. Our findings also presented adolescent’s changing viewing habits in the digital age, as a modest growth trend in mobile and Internet screen time and a decline in TV time. To make matters worse, as screen time rises, adolescents may begin to substitute screen time for healthy habits such as reading, sleeping, physical activity, and physical social interactions [30]. When adjusted for MVPA and electronic use, adequate sleep duration has a positive relationship with the reduction of depressive symptom rate, in accordance with Ojio’s study [31]. One explanation could be that sleep deprivation or insomnia can impair emotional resilience, the reserve of good feelings that people use to cope with stress and life’s obstacles [32].

Concerning stable sociodemographic information, we found that adolescent girls, senior students, living in urban areas, low family income, and single-parent or grandparent families were associated with depressive symptoms. The age-sex pattern for depression disorders was in line with other studies [33–36]. Shanghai is a metropolis with a small gap between urban and rural areas, but adolescent depression in urban areas was on the rise over three years. This might be the heavier academic pressure. In addition, it is quite understandable that those who are in poor family conditions are more likely to have psychological problems [37], which could help explain other sociodemographic findings.

Strength and limitation

Participants in our study came from 80 schools in 16 districts, which varied in size and attributes (such as junior, senior high, and vocational schools; urban and rural schools). The participants are likely a fairly typical teenage population of Shanghai, China. Another strength is the longitudinal study which helps us observe the trend of depressive symptom detection rates and review the association in modifiable lifestyle factors from 2019 to 2021. The association between modifiable lifestyle factors and depression symptoms in adolescents clarified in this study calls for more attention to the intervention of depressive symptoms in the whole society and provides a scientific basis for early behavioral intervention of depressive symptoms. However, our study has several limitations. First, the study only obtained depressive symptoms for screening, not clinical depression. Second, the use of questionnaires to assess behaviors such as physical activity, screen time, and sleep was another limitation, due to questionnaire bias and the accuracy needs to be improved. An objective measure is recommended for use in the future study.

Conclusion

In summary, we have presented the climbing trend of depressive symptom rate over three years and identified several alarming factors that were associated with adolescent depression in Shanghai, China, notably in lower level MPVA, electronic devices use, and inadequate sleep with increasing depression. These findings call for the attention of limitation of screen time as well as encouragement of outdoor activities. Adolescent mental health management strategies should continue to emphasize individual, family, and school-level approaches. Future research could use objective measures to monitor MVPA levels among adolescents as well as boost MVPA among adolescents with multi-level interventions.

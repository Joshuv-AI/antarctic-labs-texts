Serum osteopontin levels predict short-term outcomes in patients with hepatitis B-related acute-on-chronic liver failure
Authors: Yu Liu, Miao Fang, Hongying Guo, Xin Zhang, Xue Mei, Longshan Ji, Wei Yuan, Yating Gao, Jiefei Wang, Zhiping Qian, Man Li, Yueqiu Gao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultiple analyses have suggested that osteopontin (OPN) is involved in acute and chronic liver disease. We aimed to investigate the value of serum OPN in predicting the short-term prognosis of patients with hepatitis B-related acute-on-chronic liver failure (HBV-ACLF).MethodsA total of 205 patients with HBV-ACLF were enrolled in a retrospective study and stratified into survivors and non-survivors according to their 90-day prognosis. Multivariate Cox regression and receiver operating characteristic curves were used to evaluate the predictive value of serum OPN levels for the 90-day prognosis of patients with HBV-ACLF.The prevalence of different OPN levels (<36.8 ng/ml; ≥36.8 ng/ml) and their relationship with 90-day prognosis were investigated.ResultsPatients with HBV-ACLF had significantly higher serum OPN levels than patients with HBV-LC, patients with CHB, and healthy subjects. Patients were assigned to low- and high-level OPN groups according to the cut-off value of OPN (36.8 ng/ml). Survival curves revealed that patients with high OPN levels had significantly poorer survival than those with low OPN levels, as determined using Kaplan-Meier analysis. Importantly, Multivariate Cox regression analysis revealed that OPN levels were an independent risk factor for 90-day adverse outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF according to the fully adjusted Model IV.ConclusionsOur results demonstrated that plasma OPN level is a clinically meaningful biomarker for predicting the short-term prognosis of patients with HBV-ACLF.Keywords: Acute-on-chronic liver failure, Osteopontin, Hepatitis B virus, Prognosis

Introduction

Chronic liver disease (CLD) is currently an increasing global problem.1,2 Acute-on-chronic liver failure (ACLF) is a complex syndrome characterized by acute deterioration of liver function in patients with pre-existing CLD.3 In China and other Asian countries, HBV infection is the major etiology of ACLF, accounting for 80 %–85 % of cases.4It may eventually progress to multiple organ failure, with

a short-term mortality rate ranging from 30 to 90 %.5 Therefore, new biomarkers for the early diagnosis and severity prediction of ACLF are necessary to reduce mortality rates.

OPN was first identified in 1986. OPN is a multifunctional, extracellular matrix-associated, secretory glycoprotein6. The OPN protein is encoded by the secreted phosphoprotein 1 gene, which has seven exons spanning 5 kilobases in length.7

Existing research evidence suggests that OPN exhibits multifaceted clinical value in the prognostic assessment of end-stage liver disease, including acute liver failure, nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), fatty liver disease, alcoholic liver disease, chronic hepatitis B (CHB), and chronic hepatitis C.8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 Serum OPN levels are significantly elevated in patients with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) and are closely associated with liver transplant-free survival, suggesting its potential as a prognostic biomarker.17 In HCC, the expression level of OPN shows a continuous increase during carcinogenesis and is positively correlated with tumor progression.18 A continuous increase in OPN expression has also been observed during the progression of non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) and HCC.19 These studies suggest that OPN is a promising prognostic biomarker for end-stage liver diseases.

However, relatively little is known about the clinical significance of OPN expression levels in ACLF progression. In this study, we compared serum OPN levels in patients with CHB, HBV-related liver cirrhosis (HBV-LC), HBV-ACLF, and healthy subjects. In addition, the association between serum OPN levels and the outcomes of patients with HBV-ACLF was analyzed to identify potential biomarkers for predicting the prognosis of patients with HBV-ACLF.

Methods

Ethics statement

This study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethics approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center (No. 2023-S013-01). As the investigation presented little risk to the participants and did not involve any procedures, the ethics committee of the Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center waived the need for written informed consent from patients.

Study design and subjects

This study employed a retrospective design. From February 2019 to February 2023, 357 hospitalized patients with HBV-ACLF were screened at the Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center, Shanghai, China. In accordance with the consensus recommendations of the Asian Pacific Association for the Study of the Liver (APASL),20 patients who fulfilled all of the following criteria were enrolled in the study: (1) had a history of previously diagnosed or undiagnosed HBV-related chronic liver disease or cirrhosis; (2) were between the ages of 18 and 80; (3) had a serum total bilirubin level≥5 mg/dl (85 μmol/L), as well as coagulopathy, evidenced by an international normalized ratio exceeding 1.5 or a prothrombin activity falling below 40 %; and (4) had clinical manifestations of ascites and hepatic encephalopathy (HE) within 4 weeks. Patients who met one or more of the exclusion criteria were excluded from the study: (1) coinfection with HAV, HCV, HEV, or HIV; (2) conditions inconsistent with the APASL criteria; (3) other chronic liver diseases; (4) severe extrahepatic disease; (5) a history of hepatotoxic, immunosuppressant, or anticancer drug use; (6) severe comorbidities that could affect survival; or (7) pregnancy. Ultimately, 205 patients with HBV-ACLF were enrolled in this study. Additionally, 54 patients with HBV-LC, 45 patients with CHB, and 83 healthy subjects (HS) were enrolled in this study. A flowchart of this study is presented in Fig. 1. Patients with HBV-LC and CHB were diagnosed according to the criteria outlined in previous reports.21,22 Serum was collected from all subjects.

Flow chart of all enrolled HBV-ACLF patients after applying the inclusion and exclusion criteria.

Data collection

Baseline data and demographic information were collected from participants at admission, including demographic data, incidence of complications, and laboratory measurements [e.g., ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, TBIL, ALB, INR, HBVDNA, HBeAg, CR, WBC, monocytes, neutrophils, and PLT]. MELD and CTP scores were calculated for each patient in accordance with the formulas published in previous studies.23,24

Follow-up

All patients with HBV-ACLF were followed up for 360 days. Patient outcomes were recorded at 28, 90, 180, and 360 days after enrollment. Patients who underwent liver transplantation within 90 days were included in the non-survivor group. Adverse outcomes included death, liver transplantation (LT), and HCC. The primary endpoint was the outcomes at 90-day. The secondary endpoints were the 28-day, 180-day and 360-day outcomes.

Measurement of serum OPN levels

Quantification of human OPN in plasma was performed using a Human Osteopontin Quantikine ELISA Kit (R&D Systems, Cat# DOST00), according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Statistical analysis

Categorical parameters are presented as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous variables are presented as medians and interquartile ranges. For nonparametric pairwise comparisons, the Mann-Whitney U test was employed, and for categorical variables, the chi-squared test or Fisher's exact test was utilized. The Kruskal-Wallis test was used to examine continuous variables that were non-normally distributed when there were more than three variables. The Kaplan-Meier technique was utilized to estimate the survival curves, and the log-rank test was employed to compare them. The ideal OPN cut-off value for grouping and stratification was established using a receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. All patients with HBV-ACLF were divided into high and low expression groups.

We assessed possible confounding variables influencing the prognosis of patients with HBV-ACLF using univariate and subgroup analyses. To determine whether OPN had a differential influence on outcomes by subgroup, an interaction analysis was performed using the Cox model, with an interaction term consisting of OPN and a subgroup variable. Forest plots were utilized to summarize the findings of the subgroup and interaction analyses. Multivariate Cox proportional hazard models were used to calculate the risk of death at 90 days, and the corresponding hazard ratios (HRs) and 95 % confidence intervals (CIs). In the Cox regression models, some variables that showed a significant difference between the survival and nonsurvival groups at 90 days in patients with HBV-ACLF were considered as possible confounders.

SPSS 25 (IBM, Seattle, WA, USA). GraphPad Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA) and R (version 4.0.4, http://www.r-project.org) were used for all statistical analyses. A two-tailed P < 0.05 was regarded as statistically significant.

Results

Baseline characteristics of enrolled subjects

The baseline clinical characteristics of patients with HBV-ACLF, LC, CHB, and healthy subjects are shown in Table 1. There were no significant differences in the percentage of patients who were positive for HBeAg-positive (P = 0.082). The serum OPN levels in patients with HBV-ACLF [34.86 [31.61–38.36] ng/ml] were significantly greater than those in healthy subjects [18.85 [17.78–19.65] ng/ml], patients with CHB [29.19 [25.56–33.76] ng/ml], and patients with HBV-LC [28.28 [26.16–32.93] ng/ml] (P < 0.001, Table 1 & Fig. 2A). The serum OPN levels in patients with HBV-ACLF were found to be the highest, followed by those in patients with CHB and HBV-LC, while healthy subjects exhibited the lowest levels of OPN. However, no significant difference was observed between the CHB and HBV-LC groups (P = 0.867, Fig. 2A).

Clinical characteristics of non-survivors and survivors of patients with HBV-ACLF

According to the 90-day prognosis, patients with HBV-ACLF were divided into survival (n = 136) and non-survival groups (n = 69). 55 patients died, and 14 patients underwent liver transplantation within 90 days. The transplant-free survival rate among the 205 HBV-ACLF patients was 66.3 % (136/205) within 90 days. We found that non-survivors were older than survivors (51.09 ± 12.68 vs. 47.26 ± 12.73 years, P = 0.043). Non-survivors had a higher frequency of HSR (14.5 % vs. 3.7 %, P = 0.005). In addition, the MELD [24.99 [21.59–30.11] vs. 20.78 [18.03–25.28], P < 0.001]and CTP [11 [10–12] vs.10 [10–11], P = 0.014] were significantly higher in non-survivors than in survivors. Relationships between serum OPN levels in patients and hepatic

Functional reserve

A total of 304 patients with HBV-related disease were categorized according to their MELD score and CTP grade. We found that the serum OPN levels in patients with MELD scores <15 (n = 85) were lower than those in patients with MELD scores ≥15 (n = 219) (P < 0.0001) (Fig. 2C). In addition, serum OPN levels were analyzed in patients with different CTP grades. Our results showed that the serum OPN levels in patients with CTP-A (n = 35) were the lowest among the three groups, and the differences were significant (P < 0.0001) compared with those in patients with CTP-B (n = 95) and patients with CTP-C (n = 174). In addition, the levels of serum OPN in patients with CTP-B were lower than those in patients with CTP-C (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2B). The above results showed that the serum OPN levels increased with decreasing hepatic functional reserve.

In addition, patients were classified according to their disease characteristics. We found no significant differences in serum OPN levels between HBeAg-positive (n = 113) and HBeAg-negative patients (n = 191) (P > 0.05, Fig. 2D). There were significant differences between HBV DNA positive patients (n = 173) and HBV DNA negative patients(n = 131) (P < 0.01, Fig. 2E). Similarly, no significant difference was found between cirrhotic (n = 161) and non-cirrhotic patients (n = 143) (P > 0.05, Fig. 2F).

Relationship between serum OPN levels and outcomes in patients

In this study, we investigated the possibility of using OPN as a prognostic factor for identifying 90-day adverse outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF. The ROCs for OPN, Serum lactate, MELD score, OPN + LAC, OPN + MELD score, and OPN + LAC + MELD score are shown in Fig. 3A. An AUROC analysis was used to evaluate the performance of OPN, which determined that the optimal prognostic cut-off value for OPN was 36.8 ng/ml with a high AUC value [0.630 (95 % CI: 0.550–0.709)] (Fig. 3). The AUROC of OPN expression for predicting 90-day adverse outcomes was [0.630 (95 % CI: 0.550–0.709)], which was higher than that of serum lactate (AUROC = 0.623), but lower than MELD (AUROC = 0.678), OPN + LAC (AUROC = 0.650), OPN + MELD (AUROC = 0.711), and OPN + LAC + MELD (AUROC = 0.716). However, in the pairwise comparison of these models, the AUROCs were not statistically different (Fig. 3B). These results indicate that OPN may be a potential prognostic biomarker of HBV-ACLF. Patients were subsequently categorized into low (n = 133) and high OPN (n = 72) groups according to the OPN cut-off value. The percentages of the different outcomes in the two groups are shown in Fig. 4A. Patients in the high OPN group had a higher incidence of 90-day adverse outcomes than those in the low OPN group (45.8 % vs. 27.1 %, P < 0.01, Fig. 4B). Similarly, patients in the high OPN group had a higher incidence of adverse outcomes at 180 and 360 days than those in the low OPN group (52.8 % vs. 29.3 %, P < 0.01; 55.6 % vs. 32.3 %, P < 0.01; Fig. 4B). However, the incidence of 28-day adverse outcomes was not significantly different between the two groups (Fig. 4B). Two patients in the low OPN group developed HCC during the 180–360 days of follow-up (Fig. 4A).

Serum OPN is an independent risk factor for outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed to identify the prognostic factors. The results showed that the serum OPN levels were excellent [HR 1.094; 95 % CI 1.030–1.163; P < 0.01]. Moreover, age [HR 1.023; 95 % CI 1.003–1.044; P < 0.05], TBIL [HR 1.027; 95 % CI 1.006–1.048; P < 0.05] and INR [HR 1.222; 95 % CI 1.007–1.482; P < 0.05] might be independent risk factors for HBV-ACLF on multivariable analysis (Table 4). Four models (unadjusted and adjusted) were constructed to evaluate the association between OPN levels and 90-day adverse outcomes. After adjusting for confounders that may affect outcomes, OPN levels remained an independent risk factor for 90-day adverse outcomes when used as a continuous or categorical variable (Table 5).

Discussion

OPN is a potential biomarker for advanced liver disease.25 In this retrospective study, we investigated the role of OPN as a prognostic biomarker in patients with HBV-ACLF. Our study had three major findings: First, we found that serum OPN levels can be used as a potential indicator to predict the severity of HBV-related liver disease. Second, our study suggested that the optimal cut-off value of OPN for predicting the prognosis of patients with HBV-ACLF was 36.8 ng/ml, and high OPN levels contributed to adverse outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF. Finally, OPN levels were independently associated with 90-day adverse outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF.

OPN has been implicated in several liver and infectious diseases. OPN may serve as a potential biomarker for inflammatory liver diseases. A recent Japanese study of 88 patients with fulminant liver failure showed that OPN protein was expressed not only in inflammatory cells but also in regenerating hepatocytes and bile duct structures. Serum OPN levels are elevated in patients with fulminant liver failure, suggesting that OPN may play an important role in liver regeneration in response to hepatic stem cell activation.26 Tissue microarray analysis of 366 human samples showed that OPN expression continued to increase during liver cancer development.27 OPN expression increases continuously with the progression of carcinogenesis and is positively correlated with tumor progression In HCC(5). Our study demonstrated that OPN levels were significantly increased in patients with HBV-ACLF and were associated with the severity of liver injury. In particular, a recent proteomic study revealed that OPN was markedly upregulated in plasma samples from patients with HBV-ACLF, and it was a novel candidate HBV-ACLF biomarker, similar to the results of our present study.28 The serum concentration of OPN is upregulated in patients with HBV infection,29 and OPN may improve the maturation and function of DCs in the immune response to HBV antigens.30 However, we found no significant differences in serum OPN levels between HBeAg-positive and HBeAg-negative patients, and significant differences between HBV DNA negative and HBV DNA positive OPN patients. Therefore, we believe that OPN levels increase with high HBV replication.

OPN is associated with the prognosis of various liver diseases. A study in patients with alcoholic hepatitis suggested that increased OPN levels positively correlated with increasing MELD scores and that OPN is a biomarker indicating severity and predicting clinical outcomes in patients with alcoholic hepatitis.14 Multiple studies have shown that OPN has prognostic potential for severe drug-induced liver injury events.31, 32, 33 Another study of 55 individuals with acute decompensation of cirrhosis reported that OPN, ALB, and TFF-3 are highly accurate biomarkers of ACLF.34 A recent meta-analysis showed that OPN may be adopted as a promising predictive tumor biomarker for the diagnosis and prognosis of HCC and LC.35 In HCC patients with combined ACLF, the prediction model of OPN combined with MELD significantly improved accuracy compared to using the MELD score alone.36 The SURFASA dynamic scoring system developed for AIH-ACLF patients, combined with OPN and MELD parameters, can identify non-responders to hormone therapy early (on the 3rd day) with an accuracy rate of 75 %.37 In patients with ACLF combined with gastrointestinal bleeding, the predictive value of OPN was significantly better than that of the traditional CTP score.38 In another prospective study, OPN was positively correlated with TBIL and the MELD score and continued to increase in patients with worsening conditions, suggesting that OPN is an independent risk factor for the prognosis of ACLF.39, 40 Based on these studies, we extended this information to the HBV-ACLF population. Another novel finding of our study was that patients with HBV-ACLF had an increased risk of future adverse outcomes when the serum OPN level was ≥36.8 ng/ml. According to one published study, plasma OPN levels appeared to be an additional biomarker for HCC detection at a cut-off value of 128.5 ng/ml.41 A prospective, observational study showed that an OPN level >9043 ng/ml predicted poor prognosis in 54 patients with HBV-ACLF.39 Our study demonstrated that OPN expression level was associated with the clinical outcome of patients with HBV-ACLF and that high OPN expression was an independent risk factor for poor prognosis. In contrast, we conducted a study with a larger sample size and sufficient statistical power to detect such differences. Therefore, the method used in this study was appropriate and rigorous, and our study provided credible evidence of the effects of serum OPN levels on adverse clinical outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF. Therefore, we performed a retrospective study of 205 patients with HBV-ACLF to validate whether OPN could serve as a biomarker for the prognosis of ACLF. As is well known, serum lactate levels are an effective indicator for predicting short-term and long-term mortality in HBV-ACLF patients.42,43 Our research results show that the AUC of OPN, serum lactate, MELD score, and three combined indicators, including OPN + LAC, OPN + MELD, and OPN + LAC + MELD, were not significantly different. However, the AUC of the OPN + MELD and OPN + ALC + MELD combined indicators were greater than 0.7. These results indicate that OPN may provide additional predictive value, but further validation in larger cohorts is needed. We found that elevated serum OPN levels at admission were associated with an increased risk of poor outcomes in the subgroups of age, cirrhosis, infection, ascites, TBIL≥12 mg/dl, INR<2.5, ALB<35 g/L, lactate, MELD, and CTP subgroups. Furthermore, there was no such association in the following subgroups: yes-HE, yes-HSR, TBIL<12 mg/dl, INR≥2.5, and ALB≥35 g/L. Our results indicate that OPN has a greater prognostic value in patients with more severe HBV-ACLF. Therefore, patients with high serum OPN levels at admission may be at a high risk of poor prognosis and should receive aggressive monitoring and therapeutic interventions.

ACLF is accompanied by complex immune dysregulation and is characterized by systemic inflammatory response syndrome and multiple organ dysfunction. This hyperinflammatory state ultimately impairs the host defense mechanisms of immune cells, rendering patients with ACLF immunocompromised and more susceptible to secondary infections, thereby increasing organ dysfunction and mortality.44 OPN is a pleiotropic cytokine with critical roles in cell proliferation, survival, migration, inflammation, fibrosis, and tissue remodeling processes.45,46 OPN expression appears to be upregulated in response to stress, cell injury, and pathology.45 It has been reported that the acute inflammatory response to wounding results in a microenvironment with high levels of OPN, IL-1β, and IL-6.47 OPN plays a pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic role in the progression of liver fibrosis. Research has shown that OPN promotes fibrosis by activating hepatic stellate cells (HSCs), which involves the activation of the MR/OPN/HMGB1 axis.48 As a stress-induced profibrotic molecule, OPN exhibits significant profibrotic activity in experimental liver fibrosis models.44 OPN plays a core regulatory role in microenvironment remodeling in liver cirrhosis. During CLD progression, OPN promotes the bile duct response and drives disease progression by producing bile duct cell chemokines and recruiting macrophages.49 OPN palys a role in the occurrence and development of HCC. OPN affects cancer progression through multiple mechanisms, including promoting tumor microenvironment remodeling and tumor cell invasion.27,50 OPN also participates in regulating the recruitment and activation of tumor-associated macrophages, further promoting the progression of liver cancer.49,51 Given the crucial role of OPN in liver disease progression, targeted intervention strategies hold significant translational potential. Research has shown that OPN silencing attenuates the inflammatory response and disease progression.52,53 Antibodies that neutralize SPP1/OPN can provide protective effects in PSC models.54 Epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG) downregulates OPN expression by inhibiting oxidative stress, thereby alleviating experimental liver fibrosis.55 In the treatment strategy for HCC, blocking OPN-induced ROS production can effectively inhibit tumor cell proliferation and migration.56 Our previous research has also demonstrated that high mobility group B1 (HMGB1) and blood sodium levels have important clinical value in predicting the prognosis of HBV-ACLF.57, 58 OPN is secreted by hepatocytes and inflammatory cells after liver injury, hyponatremia is closely related to the inflammatory state of the disease, and increased expression of OPN induces HMGB1 expression during liver fibrosis.59 Regulating the interaction between OPN and HMGB1 may be a novel therapeutic target for NAFLD-related liver fibrosis.60 OPN not only has prognostic biomarker value in end-stage liver disease but also plays a key regulatory role in its pathophysiological processes and progression,59,61, 62, 63 demonstrating its translational potential as a therapeutic target.

The current study had some limitations. Initially, there was only one center in our study, and the results should be verified in a multi-site investigation. Second, more patients should be enrolled in this study to define the performance of OPN as a predictive biomarker. Third, this study measured OPN levels only at a single time point and did not consider the dynamic changes of OPN, which may affect the reliability of the results. Fourth, we will delve into the biological mechanisms underlying OPN's role in HBV-ACLF pathogenesis and explore its potential significance in developing targeted therapeutic strategies. In future research, comparative studies covering multiple etiologies or analyses integrating multiple omics data should be designed to reveal the universality and specificity of OPN expression.

Conclusion

In summary, our study revealed that a serum OPN concentration ≥36.8 ng/ml was a prognostic factor for poor 90-day outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF. Serum OPN was an independent risk factor for adverse 90-day outcomes in patients with HBV-ACLF according to the fully adjusted model IV. Based on these findings, serum OPN levels may be a clinically meaningful biomarker for predicting the short-term prognosis of patients with HBV-ACLF. In conclusion, we believe that our study lays the foundation for the clinical application of OPN in patients with HBV-ACLF.

Encapsulated enzyme cocktail as feed additives for improving efficiency and nutrient digestibility in broilers
Authors: Hamza Rafeeq, Muhammad Anjum Zia
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Feed cost is the major economic challenge in poultry production, and the efficient utilization of nutrients is essential for sustainable broiler farming. The present study was designed to evaluate the effects of an encapsulated enzyme mixture containing protease, lipase, cellulase, and amylase on growth performance, carcass characteristics, and nutrient digestibility in broiler chickens. A total of 120 day-old Ross-308 chicks were randomly allocated to six dietary treatments (2 replicates per treatment, 10 chicks per replicate) in a completely randomized design, including positive and negative controls and four enzyme-supplemented groups with low- and high-calorie diets at two inclusion levels (500 and 1000 U/kg). Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA with Tukey's test (p < 0.05). Results showed that enzyme supplementation significantly improved body weight gain (p < 0.0001), feed conversion ratio (p < 0.0001), and nutrient utilization compared with the negative control. Higher enzyme levels produced significant effects on carcass yield, particularly dressing percentage (p < 0.0001), breast weight (p < 0.0001), and thigh weight (p < 0.0001). Nutrient digestibility, including crude protein, crude fiber, and ether extract, was significantly enhanced in enzyme-fed groups (p < 0.0001). These findings indicate that encapsulated multi-enzyme supplementation improves performance and nutrient efficiency in broilers under both low- and high-calorie dietary conditions. The results highlight the potential of enzyme-based feed strategies to reduce feed costs and enhance poultry production efficiency.

Introduction

Poultry production is one of the fastest-growing sectors of the livestock industry and contributes significantly to global food security (Chatterjee et al., 2022; Maharjan et al., 2021). Broiler chicken is highly demanded because of its fast growth rate, good feed conversion and capability to supply cheap animal protein (Erdaw and Beyene, 2022; Pope et al., 2024). Nevertheless, the rising prices of feed materials like corn and soybean meal have emerged as a significant economic issue in the poultry production systems. The cost of feed is nearly 70 percent of the total cost of production and the increasing prices of protein and energy sources demand the need to seek alternative strategies to maximize the use of feed (Alqhtani et al., 2022; Erdaw and Beyene, 2022; Pope et al., 2024) .

Exogenous enzymes have been effectively added to poultry diets to improve nutrient digestibility and to minimize the adverse consequences of anti-nutritional factors. The enzymes protease, cellulase, lipase, and amylase improve the digestion of proteins, fibers, lipids, and starches, leading to increased nutrient absorption and growth performance (Alqhtani et al., 2022; Alshelmani et al., 2024; Sureshkumar et al., 2023). They are especially significant in corn-soy diets in which the endogenous enzyme activity might be insufficient to hydrolyze complex feed constituents. It has been recently reported that supplementation of the diet with multi-enzyme preparations can enhance body weight gain, feed conversion ratio, and carcass yield in broilers (Attia et al., 2022; Bromfield et al., 2021).

In spite of these developments, differences in the enzyme efficiency are commonly encountered because of the differences in the diet compositions, enzyme stability, and the level of inclusion. Further, data on the combined utilization of various enzymes in low- and high-calorie diets are scarce. The available research is mostly concerned with single enzyme supplementation, whereas there is limited evidence on encapsulated multi-enzyme mixtures. Encapsulation provides a shielding effect, which may increase enzyme activity and stability in the gastrointestinal tract, which may augment growth performance and nutrient utilization (Ariaeenejad et al., 2024; Weng et al., 2023).

This study was therefore conducted to evaluate the efficacy of encapsulated protease, lipase, cellulase, and amylase in corn-soy based diets of broiler chickens. Growth performance, carcass characteristics, and nutrient digestibility were assessed under different enzyme inclusion levels and dietary energy conditions. The findings provide new insights into the potential of encapsulated enzymes as feed additives in modern poultry nutrition.

Material and methods

Experimental birds and design

A total of 120 day-old Ross-308 broiler chicks were obtained from a commercial hatchery. On arrival, birds were given 250 g/L sugar solution to alleviate transport stress and were examined for physical fitness. Chicks were randomly divided into six dietary treatments with two energy levels and two enzyme inclusion levels, along with positive and negative controls. Each treatment had two replicates of 10 chicks. Birds were reared under a floor litter system using sawdust bedding and had ad libitum access to feed and water throughout the 35-day trial. Birds were vaccinated according to a standard schedule. Newcastle disease (ND) vaccine was given intraocularly on day 5 and by drinking water on day 28. Infectious bursal disease (IBD) vaccine was administered intraocularly on day 15 and through drinking water on day 22.

Experimental diets

Six experimental diets were formulated for both starter (0–21 days) and finisher (22–35 days) phases. Diet A was positive control (standard diet), Diet B was negative control (low-energy without enzymes), while Diets C and E were low-energy diets supplemented with 500 and 1000 U/kg (where U denotes enzyme activity units) enzyme cocktail, respectively. Diets D and F were high-energy diets supplemented with 500 and 1000 U/kg enzyme cocktail, respectively. Performance and carcass evaluation

Body weight, feed intake, and feed conversion ratio were recorded weekly. Two birds per replicate were slaughtered on day 35 for carcass evaluation. Dressing percentage, breast and thigh weights, and organ weights (liver, heart, gizzard) were determined as percentages of live weight.

Nutrient digestibility

Digestibility was estimated on day 35 using acid-insoluble ash as an external marker. Celite® was added at 1% in feed as a marker. Excreta samples were collected and analyzed for dry matter (DM), crude protein (CP), ether extract (EE), and crude fiber (CF) following AOAC (2000) methods.

Statistical analysis

All data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA, and treatment means were separated by Tukey’s test. Significance was declared at p < 0.05.

Results

Performance of broilers

Feed intake

During the starter phase, feed intake was highest in the negative control group (1284.3 g), which was significantly greater (p < 0.0001) than the positive control (1168.66 g). Birds fed low-calorie diet with 500 U/kg enzymes (1234.33 g) and high-calorie diet with 500 U/kg enzymes (1278 g) also showed higher intake compared to the positive control. In contrast, low-calorie diet with 1000 U/kg (1161.67 g) and high-calorie diet with 1000 U/kg (1132.67 g) showed no significant differences from the positive control. In the finisher phase, feed intake ranged from 1876.3 g in the low-calorie + 500 U/kg group to 2155.6 g in the negative control, with significant differences (p < 0.0001) across treatments. Body weight

Enzyme supplementation markedly enhanced body weight during the starter phase. The highest weight was observed in the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (988.33 g), followed by the low-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (975.67 g), both significantly higher (p < 0.0001) than controls. The finisher phase showed modest improvements, with weights ranging from 1136 g in the high-calorie + 500 U/kg group to 1231.3 g in the negative control. Overall, the greatest body weight was achieved in the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (2123.7 g), which was significantly higher than both positive and negative controls (Table 3).

Feed conversion ratio (FCR)

Starter phase FCR was significantly affected (p < 0.0001). The positive control recorded the lowest FCR (1.43), while enzyme-supplemented groups exhibited higher values, reaching 2.85 in the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg group. During the finisher phase, FCR improved in the negative control group (1.46) compared to the positive control (1.64). Enzyme supplementation showed variable effects, with values ranging from 1.65 to 1.94. Across the entire experimental period, FCR ranged between 1.67 in the positive control and 1.97 in the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg group, with all treatments showing significant differences (p < 0.0001) (Table 3).

Mortality rate

Mortality was low across all treatments. In the starter phase, mortality was observed only in the positive control (3%) and low-calorie + 500 U/kg group (1%). During the finisher phase, two deaths were recorded in the positive control and one in the low-calorie + 500 U/kg group. No mortality was observed in high-calorie enzyme-supplemented groups. Overall mortality was highest in the positive control (5%), while enzyme-supplemented groups maintained survival rates comparable to the negative control (Table 3).

Carcass characteristics

Carcass characteristics were significantly influenced by enzyme supplementation (p < 0.0001). Dressing weight increased in all enzyme-supplemented groups compared to controls. The highest dressing weight was observed in birds fed the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg diet (62.8%), followed by the low-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (61.7%). Both values were significantly higher than the positive control (49.0%) and negative control (49.8%).

Breast weight showed a similar trend. The greatest improvement was recorded in the low-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (44.4%), followed closely by the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (43.6%). Both groups were significantly higher (p < 0.0001) compared to the positive control (34.96%). Moderate increases were also noted in the high-calorie + 500 U/kg group (41.07%). Thigh weight improved significantly in enzyme-supplemented groups. Birds receiving high-calorie + 1000 U/kg diet had the highest thigh weight (30.1%), followed by the low-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (28.9%). These values were markedly greater than both controls (21.7% in positive control and 20.7% in negative control).

Organ weights also showed treatment effects. Liver weights ranged from 1.74% to 2.1%, with no consistent trend across treatments, though the negative control group had significantly higher values compared to the positive control. Heart weight was greatest in the negative control (0.70%) but declined in enzyme-supplemented groups, ranging from 0.50% to 0.59%. Crude protein (CP)

Enzyme addition enhanced protein digestibility, with the highest improvement observed in the high-calorie + 500 U/kg group (76.06%). Other groups also showed higher values, including the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg (74.3%). In contrast, the low-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (68.86%) did not show an advantage over the negative control (69.87%) (Table 5).

Ether extract (EE)

Fat digestibility improved consistently in enzyme-supplemented diets. The highest digestibility was observed in the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (80.9%), followed closely by the low-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (79.5%). These values were markedly higher than the positive control (72.9%) and negative control (73.9%) (Table 5).

Crude fiber (CF)

Enzyme supplementation had a pronounced effect on fiber digestibility. The highest digestibility was recorded in the high-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (24.4%), followed by the low-calorie + 1000 U/kg group (23.9%). Both groups showed significant improvement compared to controls (16.29% in positive control and 14.47% in negative control) (Table 5).

Discussion

The supplementation of broiler diets with a cocktail of protease, lipase, cellulase, and amylase significantly improved growth performance and nutrient utilization. These findings are consistent with earlier studies highlighting the beneficial role of exogenous enzymes in poultry nutrition (Alshelmani et al., 2024; Sheehan, 2022; Waris et al., 2025). The use of enzymes has been associated with improved digestibility of complex feed ingredients, reduction of anti-nutritional factors, and better energy extraction, which explains the enhanced performance observed in the present trial. Body weight gain and feed conversion ratio were markedly improved in enzyme-supplemented groups, particularly at higher inclusion levels, in agreement with previous reports where enzyme supplementation enhanced nutrient availability and feed efficiency (Alhotan, 2021; Lawan et al., 2022; Mirsalami et al., 2024). Earlier findings demonstrated that enzymes increase nutrient release from plant cell walls and reduce gut viscosity, leading to better growth rates. The current study also confirms that mortality was lower in enzyme-fed groups, which could be attributed to improved gut health and reduced metabolic stress.

The enzyme supplementation increased carcass yield significantly in birds compared with the control with increased breast and thigh weights indicating improved nutrient distribution to muscle growth. Other studies have shown similar results in broilers kept on diets supplemented with xylanases and proteases, which contributed to a higher yield of breast meat and total carcass weight (Al-Qahtani et al., 2021; Ekine and Onunkwo, 2020; Hossain et al., 2024). However, variable effects on organ weights, such as liver and heart, suggest that enzyme effects may be dose-dependent and influenced by dietary composition.

Enzyme inclusion also increased the nutrient digestibility. The digestibility of dry matter, crude protein, ether extract, and crude fiber was significantly higher than in the control diets, which confirms the role of enzymes in increasing nutrient retention and apparent metabolizable energy (Mohammadigheisar et al., 2021; Sumbule et al., 2021). Specifically, the significant increase in the digestibility of crude fiber indicates the importance of fibrolytic enzymes in the digestion of non-starch polysaccharides, which lowers intestinal viscosity and promotes nutrient absorption (Ekwe et al., 2020; MI El-Kelawy et al., 2020). These enhancements are in line with previous studies that supplementation of enzymes decreases competition of nutrients between the gut microbiota and the host, resulting in more nutrients being available to grow (M El-Kelawy and Refaie, 2024; Mahmoud El-Kelawy et al., 2023).

Conclusion

Addition of encapsulated protease, lipase, cellulase, and amylase in the broiler diet enhanced the growth performance, carcass yield and nutrient digestibility when compared to non-supplemented groups. The increased levels of enzyme inclusion had more effect especially on body weight gain, feed conversion ratio and dressing percentage. Enzyme-fed birds had improved digestibility of crude protein, crude fiber, and ether extract, which facilitated improved nutrient utilization. These findings show that encapsulated multi-enzyme supplementation is effective in low- and high-calorie corn-soy diets to enhance production efficiency. These results also support the application of enzyme-based feed strategies in broiler nutrition as a tool to maximize performance and contribute towards sustainable poultry production.

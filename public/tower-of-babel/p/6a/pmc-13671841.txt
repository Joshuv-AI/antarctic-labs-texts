Establishment of a prognostic score for distant recurrence in follicular thyroid carcinoma
Authors: Yasuhiro Ito, Masashi Yamamoto, Mitsuyoshi Hirokawa, Hiroo Masuoka, Minoru Kihara, Akihiro Miya, Akira Miyauchi, Takashi Akamizu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC) is known to occasionally show distant recurrence (DR); however, no prognostic scoring system has been established. Furthermore, although prognostic factors have been identified, their weights as predictors of poor prognosis remain unclear. In this study, we aimed to establish a weighted scoring system for predicting DR in FTC using conventional prognostic factors and Ki-67 labeling index (LI). We analyzed data of 597 patients with FTC without distant metastasis at initial surgery. Tumor size >4 cm, male sex, age ≥60 years, Ki-67 LI >5%, and vascular invasion (VI) ≥4 (V2) significantly and VI 0–4 (V1) marginally affected DR. Wide capsular invasion was not significantly associated with DR. Stepwise model selection using the Akaike Information Criterion identified age ≥60 years, Ki-67 LI >5%, V1, V2, and tumor size >4 cm as suitable components of the score. According to the beta coefficient value, we assigned a score of 2 to age ≥60 years, Ki-67 LI >5%, and V2, and a score of 1 to tumor size >4 cm and V1, with the sum representing the final score. We categorized patients into three categories according to the DR rates: low-risk (score 0–2; n = 388, 65.0%), intermediate-risk (score 3–4; n = 156, 26.1%), and high-risk (score 5–7; n = 53, 8.9%). The 10-year DR-free survival rates declined from the low- to the high-risk categories (96.0% vs. 90.3% vs. 54.9%). These findings indicate that high-risk patients identified using this prognostic scoring model should be carefully monitored for DR following completion thyroidectomy.

Introduction

Follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC) is the second most common thyroid gland malignancy arising from thyroid follicular cells. To date, several prognostic factors for FTC have been identified, including patient age, tumor size, and vascular invasion (VI) [1-7]. However, although the number of VI foci was reported to affect patients’ prognosis, the cutoff for the number of VI foci remains controversial [1, 5]. Additionally, wide capsular invasion (CI) was also regarded as a prognostic factor; however, two Japanese studies reported a favorable prognosis of widely invasive FTC with no VI [1, 2, 5], indicating that wide CI does not worsen the prognosis of these patients. The Ki-67 labeling index (LI), used to assess cell proliferating activity, is an established strong prognostic factor in various carcinomas. Although studies on the role of Ki-67 LI in thyroid carcinoma are limited, high Ki-67 LI was reported to have a prognostic impact in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) and FTC [3, 8-10].

Generally, the initial surgical approach for FTC is hemithyroidectomy, except in cases of the presence of distant metastasis at diagnosis (M1), because FTC is difficult to differentiate from benign adenoma on cytology. If pathological findings indicate that the tumor demonstrates high-risk features for distant recurrence (DR), completion total thyroidectomy with or without adjuvant radioactive iodine (RAI) therapy is performed, with careful monitoring of thyroglobulin and thyroglobulin antibody levels.

To the best of our knowledge, a scoring system for FTC prognosis has not been established. Furthermore, although prognostic factors have been identified, their weights as predictors of poor prognosis remain unclear. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to develop a weighted scoring model for predicting DR in FTC using conventional prognostic factors and Ki-67 LI.

Patients and methods

Study design and population

This retrospective cohort study included patients who underwent initial surgery for FTC at our institution between 1998 and 2021. FTC diagnosis was confirmed on postoperative pathological examination in all patients. None of the patients had distant metastasis at the time of initial surgery. The exclusion criteria were: 1) distant metastasis at diagnosis (M1), 2) missing pathological data, including Ki-67 LI, 3) diagnosis of oncocytic carcinoma (formerly classified as a subtype of FTC), and 4) concomitant thyroid malignancies.

This study was approved by the ethical committee of our hospital (approval number 20250612-3).

Evaluation of pathological findings

One pathologist (M.H.), blinded to the clinical data, reviewed the pathological preparations of all patients and evaluated CI and VI based on the World Health Organization (WHO) classification, as described previously [1]. Briefly, CI was divided into two categories: microscopic (minimal) and macroscopic (wide). The number of VI foci was counted in representative sections, and VI-positive cases were divided into two categories: V1 (having <4 VI foci) and V2 (having ≥4 foci). To assess the Ki-67 LI, immunohistochemical staining was performed using a primary antibody against Ki-67 (MIB-1, 1:200 dilution; Dako [Hachioji, Tokyo, Japan]), as previously described [4]. The Ki-67 LI was estimated by counting at least 500 FTC cells in a hotspot. A Ki-67 LI >5% was regarded as indicating high cell proliferation activity.

Postoperative follow-up

After surgery, patients were followed up in our outpatient clinic once or twice per year. Follow-up examination included blood tests and imaging studies, such as ultrasound and computed tomography, at the discretion of the attending clinicians.

Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.5.2; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Continuous variables were summarized using descriptive statistics, and categorical variables were expressed as counts and percentages.

DR-free survival (DR-FS) was defined as the time from surgery to the first occurrence of DR or the last follow-up. Univariable Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to evaluate the association between clinicopathological variables and DR-FS. Variables considered clinically relevant were subsequently included in a multivariable Cox proportional hazard model. Model selection was performed using a stepwise procedure based on the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), allowing forward and backward selection to identify independent predictors of DR.

To evaluate whether weighting of predictors improves prognostic performance, an alternative weighted scoring model was established. Patients were stratified into three risk groups according to the total prognostic score. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were plotted to estimate DR-FS for each risk group, and differences among groups were compared using the log-rank test. Pairwise comparisons between risk groups were performed using pairwise log-rank tests with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment for multiple comparisons. In addition, the FTC prognostic score was evaluated as a continuous variable in a Cox proportional hazards model to assess the effect of each one-point increase in the score on DR risk. Model discrimination was assessed using the concordance index (C-index).

Results

Patient characteristics

Between 1998 and 2021, 616 patients underwent initial surgery for FTC. After excluding 19 patients with distant metastasis at initial surgery (M1), 597 patients were included in this study. Table 1 presents the demographic and clinicopathological characteristics of our cohort. We previously set two age cutoffs, 30 and 60 years, for investigating prognostic factors of PTC and showed that the recurrence rate in local and distant sites was higher in young and older patients than in middle-aged patients [11]. In this study of FTC, however, no significant difference in recurrence rate was observed between patients <30 years and those aged 30–59 years. Therefore, we used a single cutoff of 60 years in this study.

High Ki-67 LI was noted in 178 (29.8%) patients, and 39 (6.5%) and 94 (15.7%) patients were classified into the V2 (≥4 VI foci) and V1 (1–3 VI foci) categories, respectively. The initial surgical approach in the majority of patients (n = 482, 70.2%) was hemithyroidectomy. Of these, 126 underwent completion total thyroidectomy as a second surgery at the attending clinicians’ discretion. Postoperative follow-up ranged from 6 to 320 months (median, 98 months; interquartile range, 69–162 months; mean, 119 months). DR occurred in 51 (8.5%) patients during the follow-up.

Overall, 53 patients received RAI (30–100 mCi) as ablation or adjuvant therapy within 1 year after total thyroidectomy or completion total thyroidectomy. Among the 51 patients who developed DR, 28 (54.9%) received a therapeutic dose (≥100 mCi) of RAI.

Association between clinicopathological factors and DR

In the univariable analysis, tumor size >4 cm (p = 0.046), male sex (p = 0.027), age ≥60 years (p < 0.001), and Ki-67 LI >5% (p < 0.001) were significantly associated with DR. VI was also considered to have prognostic relevance, as V2 was strongly associated with DR (p < 0.001) and V1 showed a marginal association (p = 0.097). In contrast, a wide CI was not significantly associated with DR (p = 0.077) and was therefore excluded from further analysis.

In the multivariable analysis, age ≥60 years, Ki-67 LI >5%, and VI (V2) emerged as independent predictors of DR (Table 2). Through stepwise model selection, age ≥60 years, tumor size >4 cm, Ki-67 LI >5%, and VI were identified as the most appropriate variables for constructing a prognostic scoring system.

Weighted prognostic scoring system construction and evaluation

The weighted scoring system was constructed based on the relative magnitude of the regression coefficients (Table 3). A score of 2 was assigned to age ≥60 years, Ki-67 LI >5%, and V2, whereas a score of 1 was assigned to tumor size >4 cm and V1. According to the distribution of the total score, patients were stratified into low-risk (score 0–2; n = 388, 65.0%), intermediate-risk (score 3–4; n = 156, 26.1%), and high-risk groups (score 5–7; n = 53, 8.9%).

Fig. 1 shows the Kaplan–Meier curves for DR-FS for the three groups. Indeed, DR-FS progressively worsened from the low-risk to the high-risk groups (low-risk vs. intermediate-risk, p = 0.020; intermediate-risk vs. high-risk, p < 0.001). The 10-year DR-FS rates were 96.0%, 90.3%, and 54.9% for the low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups, respectively.

Kaplan–Meier curves for distant recurrence-free survival in patients with follicular thyroid carcinoma with low, intermediate, and high scores

The weighted score demonstrated good discriminative ability, with a C-index of 0.730. In addition, each one-point increase in the score was associated with a significantly higher risk of DR (hazard ratio, 1.64; 95% confidence interval, 1.42–1.90; p < 0.001).

Of the 39 patients with V2, 5 (12.8%), 10 (25.6%), and 24 (61.6%) were classified as low-, intermediate-, and high-risk, respectively, according to total scores. Nine (37.5%) of the 24 high-risk patients showed DR, whereas only 1 (10%) of the 10 intermediate-risk patients and none of the 5 low-risk patients showed DR.

Thirty-three patients were classified as high-risk based on age ≥60 years, tumor size >4 cm, and Ki-67 LI ≥5%; of these, 18, 7, and 8 patients were classified as VI-negative, V1, and V2, respectively. The incidence of DR was relatively high, at 33.3% (6 of 18) in VI-negative patients, and increased to 42.9% (3 of 7) in V1 patients and 50.0% (4 of 8) in V2 patients.

In this series, among 126 patients who underwent completion total thyroidectomy as a second surgery, 76, 40, and 10 patients were classified as low-, intermediate-, and high-risk, respectively. Of these, 11 patients (5 high-risk, 5 intermediate-risk, and 1 low-risk) showed DR during follow-up. Among 53 patients who underwent RAI administration (30–100 mCi), 37, 11, and 5 patients were classified as low-, intermediate-, and high-risk, respectively. Four patients (2 high-risk, 1 intermediate-risk, and 1 low-risk) showed DR.

Among the 19 patients with distant metastasis at initial surgery (M1) excluded from the study, 7 (36.8%) died of thyroid carcinoma. However, of 597 patients without metastasis at initial surgery (M0) included in the study, only 5 (0.8%) died of thyroid carcinoma. Therefore, we did not perform an analysis on cause-specific mortality.

Discussion

In this study, we established a weighted scoring model for predicting DR in FTC. Among conventional prognostic factors, old age, large tumor size, and VI emerged as factors with prognostic relevance. This is consistent with the findings of previous studies conducted in two institutions specialized in diseases of the thyroid gland [1, 5, 7].

In 2010, we reported that high Ki-67 LI significantly affected the prognosis of patients with PTC. Subsequently, in 2016 and 2021, we demonstrated that Ki-67 LI (cutoff at 5%) was significantly associated with disease-FS in minimally and widely invasive FTC (the criteria of these FTC classifications significantly differed from now) [3, 4]. Therefore, in the present study, we adopted Ki-67 LI >5% as a marker of aggressive tumor behavior. Recently, Hellgren et al. reported that Ki-67 LI (cutoff at 4%) was significantly associated with recurrence and carcinoma-related death in patients with FTC [12]. In line with the above, and given the high beta coefficient of Ki-67 LI >5%, it was assigned a score of 2 together with age ≥60 years and V2 status.

For evaluating DR in patients with FTC, we demonstrated that VI is a very important factor [1]. However, in our series, the recurrence rates of low- and intermediate-risk patients with V2 were very low, at 0% and 10%, respectively. Furthermore, the recurrence rate of high-risk patients with age ≥60 years, Ki-67 LI >5%, and tumor size >4 cm, but negative VI, was relatively high, at 33.3%. Moreover, the recurrence rate showed a stepwise increase with VI score, reaching 42.9% for V1 and 50.0% for V2. These findings suggest that the VI score has an additive prognostic effect in these patients.

In our classification, the difference in 10-year DR-FS between the low- and intermediate-risk groups is relatively small (96.0 % vs. 90.3%), whereas the high-risk group shows a markedly poorer outcome (54.9%). A classification into more than two groups was adopted to maintain consistency with commonly used clinical stratification systems, e.g., the risk classification of PTC in Japanese guidelines [13] and the MACIS score [14], and to allow a more refined assessment of recurrence risk in FTC.

Although Hay et al. established a prognostic scoring system for PTC in a study involving 1,779 patients [14], no scoring system has been established for FTC prognosis. Our newly established scoring system for FTC is easy to apply if Ki-67 LI can be calculated. In the WHO classification, the criteria for minimal and wide CI are obscure [15]. In our institution, CI detected only by microscopy is defined as minimal invasion, whereas invasion foci that can be detected upon gross examination are regarded as wide invasion [1]. In this study, however, CI was not identified as a suitable factor for the scoring system.

The strengths of this study are that we clearly showed the importance of Ki-67 LI for evaluating the prognosis of patients with FTC and established a weighted prognostic scoring model for FTC (Graphical Abstract). Nonetheless, this study also has some limitations. First, the retrospective design is associated with inherent biases. Second, the Ki-67 LI evaluation method might differ across institutions, leading to potential interobserver variations in cell counting for calculating the Ki-67 LI. Third, since the number of patients who died of thyroid carcinoma was very small, at 5, we could not establish a prognostic score for cause-specific survival. Finally, adjuvant RAI was administered at the discretion of the attending clinicians and not routinely. Because of the resulting bias, we could not evaluate the prognostic significance of RAI administration for FTC.

In summary, we established a weighted scoring model for predicting DR in patients with FTC. According to our findings, in patients with high scores based on this model following hemithyroidectomy, completion total thyroidectomy should be considered in preparation for DR, allowing for immediate RAI treatment. Further studies enrolling a large number of patients with longer follow-up duration are warranted to establish a model for cancer-specific survival.

Andrographolide Mitigates DOCA-Salt-Induced Cardiac Hypertrophy by Regulating Calcium-Handling Genes
Authors: Muhamad Rizqy Fadhillah, Wawaimuli Arozal, Raymond Rubianto Tjandrawinata, Dewi Sukmawati, Edwina Rugaiah Monayo, Deni Noviana, Melva Louisa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and Objectives:Cardiac hypertrophy is a maladaptive response to chronic hypertension and is often associated with impaired calcium handling. Andrographolide, a bioactive diterpenoid, has shown cardioprotective effects in several preclinical models, but its role in pressure-overload-induced hypertrophy remains unclear. This study evaluated the effects of andrographolide on cardiac hypertrophy, calcium-handling gene expression, and plasma B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels in a deoxycorticosterone acetate (DOCA)-salt rat model of hypertension.Materials and Methods:Fifteen male Sprague-Dawley rats underwent unilateral nephrectomy followed one week later by DOCA administration (20 mg/kg, subcutaneous, twice weekly) and 1% NaCl drinking water. After two weeks of DOCA-salt exposure, rats were randomized to receive oral andrographolide (30 mg/kg/day in 0.5% carboxymethyl cellulose) or no treatment. Blood pressure was measured at weeks 2 and 5 using a tail-cuff system. At week 5, echocardiography was performed, followed by euthanasia and collection of cardiac tissue and plasma. BNP and cardiac calcium levels were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay and colorimetric assay, respectively. Sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca2+-ATPase 2a (SERCA2a) and ryanodine receptor (RyR) mRNA expression were quantified by PCR using β-actin as the reference gene.Results:DOCA-salt treatment significantly increased blood pressure, left ventricular hypertrophy indices, plasma BNP, and cardiac calcium levels. Andrographolide attenuated these changes and restored SERCA2a and RyR expression.Conclusion:Andrographolide alleviates DOCA-salt-induced cardiac hypertrophy, likely by restoring calcium-handling pathways, supporting its therapeutic potential in hypertension-induced cardiac remodeling.

INTRODUCTION

Cardiac hypertrophy, characterized by an increase in cardiomyocyte size and left ventricular mass, is a critical adaptive response to chronic stress but can progress to functional impairment if uncontrolled.[1] Hypertensive heart disease remains the most prevalent clinical driver of pathological hypertrophy, contributing significantly to global cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. Patients with long-standing hypertension have a six-fold risk of developing structural cardiac remodeling, which markedly increases the risk of arrhythmia, heart failure, and sudden cardiac death, underscoring the need for early intervention.[2] Despite extensive research, residual risk among patients is still present,[3] and effective pharmacological strategies to prevent or reverse hypertrophy remain limited, raising the urgency to find an effective or alternative pharmacological agent.

Andrographolide, a bioactive diterpenoid from Andrographis paniculata, has demonstrated cardioprotective effects in preclinical studies through anti-inflammatory, antioxidative, and signaling-modulating mechanisms.[4] Beyond these systemic effects, mechanistic studies suggest that andrographolide modulates key intracellular pathways involved in cardiomyocyte remodeling—including NF-κB, MAPK, PI3K/AKT, and TGF-β—which influence hypertrophic growth, fibrosis, and contractile function.[5678910] Nevertheless, whether andrographolide can confer long-term protection against stress-induced cardiac hypertrophy, particularly under chronic hypertensive loading, remains insufficiently understood. Calcium handling, mediated by proteins such as sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca2+-ATPase (SERCA2a), phospholamban, and the ryanodine receptor (RyR2), is often dysregulated in hypertrophic hearts, especially in hypertensive heart disease. Impaired calcium cycling contributes not only to contractile dysfunction but also to pathological signaling, where abnormal Ca2+ retention in the sarcoplasmic reticulum and cytosolic overload activate calcineurin–NFAT and CaMKII pathways, driving hypertrophic gene transcription.[111213] Therefore, restoring calcium homeostasis represents a mechanistically relevant therapeutic target for hypertensive hypertrophy. However, the effects of andrographolide on these pathways in the context of pressure-induced cardiac hypertrophy are still limited. The uninephrectomy (UNX) and deoxycorticosterone acetate (DOCA)-salt rat model provides a well-established platform to study hypertrophy under sustained mineralocorticoid-driven hypertension. DOCA, in combination with high salt intake following unilateral nephrectomy, induces sodium retention, volume expansion, sympathetic activation, and vascular dysfunction, ultimately generating persistent hypertension and cardiac pressure overload. This model recapitulates progressive left ventricular hypertrophy, impaired calcium handling, and neurohormonal dysregulation, making it valuable for evaluating cardioprotective interventions and their mechanistic relevance.[141516] Although andrographolide shows promising cardioprotective activity in several overload models, its chronic protection against hypertensive-cardiac injury is still questionable.

Therefore, this study aimed to evaluate the effects of andrographolide on left ventricular hypertrophy, calcium-handling gene expression, and plasma BNP levels in a chronic DOCA-salt rat model, providing mechanistic insights with translational relevance.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Animal maintenance and model

Male Sprague–Dawley rats (n = 15) were housed 2–3 per cage in a temperature-controlled room (22 ± 2°C) with a 12:12 h light–dark cycle and free access to standard chow. All animals underwent at least seven days of acclimation before any experimental procedure.

Hypertension was induced via unilateral nephrectomy under ketamine (75 mg/kg) and xylazine (7.5 mg/kg) anesthesia, with perioperative temperature maintenance. One week after surgery, rats received subcutaneous injections of DOCA (20 mg/kg in 0.1 mL dimethylformamide) twice a week and 1% NaCl in drinking water ad libitum.[17] Environmental enrichment was provided, and cages were inspected daily for health and welfare. After two weeks of uninephrectomy plus DOCA–salt treatment, rats were randomized into two groups: (1) DOCA–salt only, and (2) DOCA–salt plus andrographolide. The treatment group received andrographolide (Plamed, Xi’an, China) at 30 mg/kg/day in 0.5% carboxymethyl cellulose (CMC) via oral administration, which was chosen from one of our previous preliminary studies.[18] Animals were monitored daily for body weight, general activity, and signs of pain or distress. Humane endpoints were applied if severe pain or >20% loss of body weight occurred. Blood pressure was measured biweekly using a non-invasive tail-cuff system. At the fifth week, transthoracic echocardiography was performed to evaluate left ventricular structure and function. Rats were then euthanized with ketamine (100 mg/kg) and xylazine (7.5 mg/kg), followed by exsanguination via retro-orbital collection. Plasma and cardiac tissues were harvested for downstream analyses. The full protocol of our study is presented in Figure 1.

Research workflow

Blood pressure measurement

Systolic and diastolic blood pressure were measured using a CODA™ non-invasive tail-cuff system (Kent Scientific, USA). Before measurement, animals were acclimatized to the restrainer for 15–30 minutes. For each animal, three independent measurements were obtained at weeks 2 and 5, and the average was used for analysis. Data represent the mean of three consecutive measurements per time point.

Echocardiography

Echocardiography was performed under light anesthesia using an MVet60 system (Mindray, China) with an M-mode probe in the short-axis view. Measurements were obtained in triplicate and included ejection fraction (EF), fractional shortening (FS), Intraventricular septal in diastole (IVSd) and systole (IVSs), left ventricular posterior wall in diastole (LVPWd) and systole (LVPWs), left ventricular internal diameter in diastole (LVIDd) and systole LVIDs. Relative wall thickness (RWT) was calculated as previously described, using IVSd, LVPWd, and LVIDd measurements. IVSd+LVPWd was also reported as an index of wall thickening.[19]

Plasma and tissue biochemistry

Plasma BNP levels were measured using an ELISA kit (Elabscience, China) from retro-orbital blood. Cardiac calcium content was determined in 20 mg ventricular tissue, normalized to protein levels using a colorimetric kit (Elabscience, China).

Gene expression analysis

Total RNA was extracted from 100 mg of cardiac tissue using the Direct-zol™ RNA Miniprep kit (Zymo Research, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and 500 ng RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the ReverTra Ace™ cDNA synthesis kit (Toyobo, Japan). qPCR was performed with SYBR Green Master Mix (Toyobo, Japan) using thermal cycling conditions that included an initial denaturation at 95°C, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 s, 60°C for 30 s, and 72°C for 30 s. Relative gene expression levels of SERCA2a and RyR were normalized to β-actin using the 2ΔΔCt method. The primers used were SERCA2a (forward: 5′-CATGGACGAGACGCTCAAGT-3′; reverse: 5′-GAGCTGGCGGCTGTTCTATT-3′, amplicon size (bp) 187), RyR (forward: 5′-AACTAAATTGCGAGCCTCGG-3′; reverse: 5′-CAGTCTGGCTTGCTGGTACA-3′, amplicon product (bp) 81), and β-actin (forward: 5′-TGTTGTCCCTGTATGCCTCT-3′, reverse: 5′-TAATGTCACGCACGATTTCC-3′, amplicon product (bp) 222).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using analysis of variance (ANOVA) or the Kruskal-Wallis test through GraphPad Prism version 10.0 (GraphPad Software, LLC, San Diego, CA, USA), and continued using post-hoc analysis test, such as Tukey or Dunn test. When overall significance was not identified, a priori student or Welch t-tests were applied to evaluate pairwise comparisons between each group to avoid overly conservative findings.[2021] Data are presented as mean ± SD, and statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

RESULTS

Effect of andrographolide on systolic and diastolic pressure

At baseline [Figure 2], systolic and diastolic pressures were comparable among groups. By week 2, UNX-DOCA-Salt rats showed a marked elevation in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure compared to controls. Treatment with Andrographolide (30 mg/kg) attenuated these increases in systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure. At week 5, systolic and diastolic blood pressure remained elevated in the DOCA group, whereas Andrographolide treatment partially reduced both systolic and diastolic blood pressure values compared to DOCA alone.

Andrographolide treatment attenuated these hypertrophic changes. LVMI was significantly reduced, with parallel decreases in LVPWd, IVSd, and RWT. The IVSd+LVPWd index also decreased in the andrographolide group compared to the UNX-DOCA-Salt group, suggesting a consistent reduction in concentric wall thickening compared with untreated DOCA rats. Meanwhile, chamber dimensions (LVIDd and LVIDs), as well as systolic parameters (EF and FS), did not show significant differences across groups.

The effect of andrographolide on plasma BNP levels

Plasma BNP levels were significantly increased in the UNX-DOCA-Salt group compared with controls. Administration of andrographolide at 30 mg/kg reduced BNP levels (2.968 ± 1.08), which were markedly lower than in the untreated UNX-DOCA-Salt group [Table 1].

The effect of andrographolide on cardiac calcium handling

Cardiac tissue calcium content was significantly elevated in the UNX-DOCA-Salt group compared with controls, while treatment with andrographolide (30 mg/kg) reduced calcium levels to 1,853 ± 612 mg/mg protein [Figure 4a]. Expression of SERCA2a mRNA was also markedly increased in the UNX-DOCA-Salt group but was suppressed in the andrographolide-treated group [Figure 4b]. Similarly, RyR expression was significantly upregulated in the UNX-DOCA-Salt group, whereas andrographolide administration reduced it to near-control levels [Figure 4c].

DISCUSSION

Hypertension-induced cardiac remodeling is a multifactorial process involving hemodynamic overload, neurohormonal activation, and molecular disturbances in calcium handling.[1] The UNX-DOCA-Salt model is widely recognized as a robust experimental approach to replicate these features in vivo. Using this platform, the present study evaluated the effect of andrographolide, a bioactive diterpenoid with reported cardioprotective potential, on multiple phenotypes and molecular evidence.

Consistent with previous findings, UNX-DOCA-salt rats developed progressive systolic and diastolic hypertension, confirming successful model establishment.[17] Andrographolide attenuated blood pressure elevation, suggesting a previously unreported antihypertensive effect in this model. This effect may involve inhibition of angiotensin-converting enzyme, modulation of nitric oxide-mediated signaling, induction of apelin expression, and attenuation of endothelin-related vasoconstriction.[22232425] Echocardiography demonstrated significant cardiac hypertrophy in untreated hypertensive rats despite preserved systolic function. These changes were attenuated by andrographolide, indicating suppression of pathological remodeling. Consistently, plasma BNP levels were markedly elevated in hypertensive rats and significantly reduced following andrographolide treatment, supporting its protective effects against neurohormonal stress. Unlike previous studies conducted in reduced-ejection-fraction models,[56] the present findings demonstrate cardioprotective effects in a preserved-ejection-fraction setting. At the molecular level, hypertensive rats showed altered calcium handling, reflected by increased cardiac calcium content and dysregulated SERCA2a and RyR expression. Although pressure-overload models commonly exhibit SERCA2a downregulation and RyR abnormalities,[12] our findings suggest that DOCA-salt hypertension may induce a compensatory hypertrophic response characterized by coordinated upregulation of calcium-handling genes.[26] Andrographolide normalized these alterations, indicating stabilization of intracellular calcium cycling, consistent with previous reports describing modulation of cardiac calcium currents.[9]

Future directions and study limitations

This study has several limitations. Functional assessment was limited by the absence of direct diastolic measurements, while molecular analyses were restricted to gene-expression data without protein-level or electrophysiological confirmation. Future studies should determine whether normalization of SERCA2a and RyR expression results from suppression of neurohormonal pathways, including RAAS/CaMKII signaling, or from direct regulation of intracellular calcium handling.[1] Clarifying these mechanisms may explain whether andrographolide prevents the progression from compensatory hypertrophy to maladaptive remodeling.

CONCLUSION

The present findings demonstrate that andrographolide counteracts the structural and molecular consequences of DOCA–salt hypertension. Treatment with andrographolide led to reduced cardiac hypertrophy and lower circulating BNP levels, accompanied by restoration of calcium-regulating gene expression. These results indicate that improving calcium homeostasis may be a key mechanism underlying the cardioprotective actions of andrographolide in hypertension-driven cardiac remodeling.

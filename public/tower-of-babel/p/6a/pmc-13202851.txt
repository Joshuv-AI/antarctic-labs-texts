Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide triggered heart failure-like pathogenesis in mice by potentially targeting mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p in Wnt/β-catenin signaling
Authors: Zhihua Ren, Yixue Cai, Yonghui Duan, Xiao Wang, Jiafei Sun, Danqin Ren, Jiefeng Liang, Tingting Ku, Xia Ning, Nan Sang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNanopesticides have been progressively incorporated into contemporary agricultural systems owing to their distinctive physicochemical characteristics and superior pest management capabilities. Given their environmental persistence and propensity for bioaccumulation, nanopesticides are increasingly recognized as emerging cardiovascular toxicants, thus warranting systematic investigation of their exposure-associated cardiotoxic effects.MethodsIn the present study, the impact of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide on cardiac homeostasis was systematically investigated in a C57BL/6 mouse model following one-month exposure administered via oral gavage, with copper concentrations set at 0 (vehicle control), 1, and 5 mg/kg bw. Multiple molecular biology techniques were employed to elucidate the nanopesticide-induced cardiac impairment and the underlying mechanisms.ResultsIt was demonstrated that prolonged exposure to this copper-based nanopesticide resulted in significant deterioration of ventricular systolic and diastolic function, induction of ventricular chamber remodeling, disturbance of hemodynamic stability, and dysregulation of cardiac substructural gene expression, collectively mirroring the characteristic phenotypic manifestations and underlying molecular hallmarks of clinical heart failure. Background

The rapid advancement of nanotechnology has catalyzed the pervasive integration of nanopesticides within contemporary agricultural paradigms, where their exceptional biocidal potency, precise targeting, and controlled-release properties confer multifaceted agricultural benefits, spanning insecticidal, bactericidal, and herbicidal applications for pest management while concomitantly enhancing crop productivity [1]. However, this technological innovation introduces significant environmental and toxicological concerns, as nanopesticides may permeate ecological systems and human populations via multiple exposure pathways: occupational hazards (dermal or pulmonary absorption during application), dietary consumption of contaminated agricultural products and water resources, or environmental reservoirs in soil and water matrices, where bioaccumulation and trophic transfer amplification potentially engender substantial ecotoxicological and anthropogenic health implications [2, 3]. Notably, Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide has gained substantial commercial prominence as an advanced agricultural fungicide, exhibiting superior performance to traditional copper-based pesticides through its precision-engineered bioactive copper ion release kinetics [4]. Since its official registration in China (2001), this nanotechnology-enabled agrochemical innovation has achieved global ubiquity as a premier copper pesticide [5], yet its escalating application has concomitantly precipitated critical ecotoxicological considerations. Although comprehensive environmental monitoring data specifically quantifying nanoparticulate speciation remain scarce, extant empirical investigations have documented measurable copper accumulation across various environmental compartments, including the atmosphere (up to 200 ng/m3), pedogenic systems (~ 20 mg/kg), and aquatic environment (2.0–9.4 µg/L) [6–8]. These findings collectively underscore the imperative for rigorous biosafety assessments of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide.

Cardiovascular disease constitutes the predominant contributor to global disease burden, accounting for approximately one-third of all deaths worldwide, according to World Heart Federation statistics [9]. The pathogenesis of this disease is characterized by a sequential pathophysiological cascade [10]. This process begins with the initiation of atherosclerotic plaque formation caused by lipid deposition and inflammatory infiltration subsequent to chronic endothelial dysfunction. It is followed by acute thrombosis triggered by vulnerable plaque rupture in conjunction with platelet activation, and culminates in maladaptive myocardial remodeling driven by neurohormonal dysfunction along with programmed cardiomyocyte death. These pathological cascades ultimately converge in the development of chronic heart failure, a complex clinical syndrome with an estimated 5-year mortality rate exceeding 50% in advanced stages [11]. The syndrome manifests through cardinal pathophysiological features, including impaired excitation-contraction coupling in cardiomyocytes, progressive ventricular dilatation, and consequent hemodynamic derangements [12]. Current heart failure diagnostic protocols integrate three essential evaluation components: left ventricular systolic dysfunction as defined by ejection fraction (EF) measurements below 40%, elevated natriuretic peptide biomarkers with threshold values of 100 pg/mL for brain natriuretic peptide (BNP) in acute presentations or 300 pg/mL for N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) in chronic cases, along with characteristic imaging findings including echocardiographic wall motion abnormalities or fibrotic patterns on cardiac magnetic resonance with late gadolinium enhancement [13]. Accumulating evidence from toxicological studies has demonstrated that nanoscale particles, particularly silicon dioxide, silver, and carbon-based nanomaterials, could infiltrate and accumulate in cardiac tissue, thereby disrupting myocardial electrophysiology, inducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis, and promoting oxidative stress-mediated damage, ultimately leading to functional cardiac impairments [14]. Investigations using zebrafish (Danio rerio) models have further revealed that copper nanoparticles elicit profound developmental cardiotoxicity, as evidenced by dose-dependent perturbations in both cardiac blood pumped out and vascular parameters during embryogenesis, with the pathognomonic presentation of pericardial edema [15]. Given these findings, further investigations are imperative to elucidate the cardiotoxic effects and potential mechanisms associated with copper-based nanoparticles.

The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, characterized by precise spatiotemporal activity fluctuations, constitutes a regulatory core indispensable for cardiac morphogenesis through directing the fate determination, proliferation, and differentiation of progenitor cells during embryogenesis [16]. While its activity is typically suppressed to maintain myocardial homeostasis in the adult heart, aberrant reactivation under pathological stimuli such as myocardial infarction or pressure overload initiates a cascade of detrimental remodeling processes [17]. The translocation of activated β-catenin into the nucleus and the subsequent regulation of target genes contributing to cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, interstitial fibrosis, inflammatory responses, and metabolic disturbances have identified the dysregulation of this pathway as a common molecular nexus linking various injuries to terminal cardiac pathologies such as infarction and heart failure [17]. Simultaneously, post-transcriptional modulation of cardiac development, homeostasis, and stress responses is provided by miRNAs, which function as a layer of fine epigenetic regulatory networks by directly targeting key components of the Wnt pathway, such as β-catenin or its upstream regulators, to inhibit or enhance its signal output [18]. Consequently, a complex “miRNA-Wnt/β-catenin signaling axis” is established wherein aberrant miRNA expression is recognized as both a consequence and a propagating factor of cardiac disease [19]. Investigating whether Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide induces cardiotoxicity by disrupting this regulatory axis offers a focused perspective for elucidating its toxicological mechanisms, a rationale supported by the recognized capacity of environmental exposures like nanomaterials to perturb miRNA expression profiles [20].

This study investigated the cardiotoxic effects of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide in a mouse model, with particular emphasis on ventricular structural integrity and functional performance. The findings on Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-disrupted cardiac performance and the underlying mechanism provided new insights for the safety assessment of this kind of agricultural nanomaterial and the environmental triggers of heart failure-like diseases.

Methods

Animals

Male C57BL/6 mice, aged 2 to 3 weeks, were purchased from Beijing Vital River Laboratory Animal Technology (Beijing, China) and acclimated in a routine pathogen-free environment with a temperature range of 21–23 °C and a relative humidity of 40–60%. The animals were provided with ad libitum access to both food and water throughout the study period, and a daily photoperiod (light/dark, L/D) of 12 L/12D was guaranteed. Humane treatment of the animals was ensured to minimize any potential distress, and all procedures were conducted in strict accordance with the guidelines set forth by the Animal Ethics and Welfare Committee of Shanxi University (Taiyuan, China).

Exposure strategy of Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide

Environmental acclimatization of the mice commenced one week before the formal exposure trial. Mice were then randomly assigned to experimental groups, each consisting of 10 to 12 individuals. The copper exposure concentrations were determined based on the acceptable daily intake (ADI) recommended by the World Health Organization for adult humans, with necessary adjustments made for interspecies differences in body weight and body surface area utilizing a correction factor (Km). The exposure dosage was calculated using the following equations:

Given that the human ADI was 0.14 mg/kg body weight (bw) [21], with Km factors for humans and mice being 37 and 3 [22], an equivalent copper dose of 1.73 mg/kg bw in mice was thus derived. Accordingly, a basal exposure dose of 1 mg/kg bw was selected to correspond to the safe daily intake level of copper for mice. A higher dose of 5 mg/kg bw was selected based on its identification as a threshold for copper nanopesticide-induced systemic toxicity in rodents [23], and on human acute exposure calculation [24], whereby a mouse-equivalent dose of 3.0-3.8 mg/kg bw was derived by assuming 90% water content, a copper concentration of 57–73 mg/kg in fresh contaminated leafy vegetables, a single 300 g serving for a 70 kg adult yielding a human acute dose of 0.24–0.31 mg/kg bw, and subsequent allometric scaling (factor 12.3). Consequently, the experimental exposure doses of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide (Kocide 3000, Dupont Company, Wilmington, Germany) were set at 1 mg/kg bw and 5 mg/kg bw copper, administered via oral gavage every other day, with the nanopesticide suspension being vortexed and sonicated immediately prior to each administration to ensure uniform nanoparticle dispersion, and with the corresponding volume of water serving as the control. Following a period of one month of continuous copper exposure, the mice were euthanized by CO2 inhalation. Heart tissue samples were then harvested and processed as follows: whole hearts were weighed to obtain wet weight, and the entire heart (without dissection of individual ventricles) was used for RNA and protein analyses. A portion of the tissues was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (Solarbio, China) at ambient temperature, while the remainder was flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C for further analysis.

Characterization of Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide

The morphological properties, zeta potential, and particle size distribution of this chemical were analyzed. Detailed experimental procedures are provided in the Supplementary Material.

Echocardiographic assessment

Mice were anesthetized using 2% isoflurane (RWD Life Science, China) and subsequently positioned in a supine posture on a heating pad maintained at 37 °C to ensure stable body temperature. Following the removal of the chest and upper abdominal fur, the echocardiographic imaging was conducted using the VevoLab software operating on the Vevo 2100 imaging system (Fujifilm VisualSonics, USA), with motion-mode images captured for subsequent analysis. Key structural parameters of the left ventricle (LV), including the heart rate (HR), cardiac output (CO), left ventricular end-diastolic volume (LVEDV), left ventricular end-systolic volume (LVESV), EF, left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESD), fractional shortening (FS), and mass, were meticulously measured. For each structural and functional parameter, measurements were taken over three consecutive cardiac cycles, with the average values being used for data analysis. No pre-exposure echocardiographic baseline assessment was conducted in accordance with the 3R principles [25], which represents a limitation of the study. During echocardiography, mice were anesthetized with 1.0%–1.5% isoflurane [26]. To ensure objectivity and minimize bias, personnel responsible for image acquisition and parameter analysis were blinded to group assignments. A stratified random alternating detection protocol was implemented, whereby one animal was randomly selected from each experimental group for examination per round to control for potential diurnal variation. This cycle was repeated until data acquisition was completed for all animals.

Histopathology

Heart tissue samples from both the control group and Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide treatment groups were processed as follows: the samples were initially fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde solution for 24 h, followed by dehydration through a graded series of ethanol solutions (ranging from 70% to 100%). Subsequently, the tissues were infiltrated and embedded in paraffin. Serial sections of cardiac tissue, each with a thickness of 5 μm, were prepared using the cryomicrotome (Leica CM1950, Germany) and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E). The stained sections were subsequently examined for histopathological alterations under a standard microscope (Olympus IX73, Japan) at magnifications of 20× and 40×.

Quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted and purified from mouse tissue treated with Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide at varying concentrations using Trizol Universal Reagent (Invitrogen Corporation, USA). The relative transcriptional expressions of various cardiac substructural biomarkers, including Hcn4, Nkx2-5, Isl1, Shox2, Tbx18, Myh6, Myl7, and Tnnt2, were assessed [27, 28]. Additionally, key molecular components of Wnt/β-catenin signaling were examined, including positive regulators such as Gsk3b, Frzb, Wnt3, Wnt7a, Wnt8a, Wnt5a, Wnt3a, Wnt2b, β-Catenin, Axin1, Lef1, and Tcf7, along with two canonical inhibitors, Wnt inhibitory factor 1 (Wif1) and Dickkopf (Dkk1). The primer sequences for all the genes evaluated are provided in Supplementary Table S1. Comprehensive methodological details are documented in the Supplementary Material.

Western blotting

Target protein expression in tissues across various exposure conditions was assessed. Specific antibodies against NKX2-5 (1:1000), TNNT2 (1:1000), WNT5A (1:1000), β-CATENIN (1:1000), LEF1 (1:1000), and TCF7 (1:1000) were employed, with GAPDH (1:5000) used as the loading control. Complete experimental protocols are available in the Supplementary Material.

miRNA-gene target prediction

To investigate potential miRNA regulators implicated in the modulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, two well-characterized inhibitory genes of Mus Musculus (mmu), Dkk1 and Wif1, were subjected to in silico miRNA target prediction utilizing four extensively recognized databases, namely TargetScan (http://www.targetscan.org), mirDIP (https://ophid.utoronto.ca/mirDIP/index.jsp#r), miRmap (https://mirmap.ezlab.org), and miRDB (https://mirdb.org). Candidate miRNAs were prioritized based on high-confidence prediction scores aggregated across these platforms, with the putative miRNAs targeting Wif1 and Dkk1 subsequently compiled in Supplementary Table S2 and Supplementary Table S3, respectively. To refine the pool of candidate miRNAs, an intersection analysis was conducted to identify shared miRNAs that may concurrently regulate both target genes. This approach highlighted mmu-miR-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p as the highest-ranking shared miRNAs predicted to simultaneously target both Dkk1 and Wif1. To further substantiate their putative regulatory function, mmu-miR-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p were reverse queried within the miRmap database to predict their downstream gene targets, emphasizing those exhibiting high target scores and Wnt/β-catenin signaling-related genes. From the resultant dataset (Supplementary Table S4 and Table S5), the top predicted targets were extracted, including nine genes directly associated with the Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade. These targets were subsequently mapped and visualized via Cytoscape software (http://cytoscape.org) to delineate their potential interactions within the broader regulatory network.

miRNA expression profiling

Total RNA was isolated from both control and Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-treated mouse tissue specimens using the same protocol as described in the qPCR extraction protocol. First-strand cDNA synthesis corresponding to mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p was conducted utilizing the SPARKScript II miRNA First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (AG0502, SparkJade, China), with reverse transcription facilitated by stem-loop primers that share an identical backbone sequence (5'-GTCGTATCCAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGGTATTCGCACTGGATACGAC-3') and differ only in the last six nucleotides at the 3' end, which was designed to anneal to the 3' ends of the respective miRNAs: for mmu-miRNA-590-3p, the tail is 5'-ACTAGC-3'; for mmu-miRNA-338-5p, the tail is 5'-CACTCA-3'. Quantitative assessment of the miRNA expression was carried out using 2X SYBR Green qPCR Mix (AH0104, SparkJade, China), with Rhodamine X as passive reference dye for signal calibration and U6 small nuclear RNA (U6-snRNA) as endogenous control for amplification reactions. The target-specific primer pairs were designed as follows: forward (5’-GCGCGCGTAATTTTATGTATAA-3’) and reverse (5’-ATCCAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGG-3’) for mmu-miRNA-590-3p detection, forward (5’-CGCAACAATATCCTGGTGC-3’) and reverse (5’-ATCCAGTGCAGGGTCCGAGG − 3’) for mmu-miRNA-338-5p detection, forward (5’-GCTTCGGCAGCACATATACTAAAAT-3’) and reverse (5’-CGCTTCACGAATTTGCGTGTCAT-3’) for mmu-U6-snRNA normalization.

Dual-luciferase reporter gene assay

The dual-luciferase reporter assay system was employed to investigate the target regulation of certain miRNAs on Dkk1 and Wif1 in transiently transfected human embryonic kidney cells (HEK293). In short, the pRL-TK reporter vectors (report control, Genomeditech, China) were inserted with the sequence that contained the indicated miRNA complementary sites in the 3’-UTR of the target genes of Dkk1 (pRL-TK-Dkk1-wild type, Dkk1-WT) and Wif1 (pRL-TK-Wif1-wild type, Wif1-WT) or their mutant (pRL-TK-Dkk1-mutant, Dkk1-Mut; pRL-TK-Wif1-mutant, Wif1-Mut). HEK293 cells were cultured in DMEM medium (Cegrogen Biotech., China) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (Gibco, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Cegrogen Biotech., China) in the incubator (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) at 37℃ with 5% carbon dioxide. Upon achieving 40% confluency in 96-well culture plates, cells were co-transfected using GMTrans Liposomal Transfection Reagent (Genomeditech, China) with 100 ng of WT or Mut reporter gene constructs, 10 ng reporter control plasmid, together with 30 nM of synthetic miRNA mimic or its negative control (mmu-miRNA mimic Ncontrol). At 48-h post-transfection, cellular lysates were analyzed for luminescent signals according to the manufacturer’s protocol for the Dual-Glo Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA). Relative Renilla luciferase activity data were normalized to corresponding firefly luciferase values as an internal control, and the final quantitative results were expressed as fold changes relative to the report control values using the mmu-miRNA mimic Ncontrol.

Statistical analysis

For in vitro assays, such as qPCR and western blotting, experiments were carried out in triplicate (n ≥ 3) to ensure methodological reproducibility and robustness. The quantitative results are expressed as the mean values ± standard deviation to characterize central tendency and variability. Comparative analysis between control and Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-treated groups was conducted using one-way ANOVA followed by Dunnett’s multiple comparison test with GraphPad Prism software (version 9.0, USA). Statistical significance thresholds were established at *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01, with all tests maintaining a 95% confidence interval.

Results

Structural and physicochemical characterization of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide

SEM observation confirmed that the commercialized Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide exhibits a well-defined spherical morphology with uniform particle distribution (Fig. 1A). High-resolution microstructural examination further demonstrated the presence of crystallographically ordered lattice fringes, confirming the highly crystalline structure of the nanoparticles (Fig. 1B). Dynamic light scattering measurements determined an average hydrodynamic diameter of 337.49 ± 8.64 nm (Fig. 1C), reflecting a moderately polydisperse nanoparticle population. The zeta potential analysis yielded a surface charge of -40.07 ± 3.32 mV (Fig. 1D), suggesting moderate electrostatic stabilization of the colloidal suspension. Collectively, the observed morphological and physicochemical properties of the Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide align with previously reported data [29], confirming its structural integrity and stability for subsequent application.

Impacts of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure on mouse cardiac function and hemodynamic performance

To determine the potential cardiotoxic effects of chronic Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure, transthoracic motion-mode echocardiography, a non-invasive diagnostic modality with excellent reproducibility [30], was conducted to evaluate the alterations in both systolic and diastolic functions at the level of the left ventricular papillary muscles. Following one month of exposure to Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide at incremental copper concentrations (0, 1, and 5 mg/kg bw), two pivotal hemodynamic parameters, HR and the CO of the LV, were initially quantified, while no significant alterations were observed in fundamental physiological indices including heart weight to body weight (HW/BW, Table S6) and mean arterial pressure (MAP, calculated as diastolic pressure + 1/3 (systolic pressure – diastolic pressure)) using a non-invasive tail-cuff system (Supplementary Figure S1). The data demonstrated a statistically significant suppression of both HR and CO in high-concentration nanopesticide-exposed mice relative to those controls (p < 0.01, Fig. 2A, B), suggesting that chronic exposure to this nanopesticide compromised cardiovascular performance by impairment of ventricular systolic function and reduction of stroke volume, potentially precipitating systemic hypoperfusion and progressive systolic dysfunction.

Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide impaired mouse cardiac performance. A HR. B CO. C LVEDV. D LVESV. E SV. F EF. *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01 versus the corresponding control group

To further delineate the effects of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide on cardiac systolic properties, LVEDV and LVESV parameters, namely the end-diastolic volume and end-systolic volume of the LV, were rigorously quantified. Baseline measurements in control mice displayed baseline values of 74.37 ± 3.35 µL for LVEDV and 33.32 ± 4.53 µL for LVESV (Fig. 2C, D). Strikingly, Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure elicited a copper dose-dependent elevation in both LVEDV and LVESV (p < 0.05, Fig. 2C-D), suggestive of progressive left ventricular dilatation and adverse cardiac remodeling [31]. Furthermore, the SV and EF index, calculated from these two volumetric parameters, exhibited a marked reduction in high-concentration Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-treated groups (p < 0.05, Fig. 2E, F), reflecting severe impairment of left ventricular systolic efficiency and compromised cardiac pumping capacity. These findings collectively suggest that Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure could induce a deleterious functional remodeling of the mouse heart, culminating in compromised peripheral tissue perfusion, as corroborated by the hemodynamic deficits illustrated in Fig. 2B.

Impacts of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure on mouse ventricular architecture

To systematically characterize the cardiotoxic effects of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure, the LVEDD and LVESD parameters, corresponding to maximal and minimal ventricular dimensions throughout the cardiac cycle phases, were measured across experimental mice. Comparative analysis demonstrated statistically significant increases in both LVEDD and LVESD in Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-exposed mice relative to controls (p < 0.05, Fig. 3A, B), suggesting pathological ventricular dilatation and concomitant impairment of systolic performance. Furthermore, the FS index, derived from these dimensional parameters, was found to exhibit pronounced attenuation of left ventricular systolic velocity in chemical-treated animals (p < 0.05, Fig. 3C), accompanied by significantly reduced left ventricular anterior wall thickness in systole (LVAWs), confirming systolic impairment, while LVAWd trended upward (p < 0.05, Supplementary Figure S2), likely reflecting a compensatory response to wall stress in the dilated ventricle [32]. These findings provide additional compelling evidence for the significant deleterious impact of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide on left ventricular systolic efficiency. These findings provide robust quantitative evidence that Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure induces deleterious alterations in ventricular architecture.

Notably, gravimetric analysis demonstrated that a significant elevation in left ventricular mass (normalized to BW) was observed in mice exposed to Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide (p < 0.01, Fig. 3D), implying maladaptive pathological cardiac structural aberrations characterized by chamber dilatation. Representative echocardiographic images visually confirmed these pathological changes (Fig. 3E), demonstrating marked ventricular chamber enlargement in exposed mice compared to controls, a morphological adaptation likely driven by sustained hemodynamic stress and increased cardiac workload.

Histopathological examination of cardiac tissue sections, as shown in Fig. 3F, demonstrated a well-organized tissue architecture with uniform alignment in control mice. Conversely, dose-dependent morphological abnormalities, including diffuse eosinophilic changes, prominent cytoplasmic vacuolation, and focal hydropic degeneration manifested by cellular edema and cytoplasmic pallor, were observed in the Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-treated mice (Fig. 3F), also confirming the detrimental effects of this nanopesticide on cardiac integrity and function. The observed pathophysiological derangements, encompassing both systolic dysfunction (Fig. 2) and ventricular chamber remodeling (Fig. 3), accurately recapitulate the fundamental hallmarks of incipient heart failure pathogenesis [31], underscoring the potential cardiovascular risks associated with chronic Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure.

Assessment of heart failure-like molecular pathology induced by Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide in mice

To evaluate the induction of heart failure-like molecular pathology following Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure, serum concentrations of BNP, creatine kinase (CK), and its myocardial band isoenzyme fraction (CK-MB), well-validated clinical biomarkers released into systemic circulation in response to myocardial stress and structural damage [33, 34], were quantitatively analyzed. A significant elevation in the concentrations of these cardiac biomarkers was observed in Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-treated mice compared to control mice, with the increases of 0.66–2.81 folds for BNP, 0.18–1.16 folds for CK, and 0.12–0.93 folds for CK-MB (p < 0.05 or 0.01, Fig. 4A). The dose-dependent upregulation of these circulating biomarkers is consistent with documented clinical manifestations of heart failure pathophysiology [35, 36], indicating that Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exacerbates cardiac dysfunction in mice.

Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide induced molecular pathology resembling heart failure in mice. A Serum levels of clinical biomarkers associated with heart failure. B Schematic representation of genes involved in the cardiac substructure maintenance. C The relative transcriptional expressions of cardiac substructure-related genes. D The relative protein levels of representative biomarkers. *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01 versus the corresponding control group

To further elucidate the molecular mechanisms underlying this cardiac damage, the transcriptional expressions of genes associated with critical cardiac substructures, including the sinoatrial node (SAN), right atrium (RA), right ventricle (RV), left atrium (LA), and LV (Fig. 4B), were measured using the qPCR experiment. Specifically, a significant downregulation, approximately 0.21–0.49 folds compared to the controls (p < 0.01 or 0.05), was detected in eight critical genes (Hcn4, Nkx2-5, Isl1, Shox2, Tbx18, Myh6, Myl7, Tnnt2) across these regions (Fig. 4C), providing direct molecular evidence of extensive myocardial architectural disruption and corresponding functional deficits following this nanopesticide exposure.

The findings were further substantiated at the protein level via western blotting analysis. The relative protein levels of NKX2-5 and TNNT2, critical markers of cardiac structural integrity, were reduced to 0.74–0.83 and 0.47–0.74 folds of control values, respectively (p < 0.01 or 0.05, Fig. 4D), revealing the substantial impairment of myocardial architecture in exposed mice. Overall, these molecular-level findings reveal that chronic Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide triggers a pathological cascade resembling heart failure progression in mice, characterized by the elevated serum biomarkers of myocardial stress and injury, coupled with dose-dependent suppression of cardiac structural and functional gene expression.

Dysregulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-induced heart failure-like pathology

To study the possible regulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway in the adverse biological effects, the transcriptional alterations of the key pathway components in this pathway, including the ligand (i.e., Wnt3, Wnt7a, Wnt8a, Wnt5a, Wnt3a, and Wnt2b), receptor (i.e., Frzb), central molecule (i.e., β-Catenin), transcriptional activating factors (i.e., Axin1, Lef1, and Tcf7), and β-Catenin phosphorylation-related factor (i.e., Gsk3b) were studied. As demonstrated in Fig. 5A, significant upregulations were observed across the examined pathway genes (p < 0.05 or 0.01), indicating the profound activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling following Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure. Concomitantly, marked downregulations of Wnt/β-catenin signaling inhibitors (i.e., Dkk1, Wif1) were observed (p < 0.01, Fig. 5B), further substantiating the proposed mechanistic role of both core pathway components and their regulatory inhibitors in mediating cardiac remodeling and progression toward heart failure-like phenotypes.

B The relative transcriptional expressions of two canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling inhibitors. C The relative protein levels of key Wnt/β-catenin signaling components. *p < 0.05 and **p < 0.01 versus the corresponding control group

Core molecular mediators associated with the Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade, including WNT5A, β-CATENIN, LEF1, as well as TCF7, were quantitatively characterized in cardiac tissues across various exposure groups. Consistent expression patterns of these signaling molecules were demonstrated by western blotting analysis, with clearly discernible band intensities presented in untreated control mice (Fig. 5C). Notably, these protein levels were significantly upregulated in chemical-exposed mice relative to controls, with an obvious increase of 0.35–23.96 folds (p < 0.05 or 0.01, Fig. 5C), providing robust molecular evidence for the suggested activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-induced cardiac dysfunction characterized by heart failure-like pathological features.

miRNA modulation in Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-induced cardiac dysfunction

To systematically investigate the potential involvement of miRNAs in Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-induced cardiac dysfunction, an integrative bioinformatics approach was conducted. Multiple predictive databases, including TargetScan, mirDIP , miRmap, and miRDB, were interrogated using two canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway inhibitors (i.e., Wif1, Dkk1) as input molecule queries (Fig. 6A). Through this computational screening, seven candidate miRNAs targeting Wif1 and twenty-two miRNAs targeting Dkk1 were identified, with mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p being found to exhibit dual regulatory capacity for both inhibitors (Fig. 6B). Notably, both miRNAs were selected for further investigation, a choice supported by the previously established regulatory relationship of mmu-miRNA-590-3p with Wif1 and Dkk1 in colon cancer pathogenesis [37]. Extended bioinformatic analysis revealed that mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-338-5p contains conserved binding sites not only for Wif1 and Dkk1, but also for several critical Wnt family members (i.e., Wnt1, Wnt2, Wnt5a, Wnt7b, Wnt8b, Wnt9b), and the downstream effectors (i.e., Lef1) (Fig. 6C), suggesting their potential roles as a pivotal regulatory node within the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway following Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure.

To elucidate the potential mechanism by which Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide mediates the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, the expression of two miRNAs was consequently quantified. As shown in Fig. 6D, a significant dose-dependent upregulation of miRNA transcription was observed (2.15–2.99 folds for mmu-miRNA-590-3p and 2.80–3.23 folds for mmu-miRNA-338-5p versus controls, p < 0.01), exhibiting an inverse correlation with the downregulation of Wnt inhibitors (Fig. 5B). These findings supported the hypothesis that mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p may bind to the 3’-UTR of Wif1 and Dkk1 following Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure. In comparison, mmu-miRNA-338-5p was observed to exert a stronger regulatory effect on the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway than mmu-miRNA-590-3p, and this finding was correlated with the higher binding scores predicted for mmu-miRNA-338-5p with Dkk1 (Table S7) and Wif1 (Table S8) based on different databases.

To validate the interaction, the complementary binding of mmu-miRNA-590-3p to three target sites (i.e., Wif1 3’-UTR at sites 571–576, Dkk1 3’-UTR at sites 448–453, and 532–537, all containing the seed-matched sequence 5’-AAAAUU-3’), and that of mmu-miRNA-338-5p to three target sites (i.e., Wif1 3’-UTR at sites 235–241 and 609–615, and Dkk1 3’-UTR at sites 627–633, all containing the seed-matched sequence 5’-AUAUUGU-3’), was explored using a dual-luciferase reporter gene assay. The reporter transfection system was found to maintain stable luciferase expression regardless of individual transfection with both mmu-miRNA mimic or its Ncontrol, or various WT/Mut constructs (Fig. 6E-J). However, co-transfection of mmu-miRNA mimic with WT constructs (i.e., Wif1-WT, Dkk1-WT) resulted in significantly reduced luciferase activity compared to WT transfection alone (p < 0.01, Fig. 6E-J), showing the substantial inhibitory effect of mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p on the translation of Wif1 or Dkk1. Conversely, co-transfection with mmu-miRNA-590-3p or mmu-miRNA-338-5p mimic in the Mut systems showed significantly increased activities relative to the WT system (p < 0.01, Fig. 6E-J), confirming the indispensable role of the complementary binding of mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p with the 3’-UTR of Wif1 and Dkk1 in Wnt/β-catenin signaling regulation.

Discussion

The investigation of potential cardiotoxic effects induced by widely utilized nanosized pesticides represents a critical endeavor in assessing cardiovascular health risks. In this study, the adverse effects of cardiac systolic function and ventricular architecture of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure were explored using the mouse model, and the results revealed that chronic exposure to this nanopesticide could induce pathological manifestations resembling heart failure, a mechanism that may be mediated by the upregulation of mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p within the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway. These findings herein provide novel experimental evidence supporting a strong correlation between prolonged Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide exposure and the onset of cardiac dysfunction in mammalian systems.

Cardiovascular homeostasis exhibits particular vulnerability to environmental perturbations, with mounting empirical evidence now clearly establishing the detrimental impacts of engineered nanomaterials on cardiac performance. Among these, particular concern has been raised regarding heavy metal-based nanomaterials, which have been shown to efficiently cross intestinal epithelial barriers and achieve systemic biodistribution to secondary target organs, ultimately resulting in cardiac compromise [38]. The present investigation focuses on Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide, a widely employed agricultural nanomaterial, which was found to elicit profound cardiac pathophysiological alterations manifesting as concurrent functional and structural remodeling. Comprehensive functional assessments revealed that Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide severely compromised ventricular performances, including the diminished ventricular systolic function evidenced as significant reductions in HR and EF (Fig. 2A and E), as well as impaired pumping capacity demonstrated by a significant reduction in CO (Fig. 2B), collectively resulting in substantial hemodynamic impairment. Concomitant structural alterations were also documented, including left ventricular dilatation (Fig. 3C-D), ultrastructural tissue damage (Fig. 3F, blinded pathological scoring is recommended for future investigations), and significant downregulation of key cardiac gene networks across multiple substructures (Fig. 4B-D). This dual remodeling phenotype, when considered in conjunction with elevated circulating cardiac stress biomarkers (i.e., BNP, CK, and CK-MB) (Fig. 4A), strongly suggests a pathological progression toward overt heart failure pathology. The cardiotoxic potential of heavy metal-based nanomaterials is well-documented in the existing literature, providing substantial support for these findings. Specifically, zinc oxide nanoparticle exposure has been demonstrated to induce cardiac inflammation, myocardial fibrosis, and degenerative necrosis in rodent models [39]. Similarly, cardiac coagulation markers were upregulated following intratracheally administered silver nanoparticles, resulting in diminished local blood flow velocity and promoting thrombotic complications [40]. Additionally, silica nanoparticles have been implicated in the acceleration of atherogenesis through adsorption of circulating apolipoprotein A-I [41]. Extending prior observations of Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-induced bradycardia in zebrafish models [42], the current study yields compelling evidence of conserved cardiotoxic effects in rodent systems, with pathognomonic structural and functional abnormalities consistent with heart failure pathogenesis being identified. Overall, the cumulative evidence presented herein underscores the imperative need for both enhanced regulatory scrutiny of nanomaterial cardiovascular safety profiles and the implementation of systematic cardiovascular surveillance programs for occupationally exposed populations.

The Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway is widely acknowledged as a fundamental intracellular communication mechanism, being critically implicated in cardiac morphogenesis, tissue regeneration, structural remodeling, and the preservation of homeostasis balance throughout embryonic development to adult stages [43]. The canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling cascade is initiated through high-affinity ligand-receptor interactions between secreted Wnt ligands with their cognate Frizzled or LRP5/6 transmembrane receptors, orchestrating a precisely regulated molecular cascade that ultimately facilitates β-catenin stabilization, its subsequent nuclear translocation, and T cell factor/lymphoid enhancer factor (Tcf/Lef) mediated transcriptional activation [16]. Accumulating evidence has established the indispensable contribution of this pathway in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular disorders, particularly myocardial infarction and heart failure [44]. In the current investigation, transcriptional activation (Fig. 5A) and concomitant intensification of protein levels (Fig. 5C) of key Wnt/β-catenin signaling components, including Wnt ligands, receptors, central mediators, and downstream transcriptional regulators, were demonstrated in mice subjected to Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide, corroborating the hypothesis that this nanopesticide exerts multi-target activating effects on Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, thereby exacerbating heart failure-like pathological manifestations (Figs. 2 and 3). Notably, two typical Wnt inhibitors, Dkk1 and Wif1, exhibited pronounced downregulation upon Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide administration (Fig. 5B), further reinforcing the pivotal regulatory function of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in nanopesticide-induced cardiotoxicity. The indispensable involvement of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in cardiac ontogeny and functional integrity has been well-documented. For instance, embryonic cardiogenesis in Xenopus is severely compromised upon disruption of Wnt11, a core signaling component, with concomitant abrogation of contractile phenotype induction in embryonic explants [45]. Moreover, the specification of cardiac progenitor cells into epicardial lineages is precisely regulated through secreted Frizzled-related proteins, Wnt signaling antagonists whose spatiotemporal expression patterns may be disrupted by environmental contaminants, potentially compromising cardiac developmental programs [46]. Based on the established role of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in Cu(OH)₂-induced cardiac dysfunction, a more detailed elucidation of its precise mechanistic basis in specific cardiovascular pathologies is required, which may lead to the development of transformative therapeutic strategies for future clinical applications. To achieve this, the employment of unbiased global screening approaches, such as RNA-sequencing and proteomics, is recommended in future studies to comprehensively identify additional molecular mechanisms underlying Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-induced cardiotoxic effects, as well as to uncover other regulatory networks that may interact with the Wnt/β-catenin signaling.

A significant upregulation of mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p expression was observed following exposure to Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide (Fig. 6D), accompanied by a consequent transcriptional downregulation of Wnt/β-catenin inhibitors, Wif1 and Dkk1 (Fig. 5B), suggesting the possible negative regulatory role of these two miRNAs on both inhibitors. The direct interactions between two mmu-miRNAs and Wnt inhibitors were confirmed, with three conserved binding sites identified in Wif1 and three in Dkk1 (Fig. 6E-J), consistent with previously reported findings on its complementary binding to the 3’-UTR of Wif1 and Dkk1 mRNA [37]. Functioning as secreted Wnt inhibitors, underexpressed Dkk1 or Wif1 were demonstrated to weaken steric hindrance, directly and competitively activating the binding of Wnt ligands (i.e., Wnt3, Wnt5a) to Frizzled receptors on the cell membrane surface, consequently leading to the widespread upregulation of multiple Wnt ligands and an increase in the protein activity of downstream transcription factors LEF1 and TCF7 (Fig. 5C). Consequently, the activating effect on the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway was supported through the targeted binding of mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p to these Wnt/β-catenin inhibitors, resulting in a positive regulatory effect on this signaling cascade (Fig. 5C), with these coordinated molecular interactions mechanistically may have a strong correlation with the Cu(OH)2 nanopesticide-induced cardiac pathological manifestations resembling heart failure in mouse models. To more conclusively determine the specific role of these two miRNAs in Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide-induced cardiac pathology, future studies employing relevant inhibitors or antagonists in parallel experiments would be valuable for obtaining direct mechanistic evidence.

Conclusions

In conclusion, exposure to Cu(OH)₂ nanopesticide elicits profound cardiopathological manifestations that recapitulate the hallmark features of heart failure, encompassing both maladaptive structural remodeling and compromised systolic function performance. A key molecular mechanism underlying these effects may involve the significant upregulation of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway, mediated by the downregulation of Wif1 and Dkk1, which is initiated by the promotion of mmu-miRNA-590-3p and mmu-miRNA-338-5p expression. These findings provide novel insights into the cardiotoxic effects of copper-based agrochemicals and refine the risk assessment paradigm by identifying specific biomarkers of cardiac damage and pathway-specific therapeutic targets for potential intervention.

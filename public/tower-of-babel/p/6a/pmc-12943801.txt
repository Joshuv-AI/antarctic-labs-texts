A positive autoethnographic study of learning and growth in a beginner tennis player in Korea
Authors: Chul-Min Kim, Jeong-Yeop Shin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThe objective of this study was to explore the value of learning and growth experiences of a Korean beginner tennis player from a positive psychology perspective. Given that tennis in Korea is predominantly learned through clubs, this study explored the culture of Korean tennis clubs.MethodsThe author's tennis participation experiences were examined using a positive autoethnographic and positive psychological perspective, focusing on values such as growth, resilience, expectation, and hope. To this end, tennis journals and self-recall questionnaires were collected as data from the author, and they were analysed using domain analysis and taxonomic analysis methods proposed by Spradley.ResultThree key themes were identified. First, I experienced “new challenges and learning” through practicing basic movement skills and strategies and forming bonds with others. Second, I gained 'growth and competence' through experiences of active communication and resilience. Third, I experienced the 'value of growth through reflection and introspection' by having faith in improving my tennis skills, reflecting on the use of skills and strategies, and setting new challenges and change strategies.ConclusionThis study demonstrates that personal growth, psychological resilience, and positive relationships are central to beginner tennis learning. The development of technical and tactical skills supported both physical and cognitive development, while encouragement from peers and the coach contributed to self-concept formation, emotional regulation, and increased motivation. These experiences expanded tennis into an activity that enhances overall well-being and provided teacher educators with insights into overcoming challenges, fostering empathy, and promoting relationship-centred learning. The study underscores the importance of relationship-building within club environments and highlights the need for further research on positive learning factors.

Introduction

I, the author, am the sole participant in this study. I am a 45-year-old male and a university lecturer teaching sports education. I received tennis lessons four times a week from August to December 2022 at the club I joined and twice a week from March to October 2023. I am currently preparing for a third round of lessons. Prior to this, I had never formally learned a racket sport. As I entered my mid-40s, I thought there would be few opportunities to learn a physically intense sport. I have always enjoyed vigorous sports; for example, I run intensely on a treadmill at a nearby fitness club, reaching a target heart rate of over 170, three times a week.

Tennis is the most popular sport among university faculty and is the sport recommended by my colleagues. With the mindset that this would be the last challenging sport I take on, I chose tennis, known to be one of the most difficult racket sports. I also believed that learning tennis would allow me to deeply understand the value of racket sports through personal experience. As a pre-service teacher educator, I wanted to convey the value of education through sports to students and share insights on effective teaching and learning methods based on my own experiences. Although I had some concerns about how well I could learn and perform, I was also curious about how I would learn, understand, and gain insights from the challenging sport of tennis. I also hoped to support other beginners. Ultimately, I believe that learning tennis could positively contribute to teaching pre-service teachers regarding the value of tennis; thus, I decided to learn tennis with a positive outlook.

I had never properly learned popular racket sports. While I had taken courses in football, basketball, and volleyball at university, I had never learned racket sports such as tennis or badminton. Up until now, I had enjoyed participating in judo, track and field, and weight training. Having practised it for a long time, I have come to appreciate the value of sports. People around me often recommended racket sports, which is why I started to take an interest in them. Nevertheless, I lacked an understanding of the value of such sports, mainly because I had never experienced them first-hand. Therefore, I thought that if I could properly learn tennis, known to be one of the more technically demanding racket sports, it would be an opportunity to gain insight into another aspect of sports. This is how I began my journey with tennis. (Researcher’s self-recall questionnaire, 25 August 2022)

This quote illustrates the researcher’s past sports experiences and the motivation for learning tennis as part of daily life. The tennis learning process was accompanied by physical challenges and mental conflicts. One major difficulty is that tennis is considered one of the most challenging racket sports to master. Furthermore, the limited availability of tennis courts necessitated joining a club, raising concerns about the possibility of fully dedicating time and effort to learning the sport. However, despite being a non-contact sport, tennis remains highly competitive, while emphasising etiquette, often earning it the reputation of a “gentleman’s sport”. Thus, the positive aspects of playing tennis were expected to outweigh any potential difficulties. This conviction led to the decision to begin playing tennis to experience the value of learning through recreational sports activities.

Engaging in recreational sports activities enhances physical health, fitness, and positive social interactions in everyday life (Wendel-Vos et al., 2004). More importantly, understanding the value of recreational sports can encourage greater participation in sports as daily routines (Ko, 2022). This study examines the significance of recreational tennis. A review of previous research led to the identification of two key perspectives. First, participating in recreational sports improves physical health, fitness, situational decision-making, confidence, and self-efficacy (Rodríguez-Bravo et al., 2020). Second, from an emotional perspective, it fosters a positive attitude towards life, enhances social interactions, and broadens perspectives on sports (Lazarus, 2000; Tsai, 2018). These insights provided a foundation for examining the value of sports participation. Among the various emotional aspects experienced in recreational sports, particular attention was given to positive psychology. The reason is that my challenge to learn tennis was not an easy choice; I proceeded with the possibility of encountering difficulties and failures. Therefore, I wanted something to rely on while identifying the positive factors affecting my emotional changes.

Positive psychology examines how positive emotions shape an individual’s life, emphasising the role of personal strengths, life transitions, and achievements in fostering well-being (Seligman & Csikszentmihalyi, 2000; Seligman et al., 2006; Seligman, 2002). Additionally, positive social interactions are recognised as a key source of happiness (Rashid, 2015). From this perspective, this study explores the value of sports participation through the framework of positive psychology.

Individuals engaged in recreational sports clubs often encounter various challenges and conflicts. However, by drawing on their strengths and virtues to cultivate positive emotions, they can mitigate negative experiences, build resilience, and enhance their social connections (Rashid & Seligman, 2014; Rashid, 2015). Positive psychology posits that full engagement in sports activities fosters a sense of meaning, emotional well-being, social connectedness, and personal accomplishment, ultimately enhancing overall well-being (Jackson & Csikszentmihalyi, 1999; Seligman, 2011). Examining recreational sports through positive psychology can provide valuable insights into the sources of positive energy and meaning in leisure sports participation.

Positive psychology also aligns with the concept of authentic happiness (Seligman, 2002), which posits that individuals attain personal fulfilment by experiencing true joy and vitality while cultivating their signature strengths and virtues meaningfully. Applying personal strengths through sports participation enhances motivation, engagement, and the pursuit of excellence (Peterson & Seligman, 2004). Thus, identifying and utilising one’s signature strengths in recreational sports can foster vitality and overall well-being (Seligman et al., 2006).

This study aims to explore how participation in a tennis club in Korea contributes to a beginner player's learning, growth, and well-being, drawing on positive psychology constructs such as strengths, resilience, hope, and meaning. To address this aim, the study employs an autoethnographic approach to examine the meaning and value of the researcher’s tennis experiences and to analyse how recreational tennis participation supports well-being from a positive psychology perspective, with particular emphasis on positive emotional experiences rather than difficulties or negative outcomes.

This study highlights the importance of self-reflection in sports participation, encouraging individuals to deepen their understanding of their experiences, strengths, and motivations for continued engagement. Learning tennis is not merely about mastering technical skills but serves as a medium for social interaction and self-discovery, examining the connections between the individual, the sports community, and the learner-instructor relationship. Ultimately, the study explores the value of learning and growth in beginner tennis players through the lens of positive psychology, offering insights that may benefit beginners embarking on their sports journeys.

Characteristics and culture of Korean tennis clubs

Tennis is a highly popular recreational sport among club members in Korea. It gained much attention in Korea as a non-contact sport, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic. The number of novice players in their 20 s to 40 s joining tennis clubs has increased, and various types of courts, such as indoor courts and unmanned courts, are steadily increasing in number (Lim & Kim, 2022).

Thus, tennis is establishing itself as a popular recreational sport in Korea, with participation from a wide range of age groups, and has been evolving into a national sport. Such positive changes should be sustained in the future. To this end, an effective understanding of the cultural characteristics of tennis clubs must be gained. A representative characteristic is the “culture of respecting skilled players”. Unlike those in other countries, Korean tennis clubs often have limited court availability compared to the number of players, making doubles matches dominant (Lim, 2016). Tennis clubs typically consist of 20–50 members, with only 1–2 courts available per time slot; this necessitates careful team selection. Professional tournament matching systems are also widely applied. Club members, both male and female, are categorised into specific grades, and it is common for them to compete with players of similar skill levels.

While this system can be beneficial for highly skilled members, it may lead to feelings of discrimination and exclusion for novice players. Exclusion of beginners is common among clubs and is often considered a rite of passage for new members. Members with skill levels of A or B grade generally do not prefer partnering with beginners. They prefer playing against players of the same or higher skill levels. Typically, new members take over three years to compete with higher-level players. Lower-level players have limited opportunities to play with senior members. In reality, if there are two A-grade and two C-grade players, the A-grade players often prefer to practice among themselves. Tennis club members tend to feel uncomfortable playing against players with significantly different skill levels. Some skilled members even consider playing with or teaching lower-level players as a form of volunteering. Thus, Korean tennis clubs are characterised by a culture wherein high-level members pursue their tennis skills (Kang et al., 2010).

Doubles matches emphasise values of cooperation and consideration as partners must work together to succeed. However, when players of similar skill levels compete, mistakes are often treated lightly or overlooked. Meanwhile, matches involving beginners can lead to dismissive behaviour, conflicts of opinion, and adversarial relationships. Conflicts among tennis club members often arise during doubles games. Fundamentally, the culture of valuing skilled players is strong, making it difficult to find a culture that values doubles matches and team sports.

Consequently, those with lower skill levels are stressed simply because of their lower tennis abilities (Koo, 2008). This can lead to feelings of discrimination and exclusion among lower-level players. Exclusion of beginners is common in clubs and is often considered a form of bullying among new members. In sports clubs, new power structures can emerge depending on environmental and sociocultural contexts (Kim & Lim, 2024). Therefore, tennis club members sequentially experience the entry barrier stage, immersion stage, and stabilisation stage within clubs (Kim & Jeong, 2024). While long-term participation in a club is important, skill advancement is key. Thus, the culture of Korean tennis clubs is heavily skewed towards negative aspects such as amateur tournament formats and invisible power structures within clubs. Despite being composed of people who love tennis, the entry barrier to participating in matches is high without possessing tennis skills. As a result, many people tend to join clubs specifically for beginners or with criteria such as the same age group. This suggests the formation of another club culture counteracting the current club culture.

The tennis club culture I have experienced is notably different. Although I received recommendations from others, I chose a club that supports beginners in participating in matches, encourages growth, and pursues fair play, as opposed to following invisible orders enforced by power. A club culture dominated by power is fundamentally flawed and does not align with the purpose of club operations. A club culture that discriminates based on power or skill is highly undesirable. Therefore, this study aimed to explore the meaning of the process in which novice tennis players start exercising and grow through clubs formed with a positive culture. In particular, through a socially constructed understanding of the club, this study sought to examine what beginners experience and how they grow through their unique positive mindset.

Methodology

Understanding autoethnographic research

Autoethnography is a qualitative research methodology that integrates elements of autobiography and ethnography, allowing the researcher to explore and interpret personal experiences within a broader sociocultural context (Pak et al., 2010; Wall, 2006). This study applies an autoethnographic approach to narrate the researcher’s journey of learning tennis for the first time. The process involved joining a tennis club, training with fellow members, and documenting the positive values derived from these experiences. In particular, the study examines personal growth through participation in recreational sports, as well as the social values experienced within a tennis club, from the perspective of physical education.

A key feature of autoethnographic research is its ability to evoke emotions in readers, prompting self-reflection and critical engagement with their experiences (Humphreys, 2005). Thus, this research centres on sharing personal, factual experiences in a way that fosters empathy while ensuring credibility and validity (Chang, 2007). To prevent distortion and to ensure an accurate representation of this personal story, the study employed a tennis journal and a self-recall questionnaire as primary data sources. The tennis journal was meticulously recorded with maximum detailed descriptions, as recalling past events through text alone can be challenging over time. Additionally, specific names, dates, and locations were documented to aid in memory recall and enhance the reader’s ability to visualise the setting as if experiencing the events first-hand.

Autoethnography follows a structured qualitative research process that involves data collection, analysis, interpretation, and writing (Kim & Lee, 2011). Within this framework, accurately conveying the meanings and values embedded in the study is essential. Therefore, the research process and objectives must be clearly articulated through writing (Pak et al., 2010). In this context, this study employs a combination of realist accounts and confessional writing styles, as outlined by Van Maanen (2006). This approach facilitates the description of an honest and reflective narrative of the learning journey, detailing skill development, social interactions within the tennis club, and the values gained through these experiences.

Further, the perspective of positive autoethnography (PosAE) was applied. PosAE is a practical autoethnographic approach that encourages reflection on one's positive experiences and optimistic future outlooks (Brauer et al., 2021). In this study, the positive expression of self-reflection in the positive autoethnographic method was described according to the following three criteria (Gonot-Schoupinsky et al., 2023). First, the content related to the knowledge the author acquired while learning tennis was presented to enhance epistemological relevance. Second, the values and meanings obtained by applying positive psychological insights to the author's reflections and narratives are explored. Above all else, efforts were made to include the value of positive reflexivity as a foundation for positive autoethnography (Hoare & Gonot-Schoupinsky, 2024). Third, the potential benefits and values that readers can derive from the positive psychological insights experienced by the author are discussed. Specifically, the process of sports skill improvement experienced by the researcher while first learning tennis, and the values gained by joining a club and exercising with its members, are factually described and discussed from a positive psychology perspective. Through this approach, researchers hope to understand and navigate difficulties from a positive perspective and to share rich, deep insights (Gonot-Schoupinsky et al., 2024).

Effort was made to present the perspective of a beginner rather than that of a physical education teacher. This study captures various emotional responses encountered during the skill acquisition process, including moments of frustration, conflicts with the coach, communication difficulties with club members, and experiences of encouragement and praise. While these aspects may reveal challenging or less desirable elements of the learning process, they are essential in providing an authentic and vivid account of personal growth. By documenting positive and negative experiences, this study offers a comprehensive and reflective narrative, illustrating the developmental trajectory of a novice tennis player. Autoethnography enables researchers to share rich, in-depth insights that help individuals understand and navigate challenges from personal and cultural perspectives (Ellis & Bochner, 2000; Matthews, 2019). This characteristic of autoethnography motivated the discussion herein of the positive and negative aspects of learning tennis as a beginner without hesitation or omission.

Finally, this study provides valuable guidance for individuals embarking on their tennis or sports journeys. The purpose of autoethnographic writing is to share personal experiences in ways that benefit readers, offering them insights and direction (Bochner, 2013). Therefore, this study adheres to the principles of autoethnographic storytelling, ensuring that the learning process and the inherent value of these experiences are conveyed in a clear and relatable manner. As the researcher’s personal journey forms the core of this study, multiple rounds of revision were undertaken to refine the chronological and thematic organisation of the narrative. Consequently, this paper presents a vivid and realistic account of the learning experience, capturing the essence of acquiring tennis skills from a beginner’s perspective.

Data collection

Data were collected from the researcher’s tennis journal and self-recall questionnaire. These data sources encompass experiences from tennis lessons and matches between August and December 2022, and between March and October 2023.

The tennis journal documented positive emotions experienced while participating in tennis, areas for improvement, and problem-solving reflections. It also included observations on interactions with club members, the experiences and significance of playing doubles, and related reflections. Specifically, the journal was structured into functional, strategic, and attitudinal aspects. The functional aspect was distinguished between successful performance and areas needing improvement. Additionally, the journal captured perceptions of game partners and the broader meaning of engaging in recreational sports through tennis.

The self-recall questionnaire was the major source for this study, capturing past experiences in written form (Pak et al., 2010). The questionnaire was supplemented by additional materials such as the tennis journal, personal notes, conversations with club members, photographs, and messages, all of which aided in recollecting and documenting past experiences. Through these materials, the researcher examined the positive values derived from participating in tennis, explored the interconnections among positive aspects of the experience, and assessed the broader impact of positive emotions on daily life.

Data analysis and trustworthiness

The collected data were analysed using domain and classification analyses, as proposed by Spradley (1979). The researcher repeatedly reviewed the data to identify recurring patterns and themes, extracting key terms related to the values embedded in recreational tennis participation. Subsequently, these terms were reorganised and categorised into overarching themes, refining the experiential components into a structured framework. For example, the statement, “In defensive situations, I prioritise maintaining appropriate court coverage to ensure that my partner and I can effectively manage our respective areas”, was first treated as raw data. Through domain analysis, this statement was classified within the broader domain of “skills and strategies in doubles play”. Subsequent taxonomic analysis further delineated sub-elements such as court coverage, spatial awareness, and cooperative movement with a partner. These sub-elements were ultimately integrated into the theme of “the process of embracing challenges and achieving transformative learning experiences”. This stepwise analytical procedure was systematically applied to all data to refine categories, identify patterns, and derive themes reflecting the meanings and experiences associated with tennis learning. As a result, three thematic categories were identified: “Embracing challenges and transformative learning experiences”, “Cultivating competence and self-efficacy”, and “Growing through reflection and introspection”. Throughout the analytical process, the researcher conducted multiple reviews to ensure the accuracy and trustworthiness of the data.

Specifically, peer debriefing was conducted with two PhDs in sports pedagogy to discuss potential issues arising during the research process. To enhance the study’s trustworthiness, triangulation was employed by cross-referencing data from peer review sessions, self-recall questionnaires, and the researcher’s tennis journal. This methodological approach helped mitigate potential biases in interpretation and thematic categorisation. Table I presents the findings from the data analysis.

Results: autoethnographic reflections on learning tennis

Given the autoethnographic nature of the study, the findings are narrated in the first-person perspective, allowing for a personal and immersive account of the learning experience.

Embracing challenges and transformative learning experiences

Developing focus on forest and trees: practising fundamental movements, techniques, and strategies

In the context of tennis, the tree represents individual fundamental movements, while the forest encompasses these movements within the broader strategic and contextual framework of the game. As a beginner, my initial focus was on mastering the trees—basic movement skills—before fully grasping the strategic aspects of play. This aligns with the foundational principles of skill acquisition, where technical proficiency is prioritised in early learning stages (Rink, 2014). During lessons and practice sessions with club members, I concentrated on executing forehand and backhand strokes with precision. A significant amount of time and energy was devoted to understanding and refining these basic movements. Seeking to enhance my technical understanding and physical execution, I intensively explored various instructional materials and analysed different techniques. Through this process, I came to appreciate the importance of shadow swings as a means of reinforcing muscle memory. One particular challenge I encountered was maintaining balance while returning fast-moving shots. However, as I incorporated shadow swing drills into my training, I received feedback that my swing mechanics had improved, and my balance had become more stable. This realisation underscored the value of deliberate, repetitive practice in developing technical skills and overall game awareness.

When learning a sport, I believe that mastering fundamental movements is essential for enhancing motor skills and understanding the basic principles of movement. Therefore, in addition to taking lessons, I actively sought ways to improve my skills by watching YouTube videos and reading tennis-related journals. To address my issues with swing mechanics and balance, I researched relevant materials to better understand movement principles. I also explored tennis magazines, where I read about the stroke mechanics introduced by professional players and experts. Through these media sources, I improved my stroke technique and acquired tactical knowledge, which broadened my overall understanding of tennis. (Excerpt from the tennis journal, August 26, 2022)

In doubles, I often lost points when the opposing net player intercepted my shots. To counter this, I experimented with adjusting my positioning on the court and incorporating lobs. When returning serve, I angled the face of my forehand racket downwards to generate spin and direct the ball towards the opponent’s forehand sideline. I also adopted a strategy of aiming down the middle, exploiting the space between the opponents. These experiences underscored the importance of adapting techniques and strategies to the specific dynamics of the match. Consequently, I could refine my strategic approach to doubles play.

When returning a shot, I focus on assessing my opponent’s positioning on the court. Against forehand strokes, I often target their backhand side or the middle of the court. I have learned that in doubles, aiming down the middle is particularly effective against less experienced players, as beginners tend to have limited lateral movement and often hesitate, expecting their partner to cover central shots. Defensively, I prioritise maintaining proper court coverage, ensuring that my partner and I effectively manage our respective areas. (Excerpt from the tennis journal, November 18, 2022)

Opening up to relationships: building bonds with others

Among recreational tennis club members, doubles is the most popular format, making interpersonal relationships with partners particularly valuable. As a beginner and a more advanced player, I initially declined, feeling that my skills were inadequate and concerned about holding him back. However, he reassured me that they were short of a player and encouraged me to play without pressure. With some hesitation, I agreed to join the match. We lost 6-4 because I made numerous errors and struggled with basic movements. Feeling I had let my partner down, I apologised repeatedly. However, instead of expressing frustration, he was supportive, saying, “You have good speed and instincts. With practice, you’ll improve quickly.” That match marked the beginning of my regular doubles at the club.

I feel that learning tennis has been truly beneficial. Beyond simply improving my skills, it has allowed me to connect with club members and extend these relationships into everyday life. In the evenings, I often share a drink with fellow members, engaging in conversations centred around tennis. They have even complimented my progress, noting that my shots have become more powerful and that I have improved my ability to respond to fast-paced balls. Through these connections, I have come to appreciate the social appeal of sports participation. (Excerpt from the researcher’s self-recall questionnaire, October 16, 2022)

Having experienced first-hand how an advanced player encouraged and guided me with constructive feedback, I realised that this supportive approach is something everyone should embrace. From a positive psychology perspective, recognising and reinforcing strengths through praise and encouragement enhances those strengths and fosters confidence and resilience in overcoming weaknesses (Seligman & Csikszentmihalyi, 2000). Through my tennis journey, I came to understand that respect and consideration for others—core principles of positive psychology—act as powerful motivators in sports participation. These values extend beyond the game, highlighting the importance of mutual respect between partners and opponents. Over time, my relationships with club members deepened, evolving from on-court interactions into genuine friendships, where we shared meals and formed mentor-mentee bonds. The connections built through tennis extended into everyday life, demonstrating how positive social interactions strengthen and sustain relationships (Koc, 2017). This experience reinforced the importance of acknowledging, learning from, and engaging with fellow players, emphasising the social and psychological benefits of sports participation.

Cultivating competence and self-efficacy

Enhancing communication: developing self-motivation and self-regulation

Through communication with fellow club members, I could overcome a slump where my progress had stagnated, and my skills showed little improvement. There were times when I felt frustrated during training, particularly when my performance did not meet my expectations. On several occasions, I found myself blaming my coach out of frustration. Nevertheless, my coach consistently emphasised the importance of self-regulation—the ability to manage emotions such as anger and stress when faced with challenges. Self-regulation refers to the capacity to remain composed and control one’s emotions in difficult or unfavourable situations (Vallerand et al., 1997). Club members played a crucial role in helping me navigate these difficulties. They provided feedback by recording my training sessions, allowing me to analyse my movements, and encouraging me to push through the struggles. Their support had a profound impact, reminding me that even in difficult moments, maintaining a calm and thoughtful approach was essential. Over time, I made a conscious effort to reflect before reacting to any situation.

Sessions with my coach posed numerous challenges and moments of frustration. It was exhausting to return my coach’s balls for 20 minutes straight, but what frustrated me even more was my inability to control my shots, as the ball often flew in unintended directions. Even in actual matches, I struggled to direct my shots as planned. Each time I felt I was failing, the thought of quitting crossed my mind. However, my coach never hesitated to offer constructive advice, and my fellow club members encouraged me by acknowledging my progress. Through these experiences, I came to realise that sports are not just about physical strength and technical skills—the ability to manage emotions and regulate frustration is equally important. (Excerpt from the tennis journal, July 25, 2023)

Interactions with club members allowed me to develop self-regulation skills and, more importantly, enhanced my self-motivation to train harder. Self-motivation is closely linked to one’s ability to communicate with others, fostering passion, adaptability, and a problem-solving mindset (Bandura & Schunk, 1981). This ability enables individuals to take on challenges and strengthens perseverance and determination to persist through difficulties (Seligman, 2002). Achieving structured and professional growth in sports is rarely an individual endeavour. Collaboration with others is essential, particularly in sports that require teamwork. Communication with fellow players allowed me to reflect on challenges, find ways to overcome them, and take proactive steps towards improvement. Ultimately, through tennis, I could identify and address my weaknesses, growing with the support and guidance of my coach and club members. Thus, I was able to feel a sense of competence and participate more actively in tennis.

Strengthening resilience: persistence and the ability to endure challenges

When learning tennis, I was worried that I might not be able to perform at the level expected of me as a physical education major. I was also concerned that my mistakes in matches could strain my relationships with my doubles partners. For several months, these anxieties weighed on me, and I found it difficult to ease the pressure of competition. After lessons, beginner club members from the adjacent court would invite me to play a game. Each time, I avoided the situation, saying that I was in the early stages of learning and not yet ready for matches. I was also aware of the judgmental gazes of other club members, which made the idea of participating even more daunting. However, I eventually realised that I could not continue approaching the sport with this fearful mindset. Determined to change, I decided to focus entirely on my progress. I remained committed to my lessons and practice sessions, actively participated in games, and thoroughly reflected on my weaknesses. Letting go of my fear of others’ opinions, I concentrated solely on my growth. Whenever I played, I found myself habitually apologising, repeatedly saying “sorry” out of nervousness. Nevertheless, over time, my confidence improved, and I gradually felt more at ease. After about six months, I noticed a significant improvement in my shot accuracy and overall performance.

Looking back, I have come to appreciate just how challenging it is to engage in a sport and persist despite obstacles. Patience and perseverance are essential. Encouragement and praise from others are invaluable, but simultaneously, critical looks and judgmental gazes over my mistakes are inevitable—just as perfection is unattainable. If these negative experiences accumulate, they can lead to conflicts, heightened anxiety about participation, and ultimately, giving up altogether. However, I realised that overcoming such challenges ultimately depends on my mindset. For a long time, I had been overly conscious of others’ opinions, which was mentally exhausting. Nonetheless, by enduring, persevering, and continuously participating, I eventually recognised tangible improvements in my abilities. Patience and perseverance are fundamental forces that open up new possibilities, enabling individuals to persist and overcome difficulties without succumbing to pressure. (Excerpt from the researcher’s self-recall questionnaire, August 13, 2023)

Through this experience, I came to appreciate the value of consistency and persistence in training. I learned that sustained effort allowed me to overcome fear and self-doubt. In the past, whether facing physical or psychological difficulties, I tended to give up easily. However, this time, I remained fully committed to my development. To achieve this, I focused on technical refinement, identified weaknesses in my gameplay, and worked intensively to improve them. Most importantly, I did not allow the fear of judgement to deter me, nor did I let myself give up. Instead, I chose to confront my fears head-on. Regular lessons with my coach and matches with club members played a crucial role in my progress. Their feedback and advice helped me pinpoint mistakes, explore solutions, and develop a deeper understanding of tennis.

Growing through reflection and introspection

Building self-confidence: strengthening belief in personal competencies

Throughout my training, I remained fully focused on my lessons and practice. Before each session, I practised alternating between forehand and two-handed backhand groundstrokes, which helped me find the sweet spot on the racket. I developed greater confidence in my two-handed backhand than in my forehand. With the forehand, even a slight increase in power could easily cause the ball to sail long or disrupt my technique. The two-handed backhand, however, provided greater stability, allowing me to clear the net and land the ball deep in the court with control. Recognising this, I dedicated even more of my practice time to improving my two-handed backhand. As my training progressed, so did my confidence, and I began to feel I could continue refining my technique. From then onwards, I also focused on my swing motion. A well-executed swing is essential for generating power and accuracy in a stroke. Proper swing mechanics contribute to improved balance and precise ball impact, leading to better overall performance. To refine my technique, I practised swinging while looking in the mirror. This mirror swing exercise allowed me to observe my body rotation and movement, making necessary adjustments to improve fluidity. As I continued this practice, I gradually gained confidence in my stroke.

The sense of accomplishment I felt when I successfully executed a two-handed backhand was indescribable. It was particularly rewarding because I had never used both hands on a racket before, making each successful stroke feel like a personal achievement. Watching my movements in the mirror, analysing them, and making corrections significantly contributed to my progress. The moment I succeeded, I felt as if my hard work had been rewarded. Through this experience, I realised that the satisfaction of overcoming a challenge and achieving success is something that can only be fully understood when experienced first-hand. Moving forward, I will continue to challenge myself and push my limits, knowing that with persistent effort and dedication, I can strengthen my self-confidence. (Excerpt from the tennis journal, October 23, 2022)

Seeing forest and trees: reflecting on skills and strategy application

Throughout my lessons, I made a conscious effort to apply fundamental motor skills. After each session, I worked to incorporate what I had learned into actual gameplay. In the early stages, even returning the ball was a challenge. I could not yet focus on placing the ball strategically within the opponent’s court—I was simply trying to make clean contact with the ball on my racket. Even after months of practice, noticeable progress was slow to appear. However, at a certain point, I realised that I was making cleaner ball contact more consistently and with less effort, and my shot accuracy had improved. This was when I truly began to understand the extent of difficulty involved in tennis. As time went on, I started setting new goals for myself. As my lessons progressed, I began to consider the possibility of competing in club tournaments. With approximately two years of training, I believed it might be within reach. Now, I find myself focusing on technical refinements and game strategies that best suit my playing style. I have reached a stage where I carefully observe, reflect on, and analyse my performance, seeking continuous improvement.

Among the various tennis skills, my strengths lie in strokes and movement. My weight training background has allowed me to develop consistent physical conditioning, which is essential in improving my technique. Of course, I would not yet consider myself highly skilled, but I recognise the importance of fitness in enhancing performance. The ability to move efficiently and stay active on the court is crucial for executing strokes in any situation. While my abilities have room for improvement, I believe that with continued effort, I will continue to progress. Looking back, many memories from my lessons come to mind. I recall walking onto the court for the first time, sitting nervously in one spot before my first lesson. I remember the difference between how I felt before and after each session, the first club member who invited me to play, and the wins and losses I experienced in matches. These recollections remain vivid in my mind. (Excerpt from the researcher’s self-recall questionnaire, June 20, 2023)

As I gained more experience in tennis lessons, I began to believe that with additional effort, I could progress beyond the beginner level. A growth mindset is the belief that one’s abilities can improve through dedication and perseverance (Dweck, 2009). Encouragement and positive targeted feedback from my fellow club members and coach reinforced this belief. This process enabled me to refine my fundamental skills and develop the confidence to take on new challenges (Rink, 2014). I now find myself transitioning from a beginner to an intermediate player. Just as I overcame challenges and developed a genuine enjoyment for tennis, I hope that my experiences will encourage newcomers to build confidence and self-esteem as they embark on their journey. Additionally, as a future educator, I aim to highlight the importance of mindset, teamwork, and individual roles in sports participation and personal growth.

Embracing continuous development: broadening perspectives on tennis

Through weekly matches with club members, I became familiar with their playing styles and patterns, which allowed me to anticipate their strategies and adjust my game accordingly. While this approach contributed to my technical improvement, I eventually realised that it was not the most effective method for long-term growth. Although regular lessons and club matches were enjoyable, I recognised the importance of testing my skills in different environments. Repetition of fundamental skills is crucial in sports, but those skills must also be effectively applied in game situations. I believed that playing against members of other clubs would be an opportunity to further develop my basic techniques in various match settings. Therefore, when some of my fellow club members visited the Dan Bang Tennis Club, I joined them and played matches against members of a different club. Each of them had their distinct tennis style, and I found it challenging to adapt to their way of playing. This was because I was a beginner and lacked experience playing against unfamiliar opponents. During the matches, I often felt flustered and made frequent mistakes, struggling to perform at my usual level. Despite my efforts, I could not demonstrate the skills I had worked so hard to develop.

Wanting to assess my progress and ability, I challenged myself to play against members of Dan Bang Tennis Club. However, I quickly realised that I was at the beginner level. Many of the players were highly skilled, and despite my efforts, I struggled to perform at my best. I felt embarrassed and apologetic to my doubles partner, frustrated that I could not showcase the skills I had worked so hard to develop. Ultimately, my inability to demonstrate my abilities reflected my actual level at that point. However, I gained one valuable insight—playing against unfamiliar opponents underscored the importance of being able to execute fundamental skills in various game situations. The experience reinforced the need for adaptability and the ability to apply my skills beyond the familiar environment of my regular training and matches. (Excerpt from the researcher’s self-recall questionnaire, October 27, 2023)

Competing against members of another club boosted my confidence and strengthened my desire to take on new challenges. This positive mindset allowed me to identify weaknesses in my game and motivated me to push myself further. Following that experience, I continued seeking opportunities to play against new opponents from different tennis clubs. Over time, my initial nervousness and hesitation faded, and I noticed significant improvements in my overall game performance. My fellow club members also recognised changes in my play, commenting on my enhanced ability to execute strokes in varied match scenarios and my improved awareness of open spaces on the court. Playing in different environments and against unfamiliar opponents proved to be an effective way to refine my skills, pushing me beyond my comfort zone. More than anything else, I experienced the power of sports as a means of connection—engaging with different players, exchanging knowledge, and building relationships through our shared passion for tennis. This experience has inspired me to explore and excel in other sports as well. To me, this is the most valuable lesson I have gained from learning tennis. Beyond personal growth, my role as a sports enthusiast and future educator is an opportunity to promote the value of physical education and help others cultivate a healthy and fulfilling life through sports.

Discussion

This study entailed analyses of the experiences and meanings related to the learning of a beginner tennis player. The first story is categorised as “Experiences of Challenge and Change”, the second as “Gaining Growth and Competence”, and the third as “Reflection and Introspection Through Tennis”. In this section, the process of experience and reflection by an individual learning tennis is analysed and interpreted from a positive psychology perspective.

First, as I started playing tennis, I was able to learn basic movement skills and develop a healthy body. I first learned the forehand stroke from the coach. Particularly, I found it difficult to understand the coach's instructions and felt that my body was not responding as expected. I struggled to comprehend the aspects of space, effort, and relationships, which are elements of body movement through tennis. During this time, the precise feedback from the coach based on accurate motion analysis and the encouragement from peers empowered me to continue participating diligently without giving up. Such repetitive practice and professional feedback align with the argument advanced by Ericsson et al. (1993) that the development of expertise is achieved through long-term deliberate practice. As a result, I was able to improve my stroke skills through various exercises that considered different situations, such as the speed and distance of the ball. I also learned how to quickly move my body in the direction of the incoming ball or lightly perform a split step to prepare in place (Cools et al., 2009). Practising basic skills also positively contributed to my physical development. Beyond technical improvements, frequent movement across the court while executing fundamental techniques contributed to physical fitness and endurance. Tennis has been shown to enhance muscular strength, endurance, cardiovascular fitness, agility, power, and coordination (Kovacs, 2006). Over the course of 15–20-minute training sessions, noticeable improvements were observed in speed, directional changes, and overall stamina. This underscores the link between movement skill development and overall physical well-being, which emerged as a key outcome of this study. These findings align with prior research indicating that recreational tennis fosters physical and psychological vitality, enhances athletic ability, and broadens strategic awareness in gameplay (Ko, 2016; Lau, 2010; Levin, 2008; Richards, 2013).

Second, by reflecting on tennis activities, I developed cognitive insights. Tennis has the characteristic of being a sport that is completed through the formation of basic motor skills and strategic movements (Barling & Abel, 1983). Effective strategic execution requires not only a solid foundation in fundamental skills but also continuous self-regulated learning, consistent with Ericsson et al. (1993) assertion that deliberate practice enhances professional performance levels. The game requires players to move dynamically across the court, respond to fast-paced shots, and strategically direct the ball into open spaces to gain an advantage. Successfully incorporating these strategic elements relies on a strong foundation in fundamental movement skills (Adolph & Hoch, 2020). Through consistent practice and repetition, players refine their technical proficiency and enhance their strategic awareness, both of which are essential for effective gameplay (Barling & Abel, 1983; Holfelder & Schott, 2014; Lee, 2016).

The previous section outlined the efforts involved in mastering fundamental techniques and applying strategically refined approaches within various contexts. Over time, notable changes emerged in technical execution and strategic decision-making. A key realisation was that enhanced stroke accuracy facilitated a better understanding of open spaces on the court, leading to more effective shot placement. During undergraduate studies, the researcher participated in various sports courses; however, the theoretical concepts emphasised by instructors were not fully internalised at that time. Conversely, first-hand experience in learning tennis provided an opportunity to internalise and apply feedback and theoretical knowledge acquired during formal instruction. Notably, the learning process in tennis did not follow a linear trajectory; for example, fundamental movement skills remained essential across all stages of development. This highlights the role of cognitive growth in strategic awareness as a crucial factor in athletic improvement. These findings align with Ko (2016), who described skill acquisition in tennis and golf as a cyclical process in which technical mastery, strategic growth, performance slumps, and breakthroughs occur in a pattern resembling a rollercoaster ride. Furthermore, this aligns with Fletcher and Sarkar (2013) model, which conceptualises psychological resilience as a dynamic, adaptive process that becomes particularly evident through repeated cycles of challenge and recovery during the acquisition of complex sport skills.

Third, by communicating with club peers, I developed a positive self-image. Covington (1992) mentioned in the self-worth theory that a student's motivation to achieve high grades is a positive self-image. My self-image was shaped by forming bonds with others through positive relationships and praise from peers. Bonds with others can be created through empathy and communication in team activities and interactions (García-Moya et al., 2019). In addition, these positive relationships are directly aligned with the core concepts of Self-Determination Theory (Ryan & Deci, 2000), which emphasise the fulfilment of the basic psychological needs of autonomy, competence, and relatedness as essential for fostering intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being. The tennis club that I joined emphasises a culture of mutual consideration and respect among its members. After matches, partners would praise and encourage what I did well rather than focus on my mistakes. As a result, I considered the club similar to a sanctuary wherein I could feel comfortable. The partners always supported me mentally when I struggled with training, had difficulty acquiring skills, or faced challenges in matches. From a positive psychology perspective, strengthening positive emotions helps individuals develop personal strengths, navigate life changes, and experience a greater sense of accomplishment (Seligman & Csikszentmihalyi, 2000; Seligman, 2002). These findings suggest that positive communication and relationship-building within the recreational tennis club deepened the researcher’s engagement with the sport while also contributing to emotional resilience and psychological well-being.

Fourth, the experience of improving tennis skills and succeeding in strategic execution developed into a life activity in which I could feel the charm of tennis and enjoy it. Competence is connected to positive psychological states such as intrinsic motivation, peak experiences, and flow (Waterman et al., 2008). Competence is experienced and enhanced through the subjective evaluation of one's performance or positive feedback from others (Deci & Ryan, 2012). This is consistent with Ryan and Deci (2000) Self-Determination Theory, which posits that interest, autonomy, and competence are core components of intrinsic motivation. I gained confidence from the coach's guidance and feedback and from the encouragement from fellow members. Moreover, I felt a sense of competence through successful experiences in skill and strategy execution. Autonomy, reflecting one's emotions and participating actively in physical activities, was also an important factor (Fishbach & Woolley, 2022; Gagné & Deci, 2005). Although autonomy is based on personal emotions and feelings, the role of others acts as an important cue (Niemiec & Ryan, 2009). In other words, the various emotions and thoughts such as joy, sadness, and regrets experienced while interacting with others in the club acted as a powerful mechanism for developing autonomy. This process helped enhance resilience, the mental muscle that aids in improving skills and relationships with others (Werner, 1995; Windle et al., 2011). Through this journey, I came to understand and enjoy tennis as a sport. Moreover, it became an activity that I engaged in to improve my quality of life rather than merely out of a desire to achieve something.

In summary, from the perspective of positive autoethnography, my experience learning tennis enabled me to build a healthy mind by understanding my character strengths, the importance of others, and the meaning of play and enjoyment (Proyer & Gonot-Schoupinsky, 2025). From a positive psychology perspective, I could practice the components of the Positive Emotion, Engagement, Relationships, Meaning, Achievement (PERMA) model (Seligman, 2018). Within the community of the tennis club, through lessons with the coach, personal practice, and matches with various people, I found my strengths, overcame difficulties, discovered joy, and found a turning point for new growth. Additionally, as a teacher educator, it provided an opportunity to explore ways to use various teaching strategies in helping pre-service teachers learn about the importance of overcoming challenges in sports skill training and fostering empathy.

Through positive relationships in the sport of tennis, I gained value in both external and internal growth as I took on new challenges. To reiterate, positive relationships with others were an important factor in my learning experience while learning tennis. It is anticipated that physical education in everyday life should pursue the value of physical education. As a physical education expert, I was able to effectively understand and learn the value of physical education. However, it is natural for non-majors to have no interest in the value of physical education. Nonetheless, they should be provided with opportunities to experience the true value of physical education.

Among these, an educational method is needed to pursue the value of physical education through relationships with others. Understanding the importance of physical education within a community and relationships with others would help them grow into recreational athletes who understand their strengths and limitations, feel the charm of physical education, and practice and spread its true value.

Regarding the limitations and future research directions of this study, it narrates my experience learning tennis through autoethnography. First, as a sports education major, I have experience learning various sports. However, learning racket sports within the group of a club was a first-time experience. I applied my expertise to present the physical, emotional, and affective values gained through tennis. This could help beginners minimise vague fears and difficulties by providing information on the strengths of tennis exercises, methods, mindset, and club culture.

The importance of relationship-building in club selection is emphasised herein. Future research could present another perspective on the positive factors of learning tennis. Exploring the cultural characteristics and values within various groups in clubs would be a good research direction. Recently, clubs have been formed on various themes, such as age, gender, and occupation groups. Conducting research through these groups would considerably benefit beginners from various backgrounds. Additionally, studying the process of beginners learning sports other than tennis could be intriguing. If the beginning and growth processes of a beginner's exercise are studied through various methods such as specialised positive autoethnography, psychology, and mental health autoethnography, it would be a good study to broadly understand the attributes of social relationship structures formed within sports culture and minority participation experiences (Bamberg, 2012; Butz & Besio, 2009; Greenhalgh & Hurwitz, 1999). Additionally, it would be valuable to explore studies that adopt methodological approaches such as positive autoethnography involving multiple participants or mixed-methods research designs.

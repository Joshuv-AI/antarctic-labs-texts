Why the lipid deposition in the muscle fibers and myosepta is selective in triploid rainbow trout? A lncRNA‒mRNA study
Authors: Qianwen Li, Falie Shi, Guoliang Sun, Fulei Wei, Zezhong Wu, Rui Ma, Yuqiong Meng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Salmonids can accumulate lipids in their fillets, creating marbled features with alternate red (Muscle Fibers, MF) and white stripes (Myosepta, MS). To investigate the regulation of this important quality trait, diets with low and high lipid levels were fed to the fish and found that high lipid diet significantly elevated muscular lipid deposition in MS but not in MF. Then, a whole-transcriptome analysis was performed and results showed that the mRNA expression of ACSL1 and GADD45A was downregulated by the lncRNAs MSTRG.19477.1 and XR_005039693.1, resulting in consistent lipid contents in the MF from both groups. The lncRNAs MSTRG.21618.1, XR_005034756.1, XR_002473790.2, XR_002472790.2, and MSTRG43906.1 increased lipid deposition in MS30 by upregulating the mRNA expression of ELOVL2, DGAT2, LCAT, etc. In conclusion, the present study revealed that selective muscular lipid deposition and several lncRNAs may play key roles in regulating the marbling features of rainbow trout.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12864-025-12208-2.

Introduction

The rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss), a carnivorous fish of the salmonid family, is one of the most widely farmed cold-water fish in the world. Triploid rainbow trout are currently the main commercial trout species cultured in China; after 2–3 years of cultivation in reservoirs, triploid rainbow trout have reached a market size of approximately 4 kg [1]. Similar to other salmonids, triploid rainbow trout fillets can accumulate high lipid contents and typically exhibit a marbling appearance characterized by red and white stripes. The “red stripes” are composed of muscle fiber (MF), and the “white stripes” are mainly connective tissue named myosepta (MS) [2]. Among them, MS is distinguished by an abundance of adipocytes, contributing to 40% of the total muscular lipid content in Atlantic salmon, and the remaining lipid is distributed in the myocyte in the form of lipid droplets [3]. Both MF and MS constitute the main edible parts of salmonid fish.

Extensive studies have shown that muscular lipid deposition determines the fillet appearance, texture, elasticity, flavor, and odor of both terrestrial and aquatic animals [4, 5]. Similar to the marbling score for terrestrial animals, the width of MS is also one of the most important appearance qualities for both trout producers and customers in China. However, as the MS is the main site of muscular lipid deposition [6], the regulation of MS had received little attention. Only one study had shown that the lipid composition of MS in Salmo salar could be affected by lipid sources [2], and the underlying reasons are far from elucidated.

Although muscular lipid deposition is important for fish production, its deposition regulation still needs further in-depth study. With the development of molecular biology technology, whole-transcriptome sequencing has played a major role in revealing molecular mechanisms. In recent years, a series of studies in terrestrial animals have identified several long-chain noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) that play decisive roles in the processes of adipogenesis and metabolism [7], indicating that lncRNAs may play important roles in the lipid deposition process. LncRNAs are a class of nucleotide sequences larger than 200 nucleotides in length that do not encode proteins or encode only protein micro peptides [8]. By binding to RNA, DNA, or transcription factors, lncRNAs regulate the expression of target genes through epigenetic modifications, transcriptional control, and posttranscriptional processes [9]. As an emerging field, lncRNAs have been recognized as important regulators of muscular lipid deposition in terrestrial animals. Ma et al. [10] reported that lncRNA BIANCR knockdown inhibited bovine intramuscular adipogenesis. Zhang et al. [11] reported that lncAD regulates the adipogenic differentiation and proliferation of chicken intramuscular preadipocytes. Sun et al. [12] reported that lnc_000414 inhibited the proliferation of porcine intramuscular adipocytes. However, very few studies have been conducted on fish. Xu et al. [13] and Cao et al. [14] reported that lncRNAs and mRNAs play important roles in regulating hepatic lipid metabolism or deposition in half-smooth tongue soles and rainbow trout, respectively [15]. Studies on lncRNAs in fish muscular lipid deposition remain unexplored and deserve further study.

Therefore, whole-transcriptome sequencing was used in this study to explore the potential lncRNA‒mRNA molecular mechanisms of muscular lipid deposition in triploid rainbow trout. These results can provide useful information for improving the quality of triploid rainbow trout fillets.

Materials and methods

The present study was performed strictly following the Standard Operation Procedures of the Guide for the Use of Experimental Animals of Qinghai University. Triploid rainbow trout were obtained from local fisheries (Qinghai, China).

Feeding trials and sample collection

To investigate the mechanism of muscular lipid deposition in rainbow trout, experimental diets with lipid levels of 20% (20) and 30% (30) were formulated, and 3000 adult triploid rainbow trout, each with an initial body weight of 3.2 ± 0.1 kg were randomly allocated to six net cages. Each cage was fed one of the two experimental diets for 11 weeks, resulting in two treatments with three replicates each, as described by Cao et al. [16] After the feeding trial, muscle samples from the MF and MS parts of the right fillets were dissected carefully, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80 °C for future analytical purposes. On the basis of the differences in the experimental diets (20 and 30) and tissues (MF and MS), four groups of muscle samples were collected and named MF20, MF30, MS20, and MS30, respectively.

Construction and sequencing of mRNA and lncRNA libraries

After total RNA was extracted from the samples, ribosomal RNA was removed to increase the retention of all coding RNA and ncRNAs, and the resulting RNA was then randomly fragmented. The starting strand of cDNA was synthesized using these fragmented RNAs as templates via random hexamer primers. The second strand of cDNA was subsequently synthesized via the addition of a buffer system, deoxynucleotide triphosphates (with dUTP serving as a substitute for dTTP), RNase H, and DNA polymerase I. Following synthesis, the second strand of cDNA was purified via the QiaQuick PCR Kit and eluted with EB buffer. Next, buffer, dNTPs (dUTP instead of dTTP), RNase H, and DNA polymerase I were added to synthesize second-strand cDNA, which was purified via the QiaQuick PCR Kit and eluted with EB buffer. Next, end repair was performed, an adenine base was added, and a sequencing connector was attached. The second strand was subsequently degraded via uracil-N-glycosylase (UNG). The sizes of the PCR-amplified fragments were then selected through agarose gel electrophoresis. The final sequenced library was sequenced via the Illumina HiSeqTM 4000 platform.

Identification of lncRNAs

The steps for identifying lncRNAs were as follows: (1) string tie reconstructed transcripts; (2) the length of the retained transcripts was ≥ 200 bp, and the number of exons was ≥ 2. (3) CPC2 [17], CNCI [18], and feelnc [19] were used to predict the coding ability of new transcripts, and the absence of coding potential at the intersection of these transcripts was considered a reliable prediction.

Analysis of differentially expressed transcripts

Differentially expressed transcripts encoding RNA and lncRNA were analyzed separately. Differential expression analysis of RNA and lncRNA was conducted via DESeq2 [20] software, comparing the two groups. If a gene/transcript exhibited a false discovery rate (FDR) of less than 0.05 and an absolute fold change of ≥ 2, it was considered to be differentially expressed. These differentially expressed coding RNAs were subjected to GO and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses.

Functional enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)

GO functional enrichment analysis of DEGs represents an internationally recognized standard for classifying gene functions. It categorizes gene functions on the basis of molecular function, biological processes, and cellular components. The KEGG database serves as the main public resource for conducting metabolic analysis and regulatory network research. In this study, P values were FDR-corrected to a threshold of FDR ≤ 0.05, and GO terms and KEGG pathways satisfying this criterion were defined as significantly enriched in differentially expressed genes.

Prediction of target genes for differentially expressed lncRNAs (DElncRNAs)

Neighboring target genes can be regulated by lncRNAs in cis or distant lncRNAs in trans. Genes displaying similar expression patterns are likely closely associated with specific biological functions. On the basis of this theory, the target genes and key lncRNAs were identified. To analyze the expression patterns, the expression levels of each lncRNA and mRNA were used to calculate the correlation coefficient (COR). The lncRNA‒mRNA pairs with a |COR|>0.95 and a p-value of < 0.05 were considered coexpressed. Additionally, genes located within a 10-kb window upstream or downstream of the lncRNAs and having a |COR | of the lncRNAs > 0.95 were considered cis-target genes.

Construction of a coexpression network linking lncRNAs and mRNAs associated with muscular lipid metabolism

The coexpression constructs were selected on the basis of rigorous evaluation of degree, correlation coefficient analysis, and GO and KEGG analyses, specifically targeting DEGs (|COR|>0.95) linked to muscular lipid metabolism and deposition. These constructs were employed to identify the key lncRNAs that are involved in regulating muscular lipid deposition. Using Cytoscape, a detailed lncRNA‒mRNA signaling pathway network was constructed to delve deeper into the specific roles of the identified key lncRNAs.

Validation of sequencing data through quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT‒PCR)

RNA isolation, cDNA synthesis, and qPCR procedures were executed following previous methods [21]. Total RNA was isolated from muscle samples of triploid rainbow trout via an RNAiso kit (Tiangen, China). The absorbance measurements at 260 and 280 nm were used to assess both the quality and quantity of the RNA. Quantitative RT‒PCR was conducted to measure the mRNA expression levels of genes in muscle tissue via the TB GREEN®Premix Ex T aqTMII (Tli RNaseH Plus) kit (Takara, Japan). The PCRs were conducted in a final volume of 10 µL, comprising 1 µL of cDNA, 0.3 µL (10 µM) of forward and reverse primers, 5 µL of PCRTaqMix (2×), and 3.4 µL of ddH2O. The thermal cycling conditions involved initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, followed by denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s, annealing at 60 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 1 s. To quantify the expression levels of lncRNAs, the lnRcute lncRNA qPCR Kit (SYBR Green) was utilized in a 10 µL PCR setup containing 1 µL of cDNA, 0.3 µL (10 µM) of forward and reverse primers, 5 µL of lnR lncRNA PreMix (2×), and 3.4 µL of ddH2O. The amplification protocol comprised initial denaturation at 95 °C for 5 min, denaturation at 95 °C for 5 s, annealing at 60 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 1 s. The 2−∆∆Ct assay was employed with specific primers for relative quantification of mRNA and lncRNA expression levels, and the analysis was carried out via the 2−∆∆Ct method for target gene expression quantification. Primers specific to the genes were designed using the relevant cDNA sequences of rainbow trout, including related lncRNAs, as a reference (Table 1).

Results

Muscular lipid deposition in fish fed the experimental diets

As shown in Fig. 1a, the lipid content did not significantly differ between MF20 and MF30 (P > 0.05). However, compared with that in MS20, the lipid content in MS30 was notably elevated (P < 0.05).

Analysis of the quality of RNA sequence data

In this study, 12 libraries were created via MF and MS from triploid rainbow trout fed diets containing two different lipid levels. After preprocessing and filtering the sequenced data, approximately 7.9–11 million clean reads were obtained from each sample. After excluding the rRNA-mapped reads, each group generated more than 42 million clean reads. On average, more than 39 million clean reads (93.28%) were aligned to the designated reference genome, and over 36 million (77.47%) clean reads were uniquely mapped. A total of 6,038 known lncRNAs and 2,137 reliable novel lncRNAs were identified (Fig. 1b). The novel lncRNAs can be classified into five major groups on the basis of their genomic location concerning protein-coding genes. (Fig. 1c): Intergenic region lncRNAs (1211), Bidirectional lncRNAs (35), Intronic lncRNAs (63), Antisense lncRNAs (104), and Sense lncRNAs (230).

Differentially expressed mRNAs (DEMs) and DElncRNAs

To explore the lncRNAs associated with muscular lipid deposition, the expression levels of lncRNAs in the MF and MS of triploid rainbow trout fed experimental diets were compared. A total of 752 DEMs (210 upregulated and 542 downregulated) and 99 DElncRNAs (32 upregulated and 67 downregulated) were identified between MF20 and MF30. Similarly, in the comparison between MS20 and MS30, 740 DEMs (420 upregulated and 320 downregulated) and 106 DElncRNAs (41 upregulated and 65 downregulated) were found (Fig. 2a). To investigate the function of DElncRNAs, the associations of the lncRNAs identified in this study with mRNAs were analyzed. There were 1 and 0 antisense-mRNA pairs, 5 and 1 cis-mRNA pairs, and 223 and 1,000 trans-mRNA pairs in the MF20 vs. MF30 and MS20 vs. MS30 groups, respectively (Fig. 2b-d).

GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of DEMs

The GO functional annotation results of the target genes of the DElncRNAs in the two comparison groups revealed that most genes were enriched in specific GO terms, including mainly cellular and metabolic processes (Figure S1). The GO terms related to muscular lipid deposition included lipid synthesis, lipid transport, and lipid metabolism. KEGG enrichment analysis of the MF20 vs. MF30 groups revealed that Fatty acid biosynthesis, Fatty acid degradation, the FoxO signaling pathway, the PPAR signaling pathway, and Adipocytokine signaling pathway were significantly enriched (Fig. 3a). In MS20 vs. MS30, DEMs were enriched in Glycosphingolipid biosynthesis and Steroid biosynthesis (Fig. 3b).

Information on the DEMs and DELs related to muscular lipid deposition of fish fed with the experiment diets

In MS20 vs. MS30, five DElncRNAs were coexpressed with 12 DEMs, among which MSTRG.21618.1, XR_005034756.1, XR_002473790.2, XR_002472790.2, and MSTRG43906.1 upregulated the mRNA expression of ELOVL2, DGAT2, LCAT, CES3, ABCB11B, NME4, APOA-I-2, APOM, DPYD, NPC1L1, SOAT2, and G6PC. In addition, only one lncRNA, MSTRG.45381.4, upregulated the mRNA expression of DBI. The correlation coefficient analysis method was used to construct a coexpression network of lncRNAs and mRNAs in an academic context (Fig. 4). The central positioning of lncRNAs within the lncRNA‒mRNA coexpression network suggests a key involvement in the modulation of muscular lipid deposition in triploid rainbow trout.

Discussion

The marbled feature (constituted by MF and MS) of the fillet is an important quality of appearance for salmonids. MS is an important component of muscular lipid deposition [6]. To investigate the regulation of this quality feature, the present study revealed that the fish fed diet with a 30% lipid level significantly increased the lipid deposition of MS, but no significant change in MF. This is an intriguing finding, as we would generally expect lipids to be evenly distributed within muscle tissues. However, this study revealed that muscular lipid deposition in triploid rainbow trout is selective and that the underlying mechanisms may be complex. A review of the relevant literature revealed that very few studies have focused specifically on MF and MS, but some have indicated that metabolic differences between MF and MS do exist [22]. MF primarily plays a role in muscle contraction and movement, while MS may be more involved in muscular lipid metabolism and deposition [4, 22]. However, the differences in the mechanism and nutritional regulation of MF and MS are still far from elucidated. Studies in terrestrial animals have suggested that lncRNAs play a determining role in intramuscular lipid deposition [23], suggesting that the differential expression of lncRNAs between MF and MS may contribute to the selective deposition of lipids.

This study represents the first whole-transcriptome analysis of lncRNAs associated with muscular lipid deposition in the fillets of rainbow trout. Since that lncRNAs regulate target gene expression through coexpression with their target mRNAs, potential “lncRNA‒mRNA” interactions might play important roles in muscular lipid deposition differences in MF and MS in triploid rainbow trout.

Since there is limited research on these lncRNAs, current analyses rely primarily on the known functions of the target genes to infer their potential roles. To understand the functions of the DElncRNAs, GO and KEGG enrichment analyses of the target DEMs were performed. In the MF20 vs. MF30 comparison, several genes involved in the lipid metabolism pathway were observed. ACSL1 was downregulated in MF30. ACSL1 is a key enzyme that catalyzes fatty acid activation, providing precursors for fatty acid synthesis or entering the β-oxidation pathway, thereby influencing the overall rate and extent of lipid metabolism [24–26]. This finding was in accordance with previous studies reporting that ACSL1 may play a crucial role in the synthesis of unsaturated fatty acids [27, 28]. In addition, a recent study revealed that ACSL3 is a novel GABARAPL2 interactor that links methylation and lipid droplet biogenesis [29]. GABARAPL2 was also downregulated in MF30, which suggested that ACSL1, which belongs to the same family as ACSL3, might interact with GABARAPL2, thereby influencing the lipid metabolic processes of MF in trout. The specific regulatory mechanism of lncRNAs remains unclear and requires further investigation. In addition, ST6GALNAC3 and GADD45A were also downregulated in MF30, which could be related to the restraint of fatty acid metabolism [30, 31]. The present study also revealed that RAB39B and GABARAPL2, which are involved in the autophagy process, were significantly enriched and downregulated in MF30; thus, we speculated that autophagy might be related to lipid metabolism and deposition. Similarly, Zhang et al. [32]. reported that a specific autophagic process, lipophagy, plays a key role in the regulation of cellular lipid metabolism and the maintenance of intracellular lipid homeostasis by selectively recognizing and degrading lipid droplets. The specific functions of the autophagy-related genes RAB39B and GABARAPL2 and lipid deposition deserve further research. Overall, the downregulated lncRNAs might downregulate the expression of their target mRNAs, resulting in a consistent lipid content of MF in triploid rainbow trout fed the experimental diets.

Compared with MS20, MS30 resulted in significantly greater lipid contents and several pairs of upregulated lncRNA-mRNAs. The ELOVL2 and DGAT2 genes play important roles in the process of lipid metabolism. ELOVL2 is an elongase that participates in the production of long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids, and DGAT2 is a key enzyme that catalyzes the synthesis of triacylglycerol. The present study revealed that lncRNAs, such as MSTRG.21618.1, XR_002473790.2, MSTRG.43906.1, XR_005034756.1, and XR_002473790.2, which respond to dietary lipid levels, upregulated the gene expression of ELOVL2 and DGAT2, directly leading to increased lipid deposition in MS30. Previous studies in fish reported that by influencing the elongation of long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids, ELOVL2 and DGAT2 regulate the synthesis of lipids in fish muscle [33, 34]. These results indicate that lncRNAs may play essential roles by regulating the gene expression of ELOVL2 and DGAT2, leading to lipid deposition in rainbow trout fillets. In addition, cholesterol metabolism also seems to be related to lipid deposition in the MS of rainbow trout. SOAT2, LCAT, CES3, ABCB11B, NME4, APOA-I-2, and APOM were upregulated by their co-expressed lncRNAs in MS30. SOAT2 is responsible for the esterification of free cholesterol and fatty acids into cholesteryl esters; it affects the uptake and utilization of lipids in intestinal lipid metabolism and indirectly affects the transport of fatty acids [35]. LCAT is the only enzyme capable of esterifying cholesterol on plasma lipoproteins and plays a crucial role in promoting cholesterol transport; its activity in plasma can regulate the distribution and storage of lipids within tissues [36, 37]. CES3 is an enzyme involved in the metabolism of fatty acids and cholesterol esters. In mammals, these enzymes have been shown to play important roles in lipid digestion, absorption, and metabolism [38]. ABCB11B has also been found to be associated with cholesterol transport processes in the liver and intestine of marine scleractinian fish [39], and NME4 has been found to play an important role in cardiolipin signaling and mitochondrial autophagy [40]. APOA-I-2 encodes an important apolipoprotein that plays a regulatory role in lipid transport and storage during lipid metabolism [41]. In addition, DPYD, APOM, and NPC1L1 are also involved in steroid metabolism and lipid transport; thus, cholesterol metabolism and lipid transportation may play a role in the muscular lipid deposition of fish fillets.

In addition, the PPAR signaling pathway seemed to also be regulated by lncRNAs since increased expression of DBI and APOA-I-2 was observed in MS30. DBI activates PPARα activity by binding to fatty acids, thereby regulating fatty acid catabolism [42]. However, APOA-I-2 regulates lipid synthesis and storage through its interaction with PPARγ [43]. Therefore, DBI and APOA-I-2 may work together to regulate lipid metabolism and deposition by activating or inhibiting different PPAR isoforms (such as PPARα and PPARγ). In addition, G6PC mRNA expression was also upregulated by the same five lncRNAs as other genes, indicating that gluconeogenesis and lipid deposition share the same regulatory pathway.

A lncRNA‒mRNA network was constructed to investigate the systematic mechanisms of muscular lipid deposition in rainbow trout. The MSTRG.19477.1/XR_005039693.1-ACSL1 pair may be the key factor in regulating lipid metabolism in the MF of triploid rainbow trout fillets. In MS20 vs. MS30, we identified four key lncRNAs, namely, XR_002473790.2, MSTRG.43906.1, XR_002473790.2 and XR_005034756.1, whose genes, including ELOVL2, DGAT2, SOAT2, and APOA-I-2, respectively, were upregulated, thereby controlling lipid synthesis, transport, and the PPAR signaling pathway.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study provides the first evidence of selective lipid deposition in muscle fibers and myosepta of triploid rainbow trout, driven by distinct lncRNA-mRNA regulatory networks. High-lipid diets significantly enhanced lipid accumulation in MS but not MF, highlighting tissue-specific metabolic regulation. In MF, downregulated lncRNAs (e.g., MSTRG.19477.1, XR_005039693.1) suppressed ACSL1 and GADD45A expression, maintaining stable lipid levels despite dietary lipid variations. In MS, upregulated lncRNAs (e.g., MSTRG.43906.1) promoted lipid synthesis and transport by activating the mRNA expression of ELOVL2, DGAT2, APOA-I-2, and cholesterol metabolism genes (SOAT2, LCAT), facilitating lipid deposition. These findings reveal that lncRNAs orchestrate lipid partitioning by modulating key pathways such as PPAR signaling, fatty acid elongation, and glycerolipid metabolism. This work establishes a molecular foundation for understanding marbling traits in salmonids and offers novel targets for improving fillet quality through dietary or genetic strategies.

Testicular development, sexual maturation, and seasonal variation in spermatogenesis of Raccoons in Wakayama Prefecture
Authors: Makoto ASANO, Yukiko KATO, Kazuo SUZUKI
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigated the timing of puberty and seasonal changes in spermatogenesis in raccoons (n=156) from Wakayama Prefecture. In the 0-year class, testis size, weight, and seminiferous tubule diameter increased in winter, although only a few raccoons were sexually mature. Most raccoons in the 1-year class attained sexual maturity by autumn–winter of their second year. In the ≥2-year class, testicular activity regressed during summer. Spermatogenesis scores were used to assess age-dependent spermatogenic development. Logistic regression indicated that testes with epididymides ≥19.1 mm in size and ≥9.6 g in weight corresponded to a 50% predicted probability of the presence of morphologically mature spermatozoa. These findings provide region-specific indices of male reproductive cycles in raccoons.Keywords: raccoon, seasonal variation, sexual maturity, spermatogenesis, testicular development

The raccoon (Procyon lotor) is native to North America [24], however, in Japan the species first became established in Gifu Prefecture after raccoons escaped from Aichi Prefecture in 1962 [1]. Subsequent releases and escapes of raccoons imported as pets in various regions accelerated their spread, enabling a rapid expansion of their distribution. Currently, raccoons are naturalized in 46 of Japan’s 47 prefectures, with Okinawa Prefecture being the only exception [11].

Raccoons are seasonal breeders [24]. Studies conducted within their native range in North America have reported geographical variations in male reproductive parameters. In some regions, males reach sexual maturity at 1 year of age, as reported in North Dakota [4] and Illinois [20]. In contrast, spermatogenesis in 0-year raccoons has been observed in Maryland [3], and successful reproduction in 0-year males has been documented in Illinois [17]. These findings indicate geographical variations in the timing of spermatogenesis and sexual maturation.

Because raccoons are designated as an invasive alien species in Japan, population control programs have been implemented across many regions. Evaluating the effectiveness of these programs to reduce the raccoon population requires an understanding of region-specific reproductive characteristics that directly influence population dynamics. Wakayama Prefecture in western Japan has recorded one of the highest cumulative numbers of raccoon captures nationwide [12]. However, research on raccoon reproduction in Wakayama Prefecture remains limited [21], particularly regarding histological investigations of males. Given that testicular morphometrics and spermatogenic status provide continuous, quantifiable indicators of reproductive condition throughout the year, this study focused on male raccoons. Our objective was to clarify the timing of puberty and the seasonal patterns of spermatogenesis in raccoons in Wakayama Prefecture through morphometrics and histological examination of the testes.

Testes with epididymides were collected from the carcasses of 156 male raccoons that had been humanely euthanized as part of the legally conducted control programs for designated invasive alien species between June 30, 2009, and August 25, 2010, in Tanabe City and four neighboring municipalities in Wakayama Prefecture (Table 1). After measuring testis size (TS) and testis weight (TW), both including the epididymides, the tissues were fixed in neutral-buffered formalin for histological examination. TS was calculated as the geometric mean of the length, width, and thickness. TW was recorded as the combined weight of a pair of testes and epididymides.

The age of each raccoon was estimated based on cranial suture closure [7], degree of femoral epiphyseal closure [19], root foramina closure of canines [5], tooth eruption [13], and cementum annuli counts of the canine teeth [5]. Based on field observations and reports indicating that raccoon births in Tanabe City occur mainly between March and May [21], the birth date of all raccoons was assumed to be April 1st. Based on this assumption, raccoons (n=156) were classified into three age classes: 0-year class (n=66), 1-year class (n=71), and ≥2-year class (n=19).

For histological examination of the testes, tissues were processed into paraffin blocks according to standard procedures and sectioned at a thickness of 4 μm. Hematoxylin and eosin staining was performed for 120 raccoons (0-year class: n=41; 1-year class: n=60; ≥2-year class: n=19) to observe the seminiferous epithelium and constituent cells and obtain photomicrographs. The long and short diameters of each seminiferous tubule were measured using ImageJ software (U.S. National Institutes of Health, Bethesda, MD, USA). For tubules with a long-to-short diameter ratio <1.3, the arithmetic mean of the two diameters was considered the tubule diameter. The arithmetic mean of the 20 tubule diameters was calculated as the mean seminiferous tubule diameter (STD) for each raccoon [22]. Seminiferous epithelial stages were classified by partially modifying previously reported five-category staging systems for dogs [16] and raccoons [8, 14]. In this study, the early immature and advanced stages were further subdivided into a six-category scoring system (spermatogenesis scores [SS]: SS-0–SS-5) to allow a more detailed evaluation of testicular development and spermatogenic activity. For each raccoon, the SS was determined based on the most advanced seminiferous epithelial cell type among the selected tubules within each section (Fig. 1). SS-0 was assigned to seminiferous tubules in which germ cells were not attached to the basement membrane; SS-1 to those containing spermatogonia attached to the basement membrane; SS-2 to those containing pachytene spermatocytes; SS-3 to those containing round spermatids; SS-4 to those containing elongated spermatids without spermatozoa; and SS-5 to those containing morphologically mature spermatozoa. Sexual maturity was defined histologically as the attainment of morphologically mature spermatozoa within the seminiferous tubules (SS-5) in this study.

Seasonal changes in TS, TW, and STD were analyzed for each age class (spring: April–June; summer: July–September; autumn: October–December; winter: January–March). Statistical analyses were performed using ANOVA followed by Scheffé’s multiple comparison test (P<0.05).

Logistic regression analysis was conducted to estimate the 50% threshold values of TS and TW associated with the presence (P=1) or absence (P=0) of morphologically mature spermatozoa within the seminiferous tubules (SS-5) in 115 raccoons (0-year class: n=41; 1-year class: n=56; ≥2-year class: n=18).

Clear age-dependent differences in sexual maturation were observed between the 0-year and 1-year classes. STD showed very strong correlations with TS (r=0.95) and TW (r=0.96), indicating that changes in STD occur in parallel with overall testicular growth. TS, TW, and STD (Fig. 2) did not significantly differ between spring and summer in the 0-year class; however, they increased significantly in winter (P<0.05), indicating rapid testicular development during the first year of life. From the winter of the 0-year class to the spring of the 1-year class, TS and STD exhibited the widest range of values. During this period, raccoons exhibiting higher TS and STD values were classified as SS-5, suggesting that these values reflect progression toward sexual maturation. Although body size parameters were not quantified in the present study, similar variability in the onset of puberty has been reported in Hokkaido [14], suggesting that maturation timing may vary among raccoons based on their body growth. Given the gestation period of approximately 64 days [24] and the frequent capture of pregnant females between March and May in Wakayama Prefecture [21], the mating season in this region is considered to occur mainly from January to March. Nevertheless, only a limited number of 0-year males (n=3/10) reached sexual maturity in their first winter, suggesting that most did not attain reproductive capability during their first breeding season, consistent with findings from Hokkaido [14]. In the 1-year class, TS, TW, and STD continued to increase significantly from summer to winter (P<0.05). The maximum monthly STD value in the 1-year class (0.261 ± 0.006, n=7) was recorded in November, and most raccoons examined between September and March were classified as SS-5. These findings suggest that nearly all males in the 1-year class reached sexual maturity by the autumn-winter period of their second year and that males complete testicular preparations for the breeding season by autumn, several months prior to the mating season in Wakayama Prefecture. This pattern appears to occur slightly earlier than that reported in Hokkaido [14], where most males begin testicular development in May of their second year and complete sexual maturation by the following winter.

Logistic regression analysis revealed that TS ≥19.1 mm (area under the curve [AUC]=0.997) and TW ≥9.6 g (AUC=0.999) corresponded to a 50% predicted probability of sexual maturity, defined as the presence of morphologically mature spermatozoa (Fig. 3). These values provide an objective, histology-based measure of sperm-producing capability in raccoons in Wakayama Prefecture and are therefore comparable in usefulness to the extrusibility of the baculum [10], which has traditionally been used as a criterion for sexual maturity in male raccoons.

After raccoons attained sexual maturity, testicular activity exhibited marked seasonal variations, particularly in those aged ≥2 years. In the ≥2-year class, TS, TW, and STD showed decreasing trends toward summer, and the summer values were significantly lower than winter values (P<0.05). These findings indicate seasonal suppression of spermatogenesis during the non-breeding season, as reported in other seasonal breeders, such as raccoon dogs [18] and Asiatic black bears [23]. Summer declines in raccoon testicular parameters have also been reported in Hokkaido [14, 15] and Kanagawa Prefecture [9]. Hokkaido is in northern Japan and has relatively cool summers, whereas Kanagawa and Wakayama Prefectures are in central to southwestern Japan and experience warmer and more humid summer conditions [6]. Despite regional differences in environmental conditions, the suppression of testicular activity in summer appears to be a reproductive and physiological characteristic that is common to male raccoons across regions. Additional studies in other regions, such as Kyushu, which is further south and has a warmer climate, would help clarify the geographic variations in the testicular cycle.

SS is a histological indicator that reflects the spermatogenic status at the time of capture and was used in combination with other morphometric indices to evaluate testicular development and spermatogenic activity. Histological features corresponding to all spermatogenic scores (SS-0–SS-5) were observed in this study (Fig. 1). However, in all raccoons in which elongated spermatids (SS-4) were identified, spermatozoa were also present in other regions of the same testis. Therefore, none of the raccoons were classified as SS-4. These SS-based findings corroborate and clarify the timing of puberty and the seasonal changes in spermatogenesis described above. Despite the seasonal suppression in spermatogenesis detected in the ≥2-year class, 15 of the 30 raccoons in the 1-year and ≥2-year classes were classified as SS-5 during summer (Fig. 4) and the presence of morphologically mature spermatozoa was observed histologically in these 15 individuals. This finding, together with the documented summer and autumn births in Hokkaido [2], Kanagawa Prefecture [9], and Wakayama Prefecture [21], suggests that some male raccoons retain their reproductive capabilities outside the mating peak. The presence of early-maturing raccoons in the 0-year class and the comparable reproductive status of the 1-year and ≥2-year classes may have contributed to the increase in the population of raccoons in Japan.

Finally, this study clarified the reproductive characteristics of male raccoons in Wakayama Prefecture using histological, age-specific, and seasonal analyses. Sexual maturity was rarely observed during the first year of life but was prevalent among raccoons by the autumn to winter of their second year. In mature raccoons, testicular activity exhibited clear seasonal variation, with regression observed during summer; however, morphologically mature spermatozoa were detected in some raccoons during this period. These findings provide region-specific indices of sexual maturation and seasonal reproductive cycles, thereby clarifying reproductive patterns of male raccoons in Wakayama Prefecture.

Although these results provide a coherent overview of male reproductive patterns in Wakayama Prefecture, several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, assuming a uniform birth date of April 1st may introduce uncertainty into age classification. Second, the SS-5 classification was based solely on the presence of morphologically mature spermatozoa without distinguishing the early, peak, or regressing phases of spermatogenesis. Future studies incorporating serum testosterone assays [8, 14, 15] and immunohistochemical evaluation of androgen and estrogen receptors—which show seasonal and stage-dependent variation—would further clarify the annual reproductive cycle of male raccoons.

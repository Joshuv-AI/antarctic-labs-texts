RNA-based co-transfer of human CD8αβ with WT1-specific TCRαβ redirects tumor recognition by CD4 and γδ T-cells towards MHC class I-restricted WT1 epitopes and boosts CD8 T-cell responses with or without CD3 mRNA
Authors: Diana Campillo-Davo, Donovan Flumens, Zwi N Berneman, Fumihiro Fujiki, Gils Roex, Maarten Versteven, Heleen H Van Acker, Viggo F I Van Tendeloo, Haruo Sugiyama, Sébastien Anguille, Eva Lion
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe evaluated the redirection of CD4, γδ and CD8 T-cells towards the Wilms’ tumor protein (WT1) tumor-associated antigen, using a major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I-restricted WT1-specific T-cell receptor (TCR) introduced via RNA-based engineering. We also studied whether co-transfection of TCR mRNA in combination with CD8αβ mRNA in CD4 and γδ T-cells or with CD8αβ and CD3γδεζ mRNAs in CD8 T-cells improves antigen-specific T-cell functional activity. We transfected primary human CD4 and CD8 T-cells following our in-house-developed protocol, in which electroporation with Dicer-substrate silencing RNA (DsiRNA) suppresses de novo expression of native TCR, followed by DsiRNA-resistant transgenic TCR mRNA transfection. This method allows minimal mispairing between native and introduced TCR chains. High frequencies of transgenic MHC class I-restricted WT1-specific TCR-positive cells were obtained in expanded CD4 and γδ T-cells. Only co-electroporation of CD8 mRNA led TCR mRNA-electroporated CD4 and γδ T-cells to MHC class I-restricted antigen-specific recognition of tumor cells. Co-electroporation of CD8 T-cells with WT1-specific TCR, CD8 and CD3 mRNAs also enhanced CD8 T-cell activation and antigen-specific recognition as compared to either TCR-engineered or TCR- and CD8-engineered cells. In summary, RNA electroporation is a fast and efficient method to engineer primary human CD8, CD4 and γδ T-cells for redirecting T-cell specificity. Transgenic CD8 expression in CD4 and γδ T-cells and co-electroporation of CD8 and CD3 mRNA in CD8 T-cells enable antigen recognition when T-cells are redirected with TCRs of low/intermediate avidity, showing the potential of TCR co-receptors to improve T-cell functional activity against tumor-associated antigens in adoptive TCR-T-cell therapies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40164-026-00764-6.Keywords: T-cell redirection, T-cell receptor, Non-viral engineering, Messenger RNA, T-cell coreceptors

To the Editor:

T-cell receptor (TCR)-mediated cancer therapy has focused on redirecting CD8 T-cells [1]. CD4 [2] and γδ T-cells [3] have gained attention for adoptive T-cell treatments, but lack the CD8 co-receptor, which otherwise modulates TCR activity by interacting with major histocompatibility complex (MHC) class I molecules [4, 5]. TCRs reacting against cancer-associated self-antigens, e.g. the Wilms’ tumor protein (WT1) [6], are of low/intermediate affinity following negative selection in the thymus [4]. We aimed to enhance tumor recognition by intermediate-affinity MHC class I-restricted WT1-specific TCRs, by expressing co-receptors linked to TCR activity, using non-integrative RNA-based electroporation [7] (Additional file 1: Methods). In this study we used mRNA encoding a CD8 T-cell-derived HLA-A*02:01-restricted WT137−45-specific TCR (T37), which has an intermediate functional avidity of 19.6 nM, as measured by the half-maximal effective concentration (EC50) against titrated peptide on the U266 cell line, which has a very low intracellular expression of WT1 [5].

Similar to our observations in CD8 T-cells [7], electroporation of Dicer-substrate silencing RNAs (DsiRNAs) directed against TCR alpha (TRAC) and beta constant (TRBC) regions of wild-type TCRαβ chains reduced the expression of native TCRs in CD4 T-cells (not shown). Following DsiRNA electroporation, CD4 T-cells were electroporated with the T37 mRNA alone or in combination with CD8 mRNA, showing a significant increase in T37 TCR mRNA levels (not shown). For γδ T-cells, a single electroporation protocol either with T37 TCR, CD8 or both mRNAs was performed, as γδ T-cells do not express a native TCRαβ and do not require a first electroporation of DsiRNAs against TRAC and TRBC to prevent TCRαβ mispairing. Surface expression of T37 TCR was detected in γδ and expanded DsiRNA-pretreated CD4 T-cells (respectively T37 and ST37 conditions); and became significantly detectable by CD8 mRNA co-electroporation (ST37 + CD8 condition) in resting and expanded CD4 T-cells (Fig. 1).

T37-engineered expanded CD4 and γδ T-cells were examined whether they recognize tumor cells in an antigen-specific manner. We compared the upregulation of the CD25, CD69, CD137 and CD154 activation markers [8] and the down-regulation of CD127, which are seen in CD4 T-cells after antigen engagement [9, 10]. Co-electroporation with CD8 mRNA in ST37-engineered CD4 T-cells (ST37 + CD8 condition) resulted in a significant increase of CD25 expression in co-cultures with HLA-A*02-positive WT137−45 peptide-pulsed T2 cells and Raji-A2 cells, and of CD69, CD137, and CD154 in co-cultures with WT137−45 peptide-pulsed T2 cells (Additional file 2: Figure S1A); in a significant downregulation of CD127 in response to WT137−45 peptide-pulsed T2 and Raji-A2 cells (Additional file 2: Figure S1B); and in a significant increase in granzyme B secretion, a surrogate marker for cell-mediated cytotoxicity, in response to WT137−45 peptide-pulsed T2 and Raji-A2 cells (Additional file 2: Figure S2). Co-electroporation of CD8 mRNA significantly increased the ability of T37-engineered γδ T-cells to specifically kill HLA-A*02-positive WT137−45 peptide-pulsed T2, U266, K562-A2 and THP1 tumor cells (Additional file 2: Figure S3). We conclude that de novo transgenic expression of CD8 in primary CD8-negative T-cells via CD8 mRNA electroporation leads to MHC class I-restricted antigen-specific recognition of tumor cells. This is consistent with previous studies showing that co-introducing CD8 increases the T-cell functionality of CD4 [2, 11, 12] and γδ T-cells [13], transfected with tumor-specific TCR of intermediate affinity.

Peptide-pulsed T2 but not Raji-A2 cells induced CD69, CD137 and CD154 in ST37 + CD8-redirected CD4 T-cells and cytotoxicity in T37 + CD8-engineered γδ T-cells (Additional file 2: Figures S1, S3). This is likely due to competition for MHC binding between endogenously processed and exogenous peptides in Raji-A2 but not T2 cells [5, 14]. Activation threshold by Raji-A2 cells is reached less easily due to insufficient WT1 peptide availability.

CD8 mRNA co-electroporation did not succeed in inducing a specific response in CD8-negative T-cells to non-peptide-pulsed tumor cells that intracellularly process and express WT1, e.g. HLA-A*02-positive WT1-transduced Raji cells (Raji-A2-WT1). Therefore, we assessed using our double sequential electroporation protocol [5, 7], whether transfection of DsiRNA- and T37-engineered CD8 T-cells (ST37 condition) with mRNAs encoding CD8 or CD8 and CD3 co-receptors (respectively ST37 + CD8 and ST37 + CD8+CD3 conditions (Additional file 2: Figure S4)) correlated with improved antigen-specific recognition of target cells. T37 TCR expression reached maximum levels in ST37-, ST37 + CD8- and ST37 + CD8+CD3-engineered CD8 T-cells, without significant differences among them (respectively 92.3 ± 5.6%, 88.9 ± 8.8% and 91.8 ± 4.7% (mean ± standard deviation)). Figure 2 shows that in the ST37 + CD8 condition, electroporation with CD8 mRNA significantly increased antigen recognition by ST37-engineered CD8 T-cells on peptide-pulsed U266 cells (increased CD137 expression), on peptide-pulsed Raji-A2 cells (increased CD137 expression and granzyme secretion) and on Raji-A2-WT1 cells (increased CD137 expression). In the ST37 + CD8+CD3 condition, co-electroporation of CD3 with CD8 mRNA showed an additional significant advantage over electroporation with CD8 mRNA on peptide-pulsed Raji-A2 and on Raji-A2-WT1 cells (increased CD137 expression). The ST37 + CD8+CD3 condition also showed a significant advantage not seen with the S37 + CD8 condition on unpulsed U266 (increased CD137 expression) and on Raji-A2-WT1 cells (increased granzyme secretion). We conclude that co-electroporation of CD8 and CD3 mRNA in TCR-redirected CD8 T-cells improves antigen-specific tumor recognition, being able to detect and respond to intracellularly processed WT1 epitope.

In conclusion, we provide a non-viral RNA-based protocol to increase the in vitro functional activity of TCR-redirected T-cells by upregulation of TCR co-receptors, highlighting their importance in the context of TCR-T-cell therapies. While it would have to be confirmed in vivo, this would have the advantage of using transiently expressed TCRs derived from natural repertoires against tumor self-antigens, in order to try and avoid unpredictable treatment-related toxicities, that have been described when using lentivirally integrated tumor-targeting affinity-enhanced TCR sequences [15].

There was WT137–45/HLA-A*02:01 tetramer staining in ST37 + CD8-electroporated cells in both resting and expanded CD4 T-cells (26.7 ± 15.1% and 68.4 ± 13.2%, respectively). In ST37 conditions, T37 TCR was only detected in expanded CD4 T-cells (21.9 ± 11. %). CD8 expression was confirmed in ST37 + CD8-electroporated resting (60.7 ± 30.2%) and expanded (93.5 ± 2.1%) CD4 T-cells, whereas no CD8 expression was detected in ST37 conditions.

An anti-human pan-TCRαβ antibody was used to analyze T37 TCR surface levels on γδ T-cells. T37 TCR was expressed at high similar levels in T37 (83.5 ± 11.2%) and T37 + CD8-electroporated (78.1 ± 14.8%) γδ T-cells. A small population of γδ T cells expressed CD8 (mock: 8.6 ± 5.2%; T37: 9.6 ± 6.7%), but as seen in CD4 T-cells, CD8 levels were greatly enhanced by CD8 mRNA transfection (CD8: 91.9 ± 4.2%; T37 + CD8: 89.4 ± 6.1%).

In terms of antigen-specific functionality, in both ST37 + CD8 and ST37 + CD8+CD3 conditions, CD8 T-cells significantly upregulated CD137 expression upon encounter of WT137–45 peptide-pulsed and WT1-expressing tumor cells compared to mock-electroporated and ST37-engineered CD8 T-cells (Fig. 2A). In co-cultures with WT1-expressing Raji-A2-WT1 tumor cells, ST37 + CD8+CD3-engineered CD8 T-cells showed a significantly higher upregulation of CD137 (36.6 ± 8.2%; Fig. 2A), compared to their ST37 + CD8 counterpart (23.5 ± 10.6%). In co-cultures with WT137–45 peptide-pulsed Raji-A2 cells, a significantly increased granzyme B secretion was observed in both ST37 + CD8- and ST37 + CD8+CD3-engineered CD8 T-cells versus mock-electroporated and ST37-engineered CD8 T-cells (Fig. 2B). Importantly, against WT1-expressing Raji-A2-WT1 tumor cells, ST37 + CD8+CD3-engineered CD8 T-cells (2.2 ± 0.9 ng/mL), but not those redirected with ST37 + CD8 (1.4 ± 0.6 ng/mL), secreted significantly higher amounts of granzyme B in comparison with their ST37 CD8 T-cells counterpart (0.8 ± 0.4 ng/mL; Fig. 2B).

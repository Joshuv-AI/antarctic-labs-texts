Nanobiosensors for Rheumatoid Arthritis Biomarkers: Advances and Clinical Translational Potential
Authors: Xiaoyong Zhang, Xuewu Song, Mingyue Chen, Yifei Lei, Jinqi Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article summarizes the development of nanobiological sensors in detecting markers such as serological antibodies related to rheumatoid arthritis(RA) and compares the performance of sensing platforms. In the laboratory, sensing platforms constructed with materials like gold nanoparticles have achieved detection sensitivities from femtomolar to picomolar levels, and some studies have initially verified their availability in samples. In the field of clinical transformation, electrochemical nanobiological sensors are expected to achieve point-of-care testing for RA soon, and optical detection can be used as a high-end platform in central laboratories in the medium term. However, most sensors are at the preclinical verification stage and face many significant challenges. Clinical transformation needs to address overall issues, such as sample pretreatment. This article also explores possible future trends to help provide technical and theoretical support for early diagnosis and treatment of RA.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a common autoimmune disease, characterized by synovitis in symmetrical polyarticular joints. This disease affects about 0.5% to 1% of the global population.1 In 2020, the burden of diseases in China was relatively prominent, with an age-standardized disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) rate of 58.61 per 100,000 people, exceeding the global average of 36.4 per 100,000 people.2,3 Clinical practice shows that standardized treatment in the first year after onset can slow down the process of joint structure destruction in 90% of patients.4 However, some patients cannot even detect routine serological markers in the early stage, or even for several years, resulting in delayed diagnosis and missing the best treatment opportunity.5

At present, RA clinical detection mainly relies on enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) and chemiluminescence immunoassay. Their specificity is acceptable, but there are situations such as insufficient sensitivity, complicated procedures, strict sample requirements, and difficulty in combining multiple markers for detection, which cannot meet the needs of early diagnosis and dynamic monitoring.6 Nano-biosensors can use nanomaterials to enhance signals and convert biological recognition into quantifiable physical signals, which can detect trace markers with high sensitivity, and have potential advantages in easy operation, response speed, and miniaturization.7,8 However, their clinical applicability still needs to be verified (performance comparison is shown in Table 1). Comparison of Performance Between Nanobiosensors and Conventional RA Detection Methods

In recent years, several reviews have focused on the application of nanobiosensors in autoimmune diseases. Angela et al summarized the potential of nanomaterials in the comprehensive diagnosis and treatment of RA, focusing on the overall performance rather than the comparison of different biomarker detection strategies.25 Soleimani et al focused on the detection of inflammatory mediators, did not specifically target RA, and did not deeply explore RA-specific biomarkers.26 Islam et al examined the progress of plasma biosensors in the detection of inflammatory biomarkers, focusing on the technical platform rather than the multi-marker detection strategy driven by the clinical needs of RA.27 Eghamberdiev et al focused on the electrochemical biosensor detection of anti-CCP antibodies, and the types of nanomaterials involved, and the range of biomarkers is relatively narrow.28

Due to the deficiencies in the existing reviews, this review systematically summarizes the principles and types of nano-biosensors according to the framework of “biomarker types-nano-material types-detection performance”, compares the detection performance and technical advantages of different platforms, explores the problems faced, and the future trends. It explores the differential characteristics of RA biomarkers in disease stages and patient subtypes, as well as their role in sensor design. This review, aiming at the actual needs of RA, initially comprehensively integrates and compares different nano-sensor platforms, thereby guiding the early diagnosis and treatment of RA.

Biomarkers of RA

Serological Markers

Serological markers are measurable indicators used to reflect the presence, activity, or immune status of RA by detecting specific substances in the serum. From the perspective of clinical application, RA serological markers can be divided into three categories: markers related to diagnosis, prognosis, and research.29 See Figure 1. Representative markers include rheumatoid factor (RF), anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPAs), anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibodies (anti-CCP antibodies), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and serum amyloid A (SAA). RF is a traditional diagnostic marker with a sensitivity of 68.6% and a specificity of 91.4%.31 ACPAs belong to the key markers for early diagnosis and prognosis of RA, and their specificity is in the range of 95% to 98%. These markers participate in the inflammatory response by means of complement activation and other ways.36 Anti-CCP antibodies may be positive as early as 14 years before the appearance of symptoms, which has special value for early diagnosis.30 Anti-MCV antibodies and anti-CCP work together to make the area under the curve (AUC) reach 0.932, and the sensitivity is 89.1%.37 About 20% to 30% of patients with RA are negative for RF and ACCP antibody (seronegative RA), which makes the traditional autoantibody diagnosis less reliable.38 CRP, such as an acute-phase protein, is a key index for disease assessment and prognosis prediction.32,33 Therefore, the design of nanobiosensors needs to incorporate biomarkers to improve the detection rate of seronegative RA.

The early clinical manifestations of RA are not obvious, and serological markers play an important role in early identification and prognosis assessment. RF and anti-CCP antibodies can be positive several years before the manifestation of the disease, so as to identify high-risk groups.39 About 20% to 30% of patients with RA are negative for both RF and ACCP antibody. The proportion of undifferentiated arthritis patients with positive SAA is 15.97%, which can be used as a supplementary diagnostic marker.38 Baseline CRP, ACCP, and complex protein can predict radiological joint damage in 5 to 10 years.34 The RA biomarker spectrum is diverse in different periods and subtypes, and a single marker is difficult to fully reflect.37–39 The joint detection of multiple serological biomarkers can help the precise classification and personalized medicine of RA.

Inflammation-Related Markers

The degree of inflammation in the body and the activity of the disease can be reflected by inflammatory markers, which are divided into four categories: pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, tissue-degrading enzymes, and other inflammatory mediators.40 These primarily include tumor necrosis factor- α (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), interleukin-17 (IL-17), monocyte chemotactic protein-1 (MCP-1), matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), chondroitin oligomeric matrix protein (COMP), prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), and soluble TNF receptor (sTNF-R), among others.41,42 There are differences in the inflammatory factor profiles among patients or in different periods of the same patient. For example, the level of TNF-α in some patients increases, and the level of IL-6 or IL-17 in some patients increases significantly.42,43

TNF-α can promote the production of other pro-inflammatory factors, is abundant in the synovial fluid and tissues of RA, and is associated with systemic manifestations.42 IL-6, IL-17, and TNF-α jointly promote the activation of osteoclasts, thus causing bone erosion and aggravating inflammation.44,45 The level of MCP-1 is associated with the degree of macrophage infiltration in joints and will attract monocytes/macrophages to aggravate the local inflammatory condition.46 Cartilage and bone matrix are directly decomposed by MMPs, and their activity is closely related to the degree of joint injury.47 COMP belongs to the non-collagenous protein in cartilage matrix; it reflects the degree of cartilage degradation and is a direct marker of joint structural damage.48 PGE2 is a key molecule that causes the local joint of RA to have redness, swelling, heat, and pain. sTNF-R is a soluble receptor released by the activation of the TNF-α system, which can neutralize part of TNF-α, and can reflect the activity level of the TNF pathway.49,50 The various inflammatory markers of RA affect each other and work together. There are differences between patients and in different disease periods, which shows that it is difficult to comprehensively evaluate the inflammatory situation only by detecting a single marker.42,46 However, inflammatory markers are not exclusive diagnostic indicators of RA, and the comprehensive analysis and interpretation of autoantibodies, clinical manifestations, and imaging findings should be carried out.

Oxidative Stress Markers

Markers of oxidative stress can be used to evaluate the imbalance of the oxidation and antioxidant systems in the body, which include reactive oxygen species (ROS), reactive nitrogen species (RNS), malondialdehyde (MDA), protein carbonyls, nitrotyrosine, H2S, and carbon monoxide (CO).9 In the RA patient group, the degree of oxidative stress varies among different individuals or in different disease stages of the same patient.51,52 For example, the ROS level in the active stage is higher than that in the remission stage. ROS and others can promote the release of pro-inflammatory factors in related pathways, thus leading to intensified cell activation and destruction.51 Nitrotyrosine is highly expressed in RA synovium and is associated with inflammatory damage.53 Sahar et al detected a positive correlation between plasma protein carbonyl levels and platelet counts in active RA patients, and speculated that this might be used as a supplementary marker.52 The contents of H2S and CO in RA synovial fluid are relatively high, which can inhibit oxidative stress and inflammation.54,55 Currently, these markers are mainly used in the scientific research field, helping to analyze the pathological mechanism of RA, and then providing a theoretical basis for determining antioxidant targets and monitoring the curative effect.

Metabolomics-Based Biomarkers

Metabolomic biomarkers are quantifiable endogenous small molecule metabolites identified by metabolomic technology, which can reflect the overall metabolic situation under the action of factors related to genes, environment, or diseases.56 According to their sources, they include amino acid metabolism, lipid and fatty acid metabolism, energy metabolism, oxidative stress and nucleotide metabolism, as well as the co-metabolism of gut microbiota and the host.

The immune cell metabolism of patients with RA is reprogrammed, which is reflected in the disorder of amino acid metabolism. The level of arginine is positively correlated with clinical remission, with a β value of 0.3, and the concentration of arginine in the serum during the remission period is about 1.5 times that during the active period.10,57 The contents of cystine and serine in the patient’s plasma are in an elevated state, and the metabolism of tryptophan is associated with immune inflammation.10 The metabolic profiles of different patients or different periods of the same patient may be different. For example, some patients have abnormal tryptophan metabolism, and some are characterized by changes in arginine or lipid metabolism.10,57 Tahzeeb et al predicted the treatment remission situation by using the baseline metabolites of 220 early patients. The AUC of the optimal model can reach 0.75.58 The combined use of glyceric acid lactate and 3-hydroxyisovaleric acid has an accuracy of 96.7% in distinguishing early RA from healthy individuals.35 The AUC of the gut microbiota-related metabolite imidazole acetic acid for diagnosis is 0.928.59 The biomarkers of metabolomics have the characteristics of high throughput and multivariate, but they face problems such as standardization, verification of the cohort, and control of confounding factors in clinical transformation.57

At the clinical association level, early RA and healthy controls can be distinguished by serum metabolite combinations, with an AUC reaching 0.967, a sensitivity of 94.4%, and a specificity of 92.3%;35 the AUC of imidazole acetic acid for the diagnosis of RA is 0.928;59 51 plasma metabolites are significantly associated with the DAS28-CRP score (P < 0.05), and metabolite-based machine learning models can effectively predict disease activity;57 the urinary urea/tryptophan ratio in dogs with active RA is about twice that in the remission period, and is positively correlated with the DAS28 score (r = 0.45).10 These results show that the concentration thresholds of specific metabolites or their combinations can be used for early screening of RA, assessment of the degree of activity, and prediction of treatment effects.

From Biomarkers to Nanosensing Technology

The biomarkers of RA have extremely low abundance, involve multiple targets, and are in dynamic change, which brings great challenges to detection techniques. Nanobiosensors have more advantages than traditional methods in terms of high sensitivity, multiplex detection, and point-of-care testing. A clear technical framework has been established to deal with clinical problems, and the subsequent part will elaborate on how the technology deals with them.

Nanobiosensor

Nanobiosensors are a combination of nanotechnology and biosensing technology, which can detect biological signals efficiently and sensitively, and bring about changes in the diagnosis of early diseases and rapid point-of-care testing.

Principles of Nanobiosensors

The operation of the nano-biosensor includes three steps: recognition, conversion, and output (Figure 2). The biomolecular recognition element carries out specific capture of the relevant target, resulting in a slight change in the signal; with the characteristics of the nano-material, such as high specific surface area, the weak signal is amplified into a measurable signal; according to the material characteristics, light, electricity, and other methods are selected for output.60 The blood glucose meter amplifies the enzymatic electrical signal with carbon nanotubes, etc, to achieve high-sensitivity detection;61 the cancer diagnosis chip relies on the nanowire field effect transistor to detect the current change when the antibody is bound.62 This substance has the characteristics of high sensitivity, rapid response, etc, and may provide a new technical road for early screening of RA, etc. Figure 2How nanobiosensors work. Classification of Nanobiosensors

Nanoscale sensors can be divided into four categories according to signals: electrochemical type, optical type, piezoelectric type, and magnetoelastic type (Figure 3), and the signal transduction mechanisms are listed in Table 2.63 Electrochemical sensors rely on nanomaterials to detect relevant changes, such as electron transfer and current. Graphene has a relatively high carrier mobility, MXene has good electrical conductivity and hydrophilicity, and gold nanostructures can promote electron transfer.64 This type of sensor has low cost and power consumption, high sensitivity, and can monitor multiple targets in real time, but there are problems of being easily affected by matrix interference and having a slightly longer response time.23 Optical sensors can perform target detection on changes such as color. Plasma nanomaterials enhance spectral signals through the surface plasmon resonance (SPR) effect, and electrochemiluminescence can reduce background interference and control the spatio-temporal output.65 It has multi-channel detection ability, high resolution, and good biocompatibility, but there are problems of photobleaching, phototoxicity, and high equipment cost.22 Piezoelectric sensors follow the Sauerbrey equation; the frequency change is linearly related to the mass change. The signal can be enhanced by nanoscale structure modification; it has the characteristics of high sensitivity, simple operation, and label-free real-time monitoring, but there are problems of being affected by liquid damping interference and insufficient spatial resolution.24,66 Magnetostrictive sensors use the magnetostrictive effect to measure magnetic changes, can realize wireless passive detection, have low background noise, and can adapt to enclosed environments, but there are problems of limited sensitivity and being easily affected by magnetic field interference.67 Different sensors have different characteristics, and the appropriate selection depends on the application scenario. Detection Using Nanobiosensors in RA

Detection of RA Using Electrochemical Nanobiosensors

Electrochemical nanobiosensors with nano-materials modified on electrode surfaces detect RA biomarkers by using the change of electrical signals triggered by the binding of antigens and antibodies. Gold nanoparticles, carbon nanotubes, and graphene can increase the surface area of the electrode, thereby promoting the transfer of electrons to realize signal amplification.18 This type of sensor has development prospects in the detection of RA serological markers and can somewhat deal with the problems of large sample requirements, long detection time, and poor sensitivity of traditional methods. This section examines the performance and applicable conditions of this method in the detection of antibodies, inflammatory factors, and related biomarkers (Figure 4), so as to provide a reference for the early diagnosis of RA. Antibody Testing

Construct a label-free electrochemical immunoassay method using nitrogen-doped graphene and gold nanoparticles to detect anti-CCP antibodies in human plasma, and this method has stable and specific performance.22 The field-effect transistor potentiometric immunoassay can achieve detection at the femtomolar level, providing a new detection method for early diagnosis and activity monitoring of RA.15 Use a dual-mode immunosensor combining electrochemistry and optics to detect the target antibody with dual signals, thereby reducing the occurrence of false positives or false negatives.16 Label-free wireless sensors fabricated using nanopore arrays and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy can continuously monitor disease biomarkers, providing design insights for future implantable monitoring devices.77 It should be understood that the antibody profile of RA patients is diverse, and patients with seronegative RA (accounting for about 20–30%) cannot be identified by traditional tests, so when designing antibody sensors, the detection needs of different subtypes should be taken into account.34,38 Currently, relevant research on electrochemical sensors for directly detecting RA antibodies is still insufficient, but the existing achievements have laid a foundation for the integration and application of subsequent technologies.

Inflammatory Marker Testing

Constructing a label-free electrochemical sensor using reduced graphene oxide-Ti3C2Tx MAX phase material can detect IL-6 within 15 minutes, which greatly shortens the time compared with the ELISA method and helps improve the efficiency in early screening of RA, etc.68 The detection lower limit of the TNF-α sensor composed of aptamer and carbon hybrid material in complex samples is 5.07 ng/L, and its clinical value still needs to be verified.17 Another study integrates flexible microfluidics and electrochemical detection to achieve real-time monitoring of TNF-α in sweat, which provides a new way for wearable inflammation monitoring.75 The dual-channel electrochemical immunoassay sensor can detect CXCL7 and MMP-3 simultaneously, and its reagent consumption is reduced by three-fourths compared with ELISA;78 another study achieves simultaneous detection of IL-1β and TNF-α or IL-1β and MMP-8 on a single chip.79

When electrochemical nanobiosensors are used to detect pro-inflammatory factors such as IL-6, TNF-α, etc, they have the characteristics of high sensitivity and real-time monitoring. The activity state of RA is closely related to the level of inflammatory factors: when it is in an active state, IL-6 and TNF-α often increase, while in the remission stage they approach the normal level.42 However, the situation of inflammatory cytokines varies among different patients or at different times of the same patient. Some patients have a high level of TNF-α, and some patients have a significant increase in IL-6 or IL-17, which indicates that it is difficult to comprehensively evaluate the inflammatory status of an individual only by detecting a single biomarker.42 At present, most related research is in the concept verification period. To achieve the transformation from laboratory to clinical application or wearable device application, more clinical data are still needed to support.

Testing for Other Components

Electrochemical nano-biosensors have development potential in detecting RA-related biomarkers. By strengthening the electrocatalytic response of electrodes with micro-nano materials, the concentration of ROS/RNS can be transformed into electrical signals reflecting joint oxidative stress.80 The enzyme-free hydrogen peroxide sensor constructed with three-dimensional graphene hydrogel and nickel oxide octahedrons can be used for the assessment of RA disease activity.19 The electrode modified with vertically ordered mesoporous silica can transform the conductivity change produced by bacterial metabolism into electrochemiluminescent signals to monitor the association between intestinal flora metabolism and RA.81 The synovial fluid is processed by gold nanoparticle sensors and combined with machine learning analysis, which can distinguish osteoarthritis from RA within a short time and make a preliminary assessment of disease severity, highlighting the preliminary significance of machine learning-assisted multi-marker data interpretation.76

Most sensors have been validated in buffer solutions or diluted sera, but the long-term stability and inter-batch reproducibility of complex clinical samples such as undiluted synovial fluid still need to be systematically evaluated.68,82 There may be differences in the level of oxidative stress and metabolic profile in patients with RA, and the impact of heterogeneity on the interpretation of results should be considered when designing detection strategies.53,80 Electrochemical nanosensors expand the detection range of targets from traditional serological markers to oxidative stress products, gas signaling molecules, and intestinal metabolites, thus providing multiple diagnostic methods for early diagnosis, treatment monitoring, and personalized treatment of RA.

Antibody Testing

Detection was carried out on 30 clinical samples through a dual-target system composed of a microfluidic chip and an optical detection module. It was found that the content of anti-CCP antibody was between 6.0 and 59.0 units/mL. This system has high specificity and is suitable for point-of-care testing.69 Europium complexes combine with albumin to form nanoparticles. Using its time-resolved luminescence characteristics to eliminate the influence of background fluorescence in the delay period, human IgG is detected under the condition of high serum dilution.83 The nanoscale DNA “device” relies on antibody binding to trigger the change of DNA structure and realizes low-cost detection within five minutes.13,14 The zero-loss semiconductor technology greatly reduces the detection limit greatly reduced by about 150 times compared with the traditional surface plasmon resonance method, opening up a new path for the development of ultrasensitive sensors.70 As shown in Figure 5. Inflammatory Marker Testing

Using metasurfaces combined with multi-level signal amplification and machine learning, the detection limit of CRP can be reduced to the femtogram per milliliter level, which can be used for multiplex and high-throughput screening;11 the MMP-2 responsive nano-probe can specifically release fluorescent signals in the RA microenvironment, and has potential applications in visual diagnosis;85 the detection method based on IL-6 aptamer and silver ion-doped three-dimensional porous gold nanoparticles can quantitatively determine IL-6 in a wide concentration range.12

Testing for Other Components

Glutathione-modified ultrasmall near-infrared persistent luminescence nanoprobes of Cr3+-doped ZnGa2O4 have a signal-to-noise ratio approximately 1.9 times higher than the control group in animal models, can detect RA lesions earlier, and have fast metabolism.84 The diagnostic and therapeutic nanoprobes constructed by combining MR/CT dual-mode imaging, monoclonal antibody targeting, and enzyme-responsive drug release initially show potential in targeted imaging of RA lesions and drug delivery.86 With the help of Pt(II)-porphyrin-based phosphorescent oxygen sensing membranes, the oxygen gradient can be shown in real time, and it helps researchers find that there is a connection between local joint hypoxia and inflammation in RA.87 The means of fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) can simultaneously detect H2O2 concentration and viscosity, produce ratio-type fluorescent signals, and have the function of multi-parameter measurement.88

Biomarkers in patients with RA show diverse characteristics. There are differences in autoantibodies, etc, in different patients or at different times in the same patient. Therefore, optical sensor detection needs to cover multiple targets and perform personalized analysis.34,42,53 Currently, its physiological conditions need to be tested in terms of anti-pollution and long-term signal stability.82,83 Although it has potential, such as rapid detection, the high equipment cost and limited portability make its application at the bedside still in the exploration period. The above research shows that optical nanobiosensors are moving towards the direction of multi-dimensional intelligent application, but to move from the laboratory to the clinic, problems such as stability, cost, and multi-center verification need to be solved.

Piezoelectric Nanobiosensors

Wu Haoran et al used short peptide self-assembly to create piezoelectric hydrogels with properties such as piezoelectricity, initially showing the possibility of their monitoring in joint swelling and other aspects, and the application prospect in the rehabilitation monitoring of RA.71 Zhao et al combined piezoelectric nanowire field-effect transistors with magnetic soft robots to develop magnetically controlled piezoelectric devices with properties such as high sensitivity, bringing new possibilities for biomarker detection.72 Currently, piezoelectric sensors within the scope of RA only focus on the monitoring of physical mechanical signals. In the future, if combined with specific biochemical tests, it may help the development of diagnosis.26 Liu et al integrated the piezoelectric effect with immune regulation to create a self-powered piezoelectric hydrogel scaffold, demonstrating the potential application prospect of piezoelectric technology in the treatment of inflammatory diseases.91 It should be noted that there are individual differences in RA patients, and when monitoring piezoelectric sensor parameters, the role of heterogeneity in data interpretation should be considered.34,92

Magnetoelastic Nanobiosensors

Chen et al achieved the magnetic resonance imaging-assisted diagnosis of RA lesions by using specially modified magnetic nanoparticles as targeting probes, with a total of 87 cases.73 Dai et al developed a detection platform for anti-CCP antibody, coated magnetic beads (MB@MOF) with zirconium-based metal-organic frameworks, and efficiently modified streptavidin by one-step physicochemical adsorption.20 Compared with the traditional chemical cross-linking method, the operation time was greatly shortened by 88. Magneto-elastic sensors have the characteristics of wireless and passive, and there is the possibility of application in implantable medical monitoring and rapid on-site detection.74 However, there may be differences in the antibody levels and the severity of inflammation in patients with RA, and the clinical applicability of this detection platform needs to be verified with more samples.20,34

Photoacoustic Imaging Nanobiosensors

Photoacoustic imaging, taking advantage of the contrast brought by optics and the deep-penetration characteristics of ultrasound, is suitable for deep-tissue imaging of joints in RA.93 Deng et al used melanin nanoparticles modified with RGD peptides targeting αvβ3 to enhance photoacoustic imaging, so as to achieve early diagnosis and dynamic monitoring of RA earlier.89 Liu et al prepared mesoporous polydopamine@cerium dioxide nanoparticles, which can perform photothermal therapy under the guidance of photoacoustic imaging, relieve hypoxia, and eliminate ROS, providing a new design concept for constructing an integrated diagnostic and therapeutic nano-platform.90

Piezoelectric sensors are used for label-free real-time monitoring in basic research of RA;21 magnetoelastic sensors can be made into miniature implantable devices to track local inflammation markers for a long time;67 photoacoustic imaging sensors contribute to early diagnosis of RA.89 Currently, most related technologies are still in the R & D process, and transformation at the clinical level needs to deal with problems such as adapting to individual differences. Wearable and implantable sensors open up new directions for continuous dynamic monitoring of RA. For example, the flexible microfluidic-electrochemical integrated system developed by Ploner et al can detect TNF-α in sweat in real time, and the wireless sensor based on the magnetoelastic principle can create a miniature implantable device.20,75 Zheng et al proposed that combining nanosensors with wearable and bedside devices may be able to expand the evaluation range of RA to family and community, and promote it in the direction of “continuous monitoring”.94 Although the technology is in the prototype verification period, it shows considerable potential in remote management and personalized treatment of RA.

Challenges and Outlook

The research on the bio-nanobiosensor for biomarkers of RA has shifted from single detection to comprehensive diagnosis and treatment, and there are biomarker-related difficulties in clinical transformation.95

In the technical performance category, samples such as serum and synovial fluid are complex in structure. Non-specific adsorption will interfere with the detection accuracy, and it is necessary to explore how to maintain high sensitivity and specificity in complex situations.68 Biological pollution and others interfere with the long-term stability of the sensing interface.82 In the industrialization process, it is necessary to deal with the stability of long-term storage and the repeatability between batches in stage.82 Some optical sensors have technical problems in deep tissue penetration, and electrochemical and other sensors also encounter difficulties, such as signal drift.82,96 In the future, there may be a situation where multiple modes are integrated and artificial intelligence assists Integrate multiple modes of sensing to detect many RA biomarkers to deal with the complex situation of samples;97 construct materials for antifouling composite interfaces to inhibit pollution; standardize raw materials to ensure stable performance; design intelligent nanoprobes to improve the signal-to-noise ratio of in vivo detection;98 improve the stability of equipment by combining modification and other algorithms. Machine learning has great potential in processing high-dimensional data.99 Combining artificial intelligence models with nanosensors can bring new opportunities for RA classification, etc, and can transform high-dimensional data into auxiliary information for clinical decision-making.100

In the process of biosafety and in vivo activities, the toxicity, distribution status, degradation situation, and immunogenicity of nanomaterials are important points to pay attention to during clinical transformation.101,102 Currently, the research focuses on in vitro performance verification, and there is insufficient evaluation of the long-term retention, metabolism, and potential chronic toxicity of nanomaterials in the joint cavity and systemic organs.82 The distribution patterns of nanomaterials in the living body are different, which will affect the targeting efficiency and safety, and can also trigger immunogenic effects.102 Future exploration needs to consider the system evaluation parameters under physiological conditions to explore methods that are degradable and have low immunogenicity.103

The RA biomarker profile shows obvious heterogeneity in different stages of the disease and different subtypes of patients. It is difficult to meet the actual needs only by detecting a single biomarker.39,104 Multiple detection can quantify multiple targets at one time, thereby improving the diagnostic efficiency. Existing studies have preliminarily explored various implementation approaches; for example, Guerrero et al developed a dual-target sensor capable of simultaneously detecting RF and anti-CCP,18 while Tirado et al achieved simultaneous detection of IL-1β/TNF-α or IL-1β/MMP-8.79 However, multiplexing still faces technical challenges such as cross-interference among multiple targets, mismatched dynamic ranges, and the limited universality of signal decoupling algorithms. In the future, the exploration can focus on developing integrated microfluidic multi-channel sensing chips, improving the signal analysis method, based on the long-term biocompatibility of wearable and implantable sensors, to overcome the problem of non-specific adsorption on the sensing interface, and develop antifouling coatings and self-calibrating sensor designs.75,82

In the scenario of large-scale, multicenter clinical verification, although there are possibilities for development in the small-sample verification of many previous platforms, there is still a lack of systematic clinical verification for RA patients in multicenter, large-sample cohorts. Currently, the research has the situation of a small sample size (often less than 100 cases), and most are single-center studies, lacking independent external verification cohorts.82,95 Most studies are carried out in diluted serum or buffer solutions. Only a small number of studies attempt clinical verification, but have scarce sample numbers, and independent multicenter verification is still needed.18,69,76 To promote clinical transformation, a prospective multicenter cohort design (covering hundreds of samples) is needed, with independent training and verification sets, parallel comparison with traditional diagnostic methods (like sensitivity, etc), and following reporting standards.95,102 Constructing a standardized multicenter verification system is a key priority task in this field in the next stage.

During clinical transformation, the challenges faced by large-scale production still need to be solved. The synthesis process of many high-performance nanosensors is complicated and costly, which cannot meet the unified requirements of clinical production volume and cost.105 At the same time, there is a lack of a standardized verification system, and most studies are still at the cell or animal experiment level.102 So far, no equipment for RA diagnosis based on nanobiosensors has been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) or the European Medicines Agency (EMA), and the relevant regulatory standards are not clear.82 The current technology is in the laboratory verification stage, with a low degree of maturity, and its potential can be reflected in Table 3. Discussion and Conclusions

Nowadays, the nanobiosensors used for the detection of biomarkers of RA include electrochemical, optical, piezoelectric, magnetoelastic, and photoacoustic sensors. The research and development of electrochemical sensors started earlier. It has the characteristics of high sensitivity, low cost, and easy miniaturization in the detection of anti-CCP antibodies and other aspects. However, it may be interfered with by non-specific adsorption in complex samples, and the stability of the electrode needs to be improved.68 Optical sensors can eliminate background fluorescence and have a high signal-to-noise ratio, but they rely on complex optical systems, which makes the instrument costly and miniaturization quite difficult.83 Piezoelectric sensors can perform real-time monitoring without labeling, but they need a wired connection, which limits their application in implantable devices.72 Magnetoelastic sensors have the characteristics of wireless and passive, and are suitable for long-term monitoring of micro-implant devices. However, there is still room for improvement in their sensitivity during deep tissue monitoring.20 Photoacoustic sensors have the advantages of high optical contrast and ultrasonic penetration depth in early joint inflammation imaging. However, it has the problems of high equipment cost and slow imaging speed.90

The biomarkers of RA have expanded from traditional serological antibodies to multi-dimensional networks, covering inflammatory mediators and other contents.104 The RA biomarker spectrum is different in different periods and patient groups: in the early stage, it is characterized by the increase of anti-CCP antibodies and other levels, and in the later stage, it is characterized by cartilage degradation products and other contents;30,47 about 20% to 30% of seronegative RA patients need to be combined with inflammatory factors and other auxiliary confirmations.39 Because RA has a high degree of heterogeneity, a single sensor and so on are difficult to meet the needs, and in the future, we need to explore the way of multi-mode integration and the use of artificial intelligence auxiliary analysis to accurately diagnose RA.

In terms of the possibility of clinical transformation, electrochemical sensors have the characteristics of low cost, simple use and rapid detection, and are the closest to point-of-care testing, but attention should be paid to the anti-interference situation of complex samples and batch-to-batch stability;18,68 optical sensors have relatively high sensitivity and can be used as reference methods in central laboratories, but due to the problems of instrument cost and volume, it is difficult to be widely used at the grassroots level;11,70 magnetoelastic sensors have the development prospect of being implantable and monitored for a long time, but biosafety and in vivo stability still need to be systematically verified;73 photoacoustic sensors face challenges in short-term transformation due to the reasons of imaging speed and equipment cost.93 The clinical transformation of RA nano-biomarkers needs to pay attention to matters such as the standardization of sample pretreatment, and future research can focus on 1–2 types of potential technologies, and verify through prospective clinical cohorts to evaluate their clinical additional value to ELISA and so on.

As for the systematic review on the application of nano-biosensors in the detection of biomarkers of RA, electrochemical, optical, and other sensing platforms all have their own unique advantages. Laboratories can efficiently and sensitively detect substances related to autoantibodies. Electrochemical sensors have great development potential in early point-of-care testing due to low cost and easy miniaturization. However, most current research is still in the pre-clinical verification stage and faces difficulties such as clinical cohorts. In the future, it may be possible to promote precise real-time monitoring of diagnosis and treatment by means of multimodality and other means.

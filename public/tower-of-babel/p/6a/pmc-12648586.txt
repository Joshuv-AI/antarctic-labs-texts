Rab10 plays a protective role in the development of pathological cardiac hypertrophy
Authors: Fang Xu, Min Wu, Yihan Wang, Haiyan Luo, Xiaobo Gao, Song Lai, Cailing Lu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractProlonged cardiac hypertrophy is a main risk factor for heart failure (HF). During cardiac hypertrophy remodelling, there is an increase in protein synthesis, as well as the trafficking and localization of proteins to their functional sites. Rab GTPases, which are key regulators of vesicular formation, movement, and fusion, play a crucial role in these processes. In this study, we identified Rab10, a small GTPase belonging to the Rab family, as a novel regulator to inhibit cardiac hypertrophy. Cardiomyocyte hypertrophy was induced by Ang II or pressure overload in primary neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs) or mouse model. We found that Rab10 expression was downregulated in NRCMs or murine hearts after hypertrophic stress. Rab10 overexpression attenuated cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, whereas its silencing exacerbated the phenotype, demonstrating its essential antihypertrophic role in vitro. To determine the in vivo role of Rab10 in the heart, we generated cardiac-specific Rab10-overexpressing transgenic mice (TG). When subjected to Ang II infusion or pressure overload, Rab10 TG mice displayed an improved contractile function and attenuated hypertrophic remodelling. In contrast, AAV9-mediated cardiac-specific knockdown of Rab10 significantly aggravated pressure overload-induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy. Mechanically, Rab10 suppressed the phosphorylation of ERK1/2 and AKT, thereby attenuating the development cardiac hypertrophy. Additionally, we demonstrated that Rab10 was post-transcriptionally downregulated by miR-199a. In summary, our findings revealed a novel role for Rab10 in pathogenic cardiac hypertrophy and suggest that Rab10 may be a potential therapeutic target for cardiac hypertrophic.Keywords: Rab10, Cardiac hypertrophy, Ang II, TAC, miR-199a

Introduction

Pathological cardiac hypertrophy in response to pathological stresses such as neurohumoral factors (epinephrine, norepinephrine, angiotensin II), chronic hypertension or mechanical stress is a major risk factor for chronic heart failure (CHF), a leading cause of mortality and morbidity [1]. Despite substantial advances in clinical therapy, successful treatment of CHF remains challenging. Cardiac hypertrophy is generally believed to initially represent a compensatory response to neurohumoral factors or mechanical stress to maintain normal cardiac function during cardiac remodelling [2,3]. However, prolonged stimulation culminates in maladaptive structural remodelling, characterized by cardiomyocyte enlargement, interstitial fibrosis, and contractile dysfunction. Angiotensin II (Ang II) is a key effector of the renin-angiotensin system that plays a pivotal role in the development of pathological hypertrophy. Ang II-induced cardiac hypertrophy involves a complex signal transduction mechanism, including PLC activation, PI3K/AKT axis, and the activation of MAPKs, which promote protein synthesis, gene transcription, and cellular growth [4,5]. Transverse Aortic Constriction (TAC) is widely model to study cardiac pressure overload in vivo, recapitulates the transition from compensatory hypertrophy to overt heart failure [6].

Rab GTPases, the largest subfamily of small Ras-like guanosine triphosphatases, function as molecular switches cycling between active GTP-bound and inactive GDP-bound states. These proteins orchestrate multiple steps of intracellular membrane trafficking, including vesicle formation, motility, tethering, and fusion [7,8]. For instance, Rab5 is essential for organizing endosomes, maintaining the integrity of the entire endocytic pathway and ensuring proper cargo transport and sorting [9]. Rab4 regulates the directional mobility and transport of endocytic vesicles in astrocytes by coordinating GTP-GDP cycling, which influences interactions with motor proteins and the cytoskeleton [10]. Rab11 mediates the cholesterol-sensitive trafficking of Kv1.5 channels by regulating their recycling from endosomal compartments to the sarcolemma, thereby modulating cardiac electrical activity [11,12]. Increasing evidence showed that Rab proteins play important roles in regulating cardiac remodelling. Rab1a overexpression caused cardiac hypertrophy that progressed in a time- and transgene dose-dependent manner to heart failure. Rab4 facilitates the recycling of internalized beta-adrenergic receptors to the plasma membrane, thereby enhancing beta-AR signaling and cardiac function [[13], [14], [15]]. Rab10 is required for the insulin-stimulated redistribution of GLUT4 to the plasma membrane in adipocytes, and knockdown of Rab10 attenuates insulin-induced GLUT4 translocation [16]. Moreover, Rab10 facilitates TLR4 signaling by promoting the replenishment of TLR4 onto the plasma membrane in response to LPS stimulation [17]. Rab10 regulates ER structure and dynamics, influencing tubule growth and fusion, and is linked to phospholipid synthesis enzymes [18]. Recent studies have been shown that Rab10 and Caveolin-1 mark the intraluminal vesicles in migrasomes, with their transport regulated by Myosin Va, RILPL2, and LRRK2-mediated Rab10 phosphorylation, facilitating CSF-1 delivery for monocyte-macrophage differentiation in skin wound healing. And the phosphorylation of Rab10 by LRRK2 is regulated by the positioning of lysosomes [19,20]. In our previous study, it demonstrated that cardiac-specific overexpression of Rab10 alleviates Dox-induced cardiac dysfunction and injury [21]. We further explored the role of Rab10 in Ang II and Transverse Aortic Constriction (TAC)-induced cardiac hypertrophy.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, endogenous non-coding RNAs that bind to complementary sequences within the 3′ untranslated region (3’UTR) of protein-coding mRNAs. Recent studies have demonstrated that miRNAs exert multifaceted influences on diverse biological processes and are implicated in the pathogenesis of cardiovascular diseases. It was reported that miR-672-5p may exert inhibitory effects on cardiac hypertrophy by modulating the expression of JUN in cardiomyocytes [22]. MiR-222 exhibits a dual role in cardiac growth, promoting physiological hypertrophy while inhibiting pathological remodelling and dysfunction induced by pressure overload [23]. A cardiac-specific miRNA, miR-208a is sufficient to induce pathological cardiac hypertrophy, as characterized by the thicker ventricular wall, larger cross-section area of cardiomyocytes, and bigger ventricular chambers [24]. The previous study investigated that knockdown of endogenous miR-199 caused physiological cardiac hypertrophy. Another study investigated that miR-199a overexpression impaired autophage via mTOR activation, leading to cardiac hypertrophy and dysfunction [25,26].

In our study, using both primary cardiomyocytes and mice with cardiac-specific overexpression or knockdown of Rab10, we found that Rab10 exerts as an antihypertrophic role both in vivo and in vitro mediated by ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT signal pathways. Furthermore, Rab10 is post-transcriptionally downregulated by miR-199a, establishing a novel miR-199a/Rab10 axis that modulates pathological cardiac remodelling. These findings highlight Rab10 as a potential therapeutic target for cardiac hypertrophic diseases.

Results

Rab10 expression was downregulated in cultured cardiomyocytes and mouse hearts after hypertrophic stress

To investigate the potential role of Rab10 in cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, we constructed two cardiomyocyte hypertrophy models. Acute exposure to Ang II leads to physiological cardiac hypertrophy, which is an adaptive response that maintains cardiac hypertrophy. However, chronic exposure results in pathological hypertrophy, a maladaptive process that increases the risk of heart failure. Transverse Aortic Constriction involves surgically narrowing the transverse aorta, typically achieving a 70 % reduction in the lumen diameter, and leads to left ventricular hypertrophy and heart failure. We first examined Rab10 expression in neonatal rat cardiomyocytes (NRCMs) after Ang II treatment, and found that Ang II stimulation significantly downregulated Rab10 protein level in a time-dependent manner (Fig. 1A). Accordingly, Rab10 expression was markedly reduced in heart tissues of wild-type (WT) mice subjected to Ang II for 2 weeks (Fig. 1B). Moreover, Rab10 protein level was time-dependently decreased in WT mice subjected to transverse aortic constriction (TAC) (Fig. 1C). These data suggested that hypertrophic stimuli downregulated Rab10 expression that may play a role in the regulation of cardiac hypertrophy.

Rab10 overexpression inhibited Ang II-induced cardiomyocyte hypertrophy in vitro

To investigate the effect of Rab10 in cardiomyocytes, NRCMs were infected with recombinant adenoviruses expressing GFP (Ad-GFP) or Rab10 (Ad-Rab10) and then treated with Ang II (200 nM) for 24 h. Ad-Rab10 infection resulted in an increase of Rab10 expression by 1.2-fold compared with Ad-GFP control (Fig. 2A). Rab10 overexpression markedly attenuated Ang II-induced hypertrophic responses as indicated by reduction of cardiomyocyte size and hypertrophic marker atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP) expression (Fig. 2B-C). We examined signaling pathway such as ERK1/2 and AKT. Immunoblotting analysis showed that Rab10 overexpression significantly inhibits the activation of ERK1/2 and AKT induced by Ang II (Fig. 2D-E). There was no significant difference in these parameters between Ad-GFP and Ad-Rab10 groups after saline treatment (Fig. 2B-E). Overall, these data indicate that Rab10 overexpression inhibits Ang II-induced cardiac hypertrophy.

Knockdown of Rab10 aggravates cardiomyocyte hypertrophy after Ang II treatment

To support the role of Rab10 in cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, we infected NRCMs with recombinant adenovirus Ad-siRab10 to silence the expression of Rab10. Ad-siRab10 infection decreased endogenous Rab10 expression by about 90 % compared with scrambled siRNA control (Ad-siControl) (Fig. 3A). Compared with NRCMs infected with Ad-siControl, Ang II-induced greater increase in cell size and in ANP mRNA level was significantly enhanced in Ad-siRab10-infected NRCMs (Fig. 3B-C). Moreover, Rab10 knockdown by Ad-siRab10 further activated ERK1/2 and AKT compared with Ad-siControl (Fig. 3D-E).

Cardiac-specific overexpression of Rab10 inhibits hypertrophic remodelling induced by pressure overload

To test the general significance of these findings, we examined if Rab10 resists hypertrophy induced by pressure overload. Following 4 weeks of TAC, WT mice displayed compensatory systolic function as indicated by increased EF% and FS%, which was maintained at the normal level in Rab10-Tg mice (Fig. 4A). Moreover, TAC-induced cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis as evidenced by heart size, heart weight (HW)/body weight (BW) ratio (Fig. 4B), cardiomyocyte surface area (Fig. 4C), fibrotic area (Fig. 4E) and the mRNA expression of ANP and collagen I (Fig. 4D, F) in WT mice was significantly inhibited in Rab10-Tg mice. In addition, the activation of ERK1/2 and AKT in WT heart tissues were also markedly attenuated in Rab10-Tg mice after TAC treatment (Fig. 4G).

Knockdown of Rab10 in cardiomyocytes exacerbates pressure overload-induced cardiac remodelling

To corroborate the effect of Rab10 knockdown on cardiac remodelling in vivo, we constructed adenovirus-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) that specifically silencing the expression of Rab10 (rAAV9/siRab10). After injection into tail vein of WT mice with rAAV9/siRab10 or rAAV9/siControl for 2 weeks, fluorescence microscopy revealed that transduction efficiency of rAAV9 as indicated by GFP expression was over 90 % in the hearts (Supplementary Fig. 1 A). The expression of endogenous Rab10 was silenced by about 90 % in the hearts of mice injected by rAAV9-siRab10 compared with rAAV9/siControl (Supplementary Fig. 1B). All mice were then subjected to TAC or sham operation for 2 weeks. Echocardiography showed that the rAAV9-mediated Rab10 silencing caused a remarkable decrease of cardiac contractile function as evidenced by the decreased EF% and FS% compared with rAAV9/siControl-treated mice after TAC (Fig. 5A). Further, Rab10 cardiac-specific knockdown aggravated TAC-induced hypertrophy and fibrosis, as demonstrated by increased LV wall thickness, HW/BW ratio (Fig. 5B), cross-sectional areas of myocytes (Fig. 5C), fibrotic area (Fig. 5E) and the expression levels of ANP and collagen I (Fig. 5D-F). Correspondently, Rab10 knockdown significantly enhanced the activation of ERK1/2 and AKT in heart tissues in response to TAC treatment (Fig. 5G).

Rab10 as a downstream target of miR-199a was reduced in response to hypertrophic stimuli

To elucidate the underlying mechanism by which hypertrophic stimuli downregulates Rab10 expression in cardiomyocytes, we searched for potential miRNA via a bioinformatics analysis using TargetScan and miRanda programs. We found that Rab10 3’UTR contained the predicted binding site (ACACUGGG) of miR-199a, which has been reported to promote cardiac hypertrophy (Fig. 6A). This led us to test whether miR-199a is involved in the regulation of Rab10 expression. qPCR analysis showed that Ang II treatment markedly increased miR-199a mRNA expression in cardiomyocytes and mouse hearts (Fig. 6B), which was negatively related to downregulation of Rab10 protein level in response to Ang II or TAC (Fig. 1A-C). To validate Rab10 as a direct target of miR-199a, we constructed human Rab10–3’UTR containing reporter plasmids with wild-type (WT)-miR-199a binding region (AACACUGGG), a point mutant-miR-199a binding region (GAAAAUGAG) or a deletion mutant of binding region. Those reporter plasmids were then co-transfected into HEK293ET cells with either mimic miR-NC or mimic miR-199a for 24 h. Luciferase assay revealed that miR-199a overexpression significantly repressed the luciferase activity of WT Rab10–3’UTR reporter compared with mimic miR-NC, but had no effect on the luciferase activity of the point mutant or deletion mutant Rab10–3’UTR reporter (Fig. 6C). In contrast, miR-199a inhibitor further enhanced the luciferase activity of WT Rab10–3’UTR reporter compared with miR-NC (Fig. 6D). In addition, miR-199a overexpression significantly suppressed the luciferase activity of reporter containing Rab10–3’UTR from mouse or rat (supplementary Fig. 2 A). Further, immunoblotting analysis confirmed that miR-199a overexpression significantly decreased Rab10 protein level in both HEK293ET cells and cardiomyocytes, whereas miR-199a inhibition enhanced the expression of Rab10 (Fig. 6E and supplementary Fig. 2B). These data demonstrate that Rab10 is a downstream target of miR-199a, which downregulated Rab10 expression in cardiomyocytes.

Discussion

Cardiac hypertrophy is an adaptive response to increased workload caused by conditions like hypertension, myocardial infarction, or valvular disease. Initially, it helps maintain cardiac function by enlarging cardiomyocytes and reinforcing ventricular walls. However, prolonged stress precipitates maladaptive remodelling, characterized by excessive cardiomyocyte enlargement, interstitial fibrosis, and diastolic dysfunction, ultimately culminating in heart failure [27,28]. A hallmark of this process is the coordinated augmentation of protein synthesis and the subsequent trafficking, sorting, and precise localization of the newly synthesized proteins, which are essential for cellular expansion and remodelling [29].

Intracellular protein trafficking is a process critically governed by the small Rab GTPase family. These proteins orchestrate the entire vesicular transport cycle, from the initial budding and formation of vesicles to their cytoskeletal movement and final fusion events [7,8]. Accumulating evidence indicates that Rab proteins, which are modulated by hypertrophic stimuli, play a significant role in regulating cardiomyocyte hypertrophy. Expression of Rab1, Rab4 and Rab6 was upregulated in the β2-adrenergic receptor-induced cardiomyopathy [13]. Rab1 promotes cardiomyocyte growth in vitro and induces cardiac hypertrophy in vivo. Its overexpression demonstrates that ER-to-Golgi trafficking is a critical regulatory site for controlling cardiomyocyte growth, and cardiac-specific overexpression of Rab1 in mice markedly promotes cardiac hypertrophy partially through the abnormal expression and subcellular distribution of the PKCα protein [30]. Rab4 overexpression also results in cardiac hypertrophy via selective upregulation of β-myosin heavy chain gene expression [15]. Rab11a interacts with SNX5 and protects against cardiac hypertrophy by regulating the membrane translocation of LRP6 [31]. Rab9 mediates an unconventional, Ulk1-dependent form of mitophagy that is essential for cardioprotection against pressure overload-induced heart failure [32]. Our previous study showed that Rab10 overexpression could alleviate Dox-induced cardiac cardiotoxicity [21]. However, the function of Rab10 in cardiac hypertrophy induced by Ang II and TAC model remains unknown. The present work provides the first evidence that Rab10 functions as a critical negative regulator of pathological cardiac hypertrophy.

Our study demonstrates a novel cardioprotective role of Rab10 in attenuating cardiac hypertrophy and improving contractile function induced by hypertrophic stimuli. In this study we used transgenic mice to create a TAC model in vivo experiments, and utilized Ang II-induced hypertrophy in cardiomyocytes in vitro experiments. We found that Rab10 expression is downregulated by both Ang II and TAC in cardiomyocytes. Further study showed that cardiac-specific overexpression of Rab10 attenuates cardiac hypertrophy and improves contractile function following Ang II infusion or TAC, whereas cardiac-specific knockdown of Rab10 aggravates cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and compromises cardiac function.

Overpressure serves as a major stimulus that induces protein synthesis, and activates key signaling pathways, such as the protein kinase C (PKC) pathway, the mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPK) pathway, and the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt pathway. These pathways play crucial roles in regulating cardiac hypertrophy. It is reported that cardiac hypertrophy is mediated by ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT pathway [33]. In our study, we investigated that Rab10 inhibition attenuates cardiac hypertrophy induced by Ang II or TAC, focusing on the ERK1/2 and AKT signaling pathways. Our results showed that the cardioprotective function of Rab10 is conferred via its suppression of ERK1/2 and AKT phosphorylation, which attenuates the development of pathological cardiac hypertrophy.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, endogenous non-coding RNAs that bind to complementary sequences within the 3′ untranslated region (3’UTR) of protein-coding mRNAs. This interaction leads to either translational repression or degradation of the target mRNA, thereby modulating gene expression post-transcriptionally. A multitude of miRNAs play crucial roles in regulating cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure, and most miRNAs function to inhibit hypertrophic signaling and prevent pathological cardiac growth [34]. For example, the previous study showed miR-199a modulates cardiomyocyte cell size and is upregulated in pressure-overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy, suggesting its functional role in hypertrophic remodelling [35].

In our study, we found miR-199a is involved in the regulation of Rab10 expression. Ang II treatment markedly increased miR-199a mRNA expression in cardiomyocytes and mouse hearts. Further, it was confirmed that miR-199a overexpression significantly decreased Rab10 protein level. These data demonstrate that Rab10 is a downstream target of miR-199a, which downregulated Rab10 protein expression in cardiomyocytes. As previously reported, miR-199a promotes hypertrophy primarily through the impairment of autophagy via mTOR activation in a pressure-overload model. While the finding highlights a crucial role for miR-199a in hypertrophy, our study reveals a novel and distinct mechanism for miR-199a. In our model of Ang II-induced cardiac hypertrophy, we observed the expected upregulation of miR-199a. Our data further suggest that a distinct dysregulation of downstream signaling pathways. This places the miR-199a/Rab10 axis as a critical upstream regulator of pro-hypertrophic signals in Ang II infusion.

It is well known that pathologic cardiac hypertrophy is regulated at three levels: extracellular hypertrophic stimulus signal, cytoplasmic signal transduction, nuclear gene transcription and post-transcriptional processes [34]. Our study found that in Ang II and TAC induction, Rab10 expression is downregulation as a target of miR-199a. The signaling pathways of ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT are altered simultaneously. Further research is needed on phospho-proteomic profiling of hearts with Rab10 gain- and loss-of-function to explain a complete signaling network downstream of Rab10.

In conclusion, our study provide a insights into the regulation of Rab10 in reducing pathological cardiac hypertrophy in response to hypertrophic stimuli. We found that Rab10 as a critical inhibitor of cardiac hypertrophy. The protective effect is mediated through the suppression of the pro-hypertrophic ERK1/2 and PI3K/AKT pathways. Furthermore, we discovered that the expression of Rab10 is negatively regulated by miR-199a, which directly binds to its mRNA's 3’UTR to promote its degradation. These data suggest that modulation of Rab10 may be a potential therapeutic approach for cardiac hypertrophic remodelling.

Materials and methods

Generation of Rab10 transgenic mice

Transgenic mice expressing Rab10 in the heart were created as described in a previous study [22]. A cDNA encoding the full-length human Rab10 with a C-terminal Flag-tag (Flag-Rab10) was cloned into a cardiac-specific expression plasmid containing the α-myosin heavy chain (α-MHC) promoter between the SalI and HindIII restriction sites. The Rab10 transgene was linearized and separated from the prokaryotic sequences after digestion with NotI and microinjected into fertilized oocytes from C57BL/6 J mice. Genomic DNA was isolated from mouse toe biopsies, and transgenic mice were identified by PCR with the sense primer 5’-TTCCAGCCCTCTCTTTCTC-3′ and antisense primer 5’-ATCACCGTCAT GGTCTTTG −3′.

Culture of primary cardiomyocytes

Neonatal rat cardiac myocytes (NRCMs) were isolated from 1-day-old Sprague-Dawley rat hearts with the Neonatal Cardiomyocyte Isolation System (Worthington Biochemical Corp.). Briefly, neonatal rats were anaesthetized with tribromoethanol (0.25 mg/g i.p.) and then sacrificed by cervical dislocation. The hearts were immediately removed and quartered on ice under aseptic conditions. The quartered hearts were digested in a 0.07 % (w/v) solution of trypsin in Ca2+ and Mg2+-free Hank's balanced salt solution (HBSS) at 37 °C twice for 10 min each time. Then, mixed enzymes (collagenase II 0.04 % and trypsin 0.07 %) in HBSS were used for further digestion at 37 °C eight times for 8 min each time. The digestion proceeded at 37 °C with intermittent gentle swirling. After collagenase digestion, the cells were filtered with 40 μm cell strainers, centrifuged for 5 min at 4 °C and then resuspended in fresh medium. To achieve myocyte enrichment, native heart cells were pre-plated for 90 min in T75 flasks. The non-adherent cells were collected and cultivated on laminin-coated 60 mm dishes. Bromodeoxyuridine (0.1 mM) was added to the culture media for the first 2 days to minimize fibroblast contamination. The dishes were maintained in an incubator at 37 °C with 5 % CO2.

Recombinant adenovirus and cell infection

Recombinant adenoviruses expressing the green fluorescent protein (GFP) alone (Ad-GFP), the full-length Rab10 cDNA with a C-terminal Myc-tag (Ad-Rab10), driven by the cytomegalovirus promoter were generated using AdEasy (MP Biomedicals). To knockdown Rab10, Ad-siRab10 (5’-GCATCATGCTAGTGTATGA-3′) under the control of U6 promoter was generated. Ad-siControl was used as a control. Twenty-four hours after plating, the cells were infected with Ad-Rab10 and Ad-GFP, or Ad-siRab10 and Ad-siControl until the efficiency of infection reached 95 %. Then, the cells were treated with 200 nmol/L Ang II for 24 h. The cardiomyocytes were stained with sarcomeric α-actinin to assess the cell size. Quantification of cell surface areas in images involved the use of the Image-Pro Plus 7.0 software.

Cell transfection and dual-luciferase assay

HEK293ET cells were cultured in DMEM medium with 10 % fetal bovine serum. Lipofectamine 2000 was used for transient transfection according to the manufacturer's manual (Invitrogen). To validate Rab10 as a direct target of miR-199a, HEK293ET cells were co-transfected with miR-199a mimic or inhibitor, and Rab10–3’UTR wild-type or mutant reporter plasmid in triplicate for 24 h. Luciferase activities were measured with the dual-luciferase reporter assay (Promega). Firefly luciferase activity was used as the internal control.

Quantitative real-time PCR assay

Total RNA was extracted from cultured cardiomyocytes or fresh mouse hearts using the TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen). RNA (1–2 μg) was reverse transcribed to generate first-strand cDNA with reverse transcriptase (Takara, Japan). Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) analysis was run in triplicate using the ABI 7500 detector system according to manufacturer's instructions. The results were standardized to control values of glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH). The primer sequences are listed in Supplementary Table 1.

Western blot analysis

Protein samples were prepared from cultured neonatal cardiomyocytes and heart tissues for Western blot analysis. In total, 50–100 μg of protein was loaded onto SDS-PAGE gels, transferred to nitrocellulose membranes, and incubated with primary antibodies against Rab10 (Proteintech), β-actin (Sigma), phospho-ERK1/2, ERK1/2, phospho-AKT and AKT (Cell Signaling Technology). The blots were developed using a chemiluminescent system, and densitometry analysis was performed with the Image J software (National Institutes of Health).

rAAV9 gene delivery

Adeno-associated virus serotype 9 (AAV9) expressing siRNA (5’-GCATCATGCTAGTGTATGA-3′) against Rab10 (rAAV9/siRab10) and scramble control (rAAV9/siControl) was generated by Weizhen Technologies Co. Ltd. (Shandong, China) according to the manufacturer's protocol [38–39]. We tested the tail injection efficiency of wild-type mice with a dose of 1 × 1011 vg, and found that rAAV9 injection for 2 weeks resulted in over 90 % GFP-positive myocardial area (Supplementary Fig. 1 A). Western blot analysis indicated that the rAAV9/siRab10 specifically knocked down endogenous Rab10 protein expression by over 90 % in the heart compared with rAAV/siControl (Supplementary Fig. 1B).

Cardiac hypertrophic models and treatment

Male mice (8–10-week-old) were lightly anaesthetized with ketamine at 50 mg/kg together with xylazine at 2.5 mg/kg and then implanted with osmotic pumps (Alzet MODEL 2002; Cupertino, CA, USA) infused with Ang II or saline at a rate of 1000 ng/kg/min in Ringer's solution (0.01 mmol/L acetic acid in saline) for two weeks. The pressure overload-induced cardiac mouse model via transverse-aortic constriction (TAC) surgery was established as previously described. Briefly, after chest opening, a 8–0 prolene suture was placed around the aortic arch and ligated. Sham surgery was performed without ligation. All procedures were approved by the Animal Care and Use committee of Dalian Medical University. The investigation conformed to the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animal published by the US National Institutes of Health (NIH Publication No. 85–23, revised 1996). The cardiac function was evaluated by echocardiography using a Vevo 2100 High-Resolution Imaging System (VisualSonics Inc) as described previously [40–42].

Haematoxylin and eosin staining and immunohistochemistry

All mice were anaesthetized with an overdose of pentobarbital (100 mg/kg, i.p., Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). The hearts were isolated and fixed in a 4 % paraformaldehyde solution. Heart sections (5 μm) were stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and Masson trichrome as previously described [42]. We calculated the interstitial fibrotic area as the ratio of the total area of interstitial fibrosis to the total area of the section. The sections were incubated with TRITC-conjugated wheat-germ agglutinin (WGA, Sigma) to identify the cross-sectional area of each heart. Images were viewed under a microscope (Eclipse TE2000-U, Nikon, Tokyo, Japan).

Statistical analysis

The abovementioned data are presented as the mean ± s.e.m. Statistical analyses were performed using two-way analysis of variance (two-way ANOVA) to evaluate the main effects of the two independent variables and their interaction, followed by Tukey's post hoc test for multiple comparisons where appropriate, using GraphPad Prism software (CA, USA). A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

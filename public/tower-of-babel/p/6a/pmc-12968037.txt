Global Trends and Hotspot Analysis of Nanomaterials in the Field of Acne: A Bibliometric Perspective
Authors: Ruiqi Chen, Yan Tang, Linghan Duan, Zhihong Jiang, Mingfang Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundAcne is a chronic inflammatory disease. Recent studies have revealed significant progress in the application of nanomaterials for acne treatment; however, a systematic analysis of research trends and hotspots remains lacking. This study aims to comprehensively present the current status of research in this field and summarize frontier directions using bibliometric methods.MethodsPublications with the subject terms “nanomaterials” and “acne” were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection for the period 2012–2025. Bibliometric analysis and visualization were conducted using CiteSpace, VOSviewer, Scimago Graphica, and R language.ResultsA total of 301 articles from 547 institutions across 51 countries were analyzed. Annual publications increased from 7 in 2012 to 25 in 2025, showing a continuous upward trend. India emerged as the leading contributor in this field. Notably, Folle Camila from the University of Barcelona was identified as the most prolific author, Cairo University as the most productive institution, and the International Journal of Pharmaceutics as the core journal in this domain. Keyword analysis indicated that current research focuses primarily on optimizing nanomaterial-based delivery systems, exploring combination formulations, and developing strategies for acne scar repair. Furthermore, our study suggests that potential future hotspots may include adjuvant photodynamic therapy (PDT), safety evaluation of nanomaterials, and the development of intelligent responsive systems.ConclusionThis study systematically summarizes the research landscape and developmental trends of nanomaterials in acne treatment. The findings provide clinicians and researchers with a rapid overview of the academic frontiers in this area, offering valuable references for clinical decision-making and the identification of future research directions.

Introduction

Acne is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the pilosebaceous unit. Its clinical manifestations include seborrhea (excessive sebum production), non-inflammatory lesions (open and closed comedones), inflammatory lesions (papules and pustules), and varying degrees of scar formation.1 Acne predominantly occurs during adolescence but can affect individuals of all age groups. Although not life-threatening, the visible facial damage caused by acne often leads to permanent scarring and exerts a profound impact on patients’ psychological well-being.2 Further studies have demonstrated that patients with acne have a significantly increased risk of psychological disorders, including depression, anxiety, suicidal ideation, and social dysfunction, and that these adverse psychological outcomes can be alleviated to some extent following standardized treatment.3,4

The etiology and pathogenesis of acne are complex and primarily involve four key mechanisms: hypersecretion of sebaceous glands, abnormal follicular keratinization, proliferation of Cutibacterium acnes, and localized inflammatory responses.5 Conventional therapeutic strategies mainly consist of topical antibiotics and retinoids, as well as physical modalities such as laser therapy, chemical peeling, and photodynamic therapy (PDT).6 However, these approaches are often associated with limitations, including skin irritation, photosensitivity, antibiotic resistance, and complex treatment regimens, thereby highlighting the need for more precise and efficient therapeutic strategies to improve patient adherence.7

With the continuous advancement of nanomedicine, nanomaterial-based drug delivery systems have demonstrated considerable potential in acne treatment. By enhancing targeted delivery to the pilosebaceous unit and enabling controlled drug release, these systems facilitate drug accumulation at lesion sites, thereby reducing nonspecific skin exposure, irritation, and systemic adverse effects.8 The application of nanocarriers has expanded from single-drug delivery to encompass targeted therapy, combination treatment, and post-acne scar repair. In recent years, research focus has further shifted toward stimuli-responsive nanocarriers, adjunctive photodynamic therapy, and comprehensive safety evaluation of novel nanomaterials. For instance, pH-, temperature-, or enzyme-responsive nanosystems can achieve precise drug release in response to changes in the local microenvironment of acne lesions, thereby improving therapeutic efficacy and safety.9 As carriers for photosensitizers, nanomaterials can encapsulate methylene blue, hexyl aminolevulinate (HAL), and polyene compounds, enhancing their loading efficiency, stability, and therapeutic performance while enabling controlled release.10 Nevertheless, certain nanomaterials may pose potential risks of cytotoxicity, systemic toxicity, and ecological hazards.11 Consequently, the recently proposed concept of “Safe-by-Design” emphasizes the systematic integration of toxicological assessment, biocompatibility optimization, and dosage control strategies at the early stages of nanomaterial development to ensure long-term safety in clinical applications.12

Despite the growing significance of nanomaterials in acne therapy, existing research findings remain fragmented, multidisciplinary, and insufficiently integrated, resulting in an unclear knowledge structure and limited comprehensive understanding of the evolution of research hotspots. Bibliometric analysis, integrating quantitative evaluation and visualization techniques, provides powerful tools for elucidating knowledge structures, identifying key research fronts, and predicting developmental trends.13 Co-citation networks, keyword co-occurrence maps, and cluster analyses can clearly illustrate the intrinsic relationships among research topics and their evolutionary trajectories. However, systematic bibliometric analyses focusing on nanomaterials in acne treatment are still relatively scarce, which hampers in-depth understanding of global research priorities and collaborative networks. Therefore, this study employs the Web of Science Core Collection in combination with bibliometric methods to conduct a comprehensive analysis of this field and construct multidimensional knowledge maps, aiming to uncover research hotspots, emerging trends, and major contributors (including authors, institutions, and collaborative networks), thereby providing a scientific reference for future research and clinical translation.

Materials and Methods

Data Collection and Screening

Web of Science is a widely recognized and standardized multidisciplinary database,14 noted for its broad coverage and strong authority. The literature it indexes has undergone rigorous peer review and can comprehensively reflect high-quality global research outputs.15 To ensure international representativeness and academic breadth, this study selected the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) as the primary data source. The specific search query was: (TS=(nano*)) AND TS=(acne*) AND DPD =(2012–01-01/2025-06-24). The search terms “nano*” and “acne*” capture all relevant variants beginning with “nano” and “acne”, including nanoparticles, nanomaterials, nanocarriers, nanocomposites, nanotechnology, as well as acne vulgaris, acne lesions, and post-acne scars, thereby ensuring completeness and breadth of retrieval. The initial search identified 557 records. Only English-language Articles and Reviews were retained, while Early Access, Meeting Abstracts, Editorial Materials, Corrections, Proceedings Papers, and non-English publications were excluded, removing 14 records in total. Because database retrieval may include superficially related studies that do not substantively match the research topic, a stringent manual screening was performed in two stages: first, titles and abstracts were used to exclude irrelevant records; second, full texts were reviewed to accurately identify studies pertinent to this work. In total, 242 papers were excluded. Ultimately, 301 publications were included for systematic analysis, comprising 254 Articles and 47 Reviews. Data Analysis

In this study, we employed a combination of CiteSpace, VOSviewer, Scimago Graphica, and R language to perform a comprehensive bibliometric analysis, leveraging the unique advantages of each tool to meet different research needs. Among them, CiteSpace and VOSviewer are two of the most widely used bibliometric analysis tools in academic research.17 CiteSpace, developed by Professor Chaomei Chen, is based on co-citation analysis theory and pathfinder network algorithms. By applying a variety of visualization techniques—such as clustering analysis, burst keyword detection, and timeline mapping—it reveals the intellectual base of a discipline and its frontier evolution pathways.18 In this study, CiteSpace was primarily applied for dual-map overlays of journals, keyword timeline mapping, and burst keyword analysis, with a focus on uncovering the knowledge foundation and evolutionary trends of nanomaterials in acne research. VOSviewer, developed by Van Eck and Waltman, specializes in co-occurrence analysis, bibliographic coupling, and collaboration network analysis. Its text-mining capabilities allow for the construction of more precise knowledge maps.19 Here, VOSviewer was mainly used to analyze and visualize international research collaborations, the academic output of core institutions and authors, journal publication and citation distributions, as well as thematic clustering and keyword co-occurrence patterns in the field. A country-level collaboration map was generated using Scimago Graphica,20 and R was used for statistical analyses and data visualization to assess publication trends, country-level metrics, and keyword clustering, providing a systematic overview of the research landscape and development of nanomaterials in acne treatment.

Research Publication Trends and Statistical Analysis

To explore the developmental trends of nanomaterials in acne research, we analyzed the annual publication output (Figure 2). From 2012 to 2025, the number of publications in this field showed an overall upward trend (R2 = 0.8163), which can be divided into two distinct phases. The first phase (2012–2019) was characterized by a gradual increase in publications, with an average of 13 articles per year. This period marked the initial establishment of the field and reflected the growing academic interest in nanomaterial-based acne therapies. The second phase (2019–2025) witnessed a significant acceleration in research activity. Although data collection was terminated on June 24, 2025—resulting in incomplete data for 2025—the overall trend suggests sustained growth in research enthusiasm, with publication output peaking at 42 articles in 2024 and averaging 32.8 publications per year during this phase. Based on the combined analysis of Figures 7 and 8, research on nanomaterials for acne treatment can be divided into three developmental stages:

Stage 1 Foundational Period (2012–2016): Research during this phase primarily focused on the mechanisms of transdermal delivery of nanomaterials and the nanonization of conventional anti-acne drugs. Core keywords included cells, skin permeation, in vitro, tretinoin, drug delivery, and combination. The central objective was to evaluate whether nanocarriers provided more effective drug delivery compared to traditional administration routes, marking the first attempts to combine classical drugs with nanomaterials for acne therapy. The strong bursts of topical drug delivery and hair follicles during this period highlighted the growing emphasis on transdermal delivery and follicular-targeted therapies.

Stage 2 Growth Period (2016–2019): Research focus gradually shifted toward diversification of nanocarrier types and the statistical optimization of experimental design. Core keywords in this stage included polymeric nanoparticles, nanocarriers, optimization, and drug penetration. Notably, polymeric nanoparticles and optimization also emerged as burst keywords. Alongside, cluster #4 (box–Behnken design) became active in the timeline map, indicating that research was progressing toward greater clinical relevance, standardization, and intelligent design. The application of statistical optimization methods improved material screening efficiency and formulation performance, fostering deeper integration of pharmaceutics, statistics, and materials science. This laid the groundwork for incorporating machine learning and AI predictive models into personalized nanotherapies, driving the trend of “intelligent design of nanomedicines”.

Stage 3 Deepening Period (2019–2025): During this stage, research directions expanded from single nanocarriers to multifunctional nanomaterials for combination therapies, with a gradual shift from basic experiments toward clinical applications. Core keywords included permeation, wound healing, silver nanoparticles, and Cutibacterium acnes. The emergence of cluster #5 (acne scars) and the burst of wound healing indicated that the application of nanomaterials in acne research had moved beyond the sole focus on inflammation control to encompass skin tissue regeneration and cosmetic repair. The activation of cluster #8 (polyvinyl alcohol), along with the bursts of silver nanoparticles, chitosan, and polyvinyl alcohol, highlighted that natural polymers, metallic nanoparticles, and functional polymers have become major research foci. Notably, the absence of keywords such as toxicity and long-term safety in both the timeline map and the burst keyword analysis suggests a marked lag in toxicological and safety evaluation studies of these new materials. In addition, the burst of permeation—despite skin permeation having already appeared during the foundational stage—underscores that skin permeability remains a core challenge requiring urgent breakthroughs in this field.

Conclusion and Implications

Analysis of Publication Trends

The results indicate a continuous growth in publications related to nanomaterials in acne treatment, reflecting increasing academic attention in this field. This trend is likely associated with the advantages of nanomaterials in enhancing transdermal penetration and enabling targeted delivery. Nanomaterials demonstrate good compatibility with various anti-acne agents, including antibiotics, retinoids, and natural plant extracts, while also offering the potential for multi-mechanistic combination therapies to address complex clinical challenges such as drug resistance and scar repair.

At the national level, India, China, and the United States constitute the primary research forces in this field. India and China lead in publication output, whereas the United States exhibits substantial academic influence through highly cited papers. Although collaboration among these three countries is relatively close, cooperation between other nations remains limited. Expanding global research networks, particularly through cross-team collaboration, resource integration, and knowledge sharing, will be essential in the future.

At the institutional level, several institutions in Egypt have shown outstanding performance, indicating a concentrated advantage in this research direction. In contrast, research in India and China appears more dispersed. Therefore, strengthening inter-institutional and interregional collaboration could enhance resource integration efficiency and the quality of research outputs.

At the author and journal level, core contributors such as Folle, Camila demonstrate high academic activity, and their work has a guiding influence on the direction and methodological approaches of nanomaterial-based acne therapies. The International Journal of Pharmaceutics leads both in publication volume and citation impact, underscoring its role as a key academic platform in the field. The continued concentration of research outputs in core journals may further promote systematic and in-depth development of this research area.

Hotspots and Emerging Frontiers in the Application of Nanomaterials for Acne

In bibliometric research, the analysis of frequently used keywords helps to uncover important themes and emerging directions, while keyword bursts often serve as indicators of future research trends in the field.32 These approaches provide a rapid means of understanding the distribution, evolution, and prospective trajectories of research hotspots in nanomaterials for acne treatment. Commonly encountered keywords include acne vulgaris, drug delivery, efficacy, Cutibacterium acnes, and formulation.

The keyword time-zone evolution map (Figure 7) shows that during 2012–2016, research primarily focused on keywords such as cells, skin permeation, in vitro, tretinoin, drug delivery, and combination. Between 2016 and 2019, attention shifted toward diversification of nanocarriers and optimization of statistical experimental design, reflected by the emergence of keywords including polymeric nanoparticles, nanocarriers, optimization, and drug penetration. In recent years (2020–2025), keywords such as permeation, wound healing, silver nanoparticles, and Cutibacterium acnes have appeared frequently, suggesting that they represent current hotspots in the prevention and treatment of acne using nanomaterials. The keyword burst map (Figure 8) further illustrates the chronological evolution of keyword clusters. Based on these findings, it can be concluded that research on nanomaterials for acne primarily centers on drug delivery, combination formulations, and cosmetic or reparative applications (eg acne scar management).

Construction and Optimization of Drug Delivery Systems

Acne primarily occurs in the pilosebaceous unit,33,34 and conventional topical formulations, such as retinoids and erythromycin, exhibit limited penetration, making it difficult to reach the core of inflammation. These treatments are also often associated with adverse effects, including night vision disturbances, skin dryness, scaling, and erythema.35 In contrast, nanocarrier-based drug delivery systems can enhance drug stability,36 promote follicular accumulation, increase local bioavailability, and reduce skin irritation. They further extend drug action through controlled and targeted release, lower dosing frequency, and improve patient adherence.37

Common nanocarriers applied in acne therapy include lipid-based systems (eg solid lipid nanoparticles [SLNs], nanostructured lipid carriers [NLCs], and nanoemulsions), polymeric nanoparticles (natural or synthetic, such as PLGA), and vesicular systems (eg liposomes, niosomes, ethosomes, transfersomes, and penetration-enhancing vesicles [PEVs]). Studies have shown that lipid-based systems can significantly improve the transdermal delivery of lipophilic drugs, such as retinoids and azelaic acid, while reducing skin irritation and enabling scalable production.38 Polymeric nanoparticles provide excellent controlled-release properties,39 whereas vesicular systems offer superior targeting and penetration capabilities, making them suitable for hydrophilic antimicrobials such as benzoyl peroxide and clindamycin, although their stability remains a challenge.40

With the introduction of Quality by Design (QbD) principles and the application of statistical methods such as Box–Behnken Design for formulation optimization,41,42 personalized and controllable drug delivery systems are gradually being realized. However, as the delivery behaviors and biological effects of multiple nanocarrier types become increasingly complex, relying solely on formulation performance metrics is insufficient for comprehensive efficacy evaluation. Therefore, systematic assessment combining clinical outcomes and safety is required. Currently, acne nanotherapy systems typically incorporate multidimensional evaluations, including Investigator Global Assessment (IGA), Global Acne Grading System (GAGS), Cutibacterium acnes microbiological analysis, as well as skin barrier and imaging-based metrics,43,44 providing guidance for formulation optimization and clinical translation.

Design of Multi-Mechanism Combination Formulations

The pathogenesis of acne involves multiple interrelated factors, including sebaceous hypersecretion, follicular hyperkeratinization, Cutibacterium acnes infection, and inflammatory responses.45 Its course is characterized by complexity and stage-specific changes. Traditional monotherapies are often limited in efficacy, whereas combination therapies generally demonstrate superior outcomes. However, the inconvenience of repeated dosing reduces patient compliance.46 In recent years, nanoplatform-based multi-mechanism combination therapies have emerged as a research hotspot. By incorporating different drugs, natural bioactives, or photosensitizers into a single nanocarrier, these systems can achieve antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory, and tissue-regenerative effects simultaneously. For example, encapsulating natural compounds such as quercetin or thymol into nanoparticles or liposomes not only enhances their stability and bioavailability but also enables multi-target intervention for synergistic anti-acne effects.47,48 Recent studies have also explored hybrid nanocarriers that co-deliver conventional anti-acne agents with phytochemicals. One example is isotretinoin–quercetin nanoemulsions, which mitigate isotretinoin-associated adverse effects (eg cheilitis, erythema, pruritus, depression, teratogenicity), while simultaneously providing antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and skin-repair functions.49 Therefore, nanomaterials can serve as versatile platforms for multi-functional combination formulations, integrating diverse agents into a single delivery system. This strategy not only enhances therapeutic efficacy but also has the potential to reduce drug dosages and side effects, ultimately improving patient adherence.

Cosmetic Repair and Anti-Scar Therapy in Acne

Acne not only causes inflammatory damage but also frequently leads to cosmetic sequelae such as post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation and atrophic scarring due to abnormal tissue remodeling after inflammation, thereby markedly affecting patients’appearance and psychological well-being.50,51 Current conventional treatment modalities mainly include laser therapy, microneedling (MN), fractional radiofrequency, and platelet-rich plasma (PRP); however, their therapeutic benefits are often limited and commonly accompanied by adverse effects such as transient pain, erythema, and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.52 In recent years, the application of nanomaterials has provided new strategies for post-inflammatory repair, antifibrotic intervention, and skin regeneration. For example, nanofat has demonstrated superior efficacy and safety compared with conventional fat grafting and shows advantages in refined tissue repair.53 Recent studies indicate that stromal vascular fraction (SVF) combined with nanofat yields improved outcomes in the treatment of acne scars and further promotes recovery of epidermal and dermal thickness,52 although the fat harvesting process may cause patient discomfort. In addition, other nanomaterial-based systems have also exhibited promising reparative potential. Biomimetic hyaluronic acid–stabilized zinc oxide nanoparticles (HA-ZnO), for instance, can continuously release low concentrations of zinc ions, thereby promoting fibroblast proliferation and migration, exerting anti-inflammatory and tissue-repair effects, and contributing to the improvement and prevention of acne scarring.54

Future Trends

Nanomaterial-Assisted Photodynamic Therapy for Acne

Photodynamic therapy (PDT) is a treatment that relies on specific light sources to activate photosensitizers (PS), generating reactive oxygen species to destroy target cells.55 Due to its antibacterial, anti-inflammatory, and sebaceous gland-suppressing effects, PDT has been increasingly applied in the management of acne.56,57 However, conventional PDT is limited by poor photosensitizer penetration, significant treatment-related pain, and pronounced epidermal phototoxic reactions.58 In recent years, the introduction of nanomaterials has provided novel solutions for PDT, establishing a major research direction in this field. Nanomaterials can not only serve as photosensitizers themselves but also integrate multiple functions, such as anti-inflammatory, antibacterial, and targeted delivery.59,60 Compared with traditional photosensitizers (eg 5-aminolevulinic acid [5-ALA], methylene blue [MB], indocyanine green, and indole-3-acetic acid), nanomaterial-based systems demonstrate superior loading efficiency, stability, and therapeutic efficacy.10 Moreover, nanomaterials enable controlled release of photosensitizers, markedly enhancing their in vivo stability and treatment safety while improving patient tolerability.25 Bibliometric analysis indicates that keywords such as photodynamic therapy, nano formulation, and 5-ALA have shown sustained citation bursts in the past five years, suggesting that this technology is undergoing rapid development and diversified applications. With the emergence of wearable light sources and smart-responsive carriers, nanomaterial-based PDT is expected to offer greater clinical potential in both precision treatment and postoperative recovery of acne.

Safety Evaluation and Standardization of Novel Nanomaterials

With the widespread application of functional nanomaterials in acne therapy, their biocompatibility, safety, and long-term toxicological effects have gradually become key research concerns. Although nanomaterials possess promising therapeutic functions, their small particle size, high surface area, and elevated reactivity may pose potential cytotoxicity to skin tissue cells.61 For example, silver nanoparticles can induce inflammation, apoptosis, and barrier disruption through ion release or direct interactions with cells,62 while zinc oxide nanoparticles primarily generate reactive oxygen species (ROS), causing oxidative stress, with toxicity closely related to particle concentration and exposure duration.63 Some materials, such as chitosan and polyvinyl alcohol (PVA), although exhibiting excellent antimicrobial activity and skin-repairing properties,64 still carry risks of cytotoxicity and organ damage,11 highlighting the need for systematic evaluation of nanoparticle behavior in inflammatory skin environments.

Regarding safety assessment, conventional two-dimensional cell models are limited in replicating the three-dimensional structure and immune microenvironment of skin, whereas animal models are constrained by interspecies differences.65 In recent years, three-dimensional skin models, reconstructed human epidermis, and microfluidic skin-on-chip systems have been increasingly employed in toxicological studies, enabling more realistic evaluation of nanoparticle penetration, accumulation, ROS generation, and pro-inflammatory responses.66–68 Despite advances in safety assessment technologies, clinical translation of nanomaterials still faces regulatory challenges across different countries and regions. Definitions and classification standards for nanomaterials remain inconsistent, and standardized frameworks for long-term skin exposure are not yet fully established, particularly regarding skin penetration, tissue accumulation, and potential systemic effects.69 Regulatory agencies, including the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA), have emphasized that reliable extrapolation of preclinical toxicology data to human applications remains a key challenge, highlighting the urgent need for systematic and unified evaluation frameworks.70

Bibliometric analysis indicates that material-related keywords such as “silver nanoparticles” and “chitosan” have shown strong bursts, whereas safety-related nodes including “toxicity”, “biosafety”, and “standardization” are relatively weak, suggesting that research in this field has predominantly focused on functionality and therapeutic efficacy. In the future, establishing unified evaluation standards and strengthening interdisciplinary collaboration across toxicology, dermatology, and pharmaceutics will be essential to balance efficacy and safety in nanomaterial-based acne therapy.

Integration of Smart Responsive Controlled-Release Systems and AI-Driven Optimization Strategies

The microenvironment of acne lesions is highly dynamic, and conventional nanocarrier systems often struggle to effectively respond to these changes and reach the target sites. Stimuli-responsive nanomaterials can exploit local signals such as decreased pH (from the normal 5.5–5.8 to approximately 4.5–5.0), elevated temperature, and the accumulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines (eg IL-1β, TNF-α) to achieve targeted delivery and on-demand drug release, thereby enhancing therapeutic efficacy.71 Among these strategies, pH-responsive nanocarriers are one of the most well-established. In the anaerobic microenvironment of acne lesions, Cutibacterium acnes metabolizes fatty acids to produce propionic and acetic acids,72 leading to a significant decrease in skin surface pH.73 Based on this principle, Guo et al developed Eudragit EPO-based pH-responsive nanocarriers, enabling rapid release of adapalene under acidic conditions and sustained release under neutral conditions, thereby reducing skin irritation.74 Beyond pH-responsive systems, thermosensitive nanogels, matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-degradable carriers, and near-infrared (NIR) photothermal-responsive systems have also been increasingly applied in disease treatment.75–77

More recently, artificial intelligence (AI) has been introduced to nanomedicine formulation optimization. By modeling particle size, drug loading, and release kinetics based on high-throughput experimental data, AI enables data-driven structural screening and efficacy prediction, facilitating the development of personalized controlled-release strategies.78

Bibliometric visualization shows that keywords such as “acid-responsive” “stimuli-sensitive” and “controlled release” have exhibited significant bursts in recent years, indicating rapid growth in this field, though a mature research framework has yet to be established. In the future, smart stimuli-responsive nanocarriers are expected to evolve from single-stimulus-responsive systems to multi-stimulus platforms, and, when combined with AI optimization algorithms and multifunctional materials, may drive acne therapy toward on-demand drug release and individualized treatment.

Conclusion

Acne is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the pilosebaceous unit that remains challenging to manage due to limited drug penetration, treatment-related adverse effects, and frequent relapse. Recent advances in nanotechnology have introduced novel drug delivery systems that improve follicular targeting, enhance local bioavailability, and enable controlled or stimuli-responsive drug release, thereby offering improved therapeutic efficacy and tolerability. In addition, emerging nanomaterial-based approaches show potential not only in inflammation control but also in post-acne repair and cosmetic outcomes.

However, the clinical translation of nanotechnology-based acne therapies is still constrained by incomplete safety data, lack of standardized evaluation methods, and regulatory uncertainties. Future studies should focus on clinically relevant efficacy endpoints, long-term safety assessment, and patient-centered outcomes to facilitate the integration of nanomedicine into routine acne management. With continued interdisciplinary collaboration, nanotechnology is expected to contribute to more precise, effective, and cosmetically favorable acne treatment strategies.

Limitations and Future Research Ideas

Based on standardized bibliometric methods combined with multidimensional visualization analyses, this study systematically delineated the research landscape and hotspots of nanomaterials for acne treatment; however, several limitations should be acknowledged. The data were exclusively retrieved from the SCIE, SSCI, and A&HCI indexes of the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) and were restricted to original articles and reviews. Although this strategy helps ensure data quality, it inevitably omits certain important outputs, such as conference proceedings, preprints, or studies indexed in PubMed and Scopus (eg draft clinical guidelines or policy reports), thereby potentially compromising the completeness of the analysis. Despite the use of multiple rounds of cross-validation to minimize errors, manual screening remains susceptible to subjective bias. In addition, hotspot identification based on citation counts is inherently time-lagged, leading to the possible underrepresentation of recently published studies that have not yet accumulated sufficient citations. From a methodological perspective, VOSviewer and CiteSpace primarily construct knowledge structures based on citation relationships. While advantageous for identifying research hotspots, these tools are unable to deeply mine full-text content and may overlook innovative contributions beyond abstracts and keywords. Moreover, the generated maps are based on static datasets and thus have limited capacity to reflect clinical implementation and technological translation processes, resulting in insufficient coverage of rapidly evolving emerging directions, such as intelligent stimuli-responsive controlled-release systems, AI-assisted prescription optimization, and nanomaterial-enhanced photodynamic therapy.

In light of these limitations, future studies may advance in parallel by expanding data sources, upgrading analytical methods, and implementing dynamic visualization monitoring. Building upon high-quality core databases such as WOSCC, the integration of multi-source data from PubMed and Scopus would enable a more comprehensive representation of scholarly outputs in this field. Concurrently, incorporating text mining and deep learning techniques to extract methodological details and innovative elements from full texts could compensate for the informational constraints of traditional citation-based analyses.79 Furthermore, strengthening international and interdisciplinary collaboration networks to promote resource sharing and knowledge exchange would facilitate both theoretical innovation and clinical translation of nanomaterials in acne treatment. In summary, the further integration of multi-source data with AI-optimized algorithms may enable more precise identification of research frontiers in acne studies, foster the development of emerging directions such as intelligent controlled release, skin barrier repair, and personalized therapy, and accelerate the translation of basic research into clinical applications.

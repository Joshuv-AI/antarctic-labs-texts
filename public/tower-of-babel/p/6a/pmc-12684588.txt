High‐Profile Cardiac Arrests Correlate With Online Searches and Public Interest: A Call for Action
Authors: Kirsten E. Shaw, Larissa Stanberry, Kevin M. Harris
Published: 2025

The cardiac arrests of high‐profile athletes highlight the interplay between popular culture and public health, as these incidents often generate significant media attention surrounding cardiovascular disease. 1 , 2 Our study analyzed global online Google search trends following cardiac arrests of 3 prominent athletes, aiming to understand how these events may have impacted public interest in cardiovascular health. We hypothesized that the cardiac arrests of high‐profile athletes would lead to significant increases in Google searches for “cardiac arrest.” Understanding these patterns could help health care professionals leverage moments of heightened public interest to promote accurate cardiovascular information.

We analyzed global online search trends for the term “cardiac arrest” before and after the cardiac arrests of 3 high‐profile athletes: Christian Eriksen (Danish soccer player during a match), Damar Hamlin (American football player during a game), and Bronny James (American college basketball player during practice). We selected these 3 cases for their public visibility and global media coverage, focusing on athlete cardiac arrests within the past 3 years. Using Google Trends, which reflects search queries made through Google's search engine, we collected global search volumes for the term “cardiac arrest” as well as the athlete names for 3 months preceding and following each cardiac event to assess changes in search interest over time. 3 The 3 months before the incident were used as a baseline. The change in volumes from baseline to 4 weeks following the incidence was estimated from the parsimonious quasi‐Poisson regression as well as a 2‐factor model with a time:athlete interaction term and time in weeks as a factor to account for the nonlinear change in volume following post‐–cardiac arrest surge. The model estimates are reported with their corresponding 95% CIs and P values. The analysis was done in the RStudio environment (2024.12.0, Posit Software, PBC). Data are available upon reasonable request. No institutional review board approval was required due to use of public, nonpatient data.

We observed significant increases in online search volumes for “cardiac arrest” following each cardiac arrest (Figure [A], top). Compared with baseline, search queries for cardiac arrest increased on average by 679% (95% CI, 517–887; P<0.001) in the week of the cardiac arrest. The magnitude of increase varied between athletes, with the largest 1200% (95% CI, 968–1500; P<0.001) increase occurring after Hamlin's cardiac arrest. The number of cardiac arrest searches rapidly returned to baseline, with an average 61% (95% CI, 39–76) drop in searches 1 week following the incident (Figure [B]). In Hamlin's case, the rate of decline was slower, with return to baseline at 4 weeks following the cardiac arrest.

The number of queries related to the athletes' names also significantly increased in the month following the incident compared with the 3 months prior (Figure 1A, bottom).

Heightened public interest in out‐of‐hospital cardiac arrests, as reflected by increased Google searches following high‐profile cases, highlights media's influence on shaping public understanding of cardiovascular emergencies. Such events not only capture public attention but drive societal change. For example, Eriksen's cardiac arrest sparked global discussions about bystander cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and automated external defibrillator (AED) accessibility, with 1 defibrillator supplier reporting a 500% increase in sales and inquiries. 2 Similarly, Hamlin's cardiac arrest raised awareness of the critical role emergency action plans, CPR, and AEDs play in saving lives. 1 The prolonged search interest following Hamlin's cardiac arrest may reflect sustained media attention and his ongoing advocacy for AED and CPR training throughout his recovery.

The American Heart Association and others have seized these moments to promote initiatives for recognizing cardiac arrest, performing bystander CPR, and advocating for AED placement in public venues, including the bipartisan HEARTS (Cardiomyopathy Health Education, Awareness, Research, and Training in Schools) Act, which promotes CPR training and AED availability in schools and was publicly supported by Hamlin following his cardiac arrest. 4 The National Football League and American Heart Association also formed a coalition to improve game emergency preparedness, enhance public awareness, and expand access to lifesaving interventions. 5

Understanding these media‐driven spikes in public interest is crucial for health care professionals aiming to leverage these moments for educational and preventive interventions. The immediate moments following high‐profile athlete cardiac arrests represent an unparalleled opportunity to leverage heightened public interest to disseminate accurate and actionable health information. However, our findings demonstrate that public attention is often fleeting, and Google search trends decline rapidly within 1 week after a high‐profile event. This underscores the urgency of timely action, as delays in educational and policy‐driven initiatives risk missing the brief window when engagement is highest.

In an era where misinformation may go unchecked, it is imperative that health care professionals act quickly. Whether through national organizations, institutional channels, social media platforms, or media collaborations, we suggest a call to action for health care professionals/organizations to respond promptly with accurate, evidence‐based messaging during these windows of heightened public interest.

This is not merely an opportunity; it is an obligation to ensure that accurate health information is a leading headline in the wake of these unfortunate events.

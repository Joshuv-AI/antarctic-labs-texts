Optimization of hospital resource scheduling efficiency based on dynamic weighted distance anomaly detection algorithm
Authors: Ying Liu, Lanxian Mai, Feng Huang, Zhiyu Zeng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study developed an intelligent resource scheduling integrated model to address engineering challenges like load imbalance, low energy efficiency, and delayed anomaly detection in hospital communication and energy-saving systems. This model integrated the Dynamic Weighted Distance-Based Outlier Detection (DWD-OD) algorithm based on dynamic weighted distance with the Multimodal Resource Collaborative Optimization (MRCOF) framework at the system level. The DWD-OD algorithm improved the distance measurement method through engineering means, and constructed a dynamic weight matrix by introducing device types and load characteristics to adapt to the scale differences of heterogeneous data. By combining sliding time windows with local density threshold mechanisms, this scheme achieved real-time recognition of energy consumption events such as heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) overload or lighting anomalies. Experimental data showed that the detection accuracy of this method reached 95.3%. To achieve resource optimization, the model integrated an improved Genetic Simulated Annealing Algorithm (GSAA), forming a two-stage optimization strategy: Stage 1 managed virtual resources through OpenStack to isolate abnormal tasks; Stage 2 utilized Kubernetes for path optimization of containerized infrastructure to reduce communication latency and mitigate fragmentation risks. Based on a real dataset validation of the communication and energy system in a tertiary hospital, it was shown that under the hybrid cluster architecture, the overall energy efficiency of the system was improved to 89.7%, and the response time for critical tasks was controlled within 4.8 s. The integrated Development, Security, and Operations (DevSecOps) lightweight security control layer had increased vulnerability scanning efficiency by 27.3%. It should be pointed out that the validation of this study was only based on data from a single medical center, and some abnormal samples were enhanced by generative adversarial networks. Security assessment depends on the model simulation environment, and these factors may have certain applicability biases when extended to medical institutions of different sizes.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-44415-6.

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of medical digital transformation, hospital infrastructure is facing dual challenges: increasing complexity in communication networks and expanding energy consumption1. Under the national “dual carbon” strategy, hospitals—operating continuously 24 h a day as high-energy-consuming institutions—are increasingly constrained by resource scheduling issues in their communication and energy-saving systems. These challenges have become critical bottlenecks limiting both the quality of medical services and sustainable development capacity2,3.

Studies show that annual electricity consumption in top-tier hospitals can reach tens of millions of kilowatt-hours. Among this, Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC) systems account for 40%–60%, while medical equipment and lighting systems contribute another 25%–35%. However, current scheduling systems often suffer from severe load imbalances and rigid resource allocation schemes4, resulting in average monthly energy efficiency utilization rates of less than 65%. Even more concerning is the delayed detection of anomalous energy consumption events—such as excessive standby power in Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) machines or HVAC system overloads in operating rooms—with an average lag time of 2.3 h. These delays cause millions in annual energy waste and may trigger cascading equipment failures that jeopardize patient safety5,6.

Traditional rule-based scheduling methods cannot adapt to the real-time processing requirements of multi-source heterogeneous data. Classical machine learning approaches, such as K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), have limited applicability in medical scenarios, primarily due to their reliance on fixed feature-weighting mechanisms. This design does not account for the large disparities in energy consumption among medical devices. As a result, normal fluctuations in high-power equipment can overshadow anomalous signals from low-power devices, such as lighting systems. This imbalance produces a pronounced “masking effect.” Moreover, classical algorithms lack the capability to model spatiotemporal correlations, which has led to a prolonged stagnation in anomaly detection accuracy7,8. Their anomaly detection accuracy typically remains within 73%–78%.

This study aims to address critical technical bottlenecks in hospital resource scheduling by developing an intelligent, multimodal collaborative optimization framework that shifts energy management from “passive response” to “proactive prediction.” The primary objectives include: (1) designing a Dynamic Weighted Distance-Based Outlier Detection (DWD-OD) algorithm to address low anomaly detection accuracy caused by diverse device types and significant data scale differences in medical environments; (2) developing a two-stage resource optimization strategy that combines anomaly isolation with optimized energy allocation paths, thereby maximizing the use of renewable energy while ensuring service quality for critical medical operations; and (3) establishing an end-to-end, security-enhanced scheduling system that employs a lightweight security control layer (LSCL) to safeguard energy consumption data across its full lifecycle.

This study places special emphasis on the multimodal data characteristics generated by Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) devices9. It must account for the time-sensitive nature of physiological monitoring data (e.g., blood oxygen saturation, heart rate variability)10, while also coordinating the dynamic coupling between environmental control systems (e.g., ward temperature and humidity regulation) and energy supply systems (e.g., solar energy storage units)11.

The engineering contribution of this study is mainly reflected in three aspects. Firstly, an Adaptive Mahalanobis Distance (AMD) measurement module is designed, which dynamically adjusts feature weights by introducing a device operating state transition matrix, improving the adaptability of traditional detection algorithms in medical heterogeneous data scenarios. Secondly, a joint mechanism of sliding time window and local density threshold is constructed to capture the essential differences between the periodic fluctuations in energy consumption and sudden anomalies of medical equipment in engineering implementation. Thirdly, the improved Genetic Simulated Annealing Algorithm (GSAA) transforms Service Level Agreement (SLA) constraints into penalty terms in the fitness function, balancing solution efficiency and solution quality when solving NP hard resource optimization problems.

From a practical perspective, the study findings can be directly applied to the intelligent transformation of hospitals’ new infrastructure. The OpenStack-based virtualization isolation mechanism ensures the safe handling of anomalous energy consumption units, while the microservice architecture supported by Kubernetes container orchestration enables resource allocation at the process level, meeting the mandatory requirements of the “Medical Information System Real-Time Specification.” Moreover, the integrated security control layer, based on the Development, Security, and Operations (DevSecOps) approach, employs dual mechanisms of differential privacy protection and blockchain auditing. It offers an innovative solution for the collaborative protection of patient privacy data and energy management systems.

The significance of this study lies in its provision of innovative solutions for hospital resource management from both theoretical and practical perspectives. On the theoretical side, the proposed dynamic weighted distance anomaly detection algorithm and the genetic simulated annealing optimization framework offer new methodological guidance for handling heterogeneous medical data and addressing multi-objective constrained optimization problems. On the practical side, the integrated framework of multimodal collaborative optimization and lightweight security control effectively enhances the responsiveness of critical hospital operations and improves system energy efficiency, while significantly reducing data security risks. This directly supports hospitals in achieving refined operations and advancing national strategic goals of energy conservation and emission reduction. It also provides reliable technical support and scalable implementation pathways for building future smart hospital infrastructures. In doing so, the study demonstrates broad industry application prospects and high promotion value. Related work

In recent years, research in hospital resource management has shown a trend of multidimensional technology integration, with scholars exploring various aspects such as data mining, process optimization, and intelligent algorithms. Ferrão et al. (2021) proposed a systematic data mining framework based on Electronic Health Records (EHR)12, which dynamically predicted bed turnover rates and operating room utilization using association rule mining and random forest classification. Their study revealed the nonlinear mapping relationship between medical operation logs and resource demand. Similarly, Liu et al. (2022) developed an outpatient volume prediction model based on Genetic Programming (GP)13. By conducting symbolic regression analysis, they constructed a multivariate prediction equation incorporating weather factors and epidemiological indicators, reducing the prediction error rate to 8.7%. In the area of resource allocation mechanisms, Talaat (2022) designed an effective prediction and resource allocation method (EPRAM) in a fog computing environment14, using deep reinforcement learning to optimize the allocation priorities of CT (Computed Tomography) scanners and ICU (Intensive Care Unit) beds. This approach resulted in a 14.2% improvement in response time for delay-sensitive task scheduling. These studies provide methodological foundations for hospital resource demand forecasting and static allocation. However, they generally lack dynamic response mechanisms to real-time anomalous events and do not fully consider the coupling relationship between energy consumption characteristics and the quality of medical services.

Recent studies have progressively advanced toward deep multi-source data fusion to overcome the limitations of single-dimensional data. Zhao et al. (2022)15 developed an attention-based multimodal fusion model that effectively captured intrinsic correlations among heterogeneous sensor data in real-world human activity recognition tasks. Focusing on complex gait simulation and rehabilitation prediction, Zhang et al. (2025)16 proposed a spatio-temporal attention-based multimodal fusion network (ST-AMMFN), which significantly improved feature extraction accuracy. These studies indicate that, in medical scenarios involving heterogeneous data, dynamically modeling spatio-temporal correlations among variables is critical for enhancing detection performance. This insight provides direct methodological support for the dynamic-weight-based DWD-OD algorithm proposed in this study. In the context of multi-objective resource scheduling under complex environments, Kalra and Singh (2021)17 introduced a hybrid scheduling approach that addressed energy optimization for cloud workflows under strict deadline constraints. Aslan (2025)18 employed an artificial neural network–driven multi-objective optimization strategy to balance energy awareness and production efficiency in flow-shop scheduling scenarios. Within edge-computing environments for the IoMT, Alatoun et al. (2022)19 designed a low-latency task scheduling framework that reduced energy consumption at fog nodes while improving response time. Furthermore, the survey by Violos et al. (2025)20 emphasized that next-generation decision-making systems must incorporate cognitive and context-aware capabilities to enable sustainable computing environments. Although these studies demonstrate the advantages of hybrid heuristic algorithms in handling multi-constraint optimization problems, they also reveal an integration gap when addressing the hospital-specific triad of high reliability, low latency, and strong security requirements.

As healthcare systems become increasingly digitized, data security and integrity have emerged as indispensable dimensions of resource scheduling. Naik et al. (2025)21 proposed a hybrid deep-learning-based security framework to enhance data integrity and operational performance in Healthcare Internet of Things (H-IoT) environments. Their work highlighted the necessity of deeply integrating security mechanisms with operational workflows. Similarly, Fazrina (2024)22 investigated adaptive encryption algorithms in distributed sensor systems for intelligent energy networks. The results confirmed that dynamically adjusting encryption strength based on environmental conditions could effectively balance security and system overhead. Together, these studies provide a solid theoretical foundation for the lightweight security control layer proposed in this work. They suggest that security strategies should be treated as endogenous variables throughout the entire resource-scheduling lifecycle, rather than as external, add-on defense mechanisms.

In the areas of anomaly detection and process optimization, Rashed et al. (2023) applied process mining technology to analyze deviation patterns in emergency department patient care processes23. Using the α-algorithm and heuristic mining, they identified 23.5% of non-value-added care steps, providing data support for process reengineering. Twumasi et al. (2022) proposed a hybrid prediction framework combining Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks and robust regression for anomaly detection in blood inventory management24, reducing the root mean square error (RMSE) in blood demand forecasting to 15.8 units. Tabassum et al. (2024) focused on threat detection in intelligent health systems, employing the Isolation Forest algorithm to identify anomalous patterns in vital sign data25, achieving a detection accuracy of 89.4% in a cardiac monitoring scenario. Although these methods improve anomaly detection capabilities in specific scenarios, most feature engineering relies on manual prior knowledge, making it difficult to adaptively handle the complex scenarios of heterogeneous data and diverse device types in hospital energy systems. Moreover, they have not been integrated into a closed-loop interaction with resource scheduling decisions.

In the field of maintenance strategies and system optimization, Ahmed et al. (2022) proposed a two-stage predictive maintenance framework based on multi-objective optimization. By employing the NSGA-II (Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II), the framework balances equipment maintenance costs with failure risks, resulting in a 98.2% availability of hospital building systems26. Rahul et al. (2023) provided a systematic review emphasizing the transformative potential of big data analytics in hospital operations. They identified significant gaps in current research regarding cross-modal data fusion and real-time decision support27. Symum et al. (2020) developed a machine learning model to predict the length of stay (LOS) for chronic disease patients, achieving an Area Under Curve (AUC) value of 0.87 using the Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) algorithm28, providing new insights into bed resource planning. These advancements have driven the intelligent evolution of hospital resource management; however, their optimization objectives typically focus on a single dimension (such as time or cost) and fail to incorporate energy efficiency, safety risks, and service quality into a unified optimization framework. Additionally, they lack lightweight security mechanisms.

In the broader field of cloud and edge computing resource scheduling, a series of studies has provided valuable insights into addressing multi-objective optimization problems under complex constraints. Khaleel et al. (2023a) proposed an AI-based workflow scheduling scheme that was designed to jointly optimize cloud computing reliability and end-to-end latency control29. Building on this, Khaleel et al. (2024b) investigated a hybrid meta-heuristic method that integrated dynamic voltage and frequency scaling to optimize scientific workflow scheduling in green edge–cloud environments30. Further, Khaleel et al. (2024c) analyzed the trade-off between energy consumption and latency in cloud environments, with particular attention to processor utilization and the role of the mean gray wolf optimizer31. In addition, Khaleel et al. (2023d) introduced a hybrid multi-objective optimization algorithm for cloud job scheduling, based on merge-and-split theory32. These studies explored scheduling optimization in general-purpose computing through AI and meta-heuristic approaches, offering methodological inspiration for the present research. However, they did not directly address the unique challenges of healthcare infrastructure, including heterogeneous device data, strict clinical response time requirements, and the combined constraints of energy efficiency and security. Building on this gap, the present study seeks to develop an end-to-end solution tailored for hospital environments that deeply integrates anomaly detection, resource optimization, and lightweight security control.

Previous studies have developed relatively mature methodological frameworks across three directions—prediction, recognition, and scheduling—and each line of research has achieved notable progress within its own scope. However, clear structural discontinuities remain among these strands. Taking data-driven prediction models as an example, most existing approaches rely on static features or single-modality inputs. While such models can produce relatively stable demand trends, they struggle to capture the pronounced temporal volatility of medical device workloads. As a result, their predictive responses to sudden energy-consumption anomalies tend to be delayed. Intelligent scheduling algorithms demonstrate strong optimization capabilities, yet they are often designed under abstract computing-resource assumptions. Consequently, they lack an understanding of hospital-specific data couplings, such as the interdependencies among device operational states, energy-consumption patterns, and clinical response latency. Multimodal fusion research has shown considerable potential in handling complex sensor data. Nevertheless, most existing work remains focused on recognition and representation tasks and has not been extended to resource-management systems. This limitation prevents such methods from supporting the end-to-end decision chains required for real-time hospital scheduling.

These limitations become more pronounced when examined within the real operational context of hospital energy and communication systems. Critical medical equipment performs highly time-sensitive tasks, and energy-consumption relationships among devices are not linearly additive. Once an abnormal load occurs, scheduling strategies must rapidly reallocate resources while maintaining overall energy efficiency. Moreover, when security mechanisms operate independently of the main scheduling pipeline, information latency can amplify system risks, making timely resource isolation and migration difficult to achieve. These gaps indicate that approaches focusing solely on prediction, recognition, or scheduling are insufficient for constructing an integrated framework that spans device-state monitoring, anomaly response, resource allocation, and security control.

For this reason, the present study adopts a tightly integrated design that combines a dynamic weighted distance model, a two-stage scheduling strategy, and a lightweight security layer. The dynamic weighted distance mechanism captures genuine differences in energy-consumption characteristics across devices, enabling anomaly detection without reliance on fixed feature weights. The two-stage scheduling strategy links anomaly handling with energy-efficiency optimization, thereby eliminating the disjunction commonly observed between detection and scheduling modules. The embedded security control layer synchronizes vulnerability scanning, encryption, and access control with the scheduling process, addressing the long-neglected issue of security–scheduling coupling in existing literature. Through this integration, the scheduling system shifts from passive reaction to proactive governance and aligns more closely with the practical operational requirements of hospital environments.

Research model

Design of dynamic weighted distance-based outlier detection algorithm

In medical device energy-consumption monitoring scenarios, feature variables often exhibit substantial differences in magnitude as well as strong inter-feature correlations. Traditional Euclidean distance assumes that the feature space is orthogonal and identically distributed. As a result, numerical fluctuations from high-power devices—such as MRI systems—can completely overshadow anomalous signals from low-power devices, including sensor nodes, leading to a pronounced “large-number-dominates-small-number” effect. Although the standard Mahalanobis distance accounts for feature covariance, its reliance on a globally static covariance matrix entails high computational cost and limits its ability to adapt to real-time changes in device operating conditions. To overcome these limitations, this study adopted the AMD. By introducing a dynamic weighting matrix, AMD adjusts the contribution of each feature dimension in real time according to the current operational state of the device. This approach not only mitigates the impact of scale disparities but also resolves coupling correlations among heterogeneous data, thereby ensuring robust anomaly detection under highly variable operating conditions.

Hospital energy-consumption data exhibit pronounced non-stationarity, as they are strongly influenced by seasonal factors, time-of-day variations, and unexpected public health events—a phenomenon commonly referred to as “concept drift.” Conventional approaches based on fixed windows or full historical data analysis often suffer from significant latency. They fail to update statistical properties in a timely manner, which leads to delayed responses to newly emerging anomaly patterns. The sliding-window mechanism employed in this study effectively constructs a dynamic memory model with an inherent “forgetting” capability. It retains only local density characteristics from the most recent time period and automatically discards outdated samples as new data arrive. This mechanism allows the local density threshold in the Local Outlier Factor (LOF) algorithm to drift adaptively with changes in the environmental baseline. Consequently, the method remains sensitive to sudden anomalies while substantially reducing false positives caused by background environmental shifts.

Container orchestration and path optimization constitute typical non-deterministic polynomial-time hard (NP-hard) combinatorial optimization problems. Standard genetic algorithms (GA) and particle swarm optimization (PSO) techniques have been widely applied in such contexts. However, when faced with the high dimensionality and strict constraints of hospital resource scheduling, these methods are prone to premature convergence. These constraints include latency, energy consumption, and security requirements. This issue becomes particularly severe when the solution space contains numerous suboptimal local peaks, as population-based evolutionary strategies alone often struggle to escape local optima. To address this challenge, the MRCOF framework integrates a simulated annealing (SA) mechanism into a GA, forming the GSAA strategy. The core of this strategy lies in the introduction of the Metropolis acceptance criterion, which allows inferior solutions to be accepted with a certain probability during the early stages of the search. This mechanism endows the algorithm with controlled “oscillation” capability within the solution space, enabling it to traverse local extrema and achieve an effective balance between global exploration and fine-grained exploitation. As a result, the framework is able to identify energy-efficient configurations that more closely approximate the global optimum under strict response-time constraints.

The core objective of the DWD-OD algorithm is to address the differences in scale, distribution, and correlation of heterogeneous equipment data in hospitals by using dynamic weights and adaptive distance metrics. This allows for the accurate identification of anomalous energy consumption nodes33. As shown in Fig. 1, DWD-OD first normalizes the input multidimensional time series data to eliminate the impact of dimensional differences on distance calculations.

DWD-OD algorithm process.

Figure 1 shows the complete workflow of the proposed DWD-OD. The process begins with the input and normalization of raw energy consumption data from heterogeneous hospital devices. This step eliminates dimensional differences across data indicators. The algorithm then enters the dynamic weight calculation stage, which considers two key factors: device-type weight and real-time load fluctuation coefficient. The device-type weight is predefined based on the device’s importance in medical activities. In contrast, the load fluctuation coefficient is captured through a sliding time window to reflect the recent operational stability of the device. Together, these two factors form a dynamic weight matrix. This matrix is applied in an improved AMD calculation, allowing for more accurate measurement of differences between data points. Using these distances, the system constructs a local density distribution for each data point and calculates its LOF. Finally, the LOF of each data point is compared against a dynamic local density threshold. Based on this comparison, the system determines whether the data point is an abnormal energy consumption node and outputs a label of either “abnormal” or “normal.” This label then triggers the subsequent resource scheduling response.

Multimodal resource collaborative optimization framework

The core objective of the Multimodal Resource Collaborative Optimization Framework (MRCOF) is to integrate outlier detection results from the DWD-OD algorithm. This integration enables optimized resource scheduling, helping to achieve load balancing and maximize energy efficiency in hospital communication and energy-saving systems. As shown in Fig. 2, MRCOF consists of two stages: the first stage isolates anomalous tasks and manages virtualization resources, while the second stage optimizes energy-efficient resource allocation paths within containerized systems. A LSCL ensures the security of data transmission and scheduling processes.

Based on the anomalous energy consumption nodes detected by DWD-OD—such as air conditioning system overload or abnormal power use by medical equipment—MRCOF takes targeted action. To ensure the reliability of medical data during transmission and processing, this study’s design incorporated robust fault-tolerance mechanisms for both network link failures and computing node outages. At the communication level, the system uses a reliable transport protocol with acknowledgment and retransmission to maintain data integrity. Link status is continuously monitored in real time through heartbeat messages and timeout detection. At the infrastructure level, the platform takes advantage of high-availability features built into Kubernetes and OpenStack, including automatic instance failover and state replication. When a health check detects an edge node failure or persistent network interruption, the resource scheduler immediately triggers an exception handling process. Affected tasks are automatically rescheduled to healthy nodes, and all fault events are logged for traceability. Together, these measures create a resilient framework that minimizes the risk of service disruption from single-point failures or network fluctuations, ensuring the continuity of critical medical operations.

The configuration of core parameters in this study is determined by considering prior knowledge in the medical domain, theoretical requirements of the algorithms, and preliminary experimental validation. Device-type weights are directly set according to the critical importance of medical equipment for patient safety. This parameter is highly sensitive; reducing the weight of life-support devices decreases the algorithm’s sensitivity to their anomalies, potentially causing missed detections. The length of the sliding time window is determined by balancing detection timeliness and data stability. Shortening the window increases responsiveness to sudden anomalies but raises the false positive rate, whereas lengthening it produces the opposite effect. The local density threshold is optimized via grid search on the validation set and serves as a key hyperparameter to balance recall and precision. In the genetic simulated annealing algorithm, crossover and mutation probabilities follow an exponential decay strategy, enhancing global exploration in the early iterations and promoting local refinement later. Their initial values and decay coefficients are determined through multiple trial runs and have a significant impact on convergence speed and solution quality. The weights in the fitness function reflect the higher priority of communication latency over energy cost in hospital scenarios; adjusting these weights directly influences the scheduler’s optimization direction. Finally, the key update interval and vulnerability scan interval in lightweight attribute-based encryption are configured to balance security strength with system overhead.

To ensure that the experimental environment faithfully reproduced real-world medical scenarios, this study constructed a fine-grained simulation framework that covered the entire pipeline, from data generation to uncertainty quantification. The datasets used in the experiments were not purely synthetic. Instead, they were derived from six months of real operational logs collected from a tertiary hospital. At the data acquisition layer, telemetry data were synchronously collected at 5-second intervals from 450 terminal nodes. These nodes included MRI imaging systems, ventilator clusters, and HVAC environmental control systems. Data collection was implemented through Message Queuing Telemetry Transport (MQTT) and Simple Network Management Protocol (SNMP) interfaces. After denoising, cleaning, and temporal alignment, the raw data were divided into training (60%), validation (20%), and test (20%) sets. Because anomalous samples are inherently scarce in medical energy-consumption data, a Time-series Generative Adversarial Network (TimeGAN) was employed to augment abnormal energy-use patterns. This approach ensured class balance in the evaluation dataset.

The simulation system operated under a set of assumptions designed to closely reflect physical reality. Edge computing nodes were assumed to have limited processing capacity constrained by their Thermal Design Power (TDP). When workloads exceeded this threshold, forced frequency scaling was triggered. Network links were modeled as non-ideal. Transmission latency followed a truncated normal distribution to simulate real network jitter, and a random packet loss rate of 0.01% was introduced to reflect link instability. Task arrival patterns followed a non-homogeneous Poisson process, capturing the periodic fluctuations between peak and off-peak hospital operations.

The selection of core algorithm parameters was guided by rigorous empirical validation. Key hyperparameters—such as the sliding-window length in the DWD-OD algorithm and the crossover and mutation probabilities in GSAA—were not set heuristically. Instead, a fine-grained grid search was conducted on the validation set. The evaluation objective combined the F1 score and convergence speed as a weighted criterion. The final parameter configuration achieved an optimal balance between generalization performance and response efficiency.

To address the inherent randomness of cloud edge collaborative environments, this study introduces a strict uncertainty quantification mechanism. All performance indicators are not single run results, but report the statistical mean of 50 MCS. The performance comparison of anomaly detection algorithms is shown in Fig. 4. In this comparison, the proposed DWD-OD algorithm significantly outperforms traditional methods such as KNN and LOF. It achieves higher detection accuracy (95.3%), recall rate (97.1%), and F1-score (96.2%). Additionally, it reduces computation time to 9.8 ms per sample, which is 20.3% lower than that of KNN. This advantage is due to the AMD’s dynamic weighting of heterogeneous data, and the sliding time window mechanism’s real-time update of local density thresholds. As a result, the algorithm can capture subtle anomaly signals (e.g., occasional overload of blood oxygen devices) while avoiding false positives caused by normal device fluctuations. Additionally, the improved computational efficiency of DWD-OD verifies the effectiveness of incremental covariance updates and weight sparsification strategies. The superior detection accuracy and recall of the DWD-OD algorithm stem from its deep understanding of the heterogeneous operating characteristics of medical devices. Traditional methods, such as KNN, typically measure distances within a unified feature space. As a result, they often misclassify normal operational fluctuations of large medical equipment—such as the high-frequency load variations of ventilators—as anomalies, while overlooking subtle energy drifts in low-power systems like lighting. By introducing device-type weights and real-time load fluctuation coefficients, DWD-OD constructs a dynamically adaptive metric space. This design effectively filters out compliant operational peak noise and enables precise identification of genuine energy anomalies. In terms of computational efficiency, the algorithm achieves a low latency of 9.8 ms. This performance is primarily attributed to its incremental covariance update strategy, which avoids the repeated global recalculation required by conventional methods when processing streaming data. Consequently, DWD-OD is well suited to the real-time monitoring requirements of hospital environments.

Energy efficiency utilization comparison is shown in Fig. 5. The MRCOF achieves 89.7% energy efficiency utilization in a hybrid cluster. This represents a 34.1% improvement over traditional polling-based scheduling. Additionally, carbon emissions are reduced to 0.41 kgCO₂/kWh. These gains result from a two-stage optimization strategy. In the first stage, abnormal tasks are isolated using OpenStack, which reduces energy waste from resource contention. In the second stage, the GSAA algorithm optimizes the container deployment path by prioritizing nodes with a high share of renewable energy, such as solar-powered servers. Furthermore, the MRCOF’s abnormal task isolation success rate (96.8%) demonstrates its ability to quickly respond to sudden load changes, preventing cascading failures caused by device overload. This result directly validates the effectiveness of the MRCOF framework’s “isolate first, then optimize” strategy. Once abnormally high energy-consuming tasks are identified in the first stage, they are immediately confined to dedicated virtual machine groups. This mechanism prevents anomalous workloads from disorderly encroaching on overall cluster resources and eliminates additional energy fluctuations caused by resource contention. In the second stage, the GSAA algorithm plays a pivotal guiding role. Rather than blindly pursuing load balancing, it intelligently steers containerized tasks toward nodes with abundant renewable energy supplies, such as solar power. This energy-quality-aware scheduling enables the system to sustain high-load operation while substantially reducing reliance on the conventional power grid, thereby achieving simultaneous reductions in carbon emissions and energy costs.

Security control effectiveness and data protection

The efficiency and coverage of vulnerability scanning are shown in Fig. 8. Under the incremental scanning strategy, the DVSE reduced scanning time from 350 s in full scan mode to 165 s, resulting in a 52.9% improvement in efficiency. Furthermore, the vulnerability detection rate (97.8%) and CVE coverage rate (98.6%) significantly outperformed random sampling and rule-based methods. The reduction in the false positive rate (4.3%) is due to the CVSS risk scoring weighting mechanism. This mechanism combines exploitability and patch timeliness. It helps filter out low-risk false positives, such as historical records of patched vulnerabilities. The efficiency and accuracy of DVSE provide reliable support for the continuous security operation and maintenance of hospital systems. The breakthrough in the scanning efficiency of the DVSE engine stemmed primarily from abandoning redundant full-scale scans in favor of an incremental strategy that focused exclusively on changes. For base image layers that remained unmodified, the system directly reused historical security snapshots, thereby concentrating computational resources on newly deployed code and configuration items and substantially shortening the time window required for each scan. The CVSS risk scoring mechanism played a “filtering” role in this process by jointly considering vulnerability exploitability and patch timeliness, automatically suppressing a large number of low-risk or purely theoretical false positives. As a result, operations personnel were relieved from being overwhelmed by excessive alerts and could instead focus on mitigating high-risk vulnerabilities that posed a genuine threat of data leakage, achieving an effective balance between security coverage and operational efficiency.

In summary, the findings revealed the underlying logic of the multimodal collaborative mechanism and demonstrated that the coupling effects among system modules were substantially greater than a simple aggregation of individual technologies. The high-precision detection achieved by the DWD-OD algorithm was not an isolated accomplishment; rather, it constituted the cornerstone of the entire scheduling pipeline. By eliminating a large volume of false anomalies through a dynamic weighting mechanism, the algorithm effectively purified the decision-making environment for subsequent MRCOF scheduling. This enabled scarce computational resources to be directed toward genuinely high-value medical tasks instead of being consumed by spurious alerts. Such a cascading “cleansing–optimization” effect provided a coherent explanation for why the system continued to outperform traditional solutions in both overall response time and energy efficiency, even after the introduction of high-intensity security encryption modules. The comprehensive performance data presented in this real-world hospital deployment verification is based on the idealized network environment of high-performance computing nodes and hybrid cloud edge clusters listed in Table 2. When dealing with irregular protocol messages generated by outdated medical equipment, or facing extreme conditions such as severe packet loss and frame loss in wide area networks, the response delays of both the MRCOF and the LSCL may experience varying degrees of drift. The 4.8-second critical task response time mentioned here cannot cover all occasional network congestion scenarios.

The performance of the LSCL reinterprets the trade-off between security and efficiency. Although encryption and vulnerability scanning mechanisms introduce marginal computational overhead, this real-time edge defense establishes a necessary trust boundary. Compared to the potential medical accidents and trust crises caused by data breaches, millisecond level latency is a necessary insurance cost for the long-term stable operation of the system. It is also a manifestation of the DevSecOps paradigm’s transformation of security from passive defense to endogenous immune capabilities of the system. This ensures that energy efficiency optimization is built on the basis of fully respecting patient data privacy in an open IoMT environment.

At a fundamental level, the proposed solution redefined the operational paradigm of hospital infrastructure. Rather than pursuing algorithmic optimality along a single dimension, it achieved a dynamic equilibrium among heterogeneous device management, green computing, and information security through organic inter-module collaboration. This capacity for balance endowed hospital cyber–physical systems with adaptive resilience under complex operating conditions, enabling them to respond calmly to challenges ranging from traffic surges triggered by public health emergencies to routine workload fluctuations. Consequently, the proposed framework offered a viable technical pathway toward the development of next-generation smart hospitals with self-perception and self-healing capabilities.

Conclusions

The scheduling scheme established in this study achieved the expected indicators within the controlled experimental system, but its engineering transformation is still constrained by multiple boundary conditions. The model training data was all collected from a large comprehensive medical institution, failing to cover the differences in energy supply and communication network architecture between community clinics or specialized hospitals. The strategy of using a time series generative adversarial network to fill in the minority of abnormal samples inevitably introduces the distribution patterns of artificial data, which can weaken the model’s sensitivity to capture rare and complex unknown abnormal events in real-world scenarios. The reported improvement rates of various security protections are derived from routine scans and simulated adversarial drills based on the known CVE database. In the face of advanced persistent threats with high concealment and long-term latency characteristics, there is significant uncertainty about the actual interception effectiveness of this defense mechanism. The future research plan focuses on conducting long-term cross-domain deployment tests in collaboration with multiple heterogeneous medical centers at different levels, utilizing digital twins technology to construct a full lifecycle hospital energy consumption mapping space, and systematically evaluating and repairing the robustness defects of algorithms in complex hardware environments.

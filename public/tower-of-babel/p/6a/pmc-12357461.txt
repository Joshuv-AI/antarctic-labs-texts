Analysis of the status of leprosy symptom surveillance in Guangdong province: an exploring effort to promote early detection
Authors: Lei Chen, Quan Luo, Haoze Xue, Jing Wang, Xiaohua Wang, Cheng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo understand the implementation of leprosy symptom surveillance in Guangdong Province, explore its role in early detection of leprosy, and provide a theoretical basis for leprosy prevention and control.MethodsData from the National Leprosy Prevention and Management Information System (LEPMIS) and Guangdong Suspected Leprosy Symptom Surveillance System were collected, including symptom surveillance data (2018–2023) and newly diagnosed leprosy case records (2012–2023). The newly diagnosed leprosy case records were divided into pre-implementation (2012–2017) and post-implementation (2018–2023) of symptom surveillance. The effectiveness of the symptom surveillance program was evaluated by assessing the surveillance coverage and clinical outcomes, including but not limited to coverage rate, delayed discovery time, and early diagnosis. Database was established and checked for logical errors, followed by descriptive statistical analysis, chi-square test using SPSS 24.0.ResultsBy the end of 2023, the number of participating medical institutions was 2,891, increased by 92.3% compared to 2018 when the symptom surveillance was first initiated, and the county-level coverage rate increased from 11.3 to 97.6%. After implementation of symptom surveillance, the proportions of resident patients, Han ethnicity, and farmers or herders decreased significantly (p < 0.05). The delayed discovery time decreased from (36.2 ± 2.0) months to (31.8 ± 3.1) months, and the patients delayed discovery time decreased from (27.7 ± 1.8) months to (20.8 ± 2.5) months, both showing a statistically significant difference (p < 0.05).DiscussionOur findings indicate that the leprosy symptom surveillance program in Guangdong Province has effectively reduced delayed discovery time, particularly patients delayed discovery time in seeking medical care, thereby facilitating early detection of leprosy cases.

Introduction

Leprosy (Hansen’s disease) is a chronic infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium leprae, categorized as a neglected tropical disease (NTD). It mainly damages the skin and peripheral nerves, presenting as numb skin patches and nerve enlargement, potentially causing permanent disabilities in advanced cases [1, 2]. The extensive implementation of multi-drug therapy (MDT) recommended by World Health Organization (WHO) and combined with socioeconomic advancements has led to leprosy becoming a low-prevalence disease in China. According to statistics from the Leprosy Management Information System of China (LEPMIS), a total of 2,479 registered cases and 521 new cases of leprosy were reported. In 2018, the national prevalence was 0.018 per 10,000 inhabitants, and the detection rate was 0.037 per 100,000 inhabitants [3, 4]. Meanwhile, in Guangdong province a total of 199 registered cases and 34 new cases of leprosy were reported, with a prevalence of 0.018 per 10,000 inhabitants and detection rate of 0.030 per 100,000 inhabitants in 2023.

Although leprosy in Guangdong Province is currently at a low endemic level, Guangdong province has notified the highest number of cases in history, records showed that 97,039 people were affected by leprosy by the end of 2023, representing approximately one-fifth of all leprosy patients in China. Furthermore, Guangdong Province reported 6 pediatric leprosy cases between 2019 and 2023, with a detection rate of 0.0008 per 100,000 inhabitants in 2023, and migrant leprosy patients accounted for more than half of all new reported cases (64.7% in 2023). The early diagnosis rate for leprosy in Guangdong reached 61.8% in 2023, delayed diagnosis continues to be a critical barrier to effective disease management and transmission interruption. Neglect of leprosy among both the general population and even healthcare workers often leads to delayed diagnosis, resulting in continued transmission and disease burden [5].

To reduce delayed diagnosis and promote early diagnosis, we have implemented leprosy symptom surveillance program in Guangdong province since 2018. We propose eight early symptoms of leprosy [6]. If doctors in medical institutions find suspected cases of leprosy, the cases can be referred to a local leprosy-designated diagnosis and treatment center for professional diagnosis and treatment through the “Guangdong Suspected Leprosy Symptom Surveillance System”. To facilitate clinical physicians’ operation, our symptom surveillance system has been integrated with a WeChat mini-program, enabling physicians to efficiently upload patients’ symptoms, photographic documentation, and related clinical data.

To improve our understanding of implementation status of leprosy symptom surveillance, we analyzed the surveillance results (2018–2023) including coverage and basic information of surveillance medical institutions, characteristics of suspected leprosy cases reported by surveillance. We also compared the differences of demographic and clinical characteristics of newly reported leprosy cases before (2012–2017) and after (2018–2023) the implementation of the program Thus to evaluated the effectiveness of the symptom surveillance program by assessing coverage rate, delayed discovery time, and early diagnosis status.

Methods

Data sources

The study was conducted in Guangdong province (23°24′N 113°30′E), a southeastern coastal region of China that ranks first nationally in both population size and gross domestic product (GDP). According to the Guangdong Statistical Yearbook 2023, Guangdong province had a permanent population of approximately 126.57 million, with administrative divisions comprising 21 prefecture-level cities, 122 counties/districts, and 1612 towns/townships/street communities [7]. In China’s administrative system, prefecture-level cities serve as critical connecting nodes linking higher and lower levels of government, playing an indispensable hub role in the public health initiatives. The county-level coverage rate refer to the proportion of counties or districts of Guangdong province that have implemented the surveillace program. Information on newly diagnosed leprosy cases (2012–2023) and symptom surveillance reported cases (2018–2023) were collected from the the National Leprosy Prevention and Management Information System (LEPMIS). The system was designed by the Chinese government in 2010 and reported data on leprosy cases and symptom surveillance cases [8], as was described in (Chen L, Zheng D, Li M. et al., 2023) [9].

According to the classification standards of Health Commission of Guangdong Province, medical institutions can be categorized into: (a) general hospitals, (b) traditional chinese medicine hospitals, (c) specialized hospitals, (d) primary healthcare institutions, and (e) public health institutions [10]. Based on the participation in leprosy syndromic surveillance, we further reclassified c) specialized hospitals and e) public health institutions into: leprosy prevention and treatment institutions, maternal and child health hospitals, and other hospitals (Fig. 2). The medical institutions data was collected from the Guangdong Provincial Suspected Leprosy Symptom Surveillance System, which designed for referring suspected leprosy cases. Medical institutions participating in the surveillance program were responsible for screening and referring suspected leprosy cases. Relevant staff have received specialized training on suspicious symptoms, operational workflows, and WeChat mini-program usage prior to program initiation.

Data from both systems were collected and uploaded by staff from prefecture-level, county or district-level professional leprosy prevention and treatment institutions, or symptom surveillance involved medical institutions. Before the interviews, informed consent was obtained from each participate after full comprehension was ensured by medical staff from symptom surveillance involved medical institutions. Participants retained the right to withdraw from the survey at any stage. The study complied with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and were approved by Medical Ethics Committee of Dermatology Hospital of Southern Medical University (Lujing RD, Guangzhou, P.R China, 510091). The studies were conducted in accordance with the local legislation and institutional requirements.

Statistical methods

Database was established in Excel 2010 (Microsoft Corp., Redmond, WA, USA), and the data were cleaned by logical error detection. Geographic distribution analysis was performed using MapInfo Professional version 11.0 (Pitney Bowes Software Inc.).

Descriptive statistical analysis, chi-square test, and univariable logistic regression were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Basic descriptive analyses, including frequency, percentage, and mean (SD), were used to describe demographic and clinical characteristics. The newly diagnosed leprosy case records from 2012 to 2023 were divided into pre-implementation (2012–2017) and post-implementation (2018–2023) of symptom surveillance. Chi-square tests were used to compare differences before and after the implementation of symptom surveillance, also differences of leprosy cases detected by symptom surveillance and other methods (2018–2023). Univariable logistic regression models were used to calculate crude odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). Missing data were excluded from the corresponding analyses.

Diagnosis standard

The diagnosis of new leprosy before August 1, 2018 was based on the Leprosy Diagnosis Standard WS291-2008 [11], and after August 1, 2018 was based on the Leprosy Diagnosis Standard WS291-2018 [12]. The assignment of clinical variables in the analysis was as follows [6, 9, 13]: (1) Suspicious symptoms: I, Chronic skin diseases with unclear diagnosis or prolonged treatment failure (more than three months); II, skin patches with loss of sensation, numbness, or less sweating; III, Thinning or loss of eyebrows, or “drunken-like” facial appearance; IV, Swelling or nodular lumps on the face or earlobes; V, Erythematous edema lesions or nodular erythema lesions; VI, Thickened nerves with pain or tenderness upon touch; VII, Recurrent painless wounds, ulcers, or susceptibility to burns/scalds on the hands or feet; VIII, Inability to close eyelids fully, facial asymmetry (crooked mouth), atrophy of hand/finger muscles, claw hand, or dropped foot. (2) Skin lesion when diagnosed: 1, 1 skin lesion; 2, 2–5 skin lesions; 3. >5 skin lesions. (3) Nervous lesion when diagnosed: 1, 0 nervous lesion; 2, 1 nervous lesion, a solitary peripheral nerve trunk exhibits irregular thickening and woody induration; 3, ≥ 2 nervous lesions, two or more peripheral nerve trunks exhibit irregular thickening and woody induration. (4) Leprosy reaction: 1, no; 2, yes. (5) Treatment classification: 1, paucibacillary (PB), including Indeterminate (I), tuberculoid (TT), and borderline-tuberculoid (BT) cases with negative skin smear test for bacteria, skin lesions ≤ 5 and nerve lesion ≤ 1; 2, multibacillary (MB), including lepromatous (LL), borderline-lepromatous (BL), and mid-borderline (BB) cases with positive skin smear test for bacteria, or cases with negative skin smear test for bacteria but skin lesions > 5 or nerve lesions ≥ 2, or cases with lesions ≥ 1 and also have characteristics of size, sensory, or morphological losses of advanced lerposy patients.

Disability classification was based on the Disability Classification Standard for Leprosy, WHO, 1998 [14]. In 1988, the WHO Expert Committee on Leprosy substantially simplified the disability grading system into a three-grade (0, 1, and 2) classification system [15]. Patients with G2D have visible deformities (i.e., hand ulcers, absorption or contractures of the digits, plantar ulcers, callosities, foot drop, or claw) or severe visual impairment (i.e., cannot read the fingers at a distance of 6 m). Patients with grade 1 disability have loss of sensation or eye problems (irregular blinking) due to the presence of leprosy, but no visible deformities, including muscle weakness without clawing. Patients with grade 0 disability have no loss of sensation, no visible deformity, and no eye problems due to leprosy.

The delayed discovery time refers to the time interval between symptom onset and definitive diagnosis, including patients delayed discovery time and medical institutions delayed discovery time. Patients delayed discovery time means the duration from symptom onset to first medical consultation of leprosy. Medical institutions delayed discovery time means the duration from initial medical consultation to confirmed diagnosis of leprosy [16]. The early diagnosis is defined as the newly diagnosed leprosy cases meeting both criteria of delayed discovery time ≤ 24 months, and no visible disability. The assignment of delayed discovery time in the analysis was as follows: 1) Delayed discovery time: (1) >24 months; (2) ≤24 months. 2) Patients delayed discovery time: (1) >24 months; (2) ≤24 months. 3) Medical institutions delayed discovery time: (1) >24 months; (2) ≤24 months. 4) Early diagnosis: 1, no; 2, yes.

Result

Implementation of symptom surveillance

In 2018 the symptom surveillance was first initiated, the number of participating medical institutions increased by 92.3%, and the county-level coverage rate increased from 11.3 to 97.6% compared with the end of 2023 (Fig. 1A & B). All 21 prefecture-level cities in Guangdong province had implemented leprosy symptom surveillance in 2023, covering a total of 2,891 medical institutions. Jieyang city, located in the eastern part of Guangdong province (Fig. 1B), was among the cities included in the surveillance program, had the highest number of medical institutions involved in the program (340, 11.8%), while Shantou city, which borders Jieyang city, had the fewest (5, 0.2%). A total of 2,072 medical institutions were primary healthcare institutions, accounting for 71.7% of all reporting institutions participating in the symptom surveillance (Fig. 2).

Symptom surveillance case reporting status

From 2018 to 2023, a total of 39,223 suspected leprosy cases were reported through symptom surveillance in Guangdong Province. Among these cases, higher reporting rates were observed in male cases (22,418, 57.2%), between 18 and 44 years old (14,038, 35.8%), and migrant patients (20,758, 52.9%). 56 cases were finally confirmed as leprosy, representing 22.1% (56/253) of all newly reported cases during the same period.

Among the eight suspicious symptoms monitored, 93.2% of reported cases involved only one symptom. The most frequently reported symptom was chronic skin diseases with unclear diagnosis or prolonged treatment failure (more than three months), representing 92.0% of all eight suspicious symptoms (see detailed breakdown in Table 1, including symptom-specific percentages).

Characteristics of leprosy case before and after implementation

Of the 706 newly diagnosed cases of leprosy registered before and after the implementation of symptom surveillance, the majority were male (456, 64.4%), 18–44 years old (44.2%, 312/706), married (71.2%, 501/704), resident patients (61.5%, 434/706), Han ethnicity (90.1%, 636/706), farmers or herders (40.5%, 285/703), and primary schools or lower (39.2%, 272/694). After implementation of symptom surveillance, the proportions of resident patients, Han ethnicity, and farmers or herders decreased significantly (p < 0.05) (Table 2).

Discussion

In September 2017, the Guangdong Provincial Health and Family Planning Commission issued the “Implementation plan for leprosy symptom surveillance in Guangdong province (Pilot)”, and leprosy symptom surveillance was officially launched on January 1, 2018. The program involves healthcare workers in designated medical institutions monitoring, referring, and confirming cases based on eight suspected leprosy symptoms, aiming to improve early detection and minimize disability in new cases. By 2023, the symptom surveillance program had been implemented across all prefecture-level cities in Guangdong, and the county-level coverage rate increased from 11.3 to 97.6%, with 2891 medical institutions participating and 6882 healthcare workers involved. Most surveillance sites were county- or township-level facilities, with primary healthcare institutions accounting for 71.7% of reporting institutions, serving as the main institutions for reporting suspected leprosy symptoms.

Among the surveillance-reported suspected cases, the most frequently reported suspicious symptom was chronic skin disease—particularly those with unclear diagnoses or cases where treatment failure persisted for more than three months. These findings indicate that early-stage leprosy lacks specific clinical manifestations and presents with diverse symptoms [17], making it easily confused with common skin diseases and leading to misdiagnosis or missed diagnosis [18]. Studies have shown that leprosy can present with symptoms similar to some chronic inflammatory skin diseases such as neurodermatitis, eczema, and atopic dermatitis, leading to frequent misdiagnosis. Similar findings in Guangdong also indicate that leprosy may be confused with inflammatory dermatoses like erythema nodosum and generalized eczema due to overlapping clinical features [19, 20]. When encountering such patients in clinical practice, healthcare providers should consider the possibility of leprosy and promptly arrange for referral and confirmatory diagnosis [21].

A comparative analysis of newly reported leprosy cases before and after the implementation of symptom surveillance showed a significant reduction in the delayed discovery time which principally caused by a marked improvement in patients delayed discovery time. Similar findings of the reduction in delayed discovery time were also observed in studies conducted in Zhejiang, and Shandong province [22, 23] Moreover, our province demonstrated a shorter delayed discovery time compared to other provinces such as Hainan and Hubei [24, 25]. This may be attributed to earlier nationwide implementation of symptom surveillance in our province and the proactive involvement of medical institutions in detecting potential leprosy symptoms significantly shortened patients delayed discovery time. Paradoxically, lengthened institutional delayed discovery time indicate ongoing challenges in inter-institutional referral. In our study, we’ve implemented a WeChat mini-program enabling monitoring institution physicians to record related clinical data that automatically shared with designated medical institution upon referral confirmation. Learn from other disease approaches such ascoronavirus disease [26], we plan to develop a patient-facing version featuring designated medical institutions appointment booking and health education of leprosy to streamline referrals and reduce institutional delayed discovery time.

Our study also find the symptom surveillance program detected more cases in migrant groups after implementation, underscoring the program’s importance in this high-mobility province where one-fifth of residents are migrants [27]. The improvement in early detection rate after the implementation showing a promising effectiveness of the symptom surveillance program. However, the Grade 2 disability ratio showed an unexpected increase, indicating the need for both improved quality and expanded coverage of surveillance activities. Meanwhile, our study has certain limitations. First, our study is a cross-sectional study, therefore, we were unable to draw causal inferences. Second, recall bias may have affected the accuracy of estimating delayed discovery time, as cases often struggled to recall the date of symptom onset and first medical consultation due to the prolonged disease course of leprosy. Third, since the symptom surveillance program has been implemented province-wide, the lack of a control group may introduce confounding factors, and the conclusions might be impacted by potential bias.

In summary, we conducted a statistical analysis of the symptom surveillance results of leprosy in Guangdong Province and the information on newly reported leprosy cases before and after the implementation of the program. The study enhances our understanding of leprosy symptom surveillance and demonstrates its effectiveness in reducing delayed discovery time and improving early detection in Leprosy. The findings will provide a theoretical basis for the implementation of further prevention and control measures as well as the formulation of relevant policies.

Uniform Treatment Protocol With Hybrid Stromal Vascular Fraction and Platelet-Rich Plasma on Micro-Fat Grafting With Facelifting: A Preliminary Retrospective 3D Vectra Observational Study
Authors: Gordon Sasaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractHybrid stromal vascular fraction (SVF), a combination of mechanical SVF (M-SVF) and stromal vascular matrix (M-SVM) fraction, is an alternative method to overcome limitations of low cell counts by conventional mechanical and enzymatic methods. The aim of this report was to observe the volume retention in facial fat grafts enhanced with hybrid M-SVF and platelets in pure platelet-rich plasma (P-PRP). Using a 5:1 v/v ratio, proportional volumes of micro-fat to proportional volumes and quantitative cell counts of M-SVF/M-SVM and PRP were delivered to palpebral-malar groove/malar fat pad units and evaluated by Vectra Analysis for volumetric changes over 12 months. Collective analysis of 9 female patients (average age: 65.6 years) revealed the percentage volume retention increased from baseline to 3 months (54.9%), then progressively declined to its nadir at 5 to 7 months (38.4%), then rebounded to peak percentage values at 10 to 12 months (69.5%), P > .05. Investigators’ ratings on the Investigator Global Assessment Improvement Scale scores are assessed. There were no reported long-term side effects or complications. The application of M-SVF/M-SVM and platelets supported micro-fat grafting with simultaneous facelifting (necklifting) in a safe manner for patients older than 55 years of age in this preliminary study. However, the hypothesis-generated associations of SVF/SVM and platelets to improve volumetric fat changes were observational only, so that conclusions of a causal relationship could not be established in this limited uncontrolled trial. Thus, interpretation of results should be viewed with caution until future randomized controlled trials are completed.Level of Evidence: 4 (Therapeutic) For image description, please refer to the figure legend and surrounding text.

For nearly 130 years, the use of autologous fat endures as a commonly accepted transplant material for a wide variety of soft tissue defects because of its biocompatibility, versatility, and availability for aesthetic and reconstructive purposes.1 However, the fate of using this like tissue replacement as a graft by traditional methods has been disappointing because of unpredictable and inconsistent outcomes estimated between 25% and 90% resorption of the initial volume, leading to degrees of necrosis, absorption, cyst formation, and fibrosis.2 Despite numerous investigations, no consensus for an accepted evidence-based approach has been reached to achieve consistent graft survival.3

In 2013, Tonnard et al reported on a new concept of nanofat whose activity resided in the number of stromal vascular fraction (SVF) cells and their associated growth factors and cytokines but with a significant depletion of pericyte-embedded matrix and adipocytes.4 In a comparison study of cellular optimization of nanofat, Cohen et al determined a higher adipose cell and regenerative SVF cell count with the LipoCubeNano device (StemC Biotechnology, Inc., Istanbul, Turkey) than the SVF cell count from the Tulip's NanoTransfer system (Tulip Medical Products, Inc., San Diego, CA).5 Consequently, one of the primary values of nanofat was because of the transfer of stem cells through needles as a regenerative filler suitable for skin rejuvenation or combined with micro-fat for improved graft survival.

In 2020, Copcu and Oztan published on the use of ultra-sharp–bladed discs that downsized fat to micro-fat and nanoparticles.6,7 Upon further reduction and centrifugation, a lower layer of stromal-cell solution was isolated, and above it, a layer of stromal-cell aggregate of adipose-derived stem cells. These layers were combined to form the final product total stromal cells (TOST). Utilizing the Copcu's sharp-bladed disc system, Sowa et al purified a micronized cellular adipose matrix composed of stromal cells within preserved extracellular matrix with growth factors.8 Concurrently, Tiryaki et al developed a rotating-canal-bladed mesh device in 2 cube boxes, the first of which downsized fat to micro-fat and the second further reduced micro-fat through bladed grids down to 500 µm holes to complete the mechanical dissociation.9 The final separated product was centrifuged to isolate an upper layer of SVF (M-SVF) and a bottom layer of stromal vascular matrix (M-SVM). The SVM retained scaffolding niches for growth factors and stromal cells that escaped capture from centrifugation and exhibited profiles like those observed in enzymatic-derived SVF (E-SVF). To compensate for lower numbers of stromal cells in processed M-SVF by either the sharp disc or the rotating-canal device, both groups combined M-SVF and M-SVM to obtain nearly the same or superior stromal cell yield but higher regenerative potential in retained scaffolding than observed with E-SVF alone. In 2019, Sese et al emphasized that the number and quality of stromal cells delivered in different clinical settings may be critical in predicting outcomes.10 The minimum dosage of stromal cells required in fat grafting has yet to be determined.

To expand on these findings, the current investigation used rotating cube devices to evaluate the potential for increased micro-fat graft survival with the addition of hybrid-SVF and platelets.

METHODS

Study Overview and Informed Consent

The single-center study was conducted at the Huntington Specialty Center (Pasadena, CA) from October 2023 to February 2025 in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, Subject's Bill of Rights, and Ethics Committee at the Surgical Center. The protocol and consent form were retrospectively approved on March 12, 2025 by the IRB of the Institute of Regenerative and Cellular Medicine (protocol number: GS-MFFL-803; approval number: IRCM-2025-430). Each facelift–necklift patient was informed that the muscle adjustment would consist of a lateral SMASectomy to minimize the fullest extent its effects. Focusing on enhanced fat grafting to the deflated compartments of the anterior midface, additional sites for facial micro-fat grafting, hybrid-SVF, and platelet would be performed as indicated but not subject to 3-dimensional (3D) evaluations because the small volumes would be difficult to assess changes.

Patient Selection

Primary inclusion criteria included candidates with moderate-to-severe ptosis and deflation to the anterior midfaces and palpebral-malar grooves without previous facelift or lower eyelid surgeries within 3 years. However, candidates were included for lower lid transconjunctival or subciliary blepharoplasties without anterior volumetric fat pedicle transposition or for necklifting because these surgeries would minimally impact grafting to the anterior midface as much as was feasible. Primary exclusion criteria included candidates with active systemic disease or local infections, pregnancy, breast feeding, severe uncontrolled autoimmune diseases, bleeding disorders, and boney or alloplastic facial implants.

Preparation

All patients were required to have a medical examination, blood and chemical panels, urinalyses, electrocardiograms, and chest films. Patients at risk for herpetic infections received antiviral medication. Women of child-bearing potential had a urine pregnancy test. BMI and high-resolution digital photography in 3 standardized views were obtained at baseline and Month 12. To the greatest possible extent, the same designated person performed imaging under standard lighting conditions.

Vectra H2 3D Volumetric Analysis Imaging

Each patient cleansed her face, removed jewelry, used a hair band to expose full facial features if needed, and maintained a neutral facial expression in a seated position throughout the capture process. Vectra Analysis was confined to the palpebral-malar and malar fat pad units. The Vectra Analysis module registered preoperative and postoperative images by overlaying images to become registered mirrored images for calculation of volumetric measurements. The sensitivity of human error of the analysis was 0.2187 mL. Vectra H2 3D Volumetric Analysis Imaging (Canfield Scientific, Parsippany, NJ) was obtained at baseline, 1 to 3, 5 to 7, and 10 to 12 months.

Preparation of Platelet-Rich Plasma

In the preoperative area, the patient was prepared for whole blood withdrawal before the intravenous line was inserted. Fifty-four milliliters of whole blood were withdrawn from an antecubital arm vein and mixed with 6 mL of citrate anticoagulant in a 60 mL syringe. A 0.2 mL aliquot of anticoagulated whole blood was transferred into a micro-receptacle in an automated blood analyzer (Horiba ABX Micros 60 Analyzer, Irvine, CA) for 3 separate baseline measurements of red blood cells, white cells, and platelets. The counts were averaged for accurate baseline concentration of platelets. Utilizing an FDA-cleared device and collection containers (PurePRPII 60 mL, Emcyte Corporation, Fort Meyers, FL), 60 mL of citrated whole blood was gently introduced into a concentrating device, centrifuged (1.5 min at 3800 rpm) to separate the red blood cell content from the overlying platelet plasma suspension (PPS). The PPS was transferred into another concentrating accessory device and centrifuged a second time (6 min at 3800 rpm) to sediment a platelet buffy coat at the bottom of the collection device. The overlying platelet-poor plasma (PPP) was aspirated leaving behind between 8 and 12 mL PPP, the volume that was determined by the calculated needs in each case. The PPP was gently agitated and swirled to dislodge platelets in the sedimented buffy coat to produce the final platelet-rich plasma (PRP) product. The counts from 3 separate portions of 0.2 mL of PRP were averaged for baseline platelet concentrations in PRP.

Skin Markings

Facelift–necklift skin markings were traced transversely below the sideburns, along the preauricular crease line, retrotragal edge, around the earlobe crease line, and in the postauricular sulcus for variable distances to the hairline. The abdominal and/or hip roll donor-site markings for fat harvesting were also drawn in the standing and sitting positions. As shown in Figure 1A, the superficial and deep compartments of the malar fat pad and the deep compartment of the palpebral-malar groove were outlined. Additional enhanced micro-fat grafting sites (Figure 1B), and secondary surgical sites were marked.

Fat Harvesting

Lipo-harvesting from the abdomen or hip rolls utilized wet tumescent technique with a solution containing 50 mL 0.5% lidocaine and 1.0 mg epinephrine per liter of warm Ringer's lactate. Typically, 180 mL of lipoaspirate was harvested using a blunt tip 7.5 cm long cannula with 4 eccentric 1.8 mm holes and centrifuged to obtain condensed fat at 1800 rpm for 3 min Figure 2).

Pathways for micro-fat, total stromal vascular fraction (SVF) and stromal vascular matrix (SVM), and platelet-rich plasma (PRP) formulation (Steps 1-8).

Processing and Sizing for Micro-Fat

Adhering to the manufacturer's protocol, the condensed fat was downsized to micro-fat using the FDA-cleared hybrid-SVF LipoCube I (StemC Biotechnology, Inc.) by emulsification and mincing passages through blade grids in the closed device (Figure 2). Generally, 60 mL of micro-fat was set aside for all anticipated areas of facial grafting. The remaining volume of micro-fat was processed in the hybrid-SVF LipoCube II device by passages through bladed grids with 750 and 500 µm holes to complete the dissociation. The separated product was centrifuged at 1800 rpm for 10 min in specialized “Cell Catcher” syringes, to isolate SVM and SVF layers that were subsequently combined to yield hybrid-SVF.

Total Stromal-Cell Counting and Viability Analysis

The number and viability of nucleated cells in 3 aliquots (0.2 mL) of the combined hybrid-SVF from each patient were counted and averaged immediately in the operating room by using the Luna-STEM Automated Fluorescence Cell Counter (Logos Biosystems, Seoul, South Korea).

Graft Injection Technique

The processed micro-fat, stromal cell preparations and PRP were placed in a cool water bath for use within 4 to 5 h at the completion of the facelift/necklift and other ancillary facial surgical procedures. As described in the author's previous publications, each malar fat pad received a total volume of 10.0 mL micro-fat that was distributed in 2.0 mL aliquots into each of 5 compartments with a 1.3 mm single hole 5 cm long cannula inserted through a skin puncture in the mid-nasolabial crease line.11,12 The same cannula was inserted through a puncture site at the lateral inferior edge of the orbicularis muscle, depositing 2.0 mL micro-fat at the supraperiosteal level across the transverse palpebral-malar groove inferior to the orbital-malar ligaments. Lastly, separate volumes of hybrid-SVF and PRP were added in a 1:5 ratio to micro-fat into the compartments of the malar fat pad and the palpebral-malar groove (Video). As listed in Table 2, secondary sites, which were injected with their indicated 5:1 ratioed volumes of micro-fat to hybrid-SVF and PRP, were not evaluated by 3D Vectra Analysis. 3D Vectra Imaging Analyses of the palpebral-malar groove and malar fat pad, as a single unit, were performed at baseline, 3, 5 to 7, and 10 to 12 months.

Patient Assessment

After completion of the study, each of 3 independent nurse evaluators, who were not involved in any aspect of the study, assessed changes in the palpebral-malar grooves and malar fat pads by comparing photographs at baseline and 12 months. The Investigator Global Assessment Improvement Scale (IGAIS) utilized a “0” score at baseline appearance in preoperative photographs. The estimated degrees of change on the visual analog scale that compared baseline and Month 12 photographs were rated as 0 = no change, 1 = slight improvement, 2 = improvement, 3 = much improvement, and 4 = significant improvement.

Statistical Analysis

A sensitivity power analysis was performed using the normal distribution of the numerical variables for small sample sizes. A 2-tailed, repeated measures t test (parametric) and 2-tailed Shapiro–Wilk normality test (nonparametric) with SPSS statistics (version 17.0; SPSS, Chicago, IL) were conducted on all palpebral-malar grooves and malar fat pads at 3 intervals for a year. A 95% significance at the P < .05 level was considered statistically significant. Pearson's correlation coefficient (r) and Spearman's rank correlation coefficient (P) analyses were also used to measure a descriptive statistic linear and nonlinear correlations between 2 variables given the small sample size.

To assess whether treatment variables were associated with long-term outcomes, correlations were computed between each infusion parameter (cells/mL, total infused cells, and total platelets) and the 0-3 to 12 month delta volumetric percent change. The 0-3 to 12 month delta was defined as the difference between volumetric percent changes of milliliter values by 3D Vectra Imaging at 12 months and baseline (0-3 months), expressed as a percentage of baseline volume.

Both Pearson's correlation coefficient and Spearman's rank correlation coefficient were applied. Pearson's test quantified the strength and direction of linear relationship, whereas Spearman's test assessed monotonic associations that may not be strictly linear. For each analysis, correlation coefficients (r for Pearson, P for Spearman) and corresponding 2-tailed P-values were reported. Statistical significance was defined as P < .05.

Complications and Side Effects

There were no significant complications or adverse events observed during the year-long trial at face or donor sites. All patients demonstrated transient perioperative swelling, erythema, slight bruising, and tenderness lasting up to 4 weeks. In 3 patients, temporary numbness to skin or ears resolved within 6 months. None of the patients developed skin blisters, ischemic skin loss, infections, fat cysts or nodules, prolonged pain, or unwanted side effects at the lipo-harvested sites throughout the study. Other than mild degrees of asymmetrical retained volume changes to the midface, patients requested no secondary balancing procedures.

DISCUSSION

The current study was undertaken in response to the publications of Copcu and Oztan and Tiryaki et al who recognized the limitations of using either enzymatically (E-SVF) or M-SVF for enrichment of fat grafting.6,7,9 Although E-SVF procedures yielded >100,000 nucleated cells/mL to 1,300,000 cells/mL of lipoaspirate of viable nucleated cells, these occurrences were at the expense of enzymatic destruction of the collagen-based extracellular matrix that harbored stromal and perivascular cells within their niches.13 Conversely, a major disadvantage of mechanical isolation techniques for M-SVF was the discarding of significant portions of the extracellular matrical scaffolding and their entrapped viable stromal cells, progenitor cells, and growth factors that led to significantly lower yield from 10,000 to 240,000 cells/mL of lipoaspirate.14 With anticipated increased usage of mechanical devices, innovative protocols are being developed not only for procuring as much of the regenerative capacity in the lipoaspirates but also for obtaining high quantity and quality of cells and growth factors for various indications.15-17

To increase the TOST numbers in the final injectable M-SVF, Tiryaki et al developed the novel hybrid-SVF device that isolated and characterized M-SVF and M-SVM fractions.18,19 Combination of the 2 components produced about the same cell yield but a higher regenerative potential than that observed by E-SVF digestion. In addition, mechanical shear stress was postulated to upregulate regenerative markers, thereby promoting additional cell proliferation and differentiation.10,19,20 The addition of PRP with stromal cells derived from enzymatic or mechanical means has also been shown to promote proliferation of cultured human adipose-derived stromal cells, and increased fat graft survival in mice and patients.11,21–24 Platelets are considered frontline responders as they release growth factors and exosomes that create appropriate conditions for mesenchymal cells to differentiate and regenerate for tissue repair and vascularization.25,26 Sasaki (self-reference) reported in an underpowered trial improved micro-fat retention to the anterior midface when supplemented with TOST utilizing Copcu's sharp-bladed disc system and platelets after facelifting.6,12 However, the use of pure numbers of viable cells in micro-fat, SVF/SVM, and PRP may not accurately predict a successful outcome with fat grafting. The incorporation of vascular scoring systems, angiogenesis markers, macrophagic inflammatory markers, proteomic analyses, and other scoring techniques might prove to be useful tools during isolation techniques, quality control, and standardization of protocols for fat grafting, skin rejuvenation and hair management. The current investigation with the sharp mesh cube boxes was conducted in tandem with the adinizer-bladed disc study to analyze whether clinical findings were comparable by utilizing the same protocol.

Since Neuber first reported in 1893 the use of excised fat grafts, experimental investigations, preclinical analyses, and clinical studies have modified grafting methods to improve fat retention.27 Despite significant technical improvements and protocols for harvesting, processing, transferring, and preparing the recipient sites, one of the more significant mechanism of tissue loss is believed to arise from insufficient and timely vascularity at the recipient site.28-32 According to Yoshimura's graft replacement theory, a large portion of adipocytes did not survive from ischemic stress and deficient plasmatic diffusion in the first 3 months, in contrast to residual more ischemic-tolerant adipose-derived stromal cells that progressively endured and contributed to adipogenesis and angiogenesis replacing apoptotic and dead adipose tissue beyond 12 months.33 It remains unclear why, in the results in this study, the volume retention dropped initially and then rebounded by the ninth to twelfth month. Similar sinusoidal patterns of reduction and recovery of fat grafts were observed; however, in previous clinical investigations with standard micro-fat or enhanced fat and was demonstrated in this clinical trial of 9 patients over the age of 55 who underwent fat harvesting by syringe technique, processing of high percentage of viable micro-fat cells through the hybrid-SVF LipoCube device, and the addition of regenerative cells SFV/SVM, and PRP.11-12,34 The recovery of volume in these healthy elder patients was unexpected considering previous clinical findings of a linear decay that was postulated from possible age-related changes and decreased regenerative potential from cell aging.34,35 Although the recovery phase was speculated to occur from the regenerative capacity of the added stromal cells, activated platelets, and extracellular vesicles, this trial lacked controls to validate these assumptions. That said, the percentage retained volume changes by 3D Vectra Analysis in both palpebral-malar grooves/malar fat pads averaged 54.9% during Months 1 to 3, dropped to 38.4% from Months 5 to 7, and steadily increased to 69.5% between Months 10 and 12 (P-values between .315 and .706, Shapiro–Wilk test). Because volume restoration might be anticipated in the absence of deleterious comorbid diseases of diabetes and autoimmune vasculitis or factors such as smoking or surgeries, the author acknowledged that the use of facelifts or lower lid blepharoplasties introduced confounding factors that impacted the validity of research findings in this trial.

The data were tested with a Prism's suite of tests for normality (D’Agostino & Pearson tests, Anderson–Darling test, Shapiro–Wilk test, and Kolmoggorov–Smirnov test) and passed, and therefore, simple linear correlations were performed. No statistically significant correlations were observed between infusion parameters and the 0-3 to 12 month volumetric percent change (Figure 7, Supplemental Table 1). Pearson's analysis suggested weak positive associations for cells/milliliter (r = 0.22, P = .56) and total cells (r = 0.28, P = .46), whereas total platelets showed no relationship (r = 0.05, P = .91). Spearman's rank analysis yielded similar findings, with weak association for cells/milliliter (r = 0.23, P = .56) and total cells (r = 0.18, P = .64), and no association for total platelets (r = −0.02. P = .97). Although not statistically significant, these trends raise the possibility that higher cell concentrations or cell dose could be associated with greater long-term volumetric improvement. Given the small sample size (n = 9), the study was underpowered to detect moderate correlations. Although Investigator Global Assessment Improvement Scale scores support the Vectra Analysis findings, IGAIS interpretation of results was based on subjective analysis comparing before and after photographs. Without the incorporation of randomized independent controlled subgroups of standard fat grafting, hybrid-SVF or PRP, the attributed advantages of combining quantities of cellular stem cells or platelets to micro-fat would be difficult to interpret and should be considered only as hypothetical-generating trends in this feasibility pilot trial.

One of the strengths of this trial was the procurement and potential therapeutic implications of using high average concentrations and numbers of platelets from 1185 × 103/µL to 1597 × 103 platelets/µL (average 1351 × 103/µL), and 5.7 × 109 platelets to 7.7 × 109 platelets (average 6.4 × 109 platelets), respectively. However, platelet counts fluctuate from their normal range between 140,000/µL and 440,000/µL according to factors such as, but not limited to, patient's age, sex, menstrual cycle phase, daily stresses time of day, inflammatory state, and the presence of immune thrombocytopenia purpura. When platelet counts depart significantly from typical values for prolong periods of time, such deviations can affect clinical outcomes. The use of the platelet variation index scores may prove valuable for standardization in future studies.35

Consistent with Marx's definition of PRP, Giusti et al demonstrated the need to deliver at least 1.5 × 106/µL to induce a significant angiogenic in vitro response in endothelial cell cultures.36,37 To clarify further the importance of concentration dosage, Everts et al and his group reported that, when platelet dosages were <1.5 × 109 platelets in 14 studies, the results were not significantly different when compared with control groups.38 The recent introduction of the DEPA classification of dose, efficiency, purity, and activation for platelets proposed the following dosing definitions to predict clinical results: very high (>5 billion platelets), high dose (3-5 billion platelets), medium dose (1-3 billion platelets), and low dose (<1 billion platelets).39

Study Limitations

Although a priori and sensitivity power analysis were undertaken to assess the robustness of the proposed study, the sample size was acknowledged to be logistically constrained and lacked the power (0.8) to detect changes. The small sample size of all females in this trial was based on convenience sampling of patient accessibility and availability in a private practice setting. Sample size determination for an observational, nonrandomized trial with 9 patients is highly complex and typically considered inadequate for robust statistical analysis or generalized conclusions. Nonparametric tests were considered for small, non-normally distributed datasets but were not found to be useful because statistical significance was difficult to achieve with n = 9 and probably required a minimum of 50 to 80 patients. As a result of these statistical barriers, the author emphasized the exploratory nature of the study, utilizing descriptive statistical analysis by Pearson's and Spearman's correlation coefficients, and refrained from making strong causal inferences beyond the studied groups. Additionally, potential limitations with technological measurements must be considered with biometric devices.

Thus, small samples risked Type II errors that failed to detect a real effect such that (1) results might not be representative of a wider population, (2) confidence intervals would be wide, reducing the accuracy of findings, and (3) the influence of individual outliers would be disproportionately high. For these reasons, the objectives of this non-RCT focused on descriptive and exploratory objectives with given constraints of limited resources in a private practice setting. The author utilized means and ranges to summarize the data with visualization of graphs and charts to display individual patient data and patterns. Furthermore, each patient was treated as an individual case study, providing detailed descriptions of their characteristics, treatment, and outcomes.

Although every patient's specific region for 3D measurements of the palpebral-malar groove and malar fat pad served as an intrinsic control, natural, and augmented anatomical variations challenged the preoperative and postoperative overlay alignments. Human error was calculated by averaging the standard deviation from 10 analyses of the same photometric comparisons of specific midface site. The study lacked control groups that would include micro-fat grafting in nonfacelift patients and micro-fat grafting with either hybrid-SVF cells or PRP. Future studies will incorporate prespecified subgroup analysis with controls, larger numbers, equitable proportion male and female patients, wider spectrum of ages to reduce biases that increase risk of false positives and other statistical limitations.

CONCLUSIONS

This trial reported observations of volume retention of fat grafting to the anterior midface with the addition of hybrid M-SVF and platelets. Although the percentage volume changes increased from baseline to 3 months, progressively declined at 5 to 7 months, and rebounded to peak values at 10 to 12 months, a causal relationship could not be established in this underpowered and uncontrolled study as determined by parametric and nonparametric t tests, including Spearman's rank and Pearson's statistical analyses. Use of pure numbers of viable cells in micro-fat, SVF/SVM, and PRP may not accurately predict a successful outcome with fat grafting. Thus, interpretation of these preliminary and hypothesis-generating results are associations and causal inferences should be viewed with caution until future RCTs are completed.

Local dexamethasone and magnesium sulfate for postoperative pain management in septorhinoplasty: A clinical evaluation
Authors: Parham Khoshdani Farahani, Arash Karimi, Davood Yazdani, Farshad Hassanzadeh Kiabi, Zahra Abbasi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSeptorhinoplasty is one of the most common cosmetic surgical procedures performed today, and like other surgeries, it may be accompanied by complications. Postoperative pain control is an important aspect of patient management.ObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the effect of dexamethasone and magnesium sulfate on reducing pain intensity and hemodynamic changes after septorhinoplasty.MethodsThis double-blind, randomized controlled clinical trial included 60 patients undergoing septorhinoplasty who were randomly assigned to two groups: the S group (magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone) and the C group (control). Patients in the S group received a combination of magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone at a rate of 5–10 µg/kg/min. Patients in the C group received an equal volume of normal saline instead. Mean changes in blood pressure, heart rate, opioid consumption, and pain scores were monitored for 6 h after surgery.ResultsEighteen female and twelve male patients in the intervention group received magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone, with a mean age of 26.3 ± 7.2 years. At 1, 2, 4, and 6 h postoperatively, hemodynamic changes were significantly lower compared to the control group. No adverse effects were observed during surgery or hospitalization.ConclusionsThis study demonstrates the positive effect of combined magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone in reducing postoperative pain after septorhinoplasty, with no acute or short-term complications observed.Keywords: Septorhinoplasty, Magnesium sulfate, Dexamethasone, Pain, Surgery

Introduction

Pain is an unpleasant sensation experienced by an individual in response to tissue injury and serves as a protective mechanism that triggers a reaction to harmful stimuli.1 The intensity of pain is closely related to the extent of tissue damage, as the release of various substances particularly bradykinin in the injured tissue stimulates chemical pain receptors.2 Acute pain and postoperative pain are experienced daily by millions of people worldwide. Despite significant advances in pain management, postoperative pain remains a major clinical problem, and progress in this area has been unacceptably slow.3

The severity of postoperative pain depends on several factors, including the extent and method of incision, the type of analgesic used before and after surgery, and the duration of the procedure. Pain control is one of the most important strategies in postoperative patient management, and physicians must ensure that therapeutic interventions do not cause additional or unnecessary pain.4

Postoperative pain after nasal surgery, particularly in procedures aimed at correcting structural deformities is common due to nasal manipulation and stimulation. Clinical studies have shown that pain reduction during septorhinoplasty can be achieved using local anesthesia combined with adjunct analgesic agents.5 The use of local anesthesia alongside general anesthesia is increasingly common in head and neck surgeries. Specifically, local nerve blocks added to general anesthesia can provide effective pain control during and after surgery for various nasal conditions.6

Several complications have been reported following septorhinoplasty, which can be categorized as intraoperative, immediate postoperative, or early postoperative complications. Lidocaine and magnesium sulfate are commonly used for local injection or nerve block in nasal surgeries.7 Magnesium sulfate has shown significant benefits in general surgery due to its antiarrhythmic properties and its role in preventing recurrent fever and seizures. Moreover, magnesium has detoxifying, analgesic, and sedative properties, making it useful for managing severe pain and promoting rapid recovery and relaxation.8

Dexamethasone (DEX) is a glucocorticoid with potent anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and sedative effects.9 It is effectively used in pediatric surgeries to prolong anesthesia duration and reduce postoperative nausea and vomiting. Several studies have investigated the local administration or nerve block using magnesium sulfate, dexamethasone, or lidocaine compared with normal saline.10 Other research has demonstrated that magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone provide superior pain control and longer duration of analgesia compared to lidocaine alone.11

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the combined effect of magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone as local anesthesia after cosmetic nasal surgery and septal correction. The goal was to determine an optimal approach for postoperative pain control and to reduce the need for analgesics following cosmetic nasal procedures.

Materials and methods

This study was a double-blind randomized clinical trial conducted on a total of 60 patients who met the inclusion criteria and underwent reconstructive-cosmetic nasal surgery at Shohada Ashayer Hospital in Khorramabad. The study was performed in accordance with international ethical standards and the regulations of the Ethics Committee of Lorestan University of Medical Sciences for research involving human subjects (2021–2022).

All 60 patients underwent various nasal surgeries, including nasal bone reduction, rhinoplasty, septoplasty, and endoscopic sinus surgery, under general anesthesia. Based on demographic factors such as age, gender, BMI, and type of surgery, patients were divided into two groups of 30 each. In both groups, bilateral peripheral nerve blocks were performed, targeting the infraorbital, supratrochlear, and terminal branches of the nasopalatine nerve.

The selection process in our study followed a rigorous, prospective randomized controlled trial design with clearly defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Eligible patients were required to be between 18 and 40 years of age, classified as ASA physical status I or II, have no known allergy to magnesium sulfate or dexamethasone, and be scheduled for elective septorhinoplasty. Patients were excluded if they had cardiovascular disease, drug addiction, respiratory disorders, metabolic disorders (including diabetes mellitus), neurological disorders, or known pregnancy. All 60 patients were consecutively recruited from the elective surgery waiting list at Shohada Ashayer Hospital in Khorramabad between 2021 and 2022. Each patient underwent a comprehensive preoperative evaluation by both the surgical team and an anesthesiologist to confirm eligibility before enrollment.

Treatment protocol/anesthesia technique/surgical method

Anesthesia was induced with propofol (2 mg/kg) and atracurium (0.3 mg/kg). Endotracheal intubation was performed using a spiral endotracheal tube of appropriate size. Remifentanil (0.2–0.5 µg/kg/min) and propofol (50–100 µg/kg/min) were administered, maintaining approximately 4 ng/mL remifentanil in 1% propofol via an Orchestra pump.

In the S group, magnesium sulfate (5–10 mg/kg/min) and dexamethasone (0.5–2 mg/kg) were administered. The C group (control) received an equal volume of normal saline. The goal was to maintain heart rate (HR) between 65 and 75 bpm and mean blood pressure (MBP) between 75 and 85 mmHg.

The surgeon injected 1–2% lidocaine with epinephrine (1:100,000) into the surgical area using a 27-gauge needle to induce local vasoconstriction. Atropine (0.5 mg) was given if bradycardia (HR < 50 bpm) occurred, and ephedrine (10 mg IV) was administered if blood pressure fell below 70 mmHg. The BIS level was maintained between 40 and 60, and atracurium (0.1 mg/kg) was re-administered as a muscle relaxant if needed. At the end of surgery, magnesium sulfate, remifentanil, and propofol infusions were discontinued, and muscle relaxation was reversed using neostigmine and atropine. Induction and maintenance of anesthesia were supervised by an anesthesiologist.

In both groups, the following variables were recorded: age, sex, anesthesia duration, surgery duration, volume of administered fluids, and dose of magnesium sulfate + dexamethasone.

Pain intensity was assessed at 1, 2, 4, 6, and 12 h postoperatively. One of the investigators, blinded to group allocation, completed the questionnaires. Pain was evaluated using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS), where 0 = no pain and 10 = the worst imaginable pain. If the VAS score exceeded 4, morphine (0.1 mg/kg) was administered slowly.

Blood pressure and heart rate were measured every 30 s, and morphine requirements were recorded. Patient evaluation and follow-up were conducted by an investigator who was blinded to the treatment groups. Postoperative pain and analgesic requirements during the first 24 h were recorded and compared separately for each type of nasal surgery. The overall results were then combined and analyzed collectively.

After transferring the patient to the operating room, a needle was inserted at the tip of the nose for local injection. The intraoperative infusion rate was 20 mg/kg/h.

Standard monitoring included BIS, ECG, noninvasive blood pressure, and pulse oximetry. Although hemodynamic parameters were captured every 30 s to ensure patient safety, these high-frequency measurements were aggregated for statistical analysis to represent broader surgical phases. Premedication was administered with fentanyl (2 µg/kg) and midazolam (2 mg).

Patients were positioned in a slight Trendelenburg position (approximately 20°) to improve venous drainage. After surgery, patients were awakened and transferred to the post-anesthesia care unit (PACU) for continuous monitoring. Mean blood pressure and heart rate were recorded every 5 s during surgery and every 30 s after transfer to the ward.

Randomization and allocation concealment

Patients were assigned to either the S group or the C group using a computer-generated randomization sequence (1:1 ratio). To ensure balanced group sizes throughout the study period, block randomization was employed with a block size of four. To maintain allocation concealment, the group assignments were placed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes. These envelopes were opened by a nurse who was not involved in the patient’s clinical care or data collection, only after the patient had been enrolled and baseline assessments were completed. This mechanism ensured that both the surgical team and the investigators performing postoperative assessments remained blinded to the treatment allocation until the conclusion of the study.

Strict eligibility screening followed by concealed block randomization resulted in two groups with no statistically significant baseline differences in demographic or clinical characteristics, confirming successful comparability before intervention.

Sample size

The sample size for this study was determined based on the primary outcome of postoperative pain intensity measured by the Visual Analog Scale (VAS). For the purposes of this study, the Minimal Clinically Important Difference (MCID) was defined as a change of 1.5 units on the 0–10 VAS, as this represents the smallest change in pain that patients typically perceive as clinically meaningful in acute postoperative settings.

To achieve a statistical power of 80% (1 - β= 0.80) with a significance level of 5% (1−α = 0.05), and assuming a standard deviation of 2.0 based on pilot data, the required sample size was calculated to be approximately 28 patients per group. To compensate for a potential 10% dropout rate or loss to follow-up, the final sample size was set at 30 patients per group (total N = 60). This ensures the study is robust enough to detect meaningful differences in pain scores and hemodynamic stability.

Statistical analysis

To handle the large volume of hemodynamic data generated by high-frequency monitoring (every 30 s), raw blood pressure and heart rate measurements were reduced to Time-Weighted Averages (TWA). This was done by calculating the area under the curve for each hemodynamic variable divided by the total duration of the observation period. This strategy effectively smoothed transient fluctuations and provided a single, clinically meaningful summary statistic for each patient during the intraoperative and immediate postoperative phases.

Quantitative data were presented using central tendency indices such as mean ± standard deviation (SD), while qualitative data were expressed as percentages and proportions. Data were analyzed using Chi-square, Pearson’s, t-tests, and nonparametric tests as appropriate. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software.

Results

A total of 60 patients undergoing septorhinoplasty were evaluated (30 in the study group and 30 in the control group). The mean age in the study group was 26.3 ± 7.2 years, and in the control group 25.7 ± 3.5 years; no significant difference was found between the groups based on the independent t-test (P = 0.21).

In the magnesium sulfate + dexamethasone group, there were 12 men (40%) and 18 women (60%). In the control group, there were 12 men (40%) and 18 women (60%). The two groups showed no significant difference in gender distribution (P = 0.58).

The mean body mass index (BMI) in the magnesium sulfate + dexamethasone group was 22.9 ± 2.5, and in the control group 22.5 ± 2.2. Data distribution in both groups was confirmed to be normal using the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test (KS); therefore, the independent t-test was used for comparison, showing no significant difference between groups.

Similarly, based on the normal preoperative distribution, the mean blood pressure did not differ significantly between the two groups according to the independent t-test (125 ± 14 mmHg in the magnesium sulfate + dexamethasone group vs. 124 ± 12 mmHg in the control group) (P = 0.8).

The heart rate data in both groups did not follow a normal distribution; therefore, the groups were compared using the Mann–Whitney test. The mean preoperative heart rate in the magnesium sulfate + dexamethasone group and the control group was 75.9 ± 8.3 bpm and 74.5 ± 8.9 bpm, respectively, showing no significant difference (P = 0.3) (Table 1).

Discussion

Septorhinoplasty is one of the most common plastic and otorhinolaryngologic (ENT) surgeries. Intraoperative bleeding is one of the most important factors that can impair the surgeon’s performance during the procedure.12

The challenges associated with septorhinoplasty are significant, as each patient is unique, and achieving consistent success can be difficult. A surgeon must possess not only excellent surgical skills but also the ability to communicate effectively with patients and understand their expectations and concerns. Although the surgery itself can present considerable challenges and be a major source of anxiety, using an efficient and reproducible therapeutic approach can help reduce fear and anxiety, thereby improving outcomes.

This study describes the authors’ approach to preoperative care, anesthesia, and postoperative management using magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone in patients undergoing septorhinoplasty. While there are many variations in operative planning, all successful programs must emphasize effective communication and patient well-being as top priorities.13

Local anesthesia and nerve blocks have been used for many years, but these techniques are now employed more frequently than ever. Their major advantages either alone or in combination with general anesthesia are their simplicity and inherent safety. The effectiveness of opioids, alone or in combination with NSAIDs, in postoperative pain management is well established. The use of multimodal analgesic regimens provides complementary mechanisms that reduce opioid consumption and lower the incidence of side effects.14

It is essential to distinguish between statistical significance and clinical relevance. In this trial, the reduction in VAS scores in the Magnesium-Dexamethasone group not only reached statistical significance (P < 0.05) but also exceeded the established MCID of 1.5 units. At 1 h and 2 h postoperatively, the mean difference between groups was greater than 2.0 units, suggesting that the intervention provides a perceptible and meaningful improvement in patient comfort rather than just a mathematical shift in data points. This clinical efficacy, combined with the significant reduction in opioid consumption, supports the use of this combination in multimodal recovery protocols.

Tramadol, an atypical opioid, is widely used for the treatment of moderate to severe postoperative pain. It causes less respiratory depression and has fewer undesirable properties compared to other opioids, making it a valuable agent for postoperative pain control. It has been reported that the analgesic effects of tramadol, pregabalin, and dexamethasone overlap, and that the combined use of these drugs is more effective for pain control than the use of any single agent alone.15

Our results showed that intraoperative administration of magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone during septorhinoplasty significantly reduced pain within 6 h after surgery (P < 0.001). Moreover, morphine consumption in the magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone group was significantly lower than in the control group during the first 6 postoperative hours (P < 0.0001).

The effects of various local anesthetic agents compared to normal saline and to each other have been investigated in several studies. In a study conducted by Gildasio S. and colleagues, it was shown that systemic magnesium has a positive effect on reducing postoperative pain.16 Systemic magnesium was found to decrease both early and late pain at rest, as well as late pain during movement. Furthermore, systemic magnesium significantly reduced postoperative opioid consumption compared to the control group.

Although systemic magnesium is not routinely used as a primary intervention for minimizing postoperative pain, the findings of this analysis provide evidence that systemic magnesium can serve as one of the key agents in reducing acute postoperative pain in patients undergoing surgery under general anesthesia.

In another study conducted by Akkan and colleagues, dexamethasone and magnesium sulfate demonstrated favorable effects in reducing pain, minimizing bleeding, and controlling heart rate during endoscopic sinus surgery.17

Kilic et al. showed that magnesium infusion alone lowered pain scores and improved recovery quality, while our study found that magnesium sulfate combined with dexamethasone provided stronger early pain relief and reduced opioid use.18 Unlike Kilic’s focus on overall recovery, our research emphasized acute pain control, suggesting that the magnesium dexamethasone combination enhances postoperative analgesia as part of a multimodal pain management approach. Both studies demonstrated that magnesium sulfate effectively reduces postoperative pain after septorhinoplasty.

Ates et al. demonstrated that low-dose ketamine significantly reduced postoperative pain and opioid use for 24 h, though psychotropic side effects remain a concern.19 In contrast, our study found that magnesium sulfate combined with dexamethasone provided effective early pain relief within 6 h after surgery and reduced morphine consumption without adverse effects. While both agents act via NMDA receptor modulation, the magnesium–dexamethasone regimen offers a safer, simpler, and well-tolerated alternative for postoperative pain management. Both studies highlight the value of multimodal analgesia in septorhinoplasty.

Celebi et al. found that intraoperative esmolol infusion reduced opioid use and stabilized hemodynamics through β-adrenergic blockade.20 In contrast, our study showed that combining magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone significantly decreased early postoperative pain and morphine consumption via NMDA inhibition and anti-inflammatory effects. Both highlight the benefits of multimodal, opioid-sparing analgesia for enhanced recovery and patient comfort. Both studies aim to improve postoperative pain control in septorhinoplasty using non-opioid strategies.

Kandemir et al. found that gabapentin, dexamethasone, and their combination reduced postoperative pain, though combining them offered no extra benefit.21 In contrast, our study showed that magnesium sulfate plus dexamethasone provided stronger early pain relief and reduced opioid use. Overall, magnesium with dexamethasone demonstrated a synergistic effect for early postoperative analgesia, unlike the gabapentin–dexamethasone combination. Both studies support multimodal analgesia after nasal surgery but differ in outcomes.

Our study showed that combining magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone during septorhinoplasty provided stronger early pain relief and significantly reduced opioid use compared to control. In contrast, Shoja-Al-hagh et al. found that pre-emptive paracetamol only slightly lowered opioid consumption without notably reducing pain.22 Overall, our results highlight the superior efficacy of multimodal analgesia over single-agent paracetamol in postoperative pain management.

Bakshi et al. focused on surgical experience, patient satisfaction, and complication rates in septorhinoplasty, emphasizing counseling and realistic expectations.23 In contrast, our study evaluated the analgesic effects of magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone, showing significant reductions in postoperative pain and opioid use. While Bakshi’s work highlights long-term clinical experience, our research provides evidence-based support for improved perioperative pain management.

As shown in our subgroup analysis, procedures involving osteotomy and nasal bone reduction naturally elicited higher pain scores compared to simple cartilaginous trimming. However, the efficacy of the magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone combination remained robust across all levels of surgical complexity. This suggests that the NMDA-receptor antagonism provided by magnesium effectively modulates the more intense nociceptive signaling associated with bony fractures, while dexamethasone addresses the significant edema following major structural reconstruction. By stratifying our results, we demonstrate that this protocol is not only effective for minor cosmetic tweaks but is particularly valuable for major reconstructive cases where pain management is traditionally most challenging.

Conclusion

The comprehensive data presented above demonstrates that our patient selection process followed rigorous scientific standards, and the randomization successfully produced that were well-balanced across all measured baseline characteristics. The lack of statistically significant differences in demographic variables, preoperative clinical parameters, and surgical characteristics confirms that the groups were indeed comparable at baseline. Any observed differences in postoperative outcomes can therefore be attributed to the intervention rather than pre-existing group disparities. Compared with previous studies using single agents such as magnesium, dexamethasone, ketamine, or paracetamol, our results highlight the synergistic effect of magnesium’s NMDA receptor inhibition and dexamethasone’s anti-inflammatory action in achieving superior pain control. This combination offers a safe, simple, and efficient multimodal analgesic strategy that enhances patient comfort and recovery after nasal surgery.

Our study concludes that intraoperative magnesium sulfate and dexamethasone provide a versatile and effective analgesic bridge for the full spectrum of nasal surgeries from minor tip-plasty to major osteotomy-assisted septorhinoplasty significantly reducing the postoperative opioid burden.

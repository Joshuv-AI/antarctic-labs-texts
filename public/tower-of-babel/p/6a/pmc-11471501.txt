Based on improved joint detection and tracking of UAV for multi-target detection of livestock
Authors: Peng Shen, Fulong Wang, Wei Luo, Yongxiang Zhao, Lin Li, Guoqing Zhang, Yuchen Zhu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn agriculture, specifically livestock monitoring, drones' ability to track multiple targets is essential for advancing the field. However, limited computing resources and unpredictable drone movements often cause issues like ambiguous video frames, object obstructions, and size deviations. These inconsistencies reduce tracking accuracy, making traditional algorithms inadequate for handling drone footage. This study introduces an enhanced deep learning-based multi-target drone tracker framework that enables real-time processing. The proposed method combines object detection and tracking by leveraging consecutive frame pairs to extract and share features, enhancing computational efficiency. It employs diverse loss functions to address class and sample distribution imbalances and includes a composite deblurring module to enhance detection accuracy. Object association utilizes a dual regress bounding box technique, aiding in object identification verification and predictive motion. Live tracking is achieved by predicting object locations in subsequent frames, enabling real-time tracking. Evaluation against leading benchmarks shows that the system improves precision and speed, achieving a 4.3 % increase in Multi-Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) and a 7.7 % boost in F1 score.Keywords: Multi-object detection, UAV, Animal monitoring

Introduction

Agriculture 4.0, also known as the Fourth Agricultural Revolution, aims to enhance the productivity, efficiency, and quality of agricultural practices while reducing environmental footprints, resource consumption, and costs [[1], [2]]. This revolution in agronomy technology has gained approval from global policymakers and integrates innovative technologies such as artificial intelligence, biotechnology, big data, the Internet of Things (IoT), and robotics to drive agricultural growth. These advancements have significantly improved various aspects of agriculture, including precision farming, intelligent irrigation systems, continuous crop monitoring, and more [3,4]. Over the past decade, substantial progress in computer vision and machine learning has enriched cyber-physical systems across all sectors. Agriculture 4.0, along with rapid developments in drone technology, has enabled unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) equipped with computer vision systems to autonomously identify and track cattle in real-time, marking a significant leap forward in digital livestock management. Identifying and tracking livestock targets within drone-captured imagery [5] remains a critical challenge in drone research. However, the transition from tracking individual animals to monitoring entire herds using AI algorithms, combined with the use of autonomous drones for real-time, offline surveillance, presents an ongoing and complex challenge.

In the domain of smart farming, the advancement and implementation of Multi-Object Tracking (MOT) algorithms rooted in computer vision principles have marked a significant milestone in research. Traditional livestock management relied on manual enumeration through household surveys, a process that was not only time-consuming and labor-intensive but also challenged by the accurate tracking of mobile animals [6]. The integration of drone technology equipped with advanced MOT capabilities has introduced a revolutionary and efficient approach to monitoring livestock populations [7]. Leveraging computer vision, these systems can identify multiple targets within video feeds, analyzing attributes like appearance, location, and motion to create detailed movement trajectories [[8], [9]]. Improving multi-target tracking remains a complex challenge in computer vision research [10]. Various methods, including Kalman filters [11,12], particle filters [13,14], Multiple Hypothesis Tracking (MHT) [15,16], Joint Probabilistic Data Association Filters (JPDAF) [[17], [18], [19]], Graph-Based MOT (GBMOT) [20,21], and Random Finite Set (RFS) filtering [22], employ nonlinear motion models, probabilistic approaches, and graph theory to accurately track multiple objects in complex environments. A promising research direction in multi-objective analysis is isolating individual target characteristics to solve multi-objective optimization problems [9]. MOT applications often face challenges such as frequent occlusions, initialization issues, trajectory breaks [23], and object similarity. Detection-based tracking is the most common approach, typically involving two phases: first, object detectors are used on each video frame for precise localization and classification; then, an advanced system autonomously learns from training samples to directly extract relevant features, enhancing image recognition and natural language processing applications [24]. While manually designed features can be effective in specific contexts [25], they require expert knowledge and may struggle to adapt to new tasks. Tracking methods are categorized into two-step detection tracking (TBD) and one-step Joint Detection and Tracking (JDT) [26]. JDT offers speed benefits, whereas TBD can achieve higher accuracy with advanced detectors and trackers, depending on the application's requirements.

In this investigation, we harness the capabilities of deep learning (DL) to address the complexities of multi-target tracking by drones [27]. We present a refined model entitled “Enhanced CTRACKER for Joint Detection and Tracking”(the following is referred to as the "CTracer" model) and add a deblurring enhancement for improved quality. The ball detection and tracking become integrated in this model, which is achieved by analyzing neighboring frame pairs and common features to decrease the amount of computation time, making the whole system real time. The objective is to develop real-time multi-target tracking framework for drones. The key contributions of our research are detailed as follows:

This is particularly beneficial for drones with limited processing power, improving occlusion handling and adaptability to appearance changes, making it suitable for real-time use.

Materials and methods

Framework overview

This part details the development of our improved CTracker model customized for multi-target tracking tasks with the help of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). Our advanced approach modifies original CTracker, incorporating critical concepts and methodologies from the basic CTracker model.

This method uses sequential frames as input, combining the strengths of ResNet-50 and the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) to capture fine-grained semantic features. It employs anchor-based detection systems to predict proposals and track targets across frames. An improved CTracker model, featuring a shallower network for enhanced feature extraction, has been developed. This updated version includes an anchor-less detector that analyzes dual frames simultaneously. Additionally, a novel method for trajectory prediction in video sequences has been proposed, as illustrated in the architecture diagram in Fig. 2. The approach employs a combination of ResNet-18 and FPN for feature extraction, creating a multi-level feature pyramid. A state-of-the-art technique integrates features from the current and previous frames to ensure feature consistency. This strategy utilizes diverse loss functions to address category imbalance and improve object identification between adjacent frames. The merged features are processed through two pathways: a classification network and a regression network, which selectively filter bounding box pairs based on their classification and matching confidence scores. To enhance online tracking, Node ChainingX technology is employed to seamlessly link targets across frames, accommodating both high and low detection score targets [29]. This method is crucial for accurately capturing complex object movements in videos recorded by moving drones [30].

Two consecutive frames Ft and Ft − 1 are input into an improved CTracker joint detection and tracking model network structure, which includes the following steps: 1) extracting current frame features using ResNet-18 core and FPN, and combining them with previously saved frame features; 2) Associate the values of two frames, and the combined features are used to predict the matching boxes; 3) Detect and predict the motion of objects for future inter frame correlations.

Deblurring model

In the context of enhancing MOT in drone-captured videos, it has been observed that frame-by-frame deblurring can unintentionally reduce tracking precision, as shown in Fig. 3. Specifically, parts (a) and (b) of Fig. 3 indicate that while optical flow methods in MOT improve spatiotemporal features, reducing tracking interruptions and identity switches, the deblurring process may introduce extraneous pixels into blurred areas. These added pixels can increase the number of false positives (FP) and lower the MOTA, as noted in Ref. [31]. Therefore, the indiscriminate application of deblurring techniques to all video sequences does not necessarily lead to improved tracking performance.

Comparison of tracking performance before and after deblurring of video frames: (a) (b) Negative effects: Improper deblurring can lead to spatiotemporal inconsistency, that is, adding irrelevant features between adjacent frames, further misleading the detector.

We introduce a cutting-edge hybrid deblurring module, depicted in Fig. 4, which incorporates an encoder to generate a comprehensive deblurring sequence. This sequence marks frames with a high Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) [32] as 1 within the initial array, while others are marked as 0. Frames falling below a specified PSNR threshold, designated as MOT-PSNR (j) [32], are identified as blurry and are selected for clarity enhancement via the CDVD technique. Such frames are then encoded as 1 in the deblurring sequence, distinguishing them from the others marked as 0. Consequently, the final deblurring sequence merges the initial sequence with frames identified and encoded as 1 during the deblurring process. This method ensures the incorporation of all frames into the hybrid sequence, maximizing the deblurring algorithm's effectiveness without adding or removing reference markers as specified.

The hybrid deblurring module encodes each frame using the MOT-PSNR threshold of each sequence, and the encoder selects appropriate frames to form a new sequence for deblurring.

For each sequence, we established the PSNR equal to MOT-PSNR as the threshold. Frames that do not meet this clarity threshold are subjected to a deblurring process prior to their analysis by the tracking algorithm. Conversely, frames that exceed this threshold are considered to be sufficiently clear. Through experimentation, it became apparent that employing a static PSNR threshold across various sequences was ineffective due to the diversity in ground truths (GTs) among these sequences. Specifically, sequences with larger GTs tend to exhibit lower PSNR at comparable levels of motion blur, making the application of a universal threshold for clarity determination impractical. In response, we developed a novel MOT-PSNR threshold that accounts for the GTs across different sequences, enabling the generation of adaptable thresholds for each sequence.

In this model, a and b serve as constant parameters. "GT (j)" signifies the ground truth of the jth sequence, while the sum of all GT(i) across the dataset reflects the collective ground truths of all sequences. For frames exhibiting a PSNR value surpassing the MOT-PSNR (j) threshold, the deblurring process is deemed unnecessary, thus preventing the addition of superfluous pixel information.

Classification and regression prediction network

The drone video input is segmented into a series { F1，F2……，FN } and processed by the core system. By utilizing ResNet-18 in conjunction with the Feature Pyramid Network (FPN), the system extracts high-level semantic features, crafting multi-scale feature maps for linking targets across adjacent frames. To streamline computation, the features Ft from the current frame, denoted as {ft1， ft2……， fti}, are extracted. A Memory Sharing Mechanism (MSM) facilitates temporary data exchange between two successive frames, allowing the model to sequentially process inputs while utilizing feature data from neighboring frames. Features from consecutive frames, Ft−1 and Ft, are employed to construct object bounding boxes with consistent IDs via dual box regression. This technique considerably enhances the regression branches' ability to identify objects that might be occluded or distorted by other objects, as well as to analyze the background data of adjacent frames. As a result, this approach substantially minimizes the identity switching (IDS) issue that occurs due to object occlusion, overlap, or mixing within the current frame.

The architecture presented in Fig. 1 delineates our network's approach, which is structured around two critical branches: one for objects classification, Cls_h, and the other for bounding boxes regressing, Reg_h. The branches are developed in parallel in a coherent system, retaining a central control mechanism. The Cls_h branch is assigned with the task to categorize ten different types of target. At the same time, the localization department is responsible for determining the location of bounding boxes for positive samples within consecutive frames so that the regression process focuses on areas that contain targets with similar profiles. This approach allows for an enhancement of the original CTracker's framework of simultaneous detection and tracking which was efficient in handling sequential frame pairs. The CTracker model used RetinaNet for the first time, which employed anchor boxes as well as dual box regression branches in order to find similar bounding boxes for the objects found in the neighboring frames. The model consists of a type classification branch for judging the confidence level of various types of objects and an ID verifying branch for tracking targets consistently across frames. This system was substantially improved by embedding an anchor-free detection method called FCOS which consists of separate branches for classification and regression purposes. This improvement makes FCOS able to detect objects in double frames in unison thus greatly improving the system's detection and tracking efficiency. Besides, the improvement of the ID verification branch and the integration of the CIoU loss function hasten convergency toward an accurate object box boundaries. In a nutshell, FCOSx shows that the strengths of CTracker and FCOS complement each other, significantly improving joint detection and tracking performances.

The target forecasting methodology employs an enhanced version of FCOS, named FCOSx, which utilizes the features from adjacent frames as its input. This approach applies the FPN across the feature map, emphasizing local information zones for the purpose of paired box regression. Within this regression framework, any point located inside the ground truth bounding box is labeled as a positive sample, whereas points residing within overlapping boxes are classified as ambiguous samples. In instances of ambiguity, the model opts for the smallest bounding box as the focal point for regression. Diverging from standard detection models, FCOSx represents bounding box coordinates as l∗,t∗,r∗,b∗, enabling the selection of a larger number of positive samples [33], as shown in (2), (3).

In the case of the input image, the coordinates x0(i),y0(i) designate the upper left corner, and x1(i), y1(i) the lower right corner of the ground truth bounding box. Points (x, y) falling within this box are identified as positive samples. The distances from any such point to the nearest edges of the box are represented by l∗,t∗,r∗,b∗. Initially, the strategy of classifying positive samples based on their proximity to the center of the object led to the generation of numerous imprecise bounding boxes. To enhance the measurement accuracy of overlaps between predicted bounding boxes, the CIoU metric was introduced, which substantially improves the reliability of overlap evaluations in object detection algorithms.

This approach evaluates the dimensions, shape, and the spatial separation of bounding boxes. Utilizing the soft non-maximum suppression (soft-NMS) strategy, outlined in Ref. [34], this method systematically assigns scores to bounding boxes based on the extent of their overlap with other boxes. Following this, the scores are gradually diminished to facilitate the removal of boxes that overlap significantly. In our investigation, we have synergized the CIoU metric with soft-NMS, thereby refining the process of accurately determining overlaps and efficiently minimizing redundant bounding boxes in our final analysis.

Loss function

In the architecture we introduce, the training process is structured as an end-to-end methodology, independently outputting data for both categories and positional targets. This approach facilitates a multitasking framework that addresses both detection and tracking. Training is driven by a composite loss function that integrates classification and localization losses, optimizing the model's performance across these tasks. The significance of each loss component in the training process is calibrated using a scaling parameter tailored to adjust the impact of each type of loss.

Positioning loss

In our research, the objective is to accurately identify targets from a dataset comprised of videos captured by drones, using bounding boxes to represent these objects visually. IoU [35], a standard metric in object detection, assesses accuracy primarily when the bounding boxes of objects overlap. Nonetheless, this metric may not yield reliable results when the drone's camera angle changes mid-flight, which affects the positioning of bounding boxes. To overcome these limitations, we have implemented the enhanced CIoU loss function detailed in Ref. [36]. This advanced metric not only measures the overlap area but also incorporates the normalized distance between the centers of the bounding boxes and the aspect ratio of these boxes. The study elaborates on CIoU's capabilities and introduces the definition of LCIoU, further refining the evaluation of object detection performance.

In object detection methodologies, the central coordinates of the designated target and its predicted bounding box are denoted by bgt and b, respectively. The metric ρ measures the Euclidean distance between the centers of these two coordinates. Additionally, c represents the diagonal distance across the smallest rectangular area that encloses both bounding boxes. The dimensions w and ℎh correspond to the width and height of the area where the bounding boxes overlap.

Experiments and results

Evaluating indicator

The assessment framework we use relies on a wide range of multi-target tracking metrics as presented in Ref. [37]. These are MOTA, MOTP, MT trajectories, ML trajectories, FP, FN, IDSW. Of importance, MOTA is a reliable measure of the model's global performance for multi-target tracking scenarios, thoroughly described in equation (8) [37].

In the context of multi-target tracking, the variable 't' stands for the frame index. Also, FPt, FNt, IDSWt, and GTt are the measures of the false positive, false negatives, identity switches, and the actual number of ground truth objects in each frame respectively. When multiple errors exceed the number of real ground truth objects, the Multi-Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) index turns negative, which can range from negative infinity to 100 %. The MOTA is calculated from three main types of errors and the first type is FP as specified in equation (9) [38] which represents the numeras of objects that have been incorrectly decided as present by the detection system.

Moreover, the metric designated as the FN, outlined in reference (10) [38], measures the quantity of objects that failed to be accurately detected within the scope of the detection process.

Finally, the identity switch metric, referred to as IDSW and documented in reference (11) [37], measures the frequency of identity swaps occurring for the same tracked object across different frames during the monitoring process.

MOTP is defined as the mean overlap across all targets being tracked, as outlined in reference (12) [37].

This way, ct represents the full alignment between the actual data and the detection results. Further, dit is the overlap degree of the bounding box of a target i in frame index t. Among others, MT denotes number of tracked trajectories and ML stands for the number of lost trajectories.

IDF1 is a measure that evaluates the continuity and quality of tracking over a sequence of frames, rather than individual frame performance. This measure provides a holistic determinacy of the tracking efficacy adjusting for any changes in shape or dynamics of objects within time. An instance where an object is well-tracked at one time while being misidentified the other time is categorized under an identity error in this metric. These types of inconsistencies reveal shortcomings of identity recognition continuity between different moments, which cannot be detected with just frame-by-frame comparisons.

Dataset

During this investigation, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) were utilized to capture video footage of cattle within a specified research area. Among the footage collected, 10 distinct video datasets were selected for further study. These datasets encompassed a range of scenarios including: scenes of cattle stationary and ambulating at a regular pace, sequences capturing cattle in rapid motion, clips with varying light conditions, footage from elevated vantage points that portrayed smaller cattle and significant perspective shifts, instances of cattle frequently being obscured, and situations where cattle moved out of the drone cameras' visual range. The diversity of these scenarios reflects the real-world challenges encountered in tracking applications, making them an integral part of the dataset. From this collection, around 100 videos were uniformly selected to compile a batch. Frames from these videos were extracted, converted to JPEG format with a uniform dimension of 640x640 pixels, resulting in a total of 60,000 images of cattle. Using LabelImage software, these 60,000 images were annotated, saved as XML files, and then segmented into training, validation, and testing subsets following a ratio of 7:2:1, respectively. The tracking model developed in this study is predicated on CenterNet, enhanced by a DLA-34 backbone network, and optimized using the Adam optimization algorithm. The training parameters were defined as: a total of 160 epochs, a batch size of 24, an initial learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁴, which was decreased to one-tenth at both the 100th and 140th epochs. Additionally, considering that over 70 % of occlusions in the cattle dataset lasted fewer than 20 frames, the initial lifespan of objects in occluded areas was set to 20 frames.

For the purpose of enhancing the dataset, a series of data augmentation strategies were implemented. These included the application of random cropping, with the crop size varying from 30 % to 90 % of the original image, and scaling alterations that adjusted the size of images between half to 1.2 times their original dimensions [40]. Photometric transformations were also carried out, changing the brightness, contrast, saturation, and hue of the images to bring out variation. Another approach was used to randomly flip the images along the specified axes with a certain probability. The first value for the learning rate was set to 5 × 10⁻⁵. The training regimen was to be run for 50 epochs, with an interval in learning rate by a factor of five after each 10 epochs to improve the performance. This model was trained on a GeForce Tesla T4 GPU with batch size being kept as one during the entire training process. To correct the discrepancies in object categories in the dataset, focal loss (FL) was used with the rate for classification and regression loss set at 0.33 and 3.33, respectively. Feature extraction and integration methodology was based on training the model with adjacent frame pairs which were randomly chosen from a three-frame neighbourhood around the current point. The detection score threshold for the procedures of object detection and association was set at 0.3, using the distance2bbox module for the required calculations. The specification for the central reference area was that it should be within 24 pixels of the center of the bounding box, regardless of orientation. After abox merging, with soft_nms module, the final targets were filtered by the Gaussian method at 0.7 IoU. During the online tracking phase, a 10-frame threshold was applied for keeping unmatched trajectories at the association stage, and the minimum detection score for the tracked targets was set as 0.3 [41]. The IoU-based initial detection thresholds for creating new tracks at the first and second rounds of association were set to 0.80, 0.50, and 0.65 respectively.

Ablation experiment

In this section, we explore an ablation study for our advanced CTracker model, which consists of four distinct submodules. The foundational model (BM) is an anchor-free, center-based network featuring dual branches for classification and regression [42], utilizing ResNet-18 and FPN as its architectural backbone. Here are the detailed functionalities of each submodule:

Submodule 1 (M1): Implements the 'get_point' module to ascertain the position of identical objects across two frames using the 'use_stride_channel' function combined with IoU. It applies the CIoU loss function to determine the deviation of an object from its central position.

Submodule 2 (M2): The clsAttention subsystem is deployed to selectively focus on image areas that display objects belonging to a targeted class. This module enhances the detection capabilities by integrating class-specific feature metrics from the classification branch with general feature metrics. The synthesized metrics are then employed by the regression branch to accurately determine the location of the object.

Submodule 3 (M3): Utilizes the 'soft_nms' method to filter out low-quality bounding boxes by evaluating both detection scores and IoU scores.

Submodule 4 (M4): Incorporates the 'ChainingX' method to facilitate object connections across frames using regression techniques and accounts for objects with varying detection scores to enhance ID tracking performance.

Next, we will discuss in detail the performance of each model.

The primary objective of Module 1 (M1) is to enhance the tracking precision by focusing more accurately on the object's area and its central position across consecutive frames. M1 utilizes the CIoU loss function which incorporates the Intersection over Union (IoU), center-to-center distance, and aspect ratios, deviating from the 'centrality' approach found in the basic model's FCOS method. M1's design, while complex, enables it to achieve quicker convergence on the bounding boxes of consistent objects in drone footage. This analysis will explore the effectiveness of M1 through detailed comparisons with key tracking metrics: MOTA, MOTP, Precision (Prcn), and False Positives (FP). Data from Table 1 reveals a decrease in FP from 27988 to 11395, alongside improvements in MOTA from 11.4 % to 25.9 %, MOTP from 70.3 % to 72.8 %, and Precision from 62.8 % to 79.3 %. These enhancements underscore M1's capability to significantly refine object localization between frames, thus substantially reducing false positives and boosting the overall tracking performance.

M2 is incorporated into the target prediction network to enhance the analysis of features across adjacent frames. This function proves especially effective when monitoring objects in motion or when instances of the same category of objects overlap in the visual field. An incorrect placement of a target's bounding box significantly diminishes the detection accuracy across frames. According to data from Table 1, although there is a marginal improvement in object detection, as indicated by the reduction in False Negatives (FN) from 71,058 to 70,258, the model experienced an increase in False Positives (FP) from 11,395 to 21,831 and a decline in Precision (Prcn) from 79.3 % to 68.2 %. These changes suggest that the object localization was less precise. Moreover, the MOTA deteriorated from 25.9 % to 17.3 %, which substantially compromised the tracking performance.

M3 incorporates a strategy known as soft NMS, which proficiently eliminates low-quality bounding boxes by evaluating the overlap of detection scores and the joint IoU scores. This method effectively reduces the prevalence of low-quality bounding boxes subsequent to the initial detection process. Notably, the implementation of this strategy did not lead to a significant increase in the total computational time, as it utilizes parameters established in earlier phases. To assess M3's impact, it is essential to analyze the ID correlation metrics, such as IDP, IDR, and IDSW, along with detection metrics like FN and FP, between the BM + M1 and BM + M1+M3 configurations. Data from Table 2 illustrates an enhancement in IDP from 56.8 % to 58.3 % and an increase in IDR from 26.4 % to 27.4 %. Furthermore, IDSW was reduced from 2186 to 2096, and FP decreased from 11798 to 11461. Although M3 does not directly enhance the detection of objects, it effectively filters out poor-quality bounding boxes, thereby maintaining the number of FNs. These improvements signify M3's substantial role in refining ID matching by filtering out low-quality, false-positive bounding boxes. The upgraded CTracker model, utilizing the FCOSx approach with the M1 and M3 modules, advances the precision of object detection and data linkage, leading to an improved accuracy in object localization. The FCOSx method starts with the get_point and usestrinde_channel functions to approximate object regions, taking into account the center distance and aspect ratio as adjusted by M1's CIoU settings. M3 then assesses the detection score and overlap area of the object boxes, filtering out those of inferior quality. The application of FCOSx in the BM + M1+M3 configuration demonstrates a notable enhancement in localization accuracy over the basic model (BM), with FP decreasing from 27988 to 11053, and MOTA improving from 11.4 % to 26.3 %.

M4's approach involves compiling the positions of objects that may temporarily disappear, including those detected with lower scores. This model employs additional memory to store the coordinates of these mismatched objects and manages their connections across up to 10 frames without affecting overall storage capacity. Moreover, M4 applies Kalman filtering to refine the relationship between these object positions and their projected trajectories, thereby strengthening cross-frame ID linkages over time. Comparison with advanced technology

In our analysis, we benchmarked the enhanced CTracker joint detection and tracking model against various established models using a dataset centered on cattle. Detailed comparisons are as follows: The MOTDT model [43] mitigates issues of unreliable detections by aggregating candidate objects from both the detection and tracking stages and employs a CNN-based scoring system to refine the selection process. The SORT method [44] implements Kalman filtering and the Hungarian algorithm [45] to accurately predict and align bounding boxes with detections that exhibit the highest IoU values. The motor model [46] enhances sequence prediction by continuously updating trajectory query data. The Transformer method [47] initiates and maintains tracks autonomously based on item-specific queries, preserving track continuity by retaining identifiers from previous queries. The IOUT technique [48] utilizes intersection over union (IoU) across successive frames to assess the similarity of detections, aiding in more coherent tracking. Lastly, the GOG model [49] incorporates a minimum cost flow algorithm with a cost function based on appearance and motion data to efficiently link detection inputs across frames, facilitating accurate estimations of trajectory dynamics and the identification of entry and exit points of objects.

Discussion

The advanced CTracker model, an integration of detection and tracking functionalities, effectively amalgamates the benefits and limitations of existing methods to enhance the core architecture of object detection and tracking networks. Developed on a Joint Detection and Tracking (JDT) network platform, this model is tailored for real-time object tracking applications. By training the model with two sequential frames, it successfully preserves the continuity of feature information across frames. Unique to our approach is the method of processing a frame and then duplicating its features to the subsequent frame, thereby maintaining consistency and avoiding duplication of processing efforts, unlike typical models that process frames independently. Incorporating techniques from the SORT algorithm, our system utilizes Kalman filters and the Hungarian algorithm to facilitate data association across frames. Additionally, inspired by the modt approach, our model employs Intersection Over Union (IOU) calculations to predict trajectory paths accurately. In line with the motor method, we systematically store all track data, including tracks that do not align, to be referenced in future frames.

Table 2 in the documentation reveals that our approach significantly outperforms earlier methods when applied to the cow dataset, recording MOTA and IDF1 rates of 31.2 % and 43.6 %, respectively. In Table 3, a detailed comparison of runtime metrics shows our refined CTracker model excels in efficiency compared to other multi-object tracking methods utilized within the same dataset. Table 4 further supports the effectiveness of our hybrid deblurring strategy, which notably enhances tracking accuracy by leveraging the full potential of deblurring techniques. When parameters a = 100 and b = 305 are applied according to Formula 1, there is a substantial increase in the proportion of frames undergoing deblurring, reaching 70.7 %. Notably, our model achieves an operational speed of 77 frames per second on a Tesla T4 GPU, demonstrating a superior performance relative to competing models.

Our model has undergone extensive testing and analysis, demonstrating robust capabilities for tracking objects in aerial videos from unmanned aerial vehicles across various environmental conditions, including both day and night as well as in adverse weather. Nevertheless, it is very important to take into account that the model has some limitations in the field of the accurate detection and tracking of small objects in a complex environment. Such confines are essentially caused by two specific challenges. Initially, the model calculates the boundaries of the objects using the smallest boundary between two consecutive frames that is situated at a fixed central point. When the distance between these reference points is more than the algorithmically determined threshold of 24 pixels, the positional accuracy of the object is not guaranteed. This problem could result in the mislocalization of small objects in crowded tracking environment especially when there is the overlapping of the bounding boxes of too many small objects. Secondly, additional to the closeness in references representation for various small objects, the paths of these objects may also overlap and that could cause the model to mislabel its identification numbers. Moreover, sophisticated environmental factors like cluttered scenes, different shadow portrayal, and various lighting conditions, increase the complexity of the tracking process because it becomes difficult to separate the intended objects from other elements in the visual area.

Conclusions

An innovative approach to the CTracker, which embraces both detection and tracking, has been constructed. This approach uses input sequence of frames and employs common features for improvement of feature extraction. It uses different loss functions to efficiently deal with some challenging classes and samples. The technique includes inter-frame object association through dual-regression bounding box attention and ID verification, focusing on the inter-frame center distances, and applies single regression for predicting movements. For online tracking, the framework predicts the location of objects in subsequent frames and incorporates detection methodologies that evaluate objects based on both high and low detection scores, facilitating the identification of the same object across different frames. Furthermore, a hybrid deblurring module has been introduced to ensure precise location and deblurring of selected frames, maintaining spatiotemporal consistency and laying a solid groundwork for accurate detection. Experimental outcomes demonstrate that this approach achieved the highest MOTA and IDF1 scores across object categories in the cow dataset. While the detection and tracking of smaller objects present challenges, future enhancements may include the adoption of larger networks to improve feature separation. Additional considerations for future research could involve ensemble learning to merge capabilities of various deep learning models and to address complexities such as tracking multiple objects and cameras.

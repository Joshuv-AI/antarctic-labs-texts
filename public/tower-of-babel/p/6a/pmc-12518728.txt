CAR NK cell production from human cord blood NK progenitor cells is enhanced by stimulation of the IL-15 receptor
Authors: Yosuke Kogue, Shunya Ikeda, Yuto Maehara, Ryuhei Kawamoto, Makiko Suga, Shuhei Kida, Kumi Shibata, Kazuhito Tsutsumi, Hiraku Murakami, Emiko Mizuta, Yo Mizutani, Yuta Yamaguchi, Daisuke Okuzaki, Naoki Hosen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCord blood (CB)-derived chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) natural killer (NK) cells have demonstrated significant antitumor efficacy. We recently reported that CB-derived CAR NK cells predominantly originate from CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK cell precursors in CB. Here, we demonstrate that stimulating the interleukin (IL)-15 receptor on these NK precursors enhances the production of CAR NK cells from CB cells. In CB CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells, the IL-15 receptor was exclusively expressed on CD7+ NK cell precursors. Using K562 feeder cells that express not only 4-1BB ligand and membrane-bound (mb) IL-21 but also mbIL-15 significantly increased the production of mature NK cells from the purified NK cell precursors or T cell-depleted CB cells. The in vitro and in vivo antitumor effects of CAR NK cells generated using K562 feeder cells that express mbIL-15 were comparable to those of CAR NK cells produced using K562 feeder cells that do not express mbIL-15. These results suggest that K562 feeder cells expressing 4-1BBL, mbIL-21, and mbIL-15 can increase the production of CAR NK cells from CB cells while maintaining their cytotoxic potential. This method could also be useful for expanding NK cells from CB for any type of adoptive NK cell therapy with or without CAR transduction.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00262-025-04191-0.Keywords: NK cell, CAR NK cell, Cord blood, Progenitor cell, IL-15, Adoptive cell therapy

Introduction

Autologous anti-CD19 chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells induce remissions in many patients with B-cell malignancies [1–3]. However, high costs and long vein-to-vein times are concerns with CAR T cell therapy. In addition, the toxicities, such as cytokine release syndrome (CRS) and neurotoxicity, require CAR T cells to be administered in specialized centers, further limiting access to these potentially life-saving therapies [2, 3]. Therefore, there is growing interest in developing off-the-shelf cell therapies that are both less expensive and safer.

Natural killer (NK) cells can be genetically modified to express CARs and administered without the need for human leukocyte antigen (HLA) matching, thereby allowing the development of off-the-shelf cell therapies [4, 5]. Allogeneic cord blood (CB)-derived NK cells transduced with anti-CD19 CAR and interleukin (IL)-15 have been shown to be effective against B-cell malignancies [6–8]. In these studies, CAR NK cells were generated by co-culturing T cell-depleted CB cells with K562 feeder cells expressing 4-1BBL and membrane-bound (mb) IL-21 (K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells) [7, 9–11]. The additional expression of mbIL-15, which has been reported to play a crucial role in NK cell differentiation and proliferation in the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 feeder cells, did not increase the expansion potential of peripheral blood (PB) NK cells [12].

Recently, we found that CB-derived NK cells are not only generated by the expansion of mature NK cells in the CB. CB-derived NK cells predominantly originated from CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK precursors [13], suggesting that the method to efficiently induce NK cell differentiation from NK precursors needs to be developed.

Methods

Cells

CB cells were obtained from the Kinki Cord Blood Bank and the Hyogo Cord Blood Bank following informed consent from the donors. This study was approved by the institutional review boards of the Osaka University Graduate School of Medicine, Kinki Cord Blood Bank, and Hyogo Cord Blood Bank. K562 cells and Raji cells were purchased from ATCC and the Japan Collection of Research Bioresources Cell Bank.

RNA-seq analysis

CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells and CD7−CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells were purified from CB mononuclear cells (MNCs) using FACS and subjected to the RNA-seq analysis. The data discussed in this publication have been deposited in the Gene Expression Omnibus31 of the National Center for Biotechnology Information and are accessible through GEO Series accession number GEO: GSE295031 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE295031). The RNA-seq dataset was analyzed using BioJupies (https://maayanlab.cloud/biojupies/).

Generation of the K562-based feeder cells

Membrane-bound (mb) IL-21 was generated by assembling the signal peptide of GMCSF, mature IL-21, IgG4 hinge region, and transmembrane domain of CD4 [14]. mbIL-15 was generated by assembling the signal peptide of CD8α, the mature IL-15, and the transmembrane domain of CD8α [15]. K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells and K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-15-mbIL-21 cells were established by retrovirally transducing K562 cells (ATCC) with 4-1BBL, mbIL-15, or mbIL-21 complementary DNA (cDNA). Single-cell cloning was performed to establish the feeder cells used in the experiments.

Retrovirus production

To generate transient viral supernatants, 293 T cells were co-transfected with retroviral vector, gag-pol, and VSV-G envelope plasmids or pEQ-PAM3(-E), and pRDF with Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Supernatants containing the retrovirus were collected 48 and 72 h later. pEQ-PAM3(-E) and pRDF plasmids were gifts from Toshio Kitamura (The University of Tokyo, Japan) and Keiichiro Mihara (Fujita Health University, Japan), respectively. cDNAs of the variable regions of the κ light chain and the heavy chain of the anti-CD19 mAb FMC63 fused with CD28, CD3ζ, and T2A-IL-15 cDNAs were inserted into the retroviral vector.

Generation of CAR NK cells

T cells were depleted from CB MNCs using CD3 MicroBeads (Miltenyi Biotec). T cell-depleted CB MNCs were co-cultured with 100 Gy-irradiated K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells or K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells at a ratio of 3:2 (CB MNCs: feeder cells) in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 20 IU/mL IL-2. After 1 week, retroviruses carrying CD19 CAR-T2A-IL-15 cDNA were transduced into CB-derived NK cells using RetroNectin (Takara Bio). The cells were then re-stimulated with 100 Gy-irradiated K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells or K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells, cultured for an additional week.

Flow cytometry and cell sorting

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) used in this study are listed in Table S2. Flow cytometry and cell sorting were performed using a BD FACS Celesta or FACS Aria II (BD Biosciences) and analyzed with FlowJo software (BD Biosciences).

Cytotoxicity assays

51Cr release was performed as previously reported [13]. The cytotoxicity of CAR NK cells was also assessed using a flow cytometry-based cytotoxicity assay that utilized GFP-expressing Raji cells as targets. Target cells (1 × 104) were incubated with effector cells at the indicated effector-to-target ratio.

Cytokine release assays

CD19 CAR NK or control NK cells were tested for reactivity in cytokine release assays with ELISA kits (IFN-γ; R&D Systems). Effector cells and target cells (1 × 105 cells for each) were co-cultured for 16 h. Co-culture was performed with technical triplicate wells. Cytokine secretion was measured in culture supernatants diluted to fall within the linear range of the assay.

In vivo xenograft mouse models

Female NOG mice (In Vivo Science), 6–8 weeks old, were irradiated with 1.0 Gy 16–24 h before transplantation. The mice were intravenously injected via the tail vein with 2 × 104 Raji-luc/GFP cells and then injected with 1 × 107 CD19 CAR NK cells or control NK cells. The mice were intraperitoneally infused with VivoGlo Luciferin (Promega, 150 mg/kg body weight), anesthetized with isoflurane, and imaged on an in vivo imaging system with the Living Image software (PerkinElmer) at the indicated times. Mice were euthanized when moribund or as recommended by a veterinarian, who monitored the mice twice per week.

Statistical analysis

The unpaired two-tailed Student’s t-test was used to determine statistically significant differences between samples.

Results

IL-15 receptor is expressed on CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK progenitor cells but not on the CD7−CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells in human CB

CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK progenitor cells [13] and CD7−CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells were purified from human CB cells by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) and subjected to RNA-seq analysis (Fig. 1A, n = 3). Among several genes differentially expressed between these two populations (Table S1), we found that IL2RB/IL15RB(CD122) and IL2RG (CD132) genes were expressed in CD7+ NK progenitor cells at levels significantly higher than in the CD7− cell population (Fig. 1B). Flow cytometry analysis revealed that CD122 was expressed on all the CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK progenitor cells but not on CD7−CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells (Figs. 1C, S1). CD132 was expressed on CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK progenitor cells at levels higher than on CD7−CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells (Figs. 1C, S1). Collectively, the IL-15 receptor, which is a heterodimer of CD122 and CD132 [16], was expressed on all the CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK progenitor cells but not on CD7−CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− cells in human CB.

CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK progenitor cells were purified from CB mononuclear cells (MNCs) by FACS and co-cultured with the irradiated K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 feeder cells or K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 feeder cells in the medium containing IL-2. The feeder cells were supplemented to the culture once a week. After 3 weeks of culture, most of the cells co-cultured with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells or K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells became CD56+CD3− NK cells, while the percentage of CD56⁻CD3⁻ cells in the products was higher when co-cultured with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 feeder cells (Fig. 2B). Total numbers of CD56+CD3− NK cells produced from CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR−Lin− NK progenitor cells (5 × 103) after 21 days of culture were significantly higher when co-cultured with the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells than when co-cultured with the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells (8.7 ± 1.2 × 107 cells vs 3.7 ± 1.1 × 107 cells, n = 4, p = 0.023, Fig. 2C).

T cell-depleted CB MNCs were co-cultured with the irradiated K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells or K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells in the medium containing IL-2. After 3 weeks of culture, most cells co-cultured with the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells or K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells were CD56+CD3− NK cells (Fig. 2D). The expansion of CD56+CD3− NK cells after 21 days of culture were significantly higher when co-cultured with the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 than when co-cultured with the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells (83,369.6 ± 14,809.5-fold vs 44,454.1 ± 9829.4-fold expansion, n = 7, p = 0.049, Fig. 2E). In addition, the expansion of CD56 + CD3- NK cells was much higher when cultured in an IL-2-free medium with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells than with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells (52,999.2 ± 17,585.9-fold vs. 1371.1 ± 298.0-fold expansion, n = 3, p = 0.043, Fig. 2F, G). Expression of NK cell receptors such as CD16, NKG2D, NKp46, NKp30, NKp80, and NKG2A was similar between NK cells produced by co-culture with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells and those produced by co-culture with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells (Figs. 2H, S2A). The addition of soluble IL-15 to the medium did not increase NK cell production from CD3−CB MNCs co-cultured with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells (36,810.4 ± 14,538.1-fold vs 34,390.4 ± 11,698.0-fold expansion, n = 3, p = 0.90, Fig. S2B). These results suggest that the yield of human CB-derived NK cells was increased by using K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells instead of K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells as feeder cells.

We evaluated the viability of expanded natural killer (NK) cells after cryopreservation and thawing. One day after thawing, the post-thaw recovery rates were 37.2 ± 16.0% and 35.0 ± 12.3% (n = 3, p = 0.92), respectively, for NK cells expanded with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 and K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 feeder cells. The thawed NK cells retained their cytotoxic potential (Fig. S3A, B).

Antileukemic potential of CAR NK cells produced by co-culture with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells was comparable to that produced by co-culture with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells.

CD19-targeting CAR NK cells were generated from T cell-depleted CB MNCs using the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells or the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells as feeder cells according to the previously reported method [6–13, 17]. Transduction efficiencies of CD19 CAR were comparable between the two groups (Fig. 3A). Both CD19 CAR NK cells produced by co-culturing with the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells and those produced by co-culturing with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells exerted cytotoxicity and produced interferon (IFN)-γ when co-cultured with CD19-positive cells but not CD19-deficient cells in vitro (Fig. 3B–D).

Immunodeficient mice were transplanted with luciferase-expressing Raji cells and treated with control (non-transduced) NK cells or CD19 CAR NK cells that were produced from T cell-depleted CB MNCs using either the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells or the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells as feeder cells. Treatment with both CD19 CAR NK cells reduced tumor burden (Fig. 3E) and significantly enhanced survival of mice (K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21: p = 0.011, K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15: p = 0.015, Fig. 3F). Survival of the mice treated with CD19 CAR NK cells produced using K562 4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells is similar to that of the mice treated with CD19 CAR NK cells produced using K562 4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells (Fig. 3F).

Collectively, these results suggest that CAR NK cells produced using the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells as feeder cells showed in vitro and in vivo antitumor effects comparable with CAR NK cells produced using the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells as feeder cells.

Discussion

In this study, we found that the IL-15 receptor is expressed on CD7+CD56−CD34−HLA-DR− NK progenitor cells but not on CD7−CD56−CD34−HLA-DR− non-NK progenitor cells in CB. IL-15 reportedly has an important role in the differentiation of NK cells [18]. Consistently, the yield of human CB-derived NK cells was significantly increased by stimulation of IL-15 receptor expressed on NK progenitor cells with the K562 feeder cells expressing mbIL-15, whereas mbIL-15 expressed on the K562 feeder cells had no effect on the production of NK cells from PB-MNCs [12, 17]. The efficacy and safety of CAR NK cells generated using the K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 feeder cells have been proved in previous clinical trials. In our experiments, the antileukemic potential of CAR NK cells generated by co-culturing with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells was comparable to those co-cultured with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21 cells. These results suggest that K562 feeder cells expressing 4-1BBL, mbIL-21, and mbIL-15 can increase the production of CAR NK cells from CB cells while maintaining their cytotoxic potential, whereas the efficacy and safety of CAR NK cells generated by co-culturing with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 cells should be carefully examined in clinical trials.

The yield of mature NK cells produced from CD7⁺CD56⁻CD34⁻HLA-DR⁻Lin⁻ NK progenitor cells was similar to the yield from T cell-depleted CB MNCs. This suggests that T cell-depleted CB MNCs may include a developmentary earlier subset than CD7⁺ NK progenitor cells. This subset is presumably the CD34+CD7+ cells, which was previously reported as NK cell progenitor cells in different culture systems [19]. Future studies should focus on efficient methods for producing NK progenitor cells from CD34⁺ cells. Additionally, the percentage of CD56⁻CD3⁻ cells in the products was higher when co-cultured with K562-4-1BBL-mbIL-21-mbIL-15 feeder cells, suggesting that IL-15 signals may expand not only mature NK cells, but also NK progenitor cells. In the future, we need to clarify the detailed composition of these CD56−CD3− cells.

Adoptive transfer of natural killer (NK) cells has emerged as a promising new approach for treating solid tumors [20–22]. Unlike cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), which recognize tumor antigens in MHC complexes, NK cells target tumor cells that either have low or no MHC expression, which allows tumor cells to evade CTL attack. Therefore, NK cell therapy can be an appropriate complementary therapy in treatment of tumors that escape immunotherapy mediated by CTL, such as immune checkpoint inhibitor treatments. Our results could also be applied to expanding NK cells from CB for any type of adoptive NK cell therapy with or without CAR transduction.

The low post-thaw recovery rate of CAR NK cells, which is an important issue [23], could not be overcome by using K562 feeder cells that express 4-1BBL, mbIL-21, and mbIL-15. As was recently reported, stimulating CAR NK cells with IL-18 prior to cryopreservation may increase their recovery rate after thawing [24].

Previous clinical trials have demonstrated the safety of CAR NK cells manufactured using K562-based feeder cells. However, K562 is a human leukemia cell line. Therefore, even though the cells are irradiated at 100 Gy prior to use in co-cultures, it is essential to carefully test the CAR NK cells for residual contamination before clinical use. In the near future, we should develop a feeder-free system, such as nanoparticles carrying 4-1BB, mbIL-15, and IL-21, to address this issue.

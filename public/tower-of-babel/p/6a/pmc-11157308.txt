Multiple-stage Mpox Skin Eruption
Authors: Noriomi Ishibashi, Yoshihiro Umebayashi, Yuji Hirai
Published: 2023

A 44-year-old man with no recent travel history presented with glans head pocks. He had had sex with a male sex worker without using condoms, two weeks after which he had developed multiple-stage eruptions appearing as painless pocks on his eyebrows (Picture 1) and perioral region (Picture 2), along with inguinal purpura with bilateral lymph node swelling (Picture 3) and scabs and ulcers on his hands and glans head (Picture 4). He showed no infection with hepatitis B or C virus or human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) nor syphilis. However, polymerase chain reaction of the eruption showed positivity for mpox. After one month, the eruption, excluding the glans head, had improved. The glans head ulcers improved after over two months. Mpox dermatological manifestations comprise pocks, purpura, scabs, and ulcers. Mpox skin eruption appear in the perioral (28%) and genital area (55%) as well as on the hands (60%). Lymph node swelling most commonly occurs in the inguinal region (61%) (1).

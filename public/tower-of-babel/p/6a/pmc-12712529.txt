Immunosuppression-Free Kidney Transplantation after Haploidentical Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation in Fanconi Anemia
Authors: Jessica Kachmar, Martin Castelle, David Saint Gilles, George Terinte-Balcan, Margaux Boistault, Thomas Blanc, Julien Zuber, Evgenia Preka, Olivia Boyer, Marina Charbit, Guillaume Dorval
Published: 2025

To the Editor:

We present the case of an 8-year-old girl with Fanconi anemia (OMIM #227650), an autosomal recessive disorder causing short stature, congenital anomalies of the kidneys and urinary tract, and cytopenias.1

Six months after diagnosis, she underwent haplo-identical hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, with her mother as the donor (see Supplementary Material for additional details). The patient was maintained on immunosuppression until the age of 13 years for pulmonary graft-versus-host disease suspicion.

Serum creatinine was normal at diagnosis (32 μmol/l; estimated glomerular filtration rate of 151 ml/min per 1.73 m2). She developed chronic kidney disease at the age of 10 years, progressing to kidney failure 4 years later. Imaging revealed hydronephrosis without obstruction, consistent with Fanconi anemia.1 Chronic kidney disease was due to medication toxicity, total body irradiation, and episodes of acute tubular necrosis in the setting of congenital anomalies of the kidneys and urinary tract.

She was assessed for kidney transplantation; her mother (and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation-donor) was eligible for donation. Phenotypic chimerism showed donor origin for circulating T cells, B cells, and monocytes (see Figure S1). Neutrophils could not be assessed; thus, myeloid chimerism was suspected but not confirmed. Complement-dependent cytotoxicity and T-cell flow cytometry crossmatch were negative. There were no donor-specific anti-HLA antibodies. Therefore, no immunosuppression (induction or maintenance) was given. In Figure 1, we summarize the clinical course. Twenty-four days after transplantation, she was discharged with an estimated glomerular filtration rate of 133 ml/min per 1.73 m2. Kidney biopsy showed normal parenchyma without rejection. Five years posttransplant, her estimated glomerular filtration rate remained normal at 132 ml/min per 1.73 m2 with no proteinuria (tested every 3 months) and no donor-specific antibody (tested every 3 months for 1 year then every 6 months). Double bone marrow and kidney transplantation have been described since 2002 in Fanconi anemia.3 These patients are at increased oncologic risk (genetic predisposition, exposure to DNA-damaging agents and irradiation, and development of graft-versus-host disease in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation patients).4,5 Avoiding long-term immunosuppression therefore eliminates 1 risk factor for malignancy,S1 improves quality of life, and decreases infection risk and metabolic complications. This approach may be beneficial in other genetic DNA repair disorders, such as dyskeratosis congenita or Diamond-Blackfan anemia.

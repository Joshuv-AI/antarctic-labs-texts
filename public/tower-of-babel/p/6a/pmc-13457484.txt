Early Experience of Femoral Lengthening with the FITBONE Nail in Adolescents
Authors: Bartosz Musielak, Aubrey Franco, Nick Green, Stephen Giles, Sanjeev Madan, James A Fernandes
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAim and backgroundIntramedullary limb lengthening nails are increasingly used in patients nearing skeletal maturity. Following the temporary withdrawal of magnetically driven systems, the electrically powered FITBONE nail has been used at our institution since March 2021. This study aims to present the early experience of this nail with particular attention to complications, regenerative healing, and the learning curve.Materials and methodsA single-center retrospective study was performed, including adolescent patients who underwent femoral lengthening with antegrade or retrograde FITBONE nails and progressed to full weight-bearing (FWB). Outcome measures included length gained, distraction index (DI), weight-bearing index (WBI), mechanical axis parameters, complications, and patient-reported outcomes using the Paediatric Quality of Life (PedsQL) Questionnaire.ResultsA total of 15 (eight males, seven females) patients with a mean age of 16.9 years, with varied diagnoses of leg length discrepancy (LLD), were analysed. Mean length gained was 39 mm with a mean DI of 0.75 mm/day. Weight-bearing index in these patients, on average, was 57.4 days/cm lengthened. Four complications were observed, including two nonunions (both successfully treated) and two knee subluxations. Mechanical axis deviation improved from 12.1 to 7 mm on average. A significant reduction in WBI was observed with increasing surgical experience (Spearman's ρ = −0.58, p = 0.02). No significant changes were detected in PedsQL scores pre- and post-operatively (p > 0.05 for all domains).ConclusionFemoral lengthening using the FITBONE nail in adolescents approaching skeletal maturity is an effective and relatively safe procedure, with complication rates and outcomes comparable to other intramedullary lengthening systems. A learning curve was evident, reflected by earlier progression to FWB without an increase in complications.Clinical significanceSurgeons experienced with intramedullary limb lengthening can expect outcomes and complication profiles comparable to other motorised nail systems when using FITBONE. However, the relatively large barrel diameter (12 mm) may limit its use in patients with narrow femoral canals and should be considered during patient selection.

Introduction

Over the past three to four decades, Ilizarov's principles of distraction osteogenesis have become well established in both adult and paediatric reconstructive surgery. Early bone lengthening techniques relied on external fixation; however, complications such as pin and wire site infections led to the development of intramedullary lengthening devices, which have progressively replaced external fixators.1–9

Currently, the most commonly used devices for femoral lengthening are spindle-driven intramedullary nails, including the magnetically actuated PRECICE nail and the electrically powered FITBONE nail.1 Recently, reports of osteolysis and periosteal hypertrophy associated with stainless steel magnetically driven STRYDE nails resulted in their withdrawal, together with the temporary suspension of PRECICE nails, prompting a shift towards motor-driven lengthening systems.10–12

Although both technologies are based on the same fundamental principles of femoral lengthening, differences in their mechanisms lead to distinct implantation techniques, which may present challenges for surgeons transitioning between systems (Table 1).1 Therefore, this study presents the early experience of a leading UK paediatric limb reconstruction center with femoral lengthening using the FITBONE nail following the temporary withdrawal of magnetically driven devices.

Materials and Methods

This was a single-center retrospective study analysing femoral lengthening performed using FITBONE intramedullary nails inserted via either an antegrade or retrograde approach. Inclusion criteria comprised patients with leg length discrepancy (LLD) in whom femoral lengthening was performed using a FITBONE nail and who progressed to full weight-bearing (FWB) during follow-up. A total of 15 patients lengthened with this method met the inclusion criteria and were further analysed.

Patients presented with a variety of underlying conditions associated with LLD, including: post-traumatic shortening (three limbs), congenital femoral deficiency (two limbs), sequelae of avascular necrosis (AVN) of the hip (two limbs), contralateral limb hemihypertrophy (two limbs), post-septicemic sequelae (one limb), skeletal dysplasia (two limbs), isolated limb arthrogryposis (one limb), and idiopathic LLD (two limbs).

Study Duration and Setting

The study was conducted between June 2023 and January 2025 at Sheffield Children's Hospital.

Treatment Regimen

All patients underwent surgical lengthening following the established principles of percutaneous metaphyseal osteotomy for distraction osteogenesis.13 When simultaneous deformity correction was planned, a retrograde nail insertion technique with reverse planning was used, provided that closure of the distal femoral growth plate had been confirmed radiologically.14 In all remaining cases, an antegrade insertion technique was employed. In all retrograde procedures, poller screws were used to optimise nail position and minimise the risk of secondary displacement (Fig. 1). Skin incisions over the osteotomy sites were closed using absorbable sutures.

In all retrograde nails, surgical steps were executed according to the reverse planning, and the position of the nail was set based on the locking screws, as well as so-called poller screws

Lengthening was initiated at a mean of 7 days post-operatively (range, 4–13 days), depending on the underlying diagnosis and any intra-operative complications (Table 2). Distraction was started at a rate of 1 mm/day, divided into three equal increments, and was subsequently adjusted according to the quality of regenerate bone formation and the range of motion of the adjacent joints.

Physiotherapy, including mobilisation and ambulation, was started on postoperative day 1. Patients were initially allowed to bear up to 20% of their body weight and were instructed to perform daily exercise programmes, preferably under the supervision of a local physiotherapy team. Patients in whom the risk of joint subluxation/dislocation was increased, particularly those with congenital deficiencies, were advised to use knee extension splints. Full weight-bearing was permitted only after radiographic evidence of corticalisation of at least three of the four cortices of the regenerate bone.

Parameters Analysed

The following parameters were analysed retrospectively: Duration of surgery, length gained, distraction index (DI), weight-bearing index (WBI), and complication rates (Fig. 2). Mechanical axis deviation preoperatively and post-lengthening, as well as achievement of the planned target length, were also analysed. Additionally, patient-reported outcomes were assessed using the Paediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL) questionnaire preoperatively and at a minimum of 12 months post-operatively (mean follow-up, 23 months). Statistical analysis of PedsQL scores was performed using paired t-tests for individual domains and the total score.15

Results

In total, 15 patients (15 limbs) with a mean age of 16.9 years (range from 15.4 to 19 years) and varying primary diagnoses of LLD underwent femoral lengthening with the FITBONE nail between March 2021 and August 2022. In nine cases, the retrograde technique with distal femoral osteotomy was utilised, while in the remaining cases, the nail was inserted antegrade. In all patients treated with the retrograde technique, closure of the distal femoral growth plate was confirmed radiographically prior to surgery. The mean follow-up duration was 1.4 years.

The mean surgical time was 3 hours and 47 minutes (range: 2 hours 53 minutes to 6 hours 10 minutes). The mean length gained was 39 mm (range, 20–80 mm) with a mean DI of 0.75 mm/day. The planned target length was achieved in 87% of cases with an accuracy within 2 mm. Of note, one patient underwent lengthening of only 20 mm due to shorter stature and a preference for limb lengthening rather than contralateral shortening, which would have compromised further height. The mean WBI was 57.4 days/cm lengthened.

According to the Paley classification, four complications and one problem were recorded.16 These included two cases of regenerate nonunion, both of which were treated with bone grafting and ultimately healed; two cases of knee fixed flexion contracture with posterior subluxation, the latter occurring in patients with congenital short femora; and one superficial wound infection at the osteotomy site, which resolved with conservative treatment (Table 2).

Mechanical axis deviation improved from a mean of 12.1 mm preoperatively to 7.0 mm post-operatively, while the lateral distal femoral angle (LDFA) improved from a mean of 85° to 87°.

With increasing surgical experience, a significant reduction in WBI was observed (Spearman's rank correlation coefficient: −0.58, p = 0.02). No significant change in operative time was identified with increasing case number (Spearman's rank correlation coefficient: 0.17, p = 0.54), and no correlation was found between length gained and WBI (Spearman's rank correlation coefficient: −0.19, p = 0.49).

Analysis of patient-reported outcomes demonstrated neither statistically significant differences in physical functioning, emotional functioning, social functioning, or school functioning domains of the PedsQL questionnaire before and after treatment (p = 0.94, 0.30, 0.37, and 0.70, respectively) nor in the total PedsQL score (p = 0.42) (Table 3).

Discussion

This study presents early results of femoral lengthening using the FITBONE intramedullary nail in adolescent patients treated for LLD or short stature. While FITBONE lengthening has been widely reported in the literature, data specifically focusing on adolescent populations remain limited, with previous studies reporting cohorts of 32 and 10 patients, respectively (Table 4).6,17 Therefore, the present series of 15 adolescent patients with a mean age of 16 years provides valuable insight into outcomes and challenges of internal lengthening in this younger age-group.

The FITBONE nail is a versatile implant suitable not only for straightforward femoral lengthening but also in cases requiring simultaneous deformity correction.1,18 In our cohort, antegrade insertion via the piriformis fossa was used for isolated lengthening, whereas a retrograde approach was selected when deformity correction was planned. This approach allowed for normalising the LDFA angle to a mean of 87° and improvement of mechanical axis deviation from 12 to 7 mm. Moreover, the mean achieved lengthening of 39 mm and a DI of 0.75 mm/day are consistent with previously reported outcomes for FITBONE and other intramedullary lengthening nails.3,6–9,17–20

As reported in other series, the planned target length was not achieved in all patients, with successful completion in 87% of cases. Premature cessation of lengthening was required in patients who developed significant knee subluxation (all of whom belonged to the congenital femoral deficiency group despite previous ligamentous reconstruction), despite adherence to knee splinting protocols (Table 2). However, patients' compliance with the splints was questionable in this selected age-group. These findings underline the importance of close monitoring of joint range of motion, intensive physiotherapy, and careful patient selection. In patients at high risk of joint instability, particularly where compliance may be limited, the indications for internal lengthening should be carefully reconsidered, as options for managing joint subluxation during nail lengthening are more limited than with external fixation.

Other complications in this cohort included one superficial wound infection, which resolved with oral antibiotics, and two cases of nonunion at the regenerate site (Table 2). In both cases, impaired bone healing was caused by technical factors rather than patient-related or biological causes: One case required wide opening of the osteotomy site to retrieve a dislodged poller screw, and the other required femoral intramedullary canal extensive reaming with an osteotomy located in the diaphysis. Although compression across the lengthening site is a recognised strategy for managing poor bone formation, reversal of the FITBONE nail is technically demanding and requires clinician intervention rather than patient control.1 In this cohort, compression was not used, and after careful analysis, iliac crest bone grafting was performed, resulting in successful union without loss of achieved length.

Importantly, despite the occurrence of complications, no significant deterioration in patient-reported quality of life was observed by our patients. PedsQL scores demonstrated, in fact, small, non-significant improvements in physical, social, and emotional functioning following treatment (p = 0.94, 0.37, and 0.3, respectively). These findings suggest that substantial correction of LLD can be achieved without adversely affecting patient-reported quality of life.

With respect to regenerate healing, our findings indicate bone formation and corticalisation rates comparable to those reported for external fixator-based lengthening.21,22 However, recent literature suggests that intramedullary lengthening nails may be associated with faster regenerative healing compared with external fixation. Of note, the healing index is of remarkably less significance in patients with nail lengthenings than in cases with external fixation, where full corticalisation of three out of four cortices is essential for the frame to be removed. When a lengthening nail is used, patients may begin to bear weight up to 20 kg on the operated leg initially and will gradually increase the amount of weight put on the leg once distraction is over.24 Therefore, the index that is most important in this cohort is WBI, as it indicates how fast a patient will stop using crutches and start walking independently.

Weight-bearing strategy is increasingly recognised as a key factor influencing regenerative mineralisation. Studies by Calder et al., Bafor et al., and Elsheikh et al. have demonstrated that earlier progression to weight-bearing following intramedullary lengthening accelerates regenerate bone mineralisation, as assessed by pixel value ratio analysis.25–27 In our series, patients were initially restricted to partial weight-bearing of up to 20% of body weight, which, in some cases, was lower than the commonly cited 20 kg limit. This conservative approach likely contributed to the higher WBI observed compared with other reports.3,6,16–19 Importantly, as institutional experience increased, earlier progression to FWB was permitted, resulting in a significant reduction in WBI in more recent cases (Spearman's rank: −0.58, p = 0.02). Importantly, delayed weight-bearing was not associated with the two observed nonunions, which were likely caused by technical issues during nail insertion (Table 2).

Finally, the use of the lengthening nails in adolescents approaching skeletal maturity appears to be both safe and efficient.28–31 With straight nails for antegrade femur insertion, the entry point for the nail passes through the piriformis fossa, which traditionally was considered to be at high risk of AVN, especially in patients with open growth plates. However, no cases of AVN were observed in this cohort. This approach could potentially avoid injury to the greater trochanter and subsequent alterations in proximal femoral anatomy; however, previous study by Hammouda et al. showed that the trochanteric entry nail is rather safe in skeletally immature patients.31 Nevertheless, at the time of treatment in this cohort, a trochanteric entry FITBONE nail was not available, and the retrograde FITBONE nail technique was reserved only for skeletally mature or near-mature patients, minimising the risk for secondary deformities caused by proximal and distal femoral physeal injury.

Of note, previous reports of limb lengthening in younger, skeletally immature patients have predominantly involved magnetically driven nails, which are available in smaller diameters and allow for trochanteric entry.28–30 That type of nail is produced in different sizes, with the smallest diameter of 8.5 mm and the shortest length of 150 mm, and the choice of lateral entry in the trochanter makes it possible to insert in skeletally immature patients with a small diameter of the bone. In contrast, even the smallest size of FITBONE nail of 9 mm has a larger barrel of 12 mm, limiting its applicability in smaller femora regardless of the direction of insertion (antegrade vs retrograde). Adequate femoral canal and cortical dimensions remain a prerequisite for safe implantation (the remaining width of cortex of 3 mm for each cortex, both on the antero-posterior and lateral views after reaming, e.g., for a 12 mm nail, the width of the bone measured together with cortices needs to be at least 18 mm in each view). These design considerations continue to influence patient selection and highlight the importance of matching implant choice to individual anatomical and developmental factors.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size is relatively small. However, these surgical procedures are not commonly performed, and the age of patients is a limiting factor here. Importantly, the cohort size is comparable with previously published series in this age-group.6,17 Second, the study is retrospective and includes procedures performed by multiple surgeons with varying levels of experience, although all cases were supervised by the senior author. Finally, this report focuses exclusively on femoral lengthening, as FITBONE nails were primarily designed for femoral applications, while tibial lengthening in our institution is more commonly performed using external fixation due to the frequent need for simultaneous multilevel deformity correction.3

Conclusion and Clinical Significance

In summary, this study confirms that femoral lengthening using the FITBONE nail in adolescent patients approaching skeletal maturity is an efficient and relatively safe method with complication rates and a safety profile similar to other intramedullary lengthening systems. Future users should, however, remain mindful that the size of the FITBONE nail retains a relatively large barrel diameter (12 mm) even in smaller sizes, which may limit its applicability in patients with narrow femoral canals.

Ethics Statement

This study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Institutional Review Board approval was waived because this retrospective service evaluation involved anonymised clinical data and patient-reported outcome measures and posed no additional risk to participants. All participants provided informed consent prior to completing the questionnaire and participating in the study.

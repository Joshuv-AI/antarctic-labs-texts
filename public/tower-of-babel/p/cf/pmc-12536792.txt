IDeate-Lung02: a Phase 3 study of second-line ifinatamab deruxtecan in patients with relapsed small cell lung cancer
Authors: Taofeek K Owonikoko, Lauren Byers, Ying Cheng, Hidetoshi Hayashi, Luis Paz-Ares, Maurice Pérol, Haichuan Hu, Meng Qian, Cecilio Roi Garcia, Juliette Godard, Mei Tang, Charles M Rudin
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTPatients with small cell lung cancer (SCLC) have poor prognosis and limited treatment options beyond first-line therapy. B7 homolog 3 (B7-H3) is minimally expressed in normal tissues but highly expressed in SCLC. Ifinatamab deruxtecan (I-DXd), a B7-H3–directed antibody–drug conjugate, has demonstrated promising efficacy and a manageable safety profile in various tumours, including SCLC. IDeate-Lung02 is a global, randomized, open-label Phase 3 study of ~540 patients with relapsed SCLC. Adults with one prior line of platinum-based systemic therapy, ECOG performance status 0–1, and ≥1 measurable lesion (per RECIST 1.1) are eligible for study participation. Patients with asymptomatic untreated or previously treated brain metastases may participate. Patients are randomized 1:1 to receive I-DXd 12 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks or treatment of physician’s choice (topotecan, amrubicin, or lurbinectedin). Dual primary endpoints are objective response rate (ORR) by blinded independent central review (BICR) and overall survival. Secondary endpoints include ORR by investigator; progression-free survival, duration of response, disease control rate, and time to response, all by BICR and investigator; patient-reported outcomes; and safety. Introduction

Background and rationale

Lung cancer is the most frequently diagnosed cancer and the leading cause of cancer-related deaths worldwide [1]. Small cell lung cancer (SCLC) is a highly aggressive form of lung cancer, representing about 15% of all lung cancer cases, and is known for its fast growth and poor prognosis [2]. SCLC is primarily classified as limited-stage (LS-SCLC; Stage I–III that is amenable to radiation therapy) or extensive-stage (ES-SCLC; Stage IV or Stage III that is not amenable to radiation therapy) [3]. Due to the aggressive nature of SCLC, >70% of patients are diagnosed with ES-SCLC [4,5]. Although treatment advances over the past decade have been made [5,6], the prognosis of patients with ES-SCLC remains poor, with a median overall survival (OS) of approximately 1 year and a 5-year survival rate of <4% [4,7]. Additionally, patients with SCLC who have brain metastases have worse survival outcomes than patients without brain metastases, with a median OS of ~5 months [8].

For patients with ES-SCLC, typical first-line (1L) therapy includes etoposide plus cisplatin or carboplatin in combination with anti–PD-(L)1 therapy [3,6]. Although ES-SCLC generally responds well to 1L standard therapy, most patients relapse within 6 months and their disease is often resistant to subsequent platinum-based chemotherapy (PBC) [6]. Current second-line (2L) treatment options for relapsed SCLC are limited [3,6,9]. Physicians can choose to rechallenge with PBC in patients with a chemotherapy-free interval (CTFI) of ≥3 months (who may still be sensitive to platinum agents) [3,6]. Otherwise, available therapies include topotecan (a topoisomerase I inhibitor [10]), amrubicin (a topoisomerase II inhibitor available in Japan only [3,11]), and lurbinectedin (a DNA-alkylating agent [12]), which are associated with a short duration of response (median DOR; ~4–5 months), a median OS that rarely exceeds 9 months, a median progression-free survival (PFS) of ~3–4 months, and hematologic and gastrointestinal adverse events (AEs) [11,13–15]. More recently, tarlatamab, a bispecific delta-like ligand 3/cluster of differentiation 3-directed T-cell engager, received accelerated approval in the United States and manufacturing and marketing approval in Japan for 2L treatment of ES-SCLC following PBC [16,17]. Additionally, it was approved in the United Kingdom for the treatment of ES-SCLC in patients who have received two or more prior lines of therapy, including PBC [18]. Tarlatamab has demonstrated an objective response rate (ORR) of 40%, a median OS of 15.2 months, a median PFS of 4.3 months, and a median DOR of 9.7 months, but carries a risk of cytokine release syndrome and neurologic toxicity (including immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome), and can be associated with infections, hematologic toxicity, and hepatoxicity [16,19]. Thus, there is still a substantial unmet need for novel treatment options that offer durable clinical benefits, including systemic disease control and tolerable safety profiles, for patients with relapsed SCLC.

B7 homolog 3 (B7-H3; also known as CD276) is a transmembrane protein belonging to the B7 family of checkpoint proteins that has emerged as a promising therapeutic target in SCLC and other cancers [20]. B7-H3 is either absent or expressed at low levels in normal tissues but is overexpressed in many solid tumours [21,22]. Consistent, high expression of B7-H3 is observed across all molecular subtypes of SCLC [23], and 65% of SCLC tumours show moderate-to-high cytoplasmic and/or membranous staining by immunohistochemistry (IHC 2+/3+) [24]. Increasing evidence has shown that B7-H3 serves a role in tumour proliferation, metastasis, and resistance to treatment [20]. Moreover, in patients with SCLC, moderate-to-high tumour B7-H3 expression (IHC 2+/3+) correlates with tumours measuring ≥5 cm and shorter OS (7.4 months versus 23.8 months in patients with B7-H3 IHC 0/1+) [24]. The role of B7-H3 as a negative prognostic indicator, and its differential expression in SCLC versus normal tissue, provide a strong rationale for developing B7-H3–targeting antibody–drug conjugates (ADCs).

Ifinatamab deruxtecan (I-DXd) is a B7-H3–directed ADC comprising an anti–B7-H3 monoclonal antibody linked to a highly potent topoisomerase I inhibitor payload (DXd), designed to enhance selective tumour-cell death and reduce systemic exposure to the payload (Figure 1) [25–28]. The antibody and payload are covalently linked via a plasma-stable, tetrapeptide-based, tumour-selective, cleavable linker with an optimized drug-to-antibody ratio of approximately four [25]. The mechanism of action of I-DXd provides the potential for a better clinical benefit-risk profile than that of traditional chemotherapy agents.

I-DXd has shown promising efficacy and a manageable safety profile in two clinical studies, including a multiple-tumour study that contained a cohort of SCLC patients, and a separate study focused on patients with pretreated ES-SCLC. In the Phase 1/2 IDeate-PanTumor01 study (NCT04145622), I-DXd, at doses of 6.4–16.0 mg/kg, showed an ORR of 52.4% and median OS of 12.2 months in 21 patients with pretreated SCLC (data cutoff: 31 January 2023) [29,30]. Similarly, I-DXd demonstrated promising efficacy in 88 patients with pretreated ES-SCLC in an interim analysis of the Phase 2 IDeate-Lung01 study (NCT05280470), with numerically greater efficacy at the 12-mg/kg dose (n = 42) than the 8-mg/kg dose (n = 46); an ORR of 54.8% and 26.1% and median OS of 11.8 and 9.4 months were observed in patients who received I-DXd 12 mg/kg and 8 mg/kg, respectively (data cutoff: 25 April 2024) [31]. Moreover, among 37 patients with brain metastases at baseline in IDeate-Lung01, central nervous system (CNS) confirmed ORR was 37.8%, while a subset of 16 patients with brain target lesions at baseline showed a CNS confirmed ORR of 56.3% and a CNS disease control rate (DCR) of 100% [32]. In both studies, I-DXd has shown a manageable safety profile, consistent across multiple tumour types in IDeate-PanTumor01 and ES-SCLC in IDeate-Lung01 [29–31]. The most common treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) associated with I-DXd included gastrointestinal events (nausea, decreased appetite, vomiting, and diarrhea), hematologic events (anemia, neutropenia, and decreased lymphocyte count), fatigue, and infusion-related reactions (IRR), with most cases being Grade 1–2 [29–31]. TEAEs leading to drug discontinuation occurred in 8.0% of the total study population (N = 174) in IDeate-PanTumor01, and in 6.5% (8 mg/kg) and 16.7% (12 mg/kg) of patients in IDeate-Lung01, with the most common causes being interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis and pneumonia [29,31]. ILD/pneumonitis is an AE of special interest (AESI). Adjudicated treatment-related ILD was observed in 5.7% of the total study population in IDeate-PanTumor01 and in 8.7% (8 mg/kg) and 11.9% (12 mg/kg) of patients in IDeate-Lung01, with the majority of cases being Grade 1–2 [29,31]. The 12 mg/kg every 3 weeks (Q3W) dose of I-DXd was chosen for continued clinical development, based on efficacy, safety, and exposure-response analysis [33].

To determine if I-DXd monotherapy can provide a more favorable clinical benefit-risk profile than current standard-of-care (SOC) chemotherapies as 2L treatment for relapsed SCLC, a Phase 3 study was designed. Here, we describe IDeate-Lung02 (NCT06203210), a Phase 3 study comparing I-DXd with the SOC (treatment of physician’s choice [TPC]: topotecan, amrubicin, or lurbinectedin) in patients with relapsed SCLC who have received only one prior line of platinum-based systemic therapy.

Objective

The primary objective of IDeate-Lung02 is to evaluate whether treatment with I-DXd improves ORR by blinded independent central review (BICR) and extends OS compared with TPC (topotecan, amrubicin, or lurbinectedin) in patients with relapsed SCLC following only one prior line of systemic therapy, which must have included PBC [34].

Trial design

IDeate-Lung02 is a global, multicenter, randomized, open-label Phase 3 study. Approximately 540 patients (~270 in each arm) will be randomized 1:1 to receive either I-DXd 12 mg/kg intravenously Q3W or TPC, as shown in Figure 2 [34]. Randomization will be stratified by the following: (1) prior receipt of an anti–PD-(L)1 therapy (yes versus no); (2) CTFI, defined as the time from completion of 1L PBC to the date of documented radiologic progressive disease (<90 versus ≥90 days); (3) presence or history of brain metastases, regardless of treatment status (yes versus no); and (4) TPC (amrubicin versus lurbinectedin versus topotecan) [35]. Enrollment began in May 2024, with an estimated study completion in February 2029 [34]. This study is sponsored by Daiichi Sankyo, Inc. In October 2023, Daiichi Sankyo entered into a global development and commercialization collaboration agreement with Merck Sharp & Dohme LLC, a subsidiary of Merck & Co., Inc., Rahway, NJ, USA for ifinatamab deruxtecan (I-DXd).

Methods

Study setting

Patients are being enrolled across approximately 230 study sites in Asia, Australia, Brazil, Europe, and North America [35].

Eligibility criteria

Eligible patients are adults (≥18 years of age or minimum legal adult age; whichever is greater) with a diagnosis of relapsed SCLC with radiologically documented progressive disease following only one prior line of systemic treatment; the treatment must have included PBC for ≥2 cycles of therapy with a CTFI of ≥30 days. Patients must have histologically or cytologically documented ES-SCLC. Patients who received 1L PBC for prior LS-SCLC may be eligible if their disease progressed during or ≤6 months after completing treatment [34,35]. Patients who have received platinum rechallenge are ineligible as this is considered a 2L therapy. At least 80% of patients are expected to have received prior anti–PD-(L)1 therapy [35]. Patients must have an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0–1 and ≥1 measurable lesion per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours, version 1.1 (RECIST 1.1), as assessed by investigator [34,35]. Patients with asymptomatic untreated or previously treated brain metastases are eligible [35]. A list of key inclusion and exclusion criteria are presented in Table 1 [34,35].

Interventions

The I-DXd group will receive I-DXd 12 mg/kg intravenously on day 1 of each 21-day cycle, and the TPC group will receive one of the following SOC treatments, chosen by investigator: topotecan (1.5 mg/m2 for 5 consecutive days, starting on day 1 of a 21-day cycle), lurbinectedin (3.2 mg/m2 Q3W), or amrubicin (40 mg/m2 for 3 consecutive days, Q3W), all administered intravenously. Comparator treatments will only be utilized in countries where they are approved in the 2L for patients with SCLC who progressed on or after PBC. It is planned that ≥70% of patients treated in the comparator group will receive topotecan [35].

Before each dose of I-DXd, patients should receive premedication to prevent nausea and vomiting, with a two- or three-drug combination (e.g., a corticosteroid plus serotonin-3 receptor or neurokinin-1 receptor antagonist), per local or standard clinical guidelines. Antihistamines, acetaminophen, and/or corticosteroids are strongly recommended to prevent IRRs, following local or clinical guidelines. I-DXd is infused over ~90 min for the first dose and, if no IRRs occur, over ~30 min for subsequent doses [35].

For treatment-related AEs, investigators may reduce the dose of I-DXd (starting dose, 12 mg/kg; first dose reduction, 10 mg/kg; second dose reduction, 8 mg/kg) or delay treatment for up to 49 days from the last dose administered. If a participant requires a dose delay exceeding 4 weeks (28 days) from the planned administration date (or more than 49 days from the last dose), the investigator should consult the sponsor medical monitor to discuss the situation on a case-by-case basis. I-DXd treatment should be delayed for any suspected cases of ILD/pneumonitis, regardless of grade. Supportive treatment with systemic steroids should be considered for Grade 1 and initiated promptly for Grade ≥ 2, continuing until improvement for Grade 1 or for at least 14 days for Grade ≥ 2, followed by a gradual taper over at least 4 weeks. I-DXd must be permanently discontinued for any Grade ≥ 2 ILD/pneumonitis event. Once the dose of I-DXd is reduced due to toxicity, all subsequent cycles should be given at the lower dose, unless further reduction is necessary. No more than two dose reductions are permitted. For TPC, investigators may modify the dose per the locally approved label. Patients will continue to receive treatment until clinical or radiologic evidence of disease progression is determined by investigator, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of informed consent, death, loss to follow-up, or other reasons [35].

Outcomes

Dual primary efficacy endpoints are ORR by BICR per RECIST 1.1, and OS. Secondary endpoints include ORR by investigator; PFS, DOR, DCR, and time to response (TTR) all by BICR and investigator according to RECIST 1.1; safety (including TEAEs, Grade 3/4 TEAEs, deaths, serious TEAEs, AESIs, and TEAEs associated with dose modification or discontinuation of study drug, graded according to the National Cancer Institute’s Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, Version 5.0 [NCI-CTCAE V5.0]); immunogenicity of I-DXd (prevalence and incidence of antidrug antibodies in the I-DXd arm only); relationship between B7-H3 protein expression in tumour tissue and clinical efficacy outcomes; patient-reported outcomes (PROs); and pharmacokinetics (I-DXd arm only) [34,35]. Key statistical analysis

The null hypothesis is that the ORR and OS distributions in the I-DXd group are equal to those in the TPC group. This will be evaluated against the alternative hypothesis: that the ORR and OS distributions differ between the I-DXd group and the TPC group.

Sample size

The sample size calculation for this study is based on comparisons of ORR (per BICR) and OS between the I-DXd group and the TPC group. It was estimated that a total of 540 patients will need to be randomized to ensure that an improvement in these endpoints will be detected with 90% power [34,35].

Analysis sets

The safety analysis set includes all patients who received ≥1 dose of a study drug. The full analysis set includes all patients assigned to a study drug by randomization (the intent-to-treat population) and will serve as the primary analysis set for all efficacy analyses [35].

Primary efficacy analyses

ORR by BICR per treatment group will be presented (with 95% confidence intervals [CI] using the Clopper–Pearson method) and compared using the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test at a two-sided 1% significance level. OS will be compared between treatment groups using a stratified log-rank test. The distribution of OS will be estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, with the OS HR and its two-sided 95% CIs estimated by a stratified Cox proportional hazards regression model. OS analyses will be performed using all data up to and including the data cutoff [35].

Secondary efficacy analyses

PFS will be estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method (with the PFS HR and its two-sided 95% CI estimated by a stratified Cox proportional hazards regression model). Median PFS and 95% CI will be presented per treatment group. ORR as assessed by investigator will be presented per treatment group (with 95% CI estimated using the Clopper–Pearson method). Median DOR and 95% CI will be presented by treatment group for patients with a best overall response of complete response or partial response. DCR will be summarized descriptively (with the 95% CI calculated using the Clopper–Pearson method). TTR will be summarized descriptively [35].

Safety analyses

TEAEs will be coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA). TEAEs will also be summarized by NCI-CTCAE V5.0 grade and their relationship to the study drug. The number and percentage of patients reporting TEAEs, serious TEAEs, AESIs, and TEAEs leading to study drug discontinuation will be reported [35].

Health economics and outcomes research analyses

PROs will be provided by visit and treatment group. Continuous outcomes, including scores from the European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer Quality of Life questionnaires and the EuroQoL-5 Dimensions index and visual analog scale, will be summarized by mean, standard deviation, median, and range. Kaplan–Meier method will estimate time to definitive deterioration (with the median time and 95% CI presented by treatment group) [35].

Interim efficacy analyses

An interim analysis of OS will be conducted alongside the primary ORR by BICR analysis. The overall two-sided alpha will be controlled for the interim and primary OS analyses using the Lan-DeMets alpha-spending function with an O’Brien-Fleming stopping boundary. The boundaries for both analyses will be based on the actual number of observed deaths [35].

Monitoring

Participant timeline and monitoring

The study is expected to last for approximately 5 years and will be divided into three periods: screening, treatment, and follow-up, which includes the long-term follow-up (LTFU) period. The screening period lasts up to 28 days, from the signing of the informed consent form until randomization [35].

The treatment period starts once patients are randomized into treatment groups, with a maximum of 5 calendar days allowed between randomization and the first dose of the study drug. Treatment continues until disease progression (as determined by investigator), unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of informed consent, death, loss to follow-up, or other reasons (whichever occurs first). Radiographic tumour assessments are conducted every 6 weeks (±7 days) for 48 weeks from randomization, and then every 12 weeks (±7 days) thereafter, until disease progression (confirmed by both investigator and BICR), death, loss to follow-up, or withdrawal of informed consent. If a patient discontinues the study drug for reasons other than disease progression or loss to follow-up, tumour assessments will continue at the same frequency, of every 6 weeks (±7 days) until 48 weeks from randomization and then every 12 weeks (±7 days) during the follow-up period, until disease progression is confirmed by BICR or the patient withdraws [35].

The follow-up period begins after treatment is permanently discontinued. Patients must attend an end-of-treatment visit and a safety follow-up visit scheduled 40 (+7) days after the last dose of the study drug. Subsequent LTFU visits occur every 3 months (90 ± 14 days) to assess survival, tumour progression (for patients who discontinue treatment for reasons other than disease progression), and any further anti-cancer treatments, until death, withdrawal of consent, or other study discontinuation criteria are met [35].

Data monitoring committee

An independent data monitoring committee (DMC) will be established to safeguard the rights, safety, and well-being of participants by overseeing the progress and outcomes of the study. The DMC will consist of qualified physicians and scientists who are not investigators in the study and have no direct affiliation with the Sponsor.

Ethics

This study will be conducted in accordance with the ethical principles derived from the Declaration of Helsinki, the International Council for Harmonisation consolidated Guideline E6 for Good Clinical Practice, and all relevant regulatory requirements. The protocol is approved by institutional review boards or ethics committees at each site. Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants before enrollment.

Conclusion

At present, 2L treatment options for relapsed SCLC are limited by their modest efficacy and/or sub-optimal safety profiles [11,13–15,19]. Therefore, there is an unmet need for more effective treatment strategies that enhance response rates, prolong survival, and have a less-negative impact on quality of life for patients with SCLC in the 2L setting and beyond. IDeate-Lung02 is a global, multicenter, randomized, open-label Phase 3 study designed to investigate whether I-DXd, a B7-H3–directed ADC, improves clinical and quality-of-life outcomes compared with topotecan, amrubicin, or lurbinectedin in patients with relapsed SCLC following only one prior line of systemic treatment that included PBC. Given the promising efficacy and safety data observed in the Phase 1/2 IDeate-PanTumor01 study [29,30] and the Phase 2 IDeate-Lung01 study [31], I-DXd may provide an effective treatment option for patients with relapsed SCLC, with a favorable safety profile.

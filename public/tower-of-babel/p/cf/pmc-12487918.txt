Analysis and prediction of disease burden of stroke and its subtypes in China from 1990 to 2040
Authors: Jing Wang, Chunlong Xiao, Zhao Cheng, Hongxiang Liu, Weixi Zhang, Chuanhua Yu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground:In China, stroke burden remains severe as it is a major cause of mortality and disability. Detailed analyses across different subtypes will help optimize intervention strategies, enhance resource allocation efficiency, and ultimately reduce the overall disease burden.Methods:We conducted a descriptive analysis of the incidence, prevalence, mortality, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), years of life lost (YLLs), and years lived with disability (YLDs) of stroke and its subtypes using data (1990–2021) from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) database. A Joinpoint regression model was applied to quantitatively analyze the indicators and calculate the annual percentage change (APC) and average annual percentage change (AAPC). We applied the Bayesian age-period-cohort (BAPC) model to project trends for 2022–2040.Results:Incidence of stroke increased by 100.64% from 1990 to 2021, with ischemic stroke (IS) exhibiting the largest increase (201.13%) among all the subtypes, and the incidence being consistently higher in males than in females. The YLL/YLD ratio for stroke and its subtypes has decreased, with the YLL/YLD ratio falling from 20.13 to 9.48 in 1990–2021, indicating an increase in non-fatal burden. After adjusting for age, the age-standardized incidence rates (ASIRs) of stroke and its subtypes declined, except for IS. The age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) for subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) decreased significantly (APC: –15.31%; 2000–2004), with the largest reduction in the age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) also occurring during this period (APC: –14.22%). Furthermore, BAPC projections (2022–2040) indicate that stroke ASIRs in males will slightly decline but increase in females. Meanwhile, the ASIR of IS is expected to continue to rise. Overall, the ASMR and ASDR are projected to decline.Conclusions:Although China has made some progress in stroke prevention and control, several challenges remain. Controlling IS must be prioritized, especially due to the high stroke burden among males.

Introduction

In 2021, at level 3 of the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) all-cause hierarchy, stroke was the third most common cause of death after ischemic heart disease and coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), and the fourth leading cause of disability-adjusted life years (DALYs).[1] Its intricate pathological mechanisms and diverse clinical manifestations make it a major medical and public health challenge worldwide. Globally, stroke incidence has increased, especially in low- and middle-income countries. Developed countries have markedly decreased the burden of stroke through the implementation of evidence-based control measures and significantly reduced the mortality and disability rates of stroke through improved healthcare services.[2]

Currently, China is facing significant stroke challenge. According to the results of GBD 2019, there were 3.94 million new cases of stroke, 28.76 million illnesses, and 2.19 million deaths in China in 2019, in addition to 45.9 million DALYs.[3] Especially with rapid economic development, aging populations, and unhealthy lifestyles, the incidence of stroke continues to increase. A GBD study showed that China has the highest lifetime risk of stroke worldwide, with a lifetime risk of stroke of 41.1% in males and 36.7% in females, sex disparities are the largest worldwide.[4]

Stroke can be divided into three major subtypes: ischemic stroke (IS), intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), and subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH).[5] The pathomechanisms and clinical management of different types of stroke vary. Therefore, refining the analysis of the disease burden for each subtype can help develop more personalized interventions.

Although China has made some strides in prevention and control policies over the years, the total disease burden of stroke has not significantly improved.[6] GBD 2021 data show that although the mortality rate of stroke in China has declined in recent years, the burden of disability remains severe, resulting in a persistently high DALY rate. This not only affects the patients’ quality of life but also puts tremendous pressure on the healthcare system and society.[7,8] Therefore, with the further development of Chinese society and economy, stroke prevention and control strategies need to be continuously adjusted and optimized. Accurate risk management, enhanced health interventions for high-risk populations, and improved coverage of post-stroke rehabilitation care are important measures to reduce the burden of stroke in the future.

In this study, we systematically analyzed the burden of stroke and its subtypes in China from 1990 to 2021 to determine the long-term trend of stroke and its subtypes and to reveal the contribution of different subtypes to the total burden to provide a scientific basis for evaluating the effectiveness of existing preventive and control measures and formulating future public health strategies.

Methods

Data source and acquisition

In this study, we used the GBD results tool (https://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-2021) provided by the GBD 2021 to extract relevant indicators of the incidence, prevalence, mortality, and years of life lost (YLL) due to premature death, years lived with disability (YLD), and DALYs rates of stroke and its subtypes in China and globally from 1990 to 2021. DALY refers to the total number of healthy life-years lost from the onset of disease to death, including YLL and YLD, that is, DALY = YLL + YLD. We also extracted demographic data for China from 1990 to 2021, along with the projected demographic data for 2022–2040.

Statistical analysis method

We used Microsoft Excel 2020 (Microsoft Corporation, Washington, USA) to integrate and analyze various indicators and the rate of change to measure the extent of change. The calculation formula is as follows:

Rate of change=Indicator in 2021−Indicator in 1990Indicator in 1990×100%

Age-standardized rates were obtained by adjusting for the world standard population, and the burden of disease and change trends in different sex groups were analyzed comparatively using descriptive epidemiological analysis methods. A line graph is drawn to show the trend of each disease burden indicator for the period 1990–2021.

The changes were quantified using the Joinpoint regression model, and the annual percentage change (APC) and average annual percentage change (AAPC) were calculated using Joinpoint 4.9 software (National Cancer Institute, Maryland, USA) to evaluate the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR), prevalence rate (ASPR), mortality rate (ASMR), and DALY rate (ASDR). APC = 0 or AAPC = 0 indicates no change. APC and AAPC >0 showed an increasing trend. The APC or AAPC <0 indicated a decreasing trend. All tests were performed using two-sided tests with a test level of α = 0.05, with P <0.05 indicating that the difference was statistically significant.[9]

In this study, we applied the Bayesian age-period-cohort (BAPC) model to predict the disease burden of stroke and its subtypes in China. The BAPC model[10] is a powerful tool that combines age, period, and cohort effects, effectively capturing time-related dynamic changes and predicting future disease burden through Bayesian inference. The BAPC model was constructed by applying the BAPC package of the R software, https://cran.r-project.org to predict the disease burden of stroke and its subtypes in China from 2022 to 2040. The data were analyzed using R4.3.2 software, and the data were visualized using the ggplot2 package in R software.

Results

Disease burden for stroke and its subtypes in China from a global perspective: Comparison of global trends

Incidence

Between 1990 and 2021, stroke incidence showed a significant upward trend, with the combined incidence of males and females nearly doubling by 100.64% (95% uncertainty interval [UI]: 97.20, 104.84%), from 143.29 (128.09, 161.27) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 287.51 (252.60, 330.34) per 100,000 in 2021. The increase in incidence among males was particularly significant, with the incidence rate in males rising by 113.51% (109.46%, 115.56%), whereas that in females increased by 86.27% (82.91%, 89.18%). The global incidence of stroke, by comparison, has only increased by approximately 15.03%.

Despite the general increase in incidence, the ASIR showed a decreasing trend in both China and worldwide between 1990 and 2021. In China, the ASIR of stroke for both sexes decreased from 226.94 (95%UI: 202.92, 252.80) per 100,000 in 1990 to 204.75 (181.03, 231.50) per 100,000 in 2021, a decrease of 9.78% (8.43, 10.78%). The global ASIR decreased by 21.78% (21.96, 22.34%), demonstrating the global effectiveness of stroke control. Notably, the decrease in ASIR was more significant in females than in males, with a decrease of 16.86% (15.85, 17.76%) in Chinese females compared to that in females 25.03% (24.90, 25.17%) globally.

The changes in the incidence of different stroke subtypes varied. In China, the incidence of IS in both sexes increased between 1990 and 2021 by 201.13% (95% UI: 192.69, 205.51%), far exceeding the global increase of 27.05% (24.17, 28.42%). In particular, the incidence of IS in Chinese males increased by 223.25% (217.87, 229.13%). The ASIR of IS in China also showed an upward trend. By 2021, the ASIR of IS for both sexes in China increased by 35.72% (32.19, 38.93%), contrasting with a 15.85% (14.67, 17.08%) global decline. The incidence of ICH and ASIR in China remained above global levels in 1990 and 2021, although ASIRs for both sexes in China fell by 43.86% (42.20, 44.72%) compared with a 31.36% (29.58, 32.15%) global decrease. Compared with males and the overall level, female ASIR reductions were more pronounced: 50.02% (49.01, 50.23%) in China vs. 34.89% (33.74, 35.75%) globally. In contrast, SAH incidence and ASIR showed marked declines in China and globally, especially in China. In 2021, the ASIR of SAH for both sexes in China was 7.81 (6.88, 8.95) per 100,000, which was a decrease of 56.50% (55.26, 57.63%) compared to 1990, with a greater reduction than the global decrease of 28.78% (28.20, 29.81%) [Figure 1; Supplementary Figure 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

Prevalence

Between 1990 and 2021, the prevalence of stroke in China increased significantly, with the prevalence of both sexes increasing from 912.15 (95% UI: 850.27, 981.13) per 100,000 in 1990 to a peak of 1851.03 (1697.77, 2012.00) per 100,000 in 2021, an increase of 102.93% (99.67, 105.07%), which is much higher than the global increase of 25.77% (25.90, 26.14%). Similar to the prevalence trends in both sexes, the prevalence of stroke is increasing in both males and females in China and globally, peaking in 2021, with a higher increase in males than in females.

The ASPR of stroke in China has shown a slow increase, with a growth rate of 11.48% (10.96, 11.34%). In contrast, the ASPR of stroke at the global level exhibited an overall downward trend, decreasing by 8.48% (8.24, 8.59%) in 2021 compared with 1990.

Among the different subtypes, the prevalence of IS in China has increased significantly, reaching 1462.24 (95%UI: 1308.45, 1616.28) per 100,000 for both sexes in 2021, with a notably higher increase in males. Globally, the prevalence of IS has increased by only 36.36% (36.19, 36.39%). The ASPR of IS in China also showed an upward trend, with a 34.20% (32.11, 36.02%) increase in both sexes as of 2021, whereas it slightly declined globally. ICH prevalence for both sexes in China has shown only a modest rise since 1990, with a 16.41% (15.32, 16.43%) increase compared to just 0.43% (−0.23, 0.48%) globally. The ASPR of ICH in China has generally declined, with consistently higher male rates, similar to the global trends. In contrast, SAH prevalence trends differed, declining in China, but increasing globally. The ASPR of SAH also fell between 1990 and 2021, decreasing by 36.16% (34.95, 36.86%) in China and 16.13% (15.11, 17.24%) globally. Unlike the other two subtypes, the ASPR of SAH in China was consistently lower than the global level and higher in females than in males [Supplementary Figures 2 and 3, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

Mortality

Between 1990 and 2021, mortality of stroke in China showed an increasing trend, with both sexes mortality rate increasing from 130.10 (95% UI: 113.47, 146.33) per 100,000 in 1990 to 182.16 (153.18, 213.16) per 100,000 in 2021, an increase of 40.01% (35.00, 45.67%). The global-level trend is the opposite, with a decrease of 2.61% (0.35, 5.81%), and in 2021 it was 91.91 (83.22, 98.95) per 100,000.

The ASMR of stroke in China started to decrease yearly from 242.18 (95% UI: 213.83, 272.66) per 100,000 in 1990 and then increased slightly, rising to a peak of 231.69 (212.91, 248.03) per 100,000 around 2004 and then decreasing yearly to 138.03 (116.69, 160.32) per 100,000 in 2021, with an overall decrease of 43.01% (41.20, 45.43%). Global change is comparable but always lower than the Chinese level. In terms of sex, ASMR in China and globally has decreased more in females than in males.

During this period, the overall stroke mortality rate in China was higher than the global rate, although the trends varied across subtypes. The ASMR of the IS initially increased before subsequently declining. The ASMR of IS for both sexes increased from 75.22 (95% UI: 64.48, 88.23) per 100,000 in 1990 to 88.04 (80.56, 94.52) per 100,000 in 2004, and then declined to 64.47 (54.03, 74.82) per 100,000 in 2021; a similar trend was observed for ICH, which reached a slight peak in 2004 before declining, showing a 50.71% reduction (49.91, 52.42%) compared to 1990, a greater decrease than the global reduction of 36.58% (35.77, 37.41%); the ASMR of SAH decreases steeply to 4.72 (3.45, 5.95) per 100,000 in 2021 from 1990, a decrease of 82.71% (73.09, 83.50%), which is larger than that of the global level of 56.12% (46.23, 60.02%). The ASMR of SAH in China was comparable to that at the global level from 2004 onward [Figure 2; Supplementary Figure 4, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

DALY rate and YLL/YLD

Between 1990 and 2021, both sexes DALY rate for stroke in China increased by 15.73% (95% UI: 11.58, 19.58%), from 3230.32 (2841.43, 3641.74) per 100,000 in 1990 to 3738.59 (3170.54, 4354.82) per 100,000 in 2021. In contrast, the global trend showed a decrease of 10.67% (9.10, 12.94%). Although the DALY rate increased, the ASDR of stroke in China from 1990 to 2021 showed a similar trend of fluctuating decline as the ASMR and was always higher than the global level.

However, the DALY rate varies among the stroke subtypes. In 2021, the DALY rate of IS for both sexes in China was 1646.84 (95% UI: 1400.04, 1893.09) per 100,000, an increase of 95.19% (91.07, 93.54%) since 1990, significantly higher than the global increase of 2.98% (1.20, 3.96%). Conversely, the ASDR of IS in China showed an overall decrease, albeit to a lesser extent than the global rate. Reflecting mortality trends, the ASDR of IS for both sexes rose from 1387.93 (1188.74, 1621.40) per 100,000 in 1990 to 1525.95 (1399.80, 1640.28) per 100,000 in 2004, and then fell to 1180.98 (1009.70, 1356.67) per 100,000 in 2021, a decline of 14.91% (15.06, 16.33%), which is less than the global decline of 34.90% (34.23, 36.10%). Similarly, ICH ASDR peaked at 2591.33 (2399.17, 2791.27) per 100,000 in 2004, dropping to 1351.55 (1129.11, 1600.86) per 100,000 in 2021. The ASDR of SAH has been decreasing steeply to 115.49 (86.86, 142.50) per 100,000 in 2021 from 1990, a reduction of 81.28% (72.46, 82.17%). In 2021, the ASDR for all stroke subtypes in China and globally reached their lowest levels, and the level in males was consistently higher than that in both sexes and females [Supplementary Figures 5 and 6, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

In 1990, the YLL/YLD ratio for stroke in both sexes in China is 20.13, with the ratios for the three stroke subtypes being 8.40, 46.11, and 28.23, respectively. From 1990 to 2021, the YLL/YLD ratios for stroke and its subtypes in China declined, with a more noticeable fluctuating decrease in the ratio for ICH. By 2021, the YLL/YLD ratio for stroke is expected to decrease to 9.48 for both sexes, with IS = 5.04, ICH = 29.94, and SAH = 7.63. Among these subtypes, ICH had the highest YLL/YLD ratio, followed by SAH and IS. In addition, the ratio was consistently higher for males than females, indicating that a greater proportion of the disease burden in males was due to a fatal disease impact [Figure 3].

Changes in major risk factors for stroke

In 1990, the key stroke risk factors in China were high systolic blood pressure, household air pollution from solid fuels, smoking, a diet high in sodium, and ambient particulate matter pollution. By 2021, the attributable DALY rate for all stroke risk factors reached 3195.75 (95% UI: 2662.00, 3738.80) per 100,000. The major risk factors in 2021 are high systolic blood pressure, ambient particulate matter pollution, smoking, a diet high in sodium, and high LDL cholesterol. For males, these factors included high systolic blood pressure, smoking, ambient particulate matter pollution, diet high in sodium, and high LDL cholesterol, whereas for females, these factors were high systolic blood pressure, ambient particulate matter pollution, diet high in sodium, high LDL cholesterol, and high fasting plasma glucose. We observed that high systolic blood pressure consistently contributed the highest proportion to the DALY rate for stroke and its subtypes among all risk factors, showing a substantial increase compared with 1990. The health risks associated with indoor air pollution are decreasing, whereas the impact of outdoor air pollution is becoming increasingly significant. In addition to common risk factors (high systolic blood pressure, ambient particulate matter pollution, smoking, and a diet high in sodium), high LDL cholesterol levels had the strongest correlation with IS, and lead exposure also had a strong correlation with ICH and SAH [Figure 4].

Trend analysis of the burden of disease for stroke and its subtypes in China, 1990–2021

Joinpoint regression analysis showed that the ASIR of stroke and its subtypes in China fluctuated downward from 1990 to 2021 (except for IS), and the ASIR of IS showed a pronounced upward trend. The ASIR of IS in both sexes and females showed the largest increase from 2015 to 2019, with APC of 2.44% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 2.04, 2.84%) and 3.79% (95% CI: 3.79, 4.15%) respectively, whereas males had the largest increase in 1990 to 1994 with an APC of 2.48% (95% CI: 2.31, 2.65%) [Table 1].

The ASPR of stroke and IS in China from 1990 to 2021 showed an increasing trend, whereas the ASPR of ICH and SAH showed a decreasing trend. The ASPR of IS increased significantly, with the largest increase in the ASPR of IS in both sexes occurring between 2002 and 2011, with an APC of 1.59% (95% CI: 1.48, 1.69%). The ASPR of IS in males showed the largest increase between 2001 and 2009, with an APC of 1.67% (95% CI: 1.62, 1.72%), whereas for females, the ASPR of IS increased the most between 2003 and 2011, with an APC of 1.45% (95% CI: 1.25, 1.64%). The ASPR of SAH decreased more notably, with the largest decline in ASPR of SAH in both sexes and males occurring between 2006 and 2009, with APCs of −2.65% (95% CI: −2.86, −2.44%) and −2.45% (95% CI: −2.64, −2.27%) respectively. The ASPR of SAH in females showed the largest decrease between 2005 and 2010, with an APC of −2.58% (95% CI: −2.63, −2.53%) [Supplementary Table 1, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

The ASMR for both stroke and its subtypes in China has shown a decreasing trend, with the most significant decrease in SAH. The ASMR for SAH in both sexes and females decreased the most between 2000 and 2004, with APCs of −15.31% (95% CI: −16.59, −14.01%) and −16.26% (95% CI: −16.75, −15.76%), respectively, whereas that for SAH in males decreased the most between 2000 and 2006, with an APC of −12.90% (95% CI: −13.60, −12.19%) [Table 2].

Most ASDRs for stroke and its subtypes in China showed a decreasing trend, except from 1998 to 2004. Among them, the ASDR of stroke in China declined the most from 2004 to 2007, with APCs of −5.44% (95% CI: −6.2, −4.68%), −4.71% (95% CI: −5.79, −3.61%), and −6.34% (95% CI: −7.05, −5.63%) for both sexes, males, and females, respectively. The ASDR for IS and ICH exhibited patterns of change similar to those observed in stroke patients, with the greatest decline occurring between 2004 and 2007. The APC for the ASDR of IS in both sexes, males, and females were −4.10% (95% CI: −5.11, −3.08%), −3.69% (95% CI: −4.90, −2.47%), and −4.76% (95% CI: −5.86, −3.65%), respectively; and the ASDR of ICH were −6.01% (95% CI: −6.72, −5.29%), −5.19% (95% CI: −6.24, −4.12%), and −7.09% (95% CI: −7.84, −6.34%), respectively. However, changes in SAH were different, with a more pronounced downward trend compared with stroke and other subtypes. The ASDR of SAH for both sexes and females declined the most from 2000 to 2004, with APCs of −14.22% (95% CI: −14.81, −13.63%) and −14.94% (95% CI: −15.55, −14.33%), respectively. The greatest change in males occurred from 2000 to 2003, with an APC of 13.42% (95% CI: −15.71, −11.06%). All the above trends were statistically significant (P <0.05) [Supplementary Table 2, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

Trend prediction of disease burden of stroke and its subtypes in China, 2022–2040

As predicted by the BAPC model, the ASIR of stroke in Chinese males shows a decreasing trend between 2022 and 2040 and is predicted to decrease to a low value of 229.67 per 100,000 in 2040, whereas the ASIR of stroke in Chinese females shows a slow increasing trend during the forecast period, with a predicted ASIR of 190.12 per 100,000 in 2040. Regarding stroke subtypes, we found that the ASIR of IS will continue to increase from 2022 to 2040 and is expected to peak by 2040, with predicted ASIR of 181.39 per 100,000 and 152.49 per 100,000 in males and females, respectively, in 2040. The ASIR of ICH is expected to decrease from 2022 to 2040, with the ASIR of males and females falling to 50.13 per 100,000 and 35.53 per 100,000, respectively, in 2040. During this period, the ASIR for SAH in males will increase slightly to 9.98 per 100,000 by 2040, whereas the ASIR for females will continue to decline to 7.09 per 100,000 by 2040 [Figure 5; Supplementary Table 3, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

The ASPR of stroke in Chinese males shows an increasing and then decreasing trend from 2022 to 2040 and begins to decline in 2029, which is predicted to fall to 1375.83 per 100,000 in 2040, whereas the ASPR of stroke in Chinese females shows a significant upward trend during the prediction period and is predicted to reach 1520.33 per 100,000 in 2040. For stroke subtypes, there is a small increase in the ASPR for IS in males between 2022 and 2040. The ASPR for females is likely to increase substantially and is projected to reach 1219.33 per 100,000 in 2040. The trend of ASPR for ICH was similar to that for IS, with a larger increase in females than in males. During this period, the ASPR trend for SAH was the opposite, with a significant increase in the ASPR for SAH in males predicted to rise to 77.50 per 100,000 in 2040, whereas females will see a slight decrease. In addition, the projections show that the ASMR and ASDR for stroke and its subtypes in China will show a decreasing trend between 2022 and 2040 and are projected to reach low values by 2040 [Supplementary Figures 7–9 and Supplementary Table 3, http://links.lww.com/CM9/C531].

Discussion

In this study, the trends in stroke and its subtypes in China over the past 30 years were comprehensively analyzed using the latest GBD 2021 database. First, the study showed that the incidence of stroke in China has almost doubled, increasing by 100.64%, whereas the global incidence has increased by only 15.03%. This discrepancy is significant and highlights the health challenges faced by China during its rapid socioeconomic development, especially the heavy burden of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases. As mentioned in the GBD 2016 study, China has the highest lifetime risk of stroke among those over 25 years of age globally, reaching 39.3%.[4] It was found that the prevalence of IS (201.13%) will increase substantially in China by 2021, which is significantly higher than the global average, and is predicted to continue to rise by 2040.

This rising trend not only reflects the increasing aging population in China, but also reveals the prevalence of unhealthy lifestyles, the increasing burden of chronic diseases, and inadequate stroke prevention measures. Especially the prevalence of major stroke risk factors in China, such as hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and air pollution, coupled with unhealthy lifestyles like high-sodium, high-fat diets, high smoking rates, and increasingly widespread physical inactivity.[11] Females are more likely to suffer from diabetes and obesity, while males are more likely to engage in alcohol consumption and smoking.[12] All these factors have contributed to the increase in stroke cases. In contrast, many other countries and regions worldwide have made significant gains in stroke prevention and control. One of the greatest advances in this field has been the effective management of hypertension. Globally, approximately 54% of stroke cases are attributed to hypertension. Many countries have significantly reduced the incidence of stroke by proposing national policies to promote the screening and treatment of hypertension, as well as including hypertension through basic healthcare services.[13] Particularly, in some high-income countries, hypertension control rates have increased dramatically owing to the use of comprehensive medications, promotion of healthy lifestyles, and regular monitoring.[14] Moreover, countries have progressively strengthened health education and lifestyle interventions for populations at a high risk of stroke. For example, in North America and Europe, widely implemented tobacco control[15] and salt restriction[16] policies have significantly reduced the incidences of cardiovascular diseases and stroke. Through community health programs and campaigns, these countries have effectively raised public awareness of stroke risk factors. In the primary prevention of stroke, the widespread use of lipid-lowering and antiplatelet drugs has also helped reduce the risk of first stroke.[17]

The prevalence of stroke in both sexes increased from 912.15 per 100,000 in 1990 to 1851.03 per 100,000 in 2021, representing an increase of 102.93%. Although the prevalence of stroke has increased globally, China’s growth rate is far higher than the global average (102.93% vs. 25.77%). This increase in IS prevalence was particularly significant. Data from 1672 tertiary public hospitals in the Hospital Quality Monitoring System (HQMS) showed that 3,411,168 stroke patients were admitted in 2019. Among them, IS, ICH, and SAH accounted for 82.6 %, 14.2%, and 3.1% of cases, respectively.[18] This indicates that many post-stroke survivors are still undergoing long-term rehabilitation. This implies that China faces greater challenges in stroke rehabilitation and long-term care. The prediction shows that the standardized prevalence of stroke in females will present a significant upward trend from 2022 to 2040, suggesting that more attention should be paid to the rehabilitation of females after stroke.

It is encouraging that the ASMR of stroke in China has shown a more obvious downward trend, especially a significant decrease in the ASMR of ICH (−50.71%) and SAH (−80.36%). This phenomenon may be related to the tremendous progress in acute stroke management, stroke center construction, and improvement of stroke treatment systems in China in recent years, including improvements in medical insurance coverage and medical conditions, large-scale stroke screening, clinical diagnosis, treatment technology, and further enhancement of public awareness of stroke.[19] Since the establishment of the China Stroke Prevention Program Committee (CSPPC) under the leadership of the Ministry of Health in 2011, the healthcare system has conducted a series of in-depth preventive and therapeutic studies for stroke patients. The nationwide coverage of Chinese stroke centers, the establishment of the stroke emergency map system, and the implementation of the “Stroke 1-2-0” rapid response plan have maximized the golden emergency time, effectively increased the stroke treatment rate, and reduced the stroke mortality rate.[20,21] Furthermore, the global decline in stroke mortality (a decrease of 2.61%) indicates that stroke management strategies worldwide are making progress.This is also due to the progress and implementation of measures such as the optimization of stroke care pathways, promotion of thrombolytic therapy, and mechanical thrombolysis in some developed countries in Europe and the United States.[22]

Despite the decrease in mortality, the DALY rate has increased, and stroke remains a major public health problem in China. Between 1990 and 2021, the DALY rate for both sexes in China increased by 15.73% and decreased globally by 10.67%. Especially, the increase in the DALY rate for IS was large. In the early 2000s, China experienced profound socioeconomic changes with accelerated urbanization, industrialization, and an aging population. These changes have led to the Westernization of dietary patterns, lifestyle changes, and increased environmental pollution, resulting in a significant rise in the burden of chronic diseases,[23] particularly cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, and obesity, which saw a continuous increase in incidence. Furthermore, as health conditions and lifestyles gradually converge with those in Western countries, the incidence of chronic diseases is also increasing among younger populations. Earlier onset of these chronic diseases means that, although their mortality may be lower, they have a significant impact on healthy life expectancy, leading to a marked increase in the loss of healthy years of life, thereby driving the rise in DALY rates during this period. Furthermore, we found that the YLL/YLD ratio for stroke and its subtypes in China showed a decreasing trend from 1990 to 2021, suggesting that the proportion of nonfatal disease burden in the disease burden of stroke in China is increasing, indicating that some of the fatal disease burden has shifted to the nonfatal disease burden. This phenomenon suggests that despite improvements in acute-phase stroke care, the problems of long-term disability and inadequate rehabilitation post-stroke remain serious. Inadequate post-stroke rehabilitation services and long-term care[24] greatly affect the long-term quality of life of patients. The lack of rehabilitation services leads to the inability of stroke patients to fully regain function, thus increasing the rate of disability, which has become an important factor in the increasing rate of DALY among stroke patients in China. In the late 2000s, owing to multiple measures in stroke prevention and control (e.g., policy interventions, health education, early screening, advances in treatment technology),[25] China achieved relatively impressive results in controlling the overall disease burden of stroke, resulting in a significant decline in the ASDR of stroke in China over a certain period of time; however, the overall decline is still small compared with that of the world. China needs to invest more resources in the long-term rehabilitation and care of patients with stroke.[26,27] Furthermore, this study found that patients with ICH had a high YLL/YLD ratio, which reflects its higher lethality. Owing to its sudden onset, complex hemorrhage locations, and challenges in early treatment, ICH typically leads to higher mortality rates. Despite some efforts in ICH management over the years, no significant improvements have been observed.[28] Therefore, the early identification of ICH, optimization of treatment strategies, and strengthening of subsequent care remain key directions for future prevention and control efforts.

Except for IS, the burden of disease of stroke subtypes has shown a substantial overall decline, especially in females, and similar trends have been observed globally. However, this downward trend has not yet fully offset the increase in overall disease burden, and many stroke prevention and control issues have not been adequately addressed, particularly in males. Sex differences were particularly pronounced for stroke incidence, death, and disability. The incidence and prevalence are generally higher in males than in females, and the decline in mortality is smaller in males than in females. This is consistent with existing literature on the effects of smoking, alcohol consumption, and unhealthy lifestyles in males. This may be associated with higher rates of smoking, alcohol consumption, and work stress in males. Smoking prevalence in China in 2019 was 45.6% in males and 1.7% in females.[29] However, females may have better stroke prevention and treatment outcomes than males do. A prospective cohort study reported that unstable blood pressure control may increase the risk of IS in females with hypertension.[30] Health-related behavioral interventions, especially smoking control and physical activity, may be more effective in preventing stroke in hypertensive females, especially smoking control and physical hypertension.[31] The BAPC model predicts that the ASIR of stroke in Chinese males is likely to decrease in the coming years, suggesting that China’s stroke prevention and control measures in recent years may become progressively effective. The China Stroke Prevention and Control Report 2021 outlines that the “Stroke Screening and Intervention Program for High-Risk Groups” has begun to bear fruit and that significant progress has been made in controlling stroke risk factors in recent years, especially in controlling alcohol consumption, lowering the prevalence of smoking, and promoting a healthy diet. Given the current high risk and burden of stroke in males, further strengthening of existing prevention and control measures will be the key to reducing stroke risk in males in the future.

The epidemiological characteristics of SAH differ from those of the other stroke subtypes. The incidence of SAH increased noticeably in the late 1990s, which may be related to several factors. First, improvements in diagnostic capabilities. By the late 1990s, medical technology and imaging equipment in China began to modernize, especially with the widespread use of CT and MRI, which improved the diagnosis rate of SAH.[32] Second, changes in statistics and reporting. In some underdeveloped areas, certain diseases may have been underestimated or missed in earlier years. With the improvement of the public health system and the enhancement of health statistical reporting, particularly the timely and systematic reporting of acute cerebrovascular events, many previously undiagnosed cases have been more widely identified and reported during this period, leading to a significant increase in the incidence of SAH. In addition, the prevalence of SAH displayed distinct features. The global prevalence of SAH is higher than that in China, despite China showing a higher burden than the global prevalence for several indicators. Multiple studies[333435] have shown that the prevalence of SAH in China is lower than that in many other countries worldwide, which may be related to genetic susceptibility and lifestyle[36] and may also be related to the fact that early diagnosis and reporting of SAH in China are still somewhat inadequate. The prevalence of SAH is higher in females than in males both globally and in China, and this phenomenon of sex differences has been confirmed in many studies. The prevalence of SAH in females is 1.24 (1.09–1.42) times higher than that in males. This sex difference starts at the age of 55 years,[37] which may be related to physiological and endocrine factors, especially the decline in estrogen levels after menopause. Estrogen has a cardiovascular protective effect[38] and a postmenopausal decrease in estrogen levels leads to a decrease in vascular elasticity, increasing the risk of aneurysm rupture and SAH. Furthermore, the vascular structure and mechanism of cerebral aneurysm development in females may contribute to this sex-based difference. Although most studies have shown that females have a higher risk of SAH than males, some have also concluded that this finding does not apply to all populations[39] and that the specific manifestations may vary in different regions and populations. The complex mechanisms underlying sex differences may also be related to environmental factors, genetic backgrounds, and lifestyles, which need to be elucidated in further studies.

Although this study offers key insights into the stroke trends in China, it has some limitations. First, owing to issues with data availability and consistency, this study did not conduct an analysis at the provincial level. Although some large-scale epidemiological datasets (such as the China Disease Surveillance Network) cover certain provinces in China, geographical and temporal distribution disparities remain. If more comprehensive provincial data are available, future studies could further explore the regional differences in stroke burden to formulate more precise prevention and control measures for each province. Second, this study primarily analyzed the epidemiological trends of stroke based on macro-level data, especially personalized strategies for high-risk groups (e.g., elderly and patients with chronic disease) who represent a large portion of stroke cases.[40] Future research should focus on targeted interventions to mitigate the stroke risk in these populations. Third, the study did not sufficiently address post-acute rehabilitation and care, which are crucial for long-term outcomes and quality of life. The incorporation of individual-level clinical and long-term follow-up data is required to better understand the full stroke trajectory. Furthermore, although the BAPC model used in this study provided valuable predictive insights, it has certain limitations. For instance, the BAPC model assumes that disease trends are primarily driven by age, period, and cohort effects, potentially overlooking other influencing factors such as socioeconomic changes, public health policies, and advancements in medical technology. Future research could enhance the robustness and predictive accuracy of the model through sensitivity analyses or by comparing it with other predictive models.

In conclusion, the public health challenge of stroke in China remains critical, with the need to emphasize the prevention and control of IS. Future policies should focus on early stroke prevention and long-term rehabilitation strategies. Given the higher stroke burden in males, future studies should explore sex differences to develop targeted strategies. Future interventions could be made more precise and effective by analyzing sex, age, and lifestyle factors, ultimately reducing the overall stroke burden.

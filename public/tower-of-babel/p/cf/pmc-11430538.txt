Assessing the reliability and readability of migraine treatment information on Turkish websites
Authors: Tuba Tanyel Saraçoğlu, Gizem Gürsoy
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe quality and safety of information provided on online platforms for migraine treatment remains uncertain. We evaluated the top 10 trending websites accessed annually by Turkish patients seeking solutions for migraine treatment and assessed information quality, security, and readability in this cross-sectional study.MethodsA comprehensive search strategy was conducted using Google starting in 2015, considering Türkiye’s internet usage trends. Websites were evaluated using the DISCERN measurement tool and Ateşman Turkish readability index.ResultsNinety websites were evaluated between 2015 and 2024. According to the DISCERN measurement tool, most websites exhibited low quality and security levels. Readability analysis showed that half of the websites were understandable by readers with 9th − 10th grade educational levels. The author distribution varied, with neurologists being the most common. A significant proportion of the websites were for profit. Treatment of attacks and preventive measures were frequently mentioned, but some important treatments, such as greater occipital nerve blockade, were rarely discussed.ConclusionThis study highlights the low quality and reliability of online information websites on migraine treatment in Türkiye. These websites’ readability level remains a concern, potentially hindering patients’ access to accurate information. This can be a barrier to migraine care for both patients with migraine and the physician. Better supervision and cooperation with reputable medical associations are needed to ensure the dissemination of reliable information to the public.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12913-024-11599-4.Keywords: Migraine, Discern measurement tool, Readability index, Türkiye

Background

According to the publication of the Global Burden of Disease 2021 nervous system disorders collaborators, migraine ranks first among neurological disorders causing health loss worldwide between the ages of 5 and 19 years and ranks second after stroke between the ages of 20 and 59 years. Additionally, women are 1.69 times more affected than men [1]. In Türkiye, the incidence rate of migraine was reported as 2.38% (2.98% for females and 1.93% for males) in a study published in 2015 [2]. A study conducted in 2023 aimed to evaluate the prevalence of migraine among neurologists in Türkiye and found that 37.9% of 851 participants met the diagnostic criteria for migraine [3].

Migraine is a headache characterized by unilateral, pulsatile pain that can last 4–72 h and negatively affect quality of life [4]. Its treatment can be divided into two main categories: attack and preventive treatments. Acetaminophen, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), and triptans are effective in the treatment of acute migraine [5, 6]. Preventive treatment options include medical prophylactic drugs (beta- blockers, antidepressants, anticonvulsants, calcium channel blockers, the angiotensin receptor blockers/angiotensin converting enzyme inhibitors), botulinum toxin injections, nerve blocks, and calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) antagonists [7]. Migraine treatment continues to be a subject of curiosity as it is one of the leading causes of disability [6–8].

In a study conducted in Türkiye evaluating health literacy in patients with migraine, participants were asked how they accessed health-related information. A total of 211 participants were assessed and 104 participants received information from health professionals, 77 from relatives and friends, and 93 from written and visual media and internet [9].

According to Turkish Statistical Institute (TURKSTAT) data, the rate of internet use in Türkiye was 55.9% in 2015 and 66.3% of users searched the internet for health-related information [10]. In 2023, the proportion of individuals using the internet was 87.1%; An increasing trend from 2012 to 2023 has been observed [11].

A study examining patient internet use and behavior in accessing health information using electronic media found that patients search for information about their disease before applying to visit the doctor or hospital and for additional information after their visit [12]. Therefore, if the information obtained by patients online does not contain accurate information or is insufficient, they may be misled, making the physician’s work difficult.

Internet use and health literacy are increasing. Another study evaluating internet use for health purposes found that six out of every ten participants used the internet for health purposes [13].

Bojazar et al. evaluated Googling of migraine; the search was conducted in English and the reliability of information websites and the treatment options they offered were evaluated. They found many treatment recommendations of different qualities [14].

To our knowledge, there has been no comprehensive evaluation of online information websites on migraine treatment in Türkiye.

This study aimed to evaluate the top 10 trending websites accessed annually by Turkish patients seeking online solutions for migraine treatment from 2015 to 2024. We assessed the quality and safety of the information using the DISCERN measurement tool and the Turkish readability level using the Ateşman Turkish readability index. We also examined the content provided and the professional background of the authors.

Methods

This observational cross-sectional study evaluated the top 10 trending websites accessed annually by patients seeking online solutions for migraine treatment in Türkiye. The study assessed information quality and safety using the DISCERN measurement tool and the Turkish readability level using the Ateşman Turkish readability index. The authors of the websites, treatment content, and changes over time were also analyzed. As this study utilized publicly available internet information, ethics committee approval was not required.

Search strategy

According to TURKSTAT data, internet usage in Türkiye exceeded 55% in 2015 (55.9%) [10]. Therefore, the search began in 2015. Google was the preferred search engine for the study because it has the largest market share in Türkiye [15] (Fig. 1).

Browser Market Share in Türkiye from the beginning of 2015 to the beginning of 2024

The search and data collection, were conducted by two researchers on March 18, 2024. Two computers were used, the location was turned off, the cache and search history were reset, all cookies were blocked, private mode was used, and the search history was cleared after each year was searched. The researchers conducted the search in Küçükçekmece, Istanbul, and Ümraniye, Istanbul. To evaluate online information websites related to migraine treatment, the search term “migraine” was used between 2015 and 2024 using Google Trends. Among the top five relevant queries, “migraine treatment,” which is the only query related to treatment, was selected as the keyword to be used in this study [16]. According to Google Trend, top searches are the most frequently searched terms within the selected category, country, or region of the term entered in the same search session [17]. Figure 2 shows the popularity of the keyword “migraine treatment” over the years in Türkiye [18].

Trend of the keyword “migraine treatment” from the beginning of 2015 to the beginning of 2024

From 2015 to 2024, the dates January 1, 00:00–January 1, 00:00 were entered in the time tab from Google tools, and the search was performed separately for each year. The search results were filtered to only show results from Türkiye in the Turkish language.

Paid sponsored ads, YouTube videos, lecture notes, articles written for academic purposes, and duplications were excluded from the search, and the first 10 websites other than these were evaluated.

The included websites were evaluated with the DISCERN measurement tool and the Ateşman Turkish readability index.

Two authors independently evaluated all the websites using the DISCERN measurement tool and Ateşman Turkish readability index engine. In case of discrepancies, reassessment was performed and a consensus was reached. In addition, the profession of the authors and the types of treatment mentioned (acute, preventive, interventional, and other) on the websites providing information during the search were noted. Furthermore, according to the Google Trends data, the five provinces that had the highest keyword search during these years were noted. A higher value indicated a higher rate across all queries.

DISCERN measurement tool

The DISCERN measurement tool is a validated tool developed to assess the quality of written consumer health information on treatment options. This measurement tool consists of 16 questions divided into three parts. Section 1 contains questions 1 to 8 and assesses the reliability of information. The reliability of information is assessed in Sect. 1, which consists of the first 8 questions. Information quality is assessed in Sect. 2 and covers questions 9 to 15. In Sect. 3, the last question, question 16, assesses the evaluator’s overall impression of the source. Each question is given a score between ‘1’ (no) and ‘5’ (yes). At the end of the evaluation, the scores are between ‘8 and 40’ for the reliability section, between ‘7 and 35’ for the treatment options section and between ‘1 and 5’ for the overall score section, which evaluates the site as a whole. The total DISCERN measurement tool score at the end of all ratings is between ‘16 and 80’. According to the overall DISCERN measurement tool score, the range of ‘16 to 26’ is considered as ‘very poor’, the range of ‘27 to 38’ is considered as ‘poor’, the range of ‘39 to 50’ is considered as ‘fair’, the range of ‘51 to 62’ is considered as ‘good’ and the score above ‘63’ is considered as ‘excellent’ quality [19].

Ateşman Turkish readability index

The readability of the websites was evaluated using the Ateşman Turkish Readability Formula, adapted to the Turkish language, and obtained from “http://okunabilirlikindeksi.com/” [20]. The content of the entire text on the relevant website was evaluated using the readability formula [21]. According to the readability index, “90–100 points” means the content is easily understood by students in 4th grade and below, “80–89 points” by 5th or 6th-grade students, “70–79 points” by 7th or 8th-grade students, “60–69 points” by 9th or 10th-grade students, “50–59 points” by 11th or 12th-grade students, “40–49 points” by 13th or 15th grade (associate degree) students, “30–39 points” by undergraduates, and “‘29 points and below” by graduates.

Statistical analysis

The SPSS 23 Windows package program (IBM, New York, USA) was used for data analysis (No: 581811, 2021). The Shapiro–Wilk test and histograms were used for normality assessment. The data was analyzed using descriptive statistics. The Kruskal–Wallis test was used when the distribution was not normal and the one-way ANOVA test was used when the distribution was normal. According to the years, the DISCERN measurement tool scores were analysed by Kruskal-Wallis test and Ateşman Turkish readability scores were analysed by one-way ANOVA test. The Spearman correlation analysis was used for the correlation between the DISCERN measurement tool and Ateşman Turkish readability index.

Results

From 2015 to 2024, 90 websites were evaluated, with the top 10 websites assessed each year. The top five provinces searching for “migraine treatment” were Ardahan, Siirt, Diyarbakır, Muş, and Van. Based on Google Trends data, the keyword “migraine treatment” was selected and analyzed from 2015 to 2024. A total of 16 paid sponsored advertisement websites, 13 YouTube information websites, 5 duplicated websites, and 3 scientific articles were excluded. In total, 90 websites were evaluated, with 10 websites assessed each year.

Traditional and complementary medicine treatments were also mentioned on the evaluated websites (Table 4). The most frequently mentioned treatment method was acupuncture (n = 9), followed by ozone (n = 3). Of the authors who mentioned acupuncture, 33.3% were general practitioners, followed by physiotherapists and rehabilitation specialists (22%).

In total, only four websites mentioned medical treatment side effects (2019, 2021, and 2022 [n = 2]) and only three websites mentioned medication overuse headache (2019 and 2022 [n = 2]). All websites that included information on migraine attack treatment provided information on non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and paracetamol, but only seven websites mentioned triptans (2017, 2018, 2019 [n = 2], 2020, 2022, and 2023). The authors of the websites providing information on triptans are neurologists. Preventive treatments were grouped into antiepileptic, antidepressant, and antihypertensive treatment options, and only one website mentioned the active ingredients.

Discussion

This study primarily aimed to evaluate the quality, safety, and readability of online information websites on migraine treatment in Türkiye, revealing significant concerns about the information available to patients. The incidence of migraine in Türkiye is 2.38%, with women being more affected than men [2]. The increasing internet usage for health information emphasizes the importance of reliable online resources. Our findings indicate that most websites providing information on migraine treatment are of low quality, potentially misleading patients and complicating the work of healthcare providers.

The analysis showed that only 9% of the evaluated websites met moderate or high-quality standards according to the DISCERN measurement tool. This low quality is concerning as it implies that many patients might be receiving incomplete or incorrect information. The readability analysis revealed that half of the websites were understandable by readers with a 9th–10th grade educational level, which might be insufficient for a broader audience with varying literacy levels.

The professional backgrounds of the authors were diverse, with neurologists being the most common, but there was a notable presence of information from non-specialist sources. This could contribute to the variability in information quality and the frequent mention of profit-driven treatments. Specifically, critical treatments like greater occipital nerve (GON) blockade were rarely discussed, which might lead to underutilization of effective treatment options.

There are new developments in migraine treatment every day, and these developments are due to matter curiosity. Currently, the prevalence of internet use, the easiest information tool, is 87.5% in Türkiye according to TURKSTAT data [11]; this rate had exceeded 50% as of 2015 and is always trending upward [10].

Today, with widespread internet use, informative articles on health have increased. This increase has occurred particularly in profit-oriented private websites. Information on migraine treatment has been provided by neurologists and practitioners in many other medical fields. Migraine treatment should be provided especially in the disciplines of neurology and pain management clinics, but only 31.1% of the information was provided by neurologists, and none was provided by pain management specialists.

Websites evaluated

Migraine treatments are varied and include attack and preventive treatments, botulinum toxin injections, nerve blocks, and migraine vaccination [5–7].

There was an increase in information on attack and preventive treatments, particularly in 2019 and 2022, and the increase in the proportion of neurology specialists as authors among the websites providing information in these years is also noteworthy.

In the review of websites examined, 44.4% acknowledged the management of attacks, but unfortunately, only three of these websites provided information on medication overuse headache. Triptans, which are an important medical treatment option for migraine attacks, were mentioned by only seven websites, which is very low. It is important for migraineurs seeking information to be informed about medication side effects and medication overuse headache. Providing complete and accurate information about the treatment of attacks may reduce the burden of hospital emergency department visits and workforce loss among patients.

This study found that 35.5% of participants mentioned prophylactic treatment. These treatments were categorized into three main classes, including antihypertensive, antidepressant, and antiepileptic drugs, and were only mentioned in this way. However, only four sites mentioned the associated side effects, indicating a very low frequency. Nevertheless, an overly comprehensive presentation of side effects could discourage patients from seeking medical advice, which is a notable dilemma.

Information on GON blockage was provided only six times in the last 9 years and was not mentioned at all in the pages analyzed for migraine treatment in 2015, 2016, 2022, and 2023. The physicians who provided information about blockade were mostly anesthesiologists (n = 4, 66.7%), followed by neurologists (n = 2, 33.3%). Many studies have been conducted on nerve blocks and their effects on migraine attack frequency and pain severity. Unfortunately, the rate of mention of this important treatment on information websites was 6.7%. This may explain the insufficient information about this treatment among patients with migraine seeking online solutions.

Authors who highlighted botulinum toxin therapy included on anonymously-authored private hospital information sharing websites (n = 10), plastic surgeons (n = 6), dermatologists (n = 2) and neurosurgeons (n = 1). Only 28.5 per cent (n = 8) of the 28 neurologists who were authors in total mentioned botulinum toxin therapy, which is a very low rate. We believe that this problem should be eliminated and neurologists should adopt botulinum toxin therapy in migraine more easily.

Calcitonin gene-related peptides antagonist treatments, known as migraine vaccine in Türkiye, was on the top 10 trending websites, especially from 2020. In total, the rate of mention was 8.9%. This rate was particularly high in 2022 (40%, n = 4). This may be because this treatment, which received Food and Drug Administration approval in 2018, was at the forefront in 2020 in Türkiye and was approved in Türkiye in 2021. All the physicians who mentioned this treatment were neurologists. The rate of mention of migraine vaccine among neurologists was 28.6% (n = 8).

Websites that did not mention medical treatment, especially those that emphasized applications such as botulinum toxin, acupuncture, neural therapy, or migraine surgery, may pose a problem. The fact that these websites (n = 8) talk about a single treatment option as a “definitive solution” may mislead the public. In addition, the fact that applications such as botulinum toxin for migraine are not performed by specialists in the field and therefore provide insufficient benefit may pose a problem as it may alienate patients with migraine from the treatment. Only one of the websites mentioning botulinum toxin-A treatment mentioned the side effects of the treatment. Patients who look for online information expect a definitive solution from the treatment and do not find any information on the side effects.

Websites that mention migraine surgery as a definitive solution and do not offer other treatment options are also objectionable, particularly because they may direct the public to a specific treatment. As of 2020, migraine surgery was not mentioned in any of the top 10 popular websites.

A study investigating the preferences for traditional and complementary medicine among patients with primary headache found that 35.9% (n = 23) of patients with migraine preferred traditional and complementary medicine, with chiropractic massage being the most preferred (n = 7). Acupuncture was the least preferred (n = 1). However, 60% of patients with primary headache reported that they were not satisfied with traditional and complementary medicine methods [22]. In our study, it is interesting to note that 10% of the websites mentioned acupuncture, and this rate was even higher than that for GON blockade. On websites authored by general practitioners, there was a notable emphasis on traditional and complementary medicine treatments.

It is noteworthy that more than 90% of the websites providing information are for-profit websites. This may be the reason for the emphasis on precise statements and injection procedures such as botulinum toxin, neural therapy, dry needling and alternative medicine techniques.

DISCERN measurement tool

Unfortunately, only 9% of the websites providing information were of moderate quality or higher. This is very inconvenient as it can lead patients who seek online solutions to obtain inadequate information about treatments, eventually leading them to obtain information about a single treatment and inadequate information about side effects.

The reasons for this situation may be that most of the authors were outside the field, private hospital information may have been prepared by non-physician editorial boards, and especially profit-oriented websites have beautified the single-treatment option.

Ateşman Turkish readability index

According to TURKSTAT data, while the average duration of education in Türkiye was 7.9 years in 2015, this rate gradually increased and reached 9.3 years in 2023 [23]. The fact that 50% of the readability levels of the evaluated information websites are at the 9–10th grade level and below suggests that half of the information websites can be understood by the readers.

The internet usage rate of households in Türkiye is increasing daily, and online solutions in health are needed in Türkiye and globally.

Provinces

Another result obtained in our study is that the Ardahan, Siirt, Diyarbakır, Muş, and Van provinces were the top five provinces from which individuals searched for the keyword “migraine treatment” in Google trends.

In a study evaluating the health performance of 81 provinces in Türkiye with current decision-making methods, Ardahan ranked 63rd, Siirt ranked 49th, Diyarbakır ranked 16th, Muş ranked 71st, and Van ranked 18th [24].

One of the reasons why ‘migraine treatment’ ranks high in Google Trends in these provinces may be that Muş, Ardahan and Siirt provinces are at the bottom of the health performance rankings. However, it is known that Van and Diyarbakır are among the top 20 provinces in health performance [24]. So another reason may be the high prevalence of migraine in these provinces, although a prevalence study by province has not yet been conducted in Türkiye.

This study has several limitations. First, the evaluation was limited to the top 10 trending websites annually, which may not represent the entirety of available online information. Second, the DISCERN measurement tool and Ateşman Turkish readability index, while robust, have their own limitations in evaluating online health information comprehensively. Third, the study did not account for the dynamic nature of online content, where information can change rapidly. Finally, the search was limited to Turkish websites, and the findings may not be generalizable to other regions or languages.

The findings highlight the need for improved regulation and quality control of online health information. Medical professionals and organizations should be more involved in creating and curating online content to ensure accuracy and reliability. Increased collaboration with reputable medical associations could enhance the dissemination of high-quality information. Additionally, efforts to improve the readability of online health information can make such information more accessible to a wider audience.

Future research should expand the scope to include a broader range of websites and more frequent evaluations to account for changes in online content. Investigating the impact of poor-quality online information on patient outcomes could provide further insight into the importance of reliable online health resources. Additionally, developing new tools and methods to assess the quality of online health information more comprehensively would be beneficial. Implementing and evaluating interventions aimed at improving the quality and readability of online health information should be a priority for healthcare providers and policymakers.

Conclusion

Over the past 9 years, the quality and reliability of popular information websites on migraine treatment in Türkiye have been consistently low. Furthermore, the readability of these sites is generally only suitable for individuals with a 9th–10th grade education, which may limit accessibility and understanding for a significant portion of the population. This presents a substantial barrier to patients with migraines in Türkiye, potentially preventing them from receiving accurate and effective treatment.

Our findings underscore the urgent need for a robust control mechanism to ensure the dissemination of accurate, high-quality information to the public. Non-profit organizations, particularly national medical associations, should take a leading role in auditing and regulating online health information. These organizations must produce and endorse content that is both reliable and understandable to the general public.

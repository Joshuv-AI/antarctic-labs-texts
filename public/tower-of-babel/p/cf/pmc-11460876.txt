The effect of copper and vitamin D on osteoarthritis outcomes: A Mendelian randomization study
Authors: Huan Luo, Yue Zhang, Chen Meng, Chuan Li, Daqi Jia, Yongqing Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This study aimed to explore the causal relationship between trace elements and osteoarthritis (OA). The results showed a relatively weak association between copper and OA, while vitamin D showed a significant positive association with OA. Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was used to investigate the causal relationship between copper and vitamin D and OA. A variety of MR methods including inverse variance weighting, weighted median, MR-Egger, simple model, and multi-text mixed model were analyzed to confirm the consistency of these results. Sensitivity analysis further confirmed the reliability of these causal relationships and excluded the interference of pleiotropy. These findings add to the understanding of the potential role of micronutrients in the prevention and treatment of OA and support the idea of vitamin D as a potential therapy for the prevention and treatment of OA. Future studies should further explore the specific biological mechanisms of these trace elements and the differences in their effects in different populations in order to develop more effective treatment strategies to reduce the health burden of degenerative joint diseases.

1. Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a common chronic joint disease and is widely regarded as a degenerative joint disease. This disorder is mainly caused by the wear and degeneration of articular cartilage, resulting in a gradual deterioration of joint structure and function over time.[1–3] OA usually occurs in major load-bearing joints such as the hands, knees, hips, and spine, and its typical symptoms include pain, stiffness, and reduced mobility, which have a significant impact on patients’ quality of life.[4] Aging is a key risk factor for the development of OA, and studies have shown that women have a higher prevalence than men, which may be related to hormone levels and biomechanical differences.[5] The pathogenesis of OA involves several biological processes. The collagen webs and proteoglycans in joint cartilage gradually break down, causing the cartilage to lose its elasticity and cushioning capacity, and the surface of the joint to become rough, increasing wear and tear. In addition, the joint inflammatory response increases the levels of cytokines and enzymes in the joint fluid, further accelerating cartilage damage. Changes in subosseous bone may also occur, such as the formation of osteophytes (bone spurs).[6–8] There is currently no cure for OA, and treatment focuses on relieving symptoms and improving quality of life. Treatment methods include: drug therapy, physical therapy, lifestyle adjustment, and surgical treatment.[9–11]

Trace elements are minerals that are essential to the human body but only need a small amount to meet physiological needs. Despite their low levels in the human body (usually <0.01%), these elements are essential for maintaining normal body function and health.[12] Trace elements play a role in many biological processes, including enzyme activation, hormone synthesis, maintenance of cellular structural stability, and as part of electrolyte balance.[13–15] Copper is an essential component of many oxidoreductases and is important for the absorption and utilization of iron and for the health of the cardiovascular system and nervous system.[16,17] Calcium is an essential element for building and maintaining bone health and directly affects bone density and bone strength. Adequate calcium intake helps prevent osteoporosis, which is a factor in accelerating joint degradation.[18]

MR estimates the effect of exposure on health outcomes by using genetic variants (single nucleotide polymorphisms [SNPS]) as instrumental variables (IVs). This method is based on the principle of Mendelian genetics, that is, the genetic variation of an individual is randomly assigned.[19] MR studies can more effectively infer the causal relationship between exposure factors and health outcomes with less interference from confounding factors.[20] A review of the literature found that trace elements play a vital role in maintaining joint health and preventing and managing OA. The maintenance of joint health depends on the synergistic action of multiple trace elements that affect bone and cartilage structure, inflammatory response, and antioxidant defense.[21] In this study, through MR analysis, it was found that trace elements copper and vitamin D had a causal relationship with OA, so the influence of copper and vitamin D as exposure factors on OA was explained from the level of genetic variation. 2. Method

2.1. Trace element GWAS data download

In this study, we designed the causal relationship between copper and vitamin D in OA, and searched the genetic tool of copper and vitamin D in the GWAS database. For genetic variants that are considered IVs, 3 requirements must be met: the genetic variant is significantly associated with the exposure variable; the genetic variation was not associated with other confounding factors. Genetic variants affect results through exposure, not directly.

2.2. Copper and vitamin D genetic instruments

All IVs and exposure traits showed significant independent correlation (P < 5 × 10−6), and bidirectional linkage disequilibrium r = 0.001. Following established guidelines, we identified a set of independent SNPS that were highly correlated with the exposure variables and subsequently matched them in the results database.

2.3. OA data download

In open IEU GWAS project website at https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk to download OA GWAS (id: ebi-a-GCST007092) data. One hundred eighty OA-related SNPs were selected according to the SNPs of copper and vitamin D exposure factors. For follow-up analysis (Table 3, Supplemental Digital Content, http://links.lww.com/MD/N658).

2.4. Assessment of pleiotropy and heterogeneity

In this study, a 2-sample MR Software package was used to perform a pleiotropic test to test whether the selected SNPs were valid IVs. The applicability of IVs was verified by confirming whether there was a significant pleiotropy (P > .05). At the same time, Cochrane Q test was applied to evaluate the consistency or variability among the selected IVs. When we found that the heterogeneity was negligible, we chose the fixed-effect model. On the other hand, significant heterogeneity prompts us to adopt the random effects inverse variance weighting (IVW) analysis method.

2.5. MR analysis

In this study, MR was used to further investigate and evaluate the causal relationship between exposure variables and results by the role of gene IVs. Initially, we calculate the Wald ratio for each IV by dividing the resulting data by the corresponding exposure data. We then applied the IVW method to assess the association between exposure factors and outcomes. The analysis used inverse variance techniques to assign a weight to the Wald ratio of each SNP, taking into account the influence of meta-analysis, and using random or fixed effect estimates. If the significance level of the Cochran Q test is below the 0.05 threshold, we use a random effects model to explain the observed variability. Instead, if this threshold is not reached, we use a fixed-effect model to maintain a consistent effect size across studies. In order to enrich the results of IVW analysis, we also use other methods for analysis.

2.6. Sensitivity analysis

To determine the strength and reliability of MR causal effect estimates, we performed a series of sensitivity analyses. Initially, we used the MR-Egger intercept method to assess the likelihood of pleiotropy between the selected SNPS. If the intercept term is statistically significant (P < .05), it indicates a possible pleiotropic effect. On the contrary, there was no horizontal pleiotropy among all groups (P > .05). Second, we utilize Mendelian randomized multieffect residuals and outliers (MR-PRESSO) tests as a tool to pinpoint any anomalies in the data and correct them to obtain reliable estimates. Sensitivity analysis was then performed to assess the effect of MR-PRESSO adjustment on the causal effect of IVW. In addition, a “leave-one” approach was used, in which each SNP was removed in turn to enhance the robustness of the findings.

2.7. Result analysis and software

The results of the MR Analysis are presented in the form of estimates. For bivariate variables, we use odds ratios and 95% confidence intervals (CIs), while for continuous variables, we use a 95% CI beta value (β). These estimates were reported consistently throughout the analysis. The R4.2.0 version of MR Double sample and MR-PRESSO packaging were used for statistical analysis, and accurate and reliable results were obtained.

4. Discussion

OA is a disease of the joints that is characterized by gradual wear and degeneration of the joint cartilage. Its pathogenesis is not well understood. In this study, MR was used to investigate the causal relationship between trace elements copper and vitamin D and OA. Through comprehensive use of 5 different MR Estimation methods, including IVW, weighted median, MR-Egger, simple model and multi-sample mixed model, the correlation between these 2 trace elements and OA was verified from multiple perspectives. In addition, in order to deal with the potential problem of horizontal pleiotropy, the MR-PRESSO method was also used to identify and correct outliers to ensure the stability of analysis results. The sensitivity assessment conducted confirmed the reliability of the causal relationship. The consistent results obtained using different MR methods increase the confidence of the findings and ensure the robustness of the results by excluding pleiotropic problems. At present, a large number of studies have linked trace elements with human health, and some studies have shown that there are gender-based differences in iron content and moderate levels of public health iron deficiency among women of childbearing age.[22] Lentiviral infection in humans and rhesus monkeys affects the concentrations of 4 trace elements: copper (Cu), zinc (Zn), manganese (Mn), and selenium (Se).[23]

It has been shown that copper, as an essential trace element, plays a key role in a variety of biological processes, including antioxidant, tissue repair, and regulation of enzyme activity. However, although copper is essential for these physiological processes, the current study shows a relatively weak association between copper and OA. This may be because the physiological concentration of copper is not sufficient to have a significant effect on joint health and disease progression.[24] At the same time, abnormally elevated concentrations of copper were also observed in type 2 diabetes patients in the hippocampus, an area of the brain closely associated with cognitive function and memory. In these patients, elevated copper levels are associated with declines in cognitive function and neurodegenerative changes. This is similar to the neurological damage caused by abnormal copper metabolism in Wilson disease, suggesting that balance of copper levels is critical in nervous system health. In addition, the antibacterial and antitumor properties of copper have also been extensively studied. As a powerful antibacterial material, copper can effectively inhibit the growth of a variety of bacteria, viruses and other microorganisms, making it particularly important in controlling hospital infections and other environments.[25] At the same time, the potential of copper compounds as antitumor drugs is also being explored.[26] In the study of OA, oxidative stress is considered to be a key pathological process that can damage articular cartilage. Copper, as a component of superoxide dismutase, helps relieve oxidative stress and may have potential benefits in protecting joint health.[27] Although the association between copper and OA is weak, the antioxidant effects of copper still indicate its potential importance for joint health.[28] In conclusion, copper plays a multifaceted role in human health, including maintaining brain health, regulating immune responses, and its potential as a therapeutic drug. Despite its limited role in OA, the overall physiological role of copper and its potential applications in other diseases warrant further investigation.

The effect of vitamin D on OA has been the focus of research because vitamin D not only plays a key role in bone health and calcium metabolism, but is also involved in immune regulation and anti-inflammatory processes.[29] Vitamin D is essential for maintaining bone health, and it helps the body absorb calcium, a key mineral for maintaining bone density and structural integrity.[30] Vitamin D deficiency can lead to osteoporosis and may increase the risk of additional stress on the joints, thus speeding up the process of OA development. Moreover, studies have shown that vitamin D has a potential anti-inflammatory effect, which can affect cells and cytokines involved in the inflammatory process.[31,32] In the pathological course of OA, inflammation plays an important role in cartilage damage and joint degeneration. Vitamin D may help slow the progression of OA by reducing the release of inflammatory mediators.[33] Epidemiological studies have found that low levels of vitamin D are associated with the incidence and severity of OA.[34] Consistent with the results of this study, vitamin D had a significant positive association with OA. Vitamin D not only immune system in calcium absorption and bone health, but also reduces joint inflammation and inhibits the degeneration of cartilage cells by regulating the immune system. In recent years, research has gradually revealed the important role vitamin D may play in OA. Vitamin D is not only essential for bone health, but may also affect joint inflammation and cartilage degradation.[35] These findings support the idea of vitamin D as a potential therapy for the prevention and treatment of OA.

This study provides new genetic evidence for the role of vitamin D and copper in OA, strengthening the understanding of the possible role of these trace elements in the prevention and treatment of OA. Future research could further explore the role of specific biological mechanisms of vitamin D and copper in OA, especially the differences in effects in different populations and different joint types. In addition, clinical trials could also be conducted to verify the actual effect of vitamin D and copper supplementation in improving symptoms and quality of life in patients with OA. Through these studies, we may be able to develop more effective prevention and treatment strategies to reduce the health burden of this widespread degenerative joint disease.

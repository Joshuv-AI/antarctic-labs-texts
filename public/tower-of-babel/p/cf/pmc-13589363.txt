Greenness, blueness, and whiteness profiles of HPTLC method for the simultaneous assay of tenofovir and emtricitabine in fixed-dose combination dosage forms
Authors: Prawez Alam, Faiyaz Shakeel, Mohammed H. Alqarni, Ahmed I. Foudah, Tariq M. Aljarba, Akil Ahmad, Wadah Osman
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The literature does not have any sustainable high-performance thin-layer chromatographic (HPTLC) methods for concurrently identifying tenofovir (TEN) and emtricitabine (ECT). Therefore, the proposed study develops and verifies a sustainable reverse-phase HPTLC methodology for determining TEN and ECT concurrently in their fixed-dose combination (FDC) products. The wavelength at which TEN and ECT were simultaneously identified was 255 nm. A 70:30 v/v binary mixture of ethanol and water served as the green development system. The method was validated for accuracy, precision, robustness, sensitivity, and specificity according to ICH guidelines. The validated method was applied for the simultaneous determination of TEN and ECT in commercial FDC tablets. The method’s greenness, blueness, and whiteness profiles were evaluated using eight different tools: “the analytical eco-scale (AES), chloroform toxicity (ChlorTox), analytical GREEnness (AGREE), modified green analytical procedure index (MoGAPI), complex MoGAPI, blue applicability grade index (BAGI), carbon footprint reduction index (CaFRI), and click analytical chemistry index (CACI)”. For both medications, the devised technique was linear in the range of 25–1000 ng/band. In addition, the created approach was proven to be accurate (% recoveries = 99.12–100.70 for TEN and 100.57-101.83 for ECT), precise (% RSD = 0.85–0.99 for TEN and 0.88–0.97 for ECT), sensitive (LOD = 8.50 ng/band for TEN and 8.39 ng/band for ECT, LOQ = 25.51 ng/band for TEN and 25.19 ng/band for ECT), and robust (% RSD = 0.82–0.86 for TEN and 0.94–0.99 for ECT). Using the current method, the amount of TEN in commercial FDC tablet brands A and B was 98.58 ± 1.21% and 101.13 ± 1.30%, respectively. The amount of ECT in FDC brands A and B was 99.82 ± 1.29 and 100.64 ± 1.37%, respectively. The findings of all the greenness, blueness, and whitening tools, such as AES (93), ChlorTox (0.72 g), AGREE (0.77), MoGAPI (85), complex MoGAPI (90), BAGI (85), CaFRI (89), and CACI (87), demonstrated that the current method had the notably sustainable profiles. The greenness parameters of present method were better than reported HPTLC approaches. The findings of the study suggested that the recommended method may be applied to precisely evaluate TEN and ECT in commercial formulations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s13065-026-01875-1.

Introduction

The fumaric acid salt of tenofovir’s bis isopropoxycarbonyloxymethyl ester derivative is called tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TEN) [Figure 1A] [1]. Because it inhibits the activity of the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) reverse transcriptase, it is used to treat HIV patients either on its own or in combination with other antiviral drugs [2, 3]. Additionally, TEN is authorized to treat chronic hepatitis B [4]. HIV patients are also treated using emtricitabine (ECT) [Figure 1B], a synthetic nucleoside analog, either alone or in combination with other antiviral medicines [5, 6]. It is freely soluble in water, according to reports [7, 8]. After oral administration, it is rapidly and completely absorbed because of its high water solubility [8–10]. Both TEN and ECT belong to the class of antiretroviral drugs [2, 6]. In combination dosage forms, TEN and ECT produce synergistic antiviral effects [11]. A number of fixed-dose combination (FDC) tablets that are sold commercially contain both TEN and ECT. As a result, TEN and ECT in their FDC tablets must be measured concurrently.

A review of the literature has led to the discovery of several analytical techniques for concurrently assessing TEN and ECT in FDC tablets. TEN and ECT have been evaluated simultaneously in their FDC tablets utilizing various ultra-violet (UV) spectrophotometric and derivative UV spectrophotometric methods [12–18]. Numerous high-performance liquid chromatographic (HPLC) methods were also applied to document the simultaneous detection of TEN and ECT in their FDC products [18–26]. It has also been shown that TEN and ECT can be detected simultaneously in FDC products using a variety of ultra-performance LC (UPLC) techniques [17, 27–29]. Several high-performance thin-layer chromatographic (HPTLC) approaches were also described to analyze TEN and ECT in FDC tablets concurrently [30–35].

HPTLC is an extension of TLC, a straightforward, reliable, efficient, and rapid pharmaceutical analytical technique [36]. Green HPTLC techniques are currently not available in the literature to assess TEN and ECT simultaneously in their FDC products, despite the fact that a number of HPTLC methods have been reported to do so. Conventional HPLC, HPTLC, and UPLC techniques for the simultaneous determination of TEN and ECT rely on the large volume of organic solvents such as methanol, acetonitrile, chloroform (CHCl3) or toluene. These solvents generate hazardous chemical waste, contribute to environmental toxicity, increase laboratory disposal cost, and expose analysts to health risks. Greener HPTLC methods aim to minimize these impacts by reducing solvent consumption, replacing hazardous solvents by safer alternatives, and lowering energy usage. Therefore, the development of greener HPTLC approaches are important for the simultaneous determination of TEN and ECT. Utilizing ecologically suitable solvent substitutes to lessen the detrimental effects of hazardous or toxic solvents on the environment is one of the twelve principles of “green analytical chemistry (GAC)” [37]. A relatively new method for evaluating the efficiency of analytical processes in terms of sample analysis, cost-effectiveness, and greenness strength is called “white analytical chemistry (WAC)”. Red, green, and blue (RGB) colors, which sum up to white, are used to calculate WAC [38]. Green analysis of medicinal products is currently done using HPTLC techniques [39–46]. The utilization of eco-friendly solvents has increased significantly in the literature during the last few decades [39–41, 47, 48]. A range of greenness, blueness, and whiteness techniques for assessing the greenness, blueness, and whiteness profiles of analytical processes are described in the literature [49–56]. To assess the sustainability profiles of the current approach, we used eight distinct greenness, blueness, and whiteness approaches: the “Analytical Eco-Scale (AES) [49], the CHCl3 toxicity (ChlorTox) [50], the analytical GREEnness (AGREE) [51], the modified green analytical procedure index (MoGAPI) [52], the complex MoGAPI [53], the blue applicability grade index (BAGI) [54], the carbon footprint reduction index (CaFRI) [55], and the click analytical chemistry index (CACI) [56]”. AES, ChlorTox, AGREE, MoGAPI, and complex MoGAPI represents greenness. BAGI tool represents blueness. CaFRI and CACI represents sustainability. Whiteness was determined by averaging redness, greenness, and blueness profiles. A single greenness tool could not provide the comprehensive sustainability information about an analytical method. Therefore, eight different greenness tools were used for the comprehensive sustainability evaluation of the present HPTLC method. The semi-quantitative AES method penalizes non-greenness qualities with penalty points [49]. The solvents’ ChlorTox scale is evaluated utilizing the ChlorTox approach in comparison to standard CHCl3. Compared to non-green procedures, which have a high ChlorTox scale value, green analytical methods have a low value, indicating environmental safety [50]. Using all 12 GAC criteria, the AGREE method assesses greenness [51]. The MoGAPI tool provides an overall assessment of the method’s greenness by calculating a total score, in addition to displaying the well-known red, green, and yellow pictograms of the GAPI tool [52]. The intricate MoGAPI offers a comprehensive evaluation of technique eco-friendliness, allowing for more accurate and objective method comparisons [53]. The BAGI method evaluates the blueness characteristics of the analytical process using the ten distinct WAC principles [54]. With an emphasis on measuring greenhouse gas emissions, CaFRI is a novel web application created to evaluate and improve the sustainability of analytical techniques [55]. CACI is a useful and easy-to-use tool that offers a software solution and metric for assessing the whitening profiles of analytical techniques. The CACI method encourages the development of more straightforward implementation strategies without sacrificing performance by allowing unbiased and objective comparisons of methods [56].

The current approach aims to create and examine a fast, sensitive, and sustainable reverse-phase HPTLC technology in order to evaluate TEN and ECT in FDC tablets simultaneously. Out of 12 different principles of GAC, our method completely followed the principles 2 (related to sample amount), 4 (related to sample preparation steps and stages), 6 (related to derivatization steps), 8 (related to analysis throughput), 9 (related to energy consumption of the instrument), 10 (related to source of reagents used), 11 (related to toxicity of the reagents used), and 12 (related to safety of the operator). Furthermore, other principles like 1 (related to sample treatment steps), 3 (related to device positioning), 5 (related to automation and miniaturization), and 7 (related to waste) were free from red zones. As a result, the proposed HPTLC method was considered to be green. Additionally, we evaluated the method’s greenness, blueness, and whiteness using eight different tools, including “AES, ChlorTox, AGREE, MoGAPI, complex MoGAPI, BAGI, CaFRI, and CACI”. All greenness metrics proved the greenness, blueness, and whiteness profiles of the method. TEN and ECT were determined simultaneously using the suggested approach, which was verified utilizing the guidelines in “The International Council for Harmonization (ICH)-Q2-R2” [57].

Materials and methods

Materials

“Beijing Chemical Technology Ltd. (Beijing, China)” supplied the bulk TEN (purity: 99.3%) and ECT (purity: 99.5%). “E-Merck (Darmstadt, Germany)” supplied HPLC-grade green solvents, such as ethanol (EtOH), acetone (Ace), and ethyl acetate (EA).” The “Milli-Q (Lyon, France)” apparatus was used to obtain the HPLC-grade/deionized water (H2O). Each of the two commercially available FDC tablet brands, A and B, included 300 mg of TEN and 200 mg of ECT. According to the manufacturer, brand A contains adjuvants/excipients like microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate, croscarmellose sodium, pregelatinized starch, magnesium stearate, hypromellose, titanium dioxide, triacetin, and FD & C blue 2 aluminum lake. However, brand B contains microcrystalline cellulose, lactose monohydrate, croscarmellose sodium, pregelatinized starch, magnesium stearate, opadry AMB white, and titanium dioxide. Both brands were purchased in New Delhi, India, from a drugstore. Every extra chemical and solvent utilized was of AR grade.

Instrumentation and chromatography analysis

The “HPTLC system (CAMAG, Muttenz, Switzerland)” was used to quantify TEN and ECT concurrently in pure drug samples and commercial FDC formulations. The prepared samples were placed utilizing an “Automatic TLC Sampler 4 (ATS4) Sample Applicator (CAMAG, Geneva, Switzerland)” in the form of 6 mm bands. Sample applicator and “microliter Syringe (Hamilton, Bonaduz, Switzerland)” were connected. The stationary phase used was “glass plates (plate size: 10 × 20 cm2) pre-coated with reverse-phase silica gel (particle size: 5 µm) 60F254S plates (E-Merck, Darmstadt, Germany)”. EtOH-H2O (70:30 v/v) was utilized in binary form as the eco-friendly solvent system. In order to measure TEN and ECT simultaneously, the application rate was fixed at 150 nL/s. The plates were activated in a linear ascending mode with an 8 cm spacing using the “automated developing chamber 2 (ADC2) (CAMAG, Muttenz, Switzerland)”. Vapors from a developing system were introduced into the development chamber, which was kept at 22 °C for half an hour. At 255 nm, TEN and ECT were simultaneously detected. Slit size (4 × 0.45 mm2) and scanner speed (20 mm/s) were the settings used. Three or six replications were employed for each metric. The software used was “WinCAT’s (version 1.4.3.6336, CAMAG, Muttenz, Switzerland)”.

Calibration curves and quality control (QC) solutions for TEN and ECT

To create separate stock solutions for TEN and ECT, the necessary amounts of each reference substance were dissolved in the proper volume of the green development system, namely EtOH-H2O (70:30 v/v). In order to maintain uniformity in the quantitative analysis and sample preparation, the green development system was utilized as a diluent in this experiment. Each drug’s final stock solution included 100 µg/mL. The drug stock solutions were diluted using the mobile phase in different ratios, resulting in 25–1000 ng/band quantities of both drugs. Following the proposed method of determining the peak area of each TEN and ECT concentration, ten µL of each drug concentration were spotted onto TLC plates. The concentrations of TEN and ECT were plotted against the peak area recorded during six replications (n = 6) in order to create TEN and ECT calibration curves. Three different QC solutions were made in order to evaluate a range of validation variables.

Sample preparation to determine TEN and ECT concurrently in FDC formulations

The suggested method was applied to measure TEN and ECT in FDC tablet dosage forms in addition to concurrently quantifying these compounds in the pure drug samples. The independent sample preparation was performed for marketed formulations. To analyze the TEN and ECT in FDC formulations simultaneously, twenty tablets of each brand were taken. The average weight of each product was then computed. Each tablet brand contained 200 mg of ECT and 300 mg of TEN. Each brand of FDC tablet was crushed and ground into a fine powder using a glass crusher and mortar. The fine powder containing an equivalent amount of 200 mg of ECT and 300 mg of TEN was mixed with 1000 mL of the diluent/mobile phase. 10 mL of this stock were subsequently further diluted to produce 100 mL utilizing the diluent. The samples obtained for each formulation were filtered to exclude any insoluble materials after 10 min of sonication. Using the suggested procedure, ten µL of the collected samples were spotted to TLC plates for determining TEN and ECT simultaneously in FDC formulations.

Method development

In the first step, various pure greener solvents such as EtOH, Ace, EA, and H2O were evaluated as the mobile phase to develop a suitable HPTLC method for the simultaneous determination of TEN and ECT in FDC tablets. After the initial findings based on the chromatographic performance in terms of “retardation factor (Rf), peak tailing factor (As), and theoretical plates/meter (N/m)”, pure green alternatives were used to assess a range of binary combinations, including EtOH/H2O, Ace/H2O, EtOH/EA, and Ace/EA. When choosing the optimal greener solvent system, a number of factors were considered, such as cost, greenness profile, sensitivity of the method, analysis time, chromatographic parameters (Rf, As, and N/m), detection wavelength, and compatibility of the solvents. Ultimately, a 70:30 v/v mixture of EtOH and H2O was determined to be the most reliable solvent system for further research.

Validation studies

The developed method for measuring TEN and ECT simultaneously was validated for all parameters using the ICH-Q2-R2 standards as discussed below [57].

Linearity

Plotting the concentrations against the observed peak area allowed for the evaluation of the linear ranges for TEN and ECT. For the current method, the linearity of TEN and ECT was evaluated in the range of 25–1000 ng/band (n = 6).

System suitability

The “Rf, As, and N/m” were calculated to determine the system appropriateness factors for the proposed method of concurrently estimating TEN and ECT. The “Rf, As, and N/m” for the proposed method were calculated using their published formulae [43].

Accuracy

Using the spiking/standard addition approach as % recoveries, the accuracy of the proposed method for measuring TEN and ECT simultaneously was evaluated [57]. TEN and ECT low-QC (LQC) samples of 300 ng/band, middle-QC (MQC) samples of 400 ng/band, and high-QC (HQC) samples of 500 ng/band were obtained by mixing 200 ng/band of the previously measured TEN and ECT solutions with extra 50, 100, and 150% TEN and ECT solutions. Six replicates (n = 6) were utilized to gauge the accuracy.

Precision

The precision of the existing procedure for the simultaneous detection of TEN and ECT was assessed both within and between batches. By sampling newly made TEN and ECT solutions at the previously indicated QC levels, it was feasible to assess the intra-day variability for both medications on the same day (n = 6). To examine inter-batch variance for TEN and ECT for the proposed approach, freshly generated samples were examined over three days (n = 6) at the previously defined QC levels [57].

Robustness

By deliberately changing the composition of the green solvent system, total run length, and saturation time, the robustness of the current technique for both TEN and ECT was evaluated. Peak response (a quantitative metric) and Rf (a separation metric) were measured after the transition from EtOH-H2O (72:28 v/v) to EtOH-H2O (68:32 v/v) in the proposed mobile phase for TEN and ECT (n = 6). Similarly, the total run length was changed from 80 mm to 82 and 78 mm for evaluating the method robustness by changing the total run length. The saturation time was changed from 30 min to 32 and 28 min for evaluating the method robustness by changing the saturation time [57].

Limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ)

Using a standard deviation technique, the “LOD and LOQ” were used to evaluate the method sensitivity of the suggested method for the concurrent assessment of TEN and ECT. Six replications (n = 6) were injected with blank samples that contained neither TEN nor ECT, and the standard deviations of each were recorded. Selectivity and specificity

The Rf factors and-UV absorption spectrum of the standard were compared with those of commercial FDC tablets in order to assess the selectivity and specificity of the existing approach for testing TEN and ECT simultaneously [57].

Application of the proposed method to determine TEN and ECT simultaneously in formulations

Using the same experimental protocol as the pure TEN and ECT evaluations, the processed samples of commercially available FDC products were spotted on TLC plates for this method (n = 3). The peak areas were then noted. The TEN and ECT calibration curves were utilized in the current procedure to ascertain the amounts of these drugs in commercial FDC formulations.

Greenness, blueness, and whiteness profiles

Eight distinct tools were used to evaluate the greenness, blueness, and whiteness profiles of the proposed method for assessing TEN and ECT simultaneously: “AES [49], ChlorTox [50], AGREE [51], MoGAPI [52], complex MoGAPI [53], BAGI [54], CaFRI [55], and CACI [56]”. The semi-quantitative AES approach considers waste, tools, and all stages of the study. Reagents, energy, and trash should be used minimally or not at all in materials with an ideal score of 100. Penalty points are awarded and subtracted from the final score of 100 if any of these requirements are not met [49].

Results and discussion

Method development

Based on earlier research, a green HPTLC method was developed and optimized for the concurrent analysis of TEN and ECT in their FDC products using TLC plates precoated with aluminum backed 60F254 [60–62]. To maximize the Rf factor and peak area of TEN and ECT, a number of pure sustainable eluents were tested, including EtOH, Ace, EA, and H2O. After the initial findings, pure green eluents were used to assess a range of binary combinations, including EtOH/H2O, Ace/H2O, EtOH/EA, and Ace/EA. It was discovered that EtOH/H2O produced the best analytical results in comparison to other combinations. Next, we looked at the binary combinations of EtOH and H2O in different ratios. In order to determine the green developing systems, the following binary combinations were examined: 40:60 v/v, 50:50 v/v, 60:40 v/v, 70:30 v/v, 80:20 v/v, and 90:10 v/v of EtOH-H2O. High volatile organic solvents such as acetonitrile, methanol, or CHCl3 are usually recommended as the developing system for HPTLC analysis. Nevertheless, effective drug and pharmaceutical identification and quantification are achieved by the use of these volatile solvents. These solvents pose a serious threat to the environment and are extremely poisonous [62]. Using green organic solvents, such as H2O, to reduce the toxic impacts of hazardous solvents on the environment is one of the 12 GAC principles [37]. Because they are non-toxic to the environment, the solvents being studied—EA, Ace, H2O, and EtOH—are known to as “green solvents” [63, 64]. To lessen the environmental toxicity of the current analytical process, H2O, the greenest eluent, was mixed with other green organic eluents [62]. Validation studies

A number of parameters as discussed below were created for the simultaneous estimation of TEN and ECT using the ICH-Q2-R2 methodology [57].

Linearity

Table 2 displays the findings of the linearity evaluation of the TEN and ECT calibration plots using the current approach. Both the TEN and ECT calibration curves were linear in the 25–1000 ng/band range. Both TEN and ECT had statistically significant coefficient of determination (R2) and correlation coefficient (R) values (p < 0.05). Generally, the slope and intercept values depend on x-axis and y-axis data, respectively. The x-axis values represent the concentrations of TEN or ECT which were ranged as 25-1000 ng/band. However, the y-axis values represent the measured response for TEN and ECT which were recorded in the range of 1208–26,142 and 1324–27,988, respectively. It is obvious that the values of measured responses on y-axis are much higher than concentrations on x-axis for TEN and ECT. As a result, the intercept values for TEN and ECT are much higher than slope of the TEN and ECT calibration plots. These findings showed a strong correlation between the TEN and ECT levels and the observed responses [30–32]. These findings demonstrated that the existing technique was sufficiently linear to evaluate TEN and ECT concurrently.

LOD and LOQ

“LOD and LOQ” were used to evaluate the sensitivity of the existing method for simultaneously detecting TEN and ECT. Table 2 lists the “LOD and LOQ” values for TEN and ECT that were generated following the suggested method. Using the suggested method, the “LOD and LOQ” for TEN were determined to be 8.50 ± 0.23 and 25.51 ± 0.69 ng/band, respectively. Using the suggested method, the “LOD and LOQ” for ECT were calculated to be 8.39 ± 0.22 and 25.19 ± 0.66 ng/band, respectively. The LOD values for TEN and ECT were much lower than the lowest linear limit (25 ng/band), suggesting the high-sensitivity. However, the LOQ values for TEN and ECT drugs were within the linearity range, suggesting the suitable quantification of both medicines. Therefore, LOD is always lower than the lowest concentration of linearity. However, LOQ is within the linearity range. These findings suggested how sensitive the existing technology is for simultaneously detecting TEN and ECT.

Selectivity and specificity

The proposed technique for assessing TEN and ECT concurrently was examined for selectivity and specificity by comparing the UV-absorption spectrum and Rf factors of TEN and ECT in marketed FDC products with that of standard TEN and ECT. Figure 4 displays the overlapping UV-absorption spectra of the standards TEN and ECT as well as the commercial FDC medications (brands A and B).

Fig. 4UV-absorption spectra of reference TEN and ECT and TEN and ECT in commercial FDC tablet brands A and B

UV-absorption spectra of reference TEN and ECT and TEN and ECT in commercial FDC tablet brands A and B

The peak purities of standard TEN and ECT and TEN and ECT in commercial FDC tablets were ascertained by comparing the spectra at the peak start (S), peak apex (M), and peak end (E) positions of the spot [65, 66]. The recorded values of r (S, M) and r (M, E) for standard TEN, ECT, and marketed FDC tablet brands A and B are presented in Table S3. The peaks were uniform, according to computed data of r (S, M) and r (M, E) greater than 0.99 (Table S3) for standard TEN, ECT, and marketed FDC tablet brands A and B [67, 68]. In this study, the λmax for TEN and ECT was recorded to be 262 and 288 nm, respectively. These are TEN and ECT’s respective wavelengths. It suggests that 262 nm should be chosen if we just measure TEN. However, 288 nm should be chosen if we only measure ECT. However, the highest response was noted at 255 nm for the simultaneous measurement of TEN and ECT (Fig. 4). As a result, TEN and ECT were analyzed simultaneously at 255 nm. The highest chromatographic signal for TEN and ECT was found at 255 nm in both commercially available FDC tablets and standards. The similar Rf factors, wavelengths, and UV-absorption spectrum found in standards and marketed FDC products showed how selective and specific the suggested method was for assessing TEN and ECT concurrently.

Application of the proposed method to determine TEN and ECT simultaneously in formulations

Multianalytes have been successfully analyzed simultaneously using LC techniques, including HPLC and LC-MS systems [69]. However, rather than using conventional LC techniques, the suggested approach was employed to evaluate TEN and ECT together in commercial FDC tablets. Following the suggested procedure, the chromatographic signals of commercially available FDC tablet brands A and B were determined to have Rf = 0.62 ± 0.02 and Rf = 0.78 ± 0.02, respectively, by comparison with those of standard TEN and ECT. The HPTLC chromatograms for ECT and TEN from formulation brands A (Fig. 5A) and B (Fig. 5B) are depicted in Fig. 5. These peaks matched the TEN and ECT requirements exactly. In addition, there was no extra peak associated to formulation excipients was recorded, indicating no chemical or physical interactions between active ingredients and excipients. Using the current method, the amount of TEN in commercial FDC tablet brands A and B was ascertained. The findings were, respectively, 98.58 ± 1.21% and 101.13 ± 1.30%. Using the current method, commercial formulation brands A and B contained 99.82 ± 1.29 and 100.64 ± 1.37% of ECT, respectively. According to the United States Pharmacopeia (USP), TEN required to be assayed between 90 and 110% of 300 mg total dose. Similarly, ECT required to be assayed between 90 and 110% of 200 mg total dose [70]. The obtained assay results for TEN and ECT were according to the USP specification [70]. TEN and ECT assay percentages in commercial FDC tablets are reportedly 99.94 and 100.16%, respectively [30]. TEN and ECT test percentages in FDC tablets were 100.16 and 100.84%, respectively, according to another study [31]. The percentages of TEN and ECT assays in FDC tablets was found to be 96.63 and 97.90%, respectively by another report [32]. The percentages of TEN and ECT assays in FDC tablets was computed to be 99.78 and 99.29%, respectively by another study [33]. The assay results of present HPTLC method were similar to the most of the reported HPTLC methods [30, 31, 33]. These results indicated that TEN and ECT can be detected simultaneously in FDC formulations using the developed method.

Conclusions

For the simultaneous assessment of TEN and ECT in commercial dosage forms and biological samples, there are no green HPTLC methods available. In order to simultaneously quantify TEN and ECT in commercial FDC tablets, this study devised and verified a quick, sensitive, and eco-friendly HPTLC approach. For quantifying TEN and ECT concurrently, the recommended method is linear (between 25 and 1000 ng/band for TEN and ECT), sensitive (based on LOD and LOQ findings), accurate (based on % recovery findings), precise (based on % data), robust (based on minimal variations in peak area and Rf), and environmentally friendly (based on AES, ChlorTox, AGREE, MoGAPI, complex MoGAPI, BAGI, CaFRI, and CACI findings). The current technology was effectively used to identify the TEN and ECT contents of commercial FDC tablets. The results of the AES, ChlorTox, AGREE, MoGAPI, and complex MoGAPI evaluations demonstrate how very environmentally friendly the proposed methodology is for simultaneously evaluating TEN and ECT when compared to published HPTLC methodologies. All of these results suggested that the current method may be applied to determine TEN and ECT in marketed FDC tablets with ease. The main limitation of this work is that the proposed method was not applied for the simultaneous determination of TEN and ECT in biological fluids. Therefore, future studies can be performed to determine TEN and ECT simultaneously in biological fluids like plasma, blood, and urine to exploit the biological potential of the proposed HPTLC method.

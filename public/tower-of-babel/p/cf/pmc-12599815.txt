Letter to editor: Inclusion of single-level studies in a meta-analysis of multilevel lumbar spinal stenosis: a methodological clarification
Authors: Audai Helmi Abudayehh, Iakiv V Fishchenko
Published: 2025

To the Editor,

We read with great interest the meta-analysis by González-Murillo et al. [1], titled “Endoscopic surgery for multilevel spinal stenosis: a comprehensive meta-analysis and subgroup analysis of uniportal and biportal approaches.” This high-quality work provides valuable insights into minimally invasive endoscopic approaches for multilevel lumbar spinal stenosis and represents an important contribution to the literature in a leading spine surgery journal.

The authors explicitly stated that their review focused on adult patients with multilevel lumbar spinal stenosis and excluded studies involving populations who exclusively had one-level lumbar spinal stenosis. However, upon reviewing the included studies, we noted that the article by Kim et al. [2], titled “Clinical and radiological outcomes of foraminal decompression using unilateral biportal endoscopic spine surgery for lumbar foraminal stenosis,” was included in the analysis. This study specifically addresses outcomes for single-level lumbar foraminal stenosis, not multilevel lumbar spinal stenosis.

The inclusion of this single-level study appears inconsistent with the stated eligibility criteria and may introduce heterogeneity into the pooled results. Given the distinct pathophysiological and surgical considerations between single-level and multilevel lumbar spinal stenosis, the inclusion of such studies could affect the validity of the meta-analysis conclusions.

We respectfully submit this comment for your consideration and wish to acknowledge both the authors for their meticulous work and the Asian Spine Journal for its commitment to high-quality evidence in spine surgery.

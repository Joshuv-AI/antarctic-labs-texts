Small-Bowel Perforation That Occurred After Kickboxing Practice
Authors: Masamitsu Kuroki, Masashi Nakagawa, Junya Tsurukiri
Published: 2026

A 22-year-old professional kickboxer who was hit by a knee in the abdomen during kickboxing practice was admitted to our department with left-sided abdominal pain. His vital signs were within the normal range. However, his lactate dehydrogenase levels were elevated. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) images of the trunk revealed a fluid collection in the Douglas fossa. Although a follow-up CT examination performed 5 hours after injury revealed no remarkable change, his abdominal pain exacerbated 12 hours after injury. The CT images then showed free abdominal air. He was diagnosed with delayed traumatic small-bowel perforation (SBP) (Figure A). Laparoscopic examination revealed an isolated perforation located 50 cm distal to the ligament of Treitz, with turbid ascites (Figure B). Partial jejunal resection was performed. The patient was uneventfully discharged from the hospital after 9 days. Although musculoskeletal injuries are most frequently observed in individuals who engage in martial arts, SBP is extremely rare. SBP is a time-sensitive injury, and delayed diagnosis has been associated with an increased mortality rate (13% within 8 hours and 30.8% at ≧24 hours after injury). Shear forces, compression between the abdominal wall and spine, and burst injury caused by increased intraluminal pressure may be significant factors for SBP.

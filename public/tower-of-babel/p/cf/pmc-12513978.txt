Intense pulsed Light, Light-Emitting Diode, and hydroxychloroquine in the treatment of rosacea: A clinical observation
Authors: Chengyu Zhang, LiXin Song
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRosacea is a common, chronic inflammatory skin condition lacking specific treatment. This study investigates the efficacy of various photoelectric therapies in treating rosacea. 90 rosacea patients who visited the dermatology outpatient clinic of the hospital from August 2022 to November 2023 were analyzed (retrospective registration 20240425). The patients were categorized into three groups based on different treatment methods: the control group received oral hydroxychloroquine sulfate and topical recombinant bovine basic fibroblast growth factor; observation group 1 underwent intense pulsed light therapy in addition to the control group’s treatment; observation group 2 received light-emitting diode therapy on top of the control group’s regimen. The therapeutic effect was evaluated by physicians’ subjective evaluation and patients’ pruritus symptoms. The effective rates of observation group 1, observation group 2, and the control group were 73.3%, 66.7%, and 40%, respectively, with statistical significance (P < 0.05). There was no significant difference between observation group 1 and observation group 2 (P < 0.05). Additionally, there were no significant adverse reactions in any of the three groups. The comparison between observation group 1 and observation group 2 revealed that the two combined treatment methods achieved similar and positive therapeutic effects across various clinical manifestations. The effectiveness of both observation groups was found to be superior to that of drug therapy alone.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10103-025-04696-1.Keywords: Rosacea, Phototherapy, Intense pulsed laser , Light-Emitting diode

Introduction

Rosacea is a prevalent, recurring inflammatory condition that primarily impacts the cheeks, nose, chin, and forehead. It is clinically identified by transient erythema (persistent flushing), telangiectasia, papules, pustules, and rhinophyma [1, 2]. The disease typically occurs in individuals aged 30 to 50, with a higher incidence in female patients. Although rosacea is not life-threatening, it can significantly impact patients’ quality of life. Patients often experience depression, social anxiety, and other psychological symptoms [3].

Hydroxychloroquine (HCQ) can improve the skin’s ability to withstand UVA and inhibit mast cell activation and inflammatory transmitter expression [4, 5]. According to research, hydroxychloroquine has been widely used in rheumatology and immunology departments for a long time. With regular fundus examination (every 6 months), its side effects are relatively small, and it can be used for a long time. Patients have a high acceptance rate [6]. Recombinant bovine basic fibroblast growth factor (rb-bFGF) gel can suppress the body’s inflammatory state and promote the growth of various cells, including fibroblasts and vascular endothelial cells [7, 8]. External use can improve the local blood circulation of the skin, promote wound healing, contribute to the repair of skin lesions in rosacea patients, and delay or even reduce the deterioration of skin lesions. Intense pulsed light (IPL) 500~600 nm module can destroy abnormal blood vessels and inhibit the seepage of inflammatory factors through selective photothermal action to achieve the purpose of treatment. Another 420 nm module has anti-inflammatory effects [9]. The high-purity light source in the light-emitting diode (LED) red-yellow light, using a low-intensity laser, can reduce flushing and inflammation while promoting tissue repair and regeneration [10].

Different cutaneous lesions and symptoms of rosacea have different treatment methods. The treatment approaches are diverse; no specific treatment methods exist, and certain clinical trials are needed to treat rosacea. In this study (retrospective study, clinical trial number: not applicable), the clinical efficacy of combining sulfacetamide hydroxychloroquine tablets and rb-bFGF gel with IPL and LED light sources for the treatment of rosacea was observed. It was found that both achieved good therapeutic effects, providing certain references for the treatment of rosacea. The details are as follows:

Materials and methods

Subjects

This study has been approved by the Ethics Review Committee of the General Hospital of the Northern Theater Command (Ethical Review Number: Y [2024] No. 112). This study is a retrospective observational study and has obtained exemption from informed consent. Moreover, the clinical trial number is not applicable. Thirty patients with rosacea were included in each group, totaling 90 patients for analysis. Patient information, treatment methods, treatment satisfaction, and photos before and after treatment will be collected and stored in the dermatology database. Inclusion criteria: Patients who meet the diagnostic criteria stipulated in the 2021 “Chinese Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Rosacea”, have received treatment for no less than 1 month (observation group 1: at least 3 sessions of IPL therapy), have no severe other systemic diseases, and have been receiving treatment on time. Exclusion criteria: patients under 18 years old, those with underlying diseases and other facial skin diseases, and individuals who have received hormone and/or anti-allergic drug treatment within the last 3 months, as these groups could not be evaluated for efficacy and adverse reactions due to incomplete clinical data.

Therapy

Control group: Oral hydroxychloroquine sulfate tablets (0.2 g each time, twice daily) and external recombinant bovine basic fibroblast growth factor gel (twice daily) were finally selected for treatment over 2 months.

Observation group 1: Patients who received IPL once every 21 days for a total of 3 times in addition to oral and local therapy. The therapeutic instrument is the Harmony XL instrument (Alma Lasers Ltd.), and the therapeutic parameters were: wavelength 420 nm, 540 nm; Energy density: 7.6 ~ 10.6 j/cm2. Before the treatment, the patient’s face needs to be cleaned. During the treatment, the patient wears goggles to protect their eyes, while the doctor wears special goggles. The doctor should apply a 2 ~ 3 mm thick layer of light-coupled cold gel evenly on the patient’s face. Before the first treatment, a trial of spot treatment is conducted. The patient’s anterior auricular area is selected, and a low energy density is used. The patient’s skin reaction is observed to gradually adjust the energy density to the appropriate treatment parameters for treatment. During the treatment process, the endpoint is defined as slight skin flushing. After the treatment, the cold gel on the patient’s face should be removed immediately, and ice application for 15 ~ 20 min should be given. Finally, recombinant bovine basic fibroblast growth factor gel should be applied.

Observation group 2: Patients who were treated with LED twice a week for 3 consecutive weeks, based on oral and topical drug therapy. The treatment instrument is the LED processor of Shenzhen Peninsula Medical Co., LTD. The yellow light wavelength in the LED is 590 nm, and the optical power density is 25 mW/cm². The infrared wavelength is 830 nm, and the optical power density is 50 mW/cm². Before irradiation, the face needs to be cleaned. During the treatment, the patient should wear protective glasses. During the treatment, irradiation is performed for 10 min at 590 nm, followed by 10 min at 830 nm. The total treatment time is 20 min. Afterwards, the recombinant bovine basic fibroblast growth factor gel is evenly applied to the patient’s face.

Outcome assessment

Two attending physicians who were not involved in the patient’s treatment evaluated the patient’s clinical performance based on standardized photographs taken before and after treatment (Canon EOS R5 camera, 100 ms lens, f/8, ISO200, 30 cm from the patient’s face and 45° from the patient’s left and right sides) and medical history. Efficacy scorecards supplied by Clinician Erythema Assessment scale (CEA), Investigator Global Assessment Scale (IGA), and Verbal Rating Scale (VRS) were used to evaluate the efficacy [11–16]. According to the CEA, IGA, and VRS scoring standards, the clinical manifestations of no, almost no, mild, moderate, and severe were scored as 0, 1, 2, 3, and 4 points, respectively. The higher the score, the more severe the severity.

The efficacy index was calculated as (total pre-treatment points - total post-treatment points)/total pre-treatment points × 100%. Ineffective (efficacy index < 20%, including no improvement in skin lesions and increased skin lesions), improvement: (20%≤ efficacy index < 60%), obvious effect (60%≤ efficacy index < 90%), recovery (efficacy index ≥ 90%). Treatment effective rate = (recovery + obvious effect)/number of cases × 100%. Simultaneously, patients’ side effects after treatment with different photoelectric platforms were recorded. The patients’ erythema, erythema telangiectasia, papulopustular, and pruritus were observed.

Statistical analysis

Measured data are expressed as mean ± standard deviation (x‾ ± s), and the count data were expressed as rates (%). For data meeting the condition of normal distribution, a t-test was performed for statistical analysis. For data not meeting this condition, a rank sum test was selected for testing. The χ2 test was performed on the count data. This study utilized SPSS 26.0 software for data analysis, and all test results were considered statistically significant if P < 0.05.

Discussion

Rosacea is a common, chronic, and recurrent inflammatory skin disease [17]. Globally, about 5.5% of adults suffer from it [18]. The disfiguring and recurrent nature of rosacea has brought psychological and life-related troubles to patients [3]. In recent years, dermatologists have done a lot of research on its etiology, pathogenesis, and treatment methods. In addition to traditional drug treatment, phototherapy, new drugs, and new formulations of traditional drugs are increasingly used [19, 20]. Among them, IPL is regarded as the first-line treatment option for rosacea photodynamic therapy and has received extensive attention [1]. However, the treatment cost of IPL is relatively high, which makes it difficult for some patients to accept. In China, LED light therapy, which is more common and has a lower treatment cost, has become a new treatment option for many patients. To explore the effectiveness of IPL and LED compared with traditional drugs, the advantages and disadvantages of IPL and LED in treatment, and whether LED can be regarded as an effective therapeutic approach, we conducted this research.

IPL has a wide spectral range, with the wavelength range covering 500~1200 nm. Therefore, it can act on different skin layers simultaneously. Through the selective photothermal effect, it can destroy abnormal blood vessels and reduce telangiectasia and hyperplasia. Meanwhile, IPL can also inhibit sebum secretion and has anti-inflammatory effects [9, 21, 22]. Therefore, IPL improves symptoms such as erythema and vasodilation, stimulates skin collagen regeneration, improves skin texture and elasticity, improves skin tolerance, and restores skin barrier function [21]. This has been confirmed in many studies on rosacea. However, the efficacy of IPL treatment is greatly influenced by the operator’s experience, and there is a lack of standardized treatment protocols. Improper parameter settings may lead to burns or abnormal pigmentation. In addition, patients are prone to pain during IPL treatment, need cooling protection, and may develop transient erythema and edema after surgery, which can be relieved within 1 ~ 3 days but still cause anxiety in some patients.

Compared with IPL, the research on the therapeutic efficacy of LED is relatively limited. Studies have shown that LED can inhibit skin inflammation, alleviate erythema, telangiectasia, swelling, and other symptoms, and relieve pruritus. The red light has a wavelength of about 830 nm and a maximum penetration depth of 30 mm. It achieves rapid anti-inflammatory and detumescence effects by inhibiting a variety of inflammatory mechanisms, including inhibition of cyclooxygenase, release of growth factors, and promotion of collagen fiber production. Red light can also inhibit the pathological impulse of the peripheral nerves of the skin, to achieve a good antipruritic and analgesic effect [10, 23, 24]. The wavelength of the yellow light source is approximately 590 nm. It can be absorbed by skin fibroblasts, thereby promoting cell growth and enhancing skin resistance. This helps to reduce redness, soothe and calm the skin, and treat dilated capillaries. However, LED light therapy is less effective in improving vascular abnormalities, requires a combination of other means, and requires long-term treatment (usually greater than 6 weeks) for patients to achieve a better therapeutic effect. In addition, there is no consensus on the optimization of light dose, irradiation time, and irradiation frequency in existing studies, which affects the reproducibility of efficacy.

According to our observation results, the combination of IPL and hydroxychloroquine or LED and hydroxychloroquine both enhanced the therapeutic effect of hydroxychloroquine alone in the treatment of rosacea. Both the IPL group and the LED group achieved overall good clinical efficacy in the treatment of rosacea. Among them, IPL was more advantageous in the treatment of papulopustular and telangiectatic rosacea, while LED was less effective in telangiectasia but more effective in alleviating erythema and pruritus. Therefore, IPL or LED can be selected for treatment according to the economic conditions and time needs of the patient. However, due to the relatively small sample size of this study, the results of the treatment effect comparison may be limited. Secondly, this study failed to achieve effective long-term follow-up. We think it is necessary to continue the follow-up study and consider other evaluation measures, such as quality of life and psychological status, as well as VISIA instrument scores. In addition, it is important to determine the optimal treatment plan for the differences in treatment response of different patients.

Conclusion

Our study found that HCQ sulfate combined with rb-bFGF gel helped alleviate the clinical symptoms of rosacea patients. We also observed better therapeutic outcomes when combined with an IPL platform (observation group 1) and an LED light source (observation group 2).

An RGB-D time-series dataset of white button mushroom growth for instance segmentation
Authors: Namrata Dutt, Daeun Choi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate detection and segmentation of individual mushroom caps in densely populated cultivation beds remain key challenges for robotic harvesting of white button mushrooms (Agaricus bisporus), where precise boundary delineation is required for size estimation, grasp planning, and collision-free manipulation. The data was developed to support research on computer vision-based perception systems for autonomous mushroom harvesting. The dataset comprises of 129 time-series RGB and depth images of mushrooms from a top-view perspective, representing mushroom growth and spatial distribution under an indoor mushroom production environment. Each sample includes an RGB image, a corresponding depth image, and pixel-level segmentation annotations of individual mushrooms in COCO JSON format. The inclusion of depth information enables the extraction of geometric features such as cap height, curvature, and relative positioning between neighboring mushrooms. This dataset provides a resource for training and benchmarking algorithms in instance segmentation, growth analysis, and perception for agricultural robotics, with particular relevance to automated harvesting and RGB–D scene understanding in complex, densely populated agricultural settings.

Background

The dataset was created to support the development of perception systems for automated harvesting of white button mushrooms in indoor cultivation environments. In mushroom production beds, individual mushrooms often grow in dense clusters and may overlap or partially occlude one another. These conditions make it difficult to distinguish individual mushroom caps using RGB information alone. Accurate cap-level segmentation is needed to estimate mushroom size, identify grasping locations, and reduce unintended contact with neighboring mushrooms during harvesting operations.

To address these perception challenges, the dataset was designed to provide paired RGB and depth observations of mushroom beds over time that capture both visual and depth information of the mushrooms. A rail-mounted RGB–D imaging system was used to capture overlapping top-view frames, which were stitched into full-bed mosaics. The resulting dataset provides time-series RGB-depth mosaics and instance-level annotations of visible mushroom caps. This enables representation of different growth stages and variations in density and occlusion over time. This data article provides a detailed description of the public dataset, including the repository structure, file naming convention, data acquisition protocol, annotation format, and reuse potential for computer vision and agricultural robotics research.

Data Description

The dataset consists of 129 stitched RGB-depth mosaics of white button mushrooms (Agaricus bisporus) collected under indoor cultivation conditions. Each time point includes an RGB image, a corresponding depth map, and instance-level segmentation annotations of individual mushroom caps. The images represent time-series observations of mushroom growth and spatial distribution, capturing variations in size, density, and occlusion across different growth stages. Experimental Design, Materials, and Methods

The dataset was generated using a rail-mounted RGB-D imaging platform installed above two white button mushroom beds in an indoor cultivation facility. The imaging system, which was introduced in the related research article [1], consisted of a ZED Mini stereo camera, a 1000 mm linear rail, a stepper-motor-driven motion system, an Arduino-based motor controller (Elegoo Uno R3, Shenzhen, China), and an edge computer (Jetson Orin Nano, NVIDIA Corporation, Santa Clara, CA, USA) for image acquisition and storage (Fig. 2). In the present data article, the acquisition system is described only to document how the public dataset was produced, including image capture, RGB-depth pairing, mosaic generation, and annotation preparation.Fig. 2Overview of the rail-mounted RGB–D imaging system used for mushroom bed image acquisition. The image data were collected from two white button mushroom beds in an indoor cultivation facility. A ZED Mini stereo RGB-D camera was installed above the beds on a motorized linear rail so that sequential views could be collected across the full bed area. The camera was positioned approximately 749 mm above the mushroom beds and oriented downward to obtain top-view RGB and depth images. The mushroom beds measured 571.5 mm × 444.5 mm × 241.3 mm, and the camera field of view was not sufficient to cover the entire bed area in a single frame. Therefore, multiple partially overlapping frames were collected during each acquisition cycle.

The rail system (FSL40/FSK40) was driven by a ball screw mechanism and a NEMA 23 stepper motor, with motion controlled using an Arduino controller (Elegoo Uno r3, Shenzhen, China). Image acquisition and storage were managed using an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (NVIDIA Corporation, USA). The rail system moved the camera along the bed, and 17 RGB-D image pairs were captured at approximately 60 mm intervals during each cycle. The camera output was set to 1280 × 720 pixels for both RGB and depth acquisition (Fig. 3). Images were collected every 10 min from 18 to 21 March 2025, totaling ∼21.2 h of acquisition time (∼5 h per day on average), producing 129 acquisition cycles. Each cycle was converted into one RGB mosaic and one corresponding depth mosaic, resulting in 129 paired RGB-D mosaics in the final dataset.Fig. 3Representative (a) RGB image and (b) corresponding depth map captured at one position along the linear rail, prior to stitching into a full-bed mosaic. The RGB and depth data were acquired directly from the sensor and were not normalized. After image stitching, the mosaics measured approximately 1280 × 1634 pixels and were cropped to 615 × 988 pixels to retain the mushroom bed region of interest (Fig. 4). The images in the public repository [2], therefore, represent stitched and cropped mosaics rather than the individual raw frames captured at each rail position. Mosaic generation was completed using a custom Python workflow with OpenCV and NumPy [3]. Consecutive RGB frames were aligned using template matching [4] in the overlapping image region, and the same alignment offsets were applied to the corresponding depth frames. This procedure enabled that each RGB mosaic and its paired depth mosaic shared the same spatial coordinates. The final RGB mosaics were saved as PNG files, and the corresponding depth mosaics were saved as TIFF files.

Instance-level annotations were created from the RGB mosaics using the Roboflow annotation platform [5]. Each visible mushroom cap was manually annotated with a pixel-level mask, and the annotations were exported in COCO JSON format. The exported annotation folder also contains Roboflow-generated JPG image files with unique filename identifiers. These filenames correspond to the image entries in the COCO annotation file. Users can match the original RGB and depth mosaics using the shared date and cycle number in the filenames.

Limitations

This dataset is limited to white button mushrooms grown under an indoor cultivation environment and captured using a fixed top-view RGB-D imaging configuration. The images were collected using one imaging system and one production setting; therefore, variation in camera viewpoint, lighting condition, facility design, and mushroom variety is limited. The dataset contains stitched RGB and depth mosaics rather than all individual raw frames acquired along the rail. Depth images may include invalid or noisy pixels caused by stereo matching limitations, occlusion, and surface reflectance. Instance-level annotations describe visible mushroom cap boundaries; fully occluded cap regions were not annotated. These limitations should be considered when applying models trained on this dataset to other mushroom production systems, imaging configurations, or environmental conditions.

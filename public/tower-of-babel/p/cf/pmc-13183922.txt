Plasma proteome profiling identifies novel biomarkers and predictors for schizophrenia
Authors: Songyu Wu, Xinru Guo, Tingyi Jia, Zhouyang Sun, Ge Yu, Yixin Ouyang, Changgui Kou, Wei Bai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study performed a systematic investigation through proteomic and longitudinal follow-up data from the UK Biobank (comprising 70 schizophrenia cases and 36,135 healthy controls) to reveal the association between protein biomarkers and the onset of schizophrenia in order to provide insights into the pathogenesis of schizophrenia. Cox regressions recognized 31 significant schizophrenia related proteins (P < 1.712 × 10−5) and five of them also had non-linear associations with schizophrenia. The joint effect of 31 proteins was measured through Lasso-Cox regression, in which 17 proteins had non-zero coefficients. Mendelian randomization indicated JAM3 as having positive causal relationship (β = 0.1459, P = 0.0043) with schizophrenia. The well performance (AUC = 0.7930 (0.7908, 0.7951), G-mean = 0.6683 (0.6652, 0.6714)) of LightGBM model which consisted of protein and population features presented a strong and robust predictive ability of our findings. Furthermore, the investigation on biological functions of 31 proteins showed the enrichment in cell adhesion, integrin binding, chemotaxis and extracellular matrix organization through enrichment analyses.

Introduction

Schizophrenia (SCZ) is one of the most severe and highly heritable mental disorders [1]. About 1% of global population may be affected by SCZ in their lives with a median prevalence of 7.2 per 1000 individuals [1]. Both genetic and environmental pathogenic factors contribute to the development and progression of SCZ. However, the complex mechanisms behind SCZ have not yet been fully elucidated [2].

Proteins, as the products of gene translation, are the direct participants and carriers of many essential biological processes or body compositions such as neurodevelopment, inflammatory response and blood-brain barrier. The high-throughput large-scale precise protein sequencing technology provides researchers with an effective tool for understanding, predicting and diagnosing diseases [3]. Several case-control and cohort studies have revealed the differences in protein expressions between SCZ patients and healthy individuals and the biological processes could be categorized into synaptic function, inflammatory response, energy metabolism, oxidative stress and so on [4–7]. A recent study based on the Avon Longitudinal Study of Parents and Children (ALSPAC) in UK had revealed the relationship between several inflammatory proteins and psychosis in young adults [8]. However, these previous studies are either limited by study designs or constrained by number of participants and the scope of available proteins. The UK Biobank (UKB) is an ongoing longitudinal study which has recruited more than 500,000 participants from UK and has tested the levels of 2923 plasma proteins [9]. Using UKB proteomics datasets, researchers had revealed the molecular etiologies of many diseases, including inflammatory bowel disease, Graves’ disease and Parkinson’s disease [10–12].

Therefore, leveraging the large-scale proteomic and genetic data from the UKB, we aim to identify plasma proteins associated with the onset of SCZ, apply Mendelian randomization to infer potential causal relationships, and explore the underlying biological pathways through functional enrichment analysis.

Methods

Study design and population

The participants of this study came from the UKB. Baseline information was obtained through a serious of touchscreen questionnaires from 2006–2010. Blood samples were collected and stored in EDTA tubes and subsequently separated into plasma. The UKB has received approval from the North West Multi-Center Research Ethics Committee (REC No. 21/NW/0157), and all participants provided written electronic informed consent. This research was conducted under UKB application number 452018.

Proteomics assays

Between 2021 and 2022, the UKB Pharma Proteomics Project (UKB-PPP) measured levels of 2923 unique plasma proteins in 53,022 UKB participants using Olink™ Explore 3072 proximity extension assay. The samples were arranged into eight batches and proteins were classified into four categories: cardiac metabolism, inflammation, neurology and oncology. The sequencing data underwent a series of quality control steps including the incubation control, extension control, batch effect control, amplification control and finally normalized into a form of NPX on log2 scale. More detailed information and quality control processors could be seen in previous publications [13–15].

Ascertainment of outcome and covariates

SCZ patients were recognized through the first diagnosis records which consist of primary care data, hospital inpatient records, death registries, and self-reported medical conditions.

Based on the minimal covariate adjustment set strategy, we selected several covariates to control the potential confounding effects. Age was acquired and defined based on date of birth and date of attending an initial assessment center. Sex was acquired from central registry at recruitment or self-reported gender or NHS records. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated from height and weight which was measured during the initial Assessment Centre visit. Townsend Deprivation Index (TDI) is a comprehensive index composed of unemployment, overcrowded household, non–car ownership, and non–home ownership which correlates to psychiatric disorders and many other dysfunctions [16]. Participants’ TDI was pre-calculated before their enrollment in UKB. Ethnic was obtained through touchscreen questionnaire and further classified into White and Other in our study. All covariates were driven from the baseline data collection. Follow-up time was calculated from the baseline assessment date until one of the following events occurred: death, loss to follow-up, the first SCZ diagnosis, or the end of the follow-up period on December 19, 2022.

Statistical strategy

A total of 53,022 participants and 2923 proteins were available in baseline proteomics. The exclusion criteria are as follows: 1) Proteins or participants with missing values over 60% [17]. 2) Participants who have been detected as patients of any other mental or behavioral disorders according to ICD-10 codes F00 to F99. 3) Individuals diagnosed with SCZ prior to baseline recruitment. After exclusion, 2920 proteins and 36,205 participants were included for analysis. In sensitivity analyses, missing values for plasma proteins (missing rate < 60%) were imputed using the K-nearest neighbors (KNN) method according to previous studies [18, 19].

Baseline characteristics of the included participants were described using median (interquartile range) for continuous variables and counts (proportions) for categorical variables. Levene method were used to test the consistency of variances between groups. Differences between groups were tested by T-test or Chi-square test.

Exploration of relationship between protein biomarkers and SCZ

Cox regression was performed to estimate the longitudinal association between plasma protein levels and the onset of SCZ. Hazard ratio (HR) and its 95% CI were then calculated. Model 1 contains proteins only. Model 2 was adjusted for age, sex, BMI, TDI and ethnic. To reduce the false positive rate, we applied Bonferroni correction by dividing the significance threshold (P = 0.05) by the number of tests performed (2920). Those proteins who have passed the Bonferroni correction were recognized as SCZ related proteins. We then fitted restricted cubic splines (RCS) to examine the potential nonlinear association between protein levels and disease incidence. Considering the potential overestimation of HR caused by unbalanced distribution of the cases and non-cases of SCZ, Firth’s penalized likelihood method was conducted based on Cox regression [20]. To provide a more robust evaluation of the previous findings, we also performed bootstrap resampling. By conducting 500 bootstrap iterations, the estimated HR and their empirical distributions for SCZ related proteins were obtained. In order to further explore the joint effect of 31 SCZ related proteins on disease onset, we applied the Lasso-Cox regression model.

Mechanism exploration

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrich analyses were performed to explore the biological functions and pathways of the SCZ related proteins [21, 22]. Protein–Protein Interaction (PPI) analysis was conducted and visualized by STRING (version 12.0) and Cytoscape (version 3.10.2) to reveal the interaction of SCZ related proteins [23, 24]. Then we used Cytoscape application MCODE to separate the functional modules and subnetworks from the PPI network [25]. In addition, cytoHubba was used to recognize the top 5 hub proteins [25].

Causal inference

Two-sample mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was performed using cis-acting protein quantitative trait loci (cis-pQTLs) as candidate instrumental variables to further explore the causal relationship for those SCZ related proteins. The cis-pQTLs dataset was acquired from UKB-PPP, while the GWAS summary dataset of SCZ was sourced from Psychiatric Genomics Consortium (PGC) [26]. The Inverse Variance Weighted (IVW) method was chosen to conduct the analysis, whereas when only one pQTL was available, Wald Ratio method would be applied in replacement. In order to examine potential reverse causality, a reverse MR analysis was performed, with the UKB-PPP dataset serving as the outcome and the PGC dataset as the exposure. The significance level was set at two-tailed P < 0.05.

Establishment of disease prediction model

To assess the values of SCZ-related proteins in predicting the incidence of SCZ, we constructed stepwise feature accumulation machine learning models by Light Gradient Boosting Machine (LightGBM) methods [27]. AUC values and Geometric Mean (G-mean) values were calculated to evaluate the ability of the models.

The associations remained stable in sensitivity analyses: following multiple imputation, 30 proteins retained significance (Supplementary Table 4); Firth’s penalized regression and bootstrap analysis (500 replicates) confirmed robust estimates, with coefficients of variation ranging from 0.1205 (TNFRSF10A) to 0.4599 (ADGRG2) (Supplementary Table 5).

Function and network predication of SCZ related proteins

GO analysis shows that SCZ related proteins were significantly (Padjusted < 0.05 and Q value < 0.05) enriched in processes and cellular component such as cell adhesion, integrin binding, chemotaxis and extracellular matrix organization (Supplementary Table 7, Supplementary Fig. 3). In addition, three proteins were enriched in KEGG pathways under the category of environmental information processing (hsa04512, ECM-receptor interaction), particularly in signal transduction and cell–ECM interaction modules (Supplementary Table 8).

PPI analysis resulted in a network of 31 nodes and 70 edges with PPI enrichment P = 3.51e−13 and average local clustering coefficient = 0.532 (Fig. 3a, Supplementary Table 9). Secondary cluster contained 3 nodes and 3 edges, with an MCODE score of 3.000 (Fig. 3c). CytoHubba revealed the top 5 hub nodes in Cluster 1: ITGAM, ITGAV, ITGA11, HGF and PLAUR (Fig. 3d). Predictive performance of SCZ-related proteins

The results of descriptive statistics of AUC and G-mean values and the test of normality were shown in Supplementary Table 13 and Supplementary Table 14. The final model, containing all demographic variables and 31 SCZ-related proteins, had a good predictive performance for SCZ incidence with AUC = 0.7930, 95%CI (0.7908, 0.7951) and G-mean = 0.6683, 95%CI (0.6652, 0.6714). Furthermore, comparing with the base model, the final model had a cumulative mean AUC gain of 0.1224 with 95% CI (0.1193, 0.1255) (Supplementary Table 15). Discussion

Through analyzing proteomic data in UKB, we discovered 31 proteins related to the onset of SCZ after Bonferroni correction and adjustment of covariates. To address the bias caused by missing values and the imbalance of cases and non-cases, KNN imputation method and FIRTH correction with bootstrap strategy were conducted. And the results had demonstrated the stability and robustness of our previous findings.

RCS analysis revealed the non-linear dose-response relationship between 5 proteins and SCZ. Noticeably, three proteins: ITGAM, TFF3 and GDF15 had significant U-shaped relationships with SCZ. GDF15 has been widely recognized as a highly sensitive and non-specific marker of cellular stress response [28]. Its elevated level is closely associated with a variety of core pathological processes involving chronic inflammation, metabolic disorders, tissue damage, and aging [29, 30]. The U-shaped GDF15-SCZ risk relationship may delineate two distinct biological states: a state of suboptimal homeostasis at low levels, where GDF15 falls below an optimal protective range, and a state of active pathology at high levels, where it signals overwhelming systemic stress—together underscoring etiological heterogeneity. However, the normalizations of the original protein levels make it impossible to clarify the threshold value [15].

Then we explored the functions and interactions of 31 SCZ-related proteins. The results indicated that these proteins were highly enriched in processes including cell adhesion, integrin receptor binding, extracellular matrix-receptor interaction and chemotaxis. These processes were involved in neurodevelopment and immunoreaction [31]. Furthermore, ITGAM, ITGAV and ITGA11 was recognized as the hub proteins.

Integrins are widely expressed on the cell surface, responsible for the interaction between cells and extracellular matrix, and play an important role in cell adhesion, cell migration, signal transduction and synaptic plasticity [32]. Integrins, especially β subunit, have been shown to associated with the occurrence and development of SCZ [33]. The experimental research of Grzegorz Wiera indicated that the integrin β1 subunit and β3 may have opposite bidirectional effects in regulating inhibitory neurotransmitters and long-term synaptic plasticity, suggesting the heterogeneity and synergy of the roles of different integrin subunits in the nervous system [34]. Additionally, the single nucleotide polymorphism variations of ITGB3 were associated with the age of onset of SCZ [35]. Comparing to healthy participants, the level of integrin αIIbβIIIa were significantly higher in first episode SCZ patients without medication [35]. Furthermore, integrin β subunit were possibly associated with brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and neurotransmitters including 5-HT and glutamic acid [33]. Other composition in integrin pathway was also revealed to be associated with SCZ. A systematic review using proteomics data demonstrated that integrin cell surface interaction was the common biological pathway of SCZ and bipolar disorder [36]. The research team led by Bill Qi et al. used transcriptomics of brain tissue to identify a set of integrin-binding-related genes, including FERMT3, which were significantly associated with SCZ [37]. However, in our study, three integrin α subunits (ITGAV, ITGAM and ITGA11) were identified to be associated with the onset of SCZ. In a previous study, co-localization analysis revealed that the Reelin protein and the integrin α3 subunit are simultaneously expressed on the dendritic spines at the synapse, and by activating the phosphorylation of the Dab1 protein within the cell, they regulate the cytoskeleton and thereby affect the plasticity of the synapse [38].

Neuroinflammation is considered to be one of the possible pathogeneses of SCZ [39]. The decrease of ITGAV could lead to the malfunction of pathway including TGF-β-dependent signaling and αVβ3-regulated immune signaling. Clinical research has indicated that the reduce of ITGAV protein level could cause immune disorders and brain abnormalities [40]. ITGAM (CD11b) is wildly expressed in immune cells and has been proved to enrolled in microglial repair and neuroinflammatory processes [41, 42]. Two MR analyses also pointed out that the ITGAM involved immune cell phenotype CD33dim HLA DR + CD11b− may prevent the onset of SCZ [43, 44]. Both ITGAV and ITGAM were also recognized as the protein biomarkers of Parkinson disease in a UKB based study [12]. Meanwhile, as the biomarker of inflammation processes, ITGA11 was considered to influence depression disorder and the development of cognition impairment [17, 45]. Taken together, we hypothesize that the three α subunits of integrin families may contribute to the pathogenesis of SCZ by participating in neuroinflammatory responses and interacting with the extracellular matrix.

We further tested the correlation found in cox regression through two sample mendelian randomization combined with hereditary information, where four proteins (TFF3, JAM3, AIFM1 and IGFBP4) retained their significance. However, only JAM3 kept the consistency of direction of effect (HR > 1) in both cox regression and mendelian randomization. This could be explained by the difference in study design and the limitation of instrument variables. MR analysis used relatively stable genetic variants as research objects, while protein levels and their association in cox regression may be affected by health status and confounding factors such as medications. However, the instrumental variables selected for MR Analysis in this study were only from pQTL loci of target proteins instead of scope of whole genome, which may also bring bias to the results. Meanwhile, some recurrent cases might be inevitably recruited due to the design of UKB, which may also partly explain the inconsistent results.

This consistency makes JAM3 a potential biomarker for the onset of SCZ. JAM3 is a member of the junctional adhesion protein family, which primarily contributes to the formation of tight junctions between cells and the maintenance of cell polarity [46]. Meanwhile, JAM3 and its protein family can interact with multiple integrin subunits, such as αVβ3 and αMβ2, to mediate the adhesion and transmembrane migration of immune cells [47, 48]. The JAM3 exerts an interesting dual effect in human diseases, depending on the different pathways it is involved in under various signaling environments. Basically, the dysregulation of JAM3 levels can lead to increased permeability of vascular endothelial cells, which may further cause brain damage or promote tumor metastasis [49–51]. In the field of SCZ, a previous genome-wide methylation study highlighted the potential association between the hypomethylation of the JAM3 CpG site cg11723066 and the early onset of SCZ [52]. In conclusion, we believe that elevated JAM3 protein levels may contribute to the development of SCZ by disrupting tight junction structure and promoting neuroinflammation.

LightGBM is an efficient, and scalable machine learning algorithm used for classification, regression, and ranking tasks [28]. This method had been widely applied in disease prediction and clinical diagnosis due to its efficiency in handling large datasets and its ability to model complex relationships in medical data. The well performance of the 500-times bootstrapped, fully-adjusted LightGBM model (AUC = 0.7930, G-mean = 0.6683) in our study demonstrates that the 31 SCZ-related proteins, in combination with population features, possess strong and robust predictive ability for the onset of SCZ. However, the absence of external cross-validation may limit the generalizability of our results.

Overall, in this study, we found 31 SCZ protein biomarkers which could be used to predict the onset of SCZ. Among these protein biomarkers, integrin α subunits and JAM3 had shown the strongest evidence to the correlation with SCZ. Moreover, the evidence also suggested that the underlying biological mechanisms may involve neuroinflammation and increased vascular permeability.

The advantages of our study lie in the substantial protein biomarkers UKB provided and the longitudinal follow-up data. And mendelian randomization method together with machine learning was conducted to further confirm the causal relationship. Except the limitations mentioned before, our study was still confined by the following factors. Firstly, according to the original study design, the participants enrolled in UKB were mainly middle-aged British citizens and their medical records could not date back to very early life. However, the general peak age of SCZ is at late adolescence to early adulthood. This inconsistency may raise the concern regarding the confirmation of new onset cases, which could partly be recurrent cases, and the possibility of reverse causation. Secondly, although we conducted several methods to evaluate and resolve the bias caused by unbalanced sample size between case and control, it is still one of the major limitations of our study. Thirdly, the Olink platform that we used is a targeted proteomics assay. While it covers proteins across major biological processes, its scope is necessarily limited compared to the full plasma proteome. Furthermore, our study used Bonferroni correction method which may raise the risk of false negative. Finally, while we have provided evidence for causal relationships between several proteins and SCZ, in vitro and in vivo experiments are still necessary to uncover the underlying biological mechanisms.

Diagnostic value of systemic immune-inflammation index in differentiating testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis based on symptom duration: A retrospective observational study
Authors: Mehmet Demir, Eser Ördek, İsmail Yağmur, İbrahim Halil Albayrak, Muhammed Nur Karadeniz, Erhan Şengel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Acute scrotal pain, most commonly caused by testicular torsion or epididymo-orchitis, requires rapid differentiation to preserve testicular viability, particularly within the early hours of symptom onset. The systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), derived from platelet, neutrophil, and lymphocyte counts, has emerged as a potential biomarker in inflammatory conditions. In this retrospective study, a total of 283 patients presenting with acute scrotal pain between January 2012 and May 2025 were analyzed, including 139 patients with testicular torsion and 144 with epididymo-orchitis. Demographic characteristics, symptom duration, and complete blood count parameters were evaluated, and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio, and SII were calculated. Patients were stratified according to symptom duration (≤ 6 hours vs > 6 hours), and intergroup comparisons were performed. SII, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio, and mean platelet volume levels were significantly higher in patients with testicular torsion (all P < .05). Multivariate logistic regression analysis identified SII, mean platelet volume, and C-reactive protein levels as independent predictors of testicular torsion (P < .05). Among patients presenting within 6 hours of symptom onset, SII demonstrated acceptable discriminative ability, with an optimal cutoff value of 830 (area under the curve: 0.737), yielding 72.0% sensitivity and 75.4% specificity. These findings suggest that SII may serve as a readily available adjunctive biomarker for the early differentiation of testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis, particularly within the critical 6-hour window. Although not sufficient as a standalone diagnostic tool, its integration with clinical and imaging findings may improve early diagnostic accuracy, and further prospective multicenter studies are warranted to validate these results.

1. Introduction

Acute scrotum is a common clinical presentation among children and young adults, which is commonly caused by testicular torsion or epididymo-orchitis.[1] Testicular torsion is a surgical emergency caused by twisting of the spermatic cord, which reduces or blocks blood flow to the testis.[2] If detorsion is performed within the first 6 hours, testicular viability can be preserved in up to 91.4% of cases. After this period, the risk of testicular loss increases dramatically.[3] After this period, the risk of testicular loss increases sharply, often requiring orchiectomy. Therefore, early and accurate diagnosis of testicular torsion is crucial for preserving testicular function.[4] Epididymo-orchitis, usually bacterial in origin, causes inflammation of the epididymis and testis and is managed conservatively. However, the symptomatic overlap between these 2 conditions, particularly in pediatric and adolescent populations, complicates accurate diagnosis and can lead to unnecessary surgeries or delayed treatment.[3] Therefore, rapid and precise differentiation of these conditions is essential to guide appropriate management and preserve testicular function.[5] Color Doppler ultrasonography is the imaging modality most commonly used to distinguish testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis. However, its operator dependence and the potential for preserved flow signals in early or partial torsion can impair diagnostic accuracy.[6] Consequently, there is a need for alternative or adjunctive diagnostic methods.

The degree of systemic inflammation differs between these etiologies. Epididymo-orchitis typically elicits a more pronounced inflammatory response, whereas torsion-associated inflammation is driven largely by ischemia–reperfusion injury.[7,8] These pathophysiological distinctions suggest that hematological parameters may aid in differentiation. Recent studies have reported that hematologic parameters such as platelet-to-lymphocyte ratio (PLR) and neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) can be used in the diagnosis of testicular torsion.[3,9,10] The systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) is a new hematologic marker calculated by combining NLR with platelet count.[11] SII is reported to have higher diagnostic accuracy because it reflects the systemic inflammatory response, incorporating both immune and thrombotic components.[12] In recent years, SII has been widely used for prognostic assessment and risk stratification in various diseases.[12] However, to our knowledge, no study has evaluated the diagnostic performance of SII in differentiating testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis based on symptom duration. This study aimed to investigate the diagnostic value of SII in differentiating testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis. Therefore, this study investigated the value of SII as a rapid, reliable criterion to aid in early diagnosis and prevent testicular loss.

2. Methods

2.1. Study design and participants

This retrospective observational study was conducted in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines at a tertiary-care academic hospital between January 2012 and May 2025. The study protocol was approved by the Harran University Ethics Committee (Approval No: HRU/25.12.15) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Medical records of patients presenting with acute scrotal pain to the emergency department or urology outpatient clinic were reviewed. All patients were evaluated using standardized institutional protocols, including physical examination and color Doppler ultrasonography.

A total of 283 patients met the eligibility criteria. Patients diagnosed with testicular torsion or epididymo-orchitis based on clinical and Doppler ultrasonography findings, and with available complete blood count (CBC) data for inflammatory index calculation, were included. Patients with torsion of the testicular appendix, brucellar orchitis, liver or renal dysfunction, hematologic disorders, chronic inflammatory diseases, recent trauma, fever, or known malignancy were excluded.

Patients were classified into 2 groups according to their final diagnosis: testicular torsion or epididymo-orchitis. To assess the effect of symptom duration on hematologic parameters, each group was further subdivided into patients presenting within 6 hours and those presenting after 6 hours from symptom onset.

2.2. Diagnostic criteria and data collection

Testicular torsion was diagnosed based on patient history, acute-onset scrotal pain, physical examination findings, and Doppler ultrasonography demonstrating reduced or absent testicular blood flow. Patients with suspected torsion underwent urgent scrotal exploration, and orchiectomy was performed when the testis was considered nonviable.

Epididymo-orchitis was diagnosed based on scrotal pain or swelling, tenderness, fever, urinary symptoms, and increased blood flow on Doppler ultrasonography, and these patients were managed medically.

Demographic characteristics, symptom duration, and laboratory findings were extracted from the hospital information system. CBC parameters were analyzed using an automated hematology analyzer, and C-reactive protein (CRP) levels were measured using a nephelometric immunoassay method.

2.3. Inflammatory indices

The NLR was calculated as the ratio of neutrophil count to lymphocyte count, while the PLR was calculated as the ratio of platelet count to lymphocyte count. The SII was calculated as platelet count multiplied by neutrophil count divided by lymphocyte count. All indices were derived from laboratory data obtained at initial presentation.

2.4. Statistical analysis

Normality was tested using Kolmogorov–Smirnov. Normally distributed data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation; non-normal data as median (interquartile ratio). Between-group comparisons used Mann–Whitney U or independent t-tests, as appropriate. Categorical variables used the chi-square test (Fisher exact test if expected frequency < 5). Logistic regression (forward LR) was used to identify independent predictors. Variables significant in univariate analysis were entered into the multivariate model. During stepwise selection, only SII, mean platelet volume (MPV), and CRP remained as independent predictors, whereas NLR and PLR were excluded due to collinearity with SII, which incorporates neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet counts. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis determined cutoff values. The significance level was P < .05. To assess the adequacy of the sample size, a power analysis was conducted. The effect size (Cohen d) for the difference between 2 independent groups was calculated based on SII values, yielding Cohen d ≈ 0.36 (medium effect size). The pooled standard deviation method was applied for this calculation. Subsequently, this effect size was evaluated under a 2-tailed test assumption at a significance level of α = 0.05 using the Python statsmodels (TTestIndPower) module. The analysis demonstrated that the current sample size provided approximately 86% statistical power. Analyses were performed in SPSS 25.0 (IBM).

4. Discussion

In this study, we evaluated hematologic inflammatory parameters in patients presenting with acute scrotum. We found that SII, MPV, and CRP were independent predictors for differentiating testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis. Additionally, the diagnostic performance of SII was more pronounced in patients presenting within the first 6 hours after symptom onset. Acute scrotum represents one of the clinical emergencies requiring prompt intervention, particularly in pediatric and adolescent populations. Testicular torsion and epididymo-orchitis are the leading causes of acute scrotum. Although they present with similar features, their management strategies differ fundamentally. Consequently, early and accurate differentiation between these 2 pathologies is crucial for preserving testicular function.[4] In 1 study, it was reported that, depending on the physician’s experience, 9.6 to 25.1% of testicular torsion patients were misdiagnosed, and only 23.8% of patients were treated within the first 6 hours.[13] Thus, there is growing interest in identifying rapid, noninvasive biomarkers to aid diagnosis. In this study, we evaluated hematologic inflammatory parameters and identified SII as a significant, independent marker in both the first 6 hours and thereafter.

Leukocytes, lymphocytes, and platelets play critical roles in the inflammatory cascade.[14] Parameters derived from CBC analysis, along with composite indexes such as NLR, PLR, and SII, are widely used for diagnosis and prognostication in various diseases.[15] In testicular torsion, ischemia–reperfusion injury triggers pronounced neutrophil-mediated inflammation, whereas epididymo-orchitis is characterized predominantly by mononuclear cell infiltration secondary to infection. This pathophysiological divergence underpins the significant differences observed in hematologic indexes.[2,10] Accordingly, recent studies have investigated NLR and PLR for differentiating testicular torsion from other causes of acute scrotum and for assessing testicular viability.[9,10] Güneş et al compared patients with testicular torsion and healthy individuals and observed elevated NLR and PLR levels in the torsion group.[16] Yilmaz et al evaluated patients with acute scrotum and reported that various hematologic indexes were effective for both diagnosis and differential diagnosis.[17] Bitkin et al reported that although NLR distinguished testicular torsion and epididymo-orchitis from healthy individuals, it was ineffective in differentiating the 2 pathologies.[18] A meta-analysis similarly concluded that testicular torsion patients had lower NLR (weighted mean difference = −1.66, 95% CI = −3.25–−0.06) and PLT (weighted mean difference = −27.39, 95% CI = −48.03–− 6.75) compared to epididymo-orchitis patients.[19] However, these studies did not stratify comparisons by symptom duration. Lee et al evaluated patients with testicular torsion within the first 6 hours, the critical window for testicular viability, and reported that NLR showed high diagnostic accuracy for testicular torsion.[3] In the present study, neutrophil count and NLR were significantly elevated in the torsion group both overall and within the first 6 hours, although neither emerged as an independent predictor on multivariate analysis.

Platelet count, PLR, and MPV serve as markers of inflammation and thrombotic activity. Vural et al highlighted PLR as an inflammatory marker in testicular torsion and reported its utility in predicting torsion-related testicular loss.[20] However, other studies have questioned the diagnostic value of PLR in this context. Arikan et al found PLR to be less significant than other hematologic indexes in differentiating testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis.[10] Zhu et al, in a meta-analysis, concluded that PLR was inadequate in differentiating testicular torsion and orchitis.[19] In the present study, although PLR was significantly higher in the torsion group on univariate analysis, it did not retain independent significance in multivariate modeling, suggesting its limited utility as a standalone diagnostic criterion. MPV serves as an indicator of platelet function and is particularly associated with vascular pathologies such as cardiovascular disease.[21] In the study by Çiçek et al, testicular torsion was compared with healthy individuals, and it was reported that MPV can be used as an auxiliary examination in the differentiation of testicular torsion.[7] Lee et al demonstrated that within the first 6 hours of symptom onset, MPV was a highly effective predictor for differentiating testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis (AUC: 0.855).[3] Similarly, in the present study, multivariate analysis identified MPV as an independent and significant discriminator between epididymo-orchitis and testicular torsion (P < .05). This effect was significant both within the first 6 hours and thereafter.

SII has emerged as a comprehensive tool reflecting the systemic effects of inflammation. It has been investigated extensively in various oncologic and chronic conditions, demonstrating diagnostic and prognostic utility.[11,12,15] Studies have shown significantly elevated SII levels in inflammatory diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis and ankylosing spondylitis.[22,23] Dervisevic et al reported that the SII value may be a potentially useful marker for evaluating the inflammatory process and clinical progression of rheumatoid arthritis in systemic lupus patients.[22] Similarly, Yang et al reported that SII increased significantly during lupus flares and decreased during clinical remission in patients with systemic lupus.[23]

In the context of infectious inflammatory diseases, studies during the COVID-19 pandemic revealed that elevated SII levels were associated with severe disease course, need for intensive care, and higher mortality.[24] These data indicate that SII is useful not only in chronic inflammatory conditions but also for assessing acute systemic inflammatory responses. In this study, SII was among the most prominent parameters, demonstrating high discriminative power to differentiate testicular torsion from epididymo-orchitis both within the first 6 hours and regardless of symptom duration. Specifically, in patients presenting within 6 hours, ROC analysis identified an SII cutoff of 830 (AUC: 0.737; 95% CI: 0.651–0.823), with a sensitivity of 72% and a specificity of 75%. Accurate diagnosis of torsion before the 6-hour threshold is critical for preserving testicular viability; therefore, the incorporation of rapid, cost-effective markers such as SII could facilitate clinical decision-making.

5. Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design may have led to incomplete data capture, particularly regarding symptom duration or blood count parameters. This may have led to the exclusion of cases in which symptom duration or CBC parameters were not recorded or entered incompletely. More importantly, the retrospective nature of the study precludes the establishment of temporal or causal relationships between inflammatory parameters and the disease process. This represents a fundamental drawback of retrospective research. Second, data collection from a single center may limit the generalizability of the findings. Variations in patient profiles, clinical practices, and laboratory equipment across institutions could affect hematologic parameters; thus, multi-center prospective validation is essential. Third, inflammatory markers (NLR, PLR, SII, and MPV) may be influenced by confounders such as stress, trauma, or underlying systemic conditions. Although we excluded patients with malignancy, hematologic disorders, and chronic inflammatory diseases, undetected subclinical conditions remain a consideration.

6. Conclusion

The findings obtained in the present study clearly demonstrate that hematologic inflammation parameters (particularly SII) significantly contribute to the differentiation of testicular torsion and epididymo-orchitis, the 2 leading causes of acute scrotum. Elevated SII values are associated with testicular torsion, especially within the critical 6-hour window. While SII may serve as an adjunct to clinical and imaging evaluation, its diagnostic performance is moderate. These findings should be validated in multicenter prospective studies.

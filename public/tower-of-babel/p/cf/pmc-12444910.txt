Letter to the editor regarding ‘Clinical value of procalcitonin-to-albumin ratio for identifying sepsis in neonates with pneumonia’
Authors: Guo-Ming Zhang
Published: 2025

To the editor

We read with great interest the recent article entitled ‘Clinical value of procalcitonin-to-albumin ratio for identifying sepsis in neonates with pneumonia’ [1], published in Annals of Medicine. The authors demonstrated that the procalcitonin-to-albumin ratio (PAR) may serve as a promising novel biomarker for detecting sepsis in neonates with pneumonia. These findings provide valuable insights for distinguishing sepsis from pneumonia in neonates and may inform both prognostic evaluation and therapeutic strategies. Nevertheless, several methodological aspects warrant further discussion.

First, there are inherent limitations in the interpretation of area under the curve (AUC) values. The reported AUCs may be overly optimistic, and their clinical applicability requires further validation. Notably, AUC values ranging from 0.5 to 0.8 reflect only modest discriminatory capacity, which is generally considered to have limited clinical value [2]. In addition, the 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of the AUCs for PAP overlap with those of PCT, ABL, and CRP, suggesting that the observed differences may not be statistically significant. To formally test these differences, the use of the z test for comparing AUCs is recommended [3]. Furthermore, PAP is a derived parameter that depends on albumin measurement, which may compromise its stability and diagnostic accuracy. This further implies that PAP could be less reliable than PCT in identifying sepsis in neonates with pneumonia.

Second, the absence of key diagnostic indices reduces the interpretability of the findings. The study reported the sensitivity and specificity of the PAR but did not present these parameters for PCT, CRP, or albumin. A more comprehensive evaluation would require sensitivity, specificity, the Youden index, and positive and negative predictive values, along with 95% CIs, for each biomarker. The Youden index, which is derived from sensitivity and specificity, is widely used to compare diagnostic performance, with larger values reflecting stronger discriminatory ability [3]. Without these metrics, the comparative advantage of the PAR over established biomarkers remains uncertain.

In conclusion, we recommend Li et al. for their valuable contribution, which highlights a novel approach to improve the diagnosis of sepsis in neonates with pneumonia. Their study provides a foundation for further investigation. We hope that future research will validate these findings in larger, multicenter cohorts and provide comprehensive statistical indicators to strengthen clinical interpretation. Such efforts will ultimately contribute to the development of more accurate and reliable tools for the early identification of neonatal sepsis.

Serum HDAC3 as an early biomarker for prediction and staging of sepsis-associated AKI: prospective cohort study
Authors: Wei-ying Liu, Min Chen, Dong-yu Chen, Xing Li, Bo Xi, Fang Chen, Yi-jun Deng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionTo investigate the early predictive value of serum histone deacetylase 3 (HDAC3) levels for sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) and evaluate its ability to differentiate between various stages of acute kidney injury (AKI).MethodsA prospective cohort study was conducted among patients with sepsis admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) of Yancheng First People’s Hospital between January 2025 and October 2025. All participants met the Sepsis-3 diagnostic criteria and had no AKI at ICU admission. Patients were stratified according to the development of AKI within seven days after diagnosis, and AKI severity was staged using KDIGO guidelines. Multivariable logistic regression was used to identify independent risk factors for SA-AKI. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to assess the predictive performance of HDAC3 and related biomarkers. Subgroup analysis evaluated the discriminatory ability of HDAC3 between mild (KDIGO stage 1) and severe AKI (KDIGO stages 2–3). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was performed to examine the association between HDAC3 levels and 28-day survival.ResultsA total of 90 patients with sepsis were enrolled, of whom 50 (55.6%) developed AKI within seven days. Patients with SA-AKI exhibited significantly higher SOFA scores, APACHE II scores, PCT, CRP, and serum HDAC3 levels than those without AKI (P < 0.05). Multivariable analysis identified SOFA score, CRP, PCT, and serum HDAC3 as independent risk factors for SA-AKI. ROC analysis showed that HDAC3 achieved an AUC of 0.820 (95% CI: 0.732–0.908), which was higher than those of SOFA, CRP, and PCT. Serum HDAC3 levels showed a significant stepwise increase across KDIGO stages, and stratified analysis demonstrated an AUC of 0.828 (95% CI: 0.694–0.920) for distinguishing mild from severe AKI. Higher HDAC3 levels were also significantly associated with reduced 28-day survival (log-rank P < 0.05).ConclusionElevated serum HDAC3 levels are associated with the development, severity, and prognosis of SA-AKI. While these findings provide supportive evidence for the potential role of HDAC3 in early risk stratification and AKI staging, validation in larger, multi-center cohorts is required to confirm its clinical utility.

Introduction

Sepsis is a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection, triggers extensive inflammation and frequently leads to organ dysfunction [1, 2] Acute kidney injury (AKI) is among its most common and severe complications, with sepsis-associated acute kidney injury (SA-AKI) being particularly concerning. Over 40% of patients with sepsis develop AKI, and the mortality rate among these patients ranges from 50% to 70% [3]. Currently, the clinical diagnosis of AKI primarily relies on traditional functional markers such as serum creatinine (Scr) and urine output [4–6]. However, Scr levels typically increase only after significant renal injury has occurred, while urine output can be influenced by various factors, including volume status and diuretic use, limiting their utility for early and accurate identification of SA-AKI [7, 8]. Therefore, identifying more sensitive and timely biomarkers in blood or urine for early diagnosis is essential.

Regarding pathogenesis, ischemia-reperfusion injury, sepsis-induced immune dysregulation, and nephrotoxic drugs can activate innate immunity and trigger inflammatory cascades, leading to mitochondrial dysfunction and increased production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) [9]. These mechanisms contribute to tubular epithelial cell dysfunction and cell death, thus driving the initiation and progression of SA-AKI [10, 11]. Recent molecular studies further highlight the central roles of immune-inflammatory imbalance and metabolic reprogramming in SA-AKI pathogenesis [12–14]. Histone deacetylase 3 (HDAC3), an important member of the histone deacetylase family, participates in transcriptional regulation, inflammatory responses, and cell death pathways, playing a critical role in various kidney diseases [15, 16]. Prior research indicates that the HDAC3-miR-19b-3p/SF3B3 axis contributes to fibrosis in models of renal interstitial fibrosis, and HDAC3 expression is elevated in AKI animal models induced by cisplatin and ischemia-reperfusion [16, 17]. Selective HDAC3 inhibition (such as RGFP966) has been shown to reduce renal injury and suppress RIPK1-mediated programmed necrosis in experimental models [17], suggesting HDAC3 as a potential diagnostic marker or therapeutic target for AKI.

Although previous studies have established the involvement of HDAC3 in various kidney disease models, existing evidence is largely derived from animal experiments or tissue-based analyses. Systematic investigation of circulating HDAC3 expression patterns in patients with SA-AKI, as well as its clinical utility as an early diagnostic biomarker, remains insufficient. Therefore, this study aims to measure serum HDAC3 levels in patients who have not yet developed SA-AKI and evaluate its predictive capacity for the onset of SA-AKI within seven days, thereby providing supportive evidence for the early clinical identification of SA-AKI.

Methods

Study design and setting

This single-centre, prospective observational cohort study enrolled consecutive patients diagnosed with sepsis admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) of Yancheng First People’s Hospital from January 2025 to October 2025. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (approval No. 2025-K-108). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legally authorised representatives.

Patients

Inclusion criteria were: diagnosis of sepsis according to Sepsis-3 criteria, defined as suspected or confirmed infection accompanied by organ dysfunction (increase in SOFA score ≥ 2 points), including patients with septic shock requiring vasopressors to maintain mean arterial pressure ≥ 65 mmHg despite adequate volume resuscitation.); anticipated ICU stay > 48 h; and presence of a urinary catheter expected to remain for > 48 h. Exclusion criteria included: pre-existing chronic kidney disease or previous renal transplantation; chronic use of calcineurin inhibitors; presence of KDIGO stage [12] 2–3 acute kidney injury (AKI) at enrolment or within the preceding two weeks; ongoing renal replacement therapy at enrolment or anticipated dialysis requirement within 6 h, as judged by the attending physician; and severe comorbid conditions likely to significantly limit short-term (28-day) survival or interfere with AKI assessment. These included active malignancy (excluding localized non-melanoma skin cancers) requiring ongoing or anticipated antineoplastic therapy within 6 months; end-stage liver disease (Child–Pugh class C or decompensated cirrhosis with hepatic encephalopathy, refractory ascites, or variceal bleeding); advanced heart failure (New York Heart Association class IV or persistent left ventricular ejection fraction < 30%); end-stage respiratory disease requiring long-term home oxygen therapy or anticipated lung transplantation; and advanced neuromuscular disorders associated with complete functional dependence.

Data collection and laboratory assays

Baseline data collected within 24 h of ICU admission included demographic characteristics, smoking and alcohol history, comorbidities, infection sources, and illness severity scores (SOFA and APACHE II). Clinical variables documented included presence of septic shock, use of invasive mechanical ventilation, and Vasoactive-Inotropic Score (VIS). Routine laboratory tests comprised Scr, uric acid, haemoglobin, platelet count, white blood cell count, C-reactive protein (CRP), and procalcitonin (PCT). Scr was measured daily, and the enrolment-day value served as the baseline.

To measure HDAC3 levels, peripheral venous blood (4 mL) was collected 6–24 h after ICU admission. Samples were allowed to clot at room temperature for 30 min, centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 15 min at 4 °C, and serum aliquots were stored at − 80 °C until analysis. Serum HDAC3 concentrations were determined using a human HDAC3 ELISA kit (Nanjing Youmeng Biotech Co., Ltd.; catalogue YM-00209A1) according to the manufacturer’s instructions and standard laboratory procedures. According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the assay has a detection limit of 0.1 ng/mL and a linear dynamic range of 0.2–50 ng/mL. The reported intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation are < 10% and < 12%, respectively. All samples were analyzed in technical duplicates, and mean values were used for statistical analysis to ensure analytical reproducibility. Optical density was measured using a microplate reader (BioTek Synergy H1; BioTek Instruments, USA).

Outcomes and definitions

The primary outcome was the occurrence of acute kidney injury (AKI) within 7 days following ICU admission. The 7-day window was chosen to capture SA-AKI events during the early ICU course while allowing sufficient time for KDIGO criteria–based recognition; however, AKI events within this window may also be influenced by subsequent ICU-related exposures. AKI was diagnosed and staged according to the 2012 KDIGO guidelines [12]: an increase in Scr ≥ 0.3 mg/dL (26.5 µmol/L) within 48 h, an increase in Scr to ≥ 1.5 times baseline, or urine output < 0.5 mL·kg− 1·h− 1 for ≥ 6 h. Staging was defined as follows: Stage 1, Scr increased to 1.5–1.9 times baseline or by ≥ 0.3 mg/dL, or urine output < 0.5 mL·kg− 1·h− 1 for 6–12 h; Stage 2, Scr increased to 2.0-2.9 times baseline or urine output < 0.5 mL·kg− 1·h− 1 for ≥ 12 h; and Stage 3, Scr increased to ≥ 3.0 times baseline, or Scr ≥ 4.0 mg/dL (353.6 µmol/L), urine output < 0.3 mL·kg− 1·h− 1 for ≥ 24 h, anuria for ≥ 12 h, or initiation of renal replacement therapy. For patients younger than 18 years, a decline in eGFR to < 35 mL·min− 1·1.73 m− 2 also constituted Stage 3. Baseline creatinine was defined as the serum creatinine measured on the day of ICU admission The secondary outcome was 28-day all-cause mortality.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 and MedCalc v23.0 software. Normally distributed continuous data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (x̄±s) and were compared between groups using independent-samples t-tests. Non-normally distributed data are reported as median (interquartile range) [M (P25, P75)] and were compared between groups using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages, and comparisons between groups were made using the χ² test or Fisher’s exact test. Multivariate binary logistic regression analysis was performed to identify independent risk factors for SA-AKI. Trends across AKI stages were evaluated using the Jonckheere–Terpstra test. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to evaluate the predictive performance of each biomarker, with calculations of area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity. Optimal diagnostic cutoff values were identified using the maximum Youden index. AUC comparisons between ROC curves were performed using DeLong’s test. Kaplan–Meier survival curves were constructed to evaluate the relationship between HDAC3 levels and patient prognosis. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Comparison of clinical data between AKI and non-AKI groups

A total of 161 ICU patients meeting the Sepsis-3 diagnostic criteria [18] were screened. Based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria, 21 patients with a history of chronic kidney disease, 17 patients with KDIGO stage [12] 2–3 acute kidney injury (AKI) within the past 2 weeks, 14 patients requiring dialysis at the time of enrollment, and 19 patients with malignancies or other severe comorbidities were excluded. Ultimately, 90 sepsis patients were included for analysis. Among these, 50 were male (55.56%) and 40 were female (44.44%), with 50 patients (55.6%) developing AKI within 7 days of diagnosis. The patients were divided into the AKI group (n = 50) and the non-AKI group (n = 40). In the AKI group, 32 were male (64%) and 18 were female (36%), with a mean age of 64.62 ± 12.79 years. In the non-AKI group, 18 were male (45%) and 22 were female (55%), with a mean age of 70.05 ± 15.57 years (Fig. 1).

There were no statistically significant differences between groups in gender, age, smoking history, alcohol consumption, comorbidities, or primary infection site (P > 0.05). Comparison of serum HDAC3 levels and subgroup analysis by AKI stage

ELISA analysis showed significantly higher serum HDAC3 levels in the AKI group (42.30 ± 5.15 ng/mL) compared to the non-AKI group (36.43 ± 7.58 ng/mL; P < 0.05).Importantly, the magnitude of this difference was large (Cohen’s d = 0.93), indicating a clinically meaningful separation between groups (Table 2; Fig. 1). The AKI group was further subdivided according to KDIGO criteria into stages 1, 2, and 3, with median serum HDAC3 levels of 42.50 (41.83–43.58), 43.75 (42.73–44.60), and 44.70 (43.80–45.60) ng/mL, respectively. Differences among stages were statistically significant (H = 14.895, P < 0.001). Pairwise comparisons indicated significant differences between AKI stages 1 and 3, and between stages 2 and 3 (P < 0.05), but no significant difference between stages 1 and 2 (P = 0.122). Jonckheere–Terpstra trend analysis demonstrated a significant overall trend toward higher serum HDAC3 levels with advancing AKI stage, as supported by Jonckheere–Terpstra trend analysis (J–T = 3.895, P < 0.001), with higher levels observed in patients with KDIGO stage 2–3 compared with stage 1 (Fig. 2).

Timing of HDAC3 measurement and AKI onset

Among patients who developed SA-AKI, the time from ICU admission to the first KDIGO-defined AKI diagnosis was approximately 39 h. Because serum HDAC3 was measured within 6 h after ICU admission, HDAC3 assessment preceded the clinical diagnosis of AKI by approximately 33 h, indicating its potential utility as an early biomarker.

Multivariable logistic regression analysis for predicting AKI in sepsis patients

A multivariable binary logistic regression analysis (forward likelihood ratio method) was conducted to identify independent risk factors associated with the development of AKI in patients with sepsis. The variables included in the analysis were SOFA score, APACHE II score, VIS score, septic shock, white blood cell count, CRP, PCT, and serum HDAC3 levels. All variables included met the criterion of P < 0.05 in univariable analysis.

The results indicated that the SOFA score (OR = 1.613, 95% CI: 1.169–2.224), CRP (OR = 1.032, 95% CI: 1.009–1.055), PCT (OR = 1.108, 95% CI: 1.024-1.200), and serum HDAC3 levels (OR = 1.193, 95% CI: 1.087–1.310) were independent risk factors for AKI occurrence in septic patients (P < 0.05, Table 3). The variance inflation factors (VIFs) for each variable ranged from 1.005 to 1.092, indicating no significant multicollinearity. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test yielded a P-value of 0.437, suggesting that the model had good fit. The model’s area under the curve (AUC) was 0.869 (95% CI: 0.796–0.942), demonstrating good predictive accuracy.

Predictive value of serum HDAC3 levels, SOFA score, CRP, and PCT for SA-AKI

ROC curves were constructed using the DeLong method to evaluate the discriminatory ability of each indicator. The results showed that the AUC values for HDAC3, SOFA score, CRP, and PCT were 0.820 (95% CI: 0.732–0.908), 0.679 (95% CI: 0.569–0.789), 0.677 (95% CI: 0.566–0.788), and 0.610 (95% CI: 0.491–0.729), respectively. Using the maximum Youden index (0.550), the optimal cutoff value for serum HDAC3 was 42.15 ng/mL, with a sensitivity of 80% and a specificity of 75% ,a positive predictive value (PPV) of 80.0% (95% CI: 67.0-88.8%), negative predictive value (NPV) of 75.0% (95% CI: 59.8–85.8%), positive likelihood ratio (LR+) of 3.20, and negative likelihood ratio (LR−) of 0.27 (P < 0.05, Fig. 3; Table 4).

The DeLong comparison revealed that the AUC for HDAC3 was significantly higher than that for PCT (ΔAUC = 0.210, Bonferroni-corrected P = 0.006).

ROC curve analysis of serum HDAC3 levels in stratified AKI severity

To evaluate the discriminatory capacity of serum HDAC3 levels across varying AKI severities, the KDIGO classification was employed, categorizing KDIGO stage 1 as mild AKI and KDIGO stages 2–3 as severe AKI. ROC curve analysis based on this classification showed that the AUC for serum HDAC3 was 0.828 (SE = 0.0598, 95% CI: 0.694–0.920), with an optimal diagnostic threshold of 42.7 ng/mL, a Youden index of 0.5833, sensitivity of 83.33%, and specificity of 75.00% (P < 0.001, Fig. 4). These findings indicate that serum HDAC3 alone demonstrates good discriminatory ability for stratifying AKI severity between mild and severe stages.

Correlation analysis between HDAC3 gene expression levels and prognosis in sepsis patients

A total of 90 sepsis patients were followed for 28 days, among whom 72 (80%) survived and 18 (20%) died. Patients were categorized into a low-risk group (n = 40) and a high-risk group (n = 50) based on the HDAC3 cutoff value of 42.15 ng/mL. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that patients in the high-HDAC3 group had significantly worse 28-day survival compared with those in the low-HDAC3 group, based on the ROC-derived cutoff value (log-rank χ² = 4.935, P < 0.05; Fig. 5).

During the 7-day follow-up period, renal replacement therapy (RRT) was initiated in 38 patients in the AKI group, compared with 3 patients in the non-AKI group. Owing to the limited sample size and event numbers, no formal regression analysis was performed for RRT; therefore, these findings are presented descriptively.

Discussion

Sepsis is a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by an imbalanced response to infection, with the core pathological feature being excessive activation of the systemic inflammatory response, which may result in multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) [19]. The kidneys are among the most commonly affected organs, and SA-AKI is closely linked to significantly increased-mortality rates [20]. However, existing clinical indicators remain limited in the early identification of SA-AKI [21]. Therefore, investigating novel biomarkers related to key pathological processes in SA-AKI is of practical necessity. Identifying new, sensitive, and specific biomarkers is crucial for improving patient prognosis. Through prospective clinical data analysis, this study explored the role of HDAC3 in SA-AKI and its potential as an early diagnostic biomarker.

In this study, 90 sepsis patients were enrolled. The results demonstrated that, compared to the non-AKI group, the SA-AKI group had significantly elevated white blood cell count, CRP, PCT, SOFA score, APACHE II score, and VIS score, along with a higher incidence of septic shock. These findings suggest that patients with SA-AKI exhibit a more pronounced systemic inflammatory response and greater disease severity. Further analysis revealed that serum HDAC3 levels were significantly higher in the AKI group than in the non-AKI group. Although serum HDAC3 levels showed an overall increasing trend across KDIGO stages, as supported by the Jonckheere–Terpstra trend analysis (J–T = 3.895, P < 0.001; Fig. 2), pairwise comparisons indicated no statistically significant difference between AKI stage 1 and stage 2 (P = 0.122). The observed trend was therefore primarily driven by higher HDAC3 levels in stage 3, suggesting that HDAC3 elevation is more closely associated with advanced AKI severity rather than reflecting a uniform increase across all stages.

HDAC3, a member of the class I histone deacetylase family, is an epigenetic regulator that has been implicated in the modulation of inflammatory and stress-related pathways. Accumulating experimental evidence suggests that aberrant HDAC3 expression is associated with inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, and cell death processes in various disease models [17, 25, 26]. Previous mechanistic studies have reported that HDAC3 may enhance pro-inflammatory cytokine production (e.g., TNF-α and IL-6) through NF-κB deacetylation, contribute to tubular epithelial injury via RIPK1-mediated necroptosis, and promote oxidative stress by suppressing GPX4 activity [22–26] Although these downstream pathways were not directly examined in the present study, such mechanisms may partly explain the observed association between elevated serum HDAC3 levels and increased AKI severity in septic patients.

In multivariable logistic regression analysis, HDAC3, SOFA score, CRP, and PCT were identified as independent risk factors for SA-AKI (P < 0.05), with HDAC3 showing the highest odds ratio. ROC analysis indicated that HDAC3 achieved an AUC of 0.820 (95% CI: 0.732–0.908) for predicting SA-AKI, which was higher than those of SOFA, CRP, and PCT in this cohort. Stratified analysis based on KDIGO staging revealed an AUC of 0.828 (95% CI: 0.694–0.920) for distinguishing mild (stage 1) from severe (stages 2–3) AKI. Collectively, these findings suggest that serum HDAC3 is associated with SA-AKI occurrence and severity, supporting its potential value for risk-associated stratification. Furthermore, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated that higher HDAC3 levels were significantly associated with reduced 28-day survival (χ² = 4.935, P < 0.05).

From a biological perspective, HDAC3 may regulate the transcription of inflammatory genes, apoptotic signaling, and oxidative stress responses through epigenetic mechanisms during sepsis-related inflammation, which have been implicated in tubular epithelial injury in experimental models [27, 28]. This mechanism provides theoretical support for its potential application as an early clinical indicator. Compared with certain urinary biomarkers (such as uNGAL, uEV-CD35, miR-452) reported in previous studies [8], HDAC3 is directly involved in key inflammatory regulatory pathways, and its detection by ELISA is simple, cost-effective, and easily implementable, supporting its potential relevance as a clinically accessible biomarker.

In the context of existing SA-AKI biomarkers, several serum and urinary markers such as NGAL, KIM-1, and IL-18 have been widely investigated. Previous studies have reported AUC values ranging from approximately 0.70 to 0.85 for these biomarkers, depending on patient populations, sampling time points, and assay methods. The AUC of serum HDAC3 observed in the present study (0.820) falls within the upper range reported for established biomarkers. Importantly, HDAC3 measurement is based on a simple serum ELISA assay obtained early after ICU admission, which may offer practical advantages in clinical settings. However, direct head-to-head comparisons were not performed, and such comparisons warrant further investigation in future studies.

Although serum HDAC3 levels were associated with SA-AKI occurrence and severity in the present study, correlations between HDAC3 and other clinical indicators of inflammation or disease severity (such as CRP, PCT, or SOFA score) were not specifically analyzed. Therefore, the proposed mechanistic links are primarily supported by existing experimental evidence rather than direct clinical correlation, and should be further validated in future translational studies.

Progression to renal replacement therapy represents a clinically meaningful endpoint in SA-AKI. In the present cohort, initiation of RRT occurred predominantly in patients who developed AKI, suggesting that elevated risk markers may be associated with more severe renal trajectories. However, due to the limited number of events and the exclusion of patients requiring dialysis at enrollment, robust inferential analyses were not feasible, and this observation should be interpreted as exploratory.

Nevertheless, several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center prospective study with a relatively small sample size (n = 90), which may limit statistical power and generalizability. Although post-hoc analysis suggested an approximate power of 0.75, smaller subgroup sizes—particularly in KDIGO stage 2–3—may increase the risk of false-negative findings. Second, clinical heterogeneity among sepsis patients, including unstratified infection sources and a wide range of disease severity as reflected by SOFA score variability, may introduce residual confounding and potentially influence effect estimates. Third, while multivariable adjustment was performed, unmeasured confounders cannot be fully excluded. Fourth, serum HDAC3 was measured at a single early time point after ICU admission, and dynamic changes during the disease course were not assessed. Fifth, HDAC3 sampling occurred within a relatively broad 6–24-hour window after ICU admission; time-of-sampling variability during early sepsis may introduce measurement-related heterogeneity. Sixth, although urine output criteria were included in KDIGO-based AKI adjudication, we did not perform stratified analyses by early oliguria status to assess the incremental value of HDAC3 beyond oliguria alone. Finally, although RRT is a clinically important endpoint, we could not perform a robust RRT-focused analysis in this cohort, which warrants evaluation in larger studies.

Importantly, the present findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating. Future multi-center studies with larger sample sizes and stratified analyses based on infection source (e.g., pneumonia versus abdominal sepsis) are warranted to validate these results, reduce heterogeneity, and improve clinical applicability. Longitudinal assessment of HDAC3 dynamics and integration with established biomarkers may further enhance risk prediction and mechanistic understanding of SA-AKI.

Conclusion

This study demonstrates that serum HDAC3 levels are associated with the occurrence, severity, and prognosis of sepsis-associated acute kidney injury. HDAC3 demonstrated acceptable discriminative performance for early SA-AKI risk assessment and AKI severity stratification within this cohort. However, given the limited sample size and single-center design, these findings should be interpreted with caution. Further large-scale, multi-center studies are required to validate the clinical utility of HDAC3 and to determine its incremental value beyond established indicators.

Does neoadjuvant chemotherapy reduce surgical complexity in patients with advanced-stage epithelial ovarian cancer?
Authors: Okan Aytekin, Simge Kirmizigul Kerinc, Abdurrahman Alp Tokalioglu, Yesim Ozkaya Ucar, Fatih Kilic, Gunsu Kimyon Comert, Gokhan Ucar, Burak Civelek, Taner Turan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to determine the effect of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) on the complex surgical procedures required in addition to staging surgery for the need to achieve a residual tumor 1 cm or less in a population of stage IIIC–IV epithelial ovarian cancer patients.MethodsPatients were referred for NACT if preoperative imaging and/or intraoperative evaluation confirmed that it was not possible to achieve a residual tumor size of 1 cm or less with cytoreductive surgery or if the patient had a poor performance status and a high American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score. Introduction

The lifetime risk of developing ovarian cancer is 1.3%, which means that out of every 78 women, one is expected to suffer this type of cancer. Approximately 90% of ovarian malignancies are classified as epithelial ovarian cancers, with serous ovarian carcinomas accounting for 52% of these cases [1]. Approximately 51% of serous carcinomas are diagnosed at stage III, while 29% are diagnosed at stage IV. Due to the prevalence of ovarian cancer being diagnosed at advanced stages, its mortality rates are unfortunately higher than those of other gynecological malignancies [2].

Through years of research, we have learned that the most effective way to ensure long-term survival without the disease is to use a combination of chemotherapy and surgery. Primary cytoreductive surgery or interval cytoreductive surgery performed after neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) can lead to improved oncological outcomes if there is no residual disease or if the residual disease measures less than 1 cm [3]. To attain this objective, it may be necessary to perform surgical procedures on the upper abdomen and remove solid organs [4, 5]. Randomized controlled studies show that NACT allows for fewer organ resections and fewer surgical complications [6]. As a result, it leads to reduced surgical complications.

The aim of this study was to determine the effect of NACT on the complex surgical procedures required in addition to staging surgery for the need to achieve a residual tumor 1 cm or less than in a population of stage IIIC–IV epithelial ovarian cancer patients.

Materials and methods

Patients

The study was designed as a retrospective cohort study. Patients with stage IIIC–IV epithelial ovarian cancer, according to the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO), who underwent primary cytoreduction or interval cytoreduction after NACT at our clinic between September 2019 and August 2023 were included in the study. The clinical, surgical, and pathological data of the patients were obtained from the electronic database system of the gynecologic oncology department, as well as from patients’ files and pathological reports. Patients with non-epithelial ovarian tumors or FIGO stage I–IIIB and individuals who underwent suboptimal cytoreductive surgery (a residual tumor size larger than 1 cm) were excluded from the study. Patients for whom an interval surgery after the NACT decision could not be made or who could not continue the operation because they did not respond to chemotherapy during the interval surgery were excluded from the study. The institutional ethical committee evaluated and approved the research procedure in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (IRB: E2-23-3387/ 15.02.2023) and this manuscript conformed with the Enhancing the QUAlity and Transparency Of health Research (EQUATOR) network guidelines.

Patients were referred for NACT if preoperative imaging and/or intraoperative evaluation confirmed that it was not possible to achieve a residual tumor size of 1 cm or less with cytoreductive surgery or if the patient had a poor performance status and a high American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) score. The NACT decision, the number of cycles, and the choice of chemotherapy protocol were determined during the gynecological oncology councils. All patients received the same regimen of NACT: a platinum-based plus paclitaxel regimen. The number of cycles was determined based on the response to NACT.

Surgery

All patients underwent total hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, retroperitoneal lymphadenectomy, total omentectomy, and peritoneal cytological sampling. This surgical procedure was defined as “standard surgery.” If tumors were found in other abdominal/extra-abdominal organs or peritoneal surfaces during surgery, cytoreductive procedures were performed along with staging operations to achieve a residual tumor size of 1 cm or less. Advanced interventions were included in addition to standard surgical procedures, such as low-rectal resection, small bowel resection, colon resection, splenectomy, peritoneal stripping and/or full-thickness resection of the diaphragm, distal pancreatectomy, hepatic metastasectomy, cardiophrenic lymph node resection, appendectomy, and peritonectomy. Patients with serosal involvement and the potential to avoid organ resection did not undergo this procedure.

Surgical complexity was defined as complex or non-complex. Total hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, retroperitoneal lymphadenectomy, total omentectomy, appendectomy, and peritonectomy were defined as “non-complex surgical procedures.” Additional procedures to these surgical procedures (low-rectal resection, colon resection, small bowel resection, splenectomy, hepatic metastasectomy, diaphragmatic peritoneal stripping, full-thickness diaphragm resection, distal pancreatectomy, and cardiophrenic lymph node resection) were defined as “complex surgical procedures.” If at least one of these complex surgical procedures was performed, the patient was evaluated in the complex surgery group. Low-rectal resection was categorized as colon resection. Total or partial peritonectomy procedures performed in cases of tumors on the peritoneal surfaces were defined as peritonectomy. Diaphragmatic peritoneal stripping was categorized as a peritonectomy. No residual disease following surgery was defined as “maximal cytoreduction,” 1 cm or less of the macroscopic residual tumor was defined as “optimal cytoreduction,” and more than 1 cm of macroscopic residual tumor was defined as “suboptimal cytoreduction.” All surgical procedures, including complex surgical procedures, were performed by the same expert gynecological oncology team.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analyses were conducted using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (IBM SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) version 22.0 for Windows. The descriptive statistics were expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (min-max) for continuous variables, and as the number/percentage for categorical variables. The categorical parameters were evaluated using a chi-square test. The statistical significance level was set at p < 0.05.

Results

One hundred twenty-six patients with stage IIIC–IV ovarian cancer were included in the study. Primary cytoreductive surgery was performed in 67 patients, and interval cytoreductive surgery was performed in 59 patients after NACT. The mean age of the patients was 59.7 ± 11.16 years, with a range of 34 to 82 years. The mean serum CA 125 level at the time of initial diagnosis was 1484 ± 2132.10 IU/mL (range, 6–10,551 IU/mL). The most common histological type was high-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC) (n = 118; 93.7%). Maximal cytoreductive surgery was performed in 121 (96%) patients, and optimal cytoreductive surgery was performed in 5 (4%) patients. A total of 86 (68.3%) patients had complex surgical procedures, while 40 (31.7%) patients had non-complex surgical procedures. In addition to standard surgical techniques (total hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, retroperitoneal lymphadenectomy, and total omentectomy), the most commonly performed procedures were peritonectomy (n = 87; 69%), diaphragmatic peritoneal stripping (n = 64; 50.8%), and appendectomy (n = 48; 38.1%). The most common extra-abdominal additional surgical technique, cardiophrenic lymph node resection, was performed on 34 (27%) patients. Discussion

This investigation evaluates the effects of NACT on complex surgical procedures that are necessary for obtaining residual tumors of 1 cm or less in a population of patients with stage IIIC–IV epithelial ovarian cancer. At least one complex surgery was performed in 74.6% of patients in the primary cytoreductive surgery group and in 61% of patients in the NACT group, and there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups. However, the NACT group showed significantly reduced rates of low-rectal resection, diaphragmatic peritoneal stripping, and peritonectomy.

In their study of advanced-stage ovarian cancer, Aletti et al. defined a score for surgical complexity and grouped patients according to this score [7]. This scoring system assigns arbitrary values to surgical procedures and defines the complexity of patients accordingly. In contrast to Aletti et al.’s scoring system, our definition of surgical complexity designated procedures requiring advanced surgical experience other than total hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, retroperitoneal lymphadenectomy, total omentectomy, appendectomy, and peritonectomy as complex surgical procedures. We also designated distal pancreatectomy and cardiophrenic lymph node resection as complex surgical procedures.

In the present study, complex surgery was compared in the patient group that underwent NACT and primary cytoreductive surgery. There was no statistically significant difference between the two groups. In a study conducted by Rosen et al. on 326 stage IIIC–IV ovarian cancer patients, a large bowel resection, diaphragm resection, extensive peritonectomy, liver resection, or splenectomy was defined as aggressive surgery. The incidence of aggressive surgery was significantly lower in the group that received NACT, which contrasts with the findings of our study [8]. The surgical procedures performed in Rosen et al.’s study were not described in detail. The inclusion of extended peritonectomy in the aggressive surgical group may have caused this discrepancy. By contrast, peritonectomy was not defined as a complex surgery in our study.

Typically, patients diagnosed with ovarian carcinoma often have a form of disease that has spread extensively inside the pelvic region. This includes the nearby reproductive organs and the sigmoid colon, which may be affected by direct extension [9]. Peritoneal disease often occurs when tumor cells are released into the peritoneal fluid [10]. For these reasons, peritonectomy, low-rectal resection, and diaphragmatic peritoneal stripping operations are often required for cytoreduction in the treatment of peritoneal disease [11]. In the present study, we observed that the low rates of low-rectal resection, diaphragmatic peritoneal stripping, and peritonectomy were significantly reduced in the NACT group. Based on this background, we can conclude that NACT has a positive effect on peritoneal disease. This outcome could potentially explain the impact of interval cytoreduction surgery and hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) performed after NACT on survival, as documented by Van Driel et al. [12]. HIPEC has demonstrated efficacy in treating residual peritoneal disease, leading to improved oncologic outcomes. The positive effect of NACT on peritoneal disease thus increases the success of HIPEC.

Rauh Hain et al. [13] study on advanced-stage ovarian cancer defined splenectomy, pancreatectomy, gallbladder resection, liver resection, and diaphragmatic resection procedures as upper abdominal surgical procedures. In that study, no statistically significant difference was found in the need for upper abdominal surgery in the NACT and primary cytoreductive surgery groups. Similarly, we observed that NACT did not show a reduction in upper abdominal and extra-abdominal surgical procedures such as splenectomy, hepatic metastasectomy, full-thickness resection of the diaphragm, distal pancreatectomy, and cardiophrenic lymph node resection.

In the present study, hepatic metastasectomy was performed in 27.8% of the patients due to parenchymal or subcapsular hepatic metastases, including 26.9% of the primary cytoreduction group and 28.8% of the interval cytoreduction group, and there was no statistically significant difference between the groups. In a study on hepatobiliary surgical procedures performed in advanced-stage ovarian cancer patients, 20.9% of the patients underwent hepatobiliary resection in primary cytoreduction, similar to our study [14].

All patients in our study underwent retroperitoneal lymphadenectomy. However, the lymphadenectomy performed was not systematic. Lymphadenectomy was performed for lymph nodes that were suspicious in imaging methods and intraoperative observation. The use of systematic lymphadenectomy during surgery in patients with advanced-stage ovarian cancer is a subject of discussion. The first randomized controlled study showed that while systematic lymphadenectomy enhanced progression-free survival, it did not have a beneficial effect on overall survival [15]. Current guidelines recommend removing suspicious/high-volume lymph nodes for cytoreduction rather than performing systematic lymphadenectomy as a standard part of surgery in patients with advanced ovarian cancer [16, 17].

The main limitation of this study was its retrospective design. Furthermore, NACT decisions induced heterogeneity among patients, as some decisions depended on preoperative imaging, whereas others depended on intraoperative observation. However, this was a single-center study, and all surgical procedures were performed by the same expert gynecologic oncologist team. All patients received the same NACT regimen.

Notably, the need for at least one complex surgical procedure did not decrease in the patient group that underwent interval cytoreduction after NACT, suggesting that a NACT requirement is not an accurate indicator that a complex surgery would be avoided. However, when we examined the complex surgical procedures individually in the subgroups, we observed a significant reduction in low- rectal resection, diaphragmatic peritoneal stripping, and peritonectomy due to their effectiveness in reducing peritoneal disease. These findings confirm that interval cytoreduction may be beneficial if decisions are made to reduce the prevalence of peritoneal disease. Randomized controlled studies are needed to reach definitive conclusions on this subject.

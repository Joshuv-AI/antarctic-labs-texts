“The Kid is OK…”
Authors: Timothy Fabian
Published: 2026

Happy 10th Anniversary TSACO! You’ve come a long way in the past decade. In my inaugural Editor-in-Chief editorial in May 2016, “The world is getting smaller…,” I referred to myself as the midwife for the delivery of the new bouncing baby. However, there was a lot of anxiety surrounding the birth. Open access was a novel approach for the dissemination of medical research and education. Compared with traditional publishing, it offered two huge advantages for the transmission of knowledge: worldwide distribution and access to the publications for anyone, anywhere with access to the internet. Sounds great, but there was an important hazard noted at the birthing: authors were responsible for costs of publication (APCs)!! In many ways, taking care of a newborn is easier than dealing with childhood. Just ask Elliott.

We should look at the development pattern. In year one, there were 57 submissions and 33 publications (58% publication rate), and last year, there were 455 submissions and 212 publications (47% publication rate)—excellent growth and improving quality. The 5-year impact factor of 2.3 is quite commendable for a new journal. Our early anxieties dealing with publication costs have been partially alleviated. That thorny problem will continue to improve over time as organizational structures, including university libraries, shift budgeted funding toward open access publishing. That is the future for medical publishing.

The best thing that has happened to TSACO is turning its childhood care over to Elliott Haut and his leadership team. I knew he was a good surgeon, but I did not recognize his talent as a superb pediatrician. Elliott and his team have implemented several new initiatives that have spurred enthusiasm and expanded the scope of TSACO. Those include invited commentaries for original research articles, a new category of patient education papers, establishing organizational partnerships with 10 different groups, AAST Associate member TSACO scholarships, a manuscript review mentorship program, expansion of social media initiatives, and the continuing pursuit of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion. A list of impressive expansions, I would say.

I am thankful for AAST giving me the opportunity to be involved in the development of TSACO. It is an important educational and research endeavor. (Elliott, remember that adolescence is always challenging, but the kid is ok.)

Disease course, comorbidities, and treatment outcomes of lupus erythematosus panniculitis in adults and children: A retrospective review
Authors: Bianca Obiakor, Brook Abegaze, Alina S. Feng, Anna Haemel
Published: 2026

To the Editor: Lupus erythematosus panniculitis (LEP) is a rare subtype characterized by nodules or plaques in adipose-rich regions of the body, causing permanent scarring, lipoatrophy, and disfigurement.1 Diagnosis is challenging due to nonspecific subcutaneous inflammation.2,3 Moreover, management is complicated by a chronic, relapsing course and lack of standardized treatment approaches.1 Existing literature is largely limited to small case series and 1 heterogenous United States cohort in the Northeast.1,3,4 Here, we report the clinical characteristics, comorbidities, and treatment outcomes of LEP patients at a single tertiary center.

This retrospective study was approved by the University of California, San Francisco institutional review board, and patient consent was waived due to use of deidentified data. LEP patients were identified through electronic health record review of all University of California, San Francisco Dermatology and Rheumatology visits from January 2000 to August 2023 using International Classification of Diseases codes. We identified 43 cases with LEP; 34 (79.1%) were confirmed by biopsy, and 7 (16.3%) were confirmed by expert clinical opinion.

Of the 43 patients, 38 (88.4%) were female (Table I). Median age at symptom onset was 23.5 years (range, 6-61 years); 31 (72.1%) had adult (age ≥18) onset, 12 (27.9%) had pediatric onset. Lesions most often involved proximal upper (48.8%) and lower extremities (23.3%), face (34.9%), and trunk (18.6%). Complications included lipoatrophy or contour changes (58.1%), persistent change in pigmentation (51.2%), and ulceration (11.6%). Concomitant lupus subtypes included systemic lupus erythematosus (18.6%), discoid lupus erythematosus (16.3%), or both (18.6%); 46.5% had neither at time of LEP diagnosis. Antinuclear antibody was positive in 48.8% of cases at diagnosis. Among patients with available follow-up data, 1 patient had documented worsening of symptoms (increasing size or number of subcutaneous nodules and plaques).

All patients required multiple systemic therapies (mean 2.58 ± 2.07 agents and 6.47 ± 10.87 treatment courses). Antimalarials were prescribed in 97.7% of patients and systemic steroids in 48.8% of patients (Table II). Mycophenolate mofetil was the most common disease-modifying antirheumatic drug, prescribed in 12 patients (27.9%), followed by biologics in 4 patients (9.4%) and IVIG in 2 (4.7%). LEP diagnosis and systemic treatments

ANA, Antinuclear antibody; DMARD, disease-modifying antirheumatic drug; IVIG, intravenous immunoglobulin; LEP, lupus erythematosus panniculitis; MMF, mycophenolate mofetil; MTX, methotrexate.

Among pediatric-onset patients, 6 had SLE, 3 had DLE, and 1 had both SLE and DLE. Among adult-onset patients, 9 had SLE, 1 had DLE, and 5 had both SLE and DLE. Of the total cohort, 14 patients had SLE/DLE preceding LEP diagnosis, 4 were diagnosed concurrently, and 4 developed SLE/DLE following LEP onset.

Other includes the following conditions: C1q deficiency, undifferentiated connective tissue disease, morphea, vitiligo, Jaccoud’s arthropathy, and alopecia areata.

A total of 39 patients had adequate available treatment response data.

These findings represent one of the larger single-center cohorts of LEP patients reported to date. Similar to a prior study’s 19.2-month diagnostic delay, our observed delay of 27.8 months may contribute to more advanced lipoatrophy and disease damage at presentation.1 Incomplete treatment response and lack of remission necessitated multiple agents in all patients.

Three patients (2 pediatric, 1 adult) initially suspected of having LEP were ultimately diagnosed with subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma. Given its clinical and histologic overlap with LEP but distinct management, subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma should remain high on the differential.5

Limitations of this study include its retrospective design, modest sample size, and incomplete prereferral records, which partially reduced access to initial diagnostic work-up. Given the diagnostic and treatment challenges associated with LEP and the cumulative damage that can result, further study will be essential to establish management algorithms for this condition.

Clinical question: Is systemic inflammation associated with right ventricular myocardial work efficiency, quality of life, and adiposity in adults with repaired tetralogy of Fallot?
Authors: Nael Aldweib, Lidija McGrath, Abigail Khan, Hidemi Kajimoto, Brent Williams, Jenna Li, Craig Broberg
Published: 2026

To the Editor:

Main Finding: Elevated hsCRP was independently associated with lower RV myocardial work efficiency and greater visceral adiposity, supporting a potential link between inflammation, adiposity, and RV energetic dysfunction.

Heart failure remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality among adults with repaired tetralogy of Fallot (rTOF). Although residual hemodynamic lesions, chronic pulmonary regurgitation, ventricular fibrosis, and electromechanical dyssynchrony have been implicated in the pathogenesis of right ventricular (RV) dysfunction, the mechanisms underlying progressive RV failure remain incompletely understood [1,2]. Emerging evidence suggests that chronic low-grade inflammation may contribute to adverse ventricular remodeling and impaired myocardial energetics [3]. We therefore sought to evaluate the relationship between systemic inflammation, adiposity, RV myocardial work indices, and patient-reported health status in adults with rTOF.

Twenty adults with repaired TOF enrolled in a prospective observational study underwent assessment of high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP), RV myocardial work indices derived from pressure-strain analysis, Kansas City Cardiomyopathy Questionnaire (KCCQ-12) scores, adiposity measurements, and circulating inflammatory biomarkers. Participants were stratified according to hsCRP <3 mg/L versus ≥3 mg/L. Visceral adiposity was quantified from axial abdominal images obtained during magnetic resonance imaging. Multivariable linear regression models adjusted for age, sex, and body mass index (BMI), with a second model incorporating adiposity variables selected using Akaike information criterion procedures.

The study population had a mean age of 40.4 ± 11.7 years, and 60% were male. There were no statistically significant differences in age, sex, or BMI between groups, although participants with elevated hsCRP tended to have higher BMI and greater adiposity. Patients with hsCRP ≥3 mg/L demonstrated significantly greater visceral adiposity compared with those with hsCRP <3 mg/L (16.0 ± 6.5 vs 9.2 ± 6.5 cm, p = 0.032), whereas subcutaneous adiposity showed a similar but nonsignificant trend (23.5 ± 9.4 vs 15.3 ± 9.3 cm, p = 0.069).

Elevated inflammatory burden was associated with marked impairment in RV myocardial performance. Participants with hsCRP ≥3 mg/L had significantly lower RV myocardial work efficiency than those with hsCRP <3 mg/L (86.7 ± 5.2% vs 92.8 ± 3.5%, p = 0.009). In contrast, RV wasted myocardial work did not differ significantly between groups.

In multivariable analyses adjusting for age, sex, and BMI, hsCRP ≥3 mg/L remained independently associated with lower RV myocardial work efficiency (β = −5.69%; 95% CI -9.34 to −2.04). This association remained robust after incorporation of adiposity variables selected through stepwise model selection (β = −5.86%; 95% CI -9.22 to −2.50).

Several secondary findings demonstrated consistent directional associations supporting an adverse inflammatory phenotype. Participants with elevated hsCRP had KCCQ-12 scores that were 12.4 points lower after adjustment (95% CI -28.3 to 3.5), a magnitude generally considered clinically meaningful. Similarly, elevated hsCRP was associated with higher IL-6 concentrations (β = 3.87; 95% CI -0.13 to 7.87) and lower IL-1β concentrations (β = −5.48; 95% CI -12.82 to 1.85). Although confidence intervals crossed the null value, the magnitude and consistency of these associations support biologic plausibility and suggest that limited statistical power may have contributed to the absence of conventional significance thresholds.

The present study identifies a novel relationship between systemic inflammation and impaired RV myocardial work efficiency in adults with rTOF. Importantly, this association persisted after adjustment for demographic and adiposity-related variables, suggesting that inflammation may contribute to RV dysfunction independent of traditional risk factors. The approximately 6% reduction in RV myocardial work efficiency observed among participants with elevated hsCRP represents a substantial decrement in myocardial energetic performance.

Notably, participants with elevated hsCRP also exhibited significantly greater visceral adiposity. Visceral adipose tissue is increasingly recognized as a metabolically active endocrine organ capable of producing pro-inflammatory cytokines that contribute to systemic inflammation and cardiovascular dysfunction. The coexistence of elevated hsCRP, increased visceral adiposity, impaired RV myocardial efficiency, and worse health status supports a conceptual framework linking adiposity-driven inflammation to progressive RV dysfunction in repaired TOF. Given the growing availability of anti-inflammatory and metabolic therapies targeting adipose tissue inflammation, these observations raise the possibility that inflammatory pathways may represent modifiable therapeutic targets in this population [4].

The principal limitations of this study include its modest sample size and cross-sectional design, which preclude causal inference. Nevertheless, the consistency of the observed associations across multiple domains, including myocardial energetics, adiposity, biomarkers, and patient-reported outcomes, supports further investigation in larger longitudinal cohorts.

In conclusion, elevated hsCRP is independently associated with reduced RV myocardial work efficiency and greater visceral adiposity in adults with repaired TOF. These findings provide preliminary evidence linking chronic low-grade inflammation to RV energetic dysfunction and support future studies evaluating inflammatory and metabolic pathways as potential therapeutic targets in congenital heart disease (See Table 1).

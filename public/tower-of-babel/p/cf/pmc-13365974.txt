IFPHOR0040 “Less Weight Should Be Given to Weight”: Weight Framing, Lived Experience, and Professional Perspectives in Local Healthcare
Authors: Chloe Singleton
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWeight-focused policies, language, and practice are conceptualised as the ‘weight centred health paradigm’ (WCHP), equating weight with health and perpetuating stigma, blame and exclusion experienced by those beyond ‘healthy’ BMIs. Alternative approaches propose a ‘weight-inclusive health paradigm’ (WIHP), advocating salutogenic, compassionate healthcare. The present research aims to inform inclusive practice by using these paradigms as a lens to explore the personal framing, preferred language, and experiences of professionals and people who have lived experience of weight management (PWLE) in the East Riding of Yorkshire.MethodSemi-structured interviews were used to explore participants’ perceptions of healthy weight, preferred language, and experiences of weight framing within the health system.Results4 PWLE and 10 professionals rejected weight-centric processes and practices as harmful, associating BMI-based metrics and language with stigma and exclusion. Negative repercussions for mental health were a key theme amongst PWLE. Both groups favoured a shift toward compassionate, person-centred and holistic definitions of health embedded within practice, viewing changes to language as instrumental to achieving this. PWLE framed this as a systemic transformation, whilst professionals proposed embedding inclusivity within current practices.DiscussionParticipant accounts illustrate a health system and society characterised by the WCHP. A shift towards the WIHP through using consistent, compassionate language and approaches was framed as essential to destigmatising experiences and overcoming current barriers to healthcare. Recommendations of this research include co-producing a lexicon, revising BMI-based policies, and advocating person-centred care. These are being implemented locally through the East Riding of Yorkshire’s own ‘More Than Weight’ initiative.

Design, click-based synthesis, molecular docking, molecular dynamics and anticancer activity of new isoindoline-1,3-dione- triazole-glycopyranosyl hybrids
Authors: Mohamed N El-Bayaa, Asmaa L Alanzy, Fahad M Alminderej, May A El-Manawaty, Sabri Messaoudi, Wael A El-Sayed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe application of simulated research approaches for the discovery and development of novel compounds as promising cancer drug candidates have still garnered widespread attention as an important goal in medicinal chemistry. In such context, a novel derived series of modified glycosides based on an isoindole-1,2,3-triazole-1,4-disubstituted-aryl hybrid system were synthesized from the terminal acetylenic compound (prepared from propargylation of 2-(4-hydroxyphenyl)isoindoline-1,3-dione (1)) and different glycosyl azides via click-based strategy. Furthermore, the afforded acetylated Iso-idolinedione-N1-glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole products were diacetylated to give the derived glycosyl-triazoles with deacetylated hydroxyls. On the other hand, the C-glycosyl analogues were also prepared from the (azidoethoxy)phenyl)isoindoline-1,3-dione followed by deprotection process. The cytotoxicity behaviour of resulted isoindoline-dione based 1,2,3-triazole glycosides was investigated towards the human cancer cells: human breast (PC3), colorectal (HCT-116), and hepatic (HepG-2) lines. The influence of attachment of the sugar part on the cytotoxicity results was obvious and the type of the attached sugar moiety where the glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles with the xylopyranosyl moiety (compounds 6, 9 and 16) and galactopyranosyl (compound 8) were the most active. The latter accounted for the specific effect of the type of the sugar moiety. Docking investigation into epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) indicated potential interacting binding behaviours and mechanisms of inhibition. Molecular dynamics investigation revealed the possibility of attaining a stable binding mode of the glycoside 8 into EGFR’s site as well as the structural integrity of the catalytic domain. The electronic structure, molecular orbitals, and electrostatic potential of compound 8 were analysed by Density functional theory (DFT) calculations. HOMO-LUMO analysis demonstrated the electron-donating and electron-accepting potency, supporting the anticancer potential and aligning with the docking and dynamics results.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-52999-2.Keywords: Glycosides; 1,2,3-triazoles; Click chemistry; Cancer; Cytotoxicity; Isoindole; Molecular dynamicsSubject terms: Biochemistry, Cancer, Chemical biology, Chemistry, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Drug discovery

Introduction

Cancer has remained one of the most significant threats to human health in recent decades, and combating this terrifying disease has become one of the most important challenges facing researchers around the world. Although chemotherapeutic agents have achieved significant clinical success, their prolonged efficacy is often affected by limitations such as dose-limiting toxicity and off-target side effects1,2. The latter rising restrictions prompt the necessary for developing novel designed therapeutic agents. To overcome the limitations of traditional mono-target therapeutic structures, molecular hybridization3 has emerged as a powerful and rational approach in drug in drug design. Such approach results in prudent incorporation of two or more pharmacophoric motifs with known bioactivities or integral physicochemical behaviors into a single consolidated structural framework aiming for potentially enhanced structures, improved pharmacokinetic profiles, toxicity selectivity on cancer cells, and improved multi-target selectivity. One of the diverse potent pharmacophores, is 1,2,3-triazole, a motif known with divers’ pharmacological properties, particularly anticancer activity. Substituted triazoles have protruded as interesting prop with a wide range of bioactivities4–6 in medicinal chemistry. Such privileged core, created by the click-reaction, acquired considerable interest in designing novel bioactive structures. The latter consequence is attributable to 1,2,3-triazole ring intense dipole moment and capability for intriguing H-bonding forms, allowing its enhanced opportunity to achieve efficient interactions with biomolecular targets7. Furthermore, such pharmacophoric core has been revealed with its resistance to metabolic degeneration, and its structural incorporation with may help to enhance solubility and bioavailability of products and thereby enhancing overall pharmacophoric benefit8.

Isoindoline-1,3-dione (phthalimide) scaffold is a primary structural center in lots of acceptable compounds in medicinal chemistry9–14. It has been employed in developing new drugs with multitarget results such as antitumor15–18, xanthine oxidase inhibitors, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors19, antioxidants18, androgen receptor antagonists20, anti-convulsant21,22, antiviral activities23, antimicrobial24, hypoglycemic25,26 antihyperlipidemic25,27, anti-inflammatory28,29, anti-Alzheimer30,31 and antileishmanial activities32. Isoindoline-1,3-diones I, III, V (Fig. 1) exert selective cytotoxicity against beast MCF-7, liver HepG-2, lung A-549 carcinoma through EGFR inhibition33.

A plenty of pharmacophores enriched by sugar part by glycosidic attachment in glycosidic segments were produced revealing colossal antitumor potency34–36, in particular through via EGFR inhibition pathway37–39. A variety of heterocyclic glycosides or their acyclic isosteric structures have displayed potent cytotoxicity against human cancer cells40,41. 1,2,3-Triazole compounds incorporating sugar moieties represent one of the most potent derivatives with anticancer activity with possible mechanistic approaches41–47. On the other hand, compounds with iso-indole and related isosteric analogues as core structure incorporating sugars were reported with various bioactivities34,48,49, particularly anticancer action. Many approaches for synthesizing bio-active drug-like molecules revolves around decorating carbohydrate units with privileged bio-active structural motifs to synthesize glycohybrids with promoted water solubility, diminished toxicity, ameliorated pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamic characteristics, increased variance, molecular recognition, intracellular functions and boosted bioavailability50–53. 1,2,3-Triazole.

As an important ErbB receptor tyrosine kinase, the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) expression is increased by divers malignancies, such as breast cancer and head and neck squamous cell carcinoma,. In addition, EGFR overexpression arises in about 50% of patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)54,55. Accordingly, EGFR is revealed as a compelling target for anticancer treatment, resulting in the development of a variety of EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)56–58. They bind in the tyrosine kinase domain as a competitive inhibition of adenosine triphosphate (ATP). The scaffold comprising phthalimide and 1,2,3-triazole hybrid moieties (I, Fig. 1, IC50: 0.22 µM) presented, particularly, potent antiproliferative behavior against MCF-7 cells. In addition, it showed effective EGFR inhibition (IC50: 79 nM) exceeding erlotinib value. Furthermore, it spurred MCF-7 cells to afford apoptosis, cell cycle arrest in the S/pre-G1 phase, and DNA fragmentation. Efficient hydrogen bonding interacting modes (by triazole nitrogen atoms) have been revealed in the docking simulations of such compound comparable to that observed for reference erlotinib indicating the importance of the 1,2,3-triazole motif in blocking EGFR34.

As an important, extremely structurally related, iso-steric analogue of iso-indole, substituted indoles acquired considerable interest due to their role as potential pharmacophores. The indole-glycosyl hybrid XII with deprotected glucose moiety showed anti-leukemia properties (IC50: 1.3 µM)59,60 while the C-glycosyl-indole XIII possessed high potency against MCF-7 (breast cancer, micromolar range)52. The indole-1,2,3-triazole-glycosyl hybrid VIII revealed potent anti-oncogenic properties against MCF-7 and MDA-MB231 breast cancer cell line with (IC50: 1.05 and 11.40 µM, respectively), revealing (distinguished SI > 161). The latter structures provided better binding affinity as compared to co-crystallized inhibitor 1-(tert-butyl)-3-(p-tolyl)-1H-pyrazolo[3,4-d]pyrimidin-4-amine (PP1) with HCK (PTKs) proteins in molecular docking studies61. Indolyl derivative X, hybrids with 1,3,4-thiadiazole-glycoside-based system revealed excellent and more potent cytotoxicity against MCF-7 and HCT-116 cancerous cell lines with potential inhibitory activity against EGFR62,63. The isoindolidine based thiadiazol system linked substituted by hydrazinyl xylose sugar IX, as a glycosyl analogue with modified sugar moiety, displayed activity against PC-3 (IC50: 27.71 ± 5.60 µM) than its isosteric six-carbon sugar analogue revealing the influence of conformation. In addition, promising energy scores have been displayed by docking into epidermal growth factor (EGFR) accounting for the enhanced affinity and interactions modes into such target site. Moreover, the azole based isoindole glycoside analogue IX remained in the EGFR binding cavity over the 100 ns simulation with different orientations as revealed by molecular dynamics (MD) simulation48.

Our hypothesized strategy in the present research is to build novel scaffolds of potential pharmaceutical potency, vial molecular hybridization, leading to modified structures incorporating iso-indole, 1,2,3-triazole and glycosyl moieties, each hypothesized to enhance binding affinity for key biological targets and preferably improving the pharmacokinetic profile.

Rationale and structure-based design

The key constituents of EGFR inhibiting agents, such as Erlotinib, expounded pharmacophoric features through a terminal hydrophobic head (for connecting the hydrophobic region I), a flat hetero-aromatic core (for standing in the adenine binding pocket and assisting required H-binding interactions with amino acids in the linker area), and a hydrophobic tail (for aligning into hydrophobic region)64. The hypothesized strategy for designing targeted candidates have benefited by 1,2,3-triazole and iso-indolinedione cores as key pharmacophoric scaffolds. Click chemistry approach was applied to generate 1,2,3-triazole motif either as a distinct linker or H-bonding acceptor inside the ATP-binding pocket, possessing lipophilic units at N-1 and C-4 boosting binding affinity65–68. In addition, triazole designs usually feature quinoline and quinazoline systems behaving as metabolically stable bioisosteric permutation for amide linkages. Due to the rigid, extended, structural planarity with ring-N- and -C = O forming crucial hydrogen bonding, iso-indolinedione structures can be exploited in the same binding region in addition to the possibility of enhancing DNA-binding affinity. Such motifs had been rationally incorporated to vanquish induced resistance in mutant EGFR variants, especially the defying T790M mutation, through delicate optimization of solubility-enhancing motifs and linker moieties to enhance pharmacokinetic characteristics while preserving the potency if inhibition69. On the other hand, the acetylated glycosyl part provides terminal cyclic oxygenated moieties with distinct stereochemical conformations. Additionally, glycosyl incorporation was estimated to enhance solubility and improve bioavailability, in addition to particular influence of enhancing cellular uptake and therapeutic outcomes. Furthermore, such hybridization will help to leverage the achieved bioactivities of the parent scaffolds while providing particular chemical diversity for optimizing target activities and understanding their mechanistic foundations.

Results and discussion

Chemistry

The synthesis approach of the 1,2,3-triazole glycosides was designed to get two structurally different isoindole-triazole-glycosyl hybrid compounds. Thus, N1-glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles and C4-glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole attached to isoindole system by an aryl linker were prepared. The efficiency of click 1,3‐dipolar cycloaddition strategy leading, selectively, to functionalized 1,4-disubstituted-1,2,3-triazole system prompted our interest to apply such approach to get hybrid products possessing the 1,2,3‐trizole core moiety. The latter is a key linker simulating the interaction of the synthesized products with biological targets. The required terminal acetylenic reactant for the click process was prepared by direct alkylation reaction of the starting para-aminophenol 1 using propargyl bromide in presence of K2CO3 as an appropriate basic medium. The resulting terminal alkyne product 2 were reacted with two acetylated pyranosyl-sugar azides 3a-c via applying click conditions by the aid of Cu(I) catalysis, which led to the corresponding 1,4-disubstituted-1,2,3‐triazole‐N‐glycoides, in 77–79% yields. In-situ creation of the Cu(I) reacting species was achieved by the simultaneous reduction of copper (II) sulphate pentahydrate by sodium ascorbate, in the latter process42,70. As an efficient mixed solvent system, THF/water (2:1) was used in the click reaction that led to the regioselective production of the targeted click products 4–6. The N1‐glycosyl‐1,2,3‐triazoles based isoindole system, via an aryl linker, gave 1H‐NMR spectra that revealed the assigned signals of the glycopyranosyl hydrogens; CH3C = O and remaining chain hydrogens, at their expected normal ranges. The 13C-NMR also showed the sugar carbons and the remaining atoms at their correct regions in the assigned structures and their corresponding IR spectra also presented the peaks of the acetyl-carbonyls in the sugar moieties. A conformation of the β‐N‐glycosidic conformation of the linkage mode of the pyranosyl-sugar ring to the triazole core was achieved by coupling constant (J) values observed for the glycopyranosyl-H1 (anomeric hydrogen) at 9.9–10.4 Hz.

The acetylated glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles based isoindole core 4–6 were converted to their corresponding glycosides possessing pyranosyl-sugar moieties having free hydroxyls 7–9, respectively, via deacetylation reaction using saturated ammonia/methanol solution. (Scheme 1). The IR bands of the carbonyl in CH3C = O groups vanished in the formed triazole-glycosides while absorption bands have appeared assigned to –OH absorptions, evidencing the deacetylation reaction. In addition, their 1H‐NMR data have indicated the assigned peaks for sugar chain hydrogens and the hydroxyls besides vanishing of the CH3CO (incorporated in the protected precursors) revealing full stratification with the deacetylated glycosides structures.

Synthesis of N-glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles based isoindole system.

The structurally isosteric triazole-glycosides based isoindole system incorporating a different linkage type linking the sugar moiety with the triazole ring by a C-C bond, were also prepared. The latter requires providing the pyranosyl sugar by the terminal acetylenic functionality to achieve the click reaction with the azido-alkyl derivative of the N-phenyl-isoindole key compound. Thus, chloro-alkylation of the phenyl isoindoline compound 1 with bromochloroethane resulted in the formation of the O-chloroethyl derivative 10. The latter was then subjected to azidation process using sodium azide leading to formation of the related ethoxy-azide compound 11. The infra-red of the azide 11 provided the band characterizing the “-N3” group and, additionally, its 1H NMR data presented the triplet signals for methylene in the O-ethyl substituent in the Ph- ring.

The resulting key azide 11 was reacted with the terminal acetylenic pyranosyl sugars; galacto- and xylopyranosyl moieties 12a, b, via Cu(I)-catalyzed 1,3-dipolar cycloadition reaction (CuCAAC) that led to the corresponding glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles 13 and 14, respectively. In a similar way applied for the N-glycosides 4–6, Cu(II) species in copper sulfate was reduced the necessary Cu(I) by means of sodium ascorbate in addition to the previously used solvent system; THF–H2O mixture (2:1). In the formed 1,2,3-triazole-4C-glycosides the glycopyranosyl part was attached to triazole-C4, via a C-C linkage, as modified analogues of 1,2,3-triazole C-nucleosides. The appeared absorptions of the functionalized triazoles 13 and 14 were characteristic for the “-C = O” frequency in the O-acetyl groups at their normal regions. The pyranosyl-sugar hydrogens, the “CH3C = O” protons and the remaining protons in the assigned structures of 13 and 14, were clearly presented in related 1H NMR spectra. The assignment of sugar signals as well as the coupling constants of the anomeric proton the formed glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles accounted for the β-confirmation of the triazole-sugar attachment.

The final deacetylated triazole glycosides 15 and 16 possessing sugar free hydroxyls were formed via removal of the acetyls form their precursors 13 and 14, respectively in saturated methanolic gaseous ammonia solution (Scheme 2). The full replacement of the -O-COCH3 by -OH groups in the glycopyranosyl ring in the formed triazole-glycosides was evidenced by the disappearance of the carbonyl bands that has vanished in the IR spectra and instead the characteristic hydroxyl absorptions appeared clearly. On the other hand, the 1H-NMR data have, obviously, presented the assigned peaks attributed to the pyranosyl-sugar hydrogens as well as the signals of the hydroxyl protons in addition to the absence of the O-COCH3 signals (originally present in their acetylated precursors) indicating the efficient production of the deacetylated glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole, 15 and 16.

Anticancer activity

The prepared triazole glycosides were evaluated through in vitro screening for studying the behaviour towards the human cancer cells; HepG-2 (human liver cancer), HCT-116 (human colorectal cancer), HOS (human bone cancer), PC3 (human prostate cancer) and MCF-7 (human breast cancer) along with a human normal cell line (BJ). Such screening used the MTT colorimetric assay71,72 and doxorubicin as the standard reference. The observed cytotoxicity of the active compounds showed a dose-dependent manner on the growth of the cancer cell lines (HCT-116, HepG-2, HOS, PC3 and MCF-7) (Table 1; Fig. 3). A number of compounds exhibited a good activity on A549 human tumour cell line which was found the most susceptible in the current investigation (namely compounds 6, 8 and 9) (Table 1). Compound 16 was the most active against HCT116 human cancer cell line while compound 14 showed moderate activity against HepG2 cancer cell. Except for compounds 4 and 6, which showed moderate activity against PC3 cell line, the compounds revealed weak inhibition. Interestingly, all the previously mentioned highly or moderately active compounds showed no or little cytotoxicity against human BJ normal cell line which revealed the in vitro selectivity of such tested derivatives that could compare to the reference drug. No activity was observed on HOS cancer cell line.

On the other hand, investigating the active glycosides (which gave more than 50% cytotoxicity) on normal showed that they are all selective on cancer cells and safe on normal cells. The latter consequence revealed the selectivity interest of the tested compounds towards human cancer cell lines and their safe mode on the normal healthy BJ1 cell line comparable to the reference drug (Table 1).

By correlating the structural features with the observed cytotoxicity of the investigated products towards the human cancer cells, the significance of incorporating a sugar moiety in glycosyl-1,2,3-triazolyl system for the activity is apparent. The excessive difference in activity (against HCT116 cell line) between the glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole incorporating xylopyranosyl moiety with free hydroxyl groups and the galactopyranosyl isosteric analogue accounted for the specific effect of the type of the sugar moiety. Furthermore, the in the same direction, the activity of the free hydroxyls glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole possessing xylopyranoside was found more than that of the acetylated precursor. The enhanced activities of the glycosyl triazoles with the xylopyrazonsyl moieties (whether possessing protected or free hydroxyl glycopyranpsyl units in their structures) against A549 cells reveals the importance of such moiety for activity as the activity was much less in their iso-steric analogues with galacto- or glucopyranosyl-1,2,3-triazoles structures. In general, the results also, revealed the effect of the type of linkage since compounds incorporating glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles with 1N-linkage between the 1,2,3-triazole ring and the glycypyranosyl sugar part revealed enhanced activities more than those with 4C-linkage in the triazole ring. Although, the glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole with acetylated glucose sugar moiety showed good activity against HepG2, the analogue with the xylopyranosyl moiety with free hydroxyl groups revealed more inhibition than its isosteric analogues having the galacto- and glucopyranosyl sugar parts. Molecular docking

In-silico investigation aimed to recognize the binding interactions of the studied novel triazole glycosides 6, 8, and 16 inwards the catalytic domain of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR; PDB ID: 1M17), for assessing potential inhibitory activity. Accordingly, molecular docking, as a central tool in structure-based drug design, was employed to provide insights into ligand–protein interactions critical for anticancer drug development providing important information about the possible binding modes of such investigated compounds.

EGFR is a well-established oncogenic target, frequently dysregulated in various cancers through overexpression, amplification, or mutation. Its inhibition impairs signalling pathways that mediate tumour growth, survival, and resistance to apoptosis. The Swiss Target Prediction tool confirmed EGFR as a plausible molecular target for the studied compounds73, while prior studies have shown that doxorubicin interacts with key residues within the EGFR active site74.

This investigation focused on isoindolinedione based glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole derivatives 6, 8, and 16, which demonstrated significant antiproliferative activity as shown by the cytotoxic results assays. The 1,2,3-triazole glycoside derivative 8 is the only compound that exhibited activity against two cell lines against A549 and HepG2 cell lines, indicating superior potency. Compound 6 showed the highest potency against A549 cells, while 16 showed the highest potency against HCT116 cells. The docking simulations revealed that the investigated 1,4-disubstituted triazole-glycosides 6, 8 and 16 effectively engaged the EGFR active site, as illustrated in Figs. 7, 8 and 9 respectively showing − 5.1, -8.2, and − 7.9 kcal/mol, respectively, binding energies suggesting strong affinity for the EGFR catalytic domain.

The xylopyranosyl-1,2,3-triazole derivative 6 resulted in interaction profiles as shown in Fig. 7 revealing one hydrogen bond with Lys721, hydrophobic interactions with Val702, Phe699, Leu820, Leu694, Met769 and Cys773 in addition to polar interactions with Thr766, Th830 and Asn818.

Compound 8 (Fig. 8) established four hydrogen bonds; two bonds with Asp831 in addition to bonds with Met769 and Lys721. Furthermore, hydrophobic interactions has also been achieved with Val702, Cys773, Leu694, Leu820, Met769 and Ala719 in addition to polar interactions with Thr766 and Gln767. Compound 16 (Fig. 9) formed two H-bonds with Cys773 and Asp776; hydrophobic interactional with Val702, Leu694, Leu820 and Ala719; as well as polar interactions with Thr766 and Gln767. and VdW interactions with Lys721.

These profiles of interaction coincide, to a large extent, with those known for doxorubicin, which interacts with EGFR residues Met769, Cys773, Lys721, Val702, Leu820 and Thr76673. The engaged interactions with these critical residues underscore the potential of 6, 8, and 16 as effective EGFR inhibitors.

The docking results correlate well with the experimentally observed anticancer activities. Compound 8, exhibited activity against two cell lines (A549 and HepG2), revealing the most favourable binding energy (− 8.2 kcal/mol) and the highest number of direct hydrogen bonds with EGFR (four H-bonds with Asp831, Met769, and Lys721). These key residues are critically involved in adenosine triphosphate (ATP)-competitive binding within the EGFR kinase domain, and their simultaneous engagement by compound 8 rationalizes its broader and stronger cytotoxic profile. Compound 6, the most potent agent against A549 cells (IC50 = 54.2 µM), showed a complementary interaction profile through hydrophobic contacts with Val702, Phe699, Leu820, and Cys773, which align with the hydrophobic pharmacophoric regions required for EGFR inhibition. Compound 16, the most active compound against HCT116 (IC50 = 64.0 µM), formed two H-bonds with Cys773 and Asp776 — residues also targeted by approved EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors — explaining its selectivity towards colorectal cancer cells. Collectively, the docking data provide a structural rationale for the observed differential cytotoxicity of 6, 8, and 16 against the tested human cancer cell lines, and support EGFR as the primary molecular target for these isoindolinedione-based 1,2,3-triazole glycosides.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

A 100ns molecular dynamics (MD) simulation was fulfilled on the EGFR-glycoside 8 complex, selected due to its status as the only compound with antiproliferative activity against two cell lines (A549 and HepG2). The pretence intended to estimate the stability of 8 within EGFR binding pocket. A 100 ns production run was selected for post-docking stability validation, focusing on the local conformational relaxation and binding stability of the complex rather than statistical thermodynamic properties or rare events. The applied timescale has been demonstrated to be sufficient to assess post-docking binding stability and to reach conformational equilibrium for small ligands within the EGFR kinase domain. This duration is well-supported by recent literature on EGFR inhibitors75,76, and convergence was confirmed by the stabilization of the ligand RMSD and the sustained hydrogen bond occupancy throughout the trajectory.

The root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) analysis, shown in Fig. 10A, indicated that 8 exhibited RMSD values around 2.5 Å, reflecting minor reorientations within the binding pocket but maintaining overall stability. The RMSD value show that product 8 remained anchored in EGFR active site throughout the simulation, as reinforced by trajectory snapshot analyses.

Compound 8’s MD simulation study in the EGFR interaction region (PBD ID: 1M17), (A) RMSD (compound 8’s RMSD are shown in red, whereas protein RMSD is displayed in blue), (B) Protein RMSF (Protein residues that interact with the ligand are marked with green-coloured vertical bars), (C) 2D association diagram, and (D) MD trajectory analysis of protein-ligand contact.

Root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) analysis (Fig. 10B) highlighted the flexibility of EGFR residues, with lower RMSF values for residues directly involved in ligand binding, indicating stable interactions. 8 maintained consistent contacts with key residues, including Leu694, Ala719, Lys721, Met769, Pro770, Phe771, Glu780 and Thr830 as depicted in Fig. 10D.

Detailed interaction analysis (Fig. 10C and D) revealed that 8 formed H-binding with Thr830, Lys721 and Phe771 sustained for 74%, 72% and 70% of the emulation time, respectively. Water-mediated hydrogen bonds with Asp831 and Met769 persisted for 62% and 32%, respectively. Hydrophobic interactions with Ala719, along with polar interactional achievement with Thr766 and Thr830, were also prominent, contributing to the ligand’s stability.

A 100-ns molecular dynamics (MD) emulation was performed for the EGFR–glycosyl 8 complex, that was selected based on its dual antiproliferative activity against A549 and HepG2 cell lines. The objective of this investigation was to evaluate the conformational stability of the latter glycosyl-1,2,3-triazole derivative 8 within the EGFR binding site.

Root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) analysis (Fig. 10A) showed that the isoindolinedione based 1,2,3-triazole glycoside derivative 8 maintained an average RMSD of approximately 2.5 Å, indicating minor positional adjustments while remaining stably anchored in the active site. Trajectory snapshots further confirmed this stability.

Root-mean-square fluctuation (RMSF) analysis (Fig. 10B) revealed that there is a reduced flexibility in residues directly involved in ligand binding, reflecting consistent interactions. Notably, the glycoside 8 maintained stable contacts with the key residues such as Leu694, Ala719, Lys721, Met769, Pro770, Phe771, Glu780, and Thr830 (Fig. 10D).

The interaction profiling (Fig. 10C and D) revealed sustained hydrogen bonding between the glycosyl-1,2,3-triaazole derivative 8 and Thr830, Lys721, and Phe771 for 74%, 72%, and 70% of the process time, respectively. Water-mediated hydrogen bonds with Asp831 and Met769 were maintained for 62% and 32%, respectively. Additional hydrophobic and polar interactions with Ala719, Thr766, and Thr830 contributed significantly to the binding stability of the complex.

Computational analysis of compound 8

The electron-donating capabilities of compound 8, a unique molecule exhibiting activity against two cell lines, were initially explored. Figure 11a illustrates the optimized structure, revealing a nonplanar configuration with intramolecular interactions. Cytotoxicity activity

The newly synthesized isoindoline-based derivatives 4–11 and 13–16 were inspected for cytotoxic assessment in-vitro applying the MTT assay procedure71,72, for human lung A-549, liver HepG-2, prostate PC3, bone HOS, colorectal HCT-116 cancer cell lines, and human normal cell line (BJ) [see SI).

Molecular docking

Computational docking methods were utilized to examine the catalytic region of EGFR (PDB ID: 1M17), the targeted enzyme. The docking process was carried out at the active site of the receptor protein using the XP (extra precision) Glide simulation approach78. The procedures for preparing ligands, configuring the protein, and generating the grid box adhered to the methodologies described in previous research79.

Molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

The Desmond software was employed to perform molecular dynamics (MD) simulations with the OPLS4 force field80,81. Long-range electrostatic interactions were computed with a precision of 1e-09 using the smooth particle mesh Ewald technique, while Coulomb interactions and short-range van der Waals forces were assessed with a cutoff distance of 9.0 Å. A 100 ns MD simulation was executed under isothermal-isobaric (NPT) conditions at 300 K and 1 bar pressure. Trajectory frames were captured every 100 ps during the simulation. Preparation of the protein and ligand was carried out using Maestro software82,83. The tail region of the 1M17 crystal structure was excised before MD modeling, as it is not associated with the active site and might have led to notable instability84. The ligand’s orientation and binding stability were evaluated using Desmond’s Simulation Interaction Diagram (SID)80,81.

Conclusion

Click chemistry has been revealed as an efficient approach for the synthesis of novel glycosides of isoindolindione-1,2,3-triazole-glycosyl hybrid system bridged by alkoxy aryl spacers. The observed anticancer activities outlined, obviously, the importance of the sugar moiety in the glycosyl-1,2,3-triazolyl system in the tested products and was, dependent, to an observed extent, on the existence of specific glycosyl moieties. The triazole glycosides based isoindolindione system with xylopyranosyl glycosyl moiety, weather acetylated or free hydroxy sugar part, revealed enhanced cytotoxic activity, against HCT and A549 cancer cells. Specific structural features such as the linkage type also influences the degree of activity since glycosyl-1,2,3-triazoles with 1N-linkage were found more active. DFT also reflected the theoretical basis for the binding affinity and afforded activity of the investigated products. The HOMO-LUMO energy gap also supported the ability of active glycosides to achieve stable interactions with EGFR. The electron-donating mode supported by the structural feature aligns with the molecular docking and dynamics findings revealing the possible ability of the glycosyl triazole to achieve key interactions with EGFR residues. The possible latter interactions’ stability was also confirmed by MD investigations. Such integrated findings may, strongly, enhance future research by enabling structural optimization that achieves enhanced safety profiles without compromising anticancer potency. The cytotoxicity selectivity for cancer cells and safety toward healthy cells provides a promising foundation for clinical applications, that could overcome limitations of current therapies.

MENG-12 VISUAL DISTURBANCES AND MENINGIOMA: ONE TRAIN CAN HIDE ANOTHER: A CASE REPORT
Authors: Pape Aicha Ginette, Broalet Maman You Esperance
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractAimTo report a clinical case of olfactory sulcus meningioma.ResultMiss E.A.H., 63 years old, with no particular medical history, has been followed for two years for declining visual acuity. She was hospitalized in a psychiatric ward due to the onset of visual hallucinations coupled with psychomotor agitation. The physical examination revealed a psychosis syndrome of the hallucinatory delirium type associated with involvement of the third cranial nerve and glaucomatous retinopathy of the eyes. A brain MRI revealed a meningioma of the olfactory groove with suprasellar extension. She was referred to the Neurosurgery Department of the Saint Joseph Moscati Catholic Hospital for treatment. A transnasal endoscopic approach was performed 17 days after diagnosis. The short-term postoperative course is uneventful.ConclusionAn organic lesion, particularly a meningioma, should be considered in the presence of visual disturbances.

The diagnostic and therapeutic potential of miR-3149 in osteoporotic fractures through SMAD4
Authors: Guanwei Xu, Zhichao Yang, Sibao Zeng, Yongqi Dong, Lina Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundOsteoporotic fractures (OPF) are one of the most common bone diseases in aging societies. MiR-3149 is a short non-coding RNA, its expression level, therapeutic effects, and mechanisms in OPF remain unclear.PurposeThis study aims to explore the impact of miR-3149 on OPF progression and uncover its mechanism for influencing OPF progression via SMAD4.MethodsIn this study, we first used reverse transcription-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR) to detect miR-3149 expression levels in the serum of OPF patients. Then, we assessed miR-3149’s diagnostic value for OPF via ROC curve analysis. Using cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assays, flow cytometry, and RT-qPCR, we explored miR-3149’s effects on cell proliferation, apoptosis, and the expression of key factors RANK and NFATc1. Furthermore, we examined SMAD4 expression in RAW264.7 cells and conducted a dual-luciferase reporter assay to confirm the interaction between miR-3149 and SMAD4. Finally, we analyzed how miR-3149 regulates SMAD4 expression and investigated the effects of miR-3149-mediated SMAD4 regulation on cell function.ResultsThis study reveals that miR-3149 is markedly upregulated in the OPF group, effectively differentiating the control and case groups. MiR-3149 knockdown suppressed RAW264.7 cell proliferation and osteoclastic differentiation, while inducing apoptosis. Notably, SMAD4, a downstream target gene of miR-3149, was low-expressed in RANKL-induced RAW264.7 cells, and exhibits specific binding with miR-3149. Co-transfection with pcDNA-SMAD4 reversed the effects of mimic-miR on RAW264.7 cell proliferation, apoptosis, and osteoclastic induction.ConclusionsDown-regulating miR-3149 inhibited the osteoclastic generation ability of RAW264.7 by up-regulating SMAD4 expression.Keywords: Osteoporotic fractures, MiR-3149, SMAD4, Diagnosis

Introduction

Osteoporosis (OP) is a multifactorial metabolic bone disorder characterized by the deterioration of bone microarchitecture and a reduction in bone mass, which culminates in an increased susceptibility to fragility fractures [1]. Currently, a variety of strategies exist to address OP [2–5], including the use of calcitonin and other pharmacological agents [6], intermittent pneumatic compression [7], and low-intensity pulsed ultrasound [8]. Nonetheless, each of these interventions presents certain limitations, necessitating further investigation to ascertain their efficacy. Osteoporotic fractures (OPF), being one of the most severe complications of this condition, affect approximately 40% of individuals over the age of 50 [9]. The International Osteoporosis Foundation reports that the incidence rates of fractures among women and men over 50 are one-third and one-fifth, respectively [10]. These fractures significantly heighten disability, morbidity, and mortality rates. Alarmingly, 30–50% of patients do not regain their pre-fracture functional status, and the occurrence of a first fracture increases the risk of subsequent fractures by more than sixfold, thereby exacerbating the functional prognosis [11]. OPF constitutes the predominant type of fracture and is associated with elevated rates of delayed union and non-union. Consequently, they impose a considerable burden on healthcare systems and socio-economic well-being [12]. Therefore, early diagnosis is crucial for the intervention of OPF.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are a class of short (17–22 nucleotide) non-coding RNAs that have been evolutionarily conserved [13]. They play pivotal roles in various physiological and pathological processes through mechanisms of epigenetic regulation. Emerging studies substantiate their involvement in maintaining skeletal development and homeostasis by orchestrating the complex coupling between osteoblasts and osteoclasts [14, 15]. Moreover, miRNAs modulate bone metabolism by disrupting the balance of bone remodeling and influencing osteocyte differentiation and function. Notably, alterations in miRNA expression profiles in pathological bone conditions suggest their potential as diagnostic biomarkers for bone diseases [16]. Some studies show that in bone pathological groups, miR-365 and miR-155 expression are reduced, while miR-423 expression is increased, compared to controls [17]. Furthermore, increased plasma levels of miR-4739 have been documented in individuals diagnosed with OP and osteoporotic vertebral fractures (OVF), indicating the promising utility of miRNAs as biomarkers for diagnosing bone diseases and tracking their progression [18]. Additionally, the long non-coding RNA CASC11 regulates the process of delayed fracture healing by sponging miR-150-3p [19]. Furthermore, miR-1271-5p has been implicated in the healing processes of pilon fractures through its targeting of ZBTB7A [20].

SMAD4 serves as the pivotal transducer within the TGF-β signaling pathway, forming complexes with SMAD2 and SMAD3 to orchestrate the majority of TGF-β-induced signaling cascades [21]. Functioning as a crucial tumor suppressor in the context of carcinogenesis, mutations or deletions of the SMAD4 gene are frequently observed across various malignancies, including pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma [22], cholangiocarcinoma, gastric cancer, and prostate cancer [21]. In terms of bone metabolism, SMAD4 plays an integral role in maintaining bone homeostasis by regulating the development and functionality of both osteoclasts and osteoblasts [23]. Research indicates that serum levels of BMP2 and SMAD4 are significantly diminished in elderly patients with OPF when compared to healthy controls, with serum BMP2 exhibiting a robust positive correlation with SMAD4 levels [23]. Moreover, studies reveal that miR-664-3p targets the expression of the SMAD4 gene, thereby inhibiting osteoblast differentiation and compromising bone formation capacity [24].

This study delves into the role of miR-3149 in the progression of OPF, highlighting its potential to ameliorate this condition through the regulation of SMAD4. The findings present a novel target that could significantly enhance the diagnosis and treatment of OPF.

Materials and methods

Patient recruitment and sample collection

This study received approval from the Ethics Committee of Lishui People’s Hospital. A total of 80 patients with OPF were included in the case group, alongside 80 patients diagnosed with OP during the same timeframe, constituting the control group. The inclusion criteria stipulated that subjects must present with a first-time OPF that met the following diagnostic benchmarks: [1] a bone mineral density (BMD) T-score of ≤ -2.5 standard deviations (SD); [2] fracture characteristics consistent with the diagnostic criteria for OP. Exclusion criteria encompassed: (a) the presence of concurrent metabolic bone diseases; (b) a history of malignant tumors.

For the follow-up endpoint classification, X-ray assessments were performed, leading to the categorization of 28 cases displaying insufficient callus formation, mild decalcification, and distinctly visible fracture lines, which were designated as the delayed healing group. Conversely, 52 cases that exhibited normal callus development—characterized by the absence of imaging features indicative of delayed healing and no deformity at the fracture site after a monitoring period of two weeks—were classified as the normal healing group.

Peripheral venous blood samples were obtained from both groups under fasting conditions and collected in anticoagulant tubes. Within 30 min post-collection, samples underwent centrifugation at 1500 g for 10 min. The resulting supernatant serum layer was meticulously aspirated, aliquoted into sterile microcentrifuge tubes, and labeled with pertinent sample identification numbers and collection dates before being stored at -80 °C.

Cell culture

Cultured in DMEM (Thermo Fisher Scientific) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco), along with 100 U/mL of penicillin and 100 µg/mL of streptomycin (Thermo Fisher Scientific), RAW264.7 cells (Shangen Biotechnology) were differentiated into osteoclasts through a treatment regimen of 50 ng/mL RANKL (Sigma-Aldrich, R0525) for 7 days under conditions of 37 °C and 5% CO₂.

MiRNA mimics, MiRNA inhibitors, pcDNA-NC, pcDNA-SMAD4, and transfection experiments

In the conducted experiments, miR-3149 mimics (mimics-miR) and inhibitors (inhibitors-miR) were utilized to facilitate the overexpression and inhibition of miR-3149 in cellular models, respectively. To ensure robust experimental design, scrambled negative controls (mimics-NC) and their corresponding inhibitors (inhibitors-NC) were also employed. Additionally, pcDNA-NC was utilized as a negative control, whereas pcDNA-SMAD4 was specifically constructed as a recombinant plasmid to overexpress SMAD4. All aforementioned plasmids were procured from Thermo Fisher Scientific. Transfection procedures were carried out using Lipofectamine 2000 reagent (Sigma-Aldrich), ensuring efficient delivery of these constructs into the cells.

Dual luciferase reporter gene experiment

Fragments containing wild-type (SMAD4-WT) or mutant (SMAD4-MUT) target sequences were subsequently cloned into the pGL3-promoter vector (Promega). These constructs were then co-transfected into cells alongside miRNA mimics, inhibitors, or pcDNA-SMAD4, utilizing Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen) as the transfection reagent. Following a 48-hour incubation period, the cells were lysed, and luciferase activity was quantified using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega). The luminescence of Firefly luciferase was normalized against that of Renilla luciferase to account for variations in transfection efficiency.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay

Proliferation was assessed at intervals of 0, 24, 48, and 72 h utilizing the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Yeasen Biotech, 40203ES76). Following the seeding of 5,000 cells per well in 96-well plates, the cells were incubated under standard conditions (37 °C with 5% CO₂). Subsequently, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well. The absorbance was measured at 450 nm after a 2-hour incubation period using a Thermo Fisher microplate reader.

Cell apoptosis by flow cytometry

Apoptosis was assessed via flow cytometry employing an Annexin V-FITC/PI kit (Yeasen). Harvested cells (1 × 10⁶) were resuspended in 100 µL binding buffer and subsequently stained with 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL propidium iodide (PI). The mixture was gently vortexed and allowed to incubate in the dark for 15 min at room temperature. Following incubation, an additional 400 µL of binding buffer was added, and samples were analyzed within one hour using a BD Biosciences flow cytometer. The quantification of apoptotic populations was conducted utilizing FlowJo software.

Reverse transcriptionquantitative PCR (RTqPCR)

Total RNA was isolated utilizing Trizol reagent (Thermo Fisher) and subsequently quantified using Nanodrop One spectrophotometry. For reverse transcription, 1 µg of input RNA was utilized with HiScript III Reverse Transcriptase (Vazyme). The quantitative PCR (qPCR) amplification was conducted employing ChamQ Universal SYBR Master Mix (Vazyme) on a StepOnePlus system (Applied Biosystems), with β-actin and U6 serving as endogenous controls to ensure reliable normalization of gene expression data.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD derived from a minimum of three independent experiments. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 9.3.1, employing independent t-tests for pairwise comparisons and one-way ANOVA for multi-group comparisons. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves were generated utilizing MedCalc, while clinical parameters were analyzed using SPSS version 23. A significance threshold of p < 0.05 was established to determine statistical relevance.

Results

MiR-3149 has potential diagnostic value for OPF

This study involved the recruitment of 160 patients who were stratified into two distinct groups: a control group and an OPF group. Fasting peripheral blood samples were procured for the assessment of serum miR-3149 levels. The findings revealed a significant upregulation of miR-3149 in the OPF group (Fig. 1A). Furthermore, ROC curve analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.8835 (95% CI: 0.8339–0.9332, p < 0.001), indicating that miR-3149 is a reliable biomarker for distinguishing between patients with OP and those with OPF (Fig. 1B). The OPF patients were subsequently divided into normally-healed and delayed-healed subsets based on the status of fracture healing. Notably, miR-3149 levels were found to be significantly elevated in the delayed-healed group (Fig. 1C). A comparative analysis of various indicators between the two patient groups was conducted, revealing significant differences in BMD and RANKL expression, alongside the remarkably pronounced differential expression of miR-3149 (Table 1).

Down-regulating miR-3149 inhibits the osteoclastic differentiation of RAW264.7 cells

This study utilized the RAW264.7 cell model stimulated with RANKL (50 ng/ml) to elucidate the role of miR-3149 in osteoclastic differentiation. Initial exposure to RANKL resulted in a marked upregulation of critical osteoclast differentiation markers, RANK and NFATc1 (Fig. 2A-B), thereby confirming the successful induction of differentiation. Concurrently, a significant increase in miR-3149 expression was observed in RANKL-treated cells. Validation through transfection revealed that the RANKL + mimics-miR cohort exhibited significantly elevated miR-3149 levels compared to the RANKL + mimics-NC controls. In contrast, the RANKL + inhibitor-miR group demonstrated a significant reduction in miR-3149 expression relative to the RANKL + inhibitor-NC (Fig. 2C), affirming the efficacy of the overexpression and knockdown approaches. Functionally, proliferation assays indicated that cell viability in the miR-3149 overexpression groups consistently surpassed that of the RANKL + mimics-NC controls at all time points, while the knockdown groups exhibited decreased viability compared to the RANKL + inhibitor-NC controls (Fig. 2D). These findings suggest that the suppression of miR-3149 inhibits cellular proliferation, whereas its overexpression promotes it. Moreover, apoptosis analysis revealed a significantly reduced apoptosis rate in the overexpression groups compared to RANKL + mimics-NC, whereas the knockdown groups displayed elevated rates relative to RANKL + inhibitor-NC (Fig. 2E). These results underscore the role of miR-3149 in modulating apoptosis, with knockdown enhancing apoptotic rates and overexpression mitigating them. In further exploration, miR-3149 overexpression was associated with a substantial increase in RANK and NFATc1 expression levels, whereas knockdown resulted in a pronounced decrease in these proteins (Fig. 2F-G). Collectively, these observations establish that the depletion of miR-3149 significantly inhibits osteoclastic differentiation through the suppression of RANK and NFATc1 signaling pathways.

Interaction between miR-3149 and SMAD4

Initially, we conducted a bioinformatic intersection between computationally predicted targets of miR-3149 and documented OPF-associated genes, resulting in the identification of 11 candidate genes. From this set, SMAD4 was selected as a putative downstream target of miR-3149 (Fig. 3A). Subsequently, we assessed SMAD4 expression levels in RANKL-stimulated RAW264.7 cells. The results revealed a significant reduction in SMAD4 expression in the RANKL-treated group compared to the control, indicating a downregulation of SMAD4 during the osteoclastic differentiation process (Fig. 3B). To validate the direct interaction between miR-3149 and SMAD4, we performed dual-luciferase reporter assays. The data demonstrated that overexpression of miR-3149 significantly suppressed luciferase activity in constructs containing the wild-type SMAD4 3’ UTR (SMAD4-WT) compared to mimics-NC controls. In contrast, luciferase activity for the mutated SMAD4 reporter (SMAD4-MUT) remained unaffected, confirming that miR-3149 binds specifically to particular sites within the SMAD4 3’ UTR. Importantly, the activity of the SMAD4-MUT reporter showed no significant differences between the mimics-miR and mimics-NC groups. Furthermore, treatment with miR-3149 inhibitors markedly restored luciferase activity in the SMAD4-WT reporter when compared to inhibitor-NC controls, thus demonstrating target-specific derepression. Conversely, the activity of the SMAD4-MUT reporter remained unchanged across inhibitor groups (Fig. 3C). Additionally, we quantified the miR-3149-mediated modulation of endogenous SMAD4 mRNA levels. The results indicated that RANKL combined with mimics-miR resulted in a significant suppression of SMAD4 mRNA levels, while RANKL in conjunction with miR inhibitors significantly elevated SMAD4 mRNA levels (Fig. 3D). These findings further corroborate that miR-3149 directly targets and negatively regulates the expression of SMAD4.

MiR-3149 influences the osteoclastic differentiation of RAW264.7 cells via regulation of SMAD4

This investigation explored the influence of miR-3149, mediated through the regulation of SMAD4, on the behavior of RANKL-stimulated RAW264.7 cells. The findings revealed a marked increase in cell viability within the RANKL + mimics-miR group compared to the RANKL + mimics-NC group. Notably, this pro-proliferative effect was significantly diminished upon co-transfection with pcDNA-SMAD4 (Fig. 4A), indicating that the overexpression of SMAD4 counteracts the miR-3149-mediated enhancement of RANKL-induced proliferation in RAW264.7 cells. Moreover, the impact of miR-3149 regulation via SMAD4 on the apoptosis of RANKL-induced RAW264.7 cells was subsequently assessed. The apoptosis rate was significantly lower in the RANKL + mimics-miR group than in the RANKL + mimics-NC group. Co-transfection with pcDNA-SMAD4 resulted in a notable increase in the apoptosis rate (Fig. 4B), underscoring that the overexpression of SMAD4 mitigates the miR-3149-induced inhibition of apoptosis. Furthermore, the regulatory effects of miR-3149 through SMAD4 on the expression of RANK and NFATc1 were evaluated. Expression levels of both RANK and NFATc1 were significantly elevated in the RANKL + mimics-miR group. However, co-transfection with pcDNA-SMAD4 considerably diminished the expression levels of these factors (Fig. 4C-D), indicating that the overexpression of SMAD4 reverses the miR-3149-mediated upregulation of RANK and NFATc1. Collectively, these findings elucidate that miR-3149, by targeting and modulating the expression of SMAD4, significantly influences RANKL-induced cell proliferation, apoptosis, and the process of osteoclastic differentiation in RAW264.7 cells.

Discussion

As the global population continues to age, the annual incidence of OPF is steadily increasing, accompanied by a growing accumulation of associated risk factors. This situation presents a formidable challenge to public health and contributes significantly to the global disease burden [25]. There have been numerous studies focusing on the research of fracture-related diseases [26–33]. Moreover, the healing process of OPF encompasses not just the restoration of bone integrity but also affects patients’ daily functional capacity and overall cardiovascular health, among other considerations [9]. Although a variety of diagnostic and therapeutic approaches for OPF have been developed [34–36], it is noteworthy that, despite estrogen therapy’s proven efficacy in enhancing BMD, large-scale clinical trials have revealed its ineffectiveness in significantly reducing fracture incidence in women diagnosed with osteoporosis [1]. Furthermore, OPF frequently coexists with chronic pain and poor healing outcomes, including delayed unions and non-unions, which can severely hinder patient rehabilitation [37]. Consequently, the quest for safe and effective novel biomarkers for the early diagnosis and intervention of OPF is of paramount importance, with the ultimate goal of improving adverse outcomes for affected patients.

An increasing body of evidence has illuminated the pivotal role of non-coding RNA in the pathophysiology of bone-related diseases [38–41]. MiRNAs are extensively involved in the modulation of cellular proliferation, differentiation, apoptotic processes, and metabolic regulation. Notably, several studies have established a clear association between miRNAs and OP, highlighting their significance as key regulators of gene expression in osteoblast-mediated bone formation (such as miR-218) and osteoclast-mediated bone remodeling (notably miR-148a) [42]. Additionally, research has identified a downregulation of miR-502-3p in OPF cases, indicating its potential utility as a biomarker for both diagnosis and disease evaluation [43]. Other investigations have revealed correlations between specific miRNAs, such as miR-21 and miR-133a, and BMD [44]. Furthermore, miR-214 has been shown to play a significant role in regulating the fracture healing process by inhibiting the SOX4 signaling pathway [45]. In the current study, the proposed miR-3149 exhibits a diagnostic performance superior to that of miR-502-3p, as evidenced by an AUC of 0.8835 compared to 0.8652 for miR-502-3p. While the latter benefits from combined detection with Omentin-1 to enhance its predictive value, this necessitates a more complex and costly assay methodology. In contrast, miR-3149 serves effectively as an independent biomarker. Its streamlined detection fosters efficient screening and aligns seamlessly with the clinical imperative for early diagnosis of OPF, thereby providing significant practical advantages for broader implementation in clinical settings.

This study investigated the role of miR-3149 in OPF by examining its regulatory effects on SMAD4, as well as its potential diagnostic and therapeutic applications. The results revealed that miR-3149 was markedly up-regulated in patients with OPF, and ROC curve analysis indicated its potential to differentiate between patients with OP and those with OPF. In RANKL-induced RAW264.7 cells, overexpression of miR-3149 enhanced cell viability and inhibited apoptosis, whereas its knockdown produced the opposite effects. Furthermore, dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed the direct interaction between miR-3149 and SMAD4. The findings indicate that miR-3149, through the targeted regulation of SMAD4 expression, significantly influences proliferation, apoptosis, and osteoclastic differentiation in RANKL-induced RAW264.7 cells. In conclusion, this study posits that miR-3149 may represent a promising therapeutic target for OPF. However, additional evidence is required to establish its efficacy and safety.

Conclusion

In summary, miR-3149 is markedly upregulated in the serum of patients with OPF. The silencing of miR-3149 leads to a notable inhibition of RAW264.7 cell proliferation, promotes apoptosis, and suppresses osteoclastic differentiation through the upregulation of SMAD4.

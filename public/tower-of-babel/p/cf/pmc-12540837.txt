Enhancing indoor monitoring of visually impaired people using temporal convolutional network with optimization model in IoT environment
Authors: Faisal S. Alsubaei, Abdulrahman A. Alshdadi, Mohammed Rizwanullah
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The Internet of Things (IoT) has emerged as a powerful technology in various fields, including healthcare, assisting the elderly and disabled individuals. Solution-based IoT is widely utilized in healthcare support in diverse aspects of their daily lives and activities. These IoT-based assistive systems are crucial in uplifting the quality of life for disabled individuals and older adults. Furthermore, IoT-based interior navigation methods assist with easy accessibility and movement in areas, ensuring that disabled people can find objects with greater ease. Machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) approaches are efficiently beneficial for detecting indoor objects for visually challenged people. This paper proposes the Metaheuristic Optimization-Driven Attention Mechanism for Enhancing Indoor Monitoring of Visually Impaired People (MOAM-EIMVIP) technique. The main intention of the MOAM-EIMVIP technique is to develop an IoT-based DL model for indoor monitoring of disabled individuals. Initially, the data pre-processing stage applies min-max normalization to convert input data into a suitable format. The sine-cosine algorithm (SCA) is employed to select the feature process to detect and choose the most relevant features from input data. Moreover, the temporal convolutional network-attention mechanism (TCNA) model is implemented for classification. Finally, the Eurasian oystercatcher optimizer (EOO) method alters the hyperparameter tuning of the TCNA model optimally and results in higher classification execution. An extensive simulation is performed to highlight the performance of the MOAM-EIMVIP approach under the HAR dataset. The experimental analysis of the MOAM-EIMVIP approach portrayed a superior accuracy value of 99.30% over existing methods.

Introduction

IoT has a substantial capability to improve the existence of disabled individuals. By combining technologies and smart gadgets, the IoT provides a novel solution to tackle several challenges experienced by disabled individuals and promote inclusiveness1. Using IoT-enabled assistive applications and devices, people with disability can attain amplified independence, admittance to crucial services, and enhanced living standards. IoT gadgets with actuators, connectivity and sensors allow real-world assistance and monitoring in medical care, mobility and accessibility2. For example, wearable gadgets can track essential signs and transfer data to medical care experts, offering early clinical support and personalized care3. Smart home technologies can automate jobs, creating living space available and allowing people to regulate their settings over voice commands or another adaptable interface. Likewise, IoT-based navigation methods promote simple movements and availability in public spaces, ensuring that disabled individuals can navigate buildings, streets and transportation4. Whereas IoT provides immense possibilities, it is vital to tackle security and confidentiality issues to safeguard the sensitive data gathered by these gadgets5.

Although a global positioning system (GPS) provides a sufficient indoor and outdoor localization solution, it is still an open challenge. Various hardware and algorithmic methods are based on the preferred cost and accuracy. The accessible localization method offers position-related data, guidance and advertisements in significant indoor places6. These methods are also employed for asset tracking in industrial settings. Some critical localization applications are familiar to clinical purposes. Nevertheless, the latest developments in IoT applications have proven they are expensive for medical care monitoring7. A novel perception of indoor localization methods is generated, which is the co-relation of indoor localization information with the health care status of people, particularly for disabled people. The indoor positioning system (IPS) introduces real-time localization of objects or people, and spaces are enveloped by different settings, employing a network for transmitters and receivers8. DL and ML have made meaningful challenge feature extractors from the raw sensor data9. Persons with cognitive or physical impairments frequently face challenges handling day-to-day jobs, including controlling their home setting. With the growth of smart technologies and connected devices, there is an increasing opportunity to progress systems which provide autonomy and assistance to people with disability10.

Related works

Nguyen et al.11 present an innovative, low-cost smart wheelchair method. The presented model employs a reasonable webcam and an Nvidia jetson nano-embedded computer to recognize and process six diverse hand gestures to aid wheelchair navigation. This method leverages the YOLO-v8n technique. Feature extract ROI of the hand gesture and background, considerably enhancing the precision of gesture recognition. Lee and Zhang12 progress an innovative artificial intelligence of things (AIoT)-based structure for forecasting multi-dimensional IEQ. The presented structure combines IoT with DL models to systematically integrate multi-dimensional IEQ data and multi-modal feedback from occupants. Alfahad et al.13 advance an auto-adjusting model to choose coefficients dependent on the modern automatic RSSI class. This model is accepted by the IoT incorporated with BLE to accomplish these objectives. The advanced method comprises data aggregation, storage, Bluetooth beacons and a web interface for real-world visualization and tracking. The RSSI footprinting technique is accepted to identify a specific zone with IEQ. Alzahrani14 projected an innovative TCN-based fall activity recognition system for disabled persons (TCN-FARSDP) methodology. This image pre-processing is accomplished by employing GF to remove noise and enhance the clarity of the acquired image. Afterwards, the ensemble of feature extractor techniques contains three methodologies: DenseNet121, MobileNetV3Large, and NASNetMobile. A TCN classifier is employed to recognize fall activities. Finally, fine-tuning is executed using the Adamax to improve the model’s stability and convergence. Li et al.15 projected an intellectual communication method for BIM techniques that depends on gesture recognition. To aid intuitive and natural human-machine communication among BIM and gesture techniques, it has custom-designed six targeted gestures for HBIM. Moreover, a CNN-based gesture recognition model was advanced to recognize gesture features. Eventually, several operations are applied to the BIM technique. Qin et al.16 developed a DL-based solution for identifying abnormal neck behaviour. They suggested a method named NABNet, which integrates OD reliant on YOLO-v5s with pose estimation depending on Lightweight OpenPose. This technique employed NABNet on the cloud and edge gadgets.

Wu et al.17 present TBDNet, combining tracking-before-detect (TBD) from traditional signal processing into learning-based techniques. TBDNet emulates first-track-then-detect pipeline. It maps input heatmap sequences to higher-level frame-to-frame aspects to adjust targets’ time-varying intensity distribution and motion pattern. Sampath et al.18 precisely estimate indoor positions by integrating radio frequency identification (RFID) data with a deep q-learning (DQL) and adaptive emperor penguin colony optimizer (AEPCO) model, improved by Gaussian filtering (GF) technique to enhance the quality of training and eliminate received signal strength indicator (RSSI) noise. Thandu and Rathinam19 improve indoor navigation for VIPs by utilizing an AI chatbot powered by natural language processing (NLP) and volcano eruption optimization (VEO) for real-time, accessible guidance. Dikkatwar et al.20 analyze and rank wearable assistive technologies for visually impaired people (VIP), emphasizing capabilities, user-informed metrics, and future research directions. Valipoor, de Antonio, and Cabrera21 propose a user-centred analysis and design framework for indoor scene understanding assistive solutions for VIPs, addressing key challenges and giving guidelines for choosing appropriate technologies. Afif et al.22 predict optimal indoor temperature for occupant comfort by incorporating IoT-based monitoring with multivariate statistical analysis and locally weighted regression. Saraswathi et al.23 developed a voice-controlled app utilizing the DL model to analyze and describe scenes, with real-time on-device processing, feature extraction, and audible feedback for visually impaired users. Paul et al.24 developed a smart assistive stick integrating ultrasonic sensors, GPS, global system for mobile communications (GSM), and Artificial Intelligence (AI)-based object detection to improve independent mobility and safety for the VIP. Pydala, Kumar, and Baseer25 introduce VISISENSE. This IoT-based assistive technology system improves navigation support for the visually impaired through advanced object detection, edge computing, and cloud-based computer vision models. Hossain, Anjom, and Chowdhury26 developed a lightweight U-Net-based methodology with obstacle distance detection for real-time footpath navigation, enabling visually impaired users to receive audio-guided directions via an Android application. Nandhini et al.27 compare three object detection algorithms, namely YOLOv3 for image-based object detection, OpenCV for video-based detection without action recognition, and CNN for real-time action detection with improved accuracy.

Despite the improvements in assistive technologies for VIPs, various limitations remain. Many existing models depend on complex and expensive hardware, restricting their accessibility and affordability for users. Additionally, while some systems integrate AI and DL, they predominantly suffer from high computational requirements, which affect real-time performance, particularly on mobile or embedded devices. Furthermore, existing object detection and activity recognition methods may not always attain the desired accuracy under diverse environmental conditions, resulting in potential safety concerns. Moreover, existing systems often lack adaptability to diverse indoor environments and fail to give comprehensive, context-aware assistance. There is also a requirement for more effectual and scalable solutions that provide seamless integration across various devices and settings while ensuring user comfort and accuracy in navigation and interaction.

Stage I: min-max normalization

At first, the data pre-processing stage applies min-max normalization to convert input data into a proper format28. This normalization is chosen as it scales the overall features to a fixed range, usually between 0 and 1, ensuring uniformity across variables with diverse units or scales. This normalization model is effectual when the data is not normally distributed, as it does not make assumptions about the underlying distribution. Rescaling the data enhances ML models’ convergence speed and stability, particularly gradient-based methods. Moreover, min-max normalization is computationally efficient and easy to implement, making it an ideal choice for pre-processing massive datasets in DL and other ML applications.

Data normalization is an essential stage in data pre-processing. It involves converting data into a standard form to decrease data complexity, increase data quality, and remove redundancy. This method typically standardizes numeric data by scaling it to a constant set of values, making it simpler to analyze and compare.

Stage II: SCA-based feature selection

For the selection of feature process, the SCA technique is utilized to detect and choose the most relevant features from input data29. This technique is preferred because it can effectively explore the search space and select the most relevant features, enhancing the model’s accuracy and performance. The characteristics of the SCA model replicate the natural sine and cosine functions to balance exploration and exploitation during optimization, making it robust for high-dimensional and complex datasets. Unlike conventional feature selection methods, the SCA model does not depend on gradient information, making the technique appropriate for non-linear issues. Its global search capability also confirms that it can avoid local optima, enhancing generalization. Additionally, SCA’s simplicity and ease of implementation make it an ideal choice over other optimization techniques. Figure 2 illustrates the steps involved in the SCA methodology.

Stage III: activities recognition using the TCNA model

Moreover, the proposed MOAM-EIMVIP technique applies the TCNA model for classification. This model is chosen due to its ability to effectively capture sequential dependencies in time-series data, which is significant for walking, sitting, or standing. The attention mechanism confirms that crucial time steps attain higher weight, while the convolutional layers allow the model to learn hierarchical temporal features, improving accuracy. Unlike conventional RNNs, this model handles more extended sequences without the vanishing gradient problem, resulting in improved recognition of complex activities. Furthermore, TCNA is computationally effectual and capable of scaling to large datasets, making it an ideal choice for real-time activity recognition tasks. Its capability to concentrate on key patterns in time-series data sets it apart from other models that treat all time steps equally. Figure 3 depicts the architecture of the TCNA model.

Finally, the EOO approach optimally alters the hyperparameter tuning of the TCNA model and results in higher classification performance31. The model’s flexibility also plays a crucial part in making the model highly appropriate for both continuous and discrete parameter tuning. Conclusion

In this paper, the MOAM-EIMVIP technique is proposed. The main intention of the MOAM-EIMVIP technique is to develop an IoT-based DL model for indoor monitoring of disabled individuals. Initially, the data pre-processing stage applies min-max normalization to convert input data into a suitable format. The SCA selects the most relevant features from input data to select the feature process. Moreover, the TCNA classifier is implemented for the HAR classification process. Finally, the EOO method optimally alters the TCNA model’s hyperparameter tuning, resulting in higher classification execution. An extensive simulation is performed to highlight the performance of the MOAM-EIMVIP approach under the HAR dataset. The experimental analysis of the MOAM-EIMVIP approach portrayed a superior accuracy value of 99.30% over existing methods. The limitations of the MOAM-EIMVIP approach comprise restricted generalization across diverse real-world scenarios due to the constrained nature of the dataset. The scalability of the system under high data throughput or large-scale deployment remains unexplored. Real-time responsiveness and adaptability to growing input patterns were not evaluated. User personalization features are minimal, which may affect accessibility for a broader user base. Furthermore, the interpretability of the model outputs could be enhanced for better trust and transparency. Future works can explore integration with adaptive feedback systems, extend analysis on cross-domain datasets, improve user-centered design, and improve interpretability for practical deployment.

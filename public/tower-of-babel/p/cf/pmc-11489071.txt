Prenatal p25-activated Cdk5 induces pituitary tumorigenesis through MCM2 phosphorylation-mediated cell proliferation
Authors: Yingwei Huang, Qiqi Wang, Weiwei Zhou, Yawei Jiang, Kai He, Wei Huang, Yating Feng, Hong Wu, Lijuan Liu, Yue Pan, Yihua Huang, Zirui Chen, Wei Li, Yaowei Huang, Guanchuan Lin, Yulong Zhang, Yongyan Ren, Kaibiao Xu, Yanlin Yu, Yuping Peng, Xinghua Pan, Suyue Pan, Hailiang Hu, Yafang Hu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Aberrant expression of cyclin-dependent kinase 5 (Cdk5) has been reported in pituitary adenomas. However, the role of Cdk5 in the tumorigenesis remains unclear. We show that prenatal p25-activated Cdk5 phosphorylates minichromosome maintenance protein 2 (Mcm2), enhancing minichromosome maintenance (MCM) family proteins and driving intermediate lobe-located melanotrope-originated pituitary tumorigenesis. In a mouse model with CaMKII promoter-driven transgenic induction of p25, we observed intermediate lobe-originated pituitary adenoma producing non-functional proopiomelanocortin (POMC)-derived peptides under persistent p25 overexpression. Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed Mcm2 may play an important role during tumor progression. Subsequently, Mcm2 was identified as a potential phosphorylated substrate of Cdk5, mediating the tumorous proliferation of melanotrope cells. Silencing Cdk5 or Mcm2 suppressed cell proliferation and colony formation in the 293T cell lines. Therefore, our findings provide a new mouse model of intermediate lobe-originated pituitary adenoma induced by p25/Cdk5 and unveil a previously unappreciated role of Cdk5 and Mcm2 in pituitary adenoma tumorigenesis.

Introduction

Pituitary adenomas are a diverse group of tumors arising from the pituitary gland. While most are benign and slow-growing, some can be invasive or recurrent, leading to hormone imbalances, visual impairments, and other complications [1]. Traditional treatments, such as surgical resection and radiosurgery, often result in the loss of residual pituitary function, causing hormone insufficiencies. Consequently, alternative treatments like immunotherapy, dopamine agonists for lactotroph adenomas [2], and somatostatin analogs for acromegaly [3,4] are increasingly used.

Molecular studies on pituitary adenomas benefit from genetically engineered animal models, particularly mouse models where tumors originate from intermediate or anterior pituitary lobes [5]. These pituitary tumors often result from deficiencies in cell cycle regulators like Rb, p27, and p18. For example, Malcolm et al. developed a melanocytic pituitary adenoma model in the intermediate lobe by expressing SV40 large T antigen driven by a rat proopiomelanocortin (POMC) promoter [6]. The POMC promoter drives the production of peptides such as adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), α-melanocyte stimulating hormone (α-MSH), and β-endorphin. Similarly, disrupting the cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK)-inhibitory domain of p27 or deleting p18 led to intermediate lobe hyperplasia [[7], [8], [9]]. However, the timeline of pituitary adenoma development and the mechanisms underlying intermediate lobe-originated tumorigenesis remain unclear. p25 is a cleaved product of p35 by calpain under neurotoxic stimuli [10]. Both p25 and p35 are detectable in neuronal and tumoral cells, and can bind and active kinase Cdk5. However, due to different subcellular localization and half-life time between p35 and p25, p35/Cdk5 phosphorylates proteins involved in brain development and neuronal differentiation while p25/Cdk5 plays a critical role in neurodegenerative diseases and cancers [11]. Aberrant Cdk5 activity has been observed in pituitary adenomas, with high levels of Cdk5 or p35/p25 detected [12]. And evidence show that Cdk5 phosphorylates kinase insert domain receptor (KDR), promoting angiogenesis in invasive prolactin pituitary adenomas [13,14]. Although an enlarged pituitary was noted in a mouse model with p25 overexpression under the CMV promoter, details remain sparse [15].

To explore pituitary adenoma tumorigenesis further, we generated a bi-transgenic mouse model (Bi-Tg). In this bitransgenic model, p25-GFP expression is regulated via a tetracycline-controlled transactivator (tTA) system, which is driven by the CamkII promoter [16]. We tracked tumor growth, mapped its development using single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), and elucidated the mechanism of aberrant Cdk5 activity in intermediate lobe-originated pituitary adenoma tumorigenesis.

Material and methods

Mice

The generation of Bi-Tg mice followed a slightly modified protocol base on previous reports [16]. Briefly, C57BL/6-Tg (RRID:IMSR_JAX:005706) mice, in which p25-GFP is controlled by TetO promoter, and B6;CBA-Tg (RRID:IMSR_JAX:003010) mice containing tTA driven by CamKII promotor, were obtained from Jackson Laboratory (CA) [17]. Bi-Tg mice were generated from heterozygote crosses between the two mouse lines aged 8 to 20 weeks, which were genotyping confirmed based on a PCR-based strategy. To overexpress p25-GFP, mice (p25OE) were provided regular water. To suppress p25-GFP expression, mice (p25OE-Dox) received doxycycline (200 μg/mL in water, three times a week) starting from the 12th week (Fig. 1E and 1G). Wide type (WT) mice, littermates of p25OE mice, served as controls. Experiments were conducted on female mice at various ages (4th to 40th weeks), weighing 10–35 g. Control animals were age-matched, had similar weights, and were in good health. All mice were group-housed under a 12 h light/dark cycle with standard conditions of temperature (∼22 °C) and humidity (30 %∼50 %), with ad libitum access to food and water. Genotyping of mice

Genotyping was performed using the Mouse Tail Direct PCR Kit (B40013; Bimake, Houston, USA) following the manufacturer's instructions. Results were visualized by agarose gel electrophoresis. PCR primers used for amplification are listed in key resources table.

Patient pituitary adenoma specimens

Thirty-six pituitary adenoma specimens were collected from patients who underwent surgery at Nanfang Hospital (Southern Medical University, Guangzhou, China). Clinical information, diagnostic classifications and immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining results are provided in Table S6. Due to the lack of normal pituitary samples as controls, the experiments on pituitary adenoma specimens are observational. The study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Nanfang Hospital (#NFEC-2015-033) to ensure ethical standards. All patients or their families signed informed consent forms.

293T cell line

The 293T cell line was authenticated by Ige biotechnology (Guangzhou, China) using STR profiling. The 293T cells were incubated in DMEM supplemented with 10 % fetal bovine serum and 1 % penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C with 5 % CO2.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)

MRI measurements was conducted as previously described [17] using a 7.0 T small animal MRI system (PharmaScan 7.0 Tesla, Siemens, Germany). Parameters included: repetition time = 1500 ms, TE = 8.0 ms, matrix size = 256 × 256, image size = 20 mm × 20 mm, slice thickness = 0.7 mm, number of slices = 15, observation position = sagittal.

The Region of Interest (ROI) tool and Measure function of ImageJ (NIH, Bethesda, MD) were used to calculate the area of the pituitary region in each slice. The pituitary volume was determined by summing the areas of all slices and multiplying by the slice thickness. The formula used is: Pituitary Volume = (∑Slice Areas) × Slice Thickness.

Hematoxylin-Eosin (H&E) staining

Pituitary glands were collected, perfused with PBS, and fixed in 4 % paraformaldehyde for 24 h. The tissues were then embedded in paraffin and sectioned at 4 μm. Sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated and stained with hematoxylin for 5 min and eosin for 1 min. Sections were then washed, cleared, and mounted using neutral resin with coverslips.

Immunohistochemistry

Paraffin sections were used for IHC. After deparaffiniation and rehydration, sections were subjected to antigen retrieval using 0.01 M sodium citrate buffer (pH 6.0) and endogenous peroxidase activity was quenched with 3 % hydrogen peroxide. Sections were washed, blocked with goat serum for 10 min and incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. After washing, sections were incubated with biotin-labeled goat anti-mouse/rabbit IgG polymer solution for 10 min, followed by HRP-labeled streptavidin for another 10 min. Sections were washed, incubated in DAB solution until brown precipitates appeared, then mounted. The antibodies used are listed in the key resources table.

Immunofluorescence (IF)

Pituitary tissues were dehydrated in 10 %, 20 % and 30 % sucrose solutions for 3, 16 and 24 h, respectively. Then they were embedded in OCT compound, frozen in liquid nitrogen and sectioned at 4 μm. Sections were washed, permeabilized, and incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. After washing, sections were incubated with secondary antibodies at room temperature in the dark for 1 h, then mounted in antifade medium with DAPI and observed under a confocal fluorescence microscope. Antibodies are listed in the key resources table.

Western Blot (WB)

Pituitary tissues or cells were lysed in cell lysis buffer with protease and phosphatase inhibitors. Fifteen micrograms of protein was separated on SDS-PAGE gels (10 % separating gel, 5 % stacking gel). Membranes were blocked with 5 % bovine serum albumin (BSA) and incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. After washing, membranes were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1 h at room temperature. Membranes were washed, treated with ECL substrate, and analyzed using a chemiluminescence imager (Tanon-5200, China). Antibodies used are listed in the key resources table.

Co-Immunoprecipitation (co-IP)

Ten micrograms of protein were taken as input controls, while 5 mg were incubated with specific pulldown antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Forty microliters of protein A/G magnetic beads were added to each lysate, incubated for 1 h at room temperature and washed six times with TBST. Beads were resuspended in protein loading buffer and subjected to WB analysis. Pulldown antibodies are listed in the key resources table.

Enzyme linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Commercially ELISA kits (listed in the key resources table) were used to measure serum levels of adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) (CSB-E06874m; Cusabio, Wuhan, China) and α-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (α-MSH) (CSB-E15876m; Cusabio, Wuhan, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured and all samples were run in duplicates.

Mass Spectrometry (MS) analysis

MS analysis of the co-IP complexes with anti-Cdk5 antibody from mouse pituitaries was performed by APTBIO Co. (Shanghai, China). Peptides were analyzed using Q Exactive MS/ Easy nLC (Thermo, USA). MS/MS spectra were evaluated using the MASCOT engine embedded into Proteome Discoverer 1.4 (Thermo, USA) against the UniProt Mus musculus database (87890 sequences, downloaded on February 17, 2022). Potential phosphopeptide were identified by searching for phosphorylation of serine, threonine, and tyrosine. Tandem MS spectra were compared against the Mcm2 amino acid sequence, and phosphopeptides were manually confirmed.

Single-cell RNA sequencing

scRNA-seq was performed on pituitaries from two genotypes at the 14th and 40th weeks in four independent runs. A single-cell suspension containing 5,000–8,000 cells were loaded onto a 10x Genomics single-cell A chip. cDNA libraries were prepared using a 10x Genomics Single Cell 3’ Reagent Kit v3.1 and sequenced on a HiSeq X Ten instrument by SequMed Bio (Guangzhou, China). Raw data were processed using Cell Ranger v3.0.2 (10x Genomics) and aligned to the mouse reference genome (GRCm38/mm10). Expression matrices were analyzed using Seurat v4.0 in R environment [18]. A total of 24,754 cells were retained. Gene expression matrices were normalized and integrated using the SCTransform pipeline based on the top 3,000 variable genes [19], followed by principal component analysis and cell clustering. Nonlinear dimensional reduction was performed using UMAP on the top 40 principal components [20]. Differential expression analysis was performed using FindAllMarkers, and cell types were determined based on known markers in the Cell Marker database and literature [21]. The Slingshot package was used for pseudotime trajectory analyses to trace tumor progression [22]. Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) enrichment of upregulated genes was conducted using clusterProfiler v4.8.3 with a significance threshold of 0.05 [23].

RNAi or plasmids treatment

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) and plasmids containing cDNAs of Cdk5, Mcm2 and Mcm2 mutant were synthesized by Guangzhou Ige Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The transfection was performed according to the instructions of Lipofectamine 3000 Transfection Reagent (L3000150; Invitrogen, Shanghai, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions. All experiments were performed in triplicate. Plasmids and siRNA sequences are listed in the key resources table.

Viability assay

293T cells were seeded at 2,000 cells per well in 96-well plates and incubated overnight at 37 °C with 5 % CO2. After 12 h, cells were incubated with predetermined concentrations of ciprofloxacin or siRNA and incubated for 48 h. Cell proliferation was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) (CK04-05; Dojindo Laboratories, Japan) according to the kit instructions. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured, and IC50 values were calculated using GraphPad Prism.

Colony formation assay

293T cells were seeded at 500 cells per well in six-well plates (1,000 cells per well for transfected cells) and incubated at 37 °C with 5 % CO2 for 7 days. Depending on experimental requirements, the medium was supplemented with or without ciprofloxacin. After 7 days, cell colonies were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with crystal violet for 15 min. Plates was air-dried for clear visibility of colonies. Colony counting was performed using Fiji software (an extended version of ImageJ).

Results

Intermediate lobe-enlarged pituitary adenoma develops in p25 overexpressing Bi-Tg mouse

The p25-GFP Bi-Tg mouse model has been previously used to study neurodegeneration, with p25-GFP induced at postnatal 4–6 weeks [16]. Additionally, in this model administration of a Cdk5 inhibitory peptide can prevent neuronal loss and alleviate behavior changes [24].

In the present study, we crossed TetO-p25-GFP Tg mice with CamKII-tTA Tg mice, resulting in p25 over-expression (p25OE) from the embryonic stage in the absence of doxycycline. Notably, an abnormal enlarged pituitary gland was observed in 40-weeks-old p25OE mice compared to the WT controls (Fig. 1A). This observation aligns with previous findings where CMV promoter-driven p25 overexpression also led to pituitary enlargement [15]. The p25OE mice exhibited a significantly reduced survival rate (58.3 % vs. 100 % in WT mice, p = 0.0141) (Fig. 1B). To monitor the pituitary gland's growth, MRI scans were conducted at 28, 32, 36 and 40 weeks, revealing a gradual increase in pituitary volume in p25OE mice, significantly larger than WT at the 32nd (p = 0.0002), 36th (p < 0.0001) and 40th weeks (p < 0.0001) (Fig. 1C). MRI scans of a pair of mice over time further illustrated these changes (Fig. 1D). These results suggest that embryonic p25 overexpression induces pituitary gland enlargement, distinct from the neurodegenerative effects seen with postnatal p25 induction [16,24]. H&E staining of the enlarged pituitaries in p25OE mice revealed proliferative tumor cells, particularly numerous heterogeneous tumor cells in the cell division cycle at 40 week (Fig. 1E). Notably, proliferative tumor cells were first observed in the intermediate lobe at 12 week, with rapid proliferation as p25 expression continued, suggesting that p25 may specifically regulate cell growth in this region. Importantly, the tumor cell proliferation was suppressed when p25OE mice were treated with doxycycline from the 12th week (Fig. 1, Fig. 1), indicating that pituitary adenoma development was dependent on p25 expression. Collectively, the embryonic p25 expression resulted in pituitary adenoma in the intermediate lobe, in addition to the neuruodegenerative phenotype caused by postnatal p25 induction.

The pituitary adenoma synthesizes but does not secrete POMC-derived peptides

To further characterize the enlarged pituitary tumor, IF staining for Ki-67, a proliferation marker, revealed significantly more Ki-67 expression in the intermediate lobe of p25OE mice compared to WT mice, with Ki-67-possitive cells co-localized with p25-GFP (Fig. 2A). Immunohistochemistry staining confirmed the increased number of Ki-67-positive cells in p25OE mice at the 16th and 40th week (p = 0.005 and p < 0.001, respectively) (Fig. 2, Fig. 2), indicating that p25 overexpression promotes cell proliferative in the pituitary gland. As the pituitary gland is responsible for hormone production, we examined hormone expression in p25OE tumor cells (which shown as Ki-67-positive) using IHC. The tumor cells stained positively for ACTH, α-MSH, and β-endorphin, all of which are derived from the polypeptide precursor POMC, in 16-week-old p25OE mice (Fig. 2D). However, no positive staining was observed for other hormones like follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) or prolactin (PRL) in tumor cells (data not shown), suggesting that POMC-derived peptides were synthesized in the tumor. However, plasma levels of these POMC-peptides did not differ significantly between p25OE and WT mice (Fig. 2E), implying that the pituitary tumors in p25OE mice retain the ability to synthesize POMC-derived peptides but lost their secretory function, resembling nonfunctional pituitary adenomas in humans.

scRNA-seq reveals intermediate lobe-originated melanotropes as the tumor-proliferative cells with highly expression of MCM family genes

To explore the molecular mechanisms underlying p25-induced pituitary adenoma, we performed scRNA-seq on pituitary samples from WT and p25OE mice at the 14th and 40th weeks. Four pools of dissected pituitary tissues from three female mice for each group were processed, and single cells were captured using the 10x Genomics single-cell A chip. After library preparation and sequencing, the data were aligned to the mouse reference genome, yielding 24,754 high-quality cells. Gene expression matrices were normalized and integrated using the SCTransform pipeline and R package Harmony (v1.2.0), followed by principal component analysis and UMAP-based dimensional reduction [[18], [19], [20],25]. Thirteen cell clusters were identified based on known pituitary cell markers [21] and the Cell Marker database (Table S1, Fig. 3A and 3E). Notably, p25OE mice showed a significantly higher percentage of melanotropes at both 14 and 40 weeks compared to WT mice (Table S2, Fig. 3B), suggesting that p25 overexpression promotes melanotrope proliferation. These melanotropes highly expressed Pax7, a melanotrope-specific marker, and Pcna, a marker of tumor progression (Fig. 3C–E), indicating active proliferation. The percentage of Pcna expressing melanotropes was significantly higher in p25OE mice than that in WT, supporting the idea that melanotropes are the tumor progenitors in p25OE mice (Fig. 4A). To investigate the evolution of melanotropes during the pituitary adenoma development, we re-analyzed the scRNA-seq data for melanotropes of both p25OE and WT mice. Melanotropes were further divided into subclusters, and slingshot analysis revealed two distinct developmental trajectories: one hormone secretion-related (from pcna− to pcna+3, both at week 14 and 40), and the other tumor proliferation-related (from pcna− to pcna+1 at week 14 and to pcna+4 at week 40) (Table S3, Fig. 4B). These findings were supported by IHC staining of pituitary tumors, showing PCNA positive proliferating cells and ACTH positive-secreting cells were different populations (Fig. 4C). KEGG analysis of the Pcna+ melanotropes indicated enrichment in cell cycle and DNA replication pathways, with high expression of Mcm2, Mcm3, Mcm5, Mcm6, Mcm7, and Pcna (Table S4, Fig. 4D, F). The higher expression of MCM proteins was confirmed by WB analysis (Fig. 4G). These data suggest that pituitary adenoma in p25OE mice originates from proliferating intermediate lobe melanotropes, which overexpress MCM family genes.

Cdk5 phosphorylates Mcm2 at Ser27 to enhance cell proliferation

Given that p25 is a specific activator of Cdk5 [11], we hypothesized that p25-induced Cdk5 activation might phosphorylate potential substrates and promote tumor growth. To identify specific phosphorylated substrates, we performed MS analysis of anti-Cdk5 IP complex from the pituitary glands of p25OE and WT mice at the 40th week. MS identified a rich profile of Mcm2 peptides in p25OE mice, but not in WT mice, with phosphorylated site at Ser27 (Fig.5A and Table S5). The phosphorylation residues of the Mcm2 at Ser27 is contained in the conserved domain of the Cdk5 substrate (Ser/Thr)-Pro-X-(Arg/Lys/His) [29]. This suggests that Mcm2 is a potential phosphorylation target of Cdk5. Co-immunoprecipitation confirmed the interaction between endogenous Cdk5 and Mcm2 in mouse pituitary tissue (Fig. 5B). Further co-IP experiments with FLAG-tagged Cdk5 and myc-tagged Mcm2 in 293T cells demonstrated Cdk5-Mcm2 binding (Figs. 5C, S1B). Since phosphorylated form of Mcm2-Ser27 is crucial for initiating DNA replication and the ATPase activity of the MCM complex [30,31], we assessed Mcm2 and p-Mcm2(Ser27) levels in mouse and human pituitary adenomas. Both forms of Mcm2 were significantly upregulated in p25OE mice compared to controls (Figs. 5D, S1C). IHC staining of human pituitary adenomas showed high expression of p-Mcm2(Ser27), Mcm2, CDK5 and p35 in both functional and nonfunctional adenomas (Fig. 5E, Tables 1 and S6). To delineate the effect of the Cdk5-Mcm2 pathway on cell proliferation, we first inhibited Mcm2 with ciprofloxacin, an MCM family inhibitor [32], in 293T cells, which significantly reduced cell viability and colony formation at 60 µM and 120 µM concentrations (Fig. S1D and E). Similarly, MCM2 knockdown via siRNA suppressed cell proliferation (Fig.5F–H). CDK5 knockdown also inhibited cell proliferation, as shown by siCDK5-p (Figs. 5I, S1 F and H), with a corresponding reduction in p-MCM2(Ser27) levels (Fig.S1G). To test if MCM2 mediates CDK5 effects on proliferation, we overexpressed WT MCM2 or mutant MCM2-S27A in siCDK5-treated 293T cells. Colony formation was rescued by WT MCM2 but not by the S27A mutant (Fig. 5J), indicating that p25-activated CDK5 promotes cell proliferation through phosphorylating MCM2, the core member of the MCM complex.

Discussion

In this study, we developed a pituitary adenoma mouse model induced by embryonic expression of p25. The resulting tumor is located in the melanotropes of pituitary gland and produces POMC peptides without secretory function. Importantly, we found that p25-activated Cdk5 promotes pituitary adenoma tumorigenesis by phosphorylating Mcm2 at Ser27. This activation enhances MCM family protein activity and drives cell proliferation. Interestingly, p25 overexpression leads to two developmental trajectories in the intermediate lobe: one pathway results in Pcnalow melanotropes with secretory functions, while the other generates Pcnahigh melanotropes with proliferative activity (Fig. 4B).

The murine pituitary consists of anterior, intermediate, and posterior lobes, while the human pituitary mainly comprises adenohypophyseal hormone-secreting neuroendocrine cells and posterior lobe pituicytes, with very few intermediate lobe cells remaining [33,34]. Pituitary adenomas can arise from various cell types [35]. We demonstrated that embryonic expression of p25, which activates CDK5, induces pituitary adenoma in the intermediate lobe, producing POMC-derived peptides. Our findings align with other studies where transgenic mice expressing POMC-promoter-driven SV40 large T antigen, p27 and p18 also developed pituitary adenomas originating from the intermediate lobe [[6], [7], [8], [9]]. Using scRNA-seq, we characterized the evolutionary trajectory of melanotropes during p25-induced tumorigenesis, identifying two distinct paths: one leading to hormone-secreting melanotropes and the other to Pcna+ proliferative cells. Notably, Pcna+ melanotropes co-expressed the proneural gene Ascl1 and highly expressed MCM family genes, which may contribute to the proliferation of pituitary gland. ASCL1 is crucial in the early pituitary development and differentiation of corticotropes and gonadotropes in mice, and plays a role in stem cells in humans [33,36]. Additionally, MCM family proteins are highly expressed in neuroendocrine prostate cancer, corroborating our finding that Ascl1 and MCM genes are upregulated in proliferative Pax7+ melanotropes.

Cdk5, a serine/threonine kinase, phosphorylates numerous substrates and has been implicated in various human cancers [37]. The critical substrates include Rb in medullary thyroid carcinoma [38], KDR in invasive prolactin pituitary adenomas, TRIM59 in glioblastoma [39], STAT3 in prostate cancer [40], and PI3K isoform A in glioblastoma [41]. Here, we identified Mcm2 as a novel Cdk5 target in our pituitary adenoma model. While previous studies have shown a correlation between Mcm2 and Cdk5 in MPA-induced gastric cancer cell [42], no direct association between Cdk5 and Mcm2 has been established. Our data provide the first evidence that Cdk5 can phosphorylate Mcm2 and promote pituitary tumorigenesis.

MCM family proteins, consisting of six members (Mcm2-7), form a heterohexameric complex that acts as a key replication enzyme and is crucial for initiating DNA synthesis in eukaryotes. Mcm2, a core member, is phosphorylated at Ser27, Ser41, and Ser139 by Cdc7, which is critical for DNA replication and ATPase activity of the MCM complex in cancer cells [43]. In our study, Mcm2 was the only identified MCM member phosphorylated at Ser27 by Cdk5, but not Cdc7, in p25OE mice, suggesting that Ser27 phosphorylation is crucial for activating the MCM complex and DNA synthesis. Our in vitro experiments showed that silencing Cdk5 or Mcm2 reduced 293T cell proliferation, and the Mcm2 S27A mutant could not rescue the proliferation and colony formation reductions caused by siCDK5. In human pituitary adenomas, CDK5 and MCM2 are significantly upregulated [13,14,44]. Similarly, we found high expression of p35, CDK5, Mcm2, and p-Mcm2(Ser27) in various human pituitary adenomas, both functional and non-functional. This suggests that the Cdk5/p25-Mcm2-MCM pathway may play a critical role in human pituitary adenomas development.

In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that Cdk5/p25 phosphorylates Mcm2 at Ser27 to enhance MCM family proteins and promote cell proliferation, thus contributes to the intermediate lobe-originated pituitary tumorigenesis in p25OE mice. However, how and why CDK5 activation of the MCM complex specifically drives the proliferation of intermediate lobe-localized melanotropes warrants further investigation.

Retear after Rotator Cuff Repair
Authors: Aldo Fransiskus Marsetio
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Fixation failure is still being a main concern after a repair of rotator cuff tear. Risk factors for retear after rotator cuff repair may rise from the patient side and surgeon side. Non-modifiable risk factors from the patient side include: age, fatty infiltration, muscle atrophy, chronicity and time to surgery, tear size and retraction, critical shoulder angle and acromiohumeral interval. Meanwhile, some factors on the patients are modifiable, include: diabetes mellitus, hyperlipidemia, high body mass index, bone mineral density, smoking habit, exercise and work level. On the other hand, surgery also play important role in determining the prognosis of the repair, namely arthroscopic VS open, suture technique, biological augmentation, and corticosteroid injection. Additionally, immobilization period, ROM exercise and strengthening exercise may also affect the risk of retear. By understanding the contributing risk factors, it is expected that surgeon can prevent or overcome the retear.

Cardiovascular Outcomes Among Patients with COPD Prescribed with LABA or LAMA: A Real-World Territory Wide Study
Authors: Chun Ka Wong, Eugene C. C. Cheng, Ali Choo, Chung Ki Tsui, Audrey Tsznam Ko, Ting Fung Ma, Hung-Fat Tse, James Chung Man Ho, Wang Chun Kwok
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThis study aims to compare cardiovascular outcomes among patients who received long-acting beta-agonist (LABA) and long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA) and thereby better inform the choice of pharmacotherapy prescription for patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).MethodA real-world territory-wide retrospective cohort study using electronic data patient databases in Hong Kong was conducted. Patients with COPD without cardiovascular disease who were new users of LABA or LAMA from public hospitals in Hong Kong between 2010 and 2019 were identified and were followed up for 3 years after treatment initiation. Incidences of cardiovascular events were compared. Propensity score matching was employed to match important patient characteristics and clinical features.ResultsAfter the propensity score matching, 5020 patients were included: 2510 treated with LABA and 2510 with LAMA. At 3-year follow-up, 19.6% of patients experienced cardiovascular events. LAMA treatment was associated with a higher incidence of cardiovascular events compared to LABA (20.2% vs 19.0%, adjusted hazard ratio [aHR] 1.14, p = 0.04). This difference was primarily driven by increased risks of new-onset atrial fibrillation (7.93% vs 6.53%, aHR 1.30, p = 0.01). Incidence rates were similar between groups for ventricular tachycardia and fibrillation (0.52% vs 0.80%, p = 0.32), acute coronary syndrome (4.06% vs 3.82%, p = 0.33), ischemic stroke (2.39% vs 2.59%, p = 0.98), and heart failure hospitalization (10.8% vs 9.84%, p = 0.05).ConclusionPatients with COPD treated with LAMA might have higher incidence of atrial fibrillation when compared with LABA but not other cardiovascular events.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12325-025-03375-5.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a progressive respiratory condition that poses significant health burdens across the world [1]. Long-acting inhaled bronchodilators, namely long-acting beta-agonist (LABA) and long-acting muscarinic antagonist (LAMA), are currently the key therapies in COPD management. Their clinical efficacies to improve lung function, quality of life, and exacerbations have been demonstrated in several randomized clinical trials [2, 3]. The choice of using LABA or LAMA monotherapy, or LABA/LAMA dual therapy, or in combination with inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) is based on individuals’ symptoms and risk of exacerbation according to the Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease (GOLD) recommendations [4]. The latest GOLD recommendation states that patients with group A COPD should be treated with a bronchodilator, but it does not specify if LAMA or LABA should be used.

The use of LABA and LAMA has been found to be associated with increased cardiovascular risks in several real-world studies. Large-scale observational studies demonstrated that patients with COPD with recent initiation of LABA or LAMA therapy had an increased risk of developing severe cardiovascular events [5, 6]. Given that patients with COPD are prone to cardiovascular comorbidities and death [5], cardiovascular safety is therefore an important factor to consider when determining which bronchodilator to prescribe. This study aims to contribute to the existing body of evidence by further examining their comparative cardiovascular safety profile and thereby to promote informed prescription decisions in clinical practice.

Methods

Study Design and Data Source

We performed a real-world territory-wide retrospective cohort study to compare the cardiovascular outcomes in adult patients with COPD who were newly prescribed with LABA versus LAMA monotherapy. We used patient databases retrieved from Clinical Data Analysis and Reporting System (CDARS), a territory-wide electronic registry managed by the Hong Kong Hospital Authority, who is the sole public healthcare provider in Hong Kong. CDARS contains patients’ health records from all public healthcare services in Hong Kong, including diagnosis, procedures performed, medication dispensing and prescribing history, laboratory results, and demographic information.

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the University of Hong Kong and Hospital Authority Hong Kong West Cluster (Approval number UW 22-639). Informed consent was waived as it was a retrospective study without active patient recruitment, and all retrieved clinical data were already de-identified. The study was conducted in accordance with the declaration of Helsinki.

Study Population

Adult patients with COPD aged 18 or above, who have made at least one outpatient visit from 1 January 2010 to 31 December 2019 were identified. Only new users of LABA or LAMA within that period were included in the analysis. New users were defined as patients with no prior prescription record of LABA or LAMA in CDARS. Patients with the following characteristics were excluded: 1. prescribed with short-acting bronchodilators only, 2. prescribed with LABA and LAMA dual therapy, 3. switched therapy between LABA and LAMA during the study period, 4. prescribed with ICS alone or ICS with long-acting bronchodilators, 5. without any bronchodilator prescriptions, 6. had coexisting asthma and bronchiectasis, 7. had established cardiovascular diseases (i.e., acute coronary syndrome (ACS), ischemic stroke, atrial fibrillation (AF), ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation, and heart failure) since we hope to identify whether LABA or LAMA is associated with new-onset adverse cardiovascular events. Subjects were followed up for 3 years for the occurrence of study outcomes starting from LABA or LAMA treatment initiation.

Baseline Characteristics

We collected patients' baseline demographic data including age and sex, as well as baseline medical conditions including COPD, hypertension, diabetes mellitus and the history of non-invasive ventilation and mechanical ventilation use. The following underlying diseases were excluded: asthma, bronchiectasis and established cardiovascular diseases (ACS, ischemic stroke, heart failure, AF, ventricular tachycardia, ventricular fibrillation) [7]. Drug prescriptions including aspirin, warfarin, non-vitamin K anticoagulants, and statin were also extracted. All collected information was coded using International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision (ICD-9) and International Classification of Primary Care (ICPC) codes (Supplementary Table S1). The diagnostic code of COPD in CDARS was validated with overall positive predictive value of 81.5% (95% CI 76.1%–86.9%) [8]. In the validation process, a valid case was defined as the presence of COPD symptoms together with airflow limitation (i.e. FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.7) demonstrated in spirometry which was not be fully reversed.

Propensity Score Matching

We performed propensity score matching using the MatchIt package in R (version 4.5.4). Patients receiving LABA were matched 1:1 to those receiving LAMA using greedy nearest-neighbor matching without replacement, based on a logistic regression model including the following covariates: age, sex, history of smoking, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, non-invasive ventilation, mechanical ventilation, CKD, plus the use of aspirin, warfarin, non-vitamin K oral anticoagulant, and statin [9]. A standardized mean difference of < 0.2 after matching was considered satisfactory.

Study Outcomes

The primary outcome is composite cardiovascular outcome comprising ACS, ischemic stroke, AF, ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation, and heart failure hospitalization at 3 years. Secondary outcomes include individual components of the cardiovascular events at 3 years.

Statistical Analysis

Patient demographics and baseline medical conditions were described using actual frequency, and mean ± standard deviation (SD), with comparison between patients in LABA and LAMA group done by independent t test or Mann–Whitney U test. The incidence of cardiovascular outcomes in patients at 3 years was documented. Time-to-event analysis was performed using Kaplan–Meier estimates. Univariate and multivariate regression with Cox proportional hazard model was used to compute hazard ratio (HR), adjusted HR (aHR), and their 95% confidence intervals (95% CI). The HR was compared between the two groups, with the LABA group being the reference group. Confounding variates were identified by univariate Cox regression on the propensity score-matched population. The identified variates, including age, CKD, and use of aspirin and statin, were then adjusted in multivariate Cox regression. Forest plot of subgroup analysis was generated using SubgrPlots v0.1.3. All data analyses were performed using R v4.0.3. Kaplan–Meier curves were generated using lifelines v0.19.5 and matplotlib v3.7.1 with python v3.11.7 [10]. P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Study Population

In the period from 1 January 2010 to 31 December 2019, a total of 71,489 adult patients with COPD had follow-up visits under the HKHA. Among them, 3239 patients had coexisting asthma or bronchiectasis, 6896 received both LABA and LAMA, 17,251 were not prescribed any bronchodilators, 24,823 patients were prescribed COPD therapies other than LABA or LAMA, 7922 had missing medical records, and 942 had established cardiovascular diseases. Excluding them gives a total of 10,416 eligible subjects, with 2510 on LABA and 7906 on LAMA.

The LAMA group had a higher proportion of female patients (p = 0.003) and aspirin users (p < 0.001), and were older (p < 0.001) than patients in the LABA group. To balance the covariates, 1:1 propensity score matching was performed, resulting in 5020 patients included in final analysis with 2510 patients in each treatment group (Fig. 1). In the propensity matched cohort (n = 5020), the mean age was 72.8 ± 9.1 years, 8.01% of the patients were female. Cardiovascular Events

Overall, an alarmingly large proportion of patients developed cardiovascular events at 3 years (19.6%). Patients treated with LAMA were associated with a higher incidence of cardiovascular events, with 506 (20.2%) patients in the LAMA group and 477 (19.0%) patients in the LABA group developing the primary composite outcome (HR 1.15, 95% CI 1.02–1.31, p = 0.03). After adjustment for confounding factors, the association remains with aHR 1.14 (95% CI 1.01–1.29, p = 0.04) (Table 2 and Fig. 2). Arrhythmia

Further analysis revealed that the higher incidence of cardiovascular event in the LAMA group was primarily driven by new-onset AF. In total 363 (7.23%) patients developed incident AF at 3 years, with 164 (6.53%) in the LABA group and 199 (7.93%) in the LAMA group (HR 1.31, 95% CI 1.07–1.61, p = 0.01). Multivariate analysis confirmed that the LAMA group had a higher risk of incident AF with aHR 1.30 (95% CI 1.06–1.60, p = 0.01) (Table 2 and Fig. 3). Increased AF risk was consistently observed across key subgroups (Supplementary Fig. S1). As a beta-adrenergic blocker is commonly used in patients with AF, further analysis was performed to compare their use in the two treatment groups. On the other hand, there is no significant difference in the incidence of ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation between the two groups. Thirty-three (0.66%) patients developed ventricular tachyarrhythmia during the follow-up period, with 20 (0.80%) in the LABA group and 13 (0.52%) in the LAMA group (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.35–1.41, p = 0.32) (Table 2).

Acute Coronary Syndrome

A total of 198 (3.94%) patients developed ACS during the follow-up period, with 96 (3.82%) in the LABA group and 102 (4.06%) in the LAMA group. The HR for ACS was 1.16 (95% CI 0.88–1.54, p = 0.28) for the LAMA group, suggesting similar risks of ACS among patients prescribed with LABA or LAMA (Table 2).

Ischemic Stroke

A total of 125 (2.49%) patients developed ischemic stroke during the follow-up period, with 65 (2.59%) in the LABA group and 60 (2.39%) in the LAMA group. The HR was 1.00 (95% CI 0.71–1.43, p = 0.98) for the LAMA group, suggesting similar risks of CVA among patients prescribed with LABA or LAMA (Table 2).

Heart Failure Hospitalization

A total of 517 (10.3%) patients developed CHF during the follow-up period, with 247 (9.84%) in the LABA group and 270 (10.8%) in the LAMA group. The HR was 1.19 (95% CI 1.00–1.41, p = 0.05) for the LAMA group, suggesting similar risks of heart failure hospitalization among patients prescribed with LABA or LAMA (Table 2).

Discussion

We conducted a real-world population-based study of 5020 adult patients with COPD without underlying cardiovascular diseases in Hong Kong to investigate the comparative cardiovascular risks associated with LABA versus LAMA. We show that patients with COPD treated with LABA or LAMA had an overall high incidence of cardiovascular events. Patients treated with LAMA had a higher incidence of cardiovascular events than those treated with LABA, primarily driven by a higher rate of new-onset AF. The incidence of all other cardiovascular events analyzed was similar between the two treatment groups.

COPD and Cardiovascular Disease

COPD and cardiovascular disease commonly occur together [5, 11, 12], as both disease entities have shared risk factors—particularly cigarette smoking. In addition, COPD leads to systemic inflammation [13], which may exacerbate cardiovascular conditions such as atherosclerosis [14]. Previous studies reported that 14.7–18.2% patients with COPD had concomitant cardiovascular diseases [15, 16]. In our study, we included patients with no known cardiovascular disease. Strikingly, 19.6% of patients in our cohort developed new-onset cardiovascular complications, highlighting the need to optimize comprehensive cardiovascular care for patients with COPD.

Evidence from Previous Studies on LABA vs LAMA

Previous studies reported conflicting data on the risk of cardiovascular events among patients with COPD treated with LAMA versus LABA. Previous large-scale observational studies from the UK and Taiwan did not reveal differences in the rate of cardiovascular events between the two treatment options [17, 18]. However, there were reports of combinational COPD therapies with LAMA being associated with a higher cardiovascular risk when compared to control [19]. Nevertheless, these studies mostly involved patients who were taking combination therapy, including inhaled corticoid steroid, which itself may exacerbate cardiovascular condition [20]. To provide more robust data to address the clinical knowledge gap, we performed a head-to-head comparison involving patients taking LAMA or LABA monotherapy only, without the confounding effect of alternative therapies. In addition, we excluded all patients with prior cardiovascular or cerebrovascular diseases to reduce confounding factors and to facilitate detection of new-onset AF.

In randomized controlled trials, cardiovascular safety of LABA and LAMA was also tested. In a post hoc analysis of the UPLIFT® study, cardiovascular events were not increased among patients treated with LAMA [21] but a trend towards increased risks of arrhythmia was noted. The percentage of patients developing arrhythmia in the UPLIFT® study was similar to what was reported in our cohort, while the rate of heart failure was higher in our cohort than what was reported in the UPLIFT® study.

In a post hoc analysis of the 3-year TORCH dataset, the risk of cardiovascular events in patients with moderate to severe COPD was not elevated among the patients treated with salmeterol alone or in combination [22]. The 3-year cardiovascular event rates from the TORCH dataset were also similar to our cohort.

Association Between LAMA Use and Incident AF

Overall, the incidence of new-onset AF is high among patients with COPD requiring long-acting bronchodilator therapies. At 7.23% per year, the incidence is significantly higher than that in the general population and other patient groups [23]. The high prevalence and incidence of AF among patients with COPD are attributed to a number of pathogenic mechanisms [24]. As autonomous nervous system agents, LAMAs might lead to sympathetic overactivation which could result in AF development [17]. As demonstrated in our cohort, the prevalence of risk factors related to the development of AF is high among patients with COPD, including advanced age, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and cigarette smoking [25]. Furthermore, there were experimental data demonstrating a possible role of hypoxia in triggering atrial fibrillation [26].

In our cohort, we observed a higher incidence of AF among patients treated with LAMA when compared with LABA (7.93% vs 6.53%, aHR 1.30). It is well known that dysregulation of the autonomous nervous system will increase AF risk [27]. Although direct inhibition of muscarinic receptors in the atrial myocardium is less likely owing to trivial systemic absorption of LAMA from the airway [28], it is uncertain whether LAMA may cause indirect disruption to the autonomous nervous system as previously reported by researchers [29], thereby increasing AF risk. The association of LAMA use with proinflammatory state is another plausible mechanism by which it may increase AF risk [30–32]. Further clinical and preclinical studies are required to further evaluate the pathogenic mechanisms involved.

Clinical Implication

Overall, patients with COPD taking long-acting bronchodilators have high prevalence and incidence of AF [33, 34], particularly those receiving LAMA. As most patients with COPD carry risk factors for developing AF-associated cardioembolic stroke and cardiovascular mortality [33, 35], it is important for clinicians from respiratory, family, and emergency medicine, who manage most of the patients with COPD, to understand which subset of patients require anticoagulation therapy for stroke prevention according to the CHA2DS2-VASc criteria [36, 37].

Beyond AF, findings from our study and others demonstrated no increase in risks of ACS, ischemic stroke, ventricular tachyarrhythmia, and heart failure with LAMA use [38, 39]. As LAMA in patients with COPD was linked to improved symptoms, quality of life, lung function, and reduced exacerbations [2], it is still advisable to use LAMA in accordance with the latest recommendation from guidelines [4].

There are several limitations in our study. First, observations from our retrospective study represent associations rather than definitive causative relationships. Prospective studies, particularly randomized trials, are required to obtain definitive conclusions regarding the role of LAMA in triggering cardiovascular events. Second, most patients involved in the study cohort were of Chinese ethnicity, which may limit the generalizability of the study results. In addition, certain etiologies such as alpha antitrypsin deficiency were underrepresented among the Chinese population. Third, lung function test parameters were not available for retrieval. Addition of spirometry results may further improve the propensity score matching process. The symptoms burden of the patients (e.g., COPD assessment test and Modified Medical Research Council Dyspnea Scale) was not available in CDARS. This might introduce bias as the symptom burden might affect the choice of bronchodilator, although the GOLD recommendations did not specify which single LABA or LAMA bronchodilator should be preferred as the initial agent of choice. Fourth, data about drug compliance and cumulative dosages were not included in this study.

Conclusion

Patients with COPD treated with LAMA were associated with a higher incidence of atrial fibrillation when compared with LABA. Otherwise, no increase in other cardiovascular events was observed.

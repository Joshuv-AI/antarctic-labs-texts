Teaching surgeons to question the evidence: should surgical meta-research be embedded early in undergraduate and residency training?

Why teach surgical meta-research early?

The development of soft skills in medical students and residents has been described as a key component in the training of professionals within surgical fields. While emphasis has been placed on general research-related skills, such as clinical research training, scientific writing, interpersonal competencies, and resource management, a significant and underexplored gap remains in the ability to critically appraise scientific evidence.

Embedding surgical meta-research during undergraduate and residency training aligns with Miller’s “knows-how/shows-how” levels: learners audit real trials, quantify bias, and design corrective strategies[5]. Active surgical meta-research tasks outclass passive EBS lectures and dismantle the hidden curriculum that often prioritizes senior opinion over data. Moreover, surgical meta-research naturally nurtures open-science practices (protocol registration or data sharing) that funders and regulators increasingly require[6]. These arguments align with several propositions put forward by The Lancet Commission on Global Surgery regarding the strengthening of academic surgery and the evidence base in surgical practice.

The objective of these action-oriented approaches lies in identifying key elements required to promote transparency, quality, and scientific relevance in surgical research, while enabling medical students and residents to strengthen specific skills. For this reason, the approach to each core component should be tailored to the needs and guidelines of the curricula.

Implementation tips

As established by meta-research standards[1,4], this is a rigorous process that must be conducted step by step, given that the critical appraisal of scientific evidence requires the examination of specific conceptual, clinical, and methodological domains from which surgical evidence emerges[1,4]. This rationale served as one of the key arguments that distinguished surgical meta-research from traditional meta-research[1,4]. More importantly, it supports the practical implementation of surgical meta-research within the learning process and related activities. In conclusion, surgical meta-research has evolved from concept to an indispensable educational tool. Integrating its methods at the earliest stages of surgical training will forge clinicians capable of interrogating, generating, and applying high-quality evidence[10]. We invite curriculum committees and surgical societies to adopt the surgical meta-research actions summarized in Figure 1 and to monitor their impact on forthcoming students’ cohorts.

Through the arguments presented and the proposed roadmap, this correspondence introduces an innovative approach to incorporating the training and conceptualization of surgical meta-research into medical students and residents’ education, with the aim of strengthening competencies in critical appraisal and the translation of knowledge applicable to surgery.

This analysis proposes an educational framework rather than reporting outcomes from a controlled implementation. Effects may vary by institutional resources, mentorship availability, and baseline research culture. Some activities (e.g., registry use or data-sharing workflows) may require additional training time. Future work should prospectively evaluate competency gains, reproducibility literacy, and downstream effects on trial design and reporting.

Bactericidal activity and Inhibition of levofloxacin-induced resistance by antibacterial combination against hypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae
Authors: Alimire Aimaiti, Qingqing Xu, Li Ding, Yi Li, Dan Li, Jinshan Suo, Xiaoyu Zhao, Xiaogang Xu, Minggui Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHypervirulent Klebsiella pneumoniae (hvKP) infection is characterized by its potential severity and metastatic propensity, underlying the importance to employ bactericidal treatment promptly for infection source control. The aim of this study is to investigate the bactericidal activity of antibacterial combination against hvKP, with a particular focus on whether levofloxacin-based combinations can suppress levofloxacin-induced efflux pump related drug resistance.MethodsWe conducted in vitro antimicrobial susceptibility tests on 12 hvKP clinical isolates and the reference strain NTUH-K2044. Checkerboard assays were conducted to evaluate the combination activity of levofloxacin with amikacin or ceftazidime. Time-kill experiments were performed to determining the bactericidal activities of levofloxacin, amikacin, ceftazidime, and the levofloxacin-based combinations against NTUH-K2044. Additionally, the mechanisms underlying induced quinolone resistance were investigated.ResultsTwelve clinical hvKP isolates were highly susceptible to levofloxacin, amikacin, and ceftazidime. The levofloxacin–amikacin and levofloxacin–ceftazidime combinations both demonstrated antibacterial activities as indifference. Time-kill experiments showed that an eight-fold or lower minimal inhibitory concentration (≤ 8×MIC) of levofloxacin, ≤ 1×MIC of amikacin and of ceftazidime, exhibited bactericidal activity against NTUH-K2044 during the initial 1–4 h with various levels of bacterial counts decreasing followed by regrowth. The regrowth samples had high levofloxacin MICs indicating the occurrence of induced resistance. Real-time PCR and wild-type oqxR complementation demonstrated point mutations in oqxR of NTUH-K2044, resulting in an overexpression of the efflux pump encoding gene of oqxAB. Both the levofloxacin–amikacin and levofloxacin–ceftazidime combinations exhibited bactericidal synergy without bacterial regrowth when they were tested with 2×MIC of levofloxacin combined with either 1/2×MIC of amikacin or 1×MIC of ceftazidime.ConclusionsThese results demonstrated that levofloxacin combined with amikacin or ceftazidime exhibited superior bactericidal activities compared to levofloxacin against hvKP and might prevent levofloxacin-induced resistance related to oqxAB overexpression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12941-025-00826-3.Keywords: Klebsiella pneumoniae, Levofloxacin, Amikacin, Ceftazidime, Bactericidal activity, oqxAB, oqxR

Background

Klebsiella pneumoniae, a clinically important pathogen, has been categorized into classical K. pneumoniae (cKP) and hypervirulent K. pneumoniae (hvKP) based on their distinct phenotypic characteristics [1–3]. hvKP strains was first reported as the causative agent of community-acquired pyogenic liver abscess in 1986 [4]. Described as a virulent pathogen, hvKP is characterized by its ability to exhibit subsequent metastatic spread to the eye, lung, and central nervous system (CNS), which is atypical for other members of the Enterobacteriaceae family, including the cKP [2, 5]. Compared to cKP, hvKP strains are susceptible to commonly used antibiotics including cephalosporins, aminoglycosides and fluoroquinolones [2, 5, 6]. For liver (or other intra-abdominal) abscess or pneumonia, third-generation cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones and aminoglycosides were recommended in empiric suggestions [5, 7]. Levofloxacin, a member of the typical fluoroquinolones with low protein binding and extensive tissue distribution, is recommended for treating liver (or other intra-abdominal) and prostate abscesses, as well as pneumonia resulting from hvKP infection [2, 8]. Furthermore, several studies demonstrated that levofloxacin can attain adequate vitreous concentrations following oral or combined topical administration [9, 10].

hvKP-related invasive syndrome, bacteremic dissemination associated endophthalmitis, CNS involvement, and necrotizing fasciitis, contribute to mortality rates ranging from 3% to 31% [5, 7]. And more than 70% of patients with ocular or CNS metastasis may develop long-term visual or neurological disabilities [5]. Thus, it is advisable to employ initial effective treatment for infection source control and having a better prognosis [2, 4, 7]. A retrospective analysis showed that combination antimicrobial therapy was associated with greater initial appropriate therapy in patients with severe sepsis caused by gram-negative bacilli [11]. A clinical benefit with combination therapy was also seen in neutropenic patients with bacteremia [12]. Al-Hasan et al. reported that combination therapy including fluoroquinolone and a β-lactam was associated with a survival advantage in less severely ill patients with Gram-negative bacilli bacteremia [13]. Additionally, another study showed that β-lactam and aminoglycoside combinations could decrease mortality in critically ill patients with Klebsiella species bacteremia [14]. Thus, in order to control infection source and achieve a better prognosis in serious hvKP infections, early and effective antibiotic combination therapy is crucial.

Levofloxacin was the most frequently used fluoroquinolone in combination therapy in gram-negative bacilli related bacteremia [13]. One reason for levofloxacin combination may be related to the wide mutant selection window (MSW) value and high mutant prevention concentration/minimum inhibitory concentration (MPC/MIC) ratio of levofloxacin, which implied the higher possibility of enrichment of resistant subpopulation [15, 16]. Similarly, Rodrı´guez-Martı´nez observed induced-resistance in K. pneumoniae when exposed to 4×MIC ciprofloxacin [17]. It is also reported that addition of a second antibiotic to fluoroquinolone treatment regime has been shown to lower its MPC [16, 18]. Further, a study conducted by Zhanel reported that the MPC/MIC ratio of levofloxacin was reduced when combined with ceftazidime against Pseudomonas aeruginosa [18]. However there is limited research on the in vitro antibacterial activity of levofloxacin or a levofloxacin-based combination against hvKP-related infections.

The aim of this study is to investigate the bactericidal activity of antibacterial combiantion against hvKP, with a particular focus on whether levofloxacin-amikacin and levofloxacin-ceftazidime combinations can suppress levofloxacin-induced drug resistance. It is expected that our findings will be helpful for the clinical management of hvKP infections.

Methods

Molecular characteristics of isolates

A total of 12 clinical hvKP isolates were collected between 2019 and 2020 from Huashan Hospital in Shanghai, China. These isolates were collected from patients with community-acquired liver and CNS abscesses, pneumonia, bacteremia, and endophthalmitis. Strain hypermucoviscosity was identified using the string test. Whole genome sequencing was performed on 12 clinical isolates. And virulence-encoding genes including rmpA, rmpA2, iucA, iroB and peg-344 were identified using BLASTn search of draft genome assemblies with a minimum identity of 80% [2, 7]. KL type and multilocus sequence typing (MLST) were analyzed using Kleborate (v2.3.2) [19]. Core genome single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were mapped to the NTUH-K2044 (NC_012731.1) using Snippy (v4.6.0, https://github.com/tseemann/Snippy), and recombination sites were inferred using Gubbins (v1.4.9). Phylogenetic trees were constructed with FastTree (v2.1.11) to generate maximum likelihood trees, which were subsequently visualized and annotated using chiplot (www.chiplot.online).

Antibacterial susceptibility testing

Antibacterial susceptibility testing was performed against the 12 isolates and the reference strain NTUH-K2044 [20], with levofloxacin (standard powder was provided by Daichi Sankyo, Shanghai), amikacin and ceftazidime (standard powders from National Institutes for Food and Drug Control, Beijing, China), by broth microdilution method according to the CLSI guideline [21]. Additionally, the efflux pump inhibition tests with PAβN (25 mg/L) were performed using derived strains of NTUH-K2044 (Table 2).

Checkerboard assays

Checkerboard assays were conducted in duplicate on separate days to evaluate the combination activity of levofloxacin with amikacin or ceftazidime against these 12 clinical isolates and NTUH-K2044. The fractional inhibitory concentration index (FICI) of this combination against each isolate was calculated as follows: FICI index = (MIC of drug A in combination / MIC of drug A alone) + (MIC of drug B in combination / MIC of drug B alone). The interpretation of FICI was as follows: FICI ≤ 0.5, synergism; FICI >0.5 and ≤ 4, indifference; FICI >4, antagonism [22].

Determination of the MPC and levofloxacin-related mutation frequency

The MPC of levofloxacin was determined with a method described by H. Nordqvist [23], with modification. The NTUH-K2044 strain was cultured on Mueller-Hinton (MH) agar and incubated at 37 °C overnight. Subsequently, the bacterial cells were transferred to MH broth and incubated overnight, reached about 109 CFU/mL naturally. A volume of 100 µL bacterial suspension was plated onto MH agar plates containing levofloxacin in two-fold dilution (1×MIC – 64×MIC). MPC was defined as the lowest concentration of levofloxacin that inhibited all visible growth after 48 h of incubation at 37 °C. Mutational frequencies for each antibiotic MIC multiple were calculated by dividing the number of mutant colonies by the initial bacterial inoculum [18]. All experiments were conducted in triplicate.

Time–kill experiments

The hvKP clinical isolates shared similar molecular characteristics and antibiotic susceptibility with reference strain NTUH-K2044. Therefore, the bactericidal activities of levofloxacin, amikacin, ceftazidime, and the levofloxacin-based combinations were determined using time-kill experiments in NTUH-K2044. Samples were collected at 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 24 h after the antibacterial addition according to the procedures described in a previous study [24]. The limit of detection (LOD) was set at 100 CFU/mL. Bactericidal activity was defined as a ≥ 3 log10 CFU/mL reduction compared to the initial inoculum at the 24th h, while synergy was considered when there was a ≥ 2 log10 CFU/mL reduction for the antibiotic combinations compared to their more active constituent [25].

Gene sequencing related to levofloxacin resistance

The NTUH-K2044 derived strains, detailed in Table 1, were collected after 12 h of exposure to levofloxacin in the time-kill experiment. Whole genome sequencing was performed on NTUH-K2044-2A and further blasted quinolone resistance genes, such as the QRDR (quinolone resistance-determining region) variation, the drug permeability gene (ompK36), efflux pumps (acrAB-tolC and oqxAB), and regulators (oqxR, rarA, and ramA). QRDR, oqxR and ramA mutation also were analyzed in NTUH-K2044-2A, NTUH-K2044-2B, and NTUH-K2044-4 strains. Additionally, oqxR sequencing was conducted for NTUH-K2044 wild-type oqxR complemented strains using primers (oqxR-F and oqxR-R, in Table S1).

Determination of the gene expression using real-time PCR

The transcriptional expression levels of the efflux pump genes (acrB and oqxB) and regulators (ramA and rarA) were detected in the derived strains and their transformed counterparts using real-time PCR [26, 27]. After RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis, amplifications were performed using the SYBR Premix Ex Taq kit (Accurate Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Hunan, China) on the QuantStudio 1 Real-Time PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Data were analyzed using the 2− ΔΔCt method in triplicate. The rrsE was chosen as a reference and strain NTUH-K2044 as the self-calibrator. All the primers used are listed in Supplementary Table S1.

Complementation of the oqxR gene

The wild-type oqxR gene of strain NTUH-K2044 (791 bp, amplified by primers oqxR-inf-F and oqxR-inf-R) was cloned into pPuc57 using the OK Clon DNA Ligation Kit (Accurate Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Hunan, China). After verification of the construct by sequencing, the recombinant plasmid was electroporated into the derived strains. Transformants were selected on Luria-Bertani (LB) agar plates containing 50 mg/L of kanamycin. The empty pPuc57 served as a negative control. Additionally, the MICs of levofloxacin, nalidixic acid, ciprofloxacin, norfloxacin, and tetracycline, as well as chloramphenicol were also determined for the NTUH-K2044 derived strains and transformants. Table 1 provides the plasmids used and constructed in this study.

Results

Molecular characteristics of strains

All the 12 clinical hvKP isolates exhibited hypermucoviscosity (Fig. 1). Nine strains were classified into the KL1 capsule type, and three strains were classified as KL2 (belongs to ST65). The dominant MLST type was ST23 with six strains, while three strains were classified as ST65 (one was ST65-1LV) and one each was assigned to ST700, ST367 and ST218-1LV. Reference strain NTUH-K2044 was classified as ST23 and KL1 type. Related data of clinical isolates were presented in Fig. 1.

In vitro antimicrobial activity and mutation frequency

All of the 12 hvKP isolates were susceptible to levofloxacin, amikacin, and ceftazidime, and the MICs ranges were 0.03–0.5 mg/L, 0.5–2 mg/L, and 0.125–0.5 mg/L, respectively (Fig. 1). In the checkboard assays, both of levofloxacin–amikacin combination and levofloxacin–ceftazidime combination demonstrated indifference in all 12 clinical strains. No synergy and antagonism were detected.

MIC values of levofloxacin, amikacin, and ceftazidime against NTUH-K2044 were 0.06 mg/L, 1 mg/L and 0.125 mg/L. And both of levofloxacin–amikacin combination and levofloxacin–ceftazidime combination demonstrated indifference against NTUH-K2044. MPC of levofloxacin against NTUH-K2044 were 1 mg/L (16×MIC). And mutation frequency against levofloxacin concentration with 1×MIC, 2×MIC, 4×MIC and 8×MIC were 5.12 × 10− 6, 4.98 × 10− 7, 1.37 × 10− 7 and 4.71 × 10− 8, respectively.

Time-kill experiments for levofloxacin, amikacin and ceftazidime

The representative NTUH-K2044 strain was subjected to time-kill experiments to evaluate bactericidal activities of levofloxacin, amikacin, and ceftazidime. Figure 2(a) shows that 16×MIC (1 mg/L) of levofloxacin exhibited bactericidal activity during the 1st h, with bacterial counts remaining below the LOD value in subsequent hours. Levofloxacin with 1/2×MIC concentration slowed down the bacterial growth during the initial 12 h compared to the control sample, and the bacterial counts reached control levels of 9 log10 CFU/mL by the 12th h. However, 1×MIC (0.06 mg/L), 2×MIC, and 4×MIC of levofloxacin exposures resulted in reductions of bacterial counts by approximately 1 log10 CFU/mL, > 4 log10 CFU/mL, and > 4 log10 CFU/mL, respectively, after 1 h followed by subsequent regrowth. And the bacterial counts reached approximately 9 log10 CFU/mL at the 24th h. However, 8×MIC of levofloxacin showed bactericidal activity during the 1st h, resulting in bacterial counts below the LOD value during the initial 6 h, followed by regrowth.

Levofloxacin-induced multi-drug resistance related to the efflux pump OqxAB

The derived strains of NTUH-K2044-2A, NTUH-K2044-2B, and NTUH-K2044-4, sampled after 12 h of exposure to 2×MIC and 4×MIC of levofloxacin exhibited increases in the levofloxacin MICs from the original 0.06 mg/L to 2 mg/L, 0.5 mg/L, and 1 mg/L, respectively. The efflux pump inhibitor, PAβN, reduced the MICs of levofloxacin by 8-fold to 16-fold in these derived strains (Table 2). Compared to NTUH-K2044, the MICs of nalidixic acid and chloramphenicol increased to the resistance level in the three derived strains, while varying levels of increases in the MIC of ciprofloxacin, norfloxacin, and tetracycline were observed. Furthermore, oqxR mutations, C12G (Tyr 4 stop codon), G138A (Met 46 Ile), and C295T (Arg 99 Cyx), were identified in derived strains NTUH-K2044-2A, NTUH-K2044-2B, and NTUH-K2044-4 respectively, without QRDR and ramA variation.

In addition, significant overexpression in oqxB (349.83-fold, 157.51-fold, and 118.82-fold) and rarA (620.86-fold, 31.43-fold, and 18.44-fold) were observed in three derived strains, without overexpression of acrB and ramA (Table 2). After complementation of wild-type oqxR, the oqxB and rarA transcriptional levels decreased remarkably in corresponding transformed strains. The expression levels of oqxB decreased by 277.6-fold (349.83 vs. 1.26), 238.7-fold (157.51 vs. 0.66), and 52.6-fold (118.82 vs. 2.26) after wild-type oqxR complementation in strains NTUH-K2044-2A, NTUH-K2044-2B, and NTUH-K2044-4, respectively (Table 2). Correspondingly, the expression levels of rarA also showed decreases of 110.5-fold (620.86 vs. 5.62), 25.6-fold (31.43 vs. 1.23), and 13.7-fold (18.44 vs. 1.35) in the respective wild-type oqxR complemented strains.

Discussion

Consistent with previous reports [28, 29], ST23 (K1 serotype) is the most common sequencing type, followed by ST65 (K2 serotype) in hvKP isolates of this study. Differing from infB1 in ST65, KP20-2463 strains harbored infB136 variant, classified into ST65-1LV type. Another KP19-2836 strain belonged to ST 218-1LV, it harbors mdh196 with single-locus (mdh1) variant of ST218. All the 12 hvKP isolates were susceptible to levofloxacin, amikacin, and ceftazidime. However, during exposures to lower concentrations of levofloxacin (2×MIC to 8×MIC), a bacterial regrowth was observed despite an initial inhibition being demonstrated in the time-kill experiments, indicating the possibility of levofloxacin-induced resistance. The MPC/MIC ratio of NTUH-K2044 was 16 for levofloxacin, and mutation frequencies were decreased from 5.12 × 10− 6 to 4.71 × 10− 8 as increasing concentration of levofloxacin exposure. This is consistent with findings from other studies [15, 18].

Levofloxacin combined with amikacin or ceftazidime exhibited favorable bactericidal synergy and suppressed induced resistance against NTUH-K2044 using the low drug concentrations in the time-kill experiments. Study showed that employing second antimicrobial with levofloxacin could help minimize the selection of resistance bacterial cells [16, 18]. For example, the MPC/MIC ratio decreased four to eight fold in the levofloxacin-ceftazidime combination against Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and the mutational frequencies also decreased in combination [18]. These imply that combination therapy may be a potential solution for prevention of the induced resistance of levofloxacin in hvKP infections.

Mutations of oqxR lead to overexpression of oqxAB and confer resistance to various antibacterial agents in K. pneumoniae [26, 30]. Mutations Tyr4 to stop coding, Met46Ile and Arg99Cys in repressor oqxR were identified in induced resistant strains, without variation in QRDR and other efflux pump regulators [30]. Three NTUH-K2044 derived strains induced by levofloxacin exhibited decreased susceptibility to levofloxacin and other antibacterial agents compared to the initial NTUH-K2044. The above reported mutations in oqxR were present in those three levofloxacin induced strains with decreased antimicrobial susceptibility.

To certify the role of oqxR in the three NTUH-K2044 induced resistant strains, the wild-type oqxR complementation were performed. After wild-type oqxR complementation, the oqxB and rarA transcriptions repressed remarkably in the three transformed strains, while 2-fold to 4-fold decreases in the corresponding MIC of nalidixic acid and chloramphenicol were observed (Table 2). Interestingly, the MICs of quinolones (levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin or norfloxacin) or tetracycline in the three strains with wild-type oqxR complementation were unchanged or only had 2-fold decrease comparing with their corresponding oqxR mutant strains. Similarly, Mark Veleba reported that the MICs of ciprofloxacin and chloramphenicol were unchanged in oqxR-mutated K. pneumoniae after wild-type oqxR complementation, while there was a remarkable decrease in oqxA transcription [26]. Therefore, this parallel misalignment between some antibacterial susceptibility and efflux pump expression still requires further study.

In conclusion, both the levofloxacin–amikacin and levofloxacin–ceftazidime combinations exhibited bactericidal synergy and effectively suppressed induced resistance in levofloxacin. Exposure to lower concentrations of levofloxacin monotherapy could induce point mutations in the repressor oqxR gene, leading to overexpression of the oqxAB pump encoding gene and resulting in resistance to levofloxacin.

IFPHOR0033 Redesigning Preventive Care Delivery in General Practice: A Data-Led and AI-Supported Model to Improve NHS Health Check Outcomes
Authors: Matt Sweeney, Dillon Sykes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNHS Health Checks are a core cardiovascular prevention programme in England, yet substantial variation exists in completion rates, risk identification and reach into underserved populations. Increasing invitation volume alone does not reliably improve outcomes and may increase workload without increasing impact. This pilot, delivered in Lancashire in partnership with Lancashire County Council, examined whether redesigning delivery through data-led cohort prioritisation and AI-supported patient engagement could improve preventive outcomes within existing NHS capacity.MethodsRedmoor Health worked with four GP practices over a 12-week period to implement a structured delivery model. The intervention combined: (1) risk-informed patient segmentation and prioritised cohort sequencing; (2) paced invitation waves aligned to appointment capacity; (3) redesigned, cohort-profiled SMS invitations; (4) AI avatar explainer videos and practice-branded reinforcement communications to reduce uncertainty and increase confidence to attend; and (5) a live dashboard enabling weekly performance review and adaptive management. No additional clinical appointment capacity was funded. Outcomes were assessed over an 18-week window.ResultsCompleted NHS Health Checks increased 2.6-fold (394 to 1,010). Patients identified with previously unrecognised cardiovascular risk increased 2.7-fold (77 to 211). Attendees were drawn from slightly more deprived populations on average, indicating improved equity of reach. Improvements were achieved without increasing clinic capacity.ConclusionPreventive impact can be materially improved through delivery redesign rather than expanded resource. Combining targeted cohort pacing, AI-supported reassurance content and live performance visibility improved attendance efficiency and identification of unmet need. The Lancashire model offers a scalable approach for strengthening prevention within constrained primary care systems.

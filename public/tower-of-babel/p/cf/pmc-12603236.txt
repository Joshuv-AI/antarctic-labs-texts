A comprehensive descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analysis of antibiotic products’ demand pre-, amid, and post-COVID-19 in Iran
Authors: Mohadese Zalpooli, Arash Nemati
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
COVID-19 has affected healthcare businesses such as medicine and face mask distribution networks, particularly the antibiotics distribution sector, because antibiotics significantly combat infections during COVID-19. Hence, re-coordinating the antibiotics supply chain according to changes in antibiotic consumption rates is crucial post-COVID-19 to avoid shortages and the perishing of antibiotics. This paper proposes a data analytical approach to investigate the antibiotics demand fluctuations before, during, and after COVID-19 using several descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analyses, including a graphical trend study, descriptive statistics, two-sample mean hypothesis test, Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), time series machine learning forecasters, and Pareto analysis. The proposed data analytical approach is employed to analyze the consumption rate of the 73 popular antibiotic products belonging to the top-11 list of antibiotics in Iran, including Amoxicillin, Co-amoxiclav, Azithromycin, Cefalexin, Cefixime, Ciprofloxacin, Clindamycin, Doxycycline, Levofloxacin, Metronidazole, and Penicillin. The consumption volume and Doses used by 1000 Inhabitants per Day (DID) are collected for ten years, from 2013 to 2023, in a data set of size 3582. The results showed the considerable impacts of COVID-19 on the consumption trend of Amoxicillin and Azithromycin, a decline in Penicillin parental consumption in 2021 due to COVID-19 vaccination, and a higher share of parental Levofloxacin during COVID-19. In addition, several prescriptive analyses are proposed.

Introduction

Business analytics helps to enhance healthcare operations efficiency in several aspects, such as care quality, service accessibility, and financial achievements1–4. Data analytics have been used widely for descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analysis of several Pharmaceutical supply chain parameters, such as demand, supply, and inventory, to prevent medicine shortages or expiration5–10. COVID-19 has affected the pharmaceutical supply chain from different points of view, including the popularity of medications and emerging vaccine technologies, imposing redesigns of the pre-COVID-19 pharmaceutical supply chain network11–15.

Antibiotics have played a significant role in fighting respiratory infections during COVID-19 and caused the surveillance of millions of people16–20. However, overuse of antibiotics could result in antibacterial resistance as a common healthcare system issue in areas with convenient accessibility of the public to antibiotics, like Iran, resulting in a probable high antibiotics consumption rate in the long term21–24. The antibiotics consumption rate trend analysis based on time series data sets on sale volume has gained significant notice, particularly post-COVID-1915,22,24–31. Doses used by every 1000 Inhabitants per Day (DID) and the annual or monthly consumption volume of antibiotics are commonly used indices in the antibiotics consumption rate trend analysis problem, which have undergone descriptive analysis and trend study based on the corresponding graphs. However, the following questions have remained unanswered about the antibiotic consumption rate data analysis:

This paper proposes a data analytical framework for investigating the consumption rate trend of antibiotics involving descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analysis. In this framework, hypothesis tests are employed to evaluate the statistical significance of changes that occurred in the antibiotic consumption rates before, during, and after COVID-19, including Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) and two-sample mean tests. The proposed framework is used in the case study of Iran to explore the changes occurred in the consumption rates’ trend of eleven commonly used classes of antibiotics, including Amoxicillin, Co-amoxiclav, Azithromycin, Cefalexin, Cefixime, Ciprofloxacin, Clindamycin, Doxycycline, Levofloxacin, Metronidazole, and Penicillin, and the corresponding 79 types of antibiotics product based on a national data set, from 2013 to 2023. In addition, the future demand for each antibiotic belongs to an 80-percent class of the Pareto analysis, and the most commonly used products of each antibiotic are forecasted using regression models. Also, a prescriptive analysis is provided according to the descriptive and predictive analysis results and the uncertainty involved in Iran’s inhabitants because of the high rate of illegal immigrants living in Iran. Literature review

This section reviews the literature on the antibiotic consumption rate in a region, such as a country, to highlight the research gaps.

Antibiotics demand analysis

McManus et al. (1997) studied the incremental trend of top-10 antibiotics consumption in the Australian community in the period of 1990–1995 and compared it with other developed countries32. Campos et al. (2007) studied the 20-antibiotic consumption rate trend in Spain, between 2002 and 200533. Abdollahiasl et al. (2011) used descriptive analysis to show that there were no significant seasonal patterns in the antibiotic consumption rate in Iran from 2000 to 200934. Kotwani and Holloway (2011) employed descriptive analysis to show that there were no significant seasonal patterns in the antibiotics consumption rate but a considerably high consumption rate in New Delhi, India, based on a monthly data set, December 2007- November 200835. Wirtz et al. (2013) studied the antibiotic consumption rate trend in Chile, Colombia, Venezuela, and Mexico, between 1995 and 2009, using the corresponding DID graphs36. Vaz et al. (2014) used descriptive graphical tools to show the decline that occurred in antibiotic prescribing in three regions of the USA, including New England, the Mountain West, and the Midwest, relying on a 2000–2010 data set37. Lin et al. (2016) studied the trend of 5 groups of antibiotics in Shanghai from 2009 to 2014 using bar charts38. Olesen et al. (2018) studied the consumption of several antibiotics among US older adults using descriptive analysis and a data set from 2011 to 201539. Jackson et al. (2019) studied changes in global antibiotic use between 2011 and 2015, and concluded that antibiotic use increased slightly in this period40. Abbasian et al. (2019) investigated the changes in the DID of antibiotics in Iran based on a 2000–2016 data set using descriptive analysis41. Also, they compared Iran’s antibiotic consumption rate in 2016 with the corresponding statistics from the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) countries, depicting that Iran has consumed antibiotics about three times the average. Zhang et al. (2019) used descriptive analysis to study the trend of several antibiotics consumption in primary care centers of Hubei province, China, based on DID collected from 2012 to 201742. Allwell-Brown et al. (2020) studied the trends in self-reported antibiotic use in low-income countries from 2005 to 2017 and proposed a systematic analysis of 132 national surveys from 73 countries. Song et al. (2020) investigated antibiotic consumption rates in the Shangdon province, China, between 2012 and 2017, using the corresponding DID graphs43. However, their study was limited to descriptive analysis in a time frame pre-COVID-19.

Malcolm et al. (2020) investigated the antibiotic prescription for different age classes during COVID-19 in Scotland using descriptive analysis44. Yuan et al. (2020) reviewed pharmaceutical information related to the trend and consumption of antibiotics in Shaanxi Province, western China, between 2015 and 2018. They used trend bar charts and concluded that the antibiotic prescriptions declined after the initial urgent conditions of COVID-19. However, they had no focus on the changes that occurred in the antibiotic prescriptions before, during, and after COVID-19 and proposed no forecasts for the future. Trinh et al. (2021) compared the antibiotic consumption rate estimations according to two sources of data, including retailers and reimbursement, based on 2012–2017 data sets using the Pearson correlation coefficient and Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA)45. Tomas et al. (2021) studied the changes that occurred in several types of antibiotic consumption rates in ten years, from 2010 to 2019, in Serbia using descriptive analysis46.

Gonzalez-Zorn (2021) used time series data on antibiotic consumption between January 2017 and March 2023 from Spain to show that the need for Azithromycin was drastically increased for respiratory infection treatment during COVID-19. Del Fiol et al. (2022) used descriptive analysis tools, such as jointpoint regression, mean, standard deviation, and ANOVA, to compare the consumption of several antibiotics before and during COVID-19 based on quarterly time series data sets collected from January 2014 to July 2021 in Brazil47. Also, they compared the average antibiotic consumption with the mean of several previous years using ANOVA, but did not investigate the changes after COVID-19 and forecasts for the future. Bednarčuk et al. (2023) discussed the increase in antibiotic prescriptions during COVID-19 and compared the corresponding rates with pre- and post-COVID-19 using descriptive analysis based on data sets from the Republic of Srpska, 2018–2022. However, they provided no statistical significance analysis on antibiotic consumption rate changes and forecasts for future planning. Also, they did not consider any analysis of popular forms of each antibiotic group. Perrella et al. (2023) compared antibiotic consumption during COVID-19 based on monthly statistics from 2019 to 2021 in Italy using descriptive analysis, including Pearson correlation between years’ patterns and graphical illustrations. However, they did not compare the COVID-19 statistics with those from before and after the pandemic and proposed no forecasts.

He et al. (2023) investigated several antibiotics’ consumption trends and seasonality using descriptive analysis and multiple regression models based on a data set from 2012 to 2016 collected in Hefei, China. Their focus was not on antibiotic consumption during or after COVID-19. Nandi et al. (2023) investigated the significance of the impact of several factors, including COVID-19 cases, vaccination rates, region, and time, on antibiotic consumption rates during the COVID-19 pandemic, based on monthly statistics from 71 countries between January 2018 and May 2022, using multiple linear regression models. However, they did not focus on the changes that occurred in the antibiotic consumption rate before, during, and after COVID-19. Alzueta et al. (2023) used descriptive analysis and significance tests in time series simple linear regression models to illustrate that the number of people treated with critical antibiotics, such as amoxicillin, amoxicillin-clavulanic acid, and levofloxacin, decreased significantly during COVID-19 compared to before COVID-19 in Navarre in Spain, based on quarterly data from 2018 to 202148. However, this research did not investigate the trend of antibiotic consumption rate after COVID-19 in comparison to before and during COVID-19. Rockwood et al. (2023) used descriptive analysis to classify several antibiotics according to cost and consumption volume in three classes based on a data set gained in 2019 in Sri Lanka49. Elsafi et al. (2024) analyzed the annual antibiotics consumption trend in Saudi Arabia from 2018 to 2022 using hypothesis tests and paid no attention to predictive and prescriptive analysis50. Rezaei et al. (2025) employed interrupted time series analysis to study the impacts of COVID-19 on the overall consumption rate of antibiotics in Iran. They used a monthly data set of DID from March 2016 to March 2022 without considering antibiotic type and the corresponding products. Also, they neglected prescriptive and predictive analysis, ANOVA, and antibiotic classification. Ji et al. (2025) analyzed a monthly time series data set of antibiotics based on DID criteria from pre- and amid-COVID-19, 2017–2020, from Shaanxi province, China, using interrupted time-series analysis. However, they neglected antibiotic products, predictive analysis, ANOVA, post-COVID-19 demand, and classification.

Gap analysis

According to Table 1, most literature on antibiotic consumption trend analysis has concentrated on descriptive analysis, and no attention has been paid to predictive and prescriptive analysis. In addition, the literature lacks any analysis of the various antibiotic products with different doses. Several statistical tools, such as ANOVA, regression models, and hypothesis tests on consumption trends, received rare attention in the literature. Although some research focused on investigating the antibiotic consumption rate during and after COVID-19, they neglected the mentioned analyses, and did not notice the changes that occurred in antibiotic consumption rates pre-, amid, and post-COVID-19 simultaneously. This paper proposes an analytical framework employing several descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive analyses for antibiotics consumption analysis pre-, amid, and post-COVID-19 to investigate antibiotic product consumption trend after COVID-19 using all the mentioned tools.

The proposed data analytical framework

The initial step in the antibiotics consumption trend analysis problem is to identify the commonly used antibiotics. Although there might be more than 130 different antibiotics, only 10–12 types of antibiotics satisfy more than 90% of the demand in most healthcare systems41,48,51. Like all data analytical frameworks, the next step is data collection, which involves gathering annual historical data on various types of antibiotic consumption volumes, followed by pre-processing actions such as missing data management, aggregation, and integration. Then, the DID calculation using the inhabitant’s statistics and the standard values of the Defined Doses per Day (DDD) is the next step. After that, the descriptive analysis of DID and consumption volumes, including trend study via graphical tools and descriptive statistics, such as mean (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\bar {X}$$\end{document}), standard deviation (S), minimum value (Min), maximum value (Max), and range (R), is considered. Also, the significance of several scenarios on changes in average antibiotic consumption rates during pre-, amid, and post-COVID-19 should be examined using ANOVA and two-sample mean hypothesis tests. The most popular antibiotics could be recognized through antibiotic classification using Pareto analysis based on DID or consumption volume. Forecasting the required volumes of popular antibiotics in the future using machine learning forecasters, such as simple linear regression models, provides healthcare authorities with helpful insights. Finally, prescriptive analysis based on the results of descriptive and predictive analyses is the final step. Figure 1 illustrates the proposed data analytical framework concisely.

Case study

According to reports from the World Health Organization (WHO), Iran has a high degree of overuse of antibiotics52. Also, sometimes, for example autumn and winter of 2022, Iranian people have suffered from antibiotic’s severe shortages. Hence, any analytical framework for the antibiotic consumption trend could provide Iran’s healthcare authorities with helpful insights to coordinate the antibiotics supply chain involving 120 antibiotics suppliers, including manufacturers and import companies.

Data set collection and pre-processing

In this study, the historical data on antibiotic consumption volume in Iran during the last ten years, from 2013 to 2023, were collected from the yearly reports of the Food and Drug Administration of Iran to investigate the trend of antibiotic consumption and the corresponding changes that occurred because of COVID-19. The yearly sales of each product by several retailers are aggregated to determine the cumulative yearly volumes to reach each antibiotic type’s pure annual sales, and the resulting annual data sets are integrated using Python, and several typo errors are corrected. Finally, a clean data set involving 4324 records is provided.

Discussion and prescriptive analysis

According to Fig. 2, COVID-19 caused a considerable increase in the popularity of Azithromycin, Cefixime, and Metronidazole, and a decrease in the Penicillin consumption rate, verified by quantitative statistical tests in Table 5. Hence, the decision-makers in the Food and Drug Administration of Iran should take action to re-coordinate 120 antibiotic suppliers to redesign the production plans to meet the changing demand for several antibiotic products, particularly those listed in Fig. 6. Iran’s antibiotics market showed a shortage of popular antibiotics during winter 2022 due to neglecting the mentioned re-coordination. Therefore, proposing a tactical plan for antibiotic supply in the mid-term, like 3 years, using the forecasts provided in Tables 6 and 7 is recommended.

On the other hand, continually monitoring the antibiotic consumption rate and volume on a monthly and annual basis to re-predict the demand for various antibiotic products seems essential. As a reason, Fig. 8 depicts a soaring in the demand for Metronidazole by 2026, even more than Amoxicillin. While Fig. 10 illustrates the details of the corresponding fitted simple linear regression model as an accurate forecaster, there may be a need for dynamic causal forecasters to predict demand for antibiotics based on several indicators, such as sanctions, raw material import issues, governmental policies, and medicine warehouses.

Fig. 10The fitted forecaster for Metronidazole demand.

The fitted forecaster for Metronidazole demand.

If the drastically increasing trend in demand for Metronidazole continues in the future, it will be another impact of COVID-19 on the antibiotics consumption rate. Figure 2 shows a gradually increasing trend of Metronidazole popularity during and after COVID-19, and all the ANOVA and two-sample mean hypothesis tests, illustrated in Table 5, verified that a significant change occurred in the Metronidazole consumption rate during and after COVID-19.

Figure 7 shows a drastic decrease in the tendency to use the parenteral type of Penicillin in Iran’s antibiotics market. In other words, the share of parenteral Penicillin has decreased from 70% to 30% since 2020. Hence, it is necessary to follow a warehousing monitoring and re-plan the production lines and storage to prevent parenteral Penicillin waste and oral Penicillin shortage in the future. Overall, antibiotics supply chain planning, incorporating several resilience strategies, such as multi-channel sourcing, offshore sourcing, surplus domestic production capacities, and long-term mass storage and regional small inventory, considering antibiotics’ perishability, can be taken into account by healthcare authorities to prevent antibiotics shortage, like what occurred in Iran in 2022.

According to Abbasian et al. (2019), Iran had the first place among more than 30 developing countries in terms of antibiotic consumption rate, with an overall DID of about 60 in 2016. Unfortunately, any updated reports comparing Iran’s antibiotics consumption rate in terms of DID have not been published post-COVID-19. However, the high DID scores of Iran have two main reasons. Firstly, antibiotics are available to the public and everybody can buy them with no need for a specialist’s prescription and in unlimited quantity, probably resulting in antibiotic resistance in the long term. Hence, making boundaries on freely antibiotics accessibility by the public will mitigate the resistance issue and decrease the corresponding DID scores. Secondly, Iran has long eastern borders with Afghanistan, with low control in some places. Hence, millions of Afghan illegal immigrants have lived in Iran for decades. According to official reports from Iran’s Ministry of Interior, more than 1.5 million illegal and 1 million legal Afghan immigrants were living in Iran in 2013. In recent years, particularly because of the crisis in Afghanistan in 2021, the unofficial forecasts on illegal Afghan immigrants are more than 8 million and even 11 million individuals in October 2023, more than 11% of Iranian inhabitants. The illegal inhabitants increase the annual antibiotic consumption volume, and the corresponding impact on DID could not be assessed due to the lack of an estimation of their population size. Therefore, providing exact statistics on the real value of inhabitants in Iran by governmental authorities could provide healthcare managers with actual DID and more accurate forecasts and planning for antibiotics supply.

In this study, the Iranian inhabitants are assumed to be the official Iranian population statistics, with no attention to immigrants. To investigate the impacts of neglected illegal immigrants on DID statistics, the corresponding numbers are supposed to be 1.5, 2, 5, 8, and 9 million in 2013–2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2023, respectively. Figure 11 illustrates the surplus annual DID of each antibiotic corresponding to the illegal Afghan immigrants in Iran from 2013 to 2023.

Fig. 11The surplus annual DID of antibiotics due to illegal Afghan immigrants.

The surplus annual DID of antibiotics due to illegal Afghan immigrants.

Figure 11 shows that even lower estimates of Afghan immigrants could affect the antibiotics DID statistics significantly, particularly for the most popular and easily accessible antibiotics like Amoxicillin and Azithromycin, but the antibiotics consumption volumes were never affected. Hence, there is a need to determine the number of individuals living in Iran, including illegal immigrants, to estimate real DID statistics.

Conclusions, limitations, and recommendations

This paper focused on the antibiotics consumption rate analysis and proposed a data analytical framework to investigate the changes that occurred in antibiotics popularity because of COVID-19. The proposed framework involves several descriptive and predictive analysis tools, including ANOVA, two-sample mean hypothesis test, regression models, and descriptive statistics, to judge shifts that occurred in the antibiotics consumption rate trends pre-, amid, and post-COVID-19, to provide the corresponding prescriptive analysis. The results of applying this framework in the case study of Iran showed the popularity of Amoxicillin, Azithromycin, and Cefixime among the top-11 antibiotics, and Amoxicillin-Cap-250 mg, Azithromycin-Tab-500 mg, and Cefixime-Tab-400 mg in the antibiotic products. In addition, COVID-19 had a considerable impact of COVID-19 on the consumption trend of Azithromycin, Levofloxacin, Metronidazole, and Penicillin, and a decline in Penicillin parenteral consumption in 2021 due to COVID-19 vaccination, and a higher share of parenteral Levofloxacin during COVID-19. In addition, the demand for antibiotics in Iran in 2024–2026 was forecasted, and several prescriptions were proposed for Iran’s healthcare authorities.

This paper discussed the uncertainty surrounding the inhabitants of Iran due to a high number of illegal Afghan immigrants. The proposed data analytical framework could be modified using uncertainty-facing strategies, like considering fuzzy inhabitants’ numbers in future works. In addition, the annual antibiotic consumption rates are studied in this paper, whereas a significant proportion of antibiotics is used, particularly by the public in Iran, during seasonal Flu outbreaks. Hence, focusing on the monthly or seasonal pattern of antibiotics could facilitate the seasonal trends of antibiotic consumption to provide healthcare authorities with more efficient antibiotic demand forecasts and supply plans. Accordingly, there will be sufficient data on post-COVID-19 in the monthly and seasonal analysis of antibiotic consumption rates to employ Interrupted Time Series (ITS) analysis to provide more detailed insights on the impacts of COVID-19 in future work53,54. Comparing several machine learning and deep learning causal or time series forecasters, such as multiple regression models, artificial neural networks, ARIMA, seasonal ARIMA (SARIMA), and Long Short-Term Memory neural networks (LSTM) could be recommended for future study to provide more accurate forecasts. In addition, the classification of antibiotics using machine learning and deep learning classifiers like Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and LSTM, could provide healthcare decision-makers with beneficial information in future works. Finally, updating the results of COVID-19 impacts on antibiotic consumption rate in the future and using the proposed framework for other types of drugs, particularly applied for Cardiac and Respiratory diseases, in Iran or another case study, could be considered in related future studies.

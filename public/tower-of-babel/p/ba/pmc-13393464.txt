Contribution to floral structure and palynological features of Turkish Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae taxa
Authors: Seher Güven, Serdar Makbul, Eyüp Sarıkaya, Duygu Aydoğmuş, Kamil Coşkunçelebi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe Apocynaceae are characterized by highly specialized flowers, including three derived structures, corona, gynostegium and pollinarium, which play an important role in explaining the evolutionary relationships among the major groups of the family. The floral morphology and microstructure of pollinaria in pollinia-bearing subfamilies, Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae, are not well understood. In the present study, we examined the detailed floral and pollen characters of six species from Türkiye—Asclepiadoideae: Araujia sericifera Brot., Cionura erecta (L.) Griseb., Cynachum acutum L. and Gomphocarpus fruticosus (L.) W.T.Aiton; Periplocoideae: Periploca gracilis Boiss. and P. graeca L.—using light and scanning electron microscopy for taxonomic insights.ResultsMacro- and micromorphological observations were performed on both herbarium samples and fresh field specimens preserved in 70% ethanol. Floral and pollen descriptions were amended, and a dichotomous identification key was prepared for the studied taxa. Morphological and palynological traits were also analyzed numerically. Principal components analysis revealed that length of pedicel, sepal, petal and corona segment, pubescence of anther (hairy/glabrous), shape of stigma (unlobed/bifid), length of pistil, type of pollen aggregation (tetrad/pollinium), number of tetrad mass or pollinium per pollen translator (single/in pairs), pollen translator structure (receptacle + stalk + adhesive disc or corpusculum + caudicles), and pollen grain dimensions were diagnostically informative traits for distinguishing taxa at both subfamily and genus levels.ConclusionsThe dendrogram inferred from macro- and micromorphological floral and pollen traits generally agrees with the traditional subfamilial classification of the Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae. These findings offer insights into the evolution and diversification of pollinia-bearing Apocynaceae, and may reflect the role of key traits in reproductive strategies and ecological interactions.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08939-3.

Introduction

In the most recent classification, Apocynaceae sensu lato is recognized as a combination of two previous families: Apocynaceae sensu stricto and Asclepiadaceae. At the recent circumscription, the family is divided into five major groups: two non-monophyletic informally recognized as rauvolfioids and apocynoids grades (lacking pollinia), and three monophyletic, Periplocoideae, Secamonoideae, and Asclepiadoideae subfamilies, known as the pollinia-bearing subfamilies [1]. The enlarged Apocynaceae is one of the largest 10 angiosperm families containing approximately 5,350 species belonging to 378 genera in various habitats and growth forms. Members of the family include large trees, shrubs, lianas, and epiphytes in tropical rainforests, succulents in dry, open landscapes, and perennial herbs in temperate grasslands [1–3].

The Apocynaceae have attracted much interest from plant taxonomists due to their highly specialized floral construction. Flowers of the family are characterized by 3 derived structures: (I) corona, a sterile floral structure belonging to the corolla or androecium, (II) gynostegium, a reproductive structure formed by union of the gynoecium and androecium, and (III) pollinarium, a functional pollen dispersal unit consisting of tetrad masses or pollinia joined to a specialized translator produced by a hardened stylar exudate [1, 2, 4, 5]. In the current classification of Apocynaceae, the reproductive features of flower play a key role in delimiting the major groups (grades and subfamilies). Of the two grades, rauvolfioids, considered to be the most early divergent group of Apocynaceae, are almost always characterized by sinistrorse corolla lobe (rarely dextrorse) and anthers free from style-head (lacking a gynostegium). In contrast, the remaining taxa of the APSA clade (Apocynoids, Periplocoideae, Secamonoideae and Asclepiadoideae) typically have dextrorse corolla lobe (rarely sinistrorse or valvate) and anthers adnate to style head in the form of a gynostegium [1]. Furthermore, pollen characteristics have long been recognized as important evolutionary traits for major groups in Apocynaceae. Both rauvolfioids and apocynoids, which are, palynologically, the most early divergent groups in Apocynaceae, commonly have monad pollen grains without translator or rarely with tetrads shed on undifferentiated translators [2]. These two groups have traditionally been differentiated from the remaining pollinia-bearing subfamilies (Periplocoideae, Secamonoideae, and Asclepiadoideae), which are characterized by the aggregation of pollen grains into tetrad masses or pollinia joined to quite specialized translators (acellular bodies derived from style-head exudates) [2, 5].

In the Apocynaceae, highly characteristic floral structures, gynostegium and corona, have an important role in pollinator attraction, reward, and/or positioning [6]. The complexity of floral structures varies depending on the pollinator interactions within the Apocynaceae. Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae, the two subfamilies evaluated in this study, have contrasting flowers, which have led them to develop distinct pollination mechanisms. In Periplocoideae, the pollination mechanism is defined as open, because the pollinator can easily reach the sticky translator carrying tetrads, which hangs directly above the nectar trough (anthers lack lignified guide rails) on the style-head. On the other hand, the pollination mechanism of the Asclepiadoideae is enclosed since the pollinia are attached to a clamp-shaped translator hidden behind the guide rails of adjacent anthers. In this system, the pollinator’s proboscis or leg is caught and guided into the pollination unit [1, 2]. Among the advantages of transportation of pollen grains in the form of tetrad masses or pollina are the strengthening adaptation of Apocynaceae representatives to drier climatic conditions by delaying pollen desiccation. Additionally, this mechanism increases pollination rates for small populations [7].

Revealing the floral structure and pollen morphology in detail not only contributes to the differentiation of Apocynaceae taxa but also provides valuable information about the pollinators of these plants, their habitats and ecological conditions. This is especially true for members of Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae. Flower morphology and palynological features, regarding Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae were generally outlined in several studies [2, 4–6, 8]. These investigations showed that morphological features on the shape, size and origin of corona; shape and pubescence of anther and stigma; palynological traits of the shape and size of pollen masses (tetrad and pollinium) and translator structures (spoon shaped translator, corpusculum and caudicle); surface ornamentation of tetrad and pollinia were the most valuable traits in delimiting the genera of Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae [1–5, 8–19].

Floral and palynological traits of Apocynaceae are crucial for understanding evolutionary relationships. These traits are similarly expressed in the Turkish taxa of Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae, providing a valuable opportunity to examine their floral and palynological variation within the local flora. The first taxonomic revision of Turkish Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae was made by Browicz [20], who treated both of them under the family Asclepiadaceae. Browicz [20] assumed that more comprehensive studies, especially on reproductive structures such as corona, gynostegium, and pollinium, were needed to separate the members of Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae in Türkiye. Güner [21] listed 11 genera, including Amsonia Walter, Araujia Brot., Cionura Griseb., Cyprinia Browicz, Gomphocarpus R.Br., and Nerium L., each represented by a single species, as well as Apocynum L. (2 subspecies), Cynanchum L. (2 subspecies), Periploca L. (2 varieties), Vinca L. (3 species and 2 subspecies) and Vincetoxicum Wolf (7 species and 4 subspecies) under the expanded Apocynaceae (Apocynaceae sensu stricto and Asclepiadaceae) in the vascular plant checklist of Türkiye, without any taxonomic treatment. Subsequent comprehensive taxonomic studies on the Turkish Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae taxa resulted in the description of V. coskuncelebiana S. Makbul & S. Güven (syn. V. anatolicum S. Güven, S. Makbul & Coşkunç.) as a new species [22]. Among the Periplocoideae taxa, Cyprinia was merged with Periploca, and P. graeca var. vestita was reduced to synonymy under P. graeca [23, 24]. Of the Asclepiadoideae taxa, V. raddeanum Alboff was reduced to synonymy under V. tmoleum Boiss., and V. hirundinaria Medik. was reclassified as V. hirundinaria subsp. hirundinaria [22, 25]. In the present study, this updated taxonomic treatment is followed for the investigated taxa. According to the recent taxonomic studies [22–25], the subfamilies Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae are represented by 6 genera and 18 taxa, including infraspecific taxa in Türkiye. Periplocoideae is represented by two species, Periploca gracilis Boiss. and P. graeca L., while Asclepiadoideae comprises the remaining taxa, including Araujia sericifera Brot., Cionura erecta (L.) Griseb., C. acutum L. subsp. acutum, C. acutum subsp. sibiricum (Willd.) Rech.f., Gomphocarpus fruticosus (L.) W.T.Aiton, and Vincetoxicum Wolf, with 6 species and 5 subspecies. In the previous studies based on the cytological and molecular data, Vincetoxicum was recovered as monophyletic within the subfamily [22, 25]. In addition, morphological [22, 26], anatomical [27, 28] and palynological data [29] also provided valuable information for delimitation of the Turkish Vincetoxicum at the species level. However, to date, limited studies have been conducted in Türkiye on the genera with pollinia-bearing subfamilies (Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae), which have taxonomic deficiencies and complexities in terms of morphology of reproductive organs in the Flora of Türkiye [20]. Recently, morphological and corological features of Periplocoideae [24], fruit and seed micromorphology of Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae (excluding Vincetoxicum) [23], micromorphological and anatomical features of stem and leaf parts of taxa of C. erecta. [30] and A. sericifera [31] from Türkiye was examined from a taxonomic point of view. Despite all these cited studies, there are still gaps in the biosystematics properties of both subfamily members. The present study aims to investigate the detailed floral and pollen characteristics of the four Asclepiadoideae species (A. sericifera., C. erecta, C. acutum and G. fruticosus and pollen features of the two Periplocoideae species (P. gracilis and P. graeca), for the first time, will provide important taxonomical contributions to systematics of the both subfamilies.

Taking into consideration the points raised so far, the objective of the present study is (I) to document and illustrate detailed descriptions of floral and pollen morphology of 3 species and 2 subspecies of Asclepiadoideae and pollen morphology of 2 species of Periplocoideae from Türkiye using light and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM), (II) to evaluate the taxonomic or diagnostic importance of floral morphological and palynological characteristics within the examined Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae taxa.

The examined plant material was collected from 13 different populations (p) in Türkiye belonging to seven taxa of the two subfamilies: Periplocoideae, including P. gracilis (1p) and P. graeca (2p), and Asclepiadoideae, comprising A. sericifera (1p), C. erecta (4p), C. acutum subsp. acutum (3p), C. acutum subsp. sibiricum (1p) and G. fruticosus (1p). Floral and palynological data belonging to the four Asclepiadoideae species and palynological data of the two Periplocoideae species were observed and measured for the first time in the present study. Data of floral features related to the examined Turkish Periplocoideae species (Appendix) were obtained from a previous study performed by Güven and Sarıkaya [24]. The dried herbarium specimens were rehydrated in tap water at 75 °C for 2 h and then stored in 70% ethanol [33]. All measurements and observations related to pollen morphology of the examined Periplocoideae taxa and both flower and pollen morphology of the studied Asclepiadoideae taxa were made on mature flowers belonging to 10 different individuals per population for seven taxa (a total of 130 individuals were examined: P. gracilis (10 individuals), P. graeca (20 individuals), A. sericifera (10 individuals), C. erecta (40 individuals), C. acutum subsp. acutum (30 individuals), C. acutum subsp. sibiricum (10 individuals) and G. fruticosus (10 individuals). Measurements obtained from replicate observations of flowers and pollinia were averaged per population, and these mean values were used in subsequent multivariate analyses. All photographic measurements were performed using scale-calibrated images, and multiple replicates were taken for each trait to ensure accuracy and minimize potential errors.

The formal identification was carried out by Dr. Seher Güven according to Flora of Türkiye [20], Flora of Russia [34] and Flora of Europe [35], and then the identified plant samples were stored as voucher specimens in the Herbarium of Recep Tayyip Erdogan University, Department of Biology (RUB)—a publicly accessible herbarium. Specimens belonging to G. fruticosus were examined at the Herbarium of Istanbul University, Faculty of Pharmacy (ISTE). The collector numbers provided in Table 1 correspond to the voucher specimen numbers. Taxonomic names were assigned according to Venter [36] and Endress et al. [1], and cross-checked with the Plants of the World Online Database (POWO) [37].

Morphological studies

Mature flowers of Asclepiadoideae were examined and photographed using a Leica S6D digital camera attached to the stereomicroscope. All measurements and observations were performed on the saved photographs. The floral parts such as corolla, corona, stamen and pistil were illustrated by hand. The terminology of floral characteristics follows that of Endress and Bruyns [4], Endress [5], and Fishbein et al. [2].

Palynological studies

Polleniferous materials of both the examined Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae taxa were obtained from mature flowers, following Güven et al. [29]. For light microscopy studies, pollinaria were directly dissected from the fixed flowers and preserved in 100% ethanol, then mounted with glycerine–gelatine in order to obtain permanent slides. The obtained pollen grain slides were photographed using a research light microscope BAB Bs200 Pro Comet Image Process and Analysis Software. For SEM studies, pollinaria, which were preserved in 100% ethanol, were dried outdoors and transferred to aluminum stubs, coated with gold for 4 min in a sputter-coater, and then examined and photographed using a JEOL-JSM 6610 SEM microscope at the Central Research Laboratories of Recep Tayyip Erdogan University [29]. Palynological measurements and observations were performed on the saved photographs. The terminology of pollen characteristics follows that of Endress and Bruyns [4], Verhoeven and Venter [8], Fishbein et al. [2], and Endress [5].

Multivariate data analysis

In multivariate data analysis, data obtained from 22 morphological and 26 palynological traits (Table 2) belonging to six species (seven taxa) (Table 1) were evaluated numerically. Some of the most distinguishing characteristics were illustrated with drawings (Fig. 1). In multivariate data analysis, mean values ​​​​of each morphological and palynological character were evaluated separately per population, for the seven taxa. Firstly, a raw data matrix generated by the average measurements of 48 traits was used for preliminary multivariate data analysis in order to determine the most effective characters in distinguishing the examined taxa. Based on these analyses, 24 traits (Table 2 and Appendix) showing the highest variance and taxonomic informativeness were selected for subsequent Cluster Analysis (CA) and Principal Components Analysis (PCA). Of the selected 24 characters, 14 characters were nominally scored (0, 1 or 2) and the remaining 10 characters were quantitative, including linear measurements and digit numbers. Missing data were minimal and were accounted for by substituting population mean values for the respective traits. For CA, a pairwise matrix of resemblance values was calculated from the raw standardized data matrix, using Gower’s coefficient of resemblance designed for mixed data sets. A dendrogram was generated by the Unweighted Pair-Group Method (UPGMA) by using arithmetic averages. For PCA, the raw mixed data (binary and continuous) were used to create a correlation matrix. Results

Floral properties

Asclepiadoideae

In the examined sub-family Asclepiadoideae of Apocyanaceae, the floral characteristics are inflorescences terminal, lateral, axillar or extra-axillar, composed of branched or umbellate cymes (rarely flowers solitary axillary) (Fig. 2). Flowers are bisexual, pentamerous and hypogynous. Corolla is rotate or campanulate, white, yellowish or pinkish. Corona has five separate segments inserted at inner base of the corolla tube, alternating with the petals (corolline corona), or with five separate or partially fused segments attached to the back of the stamens (gynostegial corona); corona segments are laminaris or succulent, basally unsegmented or with an inner lobe. Stamens are fused with the gynoecium to form gynostegium. Filaments are very short, thick; anthers are glabrous, 2-locular, with an apical appendage postgenitally fused to the base of style-head or above its thickest portion, margins are either narrowly lignified margins along the both sides forming five guide rails between the anthers, or membranous margins without guide rails. Gynoecium, forming a central column, is apocarpous, semi-inferior; two ovaries and styli are joined to the unlobed or apically bifid style-head, which includes five stigmatic zones located alternately with the anthers. Araujia sericifera Brot. in Trans. Linn. Soc. London 12: 62 (1818) [39]: Flowers are solitary axillary (rarely in inflorescences axillary of 2–5 flowers); pedicel is 17.6 ± 3.4 mm long (Fig. 2a), with crisped indument. Calyx segments are divided almost to the base; sepals are 7.48 ± 1.03 × 15.02 ± 1.68 mm, ovate or ovate-lanceolate, acute at apex, abaxially with dense crisped indumenta. Corolla is campanulate, swollen at the base, five-lobed near the apex; petal lobes are 7.74 ± 1.27 × 23.24 ± 2.11 mm, white or yellowish, patent, ovate, obtuse at apex, adaxially glabrous and abaxially with white minute hairs (Fig. 3a–b). The corolline corona is white or yellowish; corona segments are 2.67 ± 0.21 mm long, succulent, ligulate, slightly curved outwards at margins, obtuse at apex (Fig. 3c–f). Stamens are 3.46 ± 0.13 × 4.10 ± 0.23 mm; anthers have an apical appendage postgenitally fused to the base of style-head, margins are narrowly lignified along the both sides forming five guide rails between the anthers (Fig. 3g–h). The style-head is rostrate and apically bifid; pistil is 10.85 ± 0.52 mm long (Fig. 3i–j).

Cionura erecta (L.) Griseb. in Spic. Fl. Rumel. 2: 69 (1844) [40]: Inflorescences are terminal or lateral, erect, with branched cymes bearing 22–58 flowers. Peduncle is 26.6 ± 9.8 mm and pedicel is 9.6 ± 2.5 mm long (Fig. 2b); peduncle and pedicel with crisped indumenta. Discussion

Floral features

The inflorescence type of the studied Asclepiadoideae taxa is basically a cyme developed mainly on axillary and/or terminal parts, except for A. sericifera with solitary flower per node (Fig. 2). Floral features, commonly used as key characters in many floras and revision studies, had always played an important role in explaining the evolutionary relationships among the major groups of Apocynaceae [4, 6]. Previous morphological studies showed that Periplocoideae were characterized with the type of inflorescence varying from terminal and/or axillary branching thyrses to extra-axillary branching ones with racemose, corymbose or sciadioidal inflorescences [1, 24]. However, the Asclepiadoideae were reported to exhibit mostly extra axillary, and less commonly axillary inflorescences, which were rarely solitary (one inflorescence per node) or paired (two inflorescences per node) [1], consistent with the observations of the present study.

Significant differences are determined within the examined Asclepiadoideae taxa by means of corolla morphology at genus level. In the examined Asclepiadoideae taxa, white, yellowish or pinkish petals are glabrous (C. erecta and C. acutum), or exhibite lanate indumenta along right margins (G. fruticosus), and white minute hairs on the abaxial side (A. sericifera). Two corolla types are observed in Asclepiadoideae such as rotate and campanulate. A. sericifera clearly differentiates from the remaining Asclepiadoideae taxa by its campanulate flowers, but the given character (X6) is insignificant for meaningful taxonomic discrimination from the studied taxa (Table 3). Consistent with our findings, Fishbein et al. [2] stated that corolla shape within the APSA clade is highly labile due to the frequency of occurrence of campanulate, rotate, and tubular corollas and rarity of funnelform and salverform forms, than among the rauvolfioid lineages with predominant salverform corollas. Similarly, it was stated that type (campanulate/rotate), color (white, yellow, yellowish-green, blackish-purple, and brown), and pubescence (glabrous or with hirsute, lanate, pilose, and crisped indument) of corolla exhibited significant variation among the Turkish Vincetoxicum [22, 25] taxa at species and even sub-species levels. In addition, of the two examined Turkish Periploca taxa, P. gracilis with uniform white or greenish-yellow, and glabrous petals, could easily be distinguished from P. graeca in terms of petal color (purple-violet) and pubescence (with villous indument along margins). However, principal components analysis showed that corolla type (X6) and pubescence of petal inner surface (X9) were not among the most important traits to distinguish the examined taxa in the present study (Table 3).

In the present study, the observed variation in corona structure supports its diagnostic value in distinguishing closely related taxa. Among the examined Asclepiadoideae taxa, only A. sericifera has corolline corona, while the remaining four Asclepiadoideae taxa exhibit gynostegial corona (Fig. 12). It has been observed that the two Turkish Periplocoideae species, which are characterized with filiform, basally tri-segmented corolline corona segments, are also quite different by means of corona shape. P. gracilis has apically bifid corona segments, while those of P. graeca are unsegmented. The significance of corona is also demonstrated in previous studies on Turkish Vincetoxicum taxa [22, 25]. Corona is a special floral structure and plays a key role in pollination of the Apocynaceae taxa by producing and storing nectar to attract pollinators [45], and guiding body parts of insects for pollinia removal and insertion [5, 6]. Two main types of corona were defined for Apocynaceae taxa depending on their location: (I) corolline corona arising from corolla, and (II) gynostegial corona derived from androecium. There is a great variety of corona forms as rings, scales, pads, or cups, often with highly specialized components within the Apocynaceae. Corolline coronas could be seen in the form of free lobes, or fused to varying degrees into petaloid structures, whereas gynostegial corona displayed two basic forms composed of free staminal segments or united staminal segments joined to eachother by smaller interstaminal parts [2, 6, 46, 47]. Fishbein et al. [2] confirmed that the corolline corona was an ancestral character, indicating an evolutionary transition within the family associated with increasing floral specialization and pollination efficiency. However, no homogeneity was determined in the corona morphology within the large groups of the Apocynaceae family. In rauvolfioids, apocynoids and Periplocoideae coronas were mostly corolline, whereas the majority of the Asclepiadoideae were chacterized by gynostegial coronas [1, 2], reflecting a general evolutionary trend toward derived corona forms in Asclepiadoideae. The examined Periplocoideae taxa, representing a more basal group, are characterized by the ancestral corolline corona. In contrast, the majority of the examined Asclepiadoideae taxa, considered a more derived lineage, predominantly exhibit the gynostegial corona. This pattern indicates an evolutionary shift from corolline to gynostegial corona within the family, supporting the generally accepted view.

Significant differences in gynostegium morphology were observed between the two studied subfamilies. In the Asclepiadoideae taxa examined, the gynostegium is characterized by glabrous anthers with lignified guide rails along both margins (except in G. fruticosus, where guide rails are absent). The style-head is unlobed in C. acutum and G. fruticosus, but bifid in A. sericifera and C. erecta. Also, the gynostegia of the two Turkish Periplocoideae species are characterized by anthers with non-lignified, membranous margins, abaxially hispid indumentum, and an unlobed style-head. Principal components analyses also revealed that pubescence of the anther (X16), shape of style-head (X17), and length of pistil (X18) are among the partially effective characters in delimiting the investigated taxa at both subfamily and genus levels (Table 3). The gynostegium is a key evolutionary feature in Apocynaceae, formed by the fusion of anthers and the style-head. It is absent in basal groups but becomes progressively more complex in Periplocoideae, Secamonoideae, and Asclepiadoideae. This increasing structural complexity is associated with enhanced pollination efficiency and specialization, reflecting both phylogenetic relationships and functional adaptation. The patterns observed in the present study are in agreement with this evolutionary trend and further support the role of gynostegium morphology in distinguishing major lineages within the family [1, 2, 5].

Palynological features

Style-head differentiation and pollen morphology constitute the main pathways for the evolution of the major groups in Apocynaceae [1]. The enlarged upper part of the postgenitally fused region, the style-head, not only performs as a stigma, but also produces adhesive components forming the transporter parts of the pollen grains in Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae [1, 2, 5]. The main distinguishing characters of the investigated taxa, including style-head differentiation and pollen morphology, are illustrated in Fig. 1. Pollen grains are transported in two quite specialized forms within the investigated taxa: in the form of a large tetrad mass shed onto a translator composed of a receptacle, stalk, and adhesive disc in Periplocoideae (Fig. 1b), and in the form of two pollinia linked to a central corpusculum via two caudicles (pollinarium) in Asclepiadoideae (Fig. 1d, f). The present principal components analyses revealed that pollen aggregation (X19), number of tetrad mass or pollinium on a pollen translator (X20), and pollen translator structure (X21) were found to be partially effective characters in delimiting the investigated taxa at the subfamily level (Table 3).

Although the two examined Periploca species (P. gracilis and P. graeca) reflect the general palynological characteristics of the subfamily, shape of receptacle and size of translator parts differ from each other. In the investigated P. gracilis, receptacle is spoon shaped and receptacle, stalk and adhesive disc is smaller in size compared to the examined P. graeca species with elliptical receptacle. At anthesis, pollen grains, which are arranged in rhomboidal or decussate tetrads, shed onto the receptacle from each two adjacent anther theca, form a loosely connected pollen mass. The present findings related to tetrad morphology of P. gracilis and P. graeca are compatible with those of Verhoeven and Venter [48].

In the present study, two types of pollinium shape are identified: ovate in A. sericifera and C. acutum; and obovate in C. erecta and G. fruticosus. Among the examined taxa, A. sericifera and G. fruticosus species had the largest pollinia, while C. erecta had the smallest pollinium. Within the Asclepiadoideae members, the pollinium shows great diversity in shape such as reniform, clavate, circular, elliptical, square, lanceolate, ovate, obovate and oblong, and this character contributes significantly to the distinction of taxa at both generic and specific levels [8, 17, 29]. Feng et al. [14] reported pollinium shape as perprolate (width/length < 0.5) for C. acutum subsp. sibiricum, unlike our data (ovate and with the rate of width/length 0.66). The data on pollinium width and length has provided useful results in distinguishing Asclepiadoideae members at both the genus and species levels as agreed by Chang et al. [13], Feng et al. [14], and Güven et al. [29].

In the current study, pollen grain surface is smooth in A. sericifera and C. erecta, psilate and sparsely rough in both subspecies of Cynanchum, and crystalized in G. fruticosus. Pollen grain surface ornamentation is among the widely used palynological characters to elucidate the taxonomic problems in developed plant groups [49]. This character varies at both the genus and species levels within the members of Asclepiadoideae [9, 11]. Mo et al. [11] reported that pollen grain surface ornamentation of some Asclepiadoideae taxa was rugose, papillose-smooth, sinuous-striate, sinuous-smooth and sulcate-granulate. Similar to our findings, pollen grain surface was reported as psilate for C. acutum [14, 16, 17]. Güven et al. [22, 29] reported that pollen grain surface of Vincetoxicum taxa varied from rugulate to gemmate. In the present paper, pollen grain width and length are important diagnostic characters, whereas pollen grain ornamentation are negligible to distinguish the examined taxa.

Among the examined taxa, majority of the taxa has pendulous pollinia with an exception to C. erecta with erect pollinia (Fig. 12). Pollinia orientation within Asclepiadoideae varies from erect to horizontal, or pendulous [6, 15]. This character was determined as stable at the genus and species levels in the subfamily Asclepiadoideae, but showed significant changes at the suprageneric level [15, 16].

In the taxa examined (except for A. sericifera), caudicles are connected to the corpusculum near the tip and to the pollinia near the base. However, in A. sericifera, it is observed that the caudicles are attached to the pollinia at the base and to the corpusculum along the two sides, and it is clearly distinguished from other examined taxa by a winged corpusculum (Fig. 12). Many studies on the Asclepiadoideae demonstrated that, in a pollinarium, caudicles may be attached to the pollinia from the base, near the base or in the middle, and to the corpusculum from the tip, near the tip, from the base, near the base or the middle [29, 43]. Shape of the caudicles, contributing significantly to the distinction of the examined taxa, is determined as flattened in A. sericifera, cylindrical in C. erecta and G. fruticosus, and triangular in C. acutum (Fig. 12). Among the studied taxa, the largest caudicle is measured for A. sericifera and the smallest one for C. erecta. Palynological studies on members of Asclepiadoideae revealed that caudicles may be cylindrical, triangular, flattened, straight or curved, and their sizes vary significantly among taxa. These variations in caudicle attachment shape and size provide useful diagnostic characters for distinguishing both genera and species within the taxa studied and reflect structural diversification associated with pollination mechanisms [10, 15, 29].

C. erecta with an oblong corpusculum was clearly distinguished from the other examined taxa characterized with ovate corpusculum (Fig. 12). Among the taxa examined, A. sericifera has the largest corpusculum and C. erecta has the smallest one. It is known that the shape of corpusculum in the Asclepiadoideae greatly varied among the species such as ovate, oblong, circular, elliptical, rhombic and square. Similarly, differences in corpusculum shape and size contribute to the clear delimitation of taxa at both genus and species levels and further support their taxonomic significance within Asclepiadoideae [18, 29].

Pollen grains are traditionally considered conservative structures with strong genetic control; however, accumulating evidence demonstrates substantial intraspecific variability in palynological traits driven by ecological conditions [50]. To evaluate environmental effects, samples from different populations of some taxa were included. Although habitat conditions may cause minor variation in pollen traits, numerical analyses consistently grouped individuals of the same species together, indicating that palynological characters are stable and reliable at the species level.

A floral and palynological perspective on Periplocoideae ve Asclepiadoideae

In some earlier classifications, Asclepiadaceae and Periplocoideae were treated as distinct families due to their diverse flower and pollen grain characteristics [51–55]. In Periplocoideae, the style-head has five vertically oriented grooves between anthers, where pollen translators are secreted. Each translator consists of a basal adhesive disc, a stalk, and an apical receptacle, onto which tetrads are shed, forming a loosely connected pollen mass at anthesis [4, 5]. On the other hand, Asclepiadoideae are characterized with five pollinaria located alternately with the anthers on each stigmatic zone near the lower edges of the style-head, in which translator parts (corpusculum and caudicles) are secreted. Each pollinarium consists of a pair of pollinia attached to a central corpusculum by two flexible arms [4, 5]. However, both Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae are related at a lower level because their flowers expose a similar synorganization between androecium and gynoecium, forming a gynostegium. The style-head produces specialized translators that are connected to aggregated pollen grains or pollinia. These features represent key synapomorphic characters shared by both subfamilies. Such an anther–style synorganization is required for formation and transport of aggregated pollen grains in both Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae. The two subfamilies only develop distinct floral structures and pollination mechanisms in order to reach their goals [1, 2, 5]. In contrast, differences in translator structure and pollinarium organization represent lineage-specific derived traits that distinguish the two subfamilies.

The examined taxa of Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae were evaluated using cluster analysis and principal components analysis based on the selected 24 morphological and palynological traits. Both analyses showed a general grouping tendency rather than a complete separation at subfamily and genus levels. In both the dendrogram derived from UPGMA (Fig. 12) and the ordination resulting from PCA (Fig. 13), the members of Periplocoideae grouped closely, taxa of Cynanchum and Cionura positioned closer to this group, while taxa of A. sericifera and G. fruticosus were distinctly aggregated in separated locations away from the remaining Asclepiadoideae taxa. However, the overall pattern indicated partial overlap between Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae, suggesting that the separation is not completely distinct in multivariate space. The investigated Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae taxa exhibited quite different flower and pollen morphology at the subfamily level. All the Periplocoideae taxa used in numerical analyses are characterised by corolline coronas with filiform segments, 4-locular anthers with abaxially a hispid indument, and pollen grains in tetrads carried by spoon-shaped structures, while those of Asclepiadoideae have corolline or gynostegial coronas with laminaris or succulent segments, glabrous, 2-locular anthers, and pollen grains in pollinia carried by clip-like transporters (Fig. 12). Within the taxa examined, several characters show different evolutionary significance. In Periplocoideae, tetrad-based pollen structure and spoon-shaped pollen transport system and in Asclepiadoideae, the presence of pollinia and a specialized pollinarium system consisting of corpusculum, caudicles, and pollinia is also regarded as synapomorphic features. In contrast, pedicel, sepal, petal and corona segment lengths, stigma shape, pistil length, shape and size of pollen translator, and pollen grain size, represent more variable and potentially derived (autapomorphic) features (Table 3). Similar to our results, Sarıkaya and Güven [23] have recently identified important fruit and seed morphological characteristics that contribute to the distinction of the species constituting the subject of the present study at both subfamily and genus levels. They reported that the two Turkish Periploca taxa, which were characterized by a pair of follicles, flattened, narrowly oblong, and winged seeds with rugulate ornemantation, were clearly distinct within Periplocoideae based on these fruit and seed traits. In contrast, taxa of Araujia, Cionura, Cynanchum and Gomphocarpus (Asclepiadoideae) were characterized by single follicles, and ovoid to narrowly ovoid, winged or wingless seeds, with a distinct convex raphae side and a concave hilar side, and smooth to tuberculate or colliculate ornemantation [23].Fig. 13Ordination resulting from principal components analysis (PCA) of the investigated Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae taxa based on 24 floral and pollen characters, (a) PCA case scores, (b) PCA variable loadings

Araujia sericifera and Gomphocarpus fruticosus were positioned far from the other examined taxa in the present numerical analysis (Fig. 12). Both A. sericifera, a recently naturalized species for Türkiye, and G. fruticosus, an ornamental plant, are quite remarkable due to their floral and pollen characteristics. We observed that both species have specific succulent coronas and the largest pollinia within the investigated Asclepiadoideae taxa. Additionally, A. sericifera is the only Asclepiadoideae taxon with individual flowers, campanulate corolla, corolline corona, and winged corpusculum. Similarly, these two species draw attention with their characteristic large fruits, and wingless and warty seeds with tuberculate ornamentation. While follicles of A. sericifera are oblong-ovoid and densely covered with short crisped hairs, those of G. fruticosus are inflated-ovoid with a short beak at apex, and echinate surface composed of soft bristles up to 1 cm [23].

According to Browicz [20], Cynanchum acutum is represented by two subspecies in Türkiye, separated from each other by leaf and fruit morphology. The first one, C. acutum subsp. acutum, has leaves one to two times as long as broad, broadly ovate-cordate or narrow linear with basal lobes scarcely divergent, and 6–15 cm long follicles. The second, C. acutum subsp. sibiricum, is characterized by leaves one to three times as long as broad, broadly cordate to hastate-cordate, or oblong-hastate, basal lobes distinctly divergent, and follicles 12.5–16 cm in length. However, Sarıkaya and Güven [23] determined that the follicle length gave similar results within individuals collected from different populations of C. acutum subsp. sibiricum (110–150 mm) and C. acutum subsp. acutum (90–175 mm). Therefore, the follicle length was found to be insufficient to distinguish the subspecies, as being the seed morphology [23]. The present morphological and palynological examinations showed that these two subspecies exhibited very similar morphological and palynological characteristics. However, the level of inter connection of corona segments partly differs at the subspecies level. While corona segments were fused up to 2/3 of their length in all the C. acutum subsp. sibiricum (Fig. 7c–d) and the majority of C. acutum subsp. acutum (Fig. 6c–d), some taxa of C. acutum subsp. acutum had distinct coronas with segments divided to the base (Fig. 5c–d). Detailed examinations on samples collected from different populations of C. acutum subsp. acutum, demonstrated that corona segments were partially united in some populations and were free in others. The current results revealed two corona types (free and united) for C. acutum subsp. acutum for the first time, and this difference has been accepted as a variation within the subspecies. In addition, as seen in Fig. 12, all the examined individuals belonging to two subspecies of C. acutum from a total of four populations clustered under the same branch at the 94% similarity level. However, specimens of C. acutum subsp. acutum from two populations (S. Güven 190 & S. Makbul and S. Güven 183 & S. Makbul) clustered with C. acutum subsp. sibiricum, while the third one, C. acutum subsp. acutum (S. Güven 188 & S. Makbul), formed a separate branch. It can easily be seen in Appendix that C. acutum subsp. acutum individuals with S. Güven 188 & S. Makbul collector no, have pedicel (X1), sepal (X3, X4), petal (X7, X8), stamen (X14, X15), pistil (X18) and pollen grains (X22, X23) in larger size compared the remaining C. acutum individuals.

Conclusion

Consequently, in the present study four Asclepiadoideae species (A. sericifera, C. erecta, C. acutum and G. fruticosus), which have important deficiencies in their descriptions in the flora of Türkiye (Browicz 1978), were investigated by means of both flower and pollen morphology, and two Periplocoideae species (P. gracilis and P. graeca) by means of pollen morphology, for the first time. The results highlight that several reproductive characters such as length of pedicel, sepal, petal and corona segment, pubescence of outer surface of anther, shape of stigma, length of pistil, type of pollen aggregation, number of tetrad mass and pollinium per pollen translator, pollen translator structure, and width and length of pollen grains were effective in distinguishing the examined taxa at subfamily and genus levels. These findings emphasize the taxonomic value of combined floral and palynological traits in distinguishing closely related taxa and contribute to a better understanding of character evolution within Apocynaceae. Additionally, despite the relatively limited taxon sampling, this study is an important breakthrough for the Turkish taxa of Periplocoideae and Asclepiadoideae by explaining the deficiencies in flower and pollen morphology of the examined species in the flora of Türkiye. Notably, the identification of two corona types (free and united) within C. acutum subsp. acutum provides additional evidence supporting infra-specific distinction of the species C. acutum.

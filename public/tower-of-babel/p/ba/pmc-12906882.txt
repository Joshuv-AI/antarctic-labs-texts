Risk Factors Influencing Recurrence Rates Following Open Excision of Wrist Ganglion Cysts: A Retrospective Cohort Study
Authors: Baris Acar, Ahmet Senel, Ahmet Sinan Kalyenci, Saltuk Bugra Tekin, Onur Gunday
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesGanglion cysts are the most common benign masses of the hand and wrist, frequently requiring surgical intervention due to symptoms such as pain, weakness, or aesthetic concerns. Open excision is a widely used method with varying recurrence rates reported in the literature. However, risk factors contributing to recurrence remain incompletely understood. Our study aims to evaluate the recurrence rates following open excision of wrist ganglion cysts and identify patient-related and surgical factors influencing recurrence.MethodsPatients who underwent wrist ganglion excision between 2012 and 2024 were identified using the National Healthcare Implementation Communique code 611650. Exclusion criteria included cases without ganglion pathology confirmation, follow-up of less than one year, volar cysts, multiple cysts, first-time surgery for recurrence at the institution, incomplete medical records, and arthroscopic excision. Recurrence rates were calculated, and patient demographics, cyst characteristics, and surgical variables were recorded. Surgical experience was defined as years worked as an orthopedic surgeon. Cyst volume was measured using Magnetic Resonance Imaging data.ResultsA total of 109 patients with dorsal wrist ganglion cysts who underwent surgical excision were retrospectively reviewed. The overall recurrence rate is 11.9%. Ganglion cysts are more commonly observed in women and laborers. Surgical experience and the use of a tourniquet were found to be associated with recurrence (p<0.001 and p=0.019, respectively). Correlation analysis revealed no significant correlation between recurrence and age, follow-up duration, or cyst volume. According to the multivariate regression analysis, the most significant risk factors for recurrence are tourniquet use and surgical experience.ConclusionThe use of a tourniquet and high surgical experience reduce the recurrence rate after open excision of ganglion cysts.

Ganglion cysts are among the most common benign soft tissue masses of the hand and wrist. These cysts, most frequently located on the dorsal aspect of the wrist, may cause pain, functional limitations, or aesthetic concerns, prompting patients to seek treatment.[1] Although non-surgical methods such as observation or aspiration are commonly employed as initial approaches, the high recurrence rates of aspiration, about 80%, often necessitate surgical intervention.[2,3] Surgical excision, particularly open excision, remains a widely used treatment method due to its lower recurrence rates compared to aspiration. However, recurrence after open excision has been reported in a broad range, from 1% to 50%, highlighting the need to identify and understand factors that contribute to recurrence.[4]

Factors influencing recurrence rates following open excision include patient demographics, cyst location, and surgical technique. Studies have shown that volar ganglion cysts, male gender, younger age, and procedures performed on the dominant hand may be associated with higher recurrence rates.[5,6] Additionally, surgeon experience has emerged as a critical variable, with less experienced surgeons demonstrating higher recurrence rates in certain studies.[7] These findings emphasize the multifactorial nature of recurrence and the importance of addressing these variables in clinical practice.

The aim of this study is to evaluate the risk factors associated with recurrence following open excision of wrist ganglion cysts, with a focus on identifying predictors of recurrence that can guide surgical decision-making and improve outcomes.

Methods

This retrospective study was conducted following the approval of our Hospital Ethics Committee (Approval no: 132, date: 29.11.2024). Informed consent was obtained from all patients. This study adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

The overall recurrence rate was calculated, and factors potentially influencing recurrence were recorded. These included patient-related parameters (age, sex, occupation, dominance of the hand), the use of a tourniquet during surgery, cyst size, and surgeon experience. All surgeons routinely perform dorsal ganglion cyst excision. Surgical experience was defined as the number of years the surgeon had been practicing as an orthopedic surgeon.

To determine cyst volume, Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) measurements were utilized. Medial-Lateral (M-L) and Anterior-Posterior (A-P) dimensions were measured on axial sections, and Proximal-Distal (P-D) dimensions were measured on coronal sections (Fig. 1). Cyst volume was then calculated using the formula:[8]

Volume (mm)=4/3×π×(M-L)×(A-P)×(P-D).

Surgical Method

Under local anesthesia, with or without a tourniquet, a longitudinal or transverse incision was made over the ganglion cyst. The tourniquet was not used in some cases due to patient intolerance, preexisting vascular conditions, or surgeon preference. The surrounding structures, including the radial artery, superficial radial nerve, and tendons, were carefully dissected and preserved. The cyst was excised completely along with its stalk, ensuring no residual tissue was left behind. After irrigation with saline, the skin was closed. A sterile dressing and compressive bandage were applied. No immobilization was used in any patient.

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 22.0 (Chicago, Illinois, USA, IBM). The normality test was performed using Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Skewness-Kurtosis tests. Quantitative data were expressed as mean, standard deviation (SD), maximum, and minimum. Categorical data were expressed as number (n) and percentage (n%). The Mann-Whitney U test was conducted to compare the recurrence groups (yes vs. no) in terms of cyst diameter and surgeon experience. Comparison between the recurrence groups for categorical data was performed using the Chi-Square test. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to investigate associations between recurrence and variables. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was used to assess independent variables affecting recurrence. ‘0’ was used for no recurrence, and ‘1’ was used for yes recurrence in the logistic regression model. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

The study consisted of 109 patients. The mean age was 38.62 (range, 16–70). The mean follow-up duration was 51.52 (range, 10–120) months. Our overall recurrence rate was 11.9% (13 of 109 wrists). Table 1 presents the demographic and descriptive data. Ganglion cysts were more commonly observed in women (65.1%), in the dominant wrist (67.9%), and in laborers (41.3%) (Fig. 2).

When the relationship between general variables and recurrence was examined, a significant correlation was found with the use of a tourniquet and surgical experience (p=0.019 and p<0.001, respectively). Table 2 summarizes the risk factors associated with recurrence. In the Spearman correlation analysis, no correlation was found between recurrence and follow-up time, age, or cyst volume (p=0.721, r=−0.035; p=0.310, r=−0.099; p=0.154, r=0.138, respectively). A negative correlation was detected between surgical experience and recurrence (p=0.042, r=−0.196) (Table 3).

The results of multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that the use of a tourniquet was the most important risk factor (OR=0.240; 95% CI=0.640–0.900) for recurrence, followed by surgical experience (OR=0.886; 95% CI=0.798–0.985) (Table 4).

Discussion

The overall recurrence rate in our study was approximately 12%. A review of the literature reveals a wide range of recurrence rates. In their systematic review, Horvath et al.[9] reported that recurrence rates after ganglion cyst excision varied heterogeneously, ranging from 6% to 41%. Kang et al.[10] reported a 9% recurrence rate in their prospective study. Similarly, Clark et al.[11] reported an overall recurrence rate of 11.2% across seven different studies in a review comparing arthroscopic and open excision methods.

The relationship between patient-related factors and recurrence is controversial, and there is no consensus. Cluts et al.[2] reported that recurrence was more common in males but was not affected by age or the location of the cyst. Kim et al.[5] found that recurrence increased on the dominant side, in individuals under 24 years of age, and in females. Rollins et al.[12] demonstrated that the recurrence rate decreased in males. In a study with aspiration guided by ultrasonography, recurrence was not influenced by demographic data.[13] Dias et al.[14] reported that recurrence increased as the follow-up period lengthened. In our study, recurrence was not affected by patient-related factors or follow-up duration.

Another factor that is less frequently mentioned in the literature but may be associated with recurrence is cyst volume. As seen in the correlation analysis of our study, no relationship was found between cyst volume and recurrence. Schicke et al.[15] reported that recurrence was more common in larger cysts, while another study found no effect of cyst size on recurrence.[16]

In the literature, only one study evaluating the relationship between tourniquet use and recurrence has been identified. Uludağ et al.[16] reported that tourniquet use does not affect recurrence. Similarly, Ertem et al.[17] found no relationship between tourniquet use and recurrence rates following the excision of giant cell tendon tumors. In our study, we observed significantly lower recurrence rates in patients who underwent surgery with a tourniquet. We believe that better hemostasis achieved under tourniquet use allows improved visualization of the mass and more precise removal of the stalk, thereby reducing recurrence.

In orthopedic surgeries, it is well established that complication rates decrease with increasing surgical experience. Previous studies have demonstrated that recurrence rates following ganglion cyst excision decline as surgical experience improves.[2, 5, 18] In our study, we employed the more robust method of correlation analysis and identified a significant inverse relationship between surgical experience and recurrence rates. We hypothesize that this is attributable to enhanced dissection skills with increased experience, facilitating more complete excision of the mass, including its stalk, thereby minimizing the risk of recurrence.

Limitations

Limitations of our study include the retrospective design, potential changes in recurrence rates due to patients lost to follow-up or those who could not be reached, and the possibility of misclassifying a new cyst located very close to the previously excised one as a recurrence, potentially leading to an overestimation of the recurrence rate.

Conclusion

Our study demonstrated that the absence of tourniquet use and limited surgical experience are risk factors for recurrence after ganglion cyst excision. We recommend the use of a tourniquet during ganglion cyst excision surgery.

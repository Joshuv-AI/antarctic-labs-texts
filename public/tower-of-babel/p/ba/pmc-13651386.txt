Spatiotemporal epidemiology of hepatitis B in Central China: urbanization drivers and implications for adult-targeted control strategies
Authors: Yanquan Mo, Shanhui Li, Ziqian Zhao, Huiqun Jia, Jingya Zhao, Feng Liu, Caixia Dang, Liyang Guo, Yuanyong Xu, Yeqing Tong, Hui Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatitis B virus (HBV) infection remains a significant global public health challenge. Hubei Province, a historically high-prevalence area in central China, continues to face persistently high rates of adult HBsAg carriers despite effective reduction of childhood transmission through universal vaccination, hindering progress toward HBV elimination goals. This study aims to identify key drivers behind the rising hepatitis B incidence in the region, forecast future trends, and inform targeted control strategies.MethodsThis population-based spatiotemporal epidemiological study analyzed hepatitis B surveillance data from Hubei Province between January 2015 and December 2024, covering 631,361 reported cases across 17 prefecture-level divisions. Spatial clustering was assessed using Global Moran’s I statistics. Urbanization-related spatial drivers were identified via geographically weighted random forest modeling with SHAP value interpretation, and future incidence trends were predicted using Holt-Winters time series forecasting.ResultsThe average annual incidence was 106.19 per 100,000 population, showing a U-shaped temporal trend: declining from 111.75 per 100,000 in 2015 to 83.58 in 2020, then rising to 125.86 in 2024. Adults aged 30–50 years consistently accounted for 40–50% of total cases. Significant spatial clustering was detected (Global Moran’s I: 0.124–0.307, P < 0.05), with persistent high-risk zones concentrated in the Wuhan metropolitan area. The geographically weighted random forest model identified urbanization rate as the primary spatial driver (SHAP value ≈2.0), followed by higher education enrollment rate (≈1.0) and passenger traffic volume (≈0.8). Time series forecasting projected monthly incidence rates of 8.98–10.41 per 100,000 over the subsequent 18 months (R2 = 0.892, MAPE = 1.74%).ConclusionIn Hubei Province, the hepatitis B burden has concentrated in adults born before the vaccine era, underscoring horizontal transmission risks in this susceptible cohort. Urbanization significantly impacts the spatial distribution of hepatitis B, with high-risk areas mainly concentrated in the Wuhan metropolitan region. It is crucial to address urbanization-related factors affecting HBV transmission and to implement targeted screening and revaccination for adults aged 30–50, particularly among high-risk individuals, mobile populations, and rural residents, to reduce incidence and alleviate the regional disease burden.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-026-28412-y.

Introduction

Hepatitis B, caused by the hepatitis B virus (HBV), is a major global public health concern. HBV infection can progress from acute or chronic hepatitis to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), making it one of the leading causes of liver disease-related mortality worldwide [1]. China was once a high-prevalence country for hepatitis B with a substantial disease burden; prior to the implementation of routine immunization, the national hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) carrier rate approached 10%, imposing heavy economic burdens on families and society [2].

To address this, China implemented sequential hepatitis B prevention measures: routine neonatal hepatitis B vaccination in 1992, official inclusion of the vaccine in the national immunization program in 2002, and free universal neonatal vaccination since 2005 [3]. These interventions fundamentally altered the epidemiological pattern of hepatitis B in China, effectively establishing an immune barrier in children and transitioning the country from a high- to a medium-prevalence region [4, 5]. In 2016, the World Health Organization (WHO) proposed the global strategic goal of “eliminating viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030”, with specific targets including a 90% reduction in new chronic infections, a 65% reduction in mortality, and 90% diagnosis and 80% treatment coverage [6]. This global mandate sets higher standards for China’s hepatitis B control efforts, presenting both new opportunities and challenges [7].

HBV transmission is multifaceted: mother-to-child vertical transmission (MTCT) remains a primary route for new infections, with risk closely linked to maternal high viral load (HBV DNA levels) and HBeAg positivity [8, 9]. Horizontal transmission risks include unsafe sexual behavior, iatrogenic exposure (e.g., unsterile injections, surgical procedures), and high-risk behaviors such as needle sharing [4]. Accurate identification of transmission risk factors and development of robust predictive models are critical for optimizing control strategies, rationalizing healthcare resource allocation, and quantifying transmission dynamics. While HBV prediction models have evolved from traditional statistical approaches to machine learning paradigms—with decision trees, random forests, support vector machines, and advanced ensemble algorithms (e.g., XGBoost) demonstrating superior accuracy and robustness in predicting MTCT and new infections—core limitations persist, including data silos and inadequate model generalization [10, 11].

Hubei Province, with 58 million people and a key role in the Yangtze River Economic Belt, provides a valuable case for studying regional HBV challenges. Despite urbanization rising from 56.3% in 2015 to 65.8% in 2024 [12], which changed population movement, and maintaining over 95% neonatal vaccination coverage, adult HBsAg rates stay high and immunity in some groups is weak [2]. This study uses an integrated approach combining Geographically Weighted Random Forest (GWRF) to find local HBV transmission drivers and Holt-Winters time series forecasting to track seasonal trends [13, 14]. Using 10 years of surveillance data (2015–2024) from over 630,000 hepatitis B cases along with other sources, the study seeks to clarify HBV trends and regional risk factors. The results will support more targeted HBV prevention strategies in China.

Methods

Study design and data sources

This study analyzed hepatitis B surveillance data from Hubei Province from January 2015 to December 2024. The study included 631,361 reported cases from the statutory infectious disease surveillance system, covering all 17 prefecture-level divisions (eFigure 1). The dataset comprised monthly incidence rates, case numbers, and mortality data stratified by region and age group. Socioeconomic indicators (urbanization rate, gross regional product, higher education enrollment rate, passenger traffic volume, hospital beds, and licensed physicians) were sourced from the Hubei Statistical Yearbook (2015–2024) [12]. Prefecture-level HBV vaccination coverage data were provided by the Hubei Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention, based on routine Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI) administrative reporting, and reflect neonatal/programmatic coverage rather than population-wide adult immunity. The vectorized municipal-level map was obtained from the National Fundamental Geographic Information System of China. The study was exempt from ethics review as it used deidentified surveillance data without patient involvement.

Case classification and primary outcome definition

In China's statutory infectious disease surveillance system, hepatitis B cases are reported as either acute or chronic according to the national diagnostic standard (WS 299), based on clinical presentation and serological markers—most notably IgM anti-HBc for acute infection—at the time of reporting [15]. By convention, acute hepatitis B denotes a recently acquired and typically self-limiting infection, whereas chronic hepatitis B is defined by the persistence of HBsAg for at least six months [16]. Notably, the reported "chronic" category predominantly captures newly identified rather than newly infected cases, such that its counts are strongly influenced by screening intensity, healthcare utilization, and diagnostic access rather than by incident transmission alone. Because total hepatitis B incidence integrates both the transmission signal (acute cases) and the case-finding/healthcare-access signal (newly identified chronic cases), and because it is the metric underlying provincial prevention and control policy, total incidence was used as the primary analytical outcome of the GWRF model. Separate GWRF models for acute and chronic incidence were additionally fitted under identical configurations and hyperparameter setting.

Results

Epidemiological profile of hepatitis B in Hubei Province, 2015–2024

Between 2015 and 2024, a total of 631,361 hepatitis B cases were reported in Hubei Province, with an average annual incidence rate of 106.19 per 100,000 population. The overall trend in incidence initially decreased followed by a subsequent increase: from 111.750 per 100,000 in 2015 to 83.582 per 100,000 in 2020, representing a decline of 25.2% (Fig. 1A). After 2020, the incidence gradually rebounded, reaching 125.863 per 100,000 in 2024, which corresponds to an increase of 50.6% compared to the 2020 rate.Fig. 1Hepatitis B surveillance data in Hubei Province, 2015–2024. Hepatitis B surveillance data in Hubei Province, 2015–2024. A Annual cases and incidence rate.; B Age distribution of reported cases

Regarding age distribution, the cases exhibited a pronounced concentration among the adult population (Fig. 1B). The 30–50 age group accounted for the highest proportion, constituting 40%–50% of total cases. Within this group, the number of cases ranged from 8,000 to 12,000 in the 30–40 subgroup and 7,000 to 11,000 in the 40–50 subgroup. The 20–30 age group represented 15%–20% of cases, with an annual average of 2,000–3,000 cases, while the 50–60 age group accounted for 12%–15%. Fewer cases were recorded among those aged 0–20 and over 60, both consistently remaining below 2,000 cases. The proportional distribution across age groups remained stable throughout the study period, indicating that adults aged 30–50 represent the key demographic for sustained prevention and control efforts.

Spatial distribution patterns and regional disparities of hepatitis B in Hubei Province

Substantial spatial heterogeneity in hepatitis B incidence was observed across the 17 prefecture-level cities in Hubei Province. Based on the decadal average incidence rates, Tianmen City recorded the highest incidence (244.30 per 100,000), followed by Shiyan City (185.65 per 100,000) and Huanggang City (168.45 per 100,000), as illustrated in eFigure 2 in Supplement 1. Intermediate-high rates were identified in Jingzhou and Xianning (147.36–138.53 per 100,000), while moderate rates were observed in Xiaogan and Qianjiang (117.87–115.98 per 100,000). The Enshi Prefecture exhibited the lowest incidence (105.96 per 100,000), corresponding to only 93.7% of the provincial average (113.06 per 100,000).

Regarding spatiotemporal evolution, high-incidence areas (≥ 150 per 100,000) during 2015–2018 were primarily concentrated in Tianmen, Shiyan, and Huanggang. From 2019 to 2022, incidence declined in most regions, leading to a more moderated spatial distribution pattern. In the 2023–2024 period, a resurgence was observed in previously high-incidence areas, accompanied by an upward trend in Wuhan, Huangshi, and Ezhou. This resulted in a noticeable expansion of the spatial extent of high-risk regions (eFigure 2).

Spatial clustering characteristics of hepatitis B in Hubei Province

Global spatial autocorrelation analysis revealed that the annual Moran's I values ranged from 0.078 to 0.307, with corresponding Z-scores between 1.384 and 5.158. The P-values for the period 2017–2024 were all statistically significant (Global Moran’s I: 0.124–0.307, P < 0.05), indicating the presence of significant spatial clustering. The clustering intensity peaked in 2019 (Moran's I = 0.307) and remained significant in 2024 (Moran's I = 0.148, P < 0.01).

Local spatial autocorrelation analysis identified distinct spatial patterns. From 2015 to 2018, high-high clusters were primarily distributed in Ezhou, Huangshi, and eastern Huanggang around Wuhan, forming a relatively stable core area of high incidence. Low-low clusters were consistently observed in the Enshi Prefecture and its surrounding areas. During 2019–2022, the extent of high-high clusters contracted. However, in 2023–2024, high-high clusters reemerged in Wuhan and its adjacent regions, with their spatial coverage further expanding by 2024. Low-low clusters remained persistently stable in the Enshi area throughout the study period, while sporadic high-low and low–high outliers were distributed along the transition zones between high and low incidence areas (Fig. 2).Fig. 2Local spatial autocorrelation (LISA) cluster maps of hepatitis B in Hubei Province, 2015–2024

Identification of influencing factors and spatial heterogeneity analysis based on geographically weighted random forest model

The Geographically Weighted Random Forest (GWRF) model achieved a satisfactory goodness-of-fit, with R2 values of 0.8798 and 0.8666, and RMSE values of 1.4790 and 1.5691 for the training and test sets, respectively (Figs. 3A-B). SHAP analysis revealed urbanization rate as the most influential factor (mean absolute SHAP value ≈ 2.0), followed by higher education enrollment rate (≈1.0) and passenger traffic volume (≈0.8), while other factors showed comparatively weaker effects (SHAP values < 0.5) (Fig. 3D). Given the limited number of spatial units, we treat the province-level ranking of leading drivers as the principal, generalizable finding, while interpreting finer-grained, city-specific estimates with caution. To examine whether case type influenced these patterns, separate GWRF models were additionally fitted for acute and chronic incidence (eFigures 3–4): the chronic-incidence model performed comparably to the total-incidence model (test R2 = 0.896), whereas the acute-incidence model showed limited explanatory power (test R2 = 0.563), in line with the differing epidemiology of the two outcomes. Discussion

This study investigates the new challenges in hepatitis B control in Hubei Province, a central Chinese high-prevalence region, by analyzing a decade of spatiotemporal surveillance data. Through spatial autocorrelation analysis, geographically weighted random forest (GWRF) model and Holt Winters prediction model, we found that the incidence rate of hepatitis B in Hubei Province presented a significant U-shaped time curve, the high-risk areas were concentrated in the Wuhan metropolitan area, and there were significant differences between different cities in the urbanization phase dependent effect. These findings explain the reason why the incidence rate of hepatitis B increases under the high coverage vaccination rate, and provide a scientific basis for formulating more precise public health strategies.

The pre-2020 declining incidence (111.75 to 83.58 per 100,000) validates the effectiveness of national immunization programs (≥ 95% neonatal vaccination coverage) and enhanced mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) blocking, consistent with China’s overall progress in child HBV control [19, 20]. However, the 51% rebound (to 125.86 per 100,000) post-2020 signals emerging challenges. This resurgence is multifactorial and must be read in its surveillance context. During 2020–2022, pandemic disruptions—suspended routine examinations, screening, and chronic-disease follow-up, plus diversion of public-health staff—likely reduced detection of mild and occult HBV cases, producing an artifactual trough rather than a true prevalence decline [21, 22]. Post-2023 service restoration plausibly drove catch-up detection of accumulated cases, mirroring the "suppression–rebound" pattern documented for blood-borne diseases in mainland China [23]. Relaxed mobility may have additionally amplified genuine cross-regional transmission; both mechanisms likely co-occur and require multidimensional evidence to disentangle. Critically, adults aged 30–50 years (born pre-vaccine era, 1974–1994) consistently accounted for 40–50% of cases, indicating persistent immunity gaps and ongoing horizontal exposure risks (e.g., occupational exposure, population mobility) poorly addressed by current child-centric interventions [20, 24, 25].

Spatially, significant and stable clustering (Global Moran’s I: 0.124–0.307, P < 0.05) with high-risk zones concentrated in the Wuhan metropolitan area highlights geographic structuring of HBV risk [25]. Our geographically weighted random forest (GWRF) model identified urbanization rate as the primary driver (SHAP value≈2.0), with stage-dependent bidirectional effects: rapid urbanization (e.g., Huanggang, Shiyan) intensified transmission via population concentration, while mature urbanization (e.g., Wuhan, Huangshi) reduced risk through robust public health systems [26, 27]. These divergent effects likely reflect urbanization acting as a composite proxy: in rapidly urbanizing prefectures such as Huanggang and Shiyan, large rural-to-urban migrant inflows, expanding occupational exposures, and primary care lagging behind population growth elevate risk [28]; whereas in mature centres such as Wuhan and Huangshi, denser screening, antenatal, and occupational health services may attenuate it [29]. Because these city-specific contrasts rest on limited spatial units, we regard them as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmed. Secondary drivers (higher education enrollment rate, passenger traffic volume) further reflect region-specific transmission dynamics, emphasizing the inadequacy of “one-size-fits-all” interventions [30]. Consistent with this, disaggregated modelling indicated that the total-incidence signal is dominated by the chronic burden—well explained by structural socioeconomic determinants [20]—whereas acute incidence, more closely tied to short-term population mobility and behavioural exposures [31], was less predictable from annual city-level aggregates, reinforcing the value of total incidence as a stable, policy-relevant outcome. The risk-side association of vaccination coverage in some prefectures likely reflects ecological aggregation and a cohort mismatch: coverage indexes recent neonatal cohorts while the case burden lies in pre-vaccine adults, so prefectures with stronger screening register both higher coverage and more detected cases.

Holt-Winters forecasting (R2 = 0.892, MAPE = 1.74%) projects sustained high monthly incidence (8.98–10.41 per 100,000) over the next 18 months. Because the model assumes that the historical level, trend, and seasonal structure persist, this forecast should be interpreted as a projection conditional on current patterns continuing rather than a definitive prediction; in particular, the post-2023 restoration of services and partly catch-up reporting may bias the near-term forecast upward, and any structural change in healthcare-seeking behaviour would alter the trajectory. The point forecast is therefore reported together with its 95% prediction interval (Fig. 5D). Even allowing for this uncertainty, the projected persistence of incidence at elevated levels, combined with the post-2020 rebound, reinforces the need for sustained, geographically targeted prevention and control to avert a long-term high disease burden [32]. Integrating spatial clustering detection, driver identification, and temporal forecasting, our framework enables a shift from reactive surveillance to proactive [30], geographically tailored control—supporting four key interventions: (1) targeted screening and catch-up vaccination for 30–50-year-olds in high-burden clusters; (2) strengthening public health infrastructure in rapidly urbanizing regions; (3) improving healthcare access in resource-constrained peripheries; and (4) establishing interoperable migrant health records.

This study's findings are broadly applicable to regions similar to Hubei Province that are undergoing rapid urbanization and facing resource constraints, providing a replicable paradigm for precision control [33, 34]. However, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, passive surveillance cannot disentangle pandemic under-ascertainment, catch-up reporting, and genuine change, so incidence trends warrant caution and the Holt-Winters forecast—which assumes persistent seasonality and trend—should be read with its prediction intervals. Second, the 10-year window may miss long-term secular trends. Third, GWRF spatial coverage is limited: the province-level driver ranking is robust, but city-specific SHAP estimates (e.g., opposite urbanization effects in Wuhan vs Huanggang) are exploratory and need corroboration. Fourth, prefecture-level vaccination coverage reflects neonatal/programmatic uptake, not adult immunity, and is ecologically aggregated.

Conclusion

In conclusion, our study reveals the evolving epidemiological profile of hepatitis B in Hubei Province, characterized by a concentration of disease burden in adults born before the vaccine era, persistent adult susceptibility, and pronounced spatial clustering driven by urbanization. Through integrated spatiotemporal analysis, we demonstrate that targeted interventions addressing region-specific risk factors—particularly for the 30–50-year-old cohort in high-burden clusters—are essential to counter the projected incidence increase. These findings underscore the necessity of precision public health strategies to achieve sustainable HBV control and progress toward elimination goals.

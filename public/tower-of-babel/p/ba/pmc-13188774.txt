Primary or specialist care at home: multicenter validation study of the “CP.BA.SPE 2025” questionnaire for the identification of the level of palliative care in advanced cancer patients
Authors: Raffaella Bertè, Cristina Pittureri, Nicoletta Crosignani, Gianfranco Martucci, Danila Valenti, Marcella Saccò, Loretta Gulmini, Marco Maltoni, Paolo Vacondio, Simone Storani, Fabrizio Zanotto, Elena Amaducci, Luigi Montanari, Emanuela Scarpi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundReliable tools to discriminate between primary and specialist home palliative care are scarce, and no questionnaire has yet been validated in domiciliary oncology practice. The aim of the study was to validate the 17-item CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire for identifying advanced cancer patients who need specialist home palliative care.MethodsCross-sectional multicentre study (pilot+multicentre validation) conducted in eight Home Palliative Care Units (HPCU) across Emilia-Romagna, Italy (March 2022-June 2024).The CP.BA.SPE 2025 was completed together with the HPCU’s independent judgement of the appropriate care level (gold standard). Item weights were derived with multivariable logistic regression; internal consistency (Kuder-Richardson-20), inter-rater reliability (Cohen’s κ), calibration (Hosmer–Lemeshow) and discrimination (ROC Area Under Curve-AUC) were calculated. A total score ≥14 defined “complex” cases. 543 consecutive adults with metastatic or locally-advanced cancer and Australia-modified Karnofsky Performance Status ≤50 were assessed at first home visit. ResultsThe CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire scores ranged 0-36.5. With the 14-point cut-off, sensitivity was 0.79 (95% CI 0.74-0.84) and specificity 0.82 (95% CI 0.78-0.87), AUC 0.88 (95% CI 0.85-0.91), Brier score 0.14. Inter-rater agreement among individual professionals was excellent (κ 0.83-0.92); agreement between HPCU and an external comparison team was moderate (κ 0.43). Internal consistency was acceptable (Kuder-Richardson-20 = 0.65). The tool classified 251/543 patients (46.3%) as complex, aligning with international estimates of specialist-care need.ConclusionsCP.BA.SPE 2025 is quick to administer, reproducible and shows good diagnostic accuracy for directing advanced cancer patients to the appropriate level of home palliative care. Its adoption could foster equitable resource allocation within home-care networks; external and longitudinal validation are recommended.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12904-026-02072-6.Keywords: Complexity, Home Palliative Care, Questionnaire Validation, Resource Allocation, Advanced Cancer

Background

The growing and ageing population will lead to an increase in chronic degenerative diseases. It is estimated that the incidence of the need for palliative care in Europe might be around over four million people, 42% of whom will be cancer patients [1]. Italy has a population of 60 million, 4.5 million of whom live in the Emilia-Romagna region. Data from the region of Emilia-Romagna show an upward trend in the number of cancer patients in home palliative care, from 6.456 patients followed in 2020 to 9.554 in 2024. It can be estimated that the take-over by Palliative Care, within the Local Networks of Palliative Care (RCP) should affect about 21.910 patients per year, equivalent to 560 deaths per 100.000 residents [2] RCPs in Emilia-Romagna are 8, one for each local health service.

The Emilia-Romagna Regional Council Resolutions 560/2015 and 1770/2016 establish that palliative care at home is provided by the Home Palliative Care Unit (HPCU), made up of General Practitioners (GP), Palliative Care Physicians, and Nurses dedicated to and trained in palliative care and the professional figures necessary to respond to the needs of the patient and his family [3, 4]. They are asked to identify, through the use of validated and shared multidimensional assessment tools, the level of care -primary or specialist- where the patient can find the most appropriate response in relation to needs, disease instability, prognosis, and end-of-life choices. The general practitioner coordinates the primary level of the HPCU, which the palliative care physician supplements with his or her advice. The specialist level is coordinated by the palliative physician, who, in agreement with the GP, assumes clinical responsibility for the case.

To implement the organisation structured in this way, it is indispensable to introduce a tool for the assessment of the clinical complexity of care to identify the correct level of care in which to ascribe advanced-stage cancer patients taken care of at home by the HPCU. A structured assessment of the complexity of the individual patient may in fact facilitate the determination of primary or specialist care needs and thus decisions about the level of care, guiding the integration of services and the management of resources [5, 6]. Although there is no standard definition of complexity, it is generally agreed that it is a multifactorial concept that not only includes patient-dependent elements but is also influenced by other aspects of the care setting [6, 7].

In the literature we find various models of clinical complexity [5–8] and instruments for assessing the need for primary or specialised care, which are influenced by the definitions of complexity adopted and the care systems in which they have been tested [5, 6]. The application of complexity classifications also remains limited, and it is not known how these classifications affect patient care and the utilisation of healthcare services [5].

The clinical judgement of the HPCU team, which defines the clinical-socio-assistential and organisational complexity through a multi-professional and multi-dimensional assessment to highlight the physical, psychological, and socio-economic needs of the patient and his/her family, as well as the management and organisational problems of the system that may arise during the period of care, is currently the gold standard in Italy for defining a patient’s eligibility for primary or specialised palliative care at home.

The ability to recognise the complex needs of the oncological patient in an advanced stage of illness in home palliative care in the Italian context is believed to find support in the co-construction and validation of the questionnaire object of the research “Validation of a system for evaluating the clinical care complexity of the oncological patient in home palliative care,” approved for funding between network programs within the framework of the Emilia-Romagna finalised health research program (FIN-RER) with Resolution no. 861 of 14/06/2021.

The aim of this study was to pretest and assess the understanding of the new CP.BA.SPE (Cure Palliative di BAse-SPEcialistiche) 2025 questionnaire, as well as to enhance its content validity and the reliability of the data, to identify the most appropriate level of care (primary or specialist) for advanced-stage cancer patients requiring home palliative care.

Methods

Questionnaire development

The questionnaire proposed originates from a previous phase of evaluation and approval of its contents by teams composed of general practitioners, palliative care physicians, and nurses of the RER who participated in the non-profit observational prospective study “Evaluation of the complexity of taking care of the oncological patient in advanced stages of illness: elaboration of an instrument to measure the need for primary or specialist home palliative care"9. The study developed a questionnaire to evaluate the clinical, psycho-social, and healthcare complexity of advanced-stage cancer patients needing home palliative care. The questionnaire collected demographic, clinical, and symptom-related data using established palliative care tools. It also assessed factors such as clinical stability, family care, psycho-social complexity, and the presence of refractory symptoms.

The specialist team is activated as the complexity of the patients’ needs increases, guaranteeing high levels of competence (description of Emilia-Romagna healthcare model in Table 1).

Pilot study

The present study consists of two parts: a pilot study aimed at evaluating the new CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire, originated from the previous phase of questionnaire development [9] and designed to identify the most appropriate level of care (primary or specialist) for advanced-stage cancer patients requiring home palliative care, and a cross-sectional multicenter validation study to assess its clinical utility.

During the pilot study, following enrolment, each researcher independently filled out the new CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire (in paper form).

RCP Piacenza set up a structured interview to investigate the clarity of the individual items (poor, good, excellent) and the time of completion (5, 10, 15 or more than 15 min). At the end of the pilot study it also conducted a meeting with the researchers where they were asked to bring to light any difficulties regarding both the implementation of the study (enrolment criteria, collaboration of General Practitioners, patient consent) and the individual items (redundant, missing, confusing questions). For each item, the level of concordance, the opinion of the researchers and their suggestions were considered. For each item with moderate or lower concordance, the review team considered the researchers’ judgement regarding clarity and reworded the item according to their suggestions or removed it if it was considered misleading. In addition, terms that were not of consistent interpretation were modified. The changes made were endorsed by the researchers. The questionnaire obtained was then used for the multicentre validation study.

Instrument

The instrument was constructed with complexity in mind as a multifaceted model that includes the clinical, psychological, and social characteristics of the patients; the extent of the resulting needs and care requirements; and the type of skills required of healthcare professionals [5, 6].

The questionnaire consists of 17 items and is structured in the following dimensions (see Additional file 1):

Patient autonomy using the Australian CP-adapted version of the Karnofsky performance status [10]. The Australia-modified Karnofsky Performance Status (AKPS) is a measure of the patient’s overall performance status. It is a single score between 0 and 100 (by 10 steps, 11 levels) assigned by a clinician based on observations of a patient’s ability to perform common tasks relating to activity, work, and self-care. A score of 100 signifies normal physical abilities with no evidence of disease. Decreasing numbers indicate a reduced performance status.

Study population

The study population consisted of Italian adult patients with metastatic or locally advanced cancer disease eligible for home palliative care, with AKPS ≤ 50 and an estimated survival < 6 months (assessed using the surprising question: would you be surprised if this patient died within 6 months? ), who were capable of providing informed consent. Patients were not included in the study if they had absent or insufficient family support, a carer unable to provide continuous care for the patient, a domicile not suitable to host the patient (for structural or hygienic factors), or were cared for by the professionals composing the comparison team.

The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (1964 and its later revisions), Italian Law 38/2010 on palliative care, and the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR 2016/679). It was approved by the Area Vasta Emilia Nord Ethical Committee (AVEN) 1100/2021/OSS/ AUSLPC data 22/02/2022 and the Ethics Committees of each participating centre. The study is part of a FIN-RER Program of the Emilia-Romagna Region Resolution No. 861 of 14/06/2021 to address the validation of a system for evaluating clinical care complexity.

All eligible patients (or their legal representatives) received oral and written information about the study and signed a dedicated, written informed-consent form covering both participation and the processing of their data. Personal identifiers were replaced with alphanumeric codes (pseudonymisation) and the research database was hosted on REDCap®[17], with access restricted to authorised study personnel only.

Multicenter validation

The prospective, multicenter validation study, launched in March 2022 and completed in June 2024, involved the 8 local Networks of Palliative Care (RCP) of the Emilia-Romagna Region and the IRCCS Istituto Romagnolo per lo Studio dei Tumori (IRST) “Dino Amadori” of Meldola. The 8 RPCs recognised the HPCU involved in the study, which included 60 doctors and palliative nurses who were trained for 14 h to standardise their primary knowledge, as well as over 400 general practitioners.

For each enrolled patient, the palliative care physician, nurses, and general practitioners electronically completed the new CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire and specified the most appropriate mode of management (primary or specialist) by filling out a computerized form.

Each healthcare professional’s judgement was recorded individually without any comparison with the team and then colloquially in accordance with the most probative criteria used in current clinical practice.

The leading centre has created teams composed of a general practitioner, a doctor, and a palliative nurse trained to carry out the study, known as the ‘comparison team’. The groups were composed of 9 professionals who rotated to ensure a multiprofessional team for each assessment with the participation of different subjects similar to HPCUs. Without visiting the patient or knowing the HPCU’s final decision, the comparison team reviewed the new CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire for each enrolled patient. The professionals of the comparison team shared their views on the most suitable mode of management (primary or specialist), first individually and then collectively.

The multicentre validation study was designed to assess the convergent validity of the questionnaire, to calibrate the instrument and optimise its classification performance, and to assess inter-rater reliability.

Statistical analysis

The aim of this study was to pretest and assess the understanding of the new CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire, as well as to enhance its content validity and the reliability of the data.

Descriptive analysis was performed and data were summarised by mean, standard deviation (SD), median, and range/interquartile range (IQR) in case of not normal distribution for the quantitative variables and by frequency tables for the qualitative variables.

Cohen’s kappa coefficient (K) and 95% confidence interval (95% CI) were used to measure inter-rater agreement between two raters [18]. Agreement was considered moderate if kappa values were between 0.41 and 0.60, good for kappa values between 0.61 and 0.80, and excellent if kappa > 0.80.

Internal consistency was assessed using the Kuder-Richardson (KR) formula [19]. A value greater than 0.6 indicates acceptable reliability.

To assess the impact of each item on outcome and to estimate odds ratios (OR) and their 95% confidence interval (CI), logistic multivariable regression model was used. For each item, a partial score was determined by dividing the value of the regression coefficient by the smallest regression coefficient of the model, and the results were rounded to the nearest integer or to the nearest integer + 0.5. The coefficients quantify the contribution of each predictor to the outcome risk estimation. The total score for each patient was calculated as the sum of the partial scores. According to the best cutoff values determined using the ROC curve, two patient groups were recognised. The model performance was evaluated using the Brier score, and calibration was evaluated through the Hosmer and Lemeshow test [20]. Discrimination was evaluated through the area under the receiver’s operating characteristic curve (AUC) [21]. Sensitivity, specificity, and positive and negative predicted values were calculated.

The pilot study sample size consisted of enrolling approximately 4 patients for each RCP [22]. The sample size for the multicentre validation study was calculated by enrolling 30 patients for each item as per the Consensus-based Standards for the selection of health status Measurement Instruments (COSMIN) checklist [23]. This sample was expected to be enrolled over a period of 12 months. With this sample size, the estimate of the statistical concordance index, equal to 90%, will have a precision level of 2% (95% CI 88%-92%, alpha = 0.05, 2-tailed).

No multiple imputation method to handle missing data was applied, and no multiplicity test correction was performed.

All p-values were two-sided and p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical data analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Pilot study

In the Pilot study, the CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire was applied to 35 patients enrolled from November 2022 to March 2023 in the HPCU of the Palliative Care Networks of the Emilia-Romagna Region. The median age of the population was 72 (IQR 61–83) years, they were mostly male (62.5%), with oncological pathological site in the respiratory tract (25.0%) or gastro enteric system (21.9%) and with only one site of metastasis (42.9%).

70% of the items were found to have good/very good clarity for all researchers. The items “Anxiety, depression, anxiety, sleep disturbances, agitation” and “Uncontrolled symptoms and/or frequent/urgent therapeutic re-evaluation of the care plan” were considered unclear by only one researcher (6.3%). 25% of the researchers expressed doubts about the clarity of the item on fragile family environment. In fact, the question raised doubts about the family members to be assessed (relatives, neighbours, carers). The item considered to be unclear by 37.5% of the researchers was the one describing whether the staffing level influenced the choices of the care level assigned to the enrolled patients.

The AKPS (K = 0.89, 95% CI 0.69-1.00) and the individual items of the assessment of treatment complexity: item 3 A: Need for paracentesis, thoracentesis, transfusion, complex medication (K = 0.93, 95% CI 0.79-1.00) and item 3B: Refractory symptom requiring palliative sedation (K = 1.00, 95% CI 1.00–1.00) showed an excellent level of agreement. These two areas also achieved a good to excellent clarity rating for more than 85% of the respondents.

Dimension 2 “Presence of symptoms” reported results with K values between 0.86 and 0.58. Concordance was excellent for dyspnoea (K = 0.84, 95% CI 0.62-1.00) and nausea/vomiting (K = 0.86, 95% CI 0.67-1.00). Moderate level of agreement was reported for the other symptoms (K = 0.60, 95% CI 0.33–0.86).

For dimension 4 Clinical stability’ the agreement was good for item A (Uncontrolled symptoms and/or frequent/urgent therapeutic re-evaluation of the care plan: K = 0.68, 95% CI 0.42–0.94) and item C (Two or more visits to the emergency department in the last 6 months: K = 0.75, 95% CI 0.47-1.00), while item B (Gradual evolution of the clinical picture with increased care intensity in the next 2 weeks) showed moderate concordance (K = 0.53, 95% CI 0.23–0.83). From the comparison with the professionals who participated in the study and by evaluating the structured interview on the clarity of the questions, it was decided to remove this assessment item and to reshape the other two items.

Dimension 5 ‘Psycho-relational need’ returned concordance values with K-values between 0.33 (95% CI 0.04–0.62) and 0.62 (95% CI 0.35–0.89) and doubts about the respondents’ understanding of the content. The items were reshaped.

Dimension 6 concerning the ‘Care team’ was the one with the lowest value and the most doubts about interpretation, in fact the result for subscale B ‘the staffing of the specialist team (insufficient personnel) influences the choice of level of care’ was a K = 0.05 (95% CI 0.02–0.11). Since the objective of the multicenter validation study is to identify the criteria that determine the choice of care, with equal access to basic and specialist CPs, subscale B was removed from the questionnaire but will be monitored as a separate question in order to be able to make observations with respect to the influences that the scarcity of resources can generate on both care choices and care outcomes. When analysing the outcomes, the empirical clinical choice (assessment of the basic or specialised level of care deemed optimal for that patient) detectable by the questionnaire versus the organisational choice implemented will be compared. With regard to subscale A, the maximum K-value detected was 0.64 (95% CI 0.33–0.96) while the minimum value was 0.30 (95% CI -0.06-0.66). In the discussion with the investigators it emerged that the doubts about the items relating to the need for support from the core team were related to the organisational differences of the participating centres, which lead to what was defined as the core team being identified differently.

The CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire proved to be a fast-filling instrument (mean value 9 min, SD 9 min, median value 2.5 min, range 5–15). The professionals involved in the pilot study did not encounter any difficulties with regard to the inclusion criteria, the collaboration with the GPs of the enrolled patients, the way in which the invitation to participate in the study was accepted by the patient and family members, and obtaining consent from the patients. In only one case did the HPCU consider that, although the criteria for enrolment were present, there were no conditions for proposing the study given the high emotional burden of the patient at the time of admission.

Inter-rater agreement/reliability of each healthcare professional’s judgement ranged between 0.83 (95% CI 0.79–0.88) and 0.88 (95% CI 0.84–0.92) for the HPCU team and between 0.90 (95% CI 0.86–0.93) and 0.92 (95% CI 0.89–0.95) for the comparison team (Table 4).

The internal consistency as evaluated using the Kuder-Richardson formula was 0.65, suggesting an acceptable reliability.

Univariate logistic regression analysis reveals that all items were significantly associated with the gold standard choice of care (Table 5). To optimise classification performance, the status of the features considered in the predictive model was assessed, and partial scores were assigned, generating a total score for each patient. The assigned weights ranged from 1.0 to 8.0, while the total score ranged from 0.0 to 36.5 (Table 6). Based on these results, each patient was associated with two different groups according to the cutoff of 14.0: 291 patients (53.7%) had a total score < 14.0, and 251 (46.3%) had a total score ≥ 14.0. Patients with a total score ≥ 14.0 had a probability of being classified as complex with a need for specialist palliative care.

Discussion

This multicenter study evaluated the effectiveness of the CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire in assessing the complexity of patients receiving home palliative care. A total of 543 patients were observed, and the questionnaire classified 46.3% (251 patients) as complex - requiring specialist palliative care - and 53.7% (291 patients) as suitable for primary palliative care (HPCU team judgement). The questionnaire items were compared with evaluations made by HPCU, and all items, except one (burnin and/or pain in the oral cavity and/or/dysphagia), showed statistical significance, confirming their relevance in determining care level [9].

Multinomial logistic regression revealed that factors like pain, clinical instability, and complexity of treatment significantly increased the likelihood of needing specialist care. Pain alone doubled this likelihood. These findings align with Italian legislation, which mandates more intensive support and physician involvement for patients under specialist palliative care.

The regression analysis assigned predictive weights to questionnaire items, yielding high diagnostic accuracy − 79% sensitivity and 82% specificity. Agreement between HPCU professionals and a separate comparison team reviewing patient cases (without direct contact) was also strong, demonstrating the questionnaire’s reproducibility. Moderate agreement (70.9%) was observed between HPCU teams and the comparison group, with further analyses underway to explore discordant cases.

An analysis of 154 discordant cases revealed two data points that strongly differed from the consistent group. For 28 patients, although they had complex needs, the care provided did not involve a palliative physician or increased care intensity. This discrepancy did not reflect a lower complexity but was influenced by variations in care context. Further statistical analysis is ongoing to better understand these mismatches, and patient outcomes are being monitored.

Importantly, the CP.BA.SPE 2025 demonstrated acceptable internal consistency by COSMIN criteria [23], showing it is reliable despite the heterogeneity of the assessed factors.

In comparison to other tools like IDC-PALL [11] and PALCOM [12], which use three levels of complexity, CP.BA.SPE 2025 adopts a simpler two-level model (primary vs. specialist care). Although this limits direct comparability, the results were consistent with global estimates from the WHO that suggest 30–45% of patients require specialist palliative care [7]. Notably, CP.BA.SPE 2025 achieved higher specificity than both ID-PALL [24] and PALCOM [25], indicating a better capacity to correctly identify patients not needing specialist care.

While few studies assess complexity in palliative care, none have specifically focused on patients receiving home-based care. Due to the lack of a standard benchmark in Italy, the CP.BA.SPE 2025 questionnaire was tested against a clinical evaluation by a multidisciplinary team. The unique characteristics of home care likely contributed to differences in results compared to studies conducted in hospital, hospice or mixed-care settings [11, 12, 24].

The study highlighted that functional status, a key factor in other studies [11, 12, 25, 26], was less significant in this context. This is possibly due to fewer bedridden patients in the sample and the availability of municipal social services that independently manage dependent patients.

Pain emerged as the most impactful symptom in identifying complex cases, consistent with findings from other research [12, 25, 26]. The high reproducibility of the questionnaire across professional groups and its clear scoring system make it a practical tool for multidisciplinary teams.

The accuracy of CP.BA.SPE 2025 can improved resource allocation by distinguishing patients who need specialist care from those manageable with primary care. The tool supports more objective decision-making in complex cases and promotes consistent care across local networks. A defined score cutoff further enhances its day-to-day clinical utility.

However, limitations exist. The study included only cancer patients, reducing generalisability to non-oncological contexts. Also, the questionnaire was used only at the initial assessment, not accounting for changing complexity over time. Future research should evaluate its predictive power and applicability to broader patient populations.

Conclusions

In conclusion, CP.BA.SPE 2025 is a valid, sensitive, specific, and user-friendly tool for assessing patient complexity in home palliative care, supporting equitable and efficient service delivery across the care network.

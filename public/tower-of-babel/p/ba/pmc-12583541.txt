Surface chemistry–driven CO2 activation and conversion on V2C MXenes
Authors: Annu Kajal, Kamal Kumar, Archana Singh Kharb, Abhishek K Mishra
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn the present work, we have investigated V2C MXene, belonging to the M2X family of the MXenes, which has a higher specific surface area when compared to the Ti2C MXene. We employed density functional theory (DFT) to investigate the structural and electronic properties of V2CT2 (T= -O, -Cl, -F, -S, and -OH), followed by a detailed investigation of CO2 molecule activation studies. Pristine V2C and OH-terminated V2C showed high adsorption energies of -2.0 and − 3.0 eV, respectively, and Bader charge transfer of −1.69 and − 1.81 e−, respectively. Our calculations indicate that V2C MXene activates the CO2 molecule through chemisorption, resulting in a strong interaction. The OH-terminated V2C MXene not only activates the CO2 but simultaneously converts it into *COOH, a crucial intermediate in CO2RR. Our findings indicate that functionalization plays a significant role in enhancing the properties of MXenes. Overall, our study presents an insight into MXenes as a promising material for CO2 capture and conversion and related electrochemical applications.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-22339-x.Keywords: V2C MXene, DFT, CO2, Formic acid, Electrochemical conversionSubject terms: Chemistry, Energy science and technology, Materials science

Introduction

Rising CO2 levels in the atmosphere is one of the most serious threats to humankind, and as of January 2025, according to the global monitoring laboratory NOAA, reports the CO2 levels have increased to 426.65 parts per million (ppm)1. Rising CO2 levels are driving global warming, climate change, and ecological disruption, demanding urgent mitigation2,3. Conventional CCUS approaches and materials (e.g., activated carbon, silica, zeolites, MOFs) face challenges of high energy cost, limited scalability, and poor selectivity, highlighting the need for novel alternatives4–7. The discovery of graphene in 2003 revolutionised two-dimensional (2D) materials and inspired scientists to discover more. The successful synthesis of Ti3C2 (titanium carbide) in 2011 marked the emergence of a new class of two-dimensional materials known as MXenes8. In recent years, MXenes have attracted considerable interest because of its distinctive features, which include high electronic conductivity, exceptional mechanical and electrical properties, a tunable surface that allows for the modification of its electronic properties, and a high specific surface area that provides additional active sites9,10. Owing to their unique properties, MXenes have been explored for various applications like batteries11,12, supercapacitors13,14, sensors15,16, gas-sensing16, electro-, and photocatalysis (oxygen reduction reaction (ORR)17 & oxygen evolution reaction (OER)18,19, hydrogen evolution reaction (HER))20, carbon dioxide reduction reaction (CO2RR)21–24 & nitrogen reduction reaction (N2RR)25,26. MXenes have been explored mainly for supercapacitor and battery applications. In 2017, Li et al., investigated the M3C2 MXene family for the electrochemical CO2RR using DFT and encouraged the experimental testing of these materials in a moist environment27. Later, Handoko et al., studied the -O functionalized M2X and suggested that they can be a potential material for CO2RR as the -O functionalized stabilises the reaction intermediate, and has low overpotential and good selectivity28. As a result of its rich surface chemistry, tunable surface, and surface termination, it is a promising material for CO2 adsorption29,30.

Of all the MXenes studied for CO2 adsorption, V2C is an intriguing MXene family member because of its remarkable properties. Synthesized in 2013, V2C emerged as a noteworthy electrode material for the Li-ion storage battery, having a reversible capacity of 260 mAh g−1 at 1C compared to 170 mAh g−1 of Nb2C at the same rate31. V2C has higher SSA when compared to Ti3C2, which suggests the potential for better adsorption32 V2C exhibits high purity (> 90 wt%) when compared to other MXenes and outperforms Ti2C and Nb2C in cyclic capacity and reversible capacity33 It also shows a high Li storage capacity of approx. 940mAh g−1 which is higher than ordinarily used graphite anodes (theoretical value 372mAh g−1 )34 Other than that, its layered structure exhibits good stability, conductivity, and hydrophilicity. V2C’s pseudo-capacitance behaviour further supports its superiority in energy storage applications35. Due to all these unique properties, V2C presents itself as a captivating MXene for electrochemical applications.

Moreover, functionalization has proved to be a significant method for enhancing and modulating the electronic distribution, which leads to enhanced electrochemical properties. Fluorine-functionalized Nb2C has effectively enhanced HER and overall activity by decreasing the charge transfer resistance and weakening H adsorption36. It can also impact the adsorption capacity. Zhang et al., found that F-terminated MXene is more beneficial for the HER performance than O-terminated MXene, where the Pd/Nb2C-HF was able to achieve an overpotential of 34 mV, 10 mA current density, and a small Tafel slope of 34 mV dec− 1, which showed it is beneficial for HER performance37. Not only does it tune the magnetic property it also enhances the optical adsorption characteristics. Cr2NO2 showed the highest adsorption of 52.45 × 104 cm−1, followed by Cr2CO2, Cr2CF2, and Cr2NF2. In a study done by Ul Haq et al., on Cr2MX2, the value of the magnetic moment ranged from 3.409 µB to 6.708 µB for Cr2CO2 to Cr2NF2, which suggests magnetic property changes as a result of different functional groups38. Hydroxyl and fluorine functionalization has also led to the expansion of the band gap and improved Seebeck coefficient, which indicates enhanced thermoelectric properties. Oxygen, hydroxyl, and fluorine functionalization have been seen to exhibit superconductivity39. Parey et al., showed that using an O-surface termination on group IV (Ti2X and Zr2X; X = C, N, or B) MXenes can significantly improve their catalytic activity for the CO2 reduction and selectivity for the CH4. Surface functionalisation also allows fine-tuning of the binding of CO2 molecules, which is important for optimizing the performance of CO2 RR40.

In this study, we perform a detailed DFT investigation of CO2 interactions with both pristine and functionalized V2C MXene (–O, –F, –Cl, –S, –OH). Our findings uncover two major insights: (i) pristine V2C strongly activates CO2 through bending, charge transfer, and vibrational frequency modifications, in contrast to many other MXenes surfaces where CO2 typically remains linear; and (ii) on –OH terminated V2C, CO2 spontaneously converts into an intermediate (*COOH), crucial for formate formation. These results extend beyond established trends and provide fresh mechanistic understanding of how V2C surface chemistry governs CO2 capture and catalytic conversion.

Computational details

Density functional theory (DFT) based calculations were employed using the Quantum Espresso simulation Package41. We used Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE)42 within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) as the exchange-correlation functional43. Ultra-soft pseudopotentials (USPP) were used to replace the core electrons, and the energy cutoff for the plane-wave basis set and the charge density were 46 and 368 Ry, respectively (a convergence test is performed and is given in the supplementary information, Figure S1). Long-range dispersion interactions (van der Waals interactions) were accounted for using the Grimme-D3 correction44,45. In addition, we applied a vacuum spacing of 15Å in the z-direction (out-of-plane) to ensure accurate adsorption energy calculations by preventing artificial periodic interactions. For Brillouin zone sampling, we used a Monk-Horst-Pack grid46. With 8 × 8 × 1 k-points for unit cell calculations consisting of 2 V and 1 C atom. For electronic density of states (DOS), we used a denser mesh of 16 × 16 × 1 k-points.

Results and discussion

Structure optimization and electronic properties of pristine V2C

V2C naturally adopts a hexagonal structure, which lies in the P63/mmc space group. We first optimized its geometry and found the lattice parameters to be a = b = 2.88 Å, where the average V-C bond length was found to be 1.99 Å. The calculated lattice parameters match well with the earlier DFT works (Table 1). We also plotted the electronic density of states (DOS) of V2C, which is in agreement with earlier DFT works of Salami et al.47 showing its metallic nature (Fig. 2), and is also reflected in the band structure (Figure S2).

Functionalization alters peak positions and intensities due to orbital contributions from surface terminations, yet no band gap opens, and the metallic character dominated by V(3d) states remains intact.

We next functionalized the V2C with different surface functionalization groups, namely oxygen, sulphur, fluorine, chlorine, and hydroxyl; their structures are shown in Fig. 1. Even after functionalizing, the V2C structure maintains its metallic character (Fig. 2). When we contrast these structures, we find some interesting changes. V2C in its pristine form is a neat, layered structure with the vanadium atoms wedged between the carbon layers. But when different functional groups are added, the surface atoms shift their atomic positions. For instance, when a more electronegative atom like chlorine or fluorine is added, it leads to the elongation of the V-C bond as they tend to withdraw the electron density, which distorts the lattice symmetry and alters the bond angles. When -OH and -O groups are incorporated, it adds to the complexity as hydrogen bonding gets involved, and oxidation can also affect overall stability. These results suggest that functionalization does not simply modulate the electronic characteristics, it also makes a very significant contribution to the structural stability and durability of the MXene, something that might have very important implications for applications in catalysis, energy storage, etc.

Electronic properties

To investigate the electronic properties, we calculated the DOS and PDOS of pristine V2C and functionalized V2CTx unit cells. The PDOS analysis of bare V2C MXene and functionalized V2C (V2CT2, where T = S, F, O, Cl, OH) reveals that functionalization does not alter the metallic nature of V2C, as the band gap remains zero for all six materials. In the bare V2C MXene, the V(3d) orbital contributes significantly to the electronic states from − 3 to 3 eV, underscoring its metallic character. Functionalization, however, causes a shift, concentrating the V(3d) orbital’s contribution between 0 and 2 eV, just above the Fermi level. This shows that even though the material retains its metallic character after functionalization, the electronic state around the Fermi level is influenced, which can further impact the charge transfer and conductivity.

Carbon atoms are bonded with both surface termination and vanadium, with C(2p) and V(3d) orbital interactions indicate covalent bonding. The overlap of these orbitals suggests p-d hybridization, where carbon’s p orbitals and vanadium’s d orbitals combine to form bonding states that contribute to the material’s electronic structure. Understanding the hybridization becomes important to study the conductive properties of V2C MXene, as the distribution of the electronic states near the Fermi energy level is directly affected by that. In a nutshell, functionalization changes the electronic structure of the V2C MXene but doesn’t change the metallic character. This paves the way for optimizing the properties while maintaining their metallic character.

In addition to altering the electronic properties, functionalization also affects the stability of the structure, as different functional groups bond with the surface in varying ways. O-functionalization yields strong bonding with the surface of MXene, resulting in an increase of cohesive energy from 3.8 eV of pristine to 5.49 eV. Similarly, the -OH functional group increases the stability with a cohesive energy of 4.76 eV. While the -S and -F functional group doesn’t have much impact on the stability, -Cl decreases the stability (Ecoh = 2.49 eV). As the size of chlorine is larger than that of vanadium, it induces strain in the bonds, leading to structural unstability. These variations highlight the role of the functional group.

Adsorption of CO2 on pristine V2C

In this section, we investigate the CO2 molecule adsorption on bare and functionalized V2C Mxene (V2CT2). We investigated the interaction of the CO2 molecule on three possible sites: (a) on the top of the hexagonal centre, (b) on the top of the V atom, (c) on the top of the vacant site right above the C atom, as shown in Fig. 3. We tried the vertical, horizontal, and other configurations on these 3 positions and investigated 6 different configurations for each. The configuration with the highest adsorption energy is shown in Fig. 1.

Adsorption sites of CO2 on pristine V2C are represented by a, b, and c, where CO2 was adsorbed horizontally and vertically on each site.

When the CO2 atom is brought near the MXene surface, it binds with the V2C MXene with a calculated adsorption energy of -2.0 eV. Here, we note that the Vanadium atom of the V2C interacts with the Carbon of the CO2, increasing the bond length of V-C (from its initial value of 1.99 Å to 2.01 Å). As the V atom moves upwards to connect with the CO2 molecule by forming a V-C bond of 2.02 Å, as shown in Fig. 1. We observe that the CO2 molecule bends with ∠OCO to be 114.4̊ and the C-O bond lengths expand to 1.46 Å from its original value of 1.16 Å. Here, the bonding occurs via the partially filled atomic orbitals, as the 2s and 2p orbitals of the carbon atom in the CO2 molecule and the 3d orbitals of vanadium participate in the interaction. They interact through a Dewar interaction, in which the metal donates electron density into the carbon’s π* (antibonding orbital). Simultaneously, the carbon donates electron density from its filled π orbital into the available (vacant or partially filled) 3d states of vanadium, leading to π-back donation. Therefore, a strong bond between them makes bare V2C MXene a good candidate for CO2 adsorption. We also performed the Bader charge analysis and found that the charge gets transferred from MXene to the CO2 in pristine V2C. The Bader charge transfer (Δq_CO2) was calculated as the difference between the net charge on CO2 after adsorption and its charge in the isolated state, using Bader charge analysis. A negative Bader charge transfer corresponds to charge transfer from CO2 to the MXene surface, which facilitates bending of the molecule and indicates activation. A value close to zero suggests negligible charge transfer and weak physisorption. We have given charge density difference plots in Fig. 4, which show negative charge transfer for pristine V2C, which suggests that CO2 acts as an electron acceptor while getting chemisorbed on the surface of V2C.

Adsorption of CO2 on functionalized V2C (V2CT2, T = O, S, F, Cl, OH)

The calculated adsorption energy for the pristine V2C was found to be -2.0 eV, suggesting chemisorption, as the typical range for chemisorption is 0.8–2.4 eV, and the CO2 molecule bent as the bond angle was reduced by 180° to 114.38°. In the PDOS (shown in Fig. 5), the appearance of new orbitals around − 9 to -7 eV leads to the same conclusion. To further confirm the activation of the CO2 molecule, we calculated the Bader charge, which was determined to be −1.69e-, indicating charge transfer from MXene to the CO2 molecule. Additionally, the optical phonon frequencies were also computed. The frequencies of the CO2 adsorbed on V2C MXene were found to be 1002, 741, and 730 cm−1, which correspond to the asymmetric, symmetric, and bending frequency. Whereas the linear CO2 showed the optical frequencies as 2539, 1416, 566 cm−1 (as shown in Table 2). The decrease in the value of the optical frequency suggests weakening of the bond strength. The decrease in C = O stretching frequency suggests bond weakening due to interaction with the surface of MXene, and that is often seen in the cases of chemisorption. This confirmed that the CO2 molecule is getting activated on the pristine V2C. The interaction is occurring as the vanadium metal has readily available electrons, confirmed by the PDOS of it in which the vanadium 3D orbitals electrons are near the Fermi level. When the CO2 interacts with the pristine V2C, the vanadium donates its electrons to the π* of the carbon of the CO2, weakening the C-O bond that leads to the increase in the bond length of C-O from 1.16Å to 1.31Å, all of this suggests that the interaction of MXene with the CO2 molecule leads to the activation of CO2.

Building on these observations, we further explored CO2 adsorption on V2C MXene surfaces with different functional groups. For the -F termination, the computed adsorption energy was − 0.1 eV, which falls within the physisorption range and thus indicates only weak, non-covalent binding. The CO2 molecule remained linear, and Bader charge analysis revealed negligible charge transfer, confirming the absence of electronic activation. Comparable behavior was obtained for -O, -S, and -Cl terminations, where the adsorption energies also corresponded to physisorption and no noticeable molecular distortion or charge redistribution was observed. Overall, these findings indicate that such surface terminations do not substantially improve the binding strength or reactivity of CO2 on V2C, limiting their effectiveness for catalytic or capture applications under the examined conditions.

Among all the functionalizations, -OH functionalization exhibited CO2 activation, with a calculated adsorption energy for the CO2 molecule was − 3.0 eV. The calculated Bader charge came out to be − 1.81e−. The CO2 molecule bent with a decrease in the bond angle from 180° to 128.61° and elongation of the C-O bond from 1.16Å to 1.27Å. That shows the bending and activation of the CO2 molecule, and V2C(OH)2, exhibited distinct feature as the hydrogen atom in the -OH group forms a hydrogen bond with one of the oxygen atoms of CO2, which not only stabilizes the adsorbed molecule but also facilitates the charge transfer, indicating formation of a reaction intermediate *COOH (as shown in Fig. 6). This intermediate is a key step in the conversion process of the Carbon dioxide reduction reaction (CO2RR). This means that the OH-terminated V2C facilitated the bond breaking and furthered the conversion process. So, we were able to achieve the activation and the conversion of the CO2 molecule in one step.

Conclusion

In this work, we explored CO2 adsorption and activation on bare and functionalized V2C MXene using density functional theory (DFT). Our calculations reveal that CO2 exhibits moderate adsorption on pristine V2C with an adsorption energy of approximately − 2.0 eV, accompanied by a charge transfer of -1.69 e− and a noticeable bending of the O-C-O bond angle from 180° to 114.38°, indicating molecule activation. The vibrational frequency of the CO2 asymmetric stretch mode on pristine V2C decreases significantly from 2539 cm−1 to 1002 cm−1 upon adsorption, signalling bond weakening. Such a substantial decrease in vibrational characteristics underscores the strong interaction between CO2 and the MXene surface. Surface functionalization strongly influences these interactions: notably, OH-terminated MXene shows chemisorption with an adsorption energy of -3.0 eV, facilitating CO2 conversion via the formation of a *COOH intermediate in a single step. This single-step formation reduces the energy barriers typically associated with CO2 reduction, pointing toward enhanced catalytic efficiency. Furthermore, comparative analysis with other surface terminations highlights the critical role of functional groups in modulating adsorption strength, providing avenues for tailored catalyst design. These quantitative insights demonstrate the tunable potential of V2C MXene surfaces for effective CO2 capture and activation, providing a solid basis for future experimental validation.

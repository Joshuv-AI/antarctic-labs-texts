Dual-scale shifted window attention network for medical image segmentation
Authors: De-wei Han, Xiao-lei Yin, Jian Xu, Kang Li, Jun-jie Li, Lu Wang, Zhao-yuan Ma
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Swin Transformer is an important work among all the attempts to reduce the computational complexity of Transformers while maintaining its excellent performance in computer vision. Window-based patch self-attention can use the local connectivity of the image features, and the shifted window-based patch self-attention enables the communication of information between different patches in the entire image scope. Through in-depth research on the effects of different sizes of shifted windows on the patch information communication efficiency, this article proposes a Dual-Scale Transformer with double-sized shifted window attention method. The proposed method surpasses CNN-based methods such as U-Net, AttenU-Net, ResU-Net, CE-Net by a considerable margin (Approximately 3% \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sim$$\end{document}∼ 6% increase), and outperforms the Transformer based models single-scale Swin Transformer(SwinT)(Approximately 1% increase), on the datasets of the Kvasir-SEG, ISIC2017, MICCAI EndoVisSub-Instrument and CadVesSet. The experimental results verify that the proposed dual scale shifted window attention benefits the communication of patch information and can enhance the segmentation results to state of the art. We also implement an ablation study on the effect of the shifted window size on the information flow efficiency and verify that the dual-scale shifted window attention is the optimized network design. Our study highlights the significant impact of network structure design on visual performance, providing valuable insights for the design of networks based on Transformer architectures.

Introduction

Semantic segmentation of medical images, which separates object regions from background regions in medical images. Since each pixel belongs to a specific object or the background, the category label of the object (high-level semantic information) is essential for the accurate classification of pixels. Thus, semantic segmentation is a vision task with both high-level and low-level attributes. Fully exploiting the long-range high-level contexts in semantic segmentation is critical for pertaining the whole structure of objects. Contextual information represents the visual consistency of different positions of an object. For example, an organ region in the image normally has similar visual attributes, such as illuminance and texture, which differ significantly from those of the background regions. When we classify objects and background regions in medical images, we need to fully model the long-range correlations between image pixels. In the classic convolutional neural networks (CNN) represented by AlexNet1, VGG2, the long-range correlation of images is captured by repeatedly stacking convolutional layers and pooling layers. The parameters of the convolutional kernels are iteratively trained to assign weights to the pixels it covers, and capture the local correlation. The pooling layer reduces the resolution of the feature maps so that the convolutional kernel with a limited receptive field can cover a larger area of the feature, thus long-range correlation is captured. Belfin et al.3 fully utilizes the characteristics of convolutional layers and pooling layers, firstly employed VGG16 for image classification, followed by the application of Mask-RCNN for image segmentation. Specific modifications have been made to improve the performance of image semantic segmentation. Fully convolutional network (FCN)4 used a downsampling encoder to extract the high-level features and applied a convolutional layer to render the segmentation mask. SegNet5 designed an encoder-decoder architecture to hierarchically upsample the feature maps. U-Net6 introduced skip connections to merge feature maps from the encoder and decoder of the same level. To overcome the small effective receptive field of the convolutional kernels, PSPNet7 added a pyramid pooling module (PPM) to the highest-level connection of the FCN to obtain multi-scale features. However, these CNN-based methods are indirect in the aspects of modeling long-range correlations and have drawbacks. Convolution is a spatially local operation, making it too inefficient to capture long-range correlations8. Local operations make this even more difficult when information needs to be passed back and forth between two locations that are far apart.

The self-attention mechanism introduced by Transformer9 is one of the effective means to model long-range correlations. The self-attention first performs three linear transformations on the input feature vectors to generate three sets of vectors: Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V). Then, regularized similarities of the Q and K vectors are calculated. Finally, weighted sum of the V vectors using the similarity values as the weights is generated. As a consequence, the self-attention mechanism is a feature operation at the global scale and solves the difficulties of long-range correlation modeling of local operations. In order to increase the diversity of feature correlation calculations, Transformer introduces a multi-head self-attention (MHSA) method, and finally merges the features by channels at the output layer. Self-attention methods have achieved great success in the field of natural language and have been introduced into computer vision tasks.

In computer vision, previously transformer is either applied with the conjunction of CNNs or used to replace some components in CNNs while still maintaining its overall structure10. Non-local network11 computes the similarity between a position on the feature map and all other positions, as the weight of the feature of the current position. Experiments verify that the non-local method effectively improves the performance of visual tasks. However, the computational complexity of non-local operation, \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$O(N^2)$$\end{document}O(N2) where \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$N$$\end{document}N is the size of the feature map \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(H\times W)$$\end{document}(H×W), is significantly larger than that of the convolutional operation. An improvement method is to embed the non-local module into the high-level layers of CNNs, and only apply it on the low-resolution feature maps, thereby reducing the computational cost required for spatial attention calculation. This compromised hybrid network sacrifices part of the advantages of spatial self-attention to capture long-range correlations. Vision Transformer (ViT)12 is one of the early representative methods for image recognition that fully uses the spatial self-attention mechanism. ViT divides an image (resolution of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$224\times 224$$\end{document}224×224) into non-overlapping \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$16\times 16$$\end{document}16×16 small patches, and each patch is transformed into a fixed-size vector by patch embedding. Then, ViT performs operation on these vectors to calculate the correlation between patches, thus realizing the correlation modeling in the whole image range. In order to maintain the positional relationship of pixels in the image, ViT additionally introduces positional embedding for each patch. Since the computational complexity of self-attention is related to the square of the size of the vector, the MHSA computed on \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$16\times 16$$\end{document}16×16 patches greatly reduces the complexity compared to the entire image. Although ViT achieves excellent performance on the image classification task, large volume of data is required for its pre-training phase in order to perform well.

Challenges in adapting Transformer from NLP to vision arise from differences between the two domains which incorporate that visual entities have large variations in their scale and visual images have high-resolution pixels. ViT is not suitable for serving as a general-purpose backbone for computer vision since it has low-level resolution feature maps and its expensive quadratic computational complexity. Thus researchers proposed a general-purpose Swin Transformer13. Swin Transformer (SwinT) divides the image into three levels: image, window and patch. Each patch covers \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4\times 4$$\end{document}4×4 pixels, and adjacent \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7\times 7$$\end{document}7×7 patches form a window. SwinT calculates MHSA for the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7\times 7$$\end{document}7×7 patches inside each window, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to capture local features and model the short-range correlations, while maintaining the low computational complexity of ViT. Furthermore, in view of the lack of multi-resolution features in ViT, SwinT introduces the Patch Merging (PM) operation. PM uses the fully connected layer to reduce the resolution of the feature map to 1/4, and doubles the number of channels, to mimic the hierarchical structure of CNNs. Since the MHSA calculation only occurs inside the window, the flow of information between windows is disabled. To solve this problem, SwinT introduces the shifted window attention method, which comprises two steps. The first self-attention is calculated in the original window partitioned. Then the grid that partitions the windows is shifted to the bottom right direction by three patches, so that the new window covers the first four adjacent windows at the first step, illustrated in Fig. 1a. Through the cyclic calculations of the original window attention and shifted window attention, SwinT effectively enables the flow of information between windows, making the feature information inside a window diffuse in neighboring windows, thus speeding up the modeling of long-range correlations. Compared with the previous visual Transformers, SwinT has acquired a good balance in terms of performance and computational complexity. It has achieved excellent performance in multiple visual tasks, and has been widely adopted in medical and other image analysis tasks14,15. Despite all the merits of SwinT, after examining the shifted window attention operation, we believe that the single-scale shifted window attention scheme designed by SwinT has not achieved the purpose of optimizing information communication. In this article, we propose a dual-scale shifted window attention method in which the shifted window is partitioned twice the size of the original window. That is, the shifted attention is calculated in a new window with a size of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$14\times 14$$\end{document}14×14 patches, see Fig. 1b. Repeating the single-sized original window attention and the double-sized shifted window attention, our network can realize faster information flow in more uniform directions. This article studies the effect of the shifted window size on the efficiency of information flow from both theoretical and experimental aspects, and verifies that the dual-scale shifted window attention can achieve more optimized performances on multiple medical image segmentation tasks.Figure 1Different scale shifted window partitions. Material and methods

Overview

Inspired by the work of Swin Transformer13, we propose a Dual-Scale Swin Transformer Network (DS-SwinT). The encoder of this network applies dual-scale shifted window attention to extract the image features and continuously down-sample the feature maps to obtain the hierarchical encoding features. The decoder of our network uses the UPerNet16 as the backbone, which is based on the feature pyramid network (FPN)17 architecture and can achieve hierarchical image reconstruction. For our network shown in Fig. 2, we modify the output layer to a more suitable structure for image segmentation. The following sections will explain our network structure in detail and introduce the medical image segmentation datasets on which we train and test our network.Figure 2The overall network architecture of our Dual-Scale Swin Transformer (DS-SwinT). The decoder uses the UPerNet to fuse feature maps of various scales and pixel shuffle to restore the original resolution for image segmentation.

Methods

Shifted window attention

Swin Transformer (SwinT) extends the image-patch hierarchy in ViT12 to the image-window-patch one and uses the window-based self-attention to replace the global self-attention of patches in the whole image range. To break the limitation of information exchange between windows, SwinT introduces the shifted window attention mechanism. It contains two steps. The first step is the original window partition and window-based attention calculation. In the second step, the grid of windows shifts by half the window size in both the x- and y- directions. As a result, each of the shifted windows can cover parts of the four original windows (Fig. 1). We refer to the two steps, the original window attention and the shifted window attention, respectively. After the original window attention, the \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7\times 7$$\end{document}7×7 patches in each window aggregate the information of other patches in the same window. In the shifted window attention step, each window contains patches from the four neighboring original windows. Calculating the window attention in the shifted window mixes information from different original windows. Consequently, information from one original window flows into four neighboring shifted windows. Cyclic operations of original window attention and shifted window attention enable local contextual information to spread efficiently in the two-dimensional space (Fig. 3, information flow direction arrow), so the model can effectively capture short-range and long-range correlations of images. In this article, we study the effect of information flow caused by shifted window partition, propose a measure to quantify the efficiency of information flow, and point out that the single-scale window partition method may not be an optimal solution. Furthermore, we propose a dual-scale shifted window partition scheme, analyze the effect of window size on the efficiency of information flow and the performance of visual tasks from both theoretical and experimental aspects, and propose the optimal network architecture for medical image segmentation tasks.Figure 3Illustration of information flow in a shifted window attention step of the single-scale Swin Transformer.

Measure of information flow efficiency

This article defines a measure to quantify the efficiency of information flow between different windows. To construct long-range correlations efficiently, the flow of information in a two-dimensional image should have comprehensive directions and an appropriate speed. By analyzing the shifted window attention structure of Swin Transformer, we define two parameters, i.e., the type and the connectivity of a window. First, we consider the type of window. As illustrated in Fig. 3, the gray grid is the grid of patches, with a size of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$4\times 4$$\end{document}4×4 pixels. The black grid is the grid of the original windows, and each window contains \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7\times 7$$\end{document}7×7 patches. The red grid is the grid of the shifted windows, the same size as the black grid. The red grid is generated by shifting the black one to the bottom right direction by a distance of 3 patches. As seen from Fig. 3, after the grid shift, each black window is divided into four sub-windows which are assigned to each of the four nearest neighboring red windows. Since the black and red windows share an identical size, and the division method of each black window in the red grid system is the same, we refer to these black windows as having a unified type. Second, the efficiency of information flow is described as follows. A black window is divided into four sub-windows by the red grid, numbered (0,0), (0,1), (1,0), and (1,1) from top to bottom and from left to right, and labeled with blue, purple, green, and orange colors, respectively. Each sub-window is grouped into a red window with three other sub-windows, individually from one of the four neighboring black windows. During the shifted window attention process, the information contained in the four sub-windows of a red window is exchanged. The directions of the information flow of each sub-window are denoted by arrows in Fig. 3. It can be easily seen that information from a black window flows into the eight neighboring black windows in a shifted window attention step. We refer to the number of target black windows of information transmission the connectivity of a black window. The efficiency of information flow is consequently defined as the expected connectivity of all the black windows. In the case of SwinT, the connectivity of a black window is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$conn=8$$\end{document}conn=8. Since all the black windows are the same type, the efficiency of information flow is \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$eff_7=E(conn)=8$$\end{document}eff7=E(conn)=8.

Window partitions that impede information flow

Let us review the case of red window size = 9. Since 7 and 9 are co-prime numbers, the red grid and black grid will overlap every \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7\times 9=63$$\end{document}7×9=63 patch, marked by the blue rectangle in Fig. 6a. Therefore, the two-dimensional plane is divided into subspaces with a size of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$63\times 63$$\end{document}63×63. We consider the black windows located at either side of the overlapped grid division lines, as marked in yellow in Fig. 6a. These windows have connectivities of 3, 5 or 7. The sub-windows of these black windows divided by the red grid are reassigned to new red windows after shift, and all of these red windows are located on one side of the overlapping line. In other words, the information flow of the black window cannot cross these overlapping lines, which is equivalent to saying that the lines block the flow of information in certain directions. Therefore, the square area surrounded by overlapping lines of the red and black grid lines forms a closed subspace. Information flow between black windows can occur in this area, but there is no information flow between black windows inside and outside the area. Since any integer between 8 and 13 is not a multiple of 7, there are periodic overlapping lines between the red grid and the black grid dividing line with red window size = 8–13. They appear cyclically at intervals of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$7\times$$\end{document}7× red window size patches, so the two-dimensional space is divided into periodically closed subspaces. Long-range correlations can only be modeled inside these subspaces, not at the global scale. Only in the case of the red window size = 14, since 14 is a multiple of 7, the black and red grids have no overlapping lines. Thus the boundaries that hinder the flow of information do not exist, and the image-level long-range correlation can be effectively modeled, see Fig. 6b. On the other hand, it can also be seen that a window with connectivity less than 8 is located at the overlapping line, and its information flow is prohibited in some directions. Only a black window with connectivity greater than or equal to 8 has omnidirectional connectivity, and its information can flow to neighbor windows in all directions. Dual-scale Swin transformer

Above all, we propose a Dual-scale Swin Transformer (DS-SwinT), which is composed of an encoder and a decoder for the medical image segmentation tasks, illustrated in Fig. 2. The encoder of our network uses a shifted window partition scheme similar to Swin Transformer to break the boundaries between windows so that the information contained in the windows can flow on the whole image scale. The difference is that our shifted window partition is twice the size of the original window. Theoretical analysis in the previous sections shows that the doubled size of the shifted window makes the information flow faster, and the diversity of information flow directions is unaffected. In the subsequent experimental chapters, we will verify that the dual-scale shifted window attention module boosts the efficiency of information flow and the network’s performance. It is worth mentioning that the double-sized shifted window partition proposed in this article is a plug-and-play method, which can be implemented with a tiny amount of modifications in the SwinT code, and can also be applied to all backbone networks based on window attention.

We adopt the Unified Perceptual Parsing Network (UPerNet)16 as our main part of decoder. Following Feature Pyramid Network (FPN)17, UPerNet uses a bottom-up encoder to extract image feature maps for each layer, and then uses a top-down decoder for the highest-level feature map. Unlike FPN, UPerNet introduces the pyramid pooling method in the highest-level feature map of FPN, thus fully using the global and local image correlation. The output feature map size of UPerNet is the same as patch embedding in the Dual-Scale Transformer, but 1/4 of the input image size. We must upsample the feature map further to output the high-resolution segmentation mask image. Inspired by U-Net6, we concatenate the feature maps of patch embedding and the output of UPerNet to fuse the shallow and deep feature maps. And then, the combined feature vectors will pass two pixel-shuffle modules and 3x3 convolution. The proposed modification is indicated by the left-most connection in Fig. 2.

Results and discussion

We train and test the proposed DS-SwinT model on four medical image segmentation datasets, namely the skin lesion dataset ISIC_201719, the gastrointestinal polyp dataset Kvasir_SEG18, the surgical instrument dataset MICCAI EndoVisSub-Instrument, and an autonomously collected and annotated coronary artery angiographic dataset CadVidSet. These datasets have different imaging modalities, disease types, target shapes, and target sizes. By testing our model on datasets with huge variances, we show that our model has good adaptability.

Conclusions

This article analyzes the mechanism of shifted window attention introduced by Swin Transformer in detail and studies the influence of different sizes of the shifted window on the spatial partition and the patch information communication efficiency from theoretical and practical aspects. Then we propose a network architecture that optimizes the information exchange, the Dual-Scale Swin Transformer. We train and test the network on various medical image segmentation datasets, and verify that our dual-scale network can achieve state-of-the-art performances.

Protocol for delivering proteins, peptides, and shRNAs via the intrathecal route in the experimental allergic encephalomyelitis mouse model
Authors: Anup Bhusal, Kyoungho Suk
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe blood-brain barrier hinders drug delivery to the central nervous system (CNS), particularly for large molecules. Here, we present a protocol for delivering proteins, peptides, and short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) via the intrathecal (IT) route in the experimental allergic encephalomyelitis (EAE) mouse model. We describe steps for developing EAE in mice and administering treatments intrathecally. The insights into treatment efficacy that can be provided by this protocol make it an important tool for CNS research.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Bhusal et al.1

Before you begin

The intricate nature of the experimental allergic encephalomyelitis (EAE) mouse model of neuroinflammation presents challenges in achieving consistent results across various research groups.2,3,4,5 However, our method enables the establishment of reliable EAE models in mice, enhancing animal welfare standards and decreasing the number of mice needed for experiments. Additionally, our protocol facilitates the access and delivery of drugs or other experimental agents to the CNS, particularly the spinal cord, via the intrathecal (IT) route, reducing tissue damage compared to the intraspinal route. Moreover, this approach provides an advantage over oral or intraperitoneal routes, especially for larger molecules facing barriers in accessing the CNS.6

Institutional permissions

The animal experiments reported here require approval from the institutional animal care and use committee. Here, animal experiments were performed per approved animal protocols and guidelines established by the Animal Care Committee of Kyungpook National University (No. KNU 2022–0290). Per the protocol, three to five mice were housed per cage under standard conditions, with a 12/12 h light/dark cycle. Euthanasia was performed as appropriate, and the mice were disposed of in a designated manner.

Mice

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is often more prevalent in women than in men.7,8 Similar gender disparities have been observed in an animal model of MS.9,10 For these reasons, we use female C57BL/6 mice to induce EAE. Female C57BL6 mice were obtained from Samtako (Osan, South Korea).

Expected outcomes

Mice will develop a chronic form of EAE with ascending paralysis and progressive motor deficits starting from the tail and progressing to the limbs within 12–14 days after MOG35-55 immunization. These mice will closely mimic the various aspects of MS, including neuroinflammation, inflammatory infiltration, demyelination, and axonal loss. The spinal cord and brain samples can be collected at different time points for RNA analysis or histology. In this study, PGLYRP1 protein injection potentiated EAE severity, as assessed by the course of clinical scores (Figure 2A). The disruptions of the blood–brain and blood–spinal cord barriers as well as subsequent immune cell infiltration are pathophysiological hallmarks of MS. Consistent with the disease score, Hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining of the lumbar spinal cords of EAE mice showed parenchymal infiltration of immune cells (Figure 2B). Quantification and statistical analysis

All the animal experiments were age-matched. The power analysis was carried out using G∗Power software (version 3.1) to compute the sample size for the experiment.11 The “n” value in the figure legend indicates the total number of animals used for the experiment. Statistical tests were performed using GraphPad software (GraphPad Prism version 10, La Jolla, CA). All results are expressed as the mean ± SEM. Statistical comparisons were made employing one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test. Differences for which p values were <0.05 were considered to be statistically significant.

Limitations

There are some potential limitations to this protocol. The varying onset of EAE symptoms in individual mice makes evaluating the effect of biomolecules or drugs challenging. Further, EAE models primarily capture the inflammatory aspects of MS, and inadequately represent the neurodegeneration and axonal loss characteristic of progressive MS. In addition, injecting different biomolecules via the IT route carries the chances of infection and related immunological responses.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Failure of EAE development and low disease scores in mice (related to Steps 5–9).

Mouse strains differ in their susceptibility to EAE based on genetic factors that affect immunity and vulnerability to autoimmune diseases. Housing conditions, diet, and the composition of the microbiota are some of the environmental factors that can affect the development and severity of EAE in mice.

Potential solution

•Optimizing for mouse strains that have greater chances of exhibiting robust disease signs, such as C57BL/6 or SJL, will increase the possibility of full-blown disease occurrence.•Ensuring that housing conditions, diet, and other environmental variables are uniform across different experimental groups can minimize differences and maintain uniformity in the outcome of the illness.•Optimizing the dosage of MOG and CFA is crucial. Insufficient dosage may not effectively induce EAE, while excessive dosage can cause severe inflammation and distress in the animals.•The experimental groups must be age- and sex-matched for reproducibility. It has been observed that female mice tend to have a higher susceptibility to EAE compared to males.•The age of the recipient mice significantly impacts EAE severity, with middle-aged mice being more susceptible to severe and chronic disease compared to young adults.

Problem 2

The small size and delicate anatomy of mice make it difficult to perform accurate IT injection (related to Steps 11–14).

A deep injection into the spinal cord can result in severe neurological damage, paralysis, or death. Conversely, injections that are too shallow and do not reach the intrathecal region can result in suboptimal or failed effects.

Potential solution

•Appropriate training sessions, practice, and knowledge about mouse anatomy can help researchers improve their techniques for IT administration. This can minimize tissue damage and increase the accuracy of needle placement.•Generally, optimal volume ranges from 5 to 10 μL that should be delivered to intrathecal space. Larger amounts may increase backflow risk, but lesser levels may not offer adequate delivery.•During the injection process, carefully observe the needle movement. A slight resistance should be felt as it penetrates the dura mater before entering the intrathecal space.

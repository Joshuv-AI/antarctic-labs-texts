Giant Cardiac Silhouette in an Older Woman With Idiopathic Chylopericardium
Authors: Koki Hanamoto, Junya Tanabe, Tadashi Takasaki, Kazuhiro Yamazaki, Kazuaki Tanabe
Published: 2026

A 74-year-old woman with persistent atrial fibrillation presented with 1 month of worsening exertional dyspnea. Her vital signs were as follows: blood pressure 108/80 mmHg, and pulse rate 63 beats/min. Chest radiography revealed a cardiothoracic ratio of 83% (Figure A). Transthoracic echocardiography revealed massive pericardial effusion with right ventricular diastolic collapse, indicating cardiac tamponade (Figure B,C). Emergency pericardiocentesis drained 1,000 mL of milky fluid (Figure D). Biochemical analysis of the fluid showed a triglyceride concentration of 980 mg/dL and a cholesterol-to-triglyceride ratio of 0.13, thereby confirming the diagnosis of chylopericardium. The patient had no history of thoracic surgery, trauma, or systemic disease, and the T-SPOT was negative. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography revealed neither obstructive tumor lesions involving the lymphatic pathways nor subclavian vein occlusion. Lymphoscintigraphy demonstrated tracer leakage into the pericardial sac without thoracic duct obstruction, indicating idiopathic chylopericardium (Figure E).

Pericardial effusion re-accumulated despite a fat-restricted diet, and she underwent pericardial resection via median sternotomy without cardiopulmonary bypass. Dense adhesions between the epicardium and pericardium were noted on the left cardiac surface; however, the thoracic duct could not be identified. The left phrenic nerve was identified from the pleural side, and approximately 10×5 cm of pericardium was resected ventrally from this point (Figure F). Although serous pleural effusion continued to drain from the left thoracic tube, the absence of chyle leakage was confirmed under a regular diet, and the thoracic drain was removed on postoperative day 21. The pleural effusion had resolved by 1 month after discharge (Figure G). During subsequent follow up, no recurrence has been observed. Chylopericardium is rare, often refractory to conservative therapy, and may require surgery even in older patients.1,2

LDCT combined with serum miR-4638-3p expression enhances NSCLC diagnostic efficacy and rs6601178 polymorphism susceptibility association
Authors: Zeyu Meng, Ting Xue, Bo Jiang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDespite reducing lung cancer mortality, low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) is limited by a high false positive rate. This study integrates serum miR-4638-3p with LDCT to assess its diagnostic value in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), explore miR-4638-3p’s mechanisms, and analyze the rs6601178 polymorphism’s association with NSCLC susceptibility.MethodsParticipants comprised 152 healthy controls (HC), 152 patients with benign lung disease (BLD), and 152 NSCLC patients. Serum miR-4638-3p and PTEN levels were measured by RT-qPCR; LDCT scans were performed and interpreted according to established clinical protocols; rs6601178 genotyping was performed using PCR-RFLP. Statistical analyses included logistic regression, ROC analysis, and AUC comparisons. Cell function was detected using CCK-8 and Transwell methods, and the mechanism was explored using PPI analysis and dual-luciferase reporter experiments.ResultsmiR-4638-3p expression was significantly elevated in NSCLC compared to BLD and HC groups and emerged as a robust independent predictor for distinguishing BLD from NSCLC. The rs6601178 A allele frequency was reduced in NSCLC relative to HC. The AA genotype was associated with decreased risks of poor tumor differentiation, lymph node metastasis, and advanced-stage disease (III/IV). Knockdown of miR-4638-3p inhibited the proliferation, migration and invasion of NSCLC and validated PTEN as its direct target gene.ConclusionSerum miR-4638-3p serves as a potent diagnostic biomarker for NSCLC. Its integration with LDCT significantly improves diagnostic accuracy, potentially decreasing unnecessary invasive interventions. The rs6601178 A allele confers protection against both NSCLC development and progression. Background

Globally, lung cancer remains the primary cause of cancer mortality [1]. Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) represents roughly 85% of lung cancer [2]. While the 5-year survival rate for advanced NSCLC is poor, patients with localized, surgically resectable tumors achieve survival rates exceeding 80% [3–5]. Consequently, early detection of the disease is key to enhancing patient survival [6, 7]. Screening via low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) has demonstrated efficacy in lowering lung cancer-specific mortality [6, 8, 9], particularly by enabling earlier diagnosis among high-risk groups [10, 11]. Although LDCT has reduced lung cancer mortality, its falsely positive rate of up to 96% leads to a substantial number of unnecessary invasive examinations, imposing a significant burden on both healthcare systems and patients. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop a highly specific auxiliary tool for differential diagnosis to conduct secondary screening of LDCT-positive nodules [8, 12, 13].

Circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) represent promising non-invasive biomarkers, largely owing to their remarkable stability in blood and diagnostic utility [14, 15]. Research demonstrates that integrating specific serum miRNA profiles with LDCT significantly refines diagnostic protocols. For example, combining serum miR-155-5p levels with LDCT enhances the diagnostic accuracy for early-stage lung cancer while reducing false positives [12]. Similarly, miR-17-5p exhibits markedly elevated expression in the peripheral blood of NSCLC patients, indicating its potential for clinical application [16]. These findings underscore the value of miRNAs within comprehensive diagnostic approaches. However, current investigations into these miRNAs are largely confined to phenotypic associations, and their specific regulatory mechanisms in the initiation and progression of lung cancer remain insufficiently explored. Notably, miR-4638-3p is prominently linked to lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), the major subtypes of NSCLC. Analysis of TCGA data for these cancers shows marked overexpression of this miRNA (|log2FC| > 1.5, P < 0.01). Moreover, recent mechanistic studies have revealed that miR-4638-3p plays a pivotal role in breast cancer by modulating the TGF-β1/ATF3 signaling pathway. For instance, a 2022 study demonstrated that miR-4638-3p directly regulates TGF-β1-induced ATF3 expression, thereby influencing cell proliferation and apoptosis [17]. These findings collectively support the notion that miR-4638-3p exerts a critical regulatory function within oncogenic pathways. Therefore, this study will subject miR-4638-3p to further analysis as a candidate biomarker.

While progress has been made in the study of circulating microRNAs as early diagnostic biomarkers for lung cancer, most research remains focused on the expression analysis of individual miRNAs. The in-depth exploration of genetic variation, particularly miRNA single nucleotide polymorphisms, has been largely neglected. Genetic polymorphisms are strongly linked to lung cancer susceptibility [18–21]. Specifically, single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) located within miRNA genes, or their target binding sites can disrupt miRNA expression, maturation, or target interactions, consequently modulating cancer risk and progression [22]. This highlights the critical role of genetic variation in disease pathogenesis [23].

This study aims to evaluate the clinical utility of the novel biomarker miR-4638-3p in combination with LDCT for the screening of NSCLC, and to elucidate the molecular mechanism by which miR-4638-3p contributes to the pathogenesis and progression of NSCLC through targeted regulation of PTEN expression. Furthermore, the association between the miR-4638-3p rs6601178 polymorphism and susceptibility to NSCLC is investigated, along with an exploration of the relationship between this genotype and the clinicopathological characteristics of patients. The findings are expected to provide a novel theoretical foundation for the early screening and risk assessment of NSCLC.

Materials and methods

Demographic and clinical characteristics of subjects

A cohort of 456 age-matched participants was recruited for this study. Participants included 152 healthy controls (HC), 152 individuals with benign lung diseases (BLD), and 152 patients newly diagnosed with histopathologically confirmed NSCLC. The study population comprised patients diagnosed with NSCLC and healthy individuals undergoing routine health examinations at People’s Hospital of Anshun City Guizhou Province between March 2022 and June 2023. Participants were recruited using a consecutive enrollment method. Inclusion criteria were a pathologically confirmed lung cancer diagnosis (for NSCLC group), comprehensive clinical documentation, mental clarity, and the ability to cooperate with experimental procedures. Exclusion criteria were presence of additional malignancies, significant dysfunction in major organs (e.g., liver, kidneys), contraindications for CT scanning, or uncontrolled severe infections. Clinical characteristics were collected, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), smoking history, alcohol consumption history, hypertension status, and diabetes status. The study protocol received approval from the People’s Hospital of Anshun City Guizhou Province institutional review board, and written informed consent was obtained from every participant.

Cell culture

Human non-small cell lung cancer cell lines A549, H1299, PC9, and bronchial epithelial cells HBE were purchased from the cell bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China).A549 cells were cultured in DMEM/F12 medium (Mellen Biologicals, Dalian, China), and H1299, PC9 and HBE cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium (Mellen Biologicals, China). All media were supplemented with 10% foetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells were routinely cultured at 37 °C in a 5% CO₂ incubator.

Sample collection and RNA extraction

Peripheral venous blood samples (5 ml) were obtained from all study participants, including NSCLC patients prior to treatment initiation. Serum was isolated within two hours of collection and stored at -80 °C. Total RNA was isolated using the miRNeasy Serum/Plasma Kit (Qiagen). Subsequent cDNA synthesis was performed using the miScript II RT Kit (Qiagen).

Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR)

RT-qPCR analysis targeting miR-4638-3p was performed using the miScript SYBR Green PCR Kit (Qiagen) alongside sequence-specific primers. Forward primer: 5’ACTCGGCTGCGGTGGACA3’, reverse primer༚5’GAACATGTCTGCGTATCTC3’. The thermocycling conditions: Initial denaturation at 95℃ for 1 min, followed by 50 cycle of 95 C for 30 s, 55 C for 45 s, and 72℃ for 35 s. Small nuclear RNA U6 served as the endogenous normalization control. U6 forward primer 5’CGCTTCGGCAG CAC ATATACTA3’. Relative miR-4638-3p expression levels, normalized to the mean value of the HC cohort, were determined using the 2−ΔΔCt calculation method.

TCGA lung cancer RNA-seq cohorts

RNA sequencing data for 478 LUSC, 513 LUAD, and 91 normal samples were obtained from TCGA (GDC portal, March 2023 release). Raw counts were processed using R (v4.2.2) and TCGAbiolinks. Low-expression genes (sum counts < 10) were filtered, data normalized to TPM and log2-transformed, and annotated with GENCODE v36. Quality control excluded samples with incomplete survival data, FFPE status, or PCA outliers (> 3 SD), yielding the final dataset. TCGA (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/) has original ethical approvals; this secondary analysis required no additional ethics review.

LDCT scan and interpretation

All participants received LDCT examinations performed on a 256-detector row spiral CT scanner (Revolution CT, GE Healthcare). Scans utilized a standardized low-dose protocol (120 kV, automatic tube current modulation with a noise index of 25, and 1.25-mm slice thickness). Two experienced thoracic radiologists, blinded to all patient clinical and molecular information, independently assessed the images. Each scan was categorized as either “positive” or “negative” based on Lung-RADS criteria (v1.1). Any initial disagreements between the readers were resolved through consensus discussion.

Genotyping of rs6601178

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood leukocytes. Genotyping of the miR-4638-3p rs6601178 (G > A) polymorphism was performed using the RT-PCR method. Specific primers were used for PCR amplification, and genotypes were determined via restriction enzyme digestion. Quality control measures included repetitive analysis of 10% randomly selected duplicate samples and inclusion of negative controls.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 24.0 and GraphPad Prism 9.0. Continuous data are reported as mean ± standard deviation. Group comparisons for these variables employed Student’s t-test or analysis of variance (ANOVA). Categorical variables are summarized as frequencies (percentages), with intergroup differences assessed using the chi-square (χ²) test. Agreement between LDCT scan findings and pathological diagnoses was evaluated using the Kappa statistic. Multivariate logistic regression analysis was employed to evaluate the relationship between miR-4638-3p expression and the risk of NSCLC, adjusting for potential confounding factors including age, gender, smoking history, and history of hypertension. Diagnostic performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. From these curves, the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity were derived. The relationship between the rs6601178 polymorphism and NSCLC risk was examined under codominant, recessive, and allelic genetic models. The HC cohort was assessed for conformity to Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE). Statistical significance was defined as a two-tailed P-value < 0.05.

Combined serum miR-4638-3p and LDCT enhances discrimination of NSCLC from BLD

LDCT results showed moderate agreement with pathological diagnosis (Kappa = 0.513, P < 0.001), resulting in 118 true positives, 40 false positives, 34 false negatives, and 112 true negatives (Table 2). Figure 2A illustrates that after adjusting for age, gender, BMI, smoking history, drinking history, hypertension, and diabetes within a multivariable logistic regression model, elevated serum miR-4638-3p expression emerged as the sole significant independent predictor distinguishing BLD from NSCLC (Odds Ratio [OR] = 6.701; 95% Confidence Interval [CI]: 3.987–11.262; P < 0.001). Notably, none of the traditional clinical factors demonstrated significant associations (all P > 0.18). For discriminating BLD from NSCLC. The miRNA test achieved an AUC of 0.883, with sensitivity and specificity of 79.6% and 80.3%, respectively (Fig. 2B). In contrast, LDCT alone displayed only moderate diagnostic accuracy (AUC = 0.757, P < 0.001), yielding a sensitivity of 77.6% and specificity of 73.8% (Fig. 2C).

Knockdown of miR-4638-3p inhibits NSCLC cell proliferation, migration, and invasion

To investigate its biological role, miR-4638-3p expression was profiled across cell lines. As shown in Fig. 3A, it was significantly upregulated in all three NSCLC lines (A549, H1299, PC9) versus the normal HBE line (all P < 0.001), particularly in A549 and PC9. Subsequently, A549 and PC9 cells were treated with a specific miR-4638-3p inhibitor. This treatment resulted in a significant knockdown of the miRNA, confirming the inhibitor’s efficacy for functional studies (P < 0.01, Fig. 3B-C). CCK-8 assay showed that after knockdown of miR-4638-3p, the proliferative ability of A549 and H1299 cells was significantly lower than that of the control group (P < 0.01, Fig. 3D-E). transwell assay showed that after knockdown of miR-4638-3p, the migratory invasion of A549 and H1299 cells had significantly lower migratory invasion ability (P < 0.01, Fig. 3F, G,H, I), and miR-4638-3p played a pro-proliferative role in NSCLC cells. These results suggested that knockdown of miR-4638-3p inhibited the value-added migration and invasion of NSCLC cells.

PTEN was the target gene of miR-4638-3p

To investigate the potential molecular mechanism of miR-4638-3p in NSCLC, we predicted its target genes using miRDB, TargetScan, and GeneCards databases. Venn diagram analysis identified 393 overlapping candidate genes among the three databases (Fig. 4A). PPI network analysis revealed that the tumor suppressor gene PTEN exhibited the highest node-degree, suggesting that PTEN may serve as a key candidate target (Fig. 4B). Sequence alignment further demonstrated complementary binding sites between miR-4638-3p and the 3’UTR of PTEN (Fig. 5A). Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-4638-3p mimics markedly reduced luciferase activity of the wild-type PTEN 3’UTR reporter, but showed no significant effect on the mutant construct (Fig. 5B, C). Clinical sample detection showed that PTEN expression was significantly lower in the serum of NSCLC patients than in HC and BLD groups (Fig. 5D). Similarly, PTEN expression in NSCLC cell lines was obviously lower than that in normal bronchial epithelial cells (Fig. 5E), and its expression pattern was inversely correlated with miR-4638-3p expression in both serum and cells. Following transfection with the miR-4638-3p inhibitor, the expression levels of its corresponding target gene PTEN were significantly upregulated compared to the blank control group. This finding indicates that the miR-4638-3p inhibitor employed effectively suppresses endogenous miR-4638-3p function, thereby releasing miR-4638-3p’s post-transcriptional repression of PTEN (Fig. 5F). This consequently leads to a marked elevation in PTEN mRNA levels, providing reverse validation of the negative regulatory relationship between miR-4638-3p and PTEN. This establishes a reliable experimental foundation for subsequent mechanistic investigations.

Association of rs6601178 polymorphism with NSCLC susceptibility

Analysis of the rs6601178 polymorphism revealed significant differences in genotype and allele distributions between NSCLC patients and HC, as detailed in Table 3. While the genotype distribution in the HC group conformed to Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE; P = 0.849), notable differences were observed between the groups. Under the codominant model, the frequency of the AA genotype was significantly lower in NSCLC patients (6.6%) compared to HC subjects (12.5%) (P = 0.037; OR = 0.418, 95% CI: 0.181–0.964). Similarly, the allele model analysis demonstrated a marked reduction in the frequency of the A allele among NSCLC cases (27.6%) relative to controls (35.9%) (P = 0.029; OR = 0.683, 95% CI: 0.484–0.963). Evaluation under the dominant model (GG vs. GA + AA). Carriers of the GA or AA genotypes exhibited a trend towards reduced NSCLC risk, although this finding did not reach statistical significance (OR = 0.654, 95% CI: 0.415–1.029; P = 0.066). Evaluation under the recessive model (GG + GA vs. AA). Similarly, the AA genotype showed a trend towards a protective effect, but the association was not statistically significant (OR = 0.439, 95% CI: 0.221–1.099; P = 0.079).

Association of rs6601178 genotype with clinicopathological features in NSCLC

Stratified analysis based on rs6601178 genotype (GG vs. GA/AA) in the NSCLC patients (Table 4) demonstrated no significant associations with age, sex, BMI, smoking history, drinking history, hypertension, or diabetes (all P > 0.25). However, significant correlations were observed between the genotype and key pathological indicators of tumor progression: carriers of the GA/AA genotypes exhibited a significantly higher proportion of well-differentiated tumors compared to GG homozygotes (67.6% vs. 51.3%, P = 0.041), a significantly lower rate of lymph node metastasis (25.7% vs. 47.4%, P = 0.005), and a markedly reduced prevalence of advanced-stage (III/IV) disease (23.0% vs. 51.3%, P < 0.001). Collectively, the rs6601178 GA/AA genotype was significantly associated with improved tumor differentiation, reduced aggressiveness (manifested by decreased lymph node involvement), and earlier TNM stage at diagnosis.

Discussion

This research provides the first systematic evidence establishing circulating miR-4638-3p as a valuable non-invasive diagnostic biomarker for NSCLC. Critically, the serum expression levels of miR-4638-3p demonstrate a progressive increase from HC to patients with BLD and further to those with NSCLC. This trend strongly aligns with the notable upregulation of miR-4638-3p observed in LUAD/LUSC tissues within the TCGA database, implying its potential involvement in early lung carcinogenesis. Crucially, multivariate logistic regression analysis, after adjustment for common confounders, identified serum miR-4638-3p as the sole significant independent predictor capable of distinguishing BLD from lung cancer. Its predictive power markedly surpassed that of conventional factors. Regarding diagnostic performance, miR-4638-3p detection alone demonstrated outstanding efficacy, significantly exceeding that of the widely adopted LDCT screening. This underscores its potential as a highly effective standalone non-invasive marker. This finding concurs with the reported high sensitivity of other miRNAs (e.g., miR-1290, miR-29c-3p) for lung cancer detection [24]. Subsequent validation confirmed significant benefits for the integrated diagnostic strategy. The developed model demonstrated exceptional performance, achieving a sensitivity of 86.8% and specificity of 86.2%. This represents a notable concurrent enhancement in both critical metrics, an area where synergistic improvements are especially valuable. These findings align with recent research indicate that combining miR-155 levels with LDCT improved diagnostic precision for NSCLC [12]. Collectively, this study substantiates the clinical value of employing a combined “miRNA + LDCT” approach for early lung cancer detection. This strategy offers potential solutions to two major drawbacks of LDCT: high false-positive and false-negative rates. Mitigating false positives could avert unnecessary invasive procedures and patient anxiety, while reducing false negatives would decrease the likelihood of missed diagnoses, allowing more patients with early-stage disease to access timely interventions. Consequently, it presents a highly promising avenue for refining current lung cancer screening protocols and enhancing their cost-effectiveness. Notably, this approach is particularly well-suited for supplementary diagnosis and risk stratification in individuals with indeterminate LDCT findings. Furthermore, the convenience of blood-based assays supports wider implementation and facilitates serial monitoring.

This study highlights biological phenomena possessing significant underlying mechanisms. The consistent elevation of serum miR-4638-3p levels, coupled with its expression within serum sample, indicates it is likely actively secreted or released (e.g., via exosomes) by tumor cells into the circulation. miR-4638-3p was upregulated in NSCLC and demonstrates high diagnostic accuracy. Further mechanistic studies indicate that miR-4638-3p may influence the biological functions of NSCLC cells by regulating its target gene PTEN, suggesting that it may play an oncogenic role in this type of tumor. This aligns with the regulatory role of miRNAs in key stages of lung cancer development, such as controlling malignant biological behaviours in tumour cells including proliferation, apoptosis, invasion and metastasis [25].

Analysis of the rs6601178 polymorphism’s role in NSCLC susceptibility has now established a significant link between this genetic variation and both disease risk and tumor behavior. Compared to HC, NSCLC patients exhibited a notably lower frequency of the A allele and AA genotype at rs6601178. Under the allelic model, possession of the A allele was associated with a reduced risk of developing NSCLC. Further analysis using the codominant model indicated that the AA homozygous genotype conferred the strongest protective effect relative to the GG genotype. This protective pattern aligns with findings regarding the miR-499 C > T polymorphism and lung cancer risk in an Iranian cohort [22]. Regarding tumor characteristics, stratification of NSCLC patients revealed that individuals carrying at least one A allele (GA/AA) demonstrated significantly more favorable pathological profiles. This association between genotype and tumor pathology is consistent with documented effects of miR-21 SNPs (rs1292037, rs6504593) [26]. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that the rs6601178 A allele acts as a protective genetic factor against NSCLC development and is associated with less aggressive tumor biology and improved clinical stage at presentation. However, current research on miRNA SNPs in lung cancer remains heterogeneous. The variability in individual responses or disease progression observed in our study may be analogous to the findings of Tas et al. [27], where polymorphisms in *mir-219-1 were linked to altered risk profiles in lung cancer, underscoring the heterogeneous impact of miRNA SNPs. Validating these findings requires larger cohorts and greater attention to ethnic diversity in future investigations. It is worth noting that the rs6601178 polymorphism offers a novel genetic marker for evaluating NSCLC susceptibility. Screening efforts can be enhanced by identifying high-risk individuals carrying the GG genotype. For GG homozygotes already diagnosed with lung cancer, their tumors often exhibit aggressive characteristics, including poor differentiation, high metastatic potential, and advanced stage. This necessitates more intensive therapeutic approaches and vigilant monitoring, thereby advancing precision prevention and personalized treatment strategies for the disease.

Although this study revealed the potential value of miR-4638-3p in NSCLC, we must remain cautious when considering its clinical translation prospects. Currently, the main challenges facing individual miRNA biomarkers include the standardisation and reproducibility of detection across different platforms and laboratories, as well as the lack of a unified data standardisation strategy. These factors all impede the establishment of stable diagnostic thresholds. Furthermore, as this study adopted a single-centre, small-sample design, the conclusions may be subject to population-specific biases. Therefore, this biomarker must undergo rigorous validation through large-scale, multicentre, prospective cohort studies before it can be used clinically. Furthermore, in the future, cost-effectiveness analyses need to be conducted to systematically evaluate the integration value of this marker in the clinical screening cohort, and ultimately determine whether this biomarker can be effectively and widely applied in real clinical scenarios.

Conclusion

Serum miR-4638-3p suggests potential as a sensitive, non-invasive biomarker for lung cancer diagnosis. Its elevated levels may help distinguish between healthy individuals, those with BLD, and NSCLC with diagnostic performance that appears comparable to or better than LDCT alone. Combining miR-4638-3p with LDCT may improve diagnostic accuracy and reduce the rates of misdiagnosis and missed diagnosis. In addition, the A allele of rs6601178 may act as a protective factor, potentially associated with lower NSCLC risk and more favorable patient outcomes. In vitro experiments suggest that miR-4638-3p may play a role in NSCLC progression, possibly through targeting PTEN.
